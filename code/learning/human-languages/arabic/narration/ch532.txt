Arabic, chapter 532: A life and its end.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
والد (wālid) — a parent; a father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for old age, then the Arabic for a nanny.

You'll want to know: والد.
والد — wālid — "a parent; a father".
سافر والدي إلى المدينة. sāfara wālidī ilā al-madīna. My father travelled to the city.
الوالدان means the parents; والدة is a mother.

The word, taken apart.
والد (wālid) is built on the native Arabic root w-l-d, "to give birth" (Lane). Walad, a boy, and mīlād, birth, which you know, share the root.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

You'll want to know: ربيب.
ربيب — rabīb — "a stepson".
يحب الرجل ربيبه مثل ابنه. yuḥibbu ar-rajul rabībahu mithla ibnihi. The man loves his stepson like his own son.
ربيبة is a stepdaughter.

The word, taken apart.
ربيب (rabīb) is built on the native Arabic root r-b-b, "a lord, an owner; to rear" (Lane). Lane gives rabīb as a wife's son, reared by her husband.

Lesson 5 of 5.
مات (māta) — to die.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for kinship, then the Arabic for to lead a life.

You'll want to know: مات.
مات — māta — "to die".
مات الكلب العجوز في الشتاء. māta al-kalb al-ʿajūz fī ash-shitāʾ. The old dog died in the winter.
Present: يموت, yamūt.
توفي is the polite word for a person.

The word, taken apart.
مات (māta) is built on the native Arabic root m-w-t, "to die" (Lane). Mayyit, dead, which you know, is from the same root.
