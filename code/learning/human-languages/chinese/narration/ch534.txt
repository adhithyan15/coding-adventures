Mandarin Chinese, chapter 534: I'd Rather.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nìngkě — would rather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give up all hope, then the Mandarin for may your wishes all come true.

You'll want to know: nìngkě.
nìngkě — "would rather".
wǒ nìngkě zǒulù yě bù xiǎng děng chē — I'd rather walk than wait for the bus.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nìngkě is built from nìng and 可 'may', both inherited from Middle Chinese, neng and khaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'calm' and, after an older dictionary, as a word of wishing. The falling tone of nìng is not the regular outcome of neng, which gives níng.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Lesson 2 of 5.
jǐnliàng — as far as one can.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for may your wishes all come true, then the Mandarin for would rather.

You'll want to know: jǐnliàng.
jǐnliàng — "as far as one can".
rúguǒ nǐ yǒu wèntí wǒ huì jǐnliàng bāng nǐ — If you have a problem, I'll do what I can to help.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jǐnliàng is built from jǐn 'to the full' and 量 'an amount'. No dictionary we use records an older form of jǐn as this word writes it, so its history is not settled. 量 is inherited from Middle Chinese ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
假如 (jiǎrú) — supposing that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for would rather, then the Mandarin for as far as one can.

You'll want to know: 假如.
假如 — jiǎrú — "supposing that".
假如明天下雨我们就不去了 jiǎrú míngtiān xiàyǔ wǒmen jiù bú qù le — Supposing it rains tomorrow, we won't go.

The word, taken apart.
假如 (jiǎrú) is built from 假 jiǎ and 如 'like', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaeX and nyo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to borrow', 'false' and 'for the time being': a borrowed case.

You'll want to know: xǔnuò.
xǔnuò — "to give one's word; to pledge".
rúguǒ nǐ xǔnuò le jiù yídìng yào zuò dào — If you've given your word, you must keep it.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xǔnuò is built from xǔ 'to allow' and nuò 'to say yes', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xjoX and nak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to permit' and the second, after an older dictionary, 'to answer'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
万事如意 (wànshì rúyì) — may all go as you wish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for supposing that, then the Mandarin for to give one's word.

You'll want to know: 万事如意.
万事如意 — wànshì rúyì — "may all go as you wish".
希望你万事如意 xīwàng nǐ wànshì rúyì — I hope everything goes well for you.

The word, taken apart.
万事如意 (wànshì rúyì) is built from 万 'ten thousand', 事 'a matter', 如 'like' and 意 'what one means', all inherited from Middle Chinese, mjonH, dzriH, nyo and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): every matter as you would have it.
