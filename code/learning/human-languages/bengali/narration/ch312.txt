Bengali, chapter 312: A Wedding in the Family.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অনুষ্ঠান (ônushṭhān) — a ceremony; a function.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to go round, then the Bengali for packed.

You'll want to know: অনুষ্ঠান.
অনুষ্ঠান — ônushṭhān — "a ceremony; a function".
বিয়ের অনুষ্ঠান সন্ধ্যায় শুরু হল। biyer ônushṭhān sôndhyāy shuru hôlô. The wedding ceremony began in the evening.

The word, taken apart.
অনুষ্ঠান (ônushṭhān) is Sanskrit anuṣṭhāna, 'carrying out, performance' (Monier-Williams), as of a rite; today it is any ceremony or programme.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Bengali for a ceremony, then the Bengali for a garland.

You'll want to know: সানাই.
সানাই — sānāi — "a shehnai (a wedding pipe)".
বিয়েতে একটা লোক সারাদিন সানাই বাজাল। biyete ekṭā lok sārādin sānāi bājālô. At the wedding a man played the shehnai all day.

The word, taken apart.
সানাই (sānāi) is usually traced to Persian shahnāī, 'the king's pipe'; its reedy sound means a wedding in Bengal.

Lesson 4 of 5.
উলু (ulu) — ululation (the women's festive cry).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a garland, then the Bengali for a shehnai.

You'll want to know: উলু.
উলু — ulu — "ululation (the women's festive cry)".
বর এলে মহিলারা উলু দেন। bôr ele môhilārā ulu den. When the groom arrives, the women give the festive cry.

The word, taken apart.
উলু (ulu) comes down from Sanskrit ululi, 'crying aloud' (CDIAL 2358, which lists Bengali ulu, the sound women make at festivals). উলু দেওয়া, to give the cry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
বরযাত্রী (bôrjātrī) — the groom's wedding party.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a shehnai, then the Bengali for ululation.

You'll want to know: বরযাত্রী.
বরযাত্রী — bôrjātrī — "the groom's wedding party".
বরযাত্রীরা রাত আটটায় পৌঁছাল। bôrjātrīrā rāt āṭṭāy poũchhālô. The groom's party arrived at eight at night.

The word, taken apart.
বরযাত্রী (bôrjātrī) joins বর, the bridegroom, to যাত্রী, a traveller — Sanskrit yātrin, 'being in a procession' (Monier-Williams): those who travel with the groom to the bride's house.
