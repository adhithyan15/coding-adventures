# নেই (nei) — the third negator, for things that are not there

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-LEX-C15-SHARI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two negators so far: one after a verb, one for a sentence with no
verb. A third is coming, and it exists because Bengali refuses to build this one
out of the first two.

- [YOU RECALL: two chapters back, the woven strip of cloth — *shāṛi*]

## You'll want to know: নেই
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-AMAR-01] -->

> **আমার চা নেই** — *āmār chā nei* — **I have no tea.**

> **আমার ভাই নেই** — *āmār bhāi nei* — **I have no brother.**

Chapter five taught **আমি ভালো আছি**, "I am well," on the stem **আছ-**, which
means *to be present*. **নেই** is that stem's denial, and it comes as a single
word rather than as **আছি না**, which no Bengali says.

## Grammar Lens: having, in a language with no verb for it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-AMAR-01] -->

Bengali has no verb *to have*. It says *of-me tea is-present*, using the same
**আমার** chapter three taught for *my*. So **আমার চা নেই** is literally *of me,
tea is not present* — and the same shape covers **I have no tea** and **there is
no tea of mine**, because in Bengali those were never different sentences.

**নেই** does not change for the person. **আমার নেই**, **তোমার নেই**: one form for
everybody, which makes it the easiest of the three negators and the one a
traveller reaches for first.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NA-VERB-01, BN-SCRIPT-ACHHI-READ-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which stem is **নেই** the denial of? (**আছ-**, the *being present*
stem behind **আছি**.) Why is there no **আছি না**? (**Because the negator and the
stem fused into one word** long ago.) And how does Bengali say *I have*?
(**With আমার** — *of me*, and no verb *to have* at all.)
