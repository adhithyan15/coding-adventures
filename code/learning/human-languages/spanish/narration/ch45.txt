Review — the present tense, whole.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes for one table that cannot be read aloud. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Grammar Lens: eighteen forms, and you built each one until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Nothing new in this chapter. Say "we eat" and "we live."

Grammar Lens: eighteen forms, and you built each one.
There is a table here I cannot read to you — 4 columns and 6 rows, and it has more columns than can be held in the ear at once. Its columns are: who, -ar, -er, -ir. Come back and look at it when you have stopped.
This is the present tense of the Spanish language. Not a summary of it, not the regular part of it with exceptions pending — every regular verb in Spanish sits in that grid, and there are thousands of them.
Two things are worth reading off it before you close the chapter:
The yo row is the same in all three families. It always was. That is why the second and third families cost so little when they arrived.
-er and -ir differ in exactly two boxes — nosotros and vosotros. Four of their six forms are identical.
So the whole grid is really one set of endings with a family vowel, plus two exceptions. That is the shape you have actually learned.
