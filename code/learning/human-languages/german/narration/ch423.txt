German, chapter 423: Im Hafen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Anker — the anchor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to rock, then the German for the captain.

You'll want to know: der Anker.
der Anker — "the anchor".
Der Anker fällt ins Wasser. The anchor drops into the water.
Same form in the plural. Vor Anker liegen — to lie at anchor.

The word, taken apart.
Anker is an early borrowing from Latin ancora 'anchor' (Lewis & Short), already in Old High German as anker (EtymDB). English anchor comes from the same Latin word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Leuchtturm — the lighthouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the anchor, then the German for to anchor.

You'll want to know: der Leuchtturm.
der Leuchtturm — "the lighthouse".
Nachts sieht man das Licht vom Leuchtturm. At night the light from the lighthouse can be seen.
Plural: die Leuchttürme.

The word, taken apart.
Leuchtturm is built from leuchten 'to shine', an inherited Germanic verb related to Licht 'light', and Turm 'tower', which goes back through Old High German turn to Latin turris (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Matrose — the sailor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to anchor, then the German for the lighthouse.

You'll want to know: der Matrose.
der Matrose — "the sailor".
Ein Matrose wirft das Seil an Land. A sailor throws the rope ashore.
Plural: die Matrosen; weak noun: den Matrosen.

The word, taken apart.
Matrose was borrowed from Dutch matroos, which came from French matelot 'sailor', perhaps first 'bunkmate' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
kentern — to capsize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the lighthouse, then the German for the sailor.

You'll want to know: kentern.
kentern — "to capsize".
Bei starkem Wind ist ein kleines Boot gekentert. In a strong wind a small boat capsized.
Perfekt with sein: das Boot ist gekentert.

The word, taken apart.
kentern came into standard German from Low German kenteren, a sailors' word; it is usually linked to Kante 'edge', a boat tipped over on its side. EtymDB does not list it, so the line is hedged.
