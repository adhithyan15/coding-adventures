Portuguese, chapter 430: A casa de banho não funciona.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pingar — to drip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the coat hanger, then the Portuguese for the bathrobe.

You'll want to know: pingar.
pingar — "to drip".
A torneira pinga toda a noite. The tap drips all night.
Um pingo — a drop. In Portugal um pingo is also a small coffee with milk.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of pingar is not settled; it may imitate the sound of a falling drop, and EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o ralo — the drain (in a shower or sink).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bathrobe, then the Portuguese for to drip.

You'll want to know: o ralo.
o ralo — "the drain (in a shower or sink)".
A água não desce pelo ralo do duche. The water does not go down the shower drain.
As an adjective ralo means thin, sparse: cabelo ralo.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB traces ralo to Latin rallum 'scraper', but the origin is not settled; the adjective ralo 'thin' comes from Latin rārus.

Lesson 4 of 5.
desentupir — to unblock, to unclog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the drain, then the Portuguese for to block.

You'll want to know: desentupir.
desentupir — "to unblock, to unclog".
O canalizador desentupiu o ralo numa hora. The plumber unblocked the drain in an hour.
Built from des- and entupir.

The word, taken apart.
desentupir is built from des- 'un-' and entupir 'to block', whose own origin is not settled; EtymDB has no entry for either word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o autoclismo — the toilet cistern, the flush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to block, then the Portuguese for to unblock.

You'll want to know: o autoclismo.
o autoclismo — "the toilet cistern, the flush".
O autoclismo não para de correr. The cistern keeps running.
Puxar o autoclismo — to flush. In Brazil: a descarga.

The word, taken apart.
autoclismo is a learned coinage from Greek autós 'self' and klýsma 'liquid for washing out' (EtymDB).
