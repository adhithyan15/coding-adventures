Hindi, chapter 367: Praise and Blame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सराहना (sarāhnā) — to commend, to praise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to mislead, then the Hindi for compelled.

You'll want to know: सराहना.
सराहना — sarāhnā — "to commend, to praise".
सबने उसके काम को सराहा। sabne uske kām ko sarāhā. Everyone praised his work.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मेरे काम को सराहा, usne mere kām ko sarāhā.

The word, taken apart.
सराहना (sarāhnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit ślāghate, "praises" (CDIAL 12734).

Lesson 2 of 5.
तारीफ़ (tārīf) — praise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for compelled, then the Hindi for to commend.

You'll want to know: तारीफ़.
तारीफ़ — tārīf — "praise".
शिक्षक ने मीरा की तारीफ़ की। śikṣak ne Mīrā kī tārīf kī. The teacher praised Mira.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
तारीफ़ (tārīf) is usually traced to Arabic taʿrīf, "making known, description", which reached Hindi through Persian. The sense of praise is a Persian and Hindi one.

Lesson 4 of 5.
आलोचना (ālocnā) — criticism.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for praise, then the Hindi for fault.

You'll want to know: आलोचना.
आलोचना — ālocnā — "criticism".
सरकार की नई नीति की बहुत आलोचना हुई। sarkār kī naī nīti kī bahut ālocnā huī. The government's new policy was much criticised.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
आलोचना (ālocnā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit ālocana, "considering, reflecting" (Monier-Williams). Hindi uses it for judging something's faults.

The word, taken apart.
झेलना (jhelnā) is inherited from jhēlati, "undergoes", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5413).
