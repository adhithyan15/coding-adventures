# મારું (mārũ) — "my," and gender again

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The word that makes the name yours — and, like *sārũ*, it changes with
gender.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUN-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01] -->

**મા** (*mā*) + **રું** (*rũ*, with the nasal) → **મારું** (*mārũ*).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01] -->

**મારું** (*mārũ*, "my") is on the first-person root **ma-** — the same *ma-/me-*
behind English **me**, **my**, **mine**, Latin *meus*, and Hindi *merā*. Gujarati's
"my" and English's "my" are, at the root, one word.

## Grammar Lens: "my" agrees in all three genders
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01] -->

Because *nām* ("name") is **neuter**, "my name" is **મારું નામ** (*mārũ nām*) —
the neuter *mārũ*. The full set:

- **મારો** (*māro*) — masculine (e.g. *māro dēsh*, "my country")
- **મારી** (*mārī*) — feminine (e.g. *mārī bhāṣā*, "my language")
- **મારું** (*mārũ*) — **neuter** (e.g. *mārũ nām*, "my name")

Every possessive works this way — three forms, chosen by the noun's gender. It is
the thread you first pulled in the earlier *sārũ* lesson.
