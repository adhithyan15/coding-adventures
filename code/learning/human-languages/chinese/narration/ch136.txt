Mandarin Chinese, chapter 136: Because of the Weather.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
qíngtiān — sunny day; fine weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a match, then the Mandarin for to work out.

You'll want to know: qíngtiān.
qíngtiān — "sunny day; fine weather".
jīntiān shì qíngtiān wǒ xiǎng chūmén — It's a sunny day, so I feel like going out.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qíngtiān is built from qíng 'clear' and 天 'sky', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjeng and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'the sky clearing'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
yīntiān — overcast day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to work out, then the Mandarin for a sunny day.

You'll want to know: yīntiān.
yīntiān — "overcast day".
jīntiān shì yīntiān yǒudiǎnr lěng — It's overcast today, and a bit cold.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yīntiān is built from yīn 'shade, dark' and 天 'sky', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'im and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The first has the same old form as the 音 of 音乐 but is a different word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
guāfēng — to be windy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a sunny day, then the Mandarin for an overcast day.

You'll want to know: guāfēng.
guāfēng — "to be windy".
wàibian guāfēng le — It's got windy outside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guāfēng is built from guā 'to scrape' and fēng 'wind', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaet and pjuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). For the wind the first was once written with another character, which has no Guangyun reading, so how scraping came into the word is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎléi — to thunder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an overcast day, then the Mandarin for to be windy.

You'll want to know: dǎléi.
dǎléi — "to thunder".
dǎléi le wǒmen bù chūmén le — It's thundering, so we're staying in.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎléi is built from 打 'to strike' and léi 'thunder', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and lwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), though dǎ is not the sound taengX would regularly give. The dictionary explains the second, after an older book, as yin and yang pressing on each other.

Lesson 5 of 5.
liángkuai — pleasantly cool.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be windy, then the Mandarin for to thunder.

You'll want to know: liángkuai.
liángkuai — "pleasantly cool".
qiūtiān hěn liángkuai — Autumn is nice and cool.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liángkuai is built from liáng 'cool' and 快, glossed 'to one's liking', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ljang and khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): cool and pleasant.
