French, chapter 400: Autour de la ville en voiture.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le périphérique — the ring road (round a city).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a team, then the French for a birthday.

You'll want to know: le périphérique.
le périphérique — "the ring road (round a city)".
Le soir, il y a beaucoup de voitures sur le périphérique. In the evening there are a lot of cars on the ring road.
In everyday speech people often say le périph.

The word, taken apart.
périphérique is built on périphérie 'outer edge', a learned word that is usually traced through Late Latin peripheria to Greek periphéreia 'circumference', from peri 'around' and phérein 'to carry'. EtymDB has no reliable source line for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'échangeur — the interchange (between motorways).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the ring road, then the French for the outskirts.

You'll want to know: l'échangeur.
l'échangeur — "the interchange (between motorways)".
Après l'échangeur, nous avons pris l'autoroute du sud. After the interchange we took the southbound motorway.
A masculine noun: un échangeur.

The word, taken apart.
échangeur is built on échanger 'to exchange' with the ending -eur. Échanger comes from Old French eschangier, which is inherited from Vulgar Latin excambiāre (EtymDB): the place where cars exchange one road for another.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bretelle — the slip road; the strap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the outskirts, then the French for the interchange.

You'll want to know: la bretelle.
la bretelle — "the slip road; the strap".
Le camion a pris la bretelle pour quitter l'autoroute. The lorry took the slip road to leave the motorway.
Les bretelles are also braces that hold up trousers.

The word, taken apart.
bretelle was borrowed from Old High German brittil 'rein, strap' (EtymDB). A strap ties two things together, and so does a short road between two big ones.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bifurquer — to turn off, to fork.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the interchange, then the French for the slip road.

You'll want to know: bifurquer.
bifurquer — "to turn off, to fork".
Au village, la route bifurque vers la montagne. At the village the road forks towards the mountains.
La bifurcation — the fork in the road.

The word, taken apart.
bifurquer is a learned formation on Latin bifurcus 'with two prongs', from bi- 'two' and furca 'fork' (Lewis & Short).
