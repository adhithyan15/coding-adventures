# Where the asking word stands

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-PHRASE-TUMCHE-NAAV-KAAY-AAHE, MR-PHRASE-TUMHI-KASE-AAHAT] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have said **तुमचं नाव काय आहे?** and **तुम्ही कसे आहात?** for many
chapters. Both are questions. Neither one told you the rule they obey.

## Grammar Lens: nothing moves to the front
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-WH-ORDER]; assesses=[MR-LEX-KAAY, MR-LEX-KASA, MR-GRAMMAR-VERB-FINAL] -->

English hauls its asking word to the front of the sentence: *your name is Aruṇ*
becomes *what is your name*. Marathi does not move anything. The asking word
simply stands **in the slot where the answer would stand**, and the verb stays
last.

> **तुमचं नाव अरुण आहे.** → **तुमचं नाव काय आहे?**
>
> **तुम्ही पुण्यात राहता.** → **तुम्ही कुठे राहता?**

Read those two pairs across. In each, exactly one word changed and nothing else
moved. That is the whole rule, and it is worth having as a rule rather than as
two memorised phrases: it means every asking word you own works in every
sentence you own, without rearrangement.
