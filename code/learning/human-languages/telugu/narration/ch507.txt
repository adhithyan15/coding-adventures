Telugu, chapter 507: Character.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
స్వభావం (svabhāvaṁ) — temperament; nature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for peace of mind, then the Telugu for relief.

You'll want to know: స్వభావం.
స్వభావం — svabhāvaṁ — "temperament; nature".
మా తాతయ్యది చాలా మంచి స్వభావం. mā tātayyadi cālā mañci svabhāvaṁ. My grandfather has a very good nature.
Whose nature it is: -ది స్వభావం (svabhāvaṁ), as in మా తాతయ్యది.

The word, taken apart.
స్వభావం (svabhāvaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit svabhāva, "one's own state, inborn disposition" (Monier-Williams), from sva, "own", and bhāva, "being".

ఆమె వ్యక్తిత్వం అందరికీ నచ్చుతుంది. āme vyaktitvaṁ andarikī naccutundi. Everyone likes her personality.

The word, taken apart.
వ్యక్తిత్వం (vyaktitvaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit vyaktitva, "distinctness, individuality, personality" (Monier-Williams), built on vyakti, "an individual".

Lesson 3 of 5.
మంచితనం (mañcitanaṁ) — goodness; decency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for temperament, then the Telugu for personality.

You'll want to know: మంచితనం.
మంచితనం — mañcitanaṁ — "goodness; decency".
అతని మంచితనం వల్ల అందరూ అతన్ని నమ్ముతారు. atani mañcitanaṁ valla andarū atanni nammutāru. Because of his decency, everyone trusts him.
-తనం makes an abstract noun: మంచి, good; మంచితనం (mañcitanaṁ), goodness.

The word, taken apart.
మంచితనం (mañcitanaṁ) is built from మంచి, "good", and -తనం, the ending that makes abstract nouns; the origin of మంచి is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
అహంకారం (ahaṅkāraṁ) — arrogance; ego.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for goodness, then the Telugu for humility.

You'll want to know: అహంకారం.
అహంకారం — ahaṅkāraṁ — "arrogance; ego".
డబ్బు వచ్చాక అతనికి అహంకారం పెరిగింది. ḍabbu vaccāka ataniki ahaṅkāraṁ perigindi. Once he had money, his arrogance grew.
The opposite of వినయం (vinayaṁ). అహం, I, + కారం, making.

The word, taken apart.
అహంకారం (ahaṅkāraṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit ahaṁkāra, "self-consciousness, egotism, pride" (CDIAL 991), from aham, "I", and kāra, "making".
