## HL-C392 — Telugu teaches the demonstrative pronouns and never the adjectives

Found while closing `TE-A1-L-08`, and filed here rather than fixed there because
**it is a lexical gap wearing a script gap's clothes.**

### What the corpus has

`TE-C41-this` teaches **ఇది** *idi* — "this one, the thing near me" — and
`TE-C41-that` teaches **అది** *adi* — "that one, the thing over there".
`TE-C41-deixis-system` names the **ఇ- / అ- / ఎ-** pattern behind them.
`TE-C42-adjective-system` teaches that an adjective sits in front of its noun
and does not change.

### What it does not have

The demonstrative **adjectives**: **ఈ** *ī* ("this ___") and **ఆ** *ā*
("that ___"), the forms that go in front of a noun. ఈ appears **zero** times in
365 lesson bodies. A learner who has finished this track can say *idi* while
pointing, and cannot say the Telugu for "this book" or "today".

### Why it surfaced as script debt

`TE-A1-L-08` asked for the independent vowel letters, and **ఈ** is one of the
five that has no lesson. The reason it has no lesson is that no word in the
corpus uses it — and the reason no word uses it is this gap, not a property of
Telugu. That makes ఈ different from ఊ, ఌ, ఓ and ఔ, which are also unused but do
not name a missing everyday word.

**Teaching the letter would not have fixed this.** A shape with nothing to read
it in is not a repair; the word has to land first, and then the letter has an
anchor the way every other script lesson in this track does.

### What the fix looks like

A chapter, or an addition to chapter 41, that puts the pronoun/adjective pair
side by side:

| pointing at it | in front of a noun |
|---|---|
| ఇది *idi* — this one | ఈ *ī* — this ___ |
| అది *adi* — that one | ఆ *ā* — that ___ |

The right-hand column is exactly the **ఇ- / అ-** system `TE-C41-deixis-system`
already teaches, applied where the reader has not yet seen it — the same shape
as the gap Malayalam's `ML-A1-PRON-03` and Tamil's `TA-A1-PRON-03` turned out to
have. A rule gets taught, the rule is correct, and half of what it predicts is
never handed over.

Once ఈ has a word, `TE-S1NN-letter-ii` can be written the way this track writes
every other letter lesson, and the vowel column moves from nine taught to ten
for a reason that is about the language rather than about the chart.
