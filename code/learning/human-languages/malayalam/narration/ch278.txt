Lesson 1 of 5.
വളവ് (vaḷavŭ) — a bend, a curve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for on that day, then the Malayalam for meanwhile.

You'll want to know: വളവ്.
വളവ് — vaḷavŭ — "a bend, a curve".

The word, taken apart.
വളവ് (vaḷavŭ) is native Dravidian, the noun of the verb "to bend, be curved"; DEDR 5314 lists Malayalam vaḷavu, "bend", itself, beside Tamil vaḷai and Kannada baḷaṅku.

Lesson 4 of 5.
കെട്ടിടം (keṭṭiṭaṁ) — a building.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a traffic signal, then the Malayalam for traffic.

You'll want to know: കെട്ടിടം.
കെട്ടിടം — keṭṭiṭaṁ — "a building".

The word, taken apart.
കെട്ടിടം (keṭṭiṭaṁ) is native Dravidian, "a built place": the verb "to tie, build" (DEDR 1147, with Tamil kaṭṭu), and iṭam, "place" (DEDR 434, with Tamil iṭam). DEDR 1147 compares Sanskrit ghaṭṭa.
