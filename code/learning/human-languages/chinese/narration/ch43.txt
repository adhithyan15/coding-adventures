Mandarin Chinese, chapter 43: Coming and Going.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
去 (qù) — to go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cheap, then the Mandarin for expensive.

You'll want to know: 去.
去 — qù — "to go".
我去大学 wǒ qù dàxué — I'm going to the university.
The new character, in pieces: 去 (qù) — a shape like 十 standing on a long bar, and under it a two-stroke piece. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
去 (qù) is inherited from Middle Chinese khjoH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to leave'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: qù, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ qù dàxué — I'm going to the university.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for cheap, then the Mandarin for expensive, then say qù again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 去 (qù) mean? ("To go".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 6.
去 (qù) — a cross on a bar, and a corner below.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a cross on a bar, and a corner below and Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
十 大.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shí, dà]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a cross on a bar, and a corner below.
去 (qù).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, three strokes. A short horizontal, a vertical through it, then a long horizontal under them: 十 standing on a wide base.
The bottom piece, two strokes. A stroke that falls to the left and turns out to the right, then a dot inside the turn.
3 + 2 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 去 (qù), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 去 (qù)? (Two.) Which character you know is in the top piece? (十, on a long bar.) What is the last stroke? (A dot.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 去 (qù), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: chūmén.
chūmén — "to go out".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chūmén is built from chū 'to come forth' and mén 'door', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 6 of 6.
xuéxiào — school.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to walk, then the Mandarin for to go out.

You'll want to know: xuéxiào.
xuéxiào — "school".
wǒmen qù xuéxiào — We're off to school.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xuéxiào is built from 学 'learning', inherited from Middle Chinese haewk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), and a second character the dictionary records as haewH, the title of an officer. The school sense is not given there, so how it came to mean school is not settled.
