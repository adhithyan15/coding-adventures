# यहाँ मत बैठिए — a notice is the whole chapter in three words

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C85-IMP-04, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the negative command word and the respectful ending. A public
sign is made of nothing else.

## Grammar Lens: why a notice is always at the aap level
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C85-IMP-02, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-01, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-04] -->

A sign does not know who is reading it, so it addresses a stranger — and a
stranger takes **आप**. That single fact explains the shape of almost every
notice a reading paper prints:

| on the sign | at the tum level it would be |
|---|---|
| यहाँ मत बैठिए | यहाँ मत बैठो |

The second is what a friend says to you. The first is what a wall says to
everybody, which is why **-iye** is worth more to a reader than **-o**.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C85-IMP-04, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-02, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-01] -->

Read these aloud. Every word has been taught:

> यहाँ मत बैठिए।
> यहाँ मत बोलिए।
> कृपया सुनिए।

Three notices, and the pattern is identical in all three: the respectful ending,
with *mat* in front when the notice forbids and nothing in front when it asks.
