## You'll want to know: అతను
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C79-WHO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**అతను** (*atanu*) — "he", **ఆమె** (*āme*) — "she", **వాళ్ళు** (*vāḷḷu*) — "they".

Until now you could speak about yourself and to one other person. You could not speak *about* anybody, which is half of what an exam asks you to do.

All three start on the **అ-** of అది and అక్కడ: the far one, the one not here. అతను is literally "that man".

## Grammar Lens: the third person is where Telugu marks gender
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C79-WHO-03]; assesses=[] -->

You were told Telugu marks no gender on **I** or **you**, and that is still true. The third person is the exception: **అతను వెళ్తాడు** "he goes" ends in **-ఆడు**, **ఆమె వెళ్తుంది** "she goes" ends in **-ఉంది**, and **వాళ్ళు వెళ్తారు** "they go" takes the same **-ఆరు** you already use for మీరు. Three endings, and the verb tells you who without the pronoun.
