Lesson 1 of 6.
la croisière — the cruise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the waistcoat, then the French for to get off.

You'll want to know: la croisière.
la croisière — "the cruise".
Mes grands-parents ont fait une croisière sur le fleuve. My grandparents went on a river cruise.
Une vitesse de croisière — cruising speed.

The word, taken apart.
croisière is built on croiser 'to cross, to sail to and fro', from croix 'cross', which is inherited from Latin crux (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.

You'll want to know: accoster.
accoster — "to come alongside, to dock; to approach (someone)".
Le bateau accoste au port vers midi. The boat docks in the harbour around noon.
Accoster quelqu'un — to go up to someone in the street.

The word, taken apart.
accoster is built on coste, the old form of côte 'side, coast', which is inherited from Latin costa 'rib, side' (Lewis & Short): to bring the ship's side to the shore.

You'll want to know: tanguer.
tanguer — "to pitch (of a boat); to sway".
Avec le vent, le bateau tangue beaucoup. In the wind the boat pitches a lot.
Tanguer is front to back; rouler is side to side.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of tanguer is not settled. It is a sailors' word, and EtymDB gives no reliable source for it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
chavirer — to capsize; to tip over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to come alongside, then the French for to pitch.

You'll want to know: chavirer.
chavirer — "to capsize; to tip over".
La petite barque a chaviré près des rochers. The little boat capsized near the rocks.
Figurative: son cœur chavire — her heart turns over.

The word, taken apart.
chavirer was borrowed from Provençal capvirar 'to turn upside down' (EtymDB), from cap 'head', from Latin caput, and virar 'to turn'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dériver — to drift; to derive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to pitch, then the French for to capsize.

You'll want to know: dériver.
dériver — "to drift; to derive".
Sans moteur, le canot a dérivé toute la nuit. Without an engine the dinghy drifted all night.
À la dérive — adrift.

The word, taken apart.
dériver 'to drift' is usually linked to English drive, reshaped after the older dériver 'to divert a stream', from Latin dērīvāre (Lewis & Short). The details are not settled.
