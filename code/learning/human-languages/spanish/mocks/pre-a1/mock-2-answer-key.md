# Mock 2 — answer key and administration notes

**Form id:** `pre-a1-mock-2` · **Paper:** [`mock-2-paper.md`](mock-2-paper.md) ·
**Rubric:** [`rubric.md`](rubric.md)

Same shape as Mock 1, **no shared item**. Objective items are right or wrong;
rated items use the rubric's four dimensions, 0–5 each, scaled. Accepted
regional variants are listed per item and are **not exhaustive**.

---

## Paper 1 — Reading (25)

### Part 1 — Match the word (6 × 2.5 = 15)

| # | word | answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | la taza | **A** (cup) |
| 2 | el caballo | **F** (horse) |
| 3 | la cuchara | **B** (spoon) |
| 4 | el amanecer | **E** (sun rising) |
| 5 | la bolsa | **D** (bag) |
| 6 | la salida | **C** (door / way out) |

> **Item 6 is the one abstract word in the set**, and it is here on purpose. The
> other five name objects a picture can show directly; *salida* names a
> **function** — the way out — and the picture shows a door. A candidate who has
> learned the word as a shape rather than a meaning will hesitate. This is the
> item most likely to separate memorisation from comprehension in Part 1.

### Part 2 — Choose the reply (4 × 2.5 = 10)

| # | answer | why the distractors fail |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **(a) No hay de qué** | *¿Y usted?* returns a question nobody asked; *con permiso* asks leave for something not being done |
| 8 | **(a) Por supuesto** | *con permiso* asks leave, so the reply has to **grant** it; naming yourself and saying goodbye both answer a move nobody made |
| 9 | **(a) De nada** | *lo siento* apologises where nothing went wrong; *¿quién?* asks about a person |
| 10 | **(b) Encantado** | *mucho gusto* is an introduction; only *encantado* answers one |

## Paper 2 — Listening (25)

### Recording script

Unhurried natural pace. Each item **twice**, eight seconds apart.

**Part 1 (items 11–16)** — one word each, 1–2 seconds:

> 11. pronto  ·  12. el tío  ·  13. la boca  ·  14. bien  ·  15. el plato  ·  16. la abuela

**Part 2 (items 17–20)** — one short exchange each, 3–8 seconds:

> **17.** — ¿Cómo te llamas? — Me llamo Carlos.
> **18.** — ¿Cómo está usted? — Regular.
> **19.** — Pan, por favor. — Sí, señora.
> **20.** — Mil gracias. — No hay de qué.

### Part 1 answers (6 × 2.5 = 15)

11 **(c)** · 12 **(b)** · 13 **(b)** · 14 **(a)** · 15 **(b)** · 16 **(b)**

> **Items 12, 13 and 15 are the measure.** *Hijo / tío / hilo* differ in one
> consonant. *Vaca / boca* differ in one, and the third option *olla* shares the
> vowel shape without sharing a consonant, so a candidate hearing only the vowels
> cannot guess it. *Precio / plato / pecho* share an opening *p* and nothing
> else, which is the same job done at word level rather than syllable level.
>
> Note what is deliberately **absent**: no item on either form turns on the
> Peninsular *z*, because the rubric grants seseo full credit and an item a
> seseante candidate cannot answer would contradict it.

### Part 2 answers (4 × 2.5 = 10)

| # | accept | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | **Carlos** | a proper name, not a taught word — see the note in Mock 1's key. Any spelling a Spanish reader reads back as *Carlos* earns the mark |
| 18 | **regular** | accept *más o menos* if the candidate paraphrases; the heard word is *regular* |
| 19 | **pan** | accept *el pan* |
| 20 | **no hay de qué** | accept *de nada* — the candidate is reporting the function, and both formulas fill it |

> Item 18 is the one item on either form whose correct answer is a **negative**
> report. *Regular* means not-especially-well, and a candidate who has only ever
> rehearsed *bien* will hear it, not recognise it as an answer to *how are you*,
> and write nothing. That is worth one item: a learner who can only understand
> the cheerful reply has not understood the question.

## Paper 3 — Writing (25)

### Part 1 — Look, cover, write (2 × 2.5 = 5)

**Invigilator:** show each word alone for **five seconds**, then cover it. No
second showing.

| # | show | accept |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | **señor** | *señor*; accept *senor* **only** as a missing accent — a plain *n* for *ñ* is not accepted |
| 22 | **adiós** | *adiós*; accept *adios* |

### Part 2 — Dictation (3 items, 10 points)

**Dictate, each twice:**

> 23. **con permiso**  ·  24. **lo siento**  ·  25. **hasta luego**

| # | accept | common misses |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | con permiso | two words, and both are needed: *permiso* alone is a noun, not the formula |
| 24 | lo siento | — |
| 25 | hasta luego | **the silent *h* is the whole point**: *asta luego* is not accepted. Spanish's *h* is written and never said, so this spelling can only come from having read the phrase |

> Each mock puts exactly one item on a letter the sound cannot supply. Mock 1
> used *llamo* against yeísmo; this form uses the silent **h**. The two are the
> same test of the same thing — that the candidate has *read* Spanish and not
> only heard it.

### Part 3 — Write the answer (2 × 5 = 10, rated)

**26.** Reply to *Mucho gusto.*

Full credit for *Mucho gusto*, *Encantado* / *Encantada*. Either
gender ending is correct and the rater must not assume which the candidate
should use.

**27.** Say you did not understand.

Full credit for *No entiendo*. Accept *Perdón, no entiendo* and *No entiendo,
más despacio, por favor*. A bare *perdón* is a real move in the right direction
but does not say the thing asked for; dimension 1 caps at 3.

## Paper 4 — Speaking (25, all rated)

**Interlocutor:** natural unhurried pace, **one** slow repetition per prompt on
request, recorded. Do not prompt with the answer.

### Part 1 — Greet and say who you are (2 × 5 = 10)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | *Buenos días.* | *Buenos días* returned, or *hola*, or both |
| 29 | *¿Cómo se llama?* | *Me llamo …*; accept *Soy …* |

Item 29 drops the explicit *usted* that Mock 1's item 29 carried. The question
is the same and so is the answer — which is the point. A candidate who has
rehearsed the answer to one phrasing should not be thrown by the other, and this
pair checks that across the two sittings.

### Part 2 — Familiar questions (2 × 4 = 8)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | *¿Cómo está usted?* | *Bien*, *Bien, gracias*, *Regular*, *Más o menos* |
| 31 | *Gracias.* | *De nada* · *No hay de qué* · *Por supuesto* |

> Item 31 gives the candidate no question at all — only *gracias*, which has to
> be recognised as a move that **requires** a reply. Item 30 asked something;
> this one asserts something, and a beginner who has learned to answer questions
> will often let it pass in silence. Saying nothing scores 0 on dimension 1, and
> the item is here because closing an exchange is as much a skill as opening
> one.

### Part 3 — Ask for something (2 items, 7 points: 4 + 3)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | picture of bread | *Pan, por favor* · *Un pan, por favor* |
| 33 | someone blocking a doorway | *Con permiso* · *Perdón, con permiso* |

> **Item 33 is this form's hardest item and its most useful one.** *Con permiso*
> and *perdón* are not interchangeable, and the difference is the one piece of
> Spanish courtesy an English speaker most reliably gets wrong: *con permiso*
> asks leave **before** you inconvenience someone, *perdón* apologises
> **after**. A candidate who says *perdón* alone has apologised for something
> not yet done — understandable, courteous, and the wrong move. Score dimension
> 1 at 3, and full marks on the rest.
