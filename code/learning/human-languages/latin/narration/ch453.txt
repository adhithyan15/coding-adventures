Latin, chapter 453: Leader, Knowing, To escort, To find, To track.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ductor, ductōris — a leader, one who leads the way; a commander.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a roundabout way, then the Latin for to wander about.

You'll want to know: ductor, ductōris.
ductor, ductōris — "a leader, one who leads the way; a commander".
Ductor viātōrēs per montēs dūcit. The leader takes the travellers through the mountains.
Third declension, masculine: ductor, ductōris m., from dūcō 'to lead'. Dux itineris is the usual phrase for a guide.

The word, taken apart.
ductor is built on dūcō 'to lead' with the suffix -tor 'one who' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English conductor comes from a compound of the same verb.

Lesson 2 of 5.
gnārus, gnāra, gnārum — knowing, familiar with, expert in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to wander about, then the Latin for a leader.

You'll want to know: gnārus, gnāra, gnārum.
gnārus, gnāra, gnārum — "knowing, familiar with, expert in".
Pastor regiōnis gnārus est et viam scit. The shepherd knows the region well and knows the way.
An adjective of three endings, with a genitive: locōrum gnārus, who knows the places. The opposite is ignārus.

The word, taken apart.
gnārus is inherited and comes from the same root as (g)nōscō 'to get to know' and nārrō 'to tell' (Lewis & Short), from PIE ǵneh₃- 'to know'. English know is a distant cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prōsequor, prōsequī — to escort, to see off, to accompany; to pursue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a leader, then the Latin for knowing.

You'll want to know: prōsequor, prōsequī.
prōsequor, prōsequī — "to escort, to see off, to accompany; to pursue".
Amīcī viātōrem ad portam urbis prōsequuntur. Friends escort the traveller as far as the city gate.
Deponent: prōsequor, prōsequī, prōsecūtus sum. Escorting a departing friend part of the way was a duty. English prosecute and pursue come from it.

The word, taken apart.
prōsequor is built from prō- 'forward, onward' and sequor 'to follow' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English prosecute comes from its participle, and pursue from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
reperiō, reperīre — to find, to find out, to discover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for knowing, then the Latin for to escort.

You'll want to know: reperiō, reperīre.
reperiō, reperīre — "to find, to find out, to discover".
Tandem viātōrēs caupōnam reperiunt. At last the travellers find an inn.
reperiō, reperīre, repperī, repertum. English repertory comes from repertōrium, a list of things found.

The word, taken apart.
reperiō is built from re- and pariō 'to bring forth, to get' (Lewis & Short): to get again, to find. English repertory and repertoire come from repertōrium, built on it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vestīgō, vestīgāre — to track, to trace; to search out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to escort, then the Latin for to find.

You'll want to know: vestīgō, vestīgāre.
vestīgō, vestīgāre — "to track, to trace; to search out".
Canis vestīgia lupī in nive vestīgat. The dog tracks the wolf's footprints in the snow.
vestīgō, vestīgāre, vestīgāvī, vestīgātum. English investigate comes from investīgāre.

The word, taken apart.
vestīgō goes with vestīgium 'footprint, track'; Lewis & Short call the origin doubtful, so which came first is not settled. English investigate comes from the compound investīgāre.
