Lesson 1 of 5.
まくら (makura) — a pillow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to soak, then the Japanese for to relax.

You'll want to know: まくら.
まくら — makura — "a pillow".
この まくらは かたいです。 — kono makura wa katai desu — "This pillow is hard."

The word, taken apart.
まくら (makura) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
もうふ (mōfu) — a blanket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to relax, then the Japanese for a pillow.

You'll want to know: もうふ.
もうふ — mōfu — "a blanket".
さむいので、もうふを もう いちまい ください。 — samui node, mōfu o mō ichimai kudasai — "It is cold, so please give me one more blanket."

The word, taken apart.
もうふ (mōfu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are mō, "fur" (Middle Chinese maw) and fu, "cloth" (puH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ざぶとん (zabuton) — a floor cushion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a pillow, then the Japanese for a blanket.

You'll want to know: ざぶとん.
ざぶとん — zabuton — "a floor cushion".
ざぶとんに すわって ください。 — zabuton ni suwatte kudasai — "Please sit on the cushion."

The word, taken apart.
ざぶとん (zabuton) is Sino-Japanese (kango): za, "seat" (Middle Chinese dzwaH), read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese, and the taught futon, "bedding", voiced as -buton; futon is usually traced to a Chinese word for a rush cushion (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
めざめる (mezameru) — to wake from sleep; to awaken.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a blanket, then the Japanese for a floor cushion.

You'll want to know: めざめる.
めざめる — mezameru — "to wake from sleep; to awaken".
りょかんの ふとんで あさ はやく めざめました。 — ryokan no futon de asa hayaku mezamemashita — "I woke up early in the morning on the inn's futon."
Polite めざめます, mezamemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): こどもが よなかに めざめました。, kodomo ga yonaka ni mezamemashita.

The word, taken apart.
めざめる (mezameru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from me, "eye", and zameru, "waking" (from sameru, "to wake up"); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ねころぶ.
ねころぶ — nekorobu — "to lie down; to stretch out".
たたみの うえに ねころびました。 — tatami no ue ni nekorobimashita — "I stretched out on the tatami."
Polite ねころびます, nekorobimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもが たたみに ねころびました。, kodomo ga tatami ni nekorobimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ねころぶ (nekorobu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ne, "sleeping" (from neru) and korobu, "to tumble"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
