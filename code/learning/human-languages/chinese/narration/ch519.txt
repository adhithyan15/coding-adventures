Lesson 1 of 6.
春光 (chūnguāng) — the beauty of spring; spring sunshine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to put up an umbrella, then the Mandarin for a thick fog.

You'll want to know: 春光.
春光 — chūnguāng — "the beauty of spring; spring sunshine".
春光最好的时候树都开花了 chūnguāng zuì hǎo de shíhou shù dōu kāihuā le — When spring is at its loveliest, the trees are all in blossom.

The word, taken apart.
春光 (chūnguāng) is built from chūn 'spring' and 光 'light', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwin and kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older book, as the head of the four seasons, when all things come out.

Lesson 3 of 6.
四季如春 (sìjì rú chūn) — mild like spring all year round (of a place).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 四季如春.
四季如春 — sìjì rú chūn — "mild like spring all year round (of a place)".
这儿四季如春我最喜欢这儿的阳光 zhèr sìjì rú chūn wǒ zuì xǐhuan zhèr de yángguāng — It's like spring here all year round, and the sunshine is what I love most.

The word, taken apart.
四季如春 (sìjì rú chūn) is built from 四季 'the four seasons', 如 'like' and 春 'spring', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sijH, kjwijH, nyo and tsyhwin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses 如 'to resemble' and the last, after an older book, as the head of the four seasons: every season like the spring.

Lesson 4 of 6.
xuějǐng — a snowy landscape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for early in the morning, then the Mandarin for like spring all year round.

You'll want to know: xuějǐng.
xuějǐng — "a snowy landscape".
wǒ méi kàn guo zhème hǎokàn de xuějǐng — I've never seen such a lovely snowy landscape.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: xuě is said with a rising tone before jǐng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
xuějǐng is built from 雪 'snow' and jǐng 'a view', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjwet and kjaengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older book, as rain that has set, and the second 'bright' and 'an image'.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: chéngliáng.
chéngliáng — "to sit in the cool (out of the sun)".
xiàtiān de wǎnshang dàjiā dōu zài wàibian chéngliáng — On summer evenings everyone sits outside in the cool.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chéngliáng is built from chéng 'to ride on' and liáng 'cool', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zying and ljang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to drive' and 'to mount' and the second 'thin' and 'cold': to take the cool air.
