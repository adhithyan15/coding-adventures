Urdu, chapter 494: Looking for Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
انٹرویو (inṭarvyū) — an interview (for a job).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a fruit seller, then the Urdu for a greengrocer.

You'll want to know: انٹرویو.
انٹرویو — inṭarvyū — "an interview (for a job)".
کل میرا ایک کمپنی میں انٹرویو ہے۔ kal merā ek kampanī meṅ inṭarvyū hai. Tomorrow I have an interview at a company.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
انٹرویو (inṭarvyū) is borrowed from English interview.

Lesson 2 of 5.
روزگار (rozgār) — employment; a livelihood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a greengrocer, then the Urdu for an interview (for a job).

You'll want to know: روزگار.
روزگار — rozgār — "employment; a livelihood".
اس شہر میں روزگار کم ہے۔ is shahr meṅ rozgār kam hai. There is little work to be had in this city.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
روزگار (rozgār) is usually traced to Persian rōzgār, "the days, the times", built on rōz, "a day"; the sense "a livelihood" is the one Urdu uses.

Lesson 3 of 5.
اسامی (asāmī) — a vacancy (a post to fill).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an interview (for a job), then the Urdu for employment.

You'll want to know: اسامی.
اسامی — asāmī — "a vacancy (a post to fill)".
دفتر میں ایک اسامی خالی ہے۔ daftar meṅ ek asāmī khālī hai. There is a vacancy in the office.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
اسامی (asāmī) is usually traced to Arabic asāmī, "names", the plural of ism, "a name"; the sense "a post to be filled" came later, and the sources we use do not show how it arose.

Lesson 4 of 5.
برطرف (bartaraf) — dismissed from a job.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for employment, then the Urdu for a vacancy.

You'll want to know: برطرف.
برطرف — bartaraf — "dismissed from a job".
کمپنی نے دو ملازموں کو برطرف کیا۔ kampanī ne do mulāzimoṅ ko bartaraf kiyā. The company dismissed two employees.

The word, taken apart.
برطرف (bartaraf) is usually traced to Persian bar-taraf, "set aside", built from bar, "on, to", and taraf, "a side", the word Urdu also has as طرف (taraf); Urdu uses it for someone put out of a job.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بھرتی کرنا (bhartī karnā) — to recruit; to take on (staff).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a vacancy, then the Urdu for dismissed from a job.

You'll want to know: بھرتی کرنا.
بھرتی کرنا — bhartī karnā — "to recruit; to take on (staff)".
کارخانے نے دس نئے مزدور بھرتی کیے۔ kārkhāne ne das naye mazdūr bhartī kiye. The factory took on ten new labourers.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے دو آدمی بھرتی کیے, us ne do ādmī bhartī kiye.

The word, taken apart.
بھرتی کرنا (bhartī karnā) is built from bhartī, "filling up; an intake", on the stem of bharnā, "to fill", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit bharati, "bears, fills" (CDIAL 9397), and karnā, "to do".
