## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C51-CITY-03]; assesses=[] -->

**medicus** — "**a doctor**."

Not, as you might expect, from a word for healing. **Medicus** is from *medeor*,
"I attend to," and behind that is Proto-Indo-European ***\*med-***, "to measure,
to take the appropriate measures." A doctor, to a Roman, is someone who works out
the right amount.

The root spread widely on that meaning:

- **meditate** — to measure a thing over in the mind.
- **moderate** and **modest** — kept within measure.
- **mete**, as in "mete out" — an inherited English **cousin**, not a loan.
- **remedy** — *re-* plus *medeor*, attending to a thing again.

Every Romance language kept the word: Spanish *médico*, Italian *medico*,
Portuguese *médico*. English took **medicine**, **medical** and **medic** from the
same family, and French gave us **medico** back as slang.
