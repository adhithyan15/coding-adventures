# el helado — something frozen becomes a treat

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C370-WHERE-53] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall **el hielo**, ice. Its cold family leads to **el helado**,
ice cream.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-HELADO]; assesses=[] -->

**Helado** is the participle of **helar**, to freeze: its first sense is
“frozen.” Used as a noun with **el**, it names ice cream. **Hielo**, **helar**
and **helado** all continue the Latin cold-family already met in the ice
lesson. The silent **h** links their modern spelling even though it makes no
sound.

## Grammar Lens: thing or description?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HELADO] -->

With an article, **el helado** is the food. After a noun, **helado / helada**
can describe something frozen: **agua helada**. For the mock café message,
listen for the noun **un helado**.
