Hindi, chapter 341: Turning the Pages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पन्ना (pannā) — a page; a leaf (of a book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a chapter of a book, then the Hindi for an outcome.

You'll want to know: पन्ना.
पन्ना — pannā — "a page; a leaf (of a book)".
बारिश में इस किताब के कुछ पन्ने फट गए। bāriś meṁ is kitāb ke kuch panne phaṭ gae. Some pages of this book got torn in the rain.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
पन्ना (pannā) goes back to Sanskrit parṇa, "a leaf" (CDIAL 7918), where CDIAL gives Hindi pannā, a leaf or a page. A page of a book is a leaf, as in English.

पुरानी किताब की जिल्द टूट गई। purānī kitāb kī jild ṭūṭ gaī. The old book's binding came apart.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
जिल्द (jild) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic jild, "skin, hide": books were once bound in leather.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बिकना (biknā) — to be sold; to sell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a title, then the Hindi for a reader.

You'll want to know: बिकना.
बिकना — biknā — "to be sold; to sell".
पहले हफ़्ते में ही वह उपन्यास बहुत बिका। pahle hafte meṁ hī vah upanyās bahut bikā. The novel sold very well in its very first week.
Past (it …): वह किताब बिक गई, vah kitāb bik gaī.

The word, taken apart.
बिकना (biknā) goes back to Sanskrit vikrīyate, "is sold" (CDIAL 11642). It is the intransitive partner of बेचना: you बेचते हो a book, and the book बिकती है.
