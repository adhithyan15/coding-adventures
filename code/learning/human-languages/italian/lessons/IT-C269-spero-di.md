# spero di — saying what you are reaching for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-CONTENTO-DI-02, IT-LEX-C86-VOLERE86-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I'm happy to be here". (*Sono felice di essere qui.*) Then "I
would like to travel". (*Vorrei viaggiare.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a past and a future. Last lesson was how it felt;
this one is what you want next.

## You'll want to know: spero di
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-SPERO-DI-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C86-VOLERE86-04, IT-LEX-C60-VERBI5-03, IT-LEX-C49-VERBI3-04] -->

**spero di** + infinitive — "I hope to".

> **Spero di** viaggiare. — I hope to travel.
> **Spero di** imparare bene l'italiano. — I hope to learn Italian well.

You have **sperare**, "to hope"; **spero** is its "I" form. The **di** is the
same small word as in *contento di* and *credo di sì*: it hooks the infinitive
on, and Italian will not leave it out. English says *I hope to*; Italian says
*spero di*.

The *di* works when the hoper and the doer are the same person. Hoping
something for **someone else** takes *spero che* and the *congiuntivo*, which
is a later chapter's work.

## Grammar Lens: il mio sogno è
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-IL-MIO-SOGNO-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C152-SAL152-05, IT-LEX-C259-LAV259-01] -->

An ambition is bigger than a hope, and it has its own frame:

> **Il mio sogno è** vivere al mare. — My dream is to live by the sea.
> **Il mio sogno è** studiare all'università. — My dream is to study at
> university.

**il sogno**, "the dream", you have; the article stays in front of *mio*, as
it has throughout this book. After *è* the infinitive follows bare, with no
*di*: the dream **is** the doing.

Three strengths, then, for the future: *vorrei viaggiare*, I'd like to;
*spero di viaggiare*, I hope to; *il mio sogno è viaggiare*, it is my dream.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**sperare** is inherited from Latin *spērāre*, "to hope, to expect" (EtymDB
2.1; Lewis & Short, under *spero*). English **desperate** is the same verb
with *dē-* in front, "hope taken away", and **la speranza**, "hope", which you
met in chapter one hundred and eighty-six, is its noun.
