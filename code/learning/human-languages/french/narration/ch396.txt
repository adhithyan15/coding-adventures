French, chapter 396: Installer un logiciel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
installer — to install; to set up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to connect, then the French for to access.

You'll want to know: installer.
installer — "to install; to set up".
J'ai installé une nouvelle application sur mon téléphone. I installed a new app on my phone.
S'installer — to settle in, to move in.

The word, taken apart.
installer is usually traced to Medieval Latin installāre "to put into a stall, to install in office", built on a Germanic word for a stall; EtymDB lists relatives such as Old English steall "a standing place".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: l'appareil.
l'appareil — "the device, the appliance".
Cet appareil ne marche plus. This device does not work any more.
Un appareil photo — a camera. On the phone, qui est à l'appareil? — who is speaking? Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
appareil is formed on appareiller "to prepare, to fit out" (EtymDB), from a Vulgar Latin appariculāre, built on Latin parāre "to prepare" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le logiciel — the software, the program.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to uninstall, then the French for the device.

You'll want to know: le logiciel.
le logiciel — "the software, the program".
Ce logiciel est gratuit. This software is free.
Québec also says le programme.

The word, taken apart.
logiciel is usually described as a French coinage of the twentieth century from logique "logic" and -iel, made to replace English software. logique comes from Greek logikḗ "the art of reasoning".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la mise à jour — the update.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the device, then the French for the software.

You'll want to know: la mise à jour.
la mise à jour — "the update".
Mon téléphone fait une mise à jour cette nuit. My phone is doing an update tonight.
Mettre à jour — to update. Plural les mises à jour.

The word, taken apart.
mise à jour is a putting up to date. mise is the participle of mettre, from Latin mittere "to send, to put", and jour is inherited from Latin diurnum "daily" (EtymDB).
