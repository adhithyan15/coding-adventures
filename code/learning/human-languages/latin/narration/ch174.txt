Lesson 1 of 5.
patientia, patientiae — patience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the liver, then the Latin for a kidney.

You'll want to know: patientia, patientiae.
patientia, patientiae — "patience".

The word, taken apart.
patientia is built on patior, "to suffer" (Lewis & Short), through patiēns, "enduring", with -ia, an ending for a quality.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lacrima, lacrimae — a tear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a kidney, then the Latin for patience.

You'll want to know: lacrima, lacrimae.
lacrima, lacrimae — "a tear".

The word, taken apart.
lacrima is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dakru-, "tear" (EtymDB 2.1). Old Latin had dacruma with d (Lewis & Short); Greek dakry, "tear", and English tear are cousins.

Lesson 5 of 5.
animus, animī — mind, spirit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a laugh, then the Latin for a voice.

You'll want to know: animus, animī.
animus, animī — "mind, spirit".

The word, taken apart.
animus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2enh1mos, "breath", from h2enh1-, "to breathe" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek anemos, "wind", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). anima, "breath, soul", is its partner.
