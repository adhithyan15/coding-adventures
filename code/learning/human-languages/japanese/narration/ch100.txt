ごみ (gomi) — rubbish.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ごみ.
ごみ — gomi — "rubbish".

The word, taken apart.
ごみ (gomi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
はさみ (hasami) — scissors.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a stick, then the Japanese for a board.

You'll want to know: はさみ.
はさみ — hasami — "scissors".

The word, taken apart.
はさみ (hasami) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, usually taken as the noun of hasamu, "to hold between", a verb JMdict lists. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ざっし (zasshi) — a magazine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a board, then the Japanese for scissors.

You'll want to know: ざっし.
ざっし — zasshi — "a magazine".

The word, taken apart.
ざっし (zasshi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are zatsu, "mixed" (Middle Chinese dzop) and shi, "record" (tsyiH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). Zatsu is shortened to zas before shi.
