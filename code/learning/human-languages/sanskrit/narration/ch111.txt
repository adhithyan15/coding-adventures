Lesson 1 of 5.
वात्या (vātyā) — a storm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lightning, then the Sanskrit for thunder.

You'll want to know: वात्या.
वात्या — vātyā — "a storm".

The word, taken apart.
vātyā "a strong wind, a gale" is built on vāta "wind" (CDIAL 11505), from the root √vā "blow". MW sets √vā beside Latin ventus and English wind: an inherited Indo-European root.

Lesson 2 of 5.
नीहारः (nīhāraḥ) — mist, fog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for thunder, then the Sanskrit for a storm.

You'll want to know: नीहारः.
नीहारः — nīhāraḥ — "mist, fog".

The word, taken apart.
nīhāra "mist, fog, hoar-frost" is old, in the Rigveda. MW splits it as nī- "down" + hāra, pointing to the verb ni-√hṛ, but does not explain the sense, so its formation is uncertain. Hindi kept it as nīhār "fog, mist" (CDIAL 7574).

You'll want to know: शाखा.
शाखा — śākhā — "a branch".

The word, taken apart.
śākhā "a branch" is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB). Its cousins include Lithuanian šakà "branch" and Persian shākh "branch, horn" (EtymDB), and Pali kept it as sākhā (CDIAL 12376).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पर्णम् (parṇam) — a leaf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for mist, then the Sanskrit for a branch.

You'll want to know: पर्णम्.
पर्णम् — parṇam — "a leaf".

The word, taken apart.
parṇa first meant "a feather, a wing", and then "a leaf", the plumage of a tree (MW). It is inherited from Indo-European, and English fern is a cousin (EtymDB). It lives on as Hindi pān "leaf, betel leaf" (CDIAL 7918).

You'll want to know: कुसुमम्.
कुसुमम् — kusumam — "a flower".

The word, taken apart.
kusuma "a flower" appears from Manu on; MW cites only a grammarians' root √kus for it, so its origin is uncertain. Pali kept it unchanged as kusuma (CDIAL 3377).
