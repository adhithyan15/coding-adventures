# -ಅನ್ನು (-annu) — the person or particular thing an action reaches

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C104-JOBS-03, KA-LEX-C104-JOBS-05, KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-STACKING-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Your first visible case ending was **-ಗೆ**: one piece on the end for
one job. Say the Kannada for the police and for a shop. This lesson gives a
second ending and one new job.

## Grammar Lens: -ಅನ್ನು marks the object
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C104-ACCUSATIVE-ANNU-06]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C104-JOBS-03, KA-LEX-C101-OPEN-04] -->

> **ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು ಕರೆಯಿರಿ**
>
> *polīsarannu kareyiri* — **please call the police**

The calling reaches **ಪೊಲೀಸರು** (*polīsaru*), “the police”. Kannada puts
**-ಅನ್ನು** (*-annu*) on that object: **ಪೊಲೀಸರು → ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು**. The final sound
joins the ending, but the job stays visible.

With a person, the object ending is required. With a non-human thing, it often
marks a particular thing already in view:

| the word | as the object | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ಪೊಲೀಸರು *polīsaru* | ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು *polīsarannu* | the police, as the people called |
| ಅಂಗಡಿ *aṅgaḍi* | ಅಂಗಡಿಯನ್ನು *aṅgaḍiyannu* | the particular shop, as the thing acted on |

This is the **accusative**, the direct-object case. Keep the practical question:
**who or what does the action reach?** Put its marker on that word.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C104-ACCUSATIVE-ANNU-06, KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-STACKING-01] -->

Two case endings, two jobs: **-ಗೆ** points to a receiver or experiencer;
**-ಅನ್ನು** marks the object an action reaches. Kannada keeps the jobs in
separate pieces rather than hiding both inside one ending.
