Lesson 1 of 5.
ਘਾਹ (ghāh) — grass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for cumin, then the Punjabi for a fritter.

You'll want to know: ਘਾਹ.
ਘਾਹ — ghāh — "grass".

The word, taken apart.
ਘਾਹ (ghāh) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghāsa, "food, pasture grass" (CDIAL 4471).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ਬੀਜ (bīj) — a seed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a fritter, then the Punjabi for grass.

You'll want to know: ਬੀਜ.
ਬੀਜ — bīj — "a seed".

The word, taken apart.
ਬੀਜ (bīj) is a semi-tatsama: Sanskrit bīja, "seed, semen", borrowed after the old sound changes had run their course and then lightly reshaped (CDIAL 9250). CDIAL lists the inherited Punjabi form as bī.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਜੜ੍ਹ (jaṛh) — a root.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for grass, then the Punjabi for a seed.

You'll want to know: ਜੜ੍ਹ.
ਜੜ੍ਹ — jaṛh — "a root".

The word, taken apart.
ਜੜ੍ਹ (jaṛh) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from jaṭā, "hair twisted together; fibrous root" (CDIAL 5086). CDIAL reports views that the Sanskrit word is from Dravidian, or ultimately from Munda.
