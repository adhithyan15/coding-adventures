## Grammar Lens: a list that was closed, reopened on purpose
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-01, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-03] -->

The wardrobe chapter called its list covered end to end. It was —
for what that exam asked. *Pantalón*, *falda*, *camisa*, *zapato*, *vaqueros*,
*jersey*, *bolso*: enough to say what you are wearing.

What it could not do is answer a question about **weather**.

| the old list | this chapter |
|---|---|
| what you are wearing | what the cold makes you wear |

That is the difference between the two exams. The first asks you to describe;
the second asks you to decide, and deciding needs a number. *Hace frío* was
never going to be enough once somebody writes *estamos a dos grados* on a
screen at a bus stop.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-04] -->

Two of the four have a settled origin, a third has a guess, and the fourth
refuses.

| word | underneath |
|---|---|
| el grado | *gradus*, a **step** — a thermometer is a staircase |
| el abrigo | *apricari*, to **bask in the sun** — a shelter you wear |
| la chaqueta | *Jacques*, a peasant's name — probably |

*La bufanda* has no agreed origin at all, and its lesson says so rather than
covering over, the way *el patio* and *el gorro* did before it.
