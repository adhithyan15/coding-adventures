# ich sagte — "I said," in one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-TE-01, GE-LEX-SAGEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Run the new ending on the verb this chapter started from.

## You'll want to know: ich sagte
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-SAGTE-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-TE-01] -->

> **ich sagte** — "I said."

Stem *sag-*, ending *-te*. Two syllables and it is finished, where the *Perfekt*
took three words to say the same thing:

> *ich **sagte*** · *ich habe **gesagt***

Both are "I said." Neither is more past than the other, neither is more polite,
and neither is wrong. Hold that in mind while the differences that do exist
arrive.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SAGTE-01] -->

- `long-a` — the stem vowel is the same long *a* as in *sagen*; the new ending
  does not shorten it.
- `final-te` — the *-te* is a full unstressed syllable, *tuh*, not a bare *t*.
  Hear the difference against the participle: *ge-SAHKT* has one syllable at the
  end, *SAHK-tuh* has two.
