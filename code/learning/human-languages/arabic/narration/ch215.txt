Lesson 1 of 5.
هوية (hawiyya) — an identity card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for cotton, then the Arabic for wool.

You'll want to know: هوية.
هوية — hawiyya — "an identity card".

The word, taken apart.
هوية (hawiyya) is built from the pronoun huwa, "he", with the abstract ending -iyya: "he-ness", usually explained as a coinage of the medieval philosophers for identity.

Lesson 2 of 5.
تأشيرة (taʾshīra) — a visa.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for wool, then the Arabic for an identity card.

You'll want to know: تأشيرة.
تأشيرة — taʾshīra — "a visa".

The word, taken apart.
تأشيرة (taʾshīra) is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-sh-r, "to saw, to notch" (Lane). Taʾshīra comes from the modern Form II verb ashshara, "to mark (a document)", a sense not in Lane: a visa is a mark in a passport.

أمتعة (amtiʿa) is built on the native Arabic root m-t-ʿ, "to enjoy; goods" (Lane). Amtiʿa is the plural of matāʿ, "goods, belongings".

Lesson 4 of 5.
شاحن (shāḥin) — a charger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a visa, then the Arabic for luggage.

You'll want to know: شاحن.
شاحن — shāḥin — "a charger".

The word, taken apart.
شاحن (shāḥin) is built on the native Arabic root sh-ḥ-n, "to load, to fill" (Lane). Shāḥin is the active participle, "one that loads"; the charger is a modern sense.
