Mandarin Chinese, chapter 296: A Crash.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
意外 (yìwài) — mishap; something unexpected.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for after the event, then the Mandarin for right there on the spot.

You'll want to know: 意外.
意外 — yìwài — "mishap; something unexpected".
意外发生的时候我在家 yìwài fāshēng de shíhou, wǒ zài jiā — When the accident happened, I was at home.

The word, taken apart.
意外 (yìwài) is built from 意 'an intention' and 外 'outside', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'iH and ngwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'will' and the second 'outside' and 'far': outside what anyone intended.

Lesson 3 of 5.
shāchē — to put on the brakes; a brake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for something unexpected, then the Mandarin for a car crash.

You'll want to know: shāchē.
shāchē — "to put on the brakes; a brake".
kuài shāchē, bùrán jiù zhuàng le — Brake, quick, or we'll hit it!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shāchē is built from shā 'to stop' and 车 'a vehicle'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun lists the first as tsrhaet, 'the pillar of a temple', the source of its other reading chà; the shā of 'to brake' has no source we can open, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 新手.
新手 — xīnshǒu — "novice; beginner".
wǒ shì xīnshǒu, kāichē hěn màn — I'm a beginner, so I drive slowly.

The word, taken apart.
新手 (xīnshǒu) is built from 新 'new' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sin and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a new hand, as in English.
