# Review — if, and otherwise

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C283-COMPARISON-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C283-DURATION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the train goes faster than the bus". (*dhūmayānaṃ lokayānāpekṣayā śīghraṃ gacchati*.) Then "from morning until evening". (*prātaḥ ārabhya sāyaṃ paryantam*.)

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C284-IF-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-IF-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-CONDITIONAL-REAL-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-CONDITIONAL-REAL-02, SA-LEX-C284-ANYATHA-01, SA-LEX-C76-WEATHER-01, SA-LEX-C86-WEEKEND-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s each]
- [YOU SAY: If it rains, I stay at home.]
- [YOU SAY: If he does not come, we will go.]
- [YOU SAY: If there is time tomorrow, I will see the city.]
- [YOU SAY: If you are tired, please sit down.]
- [YOU SAY: Come quickly, otherwise the bus will leave.]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C284-IF-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-IF-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-CONDITIONAL-REAL-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C284-CONDITIONAL-REAL-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] **यदि** or **तर्हि**, to open the condition? (**यदि**.) For tomorrow, which form in both halves? (The future.) Now the other way round: what does **अन्यथा** add? ("Otherwise": what happens if not.)
