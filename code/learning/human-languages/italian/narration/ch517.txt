Italian, chapter 517: L'orchestra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'orchestra — orchestra.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for accordion, then the Italian for to strum.

You'll want to know: l'orchestra.
l'orchestra — "orchestra".
L'orchestra della città suona ogni venerdì. The city orchestra plays every Friday.
Il direttore d'orchestra — the conductor.

The word, taken apart.
orchestra is a learned borrowing, through Latin, from Greek orkhḗstra "the dancing floor of a theatre" (EtymDB), from orkheîsthai "to dance".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: lo spartito.
lo spartito — "sheet music, score".
Ho dimenticato lo spartito a casa. I left the sheet music at home.
Leggere lo spartito — to read music.

The word, taken apart.
spartito is the past participle of spartire "to divide up", built on partire "to divide", from Latin partīrī (Lewis & Short): the music is divided into bars.

Lesson 4 of 5.
accordare — to tune (an instrument).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for symphony, then the Italian for sheet music.

You'll want to know: accordare.
accordare — "to tune (an instrument)".
Prima del concerto accordo la chitarra. Before the concert I tune the guitar.
Reflexive accordarsi — to agree.

The word, taken apart.
accordare is inherited from Vulgar Latin accordāre (EtymDB), usually linked to Latin cor, cordis "heart"; the musical sense was helped by corda "string".

Lesson 5 of 5.
comporre — to compose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sheet music, then the Italian for to tune.

You'll want to know: comporre.
comporre — "to compose".
Ha composto una canzone per il compleanno di sua madre. He composed a song for his mother's birthday.
Like porre: compongo, componi; past participle composto. Also to dial: comporre un numero.

The word, taken apart.
comporre is inherited from Latin compōnere "to put together, to arrange" (EtymDB), from pōnere "to put".
