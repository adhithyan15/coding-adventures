# و — a head, a tail, and no hand to the left

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-ALIF-01, UR-SCRIPT-JOINING-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the letter that takes the hand of what is on its right and
offers nothing to the left, so that a gap opens inside a word. (**ا**.)

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-SCRIPT-VAO-01]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-JOINING-01, UR-SCRIPT-ALIF-01, UR-SCRIPT-YE-01] -->

> و

A **small round head** at the top, closed like a tiny loop, and then a **tail**
that sweeps down and away to the left, dropping below the baseline. One
movement, no dots.

Its name is **و** *vāʾo*.

Now the part that matters more than the shape. **vāʾo is a non-joiner**, the
second one you have met. Like alif, it accepts the letter on its right and hands
nothing to the letter on its left, so the run stops after it and whatever follows
begins as if starting fresh. When you see a break inside an Urdu word, you now
have two suspects instead of one.

With nothing ever attached on its left, vāʾo has no separate middle face to
learn. It looks the same wherever it stands; only whether something is joined to
its right changes.

And it carries more than one sound. Vāʾo does four jobs:

- the consonant **v** or **w**
- the long vowel **ū**
- the long vowel **o**
- and in some Persian borrowings, nothing at all — a letter written and not said

Which one it is comes from the word, not the shape, exactly as with ye. Having
heard the word is what tells you.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-VAO-01, UR-LEX-VOH, UR-LEX-HUN, UR-SCRIPT-NUN-GHUNNA-WRITE-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Three words, three of vāʾo's values. Copy each with the model in front of you.

> وہ
>
> ہوں
>
> کون

**وہ** *voh* — the far pointer from the last lesson. Vāʾo opens it as a
consonant, and because it hands nothing leftward, **ہ** begins a fresh run right
after it.

**ہوں** *hūṅ* — "I am." Vāʾo is the long **ū** here, and the break after it is
the same non-joining behaviour, with **ں** starting clean.

**کون** *kaun* — "who," the k- question word you met beside *kahāṅ*. Vāʾo is the
**o** of the diphthong.

One letter, three sounds, in three words you already say.
