## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,300 words in chapters 293–552; Arabic attains B1

- **260 new chapters (293–552) teach 1,300 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 507 of them are verbs, given in the perfect 3rd person masculine singular with their present (*ittaṣala, yattaṣilu*). Each lesson gives:
  - the word in unvocalized Arabic script with its romanization;
  - a short Modern Standard Arabic example, its romanization word for word, and its translation;
  - a note on the present tense, a broken plural, the preposition the verb takes, or a false friend;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one B1 spine node:
  - 293–357, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (325 words): story connectors and time words, news and media, incidents and emergencies, the police and the courts, life events and ceremonies, and history and legends.
  - 358–422, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (325 words): opinion and argument, cause and consequence, society, politics and the law, education, then conditions, exceptions and likelihood.
  - 423–487, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (325 words): the road, trains, planes and boats, lodging, the town and its services, shopping, nature, the weather, and the environment.
  - 488–552, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (325 words): feelings and character, health and the body, work and careers, family and relationships, hobbies, the arts, sport, and the home.
- **Arabic now teaches 2,572 headwords at or below B1**, past the level gate's target of 2,500. With the B1 core (chapters 283–292), Arabic attains B1.
- **Romanization follows the track's A2 chapters:** *q* for ق, *ʿ* for ع, *gh* for غ, *ʾ* only inside or at the end of a word, ā ī ū for long vowels, doubled consonants for shadda, and pausal *-a* for ة.
- **Sources.** Native roots were checked against Lane's *Arabic-English Lexicon*, and loans and Semitic cognates against EtymDB 2.1. Anything neither source supports is hedged as "usually traced to …" or marked not settled. Persian, Greek, Aramaic, Syriac, Turkish and European loans each say where they came from.
- **Independent review.** Each tranche was reviewed separately, and the reviews fixed errors in 119 of the 1,300 words (9.2%). Most were etymology claims Lane does not support, the wrong derived form (Form VII against VIII), and later-chapter words hidden behind an attached article in an example.
- **No forward references and no hidden duplicates.** Each candidate headword was screened against the text of every earlier lesson, and against the article ال-, attached pronouns, ة/ه and hamza-seat variants of taught words. Arabic's forward-reference count stays at 0.
- **Root slugs reuse the track's own spelling** for a root the track already names: *jamaa-gather*, *zahara-appear*, *ithnayn*, *ayn-f-w*, *sawad-black*, *khariif-autumn* and *h-w-l*. No new root-slug split.
