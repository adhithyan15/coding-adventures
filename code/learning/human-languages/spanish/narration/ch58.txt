Lesson 1 of 1.
Synthesis — describing things.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two verbs for to be. Which is for what a thing is? (Ser.) Which is for how it is right now? (Estar.)

What you've built.
You met ser and estar as a pair long before this book had a single word to put after them. Now it does, and the pair finally does its job:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
La casa es grande., The house is big — that is what it is.
Estoy cansado., I am tired — today.
Soy profesor., I am a teacher — and no un.
Three sentences, three different jobs for to be, and nothing memorised as a phrase.

Grammar Lens: which words shift.
The ending tells you, and you now have one of each:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
ending: -o. shifts?: yes. example: cansado becomes cansada.
ending: -e. shifts?: no. example: grande stays grande.
ending: -ista, -ante. shifts?: no. example: artista, estudiante.
So you never have to guess. Look at the last letter.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: La casa es grande]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Estoy cansado — and mean it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
Say: the house is big, but I am tired.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
A woman says she is tired. Which word changes?
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Put the words in Spanish order: grande / casa / una.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ser or estar for tired? (Estar.) Where does the describing word go? (After the noun.) And what tells you whether it shifts? (Its last letter.)
