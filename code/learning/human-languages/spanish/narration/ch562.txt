Spanish, chapter 562: Volver a empezar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
volver a — to do again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the return, then the Spanish for the absence.

You'll want to know: volver a.
volver a — "to do again".
Volvió a llamar a las diez. She called again at ten.
Volver a + infinitive: No volveré a hacerlo — I won't do it again.

The word, taken apart.
volver a is built on volver 'to return', which is inherited from Latin volvere 'to roll, to turn' (Lewis & Short). To do something again is to turn back to it.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 3 seconds]
What does volver a mean? ("To do again".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
acabar por — to end up (doing).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the absence, then the Spanish for to do again.

You'll want to know: acabar por.
acabar por — "to end up (doing)".
Acabó por aceptar el trabajo. He ended up accepting the job.
Acabar por + infinitive, or acabar + gerund: Acabó aceptando.

The word, taken apart.
acabar por is built on acabar 'to finish', made from a- and cabo 'an end', which comes from Latin caput 'head' (Lewis & Short). To end up doing something is to reach its end.

Lesson 4 of 5.
echarse a — to burst out (doing).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to end up, then the Spanish for to start.

You'll want to know: echarse a.
echarse a — "to burst out (doing)".
Al ver la carta, se echó a reír. When she saw the letter, she burst out laughing.
Mostly with reír and correr: Se echó a correr.

The word, taken apart.
echarse a is built on echar 'to throw', which is inherited from Latin iactāre 'to throw about' (Lewis & Short). To burst out crying is to throw yourself into it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
alejarse — to move away, to go off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to start, then the Spanish for to burst out.

You'll want to know: alejarse.
alejarse — "to move away, to go off".
El coche se alejó despacio. The car moved slowly away.
Alejarse de — to move away from.

The word, taken apart.
alejarse is built on lejos 'far', which comes from Latin laxius 'more loosely, further apart', from laxus 'loose' (Lewis & Short), with a- and -ar.
