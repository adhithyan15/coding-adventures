Tamil, chapter 55: The Rest of You.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
கழுத்து (kaḻuttu) — the neck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new run of words: what did kiṇṇam mean, and what did peṭṭi mean?

You'll want to know: கழுத்து.
கழுத்து (kaḻuttu) — "the neck".
Malayalam കഴുത്ത് (kaḻuttŭ) is the same word, ழ and all — that letter again, the one only these two sisters still write. Telugu went its own way here, to మెడ (meḍa).
It is the neck outside and the throat inside, both in one word. A garland goes round the கழுத்து (kaḻuttu), and so does the thread tied at a wedding.

What you've built.
The first of five more pieces of you.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kaḻuttu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it once more, slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: peṭṭi, then kaḻuttu, and hold the ழ in the middle]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does கழுத்து (kaḻuttu) mean? ("the neck".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
முதுகு (mutuku) — the back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kaḻuttu mean?

You'll want to know: முதுகு.
முதுகு (mutuku) — "the back".
Malayalam has മുതുക് (mutukŭ), letter for letter until the last one. Telugu takes వీపు (vīpu) and shares nothing with either.
To show somebody your முதுகு (mutuku) is to turn away from them, in Tamil much as in English — and the phrase is meant as unkindly in one language as in the other.

What you've built.
Two: what a garland goes round, and what you turn on somebody.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it once more, slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kaḻuttu, then mutuku, the top of you and the length below it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does முதுகு (mutuku) mean? ("the back".) Say it once more.


Lesson 3 of 5.
உதடு (utaṭu) — a lip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mutuku mean?

You'll want to know: உதடு.
உதடு (utaṭu) — "a lip".
This is where the family stops agreeing. Malayalam says ചുണ്ട് (cuṇṭŭ), Telugu పెదవి (pedavi), Tamil உதடு (utaṭu) — three words with nothing in common but the thing they name. It happens: a part of the body every language has a name for is not therefore a part every language named at the same time.
The ட in the middle is the one made with the tongue curled back. Keep it curled and the word comes out right.

What you've built.
Three, and this one the three sisters worked out separately.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: utaṭu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it once more, slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mutuku, then utaṭu, and curl the tongue back for the ட]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does உதடு (utaṭu) mean? ("a lip".) Say it once more.


Lesson 4 of 5.
நகம் (nakam) — a nail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did utaṭu mean?

You'll want to know: நகம்.
நகம் (nakam) — "a nail".
The nail on a finger and the claw on an animal, in one word. Malayalam writes നഖം (nakhaṁ), with a breathed letter in the middle that Tamil's own set does not carry, so Tamil prints it plain: நகம் (nakam). The two words are heard as one and written as two.
Telugu holds on to something else entirely, గోరు (gōru).

What you've built.
Four: neck, back, lip, and the hard thing on the end of a finger.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it once more, slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: utaṭu, then nakam, the softest part of you and the hardest]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does நகம் (nakam) mean? ("a nail".) Say it once more.


Lesson 5 of 5.
எலும்பு (elumbu) — a bone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nakam mean?

You'll want to know: எலும்பு.
எலும்பு (elumbu) — "a bone".
Malayalam എല്ല് (ellŭ) and Telugu ఎముక (emuka) sit either side of Tamil's எலும்பு (elumbu): the same opening, and then three different amounts of word after it.
elumbum tōlum — "bone and skin" — is what Tamil says of somebody who has gone very thin. English says the same two things in the other order.

What you've built.
Five more of you: neck, back, lip, nail, and what holds the whole lot up.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: all five in order — kaḻuttu, mutuku, utaṭu, nakam, elumbu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does எலும்பு (elumbu) mean? ("a bone".) Say it once more.
