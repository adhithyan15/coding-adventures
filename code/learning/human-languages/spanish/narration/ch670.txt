Spanish, chapter 670: Cómo llegar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
indicar — to show, to point out, to indicate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to point to, then the Spanish for located.

You'll want to know: indicar.
indicar — "to show, to point out, to indicate".
¿Me puede indicar el camino a la estación? Can you show me the way to the station?
A polite way to ask for directions.

The word, taken apart.
indicar is a learned borrowing from Latin indicāre "to point out, to show" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
doblar — to turn (a corner); to fold; to dub.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to show, then the Spanish for the instruction.

You'll want to know: doblar.
doblar — "to turn (a corner); to fold; to dub".
Dobla a la izquierda en la plaza. Turn left at the square.
Very common for turning in Latin America; Spain also says girar. Una película doblada is a dubbed film.

The word, taken apart.
doblar is inherited from Late Latin duplāre "to double" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), built on duplus "double".

Lesson 4 of 5.
atravesar — to cross, to go through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the instruction, then the Spanish for to turn.

You'll want to know: atravesar.
atravesar — "to cross, to go through".
Atravesamos el parque a pie. We went through the park on foot.
The e becomes ie under stress: atravieso, atraviesa.

The word, taken apart.
atravesar is built on través "crossways", which is inherited from Latin trānsversus "lying across" (EtymDB), from trans and vertere "to turn".

Lesson 5 of 5.
desviarse — to turn off, to leave the road; to go off course.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to turn, then the Spanish for to cross.

You'll want to know: desviarse.
desviarse — "to turn off, to leave the road; to go off course".
Desvíate en la próxima salida. Turn off at the next exit.
The i takes a written accent in some forms: te desvías, se desvía.

The word, taken apart.
desviarse is built on desviar, from Late Latin dēviāre "to turn aside, to stray" (EtymDB), built on via "road".
