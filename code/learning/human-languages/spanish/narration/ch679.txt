6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
la comisaría — the police station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tower, then the Spanish for the gallery.

You'll want to know: la comisaría.
la comisaría — "the police station".
La comisaría está al lado del ayuntamiento. The police station is next to the town hall.
In Latin America also la estación de policía.

The word, taken apart.
comisaría is built on comisario "commissioner", from Medieval Latin commissārius "one entrusted", from committere "to entrust".

What you've built.

la embajada — "the embassy".
Perdí el pasaporte y llamé a la embajada. I lost my passport and phoned the embassy.
The person is el embajador, la embajadora.

The word, taken apart.
embajada came from Occitan ambaissada "embassy" (EtymDB), from Late Latin ambactia, built on Gaulish ambactos "a servant, a messenger".

Lesson 3 of 6.
el estanco — the tobacconist’s (Spain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the police station, then the Spanish for the embassy.

You'll want to know: el estanco.
el estanco — "the tobacconist’s (Spain)".
El estanco de la plaza abre los domingos. The tobacconist on the square opens on Sundays.
In Spain el estanco sells tobacco, and stamps too.

The word, taken apart.
estanco is built on estancar "to hold back", which EtymDB traces to Latin stagnum "standing water"; the shop once sold goods the state kept for itself.

Necesito un sello para esta carta. I need a stamp for this letter.
In Latin America la estampilla, and in Mexico el timbre.

Lesson 5 of 6.

You'll want to know: el aseo.
el aseo — "the toilet, the restroom (Spain); cleanliness".
¿Dónde están los aseos? Where are the toilets?
Los aseos on a sign in Spain; Latin America says el baño.

The word, taken apart.
aseo is built on asear "to tidy, to clean" (EtymDB). The origin of asear is not settled.
