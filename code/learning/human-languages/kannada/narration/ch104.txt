Lesson 1 of 7.
ವ್ಯಾಪಾರ (vyāpāra) — business, trade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: ask where somebody is from, answer “from Bengaluru”, then recognise the question that asks whether a male addressee is from Karnataka.

You'll want to know: ವ್ಯಾಪಾರ.
ವ್ಯಾಪಾರ (vyāpāra) — "business, trade".
ನನ್ನದು ವ್ಯಾಪಾರ (nannadu vyāpāra) — "mine is business", an answer to ನಿಮ್ಮ ಉದ್ಯೋಗ ಏನು?

The word, taken apart.
ವ್ಯಾಪಾರ (vyāpāra) is Sanskrit vyāpāra, "occupation, business", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 12205).

What you've built.
One answer to what do you do.

Lesson 6 of 7.
-ಅನ್ನು (-annu) — the person or particular thing an action reaches.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Your first visible case ending was -ಗೆ: one piece on the end for one job. Say the Kannada for the police and for a shop. This lesson gives a second ending and one new job.

Grammar Lens: -ಅನ್ನು marks the object.
ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು ಕರೆಯಿರಿ polīsarannu kareyiri — please call the police.
The calling reaches ಪೊಲೀಸರು (polīsaru), “the police”. Kannada puts -ಅನ್ನು (-annu) on that object: ಪೊಲೀಸರು becomes ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು. The final sound joins the ending, but the job stays visible.
With a person, the object ending is required. With a non-human thing, it often marks a particular thing already in view:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
the word: ಪೊಲೀಸರು polīsaru. as the object: ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು polīsarannu. meaning: the police, as the people called.
the word: ಅಂಗಡಿ aṅgaḍi. as the object: ಅಂಗಡಿಯನ್ನು aṅgaḍiyannu. meaning: the particular shop, as the thing acted on.
This is the accusative, the direct-object case. Keep the practical question: who or what does the action reach? Put its marker on that word.

What you've built.
Two case endings, two jobs: -ಗೆ points to a receiver or experiencer; -ಅನ್ನು (-annu) marks the object an action reaches. Kannada keeps the jobs in separate pieces rather than hiding both inside one ending.

Lesson 7 of 7.
ನನ್ನನ್ನು, ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು, ಅವನನ್ನು — familiar pronouns in the object seat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ನಾನು, ನೀನು, ನೀವು, ಅವನು, ಅವಳು, ಅದು, ಅವರು. Those are the pronouns when they do the action. Now move each one to the place where the action reaches it.

Grammar Lens: the same -ಅನ್ನು, on changed pronoun bodies.
[a table of 7 rows, read aloud]
the pronoun: ನಾನು nānu — I. as the object: ನನ್ನನ್ನು nannannu — me.
the pronoun: ನೀನು nīnu — familiar you. as the object: ನಿನ್ನನ್ನು ninnannu — you.
the pronoun: ನೀವು nīvu — respectful you. as the object: ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು nimmannu — you.
the pronoun: ಅವನು avanu — he. as the object: ಅವನನ್ನು avanannu — him.
the pronoun: ಅವಳು avaḷu — she. as the object: ಅವಳನ್ನು avaḷannu — her.
the pronoun: ಅದು adu — it / that. as the object: ಅದನ್ನು adannu — it / that.
the pronoun: ಅವರು avaru — they or respected he/she. as the object: ಅವರನ್ನು avarannu — them or that respected person.
The pronoun body changes before -ಅನ್ನು (-annu) lands. Do not try to glue the ending onto the full dictionary form. Learn each left-to-right pair, then hear the one shared ending at the right edge.
ನನ್ನನ್ನು ಕರೆಯಿರಿ — nannannu kareyiri — please call me ಅವರನ್ನು ಕರೆಯಿರಿ — avarannu kareyiri — please call them, or that respected person.
The respectful/plural choice stays intact: ನೀವು becomes ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು and ಅವರು becomes ಅವರನ್ನು still carry respect as well as number.
