# vous avez — you have

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The second plural ending, and a form you will use with strangers far
more often than with crowds.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-VERB-AVOIR-AVEZ-06]; assesses=[] -->

- **vous avez** = *voo-za-VAY*. The same buzzing liaison as *nous avons*.
- `vowel-e-acute` — the **-ez** is the bright, narrow *ay* of **février**. The
  **z** is silent.
- Say **nous avons, vous avez** together: *-ons*, *-ez*, the two plural endings
  of nearly every French verb.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**avez** is Latin **habētis**, and it goes with **vous**.

**vous** is two things at once. It is the plural "you" — several people. And it
is the polite "you" for **one** person, which is the use you will need first and
most: a shopkeeper, a stranger, anyone you have not been invited to call *tu*.

So *vous avez* is what you say to one person you are being careful with. Getting
this wrong is one of the few grammar mistakes in French that a listener hears as
a mistake about **them** rather than about the language, which is why *tu* and
*vous* is worth more attention than most endings.

The verb form does not change between the two meanings. Only who you are looking
at does.
