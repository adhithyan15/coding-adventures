Lesson 1 of 5.
सूँघना (sū̃ghnā) — to smell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to throw, then the Hindi for to taste.

You'll want to know: सूँघना.
सूँघना — sū̃ghnā — "to smell".

The word, taken apart.
सूँघना (sū̃ghnā) (sūṁghnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from śiṅghati, "smells" (CDIAL 12579). The Sanskrit verb is known only from a list of verb roots.

Lesson 2 of 5.
मनाना (manānā) — to celebrate, to persuade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to taste, then the Hindi for to smell.

You'll want to know: मनाना.
मनाना — manānā — "to celebrate, to persuade".

The word, taken apart.
मनाना (manānā) is the causing form of mānnā, "to accept, honour": to persuade is to make someone accept, and to celebrate is to honour a day. CDIAL lists both under Sanskrit manyate, "thinks, honours" (CDIAL 9857).

Lesson 4 of 5.
उबालना (ubālnā) — to boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to celebrate, then the Hindi for to grind.

You'll want to know: उबालना.
उबालना — ubālnā — "to boil".

The word, taken apart.
उबालना (ubālnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ubbāl, "make boil", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 2339). Morgenstierne traced it to ud-, "up", and an Indo-European root "to boil"; CDIAL finds the forms hard to fit and notes other possible sources.
