# f → h — why the h says nothing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SOUND-H-SILENT, ES-LEX-HOLA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Since your first chapter you have known that the Spanish *h* is
silent. You were told it as a fact. Here is the reason, and it turns the fact
into a tool.

## Grammar Lens: a letter standing on a grave
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SOUND-F-TO-H-DECODER]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HABLAR, ES-ETYMON-FABULARI] -->

> **Where Spanish has a silent *h-*, Latin very often had an *f-*.**

You already hold the proof. *Hablar* comes from Latin **fabulārī** — and
English took the same root and kept the *f*: **fable**, **fabulous**.

> *hablar* ← *fabulārī* → **fable**

The sound did not vanish overnight. Latin *f-* weakened to a breathy *h*, the
breath faded, and eventually nothing was left — but the spelling had already
settled, so Spanish went on writing a letter it had stopped saying. **The
silent *h* is a gravestone.** It marks where a sound used to be.

Which makes it a decoder. Meet an unfamiliar Spanish word beginning with *h-*,
put an *f* back, and look for the English:

| Spanish | put the f back | English friend |
|---|---|---|
| *hijo* | *fijo* ← *fīlium* | **filial**, **affiliate** |
| *hierro* | *fierro* ← *ferrum* | **ferrous**, **ferric** |
| *harina* | *farina* | **farinaceous** |
| *higo* | *figo* ← *fīcus* | **fig** |
| *hoja* | *foja* ← *folia* | **foliage**, **portfolio** |

Not one of those five words has been taught in this book, and you can read all
five.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SOUND-F-TO-H, ES-SOUND-F-TO-H-DECODER, ES-SOUND-H-SILENT] -->

Two honest limits, because a decoder you trust too far will mislead you.

**Not every *h* is a dead *f*.** *Hola* is not, and *hasta* came from Arabic.

**And not every Latin *f* became *h*.** *Fácil*, *fuerte* and *forma* kept
theirs; the change left *f* alone before *ue* and *r*.

So this is a **lead**, not a guarantee. Put the *f* back and believe it when an
English word appears.
