Italian, chapter 414: Strade di campagna.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la statale — the main road, the state highway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the diversion, then the Italian for to divert.

You'll want to know: la statale.
la statale — "the main road, the state highway".
Prendiamo la statale, l'autostrada costa troppo. Let us take the main road, the motorway costs too much.
Short for la strada statale, a road run by the state.

The word, taken apart.
statale is built on stato 'state', a learned borrowing from Latin status 'standing, condition', from stāre 'to stand' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la provinciale — the provincial road, the secondary road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to divert, then the Italian for the main road.

You'll want to know: la provinciale.
la provinciale — "the provincial road, the secondary road".
La provinciale passa per tre paesi. The provincial road goes through three villages.
Short for la strada provinciale; smaller than la statale. As an adjective it can also mean narrow-minded.

The word, taken apart.
provinciale is built on provincia 'province', a learned borrowing from Latin prōvincia 'a charge, a province' (Lewis & Short).

la curva — "the bend, the curve".
Rallenta, c'è una curva pericolosa. Slow down, there is a dangerous bend.
In a stadium, la curva is the end stand where the keenest fans sit.

The word, taken apart.
curva is inherited from Latin curva 'curve' (EtymDB), from curvus 'bent'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la buca — the pothole, the hole (in the ground).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the provincial road, then the Italian for the bend.

You'll want to know: la buca.
la buca — "the pothole, the hole (in the ground)".
La strada è piena di buche. The road is full of potholes.
La buca is a hole in the ground; il buco is a hole in anything. La buca delle lettere — the letterbox.

The word, taken apart.
buca goes with buco 'hole'. The origin is not settled; some link it to Latin bucca 'cheek, mouth'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lo sterrato — the dirt road, the unpaved track.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bend, then the Italian for the pothole.

You'll want to know: lo sterrato.
lo sterrato — "the dirt road, the unpaved track".
Dopo il paese c'è un lungo sterrato. After the village there is a long dirt road.
As an adjective: una strada sterrata.

The word, taken apart.
sterrato is built from s- and terra 'earth', which is inherited from Latin terra (EtymDB): a road of bare earth.
