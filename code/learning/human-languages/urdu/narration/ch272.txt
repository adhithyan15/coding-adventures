Urdu, chapter 272: Feelings and Hopes.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ڈر (ḍar) — fear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I'm hungry". (mujhe bhūk lagī hai.) Fear is said the same way.

You'll want to know: ڈر.
ڈر — ḍar — "fear". It is masculine.
مجھے ڈر لگتا ہے۔ mujhe ḍar lagtā hai. — "I'm afraid."
Fear strikes you, as hunger does: مجھے, and لگتا (lagtā) for a masculine ڈر (ḍar). What you fear takes سے (se):
مجھے کتوں سے ڈر لگتا ہے۔ mujhe kuttoṅ se ḍar lagtā hai. — "I'm afraid of dogs."

The word, taken apart.
ڈر (ḍar) is inherited. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit dara, "fear" (CDIAL 6186), and lists the Hindi form ḍar, with the curled-back ḍ. Its first letter is new to your hand; the next lesson takes it out.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ḍar, then mujhe ḍar lagtā hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She got scared" — use ḍar lagā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ڈر (ḍar) mean? ("Fear".) "I'm afraid of dogs"? (مجھے کتوں سے ڈر (ḍar) لگتا ہے.)


Lesson 2 of 7.
ڈ (ḍāl) — the letter that opens ڈر (ḍar).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ڈ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ث once, and name it. (se.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ڈر (ḍar), "fear". This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ڈ.
ڈ — ḍāl, the sound ḍ, made with the tongue curled back, as for ٹ (ṭe) and ڑ (ṛe).
It opens ڈر (ḍar). You already write د: ڈ (ḍāl) is that shape wearing the small mark that ٹ and ڑ wear above them. Like د, it does not join the letter after it.

Writing: ڈ.
Put your pen on ڈ (ḍāl) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at ڈر (ḍar) and point at ڈ (ḍāl) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ڈ (ḍāl) three times, saying ḍ as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ڈر (ḍar) once, with the small mark above its first letter]
[once you have stopped driving — read: ڈر (ḍar) aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ڈ? (ḍāl, ḍ.) What tells it from د? (The small mark that ٹ wears.) Which word did it come from? (ڈر, ḍar, "fear".)


Lesson 3 of 7.
میں خوش ہوں (maiṅ khush hūṅ) — how I feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'm afraid". (mujhe ḍar lagtā hai.) Then "happy" and "sad". (khush, udās.) Urdu says a feeling two ways.

You'll want to know: I am happy, she was sad.
میں خوش ہوں۔ maiṅ khush hūṅ. — "I'm happy."
کل وہ اداس تھی۔ kal voh udās thī. — "Yesterday she was sad."
میں پریشان تھا۔ maiṅ pareshān thā. — "I was worried."
The feeling word stays as it is; ہوں, تھی, تھا follow the person. With ہوا (huā) the feeling arrives:
یہ سن کر وہ خوش ہوئی۔ yih sun kar voh khush huī. — "Hearing this, she was pleased."

You'll want to know: what strikes you.
مجھے ڈر لگا۔ mujhe ḍar lagā. — "I got scared."
مجھے یہ شہر اچھا لگا۔ mujhe yih shahr achchhā lagā. — "I liked this city."
A feeling that strikes you, or a thing that seems good, takes مجھے and لگنا, and the verb follows the thing: شہر is masculine, اچھا لگا. To ask:
آپ کو فلم کیسی لگی؟ āp ko film kaisī lagī? — "How did you like the film?"

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I was happy" — maiṅ khush thā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was sad" — maiṅ udās thā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was worried" — maiṅ pareshān thā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you were worried yesterday, then that you liked this city.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"She was sad"? (وہ اداس تھی.) "I got scared"? (مجھے ڈر (ḍar) لگا.) "I liked the film"? (مجھے فلم اچھی لگی.)


Lesson 4 of 7.
بننا (bannā) — to become, to be made.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I'm happy". (maiṅ khush hūṅ.) Then "to make". (banānā.) This lesson gives its partner.

You'll want to know: بننا.
بننا — bannā — "to become; to be made".
وہ استاد بنی۔ voh ustād banī. — "She became a teacher."
یہ گھر پچھلے سال بنا۔ yih ghar pichhle sāl banā. — "This house was built last year."
It has no object, so no نے: بنی follows her. Its partner بنانا (banānā), "to make", takes an object and نے: اس نے گھر بنایا (us ne ghar banāyā).

The word, taken apart.
بننا (bannā) is inherited. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit vanati, "desires, gains, makes ready" (CDIAL 11260), and gives "to be made" as a Hindi sense.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: bannā, then voh ustād banī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He became a teacher" — voh ustād banā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does بننا (bannā) mean? ("To become, to be made".) Does it take نے? (No: بنانا does.)


Lesson 5 of 7.
امید (ummīd) — hope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I think she will come". (mujhe lagtā hai ki voh āe gī.) Now say it as a hope.

You'll want to know: امید.
امید — ummīd — "hope". It is feminine.
مجھے امید ہے۔ mujhe ummīd hai. — "I hope so."
مجھے امید ہے کہ وہ آئے گی۔ mujhe ummīd hai ki voh āe gī. — "I hope she'll come."
Word for word, "to me there is hope": مجھے, then کہ (ki) and what you hope, in the future.

The word, taken apart.
امید (ummīd) is usually traced to Persian ummēd, "hope".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ummīd, then mujhe ummīd hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I hope it won't rain" — mujhe ummīd hai ki bārish nahīṅ ho gī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does امید (ummīd) mean? ("Hope".) "I hope she'll come"? (مجھے امید (ummīd) ہے کہ وہ آئے گی.)


Lesson 6 of 7.
بننا چاہتی ہوں (bannā chāhtī hūṅ) — what I want to become.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "she became a teacher". (voh ustād banī.) Then "I want to go". (maiṅ jānā chāhtā hūṅ.) Put the two together.

You'll want to know: I want to become.
میں استاد ہوں۔ maiṅ ustād hūṅ. — "I'm a teacher."
میں استاد بننا چاہتی ہوں۔ maiṅ ustād bannā chāhtī hūṅ. — "I want to become a teacher."
چاہنا (chāhnā) takes بننا whole, as it takes جانا: بننا چاہتی ہوں (bannā chāhtī hūṅ), a woman speaking.
وہ نرس بننا چاہتا ہے۔ voh nars bannā chāhtā hai. — "He wants to become a nurse."

You'll want to know: my dream, my hope.
میرا خواب استاد بننا ہے۔ merā khvāb ustād bannā hai. — "My dream is to become a teacher."
خواب (khvāb) is masculine: میرا. A hope looks ahead, so it takes the future:
مجھے امید ہے کہ میں استاد بنوں گی۔ mujhe ummīd hai ki maiṅ ustād banūṅ gī. — "I hope I'll become a teacher."
