# el cumpleaños — the years you have completed

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CUANTOS-ANOS, ES-LEX-C349-CLOCK-19, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *¿Cuántos años tienes?* Say **el aniversario**. Say **la boda**.
One day a year answers the first question, and it is not called an *aniversario*.

## You'll want to know: el cumpleaños
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CUANTOS-ANOS] -->

**el cumpleaños** — the birthday.

It ends in **-s** and it is **singular**: *el cumpleaños*, *los cumpleaños*. The
word does not change; only the article tells you how many.

*¡Feliz cumpleaños!* — happy birthday. *Mañana es mi cumpleaños* — tomorrow is
my birthday.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-02, ES-ETYMON-QUANTUS-ANNUS] -->

Two words fused into one: **cumple** plus **años**.

**cumplir** is to complete, to fulfil, to carry something through to its end.
*Cumplo treinta* — I complete thirty. So a **cumple-años** is the day the years
get completed, and the **-s** you can hear is the plural of *años* trapped
inside a word that is now singular.

That is why *el cumpleaños* looks plural and is not. It is not an exception to
be memorised; it is a sentence that hardened.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-02, ES-LEX-C349-CLOCK-19] -->

Spanish has two words for the same square on the calendar, and they disagree
about what is happening.

| the word | what it says is happening |
|---|---|
| el aniversario | the **year turns** |
| el cumpleaños | **you complete** years |

*Aniversario* is *annus* plus *vertere*, the year coming round again — the
calendar does the work, and you stand still. *Cumpleaños* puts the work on you:
the years are something you finish, one at a time, like a task.

Spanish keeps both and uses them for different things. A wedding gets an
**aniversario** because a date recurs; a person gets a **cumpleaños** because a
person is doing the ageing. That is not a rule anyone wrote down — it is the
metaphor inside each word still deciding where it is welcome.
