Lesson 1 of 5.
ਨਾਸ਼ਤਾ (nāshtā) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for an orange, then the Punjabi for a lemon.

You'll want to know: ਨਾਸ਼ਤਾ.
ਨਾਸ਼ਤਾ — nāshtā — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
ਨਾਸ਼ਤਾ (nāshtā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian nāshtā, "not having eaten". The meal is named for the fast it ends.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ਆਂਡਾ (āṇḍā) — an egg.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a lemon, then the Punjabi for breakfast.

You'll want to know: ਆਂਡਾ.
ਆਂਡਾ — āṇḍā — "an egg".

The word, taken apart.
ਆਂਡਾ (āṇḍā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from āṇḍa, "egg" (CDIAL 1111). CDIAL reports Mayrhofer's view, after Kuiper, that the word is of Munda origin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਘਰ (ghar) — a house, home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for breakfast, then the Punjabi for an egg.

You'll want to know: ਘਰ.
ਘਰ — ghar — "a house, home".

The word, taken apart.
ਘਰ (ghar) is inherited. CDIAL lists it under ghara, "house", and reports that it is generally taken as a popular form of Sanskrit gṛha, "house", a link it calls phonetically difficult (CDIAL 4428).

You'll want to know: ਖਿੜਕੀ.
ਖਿੜਕੀ — khiṛkī — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
ਖਿੜਕੀ (khiṛkī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from khaṭakkikā, "side door" (CDIAL 3770). CDIAL lists Punjabi khiṛkī, "back door, window".

Lesson 5 of 5.

ਕੁਰਸੀ — kursī — "a chair".

The word, taken apart.
ਕੁਰਸੀ (kursī) is a loan from Arabic kursī, "chair, throne", which reached Punjabi through Persian. Its Arabic root is k-r-s (Lane).
