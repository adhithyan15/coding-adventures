# Ich lerne Deutsch — your first real sentence

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-02, GE-LEX-LERNEN-01, GE-LEX-DEUTSCH-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new is coming. Every piece of the next line has had its own
lesson — and that is exactly the point.

## You'll want to know: Ich lerne Deutsch
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ICH-LERNE-DEUTSCH-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-02, GE-LEX-LERNEN-01, GE-LEX-DEUTSCH-01, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-STEM-01] -->

> **Ich lerne Deutsch.** — "I am learning German."

| Piece | Where it came from |
|---|---|
| *ich* | the introductions chapter |
| *lern-* | the stem of *lernen* |
| *-e* | the *ich* ending |
| *Deutsch* | the last lesson |

This is the first sentence in the book that was **not handed to you**. You built
it: a pronoun, a stem, an ending, a noun. Change any piece and it still works.

Remember there is no separate "am …-ing" in German. *Ich lerne* covers both "I
learn" and "I am learning," so this one line does the work of two English
tenses.

## Grammar Lens: swap the pieces
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-LERNE-DEUTSCH-01, GE-LEX-ICH-WOHNE-IN-02, GE-LEX-WAS-MACHST-DU-02] -->

The same slots take other words, and you already have all of them:

> *Ich wohne in Berlin.* · *Was machst du?* · *Ich lerne Deutsch.*

Three sentences, one machine. From here the language is combinations, not
memorised lines.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-LERNE-DEUTSCH-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say "I am learning German." (*Ich lerne Deutsch.*) Which four pieces
is it made of? (*Ich*, the stem *lern-*, the ending *-e*, and *Deutsch*.) How
does German say "I am learning" as opposed to "I learn"? (It does not — *ich
lerne* is both.)
