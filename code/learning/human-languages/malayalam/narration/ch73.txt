Malayalam, chapter 73: Why, and Because.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
എന്തുകൊണ്ട് (entukoṇṭŭ) — "by what", which is how Malayalam asks why.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the word for what. You have had it since the second chapter, inside the question you ask for somebody's name.
[pause 2 seconds]
Today it grows an ending, and becomes a different question.

You'll want to know.
എന്തുകൊണ്ട് — entukoṇṭŭ — why.
Look at the front of it. എന്ത് is sitting there, the word you already have. What follows is കൊണ്ട് (koṇṭŭ), an ending that means by means of — so the whole question asks, quite literally, "by what?"
That is worth noticing rather than memorising. Malayalam did not invent a new word for why; it took the word for what and asked what caused the thing.
Your question words now cover four of the five things a person asks about:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
എന്ത് entŭ, what.
ആര് ārŭ, who.
എവിടെ eviṭe, where.
എന്തുകൊണ്ട് entukoṇṭŭ, why.

The word, taken apart.
എന്തു is entŭ with its final mark opened out, which is what this script does when something follows. കൊണ്ട് is the piece carrying the meaning of with or by.
Three of the four question words in that table open on എ — entŭ, eviṭe and now entukoṇṭŭ. That is the letter the pointing chapter named as the one that asks: change a word's front from ഇ to അ to എ and it walks from near to far to a question. Today's word keeps the എ because it is built on a word that had it already.
ആര് is the odd one out and starts on ആ. Question words are a family, not a rule — most of them carry the asking letter, and who does not.

Lesson 2 of 4.
കാരണം (kāraṇaṁ) — a reason, and the answer to the question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask why in Malayalam. Say which word is hiding inside it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now you need something to answer with.

You'll want to know.
കാരണം — kāraṇaṁ — a reason; and so, "because".
It is a noun first. Kāraṇaṁ is a reason, a cause, the thing behind something — and Malayalam then uses that same noun to join two statements, the way English uses the reason is before sliding into because.
The word is Sanskrit, कारण (kāraṇa), taken whole. That puts it in the same company as ചിന്ത, the thought-noun that cintikkuka stands on, and സുപ്രഭാതം, the good-morning this book teaches — Malayalam borrows a Sanskrit noun and puts it to work without reshaping it.

Grammar Lens: it sits between the two halves.
Say the thing, then the word, then the reason:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
what happened, എനിക്ക് മലയാളം ഇഷ്ടം.
the joiner, കാരണം (kāraṇaṁ).
why, (your reason).
This is the everyday one, the word a person reaches for in speech. It asks nothing of the sentences on either side of it — neither half is rebuilt, the same way the quotative left a quoted sentence alone.

Lesson 3 of 4.
എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ (entukoṇṭennāl) — the long one, on the page.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the everyday word for because.
[pause 2 seconds]
Today's is the one you will read rather than say, and it is long enough to be worth taking slowly.

You'll want to know.
എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ — entukoṇṭennāl — because (written, explaining).
Put it beside the question from two pages ago and the first half is the same letters:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
എന്തുകൊണ്ട് (entukoṇṭŭ), why.
എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ (entukoṇṭennāl), because.
The answer carries the question inside it. That is the thing to see on the page — a long unfamiliar word with a word you know at the front of it stops being unfamiliar, and this one is signposted more clearly than most.
This book does not take the tail apart for you. The pieces after എന്തുകൊണ്ട് (entukoṇṭŭ) are read different ways by different grammars, and guessing between them would teach you something that might be wrong.

Grammar Lens: which one to use.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
speaking, everyday, കാരണം (kāraṇaṁ).
writing, explaining, എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ (entukoṇṭennāl).
Both join a statement to its reason and neither rebuilds either half. Reach for കാരണം (kāraṇaṁ) when you talk. Meeting the long one on a notice or in a written explanation is what this lesson is for, and a reader who can see the question word inside it has already got most of the way.

Lesson 4 of 4.
(asking and answering why) (kāraṇaṁ ōrmma) — from cold.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Ask why, and say which word you already had is sitting at the front of it.

Grammar Lens: a question and two answers.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
to ask, എന്തുകൊണ്ട് entukoṇṭŭ.
to answer, speaking, കാരണം kāraṇaṁ.
to answer, writing, എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ entukoṇṭennāl.
Until this chapter, cause could not be handled in either direction. There was no way to ask what was behind something and no way to give it. Three words close both halves at once, and two of the three are built on material you had: entukoṇṭŭ on the word for what, and the written one on entukoṇṭŭ itself.
കാരണം (kāraṇaṁ) is the one to have in your mouth. The long one is for reading.
