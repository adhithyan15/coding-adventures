French, chapter 464: La canicule.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la canicule — the heatwave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to thaw, then the French for to shiver.

You'll want to know: la canicule.
la canicule — "the heatwave".
Pendant la canicule, les gens restent chez eux l'après-midi. During the heatwave people stay at home in the afternoon.
In Québec also la vague de chaleur.

The word, taken apart.
canicule is a learned borrowing from Latin canīcula 'little dog', the name of the Dog Star, which rises in the hottest days of summer (EtymDB); it is built on canis 'dog'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
moite — clammy, sticky (with damp heat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the drought, then the French for the humidity.

You'll want to know: moite.
moite — "clammy, sticky (with damp heat)".
Dans le métro, l'air est chaud et moite. In the metro the air is hot and clammy.
Les mains moites — sweaty hands. Same form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
moite comes from Old French moiste (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin mūcidus 'mouldy, slimy' (Lewis & Short); the details are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: transpirer.
transpirer — "to sweat, to perspire".
Il transpire beaucoup quand il fait chaud. He sweats a lot when it is hot.
A little more polite than suer.

The word, taken apart.
transpirer is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin transpīrāre 'to breathe through' (EtymDB), from trāns and spīrāre 'to breathe'.
