# equivocarse — when two things sound the same

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el pedido*. The restaurant has sent two salads instead of a
salad and a soup. Say what they did.

## You'll want to know: equivocarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

**equivocarse** — to be mistaken, to get it wrong.

*Nos hemos equivocado* — we have made a mistake. It is how a shop apologises
without naming who is at fault.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-03] -->

**aequus** equal, plus **vox** voice. *Equal voice* — two things that sound the
same and get taken for each other.

That is a precise picture of what a mistake is, and the same two pieces give you
a word you may already recognise:

| word | the equal voice in it |
|---|---|
| equivocarse | to confuse two things |
| equívoco | ambiguous |

**It is almost always reflexive.** You say *me equivoco*, not *equivoco*, because
the error happens **to you** rather than being done to something else. Spanish
uses that reflexive for a whole family of things that befall a person —
*caerse*, *olvidarse*, *perderse* — and it is worth noticing as a group rather
than memorising one at a time.
