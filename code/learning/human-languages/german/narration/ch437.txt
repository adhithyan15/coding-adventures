German, chapter 437: Durch die Stadt.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
bummeln — to stroll, to wander around; to dawdle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the one-way street, then the German for the passer-by.

You'll want to know: bummeln.
bummeln — "to stroll, to wander around; to dawdle".
Am Samstag bummeln wir durch die Fußgängerzone. On Saturday we stroll through the pedestrian zone.
Perfekt with sein for moving: wir sind durch die Stadt gebummelt. Der Stadtbummel — a stroll round town.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of bummeln is not settled. It is often explained as a sound word for something swinging slowly to and fro, like a bell; EtymDB lists the sense 'to loaf'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
schlendern — to saunter, to amble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the passer-by, then the German for to stroll.

You'll want to know: schlendern.
schlendern — "to saunter, to amble".
Nach dem Essen schlendert er langsam zum Hotel. After the meal he saunters slowly back to the hotel.
Perfekt with sein: wir sind am Ufer entlanggeschlendert.

The word, taken apart.
schlendern came into the standard language from Low German. EtymDB links it with Middle High German lenderen 'to loiter'; the older history is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
erkundigen — to ask about, to enquire (mostly reflexive: sich erkundigen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to stroll, then the German for to saunter.

You'll want to know: erkundigen.
erkundigen — "to ask about, to enquire (mostly reflexive: sich erkundigen)".
Sie erkundigt sich nach dem Weg zum Bahnhof. She asks the way to the station.
Reflexive, with nach: ich erkundige mich nach den Öffnungszeiten.

The word, taken apart.
erkundigen is built from er- and kundig 'well informed', from the old adjective kund 'known', an inherited Germanic word related to kennen 'to know'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
die Auskunft — the information; the information desk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to saunter, then the German for to ask about.

You'll want to know: die Auskunft.
die Auskunft — "the information; the information desk".
Am Bahnhof gibt uns eine Mitarbeiterin Auskunft. At the station a member of staff gives us information.
Plural: die Auskünfte. Die Auskunft is also the information desk itself.

The word, taken apart.
Auskunft is usually traced to Middle High German ūzkunft 'a way out', built from aus 'out' and Kunft, from kommen 'to come'; the sense 'information' came later. EtymDB does not list the word, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 5 of 6.
auskennen — to know one's way around (mostly reflexive: sich auskennen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to ask about, then the German for the information.

You'll want to know: auskennen.
auskennen — "to know one's way around (mostly reflexive: sich auskennen)".
In dieser Stadt kenne ich mich gut aus. I know my way around this town well.
Reflexive and separable: ich kenne mich aus, ich habe mich ausgekannt.

The word, taken apart.
auskennen is built from aus 'out, through' and kennen 'to know', inherited from Old High German kennan and Proto-Germanic kannijaną (EtymDB).
