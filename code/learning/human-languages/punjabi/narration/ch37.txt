Punjabi, chapter 37: The Sentence That Says No.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) inside a sentence — not, inside a sentence — the pattern the book has never shown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
From the first chapter of this book: the two words that answer a question. And from the third: the word for I.
[your turn — recall: hā̃, nahī̃ — then maiṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
You have been able to say no since page one. You have never been able to say that something is not so.

The pattern, taken apart.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) goes directly before the verb, and nothing else in the sentence moves:
ਮੈਂ (…) ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) (verb) — "I do not (verb)".
That is the whole rule. Punjabi does not add a helper the way English adds do — it drops one word in front of the verb and leaves the rest alone.
Because the position is fixed, every sentence you already own has a negative twin, and you can make it without learning anything else.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nahī̃ alone, as the answer you already had.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: a sentence you can already say, then the same sentence with nahī̃ in front of its verb.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the positive and the negative, back to back.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) stand in a sentence? (Directly before the verb.) What else has to change? (Nothing.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਸਮਝ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਈ (mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī) — I did not understand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back, where ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) stands. From the verb chapter, the verb to understand. And two chapters back, the word for to me.
[your turn — recall: nahī̃ before the verb, samajhṇā, mainū̃]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The sentence, taken apart.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਸਮਝ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਈ — mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī — "understanding did not come to me".
Punjabi does not make you the subject of understanding. It makes understanding something that arrives, and you are the one it arrives at — which is exactly the shape you already met in ਮੈਨੂੰ … ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ, "X is pleasing to me".
So this is not a new grammar. It is the dative frame you own, with a different noun in it and ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) in the slot the last lesson gave you.

Why it's said this way.
This is the sentence that buys you a second attempt. Without it a learner whose conversation has run away has only silence or an English apology, and the conversation ends there. It is worth more than any noun in this book, and it should arrive fast enough to be said without assembling it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī, slowly, then at speed.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: mainū̃ … pasand hai and mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī — one frame.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: somebody says something too fast; reply with it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say that you did not understand. Which earlier frame is it built on, and who is the subject of the sentence? (ਮੈਨੂੰ … ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ; the understanding is, not you.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) reaches the page — not, on the page — four signs, every one of them old.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — four pieces, none new until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say where ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) stands in a sentence before anything is uncovered.

Script — four pieces, none new.
Uncover ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) and name it in order:
ਨ — na, ਹ — ha, the ੀ stroke, the ਂ above.
The last of those is the bindi, and it is doing real work: it puts the vowel through the nose. A ਨਹੀ without it is not this word.
This is the first of seven chapters, and it is worth saying now what holds for almost all of them: the words this book most needed were already writable. Every sign here was taught long ago.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Keep the model in view and copy ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) once. Check the bindi is there.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) from memory, then uncover and compare. Which mark would change the word if you left it off? (The ਂ above.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਨਹੀਂ ਪਤਾ (mainū̃ nahī̃ patā) — I don't know.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two lessons back, the sentence that says you did not follow. And one lesson back, that word on the page.
[your turn — recall: mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī, and ਨਹੀਂ written]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The sentence, taken apart.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਨਹੀਂ ਪਤਾ — mainū̃ nahī̃ patā — "I don't know".
ਪਤਾ is the new word, and it is a noun — the knowledge itself, not an act of knowing. So the sentence says, word for word, to me not knowledge. The same dative frame again: the third sentence in three lessons to put ਮੈਨੂੰ at the front and leave you out of the subject slot.
Punjabi keeps these two admissions apart, and so should you:
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਸਮਝ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਈ (mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī) — I did not follow you.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਨਹੀਂ ਪਤਾ (mainū̃ nahī̃ patā) — I do not have the fact.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: mainū̃ nahī̃ patā, flat, as a whole reply.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the two admissions, back to back.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: asked a question you cannot answer, give the right one of the two.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say you do not know. Then say you did not follow. What kind of word is ਪਤਾ? (A noun — the knowledge itself.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
Saying no to a sentence, all together — contradiction and repair, scored as one performance.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
No new words. One run through what this chapter added.

What you've built.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) directly before the verb, and nothing else moving.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਸਮਝ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਈ (mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī) — I did not follow you.
ਮੈਨੂੰ ਨਹੀਂ ਪਤਾ (mainū̃ nahī̃ patā) — I do not have the fact.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) on the page, bindi and all.
ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) has been in this book since its first chapter and could only ever be a whole answer. It can now be put inside a sentence, which means every statement you meet can be contradicted and every statement you make can be denied.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: someone states something you disagree with. Contradict it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the two admissions, in the order you would really need them.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ਨਹੀਂ (nahī̃) from memory, then check the bindi.]
[your turn — run: refuse a yes-or-no question, then say why you cannot answer it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Contradict a statement, then admit you do not know. Next chapter: the word for and, which this book has never had either.
