Lesson 1 of 6.
回来 (huílái) — to come back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cinema, then the Mandarin for museum.

You'll want to know: 回来.
回来 — huílái — "to come back".
我下个月五号回来 wǒ xià ge yuè wǔ hào huílái — I'll be back on the fifth of next month.
The new character, in pieces: 回 (huí) — six strokes: a large frame, a small 口 inside it, and the bar that closes the large frame last. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
回来 (huílái) is built from 回 (huí) 'to turn back' and 来 'to come', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwoj and loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to turn round'.

Script — 口 inside a larger frame.
回 (huí).
Two pieces: a large frame, and 口 inside it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The frame, first two strokes. A vertical down the left side, then a stroke along the top that turns down the right side.
口 inside, three strokes: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, and the bar that closes it.
Last, the frame's foot. One bar along the bottom closes the large frame, the way the last stroke of 国 does.
2 + 3 + 1 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 回 (huí), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 回 (huí)? (Six.) What goes inside the frame? (口.) Which stroke comes last? (The bar that closes the large frame.)

Lesson 6 of 6.
chūchāi — to travel for work; to go on a business trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go home from abroad, then the Mandarin for to go abroad.

You'll want to know: chūchāi.
chūchāi — "to travel for work; to go on a business trip".
bàba hòutiān yào chūchāi — Dad is going away on business the day after tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chūchāi is built from 出 'to go out' and chāi 'an errand'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhwit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the second under several readings, glossed 'uneven' and 'to choose', and gives no errand, so how the word came to mean a work trip is not settled.
