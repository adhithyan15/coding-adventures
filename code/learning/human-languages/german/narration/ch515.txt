German, chapter 515: Chor und Orchester.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Chor — choir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for verse, then the German for to hum.

You'll want to know: der Chor.
der Chor — "choir".
Meine Mutter singt in einem Chor. My mother sings in a choir.
Plural: die Chöre. Ch sounds like k.

The word, taken apart.
Chor is a learned borrowing from Latin chorus, from Greek chorós "a dance, a band of dancers and singers" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Orchester — orchestra.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hum, then the German for choir.

You'll want to know: das Orchester.
das Orchester — "orchestra".
Das Orchester hat sechzig Musiker. The orchestra has sixty musicians.
Plural: die Orchester. Ch sounds like k.

The word, taken apart.
Orchester comes, through Latin orchēstra, from Greek orchḗstra; in the Roman theatre it was the space in front of the stage where the senators sat (Lewis & Short), and the musicians took it over later.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Dirigent — conductor (of music).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for choir, then the German for orchestra.

You'll want to know: der Dirigent.
der Dirigent — "conductor (of music)".
Am Ende verbeugte sich der Dirigent. At the end the conductor took a bow.
Weak noun: den Dirigenten. Plural: die Dirigenten.

The word, taken apart.
Dirigent comes from Latin dīrigēns "directing, steering" (EtymDB), the present participle of dīrigere "to direct".

Lesson 4 of 5.
dirigieren — to conduct (music).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for orchestra, then the German for conductor.

You'll want to know: dirigieren.
dirigieren — "to conduct (music)".
Heute dirigiert eine junge Frau das Konzert. Today a young woman is conducting the concert.
Regular, Perfekt without ge-: hat dirigiert.

The word, taken apart.
dirigieren is a learned borrowing from Latin dīrigere "to run, to direct" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
komponieren — to compose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for conductor, then the German for to conduct.

You'll want to know: komponieren.
komponieren — "to compose".
Er hat schon als Kind Lieder komponiert. He was composing songs even as a child.
Regular, Perfekt without ge-. The composer is der Komponist.

The word, taken apart.
komponieren is a learned borrowing from Latin compōnere "to put together", built on pōnere "to put" (Lewis & Short).
