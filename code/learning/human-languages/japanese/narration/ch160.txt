Japanese, chapter 160: Cleaning Up Dust and Dirt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
そうじ (sōji) — cleaning; sweeping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to stack, then the Japanese for sorting out.

You'll want to know: そうじ.
そうじ — sōji — "cleaning; sweeping".
そうじは どようびです。 — sōji wa doyōbi desu — "The cleaning is on Saturdays."

The word, taken apart.
そうじ (sōji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sō, "sweep" (Middle Chinese sawX) and ji, "remove" (drjo) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

ちり — chiri — "dust specks; litter".
ちりとりで ちりを ひろいます。 — chiritori de chiri o hiroimasu — "I pick up the dust with a dustpan."

The word, taken apart.
ちり (chiri) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled; the kanji JMdict gives for it was attached later (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
よごれる (yogoreru) — to get dirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for cleaning, then the Japanese for dust specks.

You'll want to know: よごれる.
よごれる — yogoreru — "to get dirty".
こどもの ふくは よく よごれます。 — kodomo no fuku wa yoku yogoremasu — "Children's clothes often get dirty."
Polite よごれます, yogoremasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
よごれる (yogoreru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
よごす (yogosu) — to make dirty; to soil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dust specks, then the Japanese for to get dirty.

You'll want to know: よごす.
よごす — yogosu — "to make dirty; to soil".
いぬが ゆかを よごします。 — inu ga yuka o yogoshimasu — "The dog makes the floor dirty."
Polite よごします, yogoshimasu; a godan verb. Its intransitive partner is よごれる, yogoreru.

The word, taken apart.
よごす (yogosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as yogoreru, "to get dirty"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
