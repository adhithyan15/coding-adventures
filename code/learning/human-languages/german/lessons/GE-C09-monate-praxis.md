# Practice — half a Roman year

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MONAT-01, GE-LEX-JANUAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] No new words. Six names and the container they sit in.

## Grammar Lens: what German owns and what it bought
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-NATIVE-VS-LOAN-01, GE-LEX-MONAT-01, GE-LEX-MAERZ-01] -->

| what | German | where from |
|---|---|---|
| the **unit** | *der Monat* | inherited — it is *der Mond* |
| the **names** | *Januar … Juni* | Latin, taken whole |

One line, and it is the chapter. German had a moon and needed no help naming the
span. It had no twelve-month calendar, so when one arrived it took the labels
that came with it.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-MAERZ-MARS-01, GE-LEX-FEBRUAR-01, GE-LEX-MAI-01, GE-LEX-JUNI-01] -->

Notice what the six names are *for*, because they are not one kind of thing:

* ***Januar*** — a god, **Janus** of doorways.
* ***Februar*** — not a god at all, but a **festival**, the *Februa*.
* ***März*** — a god, **Mars**, who is also in *Dienstag* under another name.
* ***April*** — unknown, and unknown already in Rome.
* ***Mai*** — a goddess, **Maia** of growth.
* ***Juni*** — a goddess, **Juno**, queen of the gods.

Four deities, one ritual, one shrug. A calendar is not a designed object; it is
a pile of things people happened to call months, kept because nobody stopped.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MONAT-01, GE-ETYMON-MAERZ-MARS-01, GE-LEX-MAERZ-01, GE-GRAMMAR-NATIVE-VS-LOAN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say the first six months. (**Januar, Februar, März, April, Mai,
Juni**.) Which word for the span is German's own? (***Der Monat***, from
*Mond*.) Which month shares a god with *Dienstag*? (***März***.) Which one is
named for a festival? (***Februar***.) Which one nobody can explain?
(***April***.)
