Lesson 1 of 5.
o escritório — an office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a stadium, then the Portuguese for a factory.

You'll want to know: o escritório.
o escritório — "an office".

The word, taken apart.
escritório is a "writing room": built on the past participle of escrever, "to write" ( from Latin scribere), after Medieval Latin scriptorium.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a esquina — a street corner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a factory, then the Portuguese for an office.

You'll want to know: a esquina.
a esquina — "a street corner".

The word, taken apart.
esquina probably comes from a Gothic word for the shinbone, a jutting edge. EtymDB 2.1 traces it only to Old Portuguese esquina, so the link is the usual account, not a certain one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for an office, then the Portuguese for a street corner.

You'll want to know: o cruzamento.
o cruzamento — "a crossroads".

The word, taken apart.
cruzamento is "a crossing": built from the verb "to cross" and -mento. The verb is built on the word for a cross from Latin crux, "cross" (Lewis & Short).
