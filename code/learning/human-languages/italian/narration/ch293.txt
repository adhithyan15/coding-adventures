Italian, chapter 293: Ladri in casa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lo scasso — the break-in, the forced entry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the safe, then the Italian for the loot.

You'll want to know: lo scasso.
lo scasso — "the break-in, the forced entry".
Hanno trovato segni di scasso sulla porta. They found signs of forced entry on the door.
A police word: segni di scasso, signs of forced entry.

The word, taken apart.
scasso is built on scassare "to break open", from s- and cassa "box", from Latin capsa "a case" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'antifurto — the burglar alarm, the anti-theft device.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the break-in, then the Italian for to force open.

You'll want to know: l'antifurto.
l'antifurto — "the burglar alarm, the anti-theft device".
L'antifurto della macchina ha suonato tutta la notte. The car alarm went off all night.
Also an adjective: un sistema antifurto.

The word, taken apart.
antifurto joins Greek anti- "against" with furto "theft", which is inherited from Latin fūrtum (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'allarme — the alarm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to force open, then the Italian for the burglar alarm.

You'll want to know: l'allarme.
l'allarme — "the alarm".
Qualcuno ha dato l'allarme. Someone raised the alarm.
Dare l'allarme — to raise the alarm.

The word, taken apart.
allarme comes from the cry all'arme! "to arms!"; arme goes back to Latin arma "weapons" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: il vandalo.
il vandalo — "the vandal".
I vandali hanno rotto tutte le finestre della scuola. Vandals broke all the school windows.
Plural i vandali.

The word, taken apart.
vandalo comes from Latin Vandalus (EtymDB), the name of a Germanic people who sacked Rome in the year 455.
