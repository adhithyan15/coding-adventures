Lesson 1 of 6.
さかん (sakan) — thriving; popular.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to ring, then the Japanese for to ring out.

You'll want to know: さかん.
さかん — sakan — "thriving; popular".
今も その まつりは さかんです。 — ima mo sono matsuri wa sakan desu — "The festival is still going strong today."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
さかん (sakan) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

おまいり — omairi — "a visit to a shrine; worship at a temple".
まいつき じんじゃに おまいりに いきます。 — maitsuki jinja ni omairi ni ikimasu — "Every month I go to the shrine to pray."

The word, taken apart.
おまいり (omairi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from o-, "the polite prefix", and mairi, "going (humbly)" (from mairu) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 6.
しゅうかん (shūkan) — a custom; a habit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a visit to a shrine, then the Japanese for to beckon.

You'll want to know: しゅうかん.
しゅうかん — shūkan — "a custom; a habit".
日本には おもしろい しゅうかんが あります。 — nihon ni wa omoshiroi shūkan ga arimasu — "Japan has some interesting customs."

The word, taken apart.
しゅうかん (shūkan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shū, "learn" (Middle Chinese zip) and kan, "accustomed" (kwaenH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: うけつぐ.
うけつぐ — uketsugu — "to inherit; to carry on".
あには ちちの しごとを うけつぎました。 — ani wa chichi no shigoto o uketsugimashita — "My brother took over our father's work."
Polite うけつぎます, uketsugimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): むすこが みせを うけつぎました。, musuko ga mise o uketsugimashita.

The word, taken apart.
うけつぐ (uketsugu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from uke, "receiving" (from ukeru), and tsugu, "to succeed to" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
