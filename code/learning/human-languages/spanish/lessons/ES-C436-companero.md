## You'll want to know: el compañero
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-01]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-COMPANION-03] -->

**el compañero / la compañera** — the colleague, the classmate, the companion.

**You were given the etymology long ago and never the word.** The bread lesson
told you it means *one you share bread with*; this lesson hands you the word to
use.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-01, ES-LEX-PAN-01] -->

**com-** with, plus **pan**, bread, plus **-ero**, the person-ending you met on
*la portera*. Three pieces you already hold, and not one of them new.

What is worth adding is how wide the word runs in practice:

| phrase | who it means |
|---|---|
| un compañero de trabajo | a colleague |
| un compañero de clase | a classmate |
| mi compañero | a partner, depending on context |

On its own, **mi compañero** is ambiguous between a work partner and a life
partner, and Spanish speakers disambiguate with *de trabajo*, *de piso*, *de
clase* when it matters. That ambiguity is not sloppiness; the word never
promised more than *the one I share things with*.
