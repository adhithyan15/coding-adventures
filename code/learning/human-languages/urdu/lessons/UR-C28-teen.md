# تین — three, and how much of a word a language can keep

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-DO, UR-ETYMON-DO-TWO-PIE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **do** and its English cousin.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-TEEN]; assesses=[] -->

> **تین** — *tīn* — **three**

## The word, taken apart — the same root, two distances
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-ETYMON-TEEN-THREE-PIE]; assesses=[UR-LEX-TEEN, UR-LEX-EK] -->

**تین** continues Sanskrit *tri*, from Indo-European \**treyes*,
"three." English **three**, Latin **trēs** and Greek **treîs** are the same
word.

Now set it beside the Persian for three, *se*. Both descend from \**treyes*.
Urdu's form still has the *t*; Persian's has lost the *t* AND the *r*, leaving
one syllable. Same root, two roads, and one of them wore the word down much
further.

An English speaker hears *tīn* and gets **three** for nothing. Hearing *se* as
**three** takes a moment.
