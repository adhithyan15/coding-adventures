Italian, chapter 343: Ho i miei dubbi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dubitare — to doubt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the compromise, then the Italian for to give in.

You'll want to know: dubitare.
dubitare — "to doubt".
Dubito che arrivi in orario. I doubt that he will arrive on time.
Dubitare che takes the subjunctive. Dubitare di qualcuno — not to trust someone.

The word, taken apart.
dubitare is a learned borrowing from Latin dubitāre "to waver, to be uncertain, to doubt" (EtymDB), related to dubius "doubtful", a word linked with duo "two": a mind pulled two ways.

Lesson 2 of 5.
scettico — sceptical.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to give in, then the Italian for to doubt.

You'll want to know: scettico.
scettico — "sceptical".
Sono un po' scettico su questa dieta. I am a bit sceptical about this diet.
Plural scettici, feminine scettica. Essere scettico su qualcosa.

The word, taken apart.
scettico is a learned borrowing, through Latin scepticus, from Greek skeptikós "inquiring, reflective", from sképtesthai "to look about carefully"; a related Greek word passed into Latin as scopos "a mark to aim at" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: negare.
negare — "to deny".
Il ragazzo nega di aver rotto il vetro. The boy denies having broken the glass.
Negare di aver fatto qualcosa — to deny having done something.

The word, taken apart.
negare is inherited from Latin negāre "to say no, to deny, to refuse" (Lewis & Short), a verb built on the same ne- "not" as nōn.

Lesson 4 of 5.
assurdo — absurd.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sceptical, then the Italian for to deny.

You'll want to know: assurdo.
assurdo — "absurd".
È assurdo pagare così tanto per un caffè. It is absurd to pay so much for a coffee.
Ma è assurdo! — that is ridiculous! Feminine assurda.

The word, taken apart.
assurdo is a learned borrowing from Latin absurdus "out of tune, incongruous" (EtymDB), built from ab "away from" and surdus "deaf, dull-sounding": a note that does not fit the tune.

Lesson 5 of 5.
discutibile — questionable, debatable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to deny, then the Italian for absurd.

You'll want to know: discutibile.
discutibile — "questionable, debatable".
La scelta dell'allenatore è discutibile. The coach's choice is questionable.
It politely suggests something is wrong: una scelta discutibile.

The word, taken apart.
discutibile is built on discutere "to discuss", from Latin discutere "to strike apart, to break up, to scatter" (Lewis & Short), built from dis- and quatere "to shake": a matter that can be shaken.
