5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
causidicus, causidicī — an advocate, a pleader for hire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to do with energy, then the Latin for to tire out.

You'll want to know: causidicus, causidicī.
causidicus, causidicī — "an advocate, a pleader for hire".
Causidicus prō reō dīxit. The advocate spoke for the accused.
causidicus, causidicī m., from causa, a case, and dīcō: a case-speaker, often said with a little scorn.

The word, taken apart.
causidicus is built from causa "a case at law" and dīcō "to say" (Lewis & Short): one who speaks cases.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cliēns, clientis — a client, a dependant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to tire out, then the Latin for an advocate.

You'll want to know: cliēns, clientis.
cliēns, clientis — "a client, a dependant".
Clientēs māne ad domum patrōnī veniunt. The clients come to their patron's house in the morning.
cliēns, clientis m. A client owed his patron greeting and support; the patron owed him protection and help at law. English client.

The word, taken apart.
cliēns is usually traced to an old verb clueō "to hear, to be called" (Lewis & Short): one who listens to his patron. English client comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
nāviculārius, nāviculāriī — a shipowner, a shipper.

nāviculārius, nāviculāriī — "a shipowner, a shipper".
Nāviculārius frūmentum trāns mare vehit. The shipowner carries grain across the sea.
nāviculārius, nāviculāriī m., built on a little word for a ship, from nāvis. The great grain ships of Rome were run by nāviculāriī.

The word, taken apart.
nāviculārius is built on nāvicula "a little ship" (Lewis & Short), a small form of nāvis "a ship".

Lesson 4 of 5.
librārius, librāriī — a copyist, a scribe; a bookseller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a client, then the Latin for a shipowner.

You'll want to know: librārius, librāriī.
librārius, librāriī — "a copyist, a scribe; a bookseller".
Librārius librōs dīligenter scrībit. The copyist writes out books carefully.
librārius, librāriī m., built on liber, a book, with a short i. Copyists were often slaves trained to write a fast clear hand.

The word, taken apart.
librārius is built on liber, librī "a book" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -ārius of a man busy with something. English library comes from the same word through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
agrīmēnsor, agrīmēnsōris — a land surveyor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a shipowner, then the Latin for a copyist.

You'll want to know: agrīmēnsor, agrīmēnsōris.
agrīmēnsor, agrīmēnsōris — "a land surveyor".
Agrīmēnsor fīnēs agrī mētītur. The surveyor measures the boundaries of the field.
agrīmēnsor, agrīmēnsōris m., from agrī, of a field, and mētior, to measure (participle mēnsus). A late word, in Ammianus and Cassiodorus.

The word, taken apart.
agrīmēnsor is built from agrī, the genitive of ager "a field" and mēnsor "a measurer" (Lewis & Short), the agent noun of mētior "to measure".
