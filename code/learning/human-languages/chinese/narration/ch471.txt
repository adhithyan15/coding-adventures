Mandarin Chinese, chapter 471: Eyes.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yǎnlèi — tears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for slapdash, then the Mandarin for slow-witted.

You'll want to know: yǎnlèi.
yǎnlèi — "tears".
wǒ dì-yī cì kàn dào bàba de yǎnlèi — It was the first time I had seen my dad in tears.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǎnlèi is built from yǎn 'eye' and lèi 'tears', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngeanX and lwijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the eye' and the second 'tears'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
眼前 (yǎnqián) — before one's eyes; right now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for slow-witted, then the Mandarin for tears.

You'll want to know: 眼前.
眼前 — yǎnqián — "before one's eyes; right now".
那个事故发生在我眼前 nàge shìgù fāshēng zài wǒ yǎnqián — That accident happened right in front of my eyes.
The new character, in pieces: 眼 (yǎn) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left an eye, five strokes, like 日 rì with one bar more; on the right six strokes, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two bars, a vertical that turns up at the foot, a short stroke falling to the left and a long one falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
眼前 (yǎnqián) is built from 眼 (yǎn) 'eye' and 前 'in front', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngeanX and dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the eye' and the second 'before'.

Script — an eye, then a right side of six.
眼 (yǎn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the eye, five strokes. Like 日, made tall and narrow, with one bar more: a vertical down the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two short bars inside, then the bar across the foot.
The right piece, six strokes. A stroke that runs right and turns down. A bar inside, and a second one under it. Then a vertical down the left that turns up to the right at the foot, a short stroke falling to the left, and last a long stroke falling to the right.
5 + 6 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 眼 (yǎn), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 眼 (yǎn) have? (Two: five strokes and six.) What is the left piece like? (日, with one bar more.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
小心眼 (xiǎoxīnyǎn) — petty; narrow-minded.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tears, then the Mandarin for right before one's eyes.

You'll want to know: 小心眼.
小心眼 — xiǎoxīnyǎn — "petty; narrow-minded".
他有点儿小心眼 tā yǒudiǎnr xiǎoxīnyǎn — He's a bit petty.

The word, taken apart.
小心眼 (xiǎoxīnyǎn) is built from 小 'small', 心 'heart' and 眼 (yǎn) 'eye', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX, sim and ngeanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a heart with a small eye, which sees only small things.

Lesson 6 of 6.
zhǎyǎn — to blink; to wink.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: zhǎyǎn.
zhǎyǎn — "to blink; to wink".
tā duì wǒ zhǎyǎn — He winked at me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: zhǎ is said with a rising tone before yǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
zhǎyǎn is built from zhǎ and 眼 (yǎn) 'eye', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsreap and ngeanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the eye moving'.
