French, chapter 375: Au lycée.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le lycée — the secondary school (ages fifteen to eighteen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the resolution, then the French for to aim.

You'll want to know: le lycée.
le lycée — "the secondary school (ages fifteen to eighteen)".
Ma sœur entre au lycée en septembre. My sister starts secondary school in September.
Belgium: l'athénée or l'école secondaire; Québec: l'école secondaire, then le cégep; Switzerland: le gymnase.

The word, taken apart.
lycée comes from Latin lycēum (EtymDB), from Greek Lúkeion, the name of a gymnasium where a famous philosopher taught.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
fréquenter — to attend, to go regularly to; to spend time with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the secondary school, then the French for the secondary-school pupil.

You'll want to know: fréquenter.
fréquenter — "to attend, to go regularly to; to spend time with".
Il fréquente un lycée près de chez lui. He attends a secondary school near his home.
Fréquenter quelqu'un — to see a lot of someone.

The word, taken apart.
fréquenter is a learned borrowing from Latin frequentāre "to visit often, to crowd" (Lewis & Short), from frequēns "crowded, frequent".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le bulletin — the school report; the bulletin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the secondary-school pupil, then the French for to attend.

You'll want to know: le bulletin.
le bulletin — "the school report; the bulletin".
Cette année, mon bulletin est meilleur. This year, my school report is better.
Un bulletin de vote — a ballot paper.

The word, taken apart.
bulletin was borrowed from Italian bullettino "a small note, a ticket" (EtymDB), built on Latin bulla "a bubble, a seal" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le proviseur — the head teacher (of a lycée).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to attend, then the French for the school report.

You'll want to know: le proviseur.
le proviseur — "the head teacher (of a lycée)".
Le proviseur a parlé aux parents hier soir. The head teacher spoke to the parents last night.
In a collège, the head is le principal. Belgium: le préfet; Québec: le directeur.

The word, taken apart.
proviseur is a learned borrowing from Latin prōvīsor "one who foresees, a provider" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from prōvidēre "to foresee, to provide for".
