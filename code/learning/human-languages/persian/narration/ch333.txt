Persian, chapter 333: Growing up and growing old.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نوجوانی (nowjavâni) — adolescence, the teenage years.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a newborn baby, then the Persian for to bring up.

You'll want to know: نوجوانی.
نوجوانی (nowjavâni) — "adolescence, the teenage years".
در نوجوانی فوتبال بازی میکردم. Dar nowjavâni futbâl bâzi mikardam. In my teens I used to play football.
From nowjavân "a teenager": now "new" + javân "young". The past continuous tells what you used to do.

The word, taken apart.
nowjavâni is built on nowjavân "a teenager", from now "new" and javân "young", with the noun suffix -i. All of it is native Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
دانش آموخته (dânesh âmukhte) — a graduate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to bring up, then the Persian for adolescence.

You'll want to know: دانش آموخته.
دانش آموخته (dânesh âmukhte) — "a graduate".
او دانش آموخته رشته پزشکی است. U dânesh âmukhte-ye reshte-ye pezeshki ast. She is a graduate in medicine.
Formal; literally "one who has learned knowledge". The everyday word is fâregh-ottahsil.

The word, taken apart.
dânesh âmukhte is built from Persian dânesh "knowledge" and âmukhte "learned", the participle of âmukhtan "to learn". Both are native words.

Lesson 3 of 5.
سربازی (sarbâzi) — military service.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for adolescence, then the Persian for a graduate.

You'll want to know: سربازی.
سربازی (sarbâzi) — "military service".
برادرم دو سال سربازی رفت. Barâdaram do sâl sarbâzi raft. My brother did two years of military service.
From sarbâz "a soldier". Sarbâzi raftan is "to do military service".

The word, taken apart.
sarbâzi adds the suffix -i to sarbâz "a soldier", usually explained as sar "head" + bâz "one who stakes": one who stakes his head. EtymDB lists no entry.

پیری (piri) — old age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

پدربزرگم در پیری هم هر روز کتاب میخواند. Pedarbozorgam dar piri ham har ruz ketâb mikhând. Even in old age my grandfather read every day.
pir "old (of people)" + -i.

The word, taken apart.
piri is built from Persian pir "old", a native word for elderly people, with the noun suffix -i.
