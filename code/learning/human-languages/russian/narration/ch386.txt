Russian, chapter 386: Giving a talk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
доклад (doklád) — a talk, a paper (given aloud).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bachelor, then the Russian for a master’s graduate.

You'll want to know: доклад.
доклад (doklád) — "a talk, a paper (given aloud)".
На конференции я делаю доклад. Na konferéntsii ya délayu doklád. I am giving a talk at the conference.
Masculine. A prepared talk, or a report to a superior: доклад (doklád) начальнику.

The word, taken apart.
доклад (doklád) is built on докладывать "to report", from до- and класть "to lay", inherited from Proto-Slavic klasti: to lay a matter before someone.

Lesson 2 of 5.
реферат (referát) — an essay, a summary paper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a master’s graduate, then the Russian for a talk.

You'll want to know: реферат.
реферат (referát) — "an essay, a summary paper".
Студенты пишут реферат по литературе. Studénty píshut referát po literatúre. The students are writing an essay on literature.
Masculine. A short paper that sums up what sources say.

The word, taken apart.
реферат (referát) was borrowed from German Referat (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin referat "let him report", a form of referre "to bring back, to report" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
презентация (prezentátsiya) — a presentation; a launch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a talk, then the Russian for an essay.

You'll want to know: презентация.
презентация (prezentátsiya) — "a presentation; a launch".
Презентация длилась двадцать минут. Prezentátsiya dlilás dvádtsat minút. The presentation lasted twenty minutes.
Feminine. Подготовить презентацию — to prepare a presentation. Also a launch: презентация (prezentátsiya) книги.

The word, taken apart.
презентация (prezentátsiya) is usually traced, through western languages, to Late Latin praesentātiō "a showing", from praesentāre "to show", built on praesēns "present, at hand" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
понятие (ponyátiye) — a concept, a notion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an essay, then the Russian for a presentation.

You'll want to know: понятие.
понятие (ponyátiye) — "a concept, a notion".
Я не имею понятия, где он. Ya ne iméyu ponyátiya, gde on. I have no idea where he is.
Neuter. Не иметь понятия — to have no idea.

The word, taken apart.
понятие (ponyátiye) is built on понять "to understand", from по- and the old verb "to take" that also stands in снимать and занимать: a concept is what the mind takes hold of.

Masculine. A technical word, not a period of time.

The word, taken apart.
термин (términ) is usually traced, through western languages, to Latin terminus "a boundary, a limit" (Lewis & Short): a word with fixed limits to its meaning.
