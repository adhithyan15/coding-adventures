Portuguese, chapter 515: Lesões e batota.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o balneário — changing room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for judo, then the Portuguese for fencing.

You'll want to know: o balneário.
o balneário — "changing room".
Depois do jogo, os jogadores tomam banho no balneário. After the match, the players shower in the changing room.
In Brazil: o vestiário.

The word, taken apart.
balneário is a learned word built on Latin balneārius "of a bath" (Lewis & Short), from balneum "a bath".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a claque — supporters' group, fans.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for fencing, then the Portuguese for changing room.

You'll want to know: a claque.
a claque — "supporters' group, fans".
A claque cantou durante o jogo todo. The supporters sang for the whole match.
In Portugal, an organised group of fans.

The word, taken apart.
claque is usually traced to French claque, a group paid to applaud in a theatre, from claquer "to clap", an imitative verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lesionar — to get injured (lesionar-se); to injure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for supporters' group, then the Portuguese for injury.

You'll want to know: lesionar.
lesionar — "to get injured (lesionar-se); to injure".
O avançado lesionou-se no treino. The striker got injured in training.
Mostly reflexive, with the pronoun after the verb: lesionou-se — he got injured. Estar lesionado — to be injured.

The word, taken apart.
lesionar is built on lesão "injury", a learned borrowing from Latin laesiō (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: a batota.
a batota — "cheating (at games)".
O meu irmão faz batota nas cartas. My brother cheats at cards.
Fazer batota — to cheat. European Portuguese; in Brazil, trapaça.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of batota is not settled.
