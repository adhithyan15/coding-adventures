Latin, chapter 229: To decree, To try, To go on, To hold off, To remove.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dēcernō, dēcernere — to decree, to settle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a whirlpool; the top, then the Latin for order, a row.

You'll want to know: dēcernō, dēcernere.
dēcernō, dēcernere — "to decree, to settle".

The word, taken apart.
dēcernō is dē, "down, off", with cernō, "to sift, to decide" (EtymDB 2.1): to decide finally. cernō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European krey-, "to sift" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: temptō, temptāre.
temptō, temptāre — "to try, to test".

The word, taken apart.
temptō, also spelled tentō (EtymDB 2.1), is often taken as a frequentative of teneō, "to hold", but its origin is uncertain. English tempt and attempt come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cessō, cessāre — to hold off, to be idle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to try, then the Latin for to go on.

You'll want to know: cessō, cessāre.
cessō, cessāre — "to hold off, to be idle".

The word, taken apart.
cessō is a frequentative of cēdō, "to go, to give way" (Lewis & Short): to keep giving way, so to hang back. English cease comes from it.
