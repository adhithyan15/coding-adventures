## You'll want to know: どしゃぶり
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C354-TENKI354-01]; assesses=[] -->

**どしゃぶり** — *doshaburi* — "a downpour".

> **どしゃぶりに なったら、しあいは ちゅうしでしょう。** — *doshaburi ni nattara, shiai wa chūshi deshō* — "If it pours, the match will probably be called off."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**どしゃぶり** (*doshaburi*) is built from the Sino-Japanese *dosha*, written with two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, *do*, "soil" (Middle Chinese *thuX*) and *sha*, "sand" (*srae*), and the native *buri*, from *furu*, "to fall", voiced from *furi*. JMdict marks that spelling as ateji, characters chosen for their sound, so where *dosha* came from is not settled (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
