Sanskrit, chapter 521: Drums and Strings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
वादकः (vādakaḥ) — a musician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for singing, then the Sanskrit for plays an instrument.

You'll want to know: वादकः.
वादकः — vādakaḥ — "a musician".
वादकः मृदंगं वादयति। vādakaḥ mṛdaṅgaṃ vādayati. The musician plays the mridanga.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वादकः (vādakaḥ) comes from the causative of the root √vad, "to make sound", with the agent ending -aka; MW gives vādaka as a musician (MW).

वीणायाः एका तन्त्री जीर्णा अस्ति। vīṇāyāḥ ekā tantrī jīrṇā asti. One string of the veena is worn out.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
डमरुः (ḍamaruḥ) — a hand drum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a musician, then the Sanskrit for a string of an instrument.

You'll want to know: डमरुः.
डमरुः — ḍamaruḥ — "a hand drum".
बालः डमरुं वादयति। bālaḥ ḍamaruṃ vādayati. The child plays a hand drum.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
डमरुः (ḍamaruḥ) is the word MW records for a sacred drum shaped like an hourglass, the drum of Śiva; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
