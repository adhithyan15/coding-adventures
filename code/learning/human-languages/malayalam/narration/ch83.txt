Malayalam, chapter 83: Where You Came From.
4 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
നിന്ന് (ninnŭ) — from.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the ending that means in or at. Then say the word for a town, and put that ending on it.

Grammar Lens: from is built on in.
You can already say that something is in a place. This is how you say it comes out of one, and the useful part is that you do not throw the old ending away.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഊരിൽ ūril, in the town.
ഊരിൽ നിന്ന് ūril ninnŭ, from the town.
നിന്ന് (ninnŭ) goes after the word that already carries the in-ending. So from is not a new ending competing with the old one — it is a second step on top of it, and the sense follows the shape: in the town first, then out of that.
One thing is different from every ending you have met. The endings glue on: ūr plus -il is one written word. നിന്ന് (ninnŭ) is a word of its own, standing after it. It began life as a form of a verb meaning to stand — having stood in the town — and hardened into the ordinary way to say from. That history is why it behaves like a word and not like a suffix.

What you've built.
A direction, on top of a position.

Lesson 2 of 4.
എവിടെ നിന്ന് (eviṭe ninnŭ) — where from?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the word that asks where. Then say the word that means from.

You'll want to know: എവിടെ നിന്ന്.
എവിടെ നിന്ന്? — eviṭe ninnŭ? — where from?
Put the two together in the order the last lesson gave you and the question builds itself. എവിടെ already asks about a place you are at; നിന്ന് (ninnŭ) turns that into a place you came out of.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
എവിടെ?, where?
എവിടെ നിന്ന് (eviṭe ninnŭ)?, where from?
Nothing new is memorised here. The question is two words you own, in the order the last lesson gave you, and that is worth noticing in its own right: a well-built system spends its second question for free.
Answer it with any place you have:
ഊരിൽ നിന്ന് — ūril ninnŭ — from the town.

What you've built.
Both sides of an exchange, from one rule.

Lesson 3 of 4.
കേരളത്തിൽ (kēraḷattil) — what -ം does before an ending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the name of the state and the name of the country. Then say the ending that means in.

Grammar Lens: two of your place names behave differently.
Put the in-ending on both names you learned and only one of them goes quietly.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഇന്ത്യ. in it: ഇന്ത്യയിൽ indyayil.
കേരളം. in it: കേരളത്തിൽ kēraḷattil.
ഇന്ത്യ ends in a vowel and takes a -യ- to join, the same join kocciyil uses. Nothing surprising.
കേരളം does something else. The -ം is gone and -ത്ത- has arrived in its place, and only then does the ending follow.
That is not this word being difficult. Every noun ending in -ം does it, and this book has taught you a pile of them:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
പുസ്തകം pustakaṁ. in it: പുസ്തകത്തിൽ pustakattil.
കേരളം kēraḷaṁ. in it: കേരളത്തിൽ kēraḷattil.
So this is a class, not an exception, and it is a large one. Learning it once puts every -ം word you own into the in-form, which is a much better return than memorising two place names.
The -ം is a citation dress. It is what the word wears when it stands alone and is named; the moment anything is added, the word changes into its working shape first. Look for that habit rather than for a list.
Now the whole chapter arrives at once:
കേരളത്തിൽ നിന്ന് — kēraḷattil ninnŭ — from Kerala.

Lesson 4 of 4.
(where you came from) (evide ninnŭ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Ask where from, then say what each of its two words was doing.

Grammar Lens: one position, one direction, one change of shape.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
ഊര്. in it: ഊരിൽ. from it: ഊരിൽ നിന്ന് (ninnŭ).
ഇന്ത്യ. in it: ഇന്ത്യയിൽ. from it: ഇന്ത്യയിൽ നിന്ന് (ninnŭ).
കേരളം. in it: കേരളത്തിൽ (kēraḷattil). from it: കേരളത്തിൽ (kēraḷattil) നിന്ന് (ninnŭ).
Read the middle column and then the last. നിന്ന് (ninnŭ) never changes and never glues on — it follows a word that already carries the in-ending, and it does the same thing to all three.
Read the first column against the middle and the third row is the odd one. കേരളം loses its -ം and puts -ത്ത- there instead, and so does every other -ം word this book has given you. The -ം is a citation dress: what the word wears standing alone, dropped the moment anything is added.

What you've built.
An answer to the first question anybody asks you.
Two chapters ago the place words arrived and the question was still unaskable. Now both halves exist, and neither the question nor most of the answer cost a new word: eviṭe ninnŭ is two words you owned, and the in-form of every -ം noun came out of a single rule rather than a list.
That is the shape worth carrying: when the endings are regular, the work is in knowing which shape a word takes before an ending, not in learning more endings.
