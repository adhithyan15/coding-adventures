French, chapter 499: Jeux de société.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le jeu de société — board game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for torch, then the French for campfire.

You'll want to know: le jeu de société.
le jeu de société — "board game".
Le soir, nous jouons à un jeu de société. In the evening we play a board game.
Plural des jeux de société.

The word, taken apart.
le jeu de société is "a game for company": jeu is inherited from Latin iocus "a joke, play", and société from Latin societās, built on socius "companion" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: le dé.
le dé — "dice, die".
C'est à toi de lancer le dé. It is your turn to throw the dice.
Plural les dés. Un dé à coudre — a thimble.

The word, taken apart.
le dé is usually traced to Latin datum "what is given", from dare "to give" (Lewis & Short), but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: le pion.
le pion — "pawn, counter (in a game)".
Elle avance son pion de trois cases. She moves her counter forward three squares.
In school slang, le pion is also a supervisor.

The word, taken apart.
le pion is inherited from Late Latin pedō "a foot soldier" (EtymDB), built on pēs "foot" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'échiquier — chessboard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for dice, then the French for pawn.

You'll want to know: l'échiquier.
l'échiquier — "chessboard".
Ils ont posé l'échiquier sur une petite table. They put the chessboard on a little table.
Masculine. Chess is les échecs.

The word, taken apart.
l'échiquier comes from Old French eschequier, built on eschec "check", which goes back through Arabic to Persian šāh "king".

Lesson 5 of 5.
tricher — to cheat.

You'll want to know: tricher.
tricher — "to cheat".
Mon petit frère triche toujours aux cartes. My little brother always cheats at cards.
Un tricheur — a cheat.

The word, taken apart.
tricher is inherited from Old French trichier (EtymDB), usually traced to Vulgar Latin triccāre, from Latin trīcārī "to make difficulties, to trifle" (Lewis & Short).
