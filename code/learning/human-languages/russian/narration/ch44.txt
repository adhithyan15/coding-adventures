Russian, chapter 44: Fruit, Apple, Pear, Cherry, Berry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
фрукты (frúkty) — fruit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for butter; oil, then the Russian for salt.

You'll want to know: фрукты.
фрукты (frúkty) — "fruit".

The word, taken apart.
фрукты (frúkty) came through Polish frukt from Latin frūctus, "fruit, enjoyment"; it is recorded from 1705, in Peter the Great's time (Vasmer). English fruit comes from the same Latin word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
яблоко (yábloko) — an apple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for salt, then the Russian for fruit.

You'll want to know: яблоко.
яблоко (yábloko) — "an apple".

The word, taken apart.
яблоко (yábloko) from Proto-Slavic ablŭko — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian obuolỹs, Old High German apful and Old Irish aball, all "apple": English apple is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
груша (grúsha) — a pear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for fruit, then the Russian for an apple.

You'll want to know: груша.
груша (grúsha) — "a pear".

The word, taken apart.
груша (grúsha) — probably a very old loan: the first sound wavers between г- and к- (Bulgarian круша), and Lithuanian kriáušė, "pear", has the same source (Vasmer). Vasmer reports a comparison with a Kurdish word, but the donor is unknown.

Lesson 4 of 5.
вишня (víshnya) — a cherry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an apple, then the Russian for a pear.

You'll want to know: вишня.
вишня (víshnya) — "a cherry".

The word, taken apart.
вишня (víshnya) from Proto-Slavic višĭnja — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Old High German wîhsila, "sour cherry", and through it to Latin viscum, "birdlime", because cherry gum and mistletoe berries were both used to snare birds.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ягода (yágoda) — a berry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pear, then the Russian for a cherry.

You'll want to know: ягода.
ягода (yágoda) — "a berry".

The word, taken apart.
ягода (yágoda) from Proto-Slavic agoda, aga with an added -oda — inherited, a cousin of Lithuanian úoga, "berry" (Vasmer). Russian added j- at the front, as in яблоко (yábloko), "apple".
