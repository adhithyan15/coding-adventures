## You'll want to know: 问候
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C173-INVITE173-02]; assesses=[] -->

**问候** — *wènhòu* — "to send regards to; to greet".

> *nǐ bāng wǒ wènhòu nǐ bàba māma* — Give my regards to your mum and dad.

The new character, in pieces: 候 — ten strokes: the person piece, a thin vertical beside it, then on the right a small hooked top, a horizontal, and a base of five strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, two horizontals, and two long strokes falling to either side. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**问候** (*wènhòu*) is built from 问 'to ask' and 候 'to wait upon', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *mjunH* and *huwH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to ask after someone. The second is a different word from 后 'after', *huwX*.
