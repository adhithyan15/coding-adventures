# de la — the half of the partitive that was missing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DU-PARTITIF-07, FR-GRAM-AU-AUX-01, FR-LEX-PAIN-01, FR-LEX-EAU-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "some bread" and "some water". Both are yours. Now try "some
milk" — *le lait* is a *le*-word, but **la viande**, meat, is not.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-DE-LA-01]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DU-PARTITIF-07, FR-GRAM-LE-LA-GENDER-05, FR-LEX-LAIT-05, FR-LEX-DE-PREP-01] -->

> **de la** — some, in front of a *la*-word.

> **du lait** — some milk. · **de la viande** — some meat. · **de l'eau** —
> some water.

Three shapes, one rule, and it is a rule you already own from two other places:
**de** plus the article, contracting only where *le* and *les* contract.

That is worth stating plainly, because it looks like three separate things to
memorise and is one:

| the noun takes | some… |
|---|---|
| *le* | **du** |
| *la* | **de la** |
| *l'* | **de l'** |

The food chapter taught the first and third and stopped, because every noun on
its table happened to be a *le*-word or a vowel-word. The middle line was never
missing from the language, only from the table.
