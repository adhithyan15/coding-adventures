Japanese, chapter 266: Trouble in the River.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おぼれる (oboreru) — to drown; to be drowning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an escape route, then the Japanese for to rush to the scene.

You'll want to know: おぼれる.
おぼれる — oboreru — "to drown; to be drowning".
こどもが うみで おぼれました。 — kodomo ga umi de oboremashita — "A child got into trouble in the sea."
Polite おぼれます, oboremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): おとこが おぼれました。, otoko ga oboremashita.

The word, taken apart.
おぼれる (oboreru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

You'll want to know: とびこむ.
とびこむ — tobikomu — "to jump in; to dive in".
こどもが おぼれたら、すぐ とびこみます。 — kodomo ga oboretara, sugu tobikomimasu — "If a child is drowning, I jump in at once."
Polite とびこみます, tobikomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが かわに とびこみました。, chichi ga kawa ni tobikomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とびこむ (tobikomu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tobi, "leaping" (from tobu), and komu, "to go into" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ぶじ (buji) — unharmed; safe and sound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to tug, then the Japanese for at last.

You'll want to know: ぶじ.
ぶじ — buji — "unharmed; safe and sound".
こどもは ぶじでした。 — kodomo wa buji deshita — "The child was unhurt."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ぶじ (buji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are bu, "nothing" (Middle Chinese mju) and ji, "matter" (dzriH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
