Japanese, chapter 289: A Wedding.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はなよめ (hanayome) — a bride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for lively, then the Japanese for a celebration.

You'll want to know: はなよめ.
はなよめ — hanayome — "a bride".
はなよめは しろい きものを きていました。 — hanayome wa shiroi kimono o kite imashita — "The bride wore a white kimono."

The word, taken apart.
はなよめ (hanayome) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from hana, "flower", and yome, "bride" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
しょうたいする (shōtai suru) — to invite formally; to host.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bridegroom, then the Japanese for to vow.

You'll want to know: しょうたいする.
しょうたいする — shōtai suru — "to invite formally; to host".
あには しごとの ともだちも しょうたいしました。 — ani wa shigoto no tomodachi mo shōtai shimashita — "My brother invited his friends from work as well."
Polite しょうたいします, shōtaishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): あにが ともだちを しょうたいしました。, ani ga tomodachi o shōtai shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しょうたいする (shōtai suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "invite" (Middle Chinese tsyew) and tai, "wait" (dojX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The native verb suru, "to do", makes it a verb.
