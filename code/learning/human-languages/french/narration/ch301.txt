French, chapter 301: L'alerte.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'alerte — the alert, the alarm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tourniquet, then the French for the stretcher.

You'll want to know: l'alerte.
l'alerte — "the alert, the alarm".
L'alerte a été donnée à trois heures du matin. The alarm was raised at three in the morning.
Donner l'alerte — to raise the alarm; une alerte à la bombe — a bomb scare.

The word, taken apart.
alerte is usually traced to Italian all'erta "on the lookout, on the height" (EtymDB lists the French word without a parent).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le défibrillateur — the defibrillator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the alert, then the French for to alert.

You'll want to know: le défibrillateur.
le défibrillateur — "the defibrillator".
Il y a un défibrillateur à l'entrée de la mairie. There is a defibrillator at the entrance of the town hall.
Often in public places, in a box on the wall.

The word, taken apart.
défibrillateur is a learned medical word built on fibrillation, from Latin fibrilla "a small fibre", from fibra (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la sortie de secours — the emergency exit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to alert, then the French for the defibrillator.

You'll want to know: la sortie de secours.
la sortie de secours — "the emergency exit".
Les gens sont sortis par la sortie de secours. People went out through the emergency exit.
Signs also say issue de secours.

The word, taken apart.
sortie is built on sortir "to go out", which is usually traced to Latin sortīrī "to draw lots" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB lists no parent); secours is inherited from Latin succursus "help".

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: évacuer.
évacuer — "to evacuate, to clear".
La police a évacué le bâtiment. The police evacuated the building.
Also of water: évacuer l'eau de pluie — to drain away rainwater.

The word, taken apart.
évacuer is a learned borrowing from Latin ēvacuāre "to empty out" (EtymDB), built on vacuus "empty".
