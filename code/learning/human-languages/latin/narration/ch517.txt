5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vēna, vēnae — a vein.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a limb, then the Latin for joints.

You'll want to know: vēna, vēnae.
vēna, vēnae — "a vein".
Sanguis per vēnās currit. Blood runs through the veins.
Feminine, first declension. Also a vein of metal, or of talent. English vein.

The word, taken apart.
vēna is a native Latin noun; Lewis & Short guesses a link with vehō "to carry", but the origin is not settled. English vein comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nervus, nervī — a sinew, tendon; a string.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for joints, then the Latin for a vein.

You'll want to know: nervus, nervī.
nervus, nervī — "a sinew, tendon; a string".
Faber nervōs firmōs habet. The smith has strong sinews.
Masculine, second declension. In the plural also strength, vigour. English nerve.

The word, taken apart.
nervus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "sinew, tendon", and Greek neuron is a cousin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English nerve comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
pellis, pellis — a hide, skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a vein, then the Latin for a sinew.

You'll want to know: pellis, pellis.
pellis, pellis — "a hide, skin".
Pāstor pellem ovis vendit. The shepherd sells the sheep's hide.
Feminine, third declension. Mostly an animal's hide; cutis is skin as part of the body.

The word, taken apart.
pellis is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to cover, skin" (EtymDB), and Old English fell "hide" is a cousin. English pellicle, a thin skin, comes from its diminutive.

Masculine, third declension, from sūdō — to sweat.

sūdor is built on sūdō "to sweat", inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to sweat"; English sweat is a cousin (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
pulsus, pulsūs — a beat, pulse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hide, then the Latin for sweat.

You'll want to know: pulsus, pulsūs.
pulsus, pulsūs — "a beat, pulse".
Medicus pulsum vēnārum temptat. The doctor tests the beat of the veins.
Masculine, fourth declension, from pellō — to strike, push. Pulsus vēnārum — the pulse. English pulse.

The word, taken apart.
pulsus is the action noun of pellō "to push, strike" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English pulse and repulse come from the family.
