Gujarati, chapter 147: What was and where I went.
7 lessons. You can do the first 5 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
હતો — was, and an ending that listens to the subject.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask do you live here? Then say my brother and my sister: their endings -ો and -ી are about to come back. And the word for there: ત્યાં.

Grammar Lens: છે becomes હતો.
હું ત્યાં હતો. — hũ tyā̃ hato. — I was there.
In the present the copula changes for the person: છું, છે, છો. In the past it stops caring about the person and starts caring about gender, with the endings my uses:
હું ત્યાં હતી. — hũ tyā̃ hatī. — I was there, said by a woman.

You'll want to know: હતું and હતા.
A neuter subject takes હતું. More than one person, or respect, takes હતા:
ઘર નાનું હતું. — ghar nānũ hatũ. — The house was small. તેઓ અહીં હતા. — teo ahī̃ hatā. — They were here.
હતો — he, or a man saying I;
હતી — she, or a woman saying I;
હતું — a neuter thing;
હતા — more than one, or respect.
મારો મારી મારું મારા and હતો હતી હતું હતા end alike, sign for sign.

[your turn — say: તે અહીં હતી — te ahī̃ hatī, she was here]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — choose: the ending for પાણી, a neuter noun: પાણી ગરમ હતું]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 7.
ગઈકાલે (gaīkāle), સવારે, ઘરે — the -e of when and where.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I was there, then they were here. And the word for yesterday: ગઈકાલ.

Grammar Lens: -e on a time.
ગઈકાલ names the day. To put something on that day, Gujarati adds -ે:
ગઈકાલે હું ત્યાં હતો. — gaīkāle hũ tyā̃ hato. — Yesterday I was there.
The same -ે turns every part of the day, and every day, into a when:
આજ becomes આજે — āje — today;
આવતીકાલ becomes આવતીકાલે — āvtīkāle — tomorrow;
સવાર becomes સવારે — savāre — in the morning;
બપોર becomes બપોરે — bapore — at midday;
સાંજ becomes સાંજે — sānje — in the evening;
રવિવાર becomes રવિવારે — ravivāre — on Sunday.
રાત is the one that changes shape: રાત્રે (rātre), at night, keeps an older form with ત્ર.

You'll want to know: -e on a place, and the two-way કાલે.
ઘર takes it too: ઘરે (ghare), at home.
કાલે has been in the book since કાલે મળીશું, see you tomorrow. It also means yesterday, and the verb decides which:
કાલે મળીશું. — a future verb, so tomorrow;
કાલે હું ઘરે હતી. — a past verb, so yesterday.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: gaīkāle hũ ghare hato, or hatī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: રાત્રે તેઓ ઘરે હતા — rātre teo ghare hatā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: is કાલે in kāle hũ ghare hatī yesterday or tomorrow? (Yesterday: the verb is past.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the answer with નહીં: આવતીકાલે નહીં, ગઈકાલે — āvtīkāle nahī̃, gaīkāle]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does -ે do to સવાર? (It makes the time a when: in the morning.) How do you know which day કાલે means? (From the verb's tense.)


Lesson 3 of 7.
હું ગયો, હું ગઈ — the past of moving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say yesterday I was at home, and say which day કાલે means in કાલે મળીશું. Then the two verbs of movement: જવું, to go, and આવવું, to come.

Grammar Lens: one word does the whole job.
ગઈકાલે હું બજાર ગયો. — gaīkāle hũ bajār gayo. — Yesterday I went to the market.
ગયો is a past of જવું all on its own: no છું, no હતો beside it. It ends the way હતો ends, and it agrees the same way:
ગયો — a man; ગઈ — a woman;
ગયું — something neuter; ગયા — more than one, or respect.
ગઈ is not new to you. It is the first half of ગઈકાલ, yesterday: the gone day. ગયો goes back through Old Gujarati gayaü to Sanskrit gata, "gone" (CDIAL 4008).

You'll want to know: આવ્યો.
આવવું builds its past on આવ્ય-:
તે ગઈકાલે ઘરે આવ્યો. — te gaīkāle ghare āvyo. — He came home yesterday. તે આવી. — te āvī. — She came.
The same four endings again: આવ્યો, આવી, આવ્યું, આવ્યા. The feminine sheds the ્ય.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: hũ gayo, hũ gaī, ame gayā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: મારી બહેન સાંજે આવી — mārī bahen sānje āvī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તમે બજાર ગયા, ખરું? — tame bajār gayā, kharũ?, you went to the market, right?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Say that yesterday you went to the market, then that they came home.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which other words does the past of go need? (None.) What does its ending agree with? (Whoever went.) Which old word holds ગઈ? (ગઈકાલ.)


Lesson 4 of 7.
થયું — it happened.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I went, then she came. And the greeting from the second day: તમને મળીને આનંદ થયો, pleased to meet you.

Grammar Lens: the past that was there from the start.
થયો is the past of થવું, to happen, to become. In તમને મળીને આનંદ થયો the joy is the subject (to you, meeting, joy happened), and આનંદ is masculine, so the verb says થયો.
Change the subject and the ending follows it:
આનંદ થયો — joy happened: masculine;
મને ચિંતા થઈ — worry happened to me, I got worried: feminine;
કામ થયું — the work got done: neuter;
તેઓ ખુશ થયા — they became happy: more than one.
થયો goes back to Sanskrit sthita, "standing", which CDIAL lists as a past of to be (CDIAL 13768).

Lesson 5 of 7.
મોડું (moḍũ) — late.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the work got done, then he came.

You'll want to know: મોડું.
મોડું (moḍũ) — "late".
It is the partner of વહેલું, early, and like the possessives it bends to agree:
હું મોડો આવ્યો. — hũ moḍo āvyo. — I came late. (a man) તે મોડી આવી. — te moḍī āvī. — She came late. મોડું થયું. — moḍũ thayũ. — It got late.

The word, taken apart.
મોડું (moḍũ) is an old word whose origin is not settled: CDIAL lists no Gujarati form for it, and no Sanskrit source has been shown. Its -ું is the neuter ending of the adjective, which is why it turns into મોડો and મોડી.

What you've built.
One more word, and it arrives already in the past.

Lesson 6 of 7.
ડ — retroflex ḍa.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — meet one form, Writing — recall after hiding and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[your turn — recall: say late — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say ટ, ઠ and ઢ, the three tongue-curled letters you already write]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
મોડું (moḍũ) holds the one curled consonant your hand has not made yet.

You'll want to know: the fourth corner.
Gujarati's retroflex row, with the tongue curled back, has a plain and a breathy letter for each voicing:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
voiceless. plain: ટ ṭa. breathy: ઠ ṭha.
voiced. plain: ? breathy: ઢ ḍha.
The missing corner is the plain voiced one, ḍa, and મોડું (moḍũ) needs it.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — meet one form.
ડ — ḍa.
Keep ડ (ḍa) visible. Begin at the upper right and sweep left across the high shoulder. Come down through the middle into the right side of the broad lower bowl, then carry the bowl round its base and finish at its lower-left end. One stroke, no lift.
ઠ starts the same way, but its bowl curls back inward and closes. ડ (ḍa) leaves its bowl open at the lower left. If the end turns back in, you have made a ઠ.

Writing — notice and trace.
Finger-trace ડ (ḍa) once, then air-trace it once. Notice the shoulder, the drop through the middle, and the open end of the bowl.

Writing — copy with the model.
Copy visible ડ (ḍa) once. Compare only two things: is the bowl broad, and does it end open at the lower left? Repair one landmark if needed.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Writing — recall after hiding.
Hide the model and write retroflex ḍa once. Uncover ડ, compare, and stop after one repair.
[once you have stopped driving — question: Hide the model and write retroflex da from memory.]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read ડ (ḍa) ઠ મોડું (moḍũ) aloud. Which corner of the retroflex row is still missing? (None: with ડ (ḍa) all four are in your hand.)
