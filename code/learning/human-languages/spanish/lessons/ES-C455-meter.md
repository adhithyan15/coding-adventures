# meter — put it in

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C332-WORD-21, ES-LEX-C366-REF-31] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el permiso** and **la caja**. There is a Latin verb inside
the first, and you are about to use it on the second.

## You'll want to know: meter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C366-REF-31] -->

**meter** — to put in, to put inside.

*Mete la ropa en la caja* — put the clothes in the box. *Meter la llave* — put
the key in. *Se ha metido en la cama* — he's got into bed.

*Meterse* also does the social senses: *no te metas* — don't interfere.
*Meterse con alguien* — to pick on somebody.

## Grammar Lens: meter is not poner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04] -->

Both come out as "put" and they are not interchangeable:

| what you are doing | the verb |
|---|---|
| putting something **inside** something | **meter** |
| putting something **somewhere** | **poner** |

*Pon el libro en la mesa* — on the table, a surface. *Mete el libro en la
caja* — into the box, an inside. Swap them and a Spanish speaker will picture
you burying a book in a table.

*Meter* nearly always brings **en** with it, because "in" is the whole idea of
the verb. If there is no inside, you want *poner*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05, ES-LEX-C332-WORD-21] -->

Latin **mittere**, to send — and its participle *missus*.

Spanish wore the plain verb down to *meter* and shifted it from sending to
inserting, which is a short move: to send a thing into a box is to put it
there. The bookish arrivals kept the shape: *admitir*, *permitir*, *emitir*,
*transmitir*.

And there it is in a word you already have. **El permiso** is *per-* plus
*missus*: something **sent through**. English took the same set as **permit**,
**admit**, **mission**, **missile** and **message** — every one of them a
sending.

So *meter la ropa en la caja* and *pedir permiso* run on one Latin verb, one
worn smooth and one carried in from the page.
