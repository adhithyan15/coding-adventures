Japanese, chapter 463: Training Hard.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きたえる (kitaeru) — to toughen; to train (the body).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a ranking, then the Japanese for a foul.

You'll want to know: きたえる.
きたえる — kitaeru — "to toughen; to train (the body)".
まいにち はしって、からだを きたえています。 — mainichi hashitte, karada o kitaete imasu — "I run every day to toughen my body."
Polite きたえます, kitaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): せんしゅが からだを きたえました。, senshu ga karada o kitaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
きたえる (kitaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
たいりょく (tairyoku) — stamina; physical strength.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a foul, then the Japanese for to toughen.

You'll want to know: たいりょく.
たいりょく — tairyoku — "stamina; physical strength".
かれは たいりょくが あります。 — kare wa tairyoku ga arimasu — "He has a lot of stamina."

The word, taken apart.
たいりょく (tairyoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tai, "body" (Middle Chinese thejX) and ryoku, "power" (lik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
