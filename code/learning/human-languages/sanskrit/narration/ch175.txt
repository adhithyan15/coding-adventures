Sanskrit, chapter 175: Takes away, Supports, Carries, Endures, Tries.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हरति (harati) — takes away, carries off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for keeps, then the Sanskrit for hurts.

You'll want to know: हरति.
हरति — harati — "takes away, carries off".

The word, taken apart.
harati is the present tense of the root √hṛ, which MW glosses "to take, carry, carry off". The verb lives on in Hindi harnā, "to take, seize, plunder" (CDIAL 13980).

Lesson 2 of 5.
भरति (bharati) — supports, bears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hurts, then the Sanskrit for takes away.

You'll want to know: भरति.
भरति — bharati — "supports, bears".

The word, taken apart.
bharati is the present of the root √bhṛ, "to bear, carry, support" (MW). The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW sets it beside Latin ferō and English bear. Through Prakrit it became Hindi bharnā, "to fill" (CDIAL 9397).

Lesson 3 of 5.
वहति (vahati) — carries, flows.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for takes away, then the Sanskrit for supports.

You'll want to know: वहति.
वहति — vahati — "carries, flows".

The word, taken apart.
vahati is the present of the root √vah, "to carry, convey", which also says what a river does with what it carries. The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Latin vehere, the source of English vehicle. It lives on as Hindi bahnā, "to flow" (CDIAL 11453).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सहते (sahate) — endures, bears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for supports, then the Sanskrit for carries.

You'll want to know: सहते.
सहते — sahate — "endures, bears".

The word, taken apart.
sahate is the present of the root √sah. In the Rigveda it means "conquers, prevails", and later "bears, endures" (MW; CDIAL 13304). It lives on as Hindi sahnā, "to bear" (CDIAL 13304).

Lesson 5 of 5.
यतते (yatate) — tries, strives.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for carries, then the Sanskrit for endures.

You'll want to know: यतते.
यतते — yatate — "tries, strives".

The word, taken apart.
yatate is built on the root √yat, "to strive after, endeavour" (MW). MW suggests the root is probably related to √yam, with a first sense "to stretch", but gives that link only as a probability.
