## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04]; assesses=[] -->

**et** — "**and**."

The plainest word in this book, and the corpus went forty-seven chapters
without teaching it.

> **Aqua et vīnum.** — Water and wine.
> **Pater et māter.** — Father and mother.

Latin has a **second** way of saying it, and a reader meets it constantly:
**-que**, glued onto the **back** of the second word rather than standing between
the two.

> **Senātus populusque Rōmānus** — "The Senate **and** the Roman people," the
> SPQR on every Roman standard. The *-que* is that fourth letter.

*-que* is Proto-Indo-European ***\*kwe***, an enclitic Sanskrit kept as *ca* and
Greek as *te*. **Et** went the other way: it is Spanish *y*, French *et*, Italian
*e* and Portuguese *e*, and every Romance daughter kept it.
