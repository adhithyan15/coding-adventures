Russian, chapter 316: Carnival and parade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
карнавал (karnavál) — a carnival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to clink glasses, then the Russian for champagne.

You'll want to know: карнавал.
карнавал (karnavál) — "a carnival".
Летом в городе был карнавал. Létom v górode byl karnavál. In the summer there was a carnival in the town.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
карнавал (karnavál) is usually traced through French carnaval to Italian carnevale; EtymDB lists the Russian word with no parent.

парад (parád) — "a parade".
Мы смотрели парад на главной улице. My smotréli parád na glávnoy úlitse. We watched the parade on the main street.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
парад (parád) is usually traced through French or German Parade to a Romance word built on Latin parāre "to prepare" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 4 of 5.
шествие (shéstviye) — a procession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a masquerade, then the Russian for a parade.

You'll want to know: шествие.
шествие (shéstviye) — "a procession".
Шествие шло по центру города два часа. Shéstviye shlo po tséntru góroda dva chasá. The procession moved through the town centre for two hours.
Neuter.

The word, taken apart.
шествие (shéstviye) is a Church Slavonic word, šĭstvie "a going, a procession" (EtymDB), built on шествовать "to walk solemnly".

ярмарка (yármarka) — "a fair".
На ярмарке мы купили мёд. Na yármarke my kupíli myod. At the fair we bought honey.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ярмарка (yármarka) was borrowed through Polish jarmark from German Jahrmarkt "a yearly market" (EtymDB), with Jahr "year".
