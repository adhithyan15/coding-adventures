Portuguese, chapter 489: Noites mal dormidas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a insónia — insomnia, sleeplessness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to recover, then the Portuguese for recovery.

You'll want to know: a insónia.
a insónia — "insomnia, sleeplessness".
O café à noite dá-me insónias. Coffee at night keeps me awake.
In Brazil: insônia. Often plural: ter insónias.

The word, taken apart.
insónia is a learned borrowing from Latin īnsomnia "sleeplessness" (Lewis & Short), built on somnus "sleep".

bocejar is usually explained as built on boca "mouth", which is inherited from Latin bucca "cheek, mouth" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o arrepio — shiver; goosebumps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to snore, then the Portuguese for to yawn.

You'll want to know: o arrepio.
o arrepio — "shiver; goosebumps".
Aquela história deu-me arrepios. That story gave me the shivers.
Dar arrepios — to give the creeps. The verb is arrepiar.

The word, taken apart.
arrepio comes from arrepiar "to make one's hair stand on end", which is usually traced to Latin horripilāre "to bristle with hairs" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a cãibra — cramp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to yawn, then the Portuguese for shiver.

You'll want to know: a cãibra.
a cãibra — "cramp".
A meio da noite, acordei com uma cãibra na perna. In the middle of the night, I woke up with a cramp in my leg.
In Brazil also câimbra.

The word, taken apart.
cãibra is usually traced to a Germanic word meaning "bent, crooked", the same source as English cramp; the path into Portuguese is not settled.
