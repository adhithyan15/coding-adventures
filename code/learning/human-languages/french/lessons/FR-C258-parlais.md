# je parlais — the past that is still running

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-PASSE-COMPOSE-02, FR-LEX-PARLER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I spoke", with *avoir* and the participle. (*J'ai parlé.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That past has an edge: it happened, and it finished. French has a
second past with no edge at all, and every story you tell from here on needs
both of them.

## Grammar Lens: one stem, one set of endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-IMPARFAIT-01]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IR-PRESENT-02, FR-GRAMMAR-PRENDRE-STEM-03, FR-LEX-HABITER-04] -->

Start from the *nous* form of the present and take off its **-ons**. What is
left is the stem. Then add **-ais** for "I":

> nous parl-ons, **je parlais** — I was speaking, I used to speak
> nous finiss-ons, **je finissais** — I was finishing
> nous pren-ons, **je prenais** — I was taking

The other persons keep that stem and change only the ending: **je parlais**,
**tu parlais**, **il parlait**, **nous parlions**, **vous parliez**, **ils
parlaient**.

Two things to hear. Four of those six are one sound: *parlais*, *parlais*,
*parlait* and *parlaient* all end in the same open *è*, so the pronoun in
front is what tells a listener who was speaking. And *nous parlions* is one
small *i* away from the present *nous parlons*. Listen for that *i*.

This past is the **imparfait**, and almost every verb follows this recipe.
The one that refuses it comes next.

What it means is "was ‑ing", or "used to": *j'habitais à Paris*, I lived in
Paris, back then, with no start and no finish in view.

## The word, taken apart: where -ais came from
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-IMPARFAIT-AIS-02]; assesses=[FR-ETYMON-ETRE-STARE-09, FR-ETYMON-HABERE-AVOIR-08] -->

Latin already had this past, and it marked it with a **b**: *habēbat*, "he
was having", from *habēre*, the Latin verb that gave **avoir** (EtymDB 2.1).
In French the *b* wore away entirely. You met one of its forms in the
chapter on *être*: **j'étais** goes back to *stābam*, "I was standing".

Older French spelled the ending **-oit**, *il parloit*, and the spelling
**-ais** settled only in the nineteenth century, long after the sound had
changed. Neither EtymDB nor Lewis & Short records endings this late, so that
history is the usual account in histories of French, given here as one.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPARFAIT-01, FR-ETYMON-IMPARFAIT-AIS-02, FR-LEX-PARLER-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which present form do you build the stem on? (The ***nous***
form, without its *-ons*.) Build "I was taking". (***Je prenais.***) Which
Latin letter wore away from the ending? (The ***b*** of *habēbat*.) Which
past has an edge, and which has none? (***J'ai parlé*** has one; ***je
parlais*** has none.)
