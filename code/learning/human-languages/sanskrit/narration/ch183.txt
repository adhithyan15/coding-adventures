Lesson 1 of 5.
युगम् (yugam) — an age, an era.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for gratitude, then the Sanskrit for memory.

You'll want to know: युगम्.
युगम् — yugam — "an age, an era".

The word, taken apart.
yuga is built on the root √yuj, "to yoke, join" (MW). It first meant "yoke", then "a pair", "a generation", and "an age". The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares English yoke and Latin iugum, and Hindi jūā, "yoke", comes from yuga (CDIAL 10482).

Lesson 2 of 5.
मुहूर्तः (muhūrtaḥ) — an auspicious hour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for memory, then the Sanskrit for an age.

You'll want to know: मुहूर्तः.
मुहूर्तः — muhūrtaḥ — "an auspicious hour".

The word, taken apart.
muhūrta meant "a moment" in the Rigveda (CDIAL 10229) and later a fixed thirtieth of a day, 48 minutes (MW). MW files it beside muhur, "suddenly, in a moment"; its deeper origin is not settled. An auspicious hour is a well-chosen muhūrta.

Lesson 3 of 5.
होरा (horā) — an hour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an age, then the Sanskrit for an auspicious hour.

You'll want to know: होरा.
होरा — horā — "an hour".

The word, taken apart.
horā was borrowed from Greek hōra, "hour, season" (MW), with the Greek astronomy and astrology that reached India. English hour comes from the same Greek word through Latin and French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
चिरम् (ciram) — for a long time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an auspicious hour, then the Sanskrit for an hour.

You'll want to know: चिरम्.
चिरम् — ciram — "for a long time".

The word, taken apart.
ciram is the accusative of cira, "long-lasting", used as an adverb (MW). The origin of cira is uncertain: MW suggests the root √ci with a question mark. Gujarati and Marathi keep cir, "for a long time" (CDIAL 4824).

Lesson 5 of 5.
चिरात् (cirāt) — after a long time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an hour, then the Sanskrit for for a long time.

You'll want to know: चिरात्.
चिरात् — cirāt — "after a long time".

The word, taken apart.
cirāt is the ablative of cira, "long-lasting": "from a long time", so "at last" (MW). Cira itself is of uncertain origin, and CDIAL 4824 traces it back to the Rigveda.
