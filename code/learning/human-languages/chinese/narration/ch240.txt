Mandarin Chinese, chapter 240: Feeling Ill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
生病 (shēngbìng) — to get sick; to fall ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to breathe, then the Mandarin for a medical check-up.

You'll want to know: 生病.
生病 — shēngbìng — "to get sick; to fall ill".
我生病了头很疼不想吃东西 wǒ shēngbìng le tóu hěn téng bù xiǎng chī dōngxi — I'm ill: my head really hurts and I don't want to eat.

The word, taken apart.
生病 (shēngbìng) is built from 生 'to grow' and 病 'illness', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and bjaengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'worry' and 'suffering' and explains it, after an older book, as an illness grown worse.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: tù.
tù — "to throw up; to vomit".
wǒ yùnchuán yǒudiǎnr xiǎng tù — I'm seasick and feel a bit like throwing up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tù is inherited from Middle Chinese thuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to vomit'; the dictionary also lists the character with a rising tone for spitting out.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lā dùzi — to have diarrhoea; to have an upset stomach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for swollen, then the Mandarin for to throw up.

You'll want to know: lā dùzi.
lā dùzi — "to have diarrhoea; to have an upset stomach".
tā zuótiān lā dùzi le — He had an upset stomach yesterday.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lā dùzi is built from lā 'to pull' and dùzi 'belly', all three syllables inherited from Middle Chinese, lop, duX and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to break'; how the phrase came to mean an upset stomach is not settled.
