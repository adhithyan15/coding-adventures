# ع (ain) — the letter inside بعد

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C265-WAW-HAMZA-01, UR-LEX-C267-BAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ؤ once, and name it. (*vāʾo hamza*.)

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **بعد** (*bād*), "after". This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ع
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-SCRIPT-C267-AIN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ع** — *ain*.

It sits in the middle of **بعد** (*bād*). In Urdu it has no sound of its own: it colours the vowel beside it, so the *a* of **بعد** is said long, *bād*. In the middle of a word it is a small closed loop on the line, joined on both sides. Standing alone it opens out: a small open head, then a deep bowl that sweeps below the line.

## Writing: ع
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C267-AIN-01] -->

Put your pen on ع and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C265-WAW-HAMZA-01, UR-SCRIPT-C267-AIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at **بعد** and point at ع inside it]
- [YOU TRACE: ع three times, saying *bād* as you finish each one]
- [YOU WRITE: **بعد** once, joined, with the small loop in its middle]
- [YOU READ: **بعد** aloud once more]

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C267-AIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter is this — ع? (**ain**: it colours the vowel.) Which word did it come from? (**بعد**, *bād*, "after".)
