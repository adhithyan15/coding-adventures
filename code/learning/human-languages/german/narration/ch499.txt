German, chapter 499: Stricken und sticken.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Nadel — needle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for neighbourhood, then the German for hospitality.

You'll want to know: die Nadel.
die Nadel — "needle".
Die Nadel ist so klein, dass meine Oma eine Brille braucht. The needle is so small that my grandma needs glasses.
Plural: die Nadeln. Also a pin, or the needle of a pine tree.

The word, taken apart.
Nadel is inherited from Middle High German nādele and Old High German nādala, from Proto-Germanic nēþlō "needle" (EtymDB); English needle is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Faden — thread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for hospitality, then the German for needle.

You'll want to know: der Faden.
der Faden — "thread".
Der Faden ist zu kurz für den Knopf. The thread is too short for the button.
Plural: die Fäden. Den Faden verlieren — to lose the thread.

The word, taken apart.
Faden is usually traced to Old High German fadum "the span of outstretched arms", a length of thread; English fathom is a cousin. EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
stricken — to knit.

You'll want to know: stricken.
stricken — "to knit".
Im Winter strickt meine Mutter Schals für die ganze Familie. In winter my mother knits scarves for the whole family.
Regular: hat gestrickt.

The word, taken apart.
stricken is built on Old High German stric "cord, loop" (EtymDB): knitting makes loops of yarn.

Done with one hooked needle, die Häkelnadel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sticken — to embroider.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to knit, then the German for to crochet.

You'll want to know: sticken.
sticken — "to embroider".
Auf das Kissen hat sie eine Blume gestickt. She embroidered a flower on the cushion.
Regular: hat gestickt. Not to be confused with stecken — to put, to stick.

The word, taken apart.
sticken is usually traced to Old High German sticken "to prick, to stitch", related to stechen "to prick"; EtymDB lists the word without a source.
