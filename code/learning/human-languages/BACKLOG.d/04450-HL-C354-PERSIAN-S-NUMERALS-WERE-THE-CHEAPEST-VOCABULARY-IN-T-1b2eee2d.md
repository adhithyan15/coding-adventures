## HL-C354 — Persian's numerals were the CHEAPEST vocabulary in the track, and the file that measured them had to change shape to say so

**HL-C350's second zero-numeral track, closed.** Persian taught no numeral at
all across 71 lessons: *yek*, *do*, *se*, *chahâr* returned ZERO occurrences, so
a learner holding fourteen infinitives, five sky words and nine letters could
give no age, no price, no telephone number and no time. Chapters 16–20 add 26
lessons and close `FA-A1-Q-01`, `FA-A1-Q-02` and `FA-A1-KH-11`; coverage moves
71/180 (39%) to 74/180 (41%).

**THE FINDING THAT GENERALISES: in an etymology-first track the count is the
CHEAPEST vocabulary, not the dearest.** Nine of the ten Persian cardinals have
an everyday English cousin from the same Indo-European root — *do*/two,
*se*/three, *chahâr*/four, *panj*/five (the *panj* of **Punjab**, five waters),
*dah*/ten. And ONE sound law, taught on a number because that is where it is
easiest to see, does work far beyond the chapter: **Iranian turned word-initial
*s* into *h***, which is why *haft* faces **septem** and *hind* faces
**sindhu**. The very next lesson is its exception — *hasht* has no *s* in its
history and took its *h* from *haft* standing next to it. A rule and its
exception, one lesson apart, both free.

**A PROBE SHAPE WORTH COPYING: probe the RULE, not the forms it generates.**
Persian builds every number from eleven to nineteen by putting a unit in front
of *dah*, so chapter 18 teaches three teens and has the reader BUILD the other
six. `FA-A1-Q-01`'s probe therefore names `FA-GRAMMAR-TEENS-DAH` and the three
worked examples, and DELIBERATELY does not name `FA-LEX-CHAHARDAH` — an atom
that does not exist, for a lesson that was never written and should not be. The
suite asserts both halves: the rule atom is present and the invented lexical one
is absent. A probe that listed nine words would have been claiming six lessons
the track does not have, and it would have passed.

**ORDINALS COST ONE ENDING**, which is why they are here at all. Twenty tracks
enumerate an ordinal point and eighteen leave it uncovered, making it the
weakest column in the corpus. Persian's is *-om* on the cardinal, with *avval*,
"first", the single Arabic borrowing outside the rule — exactly as English
**first** and **second** sit outside **third**. Twelve cardinals plus one suffix
is twelve ordinals.

**A SCRIPT-INVENTORY PRECEDENT, and the reason it was needed.**
`uncoveredGlyphs` is a HEADWORD check, and the corpus glyph-gap queue is pinned
at empty. **پنج** needs ج and **صد** needs ص, and neither was in the
Perso-Arabic inventory, so the numerals were unteachable until they were. Both
were added as **recognition-only owners with an empty `strokeOrder`**: this
inventory records Persian-scoped provenance and its own entries say the
separately sourced Arabic ج and ص orders must NOT be borrowed, and the Persian
source on hand is a video whose per-letter timestamps could not be verified.
`validate.ts` already represents that state — "penLifts and strokeOrderSource
must be supplied together", so omitting all three is legal and means exactly
"unverified". **A drafted writing lesson for ص was CUT rather than shipped with
an invented stroke order**, which is why the track's never-taught-glyph count
moves 25 → 26: *sad* is readable and not yet writable, and the number says so.

**THE REINFORCEMENT DECOMPOSITION, which is the whole reason to state it
separately.** Persian's total moves 225 → 293, and **none of it is this
tranche's**: every atom the tranche introduces meets R1, R2 and R3, verified
atom by atom rather than by watching the total. The +68 is pre-existing debt
that growing the track from 71 to 97 lessons made JUDGEABLE for the first time —
**R4 alone goes 0 → 40, because a 71-lesson track was never long enough to be
measured on a window that starts at 80.** Script-closure violations, forward
references, atom spikes, payoff surprises and duration violations are all
unchanged, and atoms-never-revisited FALLS, 11 → 10.

Two corpus ceilings moved and both are documented at their own site:
`ruleStatements` 31 → 32 and `lessonsWithFindings` 119 → 120, for
`FA-C20-dovvom-sevvom`, whose entire content IS the *-om* rule. Its tranche
registered two and the other was rewritten away rather than absorbed.

**STILL OPEN.** `urdu` and `russian` teach no numeral at all; `chinese` teaches
*yi*–*wu* and nothing from *liu* to *shi*; `french` and `german` reach twenty
against a point demanding a hundred and want a different repair.
