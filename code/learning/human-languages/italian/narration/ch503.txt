Italian, chapter 503: Giochi da tavolo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gli scacchi — chess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for trip, then the Italian for to explore.

You'll want to know: gli scacchi.
gli scacchi — "chess".
Mio nonno mi ha insegnato a giocare a scacchi. My grandfather taught me to play chess.
Giocare a scacchi; scacco matto — checkmate.

The word, taken apart.
scacchi is the plural of scacco, which EtymDB traces through Arabic shāh "king in chess" to Persian shāh "king"; the matto of scacco matto is often linked to a phrase saying the king is beaten.

la dama — "draughts, checkers; lady".
Giochiamo a dama dopo cena? Shall we play draughts after dinner?
La dama is also a lady, or a partner at a dance.

The word, taken apart.
dama was borrowed from French dame "lady" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin domina "mistress of the house".

Lesson 3 of 5.
il dado — dice; stock cube.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for chess, then the Italian for draughts.

You'll want to know: il dado.
il dado — "dice; stock cube".
Tira il dado: tocca a te. Throw the dice: it is your turn.
The plural is i dadi. In the kitchen, il dado is a stock cube.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of dado is not settled; it is often traced to Latin datum "something given", from dare "to give".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il gioco da tavolo — board game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for draughts, then the Italian for dice.

You'll want to know: il gioco da tavolo.
il gioco da tavolo — "board game".
Il sabato sera facciamo un gioco da tavolo con gli amici. On Saturday evenings we play a board game with friends.
The plural is i giochi da tavolo.

The word, taken apart.
gioco da tavolo is built from gioco "game", inherited from Latin iocus "a joke, a jest" (EtymDB), and tavolo "table", from Latin tabula "a board" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

barare — "to cheat (at games)".
Non gioco più con te: bari sempre! I am not playing with you any more: you always cheat!
Built on il baro "a card cheat". In exams, copiare.

The word, taken apart.
barare is built on baro "a cheat at cards" (EtymDB); the origin of baro is not settled.
