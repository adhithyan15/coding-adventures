# पचास रुपये — a price, said out loud

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C72-COST-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the word for a rupee. You learned where its name came from —
a piece of metal that has been given a shape.

## Grammar Lens: put the two together
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C96-TENS-03, HI-LEX-C72-COST-02] -->

**पचास रुपये** — *fifty rupees*.

Number first, then the thing. That is the whole construction, and you have both
halves already: the number arrived in this chapter and the rupee arrived a long
way back.

**रुपये** rather than **रुपया**, because more than one is being counted. That
bend is the same one the track has been making since the adjectives, and it
costs nothing new here.

## Grammar Lens: the prices you can now read
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C96-TENS-01, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-02, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-03, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-05, HI-LEX-C72-HUNDRED-01] -->

| said | meaning |
|---|---|
| तीस रुपये | thirty rupees |
| चालीस रुपये | forty rupees |
| पचास रुपये | fifty rupees |
| सत्तर रुपये | seventy rupees |
| सौ रुपये | a hundred rupees |

Five prices, and between them most of what a tea stall, a bus fare or a bag of
vegetables will ask of you.

**This is what the counting was for.** A number on its own is a recitation; a
number in front of a thing is a price, an age or an address, and that is where
the nine rungs stop being a list and start being useful.
