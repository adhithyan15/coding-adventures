Spanish, chapter 669: Con el plano.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el plano — the street map, the plan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the excess, then the Spanish for to drag.

You'll want to know: el plano.
el plano — "the street map, the plan".
¿Tiene un plano de la ciudad? Do you have a map of the town?
El plano is of a town or a building; el mapa is of a country.

The word, taken apart.
plano is a learned borrowing from Latin plānus "even, level, flat" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): a town drawn flat on paper. The inherited form is llano "flat".

Lesson 2 of 5.
orientarse — to find your way, to get your bearings.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to drag, then the Spanish for the street map.

You'll want to know: orientarse.
orientarse — "to find your way, to get your bearings".
Me oriento bien en las ciudades. I find my way around cities easily.
The opposite is perderse.

The word, taken apart.
orientarse is built on oriente "east", a learned borrowing from Latin oriēns "rising", said of the sun (EtymDB): to find your way was first to find the east.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la ubicación — the location.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the street map, then the Spanish for to find your way.

You'll want to know: la ubicación.
la ubicación — "the location".
Te mando la ubicación del hotel. I will send you the location of the hotel.
Latin America often uses ubicar for to find or to be located.

The word, taken apart.
ubicación is built on ubicar "to place, to locate", which is built on Latin ubī "where" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
señalar — to point to, to point out; to mark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to find your way, then the Spanish for the location.

You'll want to know: señalar.
señalar — "to point to, to point out; to mark".
Me señaló el hotel en el plano. He pointed out the hotel on the map.
Señalar con el dedo — to point with your finger.

The word, taken apart.
señalar is built on señal "sign" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Late Latin signāle, from signum "a mark, a sign".

Lesson 5 of 5.
situado — located, situated.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the location, then the Spanish for to point to.

You'll want to know: situado.
situado — "located, situated".
El hotel está situado en el centro. The hotel is located in the centre.
Used with estar. Latin America often says ubicado.

The word, taken apart.
situado is the past form of situar "to place", which is built on Latin situs "position, site".
