# la guardia — the one that stays open

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-GUARDAR, ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-23] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **guardar** and **la farmacia**. Somebody is keeping watch
tonight, and it is one chemist in the town.

## You'll want to know: la guardia
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-23] -->

**la guardia** — duty, watch: the turn of being the one available.

> **Farmacia de guardia.** Servicio nocturno.

*La farmacia de guardia* — the duty chemist, the one open when the rest are
shut, posted on every pharmacy door. *Estar de guardia* — to be on call.
*El médico de guardia* — the doctor on duty.

**El guardia** with the masculine article is a different thing: a police
officer. The article carries the whole distinction.

## Grammar Lens: one word, two genders, two meanings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-03, ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02] -->

| article | what it means |
|---|---|
| **la** guardia | the duty, the watch |
| **el** guardia | the officer keeping it |

Spanish does this with a small set of words, and the rule inside it is
consistent: the **feminine** is the abstract thing, the **masculine** is the
person who does it. *La guía* is the guidebook, *el guía* is the person
showing you round.

So *la farmacia de guardia* is a chemist **on watch**, and *un guardia en la
puerta* is a man standing at it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-03, ES-LEX-GUARDAR] -->

Germanic **wardōn**, to watch over — the same root as **guardar**, which you
already have for keeping and putting away.

Spanish took a shelf of Germanic words from the Visigoths and from Frankish,
and they are recognisable by the **gu-** where English has plain **w-**:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **gu**ardar | **w**ard |
| **gu**erra | **w**ar |
| **gu**ante | (glove, from the same Frankish *want*) |

English got *guard* back from French, so **guard** and **ward** are the same
Germanic word arriving twice — once directly, once round by way of Paris.
