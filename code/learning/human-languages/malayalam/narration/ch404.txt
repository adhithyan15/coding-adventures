Malayalam, chapter 404: Old Writings and Ruins.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ചരിത്രകാരൻ (caritrakāran) — a historian.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a curse, then the Malayalam for to curse.

You'll want to know: ചരിത്രകാരൻ.
ചരിത്രകാരൻ — caritrakāran — "a historian".
ചരിത്രകാരൻ ആ കോട്ടയെക്കുറിച്ച് ഒരു പുസ്തകം എഴുതി. caritrakāran ā kōṭṭayekkuṟiccŭ oru pustakaṁ eḻuti. The historian wrote a book about that fort.
ചരിത്രം, history, + -കാരൻ, one who works at it.

The word, taken apart.
ചരിത്രകാരൻ (caritrakāran) is built on ചരിത്രം (caritraṁ), "history", from Sanskrit caritra, "going; behaviour" (CDIAL 4691), with the ending -കാരൻ.

Lesson 3 of 7.
താളിയോല (tāḷiyōla) — a palm-leaf manuscript.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a historian, then the Malayalam for ancient.

You'll want to know: താളിയോല.
താളിയോല — tāḷiyōla — "a palm-leaf manuscript".
ഗ്രന്ഥശാലയിൽ പഴയ താളിയോലകൾ സൂക്ഷിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു. granthaśālayil paḻaya tāḷiyōlakaḷ sūkṣiccirikkunnu. Old palm-leaf manuscripts are kept in the library.
Plural: താളിയോലകൾ, tāḷiyōlakaḷ.
Before paper, books were written on such leaves.

The word, taken apart.
താളിയോല (tāḷiyōla) is a native compound of താളി, usually traced to the name of a palm, and ഓല (ōla), "a palm leaf, a writing leaf" (DEDR 1070).

Lesson 4 of 7.
ലിപി (lipi) — a script; writing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for ancient, then the Malayalam for a palm-leaf manuscript.

You'll want to know: ലിപി.
ലിപി — lipi — "a script; writing".
മലയാള ലിപിയിൽ ഒരുപാട് അക്ഷരങ്ങൾ ഉണ്ട്. malayāḷa lipiyil orupāṭŭ akṣaraṅṅaḷ uṇṭŭ. The Malayalam script has many letters.
Also any system of writing.

The word, taken apart.
ലിപി (lipi) is borrowed from Sanskrit lipi, "writing" (CDIAL 11058); CDIAL traces the Sanskrit word itself to Old Persian dipi, "an inscription".

Lesson 5 of 7.
നശിപ്പിക്കുക (naśippikkuka) — to destroy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a palm-leaf manuscript, then the Malayalam for a script.

You'll want to know: നശിപ്പിക്കുക.
നശിപ്പിക്കുക — naśippikkuka — "to destroy".
യുദ്ധം ആ പുരാതന നഗരം നശിപ്പിച്ചു. yuddhaṁ ā purātana nagaraṁ naśippiccu. The war destroyed that ancient city.
Past: നശിപ്പിച്ചു, naśippiccu.
Said of war, of fire and of time: what is destroyed is the plain object.

The word, taken apart.
നശിപ്പിക്കുക (naśippikkuka) is built from Sanskrit nāśana, "destroying" (CDIAL 7086), with the Malayalam causative ending -ിപ്പിക്കുക.
