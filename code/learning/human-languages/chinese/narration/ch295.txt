Lesson 1 of 7.
zuìchū — at the very start; initially.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for at long last, then the Mandarin for and so it followed.

You'll want to know: zuìchū.
zuìchū — "at the very start; initially".
zuìchū wǒ bù xǐhuan zhèr — At first I didn't like it here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zuìchū is built from 最 'most' and chū 'the start', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tswajH and tsrhjo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'the beginning': the very start.

Lesson 2 of 7.
重新 (chóngxīn) — anew; over again from the start.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for and so it followed, then the Mandarin for at the very start.

You'll want to know: 重新.
重新 — chóngxīn — "anew; over again from the start".
你得重新写 nǐ děi chóngxīn xiě — You'll have to write it over again.
The new character, in pieces: 新 (xīn) — thirteen strokes in three pieces: top left, the piece on the left of 站, five strokes; under it a bar, a vertical with a hook, a short sweep and a dot; on the right two strokes falling to the left, a bar and a long vertical. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
重新 (chóngxīn) is built from 重 'to repeat' and 新 (xīn) 'new', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjowng and sin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, in this reading, 'to double' and 'to layer'; the same character read drjowngX, glossed 'much' and 'thick', is the source of zhòng 'heavy'.

Lesson 3 of 7.
新 (xīn) — two pieces on the left, one on the right.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. 站 机.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — zhàn, jī]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two pieces on the left, one on the right.
新 (xīn).
Three pieces: two stacked on the left, one on the right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
Top left, five strokes. The piece on the left of 站: a dot, a bar, a dot falling to the right and a short stroke falling to the left under it, then a bar across the foot.
Bottom left, four strokes. A bar, a vertical that hooks to the left at the foot, a short sweep to the lower left and a dot to the lower right: the tree piece of 机, with a hook.
The right piece, four strokes. A short sweep down to the left at the top. A long stroke down from under it that curves to the lower left. A bar across to the right. Then a long vertical down to the foot.
5 + 4 + 4 = thirteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 新 (xīn), all thirteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 新 (xīn)? (Three.) Where have you made the top left piece? (On the left of 站.) What is the last stroke? (A long vertical on the right.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
当天 (dàngtiān) — that same day; on that very day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for at the very start, then the Mandarin for over again from the start.

You'll want to know: 当天.
当天 — dàngtiān — "that same day; on that very day".
他当天就回来了 tā dàngtiān jiù huílái le — He came back that same day.

The word, taken apart.
当天 (dàngtiān) is built from 当 and 天 'a day', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tangH and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, in this departing-tone reading, 'to be in charge'; how it came to mean 'that very (day)' is not settled. Read tang, the same character gives dāng.

Lesson 6 of 7.
dāngchǎng — on the spot; there and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for on that very day, then the Mandarin for after the event.

You'll want to know: dāngchǎng.
dāngchǎng — "on the spot; there and then".
tā dāngchǎng jiù kū le — He burst into tears there and then.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dāngchǎng is built from 当 'at' and chǎng 'an open ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tang and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to face' and 'to meet' and the second 'a place for offerings' and 'a threshing floor'. Middle Chinese drjang would regularly give cháng; the dipping tone of chǎng is irregular.
