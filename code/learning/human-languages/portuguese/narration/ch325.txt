Portuguese, chapter 325: Heróis e vilões.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a personagem — the character (in a story).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the narrator, then the Portuguese for the plot.

You'll want to know: a personagem.
a personagem — "the character (in a story)".
A personagem principal é uma professora. The main character is a teacher.
In Portugal usually a personagem; in Brazil also o personagem.

The word, taken apart.
personagem came from French personnage, built on personne, from Latin persōna "a mask, a role in a play" (Lewis & Short).

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the plot, then the Portuguese for the character.

You'll want to know: o protagonista.
o protagonista — "the protagonist, the main character".
O protagonista perde tudo no início da história. The protagonist loses everything at the start of the story.
The same form for a woman: a protagonista.

The word, taken apart.
protagonista goes back to Greek prōtagōnistḗs "the first actor", from prôtos "first" and agōnistḗs "a contestant" (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o herói — the hero.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the character, then the Portuguese for the protagonist.

You'll want to know: o herói.
o herói — "the hero".
No fim, o herói salva a cidade. In the end the hero saves the city.
A woman is a heroína.

The word, taken apart.
herói is a learned borrowing from Latin hērōs (EtymDB), from Greek hḗrōs "a demigod, a hero".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o vilão — the villain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the protagonist, then the Portuguese for the hero.

You'll want to know: o vilão.
o vilão — "the villain".
O vilão do filme é muito engraçado. The villain in the film is very funny.
A woman is a vilã.

The word, taken apart.
vilão comes from Late Latin vīllānus "a farm worker" (EtymDB), from vīlla "a farm": a peasant, later a scoundrel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o desfecho — the ending, the outcome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hero, then the Portuguese for the villain.

You'll want to know: o desfecho.
o desfecho — "the ending, the outcome".
O desfecho da história surpreendeu toda a gente. The ending of the story surprised everyone.
Um desfecho feliz — a happy ending.

The word, taken apart.
desfecho is built on desfechar, from des- and fechar "to close": the knot of the story comes undone. EtymDB traces fechar through Vulgar Latin fesclāre to Latin fistulāre, built on fistula "a pipe, a tube" (Lewis & Short).
