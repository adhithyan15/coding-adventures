Mandarin Chinese, chapter 190: Shops in Town.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
书店 (shūdiàn) — bookshop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sign your name, then the Mandarin for identity card.

You'll want to know: 书店.
书店 — shūdiàn — "bookshop".
这儿的书店很大 zhèr de shūdiàn hěn dà — The bookshops here are big.
The new character, in pieces: 店 (diàn) — eight strokes: a roof of a dot, a bar and a long stroke falling to the left, and under it a vertical with a short stroke to its right, over 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
书店 (shūdiàn) is built from 书 'book' and 店 (diàn) 'shop', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syo and temH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the second, after an older book, as a place where goods are set out for sale.

What you've built.
One more word for asking where things are.

Lesson 2 of 6.
店 (diàn) — a roof over a small piece and 口.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a roof over a small piece and 口.
店 (diàn).
Three pieces: a roof, and under it a small piece over 口.
The roof, three strokes. A dot at the top, a bar across, then a long stroke from the left end of the bar, falling down to the left all the way to the foot.
The small piece, two strokes. A vertical under the roof, then a short stroke from its middle out to the right.
口, three strokes: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, then the bar that closes the foot.
3 + 2 + 3 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 店 (diàn), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 店 (diàn)? (Eight.) Which stroke falls all the way to the foot? (The third, the long one on the left.) What closes the character? (The foot bar of 口.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
shāngchǎng — shopping centre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bookshop, then the Mandarin for chemist's.

You'll want to know: shāngchǎng.
shāngchǎng — "shopping centre".
zhōumò de shāngchǎng hěn rènao — The shopping centre is busy at the weekend.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shāngchǎng is built from shāng 'trade' and chǎng 'an open ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syang and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as a cleared ground for threshing grain, with a level tone, so the rising tone of chǎng is not the regular outcome; the sense 'trade' for the first is not given there either, so how both came is not settled.
