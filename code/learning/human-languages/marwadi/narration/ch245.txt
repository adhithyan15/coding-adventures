Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 245: Neighbours Come to Call.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तेड़णो (teṛṇo) — to call someone over; to invite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a fault, then the Marwadi for a threat.

You'll want to know: तेड़णो.
तेड़णो — teṛṇo — "to call someone over; to invite".
मां पावणा तेड़ै है। mā̃ pāvṇā teṛai hai. Mother invites guests over.
Past (I …): म्हैं पावणा तेड़्या, mhaĩ pāvṇā teṛyā.

The word, taken apart.
तेड़णो (teṛṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed tēḍati, "summons"; CDIAL lists an Old Marwari noun teṛū̃, "a summons", under it (CDIAL 5947a).

Lesson 2 of 5.
जुहार (juhār) — a respectful greeting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a threat, then the Marwadi for to call someone over.

You'll want to know: जुहार.
जुहार — juhār — "a respectful greeting".
म्हैं जुहार कियो। mhaĩ juhār kiyo. I greeted them respectfully.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
जुहार (juhār) is inherited from Sanskrit jyotkāra, "a shout of acclamation"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari juhāra, a greeting said with the words "live long", under it (CDIAL 5300a).

You'll want to know: ढूकणो.
ढूकणो — ḍhūkṇo — "to draw near; to arrive at the door".
पावणो घर कनै ढूक्यो। pāvṇo ghar kanai ḍhūkyo. The guest drew near the house.
Past (he …): वो पावणो ढूक्यो, vo pāvṇo ḍhūkyo.

The word, taken apart.
ढूकणो (ḍhūkṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed ḍhukyati, "approaches"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari partner ḍhukāvaï, "brings near", and Hindi ḍhūknā, "to approach", under it (CDIAL 5592).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अचंभो (achambho) — a surprise; a wonder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a respectful greeting, then the Marwadi for to draw near.

You'll want to know: अचंभो.
अचंभो — achambho — "a surprise; a wonder".
मामो आयो! यो अचंभो है। māmo āyo! yo achambho hai. My uncle has come! What a surprise.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अचंभो (achambho) is inherited from a reconstructed ācchambha, "rigidity", and so "astonishment", the stiffness of surprise. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi acambhā, "wonder" (CDIAL 1079).
