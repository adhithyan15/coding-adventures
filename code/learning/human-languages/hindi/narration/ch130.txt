Hindi, chapter 130: Hammer, Nail, Scissors, Thread, Comb.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हथौड़ा (hathauṛā) — a hammer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a curtain, then the Hindi for a bucket.

You'll want to know: हथौड़ा.
हथौड़ा — hathauṛā — "a hammer".

The word, taken apart.
हथौड़ा (hathauṛā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from hastakūṭa, "hand hammer", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 14028). It is built from hasta, "hand", and kūṭa, "hammer".

Lesson 2 of 5.
कील (kīl) — a nail (to hammer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a bucket, then the Hindi for a hammer.

You'll want to know: कील.
कील — kīl — "a nail (to hammer)".

The word, taken apart.
कील (kīl) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kīla, "stake, peg" (CDIAL 3202). CDIAL notes that both an Indo-European and a non-Aryan source have been proposed.

You'll want to know: कंघी.
कंघी — kaṅghī — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
कंघी (kaṅghī) goes back to Sanskrit kaṅkata, "comb" (CDIAL 2598). CDIAL marks the Hindi forms as taken over from Punjabi, and calls the Sanskrit word's Indo-European origin doubtful.
