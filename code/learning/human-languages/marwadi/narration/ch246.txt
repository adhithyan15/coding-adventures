Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 246: News from Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पीहर (pīhar) — a woman's parents' home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a surprise, then the Marwadi for congratulations.

You'll want to know: पीहर.
पीहर — pīhar — "a woman's parents' home".
मां काल पीहर गई। mā̃ kāl pīhar gaī. Mother went to her parents' home yesterday.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पीहर (pīhar) is inherited from a reconstructed pitṛghara, "father's house"; CDIAL lists Marwari pīhar under it (CDIAL 8180).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Marwadi for congratulations, then the Marwadi for a woman's parents' home.

You'll want to know: सासरो.
सासरो — sāsro — "a woman's in-laws' home".
सासरो घणो दूर कोनी। sāsro ghaṇo dūr konī. The in-laws' home is not very far.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सासरो (sāsro) is inherited from Sanskrit śvāśura, "of the father-in-law"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari sāsaraü, "father-in-law's house", under it (CDIAL 12767).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a woman's parents' home, then the Marwadi for a woman's in-laws' home.

You'll want to know: नूतो.
नूतो — nūto — "an invitation (to a wedding or a feast)".
काल गांव सूं नूतो आयो। kāl gā̃v sū̃ nūto āyo. An invitation came from the village yesterday.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नूतो (nūto) is inherited from a reconstructed nimantra, "invitation". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi nautā, "an invitation to a wedding" (CDIAL 7233).

Lesson 4 of 5.
बीतणो (bītṇo) — to pass (of time); to go by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a woman's in-laws' home, then the Marwadi for an invitation.

You'll want to know: बीतणो.
बीतणो — bītṇo — "to pass (of time); to go by".
सियाळो बीत्यो। siyāḷo bītyo. The cold season went by.
Past (it …): वो महीनो बीत्यो, vo mahīno bītyo.

The word, taken apart.
बीतणो (bītṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit vṛtta, "turned", and of time "passed". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi bītnā, "to pass" (CDIAL 12069).
