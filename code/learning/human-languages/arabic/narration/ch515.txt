Arabic, chapter 515: Lying down and getting up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شخر (shakhara) — to snore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to yawn, then the Arabic for to doze off.

You'll want to know: شخر.
شخر — shakhara — "to snore".
يشخر أخي في الليل. yashkhiru akhī fī al-layl. My brother snores at night.
Present: يشخر, yashkhir.

The word, taken apart.
شخر (shakhara) is built on the native Arabic root sh-kh-r, "to snort" (Lane). Lane gives shakhara, aor. yashkhiru, as a snorting sound from the nose or throat.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to relax, then the Arabic for to lie down.

You'll want to know: نهض.
نهض — nahaḍa — "to get up, to rise".
نهض جدي من الكرسي ببطء. nahaḍa jaddī min al-kursī bi-buṭʾ. My grandfather got up slowly from the chair.
Present: ينهض, yanhaḍ.
The noun نهضة is a rising, a revival.

The word, taken apart.
نهض (nahaḍa) is built on the native Arabic root n-h-ḍ, "to rise, to stand up" (Lane). Nahaḍa is to rise up from sitting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to lie down, then the Arabic for to get up.

إغماء — ighmāʾ — "fainting, a faint".

The word, taken apart.
إغماء (ighmāʾ) is built on the native Arabic root gh-m-y, "to cover, to veil" (Lane). Ighmāʾ is the verbal noun of Form IV: being covered over, losing one's senses.
