Malayalam, chapter 529: Good Sense and Bad Habits.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
വിവേകം (vivēkaṁ) — good sense; wisdom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for sincerity, then the Malayalam for discipline.

You'll want to know: വിവേകം.
വിവേകം — vivēkaṁ — "good sense; wisdom".
പ്രശ്നം വന്നപ്പോൾ അവൾ വിവേകത്തോടെ തീരുമാനമെടുത്തു. praśnaṁ vannappōḷ avaḷ vivēkattōṭe tīrumānameṭuttu. When the problem came, she decided with good sense.
വിവേകമുള്ള means sensible; വിവേകമില്ലാത്ത, foolish.

The word, taken apart.
വിവേകം (vivēkaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit viveka, "discrimination, telling things apart" (Monier-Williams): good sense is knowing right from wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for discipline, then the Malayalam for good sense.

You'll want to know: തന്റേടം.
തന്റേടം — tanṟēṭaṁ — "self-assurance; boldness".
ഒറ്റയ്ക്ക് വിദേശത്ത് പോകാൻ അവൾക്ക് തന്റേടം ഉണ്ട്. oṟṟaykkŭ vidēśattŭ pōkān avaḷkkŭ tanṟēṭaṁ uṇṭŭ. She has the nerve to go abroad on her own.
Praise for a confident person; തന്റേടി can also mean a cheeky one.

The word, taken apart.
തന്റേടം (tanṟēṭaṁ) seems to begin with തൻ, "self", a native word (DEDR 3196); the rest of the word is not settled.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: നിഷ്കളങ്കമായ.
നിഷ്കളങ്കമായ — niṣkaḷaṅkamāya — "guileless; innocent".
കുഞ്ഞിന്റെ നിഷ്കളങ്കമായ ചിരി എല്ലാവർക്കും ഇഷ്ടപ്പെട്ടു. kuññinṟe niṣkaḷaṅkamāya ciri ellāvarkkuṁ iṣṭappeṭṭu. Everyone loved the baby's innocent smile.
നിഷ്- without + കളങ്കം a stain: without a stain, guileless.

The word, taken apart.
നിഷ്കളങ്കമായ (niṣkaḷaṅkamāya) is built from Sanskrit niṣkalaṅka, "stainless, spotless" (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam adjective ending -ആയ.

You'll want to know: ദുശ്ശീലം.
ദുശ്ശീലം — duśśīlaṁ — "a bad habit".
പുകവലി ഒരു ദുശ്ശീലമാണ്. pukavali oru duśśīlamāṇŭ. Smoking is a bad habit.
ദു- bad + ശീലം, a habit, the word already familiar.

The word, taken apart.
ദുശ്ശീലം (duśśīlaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit duḥśīla, "ill-behaved" (CDIAL 6384), built from duṣ-, "bad", and śīla, "habit, nature".

Lesson 5 of 5.
സഹിഷ്ണുത (sahiṣṇuta) — tolerance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for guileless, then the Malayalam for a bad habit.

You'll want to know: സഹിഷ്ണുത.
സഹിഷ്ണുത — sahiṣṇuta — "tolerance".
മറ്റുള്ളവരുടെ അഭിപ്രായത്തോട് സഹിഷ്ണുത വേണം. maṟṟuḷḷavaruṭe abhiprāyattōṭŭ sahiṣṇuta vēṇaṁ. One needs tolerance towards other people's opinions.
From സഹിഷ്ണു, able to bear, the same root as സഹിക്കുക.

The word, taken apart.
സഹിഷ്ണുത (sahiṣṇuta) is borrowed from Sanskrit sahiṣṇutā, the noun of sahiṣṇu, "patient, enduring" (Monier-Williams), from the root sah, "to bear".
