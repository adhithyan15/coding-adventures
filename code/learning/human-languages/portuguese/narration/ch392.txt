Portuguese, chapter 392: Pôr o dinheiro a render.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
investir — to invest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the instalment, then the Portuguese for the saving.

You'll want to know: investir.
investir — "to invest".
Ele investiu as poupanças numa casa. He invested his savings in a house.
Investir em — to invest in. Conjugated invisto, investes, investe.

The word, taken apart.
investir is a learned borrowing from Latin investīre "to clothe, to cover" (EtymDB), on vestis "garment" (Lewis & Short); the money sense came later.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o juro — the interest (on money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to invest, then the Portuguese for the investment.

You'll want to know: o juro.
o juro — "the interest (on money)".
O banco paga pouco juro. The bank pays little interest.
Mostly plural: os juros; com juros — with interest.

The word, taken apart.
juro comes from Latin iūre, a form of iūs, iūris "right, law" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): money due by right.

Lesson 4 of 5.
a taxa — the rate; the fee, the charge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the investment, then the Portuguese for the interest.

You'll want to know: a taxa.
a taxa — "the rate; the fee, the charge".
A taxa de juro subiu em janeiro. The interest rate went up in January.
Taxa is also a fee: taxa de inscrição — enrolment fee.

The word, taken apart.
taxa is built on taxar "to tax, to set a price" (EtymDB), from Latin taxāre "to touch sharply, to rate, to value" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a inflação — the inflation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the interest, then the Portuguese for the rate.

You'll want to know: a inflação.
a inflação — "the inflation".
Com a inflação, tudo está mais caro. With inflation, everything is more expensive.
A taxa de inflação — the inflation rate.

The word, taken apart.
inflação is a learned borrowing from Latin īnflātiō "a blowing up, a swelling" (EtymDB), from īnflāre, built on flāre "to blow" (Lewis & Short): prices swell.
