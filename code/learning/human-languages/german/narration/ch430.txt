German, chapter 430: Ungebetene Gäste.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Wanze — the bedbug; a hidden microphone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to squeak, then the German for faulty.

You'll want to know: die Wanze.
die Wanze — "the bedbug; a hidden microphone".
Im Bett waren Wanzen, und er hat schlecht geschlafen. There were bedbugs in the bed, and he slept badly.
Plural: die Wanzen. A hidden microphone is also eine Wanze.

The word, taken apart.
Wanze goes back to Old High German wantlūs 'wall louse' (EtymDB), from Wand 'wall' and Laus 'louse'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Kakerlake — the cockroach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for faulty, then the German for the bedbug.

You'll want to know: die Kakerlake.
die Kakerlake — "the cockroach".
In der Küche läuft eine Kakerlake über den Boden. A cockroach runs across the kitchen floor.
Plural: die Kakerlaken. Also die Schabe.

The word, taken apart.
Kakerlake probably came into German through Dutch kakkerlak, a word sailors brought back from overseas. EtymDB lists it without a source, and the deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Mücke — the mosquito, the gnat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bedbug, then the German for the cockroach.

You'll want to know: die Mücke.
die Mücke — "the mosquito, the gnat".
Abends kommen die Mücken durch das offene Fenster. In the evening the mosquitoes come in through the open window.
Plural: die Mücken. In Austria: die Gelse.

The word, taken apart.
Mücke is an inherited Germanic word; EtymDB lists English midge, Dutch mug and Swedish mygg among its cousins.

Lesson 4 of 5.
stechen — to bite, to sting (of insects); to prick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the cockroach, then the German for the mosquito.

You'll want to know: stechen.
stechen — "to bite, to sting (of insects); to prick".
Eine Mücke hat mich am Arm gestochen. A mosquito bit me on the arm.
Strong verb: sticht, stach, hat gestochen.

The word, taken apart.
stechen is inherited through Old High German stehhan from Proto-Germanic stikaną 'to pierce' (EtymDB). English stick is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
krabbeln — to crawl (of insects, babies).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the mosquito, then the German for to bite.

You'll want to know: krabbeln.
krabbeln — "to crawl (of insects, babies)".
Eine Ameise krabbelt über das Kopfkissen. An ant crawls across the pillow.
Perfekt with sein for moving: das Baby ist zur Tür gekrabbelt.

The word, taken apart.
krabbeln came into German from Middle Low German krabbelen 'to crawl' (EtymDB), a cousin of Dutch krabben 'to scratch'.
