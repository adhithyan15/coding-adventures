Gujarati, chapter 244: Maths, maps and essays.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ગણિત (gaṇit) — mathematics, maths.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for school bag, then the Gujarati for present.

You'll want to know: ગણિત.
ગણિત — gaṇit — "mathematics, maths".
મને ગણિત ગમે છે. — mane gaṇit game che — "I like maths."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગણિત (gaṇit) is Sanskrit gaṇita, "counted, reckoned", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 3996). The taught verb gaṇvũ, "to count", is built on the same root.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ભૂગોળ (bhūgoḷ) — geography.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for present, then the Gujarati for mathematics.

You'll want to know: ભૂગોળ.
ભૂગોળ — bhūgoḷ — "geography".
અમે ભૂગોળમાં નકશા ભણીએ છીએ. — ame bhūgoḷmā̃ nakśā bhaṇīe chīe — "We study maps in geography."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ભૂગોળ (bhūgoḷ) is a learned loan from Sanskrit bhūgola, "the globe", which EtymDB lists as borrowed from Sanskrit. It is built from bhū, "earth", and gola, "ball" (CDIAL 4321).

Lesson 4 of 5.
સમજાવવું (samjāvvũ) — to explain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for geography, then the Gujarati for essay.

You'll want to know: સમજાવવું.
સમજાવવું — samjāvvũ — "to explain".
ગુરુ ગણિત સમજાવે છે. — guru gaṇit samjāve che — "The teacher explains maths."
The causative of સમજવું, samajvũ, "to understand": to make someone understand.

The word, taken apart.
સમજાવવું (samjāvvũ) is built on the taught samajvũ, "to understand", with the causative -āv-. CDIAL derives samajvũ from Sanskrit saṁbudhyatē, "wakes up, understands" (CDIAL 12959).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઉકેલવું (ukelvũ) — to solve, to work out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for essay, then the Gujarati for to explain.

You'll want to know: ઉકેલવું.
ઉકેલવું — ukelvũ — "to solve, to work out".
તે અઘરો પ્રશ્ન ઉકેલે છે. — te agharo praśna ukele che — "He solves a hard problem."

The word, taken apart.
ઉકેલવું (ukelvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from utkēlyatē, "is disentangled", a reconstructed form; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to disentangle, make out a badly written letter" (CDIAL 1734).
