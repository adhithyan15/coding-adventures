Mandarin Chinese, chapter 53: Speaking and Hearing.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
说 (shuō) — to say; to speak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for eye, then the Mandarin for ear.

You'll want to know: 说.
说 — shuō — "to say; to speak".
他说你的手很好看 tā shuō nǐ de shǒu hěn hǎokàn — He says you have lovely hands.
The new character, in pieces: 说 (shuō) — 讠, the speech piece, on the left; on the right a dot and a short fall, then 口, then two legs. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
说 (shuō) is inherited from Middle Chinese sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to tell'.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — the speech piece, qǐng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the speech piece, then a mouth with legs.
说 (shuō).
Two pieces, left before right.
The speech piece, 讠, two strokes, as in 请: a dot, then a short horizontal that turns down and finishes rising to the upper right, all one stroke.
The right piece, seven strokes, top to bottom. A dot and a short left-falling stroke, leaning in toward each other. 口 under them. Then two legs: a left-falling stroke, and a vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up.
2 + 7 = nine, and five of them are 讠 and 口.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 说 (shuō), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 说 (shuō)? (Two — 讠, then the right side.) Which character you know is in the middle of the right side? (口.) How does the last stroke end? (It hooks up.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the speech piece, then a mouth.
话 (huà).
Two pieces, left before right.
The speech piece, 讠, two strokes, as in 说 (shuō).
The right piece, six strokes, top to bottom. A short left-falling stroke along the top. A long horizontal. A vertical down from the top through the bar. Then 口 at the foot.
2 + 3 + 3 = eight.

[pause 3 seconds]

The word, taken apart.
màn is inherited from Middle Chinese maenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'lazy, haughty'. The sense 'slow' is not given there; from lazy to slow is a likely step, but it is not settled.
