# terminar — reach the boundary

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TRABAJO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall **el trabajo**, the work. Now add the action that brings it
to its endpoint: **terminar**, “to finish” or “to end.”

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-TERMINAR]; assesses=[] -->

Latin **termināre** meant to set a boundary or bring something to an end. Its
base was **terminus**, a boundary. English **term**, **terminal** and
**terminate** keep the same boundary picture. Spanish **terminar** is the
everyday verb: **terminar el trabajo**, finish the work.

## Grammar Lens: the regular -ar frame
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TERMINAR] -->

For “I finish,” remove **-ar** and add **-o**: **termino**. After another verb,
keep the infinitive: **quiero terminar**. Hear the difference between a plan
and a completed statement: **quiero terminar / termino**.
