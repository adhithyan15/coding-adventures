# la empresa — a thing taken on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-01, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el vendedor* and *el comprador*. You can name both parties to a
sale. Say where the seller works, and notice you have no word for it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-06]; assesses=[] -->

**la empresa** — "the company, the firm."

From Latin **in-** + **prehendere**, "to take hold of." An *empresa* is
literally **a thing taken on** — an undertaking.

English took the same idea through French and got **enterprise**, from *entre-*
+ *prendre*, the same two pieces in the other Romance language. The words are
cousins built to the same plan.

The difference is what each language did with it afterwards. English *enterprise*
mostly means the **venture**; Spanish *empresa* means both the venture **and the
firm that carries it out**. For an A1 form asking where you work, *empresa* is
the everyday word — it is what English calls *the company*.

*Prehendere* is generous, and two of its descendants are already useful to you:
*aprender*, to learn, is to **take hold of** something; and *sorprender*, to
surprise, is to take somebody from above.

## Grammar Lens: a workplace rather than a worker
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-06, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-01] -->

The chapter has named five people by what they do. **La empresa** is the one
word in it that names a **place and an organisation** instead:

| word | what it names |
|---|---|
| el vendedor | a person |
| el abogado | a person |
| la empresa | the organisation they work for |

That is why a form has two separate lines, *profesión* and *empresa*, and why
answering one does not answer the other.
