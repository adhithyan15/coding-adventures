Persian, chapter 488: Good Character.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
باهوش (bâhush) — clever, intelligent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for self-confidence, then the Persian for courage.

You'll want to know: باهوش.
باهوش (bâhush) — "clever, intelligent".
دخترشان خیلی باهوش است. dokhtarashân kheyli bâhush ast. Their daughter is very clever.
bâ 'with' + hush 'intelligence, wits'. The opposite in speech is kamhush.

The word, taken apart.
bâhush is built on native Persian bâ, 'with', and hush, 'wits', which is usually traced to Middle Persian hōš. EtymDB has no entry for it.

شوخ (shukh) — witty, playful.

Before the new one: say the Persian for courage, then the Persian for clever.

You'll want to know: شوخ.
شوخ (shukh) — "witty, playful".
آموزگار ما خیلی شوخ است و کلاس را شاد میکند. âmuzgâr-e mâ kheyli shukh ast va kelâs râ shâd mikonad. Our teacher is very witty and cheers up the class.
shukhi 'a joke' and shukhi kardan 'to joke' are built on it.

The word, taken apart.
shukh is usually taken as a native Persian word, and shukhi, 'a joke', is built on it. No source used here gives its older form, and its origin is not settled.

خوش اخلاق (khosh akhlâq) — "good-natured, pleasant".
همسایه جدید ما خیلی خوش اخلاق است. hamsâye-ye jadid-e mâ kheyli khosh akhlâq ast. Our new neighbour is very pleasant.
khosh 'good' + akhlâq 'manners, character'. The opposite is badakhlâq.

The word, taken apart.
khosh akhlâq is built in Persian on native khosh, 'good', and akhlâq, 'character, manners', which was borrowed from Arabic aḫlāq.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سخاوتمند (sekhâvatmand) — generous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for witty, then the Persian for good-natured.

You'll want to know: سخاوتمند.
سخاوتمند (sekhâvatmand) — "generous".
او مرد سخاوتمندی است و به همه کمک میکند. u mard-e sekhâvatmandi ast va be hame komak mikonad. He is a generous man and helps everyone.
sekhâvat 'generosity' + Persian -mand 'having'. In speech also dast-o-delbâz.

The word, taken apart.
sekhâvatmand is built in Persian on sekhâvat, 'generosity', a loan usually traced to Arabic saḫāwa, with the native ending -mand, 'having'. EtymDB has no entry for it.

پرستارهای این بیمارستان خیلی دلسوز هستند. parastârhâ-ye in bimârestân kheyli delsuz hastand. The nurses at this hospital are very caring.
del 'heart' + suz 'burning' (stem of sukhtan): a heart that burns for others.

The word, taken apart.
delsuz is built on two native Persian pieces: del, 'heart', and suz, which EtymDB derives from sukhtan, 'to burn' (EtymDB).
