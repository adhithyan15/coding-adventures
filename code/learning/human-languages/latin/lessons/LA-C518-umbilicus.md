## You'll want to know: umbilīcus, umbilīcī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C518-LATU-05]; assesses=[] -->

**umbilīcus, umbilīcī** — "navel; the knob of a book-roll".

> Īnfāns parvum umbilīcum habet.
>
> *The baby has a little navel.*

Masculine, second declension. Also the knob on the rod of a book-roll, so *ad umbilīcum addūcere* — to finish a book. English *umbilical*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**umbilīcus** is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "navel", and Greek *omphalos* is a cousin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *umbilical* comes from it, and *navel* is a distant Germanic cousin.
