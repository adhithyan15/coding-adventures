# ೂ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time — a mark rather than a letter, and one that
has quietly been changing the meaning of whole sentences.

## Script you'll notice: ೂ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-136]; assesses=[] -->

**ೂ** — *ū*.

It is a **vowel sign**, not a letter: it never stands alone. Hang it on a consonant and the built-in *a* becomes a long **ū**. You have had the full letter ಊ for the start of a word; this is the same sound, worn inside one.

You already say these, and every one of them has ೂ somewhere inside it:

- **ನಾನೂ** *nānū* — I too, from the joining lesson
- **ಹೂವು** *hūvu* — a flower
- **ಮೂಗು** *mūgu* — the nose
- **ಕೂದಲು** *kūdalu* — hair

## Writing: ೂ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-136] -->

Put your pen on ೂ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
