Mandarin Chinese, chapter 313: Moving House.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhuāngxiū — to do up a home; to renovate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for it serves them right, then the Mandarin for whatever anyone says.

You'll want to know: zhuāngxiū.
zhuāngxiū — "to do up a home; to renovate".
wǒmen de xīn jiā zhèngzài zhuāngxiū — Our new home is being done up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhuāngxiū is built from zhuāng 'to pack' and xiū 'to repair', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsrjang and sjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to pack for a journey' and the second 'to put in order'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
才 (cái) — only then; not until.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for whatever anyone says, then the Mandarin for to renovate a flat.

You'll want to know: 才.
才 — cái — "only then; not until".
他十点才来 tā shí diǎn cái lái — He didn't come until ten.
The new character, in pieces: 才 (cái) — three strokes: a bar, a vertical that hooks to the left at the foot, and a long sweep down to the left across them: the hand piece of 打 with a sweep for its last stroke. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
才 (cái) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'barely' under another, more complex spelling; the simple form, read the same, is a different word there, glossed 'use', 'strength' and 'talent'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
才 (cái) — a bar, a hooked post and a sweep.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a bar, a hooked post and a sweep.
才 (cái).
One piece, three strokes.
A bar across the top.
A vertical down through the bar, hooking to the left at the foot.
A long sweep from the crossing, down to the lower left, across the vertical. In 打 this stroke rises to the right; here it falls to the left.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 才 (cái)? (Three.) How is it different from the hand piece of 打? (The last stroke sweeps down to the left.)

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: shěbude.
shěbude — "to hate to part with; to hate to leave".
wǒ shěbude líkāi zhèr — I hate to leave this place.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
The tone of 不 drops away here: it is said light, without a tone, shěbude.

The word, taken apart.
shěbude is built from shě 'to let go', 不 'not' and 得 'to get', all inherited from Middle Chinese, syaeX, pjut and tok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to release': not able to let go.
