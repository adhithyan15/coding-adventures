Mandarin Chinese, chapter 302: A Baby on the Way.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
huáiyùn — to be pregnant; to be expecting a baby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to break a bone, then the Mandarin for sure enough.

You'll want to know: huáiyùn.
huáiyùn — "to be pregnant; to be expecting a baby".
tā huáiyùn le — She's pregnant.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huáiyùn is built from huái 'to hold close' and yùn 'to carry a child', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hweaj and yingH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to embrace' and the second with this very word. yingH would regularly give yìng; the final of yùn is irregular.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sure enough, then the Mandarin for to be expecting a baby.

You'll want to know: 活.
活 — huó — "to live; alive".
他活了一百岁 tā huó le yìbǎi suì — He lived to a hundred.
The new character, in pieces: 活 (huó) — nine strokes in three pieces: the water piece of 洗 on the left, three strokes; then 千, three strokes; then 口 under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
活 (huó) is inherited from Middle Chinese hwat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'not dead' and also 'the sound of flowing water'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
活 (huó) — the water piece, 千, then 口.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the water piece, 千, then 口 and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.

The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say huó — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
洗 千.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xǐ, qiān]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the water piece, 千, then 口.
活 (huó).
Three pieces: the water piece on the left, then 千 over 口 on the right.
The water piece, three strokes. As on the left of 洗: two dots, then a stroke rising to the right.
千, three strokes. A short sweep down to the left, a bar across, and a vertical down through the bar.
口, three strokes, under 千: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, and the bar that closes the foot.
3 + 3 + 3 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 活 (huó), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 活 (huó)? (Three.) What is on the left? (The water piece, as in 洗.) What is under 千? (口.)

You'll want to know: yīng'ér.
yīng'ér — "newborn; infant".
zhège yīng'ér hěn kě'ài — This baby is very sweet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yīng'ér is built from yīng and 儿 'a child', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jieng and nye (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older word list for the pair: a baby girl is 'jieng, a baby boy nye.

Lesson 6 of 6.
shuāngbāotāi — twins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a newborn, then the Mandarin for to bring back to life.

You'll want to know: shuāngbāotāi.
shuāngbāotāi — "twins".
tāmen shì shuāngbāotāi — They're twins.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shuāngbāotāi is built from shuāng 'a pair', bāo 'the womb' and tāi 'an unborn child', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sraewng, paew and thoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'two of a kind' and the third, quoting an older dictionary, 'three months with child', and gives the second only in the pair bāotāi, so its sense 'the womb' is not spelled out there: two from one womb.
