Lesson 1 of 6.
fugir — to run away, to flee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to hold hostage, then the Portuguese for the hostage.

You'll want to know: fugir.
fugir — "to run away, to flee".
O cão fugiu de casa. The dog ran away from home.
Present: fujo, foges, foge.

The word, taken apart.
fugir is inherited from Vulgar Latin fugīre, a remake of Latin fugere "to flee" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 6.
escapar — to escape, to get away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hostage, then the Portuguese for to run away.

You'll want to know: escapar.
escapar — "to escape, to get away".
O pássaro escapou da gaiola. The bird escaped from the cage.
Escapar por pouco — to have a narrow escape.

The word, taken apart.
escapar comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB lists it with Italian scappare); it is usually traced to Vulgar Latin excappāre "to slip out of the cape", from cappa "a cape".

Lesson 5 of 6.
a perseguição — the chase, the pursuit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the escape, then the Portuguese for the fugitive.

You'll want to know: a perseguição.
a perseguição — "the chase, the pursuit".
Houve uma perseguição pelas ruas da cidade. There was a chase through the city streets.
Also persecution: a perseguição religiosa.

The word, taken apart.
perseguição is built on perseguir "to chase", from Latin persequī "to follow closely", built on sequī "to follow" (Lewis & Short).
