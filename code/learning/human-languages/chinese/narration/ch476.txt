Mandarin Chinese, chapter 476: Neck and Shoulders.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bózi — neck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a person's height, then the Mandarin for to bend.

You'll want to know: bózi.
bózi — "neck".
wǒ kàn le sān ge xiǎoshí diànnǎo bózi hěn téng — I spent three hours at the computer, and my neck really hurts.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bózi is built from bó and 子, a noun ending, both inherited from Middle Chinese, bwot and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the navel; how it came to mean the neck is not settled.

What you've built.

The word, taken apart.
shétou is built from shé 'the tongue' and 头, a noun ending here, both inherited from Middle Chinese, zyet and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under two readings and glosses zyet as the tongue in the mouth; the second is said light.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jiānbǎng — shoulder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the neck, then the Mandarin for the tongue.

You'll want to know: jiānbǎng.
jiānbǎng — "shoulder".
wǒ de jiānbǎng hěn téng bù néng ná dōngxi — My shoulder hurts so much I can't carry anything.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiānbǎng is built from jiān 'the shoulder' and bǎng. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese ken (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the part below the nape. The dictionary lists the second only as bang, in the word for the bladder, so the reading bǎng and the sense 'shoulder' are not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
yáotóu — to shake one's head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the tongue, then the Mandarin for the shoulder.

You'll want to know: yáotóu.
yáotóu — "to shake one's head".
wǒ wèn tā qù bu qù tā yáotóu — I asked him whether he was going, and he shook his head.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yáotóu is built from yáo 'to shake' and 头 'head', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yew and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to move'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
téngtòng — pain; ache.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: téngtòng.
téngtòng — "pain; ache".
chī yào yǐhòu téngtòng hǎo le yìdiǎnr — After taking the medicine, the pain eased a little.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
téngtòng is built from 疼 'pain' and tòng 'to hurt', both inherited from Middle Chinese, downg and thuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'pain' and the second 'illness' and 'to hurt'.
