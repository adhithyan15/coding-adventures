Sanskrit, chapter 526: Training Hard.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
व्यायामभूमिः (vyāyāmabhūmiḥ) — a gymnasium; an exercise ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hoists, then the Sanskrit for a challenge.

You'll want to know: व्यायामभूमिः.
व्यायामभूमिः — vyāyāmabhūmiḥ — "a gymnasium; an exercise ground".
अरुणः प्रतिदिनं व्यायामभूमिं गच्छति। aruṇaḥ pratidinaṃ vyāyāmabhūmiṃ gacchati. Arun goes to the exercise ground every day.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
व्यायामभूमिः (vyāyāmabhūmiḥ) is built from vyāyāma, "exercise", and bhūmi, "ground"; MW gives vyāyāmabhūmi as an exercising ground, a gymnasium (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
धावकः (dhāvakaḥ) — a runner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a challenge, then the Sanskrit for a gymnasium.

You'll want to know: धावकः.
धावकः — dhāvakaḥ — "a runner".
धावकः क्षेत्रं परितः धावति। dhāvakaḥ kṣetraṃ paritaḥ dhāvati. The runner runs round the field.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
धावकः (dhāvakaḥ) comes from the root √dhāv, "to run", which also gives dhāvati, with the agent ending -aka; MW gives dhāvaka as running, a forerunner (MW).

सः क्षिप्रः धावकः अस्ति। saḥ kṣipraḥ dhāvakaḥ asti. He is a quick runner.

Lesson 4 of 5.
लंघयति (laṅghayati) — leaps over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a runner, then the Sanskrit for quick.

You'll want to know: लंघयति.
लंघयति — laṅghayati — "leaps over".
धावकः रज्जुं लंघयति। dhāvakaḥ rajjuṃ laṅghayati. The runner leaps over the rope.
Past (he …): सः रज्जुं लंघितवान्, saḥ rajjuṃ laṅghitavān.

The word, taken apart.
लंघयति (laṅghayati) comes from the root √laṅgh, "to leap over, to go beyond"; its causative laṅghayati has the same sense, and the past participle is laṅghita (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
दलम् (dalam) — a team; a party.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for quick, then the Sanskrit for leaps over.

You'll want to know: दलम्.
दलम् — dalam — "a team; a party".
अस्माकं दले दश क्रीडकाः सन्ति। asmākaṃ dale daśa krīḍakāḥ santi. Our team has ten players.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
दलम् (dalam) comes from the root √dal, "to split": MW gives dala as a piece split off, a part; CDIAL also lists a dala "a party, a band", perhaps the same word, perhaps from Dravidian (CDIAL 6215); "a team" is the modern sense (MW).
