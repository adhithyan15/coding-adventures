Russian, chapter 144: To be afraid, To please, To hate, To praise, To scold.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
бояться (boyátsya) — to be afraid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to come, then the Russian for to leave.

You'll want to know: бояться.
бояться (boyátsya) — "to be afraid".

The word, taken apart.
бояться (boyátsya) from Proto-Slavic bojati sę — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian bijótis, "to fear", and Sanskrit bibhéti, "fears".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: нравиться.
нравиться (nrávitsya) — "to please, to be liked".

The word, taken apart.
нравиться (nrávitsya) comes from нрав, "temper, disposition", the Church Slavonic shape, Vasmer says, beside native норов, "stubborn temper": to please is to suit someone's liking.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ненавидеть (nenavídet) — to hate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to be afraid, then the Russian for to please.

You'll want to know: ненавидеть.
ненавидеть (nenavídet) — "to hate".

The word, taken apart.
ненавидеть (nenavídet) is не, "not", with old навидеть, "to look at gladly", from видеть, "to see" (Vasmer): to hate is not to bear the sight of someone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: хвалить.
хвалить (khvalít) — "to praise".

The word, taken apart.
хвалить (khvalít) comes from хвала, "praise". Vasmer says it has no reliable etymology; one view reshapes слава, "glory", with the х- of хула, "blame".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ругать (rugát) — to scold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to hate, then the Russian for to praise.

You'll want to know: ругать.
ругать (rugát) — "to scold".

The word, taken apart.
ругать (rugát) from Old Russian ругатися, "to mock", from rugŭ, "mockery". Vasmer compares Latin ringor, "I bare my teeth, snarl"; Machek suggested other links, so the origin is not settled.
