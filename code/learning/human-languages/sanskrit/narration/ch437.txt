Sanskrit, chapter 437: The Rainy Season.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रावृट् (prāvṛṭ) — the rainy season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for gets soaked, then the Sanskrit for slippery.

You'll want to know: प्रावृट्.
प्रावृट् — prāvṛṭ — "the rainy season".
प्रावृट् आगच्छति, नद्यः पूर्णाः भवन्ति। prāvṛṭ āgacchati, nadyaḥ pūrṇāḥ bhavanti. The rainy season comes, and the rivers fill up.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रावृट् (prāvṛṭ) is built from pra, "forth", and the root √vṛṣ, "to rain"; MW gives prāvṛṣ, whose nominative is prāvṛṭ, as the rainy season, the rains (MW).

You'll want to know: आसारः.
आसारः — āsāraḥ — "a downpour".

A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आसारः (āsāraḥ) is built from ā and the root √sṛ, "to run, to flow"; MW gives āsāra as a hard shower, a downpour (MW).

शीकरः — śīkaraḥ — "drizzle; spray".
प्रातः शीकराः पतन्ति। prātaḥ śīkarāḥ patanti. In the morning a drizzle falls.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शीकरः (śīkaraḥ) is the word MW gives, mostly in the plural, for fine or drizzling rain and spray; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
गलति (galati) — drips; trickles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for drizzle, then the Sanskrit for a drop.

You'll want to know: गलति.
गलति — galati — "drips; trickles".
पत्रात् जलं गलति। patrāt jalaṃ galati. Water drips from the leaf.
Past (it …): जलं पत्रात् गलितवत्, jalaṃ patrāt galitavat.

The word, taken apart.
गलति (galati) comes from the root √gal, "to drip, to ooze"; MW gives galati as to drip, drop, trickle, with the participle galita (MW).
