Latin, chapter 418: Debts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aes aliēnum, aeris aliēnī — debt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for ownership, then the Latin for formal purchase.

You'll want to know: aes aliēnum, aeris aliēnī.
aes aliēnum, aeris aliēnī — "debt".
Aere aliēnō premitur. He is weighed down by debt.
aes aliēnum, aeris aliēnī n.: other people's bronze, money that belongs to someone else. In aere aliēnō esse, to be in debt.

The word, taken apart.
aes aliēnum is built from aes "bronze, money" and aliēnus "belonging to another" (Lewis & Short): money that is someone else's.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dēbitor, dēbitōris — a debtor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for formal purchase, then the Latin for debt.

You'll want to know: dēbitor, dēbitōris.
dēbitor, dēbitōris — "a debtor".
Dēbitor pecūniam reddere nōn potuit. The debtor could not pay the money back.
dēbitor, dēbitōris m., built on dēbeō, to owe. English debtor.

The word, taken apart.
dēbitor is built on dēbitum "a debt" (EtymDB), from dēbeō "to owe" (Lewis & Short). English debtor comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fēnus, fēnoris — interest (on money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a debtor, then the Latin for a creditor.

You'll want to know: fēnus, fēnoris.
fēnus, fēnoris — "interest (on money)".
Pecūniam fēnore dabat. He lent money at interest.
fēnus, fēnoris n., also spelled faenus: the yield of money, as a field yields a crop.

The word, taken apart.
fēnus is usually traced to a root meaning "to bring forth, to yield", the root Lewis & Short also see in fēmina: what money produces. The account is not certain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mūtuus, mūtua, mūtuum — borrowed, lent; mutual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a creditor, then the Latin for interest.

You'll want to know: mūtuus, mūtua, mūtuum.
mūtuus, mūtua, mūtuum — "borrowed, lent; mutual".
Pecūniam mūtuam ab amīcō sūmpsit. He borrowed money from a friend.
mūtuus, mūtua, mūtuum: pecūniam mūtuam dare, to lend; sūmere, to borrow. Also mutual: amor mūtuus. English mutual.

The word, taken apart.
mūtuus is built on mūtō "to change, to exchange" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): given in exchange. English mutual comes from it.
