Latin, chapter 308: Iūdex et lēx.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iūdex, iūdicis — a judge; a juror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a conspiracy, then the Latin for a traitor.

You'll want to know: iūdex, iūdicis.
iūdex, iūdicis — "a judge; a juror".
Iūdex testēs diū audīvit. The judge listened to the witnesses for a long time.
Masculine (also feminine), 3rd declension. In big trials many iūdicēs sat together as a jury.

The word, taken apart.
iūdex joins iūs, "law, right", and the root of dīcō, "to say" (EtymDB): the one who speaks the law. English judge comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
iūdicium, iūdiciī — a trial; a judgement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a traitor, then the Latin for a judge.

You'll want to know: iūdicium, iūdiciī.
iūdicium, iūdiciī — "a trial; a judgement".
Iūdicium in basilicā habitum est. The trial was held in the basilica.
Neuter, 2nd declension. Both the trial and the verdict at its end; also 'opinion': meō iūdiciō, in my judgement.

The word, taken apart.
iūdicium is built on iūdex, "judge" (EtymDB): what a judge does, the trial and its verdict. English judicial belongs to the same family.

Lesson 3 of 5.
iūdicō, iūdicāre — to judge; to declare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a judge, then the Latin for a trial.

You'll want to know: iūdicō, iūdicāre.
iūdicō, iūdicāre — "to judge; to declare".
Senātus eum hostem iūdicāvit. The senate declared him a public enemy.
iūdicō, iūdicāre, iūdicāvī, iūdicātum, often with two accusatives: someone + what he is judged to be.

The word, taken apart.
iūdicō is built on iūdex, "judge" (EtymDB): to act as judge, to decide. English judge the verb comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lēx, lēgis — a law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a trial, then the Latin for to judge.

You'll want to know: lēx, lēgis.
lēx, lēgis — "a law".
Populus novam lēgem probāvit. The people approved the new law.
Feminine, 3rd declension. A lēx was a bill passed by the people's vote. English legal comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
lēx is inherited from Proto-Italic lēg- (EtymDB); Lewis & Short connect it, with doubt, with a root meaning "to bind". English legal and legislate come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
iūs, iūris — law, right; justice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to judge, then the Latin for a law.

You'll want to know: iūs, iūris.
iūs, iūris — "law, right; justice".
Omnēs cīvēs eadem iūra habēbant. All the citizens had the same rights.
Neuter, 3rd declension. Iūs is law and right as a whole; a lēx is one law. A look-alike iūs means 'broth'.

The word, taken apart.
iūs is inherited from Proto-Indo-European h₂yéwos, "law, right" (EtymDB). English jury, justice and jurist come from its family.
