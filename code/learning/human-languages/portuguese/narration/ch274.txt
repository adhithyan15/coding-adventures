Portuguese, chapter 274: Noutros tempos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
outrora — formerly, in the old days.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the succession, then the Portuguese for the chronology.

You'll want to know: outrora.
outrora — "formerly, in the old days".
Outrora, esta aldeia tinha muitas crianças. In the old days this village had many children.
A literary word; antigamente is the everyday choice.

The word, taken apart.
outrora fuses outra hora "another hour, another time" (EtymDB lists hora), from Latin alter "other" and hōra "hour" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ultimamente — lately, recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the chronology, then the Portuguese for formerly.

You'll want to know: ultimamente.
ultimamente — "lately, recently".
Ultimamente tenho dormido mal. Lately I have been sleeping badly.
Often with the pretérito perfeito composto: tenho trabalhado muito.

The word, taken apart.
ultimamente is built on último "last", from Latin ultimus "farthest, last" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -mente.

Lesson 3 of 5.
afinal — after all, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for formerly, then the Portuguese for lately.

You'll want to know: afinal.
afinal — "after all, in the end".
Afinal, o exame foi fácil. The exam was easy after all.
Shows that things turned out differently from what you expected. Afinal, vens ou não?

The word, taken apart.
afinal joins a and final, which comes from Late Latin fīnālis "of the end", from fīnis "end" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
gradualmente — gradually.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for lately, then the Portuguese for after all.

You'll want to know: gradualmente.
gradualmente — "gradually".
A temperatura subiu gradualmente. The temperature rose gradually.
Built on gradual, ‘step by step’.

The word, taken apart.
gradualmente is built on gradual, a learned word from Medieval Latin graduālis, from gradus "a step" (Lewis & Short).
