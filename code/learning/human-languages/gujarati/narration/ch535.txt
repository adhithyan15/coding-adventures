Gujarati, chapter 535: Signs and Omens.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
શુકન (śukan) — an omen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lottery, then the Gujarati for a chance meeting.

You'll want to know: શુકન.
શુકન — śukan — "an omen".
જો સવારે ગાય સામે આવશે તો દાદી તેને સારું શુકન માનશે. jo savāre gāy sāme āvśe to dādī tene sārũ śukan mānśe. If a cow comes towards her in the morning, my grandmother will take it as a good omen.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
શુકન (śukan) goes back to Sanskrit śakuna, "a bird", especially a bird of good or bad omen (Monier-Williams).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an omen, then the Gujarati for a superstition.

You'll want to know: સંકેત.
સંકેત — sanket — "a hint".
જો આકાશમાં કાળાં વાદળ હશે તો તે વરસાદનો સંકેત છે. jo ākāśmā̃ kāḷā̃ vādaḷ haśe to te varsādno sanket che. If there are black clouds in the sky, that is a sign of rain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ગ્રહ (grah) — a planet (in astrology).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a superstition, then the Gujarati for a hint.

You'll want to know: ગ્રહ.
ગ્રહ — grah — "a planet (in astrology)".
જો ગ્રહો સારા હશે તો દાદા આ મહિને નવી દુકાન ખોલશે. jo graho sārā haśe to dādā ā mahine navī dukān kholśe. If the planets are favourable, my grandfather will open a new shop this month.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગ્રહ (grah) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit graha, "a seizer", and so "a planet", one held to seize on a person's fortunes (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
કળવું (kaḷvũ) — to make out (to guess).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a hint, then the Gujarati for a planet.

You'll want to know: કળવું.
કળવું — kaḷvũ — "to make out (to guess)".
જો હું તેની વાત પહેલાં કળત તો હું ત્યાં ન જાત. jo hũ tenī vāt pahelā̃ kaḷat to hũ tyā̃ na jāt. If I had made out what he meant earlier, I wouldn't have gone there.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે વાત કળી, teṇe vāt kaḷī.

The word, taken apart.
કળવું (kaḷvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit kaláyati, "notices, considers", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to understand, guess" (CDIAL 2918).
