Arabic, chapter 406: What it takes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
تطلب (taṭallaba) — to require.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to contain, then the Arabic for to concern.

You'll want to know: تطلب.
تطلب — taṭallaba — "to require".
يتطلب هذا العمل صبرا كبيرا. yataṭallabu hādhā al-ʿamal ṣabran kabīran. This work requires great patience.
Present: يتطلب, yataṭallab.

The word, taken apart.
تطلب (taṭallaba) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-l-b, "to seek, to ask for" (Lane). It is Form V, to seek a thing again and again: to call for, to require.

توفر (tawaffara) is built on the native Arabic root w-f-r, "to be abundant, ample" (Lane). It is Form V, to become ample: to be available. وفر, to save, is from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
كفى (kafā) — to suffice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to require, then the Arabic for to be available.

You'll want to know: كفى.
كفى — kafā — "to suffice".
يكفي هذا الطعام لثلاثة أشخاص. yakfī hādhā aṭ-ṭaʿām li-thalāthat ashkhāṣ. This food is enough for three people.
Present: يكفي, yakfī.

The word, taken apart.
كفى (kafā) is built on the native Arabic root k-f-y, usually traced to the sense "to be enough". Kafā is a Form I verb with a weak last letter: to be enough. Its present is yakfī.
