Spanish, chapter 528: Una catástrofe.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el desastre — the disaster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for unhurt, then the Spanish for to fall.

You'll want to know: el desastre.
el desastre — "the disaster".
La fiesta fue un desastre. The party was a disaster.
In everyday speech it is anything that goes badly: ¡Qué desastre!

The word, taken apart.
desastre is usually traced to Occitan desastre or Italian disastro, where it meant 'bad star', built on des- and the word for 'star', from Latin astrum (Lewis & Short). A disaster was blamed on the stars.

You'll want to know: la catástrofe.
la catástrofe — "the catastrophe".
El huracán fue una catástrofe para la isla. The hurricane was a catastrophe for the island.

The word, taken apart.
catástrofe is a learned borrowing that comes from Greek katastrophḗ 'an overturning' (EtymDB), built on katá 'down' and stréphein 'to turn'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el terremoto — the earthquake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the catastrophe, then the Spanish for the tragedy.

You'll want to know: el terremoto.
el terremoto — "the earthquake".
El terremoto duró treinta segundos. The earthquake lasted thirty seconds.
Un sismo is a more technical word, common in Latin America.

The word, taken apart.
terremoto was borrowed from Italian terremoto, from Latin terrae mōtus 'a movement of the earth' (EtymDB), built on terra 'earth' and mōtus 'movement'.
