6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
اسباب بازی (asbâb bâzi) — a toy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a pram, then the Persian for to breastfeed.

You'll want to know: اسباب بازی.
اسباب بازی (asbâb bâzi) — "a toy".
اتاق بچه پر از اسباب بازی است. otâq-e bachche por az asbâb bâzi ast. The child's room is full of toys.
asbâb 'things, gear' (an Arabic plural) + bâzi 'play'. Plural asbâb bâzihâ.

The word, taken apart.
asbâb bâzi is built in Persian on asbâb, 'things, gear', a loan usually traced to the Arabic plural asbāb, and native bâzi, 'play': play things.

Before the new one: say the Persian for to breastfeed, then the Persian for a toy.

You'll want to know: عروسک.
عروسک ('arusak) — "a doll".
دخترم با عروسکش حرف میزند. dokhtaram bâ 'arusakash harf mizanad. My daughter talks to her doll.
Literally 'little bride': 'arus 'bride' + the diminutive -ak.

The word, taken apart.
'arusak is built in Persian on 'arus, 'bride', which was borrowed from Arabic ʿarūs, 'bride, groom' (EtymDB), with the native diminutive -ak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a toy, then the Persian for a doll.

You'll want to know: بچه داری.
بچه داری (bachchedâri) — "childcare, bringing up children".
بچه داری کار سختی است. bachchedâri kâr-e sakhti ast. Bringing up children is hard work.
bachche 'child' + dâr 'keeping' (stem of dâshtan) + -i.

The word, taken apart.
bachchedâri is built on native Persian bachche, 'child', which EtymDB traces to a Middle Persian word for 'child, baby, cub' (EtymDB), and dâr, the stem of dâshtan, 'to have, to keep'.

قایم موشک (qâyem mushak) — hide-and-seek.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for childcare, then the Persian for spoiled.

You'll want to know: قایم موشک.
قایم موشک (qâyem mushak) — "hide-and-seek".
بچه ها در حیاط قایم موشک بازی میکنند. bachchehâ dar hayât qâyem mushak bâzi mikonand. The children are playing hide-and-seek in the yard.
qâyem 'hidden' + mushak 'little mouse': hiding like a mouse.

The word, taken apart.
qâyem mushak is built in Persian on colloquial qâyem, 'hidden', usually traced to Arabic qāʾim, and native mush, 'mouse', usually traced to Middle Persian mūš, with the diminutive -ak.
