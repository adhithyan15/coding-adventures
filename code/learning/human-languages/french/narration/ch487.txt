French, chapter 487: Amoureux.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
amoureux — in love; lover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to yawn, then the French for nap.

You'll want to know: amoureux.
amoureux — "in love; lover".
Ils sont amoureux depuis le lycée. They have been in love since secondary school.
Feminine amoureuse. Tomber amoureux de quelqu'un — to fall in love with someone.

The word, taken apart.
amoureux is inherited from Old French amoros (EtymDB), built on amour, from Latin amor "love" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le coup de foudre — love at first sight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for nap, then the French for in love.

You'll want to know: le coup de foudre.
le coup de foudre — "love at first sight".
Quand ils se sont rencontrés, ça a été le coup de foudre. When they met, it was love at first sight.
Literally a lightning strike. Avoir un coup de foudre pour also works for places or objects.

The word, taken apart.
le coup de foudre is "a stroke of lightning": coup comes from Latin colaphus "a punch", and foudre is inherited from Latin fulgur "lightning" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
draguer — to chat up, to flirt with (informal); to dredge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for in love, then the French for love at first sight.

You'll want to know: draguer.
draguer — "to chat up, to flirt with (informal); to dredge".
Au café, un garçon a essayé de la draguer. At the café a boy tried to chat her up.
Informal. In Québec people say cruiser. The first sense is to dredge a river.

The word, taken apart.
draguer is built on drague "a dredge", which was borrowed from English drag. The sense "to chat up", like sweeping the streets, is recent and informal.

la tendresse is built on tendre "tender" with -esse (EtymDB); tendre is inherited from Latin tener "soft, delicate" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: caresser.
caresser — "to stroke, to caress".
Le garçon caresse doucement le chat. The boy gently strokes the cat.
Une caresse — a caress, a stroke.

The word, taken apart.
caresser was borrowed from Italian carezzare (EtymDB), built on carezza "a caress", from caro "dear", inherited from Latin cārus "dear" (Lewis & Short).
