Lesson 1 of 5.
おに (oni) — an ogre; a demon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a custom, then the Japanese for to inherit.

You'll want to know: おに.
おに — oni — "an ogre; a demon".
おには こわい かおを しています。 — oni wa kowai kao o shite imasu — "The ogre has a frightening face."

The word, taken apart.
おに (oni) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おめん (omen) — a mask.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to inherit, then the Japanese for an ogre.

You'll want to know: おめん.
おめん — omen — "a mask".
あには おにの おめんを かぶりました。 — ani wa oni no omen o kaburimashita — "My brother put on an ogre mask."

The word, taken apart.
おめん (omen) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: o-, "the polite prefix", is native Japanese, and men, "mask", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mjienH) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
こわがる (kowagaru) — to be afraid; to be scared of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an ogre, then the Japanese for a mask.

You'll want to know: こわがる.
こわがる — kowagaru — "to be afraid; to be scared of".
いもうとは おにを こわがっています。 — imōto wa oni o kowagatte imasu — "My little sister is scared of the ogre."
Polite こわがります, kowagarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもが こわがりました。, kodomo ga kowagarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
こわがる (kowagaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from kowa, "fear" (from kowai), and -garu, "to show signs of" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
