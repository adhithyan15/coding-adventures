## You'll want to know: كرّر
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-FUNC-C41-KARRIR-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C32-FAKKARA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-01] -->

> **كرّر** — *karrir* — **repeat.**

> **كرّر من فضلك** — *karrir min faḍlik* — **say it again, please.**

The *please* is not new: chapter six taught it. What is new is the verb, and the
shape it comes in — this is the first **command** in the book, and Arabic builds
one by stripping the present tense back to its bare stem.

Look at the shadda. **كرّر** doubles its middle letter, which is the same Form II
shape as *fakkara* in chapter thirty-two — a pattern that means *do it
thoroughly, do it repeatedly*. On the root ك-ر-ر, "to return", that gives exactly
*do it again*.

## Grammar Lens: the repair kit, whole
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-FUNC-C41-KARRIR-01, AR-FUNC-C37-LA-AFHAM-01, AR-CONCEPT-SHUKRAN-21, AR-CONCEPT-C23-AFWAN-01] -->

| when | say |
|---|---|
| you did not follow | *lā afham* |
| you want it again | *karrir min faḍlik* |
| you are grateful for it | *shukran* |

The inventory that measured this track said the repair kit was strong with one
hole in it: sorry, excuse me, pardon, please and thank you were all taught, and
*I do not understand* and *say it again* were not. Those two are what chapters
thirty-seven and forty-one add, and between them they needed one new verb.
