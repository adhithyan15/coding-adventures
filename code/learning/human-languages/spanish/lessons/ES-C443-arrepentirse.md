# arrepentirse — the verb that is not made of *sentir*

## You'll want to know: arrepentirse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-03] -->

**arrepentirse** — to regret, to be sorry for having done something.

*Me arrepiento de haberlo dicho* — I regret having said it. *No te vas a
arrepentir* — you will not regret it, which is what somebody says to talk you
into something.

It takes **de**, and it is always reflexive. There is no plain *arrepentir*.

**Equivocarse** is the error; **arrepentirse** is what you feel about it
afterwards.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-05] -->

Here is the trap, and this chapter has earned it.

**arrepentirse** looks like **a-** plus **re-** plus **sentir**, with the **s**
worn away. It is not. There is no *sentir* in it at all.

The middle is Latin **paenitēre**, to cause regret — the verb behind English
**penitent**, **penance** and **repent**. Spanish took *repentirse* and put its
familiar **a-** on the front.

| what it looks like | what it is |
|---|---|
| a- + re- + sentir | a- + re- + **paenitēre** |

And the trap is doubled, because **it conjugates like *sentir* too**:
*me arrepiento*, *te arrepientes*, *se arrepiente*, *nos arrepentimos*. That is
the **e→ie** break, exactly as *siento*, *sientes*, *siente*, *sentimos*.

So the form misleads and the behaviour agrees with the form. Both are accidents
of shape: any verb with a stressed **e** in its stem breaks that way, related or
not.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-05, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-04] -->

This is the strongest case yet for the test you have been building.

**reír** wore a prefix it never had, and the test caught it: take *re-* off and
nothing stands. **arrepentirse** is harder, because if you take *a-* and *re-*
off you get *pentir*, which stands on nothing — and the fact that it looks like
*sentir* is the only reason you would have thought otherwise.

What that means in practice: a resemblance is not evidence. *Concentrarse* really
does contain *centro*, and you can check it because *centro* is a word. *Sentir*
is a word too, and *arrepentirse* still does not contain it.

The honest position is the one worth keeping — take the word
apart where the pieces are real, and when they are not, learn the word and let
the history be what it is.
