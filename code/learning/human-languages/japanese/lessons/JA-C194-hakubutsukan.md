## You'll want to know: はくぶつかん
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C194-WAY194-05]; assesses=[] -->

**はくぶつかん** — *hakubutsukan* — "museum".

> **はくぶつかんは こうえんの なかに あります。** — *hakubutsukan wa kōen no naka ni arimasu* — "The museum is inside the park."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**はくぶつかん** (*hakubutsukan*) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are *haku*, which the Guangyun glosses "broad" (Middle Chinese *pak*), *butsu*, "thing" (*mjut*) and *kan*, "building" (*kwanH*) (KANJIDIC2; Guangyun and Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription, via tshet-uinh).
