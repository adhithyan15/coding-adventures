Urdu, chapter 160: Kind, Sensible, Foolish, Lazy, Hardworking.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مہربان (mehrbān) — kind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for even so, then the Urdu for otherwise.

You'll want to know: مہربان.
مہربان — mehrbān — "kind".

The word, taken apart.
مہربان (mehrbān) is a loan, usually traced to Persian mihrbān, "kind". It is built on mihr, "love, kindness".

Lesson 2 of 5.
سمجھدار (samajhdār) — sensible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for otherwise, then the Urdu for kind.

You'll want to know: سمجھدار.
سمجھدار — samajhdār — "sensible".

The word, taken apart.
سمجھدار (samajhdār) is "holding understanding". samajh belongs with samajhnā, "to understand", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit saṃbudhyate, "wakes up, understands" (CDIAL 12959), and -dār, "holder", is Persian.

بیوقوف — bevaqūf — "foolish".

The word, taken apart.
بیوقوف (bevaqūf) is "without understanding". It joins the Persian be-, "without", and Arabic wuqūf, "standing; acquaintance with a matter" (Lane, root w-q-f).

Lesson 5 of 5.
محنتی (mehnatī) — hardworking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for foolish, then the Urdu for lazy.

You'll want to know: محنتی.
محنتی — mehnatī — "hardworking".

The word, taken apart.
محنتی (mehnatī) is mehnat, "toil", with the ending -ī, "one who has it". mehnat is Arabic miḥna, "trial, ordeal", which reached Urdu through Persian (Lane, root m-ḥ-n).
