Bengali, chapter 272: Feelings and Hopes.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā) — experience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "My train has left". (āmār ṭren chheṛe gechhe.) Then জ্ঞান, "knowledge": its জ্ঞ is in this word too.

You'll want to know: অভিজ্ঞতা.
অভিজ্ঞতা — ôbhigyôtā — "experience; an experience".
গ্রামে আমার একটা নতুন অভিজ্ঞতা হল। grāme āmār ekṭā nôtun ôbhigyôtā hôlô. — "I had a new experience in the village."
An experience "happens to" you, as a thought comes to you: আমার … অভিজ্ঞতা হল. Experience you have gathered "is there": এই কাজে তার অভিজ্ঞতা আছে (ei kāje tār ôbhigyôtā āchhe), "he has experience in this work".

The word, taken apart.
অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā) is a tatsama: Sanskrit abhijñatā, which Monier-Williams gives as "the knowledge of". It is abhi-, "towards", with jña, "knowing" (the জ্ঞ of জ্ঞান, said gy), and -tā, which makes an abstract noun. Bengali uses it for knowledge got by living through something; that sense, "experience", is Bengali use, not one Monier-Williams gives.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I had a new experience in the village" — grāme āmār ekṭā nôtun ôbhigyôtā hôlô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He has experience in this work" — ei kāje tār ôbhigyôtā āchhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā) mean? ("Experience".) "I had an experience"? (আমার একটা অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā) হল.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā). Then read ক (kô): its vowel, the ô every consonant carries, is the vowel of this letter.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: অ.
অ — ô, the vowel inside every bare consonant: ক is kô. It has no sign, because a consonant carries it already; at the start of a word, with nothing to ride on, it is the letter অ: অভিজ্ঞতা (ôbhigyôtā). Now Mira's friend's name can be written: অরুণ (ôrun), Arun.
In the print of this book, অ (ô) has a headline and an upright stem on the right, the full height of the letter. Left of the stem is a large rounded bowl; its left arm ends in a slanted tip at the upper left. The bowl's right side is curled over inside the top of the bowl and ends there in a solid round knob, and a short curve joins the knob to the headline above it. A slanting leg joins the bottom right of the bowl to the foot of the stem.
আ, the first vowel you wrote, is অ (ô) with one more upright stroke after it, standing from the headline: look at the two side by side.

Writing: অ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside অ (ô) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the bowl and its tip, the knob and the short curve to the headline, the leg to the stem.
This book does not yet tell you where to start অ (ô) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 5.
আমার ভালো লাগছে (āmār bhālo lāgchhe) — how I feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I had a new experience in the village". (grāme āmār ekṭā nôtun ôbhigyôtā hôlô.) Then ভালো লাগা, "to like": "good sticks to me".

You'll want to know: feelings now.
আমার এখানে ভালো লাগছে। āmār ekhāne bhālo lāgchhe. — "I'm enjoying it here."
Many feelings come to a person, as a thought does: the one who feels is in the -র form. Going on now, they take -ছে: আমার ভালো লাগছে (āmār bhālo lāgchhe), "I'm enjoying it"; আমার ভয় করছে (āmār bhôy kôrchhe) or আমার ভয় লাগছে (āmār bhôy lāgchhe), "I'm scared", with the same লাগা as ভালো লাগা; আমার রাগ হচ্ছে (āmār rāg hôchchhe), "I'm getting angry". Words that describe a person take আমি, as before: আমি খুশি, "I'm happy"; আমি ক্লান্ত.

You'll want to know: feelings then.
In the past, a whole feeling takes the plain past, and a feeling that lasted takes -ছিল:
ছবিটা আমার ভালো লাগল, কিন্তু ঝড়ে আমার ভয় করছিল। chhôbiṭā āmār bhālo lāglô, kintu jhôṛe āmār bhôy kôrchhilô. — "I liked the picture, but I was scared in the storm."
With হওয়া, a describing word becomes a change: সে খুশি হল (she khushi hôlô), "she was pleased".

[your turn — say: "I'm scared" — āmār bhôy kôrchhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She was pleased" — she khushi hôlô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 5.
… হতে চাই (… hôte chāi) (hôte chāi) — what I want to become.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'm enjoying it here". (āmār ekhāne bhālo lāgchhe.) Then "I have to go". (āmāke jete hôbe.) Its যেতে is the -তে form you need now.

You'll want to know: want to.
আমি ডাক্তার হতে চাই। āmi ḍāktār hôte chāi. — "I want to be a doctor."
চাই is চাওয়া, "to want", in the present: "I want". With the -তে form of another verb it is "want to": হতে চাই, "want to be"; দেখতে চাই (dekhte chāi), "want to see"; যেতে চাই (jete chāi), "want to go". Here the one who wants is আমি, not আমার: wanting is something you do.

You'll want to know: my wish.
For a wish, use ইচ্ছা, "a wish", or স্বপ্ন, "a dream", which you know, with the -র form of the verb:
আমার পাহাড়ে যাওয়ার ইচ্ছা আছে। āmār pāhāṛe jāoyār ichchhā āchhe. — "I'd like to go to the mountains."
যাওয়ার ইচ্ছা, "a wish of going". Said aloud, ইচ্ছা is often ichchhe, and you will see it written ইচ্ছে too.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'd like to go to the mountains" — āmār pāhāṛe jāoyār ichchhā āchhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
