Italian, chapter 277: Quanto è durato.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
durare — to last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the chronology, then the Italian for the sequence.

You'll want to know: durare.
durare — "to last".
Il film è durato tre ore. The film lasted three hours.
In the passato prossimo, è durato is the usual form; ha durato is also heard.

The word, taken apart.
durare is inherited from Latin dūrāre "to harden; to hold out, to last" (EtymDB), built on dūrus "hard" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'istante — the instant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to last, then the Italian for the length of time.

You'll want to know: l'istante.
l'istante — "the instant".
In un istante la stanza era vuota. In an instant the room was empty.
All'istante means ‘at once, immediately’.

The word, taken apart.
istante is a learned borrowing from Latin īnstāns "pressing, present", from īnstāre "to stand upon, to press" (EtymDB): the moment that presses on you now.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'attimo — the moment, a split second.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the length of time, then the Italian for the instant.

You'll want to know: l'attimo.
l'attimo — "the moment, a split second".
Aspetta un attimo, arrivo! Wait a moment, I am coming!
Un attimo! — ‘One moment!’ — is heard everywhere.

The word, taken apart.
attimo is usually traced to Latin atomus, from Greek átomos "that cannot be cut" (Lewis & Short): the smallest slice of time.
