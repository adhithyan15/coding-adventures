# avanzado — advanced

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CURSO, ES-LEX-C323-WHEN-18] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el curso** and **antes**. The second is hiding inside today's
word, and you would not guess it from the outside.

## You'll want to know: avanzado
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CURSO] -->

**avanzado** — advanced.

> El curso de los lunes era demasiado **avanzado** para mí.

*Un nivel avanzado*, *un grupo avanzado*: it describes the class, not the
person. For the person you say *tiene un nivel avanzado*.

It agrees like any adjective — *avanzada*, *avanzados*, *avanzadas*.

## Grammar Lens: a participle doing an adjective's work
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-03] -->

*Avanzado* is the past participle of *avanzar*, to advance, standing in as an
adjective. Spanish does this constantly, and you have seen it before:

| the participle | what it describes |
|---|---|
| **invitado** | the one who was invited |
| **avanzado** | the one that has moved ahead |

[El invitado](./ES-C282-invitado.md) made the point first — Spanish names a
person by what was done to them. Here the same move names a **course** by how
far it has got.

So a *curso avanzado* is not a course that is difficult in itself. It is one
that has already travelled, and expects you to have travelled with it. That
distinction is the whole reason the woman in the recording changed groups.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-03, ES-LEX-C323-WHEN-18] -->

**Avanzar** is Latin **ab ante** — *from in front* — which became *abantiare*
and then the Spanish verb. The *ante* in it is the same *ante* you already
hold in **antes**.

To advance is to put yourself **in front**. Not faster; further along.

One thing worth knowing about the English. **Advance** has a **d** that does
not belong to it: the word arrived as *avancer*, and English later added the
*d* on the mistaken assumption that the *av-* was a worn-down Latin *ad-*. It
was not. Spanish never made that mistake, which is why *avanzar* has no *d*
and never did.
