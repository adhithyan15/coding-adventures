Italian, chapter 466: Il caldo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'afa — the sultry heat, the mugginess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the snail, then the Italian for to slither.

You'll want to know: l'afa.
l'afa — "the sultry heat, the mugginess".
Ad agosto in città c'è un'afa terribile. In August the city is terribly muggy.
Heat with no wind and damp air.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of afa is not settled; it may imitate the sound of gasping for breath.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
scottarsi — to get sunburnt; to burn oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the sultry heat, then the Italian for sultry.

You'll want to know: scottarsi.
scottarsi — "to get sunburnt; to burn oneself".
Mettiti la crema o ti scotti. Put on sun cream or you will get burnt.
Hot drinks too: mi sono scottato con il caffè.

The word, taken apart.
scottarsi is inherited from Vulgar Latin excoctāre 'to scorch' (EtymDB), built on Latin coquere 'to cook'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la siccità — the drought.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sultry, then the Italian for to get sunburnt.

You'll want to know: la siccità.
la siccità — "the drought".
Quest'estate c'è stata una lunga siccità. This summer there was a long drought.
Secco — dry; la siccità is a long spell with no rain.

The word, taken apart.
siccità is a learned borrowing from Latin siccitās 'dryness' (EtymDB), from siccus 'dry'.
