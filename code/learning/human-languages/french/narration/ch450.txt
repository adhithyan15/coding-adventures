French, chapter 450: À la caisse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le caissier — the cashier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to send off, then the French for to tie up.

You'll want to know: le caissier.
le caissier — "the cashier".
Le caissier m'a rendu la monnaie. The cashier gave me my change.
Feminine la caissière.

The word, taken apart.
caissier is built on caisse 'till, box' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin capsa 'box', probably through Provençal.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
encaisser — to cash in, to take (money); to take (a blow).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to tie up, then the French for the cashier.

You'll want to know: encaisser.
encaisser — "to cash in, to take (money); to take (a blow)".
Le commerçant encaisse le paiement. The shopkeeper takes the payment.
Informal: je ne peux pas l'encaisser — I cannot stand him.

The word, taken apart.
encaisser is built from en- and caisse 'box, till', which goes back to Latin capsa (Lewis & Short): to put the money in the till.

Lesson 3 of 5.
débiter — to charge (an account); to cut up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cashier, then the French for to cash in.

You'll want to know: débiter.
débiter — "to charge (an account); to cut up".
Le magasin a débité ma carte deux fois. The shop charged my card twice.
Le débit — the debit; also the flow of a river.

The word, taken apart.
débiter is built on débit (EtymDB), from Latin dēbitum 'what is owed', from dēbēre 'to owe'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le terminal — the card machine; the terminal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to cash in, then the French for to charge.

You'll want to know: le terminal.
le terminal — "the card machine; the terminal".
Elle pose sa carte sur le terminal. She taps her card on the card machine.
Le terminal de paiement; also an airport terminal.

The word, taken apart.
terminal is a learned borrowing from Late Latin terminālis 'of a boundary' (EtymDB), from terminus 'end, limit'.
