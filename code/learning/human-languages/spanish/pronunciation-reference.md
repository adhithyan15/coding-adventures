# Spanish Pronunciation — Reference

This is a **reference**, not a chapter. You are not meant to read it front to
back before starting. Each lesson tells you the one or two sounds you need
for *that* word, and links here if you want the fuller picture. Look things
up as curiosity strikes; otherwise, keep learning words.

Each entry has an id (e.g. `silent-h`) that lessons cite in their frontmatter
`sounds:` field, so "Sounds you'll need" notes and this page stay in sync.

## Vowels — the five pure sounds

Spanish has exactly five vowel sounds, one per letter, every time. English
has a dozen-plus and reuses letters for several; that habit is the thing to
unlearn.

- `vowel-a` — **a** as in English *father*. Always. Never *cat*, never *cake*.
- `vowel-e` — **e** as in *bed*. Always. Never the *ee* of *see*.
- `vowel-i` — **i** as the *ee* in *see* (not the English *i* of *sit*). The
  single most common vowel mistake English speakers make.
- `vowel-o` — **o** as in *note*, but short and clean, with no *w*-glide at
  the end.
- `vowel-u` — **u** as the *oo* in *food*. Always.

## Diphthongs — two vowels run together

- `diphthong-ue` — **ue** as a quick *weh* (Spanish *u* + *e*), e.g. *bueno*,
  *nueve*.
- `diphthong-ie` — **ie** as a quick *yeh* (Spanish *i* + *e*), e.g. *quiero*,
  *siete*.
- `diphthong-ei` — **ei** as in English *they*, e.g. *seis*, *treinta*.

## Consonants that differ from English

- `silent-h` — **h** is always silent, no exceptions. *hola* is *OH-la*,
  *hay* is *eye*.
- `soft-c` — **c** before *e* or *i* is a soft sound: *s* in Latin America,
  a *th* (as in *think*) in most of Spain. *gracias*, *cero*. Before *a/o/u*
  it's a hard *k* (*casa*).
- `qu-k` — **qu** is a hard *k*, and the **u is silent**: *quiero* is
  *KYEH-ro*, *qué* is *keh*. (This silent-*u*-after-*q* is inherited straight
  from Latin.)
- `ll-y` — **ll** sounds like English *y* in *yes* across most of Latin
  America: *llamo* is *YA-mo*. (Regional variation exists; *y* is the safe
  default.)
- `n-tilde` — **ñ** is the *ny* of *canyon*: *español* is *es-pa-NYOL*. A
  different letter from *n*, not a decoration.
- `rr-trill` — **rr** is a rolled/trilled *r*; a single *r* between vowels is
  a light tap. *pero* (but) vs. *perro* (dog) is a real minimal pair.
- `j-h` — **j** is a throat-*h*, rougher than English *h* (like the *ch* in
  Scottish *loch*): *jueves*.
- `g-soft` — **g** before *e/i* is the same throat sound as *j* (*gente*);
  before *a/o/u* it's a hard *g* (*gato*).
- `d-soft` — **d** between vowels softens toward English *th* in *this*:
  *nada* is closer to *NA-tha*.
- `v-b` — **v** and **b** sound essentially identical in Spanish, both a soft
  *b*.

## Stress & written accents

Three facts cover almost every word:

- `stress-default-vowel-ns` — a word ending in a vowel, *n*, or *s* is
  stressed on the **second-to-last** syllable: *ca-SA*, *ha-BLAN*, *li-BROS*.
- `stress-default-consonant` — a word ending in any other consonant is
  stressed on the **last** syllable: *ha-BLAR*, *ciu-DAD*.
- `accent-mark` — a written accent (´) overrides both rules and puts the
  stress exactly on the marked vowel: *ha-BLÓ*, *DÍ-as*. The mark can also
  distinguish otherwise-identical words (*qué* "what?" vs. *que* "that",
  *tú* "you" vs. *tu* "your").
- `accent-i` — a specific case of the above: the accent on *í* in words like
  *días* keeps the stress on the *i* where the default rule would otherwise
  misplace it.

## Punctuation

- `inverted-marks` — questions and exclamations open with an inverted mark:
  *¿cómo estás?*, *¡hola!* — so you know the intonation before you reach the
  end of the sentence.
