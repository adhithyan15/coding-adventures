Italian, chapter 405: In autostrada.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il casello — the toll booth (on the motorway).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for exactly, then the Italian for almost.

You'll want to know: il casello.
il casello — "the toll booth (on the motorway)".
Al casello paghiamo con la carta. At the toll booth we pay by card.
On the motorway you take a ticket at the first casello and pay at the last one.

The word, taken apart.
casello is built on casa 'house' with the small-size ending -ello: first the keeper's little house beside a road or a railway line. Casa is inherited from Latin casa 'cottage, hut' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la corsia — the lane (of a road); the ward (in a hospital).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for almost, then the Italian for the toll booth.

You'll want to know: la corsia.
la corsia — "the lane (of a road); the ward (in a hospital)".
Resta nella corsia di destra. Stay in the right-hand lane.
La corsia di emergenza is the hard shoulder; in a hospital, la corsia is a ward.

The word, taken apart.
corsia is built on corsa 'run, course', which goes back to Latin currere 'to run' (Lewis & Short): a lane is a strip to run along. The words are inherited from Latin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la tangenziale — the ring road, the bypass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the toll booth, then the Italian for the lane.

You'll want to know: la tangenziale.
la tangenziale — "the ring road, the bypass".
Prendiamo la tangenziale per evitare il centro. Let us take the ring road to avoid the centre.
It runs round the edge of a city and touches it like a tangent.

The word, taken apart.
tangenziale is built on tangente 'tangent', a learned borrowing from Latin tangēns 'touching', from tangere 'to touch' (Lewis & Short): the road touches the city without crossing it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: lo svincolo.
lo svincolo — "the junction, the slip road".
Esci al prossimo svincolo. Get off at the next junction.
Where you leave the motorway or move from one big road to another.

The word, taken apart.
svincolo is built on svincolare 'to set free from a bond', from s- and vincolo 'bond', a learned borrowing from Latin vinculum 'bond, link' (EtymDB): the road that lets you loose.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sorpassare — to overtake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the ring road, then the Italian for the junction.

You'll want to know: sorpassare.
sorpassare — "to overtake".
Il camion ci ha sorpassato a destra. The lorry overtook us on the right.
Il sorpasso is the act of overtaking; vietato sorpassare — no overtaking.

The word, taken apart.
sorpassare is built from sor-, a worn form of Latin super 'over', and passare 'to pass', which is inherited from Vulgar Latin passāre, built on Latin passus 'a step' (Lewis & Short).
