Japanese, chapter 205: At the Ticket Window.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
まどぐち (madoguchi) — a ticket window; a service counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to drop in, then the Japanese for lodging.

You'll want to know: まどぐち.
まどぐち — madoguchi — "a ticket window; a service counter".
まどぐちで きっぷを かいます。 — madoguchi de kippu o kaimasu — "I buy a ticket at the ticket window."

The word, taken apart.
まどぐち (madoguchi) is native Japanese (wago), built from mado, "window", and kuchi, "mouth, opening", both inherited rather than borrowed; kuchi is voiced to guchi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

The word, taken apart.
えきいん (ekiin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are eki, "relay station" (Middle Chinese yek) and in, "member" (hjwen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
かいさつぐち (kaisatsuguchi) — a ticket gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a ticket window, then the Japanese for a station attendant.

You'll want to know: かいさつぐち.
かいさつぐち — kaisatsuguchi — "a ticket gate".
かいさつぐちは ひだりです。 — kaisatsuguchi wa hidari desu — "The ticket gate is on the left."

The word, taken apart.
かいさつぐち (kaisatsuguchi) is built from Sino-Japanese kaisatsu, "checking tickets", read with on'yomi borrowed from Chinese, and native kuchi, "opening", voiced to guchi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2). The Chinese parts are kai, "to change, check" (Middle Chinese kojX), and satsu, "tag, tablet" (tsreat) (Baxter's transcription).
