## HL-C350 — Numbers are a corpus-wide gap: seven tracks cover no numeral point, four teach no numeral at all

**MEASURED AGAINST `origin/main` AT `b83766de`, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS
REPRODUCIBLE.** Two tracks made it worth checking. Marathi's count had stood at
five for forty-two chapters while its inventory recorded age, price, personal
data and the interview's opening question as all waiting on a number above
five. And Marwadi's chapters 26–31 are a market — *how much is this*, *make it
a little cheaper*, *what final price will you give*, *take the money* — with the
words for one to five returning **zero across its 257 files**. A bargaining
curriculum with no numbers is a striking single case; two tracks is enough to
ask whether it is a pattern. It is.

**Corpus-wide: 46 of 99 numeral exam points are covered (46%), and 135
uncovered points across the 23 tracks wait on numbers** — points about age,
price, a telephone number, personal data on a form, a date, the clock and
measures, every one of which the inventories themselves record as blocked
behind a numeral. Numbers are the largest single lever in the file measured
that way, and no per-track reading finds that, because in any one track it
looks like four or five ordinary gaps.

**Seven tracks cover ZERO numeral points**, and they are not one problem but
three:

- **Four teach no numeral at all.** `marwadi` (257 lessons), `persian` (71),
  `urdu` (89) and `russian` (123, which holds exactly one — *odin*, and it is
  taught as half of the *odin … drugoy* joining pattern rather than as a
  number). Their own inventory notes already say so; `MW-A1-Q-01` calls itself
  "the headline of this file".
- **One teaches half a series.** `chinese` has *yi* to *wu*, each with its own
  character and its own writing lesson, and nothing from *liu* to *shi*.
- **Two teach twenty and are measured against a hundred.** `french` and
  `german` both reach twenty in the corpus and both have a single point
  demanding 0–100, so their zero is a CEILING problem rather than an absence,
  and the repair is different in kind.

**Two column-wide findings that no per-track reading would have surfaced:**

- **Ordinals are the weakest column in the corpus.** Twenty tracks enumerate an
  ordinal point and **eighteen leave it uncovered**. Only Arabic and Spanish
  teach one. *First, second, third* is what a floor number, a date on a notice
  and every ordering word need.
- **Digits are nearly as weak.** Fifteen tracks enumerate a digits point and
  **ten leave it uncovered** — and for the tracks with an indigenous digit set
  (Arabic-Indic, Bengali, Devanagari, Gujarati, Persian, Urdu) that is a
  READING gap as well as a counting one, because a price ticket, a phone number
  and a form are all written in those shapes.

Marathi's row is the one that moves first: chapters 55–59 take its count from
five to twenty and close `MR-A1-QU-02`, `MR-A1-OR-14`, `MR-A1-NE12-03` and
`MR-A1-NE15-01`, which puts it in the same position as French and German —
reaching twenty against a point that demands a hundred.

**HOW IT WAS MEASURED, AND WHERE THE MEASUREMENT IS WEAK.** The probe is
checked in as `data/scripts/numeral-probe.mjs` and prints this table; run it
with `node code/learning/human-languages/data/scripts/numeral-probe.mjs` after
building the human-language-data package, and pass a path to get the per-point
JSON behind the summary (it writes nothing unless asked). A measurement filed as a number ages; filed as a
script it can be re-run against whatever the corpus has become.

The citable layer
is the inventory: every track has an A1 exam inventory whose numeral points name
the demand and whose probes name the atoms that satisfy it, so
`measureExamCoverage` answers the question with no judgement added. The corpus
layer — the *highest* column — is supporting evidence only, and it is
gloss-convention dependent. It matches on the HEAD of a lesson's gloss, because
matching a number ANYWHERE in a gloss reports "three pieces of Gurmukhi" and
"five source-attested shopping words" as numeral lessons and scored 73 of them
for a track that teaches none. The narrow rule has the opposite failure: Hindi
teaches one to ten in a lesson whose gloss head is not a bare numeral, so its
*highest* reads 20 off the eleven-to-twenty lesson alone. **Read the inventory
column; treat *highest* as a floor.**

One classification worth carrying into any repair, because a coverage
percentage cannot make it and a reader of the notes can: an uncovered numeral
point means one of two quite different things, and the note says which.
`MW-A1-Q-01` and `FA-A1-Q-01` are content — nobody taught it. `JA-A1-NUM-01` is
the inverse: it scores as COVERED and its own note warns that it counted on a
single numeral sitting inside a phrase.

**Not fixed here, deliberately.** Several of these tracks have their own agents.
This entry is the work queue, not the work: the per-track uncovered-point ids
above are what a tranche would close, and the *waiting* column is what closing
them would unblock.
