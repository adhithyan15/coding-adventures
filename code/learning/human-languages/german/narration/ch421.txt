6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
die Fähre — the ferry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to refund, then the German for the refund.

You'll want to know: die Fähre.
die Fähre — "the ferry".
Die Fähre fährt jede Stunde zur Insel. The ferry goes to the island every hour.
Plural: die Fähren.

The word, taken apart.
Fähre is built on the inherited verb fahren 'to travel' (EtymDB lists Fähre); English ferry comes from the same Germanic family.

Lesson 3 of 6.
die Kabine — the cabin; the changing cubicle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the ferry, then the German for the crossing.

You'll want to know: die Kabine.
die Kabine — "the cabin; the changing cubicle".
Unsere Kabine hat ein kleines rundes Fenster. Our cabin has a small round window.
Plural: die Kabinen. In a clothes shop, die Kabine is the changing cubicle.

The word, taken apart.
Kabine was borrowed from French cabine, a word linked with English cabin; both are usually traced to Late Latin capanna 'hut'. EtymDB lists the German word without a source, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 4 of 6.
die Schwimmweste — the life jacket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the crossing, then the German for the cabin.

You'll want to know: die Schwimmweste.
die Schwimmweste — "the life jacket".
Unter jedem Sitz liegt eine Schwimmweste. There is a life jacket under every seat.
Plural: die Schwimmwesten.

The word, taken apart.
Schwimmweste is built from schwimmen 'to swim, to float', inherited from Proto-Germanic swimmaną, and Weste 'waistcoat', which came through French and Italian veste from Latin vestis 'clothing' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
seekrank — seasick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the cabin, then the German for the life jacket.

You'll want to know: seekrank.
seekrank — "seasick".
Bei hohen Wellen wird sie schnell seekrank. When the waves are high she quickly gets seasick.
Die Seekrankheit — seasickness.

The word, taken apart.
seekrank is built from See 'sea, lake' and krank 'ill', both inherited: krank comes through Middle High German from Old High German krank (EtymDB), a word usually said to have first meant 'weak'.
