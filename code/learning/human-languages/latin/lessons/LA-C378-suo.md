## You'll want to know: suō, suere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C378-TEXO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**suō, suere** — "to sew, to stitch".

> Sūtor calceōs suit.
>
> *The shoemaker stitches shoes.*

*suō, suere, suī, sūtum*. *Sūtor*, a shoemaker, is the man who sews. English *suture* comes from it; English *sew* is a native cousin, not a loan.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*suō* is inherited from Proto-Italic *\*sūō* (EtymDB), from the same Proto-Indo-European root as English *sew*, which came down through Old English, not from Latin.
