Mandarin Chinese, chapter 138: Every and Other.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
每 (měi) — every; each.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have time off, then the Mandarin for the holidays.

You'll want to know: 每.
每 — měi — "every; each".
我们每个星期六都打扫 wǒmen měi ge xīngqīliù dōu dǎsǎo — We clean the house every Saturday.
The new character, in pieces: 每 (měi) — a short fall and a short bar on top, then a box: a vertical that turns right along the bottom, a bar that turns down the right side and hooks, a dot, a long bar across, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
每 (měi) is inherited from Middle Chinese mwojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'frequently' and as a particle. 'Every' is not among the glosses, so how it got that sense is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for pointing things out.

Script — a fall and a bar over a dotted box.
每 (měi).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The top, two strokes. A short left-falling stroke, then a short horizontal running right from it.
The box, five strokes. A vertical that runs down the left and turns right along the bottom; a horizontal across the top that turns down the right side, past the bottom, and hooks to the left; a dot inside the upper half; a long horizontal across the middle, out past both sides; then a dot inside the lower half.
2 + 5 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 每 (měi), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 每 (měi)? (Seven.) Which stroke runs out past both sides? (The long horizontal across the middle.) Where are the two dots? (Inside the box, one above that bar and one below it.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
zìjǐ — oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the holidays, then the Mandarin for each.

You'll want to know: zìjǐ.
zìjǐ — "oneself".
wǒ zìjǐ zuòfàn — I cook for myself.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zìjǐ is built from zì 'from' and jǐ 'self', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzijH and kiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'one's own body'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
suǒyǒu — all; every.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for oneself, then the Mandarin for other people.

You'll want to know: suǒyǒu.
suǒyǒu — "all; every".
suǒyǒu de rén dōu lái le — Everyone has come.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: suǒyǒu is said suóyǒu, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
suǒyǒu is built from suǒ 'a place', the suǒ of suǒyǐ, and 有 'to have', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjoX and hjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean all is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
qítā — other; the others.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for other people, then the Mandarin for every single.

You'll want to know: qítā.
qítā — "other; the others".
wǒ xǐhuan zhège qítā de wǒ bú yào — I like this one; I don't want the others.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qítā is built from qí, which the dictionary lists as a particle, and 他, glossed 'not I', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gi and tha (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
