Sanskrit, chapter 390: Signs of What Is Coming.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
लक्षणम् (lakṣaṇam) — a sign of something to come; a mark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for imagination, then the Sanskrit for true to fact.

You'll want to know: लक्षणम्.
लक्षणम् — lakṣaṇam — "a sign of something to come; a mark".
यदि ज्वरस्य लक्षणम् अस्ति, तर्हि वैद्यं पश्यतु। yadi jvarasya lakṣaṇam asti, tarhi vaidyaṃ paśyatu. If there is a sign of fever, see a doctor.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
लक्षणम् (lakṣaṇam) comes from the root √lakṣ, "to mark, to perceive"; MW gives lakṣaṇa "a mark, a sign, a symbol, a token, a characteristic" (MW).

व्यर्थः — vyarthaḥ — "useless; to no purpose".
यदि यन्त्रं न चलति, तर्हि तत् व्यर्थम्। yadi yantraṃ na calati, tarhi tat vyartham. If the machine does not run, it is useless.

Lesson 5 of 5.
अनुभवति (anubhavati) — experiences; feels.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for useless, then the Sanskrit for worthwhile.

You'll want to know: अनुभवति.
अनुभवति — anubhavati — "experiences; feels".
यदि भवान् ध्यायति, तर्हि शान्तिम् अनुभवति। yadi bhavān dhyāyati, tarhi śāntim anubhavati. If you meditate, you feel peace.
Past (he …): सः शान्तिम् अनुभूतवान्, saḥ śāntim anubhūtavān.

The word, taken apart.
अनुभवति (anubhavati) is built on anu, "after", and the root √bhū, "to be"; MW gives anu-√bhū "to attain, to equal" and anu-bhūta "perceived, understood", and "to experience" grows from those senses (MW).
