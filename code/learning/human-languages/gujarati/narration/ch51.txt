Gujarati, chapter 51: Father, Mother, Grandparents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પિતા (pitā) — father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for worry, then the Gujarati for tiredness.

You'll want to know: પિતા.
પિતા (pitā) — "father".

The word, taken apart.
પિતા (pitā) is Sanskrit pitā, the nominative of pitṛ, "father", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 8179).

Lesson 3 of 5.

દાદા (dādā) — "grandfather (father's father)".

The word, taken apart.
દાદા (dādā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dādda, "father or other elderly relative", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6261).

Lesson 5 of 5.
બાળક (bāḷak) — a child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for grandfather, then the Gujarati for grandmother.

You'll want to know: બાળક.
બાળક (bāḷak) — "a child".

The word, taken apart.
બાળક (bāḷak) is a semi-tatsama: Sanskrit bālaka, "young; a boy", borrowed after the old sound changes had run their course and then lightly reshaped (CDIAL 9216). CDIAL lists the inherited Gujarati form as bāḷ, "child".
