## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C289-SKY-03]; assesses=[] -->

**la nube** — "the cloud."

Latin **nubes**, and it has come down almost untouched: four letters in, four
letters out.

The good English relative is not obvious. French took *nubes* and made **nue**, a
cloud, and from *nue* built **nuance** — which meant the shading of one cloud
into another before it ever meant a shade of meaning. When you say a distinction
has nuance, you are borrowing a description of the sky.

**Nebulous** looks like a relative and is a slightly different one: Latin
**nebula** was mist rather than cloud. Close cousins, different words, and the
pair is worth keeping apart in your head.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Three, and they stack: a moment, the place above you, and the things crossing it.

*El amanecer*, *el cielo*, *la nube*. Notice that none of the three has needed a
new ending or a new pattern — they are ordinary words, and you can already hang
*azul* or *blanco* on the last two.
