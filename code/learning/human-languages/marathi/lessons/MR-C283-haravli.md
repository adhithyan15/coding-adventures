# माझी पिशवी हरवली (mājhī piśvī haravlī) — when something goes wrong

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C133-THINGS133-03, MR-LEX-C81-VERBS81-03, MR-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a bag" and "to lose". (*piśvī*, *haravṇe*.) Then "she came". (*tī ālī*.)

## You'll want to know: lost
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C283-LOST-REPORT-01]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-THREE-GENDERS, MR-LEX-C93-HOME93-03, MR-LEX-C114-TOWN114-05, MR-LEX-C253-ACT253-05] -->

> **माझी पिशवी हरवली.**
>
> *mājhī piśvī haravlī.* — "My bag is lost."

The bag is what got lost, so the past agrees with it, as **ती आली** agrees with her: **पिशवी हरवली**, **पुस्तक हरवलं** (*pustak haravlaṁ*). No **-ने** here. Then the report: **मी पोलिसांकडे तक्रार केली** (*mī polisāṅkaḍe takrār kelī*), "I complained to the police", with **तक्रार** feminine.

## You'll want to know: missed
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C283-MISSED-01]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C165-ACT165-01, MR-LEX-C113-TOWN113-05, MR-LEX-C111-TIME111-02] -->

> **माझी गाडी चुकली.**
>
> *mājhī gāḍī cuklī.* — "I missed my train."

Word for word "my train went wrong": with **चुकणे** the train is the subject. The cause comes first: **मला उशीर झाला, म्हणून माझी गाडी चुकली** (*malā uśīr jhālā, mhaṇūn mājhī gāḍī cuklī*), "I was late, so I missed my train".
