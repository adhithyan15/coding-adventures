French, chapter 473: Fierté et modestie.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
la fierté — pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to move, then the French for to deeply upset.

You'll want to know: la fierté.
la fierté — "pride".
Mon père parle de mon travail avec fierté. My father speaks about my work with pride.
Built on fier "proud"; a good kind of pride.

The word, taken apart.
la fierté is built on fier "proud" with -té (EtymDB). Fier is inherited from Latin ferus "wild, untamed" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
l'orgueil — arrogance, excessive pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to deeply upset, then the French for pride.

You'll want to know: l'orgueil.
l'orgueil — "arrogance, excessive pride".
Par orgueil, il n'a pas demandé d'aide. Out of pride, he did not ask for help.
Masculine: un orgueil. Stronger and more negative than la fierté.

The word, taken apart.
l'orgueil comes from Frankish urgōli "pride", a Germanic word related to Old English orgel "pride" (EtymDB).

Feminine vaniteuse. The noun is la vanité.

The word, taken apart.
vaniteux is built on vanité, a learned borrowing from Latin vānitās "emptiness, vanity", from vānus "empty" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
vanter — to praise; (se vanter) to boast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for vain, then the French for modest.

You'll want to know: vanter.
vanter — "to praise; (se vanter) to boast".
Mon collègue se vante de sa nouvelle voiture. My colleague is boasting about his new car.
Mostly reflexive: se vanter de quelque chose. Vanter un produit — to praise a product.

The word, taken apart.
vanter is inherited from Old French vanter (EtymDB), usually traced to a Late Latin vānitāre "to speak emptily", built on Latin vānus "empty, vain" (Lewis & Short).
