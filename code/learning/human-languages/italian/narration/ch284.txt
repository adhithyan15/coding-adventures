Italian, chapter 284: Sul posto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'inchiesta — the inquiry, the investigation by journalists or officials.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the interview, then the Italian for to interview.

You'll want to know: l'inchiesta.
l'inchiesta — "the inquiry, the investigation by journalists or officials".
Il giornale ha fatto un'inchiesta sugli ospedali. The newspaper ran an inquiry into the hospitals.
Police, judges and journalists all run inchieste.

The word, taken apart.
inchiesta is usually traced to Vulgar Latin inquaesita "a thing sought into", from Latin inquīrere "to search into", built on quaerere "to seek" (Lewis & Short).

il reportage — "the report from the spot, the feature story".
Ha scritto un reportage sulla vita in montagna. She wrote a feature on life in the mountains.
Said the French way, with the stress on the end. Plural unchanged: i reportage.

The word, taken apart.
reportage was borrowed from French reportage (EtymDB), built on reporter, a word that came into French from English.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the inquiry, then the Italian for the report from the spot.

You'll want to know: la testimonianza.
la testimonianza — "the testimony, the eyewitness account".
La sua testimonianza è molto importante. Her testimony is very important.
Rendere testimonianza — to bear witness.

The word, taken apart.
testimonianza is built on testimoniare "to bear witness" with the ending -anza; the root is Latin testimōnium "evidence", from testis "a witness" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
riferire — to report, to pass on (what was said).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the testimony, then the Italian for to testify.

You'll want to know: riferire.
riferire — "to report, to pass on (what was said)".
Ti riferisco quello che ha detto il medico. I am passing on what the doctor said.
Present like capire: riferisco. Riferirsi a — to refer to.

The word, taken apart.
riferire is a learned borrowing from Latin referre "to carry back, to report" (EtymDB), built on ferre "to carry".
