Bengali, chapter 452: Pride and Humiliation.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অহংকার (ôhôngkār) — arrogance; vanity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to hate, then the Bengali for to turn up one's nose.

You'll want to know: অহংকার.
অহংকার — ôhôngkār — "arrogance; vanity".
টাকা হওয়ার পরে লোকটার অহংকার বাড়ল। ṭākā hôoyār pôre lokṭār ôhôngkār bāṛlô. Once he had money, the man's arrogance grew.
অহংকারী, arrogant. গর্ব (taught) is a pride that can be good; অহংকার (ôhôngkār) is not.

The word, taken apart.
অহংকার (ôhôngkār) is a tatsama, Sanskrit ahaṃkāra, "I-making": the sense of self, and so egotism (Monier-Williams).

নতুন গাড়ি কিনে প্রতিবেশীর দেমাক বাড়ল। nôtun gāṛi kine prôtibeshīr demāk bāṛlô. Having bought a new car, the neighbour began to put on airs.

The word, taken apart.
দেমাক (demāk) is usually traced to Arabic dimāgh, "brain", which took on the sense of pride and conceit in Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
জাহির করা (jāhir kôrā) — to show off; to flaunt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for conceit, then the Bengali for self-respect.

You'll want to know: জাহির করা.
জাহির করা — jāhir kôrā — "to show off; to flaunt".
অরুণ বন্ধুদের সামনে নতুন ফোনটা জাহির করছিল। ôrun bôndhuder sāmne nôtun phonṭā jāhir kôrchhilô. Arun was showing off his new phone in front of his friends.
Past (he/she …): তিনি ছেলের সাফল্য জাহির করলেন, tini chheler sāphôlyô jāhir kôrlen.
What is shown off is the object: জ্ঞান জাহির করা (jāhir kôrā), to show off what you know.

The word, taken apart.
জাহির করা (jāhir kôrā) is jāhir, "made plain, on show", usually traced to Arabic ẓāhir, "manifest", through Persian, with kôrā, "to do" (CDIAL 2814): to put something where everyone must see it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
অপদস্থ হওয়া (ôpôdôsthô hôoyā) — to be humiliated; to lose face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for self-respect, then the Bengali for to show off.

You'll want to know: অপদস্থ হওয়া.
অপদস্থ হওয়া — ôpôdôsthô hôoyā — "to be humiliated; to lose face".
বন্ধুদের সামনে বাবার বকা খেয়ে অরুণ অপদস্থ হল। bôndhuder sāmne bābār bôkā kheye ôrun ôpôdôsthô hôlô. Scolded by his father in front of his friends, Arun felt humiliated.
Past (he/she …): তিনি সভায় অপদস্থ হলেন, tini sôbhāy ôpôdôsthô hôlen.

The word, taken apart.
অপদস্থ হওয়া (ôpôdôsthô hôoyā) is Sanskrit apadastha, "not in its place, out of office" (Monier-Williams), which Bengali uses for someone put down in public, a sense Monier-Williams does not give, with hôoyā, "to be" (CDIAL 9416).
