Lesson 1 of 6.
auctor, auctōris — an originator, an author; a backer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to read aloud, then the Latin for rashly.

You'll want to know: auctor, auctōris.
auctor, auctōris — "an originator, an author; a backer".
Quis huius cōnsiliī auctor fuit? Who was the author of this plan?
auctor, auctōris m. (sometimes f.). The one who makes a thing grow: a founder, a writer, the backer of a law or of a sale. English author.

The word, taken apart.
auctor is built on augeō "to increase, to make grow" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor of the one who does it. English author comes from it through French.

impulsus, impulsūs m., built on impellō, to push on (in + pellō). Impulsū alicuius, at someone's instigation. English impulse.

The word, taken apart.
impulsus is the noun built on impellō "to push on" (Lewis & Short), from in and pellō "to drive". English impulse comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
efficiō, efficere — to bring about, to cause; to make.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an originator, then the Latin for a push.

You'll want to know: efficiō, efficere.
efficiō, efficere — "to bring about, to cause; to make".
Pluvia effēcit ut domī manērēmus. The rain made us stay at home.
efficiō, efficere, effēcī, effectum. Efficiō ut + subjunctive: I bring it about that. Built on faciō. English efficient.

The word, taken apart.
efficiō is built from ex "out" and faciō "to make" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to make a thing until it is done. English efficient comes from its present participle.

Lesson 5 of 6.
excitō, excitāre — to rouse, to wake; to stir up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to bring about, then the Latin for effective.

You'll want to know: excitō, excitāre.
excitō, excitāre — "to rouse, to wake; to stir up".
Clāmor mē ē somnō excitāvit. The shouting woke me from sleep.
excitō, excitāre, excitāvī, excitātum. To call out and set moving: a sleeper, a fire, a feeling. English excite.

The word, taken apart.
excitō is the frequentative of exciō "to call out, to rouse" (Lewis & Short), from ex "out" and cieō "to set moving", the verb behind citō. English excite comes from it.
