Bengali, chapter 416: Debts and Instalments.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
কিস্তি (kisti) — an instalment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to invest, then the Bengali for to lose (money or property).

You'll want to know: কিস্তি.
কিস্তি — kisti — "an instalment".
অরুণ সাইকেলটা কিস্তিতে কিনল। ôrun sāikelṭā kistite kinlô. Arun bought the bicycle in instalments.

The word, taken apart.
কিস্তি (kisti) is usually traced to Arabic qisṭ, "a share, a portion", through Persian qist, "an instalment".

Lesson 2 of 5.
দেনা (denā) — a debt (owed); what one owes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to lose (money or property), then the Bengali for an instalment.

You'll want to know: দেনা.
দেনা — denā — "a debt (owed); what one owes".
দোকানদারের কাছে আমাদের কিছু দেনা আছে। dokāndārer kāchhe āmāder kichhu denā āchhe. We owe the shopkeeper some money.
ধার is a loan; দেনা (denā) is what is still to be paid back.

The word, taken apart.
দেনা (denā) is built on দে-, the stem of দেওয়া, to give, the Bengali verb that goes back to Sanskrit dadāti (Monier-Williams): what one has still to give.

Lesson 3 of 5.
পাওনা (pāonā) — money owed to one; one's dues.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an instalment, then the Bengali for a debt (owed).

You'll want to know: পাওনা.
পাওনা — pāonā — "money owed to one; one's dues".
কাজ শেষ করে মিস্ত্রি তার পাওনা চাইলেন। kāj shesh kore mistri tār pāonā chāilen. When the job was done, the mechanic asked for what he was owed.
The pair দেনা (denā) and পাওনা (pāonā), what you owe and what you are owed, often go together.

The word, taken apart.
পাওনা (pāonā) is built on পাওয়া, "to get", from Sanskrit prāpayati (CDIAL 8943): what one is due to get.

Lesson 4 of 5.
শোধ করা (shodh kôrā) — to pay off; to repay (a debt).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a debt (owed), then the Bengali for money owed to one.

You'll want to know: শোধ করা.
শোধ করা — shodh kôrā — "to pay off; to repay (a debt)".
দুই বছরে অরুণ সব ধার শোধ করল। dui bôchhôre ôrun sôb dhār shodh kôrlô. In two years Arun paid off all his debts.
Past (he/she …): তিনি ধার শোধ করলেন, tini dhār shodh kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
শোধ করা (shodh kôrā) joins Sanskrit śodha, "cleansing, payment" (Monier-Williams), to করা, to do: to clear a debt. CDIAL lists the spoken Bengali sodhā, "to pay off", from the same root (CDIAL 12626).

Lesson 5 of 5.
মেটানো (meṭāno) — to settle (a bill); to make good.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for money owed to one, then the Bengali for to pay off.

You'll want to know: মেটানো.
মেটানো — meṭāno — "to settle (a bill); to make good".
হোটেল ছাড়ার আগে আমরা বিল মেটালাম। hoṭel chhāṛār āge āmrā bil meṭālām. Before leaving the hotel we settled the bill.
Past (he/she …): তিনি বিল মেটালেন, tini bil meṭālen.
Also of a quarrel or a need: ঝগড়া মেটানো (meṭāno), to settle a quarrel; খিদে মেটানো (meṭāno), to satisfy hunger.

The word, taken apart.
মেটানো (meṭāno) is the causing form of মেটা, which CDIAL lists as Bengali meṭā, "to be wiped out", under Sanskrit mṛṣṭá, "rubbed, washed" (CDIAL 10299): to settle a bill is to wipe it out.
