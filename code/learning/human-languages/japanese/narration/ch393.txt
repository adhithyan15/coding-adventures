Japanese, chapter 393: Signs and Landmarks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
めじるし (mejirushi) — a landmark; a mark to go by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to turn back, then the Japanese for to stop by.

You'll want to know: めじるし.
めじるし — mejirushi — "a landmark; a mark to go by".
えきの まえの たかい たてものが めじるしです。 — eki no mae no takai tatemono ga mejirushi desu — "The tall building in front of the station is the landmark."

The word, taken apart.
めじるし (mejirushi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from me, "eye" and jirushi, "mark"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
かんばん (kanban) — a signboard; a shop sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to stop by, then the Japanese for a landmark.

You'll want to know: かんばん.
かんばん — kanban — "a signboard; a shop sign".
みせの かんばんは あかいです。 — mise no kanban wa akai desu — "The shop's sign is red."

The word, taken apart.
かんばん (kanban) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kan, "watch over" (Middle Chinese khan) and ban, "board" (paenX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
