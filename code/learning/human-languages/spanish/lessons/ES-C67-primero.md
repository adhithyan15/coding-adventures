# primero · segundo · tercero — counting in order

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-NUMBERS-1-5] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Uno, dos, tres* — how many.

[PAUSE 1s] Now the other kind of counting: not how many, but **which one in the
line**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-ORDINALS, ES-ETYMON-SEGUNDO-SEQUI]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-NOUN-GENDER] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say them: ***primero***, ***segundo***, ***tercero***, ***cuarto***,
***quinto***.

[PAUSE 2s] And the second one has a story. Latin ***secundus*** did not mean
*two*. It meant **following**, from the verb *sequi*, *to follow* — so second
place is literally *the one that comes after*. English *second* is the very
same word, and *sequence* and *consequence* are its cousins.

[PAUSE 2s] They behave as adjectives: *la* ***segunda*** *casa*, *los*
***primeros*** *libros*.
