## You'll want to know: voluptās, voluptātis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C499-VOLU-03]; assesses=[] -->

**voluptās, voluptātis** — "pleasure, enjoyment".

> Sapiēns nōn voluptātem, sed virtūtem quaerit.
>
> *The wise man seeks not pleasure but virtue.*

Feminine, third declension. Pleasure of every kind, of the senses and of the mind. English *voluptuous*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**voluptās** is built on the old adverb *volup* "pleasantly" (EtymDB), heard in the phrase *volup est* "it is a pleasure"; Lewis & Short compares Greek *elpo* "to hope" instead. English *voluptuous* comes from it.
