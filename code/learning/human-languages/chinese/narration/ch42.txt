Mandarin Chinese, chapter 42: In the Shop.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
dōngxi — thing; things.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for too, then the Mandarin for to want.

You'll want to know: dōngxi.
dōngxi — "thing; things".
nǐ mǎi shénme dōngxi — What are you buying?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dōngxi is built from dōng 'east' and xī 'west', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tuwng and sej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How east-and-west came to mean a thing is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[your turn — say: nǐ mǎi shénme dōngxi — What are you buying?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for too, then the Mandarin for to want, then say dōngxi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mài is inherited from Middle Chinese meaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to put goods out'. It differs from the word for buy only in its tone.

Lesson 3 of 6.
shāngdiàn — shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for thing, then the Mandarin for to sell.

You'll want to know: shāngdiàn.
shāngdiàn — "shop".
zhège shāngdiàn hěn xiǎo — This shop is tiny.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shāngdiàn is built from shāng, usually explained as 'trade', and diàn 'shop', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syang and temH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary explains the second as a place where goods are kept and sold.

Lesson 4 of 6.
piányi — cheap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sell, then the Mandarin for shop.

You'll want to know: piányi.
piányi — "cheap".
zhège hěn piányi — This one's cheap.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
piányi is built from two old words, both inherited from Middle Chinese: bjien 'at ease' and ngje 'fitting' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The sense 'cheap' is not given there, so how the pair came to mean it is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
guì — expensive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for shop, then the Mandarin for cheap.

You'll want to know: guì.
guì — "expensive".
nàge tài guì le — That one's too expensive.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guì is inherited from Middle Chinese kjw+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'honoured, high'. The price sense is not given there; from valued to costly is a likely step, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
