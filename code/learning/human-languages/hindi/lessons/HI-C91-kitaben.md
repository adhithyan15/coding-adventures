# किताबें — nothing to change, so something is added

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-CONCEPT-C37-KITAAB-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **बच्चा** ends in **-आ** and swapped it for **-ए**. **किताब** ends
in a consonant. Ask yourself what there is to swap.

## You'll want to know: किताबें
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02]; assesses=[] -->

**किताबें** (*kitābeṁ*) — books.

**एक किताब** — one book. **दो किताबें** — two books.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01] -->

A word with no vowel at the end has nothing to exchange, so Hindi **adds** a
syllable: **-एँ**, the same **-ए** as before with a nasal on it.

| shape of the noun | how it makes a plural |
|---|---|
| ends in -आ | swaps the ending for -ए |
| ends in a consonant | adds -एँ |

That is the whole difference, and it follows from the shape rather than from a
list to memorise: **change what is there, or add where nothing is.**

Feminine nouns ending in a consonant, like **किताब**, are much the commonest
case, and the nasal is what your ear should catch.

## Grammar Lens: the nasal you already write
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02] -->

The mark over the ending is the **anusvāra**, which you have been writing since
**हैं** and **मिलेंगे**. It is doing the same job here that it does there: this
is a plural, and the nasal is the sign of it.

| singular | plural |
|---|---|
| किताब | किताबें |
| रात | रातें |

Say both aloud and let the nasal fall on the last syllable rather than
swallowing it — that is what a listening paper is asking you to hear.
