Telugu, chapter 319: Must, Must Not, and Because.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
వెళ్ళాలి (veḷḷāli) — must, must not, had better.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want a ticket". (nāku oka ṭikkeṭṭu kāvāli.) Then "It is raining, so I am at home." (varṣaṁ paḍutōndi kābaṭṭi nēnu iṇṭlō unnānu.)

You'll want to know: -ఆలి, must.
నేను ఇప్పుడు వెళ్ళాలి. nēnu ippuḍu veḷḷāli. — "I must go now."
మీరు ఈ మందు ప్రతి రోజు తీసుకోవాలి. mīru ī mandu prati rōju tīsukōvāli. — "You should take this medicine every day."
-ఆలి sits on the verb's base: వెళ్ళు becomes వెళ్ళాలి (veḷḷāli), చేయు becomes చేయాలి, తిను becomes తినాలి, చదువు becomes చదవాలి, and వచ్చు becomes రావాలి. It does not change for the person: నేను, నువ్వు, అతను, all వెళ్ళాలి (veḷḷāli). It covers both "must" and "should".
You have said this ending for a long time. కావాలి is -ఆలి on కా-, "to become, to be": "it must be (had)", so "it is wanted".

You'll want to know: -అకూడదు, must not.
జ్వరం ఉన్నప్పుడు బడికి వెళ్ళకూడదు. jvaraṁ unnappuḍu baḍiki veḷḷakūḍadu. — "When you have a fever, you must not go to school."
ఇప్పుడు కాఫీ తాగకూడదు. ippuḍu kāphī tāgakūḍadu. — "You must not drink coffee now."
-అకూడదు: వెళ్ళకూడదు, తాగకూడదు, చేయకూడదు. ఉన్నప్పుడు (unnappuḍu, "when there is") is ఉన్న + అప్పుడు, built like -తున్నప్పుడు.

You'll want to know: -తే మంచిది, had better.
మీరు ఆసుపత్రికి వెళ్తే మంచిది. mīru āsupatriki veḷtē mañcidi. — "You had better go to the hospital."
Word for word, "if you go, it is good": advice, softer than -ఆలి. The -తే, "if", gets a lesson of its own later in this core; here keep -తే మంచిది as one piece.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I must go now" — nēnu ippuḍu veḷḷāli]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You should take this medicine every day" — mīru ī mandu prati rōju tīsukōvāli]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You must not drink coffee now" — ippuḍu kāphī tāgakūḍadu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You had better go to the hospital" — mīru āsupatriki veḷtē mañcidi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Must come"? (రావాలి.) "Must not eat"? (తినకూడదు.) "Had better go"? (వెళ్తే మంచిది.) Which word you knew already ends in -ఆలి? (కావాలి.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ఓటు (ōṭu) — a vote.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I must go" and "you must not drink coffee now". (nēnu veḷḷāli; ippuḍu kāphī tāgakūḍadu.)

You'll want to know: ఓటు.
ఓటు — ōṭu — "a vote".
ప్రతి ఒక్కరూ ఓటు వేయాలి. prati okkarū ōṭu vēyāli. — "Everyone must vote."
నేను నిన్న ఓటు వేశాను. nēnu ninna ōṭu vēśānu. — "I voted yesterday."
A vote is "put": ఓటు వేయు (ōṭu vēyu, "to cast a vote"), with వేయు, "to put". ప్రతి ఒక్కరూ (prati okkarū, "every single person") is ప్రతి, "every", with a word for "one person".
Its first letter, a long ō at the start of a word, is one you have not written. The next lesson takes it out of this word.

The word, taken apart.
ఓటు (ōṭu) is a loan from English vote, with the -ఉ that Telugu gives most borrowed nouns, as in బస్సు and టిక్కెట్టు. The English v and the long ō are kept.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ōṭu, with a long ō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Everyone must vote" — prati okkarū ōṭu vēyāli]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I voted yesterday" — nēnu ninna ōṭu vēśānu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ఓటు (ōṭu) mean? (A vote.) "To cast a vote"? (ఓటు (ōṭu) వేయు.) Where does the word come from? (English vote.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ఓ (ō) — the letter that opens ఓటు (ōṭu).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ఓ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ఒ and ో once each, and name them. (Short o at the start of a word; long ō after a consonant, as in తోలు.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ఓటు (ōṭu), "a vote". This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ఓ.
ఓ — ō, a long ō at the start of a word.
After a consonant, long ō is the sign ో. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own, ఓ, and it pairs with the short ఒ you know, the way ఏ pairs with ఎ.
In print ఓ is ఒ with a short upright stroke added at its top. Say the pair aloud: ఒ o, short; ఓ ō, held longer.

Writing: ఓ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ఓ (ō) and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Write the body of ఒ first, the way you learned it, then copy the short upright stroke at its top.
This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — find: ఓ (ō) at the start of ఓటు (ōṭu)]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ఓ three times, saying ō as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ఒ, then ఓ (ō), from memory, naming each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ఓటు (ōṭu)]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ఓ? (ō, long ō at the start of a word.) Which word did it come from? (ఓటు, ōṭu, "a vote".) How is it different from ఒ? (The short upright stroke at its top; the vowel is long.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
నడవడం వల్ల (naḍavaḍaṁ valla) — giving the reason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "because of" (valla). Then say "a vote". (ōṭu.)
[once you have stopped driving — write: ఓ (ō), its first letter, once]

You'll want to know: -అడం వల్ల.
-అడం turns a verb into a noun, "-ing": నడవడం, "walking"; చదవడం, "reading"; తినడం, "eating"; రావడం, "coming". With వల్ల, "because of", it gives a reason:
ప్రతి రోజు నడవడం వల్ల ఆరోగ్యం బాగుంటుంది. prati rōju naḍavaḍaṁ valla ārōgyaṁ bāguṇṭundi. — "Walking every day keeps you healthy."
ఎక్కువ తినడం వల్ల నాకు కడుపు నొప్పి వచ్చింది. ekkuva tinaḍaṁ valla nāku kaḍupu noppi vaccindi. — "Eating too much gave me a stomach ache."
బాగుంటుంది (bāguṇṭundi, "it stays well") is బాగా with the -ఉంటుంది of ఉండు.

Grammar Lens: two places for a reason.
నేను రాలేదు, ఎందుకంటే నాకు జ్వరం వచ్చింది. nēnu rālēdu, endukaṇṭē nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi. — "I did not come, because I had a fever."
నాకు జ్వరం వచ్చింది కాబట్టి నేను రాలేదు. nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi kābaṭṭi nēnu rālēdu. — "I had a fever, so I did not come."
The same two facts. With ఎందుకంటే the reason stands after the result, where English puts it. With కాబట్టి, or -అడం వల్ల, it stands before, and the sentence ends on its main verb, as a Telugu sentence does. Both are everyday Telugu.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Eating too much gave me a stomach ache" — ekkuva tinaḍaṁ valla nāku kaḍupu noppi vaccindi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I did not come, because I had a fever" — nēnu rālēdu, endukaṇṭē nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
