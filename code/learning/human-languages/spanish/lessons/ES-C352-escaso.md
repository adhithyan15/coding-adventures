## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C352-COUNT-15]; assesses=[] -->

**escaso** — "scarce," "in short supply."

**Poco** says *there is little*. **Escaso** says *there is less than there
ought to be* — the word carries a shortfall in it.

Behind it is a late Latin **excarpsus**, from **carpere**, *to pluck*, with
**ex-**, *out*. What is *escaso* is what is left after the good parts have been
plucked out of it. A picked-over heap.

*Carpere* is a fine root to own. An **excerpt** is a passage plucked out of a
book. A **carpet** was plucked wool before it was a floor. And **carpe diem**
is not *seize the day* so much as *pluck the day*, the way you pick fruit that
will not keep.

English **scarce** is the same *excarpsus*, arriving through Old French
**escars** — so *escaso* and *scarce* are one word, and the Spanish shape is
the more conservative of the two.
