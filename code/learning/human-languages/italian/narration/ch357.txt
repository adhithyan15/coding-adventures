Italian, chapter 357: Lasciare il lavoro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il licenziamento — the dismissal, the sacking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the candidate, then the Italian for the hiring.

You'll want to know: il licenziamento.
il licenziamento — "the dismissal, the sacking".
Dopo il licenziamento ha aperto un ristorante. After being dismissed he opened a restaurant.
Built on licenziare "to dismiss". La lettera di licenziamento — the dismissal letter.

The word, taken apart.
licenziamento is built on licenziare "to dismiss", from licenza "leave, permission", a learned borrowing from Latin licentia "freedom, permission" (Lewis & Short), from licēre "to be allowed".

Lesson 2 of 5.
dimettersi — to resign.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the hiring, then the Italian for the dismissal.

You'll want to know: dimettersi.
dimettersi — "to resign".
Il direttore si è dimesso ieri. The director resigned yesterday.
Reflexive, with essere; past participle dimesso. From a hospital, essere dimesso is to be discharged.

The word, taken apart.
dimettersi comes from Latin dīmittere "to send away, to let go, to give up" (Lewis & Short), built from dis- "apart" and mittere "to send".

Lesson 4 of 5.
disoccupato — unemployed, out of work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to resign, then the Italian for the resignation.

You'll want to know: disoccupato.
disoccupato — "unemployed, out of work".
Mio cugino è disoccupato da sei mesi. My cousin has been out of work for six months.
Also a noun: i disoccupati — the unemployed.

The word, taken apart.
disoccupato is built from dis- "not" and occupato "busy, employed", from Latin occupātus, past participle of occupāre "to seize, to fill" (Lewis & Short), built on capere "to take".

Lesson 5 of 5.
il preavviso — the notice (period), the advance warning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the resignation, then the Italian for unemployed.

You'll want to know: il preavviso.
il preavviso — "the notice (period), the advance warning".
Devo dare un mese di preavviso prima di andare via. I have to give a month's notice before leaving.
Senza preavviso — without warning.

The word, taken apart.
preavviso is built from pre- "before" and avviso "notice", from avvisare, usually traced to Old French aviser "to perceive, to inform", built on Late Latin vīsāre "to view, to behold" (EtymDB), from the family of vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).
