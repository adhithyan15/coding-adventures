# ä ö ü — the two dots that used to be an *e*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-UMLAUT-OE-01, GE-SOUND-TSCHUESS-01, GE-LEX-SCHOEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have already made two of these sounds — the **ö** of *schön* and
the **ü** of *tschüss*. Now meet the mark itself, and what it is doing there.

## You'll want to know: the umlaut
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-FRONTING-01]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-UMLAUT-AE-02, GE-SOUND-UMLAUT-OE-01, GE-SOUND-TSCHUESS-01] -->

> **Umlaut** — the two dots on **ä ö ü**, which shift the vowel *forward* in the
> mouth.

*Um-laut* is "around-sound" (*um*, around + *Laut*, sound). Say the plain vowel,
then brighten it toward *ee*:

| Plain | Umlauted | How |
|---|---|---|
| a → **ä** | *Mann* → *Männer* | *ah* becomes the *e* of *bed* |
| o → **ö** | *schon* → *schön* | *oh* becomes a rounded *eh* |
| u → **ü** | *Mutter* → *Mütter* | *oo* becomes *ee* with rounded lips |

They look like the French tréma and do the opposite job: a tréma **splits** two
vowels, an umlaut **changes** one.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-UMLAUT-GHOST-E-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-FRONTING-01] -->

The dots are a fossil. The fronting was originally caused by an **e** in the
*next* syllable, and scribes marked it by writing a tiny **e above the vowel**.
In the old German hand that *e* was two little vertical strokes — which, shrunk
and simplified, became two dots.

So an umlaut is a **flattened *e* sitting on top of the vowel**, and that is
exactly why the ASCII fallbacks put it back on the line:

| With dots | Ghost *e* restored |
|---|---|
| *Müller* | Mueller |
| *schön* | schoen |
| *Bär* | Baer |

## Grammar Lens: the umlaut does real work
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-MARKS-GRAMMAR-03]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-UMLAUT-GHOST-E-02] -->

Umlauts are not decoration. Fronting a vowel is how German marks whole
grammatical changes, so learn to *expect* it:

| Base | Umlauted | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| *Mann* (man) | *Männer* | plural |
| *Buch* (book) | *Bücher* | plural |
| *groß* (big) | *größer* | comparative |
| *Hund* (dog) | *Hündchen* | diminutive |

When a German word brightens its vowel, something grammatical happened to it —
usually *more than one*, *more*, or *smaller*.

## Writing — the two dots
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-FRONTING-01, GE-ETYMON-UMLAUT-GHOST-E-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Two dots, side by side, sitting squarely **above** the vowel — not slanted, and
not joined. Copy **schön** once with the model in view, then **Mütter**.

Say each word as you finish it, and let the vowel brighten as you write the dots.
