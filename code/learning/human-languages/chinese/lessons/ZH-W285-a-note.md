# A note to the desk, four lines that lean on each other

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C285-LIANXI-01, ZH-LEX-C206-FLOOR206-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You are staying at a hotel, and something of yours has gone missing. The front desk, *qiántái*, can help, if you tell them clearly.

[PAUSE 1s] This lesson writes to them.

- [YOU RECALL: say *liánxì*, to get in touch — **R2**]
- [YOU RECALL: say *qiántái*, the front desk — **R3**, durable]

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-DE-SHIHOU-01, ZH-LEX-C206-FLOOR206-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A note is connected when each line leans on the one before. Four short lines can hang together; four long ones can fall apart.

[PAUSE 2s] Three things do the leaning, and you own all three. **The time words lead**: 早上, 回来的时候, 明天. **The subject is said once**: 我 in the first line, and the lines after it start on the time or the verb. **Each line answers the question the last one raised**: gone, when, what now, how to reach you.

[PAUSE 2s] Your reader is the desk. Your purpose is to get the thing back.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-PROBLEM-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-DE-SHIHOU-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-YINGGAI-01, ZH-LEX-C285-LIANXI-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-POTENTIAL-01, ZH-LEX-C285-ZHAOHUI-01, ZH-LEX-C206-FLOOR206-05, ZH-LEX-C58-THINGS58-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the front desk. No name at the top: begin as you would at the desk.

- Line 1 says what is gone, with 不见了.
- Line 2 says when you last had it, with 的时候.
- Line 3 says what you need, with 请, or what you think you should do, with 我应该.
- Line 4 says how to reach you: *liánxì*, and a time or a room number.

[PAUSE 2s] A word whose characters you have not written goes in pinyin, inside the line, with its tone marks: *liánxì*, *qiántái*, *hùzhào*. Every character comes from the ones you have learned, each in the order you learned it, each in the same size of square.

[PAUSE 2s] No Chinese commas or full stops: leave a space between clauses, as this book prints them.

[PAUSE 3s] Write the note. Then read it once as the desk would, top to bottom: could they act on it?

[PAUSE 2s] Read your second line on its own. Does it stand up without the first? (**It should not quite: it says when, and the first says what**.)

[PAUSE 2s] Count the lines that start with 我. If every one does, you wrote a list, not a note.
