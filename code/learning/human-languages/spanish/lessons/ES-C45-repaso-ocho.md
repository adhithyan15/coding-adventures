# Review — all eight

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new here. Before the grid: where does an object pronoun sit,
and has that ever changed?

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ME-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-TE-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-LO, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-LA, ES-GRAMMAR-NOS-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-OS-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-PLURAL, ES-LEX-NOS-OBJECT, ES-LEX-OS-OBJECT] -->

Never, in nine chapters. Here is the complete set:

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| **me** | **me** | **nos** |
| **you** | **te** | **os** *(Spain)* |
| **it / them** — masculine | **lo** | **los** |
| **it / them** — feminine | **la** | **las** |

Eight cells, built one at a time, and this is the first page on which they have
all appeared together. That is deliberate. A grid handed to you on the day you
met the first one would have been eight things to memorise; here it is a
picture of ground you already hold.

Count the genuinely new **shapes** you had to learn across those chapters: *me*
was already yours from chapter four, *te* was one, *nos* one, *os* one, *lo*
one, *la* one — and *los* and *las* were free, because you produced them
yourself from an *-s*.

## Grammar Lens: two questions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-LO, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-LA, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-PLURAL] -->

Reading the grid as eight decisions is the hard way. It is two:

1. **Who is on the receiving end** — me, you, or something else?
2. **How many, and if it is a thing, which gender?**

The second question only bites in the bottom two rows, because only there does
a noun decide. *Me*, *te*, *nos* and *os* are settled by the conversation
itself: you always know who is talking.

So the difficulty is not eight-eighths of this table. It is one quarter of it.
