# ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ (hōgabēku anta āse ide) — wishes and hopes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C315-GURI-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C312-OBLIGATIVE-01, KA-LEX-C70-TELL-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a goal" (*guri*), "I must go" (*nānu hōgabēku*) and the word that closes a quotation (*anta*). Today all three go into one sentence.

## You'll want to know: -ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C315-WISH-BEKU-ANTA-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C113-CAN113-04, KA-LEX-C113-CAN113-05, KA-LEX-C07-ONDU-01, KA-ETYMON-C23-DINA-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C70-TELL-03, KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-02, KA-LEX-C111-LOC111-03, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-THERE-WAS-01] -->

> **ನನಗೆ ಒಂದು ದಿನ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ.**
>
> *nanage ondu dina maisūrige hōgabēku anta āse ide.* — "I would love to go to Mysuru one day."

Taken apart: "to me there is a wish (that says) 'I must go to Mysuru one day'". The wish is a little quotation: the **-ಬೇಕು** of "must", closed by **ಅಂತ**, then **ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ**, "there is a wish". In writing you will meet **ಬೇಕೆಂದು** in place of **ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ**: **ಎಂದು** is the written twin of **ಅಂತ**.

Swap **ಆಸೆ** for **ಕನಸು**, "a dream", and it becomes a dream: **… ಅಂತ ಕನಸು ಇದೆ**. For a wish you had then, **ಇತ್ತು**: **ನನಗೆ ವಿಮಾನದಲ್ಲಿ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇತ್ತು** (*nanage vimānadalli hōgabēku anta āse ittu*, "I used to long to go by plane").

## You'll want to know: what you want to become
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C315-BECOME-01]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C12-KUTUMBA-01, KA-LEX-C48-ROLE-03, KA-LEX-C48-ROLE-01] -->

> **ನನ್ನ ತಂಗಿಗೆ ವೈದ್ಯೆ ಆಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಕನಸು ಇದೆ.**
>
> *nanna taṅgige vaidye āgabēku anta kanasu ide.* — "My younger sister dreams of becoming a doctor."

**ತಂಗಿಗೆ** (*taṅgige*, "to my younger sister"); **ವೈದ್ಯೆ** (*vaidye*, "a woman doctor"), **ವೈದ್ಯ** with **-ಎ**. **ಆಗಬೇಕು** is **ಆಗು**, "to become", with **-ಬೇಕು**: "must become". The job you want to be needs no ending:

> **ನನಗೆ ಶಿಕ್ಷಕ ಆಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ.**
>
> *nanage śikṣaka āgabēku anta āse ide.* — "I would like to become a teacher."
