# ಒಂಬತ್ತು — nine, and the anusvara's third face

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C07-NUMBERS-6-10-03, KA-LEX-C07-ONDU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] In *ondu* the ಂ said *n*. In *eṇṭu* it said *ṇ*. Watch what it does
next.

## You'll want to know: ಒಂಬತ್ತು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C07-OMBATTU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ಒಂಬತ್ತು** (*ombattu*) — "**nine**." Tamil's *oṉpatu* is the same word
underneath.

Now the ಂ sits in front of **ಬ**, and a nasal made with the lips is what a *b*
wants next to it — so it says *m*. One written mark, three sounds so far, each
one decided by the letter after it and never by the mark itself.

That is the whole rule, and it holds everywhere in Kannada: read the ಂ forwards.
