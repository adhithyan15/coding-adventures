German, chapter 292: Die Rettung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
warnen — to warn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the paramedic, then the German for the emergency room.

You'll want to know: warnen.
warnen — "to warn".
Die Nachbarin warnte uns vor dem Hund. The neighbour warned us about the dog.
Vor etwas warnen — to warn about something. Die Warnung — the warning.

The word, taken apart.
warnen is inherited from Middle High German warnen "to watch over, to protect" (EtymDB), a cousin of English warn.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Rettung — the rescue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the emergency room, then the German for to warn.

You'll want to know: die Rettung.
die Rettung — "the rescue".
Die Rettung kam in letzter Minute. The rescue came at the last minute.
Plural: die Rettungen. In Austria, die Rettung is also the ambulance service.

The word, taken apart.
Rettung comes from Middle High German rettunge (EtymDB), built on retten "to save", with the ending -ung.

Lesson 3 of 5.
bergen — to bring out alive, to recover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to warn, then the German for the rescue.

You'll want to know: bergen.
bergen — "to bring out alive, to recover".
Die Feuerwehr barg zwei Menschen aus dem Auto. The fire brigade brought two people out of the car.
Strong verb: barg, hat geborgen. Geborgen also means 'safe and sheltered'.

The word, taken apart.
bergen is inherited from Old High German bergan, which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic berganą and a root meaning "to protect".

Lesson 4 of 5.
evakuieren — to evacuate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the rescue, then the German for to bring out alive.

You'll want to know: evakuieren.
evakuieren — "to evacuate".
Wegen eines Feuers wurde die Schule evakuiert. Because of a fire the school was evacuated.
Regular, without ge-: hat evakuiert. Stress: e-va-ku-IE-ren.

The word, taken apart.
evakuieren is a learned borrowing from Latin ēvacuāre "to empty out" (EtymDB), built on vacuus "empty" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
überleben — to survive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to bring out alive, then the German for to evacuate.

You'll want to know: überleben.
überleben — "to survive".
Alle im Bus überlebten den Unfall. Everyone on the bus survived the accident.
Not separable: er überlebt, er hat überlebt.

The word, taken apart.
überleben joins über "over, beyond" and leben "to live", from Proto-Germanic libjaną (EtymDB): to live beyond something.
