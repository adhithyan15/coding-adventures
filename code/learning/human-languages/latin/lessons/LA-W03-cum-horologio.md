# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04, LA-LEX-C48-DENY-04] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What this track's contract publishes.** The Latin A1 writing paper runs **30
minutes** and holds **three** parts, which is one more than most:

- dictation and orthography — 5 items, **8 to 15 words**;
- controlled sentences — 4 items, **20 to 35 words**;
- a short personal note — 3 items, **35 to 50 words**.

Those numbers come out of `task-shapes/a1.json`, not from this page.

[PAUSE 2s] Split the clock the way the parts are weighted: **six minutes**,
**ten minutes**, **fourteen minutes**. Start.

[PAUSE 3s] Stop when the time is up, even mid-word. An unfinished paper is
information; an untimed one is not.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C48-DENY-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule this track states more sharply than any other, and the
reason it does.

[PAUSE 2s] Latin is the language most people meet **only as reading**. Centuries
of teaching it that way have made "I know Latin" mean "I can construe a page",
and a four-skill contract has to refuse that or it is not measuring the same
thing at all.

[PAUSE 2s] So: each of the four papers is worth 25 points and **each must
independently reach 15**. The aggregate must also reach 60 out of 100.
Reading strength cannot hide absent writing. That is a project standard rather
than an external certificate — Latin has no awarding body issuing one — and it
is stated openly as this project's own choice.

[PAUSE 2s] Count your words against the three ranges. Being under the floor on
any one of them is the same as not having attempted it.
