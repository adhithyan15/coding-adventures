Portuguese, chapter 330: Preparar os legumes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
descascar — to peel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to conquer, then the Portuguese for the revolution.

You'll want to know: descascar.
descascar — "to peel".
A minha mãe descasca as batatas. My mother peels the potatoes.
Built on a casca, ‘the peel, the shell’.

The word, taken apart.
descascar is built from des- and casca "peel, shell, bark"; EtymDB derives casca from the verb cascar and goes no further, so the deeper origin is not settled.

You'll want to know: picar.
picar — "to chop finely, to mince; to sting".
O cozinheiro picou a cebola e o alho. The cook chopped the onion and garlic finely.
Also of insects: a abelha picou-me. Carne picada — mince.

The word, taken apart.
picar is inherited from Vulgar Latin piccāre "to prick, to puncture" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ralar — to grate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to peel, then the Portuguese for to chop finely.

You'll want to know: ralar.
ralar — "to grate".
Ela ralou o queijo para a massa. She grated the cheese for the pasta.
Queijo ralado — grated cheese. Informally, ralar-se is ‘to worry’.

The word, taken apart.
ralar is usually linked to Latin rādere "to scrape" (Lewis & Short), but the path is not settled; EtymDB has the word with no source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
desfiar — to shred (into strands); to fray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to chop finely, then the Portuguese for to grate.

You'll want to know: desfiar.
desfiar — "to shred (into strands); to fray".
O meu pai desfia o bacalhau à mão. My father shreds the salt cod by hand.
Also a fabric that frays: a camisola desfiou.

The word, taken apart.
desfiar is built from des- and fio "thread", which is inherited from Latin fīlum "a thread" (Lewis & Short): to pull apart thread by thread.

Lesson 5 of 5.
a tigela — the bowl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a tigela.
a tigela — "the bowl".
Ele pôs a fruta numa tigela. He put the fruit in a bowl.
Uma tigela de sopa — a bowl of soup.

The word, taken apart.
tigela is inherited from Vulgar Latin tēgella (EtymDB), a little tēgula "a roof tile": a curved piece of baked clay.
