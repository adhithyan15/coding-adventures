Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 177: Torn and Mended.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
फाड़णो (phāṛṇo) — to tear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a door chain, then the Marwadi for to hang up.

You'll want to know: फाड़णो.
फाड़णो — phāṛṇo — "to tear".
छोरो चिट्ठी फाड़ै है। chhoro chiṭṭhī phāṛai hai. The boy tears up the letter.
Past (I …): म्हैं चिट्ठी फाड़ी, mhaĩ chiṭṭhī phāṛī.

The word, taken apart.
फाड़णो (phāṛṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit sphāṭayati, "causes to split"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari phāḍaï, "to split" (CDIAL 13825).

Lesson 3 of 5.
टांकणो (ṭā̃kṇo) — to stitch on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to tear, then the Marwadi for a button.

You'll want to know: टांकणो.
टांकणो — ṭā̃kṇo — "to stitch on".
मां कमीज माथै बटण टांकै है। mā̃ kamīj māthai baṭaṇ ṭā̃kai hai. Mother stitches a button onto the shirt.
Past (I …): म्हैं बटण टांक्यो, mhaĩ baṭaṇ ṭā̃kyo.

The word, taken apart.
टांकणो (ṭā̃kṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit ṭaṅkati, "ties"; CDIAL lists Hindi ṭā̃knā, "to stitch, join" (CDIAL 5432). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

Lesson 4 of 5.
थेगळो (thegḷo) — a patch (of cloth).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a button, then the Marwadi for to stitch on.

You'll want to know: थेगळो.
थेगळो — thegḷo — "a patch (of cloth)".
मां थेगळो सीवै है। mā̃ thegḷo sīvai hai. Mother sews on a patch.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
थेगळो (thegḷo) comes from a reconstructed thigga, "a rag"; CDIAL lists Hindi theglī, "a patch of cloth" (CDIAL 6096). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पल्लो (pallo) — the end of a sari; the loose end of a head-cloth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to stitch on, then the Marwadi for a patch.

You'll want to know: पल्लो.
पल्लो — pallo — "the end of a sari; the loose end of a head-cloth".
म्हारो पल्लो गीलो है। mhāro pallo gīlo hai. The end of my head-cloth is wet.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पल्लो (pallo) is inherited from Sanskrit pallava, "a strip of cloth"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari palā, "the corners of a garment" (CDIAL 7970). It is a different word from pallava, "a sprout".
