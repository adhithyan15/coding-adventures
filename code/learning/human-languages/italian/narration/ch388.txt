Italian, chapter 388: Documenti in regola.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
scadere — to expire, to run out; to fall due.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to exclude, then the Italian for the condition.

You'll want to know: scadere.
scadere — "to expire, to run out; to fall due".
Il mio passaporto scade a marzo. My passport expires in March.
Conjugated like cadere, with essere: è scaduto. Of food, scaduto means past its date.

The word, taken apart.
scadere is built from s-, from Latin ex-, and cadere "to fall", inherited from Latin cadere (Lewis & Short): the date falls due.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: valido.
valido — "valid; worthwhile, sound".
Il biglietto è valido per una settimana. The ticket is valid for a week.
Of an idea or a person: un valido motivo — a sound reason.

The word, taken apart.
valido is a learned borrowing from Latin validus "strong, effective" (Lewis & Short), from valēre "to be strong, to be worth".

Lesson 3 of 5.
rinnovare — to renew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to expire, then the Italian for valid.

You'll want to know: rinnovare.
rinnovare — "to renew".
Se non rinnovi la carta, non puoi prendere libri in biblioteca. If you do not renew the card, you cannot borrow books from the library.
Rinnovare la casa — to do up the house.

The word, taken apart.
rinnovare comes from Latin renovāre "to renew, to make new again" (Lewis & Short), built from re- and novus "new".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la tessera — the card, the membership card, the pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to renew, then the Italian for the renewal.

You'll want to know: la tessera.
la tessera — "the card, the membership card, the pass".
Con la tessera del museo entri gratis. With the museum card you get in free.
Stress on the first syllable: tèssera. For buses, a monthly pass.

The word, taken apart.
tessera is a learned borrowing from Latin tessera "a square tablet, a token, a ticket" (Lewis & Short), which goes back to Greek tésseres "four", for its four corners.
