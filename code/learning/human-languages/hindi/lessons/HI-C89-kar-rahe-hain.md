# कर रहे हैं — both dials turned to plural

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have two dials: the middle word and the last. Say the pronoun for
*we*, and guess what a group does to each dial.

## You'll want to know: कर रहे हैं
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04]; assesses=[] -->

**हम कर रहे हैं।** — We are doing (it).
**आप क्या कर रहे हैं?** — What are you doing?

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03] -->

This time **both** words move:

| subject | middle | last |
|---|---|---|
| मैं | रहा | हूँ |
| वह | रहा | है |
| हम, वे, आप | रहे | हैं |

**रहे** is the plural of the same **-आ / -ई / -ए** set. **हैं** is the plural
copula. Nothing here is new — it is the two dials you already have, both turned
one notch.

At the **तुम** level the last word is **हो**: *तुम क्या कर रहे हो?*

## Grammar Lens: the question a listening paper asks
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-LEX-C85-IMP-03] -->

| level | the question |
|---|---|
| आप | आप क्या कर रहे हैं? |
| तुम | तुम क्या कर रहे हो? |

*What are you doing?* is one of the commonest openings in spoken Hindi, and the
whole difference between the two rows is the last word. That is the level
decision again, settling one more system.
