Latin, chapter 496: Fear and dread.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
paveō, pavēre — to quake with fear, be terrified.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for thunderstruck, then the Latin for to be struck dumb.

You'll want to know: paveō, pavēre.
paveō, pavēre — "to quake with fear, be terrified".
Ovēs lupum vident et pavent. The sheep catch sight of the wolf and quake with fear.
paveō, pavēre, pāvī. The noun is pavor — terror.

The word, taken apart.
paveō is a native Latin verb; EtymDB links it with a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to strike", but Lewis & Short calls its origin doubtful, so it is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
vereor, verērī — to respect; to fear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be struck dumb, then the Latin for to quake with fear.

You'll want to know: vereor, verērī.
vereor, verērī — "to respect; to fear".
Discipulī magistrum verentur. The pupils respect their teacher.
Deponent: vereor, verērī, veritus sum. Fear mixed with respect. Vereor nē… — I am afraid that…

The word, taken apart.
vereor is inherited through Proto-Italic from a Proto-Indo-European root wer- (EtymDB), and Lewis & Short compares Greek horao "to see" and English ward. English revere comes from revereor.

Lesson 3 of 6.
perterreō, perterrēre — to terrify thoroughly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to quake with fear, then the Latin for to respect.

You'll want to know: perterreō, perterrēre.
perterreō, perterrēre — "to terrify thoroughly".
Tonitrus puerōs perterret. The thunder terrifies the children.
perterreō, perterrēre, perterruī, perterritum: per- 'thoroughly' + terreō 'to frighten'.

The word, taken apart.
perterreō is built on per- "thoroughly" and terreō "to frighten" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English terrify and terror belong to the same family.

You'll want to know: terribilis, terribile.
terribilis, terribile — "frightful, terrible".
Leō vōcem terribilem habet. The lion has a terrible voice.

The word, taken apart.
terribilis is built on terreō "to frighten" with -ibilis (Lewis & Short); EtymDB traces terreō to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to tremble, to frighten". English terrible comes from it.
