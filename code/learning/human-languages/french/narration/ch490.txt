French, chapter 490: La famille au sens large.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le parrain — godfather; sponsor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for honeymoon, then the French for ceremony.

You'll want to know: le parrain.
le parrain — "godfather; sponsor".
Mon parrain m'offre toujours un livre pour mon anniversaire. My godfather always gives me a book for my birthday.
Also someone who sponsors a newcomer: le parrain d'un projet.

The word, taken apart.
le parrain is inherited, through Old French parain, from Vulgar Latin patrīnus (EtymDB), built on pater "father" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'aîné — eldest, older (sibling).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for godmother, then the French for godson.

You'll want to know: l'aîné.
l'aîné — "eldest, older (sibling)".
L'aîné de la famille a vingt ans. The eldest child in the family is twenty.
A woman is l'aînée. Mon frère aîné — my older brother.

The word, taken apart.
l'aîné comes from Old French ainsné (EtymDB), "born before": ains "before" goes back to Latin ante, and né to Latin nātus, from nāscī "to be born" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le cadet — younger (sibling); youngest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for godson, then the French for eldest.

You'll want to know: le cadet.
le cadet — "younger (sibling); youngest".
Mon frère cadet a trois ans de moins que moi. My younger brother is three years younger than me.
A woman is la cadette. C'est le cadet de mes soucis — that is the least of my worries.

The word, taken apart.
le cadet was borrowed from Gascon capdet "little chief", a title for younger sons serving as officers; it goes back to Latin capitellum, from caput "head" (Lewis & Short).
