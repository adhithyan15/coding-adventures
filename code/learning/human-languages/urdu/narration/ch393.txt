Urdu, chapter 393: Traffic Jams.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
جام (jām) — a traffic jam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to stroll, then the Urdu for to pant.

You'll want to know: جام.
جام — jām — "a traffic jam".
پل پر بڑا جام تھا۔ pul par baṛā jām thā. There was a huge traffic jam on the bridge.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
جام (jām) is borrowed from English jam, as in a traffic jam.

Lesson 4 of 6.
سرکنا (saraknā) — to edge forward; to slide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a horn (of a vehicle), then the Urdu for a conductor (on a bus).

You'll want to know: سرکنا.
سرکنا — saraknā — "to edge forward; to slide".
جام میں گاڑیاں آہستہ آہستہ سرکتی ہیں۔ jām meṅ gāṛiyāṅ āhistā āhistā saraktī haiṅ. In a jam the cars inch forward.
Past (it …): وہ بس آگے سرکی, voh bas āge sarkī.

The word, taken apart.
سرکنا (saraknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin saraknā under Sanskrit sarati, "runs, glides" (CDIAL 13250); سرکانا is the form for moving something else.

Lesson 5 of 6.
رکوانا (rukvānā) — to have (something) stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a conductor (on a bus), then the Urdu for to edge forward.

You'll want to know: رکوانا.
رکوانا — rukvānā — "to have (something) stopped".
کنڈکٹر نے اسٹاپ پر بس رکوائی۔ kanḍakṭar ne isṭāp par bas rukvāī. The conductor got the bus to stop at the stop.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے گاڑی رکوائی, us ne gāṛī rukvāī.

The word, taken apart.
رکوانا (rukvānā) is built on رکنا, "to stop", with the ending -vā- that means getting someone else to do it; CDIAL lists the partner verb, Hindi roknā, under rōkk, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 10827).

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.
