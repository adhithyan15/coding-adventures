# andata e ritorno — the going and the coming back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C63-COSE3-05, IT-LEX-C86-VOLERE86-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a ticket". (*Un biglietto.*) Ask for a coffee the polite way.
(*Vorrei un caffè.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Put the two together and you are standing at a ticket window.

## You'll want to know: andata e ritorno
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-ANDATA-E-RITORNO-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C258-COSE258-05, IT-LEX-C244-PERCHE244-04, IT-LEX-ANDARE-02] -->

**andata e ritorno** — "there and back": a return ticket.
**solo andata** — "one way", the going only.

> *Un biglietto di andata e ritorno.* — A return ticket.
> *Solo andata.* — One way only.

**ritorno** you have, "the return". **andata** is new, and you can almost
hear how it was made: it is *andato*, "gone", turned into a feminine noun, "the
going". **solo** means "only", the same as *soltanto*.

## Grammar Lens: vorrei un biglietto per…
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-BIGLIETTO-PER-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C192-COSE192-01, IT-LEX-C82-CITTA2-03] -->

The whole request takes one frame:

> **Vorrei un biglietto per** Roma, **andata e ritorno**.
> — I'd like a return ticket to Rome.

**per** plus the place names where you are going, the same *per* as in *per
andare*. No article before a city: *per Roma*, *per Milano*.

Then the questions that come back at you, and the answers you need:

> — *Andata e ritorno?* — *Sì, andata e ritorno.* / *No, solo andata.*
> — *Che binario?* — *Binario tre.*

*Che binario?*, "which platform?", is the same *che* that asks *che cosa?*.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**andata** is built on **andare**, whose own origin is not settled: EtymDB 2.1
gives it no Latin parent, and the candidates usually proposed, such as Latin
*ambulāre*, "to walk", are disputed. **ritorno** is *ri-*, "back", plus
*tornare*, "to turn", inherited from Latin *tornāre*, "to turn on a lathe"
(Lewis & Short, under *torno*); EtymDB 2.1 derives the noun through French
*retour*. Either way, a return is a turning round.
