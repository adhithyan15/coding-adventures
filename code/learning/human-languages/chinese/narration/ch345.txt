Lesson 1 of 7.
jùshuō — it is said that; reportedly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a photo album, then the Mandarin for forever and ever.

You'll want to know: jùshuō.
jùshuō — "it is said that; reportedly".
jùshuō zhège chuánshuō yǒu liǎngqiān nián le — They say this legend is two thousand years old.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jùshuō is built from jù 'to rely on' and 说 'to say', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjoH and sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to lean on' and 'to cite'.

The word, taken apart.
cóngcǐ is built from 从 'from' and cǐ 'this', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjowng and tshjeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to follow' and the second with a word for 'to stop'; the sense 'this' is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 7.
wǎngyǒu — online friend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for from then on, then the Mandarin for the origin of a custom.

You'll want to know: wǎngyǒu.
wǎngyǒu — "online friend".
wǒ hé wǒ de wǎngyǒu jiànmiàn le — I met my online friend in person.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: wǎng is said with a rising tone before yǒu, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
wǎngyǒu is built from 网 'a net' and yǒu 'a friend', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjangX and hjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a net for fish or game'. The internet sense is modern.

Lesson 5 of 7.
yóubiān — postcode.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the origin of a custom, then the Mandarin for an online friend.

You'll want to know: yóubiān.
yóubiān — "postcode".
nǐ jiā de yóubiān shì duōshao — What's your postcode?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yóubiān is built from yóu 'the post' and biān 'to arrange', a short form of a longer name. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuw and pjien (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which calls the first a lodge on the border; the dictionary lists the second under pen and pjien, both glossed 'to put in order'.
