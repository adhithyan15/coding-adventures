Marathi, chapter 38: Where, and When.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
कुठे (kuṭhe) — where.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two chapters ago you learned to add one more thing with सुद्धा. Last chapter gave you कोण and किती.
[your turn — recall: say koṇ, then kitī, then sudhā in one breath]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: कुठे.
कुठे — kuṭhe — where.
तुम्ही कुठे राहता? — tumhī kuṭhe rāhtā? — where do you live? मी पुण्यात राहतो. — mī puṇyāt rāhto. — I live in Pune.
That exchange is the second question of almost every spoken interview, and until this lesson the book could write the answer and not the question. You already had पुण्यात — the city with -ात glued on its tail. What was missing was the word that asks for it.
कुठे (kuṭhe) is the क- family again, from Sanskrit कुत्र (kutra, "where"). Its answer is a place, the way कोण wants a person and किती wants a number.
When you do not know: माहीत नाही — māhīt nāhī — I don't know. That is a complete and polite answer to कुठे (kuṭhe).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kuṭhe — where]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tumhī kuṭhe rāhtā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: māhīt nāhī — the answer when you do not know]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Ask where somebody lives, then answer with Pune.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What shape of answer does कुठे (kuṭhe) want? (A place.) Which form field on your A1 profile answers it? (शहर.)

Lesson 2 of 4.
कधी (kadhī) — when.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already put two events in order with जेव्हा … तेव्हा … and with -ल्यावर. Both of them ANSWER a question you cannot yet ask.
[your turn — recall: say a jevhā … tevhā … sentence, then the same idea with -ल्यावर]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: कधी.
कधी — kadhī — when.
तुम्ही कधी येता? — tumhī kadhī yetā? — when are you coming? उद्या. — udyā. — tomorrow.
कधी (kadhī) is Sanskrit कदा (kadā, "when"), and it is the fourth क- word in four lessons. The family is now large enough to be a rule rather than a coincidence: काय, कोण, किती, कुठे (kuṭhe), कधी (kadhī).
Notice where it sits. A Marathi time word comes early, before the verb — you saw that with उद्या भेटू. कधी (kadhī) takes exactly that slot, because it is standing in for the answer.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tumhī kadhī yetā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: koṇ, kuṭhe, kadhī — person, place, time]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nako — the word that refuses an offer, so you can decline as well as answer]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Ask when somebody is coming, then answer with tomorrow.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where in the sentence does a Marathi time word go? (Early, before the verb.) So where does कधी (kadhī) go? (The same place.)

Lesson 3 of 4.
Where the asking word stands — where an asking word stands — Marathi leaves it in the answer's own slot and moves nothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have said तुमचं नाव काय आहे? and तुम्ही कसे आहात? for many chapters. Both are questions. Neither one told you the rule they obey.

Grammar Lens: nothing moves to the front.
English hauls its asking word to the front of the sentence: your name is Aruṇ becomes what is your name. Marathi does not move anything. The asking word simply stands in the slot where the answer would stand, and the verb stays last.
तुमचं नाव अरुण आहे. becomes तुमचं नाव काय आहे? तुम्ही पुण्यात राहता. becomes तुम्ही कुठे (kuṭhe) राहता?
Read those two pairs across. In each, exactly one word changed and nothing else moved. That is the whole rule, and it is worth having as a rule rather than as two memorised phrases: it means every asking word you own works in every sentence you own, without rearrangement.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: a statement, then the same sentence with कुठे (kuṭhe) in the answer's slot]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same trick with कधी (kadhī)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say kitī and put it where a number would have gone]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Turn 'tumhī puṇyāt rāhtā' into a question about place by changing one word.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does a Marathi asking word stand? (In the answer's own slot.) What moves to the front? (Nothing.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Two ways to ask, and how to tell them apart — five asking words in one slot — and the rising voice you no longer need.

Warm-up.
[pause 4 seconds]
Marathi now gives you two kinds of question. One ends in का and is answered हो or नाही. The other carries a क- word and is answered with information.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: koṇ, kitī, kuṭhe, kadhī — the four you now own, in order]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one का question, with the voice rising at the end]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one कुठे (kuṭhe) question, with the voice NOT rising — it does not have to]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say kāy, the oldest of the family, and the sentence you have used it in since chapter nine]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Ask three questions about one person: who, where, when.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which kind of question needs the rise, and which does not? (The का question needs it; a क- question already shows itself in a word.)
