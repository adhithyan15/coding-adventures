Italian, chapter 449: Lavori in corso.

Lesson 1 of 5.
il condominio — the block of flats; the joint ownership.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the drain cover, then the Italian for to clutter.

You'll want to know: il condominio.
il condominio — "the block of flats; the joint ownership".
Nel nostro condominio ci sono venti famiglie. There are twenty families in our block of flats.
La riunione di condominio — the residents' meeting.

The word, taken apart.
condominio is a learned borrowing from Modern Latin condominium 'joint ownership' (EtymDB), built on dominium 'ownership', from domus 'house'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'impalcatura — the scaffolding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to clutter, then the Italian for the block of flats.

You'll want to know: l'impalcatura.
l'impalcatura — "the scaffolding".
C'è un'impalcatura davanti alla chiesa. There is scaffolding in front of the church.
Il palco — the stage, the platform.

The word, taken apart.
impalcatura is built on palco 'platform', which is usually traced to Lombardic balko 'beam', a Germanic word that came into Italian as a borrowing.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la gru.
la gru — "the crane (the machine and the bird)".
Dalla finestra vedo una gru gialla. From the window I see a yellow crane.
Plural unchanged: le gru.

The word, taken apart.
gru is inherited from Latin grus 'crane' (EtymDB); the machine is named after the bird's long neck.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: crollare.
crollare — "to collapse".
Il vecchio ponte è crollato. The old bridge collapsed.
Also figurative: sono crollato dal sonno — I dropped off to sleep.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of crollare is not settled; it first meant to shake.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sgomberare — to clear (out), to evacuate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the crane, then the Italian for to collapse.

You'll want to know: sgomberare.
sgomberare — "to clear (out), to evacuate".
Hanno sgomberato la strada dopo la frana. They cleared the road after the landslide.
Also sgombrare.

The word, taken apart.
sgomberare is built from s- and the root of ingombrare 'to clutter': to take the clutter away. The origin of that root is not settled.
