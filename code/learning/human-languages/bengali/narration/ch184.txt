Bengali, chapter 184: To accept, To agree, To force, To obstruct, To take a risk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মেনে নেওয়া (mene neoyā) — to accept.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to wake up, then the Bengali for to bathe.

You'll want to know: মেনে নেওয়া.
মেনে নেওয়া — mene neoyā — "to accept".

The phrase, taken apart.
মেনে নেওয়া (mene neoyā) is mene, "having accepted", a form of mānā, "to consider, obey", from Sanskrit manyate, "thinks, agrees" (CDIAL 9857), + neoyā, "to take", from Sanskrit nayati, "leads, carries off" (CDIAL 6966).

Lesson 2 of 5.
রাজি হওয়া (rāji hôoyā) — to agree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to bathe, then the Bengali for to accept.

You'll want to know: রাজি হওয়া.
রাজি হওয়া — rāji hôoyā — "to agree".

The phrase, taken apart.
রাজি হওয়া (rāji hôoyā) is rāji, "willing", from Arabic rāḍī, "content", which reached Bengali through Persian; its Arabic root is r-ḍ-w (Lane). Hôoyā, "to be", comes from Sanskrit bhavati, "becomes, is" (CDIAL 9416).
