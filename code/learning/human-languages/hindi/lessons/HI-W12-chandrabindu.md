## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-CHANDRABINDU-01]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-SCHWA-DROP-01, HI-SCRIPT-II-MATRA-01, HI-SCRIPT-U-MATRA-01, HI-SCRIPT-AI-MATRA-01, HI-SCRIPT-AA-01, HI-SCRIPT-II-01] -->

> ँ

Not a vowel. The **chandrabindu** — *chandra* "moon", *bindu* "dot" — a crescent
with a dot inside it, sitting above the head-line.

It does not change **which** vowel you say. It says: **send that vowel through your
nose.**

English does this constantly and never writes it. Say *bad*, then *ban* — the vowel
in the second is nasalised before you reach the *n*. Hindi writes what English
leaves implicit, and here it is **meaning-bearing**: drop the mark and you have a
different word.

There is a plain dot without the crescent that does a related job, and which of the
two you write depends on what else is sitting above the letter. For now: crescent
plus dot means *through the nose*.
