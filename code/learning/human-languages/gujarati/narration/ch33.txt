Gujarati, chapter 33: Sun, Sky, and River.
8 lessons. You can do the first 6 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
સૂરજ (sūraj) — hear sun.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the Gujarati words for morning and for night. Now listen twice: sūraj. It means sun — the thing whose position decides whether it is savār, bapor, or sānj.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 7 of 8.
Nadī reaches the page — river, now written with signs taught long before this chapter.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Before anything is uncovered, say the Gujarati word for river.

Guided Practice.
Uncover નદી (nadī) once. Three pieces, none new: ન and દ from chapters 1 and 2, and the ી sign on the last. Notice that ન carries no vowel sign at all — that is the inherent a the very first lesson gave you for free. Name the pieces aloud in order, then read the whole word once. This is the only word of the chapter the hand is asked for; the other four stay in the ear for now, which is the gloss-first order rather than an omission.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Read this word aloud and give its meaning: નદી (nadī).

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Keep the model in view and copy નદી (nadī) once. Repair one shape if it needs it; do not start a row.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
With the model in view, copy river once.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Why does the first sign of nadī need no vowel mark? (Because a bare consonant already carries the inherent a.)
Source: Cambridge: river.


Lesson 8 of 8.
The sky returns at R1, and the face at R4 — retrieve the five outdoor words at R1 and the four face words at their fourth spacing window.

Warm-up.

Nothing new arrives here. Say the words for sun and for river before anything is read to you.

Listening runs sound to meaning; speaking runs meaning to sound, exactly as they did one lesson ago.

Guided Practice — the distant band, the eye, ear, mouth, and nose.
You look at the ākāsh with an āṅkh. These four were introduced around position 110 and have not been asked for since; at this position they are more than a hundred lessons old, which is the fourth spacing window.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
By ear only: give the English of āṅkh, kān, moḍhũ, and nāk; say which one you look at the sky with; say which one Sanskrit itself could not settle the origin of; and say which one changed gender on the way into Gujarati.

Writing — from sound, every model covered.
