## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C306-KIN-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la hija** --- "the daughter."

Same Latin root, feminine end: **filia** beside **filius**. Spanish did not need
a second word, only a second vowel. You have met this machinery already with
*contento* and *contenta* --- here it does the work of a whole separate noun.

English has no everyday cousin either, so it leans on the same borrowed
*filial*, which quietly covers sons and daughters at once.

There is one more place the root is hiding. A Spanish **hidalgo** --- the minor
nobleman --- is a worn-down *hijo de algo*, "son of something," meaning son of
somebody with property. The rank is a description of a parent.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

A pair: change the last vowel, change who you are talking about.
