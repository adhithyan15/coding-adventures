Sanskrit, chapter 336: Fables.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नीतिः (nītiḥ) — wise conduct; a moral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for bewilders, then the Sanskrit for attracts.

You'll want to know: नीतिः.
नीतिः — nītiḥ — "wise conduct; a moral".
पितामहः बालाय नीतिं कथयति। pitāmahaḥ bālāya nītiṃ kathayati. Grandfather tells the boy about wise conduct.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नीतिः (nītiḥ) comes from the root √nī, "to lead", as in nayati; MW gives nīti as guidance, management, and right or wise conduct, prudence (MW).

लोभयति — lobhayati — "tempts; lures".
शृगालः फलेन काकं लोभयति। śṛgālaḥ phalena kākaṃ lobhayati. The jackal tempts the crow with a fruit.
Past (he …): सः काकं लोभितवान्, saḥ kākaṃ lobhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
लोभयति (lobhayati) comes from the root √lubh, "to desire greatly", as in lobha, in its causative form: to make desire, tempt, lure (MW).
