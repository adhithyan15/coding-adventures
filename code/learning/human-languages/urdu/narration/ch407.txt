Urdu, chapter 407: Repairs and Complaints.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خراب (kharāb) — out of order; bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a canopy, then the Urdu for a night's shelter.

You'll want to know: خراب.
خراب — kharāb — "out of order; bad".
کمرے کا پنکھا خراب ہے۔ kamre kā pankhā kharāb hai. The fan in the room isn't working.

The word, taken apart.
is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic kharāb, "ruin, desolation"; in Urdu a broken fan, spoilt food and bad weather are all خراب.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ٹوٹنا (ṭūṭnā) — to get broken; to break.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a night's shelter, then the Urdu for out of order.

You'll want to know: ٹوٹنا.
ٹوٹنا — ṭūṭnā — "to get broken; to break".
کھڑکی کا شیشہ ٹوٹ گیا۔ khiṛkī kā shīshā ṭūṭ gayā. The window pane has broken.
Past (it …): وہ گلاس ٹوٹ گیا, voh glās ṭūṭ gayā.

The word, taken apart.
ٹوٹنا (ṭūṭnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin ṭūṭnā, "is broken", under Sanskrit truṭyati (CDIAL 6065); توڑنا, "to break", is the form for breaking something.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ہٹانا (haṭānā) — to move (something) aside; to remove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for noise, then the Urdu for a repair.

You'll want to know: ہٹانا.
ہٹانا — haṭānā — "to move (something) aside; to remove".
ہوٹل والے نے کمرے سے پرانا بستر ہٹایا۔ hoṭal vāle ne kamre se purānā bistar haṭāyā. The hotel man took the old bed out of the room.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے میز ہٹائی, us ne mez haṭāī.

The word, taken apart.
ہٹانا (haṭānā) is built on haṭnā, "to move out of the way", which CDIAL lists under haṭṭ, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 13943): this is the form for moving something else.
