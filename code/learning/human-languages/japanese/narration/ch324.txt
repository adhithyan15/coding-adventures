Japanese, chapter 324: Complaints.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
もんく (monku) — a complaint; a grumble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for no thank you, then the Japanese for not particularly.

You'll want to know: もんく.
もんく — monku — "a complaint; a grumble".
ねだんが たかいから、きゃくが もんくを 言いました。 — nedan ga takai kara, kyaku ga monku o iimashita — "The price was high, so the customer complained."

The word, taken apart.
もんく (monku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are mon, "sentence" (Middle Chinese mjun) and ku, "phrase" (kjuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ひはんする (hihan suru) — to criticize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a formal complaint, then the Japanese for discontent.

You'll want to know: ひはんする.
ひはんする — hihan suru — "to criticize".
ねだんが あがったので、みんな かいしゃを ひはんしました。 — nedan ga agatta node, minna kaisha o hihan shimashita — "Prices went up, so everyone criticized the company."
Polite ひはんします, hihanshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): しんぶんが ひはんしました。, shinbun ga hihan shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ひはんする (hihan suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, hihan, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: hi, "criticism" (Middle Chinese phej) and han, "judgment" (phanH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
いいわけ (iiwake) — an excuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for discontent, then the Japanese for to criticize.

You'll want to know: いいわけ.
いいわけ — iiwake — "an excuse".
おとうとは おくれたので、いいわけを しました。 — otōto wa okureta node, iiwake o shimashita — "My younger brother was late, so he made an excuse."

The word, taken apart.
いいわけ (iiwake) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from ii, "saying (iu)" and wake, "reason", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
