Portuguese, chapter 301: Fogo!.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o incêndio — the fire (a large, destructive one).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to damage, then the Portuguese for the harm.

You'll want to know: o incêndio.
o incêndio — "the fire (a large, destructive one)".
O incêndio destruiu três casas. The fire destroyed three houses.
O fogo is any fire; o incêndio is one that destroys. Incêndio florestal — forest fire.

The word, taken apart.
incêndio was borrowed from Latin incendium "a conflagration, a fire" (EtymDB), from incendere "to set on fire".

Lesson 3 of 5.
explodir — to explode, to blow up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fire, then the Portuguese for the explosion.

You'll want to know: explodir.
explodir — "to explode, to blow up".
Uma botija de gás explodiu no prédio. A gas bottle exploded in the building.
Mostly in the third person: explodiu, explodem.

The word, taken apart.
explodir is a learned borrowing from Latin explōdere "to drive off by clapping, to hiss off the stage" (Lewis & Short); the sense of a bang came later.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o extintor — the fire extinguisher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the explosion, then the Portuguese for to explode.

You'll want to know: o extintor.
o extintor — "the fire extinguisher".
O empregado apagou o fogo com o extintor. The employee put out the fire with the extinguisher.
Built on extinguir, ‘to put out’.

The word, taken apart.
extintor is a learned word from Latin exstinctor "one who puts out", from exstinguere "to put out, to quench" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a queimadura — the burn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to explode, then the Portuguese for the fire extinguisher.

You'll want to know: a queimadura.
a queimadura — "the burn".
Ele tem uma queimadura na mão. He has a burn on his hand.
Built on queimar. Queimadura solar — sunburn.

The word, taken apart.
queimadura is built on queimar "to burn", which is usually traced to Latin cremāre "to burn" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -dura.
