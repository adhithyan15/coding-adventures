Hindi, chapter 354: Citizens and Votes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
वोट (voṭ) — a vote.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a proposal, then the Hindi for to hold to be.

You'll want to know: वोट.
वोट — voṭ — "a vote".
मैंने पिछले चुनाव में वोट दिया। maiṁne pichle cunāv meṁ voṭ diyā. I voted in the last election.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
वोट (voṭ) is a loan from English vote.

Lesson 2 of 5.

नागरिक — nāgrik — "a citizen".
हर नागरिक को वोट देने का अधिकार है। har nāgrik ko voṭ dene kā adhikār hai. Every citizen has the right to vote.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
नागरिक (nāgrik) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit nāgarika, "a townsman; polite" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
जनमत (janmat) — public opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a vote, then the Hindi for a citizen.

You'll want to know: जनमत.
जनमत — janmat — "public opinion".
जनमत नए क़ानून के ख़िलाफ़ है। janmat nae qānūn ke khilāf hai. Public opinion is against the new law.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
जनमत (janmat) joins two Sanskrit words, jana, "people", and mata, "an opinion" (Monier-Williams): what the people think. The compound is a modern one.

Lesson 4 of 5.
लोकतंत्र (loktantra) — democracy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a citizen, then the Hindi for public opinion.

You'll want to know: लोकतंत्र.
लोकतंत्र — loktantra — "democracy".
लोकतंत्र में लोग अपने नेता चुनते हैं। loktantra meṁ log apne netā cunte haiṁ. In a democracy the people choose their leaders.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
लोकतंत्र (loktantra) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit lokatantra, "the course of the world" (Monier-Williams). Modern Hindi gives the compound of loka, "people", and tantra, "system", a new sense: rule by the people.

Lesson 5 of 5.
थोपना (thopnā) — to impose, to force (on someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for public opinion, then the Hindi for democracy.

You'll want to know: थोपना.
थोपना — thopnā — "to impose, to force (on someone)".
कोई तुम पर अपनी राय नहीं थोप सकता। koī tum par apnī rāy nahīṃ thop saktā. Nobody can force their opinion on you.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने अपनी राय थोपी, usne apnī rāy thopī.

The word, taken apart.
थोपना (thopnā) is inherited from stōpyate, "is obstructed", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 13723). CDIAL glosses the Hindi verb "to cover, to heap"; the sense of forcing a thing on someone is not among its glosses, and may have grown from heaping a thing on.
