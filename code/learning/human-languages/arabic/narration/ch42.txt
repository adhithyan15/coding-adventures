Arabic, chapter 42: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Encounter.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six old words, with the training wheels gone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have heard, said and written every word below. Nothing here is new.
[pause 2 seconds]
What is new is the empty space beneath each word. There is no romanized line to catch you. Let the Arabic carry its own sound.

Reading.
سلام شكرًا شاي نعم لا حسنًا.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read down once, right to left, without translating.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read again. The first and third words stretch an ا into a long sound, while شكرًا and حسنًا share the little ـًا ending.

The script, taken apart.
[pause 2 seconds]
Arabic letters change coat when they join, but they do not change identity. The ح in حسنًا is the same hook body you learned in خير; the word only asks you to recognize it at speed.
[pause 2 seconds]
And لا is still the lām-alif pair from chapter five. Reading is not a new script lesson. It is trusting the shapes you already paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words in order, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which written word means tea?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two words share the written ـًا ending? (شكرًا، حسنًا.)
[pause 2 seconds]
How many new Arabic words did you meet? (None.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines that already know each other.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words standing alone. Now the words join other words, in the same way that the letters inside them join one another.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have said every line. Read the Arabic first; let meaning arrive before any romanization does.

Reading.
السلام عليكم. وعليكم السلام. ما اسمك؟ اسمي سامي. شاي أو حليب؟ شاي.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read all six lines once without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read again. The question mark tells you where a voice asks; the full stop tells you where it answers. Arabic puts its question mark the other way round — ؟ — because the line itself runs the other way.

The sentence, taken apart.
[pause 2 seconds]
اسمي سامي has no written word for is. The zero copula from chapter two is not missing ink; it is how an Arabic present-tense sentence works.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is one gift of reading whole lines: the blank between ideas can carry grammar too. You already learned that blank by ear. Now you can see it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines in order, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which written line asks for the other person's name?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two lines ask questions? (ما اسمك؟ شاي أو حليب؟).
[pause 2 seconds]
Where is is in اسمي سامي? (Nowhere on the page — Arabic does not need it here.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first whole encounter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now read one encounter from its first peace to its last.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every form has appeared before. The only new thing is holding all of them together without a romanized line underneath.

Reading.
السلام عليكم. وعليكم السلام. ما اسمك؟ اسمي سامي. مرحبًا يا سامي. شاي أو حليب؟ حليب؟ لا. شاي؟ نعم. حسنًا. شكرًا. مع السلامة.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read the whole encounter once. Do not stop to translate a word; let each turn answer the turn before it.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read it again. This time notice the hinge: شاي أو حليب؟ One choice becomes two tiny questions, then a clear answer.

The passage, taken apart.
[pause 2 seconds]
A connected passage does not have to be one speaker's paragraph. Here the connection is the work each line does: greeting invites reply, a name invites welcome, and شاي أو حليب؟ opens a choice.
[pause 2 seconds]
لا closes the first option. نعم accepts the second. حسنًا closes the choice, and شكرًا acknowledges it. The passage is connected by meaning before it is connected by grammar.
[pause 2 seconds]
The first and last lines share س-ل-م. Arabic opens the encounter with peace and closes it with safety, so the oldest root in the book gives the whole reading a frame.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the whole encounter aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Which drink is accepted in the passage?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which line calls the learner by name? (مرحبًا يا سامي.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which drink is declined, and which is accepted? (حليب: لا. شاي: نعم.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which line closes the encounter? (مع السلامة.)
