# चाहना / चाहता (*chāhnā / chāhtā*) — to want / want

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-SAKNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-SAKNA-STEM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **मैं हिंदी बोल सकता हूँ**. You can now say what you are ABLE to
do. The next question an interviewer asks is what you WANT to do.

## You'll want to know: चाहना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-CHAHNA-01]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLNA-01, HI-LEX-COME, HI-GRAMMAR-SAKNA-STEM-01] -->

> **चाहना** — *chāhnā* — **to want**

> **मैं हिंदी बोलना चाहता हूँ.** — *maiṁ hindī bolnā chāhtā hūṁ.* —
> **I want to speak Hindi.**

Put it beside the sentence from last lesson and one letter is different, and it
is the important one:

- **मैं हिंदी बोल सकता हूँ** — bare stem **बोल**
- **मैं हिंदी बोलना चाहता हूँ** — full infinitive **बोलना**

**सकना** takes the stem; **चाहना** takes the whole dictionary form. Both then sit
last and take the ending themselves. Getting the two mixed up is the ordinary
beginner's error, and hearing them back to back is the cure.

**मैं आना चाहता हूँ** — *I want to come*. Say it against **मैं आ सकता हूँ** and
listen for the **-ना**.

Inside **चाहना** sits the noun **चाह**, "longing," and its cousin **चाहिए** is
how Hindi wants a THING rather than an action. They look alike and take opposite
kinds of object; this lesson teaches only the verb.
