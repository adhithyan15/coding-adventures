Lesson 1 of 6.
shǎngyuè — to enjoy the moon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to taste food with care, then the Mandarin for trade in a shop.

You'll want to know: shǎngyuè.
shǎngyuè — "to enjoy the moon".
wǎnshang wǒmen yìqǐ zài wàibian shǎngyuè — In the evening we sat outside together enjoying the moon.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shǎngyuè is built from shǎng and 月 'the moon', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syangX and ngjwot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to bestow'. The sense 'to enjoy' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
中秋 (Zhōngqiū) — the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for trade in a shop, then the Mandarin for to enjoy the moon.

You'll want to know: 中秋.
中秋 — Zhōngqiū — "the Mid-Autumn Festival".
中秋的时候我们都吃月饼 Zhōngqiū de shíhou wǒmen dōu chī yuèbǐng — At the moon festival we all eat mooncakes.
The new character, in pieces: 秋 (qiū) — nine strokes: on the left the grain piece of 和, five strokes; on the right 火. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
中秋 (Zhōngqiū) is built from 中 'the middle' and 秋 (qiū) 'autumn', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjuwng and tshjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, among other senses, 'half', and says of the second, quoting an older dictionary, that it is when the grain ripens.

Lesson 3 of 6.

和 火.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — hé, huǒ]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the grain piece, then 火.
秋 (qiū).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the grain piece, five strokes, as on the left of 和: a short stroke falling to the left at the top, a bar, a vertical down through it, a stroke falling to the left from the crossing, and a dot to the right.
The right piece, 火, four strokes, as you wrote it: a short stroke on the left, a short stroke on the right falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the left and a long stroke falling to the right.
5 + 4 = nine.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 秋 (qiū)? (Nine.) Where have you met the left piece? (On the left of 和.) What is the right piece? (火.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
期间 (qījiān) — period (during which); during.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to enjoy the moon, then the Mandarin for the Mid-Autumn Festival.

You'll want to know: 期间.
期间 — qījiān — "period (during which); during".
中秋期间他给家人买了月饼 Zhōngqiū qījiān tā gěi jiārén mǎi le yuèbǐng — Over the moon festival he bought mooncakes for his family.

The word, taken apart.
期间 (qījiān) is built from 期 'a set time' and 间 'between', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gi and kean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the second under an older form of the character and glosses it 'a gap'. Middle Chinese gi would regularly give qí; the level tone of qī is irregular.
