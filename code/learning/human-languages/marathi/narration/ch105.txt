Lesson 2 of 5.
रिकामा (rikāmā) — empty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for expensive, then the Marathi for cheap.

You'll want to know: रिकामा.
रिकामा — rikāmā — "empty".

The word, taken apart.
रिकामा (rikāmā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from rikṇakarman, "doing empty work", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 10728). It joins a reconstructed rikṇa- to karman, "work".

Lesson 4 of 5.
गरीब (garīb) — poor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for empty, then the Marathi for rich.

You'll want to know: गरीब.
गरीब — garīb — "poor".

The word, taken apart.
गरीब (garīb) is a loan from Arabic gharīb, "a stranger", which reached Marathi through Persian. Its Arabic root is gh-r-b (Lane). Persian and Indian use moved it from "a stranger" to "poor".

Lesson 5 of 5.
घाण (ghāṇ) — dirt; dirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for rich, then the Marathi for poor.

You'll want to know: घाण.
घाण — ghāṇ — "dirt; dirty".

The word, taken apart.
घाण (ghāṇ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghrāṇa, "smelling, smell" (CDIAL 4531). CDIAL glosses the Marathi word "stink".
