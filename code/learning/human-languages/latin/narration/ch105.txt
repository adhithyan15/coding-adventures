Latin, chapter 105: Rarely, Already, Then, Much, Little.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rārō — rarely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for now, then the Latin for soon.

You'll want to know: rārō.
rārō — "rarely". English rare.

The word, taken apart.
rārō is the adverb of rārus, "thin, scattered, rare" (Lewis & Short). Where rārus comes from is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tum — then, at that time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rarely, then the Latin for already.

You'll want to know: tum.
tum — "then, at that time".

The word, taken apart.
tum is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European tom, "that" (EtymDB 2.1), a form of the pointing stem that also gave tam, "so" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
multum — much, a lot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for already, then the Latin for then.

You'll want to know: multum.
multum — "much, a lot". English multitude.

The word, taken apart.
multum is the neuter of multus, "much, many" (Lewis & Short), used as an adverb. Where multus comes from is uncertain.
