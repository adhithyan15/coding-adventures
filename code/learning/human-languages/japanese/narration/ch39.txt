Lesson 1 of 5.
にわ (niwa) — a garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for chopsticks, then the Japanese for a kitchen.

You'll want to know: にわ.
にわ — niwa — "a garden".

The word, taken apart.
にわ (niwa) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おふろ (ofuro) — a bath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a kitchen, then the Japanese for a garden.

You'll want to know: おふろ.
おふろ — ofuro — "a bath".

The word, taken apart.
おふろ (ofuro) is the polite prefix o- with furo, "bath". JMdict writes furo with a character for "wind" and one used for its sound; where furo comes from is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
たたみ (tatami) — a tatami mat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a garden, then the Japanese for a bath.

You'll want to know: たたみ.
たたみ — tatami — "a tatami mat".

The word, taken apart.
たたみ (tatami) is native: the noun made from tatamu, "to fold, to pile up", by its stem; JMdict gives both under the same character. It is inherited rather than borrowed.
