Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 5: Mharo Naam: Give One Name.
10 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 10.
Hear mhāro — one small word for "my".

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Hear pāṇī, say water, say the Marwadi word back, then write its first sign प once. Set writing aside. The next chapter begins with the ear.

You'll want to know.
mhāro — my, before a masculine singular noun.
Listen twice: mhā-ro. Say it once. The breath after m belongs to the word; do not insert a full vowel between m and h. You do not need to spell it today.

Guided Practice.
Point to yourself when you hear mhāro. Point away when you hear pāṇī. Change the order and do it once more.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say mhāro once, then stop.
Sources: SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015) and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, November 2007.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
You hear mhāro with no text. What small English word does it supply before a masculine noun?


Lesson 2 of 10.
् (virāma) — stop the built-in vowel.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Write known स once, then read म as ma. Hear mhāro once. Today one tiny mark lets म close onto the next consonant without a between them.

Script.
् (virāma).
This is the virāma. Under म, it removes the built-in a: म् is m, not ma. Keep the model visible; no joined form is required yet.

Guided Practice.
Finger-trace ् (virāma) once beneath a printed म, then trace one pencil model. Say "vowel off" as your hand stops.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the mark and draw it once beneath म.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
What does ् (virāma) do under म?


Lesson 3 of 10.
ो (o-mātrā) — turn ra into ro.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Write known सा once and point to ी in पाणी. Write र once, then draw yesterday's vowel-off mark beneath a spare म. Today adds one sign to the right of र.

Script.
र + ो (o-mātrā) becomes रो.
Attached ो (o-mātrā) replaces the built-in a. Read रो as ro. It is one sign, even though it reaches above and to the right.

Guided Practice.
Keep रो visible and copy it once. Point to र, then ो (o-mātrā), and read ro.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model, wait three seconds, and write रो once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Add the visible o sign to र and read the result.


Lesson 4 of 10.
म्हारो (mhāro) — the heard word becomes readable.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Say the Marwadi word for my and recall what respectful सा does. From the heard cue pāṇī, write पाणी. Then write the vowel-off mark and रो from memory.

You'll want to know.
म् + हा + रो becomes म्हारो — mhāro — my.
Every piece is known. The virāma removes the vowel from म so म्ह begins mh, not maha.

Guided Practice.
Look for five seconds, cover म्हारो (mhāro), wait five seconds, then write it. Uncover and compare म्ह, ा, and रो separately. Repair only the piece that differs.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read your repaired word once without romanization.
Sources: SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015) and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, November 2007.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
After hiding the model, write mhāro and give its meaning.


Lesson 5 of 10.
Hear nām — name.

Warm-up.
[pause 25 seconds]
Hear राम-राम सा and answer it. Retrieve पाणी once by ear, voice, eye, and writing. Then hear mhāro, say my, and read म्हारो once.

You'll want to know.
nām — name.
Listen twice, then say it once. Marwadi nām, Sanskrit nāman, and English name are distant members of the same old word family. That history is a memory hook; today's task is only sound and meaning.

Guided Practice.
Touch your name on a card when you hear nām. Touch yourself when you hear mhāro. Change the order once.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say mhāro nām and give the two-word meaning: my name.
Sources: SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015) and Turner, A Comparative Dictionary of the Indo-Aryan Languages.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
You hear nām. What does it mean?


Lesson 6 of 10.
न (na) — a front-of-the-mouth n.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Write independent आ once. Say nām and its meaning. Read known ण with the tongue curled back. Today the tongue moves forward.

Script.
न (na).
Read न as na, with the tongue near the teeth. Do not replace it with retroflex ण from पाणी.

Guided Practice.
Keep न visible. Finger-trace it once, then trace one pencil model. Point to न when you hear na and ण when you hear retroflex ṇa.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model and write न (na) once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Write the sign that begins nām, name.


Lesson 7 of 10.
नाम (nām) — name, with no new sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Write known भ and the vowel-off mark ् (virāma) once each. Say nām and its meaning, then write न (na) once.

You'll want to know.
न + ा + म becomes नाम — nām — name.
Nothing new is hiding here: dental न (na), known long ा, and known म.

Guided Practice.
Look for five seconds, cover नाम (nām), wait five seconds, and write it once. Uncover, compare one piece at a time, and repair only a differing piece.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read म्हारो (mhāro) नाम (nām) and say my name.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
After hiding the model, write nām and give its meaning.


Lesson 8 of 10.
Hear hai — the small closing word.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Read and write आभार once. Hear mhāro and nām in either order, then give each meaning.

You'll want to know.
hai — is.
Listen twice, then say it once. This tiny copula closes the name statement. Do not spell it yet; one vowel sign is still missing from your hand.

Guided Practice.
Hear mhāro nām rām hai. Tap four beats: my | name | Ram | is. English changes the order in translation: My name is Ram.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say the four-beat line once from sound: mhāro nām rām hai.
Sources: Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat language report and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, November 2007.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
What job does heard hai do at the end of the name statement?


Lesson 9 of 10.
ै (ai-mātrā) — turn ha into hai.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Say आभार for formal thanks. Say the closing word hai, then write known ह once.

Script.
ह + ै (ai-mātrā) becomes है.
Attached ै (ai-mātrā) replaces the built-in a. Read the whole word है as hai. Do not confuse ै (ai-mātrā) with the taller ो (o-mātrā) in रो.

Guided Practice.
Keep है visible and copy it once. Point to ह, then ै (ai-mātrā), and read hai.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model, wait three seconds, and write है once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Add the new sign to ह and read the result.


Lesson 10 of 10.
म्हारो नाम राम है। (mhāro nām rām hai) — say one name.

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Hear mhāro, nām, rām, hai in mixed order. Give each meaning.

You'll want to know.
म्हारो | नाम | राम | है। mhāro nām rām hai — My name is Ram.
The written line adds no new sign. है is known ह plus ै (ai-mātrā). Replace राम with your chosen name when speaking; keep Ram for the first writing attempt because every sign is already yours.

Guided Practice.
hear the line and point to the person naming themself.
say the line once with rām.
read the four groups without romanization.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover every written model. Hear the line once and write it. Then uncover and repair one group, not the whole sentence.
Sources: Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat language report and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, November 2007.
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
From the heard cue mhāro nām rām hai alone, write the complete Marwadi statement.
