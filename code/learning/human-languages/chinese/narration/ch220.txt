Mandarin Chinese, chapter 220: Keeping Fit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiànshēn — to work out; to keep fit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for air temperature, then the Mandarin for to clear up.

You'll want to know: jiànshēn.
jiànshēn — "to work out; to keep fit".
wǒ měitiān xiàwǔ qù jiànshēn — I go and work out every afternoon.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiànshēn is built from jiàn 'strong' and shēn 'body', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjonH and syin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'sturdy' and the second 'oneself' and 'the body'.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: jiǎnféi.
jiǎnféi — "to lose weight; to diet".
wǒ jiějie zhèngzài jiǎnféi — My big sister is trying to lose weight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎnféi is built from jiǎn 'to reduce' and féi 'fat'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese bj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), explained after an older book as having much flesh; the dictionary glosses the first, keamX there, 'to lessen'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
tiàoshéng — to skip with a rope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lose weight, then the Mandarin for to sweat.

You'll want to know: tiàoshéng.
tiàoshéng — "to skip with a rope".
wǒ nǚ'ér měitiān tiàoshéng — My daughter skips every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiàoshéng is built from tiào 'to jump' and shéng 'rope'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to leap', whose regular outcome is tiáo, not the falling tiào; the dictionary glosses the second, zying there, 'straight' and 'a cord'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zuòcāo — to do a fitness routine; to do physical exercises.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sweat, then the Mandarin for to skip with a rope.

You'll want to know: zuòcāo.
zuòcāo — "to do a fitness routine; to do physical exercises".
wǒ bàba měitiān zǎoshang zuòcāo — My dad does his exercises every morning.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zuòcāo is built from 做 'to do' and cāo 'a drill'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese tshaw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to hold'; how it came to mean a drill is not settled. No dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.
