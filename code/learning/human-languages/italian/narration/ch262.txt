Italian, chapter 262: Writing --- Four Lines, And The Full Stop That Answers.
1 lesson. It needs your eyes or your hands, so save it for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
Four lines, and the full stop that answers — four lines to one reader, joined with e, ma and perché, and the full stop that tells because from why.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson asked for writing finished on time. This one asks for writing that holds together.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one person who will read it.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Connected writing is not longer writing. Four short lines can be connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a sentence stops standing on its own.
[pause 2 seconds]
You hold three joiners. e adds. ma sets against. perché gives the reason. After perché the words keep the order they would have on their own: perché non mi piace. Nothing moves.
[pause 2 seconds]
And perché is one word for two jobs: why and because. Spoken, the voice tells them apart. On paper only the end of the line does: a question mark asks, a full stop answers.
[pause 2 seconds]
Its accent leans the other way from the one on è: é rises as it goes right, è falls as it goes right. One line can hold both, so write each one on purpose.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four lines to the person you picked, on paper.
At least one must use e or ma. At least one must give a reason with perché, and end with a full stop.
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary; the whole exercise is the joining.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Cover your first line and read the second alone. Does it still make sense without the first? If it does, you wrote a list, which is where you were one lesson ago.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then find your perché line. A full stop at its end, not a question mark? An accent that rises? If either is wrong, you wrote a question, or a different mark, and the reader will meet it before they meet your reason.
