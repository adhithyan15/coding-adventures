# చేస్తున్నప్పుడు (cēstunnappuḍu) — the scene and the event

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-GRAMMAR-C316-PAST-SIMPLE-01, TE-LEX-C32-TINU-01, TE-LEX-C50-LEAVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I am eating now". (*nēnu ippuḍu tiṇṭunnānu*.) Then "I ate". (*tinnānu*.)

[PAUSE 1s] The first is going on. The second is done. A told story needs both, one inside the other.

## You'll want to know: -తున్నప్పుడు, while it was going on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C316-SCENE-EVENT-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C40-BHOJANAM-01, TE-LEX-C130-THINGS130-03, TE-LEX-C213-SAMAYAM213-01, TE-LEX-C149-ACT149-01] -->

> **నేను భోజనం చేస్తున్నప్పుడు ఫోను వచ్చింది.**
>
> *nēnu bhōjanaṁ cēstunnappuḍu phōnu vaccindi.* — "While I was having a meal, a call came."

Take **చేస్తున్నాను**, "I am doing", drop the person ending, and add **అప్పుడు**, "then, at that time":
**చేస్తున్న** + **అప్పుడు** → **చేస్తున్నప్పుడు**, "at the time (I) was doing".

That clause is the **scene**. The thing that cut into it is the **event**, in the plain past: **వచ్చింది**.
Telugu says a call "came", where English says the phone rang.

> **మేము నడుస్తున్నప్పుడు అకస్మాత్తుగా వర్షం పడింది.**
>
> *mēmu naḍustunnappuḍu akasmāttugā varṣaṁ paḍindi.* — "We were walking when suddenly it rained."

**వర్షం** (*varṣaṁ*, "rain") with **పడింది** (*paḍindi*, "fell"): rain "falls" in Telugu.

## Grammar Lens: 'was doing' on its own
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C316-PROGRESSIVE-PAST-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C46-ASK-05, TE-LEX-C33-CADUVU-01, TE-LEX-C18-GANTA-01, TE-LEX-C07-NUMBERS-6-10-01] -->

Telugu needs no separate tense for "was doing". The same form that says "I am reading" says "I was reading"
once a past time sets the scene:

> **నిన్న రాత్రి తొమ్మిది గంటలకు నేను పుస్తకం చదువుతున్నాను.**
>
> *ninna rātri tommidi gaṇṭalaku nēnu pustakaṁ caduvutunnānu.* — "At nine last night I was reading a book."

The past is carried by **నిన్న రాత్రి**, not by the verb. **ఉన్నాను**, the "am" inside
**చదువుతున్నాను**, works the same way:

> **నిన్న నేను ఇంట్లో ఉన్నాను.**
>
> *ninna nēnu iṇṭlō unnānu.* — "Yesterday I was at home."

**ఇంట్లో** (*iṇṭlō*, "at home") is the **ఇంటి-** of **ఇంటికి** with **-లో**, "in".
