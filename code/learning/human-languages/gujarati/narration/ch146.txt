Gujarati, chapter 146: Asking yes or no.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
શું …? (śũ …?) — the yes-or-no question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say શું, what, as in your name: તમારું નામ શું છે. Then the two oldest answers in the book, હા and ના. And one sentence from the last chapter: I don't speak Gujarati.

Grammar Lens: શું at the front.
શું તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો છો? — śũ tame gujarātī bolo cho? — Do you speak Gujarati?
Inside a sentence શું means what. At the very front it means nothing at all: it marks the sentence as a yes-or-no question, and not one other word moves. English rebuilds the sentence around do; Gujarati sets a flag at the front.
તમારું નામ શું છે — what is your name — inside;
શું તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો છો — do you speak Gujarati — in front.

You'll want to know: the voice can carry it.
In speech the flag often drops, and the voice does its work: તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો છો? with the pitch rising on the last word asks the same question. On the page the question mark carries the rise.

Grammar Lens: answering.
Gujarati answers with હા or ના, and then, usually, with the verb again:
હા, હું ગુજરાતી બોલું છું. — hā, hũ gujarātī bolũ chũ. ના, હું ગુજરાતી નથી બોલતો. — nā, hũ gujarātī nathī bolto.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: શું તમે અહીં રહો છો? — śũ tame ahī̃ raho cho?, do you live here?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: with હા and the verb again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same question with no શું, the voice rising at the end]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Ask someone, politely, whether they speak Gujarati, then answer no.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does શું mean at the very front? (Nothing; it marks a yes-or-no question.) What else moves? (Nothing.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
કે નહીં? and ખરું? — two tags at the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask do you speak Gujarati? twice: once with શું, once with your voice alone. Then say નહીં, the not for one word, and સાચું, true.

Grammar Lens: … કે નહીં?
તમે આવો છો કે નહીં? — tame āvo cho ke nahī̃? — Are you coming or not?
The કે you know as that also means or between two choices. The tag offers both answers and asks the listener to pick one. It is firmer than શું: a parent at the door, a friend whose bus is leaving.

You'll want to know: …, ખરું?
ખરું (kharũ) means true, real, a close neighbour of સાચું. As a tag it asks for agreement:
તમે અહીં રહો છો, ખરું? — tame ahī̃ raho cho, kharũ? — You live here, right?
The speaker already expects હા and checks it. So the three sound different: શું in front opens the question, કે નહીં at the end asks for a pick, and ખરું at the end asks for a yes.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: તમે ચા પીઓ છો કે નહીં? — tame cā pīo cho ke nahī̃?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તે ગુજરાતી બોલે છે, ખરું? — te gujarātī bole che, kharũ?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: આપણે ચા પીએ છીએ, ખરું? — āpṇe cā pīe chīe, kharũ?, we drink tea, right?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: each one, with હા or ના and the verb again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which tag already expects yes? (ખરું?) What does કે mean in કે નહીં? (Or.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
મને, તને, તેને — the pronouns things happen to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask are you coming or not?, then you live here, right? And from the verb chapters long ago: મને ગુજરાતી ગમે છે, I like Gujarati.

Grammar Lens: the person something happens to.
In મને ગુજરાતી ગમે છે the thing liked is the subject, and I stand to one side as મને, to me. Gujarati speaks of a fever the same way: it arrives to someone.
મને તાવ છે. — mane tāv che. — I have a fever.
The to forms for one person:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
who: I. pronoun: હું. to form: મને.
who: you (familiar). pronoun: તું. to form: તને.
who: he, she. pronoun: તે. to form: તેને.

Grammar Lens: more than one.
તમને has been in the book since the second day, inside તમને મળીને આનંદ થયો: to you, meeting, joy happened. Its partners:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
who: we. pronoun: અમે. to form: અમને.
who: you (respectful). pronoun: તમે. to form: તમને.
who: they. pronoun: તેઓ. to form: તેમને.
તેઓ turns into તેમ- before the ending: તેમને, to them.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: મને ચા ગમે છે — mane cā game che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: શું તમને તાવ છે? — śũ tamne tāv che?, do you have a fever?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તેમને ગુજરાતી ગમે છે — temne gujarātī game che, they like Gujarati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Who is the subject of મને ચા ગમે છે? (The tea; I am મને, to me.) What does તેઓ become before to? (તેમને.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
મારો ભાઈ, મારી બહેન — my agrees with what is owned.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I have a fever and they like Gujarati. Then the first possessive you ever learned: મારું નામ, my name.

Grammar Lens: the ending follows the thing.
મારું ends in -ું because નામ is a neuter noun. Change the noun and my changes its last sign to match it:
મારો ભાઈ — māro bhāī — my brother: masculine, -ો;
મારી બહેન — mārī bahen — my sister: feminine, -ી;
મારું ઘર — mārũ ghar — my home: neuter, -ું;
મારા પિતા — mārā pitā — my father: -ા, for more than one, or for one person spoken of with respect.
A woman and a man say મારો ભાઈ alike. The ending follows the brother, not whoever owns him.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: મારા પિતા અહીં છે — mārā pitā ahī̃ che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: મારી ચા — mārī cā, my tea, and why it is -ી (ચા is feminine)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say my brother, my sister, and my home.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Whose gender decides the ending of my? (The thing owned.) Why મારા પિતા? (Respect takes -ા.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
તમારો ફોન, તેની બહેન — every possessive bends the same way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say my brother, my sister and my home. Then the first question you ever asked: તમારું નામ શું છે?

Grammar Lens: one owner.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
owner: હું. my, your, his: the -ું form: મારું.
owner: તું. my, your, his: the -ું form: તારું.
owner: તે. my, your, his: the -ું form: તેનું.
તેનું નામ મીરા છે. — tenũ nām mīrā che. — Her name is Mira.

Grammar Lens: more than one owner.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
owner: અમે. the -ું form: અમારું.
owner: આપણે. the -ું form: આપણું.
owner: તમે. the -ું form: તમારું.
owner: તેઓ. the -ું form: તેમનું.
તેમનું comes from the same તેમ- as તેમને. Every one of them bends exactly as મારું does, to -ો, -ી, -ું or -ા:
તમારો ફોન — tamāro phon — your phone;
તેની બહેન — tenī bahen — his sister, or her sister;
આપણું ગામ — āpṇũ gām — our village, yours and mine;
તમારા પિતા, તેના પિતા — tamārā pitā, tenā pitā — your father, his or her father: respect takes -ા.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: તારું નામ શું છે? — tārũ nām śũ che?, to a child]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: amārũ ghar, then āpṇũ ghar, and who is left out of the first]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તેમનો ભાઈ — temno bhāī, their brother]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say your phone, politely, then her sister.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What decides the last sign of તેની બહેન? (બહેન is feminine.) Which our takes the listener in? (આપણું.)


Lesson 6 of 6.
Yes or no — a practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say her name is Mira. Then our village twice: once leaving the listener out, once taking the listener in.

The exchange: likes, family and a plan.
— શું તમને ચા ગમે છે? — હા, મને ચા ગમે છે. તમને? — ના, મને ચા નથી ગમતી. મને દૂધ ગમે છે. — તમારી બહેન અહીં રહે છે, ખરું? — હા. તેનું ઘર અહીં છે. — તમે આવો છો કે નહીં? — હા, આવું છું!
