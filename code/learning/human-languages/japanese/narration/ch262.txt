Japanese, chapter 262: An Accident by the Station.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じこ (jiko) — an accident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for serious, then the Japanese for selfish.

You'll want to know: じこ.
じこ — jiko — "an accident".
きのう えきの ちかくで じこが ありました。 — kinō eki no chikaku de jiko ga arimashita — "There was an accident near the station yesterday."

The word, taken apart.
じこ (jiko) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "matter" (Middle Chinese dzriH) and ko, "circumstances" (kuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
たすかる (tasukaru) — to be saved; to survive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for selfish, then the Japanese for an accident.

You'll want to know: たすかる.
たすかる — tasukaru — "to be saved; to survive".
くるまの じこで ちちは たすかりました。 — kuruma no jiko de chichi wa tasukarimashita — "My father came through the car crash alive."
Polite たすかります, tasukarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもは たすかりました。, kodomo wa tasukarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
たすかる (tasukaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the intransitive partner of tasukeru, "to help".
