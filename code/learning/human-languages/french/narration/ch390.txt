French, chapter 390: Les comptes de la maison.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le budget — the budget.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to testify, then the French for the victim.

You'll want to know: le budget.
le budget — "the budget".
Notre budget pour les vacances est de mille euros. Our holiday budget is a thousand euros.
The final t is silent.

The word, taken apart.
budget was borrowed from English budget (EtymDB), which came from Old French bougette "a little leather bag", a small form of bouge "bag". bouge goes back to Late Latin bulga "a leather bag", a Gaulish word (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le revenu — the income.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the victim, then the French for the budget.

You'll want to know: le revenu.
le revenu — "the income".
Leur revenu a baissé cette année. Their income went down this year.
Often plural: les revenus. From revenir: what comes back to you.

The word, taken apart.
revenu is the past participle of revenir "to come back" used as a noun: what comes back to you. revenir comes from Latin revenīre, built on venīre "to come" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'abonnement — the subscription; the season ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the budget, then the French for the income.

You'll want to know: l'abonnement.
l'abonnement — "the subscription; the season ticket".
J'ai un abonnement de bus pour l'année. I have a bus pass for the year.
S'abonner à — to subscribe to. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
abonnement is built on abonner "to subscribe" (EtymDB), usually traced to Old French bonne "a boundary, a limit"; first, fixing a set sum in advance.

You'll want to know: la dépense.
la dépense — "the expense, the spending".
Le loyer est notre plus grosse dépense. Rent is our biggest expense.
From dépenser.

The word, taken apart.
dépense comes through Middle French despense (EtymDB) from Late Latin dispēnsa, from dispendere "to weigh out, to pay out", built on pendere "to weigh" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la cotisation — the contribution, the membership fee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the subscription, then the French for the expense.

You'll want to know: la cotisation.
la cotisation — "the contribution, the membership fee".
La cotisation au club de tennis est de cent euros. The tennis club fee is a hundred euros.
On a payslip, les cotisations are social security contributions.

The word, taken apart.
cotisation is built on cotiser "to pay one's share" (EtymDB), from cote "a share", from Medieval Latin quota "how great a part", from quotus "how many" (Lewis & Short).
