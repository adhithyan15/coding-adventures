German, chapter 322: Die Zutaten.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Zutat — the ingredient.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to come into being, then the German for to return.

You'll want to know: die Zutat.
die Zutat — "the ingredient".
Für den Kuchen braucht man nur fünf Zutaten. For the cake you only need five ingredients.
Plural: die Zutaten.

The word, taken apart.
Zutat is built on zutun "to add", from tun "to do, to put", Old High German tuon (EtymDB): what is put in.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Esslöffel — the tablespoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the ingredient, then the German for the pinch.

You'll want to know: der Esslöffel.
der Esslöffel — "the tablespoon".
Man nimmt zwei Esslöffel Zucker. You take two tablespoons of sugar.
Plural: die Esslöffel. Short: EL. A teaspoon is ein Teelöffel.

The word, taken apart.
Esslöffel joins essen "to eat" and Löffel "spoon", from Proto-Germanic lapilaz "spoon" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
abmessen — to measure out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the pinch, then the German for the tablespoon.

You'll want to know: abmessen.
abmessen — "to measure out".
Sie maß das Mehl genau ab. She measured out the flour exactly.
Separable, strong: misst ab, maß ab, hat abgemessen.

The word, taken apart.
abmessen is built on messen "to measure", from Old High German mezzen, Proto-Germanic metaną (EtymDB), with ab.

Lesson 5 of 5.

die Schüssel — "the bowl".
Er gab die Eier in eine große Schüssel. He put the eggs into a big bowl.
Plural: die Schüsseln.

The word, taken apart.
Schüssel comes from Middle High German schüzzel (EtymDB), usually explained as an early loan from Latin scutella "a small flat dish, a tray" (EtymDB).
