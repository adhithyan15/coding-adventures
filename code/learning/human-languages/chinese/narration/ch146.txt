Mandarin Chinese, chapter 146: Day and Night.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
白天 (báitiān) — daytime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lie in, then the Mandarin for hurry.

You'll want to know: 白天.
白天 — báitiān — "daytime".
白天这儿很吵 báitiān zhèr hěn chǎo — It's noisy here in the daytime.

The word, taken apart.
白天 (báitiān) is built from 白 'white' and 天 'day, sky', both inherited from Middle Chinese, baek and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the white, light part of the day.

What you've built.
One more word for times of day.

Lesson 2 of 7.
yèli — at night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hurry, then the Mandarin for daytime.

You'll want to know: yèli.
yèli — "at night".
yèli hěn lěng — It's cold at night.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yèli is built from yè 'night' and 里 'inside', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yaeH and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'dusk' and 'a time to rest'.

Lesson 4 of 7.
xǐng — to wake up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for at night, then the Mandarin for the middle of the night.

You'll want to know: xǐng.
xǐng — "to wake up".
wǒ bànyè xǐng le yīnwèi wàibian hěn chǎo — I woke up in the middle of the night because it was noisy outside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xǐng is inherited from Middle Chinese sengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'drunkenness passing off', that is, sobering up. Waking from sleep is not given there, so how the word came to it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for times of day.

Lesson 5 of 7.
píngshí — usually; ordinarily.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the middle of the night, then the Mandarin for to wake up.

You'll want to know: píngshí.
píngshí — "usually; ordinarily".
wǒ píngshí qī diǎn qǐchuáng — I usually get up at seven.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píngshí is built from píng 'level, even' and 时 'time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaeng and dzyi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the level, ordinary times.
