Portuguese, chapter 260: First, Then, In the End --- A Story Told in Order.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
primeiro, depois, no fim — the events, in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "first", the ordinal from chapter twenty-seven. (Primeiro.) Then "afterwards". (Depois.)
[pause 1 second]
Now the same words go to work on a story. The scene is painted; the events need a running order.

Grammar Lens: three places in a story.
Primeiro comprei o bilhete. — First I bought the ticket. Depois esperei o comboio. — Then I waited for the train. No fim, voltei para casa. — In the end, I went back home.
Depois is the plain "then, after that". It stands between two events and points from the first to the second, and you can use it as many times as the story has events. A seguir, which you met as "next", does the same job.
Depois can also take a noun, with de: depois do jantar, "after dinner", where de and o weld into do. A seguir stands better on its own.
No fim is "in the end": the last event, and usually the outcome. You have finalmente too, "finally, at last", and it carries relief: something you were waiting for has come. No fim simply closes the story. Fazia frio e chovia, e no fim voltei para casa contente.
Notice what these words do not touch: the scene. Fazia frio needs no depois. Order belongs to the events.

The word, taken apart: after, and the end.
depois is built from Latin dē and a spoken Latin postius, a form of post, "after, behind" (EtymDB 2.1, which lists it as a compound of the two). English keeps post in postpone and postscript.
o fim, "the end", has no parent in EtymDB 2.1. It is usually traced to Latin fīnis, "boundary, end" (Lewis & Short), which English kept in final and finish, and which also gave finalmente.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: depois do jantar — then a seguir, on its own]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: No fim, voltei para casa.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a scene, then events — Fazia frio. Primeiro esperei. Depois, quando o comboio chegou, entrei.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of depois and a seguir takes a noun with de? (Depois — depois do jantar.) Which closes a story, and which says "at last"? (No fim closes it; finalmente is "at last".) Which Latin word is inside depois? (Post, "after".)


Lesson 2 of 4.
contar uma história — to tell a story, which is to count it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can hold a scene still, put events in order, and say what a reason led to with por isso. This lesson adds one small word, then puts it all together.

Grammar Lens: mas, the turn in a story.
mas means "but". It joins two facts that pull against each other:
Estava sol, mas fazia frio. — The sun was out, but it was cold. Esperei muito, mas o comboio não chegou. — I waited a long time, but the train did not come.
In a story it marks the turn. E adds; mas turns.

Grammar Lens: the backbone of a told story.
contar uma história is "to tell a story", with a contar you already have. Here is what it tells, with both pasts to work with:
Era sábado. Fazia calor e estava sol. Primeiro fui ao mercado. Depois encontrei um amigo, e falámos muito. Mas no fim começou a chover, por isso voltei para casa. It was Saturday. It was hot and the sun was out. First I went to the market. Then I met a friend, and we talked a lot. But in the end it started to rain, so I went back home.
Era, fazia, estava: the scene, held still. Fui, encontrei, falámos, começou, voltei: the events, put in order by primeiro, depois and no fim. Mas marks the turn; por isso says what the rain led to.

The word, taken apart: counting it out.
contar is inherited from Latin computāre, "to reckon up" (EtymDB 2.1), which is com-, "together", plus putāre, "to settle an account" (Lewis & Short). Portuguese kept one verb for both jobs: contar até dez, to count to ten, and contar uma história. English recount has the same picture.
mas is inherited from Latin magis, "more" (EtymDB 2.1), the same Latin word that gave mais. "More" became "rather", and "rather" became "but".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the scene — Era sábado. Fazia calor e estava sol.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the events — Primeiro fui ao mercado. Depois encontrei um amigo.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the turn and the end — Mas no fim começou a chover, por isso voltei para casa.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: contar até dez, then contar uma história]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
In your story, which past held the sunshine and which one took you to the market? (Estava — imperfeito; fui — perfeito.) Which word marked the turn? (Mas.) What are the two jobs of contar? (Counting and telling, both from Latin computāre.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
(reading) — a journey, told in both pasts.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter twenty-nine gave you forty-two words in a row, all in the present. This passage is twice as long, and it happened yesterday.
[pause 1 second]
Every word in it was taught before this page.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
Ontem era sábado. De manhã fazia frio e chovia. Eu estava na estação com o meu amigo. Havia muitas pessoas, mas o comboio não chegava. Primeiro esperámos no cais. Tínhamos fome, por isso depois tomámos um café e comemos pão. Quando o comboio chegou, entrámos. A viagem era longa, mas não era aborrecida: o meu amigo contava histórias, e eu ouvia. Lá fora ainda chovia, mas no comboio estava quente. No fim, à noite, chegámos a Lisboa. O céu estava limpo e havia estrelas. Eu estava contente, e o meu amigo também.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then read it a second time and sort every verb into one of two piles: the scene, or what happened.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
The events pile is short: esperámos, tomámos, comemos, chegou, entrámos, chegámos. Six things happened, and primeiro, depois and no fim put them in order. Comemos looks like the present, and it is spelled the same; the story around it tells you it is past.
[pause 2 seconds]
Everything else is scene: the cold, the rain, the crowd, the hunger, the stars. A story is mostly background.
[pause 2 seconds]
O comboio não chegava is scene: the not-arriving went on. Quando o comboio chegou is the event that ended it. And contava is scene too; contou would have made it one finished event.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
How many events in the perfeito are in the passage?
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What was the friend doing on the train?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that passage were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Why is contava in the imperfeito? (The telling went on while the train moved: it is scene.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word opened the line where a scene ended and an event began? (Quando.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — tell it back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "first", "then" and "in the end". (Primeiro, depois, no fim.)

What you've built.
You have read a story. Now you tell one, which is the same skill turned round: a reader sorts verbs into scene and event, and a teller has to choose them.
[pause 2 seconds]
The journey from the reading, in five cues. Answer each one in Portuguese, out loud, in a full sentence. Choose the past as you go: is this cue the scene, or something that happened? And keep one mas for the turn.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the weather that morning (De manhã fazia frio e chovia.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: where you were, and what the train was not doing (Eu estava na estação, mas o comboio não chegava.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the first thing you did, then the second (Primeiro esperámos no cais. Depois tomámos um café.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what your friend was doing on the train (O meu amigo estava a contar histórias.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: where you were in the end, and how you felt (No fim chegámos a Lisboa. Eu estava contente.)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[pause 3 seconds]
Now tell the whole journey once more, in your own order and your own words, without the cues.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of your five cues were scene? (The weather, where you were, the friend's telling, how you felt.) Which were events? (Waiting, the coffee, arriving.) Which Latin word is behind depois? (Post.) And what are you doing, word for word, when you contar uma história? (Counting it — from computāre.)
