# (reading) — six lines, three of them one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words. These are things you would say.

[PAUSE 1s] You have said every one of them.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-SKILL-READ-LINES]; assesses=[IT-PRAGMATIC-QUESTO-E-02] -->

> Ciao!
> Grazie!
> Arrivederci!
> Non so.
> Che buono!
> Questo è Marco.

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and notice the list gets longer as it goes, from one word to
three.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-PRAGMATIC-NON-SO-02, IT-SKILL-READ-LINES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Non so** is two words and a complete sentence. Italian puts the
person in the verb, so *so* is already *I know*, and *non* in front of it is
the whole negative.

[PAUSE 2s] **Arrivederci** is the longest line here and the easiest to
recognise, because length and difficulty are not the same thing on a page. You
meet that word as a shape long before you take it apart.

[PAUSE 2s] Six lines, and the shortest one is the one that needs a verb.
