French, chapter 520: Chez le libraire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le libraire — bookseller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for twist, then the French for gripping.

You'll want to know: le libraire.
le libraire — "bookseller".
Le libraire m'a conseillé un bon roman. The bookseller recommended a good novel to me.
A woman is la libraire. A library is une bibliothèque.

The word, taken apart.
le libraire is a learned borrowing from Latin librārius "a copyist, a bookseller" (EtymDB), from liber "book" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
le rayon — section (of a shop); shelf; ray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for gripping, then the French for bookseller.

You'll want to know: le rayon.
le rayon — "section (of a shop); shelf; ray".
Les livres pour enfants sont au rayon du fond. The children's books are in the section at the back.
Also a ray: un rayon de soleil. That is a different word with the same form.

The word, taken apart.
This rayon, a shelf or a section, comes from Old French ree "honeycomb", a word of Germanic origin. Rayon "a ray" is a different word, from Latin radius.

Lesson 3 of 5.
feuilleter — to leaf through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for bookseller, then the French for section.

You'll want to know: feuilleter.
feuilleter — "to leaf through".
Elle feuillette un magazine dans la salle d'attente. She leafs through a magazine in the waiting room.
Spelling: je feuillette, nous feuilletons.

The word, taken apart.
feuilleter is built on feuille "leaf, page" (EtymDB), inherited from Latin folia, the plural of folium "a leaf" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le livre de poche — paperback.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for section, then the French for to leaf through.

You'll want to know: le livre de poche.
le livre de poche — "paperback".
J'achète surtout des livres de poche: c'est moins cher. I mostly buy paperbacks: they are cheaper.
En poche — in paperback.

The word, taken apart.
le livre de poche is "a pocket book": livre is inherited from Latin liber "book" (Lewis & Short), and poche "pocket" is of Frankish origin.

Lesson 5 of 5.

dévorer — "to devour".
Ma fille dévore tous les romans de cette série. My daughter devours every novel in this series.
Of food or books: dévorer un livre — to read it eagerly.

The word, taken apart.
dévorer is a learned borrowing from Latin dēvorāre "to swallow down", built on vorāre "to swallow" (Lewis & Short).
