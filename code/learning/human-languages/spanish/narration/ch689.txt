Spanish, chapter 689: Agua en el paisaje.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la cascada — the waterfall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the bay, then the Spanish for to float.

You'll want to know: la cascada.
la cascada — "the waterfall".
Caminamos hasta la cascada. We walked to the waterfall.
A very big one is una catarata.

The word, taken apart.
cascada was borrowed from Italian cascata "a fall" (EtymDB), from cascare "to fall", which goes back to Latin cadere "to fall".

The word, taken apart.
arroyo is usually traced to a pre-Roman word recorded in Latin as arrugia "a mine gallery"; the origin is not settled.

Tener lagunas — to have gaps in your memory.

laguna is inherited from Latin lacūna "a ditch, a pool, a gap" (EtymDB), from lacus "lake".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el pantano — the marsh, the swamp; the reservoir (Spain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the stream, then the Spanish for the lagoon.

You'll want to know: el pantano.
el pantano — "the marsh, the swamp; the reservoir (Spain)".
Este verano el pantano tiene poca agua. This summer the reservoir has little water.
In Spain el pantano is often a reservoir behind a dam.

The word, taken apart.
pantano was borrowed from Italian pantano "swamp" (EtymDB); where the Italian word comes from is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el acantilado — the cliff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the lagoon, then the Spanish for the marsh.

You'll want to know: el acantilado.
el acantilado — "the cliff".
El faro está sobre un acantilado. The lighthouse stands on a cliff.
A cliff by the sea; inland, la pared de roca.

The word, taken apart.
acantilado is built on acantilar (EtymDB), from cantil "a shelf of rock", which is built on canto "edge"; canto is usually traced to Latin canthus "an iron tyre".
