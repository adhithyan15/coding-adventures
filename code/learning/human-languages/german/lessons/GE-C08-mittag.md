# der Mittag — "noon"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MITTE-01, GE-LEX-TAG-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have the middle and you have the day. Put them together before
reading on — you will be right.

## You'll want to know: der Mittag
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-MITTAG-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MITTE-01, GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **der Mittag** — "noon," and literally "**mid-day**."

*Mitt-* from *Mitte*, *-tag* from *Tag*. Nothing is hidden and nothing had to be
learned: this is the third compound in two chapters you could read on sight,
after *Montag* and *Sonnabend*.

English has the identical compound — **midday** — built from the identical two
inherited words. Neither language got it from the other; each still had the
pieces and used them.

To say it is noon: *Es ist Mittag.* No number and no *Uhr*, because noon is not
an hour you count to.
