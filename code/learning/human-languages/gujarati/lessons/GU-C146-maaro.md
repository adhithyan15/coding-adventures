# મારો ભાઈ, મારી બહેન — *my* agrees with what is owned

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-DATIVE-SG-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C146-DATIVE-PL-01, GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I have a fever* and *they like Gujarati*. Then the first
possessive you ever learned: **મારું નામ**, *my name*.

## Grammar Lens: the ending follows the thing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POSS-AGREE-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUN-01, GU-LEX-BHAI, GU-LEX-BAHEN, GU-LEX-GHAR-01, GU-LEX-C51-FAMILY1-01] -->

**મારું** ends in **-ું** because **નામ** is a neuter noun. Change the noun and
*my* changes its last sign to match it:

- **મારો ભાઈ** — *māro bhāī* — my brother: masculine, **-ો**;
- **મારી બહેન** — *mārī bahen* — my sister: feminine, **-ી**;
- **મારું ઘર** — *mārũ ghar* — my home: neuter, **-ું**;
- **મારા પિતા** — *mārā pitā* — my father: **-ા**, for more than one, or for one
  person spoken of with respect.

A woman and a man say **મારો ભાઈ** alike. The ending follows the brother, not
whoever owns him.
