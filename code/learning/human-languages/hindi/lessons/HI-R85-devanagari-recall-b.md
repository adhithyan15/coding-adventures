# (स ष ल ठ ण ऋ ो ौ औ ज़) — the thin eleven

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-121] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **स**, then **ल**. Two consonants you have been reading for a
long time without being asked for them by name.

## Script: five consonants, and the retroflex row they belong to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-121, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-143, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-147] -->

| letter | sound | what marks it |
|---|---|---|
| **स** | *sa* | the plain sibilant |
| **ल** | *la* | the plain lateral |
| **ष** | *ṣa* | **retroflex** — tongue curled back |
| **ठ** | *ṭha* | **retroflex**, with breath behind it |
| **ण** | *ṇa* | **retroflex** nasal |

Three of the five are retroflex, and that is the group worth seeing together:
Hindi distinguishes a whole row of consonants made with the tongue curled back
against the roof of the mouth from the dental row made against the teeth. The
difference is inaudible to most English speakers at first and load-bearing in
Hindi.

## Guided Practice: a vowel written like a letter, and a dot
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-125, HI-SCRIPT-WRITE-125, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-135, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-134, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-150] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU READ: **ऋ** — inherited from Sanskrit, a vowel that behaves like a consonant. Hindi says it *ri*]
- [YOU WRITE: **ऋ** once, from memory]
- [YOU READ: **औ** and **ौ** — the same vowel standing alone, then hanging off a consonant]
- [YOU READ: **ो** — the hanger for *o*]

**औ** against **ौ** is the pairing the whole system runs on: a vowel gets a
full-size letter when nothing precedes it and a hanging sign when a consonant
does.
