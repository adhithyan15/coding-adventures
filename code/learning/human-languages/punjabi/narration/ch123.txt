Punjabi, chapter 123: Garlic, Ginger, Coconut, Watermelon, Papaya.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਲਸਣ (lasaṇ) — garlic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for an aubergine, then the Punjabi for a cauliflower.

You'll want to know: ਲਸਣ.
ਲਸਣ — lasaṇ — "garlic".

The word, taken apart.
ਲਸਣ (lasaṇ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from laśuna, "garlic" (CDIAL 10990).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ਅਦਰਕ (adrak) — ginger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a cauliflower, then the Punjabi for garlic.

You'll want to know: ਅਦਰਕ.
ਅਦਰਕ — adrak — "ginger".

The word, taken apart.
ਅਦਰਕ (adrak) is a semi-tatsama: Sanskrit ārdraka, "fresh ginger", borrowed after the old sound changes had run their course and then lightly reshaped (CDIAL 1341). CDIAL lists the inherited Punjabi form as addā.

You'll want to know: ਨਾਰੀਅਲ.
ਨਾਰੀਅਲ — nārīal — "a coconut".

The word, taken apart.
ਨਾਰੀਅਲ (nārīal) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nārikela, "coconut palm and fruit" (CDIAL 7075). CDIAL, following Bloch, takes the Sanskrit word from a Dravidian compound.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਪਪੀਤਾ (papītā) — a papaya.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a coconut, then the Punjabi for a watermelon.

You'll want to know: ਪਪੀਤਾ.
ਪਪੀਤਾ — papītā — "a papaya".

The word, taken apart.
Where ਪਪੀਤਾ (papītā) comes from is uncertain. It is usually linked with papaya, a Caribbean word spread by Spanish and Portuguese sailors, and CDIAL has no entry for it.
