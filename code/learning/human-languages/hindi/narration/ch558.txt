Hindi, chapter 558: Longings and Passions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ख़्वाहिश (khvāhiś) — a wish, a longing (for something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for misfortune, then the Hindi for fear, dread.

You'll want to know: ख़्वाहिश.
ख़्वाहिश — khvāhiś — "a wish, a longing (for something)".
दादी ने मेरी हर ख़्वाहिश पूरी की। Dādī ne merī har khvāhiś pūrī kī. Grandmother granted my every wish.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ख़्वाहिश (khvāhiś) is usually traced to Persian khwāhish, "a wish".

Lesson 2 of 5.
दीवाना (dīvānā) — crazy (about); mad (for).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for fear, dread, then the Hindi for a wish, a longing (for something).

You'll want to know: दीवाना.
दीवाना — dīvānā — "crazy (about); mad (for)".
मीरा पुराने गानों की दीवानी है। Mīrā purāne gānoṁ kī dīvānī hai. Mira is crazy about old songs.
Agrees like an adjective: दीवाना (dīvānā), दीवानी, दीवाने.

The word, taken apart.
दीवाना (dīvānā) is usually traced to Persian dīvāna, "mad, possessed"; Hindi uses it for someone wild about a thing or a person.

Lesson 3 of 5.
रुचि (ruci) — an interest; a liking (for something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a wish, a longing (for something), then the Hindi for crazy (about).

You'll want to know: रुचि.
रुचि — ruci — "an interest; a liking (for something)".
मीरा की संगीत में गहरी रुचि है। Mīrā kī saṅgīt meṁ gahrī ruci hai. Mira has a deep interest in music.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
रुचि (ruci) is a tatsama, taken whole from Sanskrit ruci, "light; liking, taste, relish" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
जुनून (junūn) — a passion; an obsession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an interest, then the Hindi for an ambition.

You'll want to know: जुनून.
जुनून — junūn — "a passion; an obsession".
उसे क्रिकेट का जुनून है। Use krikeṭ kā junūn hai. He is crazy about cricket.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
जुनून (junūn) is usually traced to Arabic junūn, "madness, possession", through Persian. Hindi uses it for a consuming passion.
