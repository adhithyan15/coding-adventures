## You'll want to know: 旅客
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C403-TRAVELLER403-02]; assesses=[] -->

**旅客** — *lǚkè* — "traveller; passenger on a long journey".

> **旅客都上车了** *lǚkè dōu shàngchē le* — The passengers have all got on.

The new character, in pieces: 旅 — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left a dot, a bar, a stroke that runs right and turns down with a hook, and a long stroke falling to the left; on the right a short stroke falling to the left and a bar, then a short stroke falling to the left, a vertical that turns up at the foot, a short stroke falling to the left and a long stroke falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**旅客** (*lǚkè*) is built from 旅 and 客 'a guest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ljoX* and *khaek* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as a body of troops, five hundred men after an older book; the sense 'to travel' is not given there, so how it came is not settled.
