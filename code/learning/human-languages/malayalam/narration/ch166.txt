Malayalam, chapter 166: Heavy, Fast, Thick, Soft, Hard.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ഭാരമുള്ള (bhāramuḷḷa) — heavy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for spicy, then the Malayalam for sour.

You'll want to know: ഭാരമുള്ള.
ഭാരമുള്ള — bhāramuḷḷa — "heavy".

The word, taken apart.
ഭാരമുള്ള (bhāramuḷḷa) joins Sanskrit bhāra, "burden, weight" (CDIAL 9459), to native -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697).

Lesson 3 of 5.
കട്ടിയുള്ള (kaṭṭiyuḷḷa) — thick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for heavy, then the Malayalam for fast.

You'll want to know: കട്ടിയുള്ള.
കട്ടിയുള്ള — kaṭṭiyuḷḷa — "thick".

The word, taken apart.
കട്ടിയുള്ള (kaṭṭiyuḷḷa) is native Dravidian, "having solidity": kaṭṭi, "what is condensed, solid" (DEDR 1148, with Tamil kaṭṭu), with -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697). DEDR 1148 compares Sanskrit kaṭhina, "hard" (CDIAL 2650).

Lesson 5 of 5.
കഠിനമായ (kaṭhinamāya) — hard, difficult.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for thick, then the Malayalam for soft.
[pause 1 second]
Say the written "because." (Entukoṇṭennāl.)

You'll want to know: കഠിനമായ.
കഠിനമായ — kaṭhinamāya — "hard, difficult".

The word, taken apart.
കഠിനമായ (kaṭhinamāya) joins Sanskrit kaṭhina, "hard, stiff" (CDIAL 2650), to native āya, "having become" (DEDR 333). CDIAL notes that the Sanskrit word itself may come from Dravidian. DEDR 333 compares Sanskrit ām.
