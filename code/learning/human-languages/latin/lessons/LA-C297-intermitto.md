## You'll want to know: intermittō, intermittere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C297-INCIPIO-05]; assesses=[] -->

**intermittō, intermittere** — "to break off, to interrupt".

> Propter pluviam labōrem intermīsimus.
>
> *Because of the rain we broke off the work.*

*intermittō, intermittere, intermīsī, intermissum*: to put a gap into something. Caesar uses it of a battle or a march that is broken off.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**intermittō** is built on *mittō*, "to send, to let go", with *inter*, "between" (Lewis & Short): to leave a gap. English *intermission* and *intermittent* come from it.
