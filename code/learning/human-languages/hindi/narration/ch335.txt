Lesson 1 of 6.
राक्षस (rākṣas) — a demon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a riddle, then the Hindi for a saying.

You'll want to know: राक्षस.
राक्षस — rākṣas — "a demon".
एक राक्षस जंगल में रहता था। ek rākṣas jaṅgal meṁ rahtā thā. A demon lived in the forest.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
राक्षस (rākṣas) is Sanskrit rākṣasa, "a demon" (Monier-Williams): the giant, man-eating enemy of the old tales.

Lesson 3 of 6.
वरदान (vardān) — a boon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a demon, then the Hindi for a god.

You'll want to know: वरदान.
वरदान — vardān — "a boon".
देवता ने राक्षस को एक वरदान दिया। devtā ne rākṣas ko ek vardān diyā. The god granted the demon a boon.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
वरदान (vardān) is Sanskrit varadāna (Monier-Williams), vara, a wish or a choice, and dāna, a giving: a wish granted by a god.

Lesson 4 of 6.
अमर (amar) — immortal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a god, then the Hindi for a boon.

You'll want to know: अमर.
अमर — amar — "immortal".
वरदान से राक्षस अमर हो गया। vardān se rākṣas amar ho gayā. The boon made the demon immortal.

The word, taken apart.
अमर (amar) is Sanskrit amara, "undying" (Monier-Williams), a-, not, and the family of mṛ, to die, the root of मरना.

Lesson 5 of 6.
निगलना (nigalnā) — to swallow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a boon, then the Hindi for immortal.

You'll want to know: निगलना.
निगलना — nigalnā — "to swallow".
राक्षस ने पूरी नदी निगल ली। rākṣas ne pūrī nadī nigal lī. The demon swallowed the whole river.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने फल निगला, usne phal niglā.

The word, taken apart.
निगलना (nigalnā) goes back to Sanskrit nigalati, "swallows" (CDIAL 7163). It is swallowing whole, without chewing: निगल लिया.
