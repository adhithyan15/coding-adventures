Urdu, chapter 535: Omens and Stars.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شگون (shagun) — an omen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for checkmate, then the Urdu for ludo.

You'll want to know: شگون.
شگون — shagun — "an omen".
اگر صبح بارش ہوئی تو یہ اچھا شگون ہو گا۔ agar subah bārish huī to yih achchhā shagun ho gā. If it rains in the morning, that will be a good omen.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
شگون (shagun) is inherited from Sanskrit śakuna, "a bird; an omen", under which CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin saun, "a good omen" (CDIAL 12245); the fuller form shagun is not among its Hindi rows.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

اگر ٹونا سچ ہوتا تو سب امیر ہوتے۔ agar ṭonā sach hotā to sab amīr hote. If spells were real, everyone would be rich.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ٹونا (ṭonā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin ṭonā, "sorcery", under ṭōna, a form it reconstructs and perhaps links with śakuna, "a bird of omen" (CDIAL 5480).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for ill-omened, then the Urdu for a magic spell.

You'll want to know: نجومی.
نجومی — najūmī — "an astrologer".
اگر نجومی سب جانتا تو وہ خود امیر ہوتا۔ agar najūmī sab jāntā to voh khud amīr hotā. If the astrologer knew everything, he would be rich himself.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
نجومی (najūmī) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic nujūm, "stars", the plural of najm, with the ending -ī, "one who".
