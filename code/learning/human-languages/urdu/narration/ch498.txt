Urdu, chapter 498: Clay and the Kiln.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
چاک (chāk) — a potter's wheel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to knock in, then the Urdu for to file (metal or wood).

You'll want to know: چاک.
چاک — chāk — "a potter's wheel".
کمہار چاک پر مٹی کا برتن بناتا ہے۔ kumhār chāk par miṭṭī kā bartan banātā hai. The potter makes a clay pot on the wheel.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
چاک (chāk) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin cāk, "any kind of wheel", under Sanskrit cakra, "a wheel" (CDIAL 4538).

Lesson 5 of 5.
تپانا (tapānā) — to heat; to fire (pots).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a potter's kiln, then the Urdu for a large clay pot.

You'll want to know: تپانا.
تپانا — tapānā — "to heat; to fire (pots)".
کمہار برتنوں کو آگ میں تپاتا ہے۔ kumhār bartanoṅ ko āg meṅ tapātā hai. The potter fires his pots in the flames.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے برتن آگ میں تپائے, us ne bartan āg meṅ tapāe.

The word, taken apart.
تپانا (tapānā) is the causative of tapnā, "to be hot", which goes back to Sanskrit tapyati, "is hot" (CDIAL 5684).
