Portuguese, chapter 466: Depois da chuva.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
chuviscar — to drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hurricane, then the Portuguese for the blizzard.

You'll want to know: chuviscar.
chuviscar — "to drizzle".
Está a chuviscar, mas não preciso de guarda-chuva. It is drizzling, but I do not need an umbrella.
O chuvisco — drizzle.

The word, taken apart.
chuviscar is built on chuva 'rain', which is inherited from Latin pluvia (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

desanuviar is built from des- and anuviar 'to cloud over', on nuvem 'cloud', which goes back to Latin nūbēs (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: encharcado.
encharcado — "soaked, drenched".
Cheguei a casa encharcado. I got home soaked.
Built on o charco, a pool of water.

The word, taken apart.
encharcado is built from en- and charco 'pool, puddle', which was borrowed from Spanish charco (EtymDB); the Spanish word's origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 7.
a gabardina — the raincoat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the puddle, then the Portuguese for soaked.

You'll want to know: a gabardina.
a gabardina — "the raincoat".
Saí de gabardina porque estava a chover. I went out in my raincoat because it was raining.
In Brazil: a capa de chuva.

The word, taken apart.
gabardina is usually traced to Spanish gabardina 'long cloak'; its earlier origin is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry for it.
