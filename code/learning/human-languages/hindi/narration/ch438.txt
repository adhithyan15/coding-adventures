Hindi, chapter 438: Winter Cold.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जाड़ा (jāṛā) — winter; the cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to spread over, then the Hindi for to sweat.

You'll want to know: जाड़ा.
जाड़ा — jāṛā — "winter; the cold".
जाड़े में यहाँ बहुत ठंड पड़ती है। jāṛe meṁ yahā̃ bahut ṭhaṇḍ paṛtī hai. It gets very cold here in winter.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
जाड़ा (jāṛā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit jaḍa, "dull, numb" and also "cold" (CDIAL 5090), which lists Hindi jaṛā, "cold, frost, winter".

Lesson 2 of 5.
ओला (olā) — a hailstone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to sweat, then the Hindi for winter.

You'll want to know: ओला.
ओला — olā — "a hailstone".
कल रात ओले गिरे। kal rāt ole gire. It hailed last night.
Masculine.
Usually plural: ओले गिरे, ओले पड़े, it hailed.

The word, taken apart.
ओला (olā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit upala, "a stone, a rock" (CDIAL 2223), which lists Hindi olā, "a hailstone": a stone that falls from the sky.

Lesson 3 of 5.
शीत (śīt) — cold; the cold (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for winter, then the Hindi for a hailstone.

You'll want to know: शीत.
शीत — śīt — "cold; the cold (formal)".
शीत लहर की वजह से स्कूल बंद हैं। śīt lahar kī vajah se skūl band haiṁ. The schools are closed because of the cold wave.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
शीत (śīt) is a tatsama, Sanskrit śīta, "cold, chilly", and as a noun "cold weather" (Monier-Williams); the weather news speaks of शीत लहर, a cold wave.

Lesson 4 of 5.
हिमपात (himpāt) — snowfall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a hailstone, then the Hindi for cold (formal).

You'll want to know: हिमपात.
हिमपात — himpāt — "snowfall".
पहाड़ों में कल रात भारी हिमपात हुआ। pahāṛoṁ meṁ kal rāt bhārī himpāt huā. There was heavy snowfall in the hills last night.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
हिमपात (himpāt) is a tatsama, Sanskrit himapāta, "a fall of snow" (Monier-Williams), from hima, "snow", as in हिमालय, and pāta, "a fall".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ओढ़ना (oṛhnā) — to wrap oneself in (a shawl or a blanket).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for cold (formal), then the Hindi for snowfall.

You'll want to know: ओढ़ना.
ओढ़ना — oṛhnā — "to wrap oneself in (a shawl or a blanket)".
दादी ने कंबल ओढ़ा। dādī ne kambal oṛhā. Grandmother wrapped herself in a blanket.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने चादर ओढ़ी, usne cādar oṛhī.

The word, taken apart.
ओढ़ना (oṛhnā) goes back to ōḍḍh, "put on, wear, cover", a form CDIAL reconstructs and thinks may come from Dravidian (CDIAL 2547); it lists Hindi oṛhnā, "to cover".
