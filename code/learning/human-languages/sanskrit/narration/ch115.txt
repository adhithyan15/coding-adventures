Lesson 1 of 5.
मक्षिका (makṣikā) — a fly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a heron, then the Sanskrit for a bee.

You'll want to know: मक्षिका.
मक्षिका — makṣikā — "a fly".

The word, taken apart.
makṣikā "a fly, a bee" is built on the Rigvedic makṣ "fly" with the suffix -ikā (MW). It lives on as Hindi makkhī "fly" (CDIAL 9696).

The word, taken apart.
pipīlikā "the small red ant" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and is built on pipīla "ant". CDIAL 8201, after EWA, takes the word to be of unknown non-Aryan origin. It lives on as Hindi pipṛī "small red ant" (CDIAL 8201).

Lesson 4 of 5.
चित्रपतंगः (citrapataṅgaḥ) — a butterfly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a mosquito, then the Sanskrit for an ant.

You'll want to know: चित्रपतंगः.
चित्रपतंगः — citrapataṅgaḥ — "a butterfly".

The word, taken apart.
citrapataṅga is built from citra "bright, many-coloured" and pataṅga "a flying insect, a butterfly or moth", from the root √pat "fly" (MW): "the many-coloured flier". MW does not list the pair, so it is a later coinage.

Lesson 5 of 5.
कुक्कुटः (kukkuṭaḥ) — a rooster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an ant, then the Sanskrit for a butterfly.

You'll want to know: कुक्कुटः.
कुक्कुटः — kukkuṭaḥ — "a rooster".

The word, taken apart.
kukkuṭa "a cock" is imitative: MW records kukkuṭ as a word that copies the cock's cry, and CDIAL 3208 calls it onomatopoeic. It lives on as Punjabi kukkaṛ "cock" (CDIAL 3208).
