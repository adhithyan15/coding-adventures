## You'll want to know: yōngjǐ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C406-BUSY406-01]; assesses=[] -->

*yōngjǐ* — "crowded".

> *dìtiě tài yōngjǐ le wǒmen zuò chūzūchē qù* — The metro's too crowded: we'll take a taxi.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*yōngjǐ* is built from *yōng* 'to hold close' and *jǐ* 'to squeeze', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *'jowngX* and *tsej* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hold in the arms' and the second 'to push aside'. Those forms would regularly give yǒng and jī: the tones of yōngjǐ are irregular.
