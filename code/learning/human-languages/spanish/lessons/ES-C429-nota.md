# la nota — a mark, in the oldest sense of the word

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-EXAMEN, ES-LEX-C429-EDU-01] -->

## You'll want to know: la nota
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la nota** — the mark, the grade.

*¿Qué nota tienes?* — "What mark did you get?" In Spain the answer is a number
**out of ten**, and five is where passing begins.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02] -->

Latin **nota** meant **a mark scratched onto something** — a sign cut into wax,
wood or skin so it could be told apart later.

Every modern sense grew out of that one scratch:

| word | the mark in it |
|---|---|
| **nota** | the mark a teacher puts on your work |
| **notar** | to notice — to catch the mark |
| **notable** | worth marking |

English *note*, *notice*, *notable* and *notorious* are the same word arriving
by a different road.

So the grade sense is not a metaphor. **It is the literal one**, and a written
note — a mark made on paper to be read later — is the extension.

## Grammar Lens: one word, two senses, and how to tell them apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02, ES-LEX-EXAMEN] -->

You have met *nota* before, on a message left for somebody. Both senses are
live, and what sorts them is the company the word keeps:

| what stands nearby | which sense |
|---|---|
| *examen*, *clase*, a number | the mark |
| *dejar*, *escribir*, a person | the message |

*Sacar buena nota* is the set phrase for doing well — literally "to take out a
good mark," the way you take out anything you have earned.
