Kannada, chapter 315: Feelings and Hopes.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) — gladness, happiness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "joy", "sadness" and "fear". (santōṣa, duḥkha, bhaya.) Then "My bag is lost." (nanna cīla kaḷeduhōyitu.)

You'll want to know: ಖುಷಿ.
ಖುಷಿ — khuṣi — "gladness, happiness".
ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು ನೋಡಿ ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಯಿತು. nimmannu nōḍi nanage tumbā khuṣi āyitu. — "I was very glad to see you."
ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು (nimmannu, "you"), as the one seen; ನೋಡಿ, "having seen". The gladness "happened" to me: ನನಗೆ … ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) ಆಯಿತು, with the ನನಗೆ of ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು.
ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) and ಸಂತೋಷ both mean happiness. ಸಂತೋಷ is also the word you say on meeting someone; in everyday talk about how you feel you will hear ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) a great deal.

The word, taken apart.
ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) is a loan, usually traced to Persian khushī, "happiness", from khush, "glad, pleasant". The same word is everyday Hindi and Urdu. It is not the only word you know that came in by that road: the havā of ಹವಾಮಾನ, "weather", is "air", an Arabic word that reached Kannada through Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ನನಗೆ ಸಂತೋಷವಾಯಿತು (nanage santōṣavāyitu) — how you felt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was very glad to see you". (nimmannu nōḍi nanage tumbā khuṣi āyitu.) Then "I know Kannada". (nanage kannaḍa gottu.)

You'll want to know: a feeling that comes to you.
ನಿನ್ನೆ ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಸಂತೋಷವಾಯಿತು. ninne nanage tumbā santōṣavāyitu. — "Yesterday I was very happy."
Kannada builds most feelings the way it builds ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು: the person takes ನನಗೆ, "to me", and the feeling happens to them. ಸಂತೋಷ + ಆಯಿತು becomes ಸಂತೋಷವಾಯಿತು, with the -ವ- you met in ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾಗಿದೆ. Any feeling word fits the frame:
ನನಗೆ ಭಯವಾಯಿತು (nanage bhayavāyitu) — I got scared.
ನನಗೆ ಬೇಸರವಾಯಿತು (nanage bēsaravāyitu) — I felt low; I got bored.
ನನಗೆ ದುಃಖವಾಯಿತು (nanage duḥkhavāyitu) — I felt sad.
ನನಗೆ ಕೋಪ ಬಂತು (nanage kōpa bantu) — I got angry: anger "came" to me.
For what happens time after time, ಆಗುತ್ತದೆ:
ರಾತ್ರಿ ನನಗೆ ಭಯವಾಗುತ್ತದೆ. rātri nanage bhayavāguttade. — "I get scared at night."

You'll want to know: a feeling you are in.
ಅವಳು ಈಗ ಖುಷಿಯಾಗಿದ್ದಾಳೆ. avaḷu īga khuṣiyāgiddāḷe. — "She is happy now."
ಖುಷಿಯಾಗಿದ್ದಾಳೆ is ಖುಷಿ (khuṣi) + ಆಗಿ, "having become glad", + ಇದ್ದಾಳೆ (iddāḷe, "she is"). For "I": ನಾನು ಖುಷಿಯಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (nānu khuṣiyāgiddēne), "I am happy", with the ಇದ್ದೇನೆ of ನಾನು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ.
Here the person is the subject, in ನಾನು or ಅವಳು. The ನನಗೆ frame says what came over you; the -ಆಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ frame says the state you are in. And when you cannot name the feeling, ನನಗೆ ಹಾಗೆ ಅನಿಸಿತು (nanage hāge anisitu, "that's how I felt").

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಗುರಿ (guri) — a goal, an aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a wish" and "a dream". (āse, kanasu.) Then "She is happy now." (avaḷu īga khuṣiyāgiddāḷe.)

You'll want to know: ಗುರಿ.
ಗುರಿ — guri — "a goal, an aim".
ಈ ವರ್ಷ ನನಗೆ ಒಂದು ಗುರಿ ಇದೆ: ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ ಕನ್ನಡ ಪತ್ರಿಕೆ ಓದಬೇಕು. ī varṣa nanage ondu guri ide: pratidina kannaḍa patrike ōdabēku. — "This year I have a goal: I must read a Kannada newspaper every day."
ವರ್ಷ (varṣa, "a year"). ನನಗೆ … ಇದೆ is "I have", the way ನನಗೆ ಜ್ವರ ಇದೆ is "I have a fever".
ಗುರಿ (guri) is also the mark an archer aims at, and the aim itself:
ಅವನ ಗುರಿ ತಪ್ಪಿತು. avana guri tappitu. — "His aim missed."
ಅವನ (avana, "his"); ತಪ್ಪಿತು, "it slipped, it went astray", the verb of ಬಸ್ ತಪ್ಪಿಹೋಯಿತು.

The word, taken apart.
ಗುರಿ (guri) is native Dravidian. DEDR 1847 lists Kannada kuṟi and guṟi, "a mark, sign, aim, object of aim", in an older spelling, with a letter for ṟ that modern Kannada no longer writes; beside it are Tamil kuṟi, "mark, aim, goal", and Telugu guṟi, "an aim". A mark you aim at became the goal you aim for.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ಗುರಿ (guri) mean? (A goal, an aim; also the mark you aim at.) "I have a goal"? (ನನಗೆ ಒಂದು ಗುರಿ (guri) ಇದೆ.) Is it a native word or a loan? (Native Dravidian: Tamil and Telugu have it too.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ (hōgabēku anta āse ide) — wishes and hopes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a goal" (guri), "I must go" (nānu hōgabēku) and the word that closes a quotation (anta). Today all three go into one sentence.

You'll want to know: -ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ.
ನನಗೆ ಒಂದು ದಿನ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ. nanage ondu dina maisūrige hōgabēku anta āse ide. — "I would love to go to Mysuru one day."
Taken apart: "to me there is a wish (that says) 'I must go to Mysuru one day'". The wish is a little quotation: the -ಬೇಕು of "must", closed by ಅಂತ, then ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ, "there is a wish". In writing you will meet ಬೇಕೆಂದು in place of ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ: ಎಂದು is the written twin of ಅಂತ.
Swap ಆಸೆ for ಕನಸು, "a dream", and it becomes a dream: … ಅಂತ ಕನಸು ಇದೆ. For a wish you had then, ಇತ್ತು: ನನಗೆ ವಿಮಾನದಲ್ಲಿ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇತ್ತು (nanage vimānadalli hōgabēku anta āse ittu, "I used to long to go by plane").

You'll want to know: what you want to become.
ನನ್ನ ತಂಗಿಗೆ ವೈದ್ಯೆ ಆಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಕನಸು ಇದೆ. nanna taṅgige vaidye āgabēku anta kanasu ide. — "My younger sister dreams of becoming a doctor."
ತಂಗಿಗೆ (taṅgige, "to my younger sister"); ವೈದ್ಯೆ (vaidye, "a woman doctor"), ವೈದ್ಯ with -ಎ. ಆಗಬೇಕು is ಆಗು, "to become", with -ಬೇಕು: "must become". The job you want to be needs no ending:
ನನಗೆ ಶಿಕ್ಷಕ ಆಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಆಸೆ ಇದೆ. nanage śikṣaka āgabēku anta āse ide. — "I would like to become a teacher."
