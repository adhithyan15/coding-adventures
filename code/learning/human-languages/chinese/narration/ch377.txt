Lesson 1 of 6.
jūmín — resident (of a place).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give up one's seat, then the Mandarin for square dancing (in a public square).

You'll want to know: jūmín.
jūmín — "resident (of a place)".
zhèr de jūmín dōu hěn rèxīn — The people who live here are all very kind.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jūmín is built from jū 'to dwell' and mín 'the people', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjo and mjin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stay' and the second 'the multitude'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
社区 (shèqū) — community; neighbourhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for square dancing (in a public square), then the Mandarin for a resident (of a place).

You'll want to know: 社区.
社区 — shèqū — "community; neighbourhood".
我们社区里有一家书店 wǒmen shèqū lǐ yǒu yì jiā shūdiàn — There's a bookshop in our neighbourhood.
The new character, in pieces: 区 (qū) — four strokes: a bar across the top, two strokes crossing inside, and a frame down the left and along the foot, open on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
社区 (shèqū) is built from 社 'the altar of the soil' and 区 (qū) 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyaeX and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary's entry for the second names a marsh and quotes a note on bent shoots; the sense 'an area' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 6.
区 (qū) — a bar, a cross, then the frame.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shè, xiǎo]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a bar, a cross, then the frame.
区 (qū).
Three steps: the top bar, the cross inside, the frame.
The top bar, one stroke, left to right across the top.
The cross inside, two strokes. A stroke falling to the lower left, then a stroke falling to the lower right that crosses it.
The frame, one stroke. A vertical down the left side that turns right along the foot. The right side stays open.
1 + 2 + 1 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 区 (qū), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 区 (qū)? (Four.) Which stroke comes last? (The frame, down the left side and along the foot.) Which side stays open? (The right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
小区 (xiǎoqū) — housing estate; residential compound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a resident (of a place), then the Mandarin for a local community.

You'll want to know: 小区.
小区 — xiǎoqū — "housing estate; residential compound".
我觉得这个小区很好 wǒ juéde zhège xiǎoqū hěn hǎo — I think this estate is very nice.

The word, taken apart.
小区 (xiǎoqū) is built from 小 'small' and 区 (qū) 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'tiny'. A housing estate of many blocks behind one gate is a modern use.

Lesson 6 of 6.
学区房 (xuéqūfáng) — a home near a good school.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a housing estate, then the Mandarin for the area around (a place).

You'll want to know: 学区房.
学区房 — xuéqūfáng — "a home near a good school".
大家都想买学区房因为学校好 dàjiā dōu xiǎng mǎi xuéqūfáng yīnwèi xuéxiào hǎo — Everyone wants a flat near a good school, because the school is good.

The word, taken apart.
学区房 (xuéqūfáng) is built from 学 'to study', 区 (qū) 'an area' and 房 'a house', all inherited from Middle Chinese, haewk, khju and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'a room'. A home inside a good school's catchment area is a recent use.
