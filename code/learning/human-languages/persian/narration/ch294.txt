Persian, chapter 294: Asking The Way.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ببخشید (bebakhshid) — excuse me, and the way to the station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to forgive", then "where?". (Bakhshidan, kojâ.) Then "I want to go". (Mikhâham beravam.)
[pause 1 second]
On the street the first word you need is the one that stops a stranger politely.

You'll want to know: ببخشید.
ببخشید — bebakhshid — "excuse me", before a question; "sorry", after a bump.
It is bakhshidan, "to forgive", with be- and the shomâ ending, the shape a polite request takes: "forgive". Persian stops a stranger by asking pardon first.
ببخشید، ایستگاه کجاست؟ — bebakhshid, istgâh kojâst? — Excuse me, where is the station?
Kojâst is kojâ with ast fused on, the way chi and ast became chist. The answer may start نزدیک است, nazdik ast, "it's near".

Grammar Lens: chetor mitavânam be... beravam?
Tavânestan, "to be able", has the present stem tavân-: mitavânam, I can; mitavânid, you can. Like mikhâham, it takes the subjunctive after it. Put chetor, "how", in front and you have the way:
ببخشید، چطور میتوانم به ایستگاه بروم؟ — bebakhshid, chetor mitavânam be istgâh beravam? — Excuse me, how can I get to the station?
Swap the place and the question still works: be muze, to the museum; be hotel, to the hotel.

The phrase, taken apart.
bakhshidan is native Persian. EtymDB 2.1 takes it back to a Middle Persian verb written bhš- in Pahlavi letters, and lists it in two senses, "to give, to grant" and "to forgive". Forgiving is a kind of granting, and bebakhshid asks for it in advance.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: bebakhshid, istgâh kojâst?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: bebakhshid, chetor mitavânam be istgâh beravam?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same question for the museum — be muze]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the thanks after the answer — mamnun]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does bebakhshid say, word for word? (Forgive, said politely.) "Where is the station?" (Istgâh kojâst?) "How can I get to the station?" (Chetor mitavânam be istgâh beravam?)


Lesson 2 of 3.
دست راست (dast-e râst) — on the right, and how directions are given.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "right", "left" and "straight ahead". (Râst, chap, mostaqim.) Then ask the way to the station. (Bebakhshid, chetor mitavânam be istgâh beravam?)
[pause 1 second]
Now the answer, which arrives fast, so it pays to know its shape.

You'll want to know: دست راست.
دست راست — dast-e râst — "on the right": word for word, "the right hand". دست چپ, dast-e chap, is "on the left".
ایستگاه دست راست است. — istgâh dast-e râst ast — The station is on the right.
The ezafe joins the hand to its side, the same -e as in esm-e man.

Grammar Lens: the polite instruction.
A stranger gives directions in the subjunctive with the shomâ ending, said as an instruction. You hold the shape already:
مستقیم بروید. — mostaqim beravid — Go straight on.
سر چهارراه به دست چپ بپیچید. — sar-e chahârrâh be dast-e chap bepichid — At the crossroads, turn left.
Bepichid is pichidan, "to turn", from chapter one hundred and thirty-three, on its stem pich-. Sar-e chahârrâh is "at the head of the crossroads". To a friend, someone you call to, the ending drops: bepich, turn.
So a whole answer runs: mostaqim beravid, ba'd be dast-e râst bepichid. istgâh dast-e chap ast.

The phrase, taken apart.
Both words are inherited. dast, "hand", continues Old Persian dasta, on the same Indo-European root as Sanskrit hasta, "hand" (EtymDB 2.1). râst continues Old Persian rāsta, "right, straight", and EtymDB sets it beside English right: both go back to an Indo-European word for "moved in a straight line".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: istgâh dast-e râst ast, then the same on the left]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Go straight on" — mostaqim beravid]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: sar-e chahârrâh be dast-e chap bepichid]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the whole answer back, as you would to check it: mostaqim, ba'd dast-e râst...]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"On the left"? (Dast-e chap.) "Turn right", politely? (Be dast-e râst bepichid.) Which English word does EtymDB set beside râst? (Right.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — on the street, asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Stop someone and ask the way to the museum. (Bebakhshid, chetor mitavânam be muze beravam?)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and a whole exchange. You open with bebakhshid, ask with chetor mitavânam be... beravam? or... kojâst?, and the answer comes back in the polite instruction: mostaqim beravid, be dast-e chap bepichid.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here is one exchange, start to finish:
— bebakhshid, muze kojâst? — mostaqim beravid. sar-e chahârrâh be dast-e râst bepichid. muze dast-e chap ast. — mostaqim, ba'd dast-e râst, ba'd dast-e chap. mamnun.
The third line is the habit worth keeping: say the directions back, and any mistake shows up while the stranger is still there.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the three lines of the exchange, playing both people]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, for the station]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a reason you are lost — dalil in ast ke naqshe nadâram, the reason is that I don't have a map]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: advice to a visitor — bâyad mostaqim beravid]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What do you say before the question? (Bebakhshid.) "Turn left"? (Be dast-e chap bepichid.) Why say the directions back? (So a mistake shows up at once.)
