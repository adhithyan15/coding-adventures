# ਼ · ਸ਼ — two pieces met inside a word you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-JHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-PAIRIN-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-PA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-AUNKAR-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-TIPPI-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-DULAINKAR-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-OORA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-CHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-ADDAK-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-DHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-BHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-EERI-01, PA-LEX-KANN, PA-CONTRAST-KANN-KARNA, PA-LEX-MUNH, PA-ETYMON-MUNH-MUKHA, PA-LEX-NAKK, PA-ETYMON-NAKK-NOSE] -->

- [YOU SAY: *kann* · *mūnh* · *nakk*]

## Script you'll notice: ਼ · ਸ਼
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-NUKTA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-SHA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> ਼

**pairī̃ bindī** — *(borrowed)*. A single dot placed **under** a letter to mark a sound Punjabi borrowed — mostly from Persian and Arabic. The dot is the passport stamp.

> ਸ਼

**sassā + pairī̃ bindī** — *sha*. The dotted *sassā*. Punjabi prints it as one character, and it is the *sh* of English *ship*.

Now put them where you have already heard them:

> **ਸ** + **਼** → **ਸ਼** (*sha*);  **ਗ** + **਼** → **ਗ਼** (*gha*, a Persian sound)
>
> **ਸ਼** — *sha* — **the borrowed *sh***

You are not being asked to write any of this. You are being asked to
**recognise** it the next time it goes past.

## Writing: copy what you can see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-NUKTA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-SHA-01] -->

Keep ਼ · ਸ਼ in front of you and do not cover any of them. Follow each shape once with a
finger, then copy it once with a pencil, larger than it is printed. Say its
sound as your hand stops.

> This is **observe-and-trace with the model visible**. Nothing in these
> chapters asks you to write a Punjabi word from memory; that ladder starts
> later, and it starts from these same shapes.
