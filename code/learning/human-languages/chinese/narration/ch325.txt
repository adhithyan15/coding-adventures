Lesson 1 of 6.
jiǎngshù — to tell about; to give an account of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an age in history, then the Mandarin for an impression left on you.

You'll want to know: jiǎngshù.
jiǎngshù — "to tell about; to give an account of".
tā gěi wǒmen jiǎngshù le nàge shíqī de shēnghuó — He told us what life was like in that period.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎngshù is built from jiǎng and shù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaewngX and zywit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tell' and 'to discuss' and the second 'to set forth in writing'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
去世 (qùshì) — to pass away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an impression left on you, then the Mandarin for to give an account of.

You'll want to know: 去世.
去世 — qùshì — "to pass away".
他的外公去年去世了 tā de wàigōng qùnián qùshì le — His grandfather died last year.
The new character, in pieces: 世 (shì) — five strokes: a long bar; two verticals through it; a short bar joining their feet; then a stroke down the left that turns right along the bottom. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
去世 (qùshì) is built from 去 'to leave' and 世 (shì) 'the world', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khjoH and syejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to leave' and the second 'a generation'. The sense 'the world' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 6.
世 (shì) — a long bar and two verticals.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a long bar and two verticals.
世 (shì).
One piece, five strokes.
First the long bar, from left to right.
Then two verticals through it, the left one first, each longer above the bar than below it.
Then a short bar joining the feet of the two verticals.
Last, the turning stroke: down the left side, then right along the bottom, under everything.
1 + 2 + 1 + 1 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 世 (shì), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 世 (shì)? (Five.) What comes after the long bar? (Two verticals through it.) What is the last stroke? (One that runs down the left and turns right along the bottom.)

后代 — hòudài — "descendants; later generations".
他的后代现在都在国外 tā de hòudài xiànzài dōu zài guówài — His descendants are all abroad now.

The word, taken apart.
后代 (hòudài) is built from 后 'after' and 代 'a generation', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huwX and dojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'before and after' and the second 'to take turns'.
