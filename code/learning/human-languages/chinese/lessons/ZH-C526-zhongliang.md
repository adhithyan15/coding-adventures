## You'll want to know: 重量
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C526-WEIGHT526-03]; assesses=[] -->

**重量** — *zhòngliàng* — "weight; how heavy something is".

> *zhège xiāngzi de zhòngliàng shì èrshí gōngjīn* — This suitcase weighs twenty kilos.

The new character, in pieces: 量 — twelve strokes in three pieces: 日 on top, four strokes; a long bar under it; and 里 at the foot, seven strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**重量** (*zhòngliàng*) is built from 重 'heavy' and *liàng* 'an amount', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *drjowngX* and *ljangH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'much, thick' and gives the second as units of dry measure. The dictionary lists the second again as *ljang*, 'to measure', the liáng of the word for talking things over.
