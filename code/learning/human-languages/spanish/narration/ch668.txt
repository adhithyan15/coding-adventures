Spanish, chapter 668: Hacer y deshacer el equipaje.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
deshacer — to undo; to unpack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tenant, then the Spanish for to rent.

You'll want to know: deshacer.
deshacer — "to undo; to unpack".
Deshago la maleta al llegar. I unpack my case when I arrive.
Conjugated like hacer: deshago, deshice. Deshacer la maleta — to unpack.

The word, taken apart.
deshacer is built on hacer "to make, to do" with des- "un-"; hacer is inherited from Latin facere.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: la báscula.
la báscula — "the scales (for weighing)".
Pon la maleta en la báscula. Put the suitcase on the scales.
In the bathroom it weighs you; at the airport it weighs your bag.

The word, taken apart.
báscula comes from French bascule "seesaw" (EtymDB): old scales tipped like a seesaw.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el exceso — the excess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to pack up, then the Spanish for the scales.

You'll want to know: el exceso.
el exceso — "the excess".
Pagué cincuenta euros por exceso de equipaje. I paid fifty euros for excess baggage.
Exceso de equipaje — excess baggage.

The word, taken apart.
exceso is a learned borrowing from Latin excessus "a going out, a departure" (Lewis & Short), from excēdere "to go beyond", built on cēdere "to go".

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: arrastrar.
arrastrar — "to drag, to pull along".
Arrastro la maleta por la calle. I drag my suitcase along the street.
Arrastrar los pies — to shuffle your feet.

The word, taken apart.
arrastrar is built from a- and rastrar, on rastro "trail" (EtymDB), which comes from Latin rāstrum "a rake", from rādere "to scrape".
