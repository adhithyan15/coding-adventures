Japanese, chapter 226: Questions and Exams.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しつもん (shitsumon) — a question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to look up, then the Japanese for a dictionary.

You'll want to know: しつもん.
しつもん — shitsumon — "a question".
がくせいは せんせいに しつもんを します。 — gakusei wa sensei ni shitsumon o shimasu — "The students ask the teacher questions."

The word, taken apart.
しつもん (shitsumon) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shitsu, "substance" (Middle Chinese tsyit) and mon, "ask" (mjunH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
こたえる (kotaeru) — to reply; to answer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a dictionary, then the Japanese for a question.

You'll want to know: こたえる.
こたえる — kotaeru — "to reply; to answer".
せんせいは しつもんに ていねいに こたえます。 — sensei wa shitsumon ni teinei ni kotaemasu — "The teacher replies to questions politely."
Polite こたえます, kotaemasu; an ichidan verb. The noun こたえ, kotae, "an answer", comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
こたえる (kotaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
