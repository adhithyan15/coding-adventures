Lesson 1 of 7.
dìyī — first.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for round, then the Mandarin for pink.

You'll want to know: dìyī.
dìyī — "first".
zhè shì wǒ de dìyī ge shǒujī — This is my first phone.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
一 keeps its first tone here: in a number that gives an order, yī does not change its tone.

The word, taken apart.
dìyī is built from dì 'order, rank' and 一 'one', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dejH and 'jit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 7.
一半 (yíbàn) — one half; half (of something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for pink, then the Mandarin for first.

You'll want to know: 一半.
一半 — yíbàn — "one half; half (of something)".
两千的一半是一千 liǎngqiān de yíbàn shì yìqiān — Half of two thousand is one thousand.
The new character, in pieces: 半 (bàn) — two short dots on top, two bars, and a long vertical down through them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
The tone of 一 changes: yī is said yí before the fourth tone of bàn.

The word, taken apart.
一半 (yíbàn) is built from 一 'one' and 半, inherited from Middle Chinese 'jit and panH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'a thing divided in the middle', the bàn of half past.

Lesson 3 of 7.
半 (bàn) — two dots, two bars, one vertical.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — two dots, two bars, one vertical.
半 (bàn).
One piece, five strokes.
Two short dots at the top, leaning toward each other: the left one falls to the lower right, the right one to the lower left.
Two horizontals, short over long, the two bars of 二.
A long vertical from above the shorter bar, straight down through both to the foot.
2 + 2 + 1 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 半 (bàn), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 半 (bàn)? (Five.) Which way do the two dots lean? (Toward each other.) What comes last? (The long vertical.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 半 (bàn), pinned stroke paths and medians.

The word, taken apart.
suàn is inherited from Middle Chinese swanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the number of things'. The regular sound change would give a third tone, suǎn, not suàn; why is not settled.
