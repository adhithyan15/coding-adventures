# ಸರಿ (sari) — "okay," the word that oils every conversation

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The easy middle between "yes" and "no": "okay, alright, fine,
correct." And Tamil has the very same word — the Dravidian
family shares it whole.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*(Skim if you read Kannada.)*

- **ಸ** = "sa."
- **ರಿ** = "ri" — the consonant **ರ** (ra) with the **"i" vowel sign**.

Left to right: **ಸ · ರಿ** = *sa-ri* →

> **ಸರಿ** = **sari** = "okay / correct."

Two clean syllables — no conjunct, no stacking — a good moment to feel how
smoothly Kannada's round letters read.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C01-SARI-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ಸರಿ** (*sari*) is native Dravidian for **"correct, even, right,"** and by an
easy step, **"okay, agreed."** It is the verbal nod of Kannada, just as it is
of Tamil (**சரி**, *sari*) — the **same word** across the family. Doubled,
*sari sari*, it means "yes, yes, alright."

## Grammar Lens: one shared Dravidian word, two scripts
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C01-SARI-02]; assesses=[] -->

*sari* is a small proof of how close these languages are: identical word,
identical meaning, written in two different scripts — Kannada **ಸರಿ**, Tamil
**சரி**. As you learn to read each script, watching a shared word change its
clothes but not its sound is the fastest way to feel the family resemblance.
(This "same word, different script" pattern-matching is exactly what carries
you across Kannada, Telugu, and Malayalam.)
