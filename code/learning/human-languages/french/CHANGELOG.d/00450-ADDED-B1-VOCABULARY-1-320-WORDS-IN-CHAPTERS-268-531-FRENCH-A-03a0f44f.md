## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,320 words in chapters 268–531; French attains B1

- **264 new chapters (268–531) teach 1,320 new headwords, one word per lesson.** Each lesson gives:
  - the word and its English;
  - a short example with its translation;
  - a usage note (33 of them give the Québec, Belgian or Swiss form);
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 268–333, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (330 words): time and periods, the news, investigations and justice, emergencies, life events, celebrations, history, and cooking.
  - 334–399, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (330 words): points of view and judging, consequences, work and strikes, helping out, goals, breaking rules, household accounts, and life online, with conditions and plans.
  - 400–465, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (330 words): driving and road checks, the air terminal, places to sleep and talking to the manager, neighbourhoods, services such as the hairdresser and the optician, streams and marshes, and the heatwave.
  - 466–531, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (330 words): character, laughter, health and allergies, the wider family, the outdoors, results, the theatre, books, and chores.
- **French now teaches 2,556 headwords at or below B1**, past the 2,500 the level gate asks for. With the B1 core (chapters 258–267), French attains B1.
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1 and Lewis & Short. Where neither supports a step, the lesson hedges it or says the origin is not settled, and learned borrowings are told apart from inherited words. An independent review of each tranche changed 206 of the 1,320 words (15.6%). Most changes were origin claims the sources do not support, wrong learned/inherited labels, or root slugs; the rest were examples and notes.
- **No forward references.** Every candidate headword was screened against the text of every earlier lesson before drafting, as Spanish's, Italian's and Portuguese's B1 lessons do. A plural/gender-variant check found no headword that only repeats a taught word. French's forward-reference count stays at its pin.
