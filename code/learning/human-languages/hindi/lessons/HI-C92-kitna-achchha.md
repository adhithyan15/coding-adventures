# कितना अच्छा! — a question that has stopped asking

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-04, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have **कितना** for *how much* and **अच्छा** for *good*. Put them
together and listen to what the pair wants to be.

## You'll want to know: कितना अच्छा!
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-05]; assesses=[] -->

**कितना अच्छा!** — How good! How lovely!

**कितनी अच्छी किताब!** — What a good book!

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-05, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-04] -->

Nothing has been added. This is the question word in front of an adjective,
with the question taken out of it.

| what you say | what it is |
|---|---|
| कितना अच्छा है? | how good is it? |
| कितना अच्छा! | how good! |

The difference is the verb and the voice. Drop **है**, let the pitch fall at the
end, and a question becomes an exclamation. English does the identical thing —
*how good is it?* against *how good!* — so there is nothing new to translate,
only a habit to notice.

## Grammar Lens: it still agrees, both words at once
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-05, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-02] -->

| what is being admired | what you say |
|---|---|
| a masculine thing | कितना अच्छा! |
| a feminine thing | कितनी अच्छी! |

**Both** words move together, because both are **-आ** words and both are
agreeing with the same noun. That is why the phrase rhymes with itself in either
gender, and the rhyme is a useful check: if the two endings do not match, one of
them is wrong.

This is the commonest way a Hindi speaker pays a compliment, and it costs you
nothing you did not already have.
