French, chapter 374: Mes objectifs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le but — the aim, the goal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for flexible, then the French for to consider.

You'll want to know: le but.
le but — "the aim, the goal".
Mon but, c'est de trouver un travail à l'étranger. My aim is to find a job abroad.
In football, un but is a goal. The final t is often said.

The word, taken apart.
but comes from Old French but "end, aim" (EtymDB), linked to butte "a mound used as a target"; it is usually traced to a Frankish word for a stump or a log.

You'll want to know: l'intention.
l'intention — "the intention".
Je n'ai pas l'intention de partir. I have no intention of leaving.

The word, taken apart.
intention comes through Old French from Latin intentiō "a stretching, an aim" (EtymDB), from intendere "to stretch toward", built on tendere "to stretch" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'objectif — the objective, the target; the lens.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the aim, then the French for the intention.

You'll want to know: l'objectif.
l'objectif — "the objective, the target; the lens".
Notre objectif est de finir avant juin. Our objective is to finish before June.
On a camera, l'objectif is the lens. As an adjective, objectif means objective. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
objectif comes from Medieval Latin obiectīvus (EtymDB), from obicere "to throw before, to put before" (Lewis & Short), built on iacere "to throw".

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: la résolution.
la résolution — "the resolution, the firm decision".
Ma bonne résolution pour cette année: faire du sport. My resolution for this year: to do some sport.
Les bonnes résolutions — New Year resolutions.

The word, taken apart.
résolution is a learned borrowing from Latin resolūtiō "a loosening, a solution" (EtymDB), from resolvere "to loosen", built on solvere "to loosen" (Lewis & Short).

viser is inherited through Old French from a Vulgar Latin vīsāre (EtymDB), from Latin vīsere "to view closely" (Lewis & Short), built on vidēre "to see".
