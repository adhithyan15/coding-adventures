# तुम्हें जाना चाहिए (tumhẽ jānā cāhiye) — should, and have to

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01, HI-LEX-C95-NEED-02, HI-LEX-C122-LATE-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I need tea". (**मुझे चाय चाहिए**.) Then "I have to go", the way you take leave. (**जाना है**.)

## You'll want to know: should
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C281-CHAHIYE-SHOULD-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03, HI-LEX-C70-LEISURE-05, HI-LEX-C107-DOCTOR-01, HI-CONCEPT-C15-PAANI-ROTI-01] -->

> **तुम्हें आराम करना चाहिए।**
>
> *tumhẽ ārām karnā cāhiye.* — "You should rest."

**चाहिए** after a thing is "is needed". After a verb in **-ना** it is "should". The one who should takes **को** (**आपको**), or a pronoun's short form of it (**मुझे**, **तुम्हें**, **उसे**), and **चाहिए** does not change.

- **आपको डाक्टर के पास जाना चाहिए।** (*āpko ḍākṭar ke pās jānā cāhiye.*) — You should go to the doctor.
- **हमें पानी पीना चाहिए।** (*hamẽ pānī pīnā cāhiye.*) — We should drink water.

"Should not" puts **नहीं** before the verb: **तुम्हें ऐसा नहीं करना चाहिए** (*tumhẽ aisā nahīṃ karnā cāhiye*), "you shouldn't do that".

## You'll want to know: have to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C281-PADNA-MUST-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C122-LATE-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04, HI-LEX-C153-THINGS153-04, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-FUTURE-PERSON-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01] -->

> **कल मुझे दफ़्तर जाना पड़ेगा।**
>
> *kal mujhe daftar jānā paṛegā.* — "Tomorrow I will have to go to the office."

**पड़ना** (*paṛnā*) after a verb in **-ना** says there is no choice: something outside you makes it so. The one it falls on takes **मुझे** again.

- **मुझे रोज़ जाना पड़ता है।** (*mujhe roz jānā paṛtā hai.*) — I have to go every day.
- **मुझे जाना पड़ा।** (*mujhe jānā paṛā.*) — I had to go.
- **मुझे जाना पड़ेगा।** (*mujhe jānā paṛegā.*) — I will have to go.

Three shades, then: **जाना है**, which you say as you leave, is a plan; **जाना चाहिए** is advice; **जाना पड़ेगा** is a necessity.
