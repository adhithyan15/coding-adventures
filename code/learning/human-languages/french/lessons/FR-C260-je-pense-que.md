# je pense que — giving a view

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PENSER-04, FR-GRAM-RECIT-SQUELETTE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to think". (*Penser.*) Then "I think". (*Je pense.*)

[PAUSE 1s] A story says what happened. Giving reasons starts somewhere else:
with what you think about it.

## You'll want to know: je pense que
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-JE-PENSE-QUE-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-QUE-02, FR-LEX-C90-ADJ42-02, FR-LEX-C137-VERBES89-05, FR-LEX-C67-VERBES19-01] -->

**je pense que** — "I think that". It is the everyday opinion opener.

> **Je pense que** le train est cher. — I think the train is expensive.
> **Je pense que oui.** — I think so. · **Je pense que non.** — I don't
> think so.

The *que* cannot be dropped, as English drops its *that*. After it, the verb
stays plain: *je pense que c'est cher*. A denial, *je ne pense pas
que…*, can make careful French bend the verb that follows into a form a
later chapter builds.

Two near neighbours do the same job. **Je crois que**, from *croire*, sounds
a shade less sure. **Je trouve que**, from *trouver*, is "I find that", for
a judgement you have made yourself: *je trouve que le film est ennuyeux*.

To hand the opinion back to a friend: ***Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu en
penses ?*** — "And you, what do you think of it?" The little *en* means "of
it"; keep the whole question as one piece for now.

## Grammar Lens: à mon avis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-A-MON-AVIS-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C142-LIRE142-02, FR-LEX-MAIS-03, FR-LEX-C133-QUALITES85-01] -->

You have **l'avis**, "a notice, an opinion". **À mon avis** is "in my
opinion", and it puts your standpoint first:

> **À mon avis**, le train est cher, mais le petit musée est sympathique.

Relaxed speech says **pour moi** for the same thing. *Je pense que* puts the
opinion first; *à mon avis* says whose it is before you give it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-ETYMON-PENSER-05] -->

**penser** is inherited, through Old French *penser*, from Latin *pēnsāre*,
"to weigh carefully" (EtymDB 2.1), so a thought is a thing weighed. **avis** goes back to Old French *avis*, built
on *vis*, "a view", which is inherited from Latin *vīsus*, "a look, an
appearance" (EtymDB 2.1). *À mon avis* is "at my view": how the matter
looks from where I stand.
