Japanese, chapter 268: Keeping Calm.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おちつく (ochitsuku) — to calm down; to keep calm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a cry, then the Japanese for with relief.

You'll want to know: おちつく.
おちつく — ochitsuku — "to calm down; to keep calm".
じこが あったら、おちついて ください。 — jiko ga attara, ochitsuite kudasai — "If there is an accident, please keep calm."
Polite おちつきます, ochitsukimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが おちつきました。, haha ga ochitsukimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おちつく (ochitsuku) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ochi, "falling" (from ochiru), and tsuku, "to settle" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

きゅうじょ — kyūjo — "a rescue; relief".
みんなで きゅうじょを まっていました。 — minna de kyūjo o matte imashita — "We all waited to be rescued."

The word, taken apart.
きゅうじょ (kyūjo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kyū, "rescue" (Middle Chinese kjuwH) and jo, "help" (dzrjoH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
