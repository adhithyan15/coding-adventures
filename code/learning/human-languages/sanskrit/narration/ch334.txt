Sanskrit, chapter 334: Gods and Demons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
राक्षसः (rākṣasaḥ) — a demon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for shows favour, then the Sanskrit for immortal.

You'll want to know: राक्षसः.
राक्षसः — rākṣasaḥ — "a demon".
वने एकः भयंकरः राक्षसः वसति। vane ekaḥ bhayaṅkaraḥ rākṣasaḥ vasati. A terrible demon lives in the forest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
राक्षसः (rākṣasaḥ) is built on rakṣas, "a demon, an evil being"; MW gives rākṣasa as belonging to the Rakṣas and, as a noun, a demon (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
असुरः (asuraḥ) — an asura; an enemy of the gods.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for immortal, then the Sanskrit for a demon.

You'll want to know: असुरः.
असुरः — asuraḥ — "an asura; an enemy of the gods".
देवाः असुरान् जयन्ति। devāḥ asurān jayanti. The gods defeat the asuras.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
असुरः (asuraḥ) MW reports a grammarians' derivation from √as and, beside older senses of a spirit, gives asura as an evil spirit, an opponent of the gods, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

वधः (vadhaḥ) MW gives vadha as one who kills, a deadly weapon, and the act of striking or killing, without a root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
हन्ति (hanti) — kills; slays.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an enemy of the gods, then the Sanskrit for the slaying.

You'll want to know: हन्ति.
हन्ति — hanti — "kills; slays".
वीरः खड्गेन राक्षसं हन्ति। vīraḥ khaḍgena rākṣasaṃ hanti. The hero kills the demon with a sword.
Past (he …): सः राक्षसं हतवान्, saḥ rākṣasaṃ hatavān.

The word, taken apart.
हन्ति (hanti) comes from the root √han, "to strike, kill"; MW compares Greek theinō and phonos (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the slaying, then the Sanskrit for kills.

You'll want to know: निगिरति.
निगिरति — nigirati — "swallows".
सर्पः मूषकं निगिरति। sarpaḥ mūṣakaṃ nigirati. The snake swallows the mouse.
Past (it …): सर्पः मूषकं निगीर्णवान्, sarpaḥ mūṣakaṃ nigīrṇavān.

The word, taken apart.
निगिरति (nigirati) is built from ni, "down", and the root √gṝ, "to swallow"; MW gives ni-gṝ as to swallow, gulp down (MW).
