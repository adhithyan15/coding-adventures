Latin, chapter 259: To pray, To recommend, To breathe, To give up, To exchange.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ōrō, ōrāre — to pray, to beg.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to make no sound, then the Latin for to dread.

You'll want to know: ōrō, ōrāre.
ōrō, ōrāre — "to pray, to beg".

The word, taken apart.
ōrō is often linked with ōs, ōris, "the mouth" (Lewis & Short), though the link is uncertain: to speak, then to plead and pray. ōs is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h1oh3s, "mouth" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
spīrō, spīrāre — to breathe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to pray, then the Latin for to recommend.

You'll want to know: spīrō, spīrāre.
spīrō, spīrāre — "to breathe".

The word, taken apart.
spīrō is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European (s)peys-, "to blow, to breathe" (EtymDB 2.1); Lewis & Short call it doubtful. English spirit and inspire come from its family.
