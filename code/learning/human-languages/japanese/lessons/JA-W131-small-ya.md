## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-SCRIPT-SMALL-YA-01]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-YA-01, JA-SCRIPT-SMALL-YU-01, JA-SCRIPT-CHI-01] -->

> **ゃ** — small *ya*

Same shape as **や**, in the same order, written small and sitting low: the sweep
and its hook first, then the short tick, then the descender. Three strokes, two
lifts.

It does the job **ゅ** does. It joins to the sign before it, and the two become
**one beat**:

| you write | you say | beats |
|---|---|---|
| **ちや** | *chi-ya* | two |
| **ちゃ** | *cha* | one |

That is the whole difference between the two rows: the size of one sign. The tea
word you met one lesson back is *o–cha*, two beats, because its **ちゃ** is one.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-SMALL-YA-01, JA-SCRIPT-YA-01, JA-LEX-ANCHOR-OCHA] -->

1. Write **や**, then **ゃ** beneath it, half the height, in the same three strokes.
2. Write **おちゃ** from memory. Check that the last sign sits low and small.
3. Read **ちや** and **ちゃ** aloud, one after the other, and clap the beats.
