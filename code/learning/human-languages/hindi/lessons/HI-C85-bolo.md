# बोलो — a command at the level you already know how to choose

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *tumhārā*. You learned that Hindi has three levels of *you* and
that the possessive changes with each. Say the verb *bolnā*. This chapter does
the same thing to the verb.

## You'll want to know: बोलो
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C85-IMP-01]; assesses=[] -->

**बोलो** (*bolo*) — "speak," said to somebody you would call **तुम**.

Take the verb, drop the **-ना**, add **-o**:

> *bolnā* → **bol** → **bolo**

The same operation on any verb: *karnā* → *karo*, *sunnā* → *suno*, *calnā* →
*calo*.

## Grammar Lens: the command carries the level, and nothing else has to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C85-IMP-01, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03] -->

This is the part that makes the imperative economical. In English a command is
one word for everybody — *speak* — and politeness has to be added around it:
*speak*, *please speak*, *would you mind speaking*.

In Hindi the **verb ending itself** carries the level:

| level | the form | to whom |
|---|---|---|
| तुम | **बोलो** | a friend, a sibling |
| आप | boliye | a stranger, an elder |
| तू | bol | intimate, or rude |

You already know how to choose between those three, because you chose between
*tumhārā*, *āpkā* and *terā* the same way. The pronoun does not even have to be
said: **बोलो** on its own tells the listener which one you meant.
