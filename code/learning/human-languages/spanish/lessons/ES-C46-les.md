# les — "to them," and what this system really costs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-INDIRECT-OBJECT-LE, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-PLURAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Lo* became *los*. So what does *le* become? Say it before you read
on.

## Grammar Lens: right again, and then something better
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-INDIRECT-OBJECT-PLURAL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DECIR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-LEX-DECIR, ES-LEX-LE-OBJECT] -->

> **les** — *to them*, *to you all*.

> *Le digo.* — I tell him. → ***Les** digo.* — I tell them.

Add *-s*. That is the second time an object pronoun has pluralised without
argument, and it will not be the last.

Now the part worth the chapter. You have just met the sixth and last indirect
object pronoun — and you have only ever been **taught two of them**.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-OBJECT-SETS-OVERLAP]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ME-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-TE-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-NOS-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-OS-DIRECT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-INDIRECT-OBJECT-PLURAL, ES-LEX-OS-OBJECT] -->

Here are the two sets, side by side:

| | direct (*what*) | indirect (*to whom*) |
|---|---|---|
| me | **me** | **me** |
| you | **te** | **te** |
| him / her / you | **lo**, **la** | **le** |
| us | **nos** | **nos** |
| you all *(Spain)* | **os** | **os** |
| them / you all | **los**, **las** | **les** |

Look down the two columns. **Four of the six rows are identical.** *Me*, *te*,
*nos* and *os* do not change at all — the same word does both jobs, and Spanish
never asks you to choose.

So a "whole second system" turns out to cost exactly **two new words**: *le*
and *les*. Everything else you already owned, and you owned it before you knew
there were two systems to own it in.

This is worth holding onto beyond this chapter. A grammar book will show you
two complete tables and it will look like twelve things. Count what is actually
**different** and it is two.
