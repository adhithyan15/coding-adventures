Hindi, chapter 370: The Village Meeting.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सभा (sabhā) — an assembly, a public meeting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an employee, then the Hindi for a ban.

You'll want to know: सभा.
सभा — sabhā — "an assembly, a public meeting".
शाम को गाँव में एक सभा होगी। śām ko gā̃v meṁ ek sabhā hogī. There will be a public meeting in the village this evening.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
सभा (sabhā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit sabhā, "an assembly" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ज़िला (zilā) — a district.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an assembly, then the Hindi for a village head.

You'll want to know: ज़िला.
ज़िला — zilā — "a district".
हमारा ज़िला पहाड़ों में है। hamārā zilā pahāṛoṁ meṁ hai. Our district is in the mountains.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ज़िला (zilā) is usually traced to Arabic ḍilʿ, "a side", which reached Hindi through Persian. Persian and Hindi use it for a district.

Lesson 5 of 5.
तय करना (tay karnā) — to fix (a date), to decide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a district, then the Hindi for a political party.

You'll want to know: तय करना.
तय करना — tay karnā — "to fix (a date), to decide".
सभा ने अगली बैठक की तारीख़ तय की। sabhā ne aglī baiṭhak kī tārīkh tay kī. The meeting settled the date of the next one.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दिन तय किया, usne din tay kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
तय करना (tay karnā) joins तय to करना, "to do". तय is usually traced to Arabic ṭayy, "folding up", which reached Hindi through Persian. The sense of settling a matter is a Persian and Hindi one.
