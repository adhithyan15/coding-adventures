Telugu, chapter 93: Keeping Well.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
క్షేమం (kṣēmaṁ) — wellbeing, safety.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a wound, then the Telugu for weakness.

You'll want to know: క్షేమం.
క్షేమం (kṣēmaṁ) — "wellbeing, safety".
క్షేమంగా ఉన్నారా? — "are you keeping well?", a warmer, more formal cousin of బాగున్నారా?, heard from elders and read at the top of letters.

What you've built.
A respectful way to ask after someone.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kṣēmaṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kṣēmaṁ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask kṣēmaṁgā unnārā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for a wound, then the Telugu for weakness, then say kṣēmaṁ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does క్షేమం (kṣēmaṁ) mean? ("Wellbeing, safety".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for wellbeing, then the Telugu for comfortably.

You'll want to know: సుఖం.
సుఖం (sukhaṁ) — "comfort, ease, contentment".
సుఖంగా ఉన్నారా? — "are you comfortable?", a host's question. Its opposite is the కష్టం you already have: ease against difficulty.

What you've built.
Ease, set against a word you know for its opposite.

Lesson 4 of 5.
సంతోషంగా (santōṣaṁgā) — happily, gladly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for comfortably, then the Telugu for comfort.

You'll want to know: సంతోషంగా.
సంతోషంగా (santōṣaṁgā) — "happily, gladly".
It is సంతోషం, the "pleased" of your first introductions, with -గా. సంతోషంగా (santōṣaṁgā) alone answers a request: "gladly".

What you've built.
An old word put to a new job with the ending you know.
