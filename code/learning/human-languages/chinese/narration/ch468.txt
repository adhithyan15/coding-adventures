Mandarin Chinese, chapter 468: Good Points.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shànliáng — kind-hearted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cheerful and outgoing, then the Mandarin for full of life.

You'll want to know: shànliáng.
shànliáng — "kind-hearted".
wǒ māma shì wǒ rènshi de zuì shànliáng de rén — My mother is the kindest person I know.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shànliáng is built from shàn and liáng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyenX and ljang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses each with the other: 'good'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
和气 (héqi) — good-natured; pleasant to deal with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for full of life, then the Mandarin for kind-hearted.

You'll want to know: 和气.
和气 — héqi — "good-natured; pleasant to deal with".
他很安静对人也很和气 tā hěn ānjìng duì rén yě hěn héqi — He's quiet, and he's pleasant to everyone too.

The word, taken apart.
和气 (héqi) is built from 和 'harmony' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwa and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'in tune' and 'neither hard nor soft'. The second is said light.

Lesson 4 of 5.
qínkuài — diligent with chores; quick to pitch in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for pleasant to deal with, then the Mandarin for strong in spirit.

You'll want to know: qínkuài.
qínkuài — "diligent with chores; quick to pitch in".
tā bǐ tā gēge qínkuài — He's more hard-working than his big brother.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qínkuài is built from qín 'toil' and 快 'quick', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gj+n and khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'toil' and the second as what pleases the heart; the sense 'quick' for the second is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tǐtiē — considerate; thoughtful of others' needs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for strong in spirit, then the Mandarin for quick to pitch in.

You'll want to know: tǐtiē.
tǐtiē — "considerate; thoughtful of others' needs".
wǒ bìng de shíhou tā duì wǒ hěn tǐtiē — When I was ill, he was very considerate to me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tǐtiē is built from 体 'body' and tiē 'to stick to', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thejX and thep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'the body' and the second as pawning a thing for money; the sense 'considerate' is not given there.
