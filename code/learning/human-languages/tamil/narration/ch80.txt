Tamil, chapter 80: One, the Other, and the Price.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
மற்றது (maṟṟadu) — the other one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read எப்போது. Then say: when you come, tell me.

You'll want to know: மற்றது.
மற்றது (maṟṟadu) — "the other one".
இது and அது name a thing by where it stands: near me, over there. மற்றது (maṟṟadu) names it by what it is not — the one that is left, once you have picked one up.
இது வேண்டும். மற்றது (maṟṟadu) வேண்டாம்.
idu vēṇḍum. maṟṟadu vēṇḍām. — "I want this one. Not the other."
The tail of the word is அது, the same that one that ends அதனால். Tamil builds a surprising amount out of that small word, and once you hear it inside a longer one you can usually guess what the longer one is doing.

What you've built.
A third way to point at a thing, once two of them are on the table.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: maṟṟadu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: idu vēṇḍum. maṟṟadu vēṇḍām.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — notice: the அது at the end of மற்றது (maṟṟadu) and at the start of அதனால்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: read எப்போது, then say varum pōdu, then say maṟṟadu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say ām / illai, read குடி and say -kkāga — three distances back — then set two of them against each other, one and the other]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
இது names a thing by where it is. What does மற்றது (maṟṟadu) name it by? (By being the one left over.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
ஒன்று … மற்றது (oṉṟu … maṟṟadu) — one here, the other there.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say: when you come, tell me. Then say the other one.

Grammar lens: a pair, split across two halves.
ஒன்று has been the number one since chapter seven. Put மற்றது (maṟṟadu) opposite it and the number stops counting and starts distributing.
ஒன்று இங்கே, மற்றது (maṟṟadu) அங்கே.
oṉṟu iṅgē, maṟṟadu aṅgē. — "One is here, the other is there."
Two halves, no joining word between them, and no verb in either. Tamil sets them side by side and the pairing does the work — the same silence you first met in இவர் என் நண்பர்.
It works with any two facts you can already state:
ஒன்று பெரிய வீடு, மற்றது (maṟṟadu) சிறிய வீடு.
Chapter seventy-four joined two things into one list. This does the opposite: it takes two things and holds them apart.

What you've built.
Both directions now: a way to put two things together, and a way to tell them apart.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: oṉṟu iṅgē, maṟṟadu aṅgē]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: oṉṟu periya vīḍu, maṟṟadu siṟiya vīḍu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: pālum tēnīrum against oṉṟu … maṟṟadu — held together, then held apart]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say varum pōdu, then say maṟṟadu, then say the whole pair]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say niṟuttu, read சரியா and say eppōdu — three distances back — then set two of them against each other, one and the other]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many joining words stand between the two halves? (None — the pair itself is the join.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
மற்றது (maṟṟadu) — a doubling you have read twice before.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: மற்றது — ற standing on ற and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: say one here, the other there.

Script you'll notice: மற்றது — ற standing on ற.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
piece: ம, ma, the letter on its own.
piece: ற், ṟ, ற with a புள்ளி.
piece: ற, ṟa, ற again, this time carrying its vowel.
piece: து, du, த with the ு sign.
ம, ற், ற, து becomes மற்றது (maṟṟadu).
The middle is the doubling you read in சட்டை with ட and in எப்போது with ப. A third letter, the same shape of seam: one wearing a புள்ளி, leaning on its twin.
Once you have seen it three times it stops being a thing to work out. It is a shape.
The last two letters spell து, and the word they close is அது — the that one the lesson before pointed out. On the page you can see the whole word being built out of things you have.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: ம — then ற் — then ற — then து]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: மற்றது (maṟṟadu), then எப்போது, then சட்டை — three doublings, three letters]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: in each one, the letter carrying the புள்ளி]
[your turn — recall: say maṟṟadu, then say oṉṟu iṅgē, maṟṟadu aṅgē, then read மற்றது]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say tiṟa, nāṉ uḷḷē varalāmā? and appōdu — three distances back — then set two of them against each other, one and the other]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read மற்றது (maṟṟadu). Which letter is written twice? (ற — the first one with a புள்ளி.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
விலை எவ்வளவு? (vilai evvaḷavu?) — how much does it cost?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read மற்றது (maṟṟadu). Then say: one here, the other there.

You'll want to know: the question the shop is for.
விலை எவ்வளவு? (vilai evvaḷavu?) — "How much does it cost?"
Both words have been yours for a long time. எவ்வளவு came in chapter thirty-nine and விலை in chapter sixty-eight. Standing side by side they are the question a shop exists for, and until now nothing put them side by side.
No verb, and no word for is. Tamil names the thing and asks about it:
விலை எவ்வளவு? (vilai evvaḷavu?) — the price, how much?
இது எவ்வளவு? — this one, how much?
Then chapter seventy-five lets you check what you heard, or push back on it:
விலை குறைவா? — vilai kuṟaivā? — "Is the price low?"
குறைவு is the shortfall word from chapter fifty-six, and the -ஆ on the end is the question ending. Two chapters, one sentence you can say across a counter.

What you've built.
A whole shop exchange: choose one of two, ask the price, question the answer.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — ask: vilai evvaḷavu?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — ask: idu evvaḷavu?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — push back: vilai kuṟaivā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say oṉṟu iṅgē, maṟṟadu aṅgē, then read மற்றது, then ask the price]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say mūḍu, say āṉāl and read எப்போது — three distances back — then set two of them against each other, one and the other]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many words does it take to ask a price in Tamil, and how many verbs? (Two words, and no verb.)
