French, chapter 361: Trop de travail.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
stressé — stressed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the skill, then the French for the training.

You'll want to know: stressé.
stressé — "stressed".
Je suis stressé à cause du travail. I am stressed because of work.
Feminine stressée. Built on English stress.

The word, taken apart.
stressé is built on stress, which was borrowed from English stress "strain, pressure" (EtymDB). The English word goes back to Middle English destresse "distress".

Lesson 2 of 5.
épuisé — exhausted, worn out; sold out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the training, then the French for stressed.

You'll want to know: épuisé.
épuisé — "exhausted, worn out; sold out".
Après ce voyage, je suis épuisé. After that trip, I am exhausted.
Of a book or a product: sold out.

The word, taken apart.
épuisé is the participle of épuiser "to drain dry", built on puits "a well", which comes from Latin puteus "a well" (Lewis & Short): drained like a well.

You'll want to know: efficace.
efficace — "effective, efficient".
Ce médicament est très efficace. This medicine is very effective.
One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
efficace comes from Latin efficāx "effective" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from efficere "to bring about", built on facere "to do, to make".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la productivité — the productivity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for effective, then the French for the efficiency.

You'll want to know: la productivité.
la productivité — "the productivity".
La productivité de l'usine a augmenté. The factory's productivity has gone up.
From productif, productive.

The word, taken apart.
productivité is built from productif and -ité. The family goes back to Latin prōdūcere "to lead forth, to bring out", built on dūcere "to lead" (Lewis & Short).
