Spanish, chapter 666: Bajo las estrellas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el saco de dormir — the sleeping bag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the manager, then the Spanish for the owner.

You'll want to know: el saco de dormir.
el saco de dormir — "the sleeping bag".
Hace frío, lleva un buen saco de dormir. It is cold, take a good sleeping bag.
In Latin America also la bolsa de dormir.

The word, taken apart.
saco is inherited from Latin saccus "a sack, a bag" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), and dormir from Latin dormīre "to sleep".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la esterilla — the sleeping mat; the small mat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sleeping bag, then the Spanish for to camp.

You'll want to know: la esterilla.
la esterilla — "the sleeping mat; the small mat".
Pongo la esterilla debajo del saco. I put the mat under the sleeping bag.
At the gym or the beach, una esterilla is a mat.

The word, taken apart.
esterilla is a little estera "mat", which is inherited from Latin storea "a mat of plaited straw or rushes" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la caravana — the caravan, the camper; the tailback.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to camp, then the Spanish for the sleeping mat.

You'll want to know: la caravana.
la caravana — "the caravan, the camper; the tailback".
Viajamos por la costa en caravana. We travelled along the coast in a caravan.
In Spain una caravana is also a long line of cars.

The word, taken apart.
caravana comes from Persian kārvān "a company of travellers" (EtymDB), which reached Spanish through French or Italian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la linterna — the torch, the flashlight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sleeping mat, then the Spanish for the caravan.

You'll want to know: la linterna.
la linterna — "the torch, the flashlight".
Lleva una linterna para la noche. Take a torch for the night.
A lantern on a ship or a tower is also la linterna.

The word, taken apart.
linterna comes from older lanterna (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin lanterna "a lantern, a lamp, a torch" (Lewis & Short).
