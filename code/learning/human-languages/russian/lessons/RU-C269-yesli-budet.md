# если будет дождь — the future on both sides of if

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-YESLI-CLAUSE-01, RU-GRAMMAR-NADEYUS-CHTO-01, RU-LEX-C138-FUT138-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if you like, we can go to the cinema". (*Yésli khóchesh,
my mózhem poytí v kinó.*) Then "I hope the weather will be good". (*Ya
nadéyus, shto pogóda búdet khoróshaya.*) That *будет* is the key to this
lesson.

## Grammar Lens: the future on both sides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-YESLI-FUTURE-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C59-WEATHER-01, RU-LEX-C70-WEEK1-05, RU-LEX-C69-TIME2-05, RU-LEX-C78-TOWN2-03] -->

> **Если в субботу будет дождь, мы пойдём в музей.** — *yésli v subbótu
> búdet dozhd, my poydyóm v muzéy* — If it rains on Saturday, we'll go to
> the museum.

English puts the "if" half in the present: "if it rains". When the
condition lies ahead, Russian usually puts it in the future as well: "if
there will be rain".

> **Если будет время, я позвоню.** — *yésli búdet vrémya, ya pozvonyú* — If
> I have time, I'll ring.

## Grammar Lens: a perfective points forward
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-PERFECTIVE-FUTURE-01]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-PARTNER, RU-LEX-C232-ACT232-03, RU-LEX-CHITAT, RU-LEX-C52-SCHOOL-01, RU-LEX-RASSKAZAT-01, RU-LEX-BYT] -->

**Позвоню**, **пойдём**: these look like present forms, and they are
future. A perfective verb has no present, because a deed seen whole
cannot be under way now; its present-looking forms point to one deed to
come.

> **Если я прочитаю книгу, я расскажу тебе.** — *yésli ya prochitáyu
> knígu, ya rasskazhú tebé* — If I finish the book, I'll tell you.

The imperfective builds its future with *буду* and the infinitive, as met
beside *будущий*: **Я буду читать** — I will be reading.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-BYT] -->

**будет** is the future of **быть**, which EtymDB 2.1 takes back to
Proto-Indo-European *\*bʰuH-*, "to become". The future of "to be" keeps a
root that meant becoming: what will be is what becomes.
