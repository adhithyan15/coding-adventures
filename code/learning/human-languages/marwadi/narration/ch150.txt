Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 150: A connected A2 message.
1 lesson. It needs your eyes or your hands, so save it for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
A connected message about your day — plan and write a 70–90 word Marwari message about a familiar day for a named reader and purpose.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Warm-up.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
A connected message is not a pile of vocabulary and not one long sentence. It is a short route for one reader. Before writing, draw eight empty boxes and give them these jobs:
reader and greeting;
reason for writing;
where or when your day begins;
two familiar actions;
what comes next;
one preference and one reason;
one direct question for the reader;
a close.
Write only one or two English cue words in each box. Do not draft Marwari yet.

Writing — connected composition.
There is no model, sentence bank, or copyable answer on this page.
Reader: your friend Kiran. Purpose: help Kiran understand your usual day and choose a good time to call you tomorrow.
Write 70–90 words in Marwari using Devanagari. Use every planning box. Include at least four familiar actions, two time anchors, one preference, one reason, and one direct question for Kiran. Make every detail help with the call. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose only words and forms this book has taught, and connect them yourself. If you cannot form a line honestly, make it simpler; do not borrow a Hindi form to fill the gap.
This first connected attempt is untimed. Put away romanization, dictionaries, translators, generative assistants, and spell-checkers. Do not copy from an earlier lesson. Ordinary legible Devanagari handwriting is enough.
When the whole message exists, count its words once. If it is short, add one useful taught detail to a planning box. If it is long, remove a detail that does not help Kiran. Do not replace the message with a memorised model.

Wrap-up Recall.
Review the same attempt in four separate passes:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — Kiran, the usual day, tomorrow's call, and every requested content point are present.
Comprehensibility and organisation — the eight jobs form a route rather than a list of unrelated words or lines.
Marwari vocabulary and grammar — every form comes from the taught Marwari track; a familiar shorter line is better than an invented form.
Orthographic control — Devanagari letters, vowel signs, top lines, word boundaries, and punctuation remain readable.
[pause 2 seconds]
Mark one strength and one next step. Repair only the next step; keep the original attempt so the next 70–90 word message can show real growth.
