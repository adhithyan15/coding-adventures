Hindi, chapter 81: Words on the Door.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
खुला (khulā) — open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two words back: what does veṭar mean, and what is पीना? The last five words of this run are the ones painted on doors.

You'll want to know: खुला.
खुला (khulā) — "open".
It is built from खुलना, "to open", and it names the state that opening leaves behind — the way English opened stands to open. Hindi makes these already-done forms constantly, and this is one you will meet before you are taught the pattern.
खुला (khulā) is the word on a shutter, a shop board, a gate and a ticket window. It is also used of a thing that is loose or unpacked: खुला (khulā) पैसा is loose change, coins rather than a note, which is the phrase that will save you at a counter.
The ु under the ख is the short u matra, hanging below.

What you've built.
The first of five words you read rather than say.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: say veṭar, then read पीना and say what it means]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: खुला (khulā), as though it were painted on a shutter]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: khulā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: khulā paisā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say paisā, then read होटल]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does खुला (khulā) mean? ("open".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
बंद (band) — closed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say khulā, then write खुला: ख with ु beneath it, then ल, then ा.

You'll want to know: बंद.
बंद (band) — "closed".
Persian بند (band), "tied, shut", and its family is enormous. The ancient root meant "to bind", and English bind, band and bond are its western cousins. A closed door is a tied door.
Two things to keep straight. First, बंद (band) is not the beginning of बंदर, the monkey you met among the animals — the eye will want to join them and they are unrelated. Second, बंद (band) stands opposite खुला (khulā) on exactly the same signboards, so learn them as one pair and you have read half a street.

What you've built.
The pair every shutter in the country is painted with.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: read पीना, then say it without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: khulā, then band]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: बंद (band), and check that it is not बंदर]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: खुला (khulā) once more, then बंद (band) beside it]
[your turn — recall: read रुपया, then say nāshtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does बंद (band) mean? ("closed".) And its opposite? (khulā.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रवेश (pravesh) — entrance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the pair a shutter is painted with, in both orders.

You'll want to know: प्रवेश.
प्रवेश (pravesh) — "entrance".
Two Sanskrit pieces. प्र- is "forward", and विश् (viś-) is "to enter". Going forward into a place: प्रवेश (pravesh).
That प्र- is worth keeping. It is the same ancient piece as Latin pro- and English fore-, and once you can see it you will spot it at the front of a great many formal Hindi words.
This is a written register, not a spoken one. Nobody says प्रवेश (pravesh) to a friend at a doorway; it is painted over the door, printed on a form, and read out in a station announcement. That is the same shelf कृपया sits on — the please of signs rather than the please of counters.
The opening प्र stacks प on र with the vowel-killer, as क्र did in क्रिकेट.

What you've built.
Three: खुला (khulā), बंद (band), प्रवेश (pravesh). Two states of a door and one of its two labels.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: प्रवेश (pravesh), and find the stacked प्र]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: pravesh]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: pravesh band hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say dām, then read बिल]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does प्रवेश (pravesh) mean? ("entrance".) Where do you meet it, on a sign or in speech? (On a sign.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
निकास (nikās) — exit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write प्रवेश (pravesh) from memory, starting with the stacked प्र.

You'll want to know: निकास.
निकास (nikās) — "exit".
You have most of this word already. निकलना, "to set out, to go out", has been yours since the chapter on leaving; निकास (nikās) is what that doing word turns into when you need a noun. Going out, named.
Watch the vowel change as the noun is made: निकलना has a short a, निकास (nikās) takes the long ा. Hindi lengthens a vowel to build a name out of an action often enough that you should expect it rather than be surprised.
निकास (nikās) is painted opposite प्रवेश (pravesh) over every second doorway in a station, and the two are read together or not at all.

What you've built.
Four, and the second pair of this chapter: प्रवेश (pravesh) and निकास (nikās).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nikalnā, then nikās]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: pravesh, then nikās]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: निकास (nikās), lengthening the vowel that निकलना keeps short]
[your turn — recall: read महँगा, then say veṭar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does निकास (nikās) mean? ("exit".) And प्रवेश (pravesh)? ("entrance".)


Lesson 5 of 5.
देर (der) — lateness, a delay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the four words of this chapter so far, in their two pairs.

You'll want to know: देर.
देर (der) — "lateness, a delay".
Persian دیر (der), "late, a long while". It is a naming word rather than a describing one: Hindi does not say "the train is late", it says the train is with lateness — गाड़ी देर से है.
Set it against जल्दी, which you already own for speed and for earliness. जल्दी and देर (der) are the two ends of the same measure, and a station announcement will use one or the other of them about your train.
That is where this chapter lands. गाड़ी देर से है is a sentence built entirely from words you now hold, and it is the single most useful thing a traveller can understand on a platform.

What you've built.
Five: खुला (khulā), बंद (band), प्रवेश (pravesh), निकास (nikās), देर. Enough to read a door, find a way through it, and understand why the train has not come.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: der]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: gāṛī der se hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: jaldī and der]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: निकास (nikās), then देर, and say all five in order]
[your turn — recall: say kharīdnā, then read पीना]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you say the train is late? (gāṛī der se hai.) Say it once more.
