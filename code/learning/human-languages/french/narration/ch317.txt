Lesson 1 of 5.
le défilé — the parade; a fashion show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the brass band, then the French for the public holiday.

You'll want to know: le défilé.
le défilé — "the parade; a fashion show".
Les enfants regardent le défilé du 14 juillet. The children are watching the 14 July parade.
Un défilé de mode — a fashion show.

The word, taken apart.
défilé is the past participle of défiler "to march past" used as a noun (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: le carnaval.
le carnaval — "the carnival".
Le carnaval a lieu en février. The carnival takes place in February.
Plural les carnavals.

The word, taken apart.
carnaval was borrowed from Italian carnevale (EtymDB), from Medieval Latin carnelevāre "putting away meat", from carō "flesh" and levāre "to lift".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le char — the float (in a parade); the tank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to march past, then the French for the carnival.

You'll want to know: le char.
le char — "the float (in a parade); the tank".
Les chars du carnaval sont pleins de fleurs. The carnival floats are full of flowers.
In Québec, le char is also the everyday word for a car.

The word, taken apart.
char is inherited from Latin carrus "a wagon" (EtymDB), a word of Gaulish origin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
déguiser — to dress up (as se déguiser); to disguise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the carnival, then the French for the float.

You'll want to know: déguiser.
déguiser — "to dress up (as se déguiser); to disguise".
Les enfants se sont déguisés en animaux. The children dressed up as animals.
Mostly reflexive in this sense: se déguiser en — to dress up as. Without se, to disguise: déguiser sa voix.

The word, taken apart.
déguiser comes from Old French desguiser (EtymDB), built on guise "manner, way", which is usually traced to a Frankish word.
