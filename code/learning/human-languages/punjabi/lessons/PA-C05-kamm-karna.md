# ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ (kamm karnā) — "to work"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-KARNA-05, PA-ETYMON-KR-DO-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have "to do." Put a noun in front of it and Punjabi builds a whole
family of verbs.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-KA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-MA-01] -->

**ਕੰਮ** (*kamm*, "work"): **ਕੰ** (*kaṁ*, with the *ṭippī* nasal) + **ਮ** (*ma*).
Both **ਕ** and **ਮ** are already yours.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-KAMM-05]; assesses=[PA-PHRASE-NO-PROBLEM-03] -->

**ਕੰਮ** (*kamm*) is the noun "work" — the thing done, from the same *√kṛ* family
as *karnā*. Said on its own it means a job, a task, a piece of business:
*bahut kamm hai* ("there is a lot of work").

## Grammar Lens: noun + ਕਰਨਾ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-GRAMMAR-NOUN-PLUS-KARNA-05]; assesses=[] -->

Like Hindi, Punjabi builds countless verbs as **noun + ਕਰਨਾ**:

- **ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ** (*kamm karnā*) = "work-do" = "**to work**"
- *gall karnā* = "to have a chat" (*gall*, the "matter/talk" you already know
  from *koī gall nahīṁ*)

So *maiṁ kamm kardā hāṁ* = "I work." Learn *karnā* and a whole grammar of
"do-verbs" opens up.
