Script you'll notice: ङ.
ङ — ṅa, the nasal made where k and kh are made.
It is inside सङ्ख्या (saṅkhyā), "number", from the chapter on numbers. Say saṅkhyā: the ṅ sits at the back of the mouth, ready for the kh after it. Sanskrit spells that nasal out as ङ (ṅa); Hindi writes the same word संख्या, with a dot above instead.

Writing: ङ.
Put your pen on ङ (ṅa) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is an attempt, not a model. You will write the answers, and this page will not show you any copyable Sanskrit answer.
Put away romanization, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers. Set one timer for 20 minutes. Keep it running through both tasks. Write in Devanagari. Ordinary legible handwriting is enough; decorative calligraphy is not scored.

Writing — timed assessment production.
Task 1 — practical form: 7 minutes, 40 points.
Complete all six fields in Devanagari for a new neighbourhood activity group. Short answers are enough.
Your name.
Where you live or stay.
A language you speak.
One everyday action or kind of work you do.
One drink or food you like.
One person in your family.
At seven minutes, move on even if a field is unfinished. Do not restart the timer and do not polish the form with time meant for the message.
Task 2 — message: 13 minutes, 60 points.
Reader: Mira. Purpose: help Mira recognise you and arrange your first meeting tomorrow.
Write 30–40 words in Devanagari. Greet Mira, introduce yourself, say where you live and which language you speak, mention one everyday action or kind of work and one drink or food you like, ask where Mira is, and close politely. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose and connect the Sanskrit yourself.
When the 20-minute timer rings, stop, even if a line is unfinished. Record the number of completed form fields and the message word count. An honest timed attempt is more useful than a perfect untimed one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Only after you have stopped, review the same attempt. Do not rewrite it yet. Mark one strength and one next step under each assessment criterion:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — six fields are attempted; the named reader, meeting purpose, and requested message points are present.
Comprehensibility and organisation — Mira can follow the message and see how its ideas connect.
Sanskrit vocabulary, grammar, and register — familiar words and sentence patterns carry the intended meaning in a suitable everyday tone.
Devanagari control — letter shapes, vowel-sign placement, conjuncts, word spacing, anusvara or final nasal spelling, and punctuation remain readable.
[pause 2 seconds]
Keep the paper. On another day, repeat the same two-task shape with new personal details. The goal is not to memorise this prompt; it is to make a complete Sanskrit attempt calmly inside the real limit.
