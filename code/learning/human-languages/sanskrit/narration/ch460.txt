Sanskrit, chapter 460: Character and Conduct.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
स्वभावः (svabhāvaḥ) — temperament.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for regard, then the Sanskrit for self-respect.

You'll want to know: स्वभावः.
स्वभावः — svabhāvaḥ — "temperament".
मीरायाः स्वभावः शान्तः अस्ति। mīrāyāḥ svabhāvaḥ śāntaḥ asti. Mira has a calm temperament.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्वभावः (svabhāvaḥ) is built from sva, "own", and bhāva, "being", from the root √bhū: one's own way of being; MW gives "natural state, innate disposition, nature" (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रवर्तते (pravartate) — conducts himself; proceeds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for temperament, then the Sanskrit for refined.

You'll want to know: प्रवर्तते.
प्रवर्तते — pravartate — "conducts himself; proceeds".
सः विनयेन प्रवर्तते। saḥ vinayena pravartate. He conducts himself with modesty.
Past (he …): सः विनयेन प्रवृत्तवान्, saḥ vinayena pravṛttavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रवर्तते (pravartate) is built from pra, "forth", and the root √vṛt, "to turn", the root of वर्तते; MW gives "to proceed, act", and its ppp pravṛtta "engaged in, occupied with" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for refined, then the Sanskrit for conducts himself.

You'll want to know: गुणः.
गुणः — guṇaḥ — "a virtue".
दया महान् गुणः अस्ति। dayā mahān guṇaḥ asti. Compassion is a great virtue.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गुणः (guṇaḥ) is traced to the root √grah, "to seize", in the Uṇādi list that MW follows; its first sense is "a strand of a cord", and "a good quality, virtue" comes later (MW).
