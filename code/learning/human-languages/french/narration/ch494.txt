French, chapter 494: Élever des enfants.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
élever — to bring up, to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for baby's bottle, then the French for to rock.

You'll want to know: élever.
élever — "to bring up, to raise".
Ses grands-parents l'ont élevé à la campagne. His grandparents brought him up in the country.
Accent change: j'élève, nous élevons. Also to raise animals: élever des moutons.

The word, taken apart.
élever is built from é- and lever "to raise", which is inherited from Latin levāre "to lift" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la punition — punishment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to bring up, then the French for to spoil.

You'll want to know: la punition.
la punition — "punishment".
Comme punition, il n'a pas eu de dessert. As a punishment, he did not get any pudding.
The verb is punir "to punish".

The word, taken apart.
la punition is a learned borrowing from Latin pūnītiō "punishment" (EtymDB), from pūnīre "to punish", built on poena "penalty" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
désobéir — to disobey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to spoil, then the French for punishment.

You'll want to know: désobéir.
désobéir — "to disobey".
Le chien désobéit quand il voit un chat. The dog disobeys when it sees a cat.
Désobéir à quelqu'un. A regular -ir verb: ils désobéissent.

The word, taken apart.
désobéir is built from dés- and obéir "to obey", which goes back to Latin oboedīre "to listen to, to obey", built on audīre "to hear" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la crèche — day nursery, crèche; nativity scene.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for punishment, then the French for to disobey.

You'll want to know: la crèche.
la crèche — "day nursery, crèche; nativity scene".
Le matin, je dépose ma fille à la crèche. In the morning I drop my daughter off at the nursery.
For children under three. In Belgium also la crèche; in Québec la garderie.

The word, taken apart.
la crèche comes from Frankish krippija "a manger, a crib" (EtymDB), a Germanic word related to English crib.
