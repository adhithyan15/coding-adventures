Mandarin Chinese, chapter 60: Colours.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
白 (bái) — white; pale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for trousers, then the Mandarin for pretty.

You'll want to know: 白.
白 — bái — "white; pale".
他的手很白 tā de shǒu hěn bái — His hands are very pale.
The new character, in pieces: 白 (bái) — a short fall on top of 日. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
白 (bái) is inherited from Middle Chinese baek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the colour of the west', the direction that the old scheme of five colours paired with white.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 2 of 6.
白 (bái) — a short fall over 日.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a short fall over 日 and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say bái — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
日 百.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rì, bǎi]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a short fall over 日.
白 (bái).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
A short left-falling stroke at the top, from the right down to the left.
日, four strokes, the same four your hand makes in 日: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the middle bar, and the bar that closes the bottom.
1 + 4 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 白 (bái), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 白 (bái)? (Five.) What comes before 日? (A short fall at the top.) Which number you know has 白 (bái) under its top bar? (百.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 白 (bái), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 6.
yánsè — colour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for pretty, then the Mandarin for white.

You'll want to know: yánsè.
yánsè — "colour".
wǒ ài zhège yánsè — I love this colour.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yánsè is built from yán 'face' and sè, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngaen and srik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second with the whole word, 'colour'.

The word, taken apart.
lán is inherited from Middle Chinese lam (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a plant for dye'. The colour is not given there; blue as the colour of that dye is the likely step, but it is not settled.
