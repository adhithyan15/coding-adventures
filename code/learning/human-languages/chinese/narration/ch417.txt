Mandarin Chinese, chapter 417: Customs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hǎiguān — customs at the border.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to change planes on the way, then the Mandarin for flying (of an aircraft).

You'll want to know: hǎiguān.
hǎiguān — "customs at the border".
qù hǎiguān zěnme zǒu — How do I get to customs?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǎiguān is built from hǎi 'the sea' and guān 'a barrier', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xojX and kwaen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a bar across a gate; how the pair came to mean customs is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

shēnbào — "to declare goods at customs".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shēnbào is built from shēn 'to state' and bào 'to report', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syin and pawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stretch' and 'to repeat' and the second 'to report'.
