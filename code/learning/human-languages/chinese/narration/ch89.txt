Lesson 1 of 7.
给 (gěi) — to give; to, for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

[your turn — recall: say 两个人 — liǎng ge rén, two people]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say liǎng zhī māo, two cats — R1, the chapter you have finished]

[your turn — recall: say sòng, to give as a present — R3, durable]

You'll want to know: 给.
给 — gěi — "to give; to, for".
妈妈给我一个手机 māma gěi wǒ yí ge shǒujī — Mum gives me a phone.
给我 gěi wǒ — Give it to me.
The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
给 (gěi) is in the Guangyun as kip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to supply'. That reading regularly gives jǐ, still heard in a few written words; how the everyday gěi came about is not settled.

What you've built.
The person comes first, then the thing: 给, 我, 一个手机. On its own, 给我 (gěi wǒ) is a request: give me. sòng gives a present; 给 gives anything at all.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 给我 — gěi wǒ — Give it to me.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say sòng, then gěi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 给 (gěi) mean? ("To give; to, for".) Which comes first after 给 (gěi), the person or the thing? (The person.)

Script — the silk piece, then 合.
给 (gěi).
Two pieces, left before right. The left piece is the one new shape.
The silk piece 纟, three strokes: a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, a second, longer one the same way, then a stroke rising to the right.
合, six strokes: a roof like 人, a left-falling stroke and a right-falling one; a short horizontal under it; then 口.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 给 (gěi), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 给 (gěi)? (Nine.) Which piece is new? (The silk piece, on the left.) What sits at the foot of the right piece? (口.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 给 (gěi), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: 做.
做 — zuò — "to do; to make".
你做什么 nǐ zuò shénme — What are you doing?

The word, taken apart.
做 (zuò) has no reading in the Guangyun: no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
You have written 做 since 做饭. zuò 'to sit', as in zuò chē, sounds the same and is a different word with a different character.

You'll want to know: fàng.
fàng — "to put; to place".
fàng zài zhèr — Put it here.
fàng zài zhuōzi shàngbian — Put it on the table.
Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàng is inherited from Middle Chinese pjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to drive out; to send away'. 'To put down' is a later sense the Guangyun does not give.

What you've built.
fàng, then 在, then the place: the place words of the last two chapters all fit after it.

You'll want to know: dài.
dài — "to bring; to take along".
nǐ dài shǒujī le ma — Have you brought your phone?
Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dài is inherited from Middle Chinese tajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a belt; a sash'. How a belt became 'to carry along' is not given there, so the step is not settled.

What you've built.
nǐ dài shǒujī le ma: 了 after the verb says it is done, and 吗 makes the question. The answer is dài le, or méi dài.

Lesson 7 of 7.
给我 (gěi wǒ) — getting out of the door: give, do, bring, put and stand.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now. The exchange.
A: 你做什么 nǐ zuò shénme — What are you doing? B: wǒ zhǔnbèi chūmén — I'm getting ready to go out. A: nǐ dài yàoshi le ma — Have you got your key? B: dài le. shǒujī ne — Yes. Where's my phone? A: zài zhuōzi shàngbian. gěi nǐ — On the table. Here you are. B: xièxie. fàng zài bāo lǐbian — Thanks. In the bag it goes. A: qǐng zhàn zài ménkǒu, wǒ kànkan — Stand in the doorway, let me look at you.
Five actions in one doorway: do, bring, give, put and stand. 给你, here you are, is 给 (gěi) handing something over as it is said.
