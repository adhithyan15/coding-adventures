Latin, chapter 73: Girl, Old Man, Queen, Sailor, Poet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
puella, puellae — a girl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a man, then the Latin for a woman.

You'll want to know: puella, puellae.
puella, puellae — "a girl".

The word, taken apart.
puella is the feminine of puellus, "a little boy" (Lewis & Short), a diminutive of puer, "a boy, a child": so "a little girl".

Lesson 2 of 5.
senex, senis — an old man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a woman, then the Latin for a girl.

You'll want to know: senex, senis.
senex, senis — "an old man". English senior and senate.

The word, taken apart.
senex is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European senos, "old" (EtymDB 2.1). senātus, "the senate", was first the council of elders.

rēgīna, rēgīnae — "a queen".

The word, taken apart.
rēgīna is built on rēx, rēgis, "a king" (Lewis & Short), which is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h3reg-, "to rule, a chief" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
nauta, nautae — a sailor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an old man, then the Latin for a queen.

You'll want to know: nauta, nautae.
nauta, nautae — "a sailor". English nautical.

The word, taken apart.
nauta was borrowed from Greek nautēs, "a sailor" (EtymDB 2.1). Latin also had a home-grown nāvita, "sailor", built on nāvis, "a ship" (Lewis & Short).

poēta was borrowed from Greek poiētēs, "a maker, a poet" (Lewis & Short), from poieō, "to make" (EtymDB 2.1): a poet was a maker of verses.
