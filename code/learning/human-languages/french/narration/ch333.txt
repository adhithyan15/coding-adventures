Lesson 1 of 7.
la levure — the yeast; baking powder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to season, then the French for to taste.

You'll want to know: la levure.
la levure — "the yeast; baking powder".
Le boulanger met de la levure dans la pâte. The baker puts yeast in the dough.
La levure chimique — baking powder.

The word, taken apart.
levure is built on lever "to raise", which is inherited from Latin levāre "to lift" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 7.
pétrir — to knead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to taste, then the French for the yeast.

You'll want to know: pétrir.
pétrir — "to knead".
Le boulanger pétrit la pâte pendant dix minutes. The baker kneads the dough for ten minutes.
Regular, like finir: il pétrit, nous pétrissons.

The word, taken apart.
pétrir is usually traced to Late Latin pistrīre "to knead", built on pistor "a miller, a baker" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 7.
le rouleau à pâtisserie — the rolling pin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the yeast, then the French for to knead.

You'll want to know: le rouleau à pâtisserie.
le rouleau à pâtisserie — "the rolling pin".
Le rouleau à pâtisserie est dans le tiroir. The rolling pin is in the drawer.
Un rouleau alone is a roll: un rouleau de papier.

The word, taken apart.
rouleau is built on rôle "a roll", from Latin rotulus "a little wheel", built on rota (Lewis & Short); pâtisserie is built on pâte.

Lesson 4 of 7.
étaler — to roll out, to spread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to knead, then the French for the rolling pin.

You'll want to know: étaler.
étaler — "to roll out, to spread".
Il étale la pâte avec le rouleau à pâtisserie. He rolls out the dough with the rolling pin.
Also to spread butter: étaler du beurre sur le pain.

The word, taken apart.
étaler is built on Old French estal "a place, a stall", which is usually traced to a Frankish word (EtymDB lists no parent): to lay out on a stall.

Lesson 5 of 7.
démouler — to turn out (of a tin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the rolling pin, then the French for to roll out.

You'll want to know: démouler.
démouler — "to turn out (of a tin)".
Elle démoule le gâteau quand il est froid. She turns out the cake when it is cold.
The opposite of putting into le moule.

The word, taken apart.
démouler is built on dé- and moule "mould", which comes from Latin modulus "a small measure" (EtymDB).
