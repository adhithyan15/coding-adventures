French, chapter 457: Au fond des bois.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la clairière — the clearing (in a wood).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to splash about, then the French for to stream.

You'll want to know: la clairière.
la clairière — "the clearing (in a wood)".
Au milieu de la forêt, nous avons pique-niqué dans une clairière. In the middle of the forest we had a picnic in a clearing.
Built on clair 'light'.

The word, taken apart.
clairière is built on clair 'clear, light', which is inherited from Latin clārus (Lewis & Short): the light place among the trees. EtymDB does not list it.

Tourner autour du pot means to beat about the bush.

buisson is built on bois 'wood', which is usually traced to a Germanic word for 'bush' (EtymDB lists Germanic cousins).

You'll want to know: la fougère.
la fougère — "the fern, the bracken".
Les fougères poussent sous les arbres. The ferns grow under the trees.
Often in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
fougère comes from Old French fulgiere, from Vulgar Latin filicāria 'place full of ferns', from Latin filix 'fern' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la mousse — the moss; the foam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the bush, then the French for the fern.

You'll want to know: la mousse.
la mousse — "the moss; the foam".
Les pierres du chemin sont couvertes de mousse. The stones on the path are covered in moss.
La mousse is also foam, as on beer; that is probably a different word.

The word, taken apart.
mousse 'moss' comes from Old French mosse, from Frankish mosa 'moss' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'écorce — the bark (of a tree); the peel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the fern, then the French for the moss.

You'll want to know: l'écorce.
l'écorce — "the bark (of a tree); the peel".
L'écorce de ce vieil arbre est très épaisse. The bark of this old tree is very thick.
A feminine noun. L'écorce d'orange — orange peel.

The word, taken apart.
écorce is inherited from Latin scortea 'leather garment', from scortum 'hide, skin' (EtymDB): the tree's skin.
