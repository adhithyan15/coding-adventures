Arabic, chapter 527: Farmers, profits and savings.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
فلاح (fallāḥ) — a peasant farmer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a builder, then the Arabic for a cobbler.

You'll want to know: فلاح.
فلاح — fallāḥ — "a peasant farmer".
يزرع الفلاح القمح في الربيع. yazraʿu al-fallāḥ al-qamḥ fī ar-rabīʿ. The farmer sows wheat in the spring.

The word, taken apart.
فلاح (fallāḥ) is built on the native Arabic root f-l-ḥ, "to split, to till the soil; to prosper" (Lane). A fallāḥ is one who splits the earth to till it.

You'll want to know: ربح.
ربح — rabiḥa — "to make a profit".
ربح التاجر كثيرا هذه السنة. rabiḥa at-tājir kathīran hādhihi as-sana. The merchant made a big profit this year.
Present: يربح, yarbaḥ.
In a game it also means to win.

The word, taken apart.
ربح (rabiḥa) is built on the native Arabic root r-b-ḥ, "to gain in trade" (Lane). The noun ribḥ is a profit.

Lesson 4 of 6.
أفلس (aflasa) — to go bankrupt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a gardener, then the Arabic for to make a profit.

You'll want to know: أفلس.
أفلس — aflasa — "to go bankrupt".
أفلست الشركة بعد سنتين. aflasat ash-sharika baʿda sanatayn. The company went bankrupt after two years.
Present: يفلس, yuflis.

The word, taken apart.
أفلس (aflasa) is built on the native Arabic root f-l-s and is usually traced to fals (plural fulūs), a small copper coin: to be left with coppers and no silver.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ادخر (iddakhara) — to put aside (savings).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to make a profit, then the Arabic for to go bankrupt.

You'll want to know: ادخر.
ادخر — iddakhara — "to put aside (savings)".
ادخرت أختي مالا لشراء سيارة. iddakharat ukhtī mālan li-shirāʾ sayyāra. My sister put money aside to buy a car.
Present: يدخر, yaddakhir.
وفر, to save, which you know, is the everyday verb.

The word, taken apart.
ادخر (iddakhara) is built on the native Arabic root dh-kh-r, "to store up" (Lane). Form VIII iddakhara, with dh turning into d, is to store a thing for later.
