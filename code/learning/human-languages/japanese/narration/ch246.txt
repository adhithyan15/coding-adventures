Japanese, chapter 246: Anger and Apologies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おこる (okoru) — to get angry; to tell off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to laugh, then the Japanese for a smile.

You'll want to know: おこる.
おこる — okoru — "to get angry; to tell off".
せんせいは よく おこりますか。 — sensei wa yoku okorimasu ka — "Does the teacher often get angry?"
Polite おこります, okorimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
おこる (okoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
けんか (kenka) — a quarrel; a fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a smile, then the Japanese for to get angry.

You'll want to know: けんか.
けんか — kenka — "a quarrel; a fight".
あには ともだちと けんかを しません。 — ani wa tomodachi to kenka o shimasen — "My elder brother does not quarrel with his friends."

The word, taken apart.
けんか (kenka) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ken, "noisy, boisterous" (Middle Chinese xjwon), and ka, "noisy", whose Middle Chinese xwae is listed under a variant spelling that JMdict also gives (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

きのうの しあいは ざんねんでしたね。 — kinō no shiai wa zannen deshita ne — "Yesterday's match was a pity, wasn't it."

The word, taken apart.
ざんねん (zannen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are zan, "remain" (Middle Chinese dzan) and nen, "thought, feeling" (nemH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
