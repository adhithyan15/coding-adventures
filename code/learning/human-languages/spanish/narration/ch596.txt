Spanish, chapter 596: En la universidad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el temario — the syllabus, the list of topics.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for ambitious, then the Spanish for to succeed.

You'll want to know: el temario.
el temario — "the syllabus, the list of topics".
El temario del examen es muy largo. The syllabus for the exam is very long.
In Spain it is the set of topics you study for an exam.

The word, taken apart.
temario is built on tema with -ario; tema comes from Latin thema, taken from Greek thema 'a thing laid down', from tithenai 'to put' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la matrícula — the enrolment, the registration; the number plate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to succeed, then the Spanish for the syllabus.

You'll want to know: la matrícula.
la matrícula — "the enrolment, the registration; the number plate".
Pagué trescientos euros de matrícula. I paid three hundred euros for enrolment.
In Spain it is also the car number plate; in much of Latin America that is la placa.

The word, taken apart.
matrícula is a learned borrowing from Late Latin matricula 'a list, a register', a small form of matrix, built on mater 'mother' (EtymDB).

Tengo tutoría con la profesora el jueves. I have a tutorial with the teacher on Thursday.
The time when a teacher sees students one by one.

tutoría is built on tutor, a learned borrowing from Latin tutor 'guardian, protector' (EtymDB), from tueri 'to watch over'.

la facultad — "the faculty, the school of a university; ability".
Estudio en la Facultad de Derecho. I study at the Faculty of Law.
Tener facultades para — to have the ability for.

The word, taken apart.
facultad is a learned borrowing from Latin facultas 'skill, ability', built on an old form of facilis 'easy to do' (EtymDB).

el máster — "the master's degree".
Quiero hacer un máster en historia. I want to do a master's in history.
This is the word in Spain; much of Latin America says la maestría.

The word, taken apart.
máster was borrowed from English master, which comes from Latin magister 'master, teacher' (EtymDB).
