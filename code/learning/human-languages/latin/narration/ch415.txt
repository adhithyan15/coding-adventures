Latin, chapter 415: Punishment.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
castīgō, castīgāre — to punish, to correct; to rebuke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a crime against heaven, then the Latin for unfair.

You'll want to know: castīgō, castīgāre.
castīgō, castīgāre — "to punish, to correct; to rebuke".
Pater fīlium verbīs castīgāvit. The father rebuked his son.
castīgō, castīgāre, castīgāvī, castīgātum, built on castus, pure: to set right. English castigate and chastise.

The word, taken apart.
castīgō is built on castus "pure" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to set a person right. English castigate and chastise come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
relēgō, relēgāre — to send away, to banish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for unfair, then the Latin for to punish.

You'll want to know: relēgō, relēgāre.
relēgō, relēgāre — "to send away, to banish".
Prīnceps eum procul ab urbe relēgāvit. The emperor banished him far from the city.
relēgō, relēgāre, relēgāvī, relēgātum: a milder exile, which kept a man's property and citizenship. English relegate.

The word, taken apart.
relēgō is built from re- "away, back" and lēgō "to send with a charge, to depute" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English relegate comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: prōscrībō, prōscrībere.
prōscrībō, prōscrībere — "to post for sale; to outlaw, to proscribe".
Multī cīvēs prōscrīptī sunt. Many citizens were outlawed.
prōscrībō, prōscrībere, prōscrīpsī, prōscrīptum: to post a notice, first of a house for sale, then of a man whose life and goods were forfeit. English proscribe.

The word, taken apart.
prōscrībō is built from prō "forth, in public" and scrībō "to write" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to post a notice. English proscribe comes from it.
