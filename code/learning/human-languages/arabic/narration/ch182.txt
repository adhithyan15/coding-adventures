Lesson 1 of 5.
صلصة (ṣalṣa) — a sauce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sink, then the Arabic for a shelf.

You'll want to know: صلصة.
صلصة — ṣalṣa — "a sauce".

The word, taken apart.
صلصة (ṣalṣa) is usually traced to Italian salsa, "sauce" (EtymDB), from Latin salsus, "salted"; it has no Arabic root.

You'll want to know: شطيرة.
شطيرة — shaṭīra — "a sandwich".

The word, taken apart.
شطيرة (shaṭīra) is built on the native Arabic root sh-ṭ-r, "half; to halve" (Lane). Shaṭīra is a modern word for a sandwich, "the halved one".

Lesson 3 of 5.
مثلجات (muthallajāt) — ice cream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sauce, then the Arabic for a sandwich.

You'll want to know: مثلجات.
مثلجات — muthallajāt — "ice cream".

The word, taken apart.
مثلجات (muthallajāt) is built on the native Arabic root th-l-j, "snow" (Lane). Muthallajāt is the plural of the Form II passive participle, "things chilled with snow, iced".

Lesson 5 of 5.
تنورة (tannūra) — a skirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for ice cream, then the Arabic for a meal.

You'll want to know: تنورة.
تنورة — tannūra — "a skirt".

The word, taken apart.
تنورة (tannūra) is a later word for a skirt. Lane has only tannūr, "an oven", which his sources call arabicized, originally Persian; whether the skirt word comes from it is not settled.
