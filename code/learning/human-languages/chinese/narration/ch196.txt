Mandarin Chinese, chapter 196: Tickets and Flights.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
车票 (chēpiào) — train or bus ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for password, then the Mandarin for bank account.

You'll want to know: 车票.
车票 — chēpiào — "train or bus ticket".
车票多少钱 chēpiào duōshao qián — How much is the ticket?
The new character, in pieces: 票 (piào) — eleven strokes: on top a bar over a frame with two short verticals; under it 二, a hooked vertical and two dots. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
车票 (chēpiào) is built from 车 'vehicle' and 票 (piào) 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Script — a framed top over 二 and three strokes.
票 (piào).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, six strokes. A bar across the top. Then a frame under it: a short vertical on the left, a stroke along the top that turns down the right side, two short verticals down through the top of the frame, and a bar that closes it.
The bottom piece, five strokes. 二: a short bar, then a longer one. A vertical down the middle that hooks to the left at its foot. A dot falling to the left, then a dot falling to the right.
6 + 5 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 票 (piào), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 票 (piào)? (Eleven.) What starts the bottom piece? (二.) What sits either side of the hooked vertical? (Two dots.)

机票 (jīpiào) is built from 机, short for fēijī 'aeroplane', and 票 (piào) 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese kj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of 票 (piào), so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
hángbān — scheduled flight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for train or bus ticket, then the Mandarin for plane ticket.

You'll want to know: hángbān.
hángbān — "scheduled flight".
zhège hángbān wǎndiǎn le — This flight is delayed.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hángbān is built from háng 'to sail' and bān 'a shift, a set run', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hang and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a boat' and the second as dividing jade tokens; how the pair came to mean a flight is not settled.
