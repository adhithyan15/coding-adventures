Mandarin Chinese, chapter 267: Actors and Jokes.
6 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yǎnyuán — actor; performer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a girl, then the Mandarin for middle age.

You'll want to know: yǎnyuán.
yǎnyuán — "actor; performer".
tā shì yí ge hěn hǎo de yǎnyuán — He's a very good actor.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǎnyuán is built from yǎn and yuán, both inherited from Middle Chinese, yenX and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'wide' and quotes an older dictionary for the second, 'a number of things'. The senses 'to perform' and 'a member' are not given there, so how the pair came is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
笑话 (xiàohua) — joke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a TV show, then the Mandarin for a celebrity.

You'll want to know: 笑话.
笑话 — xiàohua — "joke".
他说了一个笑话 tā shuōle yí ge xiàohua — He told a joke.
The new character, in pieces: 笑 (xiào) — ten strokes: two small pieces on top, each a short stroke falling to the left, a short bar and a dot; under them a short stroke falling to the left, then 大. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
笑话 (xiàohua) is built from 笑 (xiào) 'to laugh' and 话 'talk', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewH and hwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'glad' and the second 'speech'. In the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 6.
笑 (xiào) — two small pieces over a short fall and 大.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — dà, tiān]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two small pieces over a short fall and 大.
笑 (xiào).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, six strokes: two small pieces side by side, each three strokes. A short stroke falling to the left, a short bar to the right from it, then a dot under the bar. Then the same three again on the right.
The bottom piece, four strokes. A short stroke falling to the left across the top. Then 大: a long bar, a stroke falling to the left, and a stroke falling to the right.
6 + 4 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 笑 (xiào), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 笑 (xiào)? (Ten.) How many small pieces sit on top? (Two, three strokes each.) Which character ends it? (大.)

Lesson 6 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a celebrity, then the Mandarin for a joke.

You'll want to know: 好笑.
好笑 — hǎoxiào — "funny".
这个笑话很好笑 zhège xiàohua hěn hǎoxiào — This joke is really funny.

The word, taken apart.
好笑 (hǎoxiào) is built from 好 'good' and 笑 (xiào) 'to laugh', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawX and sjewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): good to laugh at, the way 好吃 is good to eat.
