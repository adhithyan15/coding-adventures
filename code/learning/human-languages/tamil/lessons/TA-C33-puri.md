# புரி (puri) — "to become clear," and who it becomes clear to

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-NINAI-01, TA-LEX-TERI-01, TA-GRAMMAR-TERI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two shapes. *Nāṉ ninaikkiṟēṉ* — I do the thinking. *Enakku teriyum*
— the knowing happens to me. Say both.

## You'll want to know: புரி
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-PURI-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **புரி** — *puri* — **to be understood; to become clear**

The everyday form is **புரிகிறது** (*purigiṟadu*):

> **எனக்குத் தமிழ் புரிகிறது.** — *enakkut tamiḻ purigiṟadu* — "I understand
> Tamil."

Word for word, "**to me** Tamil is becoming clear." The verb is weak: the
softened *-giṟ-* of *pōgiṟēṉ*, not the long *-kkiṟ-* of *ninaikkiṟēṉ*.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**பு** is **ப** carrying the *u* sign, which fuses into the letter's shape
rather than hanging off it; **ரி** is **ர** with the *i* sign **ி**.

## Grammar Lens: the last slot can say "it"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-GRAMMAR-PURI-02]; assesses=[TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-02, TA-LEX-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01] -->

*Teriyum* had no person slot at all. *Purigiṟadu* **fills** it, with **-அது**
(*-adu*), "**it**."

> **புரி** + **கிற்** + **அது** → **புரிகிறது**

So the thing becoming clear is *it*, and the understander is pushed out of the
verb into the dative word **எனக்கு** (*enakku*) that Chapter 6 built for this
job. Change the understander and only that word moves:

- *enakku purigiṟadu* — I understand
- *uṉakku purigiṟadu* — you understand
- *avaṉukku purigiṟadu* — he understands

Two verbs, two mechanisms, one result: **the experiencer sits in the dative.**
Not an exception — the ordinary way to report what happens inside a person.

## The word, taken apart - four sisters, four verbs, one shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-FAMILY-01, TA-LEX-PURI-01] -->

Tamil's dictionaries keep more than one **புரி**: an old literary one, "to do,
to perform," and this everyday one. Whether they are one root is **not
settled**. Certain is the verbal noun in *-தல்*: **புரிதல்** (*puridal*),
"understanding."

The family evidence is stronger. Each sister chose a different verb, and every
one puts the understander in the dative:

- Kannada **ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥವಾಯಿತು** — *nanage arthavāyitu*
- Telugu **నాకు అర్థమైంది** — *nāku arthamaindi*
- Malayalam **എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലായി** — *enikku manassilāyi*, "it entered my mind"

Different words, one grammar. Chapter 6 showed the four dative endings were
cousins; here they do the same job in the same sentence.
