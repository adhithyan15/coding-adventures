## You'll want to know: ત્રીજું
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-TRIJU]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-MU, GU-FORM-TRAN-R, GU-ETYMON-TRAN-R-RESTORATION, GU-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE] -->

The suffix entry named **ત્રણ** among the numbers **-મું** does not build on. So
the ordinal of three is not **ત્રણમું**, and the book owes you the word it is.

> **ત્રીજું** — *trījũ* — **third**

Say **ત્રણ**, then **ત્રીજું**. The opening **ત્ર** carries over untouched —
including the **r** that chapter twelve made a fuss about, the one Gujarati put
back where Hindi has none. Only the tail differs.

Endings as before: **ત્રીજો**, **ત્રીજી**, **ત્રીજું**.

## The word, taken apart — what it did not take
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-TRIJU, GU-LEX-PANCHMU, GU-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-MU] -->

Wiktionary hands **ત્રીજું** down from Old Gujarati *trījaũ*, and that from
Sanskrit *tṛtīya*. Notice what is missing from that line: no suffix, no
addition, no **-મું** in it anywhere.

**પાંચમું** was described as something Gujarati *makes*. **ત્રીજું** is
described as something Gujarati *received*. Two ordinals in, and the dictionary
is already sorting them into two piles.
