Malayalam, chapter 104: One Present, Two Englishes.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (ñān pōkunnu) — I go, and I am going.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I go. Then say it for you and for they — and notice that the verb did not move.

You'll want to know.
You were told that Malayalam's present tense is one word for everybody. Here is the part that was not said:
It is also one word for two English tenses.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു, ñān pōkunnu, I go.
ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു, ñān pōkunnu, I am going.
Those are the same Malayalam sentence, written twice. English asks you to choose — I go to work against I am going to work — and the choice does not exist here. There is nothing to get right.

Why it's said this way.
English is the one doing something extra here. It splits its present into a habitual and an ongoing form; Malayalam does not. The ending -ഉന്നു covers what you do every day and what you are doing at this moment, and a listener takes whichever the situation offers.
This is not a gap in Malayalam. When it matters which you mean, there is a way to say so — and the one this book gives you is a word in front, not a change to the verb. That is the next lesson.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 5.
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu) — right now, I am going.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I am going. Then say the word for now.

You'll want to know.
The last lesson left one thing open: if ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (ñān pōkunnu) can mean either, how do you say you mean this moment?
You put the moment in front of it.
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു. — ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu — "Right now I am going."
The verb did not change. Not one letter of it. What changed is that a word naming the moment is standing at the front, and it is a word you have owned for a long while.

Grammar Lens: the slot again.
That front position is not new either. ഇന്ന് has stood there, and നാളെ, and a named day, and an hour with an ending on it. ഇപ്പോൾ is the same slot doing the narrowest version of its job: not today, not this Monday — now.
This is how Malayalam answers a question English answers with the verb. English changes go into am going. Malayalam leaves the verb alone and adds a word — and a word is easier than a form.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ വരുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān varunnu) — and there is no word for am.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say to come. Then say right now I am going.

You'll want to know.
Take a second verb through it and watch nothing bend:
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ വരുന്നു. — ippōḷ ñān varunnu — "Right now I am coming."
You have said that verb before. varunnu was in the tense table you were given for varuka, and you drilled it there beside vannu and varuṁ. What is new is not the word — it is that it does the same double duty പോകുന്നു does.

Grammar Lens: the word you must not reach for.
English says I am going, in two words. When you go looking for the Malayalam word to put where am is, you will find ആണ് — the copula you were given early on for is, and which you have since heard carrying am as well.
Do not put it here.
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ വരുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān varunnu) is right: one verb, nothing added. Putting a copula in the middle of it — ippōḷ ñān āṇŭ varunnu — is ungrammatical, and it is written here in sound only, so that the shape never sits on the page looking like Malayalam you could copy.
ആണ് joins a thing to what it is — a name, a description. That is its whole job, and propping up another verb is not part of it. The whole of am going already lives inside വരുന്നു; there is no second word for it to be.
Malayalam does put verbs together in other places — you have met ഉണ്ട് and കഴിയും doing exactly that. This is not one of those places, and ആണ് is not one of those verbs.

Wrap-up Recall.

Right now I am coming? (ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ വരുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān varunnu).) How many words carry am coming? (One — വരുന്നു.) Does ആണ് belong in that sentence? (No — it joins a thing to what it is; it does not help another verb.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
നിങ്ങൾ എപ്പോൾ പോകും? (niṅṅaḷ eppōḷ pōkuṁ?) — asked long ago, answered now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say now, then, and when? — the three you were given as a set.

You'll want to know.
Nothing about this pair is new to you. You were taught the three beginnings as a system — ഇ- near, അ- far, എ- a question — and then handed this row of it whole:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
ഇപ്പോൾ, ippōḷ, now.
അപ്പോൾ, appōḷ, then.
എപ്പോൾ, eppōḷ, when?
What is new is the answer. You were given the question നിങ്ങൾ എപ്പോൾ പോകും? (niṅṅaḷ eppōḷ pōkuṁ?) with a future on the end of it, and no way to reply except with another future.

Guided Practice.
Now you can answer it with this moment instead:
— നിങ്ങൾ എപ്പോൾ പോകും? — niṅṅaḷ eppōḷ pōkuṁ? — "When will you go?" — ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു. — ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu. — "I'm going right now."

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

What you've built.
The question was asked in the future and answered in the present, and that is not a mismatch — it is the ordinary way to say you are on your way. The question asks about a plan; the answer says the plan is happening now.
And the answer needs one verb to do it. No word went in where English puts am.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
When? (എപ്പോൾ.) Its two partners? (ഇപ്പോൾ now, അപ്പോൾ then.) Answer നിങ്ങൾ എപ്പോൾ പോകും? (niṅṅaḷ eppōḷ pōkuṁ?) with this moment. (ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu).)


Lesson 5 of 5.
(I go, I am going, right now) (ippōḷ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say I go, then I am going, then right now I am going.

Grammar Lens: one form, and a word in front of it.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു, ñān pōkunnu, I go and I am going.
ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു, ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu, right now I am going.
The verb is the same in both rows. That is the whole chapter in one column. Where English changes go to am going and then adds right now, Malayalam never touches the verb at all — it puts a word in the slot at the front, the same slot that has held today, tomorrow, a named day and a named hour.

What you've built.
Not one word in this chapter was new. The verb ending was named for you long ago; ഇപ്പോൾ and എപ്പോൾ have both been yours for chapters. What you did not have was permission to use one form for two English tenses, and the warning that goes with it.
That warning is worth keeping. English's am has no home in ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ വരുന്നു (ippōḷ ñān varunnu). ആണ് exists, and it joins a thing to what it is. Propping up another verb is not its job — and the am going is already inside വരുന്നു, so there is nothing left for a second word to carry.

Wrap-up Recall.

Two English sentences, one Malayalam one — which? (I go and I am going, both ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു (ñān pōkunnu).) How do you pin it to this moment? (ഇപ്പോൾ, at the front.) Where does am go? (Nowhere — it is inside the verb.)
