Latin, chapter 399: Trust and Betrayal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
perfidia, perfidiae — bad faith, treachery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fixed, then the Latin for void.

You'll want to know: perfidia, perfidiae.
perfidia, perfidiae — "bad faith, treachery".
Perfidiam eius omnēs nōvērunt. Everyone knew of his treachery.
perfidia, perfidiae f., per- past + fidēs, faith: going past one's word. English perfidy.

The word, taken apart.
perfidia is built on perfidus "faithless" (Lewis & Short), from per- "past, beyond" and fidēs "faith": going beyond one's word. English perfidy comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

perfidus, perfida, perfidum — "treacherous, faithless".
Perfidō amīcō nēmō crēdit. Nobody trusts a faithless friend.
perfidus, perfida, perfidum: the opposite of fīdus, faithful.

The word, taken apart.
perfidus is built from per- "past, beyond" and fidēs "faith" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): one who goes beyond his word.

You'll want to know: fallācia, fallāciae.
fallācia, fallāciae — "a deceit, a trick".
Fallāciīs eius nōn crēdō. I do not trust his tricks.
fallācia, fallāciae f., built on fallō, to deceive. A favourite word for a slave's trick in comedy. English fallacy.

The word, taken apart.
fallācia is built on fallāx "deceitful" (EtymDB), which comes from fallō "to deceive" (Lewis & Short). English fallacy comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dēcipiō, dēcipere — to deceive, to cheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for treacherous, then the Latin for a deceit.

You'll want to know: dēcipiō, dēcipere.
dēcipiō, dēcipere — "to deceive, to cheat".
Mercātor malus multōs dēcēpit. The dishonest merchant cheated many people.
dēcipiō, dēcipere, dēcēpī, dēceptum, dē + capiō: to catch out. English deceive and deception.

The word, taken apart.
dēcipiō is built from dē and capiō "to take, to catch" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to catch someone out. English deceive comes from it through French.

testor is built on testis "a witness" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English testament and testify come from its family.
