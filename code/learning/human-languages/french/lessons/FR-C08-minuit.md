# minuit — midnight

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The second pivot, and it is built from a word you have had since the
very first chapter.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-MINUIT-07]; assesses=[] -->

- **minuit** = *mee-NWEE*. The **ui** is a single gliding sound, *wee*, not two
  vowels taken in turn.
- The final **t** is silent, as it is in **nuit** itself.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-MINUIT-NOX-08]; assesses=[] -->

**minuit** is Latin **media nox**, "the middle night," and the two halves are as
plain as they were in *midi*:

> **mi-** ("middle," *medius* again) + **-nuit** (*nox*, "night") = **the
> night's middle**

The second half is the **nuit** you learned in your first week. Latin *nox* has
the stem *noct-*, which English carries in **nocturnal** and, straight through
its Germanic side, in **night** itself.

So the two pivots of the French day are one idea applied twice: the **middle**
of the day, and the **middle** of the night. English builds *midday* and
*midnight* out of exactly the same two pieces.

## Grammar Lens: the same rule as midi
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**minuit** is the hour, so no number and no **heures**:

> **Il est minuit.** — "It is midnight."
