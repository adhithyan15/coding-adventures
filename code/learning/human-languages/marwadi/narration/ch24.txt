Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 24: Bread and Rolls: One New Consonant.
8 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
Hear roṭī — flatbread.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Recall the three-word food payoff, then say hand and lentils.

You'll want to know.
roṭī — flatbread.
The middle consonant is made with the tongue tip curled back, further back than the d of dāl. Hear it twice and say it once. The spelling waits for the next lesson, because one sign in it has not been taught yet.

Lesson 2 of 8.
ट (ṭa) — the curled t in flatbread.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one sign until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Say flatbread, recall the three-word transport payoff, then write hand.

Script — one sign.
ट — ṭa.
A single closed loop hanging under the headline. Set it beside the ठ of ठेला: ठ carries a second inner ring and a puff of breath, ट (ṭa) has neither.

Guided Practice.
Trace ट (ṭa) once. Copy it once. Write ठ beside it and say which one has the extra ring. Cover both, wait five seconds, and write ट (ṭa) from the curled sound cue.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Unicode Devanagari chart.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write the unaspirated curled Devanagari ta.

You'll want to know.
bāṭī — hard wheat rolls.
Two beats, both long, with the same curled middle consonant heard in roṭī. These are baked hard rather than soft, which is why they take a different name from ordinary flatbread. Hear it twice and say it once.
