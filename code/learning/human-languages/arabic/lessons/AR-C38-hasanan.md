# حسنًا (ḥasanan) — all right

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C05-NAAM-01, AR-CONCEPT-SHUKRAN-21] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **نعم** and **لا** answer a question. Neither of them says *I am
following you*, and a conversation needs that more often than it needs an answer.

- [YOU RECALL: the joiner from the lesson before, and the negator inside it — *lākin*]

## You'll want to know: حسنًا
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-FUNC-C38-HASANAN-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-SHUKRAN-21, AR-CONCEPT-C23-AFWAN-01] -->

> **حسنًا** — *ḥasanan* — **all right; fine; very well.**

> **حسنًا، لكن …** — *ḥasanan, lākin …* — **all right, but …**

The ending is one you have written twice already. **شكرًا** and *ʿafwan* wear
the same **ـًا**, the two-stroke *tanwīn* over an alif that turns an Arabic
adjective into an adverb. The root here is **حَسَن**, *ḥasan*, "good, beautiful."
So *ḥasanan* is literally *goodly*.

## Grammar Lens: agreeing, and half-agreeing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-FUNC-C38-HASANAN-01, AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01, AR-CONCEPT-C05-NAAM-01, AR-CONCEPT-C05-LA-01] -->

| said | does |
|---|---|
| **نعم** | answers a question |
| **حسنًا** | accepts what was said |
| **حسنًا، لكن …** | accepts, and then turns |

The third row is the shape almost every real disagreement takes. A learner who
can only say **نعم** or **لا** has to accept or refuse whole; this is the first
thing in the book that lets them do neither.
