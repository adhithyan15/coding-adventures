# उसने रोटी खाई (usne roṭī khāī) — the past that follows the thing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-STEMS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "she went". (*vah gaī*.) **खाना खाया?** leaves the eater out; this lesson puts the eater in.

## Grammar Lens: the doer takes ने
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C276-ERGATIVE-NE-01]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C37-KHAANA-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C97-ADDRESS-01] -->

> **उसने खाना खाया।**
>
> *usne khānā khāyā.* — "He (or she) ate the meal."

With a verb that has an object, such as eating, reading or saying, the past changes: the doer takes **ने** (*ne*). The pronouns fuse with it: **मैंने**, **तुमने**, **आपने**, **उसने**, **हमने**, **उन्होंने** (*maiṁne*, *tumne*, *āpne*, *usne*, *hamne*, *unhoṁne*), the last from **वे**. A name takes **ने** after it: **मीरा ने**. **उसने** is "he" or "she"; the verb no longer says which.

## You'll want to know: the verb follows the thing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C276-OBJECT-AGREEMENT-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C209-THG209-05, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02, HI-CONCEPT-C34-PADHNA-01, HI-LEX-LOOK, HI-JOIN-KI-01, HI-LEX-C45-ASK-01] -->

> **मीरा ने रोटी खाई।**
>
> *Mīrā ne roṭī khāī.* — "Mira ate a roti."

**रोटी** is feminine, so **खाई**, whoever ate it. With **खाना**, masculine, **खाया**; with books, **मैंने किताबें पढ़ीं** (*maiṁne kitābeṁ paṛhīṁ*). When the thing takes **को**, or there is none, the verb stays **-आ**: **उसने मीरा को देखा** (*usne Mīrā ko dekhā*), "he saw Mira".

| sentence | the verb follows |
|---|---|
| **वह गई** — no thing | the one who went |
| **उसने रोटी खाई** — **ने** and a thing | the thing |
| **उसने मीरा को देखा** — the thing has **को** | nothing: **-आ** |

Four verbs make this past their own way, for a masculine thing and a feminine one: **किया** / **की**, **दिया** / **दी**, **लिया** / **ली**, **पिया** / **पी** (*kiyā* / *kī*, *diyā* / *dī*, *liyā* / *lī*, *piyā* / *pī*), from **करना**, **देना**, **लेना** and **पीना**.

**कहना** (*kahnā*), "to say", is regular. Your **क्या कहा?** was its past: **उसने कहा कि वह कल आएगा** (*usne kahā ki vah kal āegā*), "he said he would come tomorrow". **लाना**, "to bring", does not take **ने**: **वह फल लाया** (*vah phal lāyā*).
