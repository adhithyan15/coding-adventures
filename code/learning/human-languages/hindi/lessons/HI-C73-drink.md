## You'll want to know: पीना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C73-EATING-05]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C37-KHAANA-01] -->

**पीना** (*pīnā*) — "to drink".

Sanskrit पिबति (*pibati*), "drinks", from a root so old that Latin *bibere*, "to
drink", is the same word wearing different clothes. English *potion* and
*potable* come down the Latin side of that family.

Now the pair. **खाना** you already own as "food" — and खाना is **also** "to eat",
exactly as गाना was both the song and the singing. Put them together and Hindi
has one of its most-used pairs:

> **खाना** — food, and to eat. **पीना** — to drink.

**मैं चाय पीता हूँ** is "I drink tea", in the frame you have used all along. And
**मैं खाना खाता हूँ** puts the word into one breath twice — once for the food,
once for the eating — with nothing between them but the ending.
