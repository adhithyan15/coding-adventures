Telugu, chapter 379: Crowds and Cries.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఎగబడు (egabaḍu) — to throng; to crowd in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to grapple, then the Telugu for to gather round.

You'll want to know: ఎగబడు.
ఎగబడు — egabaḍu — "to throng; to crowd in".
సినిమా టిక్కెట్ల కోసం జనం ఎగబడ్డారు. sinimā ṭikkeṭla kōsaṁ janaṁ egabaḍḍāru. People thronged for film tickets.
Past (I …): ఎగబడ్డాను, egabaḍḍānu.
It shows eagerness: everyone pushing to get there first.

The word, taken apart.
ఎగబడు (egabaḍu) is a Telugu verb whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
పడిపోవు (paḍipōvu) — to fall down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to gather round, then the Telugu for to throng.

You'll want to know: పడిపోవు.
పడిపోవు — paḍipōvu — "to fall down".
తోపులాటలో ఒక ముసలాయన పడిపోయాడు. tōpulāṭalō oka musalāyana paḍipōyāḍu. An old man fell down in the crush.
Past (I …): పడిపోయాను, paḍipōyānu.
పడు, to fall, with పోవు: a fall that happens to you.

The word, taken apart.
పడిపోవు (paḍipōvu) is a native compound: paḍu, "to fall" (DEDR 3852), and pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 3 of 5.
బాధితుడు (bādhituḍu) — a victim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to throng, then the Telugu for to fall down.

You'll want to know: బాధితుడు.
బాధితుడు — bādhituḍu — "a victim".
బాధితులకు ప్రభుత్వం సహాయం చేసింది. bādhitulaku prabhutvaṁ sahāyaṁ cēsindi. The government helped the victims.
Plural: బాధితులు, bādhitulu.
You know బాధ, pain: బాధితుడు (bādhituḍu), one who is made to suffer.

The word, taken apart.
బాధితుడు (bādhituḍu) is borrowed from Sanskrit bādhita, "hard-pressed, oppressed" (CDIAL 9205).

Lesson 4 of 5.
మొరపెట్టుకో (morapeṭṭukō) — to petition; to plead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to fall down, then the Telugu for a victim.

You'll want to know: మొరపెట్టుకో.
మొరపెట్టుకో — morapeṭṭukō — "to petition; to plead".
బాధితులు అధికారులకు మొరపెట్టుకున్నారు. bādhitulu adhikārulaku morapeṭṭukunnāru. The victims pleaded with the officials.
Past (I …): మొరపెట్టుకున్నాను, morapeṭṭukunnānu.
To whom takes -కు: అధికారులకు మొరపెట్టుకో (morapeṭṭukō).

The word, taken apart.
మొరపెట్టుకో (morapeṭṭukō) is a native compound: moṟa, "a cry, a scream" (DEDR 5013), and peṭṭukō, "to put for oneself" (DEDR 4390).

Lesson 5 of 5.
వేడుకో (vēḍukō) — to beg; to entreat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a victim, then the Telugu for to petition.

You'll want to know: వేడుకో.
వేడుకో — vēḍukō — "to beg; to entreat".
ఆమె దొంగను తన సంచి ఇవ్వమని వేడుకుంది. āme doṅganu tana sañci ivvamani vēḍukundi. She begged the thief to give her bag back.
Past (I …): వేడుకున్నాను, vēḍukunnānu.
Gentler than బతిమాలు; in the old stories, the weak beg the strong.

The word, taken apart.
వేడుకో (vēḍukō) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5528 lists Telugu vēḍu, "to pray, beg, entreat", here with the self-ending -kō.
