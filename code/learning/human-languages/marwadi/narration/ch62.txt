Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 62: Window, Chair, Table, Wall, Roof.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
खिड़की (khiṛkī) — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for an onion, then the Marwadi for sweets.

You'll want to know: खिड़की.
खिड़की — khiṛkī — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
खिड़की (khiṛkī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from khaṭakkikā, "side door". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 3770); the match is through Hindi khiṛkī. CDIAL thinks it first meant something made of matting.

Lesson 3 of 5.
मेज (mej) — a table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a window, then the Marwadi for a chair.

You'll want to know: मेज.
मेज — mej — "a table".

The word, taken apart.
मेज (mej) is a loan, usually traced to Persian mēz, "table". Many take the Persian word from Portuguese mesa, "table"; Marwadi says the z as j.

भींत (bhī̃t) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bhitti, "mat of split reeds; wall". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 9494); the match is through Hindi bhī̃t and Gujarati bhī̃t.

Lesson 5 of 5.
छात (chhāt) — a roof.

You'll want to know: छात.
छात — chhāt — "a roof".

The word, taken apart.
छात (chhāt) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from chatti, "covering", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 4971); the match is through Hindi chāt. It is built on the root chad, "to cover".
