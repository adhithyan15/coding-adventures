Lesson 1 of 5.
cathedra, cathedrae — a chair with a back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a friend, then the Latin for a cupboard.

You'll want to know: cathedra, cathedrae.
cathedra, cathedrae — "a chair with a back".

The word, taken apart.
cathedra was borrowed from Greek kathedra, "a seat, a chair" (Lewis & Short), from kata, "down", and hedra, "seat" (EtymDB 2.1). A bishop's cathedra gave English cathedral its name.

Lesson 2 of 5.
scamnum, scamnī — a bench.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cupboard, then the Latin for a chair with a back.

You'll want to know: scamnum, scamnī.
scamnum, scamnī — "a bench".

The word, taken apart.
scamnum is inherited from Proto-Italic skabnom, "bench", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European skabh-, "to prop up" (EtymDB 2.1); scabellum, "a footstool", is its diminutive (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
lanterna, lanternae — a lantern.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a carpet, then the Latin for a torch.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count on past the ninth hour: nōnus, decimus. Say decima hōra, then say whether it is ante or post merīdiem.

You'll want to know: lanterna, lanternae.
lanterna, lanternae — "a lantern".

The word, taken apart.
lanterna comes from Greek lamptēr, "a torch, a lamp" (Lewis & Short), from lampō, "to shine", reshaped with the ending of lucerna, "lamp".
