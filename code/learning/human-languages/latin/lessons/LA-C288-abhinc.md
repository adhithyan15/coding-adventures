# abhinc — ago, and for how long

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C263-TEMP263-04, LA-LEX-C103-ANNUS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "for a long time". (*Diū.*) Then "a year". (*Annus.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Diū* says long without saying how long. Latin can count it
out, and the case it counts in is the accusative.

## Grammar Lens: the accusative of how long
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C288-ACC-DURATION-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-HORA-01, LA-LEX-NUMBERS-1-5-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-AVI-02] -->

> **Trēs annōs Rōmae habitāvī.** — I lived in Rome for three years.
> **Duās hōrās ambulāvimus.** — We walked for two hours.

[PAUSE 2s] No word for "for": the stretch of time stands in the
accusative, *annōs*, *hōrās*, and the verb says what filled it. *Duās* is
*duo* agreeing with *hōrās*.

## You'll want to know: abhinc
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C288-ABHINC-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C103-ANNUS-04, LA-LEX-NUMBERS-6-10-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01] -->

**abhinc** — "ago". It counts back from now, and the count goes in the
accusative too:

> **Abhinc trēs annōs Rōmam vēnī.** — I came to Rome three years ago.
> **Abhinc decem diēs nāvis solvit.** — The ship sailed ten days ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Put the two together and a life has a shape: *abhinc* for when
it began, the accusative for how long it lasted. Cicero uses *abhinc* with
the accusative in his speeches and letters (Lewis & Short).

## The word, taken apart: abhinc
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**abhinc** is built from *ab*, "from", and *hinc*, "from here, from this
point" (EtymDB 2.1), and *hinc* in turn comes from *hīc*, "here". So the
word is "from this point", counted backwards into the past. Lewis & Short
note that some early writers also used it forwards, "from now on", a
sense that did not last.
