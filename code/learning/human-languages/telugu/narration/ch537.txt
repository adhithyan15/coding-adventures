Telugu, chapter 537: Childhood Games.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
బొంగరం (boṅgaraṁ) — a spinning top.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for knitting, then the Telugu for a card game.

You'll want to know: బొంగరం.
బొంగరం — boṅgaraṁ — "a spinning top".
పిల్లలు వీధిలో బొంగరాలు తిప్పుతున్నారు. pillalu vīdhilō boṅgarālu tipputunnāru. The children are spinning tops in the street.
Plural: బొంగరాలు, boṅgarālu.
బొంగరం (boṅgaraṁ) తిప్పు, to spin a top.

The word, taken apart.
బొంగరం (boṅgaraṁ) is a Telugu word for a spinning top; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a card game, then the Telugu for a spinning top.

You'll want to know: గోళీలు.
గోళీలు — gōḷīlu — "marbles".
చిన్నప్పుడు మేము గోళీలు ఆడేవాళ్ళం. cinnappuḍu mēmu gōḷīlu āḍēvāḷḷaṁ. As children we used to play marbles.
One marble is గోళీ.

The word, taken apart.
గోళీలు (gōḷīlu) is usually traced to Hindustani golī, "a small ball", from Sanskrit gōla, "a ball", with golikā, "a little ball" (CDIAL 4321).

దాగుడుమూతలు — dāguḍumūtalu — "hide-and-seek".
సాయంత్రం పిల్లలు దాగుడుమూతలు ఆడారు. sāyantraṁ pillalu dāguḍumūtalu āḍāru. In the evening the children played hide-and-seek.
దాగు, to hide, is at its heart.

The word, taken apart.
దాగుడుమూతలు (dāguḍumūtalu) is built on native dāgu, "to lie hidden" (DEDR 63), and mūta, "a closing", for the shut eyes of the seeker.

A team game of raids, played while holding one breath; also called కబడ్డీ.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
గెంతు (gentu) — to hop; to skip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for hide-and-seek, then the Telugu for kabaddi.

You'll want to know: గెంతు.
గెంతు — gentu — "to hop; to skip".
వర్షంలో పిల్లలు సంతోషంగా గెంతారు. varṣaṁlō pillalu santōṣaṅgā gentāru. The children hopped happily in the rain.
Past (I …): గెంతాను, gentānu.
Jumping for joy or in play.

The word, taken apart.
గెంతు (gentu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1212 lists Telugu gentu, "to hop, to jump", with Tamil kenti, "to hop, to skip".
