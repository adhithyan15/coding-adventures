# काम — the noun that was there all along

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-KARNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You learned **काम करना** long ago as one expression meaning *to
work*. Say it, and then ask yourself what the first half of it means on its own.

## You'll want to know: काम
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04]; assesses=[] -->

**काम** (*kām*) — work, a job, a task.

**काम पर** — at work. **मैं काम पर जाता हूँ।** — I go to work.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04, HI-CONCEPT-C05-KARNA-01] -->

**काम करना** was never one word. It is **काम** plus **करना** — literally *to do
work* — and Hindi builds an enormous number of verbs this way: a noun, plus the
verb *to do*.

| the pattern | what it makes |
|---|---|
| काम + करना | to work |
| a noun + करना | to do that noun |

Knowing this turns a memorised phrase into a **machine**. Any noun you meet can
be handed to **करना**, and the result is usually the verb a learner would
otherwise look up separately.

The word comes from Sanskrit **कर्म** (*karma*), "act, deed" — the same word
English borrowed whole. A *karma* is a thing done, and a **काम** is a thing to
do.

## Grammar Lens: which postposition a workplace takes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| मैं काम पर जाता हूँ। | I go to work. |
| मैं बस से काम पर जाता हूँ। | I go to work by bus. |

**पर** for the place you end up at, **से** for what carries you there. Both of
them trail their nouns, and the verb stays at the end however many of them you
stack in front of it.
