Gujarati, chapter 436: Hot Winds and Mercury.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
લૂ (lū) — a hot summer wind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a forecast, then the Gujarati for to flash.

You'll want to know: લૂ.
લૂ — lū — "a hot summer wind".
મે મહિનામાં બપોરે ગરમ લૂ ફૂંકાય છે. me mahināmā̃ bapore garam lū phū̃kāy che. In May a hot wind blows in the afternoon.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
લૂ (lū) CDIAL lists Hindi lūhar and Sindhi lūha, "the hot summer wind", under lūṣā, "burning", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 11099); it gives no Gujarati form, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 2 of 5.
બફારો (baphāro) — steamy heat (airless, before rain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to flash, then the Gujarati for a hot summer wind.

You'll want to know: બફારો.
બફારો — baphāro — "steamy heat (airless, before rain)".
વરસાદ પહેલાં ઘણો બફારો હતો. varsād pahelā̃ ghaṇo baphāro hato. Before the rain it was very steamy.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
બફારો (baphāro) goes back to bāṣpākāra, "having the form of vapour", a form CDIAL reconstructs; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "vapour, sultriness" (CDIAL 9224).

Lesson 3 of 5.
પારો (pāro) — mercury; the temperature (on a thermometer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a hot summer wind, then the Gujarati for steamy heat.

You'll want to know: પારો.
પારો — pāro — "mercury; the temperature (on a thermometer)".
આજે પારો ચાળીસ ડિગ્રી પર પહોંચ્યો. āje pāro cāḷīs ḍigrī par pahõcyo. Today the temperature reached forty degrees.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પારો (pāro) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit pārada, "quicksilver"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 8104). The mercury in a thermometer gave the news its પારો.

Lesson 4 of 5.
દઝાડવું (dajhāḍvũ) — to scorch; to burn (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for steamy heat, then the Gujarati for mercury.

You'll want to know: દઝાડવું.
દઝાડવું — dajhāḍvũ — "to scorch; to burn (someone)".
બપોરનો તડકો પગને દઝાડે છે. baporno taḍko pagne dajhāḍe che. The afternoon sun scorches your feet.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે હાથ દઝાડ્યો, teṇe hāth dajhāḍyo.

The word, taken apart.
દઝાડવું (dajhāḍvũ) is the causative partner of દાઝવું, "to be burnt", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit dahyatē, "is burnt" (CDIAL 6248): to make something burn.

Lesson 5 of 5.
શેકાવું (śekāvũ) — to bake (in the heat); to be roasted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for mercury, then the Gujarati for to scorch.

You'll want to know: શેકાવું.
શેકાવું — śekāvũ — "to bake (in the heat); to be roasted".
આવા તડકામાં અમે શેકાઈ ગયા. āvā taḍkāmā̃ ame śekāī gayā. We got roasted in sunshine like this.
Past (he …): તે તડકામાં શેકાયો, te taḍkāmā̃ śekāyo.

The word, taken apart.
શેકાવું (śekāvũ) is the intransitive partner of શેકવું, "to roast", which goes back to sēkk, "to heat, to roast", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 13581).
