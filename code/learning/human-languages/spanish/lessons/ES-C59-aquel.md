# aquel — the far one, and an old friend in disguise

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-ESTE, ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-ESE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Mine, here: *este libro*. Yours, there: *ese libro*.

[PAUSE 1s] Now the one across the room, belonging to neither of us.

## The word, taken apart: aquel
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-AQUEL, ES-ETYMON-AQUEL-ACCU-ILLE]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-ILLE-ILLA] -->

**aquel libro** — *that book over there*.

| | |
|---|---|
| ***aquel*** *libro* | that book, over there |
| ***aquella*** *casa* | that house, over there |
| ***aquellos*** *libros* | those books, over there |
| ***aquellas*** *casas* | those houses, over there |

[PAUSE 2s] Look at the middle of that word. ***-el-***. You have met it before,
in your **first chapter**.

[PAUSE 2s] Latin ***ille*** went two ways at once. Said quickly and without
stress, leaning on the noun after it, it wore down to ***el*** — the article.
Said with stress, and propped up in front with **accu-** (roughly *look,
there*), it stayed a pointing word: **accu + ille → aquel**.

So *el* and *aquel* are the same Latin word, and the difference between them is
two thousand years of one being emphasised and the other not.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-THREE-DISTANCES]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-AQUEL] -->

That leaves Spanish with **three**, where English has two:

| | |
|---|---|
| *este* | by me |
| *ese* | by you |
| *aquel* | by neither of us |

[PAUSE 2s] English collapses the last two into *that* and then patches the
difference back on when it matters — *that one over there*. Spanish keeps the
three apart in the word itself, so it never needs the patch.

[PAUSE 2s] And the middle one is the one to watch. If you find yourself reaching
for *aquel* because the thing is a few steps away, stop and ask the real
question: is it near the person you are talking to? If it is, it is *ese*, however
far it is from you.
