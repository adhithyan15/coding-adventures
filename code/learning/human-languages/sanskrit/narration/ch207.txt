Lesson 1 of 5.
दीव्यति (dīvyati) — gambles, plays.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for shuts, then the Sanskrit for opens.

You'll want to know: दीव्यति.
दीव्यति — dīvyati — "gambles, plays".

The word, taken apart.
dīvyati comes from the root √div, "to cast dice, play, gamble" (MW), as its present. MW keeps this √div apart from div, "sky, heaven", the word behind the god Dyaus.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: आस्वादयति.
आस्वादयति — āsvādayati — "tastes".

The word, taken apart.
āsvādayati is built on ā- and the causative of the root √svad, "to taste well, be sweet" (MW). MW derives the root, probably, from su "well" and √ad "eat", and compares its adjective svādu, "sweet", with Latin suāvis and English sweet.

Lesson 4 of 5.
उपदिशति (upadiśati) — advises, instructs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for flows, then the Sanskrit for tastes.

You'll want to know: उपदिशति.
उपदिशति — upadiśati — "advises, instructs".

The word, taken apart.
upadiśati is built on upa, "towards", and the root √diś, "to point out, show" (MW), the root of diśā, "direction". To instruct someone is to point things out to them, and the noun upadeśa means "teaching".

Lesson 5 of 5.
सम्मानयति (sammānayati) — honours.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tastes, then the Sanskrit for advises.

You'll want to know: सम्मानयति.
सम्मानयति — sammānayati — "honours".

The word, taken apart.
sammānayati is built on sam and mānayati, "esteems, honours" (CDIAL 10047), which MW calls the causative of the root √man, "to think". To honour someone is to make them thought of. MW compares √man with Latin meminisse, "to remember", and English mean.
