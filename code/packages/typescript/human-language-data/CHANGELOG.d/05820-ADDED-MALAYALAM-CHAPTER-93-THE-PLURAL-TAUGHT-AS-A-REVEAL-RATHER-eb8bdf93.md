### Added — Malayalam chapter 93, the plural, taught as a reveal rather than an introduction

- `ML-A1-N-06` closes. Malayalam A1 coverage 199/243 -> **200/243 (82%)**, with
  43 points unmapped. No new vocabulary: every noun in the chapter was already
  owned, and the ending itself was already being pronounced.
- **The point's own note was wrong, and it was checked before it was spent.** It
  called this "the cheapest grammar lesson available to this track" because
  `-kal` supposedly sat inside four taught headwords -- *nirangal*, *maasangal*,
  *shareera-bhaagangal*, *kaalangal*. Those four are FILE SLUGS.
  `ML-C11-nirangal` carries the four colour words as its headword;
  `ML-C16-kollavarsham-maasangal` carries the twelve month names. A learner never
  reads a filename.
- A token sweep of all 393 Malayalam lesson files found the sequence `-kal` in
  exactly two words -- **thinkal** (Monday) and **makal** (daughter) -- and
  neither is a plural. The suffix had been met **zero** times, not four.
- The same sweep found the better hook. **ningal**, the respectful *you* from the
  second chapter, ends in `-ngngal`, and `ML-CONCEPT-C02-RESPECT-BY-PLURAL-01`
  had already told the learner that word IS a plural -- without ever isolating
  the ending that made it one. So `ML-C93-angal` reveals a piece of language the
  learner has been saying for months.
- `ML-C93-kal` teaches the ending on a plain noun (*kutti/kuttikal*,
  *kata/katakal*) and makes the point that the front of the word does not move,
  which is the shape every ending in this track has had.
- `ML-C93-angal` teaches what a noun ending in the anusvara does instead
  (*pustakam/pustakangngal*, *maram/marangngal*), and ties it to the money
  chapter's observation that a word ending that way says in advance how it will
  behave.
- `ML-C93-counted` covers the label's second half -- a noun with a numeral in
  front needs no ending -- and **cost nothing**, because the corpus was already
  doing it correctly in three attested lines: *ampathu rupa*, *nuru rupa*,
  *rantu mani*. The lesson points at the learner's own sentences rather than
  asserting a rule from outside.
- **njangal was cut from the reveal mid-draft.** Chapter 71 sits on
  `SPINE-READ-SIGNS-AND-NOTICES`, which the walk reaches AFTER
  `SPINE-DEFINITE-REFERENCE`, so its atom is not available at chapter 93.
  Chapter number is not walk order, and `validate` is what said so.
