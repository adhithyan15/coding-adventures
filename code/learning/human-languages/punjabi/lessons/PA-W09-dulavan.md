# ੈ — one stroke becomes two

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-E-MATRA-01, PA-SCRIPT-DULAINKAR-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-DULAVA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **ਤੇ** once: the single slanting stroke above the line is the
*e* sign you met in **ਨਮਸਤੇ**. Then write **ਨੂ** from the session before, with
its mark hanging below.

## Script — one mark
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-DULAVAN-01]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-E-MATRA-01, PA-SCRIPT-MA-01] -->

Double that slanting stroke and the vowel changes: **ੈ**, the **dulāvāṁ**,
sounds *ai*. Trace **ਮੈ** twice and say *mai*.

Gurmukhi keeps a pattern here that is worth noticing on the page rather than
being told about: one stroke above for *e*, two for *ai*; one hook below for
*u*, two for *ū*. Counting strokes, not memorising shapes.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-DULAVAN-01, PA-SCRIPT-E-MATRA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: observe-trace -->

Cover the models. Write **ਤੇ**, then **ਮੈ**. Compare only the number of strokes
above the line.
