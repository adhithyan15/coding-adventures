# পড়া — to read, read

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-DDA-01, BN-SCRIPT-PA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **ড়** and say what the dot did. Then write **প**.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-PORA-READ-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-PA-01, BN-SCRIPT-DDA-01, BN-SCRIPT-NUKTA-01, BN-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01] -->

> **প** + **ড়** + **া** → **পড়া**

- **প** — with its inherent **ô**
- **ড়** — the flap: curled tongue, one strike
- **া** — the vowel sign

**pôṛā** — *to read.* Three pieces, and the middle one is the whole reason this
word waited: without the dot it would be *pôḍā*, and Bengali does not say that.

The word is worth having on the page rather than only in the ear, because it is
the one word in this book that names what the book is asking you to do. The verbs
chapter gave it to you as a sound. The flap it hangs on arrived one lesson ago.

One thing to hear rather than see. The flap is **fast**. A learner who has been
told "curl the tongue back" tends to hold the position and produce a long,
deliberate *ḍ*; the Bengali sound is a single strike in passing, gone before it
is noticed. If it feels careful, it is wrong.

And two words from the food chapter, back for a moment:

> **চা** · **জল**
