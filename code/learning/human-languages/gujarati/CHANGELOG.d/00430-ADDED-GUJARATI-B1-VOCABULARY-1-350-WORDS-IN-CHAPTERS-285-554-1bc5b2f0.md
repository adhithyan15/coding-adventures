## Added — Gujarati B1 vocabulary, 1,350 words in chapters 285–554

- Added 270 chapters of five words each (1,350 headwords, 351 of them
  verbs), one word per lesson, with eight reviews in each of five tranches:
  - 285–338: narrating (theft and the courts, accidents and fire, storms,
    floods and earthquakes, rescue and relief, the news, kings and wars,
    festivals, legends and books);
  - 339–392: reasons and conditions (cause and effect, argument and proof,
    the law, government and citizens, values, research, then conditions,
    chance, danger, effort and success);
  - 393–446: travel (trains, roads and vehicles, flights and borders, a
    place to stay, the way, the neighbourhood and the town, money and the
    market, the weather and nature);
  - 447–500: experience (feelings and character, the body and health, the
    family);
  - 501–554: crafts and work, the kitchen, the arts and wrestling, then
    plans, luck and choices, and sport.
- Every verb shows its past with the right agreement, and an object verb
  shows the -એ doer (પોલીસે ચોરને પકડ્યો); every noun's gender is checked
  against every word that agrees with it. Examples use only words taught
  earlier and only the grammar the core teaches: no passive and no short
  (subjunctive) form outside the negative future.
- The first words of chapters 285–292 practise the core's last grammar
  points again, two words each; ઝ, ઃ, ઇ, ૌ and ઋ each come back twice in
  chapters 295–323.
- Every word has a cited origin: CDIAL (with its Gujarati row), Monier-
  Williams, or a named Persian, Arabic or English donor hedged as the usual
  tracing; where no source settles a word, its note says so. No etymology is
  invented. Root slugs reuse the corpus spelling of each etymon.
- Echo words, a taught word plus a light verb, plain derivations of taught
  words, near-synonym clusters beyond two, and homographs of inflected forms
  were replaced during review.
