# fini · vendu — the other two classes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every verb you have met so far ends in **-er**. That is by far the
biggest group, and it is not the only one.

## Grammar Lens: three classes, three vowels
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-PARTICIPE-IR-RE-04]; assesses=[] -->

French verbs are sorted by how the infinitive ends, and each group makes its
participle with its own vowel:

| infinitive ends in | participle ends in | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-er** | **-é** | *parler* → *parlé* |
| **-ir** | **-i** | *finir* → *fini* |
| **-re** | **-u** | *vendre* → *vendu* |

*finir* ("to finish") and *vendre* ("to sell") are the standard examples, and
they are here as **examples of the pattern** rather than words to add to your
vocabulary — neither has appeared in this book yet. What you want from this
lesson is the shape: when you meet a verb, its infinitive ending already tells
you what its participle will look like.

That is three facts, not three hundred. And **-er** is the group that matters
most in practice: it holds the overwhelming majority of French verbs, and it is
the only group still taking new members — every verb French invents or borrows
becomes an *-er* verb.
