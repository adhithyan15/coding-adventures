Arabic, chapter 468: Plants.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نبات (nabāt) — plant life; a plant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to die out, then the Arabic for to squander.

You'll want to know: نبات.
نبات — nabāt — "plant life; a plant".
رأينا نباتا غريبا بجانب الشلال. raʾaynā nabātan gharīban bi-jānib ash-shallāl. We saw a strange plant beside the waterfall.

The word, taken apart.
نبات (nabāt) is built on the native Arabic root n-b-t, "to grow, to sprout" (Lane).

زرع جدي شجيرة (shujayra) ورد في الحديقة. zaraʿa jaddī shujayrat ward fī al-ḥadīqa. My grandfather planted a rose bush in the garden.
The pattern fuʿayla makes a small one: shujayra is a little shajara, "a tree".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ذبل (dhabala) — to wilt; to wither.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for plant life, then the Arabic for a shrub.

You'll want to know: ذبل.
ذبل — dhabala — "to wilt; to wither".
ذبلت الزهرة في الشمس. dhabalat az-zahra fī ash-shams. The flower wilted in the sun.
Present: يذبل, yadhbul.

The word, taken apart.
ذبل (dhabala) is usually traced to the native Arabic root dh-b-l, "to wither". It is the plain verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أزهر (azhara) — to bloom; to flower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a shrub, then the Arabic for to wilt.

You'll want to know: أزهر.
أزهر — azhara — "to bloom; to flower".
أزهرت الأشجار في الربيع. azharat al-ashjār fī ar-rabīʿ. The trees blossomed in spring.
Present: يزهر, yuzhir.

The word, taken apart.
أزهر (azhara) is built on the native Arabic root z-h-r, "flowers; to shine" (Lane). It is Form IV, "to put out flowers"; zahra, "a flower", shares the root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
أثمر (athmara) — to bear fruit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to wilt, then the Arabic for to bloom.

You'll want to know: أثمر.
أثمر — athmara — "to bear fruit".
أثمرت شجرة التين في الصيف. athmarat shajarat at-tīn fī aṣ-ṣayf. The fig tree bore fruit in summer.
Present: يثمر, yuthmir.
Work can bear fruit too: the sense carries over as in English.

The word, taken apart.
أثمر (athmara) is built on the native Arabic root th-m-r, "fruit" (Lane). It is Form IV, "to put out fruit".
