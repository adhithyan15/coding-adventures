# Six to ten — voice first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-ETYMON-NUMBERS-1-5-FAMILY-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Count *onru* through *aintu*. Keep your pencil down: five new
meanings and sounds come before their shapes.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-NUMBERS-6-10-01]; assesses=[] -->

| quantity | said |
|---|---|
| 6 | ***aaru*** |
| 7 | ***eezhu*** |
| 8 | ***ettu*** |
| 9 | ***onpatu*** |
| 10 | ***pattu*** |

Say the row once slowly, then once at counting speed. [REPEAT x2]

## Why it's said this way — seven keeps a rare sound
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-NUMBERS-6-10-02]; assesses=[] -->

The middle of *eezhu*, seven, has Tamil's characteristic curled *zh*. Practise
the sound now; its letter waits.

## The word, taken apart — nine leans on ten
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-GRAMMAR-NUMBERS-6-10-03]; assesses=[] -->

Nine, *onpatu*, grew from one plus ten: one short of *pattu*. Say *on-patu*,
then *onpatu*.

- [YOU SAY: *aaru, eezhu, ettu, onpatu, pattu*]
- [YOU HEAR: *eezhu*; YOU SHOW: 7]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-NUMBERS-6-10-01, TA-LEX-NUMBERS-6-10-02, TA-GRAMMAR-NUMBERS-6-10-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Count six to ten. Which word keeps the curled *zh*? (***Eezhu.***)
Which word leans on ten? (***Onpatu**, nine.*) Next, three numeral shapes.
