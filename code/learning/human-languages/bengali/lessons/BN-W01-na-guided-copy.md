# ন — copy it twice, and join the bar

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-NA-01] -->

The model stays visible for all of this lesson. Nothing is hidden today.

## Writing — guided copy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-NA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Copy **ন** once, body first and bar last, as you traced it.

Then copy it **twice side by side** — and this time draw the bodies first, both
of them, and finish with **one single bar running across the top of both**.

That one continuous line is the whole lesson. In Bengali the bar is not a mark
belonging to a letter; it is a line belonging to the **word**, which is why it is
drawn last and why it runs on. A reader's eye follows it to find where one word
stops and the next starts, so a hand that gives each letter its own short bar has
written two words where it meant one.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-NA-01] -->

Look at your pair. Is there one line across the top, or two? One line is a word.
Two is a gap you did not mean to leave.
