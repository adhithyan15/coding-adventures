Mandarin Chinese, chapter 502: Painting.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yóuhuà — oil painting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to win (a prize), then the Mandarin for a star player.

You'll want to know: yóuhuà.
yóuhuà — "oil painting".
wǒ xué le liǎng nián yóuhuà — I studied oil painting for two years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yóuhuà is built from yóu 'oil' and huà 'a painting', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yuw and hweaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second, after an older book, 'to hang': to hang up the shapes of things in the five colours.

The new character, in pieces: 画 (huà) — eight strokes: a bar on top, then five strokes as on top of 男 nán, then an open box of two strokes around them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

国画 (guóhuà) is built from 国 'a country' and 画 (huà) 'a painting', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwok and hweaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a state'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
画 (huà) — a bar, a field, an open box.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a bar, a field, an open box and Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
男 国.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — nán, guó]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a bar, a field, an open box.
画 (huà).
A bar on top.
Then five strokes, as on top of 男: a vertical, a bar that turns down, a middle bar, a middle vertical, and the bottom bar.
Then the open box, two strokes: one that runs down the left and turns right along the bottom, and last a vertical on the right, from top to bottom.
1 + 5 + 2 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 画 (huà), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 画 (huà) have? (Eight.) What sits in the middle? (The top of 男.) Which stroke is last? (The vertical on the right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
画家 (huàjiā) — painter (an artist).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an oil painting, then the Mandarin for a traditional Chinese painting.

You'll want to know: 画家.
画家 — huàjiā — "painter (an artist)".
小时候我想当画家 xiǎoshíhou wǒ xiǎng dāng huàjiā — When I was little I wanted to be a painter.

The word, taken apart.
画家 (huàjiā) is built from 画 (huà) 'a painting' and 家 'a household', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hweaH and kae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a dwelling'; the sense 'a master of an art' is not given there.
