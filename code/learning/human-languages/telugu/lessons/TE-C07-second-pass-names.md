# The name exchange again — built from four pieces

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C02-PERU-01, TE-LEX-C02-NAA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two words, from memory: the one for *name*, and the one for *my*.

## You'll want to know: four pieces, and everything they build
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C02-PERU-01, TE-LEX-C02-NAA-01, TE-LEX-C02-EMITI-01, TE-LEX-C02-NUVVU-MIIRU-01, TE-LEX-C02-NAA-PERU-01, TE-LEX-C02-MII-PERU-EMITI-01] -->

The question you learned and its answer are made of the same four pieces:

| piece | |
|---|---|
| **పేరు** *pēru* | name |
| **నా** *nā* · **మీ** *mī* | my · your |
| **ఏమిటి** *ēmiṭi* | what? |
| **నువ్వు** *nuvvu* · **మీరు** *mīru* | you, close · you, respectful |

Two side by side give the answer, **నా పేరు …** The respectful one in front
and the question word behind give the question, **మీ పేరు ఏమిటి?**

## Grammar Lens: the two things the name exchange was really teaching
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-GRAMMAR-C02-NAA-PERU-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C02-NUVVU-MIIRU-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C02-MII-PERU-EMITI-03] -->

Two points sit under those four pieces:

- **No word for *is*.** *Nā pēru Arun* is "my name Arun," and the link stays
  unsaid in every sentence of this shape.
- **Respect rides on the plural.** **మీరు** is a plural doing the work of
  politeness — the move Tamil *nīṅgaḷ* and French *vous* also make.

The familiar *your* goes with the familiar *you*: **నీ పేరు ఏమిటి?**

## Script: three letters that open numbers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-164, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-165, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-166] -->

> ఒ · ఐ · ఆ

Three vowel letters come back with the counting. Each opens a number you now
say, and each is a whole letter rather than a sign for one reason: nothing is
carrying the vowel.

| letter | the number it opens |
|---|---|
| ఒ *o* | ఒకటి — one |
| ఐ *ai* | ఐదు — five |
| ఆ *ā* | ఆరు — six |
