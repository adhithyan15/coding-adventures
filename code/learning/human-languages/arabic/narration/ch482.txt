Arabic, chapter 482: Wind and storms.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نسيم (nasīm) — a breeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to pour down, then the Arabic for to clear up.

You'll want to know: نسيم.
نسيم — nasīm — "a breeze".
شعرنا بنسيم لطيف على الشرفة. shaʿarnā bi-nasīm laṭīf ʿalā ash-shurfa. We felt a pleasant breeze on the balcony.

The word, taken apart.
نسيم (nasīm) is built on the native Arabic root n-s-m, "to blow gently; breath" (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 5.
إعصار (iʿṣār) — a hurricane; a whirlwind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to clear up, then the Arabic for a breeze.

You'll want to know: إعصار.
إعصار — iʿṣār — "a hurricane; a whirlwind".
وصل إعصار قوي إلى الساحل. waṣala iʿṣār qawī ilā as-sāḥil. A strong hurricane reached the coast.

The word, taken apart.
إعصار (iʿṣār) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-ṣ-r, "to press, to wring" (Lane). Lane gives iʿṣār as a whirlwind that raises the dust.

Lesson 3 of 5.
عصف (ʿaṣafa) — to blow hard (of wind).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a breeze, then the Arabic for a hurricane.

You'll want to know: عصف.
عصف — ʿaṣafa — "to blow hard (of wind)".
عصفت الريح في الليل. ʿaṣafat ar-rīḥ fī al-layl. The wind blew hard in the night.
Present: يعصف, yaʿṣif.

The word, taken apart.
عصف (ʿaṣafa) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-ṣ-f, "to blow violently (of wind)" (Lane). It is the plain verb; ʿāṣifa, "a storm", is its participle.

اشتد (ishtadda) is built on the native Arabic root sh-d-d, "to be firm; to bind tightly" (Lane). It is Form VIII.

Lesson 5 of 5.
خطر (khaṭar) — danger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to blow hard, then the Arabic for to grow stronger.

You'll want to know: خطر.
خطر — khaṭar — "danger".
كان الخطر كبيرا على الساحل. kāna al-khaṭar kabīran ʿalā as-sāḥil. The danger was great on the coast.
Plural: أخطار, akhṭār.

The word, taken apart.
خطر (khaṭar) is built on the native Arabic root kh-ṭ-r, "peril; to be of great weight" (Lane). It shares its root with khaṭīr, "dangerous".
