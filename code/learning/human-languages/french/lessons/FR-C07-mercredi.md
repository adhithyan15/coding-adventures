# mercredi — Wednesday

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The longest day-name of the week, and the first place to try the key
you were handed: find the god, and English will tell you the day.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-MERCREDI-06]; assesses=[] -->

- **mercredi** = *mair-kruh-DEE*, three syllables with the weight at the end.
- Two throat-**r** sounds in one word, one in each of the first two syllables.
  Take it slowly; this word is worth drilling on its own.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**mercredi** is **Mercuriī diēs**, "the day of **Mercury**" — the messenger of
the gods, and the swiftest planet. English kept the god's name in **mercurial**,
"quick-changing," and gave it to the metal that runs like water.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Apply the key from *mardi*. English *Wednesday* is **Woden's day** — and Woden
is the god the Romans matched to Mercury, both of them travellers, guides and
carriers of the dead. Same day, same role, two names. English still spells the
*d* it stopped pronouncing.
