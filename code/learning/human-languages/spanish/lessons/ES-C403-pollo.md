# el pollo — chicken, not dust

<!-- Safety: teach pollo only against polvo. Do not generate polla/pollas, do not
use echar near polvo, and do not turn this contrast into a gender drill. See
HL24 section 6.2. The missing forms are deliberate. -->

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C361-REF-21] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *the meat*. (*La carne*.)

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- *PO-yo* for most speakers: written **ll** commonly has the sound of English
  *y*. Keep the final vowel round and clear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-POLLO]; assesses=[] -->

**el pollo** — “the chicken,” especially chicken as food.

Latin **pullus** meant a young animal. Spanish **pollo** comes from that word.
Keep it distinct from **polvo**, “dust,” which comes from unrelated Latin
**pulvis**. The useful listening contrast is only **pollo / polvo**: the first
has *-ll-*, the second has *-lv-*.
