5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gemma, gemmae — a gem; a bud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lamb, then the Latin for a calf.

You'll want to know: gemma, gemmae.
gemma, gemmae — "a gem; a bud".

The word, taken apart.
gemma has no sure origin. It meant both a bud and a precious stone; Lewis & Short note that ancient writers took the stone as the first sense.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: vestīmentum, vestīmentī.
vestīmentum, vestīmentī — "a garment".

The word, taken apart.
vestīmentum is built on vestiō, "to clothe", from vestis, "clothing" (EtymDB 2.1), with -mentum, an ending for a thing.

solea is built on solum, "the ground, the sole of the foot" (Lewis & Short): what goes under the sole. English sole, of a shoe, comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
culcita, culcitae — a mattress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sandal, then the Latin for a fold of a garment; a bay.

You'll want to know: culcita, culcitae.
culcita, culcitae — "a mattress".

The word, taken apart.
culcita has no sure origin. Varro tied it to calcō, "to tread, to press down", but Lewis & Short call that doubtful. English quilt comes from it through French.
