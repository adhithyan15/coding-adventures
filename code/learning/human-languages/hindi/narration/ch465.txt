Hindi, chapter 465: Laughter and Tears.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मज़ाक़ (mazāq) — a joke; teasing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for curiosity, then the Hindi for interesting.

You'll want to know: मज़ाक़.
मज़ाक़ — mazāq — "a joke; teasing".
अरुण के मज़ाक़ पर सब हँसे। Aruṇ ke mazāq par sab hãse. Everyone laughed at Arun's joke.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
मज़ाक़ (mazāq) is usually traced to Arabic madhāq, "taste, flavour", which reached Hindi through Persian, where it came to mean a taste for fun and then a joke.

Lesson 2 of 5.
हँसाना (hãsānā) — to make someone laugh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for interesting, then the Hindi for a joke.

You'll want to know: हँसाना.
हँसाना — hãsānā — "to make someone laugh".
अरुण अपने मज़ाक़ से सब लोगों को हँसाता है। Aruṇ apne mazāq se sab logoṁ ko hãsātā hai. Arun makes everyone laugh with his jokes.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चों को हँसाया, usne baccoṁ ko hãsāyā.

The word, taken apart.
हँसाना (hãsānā) is the causative partner of hãsnā, "to laugh", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit hásati, "laughs" (CDIAL 14021).

Lesson 3 of 5.
रुलाना (rulānā) — to make someone cry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a joke, then the Hindi for to make someone laugh.

You'll want to know: रुलाना.
रुलाना — rulānā — "to make someone cry".
दादी की पुरानी कहानी ने मीरा को रुला दिया। dādī kī purānī kahānī ne Mīrā ko rulā diyā. Grandmother's old story moved Mira to tears.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को रुलाया, usne bacce ko rulāyā.

The word, taken apart.
रुलाना (rulānā) is the causative partner of ronā, "to weep", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit rōdati, "weeps" (CDIAL 10840); the vowel shortens before the causative ending, as it does in sulānā beside sonā.

Lesson 4 of 5.
आह (āh) — a sigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to make someone laugh, then the Hindi for to make someone cry.

You'll want to know: आह.
आह — āh — "a sigh".
थककर दादी ने एक लंबी आह भरी। thakkar dādī ne ek lambī āh bharī. Worn out, my grandmother heaved a long sigh.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
आह (āh) is the sound of a sigh made into a noun; Monier-Williams gives Sanskrit āha as an exclamation (Monier-Williams). āh bharnā, "to fill a sigh", is to heave one.

Lesson 5 of 5.
विलाप (vilāp) — wailing; lamentation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to make someone cry, then the Hindi for a sigh.

You'll want to know: विलाप.
विलाप — vilāp — "wailing; lamentation".
बेटी का विलाप सुनकर सब लोग रो पड़े। beṭī kā vilāp sunkar sab log ro paṛe. Hearing the daughter's wailing, everyone broke down in tears.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
विलाप (vilāp) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit vilāpa, "lamentation", from vi-lap, "to wail" (Monier-Williams).
