Spanish, chapter 644: Dentro del coche.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el motor — the engine, the motor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to speed up, then the Spanish for to circulate.

You'll want to know: el motor.
el motor — "the engine, the motor".
El motor hace un ruido raro. The engine is making a strange noise.
Arrancar el motor — to start the engine.

The word, taken apart.
motor is a learned borrowing from Latin mōtor "a mover, one that sets a thing moving" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from movēre "to move".

Lesson 2 of 5.
el volante — the steering wheel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to circulate, then the Spanish for the engine.

You'll want to know: el volante.
el volante — "the steering wheel".
Pon las dos manos en el volante. Put both hands on the wheel.
Estar al volante — to be at the wheel, to be driving.

The word, taken apart.
volante is built on volar "to fly", inherited from Latin volāre; as an adjective it means "flying" (EtymDB), and it came to name parts that turn or move freely.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el maletero — the boot (of a car), the trunk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the engine, then the Spanish for the steering wheel.

You'll want to know: el maletero.
el maletero — "the boot (of a car), the trunk".
Las maletas van en el maletero. The suitcases go in the boot.
Spain says el maletero; Mexico la cajuela; much of South America el baúl.

The word, taken apart.
maletero is built on maleta "suitcase". Maleta is usually traced to French mallette, a little malle "trunk", which goes back to a Frankish word for a bag.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el cinturón — the belt; the seat belt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the steering wheel, then the Spanish for the boot.

You'll want to know: el cinturón.
el cinturón — "the belt; the seat belt".
Ponte el cinturón, por favor. Put your seat belt on, please.
El cinturón de seguridad — the seat belt.

The word, taken apart.
cinturón is built on cintura "waist", which is inherited from Latin cinctūra "a girdle", from cingere "to put a belt round".

Lesson 5 of 5.
abrocharse — to fasten (a belt, a button).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: abrocharse.
abrocharse — "to fasten (a belt, a button)".
Abróchense el cinturón, por favor. Please fasten your seat belts.
Without se, abrochar is to fasten something: abrochar el abrigo.

The word, taken apart.
abrocharse is built on broche "clasp, brooch", which was borrowed from French broche (EtymDB).
