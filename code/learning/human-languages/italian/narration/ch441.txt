Italian, chapter 441: Vie e vicoli.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il bivio — the fork (in a road).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to carry on, then the Italian for to take.

You'll want to know: il bivio.
il bivio — "the fork (in a road)".
Al bivio prendi la strada per il mare. At the fork take the road to the sea.
Figuratively, a crossroads in life: essere a un bivio.

The word, taken apart.
bivio is a learned borrowing from Latin bivium 'a place where two roads meet', from bi- 'two' and via 'way' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la traversa — the side street; the crossbar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the fork, then the Italian for the alley.

You'll want to know: la traversa.
la traversa — "the side street; the crossbar".
La farmacia è nella seconda traversa a destra. The chemist's is in the second side street on the right.
A street that crosses a main one. In football, la traversa is the crossbar.

The word, taken apart.
traversa is inherited from Latin trānsversa 'lying across', from trānsvertere, built on vertere 'to turn' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il senso unico — the one-way street.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the side street, then the Italian for the avenue.

You'll want to know: il senso unico.
il senso unico — "the one-way street".
Non puoi entrare, è senso unico. You cannot go in, it is one-way.
Il senso vietato — no entry.

The word, taken apart.
senso unico is built from senso 'sense, direction', inherited from Latin sēnsus, and unico 'only', inherited from Latin ūnicus, from ūnus 'one' (EtymDB).
