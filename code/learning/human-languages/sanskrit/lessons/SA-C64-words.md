# (reading) — six words, and the end of each one

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word below you have already met. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them to you first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-03, SA-LEX-C24-ASK-05] -->

> ग्रामः
> मार्गः
> दीपः
> गृहम्
> वनम्
> पुस्तकम्

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and this time read the last letter of each one first.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-01, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-04, SA-LEX-C35-HOUSE-04, SA-GRAMMAR-C16-NOUN-FAMILIES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The six sorted themselves into two families before you read one of
them for meaning. Three close on **-अः**, three close on **-अम्**, and you were
told in Chapter 16 what that difference is.

[PAUSE 2s] This is the habit Sanskrit asks for and English never does. An
English word carries its job in WHERE IT STANDS. A Sanskrit word carries its job
in HOW IT ENDS.

[PAUSE 2s] So a reader of Sanskrit reads the end of a word first. It is not a
trick for beginners — it is what the writing system is built to be read with,
and the next lesson shows what it buys you.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice too that each word is printed with space around it and its
ending whole. Running Sanskrit does not keep them that separate; a first reader
needs to see the joints while they are still learning where they are.
