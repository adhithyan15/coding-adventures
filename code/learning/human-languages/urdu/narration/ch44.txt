Lesson 1 of 5.
نانا (nānā) — a grandfather (mother's father).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a grandfather, then the Urdu for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: نانا.
نانا — nānā — "a grandfather (mother's father)".

The word, taken apart.
نانا (nānā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nānna, "a term of respect for an older relative", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 7059). CDIAL compares Sanskrit nanā, "mother".

نانی (nānī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nānna, "a term of respect for an older relative", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 7059). nānī is the feminine of nānā, "grandfather". CDIAL compares Sanskrit nanā, "mother".

Lesson 5 of 5.
بچہ (bachchā) — a child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a husband, then the Urdu for a wife.

You'll want to know: بچہ.
بچہ — bachchā — "a child".

The word, taken apart.
بچہ (bachchā) is usually traced to Persian bachcha, "child". CDIAL lists close Punjabi and Sindhi words, such as Punjabi baccā, "child", under Sanskrit apatya, "offspring", and its addenda set them beside Persian bača (CDIAL 424, 14226); it gives no Hindi or Urdu form there, so which road the Urdu word took is not settled.
