Mandarin Chinese, chapter 433: From Overseas.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiēdài — to receive visitors; to take care of guests.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a full-price ticket, then the Mandarin for to be shut to the public.

You'll want to know: jiēdài.
jiēdài — "to receive visitors; to take care of guests".
zhè jiā jiǔdiàn jiēdài le hěn duō wàiguó yóukè — This hotel has had lots of foreign visitors.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiēdài is built from jiē 'to meet' and dài 'to wait on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjep and dojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to join' and the second 'to wait for'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
海外 (hǎiwài) — overseas; beyond the sea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be shut to the public, then the Mandarin for to receive visitors.

You'll want to know: 海外.
海外 — hǎiwài — "overseas; beyond the sea".
hěn duō hǎiwài yóukè zhù zài zhè jiā jiǔdiàn — A lot of visitors from overseas stay at this hotel.
The new character, in pieces: 海 (hǎi) — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left the water piece of 洗, three strokes; on the right 每 měi, seven strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
海外 (hǎiwài) is built from 海 (hǎi) 'the sea' and 外 'outside', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xojX and ngwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, as the great pool that takes in a hundred rivers, and the second 'outside'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
海 (hǎi) — the water piece, then 每.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the water piece, then 每 and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xǐ, měi]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the water piece, then 每.
海 (hǎi).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the water piece, three strokes, as in 洗: two dots, then a stroke that rises to the right.
The right piece, 每 měi, seven strokes, as you wrote it: a short stroke falling to the left and a bar; a stroke that runs down and turns right along the foot; a bar that turns down and hooks; a dot; a long bar across; and a last dot.
3 + 7 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 海 (hǎi), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 海 (hǎi)? (Ten.) What is the right piece? (每 měi.) How many dots does 每 have? (Two.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
人山人海 (rénshānrénhǎi) — huge crowds; a sea of people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to receive visitors, then the Mandarin for beyond the sea.

You'll want to know: 人山人海.
人山人海 — rénshānrénhǎi — "huge crowds; a sea of people".
zhōumò gōngyuán lǐ rénshānrénhǎi — At the weekend the park is packed with people.

The word, taken apart.
人山人海 (rénshānrénhǎi) is built from 人 'people', 山 'a mountain' and 海 (hǎi) 'the sea', said twice with 人: people like mountains, people like seas. The parts are inherited from Middle Chinese, nyin, srean and xojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
huáqiáo — overseas Chinese; a Chinese person settled abroad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for beyond the sea, then the Mandarin for a sea of people.

You'll want to know: huáqiáo.
huáqiáo — "overseas Chinese; a Chinese person settled abroad".
tā shì zhù zài hǎiwài de huáqiáo — He's one of the Chinese who live overseas.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huáqiáo is built from huá 'Chinese' and qiáo 'to live away from home', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwae and gjew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'flourishing' and the second 'to lodge' and 'a guest'; the sense 'Chinese' for the first is not given there.
