Hindi, chapter 372: Mercy and Loyalty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दया (dayā) — mercy, pity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for apart from, then the Hindi for to dig one's heels in.

You'll want to know: दया.
दया — dayā — "mercy, pity".
उसे ग़रीबों पर दया आती है। use ġarīboṁ par dayā ātī hai. He feels pity for the poor.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
दया (dayā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit dayā, "sympathy, compassion" (Monier-Williams).

The word, taken apart.
वफ़ादार (vafādār) is usually traced to Persian vafādār, "keeping faith". Its first part is Arabic wafāʾ, "faithfulness".

Lesson 5 of 5.
थामना (thāmnā) — to hold on to, to hold up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for loyal, then the Hindi for a sin.

You'll want to know: थामना.
थामना — thāmnā — "to hold on to, to hold up".
बच्चे ने माँ का हाथ थामा। bacce ne māṁ kā hāth thāmā. The child held on to his mother's hand.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मेरा हाथ थामा, usne merā hāth thāmā.

The word, taken apart.
थामना (thāmnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit stambhate, "props, stops" (CDIAL 13683).
