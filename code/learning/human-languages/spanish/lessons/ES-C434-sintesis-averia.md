# (una avería) — the message the shop leaves you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-03, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la pieza* and *la lavadora*. The message you are about to hear
contains both.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-03, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-05] -->

Read it once, as though hearing it on the telephone, then answer.

> Buenas tardes, llamo del **taller**. Su **lavadora** ya está reparada: era
> una **pieza** pequeña, así que no le vamos a **cobrar** la mano de obra.
> Podemos llevársela el miércoles por la mañana.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: what was wrong with it]
- [YOU SAY: whether you are being charged for the labour]
- [YOU SAY: when they can bring it]

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-02, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01] -->

Now the other direction. Say aloud, as if leaving the message yourself:

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: the machine does not work and I think a part is missing]
- [YOU SAY: I am taking it to the workshop on Monday]
- [YOU SAY: I have lent my charger and I need it back]

**Four chapters ago none of this was sayable**, and the grammar has not changed
once. Every sentence above uses tenses you already had. What was missing was
never structure — it was the nouns that ordinary life is actually about.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-03, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Who is calling? (**el taller**.) What was wrong? (**una pieza
pequeña**.) What do you need back? (**el cargador**.)
