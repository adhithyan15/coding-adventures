Japanese, chapter 396: Where Are You Headed?.

Lesson 1 of 5.
ゆきさき (yukisaki) — a destination.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to loosen, then the Japanese for to go past one's turning.

You'll want to know: ゆきさき.
ゆきさき — yukisaki — "a destination".
でんしゃの ゆきさきを たしかめました。 — densha no yukisaki o tashikamemashita — "I made sure of the train's destination."

The word, taken apart.
ゆきさき (yukisaki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from yuki, "going" (from yuku, another form of iku) and saki, "ahead"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

しゅうまつは まちを ぶらぶら あるきました。 — shūmatsu wa machi o burabura arukimashita — "At the weekend I strolled around the town."

The word, taken apart.
ぶらぶら (burabura) is a native mimetic word that pictures an easy, aimless stroll: an imitative Japanese form, inherited rather than borrowed, whose older origin is not settled (JMdict).
