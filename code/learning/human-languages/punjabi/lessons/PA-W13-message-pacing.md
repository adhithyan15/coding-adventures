# Pace the first two message chunks

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-A1-MESSAGE-PACING-01]; assesses=[PA-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING] -->

The whole A1 writing paper lasts twenty minutes. Its message is 30–40 words
for one named reader and purpose. Today you will time only a small opening,
not pretend to fit that whole paper into a short lesson.

## Writing — one small timed opening
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-A1-MESSAGE-PACING-01, PA-PHRASE-HOW-ARE-YOU-03] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

Close the earlier message pages. Imagine Aman writing to Manan. In ninety
seconds, make two familiar chunks: a greeting that names Manan, then one
wellbeing question. You may choose the exact taught words and punctuation;
no sample sentence is visible here. Stop at ninety seconds and record the
number of words you produced.

## Guided Practice — inspect one boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-A1-MESSAGE-PACING-01] -->

After the timer, check only whether Manan is clearly the reader. Next check
the question mark and spacing. Repairing after the clock helps learning but
does not change the recorded first attempt.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-A1-MESSAGE-PACING-01] -->

The next checkpoint uses a fresh purpose, a full 30–40-word message, and a
short clock. The old supported message lacks the required water detail, so
copying its old order would miss that purpose.
