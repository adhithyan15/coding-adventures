Lesson 2 of 5.
காயம் (kāyam) — a wound, an injury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to manage, then the Tamil for a toothache.

You'll want to know: காயம்.
காயம் — kāyam — "a wound, an injury".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of காயம் (kāyam), "wound", is uncertain. DEDR does not give this sense, but it lists kāyam, "scar", beside kāy, "to form a callus, as from a wound" (DEDR 1460), and the two may be one word. Another possible source is Prakrit ghāya, "wound" (CDIAL 4460).

Lesson 3 of 5.
மாத்திரை (māttirai) — a tablet, a pill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a toothache, then the Tamil for a wound.

You'll want to know: மாத்திரை.
மாத்திரை — māttirai — "a tablet, a pill".

The word, taken apart.
மாத்திரை (māttirai) is a loan from Sanskrit mātrā, "measure, minute portion" (CDIAL 10023). A pill is a measured dose.

You'll want to know: செவிலியர்.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of செவிலியர் (ceviliyar) is uncertain. It is built on cevili, an old word for a nurse or foster mother, and DEDR has no entry for it.
