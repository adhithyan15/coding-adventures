Latin, chapter 382: Senators and People.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
senātor, senātōris — a senator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a copyist, then the Latin for a land surveyor.

You'll want to know: senātor, senātōris.
senātor, senātōris — "a senator".
Senātōrēs in cūriā sedent. The senators sit in the senate house.
senātor, senātōris m., a member of the senātus, the council of elders (senex, an old man).

The word, taken apart.
senātor is built on senātus "the council of elders" (EtymDB), which comes from senex "an old man" (Lewis & Short). English senator comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
nōbilis, nōbile — well known; noble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for patrician, then the Latin for plebeian.

You'll want to know: nōbilis, nōbile.
nōbilis, nōbile — "well known; noble".
Nōbilis vir est, sed pauper. He is a man of noble birth, but poor.
nōbilis, nōbile: first known (it is built on nōscō, to get to know), then of a known family. The nōbilēs had a consul among their ancestors. English noble.

The word, taken apart.
nōbilis is built on nōscō, old gnōscō "to get to know" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -bilis: one who can be known. English noble comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
populāris, populāre — of the people, popular; a fellow countryman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for plebeian, then the Latin for well known.

You'll want to know: populāris, populāre.
populāris, populāre — "of the people, popular; a fellow countryman".
Ōrātor populāris plēbem ad sē trahēbat. The popular speaker drew the common people to himself.
populāris, populāre. The populārēs were politicians who worked through the people's assemblies. As a noun, populāris is a fellow countryman. English popular.

The word, taken apart.
populāris is built on populus "the people" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -āris "belonging to". English popular comes from it.
