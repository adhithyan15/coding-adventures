## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-HENHAO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**很好** is *hěn hǎo* — **two third tones side by side.**

You met this in the first chapter with 你好, and the same rule fires again: the
first of the two rises. Spoken, it is *hén hǎo*. Written, it stays *hěn hǎo*,
and the characters do not change at all.

Say it slowly once, then at speed, and let 很 rise into 好.

## Why it's said this way — fine, not very well
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-HENHAO-01, ZH-LEX-ZENMEYANG-01, ZH-LEX-HEN-01] -->

> **我很好** *wǒ hěn hǎo* — I'm fine.

That is the answer to *nǐ zěnmeyàng* and to 你好吗. It means "I'm fine", not "I'm very
well": 很 is there because 好 is an adjective and wants it in front. Put weight
on 很, and the *very* comes back.

## Reading — where the words end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-SKILL-READ-WORDS, ZH-LEX-NE-01] -->

> 我很好你呢

Five characters, and nothing on the page marks where one word stops. Find the
words before reading on.

[PAUSE 3s] 我 — 很好 — 你 — 呢. Four words; 很好 is the only one with two
characters.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**很好** is built from 很 and 好. 好 is inherited from Middle Chinese *xawX*
(Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); 很 is the character the Guangyun records
as *honX*, "defiant", and how it came to mean "very" is not settled.
