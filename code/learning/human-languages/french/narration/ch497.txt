French, chapter 497: Une journée de pêche.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
la canne à pêche — fishing rod.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to embroider, then the French for sewing.

You'll want to know: la canne à pêche.
la canne à pêche — "fishing rod".
Mon oncle m'a offert une canne à pêche. My uncle gave me a fishing rod.
La canne alone is a walking stick.

The word, taken apart.
la canne à pêche joins canne "cane, rod", from Latin canna "a reed", and pêche "fishing", built on pêcher, from Latin piscārī "to fish" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
l'hameçon — fish hook.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for sewing, then the French for fishing rod.

You'll want to know: l'hameçon.
l'hameçon — "fish hook".
Le poisson a mordu à l'hameçon. The fish took the hook.
Masculine. Mordre à l'hameçon is also to fall for a trick.

The word, taken apart.
l'hameçon comes from Old French ameçon, built on a Vulgar Latin form of Latin hāmus "a hook" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
l'appât — bait.

Before the new one: say the French for fishing rod, then the French for fish hook.

You'll want to know: l'appât.
l'appât — "bait".
Il utilise des vers comme appât. He uses worms as bait.
Masculine. The verb is appâter "to lure".

The word, taken apart.
l'appât is built on appâter (EtymDB), from à and Old French past "food", which goes back to Latin pāstus "feeding", from pāscere "to feed" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
pêcher — to fish; to catch (fish).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for fish hook, then the French for bait.

You'll want to know: pêcher.
pêcher — "to fish; to catch (fish)".
Le dimanche, nous allons pêcher au bord du lac. On Sundays we go fishing by the lake.
Do not mix it up with pécher "to sin" or le pêcher, the peach tree.

The word, taken apart.
pêcher is inherited, through Old French peschier, from Vulgar Latin piscāre (EtymDB), a form of Latin piscārī "to fish", from piscis "fish" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
le pêcheur — fisherman, angler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for bait, then the French for to fish.

You'll want to know: le pêcheur.
le pêcheur — "fisherman, angler".
Le pêcheur attend depuis le lever du soleil. The angler has been waiting since sunrise.
A woman is la pêcheuse. Do not mix it up with le pécheur "sinner".

The word, taken apart.
le pêcheur is inherited from Latin piscātor "a fisherman", from piscārī "to fish", from piscis "fish" (Lewis & Short).
