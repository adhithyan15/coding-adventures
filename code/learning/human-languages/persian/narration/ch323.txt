Persian, chapter 323: Tricks, threats and fights.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
کلاهبردار (kolâhbardâr) — a con man, a swindler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for bail, then the Persian for to be released.

You'll want to know: کلاهبردار.
کلاهبردار (kolâhbardâr) — "a con man, a swindler".
یک کلاهبردار پول پیرزن را گرفت. Yek kolâhbardâr pul-e pirzan râ gereft. A con man took the old woman's money.
Literally "hat-lifter": kolâh "hat" + bardâr "one who lifts". Kolâh-e kasi râ bardâshtan "to cheat someone".

The word, taken apart.
kolâhbardâr is built from Persian kolâh "hat" and bardâr-, the stem of bardâshtan "to lift, to take away": the one who takes your hat. All of it is native.

Lesson 2 of 6.
فریب دادن (farib dâdan) — to deceive, to trick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to be released, then the Persian for a con man.

You'll want to know: فریب دادن.
فریب دادن (farib dâdan) — "to deceive, to trick".
او با یک نامه جعلی مردم را فریب داد. U bâ yek nâme-ye ja'li mardom râ farib dâd. He tricked people with a fake letter.
Farib khordan is "to be fooled". Present farib midahad.

The word, taken apart.
farib "deceit" is a native Persian word; EtymDB lists it without a parent. dâdan "to give" is inherited Persian.

Lesson 3 of 6.
تهدید کردن (tahdid kardan) — to threaten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a con man, then the Persian for to deceive.

You'll want to know: تهدید کردن.
تهدید کردن (tahdid kardan) — "to threaten".
مرد فروشنده را تهدید کرد. Mard forushande râ tahdid kard. The man threatened the shopkeeper.
The threat takes be. Present tahdid mikonad.

The word, taken apart.
tahdid is borrowed from Arabic tahdīd, "a threat" (EtymDB lists both the Arabic and the Persian word). kardan "to do" is inherited Persian.

Lesson 4 of 6.
دعوا کردن (da'vâ kardan) — to quarrel, to have a fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to deceive, then the Persian for to threaten.

You'll want to know: دعوا کردن.
دعوا کردن (da'vâ kardan) — "to quarrel, to have a fight".
دو راننده در خیابان دعوا کردند. Do rânande dar khiyâbân da'vâ kardand. Two drivers had a fight in the street.
The other side takes bâ: bâ hamsâye da'vâ kard. Present da'vâ mikonad.

The word, taken apart.
da'vâ is borrowed from Arabic da'wā, "a claim, an assertion" (EtymDB); in Persian it became an everyday word for a quarrel. kardan "to do" is inherited Persian.

Lesson 5 of 6.
کتک زدن (kotak zadan) — to beat (up), to hit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to threaten, then the Persian for to quarrel.

You'll want to know: کتک زدن.
کتک زدن (kotak zadan) — "to beat (up), to hit".
دزدها نگهبان را کتک زدند. Dozd-hâ negahbân râ kotak zadand. The thieves beat up the guard.
Kotak khordan is "to get a beating". Present kotak mizanad.

The word, taken apart.
kotak "blows" is a native word whose origin is not settled; zadan "to strike" is inherited Persian. EtymDB lists no entry.
