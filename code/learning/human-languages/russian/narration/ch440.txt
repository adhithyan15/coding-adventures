Russian, chapter 440: Crossing the street.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
переход (perekhód) — a crossing; an underpass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to take a short cut, then the Russian for to go round.

You'll want to know: переход.
переход (perekhód) — "a crossing; an underpass".
Подземный переход рядом с остановкой. Podzémnyy perekhód ryádom s ostanóvkoy. The underpass is next to the bus stop.
A masculine noun. Пешеходный переход (perekhód) — a pedestrian crossing; подземный переход (perekhód) — an underpass.

The word, taken apart.
переход (perekhód) is built from пере- 'across' and ход 'a going', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic xodŭ 'motion' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
переходить (perekhodít) — to cross (a street); to go over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to go round, then the Russian for a crossing.

You'll want to know: переходить.
переходить (perekhodít) — "to cross (a street); to go over".
Дети переходят улицу на зелёный свет. Déti perekhódyat úlitsu na zelyónyy svet. The children cross the street on the green light.
Imperfective; perfective перейти (перейду, перейдёшь). Takes the accusative or через.

The word, taken apart.
переходить (perekhodít) is built from пере- 'across' and ходить 'to walk'. EtymDB takes the root from Proto-Slavic xodŭ 'motion'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
набережная (náberezhnaya) — an embankment, a riverside walk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a crossing, then the Russian for to cross.

You'll want to know: набережная.
набережная (náberezhnaya) — "an embankment, a riverside walk".
Вечером мы гуляли по набережной. Vécherom my gulyáli po náberezhnoy. In the evening we walked along the embankment.
An adjective used as a noun; the stress is on the first syllable.

The word, taken apart.
набережная (náberezhnaya) is built from на 'on' and берег 'a bank, a shore' with an adjective ending: the street along the bank. Берег is inherited from Proto-Slavic bergŭ (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: бульвар.
бульвар (bulvár) — "a boulevard".
Вдоль бульвара растут старые деревья. Vdol bulvára rastút stáryye derévya. Old trees grow along the boulevard.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
бульвар (bulvár) was borrowed from French boulevard (EtymDB), which goes back to Middle Dutch bollewerc 'a bulwark, a bastion'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
аллея (alléya) — an avenue (of trees), a path in a park.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: аллея.
аллея (alléya) — "an avenue (of trees), a path in a park".
Мы шли по длинной аллее. My shli po dlínnoy alléye. We walked along a long avenue of trees.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
аллея (alléya) is usually traced to French allée 'a path', which EtymDB derives from Old French aler 'to go'. EtymDB does not list the Russian word.
