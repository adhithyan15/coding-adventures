Latin, chapter 320: Pavor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pavor, pavōris — panic, terror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the watch, then the Latin for to scorch.

You'll want to know: pavor, pavōris.
pavor, pavōris — "panic, terror".
Pavor omnēs cīvēs cēpit. Panic seized all the citizens.
Masculine, 3rd declension. A sudden fear that shakes the body.

The word, taken apart.
pavor is built on paveō, "to tremble with fear" (EtymDB): the trembling itself.

Lesson 2 of 5.
metus, metūs — fear, dread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to scorch, then the Latin for panic.

You'll want to know: metus, metūs.
metus, metūs — "fear, dread".
Propter metum hostium portās clausērunt. Out of fear of the enemy they shut the gates.
Masculine, 4th declension. A steady, lasting fear, beside timor (taught).

The word, taken apart.
metus is a Latin word whose earlier origin is not settled (Lewis & Short, EtymDB). Its verb is metuō, "to fear" (taught).

Lesson 3 of 5.
formīdō, formīdinis — dread, terror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for panic, then the Latin for fear.

You'll want to know: formīdō, formīdinis.
formīdō, formīdinis — "dread, terror".
Magna formīdō mīlitēs tenēbat. Great dread held the soldiers.
Feminine, 3rd declension. There is also a verb formīdō, formīdāre, 'to dread'; English formidable comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
formīdō is explained by Lewis & Short as "the fear that makes one rigid", from a root also seen in firmus, "firm"; the link is not certain. English formidable comes from its family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pavidus, pavida, pavidum — frightened, trembling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fear, then the Latin for dread.

You'll want to know: pavidus, pavida, pavidum.
pavidus, pavida, pavidum — "frightened, trembling".
Pavida ancilla in angulō sedēbat. The frightened maid was sitting in the corner.
From the same verb as pavor. Plautus pairs it with timidus (taught).

The word, taken apart.
pavidus is built on paveō, "to tremble with fear", with the adjective ending -idus (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
trepidō, trepidāre — to panic, to be in a flurry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dread, then the Latin for frightened.

You'll want to know: trepidō, trepidāre.
trepidō, trepidāre — "to panic, to be in a flurry".
Cīvēs in viīs trepidābant, nec quid facerent sciēbant. The citizens were rushing about in alarm in the streets and did not know what to do.
trepidō, trepidāre, trepidāvī, trepidātum: anxious hurrying, the bustle of fear. English trepidation.

The word, taken apart.
trepidō is built on trepidus, "restless, alarmed" (EtymDB). English trepidation comes from it.
