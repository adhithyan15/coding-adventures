おちゃ — ocha — "tea".

Script — the shape.
ゃ — small ya.
Same shape as や, in the same order, written small and sitting low: the sweep and its hook first, then the short tick, then the descender. Three strokes, two lifts.
It does the job ゅ does. It joins to the sign before it, and the two become one beat:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: ちや. you say: chi-ya. beats: two.
you write: ちゃ. you say: cha. beats: one.
That is the whole difference between the two rows: the size of one sign. The tea word you met one lesson back is o–cha, two beats, because its ちゃ is one.

Guided Practice.
Write や, then ゃ (small ya) beneath it, half the height, in the same three strokes.
Write おちゃ (ocha) from memory. Check that the last sign sits low and small.
Read ちや and ちゃ aloud, one after the other, and clap the beats.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate small form.

ちょっと — chotto — "a little", and, said on its own with a small pause, "one moment".

Script — the shape.
ょ — small yo.
Same shape as よ, in the same order, written small and sitting low: the short bar from left to right, then the stem down through it and round the loop. Two strokes, one lift.
You now have all three small signs that join forward. Each one turns the sign before it into a single beat:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: ちゃ, ちゅ, ちょ. you say, one beat each: cha, chu, cho.
you write: しゃ, しゅ, しょ. you say, one beat each: sha, shu, sho.
So ちょ is one beat, cho, and ちょっと (chotto) is the three beats you said in the last lesson: cho, a held silence, to.

Guided Practice.

Write しゃ, しゅ, しょ in a row and read them as three single beats.

You'll want to know: おちゃを ください.
おちゃを ください — ocha o kudasai — "tea, please".
You know both words already: ocha, tea, and kudasai, the polite request at the end of itte kudasai. What is new is the small word between them, said o.
It marks the thing the request is about: tea is what you are asking for. Put any thing you can name in front of it and the request works the same way.
That word is spelled with a sign you have not written yet. It is said o, the same as お, but it is a different sign, and the next lesson writes it.

Lesson 8 of 9.
を (wo) — said o, kept for one job.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Five recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: ask for tea politely — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a doctor — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: write お, and say it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a flea — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say ugly — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
を (wo) — said o.
Three strokes, two lifts:
the short bar at the top, left to right.
starting above the bar, a long diagonal down and to the left through it; at its foot, without lifting, turn sharply back up and to the right into an arch, and come down its right side in a short stem.
a separate curve: start at the right, sweep down and left across the middle, round the lower left, and finish along the base to the right.
It is said exactly like お. The two are not interchangeable, and what separates them is not sound:
お spells o inside words, as in おちゃ (ocha).
を (wo) spells the particle that marks the thing asked for or acted on, and nothing else.
You have met this once already. は is read wa when it is the topic marker; を is read o when it is the object marker. Both are spellings kept for a grammatical word after the sound moved.

Guided Practice.
Trace を (wo) saying "bar, diagonal and arch, curve".
Write お and を (wo) side by side; say both, and hear that they are the same.
Write おちゃを ください (ocha o kudasai) from memory. Check which o is which.

Wrap-up Recall.
Which sign spells the particle? (を (wo).)
