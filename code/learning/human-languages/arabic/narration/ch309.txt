Arabic, chapter 309: Princes and palaces.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أمير (amīr) — a prince; an emir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for evidence, then the Arabic for a fingerprint.

You'll want to know: أمير.
أمير — amīr — "a prince; an emir".
كان في المدينة أمير شاب. kāna fī al-madīna amīr shābb. There was a young prince in the city.
Plural: أمراء, umarāʾ.
The feminine is أميرة, a princess.

The word, taken apart.
أمير (amīr) is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-m-r (Lane), "to command", the root of amr, an order. An amīr is one who commands; English emir and admiral are usually traced to it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
قصر (qaṣr) — a palace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a fingerprint, then the Arabic for a prince.

You'll want to know: قصر.
قصر — qaṣr — "a palace".
زرنا قصرا قديما قرب البحر. zurnā qaṣran qadīman qurba al-baḥr. We visited an old palace near the sea.
Plural: قصور, quṣūr.

The word, taken apart.
قصر (qaṣr) is filed by Lane under the root q-ṣ-r, "to confine, to keep within". It was borrowed from Aramaic qaṣrā (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin castra, a camp; the Latin step is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تاج (tāj) — a crown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a prince, then the Arabic for a palace.

You'll want to know: تاج.
تاج — tāj — "a crown".
لبست الملكة تاجا من الذهب. labisat al-malika tājan min adh-dhahab. The queen wore a crown of gold.
Plural: تيجان, tījān.

The word, taken apart.
تاج (tāj) was borrowed from Middle Iranian (Parthian) tāg, "a crown" (EtymDB), an old borrowing; Aramaic tāgā is its cousin. Arabic made a verb from it: tawwaja, to crown.

Lesson 4 of 5.
عرش (ʿarsh) — a throne.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a palace, then the Arabic for a crown.

You'll want to know: عرش.
عرش — ʿarsh — "a throne".
جلس الملك على العرش. jalasa al-malik ʿalā al-ʿarsh. The king sat on the throne.
Plural: عروش, ʿurūsh.

The word, taken apart.
عرش (ʿarsh) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-r-sh (Lane), where ʿarasha is "to build a roof or a trellis". A raised seat under a canopy became the throne.

Lesson 5 of 5.
توج (tawwaja) — to crown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a crown, then the Arabic for a throne.

You'll want to know: توج.
توج — tawwaja — "to crown".
توج (tawwaja) الأمير ملكا بعد أبيه. tuwwija al-amīr malikan baʿda abīhi. The prince was crowned king after his father.
Present: يتوج, yutawwij.
In sport: توج (tawwaja) الفريق بالكأس, the team won the cup.

The word, taken apart.
توج (tawwaja) comes from tāj, "a crown", an Iranian loan (EtymDB). Arabic gave it the Form II pattern: "to put a crown on".
