Gujarati, chapter 464: A Sensible Head.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
શાણું (śāṇũ) — wise; discreet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to babble, then the Gujarati for cheerful.

You'll want to know: શાણું.
શાણું — śāṇũ — "wise; discreet".
શાણો માણસ ગુસ્સામાં કંઈ બોલતો નથી. śāṇo māṇas gussāmā̃ kãī bolto nathī. A wise man says nothing in anger.

The word, taken apart.
શાણું (śāṇũ) goes back to a reconstructed sajāna-, "clever"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "wise, discreet, courteous" (CDIAL 13088).

Lesson 2 of 5.
વિવેક (vivek) — good sense; courtesy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for cheerful, then the Gujarati for discreet.

You'll want to know: વિવેક.
વિવેક — vivek — "good sense; courtesy".
મહેમાન સાથે વિવેકથી બોલો. mahemān sāthe vivekthī bolo. Speak to guests with courtesy.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વિવેક (vivek) is a tatsama, Sanskrit viveka, "discrimination, consideration", as Monier-Williams records it; Gujarati uses it for good sense and good manners.

You'll want to know: ઠાવકું.
ઠાવકું — ṭhāvkũ — "composed; sober".
આટલી નાની ઉંમરે પણ મીરા ઘણી ઠાવકી છે. āṭlī nānī ummre paṇ Mīrā ghaṇī ṭhāvkī che. Even at so young an age Mira is very composed.

The word, taken apart.
ઠાવકું (ṭhāvkũ) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "steady, firm, discreet", under Sanskrit sthātavya, "to be stood in" (CDIAL 13751): someone who stands firm.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ગાંઠવું (gā̃ṭhvũ) — to heed; to pay attention to (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for well-behaved, then the Gujarati for composed.

You'll want to know: ગાંઠવું.
ગાંઠવું — gā̃ṭhvũ — "to heed; to pay attention to (someone)".
તોફાની દીકરો કોઈનું ગાંઠતો નથી. tophānī dīkro koīnũ gā̃ṭhto nathī. The naughty son heeds no one.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે કોઈનું ન ગાંઠ્યું, teṇe koīnũ na gā̃ṭhyũ.

The word, taken apart.
ગાંઠવું (gā̃ṭhvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit granthayati, "ties"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to tie" (CDIAL 4353), and Gujarati also uses it, mostly with a negative, for heeding someone.
