Mandarin Chinese, chapter 201: Post and Parcels.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yóupiào — postage stamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to check in, then the Mandarin for to check out.

You'll want to know: yóupiào.
yóupiào — "postage stamp".
wǒ yào mǎi liǎng zhāng yóupiào — I'd like two stamps, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yóupiào is built from yóu 'post' and 票 'a slip'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese hjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as a lodge at a border; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
kuàidì — express delivery; courier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to check out, then the Mandarin for postage stamp.

You'll want to know: kuàidì.
kuàidì — "express delivery; courier".
wǒ yòng kuàidì jì shū — I'm sending the books by courier.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kuàidì is built from 快 'fast' and dì 'to pass along', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khwaejH and dejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'pleased' and the second 'to take turns'; how the pair came to mean a courier is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fāsòng — to send electronically; to send a message or an email.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for courier service, then the Mandarin for email.

You'll want to know: fāsòng.
fāsòng — "to send electronically; to send a message or an email".
wǒ yǐjīng fāsòngle yóujiàn — I've already sent the email.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fāsòng is built from fā 'to send out' and sòng 'to send off', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjot and suwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to start' and 'to open out', and the second 'to send away'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
shōudào — to get something sent; to receive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for email, then the Mandarin for to send electronically.

shōudào — "to get something sent; to receive".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shōudào is built from shōu 'to gather in' and 到 'to arrive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuw and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): taking in what has arrived.
