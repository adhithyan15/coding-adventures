# prīmum, deinde, tum, postrēmō — the rungs of a story

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-LEX-C157-ACT157-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I came" and "I saw". (*Vēnī. Vīdī.*) The verb for "to win"
is *vincō*, and its perfect is **vīcī**, "I won".

[PAUSE 1s] Suetonius reports that when Caesar celebrated his victory in
Pontus, a placard carried three words: **vēnī, vīdī, vīcī**. Three events,
three perfects, and not one joining word.

## Grammar Lens: four rungs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C282-ORDO-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C55-ORDINAL-01, LA-LEX-C101-VERBA7-05, LA-LEX-C105-QUANTUM-03, LA-LEX-C208-TEMP208-02] -->

Caesar could leave the order bare because three short perfects explain
themselves. A longer story names its rungs, with four words you already own:

> **Prīmum** ad forum ambulāvī. — First I walked to the forum.
> **Deinde** amīcum vīdī. — Next I saw a friend.
> **Tum** in caupōnam intrāvimus. — Then we went into the inn.
> **Postrēmō** domum ambulāvī. — Last of all I walked home.

[PAUSE 2s] Each rung carries a **perfect**, because each is an event.

[PAUSE 2s] **Deinde** and **tum** both come out as "then", and they are not
twins. *Deinde* is "next", the following step. *Tum* is "then, at that
moment": it points at a time, and the scene can stand there with it. *Tum
pluēbat*, at that moment it was raining.

## The word, taken apart: prīmum
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C55-ORDINAL-01] -->

**prīmum** is the neuter of *prīmus*, "first", standing alone as an adverb
(Lewis & Short list it under *primus*): "as the first thing". *Postrēmō*
comes from *postrēmus*, "last", too, though it is the ablative, "at the
last", and *deinde* is built from *dē* and *inde*, "from there": from that
point on.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C282-ORDO-01, LA-LEX-C101-VERBA7-05, LA-LEX-C105-QUANTUM-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "First", "next", "then", "lastly"? (***Prīmum, deinde, tum,
postrēmō.***) Which "then" points at a moment and can hold a scene?
(***Tum.***) Which tense goes on each rung? (The **perfect**: each is an
event.) What did Caesar's three words say? (***Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī***: I came,
I saw, I won.)
