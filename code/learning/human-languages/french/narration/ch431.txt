Lesson 1 of 6.
la réclamation — the complaint, the claim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the shutter, then the French for the blind.

You'll want to know: la réclamation.
la réclamation — "the complaint, the claim".
Nous avons fait une réclamation à la direction de l'hôtel. We made a complaint to the hotel management.
Formal; le service des réclamations — the complaints department.

The word, taken apart.
réclamation is a learned borrowing from Latin reclāmātiō 'a crying out against' (EtymDB), from clāmāre 'to shout'.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: rouspéter.
rouspéter — "to grumble, to moan".
Mon oncle rouspète toujours contre le service. My uncle is forever grumbling about the service.
Informal. Il rouspète — he moans.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of rouspéter is not settled; it is an old slang word. EtymDB lists it without a source.

La musique des voisins nous a dérangés toute la nuit. The neighbours' music disturbed us all night.
Ne pas déranger — do not disturb, on a hotel door.

déranger comes from Old French desrengier 'to throw out of line', from des- and rengier 'to put in a row', built on reng 'row' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
mécontent — unhappy, dissatisfied.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to grumble, then the French for to disturb.

You'll want to know: mécontent.
mécontent — "unhappy, dissatisfied".
Les clients sont mécontents du petit-déjeuner. The guests are unhappy with the breakfast.
Mécontent de — unhappy with; le mécontentement — discontent.

The word, taken apart.
mécontent is built from mé- 'badly, not' and content 'glad', from Latin contentus 'satisfied', from continēre, built on tenēre 'to hold' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

What you've built.
