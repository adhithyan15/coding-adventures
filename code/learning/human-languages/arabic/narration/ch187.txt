Lesson 1 of 5.
خلاط (khallāṭ) — a blender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a painting; a board, then the Arabic for a machine.

You'll want to know: خلاط.
خلاط — khallāṭ — "a blender".

The word, taken apart.
خلاط (khallāṭ) is built on the native Arabic root kh-l-ṭ, "to mix" (Lane). Khallāṭ is a modern faʿʿāl word, "the mixer".

Lesson 2 of 5.
صنبور (ṣunbūr) — a tap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a machine, then the Arabic for a blender.

You'll want to know: صنبور.
صنبور — ṣunbūr — "a tap".

The word, taken apart.
صنبور (ṣunbūr) is a native noun, filed under ṣ-n-b-r (Lane). Lane's ṣunbūr is a lone palm tree, slender at its base, and also the metal pipe of a leather water vessel, from which the tap comes.

Lesson 3 of 5.
أنبوب (unbūb) — a pipe, a tube.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a blender, then the Arabic for a tap.

You'll want to know: أنبوب.
أنبوب — unbūb — "a pipe, a tube".

The word, taken apart.
أنبوب (unbūb), "the joint of a reed", is an old native noun, filed under the root n-b-b (Lane). Lane's unbūb is the length of reed between two knots, and the spout of a jug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: سلك.
سلك — silk — "a wire".

The word, taken apart.
سلك (silk) is built on the native Arabic root s-l-k, "to go in; a thread" (Lane). Lane's silk is a thread; the wire is a later sense.
