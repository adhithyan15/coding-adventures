# arreglar — putting it right

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-REPARAR, ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **reparar** and **estropeado**. One undoes the other.

## You'll want to know: arreglar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-04] -->

**arreglar** — to fix, to mend, to sort out.

*¿Me puedes arreglar la bici?* — can you fix my bike for me? *Han arreglado el
ascensor* — they've fixed the lift. *Ya está arreglado* — it's sorted now.

And beyond machinery: *arreglar los papeles* — sort the paperwork out.
*Arreglar una habitación* — tidy a room. *Arreglarse* — get yourself ready to
go out.

## Grammar Lens: arreglar and reparar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01, ES-LEX-REPARAR] -->

Both mean to fix, and a Spanish speaker reaches for them differently:

| word | where it sits |
|---|---|
| **arreglar** | everyday speech, and things that are not machines |
| **reparar** | the workshop, the invoice, the written notice |

*He arreglado la silla* is what you tell a friend. *Reparación de calzado* is
what the shop window says. You can *arreglar* an argument or a haircut;
*reparar* neither.

Where the object is a broken machine, the two overlap and either is correct.
Take **arreglar** as your default and you will be right far more often.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01, ES-LEX-REGULAR] -->

Break it as **a-rregl-ar**, and the middle is **regla** — a ruler, a rule —
from Latin **regula**, the carpenter's straight edge.

So to *arreglar* something is to bring it back to the straight edge: the plank
is warped, you true it. You met the same *regula* in **regular**. English took
it too, in *regulate*, *rule* and *ruler*, and kept the tool and the law in
one word as Spanish does.

The **rr** is where the *a-* meets the *r-* of *regla*. Spanish doubles it and
rolls it, and the doubling is the only trace left of the prefix.
