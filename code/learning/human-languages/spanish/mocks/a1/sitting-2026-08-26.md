**No. Both mocks return `NO APTO`, and not narrowly.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mock 1** | **3,00 / 50** — needs 30 | **11,58 / 50** — needs 30 | 14,58 / 100 | **NO APTO** |
| **mock 2** | **0,00 / 50** — needs 30 | **4,00 / 50** — needs 30 | 4,00 / 100 | **NO APTO** |

Per paper:

| paper | mock 1 | mock 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Comprensión de lectura | 3 / 25 | 0 / 25 |
| Comprensión auditiva | 7 / 25 | 4 / 25 |
| Expresión e interacción escritas | 0,00 / 25 | 0,00 / 25 |
| Expresión e interacción orales | 4,58 / 25 | 0,00 / 25 |

**86 of 100 objective items were failed.** Not one of the four papers, in either
mock, reached even half of its own minimum.

This is the first time anything in this project has been sat. Five HL09 §3.1
criteria were already green for Spanish A1 — headwords, verbs, reinforcement
windows, spine nodes, writing stages. All five measure the corpus. This measures
a learner, and it disagrees with all five.

---

## 1. Why the number is credible in the failing direction

A failure is easy to manufacture by writing hard items or matching strictly. Four
guards were put in place *before* the result was known, and all four push the
score **up**, not down:

1. **The items came from the syllabus, not the book.** Every item realizes PCIC
   A1 inventory points (`rubric.md` §5), sampled across the 20 specific-notion
   areas. Domains the coverage report flags as unmapped were sampled at their
   natural rate — not avoided, not over-weighted.
2. **The matcher is deliberately lenient.** It accepts singular for plural,
   decomposes multiword headwords (`hace sol` satisfies `sol`), credits verbs
   taught only through conjugation lessons rather than a citation headword, and
   accepts any numeral. This grew the taught-token set from ~900 to **2 320**.
   **The scores did not move by a single point.** The result is not a
   normalization artifact.
3. **The A2 tier was granted as a sensitivity test** (§4). Still `NO APTO`.
4. **The writing band model was tested both ways** (§5.3). Still `NO APTO`.

### 1.1 How I prevented myself from using untaught Spanish

This is the part that would otherwise be worthless, so it was made structural
rather than a matter of my good faith.

I did not answer the items. **I never scored an item by reading it and deciding
whether I could answer it** — that would have leaked my own Spanish into the
result, and I would have passed every item in both mocks.

Instead, each item in each answer key declares a **`requires:`** line naming the
lexemes and grammar it depends on. A script then:

- extracts the taught set from the corpus mechanically — lesson frontmatter →
  curriculum path segment → spine node → CEFR stage, which is exactly the
  derivation `levels.ts` uses, so the scope is the project's own, not mine;
- marks an item correct **only if every entry on its `requires:` line is in that
  set**; and
- applies the real DELE group rule to the totals.

The oracle is the corpus. My own Spanish has no vote at any point in the scoring
path. The extraction was validated independently before use: it reproduces the
project's own pinned figure of **40 verbs at or below A1 exactly**.

> **One honest discrepancy.** My extraction counts **689** distinct headwords at
> or below A1 where the project's ledger pins **617**. The difference is cell
> parsing — I split multi-form headword cells such as `el hermano, la hermana`
> and count non-`type: word` lessons that still carry a `headword:`. My count is
> the **larger** one, so the sitting was run against a slightly *more* generous
> lexicon than the ledger claims. This moves the result away from failure, and
> no pinned count was altered.

## 2. What actually went wrong: a verb famine, not the orthography gap

The brief predicted the alphabet, capitalisation and punctuation would bite.
**They do — but they are not what sinks the exam.** Classifying all 86 failed
objective items by cause:

| cause of failure | items |
|---|---|
| a missing high-frequency **verb** is involved | **62 / 86** |
| fails on missing verbs **alone** | 16 |
| fails on missing everyday **nouns** alone | 23 |
| orthography involved at all | **3** |
| fails on orthography **alone** | **0** |

The ten most-missed items across both mocks are almost all verbs:

`gustar` (9 items) · `hacer` (9) · `tener` (8) · `comprar` (5) · `poder` (5) ·
`querer` (4) · `preferir` (4) · `aprender` (4) · `abrir` (4) · `costar` (4)

### 2.1 The structural cause

These verbs are not missing from the book. They are **parked above A1** by the
shared spine:

| lexeme | lesson | spine node | **stage** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tener` | `ES-C08-tener` | `SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-DO` | **A2** |
| `gustar` | `ES-C35-gustar` | `SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-DO` | **A2** |
| `hacer`, `poder`, `querer`, `decir` | `ES-C12-yo-go` &c. | `SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-DO` | **A2** |
| `habitación` | `ES-C268-habitacion` | `SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL` | **B1** |
| `porque` | `ES-C38-porque` | `SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS` | **B1** |

`porque` at B1 is the clearest single symptom: the ordinary causal connector,
needed to answer "¿por qué…?" at A1, sits two levels above the exam that asks
for it.

Meanwhile the 40 verbs that *are* taught at or below A1 are dominated by
peripheral ones — `borrar`, `colgar`, `cruzar`, `empujar`, `girar`, `gritar`,
`lanzar`, `mezclar`, `quemar`, `saltar`, `soñar`, `volar`. They satisfy the
count. They do not appear on the exam.

### 2.2 This is HL09 §3.1's own failure mode, one level deeper

HL09 §3.1 already caught this shape once: 584 headwords of which seven were
verbs, and it added criterion 2b, a verb quota. Spanish now passes 2b — 40 verbs
against a floor of 40.

**It passes the quota and still cannot answer the exam**, because criterion 2b
counts verbs and asks nothing about *which* verbs. In §3.1's own words, *a total
can always be reached by the wrong parts* — and that is exactly what has
happened to the partition that was added to prevent it. The composition rule
needs its own composition rule: a frequency or functional floor, not just a count.

The grammar is **not** the problem. The corpus teaches 136 grammar/morphology
atoms at or below A1 — the complete present indicative for all three
conjugations, preterite, imperfect, gerund and progressive, clitic object
pronouns, possessives, demonstratives, articles, agreement, personal *a*,
`hay` and `hay que`. The machinery is built. There is almost nothing to run it on.

## 3. What the known gaps actually cost

### 3.1 Orthography — real, narrow, and decisive exactly where it lands

The inventory's 50 unmapped points include the alphabet, capital letters, and
every punctuation mark except `¿?`/`¡!`. These never touch Reading or Listening.
They land entirely on **Writing**, and there they are decisive:

> With **all** missing vocabulary granted, the Writing paper still scores
> **8,33 / 25** — band 1, *level not achieved*. Granting the orthography points
> as well lifts it to **16,67 / 25** — band 2, the A1 pass band.

**The orthography gap costs exactly one full band, 8,33 points of Grupo 1, and
no amount of vocabulary buys it back.** Both written tareas require an email
address field: that needs `@`, the `.` in the domain, and lower-case convention
— PCIC `A1-O4-03` (symbols), `A1-O3-01` (the full stop), `A1-NE09-06` (the
internet and dictating an e-mail address). All three are unmapped.

### 3.2 The brief's expectation about spelling aloud is not borne out

The brief expected that "a candidate must spell a name aloud and dictate an email
address". **For the oral paper this is not true of DELE A1.** The official guide's
three oral tareas are a personal presentation, a prepared topic monologue, and a
conversation with the examiner; a search of the full guide for *deletrear*,
*alfabeto*, *abecedario*, *arroba* and *dictado* returns nothing. Email addresses
appear **only in the written exam**, as a form field in Tarea 1.

So the gap is real and it does bite — but in Prueba 3, not Prueba 4, and the
inventory point that names it (`A1-NE09-06`) is about the written form.

### 3.3 The other flagged domains

Clothing, cinema/music, the internet and police/fire were all flagged unmapped.
Clothing is the interesting case: the corpus **does** teach `la falda`, `el
vestido`, `el zapato`, `el pantalón`, `el guante`, `la ropa`, yet `A1-NE12-02`
is unmapped because no atom carries that point's probe. Cost across both mocks:

| domain | items lost |
|---|---|
| internet / email (`wifi`, `internet`, `ordenador`, `correo electrónico`) | 7 |
| cinema, music, photography (`película`, `música`, `concierto`, `fotografía`) | 4 |
| clothing (`camisa`, `camiseta`, `talla`) | 3 |
| commerce and price (`euro`, `costar`, `barato`, `caro`, `gratis`) | 9 |

`euro` is absent from all 1 030 lessons at every level. So are `trabajo` (the
noun) and `planta`. A candidate cannot read a price tag or a job ad.

## 5. What would have to be taught to pass

Each bundle was added to the A1 corpus and both mocks re-sat under the real rule.

| bundle | added | mock 1 | mock 2 | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** baseline | — | G1 3,0 · G2 11,6 | G1 0,0 · G2 4,0 | NO APTO |
| **B** the 5 most-missed verbs | `gustar hacer tener poder querer` | G1 3,0 · G2 13,6 | G1 0,0 · G2 12,3 | NO APTO |
| **C** all 34 high-frequency verbs held at A2+ | +34 | G1 14,3 · G2 20,3 | G1 12,3 · G2 14,3 | NO APTO |
| **D** those verbs **and** ~100 everyday nouns | +136 | G1 32,3 · G2 41,7 | G1 33,3 · G2 41,7 | **APTO** |
| **D+** bundle D **and** the orthography points | +136 & ortho | G1 41,7 · G2 41,7 | G1 41,7 · G2 41,7 | **APTO** |

Three things follow.

1. **Verbs alone are not enough.** Bundle C moves every paper and still fails
   both groups in both mocks. The nouns matter as much as the verbs.
2. **The minimum viable addition is roughly 136 lexemes** — about 34 verbs and
   about 100 everyday nouns — on top of the 689 already taught. Note what this
   says: the corpus does not need to be *bigger* so much as differently
   composed. It is already at 689 of a ~600 target.
3. **Bundle D passes by 2,3 points.** Grupo 1 lands on 32,3/50 against a
   30,00 minimum, and only because Reading carries a Writing paper still stuck
   on band 1. Add the orthography points and Grupo 1 goes to 41,7 — a real
   margin instead of a scrape. **Orthography is the difference between passing
   and passing safely.**

### 5.1 Concrete recommendation

- Re-stage the `SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-DO` verb cluster (`tener`, `gustar`, `hacer`,
  `poder`, `querer`, `decir`, and the `-go` verbs) from A2 to A1, and `porque`
  from B1 to A1 or below. This is a **spine staging** decision, not new lesson
  writing — much of the material already exists.
- Add the ~100 missing everyday nouns, weighted to commerce/price, transport,
  work, education, and internet/email.
- Close the orthography half of `phonology-orthography`: the alphabet, capitals,
  the full stop, the comma, and the `@` symbol. This is the cheapest single
  intervention with the largest effect on Grupo 1.
- Give HL09 §3.1 criterion 2b a composition rule of its own — a frequency floor,
  so a verb quota cannot again be filled by `volar` and `empujar`.

### 5.2 What this does **not** license

Nothing here says the book is bad. It says the A1 **claim** is unsupported by
performance evidence, which is precisely what HL16's mock requirement exists to
find out. The grammar ramp is genuinely strong and is not implicated.

### 5.3 Modelling choices that were tested and did not change the verdict

- **Writing band 0 vs 1.** The baseline model assigns band 0 when >40 % of
  required lexis is missing. Forcing band 1 instead (a candidate who writes
  *something* partially relevant) gives Writing 8,33 and mock-1 Grupo 1 = 11,3.
  Still `NO APTO`.
- **Listening is scored from a transcript**, so it carries no decoding load.
  These listening scores are an **upper bound**; live audio would be lower.
- **Speaking is scored from required lexis**, not a live performance. Also an
  upper bound.

## 6. Does `task-shapes/a1.json` agree with the real exam?

**Yes — on every checkable point.** It was written independently and earlier,
from the same Cervantes sources, and it holds up:

| | `task-shapes/a1.json` | official guide | |
|---|---|---|---|
| paper timings | 45 / 25 / 25 / 10 min | 45 / 25 / 25 / 10 | agree |
| written session | 95 min | 45+25+25 = 95 | agree |
| reading items | 5 + 6 + 6 + 8 = 25 | 5 + 6 + 6 + 8 = 25 | agree |
| listening items | 5 + 5 + 8 + 7 = 25 | 5 + 5 + 8 + 7 = 25 | agree |
| points per skill | 25 | 25 | agree |
| oral preparation | 10 min | 10 min | agree |
| audio replays | 2 | 2 | agree |
| written response lengths | 15–25, 30–40 words | 15–25, 30–40 | agree |
| **pass rule** | `passRule.note`: 25 pts/skill, ≥30/50 in **reading+writing** and **listening+speaking** | identical | **agree** |

Its stimulus-length bounds (150–175, 175–210, 20–30, 160–185 words) match the
guide's published ranges too. **No discrepancy was found.** The file is notably
careful in one respect worth naming: it sets the four
`independentSkillThresholds` to `null` and states in its own note that Instituto
Cervantes publishes no such thresholds and the project must add them rather than
misattribute them — exactly the HL18 §4 discipline.

### 6.1 One trap this exercise did hit

Research initially produced a paraphrase of the Cervantes "cómo son las pruebas"
page claiming Grupo 1 = the two *comprehension* papers and Grupo 2 = the two
*production* papers. **That is wrong**, and it would have inverted the entire
result. The official guide and the Instituto Cervantes de Seúl page both give
reading+writing / listening+speaking. `task-shapes/a1.json` already had it right.

### 6.2 Left open, as instructed

The inventory's `phonology-orthography` dimension stays **partial**. The PCIC
publishes A1 and A2 pronunciation as one undivided band, so there is no A1-only
boundary to restate. That is an owner decision and is untouched here. Note that
it is the *orthography* half — which **is** source-separated and **is**
enumerated — that this sitting found decisive; the unresolved pronunciation half
was never load-bearing for a paper-based mock.

## 7. What this closes, and what it does not

Spanish's assessment contract referenced **26 distinct artifacts that did not
exist**: 21 mock files across all seven rungs, and 5 task-shape inventories
(`a2`, `b1`, `b2`, `c1`, `c2`). This change builds **three** of the 21 — the A1
rubric and the two A1 answer keys — leaving **23** still dangling.

Two clarifications, since referenced-vs-distinct is easy to slip on.
*Occurrences* of a path in `assessment.json` outnumber *distinct artifacts*,
because one `rubric.md` is cited by both mocks at a level; 26 is the distinct
figure and the one to reason with. And `task-shapes/a1.json` — which these mocks
were checked against in §6 — cites only **URLs**, not local files, so nothing it
references can dangle. That is not true of the other rungs, which do not exist.
