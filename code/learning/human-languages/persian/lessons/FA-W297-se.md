# ث (se) — the letter inside مثل

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-TE-01, FA-SCRIPT-C25-SAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ت and ص once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **مثل** (*mesl*), "like". You met it a moment ago. This
lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ث
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-C297-SE-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C297-MESL-01] -->

**ث** — *se*, the sound *s*.

It is the middle letter of **مثل**, joined on both sides, **ـثـ**. Its body is
the body of **ت**, from chapter fifteen, but it carries **three dots above**
instead of two. In the middle of a word that body shrinks to a single small
tooth, and only the dots tell **ث** from **ت**.

You now hold three ways to write *s*: **س**, **ص** and **ث**. As with the four
*z* letters, Persian says them alike, and the spelling remembers which came
from where. Nearly all words with **ث** came from Arabic.

## Writing: ث
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C297-SE-01] -->

Put your pen on ث and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

## Guided Practice

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-TE-01, FA-LEX-C297-MESL-01, FA-SCRIPT-C297-SE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter is this — ث? (***se***, *s*.) Which word did it come
from? (**مثل**, *mesl*, "like".) How many dots, and where? (**Three, above.**)
