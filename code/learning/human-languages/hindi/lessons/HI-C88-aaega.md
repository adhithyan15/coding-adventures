# आएगा — the ending that tells you who, not when

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02, HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say *I will come*. Point at somebody else and try to say it
about them — and notice which part of the word has to move.

## You'll want to know: आएगा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-03]; assesses=[] -->

**आएगा** (*āegā*) — "he will come."
**आएगी** (*āegī*) — "she will come."

**वह आएगा।** — He will come. **वह आएगी।** — She will come.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-03, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02] -->

Set the two first persons beside the third:

| who | ending |
|---|---|
| मैं | -ऊँगा |
| वह | -एगा |

The **-गा** has not moved. It is the piece in front of it that changed, from
**-ऊँ-** to **-ए-**, and that piece is the one carrying the person.

So the future has the same division of labour the present had: **one slot for
who, one slot for when.** Learning the tense is learning that the two slots are
separate.

## Grammar Lens: the form a paper prints
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-03, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02] -->

This is the commonest future in reading material, because most sentences on a
page are about somebody other than the reader:

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| वह आएगा। | He will come. |
| वह जाएगी। | She will go. |

A bus, a train or a shop takes the same ending as a person, and the gender of
the **noun** decides which one. A practice paper prints *the bus will come* with
**-एगी**, because बस is feminine.
