Mandarin Chinese, chapter 46: The Week.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
星期 (xīngqī) — week.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for now, then the Mandarin for tomorrow.

You'll want to know: 星期.
星期 — xīngqī — "week".
一个星期有七天 yí ge xīngqī yǒu qī tiān — There are seven days in a week.
The new character, in pieces: 星 (xīng) — 日 above 生; 期 (qī) — an eight-stroke piece on the left and a moon piece on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
星期 (xīngqī) is built from 星 (xīng) 'star' and 期 (qī) 'appointed time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, seng and gi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean a week is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for times and partings.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for now, then the Mandarin for tomorrow, then say xīngqī again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — 日 above 生.
星 (xīng).
Two pieces you know, top to bottom: 日, then 生.
日, four strokes, short and wide on top.
生, five strokes, as in 学生: a left-falling stroke, a horizontal, a shorter horizontal, the vertical through them, and the long bar at the foot.
4 + 5 = nine.

有 日.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — yǒu, rì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — eight strokes, then the moon.
期 (qī).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, eight strokes. A horizontal at the top. Two verticals down through it. Two short horizontals between them. A long horizontal across the foot. Then a left-falling stroke and a dot under it.
The moon, four strokes. A left-falling stroke down the left. A horizontal that turns down the right and hooks. Two short horizontals inside. It is the lower part of 有.
8 + 4 = twelve.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 期 (qī), all twelve, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 期 (qī)? (Two — eight strokes, then the moon.) What is under the long bar of the left piece? (A left-falling stroke and a dot.) How does the moon begin? (With a left-falling stroke.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
星期一 (xīngqīyī) — Monday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tomorrow, then the Mandarin for week.

You'll want to know: 星期一.
星期一 — xīngqīyī — "Monday".
今天星期一 jīntiān xīngqīyī — Today's Monday.
Tone: 一 keeps its first tone here, yī. It counts a day in order, and the change you know from yìbǎi does not apply.

The word, taken apart.
星期一 (xīngqīyī) is built from 星期 (xīngqī) 'week' and 一 'one': day one of the week.

Lesson 6 of 7.
zhōumò — weekend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for Monday, then the Mandarin for moment.

You'll want to know: zhōumò.
zhōumò — "weekend".
zhōumò wǒ xǐhuan kàn shū — At the weekend I like to read.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhōumò is built from zhōu 'week' and mò 'end'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese mat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the tip of a tree' and 'end'. The first character is recorded there as tsyuw, 'a full circuit'; the sense week is not given, so how it came to name the week is not settled.
