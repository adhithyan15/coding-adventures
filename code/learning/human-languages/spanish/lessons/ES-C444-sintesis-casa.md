# (un aviso del casero) — read it, then do it yourself

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la calefacción* and *cubrir*. The notice you are about to read
uses one of them, and the reply uses the other.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-02, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-03] -->

Read both, then answer.

**1 — un aviso en el portal**

> Se informa a los vecinos de que la **calefacción** quedará apagada el martes
> por trabajos en la caldera. Los suelos de **madera** del portal se barnizarán
> el mismo día. Rogamos no pisar.

**2 — un mensaje al casero**

> Buenos días. La ventana del salón no cierra bien y entra frío. También he
> **plantado** las macetas que dejó en la terraza, pero habrá que **cubrirlas**
> si hiela. ¿Puede pasar el técnico esta semana?

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: what will be off on Tuesday, and why]
- [YOU SAY: what the residents are asked not to do]
- [YOU SAY: the two things the tenant reports, and the one request]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-05, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01] -->

Look at **cubrirlas** in the second message.

The pronoun is stuck on the **end** of the infinitive: *cubrir* plus *las*, the
flowerpots. Spanish attaches the object to an infinitive rather than putting it
in front, and does the same to a command — *cúbrelas* — and to a gerund.
English never does this, which is why *habrá que cubrirlas* looks like one long
word and is two.

The first notice will not do that, because it has no people in it: *se informa*,
*se barnizarán*, *rogamos*. It is the impersonal register you met on the
council's page, and the contrast between the two texts is the whole point of
reading them together. A neighbour writes with pronouns; a notice writes without.

Even the **-ito** ending belongs to the message and not the notice. Nobody puts
*un momentito* on a formal sign.
