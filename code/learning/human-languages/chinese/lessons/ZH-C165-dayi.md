## You'll want to know: 大衣
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C165-COAT165-01]; assesses=[] -->

**大衣** — *dàyī* — "overcoat; coat".

> **我的大衣在哪儿** *wǒ de dàyī zài nǎr* — Where's my coat?

The new character, in pieces: 衣 — six strokes: a dot and a horizontal on top, then a long stroke falling to the left, a short vertical that flicks up to the right, a short stroke falling to the left, and a long stroke falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**大衣** (*dàyī*) is built from 大 'big' and 衣 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *daH* and *'j+j* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the garment of the upper body: the big garment over the rest.
