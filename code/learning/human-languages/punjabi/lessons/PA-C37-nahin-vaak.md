# ਨਹੀਂ inside a sentence

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-HAAN-NAHIN-01, PA-LEX-MAIN-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] From the first chapter of this book: the two words that answer a
question. And from the third: the word for **I**.

- [YOU RECALL: *hā̃*, *nahī̃* — then *maiṁ*]

You have been able to say **no** since page one. You have never been able to say
that something **is not so**.

## The pattern, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-NEG-CLAUSE-01]; assesses=[PA-GRAMMAR-MAIN-HAAN-03] -->

**ਨਹੀਂ** goes **directly before the verb**, and nothing else in the sentence
moves:

> ਮੈਂ *(…)* **ਨਹੀਂ** *(verb)* — "I do **not** *(verb)*"

That is the whole rule. Punjabi does not add a helper the way English adds
*do* — it drops one word in front of the verb and leaves the rest alone.

Because the position is fixed, every sentence you already own has a negative
twin, and you can make it without learning anything else.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-NEG-CLAUSE-01, PA-LEX-HAAN-NAHIN-01] -->

- [YOU SAY: *nahī̃* alone, as the answer you already had.]
- [YOU BUILD: a sentence you can already say, then the same sentence with

- [YOU CONTRAST: the positive and the negative, back to back.]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-NEG-CLAUSE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Where does **ਨਹੀਂ** stand in a sentence? (Directly before the verb.)
What else has to change? (Nothing.)
