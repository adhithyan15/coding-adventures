Hindi, chapter 414: Bathrooms and Comfort.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गुसलख़ाना (gusalkhānā) — a bathroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to stop over, then the Hindi for to spread out.

You'll want to know: गुसलख़ाना.
गुसलख़ाना — gusalkhānā — "a bathroom".
हमारे कमरे का गुसलख़ाना छोटा है। hamāre kamre kā gusalkhānā choṭā hai. The bathroom in our room is small.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
गुसलख़ाना (gusalkhānā) is usually traced to Persian ġusl-khāna, a room (khāna) for washing (ġusl); Hindi speech smooths ġusl to gusal.

Lesson 2 of 5.
शौचालय (śaucālay) — a toilet (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to spread out, then the Hindi for a bathroom.

You'll want to know: शौचालय.
शौचालय — śaucālay — "a toilet (formal)".
स्टेशन पर शौचालय कहाँ है? sṭeśan par śaucālay kahā̃ hai? Where is the toilet at the station?
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
शौचालय (śaucālay) is a learned compound of Sanskrit śauca, "cleanness, purification", and ālaya, "a house, a dwelling" (Monier-Williams); it is the word on public signs.

Lesson 4 of 5.
आरामदेह (ārāmdeh) — comfortable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a toilet, then the Hindi for a water heater.

You'll want to know: आरामदेह.
आरामदेह — ārāmdeh — "comfortable".
होटल का बिस्तर बहुत आरामदेह था। hoṭal kā bistar bahut ārāmdeh thā. The hotel bed was very comfortable.

The word, taken apart.
आरामदेह (ārāmdeh) is usually traced to Persian ārāmdih, "giving rest", from ārām, "rest", the word in आराम से. It does not change for gender.

Lesson 5 of 5.
जगाना (jagānā) — to wake (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a water heater, then the Hindi for comfortable.

You'll want to know: जगाना.
जगाना — jagānā — "to wake (someone)".
मुझे सुबह पाँच बजे जगाइए। mujhe subah pā̃c baje jagāiye. Please wake me at five in the morning.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे जल्दी जगाया, usne mujhe jaldī jagāyā.

The word, taken apart.
जगाना (jagānā) is the causative partner of जागना, from Sanskrit jāgrati, "are awake" (CDIAL 5175), which lists Hindi jagānā: you जागना yourself, and you जगाना someone else.
