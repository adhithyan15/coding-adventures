Arabic, chapter 290: Bigger, Better, For How Long.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
أكبر من (akbar min) — bigger than.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it was a new experience", then "I hope to travel". (Kānat tajribatan jadīda, atamannā an usāfira.)
[pause 1 second]
To describe an experience well, you set it beside another: the city or the village, the train or the bus.

Grammar Lens: the shape aCCaC, and min.
Take the three root letters of a describing word and pour them into the shape a-C-C-a-C: kabīr, big, becomes أكبر akbar, bigger; ṣaghīr becomes aṣghar; sarīʿ, fast, becomes asraʿ. من — min, "from" — is the "than":
المدينة أكبر من القرية. — al-madīna akbar min al-qarya — The city is bigger than the village.
The shape does not change for a feminine noun: al-madīna akbar, al-qiṭār asraʿ min ad-darrāja. You have owned two of these since chapter one hundred and seventy-six: akthar, more, is the shape of kathīr, many, and aqall, less, of qalīl, few.

Grammar Lens: the biggest, and better.
Put al- on it for "the biggest": al-akbar. Or set it before a noun with no article: أكبر مدينة akbar madīna, the biggest city.
"Better" has a shape of its own: أفضل afḍal, from the root of faḍḍala, to prefer, and of min faḍlik.
القطار أفضل من الحافلة. — al-qiṭār afḍal min al-ḥāfila — The train is better than the bus.
Al-afḍal is "the best".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The train is faster than the bicycle" — al-qiṭār asraʿ min ad-darrāja]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "the biggest city" — akbar madīna]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Bigger than"? (Akbar min.) "Smaller"? (Aṣghar.) "The best"? (Al-afḍal.) Which two words were in this shape all along? (Akthar and aqall.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
منذ ثلاث سنوات (mundhu thalāth sanawāt) — for three years.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the city is bigger than the village", then "the biggest city". (Al-madīna akbar min al-qarya, akbar madīna.)
[pause 1 second]
An experience also has a length. Mundhu, from chapter two hundred and thirty-three, measures it.

Grammar Lens: mundhu and the present.
For something that began in the past and is still going on, Arabic keeps the present and adds mundhu with the span:
أعمل هنا منذ ثلاث سنوات. — aʿmalu hunā mundhu thalāth sanawāt — I have been working here for three years.
English says "have been working"; Arabic says "I work, since three years". With a point in time instead of a span, mundhu is "since": anā hunā mundhu aṣ-ṣabāḥ, I've been here since the morning.
Counting takes care. Sana, a year, ends in ة, and three to ten take the opposite ending to their noun: thalāth sanawāt, three years, but thalāthat ashhur, three months. For two, the noun takes -ayn: sanatayn, two years; usbūʿayn, two weeks.

Grammar Lens: ago.
For a finished event, use the past and قبل — qabla, "before" — with the span:
وصلت قبل أسبوع. — waṣaltu qabla usbūʿ — I arrived a week ago.
Mundhu with a past verb can say "ago" too: waṣaltu mundhu usbūʿ. With the present it can only mean "for".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I have been working here for three years" — aʿmalu hunā mundhu thalāth sanawāt]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "for three months", then "for two weeks" — mundhu thalāthat ashhur, mundhu usbūʿayn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I arrived a week ago" — waṣaltu qabla usbūʿ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I have been learning Arabic for two years" — ataʿallamu al-ʿarabiyya mundhu sanatayn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: how long you have lived where you live — askunu hunā mundhu …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"For three years", with a present? (Mundhu thalāth sanawāt.) "A week ago"? (Qabla usbūʿ.) "Two years"? (Sanatayn.) Which tense goes with mundhu for "for"? (The present.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — bigger, better, and for how long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was happy" and "I felt afraid". (Kuntu saʿīdan, shaʿartu bi-l-khawf.) Then "I want to become an engineer". (Urīdu an uṣbiḥa muhandisan.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and an experience can be weighed and measured. Weighed with the shape aCCaC and min: akbar min, asraʿ min, afḍal min; with al- or before a noun for the most: al-afḍal, akbar madīna. Measured with mundhu and the present, for what is still going on, and qabla with the past, for "ago".
[pause 2 seconds]
Both in one breath:
سافرت بالطائرة قبل سنة، وكانت أفضل تجربة. — sāfartu bi-ṭ-ṭāʾira qabla sana, wa-kānat afḍala tajriba — I travelled by plane a year ago, and it was the best experience.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "The plane is faster than the train" — aṭ-ṭāʾira asraʿ min al-qiṭār]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "This pencil case is smaller" — hādhihi al-miqlama aṣghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I have been waiting for the information since the morning" — antaẓiru al-maʿlūmāt mundhu aṣ-ṣabāḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: something you did a year ago, and how long you have done something else]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Better than"? (Afḍal min.) "The best experience"? (Afḍal tajriba.) "For two years", with a present? (Mundhu sanatayn.) "A year ago"? (Qabla sana.)
