## You'll want to know: duìfāng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C348-TALK348-04]; assesses=[] -->

*duìfāng* — "the other side; the other party".

> *nǐ yīnggāi tīng duìfāng shuō wán* — You should hear the other side out.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*duìfāng* is built from 对 'facing' and *fāng* 'a side', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *twojH* and *pjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to answer' and 'to match', and the second 'the four quarters'.
