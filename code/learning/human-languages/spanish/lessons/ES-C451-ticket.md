# el ticket — the receipt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BILLETE, ES-LEX-C313-ASK-19] -->

till prints.

## You'll want to know: el ticket
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BILLETE] -->

**el ticket** — the receipt, the slip from the till.

*¿Quiere el ticket?* — would you like the receipt? *Guarde el ticket para
cambios* — keep the receipt for exchanges. *El ticket de la compra* — the
shopping receipt.

Said **TI-ket**. Spanish also writes it **tique**, and the shorter **el
recibo** covers the same ground in more formal use — on an invoice, or where
money has changed hands between businesses.

## Grammar Lens: three slips of paper, three words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-05, ES-LEX-BILLETE] -->

English calls all of these a ticket. Spanish will not:

| what it does | the word |
|---|---|
| carries you somewhere | **el billete** |
| gets you in | **la entrada** |
| proves you paid | **el ticket** |

A train journey is *un billete*. A concert is *una entrada*. What the
supermarket hands you is *un ticket*, and asking for *un billete* at a till
would suggest you wanted a banknote, since *billete* covers that too.

Sort them by the job rather than by the paper, and all three stay apart.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-05, ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-04] -->

This word has been around the houses.

Old French **estiquier** meant to stick — a Frankish word, Germanic in origin,
the same family as English *stick*. From it came **estiquet**, a little notice
*stuck* onto something: a label.

English borrowed *estiquet* and wore it down to **ticket**, meanwhile
narrowing it from any label to a paid-for slip. Spanish then borrowed the
English word back, keeping the English spelling.

Spanish also kept the original French form separately, as **la etiqueta** — a
label, and by extension the rules of polite behaviour, which were once
literally the notices posted at court telling visitors how to behave. One
Frankish word for sticking something on a surface, and it has ended up as a
supermarket receipt and as the word *etiquette*.
