Italian, chapter 315: Tanti auguri.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
celebrare — to celebrate formally; to hold (a ceremony).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the heir, then the Italian for the grave.

You'll want to know: celebrare.
celebrare — "to celebrate formally; to hold (a ceremony)".
Abbiamo celebrato i cento anni della scuola. We celebrated the school's hundredth anniversary.
More formal than festeggiare.

The word, taken apart.
celebrare is a learned borrowing from Latin celebrāre "to frequent; to honour with a crowd" (Lewis & Short), from celeber "crowded".

Lesson 4 of 5.
gli auguri — best wishes, greetings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the recurring occasion, then the Italian for the anniversary.

You'll want to know: gli auguri.
gli auguri — "best wishes, greetings".
Tanti auguri di buon compleanno! Happy birthday and all the best!
Fare gli auguri a qualcuno. Singular l'augurio.

The word, taken apart.
auguri is the plural of augurio, a learned borrowing from Latin augurium "an omen read by an augur" (Lewis & Short).
