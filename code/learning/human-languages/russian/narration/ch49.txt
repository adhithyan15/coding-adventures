Russian, chapter 49: Table, Chair, Bed, Lamp, Key.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
стол (stol) — a table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a window, then the Russian for a door.

You'll want to know: стол.
стол (stol) — "a table".

The word, taken apart.
стол (stol) from Proto-Slavic stolŭ — inherited; Old Russian stolŭ also meant "throne, seat" (Vasmer). Vasmer compares Gothic stōls, "chair", and Lithuanian stãlas, "table", but also weighs the root of стелить, "to spread", so the deeper origin is open.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a door, then the Russian for a table.

You'll want to know: стул.
стул (stul) — "a chair".

The word, taken apart.
стул (stul) — first recorded in 1578, perhaps borrowed from Old Norse stóll or Low German stuhl, "chair" (Vasmer). A High German loan would have begun шт-.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a chair, then the Russian for a bed.

You'll want to know: лампа.
лампа (lámpa) — "a lamp".

The word, taken apart.
лампа (lámpa) came from German Lampe or French lampe, from Latin lampada and Greek lampás, "a torch"; the old form лямпа came through Polish (Vasmer).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ключ (klyuch) — a key.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bed, then the Russian for a lamp.

You'll want to know: ключ.
ключ (klyuch) — "a key".

The word, taken apart.
ключ (klyuch) from Proto-Slavic ključĭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Greek kleís, "key", and Latin clāvis, "key", and claudō, "I shut". The ключ (klyuch) that means "a spring" may be the same word or another.
