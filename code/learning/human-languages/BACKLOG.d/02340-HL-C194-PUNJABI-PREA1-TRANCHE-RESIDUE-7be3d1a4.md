## HL-C194 — what the Punjabi pre-A1 courtesy tranche left behind

Chapters 31–36 realized the last fourteen unrealized pre-A1 spine concepts for
Punjabi and taught nine of its seventeen never-taught Gurmukhi glyphs. Four
items were measured and deliberately not done.

**Eight Gurmukhi glyphs are still shown and never taught: ਈ ਘ ਝ ਟ ਧ ਵ ੍ ੜ.**
Six are ordinary base letters and would fall out of six more everyday words.
The other two are structural and want their own sessions: **੍**, the subjoining
mark that tucks one letter under another (the reader has already seen the
finished result in **ਪੜ੍ਹਨਾ** and in **ਕੱਲ੍ਹ**, both taught by ear only), and
**ੜ**, the retroflex flap that carries Punjabi's own sound inventory. The
forty script-closure violations in Chapters 2–13 will not fall until those two
land, because every one of them sits in a lesson written before the writing
runway existed.

**Two forward references were surfaced, not created.** **ਮੈਨੂੰ** is used in the
liking lesson and in the tea request long before this tranche names it, and the
two letters of **ਨਾ** close every infinitive the verb chapters teach. Both
readings were invisible while no lesson claimed to teach either string; naming
them made the earlier uses measurable. The repair is to teach the object
pronoun before the liking lesson needs it, which means an insertion in the
early chapters rather than an addition at the end.

**Eight Punjabi headwords still carry no romanization** — the four farewell
lessons and the four first-verb lessons, all schema v1. Each one is a lesson
whose Gurmukhi becomes free exposure the moment somebody writes down how to say
it, so this is eight lines of frontmatter against eight lessons' worth of
script-closure debt. It was left alone here only because those two chapters are
legacy schema and a migration should move them together.

**Pre-A1 vocabulary stands at 50 of 300.** The tranche moved it by fourteen and
the remaining 250 will not come from spine concepts, because there are none
left unrealized at this level. The next tranche has to be chosen from
frequency rather than from the ledger, and the verb count is the sharper gap:
one verb headword against the five the level asks for, because Punjabi's
sixteen verb lessons all sit at A1 and above.
