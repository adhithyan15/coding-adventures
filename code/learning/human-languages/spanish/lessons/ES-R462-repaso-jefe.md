# (reducir, decidirse, el jefe) — from cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Close the lessons before this one. Say all three: *to cut back*,
*to make your mind up*, *the boss*.

## Grammar Lens: one word, two roads, four hundred years apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03] -->

| the road | the word |
|---|---|
| worn down through Spanish speech | **la cabeza** |
| borrowed from French, much later | **el jefe** |

Both are Latin *caput*, head. The first went through centuries of mouths and
came out as a body part; the second was picked up whole from a neighbour and
came out as a person.

You have watched this twice before — *rueda* against *rotativo*, *consejo*
against *consultar* — but those pairs both came from Latin. This one arrived
through a **third** language, which is why it looks like neither.

## Grammar Lens: two verbs, two pictures of cutting and leading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02] -->

| verb | the picture inside it |
|---|---|
| **reducir** | *re-ducere*, to **lead back** |
| **decidirse** | *de-caedere*, to **cut off** |

Deciding cuts the other options away; reducing leads the number back down.
Both are physical, and both are still doing the work: *la ducha* leads water,
*preciso* is cut to the edge.

And *decidirse* is the fourth *-se* pair you have met, after *mudarse*,
*acercarse* and *arreglarse*. Here the *-se* adds effort — *no me decido* is
dithering, where *no decido* would be a job description.
