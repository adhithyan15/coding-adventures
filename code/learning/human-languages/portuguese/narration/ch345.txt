Portuguese, chapter 345: Prós e contras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a vantagem — the advantage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for objective, then the Portuguese for subjective.

You'll want to know: a vantagem.
a vantagem — "the advantage".
A grande vantagem desta casa é o jardim. The great advantage of this house is the garden.
Tirar vantagem de — to take advantage of.

The word, taken apart.
vantagem is usually traced to Old French avantage, built on avant "before", which EtymDB derives from Late Latin abante "before, in front", from ab and ante "before" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short); Portuguese dropped the first a-.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o aspeto — the aspect, the side; the appearance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the advantage, then the Portuguese for the disadvantage.

You'll want to know: o aspeto.
o aspeto — "the aspect, the side; the appearance".
Há um aspeto que ainda não discutimos. There is one aspect we have not discussed yet.
European aspeto (AO90); Brazil aspecto. Ter bom aspeto — to look good.

The word, taken apart.
aspeto is a learned borrowing from Latin aspectus "a seeing, a sight, an appearance" (Lewis & Short), from aspicere "to behold", built from ad and specere "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a crítica — the criticism; the review (of a film or a book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the disadvantage, then the Portuguese for the aspect.

You'll want to know: a crítica.
a crítica — "the criticism; the review (of a film or a book)".
Ele não aceita bem as críticas. He does not take criticism well.
Fazer uma crítica — to criticise; o crítico — the critic.

The word, taken apart.
crítica is a learned word from Greek kritikḗ "the art of judging", built on krínein "to separate, to judge"; Latin borrowed the adjective as criticus "fit for deciding" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: ponderar.
ponderar — "to weigh up, to consider carefully".
Ponderei bem antes de mudar de casa. I thought it over carefully before moving house.
Ponderar os prós e os contras — to weigh up the pros and cons.

The word, taken apart.
ponderar is a learned borrowing from Latin ponderāre "to weigh a thing, to ponder" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built on pondus, ponderis "weight".
