Malayalam, chapter 144: So, Purpose, Result, Cause, On account of.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
അതുകൊണ്ട് (atukoṇṭŭ) — so, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a habit, then the Malayalam for a qualification.

You'll want to know: അതുകൊണ്ട്.
അതുകൊണ്ട് — atukoṇṭŭ — "so, therefore".

The word, taken apart.
അതുകൊണ്ട് (atukoṇṭŭ) is native Dravidian, "taking that": atŭ, "that" (DEDR 1), with koṇṭŭ, the past form of the verb "to take" (DEDR 2151, with Tamil koḷ).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ഉദ്ദേശ്യം (uddēśyaṁ) — a purpose, an intention.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a qualification, then the Malayalam for so.
[pause 1 second]
Two older, literary words for night: iravŭ and iruḷ. Which one is also the Tamil word? (Iravŭ.)

You'll want to know: ഉദ്ദേശ്യം.
ഉദ്ദേശ്യം — uddēśyaṁ — "a purpose, an intention".

The word, taken apart.
ഉദ്ദേശ്യം (uddēśyaṁ) from Sanskrit uddēśya, "to be aimed at, intended", taken over whole as a tatsama, with Malayalam's noun ending -ṁ.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for so, then the Malayalam for a purpose.

You'll want to know: ഫലം.
ഫലം — phalaṁ — "a result".

The word, taken apart.
ഫലം (phalaṁ) from Sanskrit phala, "fruit", taken over whole as a tatsama, with Malayalam's noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 9051). A result is the fruit of an action.
