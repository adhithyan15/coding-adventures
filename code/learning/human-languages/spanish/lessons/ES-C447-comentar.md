## You'll want to know: comentar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PENSAR-01] -->

**comentar** — to comment on, and very often no more than **to mention**.

*Me comentó que se jubila* — he mentioned to me that he is retiring. *¿Lo
comentamos mañana?* — shall we talk it over tomorrow? *Sin comentarios* — no
comment.

That middle sense is the one to take. In everyday Spanish **comentar** is much
closer to *mention* or *talk over* than to the English *comment*, which sounds
like a verdict. A Spanish speaker *comenta* things all day without passing
judgement on any of them.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-02, ES-ETYMON-PENSAR-02] -->

Latin **com-** together, plus **mens**, **mentem** — the **mind**.

To *comentar* is **to mind something over with somebody**. The mind is sitting
inside the word, and it is the same *mentem* behind **la mente**, **mental**, and
the ending **-mente** that turns a Spanish adjective into an adverb:
*rápidamente* is, at root, *with a rapid mind*.

| word | the mind in it |
|---|---|
| comentar | to turn over together |
| la mente | the mind itself |
| rápida**mente** | with a rapid mind |

That last row is worth keeping. Every Spanish adverb in **-mente** is a fossil
of the word for mind, agreeing in the feminine because *mens* was a feminine
noun — which is why it is *rápida*mente and never *rápido*mente.
