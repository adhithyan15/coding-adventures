# ہے — the smallest word, in every sentence you own

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-BARI-YE-01, UR-SCRIPT-HE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write ہے. Two letters: the small loop, then the wide flat sweep.
Say it: **hai**.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-MERA-NAAM-HAI, UR-LEX-MAAN, UR-LEX-YIH, UR-LEX-KAAM] -->

Four sentences. Read each from the right, word by word, and say it before you
move on.

> میرا نام ... ہے۔
>
> یہ میری ماں ہے۔
>
> یہ کام ہے۔
>
> یہ کالا ہے۔

*merā nām ... hai.* — *yih merī māṅ hai.* — *yih kām hai.* — *yih kālā hai.*

Look at what sits at the end of every one of them. **ہے** is where an Urdu
sentence lands, in the place English puts *is* in the middle. You have been
producing that landing by ear since the very first sentence you learned; from
today you can also see it.

## Guided Practice — by ear
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-YIH, UR-LEX-KAAM, UR-LEX-KAHAN, UR-LEX-MAAN, UR-LEX-HONA, UR-LEX-LAL, UR-LEX-KALA] -->

Page shut. Point at things around you and land each sentence on *hai*.

- [YOU SAY: **yih kām hai** — this is work]
- [YOU SAY: **yih merī māṅ hai** — this is my mother]
- [YOU SAY: **yih lāl hai**, then **yih kālā hai**]
- [YOU SAY: **kahāṅ?** — and hum the nasal at its end]
- [YOU LISTEN: to *hai*, *haiṅ*, *hūṅ* one after another, and say which is
  yours when you speak about yourself]

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-BARI-YE-01, UR-LEX-MAAN, UR-ETYMON-MAAN-MATER, UR-ETYMON-KAAM-KARMA] -->

- [YOU WRITE: یہ کام ہے۔ — three words, right to left]
- [YOU WRITE: ہے on its own, ten times, until the flat tail stops curling up]
- [YOU SAY: which of *māṅ* and *mādar* is inherited, and which is borrowed]
- [YOU SAY: which of *kām* and English *karma* was worn short, and why]
