Latin, chapter 80: Cheese, Oil, Grapes, Olives.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cāseus, cāseī — cheese.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for breakfast, then the Latin for meat.

You'll want to know: cāseus, cāseī.
cāseus, cāseī — "cheese".

The word, taken apart.
cāseus is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root kwat-, "to ferment, to turn sour" (EtymDB 2.1). English cheese was borrowed from cāseus very early.

Lesson 2 of 5.
oleum, oleī — oil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for meat, then the Latin for cheese.

You'll want to know: oleum, oleī.
oleum, oleī — "oil". Pressed from the olive.

The word, taken apart.
oleum was borrowed from Greek elaion, "olive oil" (Lewis & Short), probably from an older Greek elaiwon (EtymDB 2.1). English oil comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
holus, holeris — vegetables, greens.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for oil, then the Latin for a grape.

You'll want to know: holus, holeris.
holus, holeris — "vegetables, greens".

The word, taken apart.
holus is inherited. Old Latin had helus, "greens", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European ghelh3-, "green, yellow" (EtymDB 2.1). English yellow is a cousin.
