# ¿A qué se dedica? — the question about work

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-05, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-06] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el abogado* and *la empresa*. One answers what somebody does and
one answers where. A conversation about work needs both.

## Grammar Lens: naming a profession takes no article
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-05, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-01, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-03] -->

This is the one grammatical trap in the answer, and English speakers fall into
it every time:

| | |
|---|---|
| correct | Soy abogado. |
| wrong | Soy **un** abogado. |

English says *I am **a** lawyer*. Spanish drops the article when the sentence
states nothing but the profession, because the bare noun is working as a
description rather than picking out one individual.

The article comes back as soon as you add anything to it: *Soy **un** abogado
trabajador* — "I am a hard-working lawyer." Adding the adjective turns the
description into a particular kind of thing, and a particular thing takes an
article.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-05, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-06, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-03, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-04] -->

Read aloud. Every word has been taught:

> — ¿A qué se dedica?
> — Soy abogado. Trabajo en una empresa grande.
> — ¿Y su hermana?
> — Es policía. Mi hermano es bombero.

Three professions, no article on any of them, and *una empresa* taking one
because it is a thing rather than a description.
