# vingt — twenty

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three built numbers in a row, and then this — which is not built out
of anything at all.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-VINGT-05]; assesses=[] -->

- **vingt** = *VA(N)*. The **in** is the nasal vowel you have in **vin** and
  **pain**.
- `silent-final` — the **g** and the **t** are both silent. Four written letters
  after the vowel, none of them heard.
- Say **vin** and **vingt** aloud: standing alone they are **identical**. Only
  the spelling and the sentence tell them apart.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**vingt** is Latin **vīgintī**, and that is the whole story: an old, single,
inherited word, like *dix* and unlike *dix-sept*.

So the count you have just walked through has **three** systems in it, not two:

- 11–16 — inherited, **fused**, digit first
- 17–19 — **built**, transparent, ten first
- 20 — inherited again, a **single word** built from nothing

Twenty is not the end of a pattern. It is the start of a different one, because
*vingt* is the number French will later count **in**.
