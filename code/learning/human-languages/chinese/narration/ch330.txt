Lesson 1 of 6.
jìchéng — to inherit; to take over (from a parent).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to vanish without a trace, then the Mandarin for the ending of a story.

You'll want to know: jìchéng.
jìchéng — "to inherit; to take over (from a parent)".
tā èrshí suì de shíhou jìchéng le tā bàba de shūdiàn — When he was twenty he took over his father's bookshop.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìchéng is built from jì and chéng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kejH and dzying (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to carry on' and the second 'to receive'.

王子一个人走了很长的路 wángzǐ yí ge rén zǒu le hěn cháng de lù — The prince walked a long way on his own.
The new character, in pieces: 王 (wáng) — four strokes: three bars, the middle one the shortest and the bottom one the longest, with a vertical through the middle. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
王子 (wángzǐ) is built from 王 (wáng) 'a king' and 子 'a son', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjwang and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'great' and 'a ruler' and the second 'a child'.

Script — two bars, a vertical, a long bar.
王 (wáng).
One piece, four strokes.
Two bars first, the top one, then a shorter one in the middle.
Then the vertical, from the top bar down through the middle one to the foot.
Last, the longest bar, along the foot, closing the vertical.
2 + 1 + 1 = four.

Lesson 5 of 6.
王后 (wánghòu) — queen (a king's wife).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a prince, then the Mandarin for a king in a story.

You'll want to know: 王后.
王后 — wánghòu — "queen (a king's wife)".
国王和王后有一个女儿 guówáng hé wánghòu yǒu yí ge nǚ'ér — The king and queen had a daughter.

The word, taken apart.
王后 (wánghòu) is built from 王 (wáng) 'a king' and hòu 'a queen', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjwang and huwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the second under both huwX and huwH, 'a sovereign', and under huwH also 'an empress'. This hòu is a different word from the 后 of 后来, whose older form the dictionary lists under huwX, 'before and after'.
