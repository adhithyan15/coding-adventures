German, chapter 433: Wo ist Norden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
orientieren — to get one's bearings (mostly reflexive: sich orientieren).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to compensate, then the German for the compensation.

You'll want to know: orientieren.
orientieren — "to get one's bearings (mostly reflexive: sich orientieren)".
In der fremden Stadt orientiert er sich am Fluss. In the unfamiliar city he gets his bearings from the river.
Mostly reflexive: ich orientiere mich. Past participle without ge-: ich habe mich orientiert.

The word, taken apart.
orientieren was borrowed from French orienter 'to set facing east', built on Latin oriēns 'rising (sun), the east', from orīrī 'to rise' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the German verb.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Kompass — the compass.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the German for the compensation, then the German for to get one's bearings.

You'll want to know: der Kompass.
der Kompass — "the compass".
Im Wald hilft ihr der Kompass. In the forest the compass helps her.
Plural: die Kompasse.

The word, taken apart.
Kompass was borrowed from Italian compasso 'compass, pair of compasses', usually traced to a Vulgar Latin verb built on Latin passus 'step'. EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Himmelsrichtung — the point of the compass, the direction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to get one's bearings, then the German for the compass.

You'll want to know: die Himmelsrichtung.
die Himmelsrichtung — "the point of the compass, the direction".
Norden, Süden, Osten und Westen sind die vier Himmelsrichtungen. North, south, east and west are the four points of the compass.
Plural: die Himmelsrichtungen.

The word, taken apart.
Himmelsrichtung is built from Himmel 'sky, heaven' and Richtung 'direction', from richten 'to direct'. Both are inherited, and Richtung shares its root with recht 'right', Proto-Germanic rehtaz.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zurechtfinden — to find one's way around (mostly reflexive: sich zurechtfinden).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the compass, then the German for the point of the compass.

You'll want to know: zurechtfinden.
zurechtfinden — "to find one's way around (mostly reflexive: sich zurechtfinden)".
Nach einer Woche findet sie sich in der Stadt gut zurecht. After a week she finds her way around the town well.
Reflexive and separable: ich finde mich zurecht, ich habe mich zurechtgefunden.

The word, taken apart.
zurechtfinden is built from zurecht 'into the right place', from recht 'right' (Proto-Germanic rehtaz), and finden 'to find'; both are inherited.

Lesson 5 of 5.
verirren — to get lost (mostly reflexive: sich verirren).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the point of the compass, then the German for to find one's way around.

You'll want to know: verirren.
verirren — "to get lost (mostly reflexive: sich verirren)".
Im Nebel haben sich die Wanderer verirrt. In the fog the hikers got lost.
Reflexive: ich habe mich verirrt. On foot, people also say sich verlaufen.

The word, taken apart.
verirren is built from ver- and irren 'to wander, to go astray', inherited from Old High German irren and Proto-Germanic irzijaną (EtymDB).
