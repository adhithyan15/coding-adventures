## You'll want to know: 老公
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C272-KIN272-02]; assesses=[] -->

**老公** — *lǎogōng* — "hubby; husband (informal)".

> **我老公先回家了** *wǒ lǎogōng xiān huí jiā le* — My husband has gone home ahead of me.

The new character, in pieces: 老 — six strokes: a short bar, a vertical and a long bar, then a long stroke falling to the left; under them a short stroke falling to the left and a vertical that bends right and hooks up, the right half of 比. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**老公** (*lǎogōng*) is built from 老 'old' and 公, both inherited from Middle Chinese, *lawX* and *kuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'aged' and the second, among other senses, 'a father' and 'an official'. How the pair came to mean a husband is not settled.
