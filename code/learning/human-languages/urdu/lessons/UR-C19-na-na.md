# نہ … نہ … — neither, nor

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB, UR-LEX-PANI, UR-LEX-CHAI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **نہیں** stands in front of a verb. Urdu has a shorter form of the
same word that stands in front of a **noun**, and it is almost always doubled.

- [YOU RECALL: the two items before this one — *maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā*, *kyā āp ṭhīk haiṅ?*]

## You'll want to know: نہ … نہ …
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-NA-NA]; assesses=[UR-LEX-PANI, UR-LEX-CHAI] -->

> **نہ پانی، نہ ہوا** — *na pānī, na hawā* — **neither water nor air.**

One **نہ** in front of each thing refused. Strip the **-یں** off **نہیں** and
what is left is **نہ**, the older and shorter negator: the two are the same word
at different stages of wear, which is why the doubled refusal sounds like the
plain one cut short.

## Grammar Lens: three negators, told apart by what follows them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-NA-NA, UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB, UR-LEX-NAHIN] -->

| form | stands before | example |
|---|---|---|
| **نہیں** | a verb | *maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā* |
| **نہیں** | alone | the complete answer *no* |
| **نہ … نہ** | each refused noun | *na pānī, na hawā* |

Urdu has a third, *mat*, which belongs in front of a command — and this book
has no command yet, so it waits. Its second letter has no lesson in the script
ladder either, which is a second reason to leave it where it is.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-NA-NA, UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which negator stands in front of a verb, and which in front of a
noun? (**نہیں** before a verb; **نہ** before each refused noun.) How many
**نہ** does *neither … nor* need? (**Two, one for each thing**.) And what is
**نہ** to **نہیں**? (**The same word, shorter and older**.)
