# कारण (kāraṇ) — because

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-JOIN-KAA-WHY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can now ask **का?**. Ask it out loud. Then notice that you have
no way whatever to answer it.

## Grammar Lens: कारण takes a whole sentence after it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-JOIN-KAARAN]; assesses=[MR-LEX-AVADNE, MR-LEX-SHIKNE, MR-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-GENDER] -->

> **मी मराठी शिकतो कारण मला मराठी आवडते.**
> — *mī marāṭhī shikto kāraṇ malā marāṭhī āvaḍte.* —
> **I am learning Marathi because I like Marathi.**

**आणि**, **किंवा** and **पण** put two things side by side as equals. **कारण**
does something new: everything after it is *subordinate* — it exists only to
explain the half in front. That is the first time in this book that one Marathi
clause has been put **inside** the job of another.

The order is fixed and it is the useful way round: **the claim first, the reason
second**. English can lead with *because*; Marathi's ordinary sentence does not.

**कारण** is Sanskrit **कारण** (*kāraṇa*), "a cause," built on the root **कृ**
(*kṛ-*), "to do" — the same **कृ** inside **करणे**, the verb this book gave you
for *to do*. Marathi's *because* is, at root, *the doing of it*.

A short answer works too. Asked **का?**, you may reply with **कारण** and one
clause, and nothing else is needed.
