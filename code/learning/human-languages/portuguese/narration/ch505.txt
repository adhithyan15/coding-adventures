Portuguese, chapter 505: Pincéis e telas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o pincel — paintbrush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for clay, then the Portuguese for pottery.

You'll want to know: o pincel.
o pincel — "paintbrush".
Ela pinta com um pincel muito fino. She paints with a very fine brush.
Plural pincéis. A hairbrush is uma escova.

The word, taken apart.
pincel comes from Catalan pinzell "paintbrush" (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin pēnicillum "a painter's brush".

Lesson 3 of 5.
a tela — canvas.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for paintbrush, then the Portuguese for watercolour.

You'll want to know: a tela.
a tela — "canvas".
O pintor comprou uma tela grande. The painter bought a large canvas.
In Brazil it also means a screen; in Portugal a screen is o ecrã.

The word, taken apart.
tela is inherited from Latin tēla "a web, cloth on a loom" (EtymDB), from texere "to weave".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o cavalete — easel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for watercolour, then the Portuguese for canvas.

You'll want to know: o cavalete.
o cavalete — "easel".
Pôs o cavalete perto da janela para ter luz. She put the easel near the window to get light.
Literally "a little horse": it stands on legs.

The word, taken apart.
cavalete is usually traced to Italian cavalletto "a little horse, a trestle", from Latin caballus "horse" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a moldura — (picture) frame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for canvas, then the Portuguese for easel.

You'll want to know: a moldura.
a moldura — "(picture) frame".
Comprei uma moldura para a fotografia dos avós. I bought a frame for my grandparents' photograph.
The frame of glasses is a armação.

The word, taken apart.
moldura was borrowed from Spanish moldura (EtymDB), built on molde "a mould", which goes back to Latin modulus "a small measure", from modus.
