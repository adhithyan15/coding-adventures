Hindi, chapter 281: Should, Must, and So.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तुम्हें जाना चाहिए (tumhẽ jānā cāhiye) — should, and have to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I need tea". (मुझे चाय चाहिए.) Then "I have to go", the way you take leave. (जाना है.)

You'll want to know: should.
तुम्हें आराम करना चाहिए। tumhẽ ārām karnā cāhiye. — "You should rest."
चाहिए after a thing is "is needed". After a verb in -ना it is "should". The one who should takes को, or a short form of it (मुझे, तुम्हें, आपको, उसे), and चाहिए does not change.
आपको डाक्टर के पास जाना चाहिए। (āpko ḍākṭar ke pās jānā cāhiye.) — You should go to the doctor.
हमें पानी पीना चाहिए। (hamẽ pānī pīnā cāhiye.) — We should drink water.
"Should not" puts नहीं before the verb: तुम्हें ऐसा नहीं करना चाहिए (tumhẽ aisā nahīṁ karnā cāhiye), "you shouldn't do that".

You'll want to know: have to.
कल मुझे दफ़्तर जाना पड़ेगा। kal mujhe daftar jānā paṛegā. — "Tomorrow I will have to go to the office."
पड़ना (paṛnā) after a verb in -ना says there is no choice: something outside you makes it so. The one it falls on takes मुझे again.
मुझे रोज़ जाना पड़ता है। (mujhe roz jānā paṛtā hai.) — I have to go every day.
मुझे जाना पड़ा। (mujhe jānā paṛā.) — I had to go.
मुझे जाना पड़ेगा। (mujhe jānā paṛegā.) — I will have to go.
Three shades, then: जाना है, which you say as you leave, is a plan; जाना चाहिए is advice; जाना पड़ेगा is a necessity.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 5.
अतः (ataḥ) — therefore, in formal writing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "so, therefore" the everyday way. (इसलिए, isliye.)

You'll want to know: अतः.
अतः — ataḥ — "therefore". It is formal. You will read it in notices, letters and the news; in speech, Hindi says इसलिए.
कल छुट्टी है, अतः दफ़्तर बंद रहेगा। kal chuṭṭī hai, ataḥ daftar band rahegā. — "Tomorrow is a holiday; the office will therefore stay closed."
Its last sign, the two dots ः (ḥ), is new, and it has the next lesson to itself. It is said as a light breath of h after the vowel: ataḥ.

The word, taken apart.
अतः (ataḥ) is a tatsama, a Sanskrit word Hindi uses unchanged. Monier-Williams gives its sense as "from this", and so "hence, for this reason": the result comes from what was said before. CDIAL has no row for it.

[your turn — say: the same sentence the everyday way — kal chuṭṭī hai, isliye daftar band rahegā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 3 of 5.
◌ः (ḥ) — two dots and a breath.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ◌ः, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Say अतः (ataḥ), "therefore". Then find the dot of ◌ं in मैं: one dot, above the head-line.
[pause 2 seconds]
Today's sign has two dots, and they stand beside the line, not above it.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ◌ः.
◌ः — the visarga, ḥ: a light breath of h after the vowel before it.
Where it sits: to the right of its syllable, two dots one above the other. In print they hang under a short piece of head-line.
What it is made of:
an upper dot.
a lower dot.
a short piece of headline above them.
Worked through: त + ◌ः (ḥ) = तः, the end of अतः (ataḥ).
It is rare in everyday Hindi. You will meet it in formal words taken straight from Sanskrit, such as अतः (ataḥ).

Writing: ◌ः — follow the numbered strip.
1. start at the top of the upper dot and curve down its left side.
2. without lifting, round the bottom and up the right side to close the loop.
3. lift, then start at the top of the lower dot and curve down its left side.
4. without lifting, round the bottom and up the right side to close the loop — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1. The upper dot first, then the lower.
The short piece of head-line stays grey in the strip: in a word it is part of the head-line, which you draw last, once, across the whole word.
Two strokes — four movements, one pen lift; the upper dot, then the lower, each a small loop drawn anticlockwise from its top, as ं is; the short piece of headline the printed sign carries is left undrawn, because in a word it is part of the one headline drawn last across the whole word; cited to native writers' pen traces in HP Labs India's LipiTk Devanagari recognizer, class 60.
This is one attested order, and handwriting varies by writer. Source: HP Labs India, Lipi Toolkit, Lipi Indic Character Recognizers 4.0, Devanagari recognizer, class 60 (ः (ḥ), visarga): stored online-handwriting prototypes from native writers (MIT licence, 2012).

Lesson 4 of 5.
बारिश हो रही थी, इसलिए … (bāriś ho rahī thī, isliye …) — the cause, then so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "because of the rain". (बारिश की वजह से.) Then the two words for "therefore", the everyday one and the formal one. (इसलिए, अतः (ataḥ).)

You'll want to know: cause, इसलिए, result.
बारिश हो रही थी, इसलिए मैं नहीं गया। bāriś ho rahī thī, isliye maiṁ nahīṁ gayā. — "It was raining, so I didn't go."
इसलिए stands between two whole sentences: the cause first, then the result. क्योंकि turns the order round.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
order: cause, इसलिए, result. sentence: बारिश हो रही है, इसलिए मैं घर पर हूँ। (bāriś ho rahī hai, isliye maiṁ ghar par hūṁ.)
order: result, क्योंकि, cause. sentence: मैं घर पर हूँ, क्योंकि बारिश हो रही है। (maiṁ ghar par hūṁ, kyoṁki bāriś ho rahī hai.)
In formal writing, अतः (ataḥ) takes the place of इसलिए.

You'll want to know: तो, so then.
बारिश हो रही है? तो छाता लीजिए। bāriś ho rahī hai? to chātā lījie. — "It's raining? Then take an umbrella."
तो (to), "so, then", takes up what was said and draws the next step from it. It opens a reply:
तो चलिए। (to calie.) — So let's go.
ठीक है, तो कल मिलते हैं। (ṭhīk hai, to kal milte haiṁ.) — All right, so see you tomorrow.
तो has a bigger job still, after अगर, "if"; it comes when the book reaches conditions.
