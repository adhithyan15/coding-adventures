Sanskrit, chapter 538: Fate and Omens.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शकुनः (śakunaḥ) — an omen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for covetous, then the Sanskrit for a gambler.

You'll want to know: शकुनः.
शकुनः — śakunaḥ — "an omen".
यदि शकुनः शुभः अस्ति, तर्हि वयं श्वः यात्रां गच्छामः। yadi śakunaḥ śubhaḥ asti, tarhi vayaṃ śvaḥ yātrāṃ gacchāmaḥ. If the omen is good, we set out tomorrow.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शकुनः (śakunaḥ) is the word MW gives for a bird, above all a large bird of good or bad omen, and so an omen; MW reports a derivation from √śak, "to be able", only as "said to be", so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
नियतिः (niyatiḥ) — destiny.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a gambler, then the Sanskrit for an omen.

You'll want to know: नियतिः.
नियतिः — niyatiḥ — "destiny".
यदि नियतिः सत्यम् अस्ति, तर्हि अहं किमर्थं चिन्तयामि? yadi niyatiḥ satyam asti, tarhi ahaṃ kimarthaṃ cintayāmi? If destiny is real, why do I worry?
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नियतिः (niyatiḥ) is built on ni, "down", and the root √yam, "to hold, to restrain"; MW gives niyati as the fixed order of things, necessity, destiny (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
मंगलम् (maṅgalam) — auspiciousness; good fortune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an omen, then the Sanskrit for destiny.

You'll want to know: मंगलम्.
मंगलम् — maṅgalam — "auspiciousness; good fortune".
यदि गृहे मंगलम् अस्ति, तर्हि माता प्रसन्ना भवति। yadi gṛhe maṅgalam asti, tarhi mātā prasannā bhavati. If all is well at home, my mother is happy.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
मंगलम् (maṅgalam) is the word MW gives for happiness, welfare, and anything auspicious, a good omen; MW reports a derivation from a root √maṅg only after the grammarians, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
गणकः (gaṇakaḥ) — an astrologer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for destiny, then the Sanskrit for auspiciousness.

You'll want to know: गणकः.
गणकः — gaṇakaḥ — "an astrologer".
यदि गणकः सत्यं वदति, तर्हि श्वः वर्षा भविष्यति। yadi gaṇakaḥ satyaṃ vadati, tarhi śvaḥ varṣā bhaviṣyati. If the astrologer is right, it will rain tomorrow.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गणकः (gaṇakaḥ) comes from the root √gaṇ, "to count", which also gives gaṇayati, with the agent ending -aka; MW gives gaṇaka as one who calculates nativities, an astrologer (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
उपतिष्ठते (upatiṣṭhate) — draws near; presents itself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for auspiciousness, then the Sanskrit for an astrologer.

You'll want to know: उपतिष्ठते.
उपतिष्ठते — upatiṣṭhate — "draws near; presents itself".
यदि शुभः समयः उपतिष्ठते, तर्हि वयं यात्रां गच्छामः। yadi śubhaḥ samayaḥ upatiṣṭhate, tarhi vayaṃ yātrāṃ gacchāmaḥ. If a lucky moment comes, we set out on the journey.
Past (he …): सः गुरुम् उपस्थितवान्, saḥ gurum upasthitavān.

The word, taken apart.
उपतिष्ठते (upatiṣṭhate) is built on upa, "near", and the root √sthā, "to stand", which also gives tiṣṭhati; MW gives upasthā as to stand or place oneself near, to be present, to approach; its past participle is upasthita (MW).
