Mandarin Chinese, chapter 114: Paying.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mǎidān — to pay the bill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to roast, then the Mandarin for to fry.

You'll want to know: mǎidān.
mǎidān — "to pay the bill".
fúwùyuán mǎidān — Excuse me, the bill, please!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mǎidān is built from 买 'to buy' and dān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, meaX and tan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'single'. Paying the bill is a modern sense, and how the pair came to it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

Lesson 3 of 5.
xiànjīn — cash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay the bill, then the Mandarin for to pay.

You'll want to know: xiànjīn.
xiànjīn — "cash".
nǐ yǒu xiànjīn ma — Have you got any cash?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiànjīn is built from xiàn and jīn 'gold, money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, henH and kim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to show'; it is the xiàn of xiànzài 'now', and money there and then is the likely sense of the pair, though it is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
shuākǎ — to pay by card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay, then the Mandarin for cash.

You'll want to know: shuākǎ.
shuākǎ — "to pay by card".
wǒ kěyǐ shuākǎ ma — Can I pay by card?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
kěyǐ is said kéyǐ: two third tones, and the first rises.

The word, taken apart.
shuākǎ is built from shuā 'to brush, to swipe' and kǎ 'card'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese srwaet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is borrowed, usually traced to English card.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zhǎoqián — to give change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cash, then the Mandarin for to pay by card.

You'll want to know: zhǎoqián.
zhǎoqián — "to give change".
tā méiyǒu zhǎoqián — He didn't give me my change.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǎoqián is built from zhǎo 'to look for' and qián 'money'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese dzjen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.
