Latin, chapter 297: Incipere et fīnīre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
incipiō, incipere — to begin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a gap, then the Latin for a moment.

You'll want to know: incipiō, incipere.
incipiō, incipere — "to begin".
Mīlitēs mūrum aedificāre incēpērunt. The soldiers began to build a wall.
Perfect incēpī. With an infinitive: incipit loquī, he begins to speak. English incipient.

The word, taken apart.
incipiō is built on capiō, "to take", with in- (Lewis & Short): to lay hold of a task, to take it in hand. English incipient comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
coepī, coepisse — I began, I have begun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a moment, then the Latin for to begin.

You'll want to know: coepī, coepisse.
coepī, coepisse — "I began, I have begun".
Ubi nox vēnit, puellae canere coepērunt. When night came, the girls began to sing.
A perfect with no present in classical use: coepī, coepisse, participle coeptus. It serves as the past of incipiō.

The word, taken apart.
coepī is the perfect of an old verb coepiō, which Lewis & Short take as co- and apiō, "to fasten": to join on, to set about.

Lesson 3 of 5.
fīniō, fīnīre — to finish; to set limits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to begin, then the Latin for I began.

You'll want to know: fīniō, fīnīre.
fīniō, fīnīre — "to finish; to set limits".
Hōrā nōnā labōrem fīnīvimus. We finished the work at the ninth hour.
fīniō, fīnīre, fīnīvī, fīnītum. From fīnis (taught): first 'to mark a boundary', then 'to end'. English finish, finite.

The word, taken apart.
fīniō is built on fīnis, "end, boundary" (EtymDB): to set an end. English finish comes from it through French, and finite from its participle.

Mūrum novum tandem perfēcērunt. At last they completed the new wall.
perficiō, perficere, perfēcī, perfectum: per 'through' + faciō. English perfect.

The word, taken apart.
perficiō is built on faciō, "to make, to do", with per-, "through" (EtymDB): to do right through. English perfect comes from its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
intermittō, intermittere — to break off, to interrupt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to finish, then the Latin for to complete.

You'll want to know: intermittō, intermittere.
intermittō, intermittere — "to break off, to interrupt".
Propter pluviam labōrem intermīsimus. Because of the rain we broke off the work.
intermittō, intermittere, intermīsī, intermissum: to put a gap into something. Caesar uses it of a battle or a march that is broken off.

The word, taken apart.
intermittō is built on mittō, "to send, to let go", with inter, "between" (Lewis & Short): to leave a gap. English intermission and intermittent come from it.
