Persian, chapter 21: Reading — Words, Answers, and the Figures.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) (salām, mamnūn, bale, na, nān, māh) (bale, na, mamnūn, salām, avval, sevvom) (۰ ۱ ۲ ۳, ۴ ۵ ۶, ۷ ۸ ۹, ۱۰, ۲۰, ۱۰۰) — six words, nothing said first.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have written every letter in every one of these. Not one word is new, and not one shape inside them is new either.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them before you read them.

Reading.
سلام ممنون بله نه نان ماه.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once. Do not take each word apart — look at the whole shape and let it arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to unpick. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Persian joins its letters, so a word is not five shapes standing in a row. It is one shape.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why reading a word cold is a different skill from writing it. Writing, you make the joins one at a time and know what is coming. Reading, the joins are already made and the whole word has to land at once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Look at نان and نه. Both open with the same letter, and the difference is at the end, which is exactly where a reader is most likely to stop looking.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which word is bread?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two open with the same letter? (نان and نه.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) (salām, mamnūn, bale, na, nān, māh) (bale, na, mamnūn, salām, avval, sevvom) (۰ ۱ ۲ ۳, ۴ ۵ ۶, ۷ ۸ ۹, ۱۰, ۲۰, ۱۰۰) — the answers, on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you six words. These are six you would answer with.
[pause 1 second]
Nothing here is new. What is new is meeting them where nobody is speaking.

Reading.
بله نه ممنون سلام اول سوم.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice the list changes halfway. The first four are things you say to a person. The last two are things you read off a door.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
اول and سوم are the two you are most likely to meet written before you ever meet them spoken: a floor, a platform, a queue.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the argument for reading them now. An answer you can only recognise by ear is one you can only give when somebody asks out loud. The same words on a sign do not wait for you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and the last two are the ones a street will hand you first.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which line would be on the first floor?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two would you read off a door? (اول and سوم.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) (salām, mamnūn, bale, na, nān, māh) (bale, na, mamnūn, salām, avval, sevvom) (۰ ۱ ۲ ۳, ۴ ۵ ۶, ۷ ۸ ۹, ۱۰, ۲۰, ۱۰۰) — the figures.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six answers. These are not words at all.
[pause 1 second]
You drew every one of them. Reading them is the same ten signs coming the other way.

Reading.
۰ ۱ ۲ ۳ ۴ ۵ ۶ ۷ ۸ ۹ ۱۰ ۲۰ ۱۰۰.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read across each line once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time read the last three as amounts rather than as signs. Ten. Twenty. A hundred.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
These ten signs are the only Persian most travellers read before they read any word at all. A price, a floor, a platform, a phone number: none of them waits for you to know the letters.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice also which way they run. The digits go left to right inside a number even though the words around them run right to left, so ۱۰۰ is a one and two zeros in the order you already expect.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the whole trick, and it is why a figure is the first thing a reader can take from a Persian page.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What number is on the last line?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the ten signs were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which way does a Persian number run? (Left to right, inside a line that runs the other way.)
