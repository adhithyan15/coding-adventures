Arabic, chapter 48: Letters From Please, Sorry and Tomorrow.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ض (ḍād) — the letter inside فضلك.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ض and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ف and ق once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say من فضلك (min faḍlik), "please". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ض.
ض — ḍād, a heavy d.
It is inside من فضلك (min faḍlik), "please". It is ص (ṣād), which you already write, with one dot above. Arabic is sometimes called the language of the ḍād, because the sound is so rare in other languages.

Writing: ض.
1. start here: close the oval clockwise from its lower-left junction.
2. turn left and rise into the short shoulder without lifting.
3. lift, restart at the baseline junction, and sweep through the trailing bowl.
4. lift again, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: س ش ص ض (ḍād), independent Daad at 00:43.1–00:46.3 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).

Lesson 2 of 4.
آ (alif madda) — the alif that opens آسف.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: آ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ق and ض (ḍād) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say آسف (āsif), "sorry". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: آ.
آ (alif madda) — alif with a madda, a long ā at the start of a word.
It opens آسف (āsif), "sorry". The wavy stroke on top, the madda, stretches the vowel: where a plain ا at the start of a word needs a hamza to say a, آ (alif madda) says a long ā.

Writing: آ.
Put your pen on آ (alif madda) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this character or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ز and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ض (ḍād) and آ (alif madda) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say أزرق (azraq), "blue". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ز.
ز — zāy, the sound z.
It is inside أزرق (azraq), "blue", from أحمر أزرق. It is ر (rāʾ), which you already write, with one dot above, and like ر it never joins to the letter after it.

Writing: ز.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend through the short stroke.
2. without lifting, sweep left through the lower curve without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet ر ز (zāy) و, independent Zay body followed by its upper dot (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-23).

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say إلى الغد (ilā l-ghad), "see you tomorrow". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: غ.
غ — ghayn, a gh gargled at the back of the throat.
It is inside إلى الغد (ilā l-ghad), "see you tomorrow". It is ع (ʿayn), which you already write, with one dot above, the third family where a single dot turns one sound into another.

Writing: غ.
1. start here: sweep left from the upper-right tip and shape the open head.
2. continue down and around the lower bowl without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet ع غ (ghayn), independent Ghayn at 00:02.4–00:04.0 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-23).
