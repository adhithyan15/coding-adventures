Latin, chapter 338: Legiō.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
legiō, legiōnis — a legion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a contest, then the Latin for applause.

You'll want to know: legiō, legiōnis.
legiō, legiōnis — "a legion".
Decima legiō fortissima erat. The tenth legion was the bravest.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from legō, 'to choose': a picked levy, about five thousand men. English legion.

The word, taken apart.
legiō is built on legō, "to gather, to choose" (Lewis & Short): a body of chosen soldiers. English legion comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 6.
cohors, cohortis — a cohort; an enclosure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for applause, then the Latin for a legion.

You'll want to know: cohors, cohortis.
cohors, cohortis — "a cohort; an enclosure".
Cohors ad portam castrōrum stābat. A cohort was standing at the gate of the camp.
Feminine, 3rd declension. A legion had ten cohorts. The same word meant a farmyard, which gives English court.

The word, taken apart.
cohors is inherited, and its earlier history is not settled. It first meant "an enclosure, a farmyard" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), and then the body of soldiers drawn up together. English court comes from it through French, and cohort directly.

You'll want to know: signifer, signiferī.
signifer, signiferī — "a standard-bearer".
Signifer signum in hostēs tulit. The standard-bearer carried the standard against the enemy.
Masculine: signum (taught) + ferō. Losing the standard was a deep disgrace.

The word, taken apart.
signifer joins signum, "sign, standard", and -fer, "carrying", from ferō (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
tribūnus, tribūnī — a tribune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a centurion, then the Latin for a standard-bearer.

You'll want to know: tribūnus, tribūnī.
tribūnus, tribūnī — "a tribune".
Tribūnus mīlitum ad castra advēnit. The military tribune arrived at the camp.
Masculine. Tribūnus mīlitum: an officer of a legion; the people's tribunes protected ordinary citizens.

The word, taken apart.
tribūnus is built on tribus, "tribe" (EtymDB): first the head of a tribe. English tribune comes from it.
