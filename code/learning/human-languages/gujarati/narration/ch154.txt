Gujarati, chapter 154: Saris, scarves and cloth.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કપડું (kapḍũ) — cloth, garment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for veranda, then the Gujarati for stairs.

You'll want to know: કપડું.
કપડું — kapḍũ — "cloth, garment".
આ કપડું નરમ છે. — ā kapḍũ naram che — "This cloth is soft."
The plural કપડાં, kapḍā̃, means clothes.

The word, taken apart.
કપડું (kapḍũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karpaṭa, "patched garment, rag"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "any garment" and asks whether it came by way of Hindi (CDIAL 2871).

Lesson 3 of 5.
દુપટ્ટો (dupaṭṭo) — scarf, stole.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for cloth, then the Gujarati for sari.

You'll want to know: દુપટ્ટો.
દુપટ્ટો — dupaṭṭo — "scarf, stole".
તેનો દુપટ્ટો લાલ છે. — teno dupaṭṭo lāl che — "Her scarf is red."

The word, taken apart.
દુપટ્ટો (dupaṭṭo) is built from du-, "two", and paṭṭo, "strip of cloth". Under Sanskrit paṭṭa, "cloth", which it thinks probably non-Aryan, CDIAL lists the kindred Gujarati pāṭ, "strip of cloth", and pāṭo, "bandage", but no form of the compound itself (CDIAL 7700).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઓઢવું (oḍhvũ) — to wrap oneself in, to cover oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for scarf, then the Gujarati for to take off.

You'll want to know: ઓઢવું.
ઓઢવું — oḍhvũ — "to wrap oneself in, to cover oneself".
શિયાળામાં હું ધાબળો ઓઢું છું. — śiyāḷāmā̃ hũ dhābaḷo oḍhũ chũ — "In winter I wrap myself in a blanket."

The word, taken apart.
ઓઢવું (oḍhvũ) is inherited through Prakrit. It comes from ōḍḍh, "put on, wear, cover", a reconstructed form that CDIAL takes to be borrowed from Dravidian, beside Tamil uṭu-, "to dress", though it notes that Burrow derives the Tamil verb otherwise (CDIAL 2547).
