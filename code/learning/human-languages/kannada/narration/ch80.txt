Lesson 1 of 8.
ಧ (dha) — the breath that opens the thank-you word.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ಧ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say thank you in Kannada. It begins on today's character, and it has done since the first chapter.

Script you'll notice: ಧ.
ಧ — dha.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not dh, it is dha.
ಧ (dha) is ದ with a puff of air after it. Hold a hand in front of your mouth: say ದ and the air stays put; say ಧ (dha) and it comes out.
This is the last big idea left in this script, and it accounts for seven of the characters you have never been shown. Kannada gives the breathy partner of a consonant its own shape rather than leaving you to guess:
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
no breath: ದ. breath: ಧ (dha).
The six that follow are the same move on six other letters.
You already say these:
ಧನ್ಯವಾದ dhanyavāda — thank you.
ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ madhyāhna — noon.
ಶುಭ ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ śubha madhyāhna — good afternoon.

Writing: ಧ.
Put your pen on ಧ (dha) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start the character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 8 of 8.
(ಖ (kha) ಘ (gha) ಠ (ṭha) ಢ (ḍha) ಧ (dha) ಫ (pha) ಭ (bha)) (usiru) — seven characters, one move.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes for one table that cannot be read aloud. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: seven shapes, one move until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say thank you, and say good morning. Name the breathy character inside each.

Script: seven shapes, one move.
Seven characters, and they are one move done seven times: take a consonant you already read and let the breath out after it. Hold a hand in front of your mouth and the difference is not subtle.
There is a table here I cannot read to you — 8 columns and 1 rows, and it has more columns than can be held in the ear at once. Its columns are: no breath, ಕ, ಗ, ಟ, ಡ, ದ, ಪ, ಬ. Come back and look at it when you have stopped.
Read the bottom row against the top and the shapes stop being seven new things to memorise. English does not write this difference at all, which is why the romanization has to spell it with an added h — and why ph on that line is a p with breath, never the f of phone.
Two of the seven are in one word each, and both words are months: ಮಾಘ and ಆಷಾಢ. ಠ (ṭha) is in ಜ್ಯೇಷ್ಠ, three times in the whole book, and it was the last character these pages printed that nothing had taught.
