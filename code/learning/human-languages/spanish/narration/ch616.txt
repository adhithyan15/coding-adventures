Spanish, chapter 616: Imprevistos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el imprevisto — the unexpected problem; unforeseen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the intention, then the Spanish for to set out to.

You'll want to know: el imprevisto.
el imprevisto — "the unexpected problem; unforeseen".
Tuve un imprevisto y llegué tarde. Something came up and I arrived late.
Tener un imprevisto — something unexpected comes up.

The word, taken apart.
imprevisto is built on previsto, the participle of prever, a learned borrowing from Latin praevidere 'to foresee', with in- 'not' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
anticipar — to bring forward; to anticipate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to set out to, then the Spanish for the unexpected problem.

You'll want to know: anticipar.
anticipar — "to bring forward; to anticipate".
Anticipamos la reunión al viernes. We brought the meeting forward to Friday.
Adelantar is more everyday. Anticiparse a — to get ahead of.

The word, taken apart.
anticipar is a learned borrowing from Latin anticipare 'to take beforehand', built from ante and capere 'to take' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
el contratiempo — the setback, the mishap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to bring forward, then the Spanish for to postpone.

You'll want to know: el contratiempo.
el contratiempo — "the setback, the mishap".
Tuvimos un contratiempo con el coche. We had a setback with the car.
A small problem that upsets a plan.

The word, taken apart.
contratiempo is built from contra 'against' and tiempo, inherited from Latin tempus 'time' (Lewis & Short).
