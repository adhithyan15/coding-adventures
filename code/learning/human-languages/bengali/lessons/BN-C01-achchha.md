# আচ্ছা (āchchhā) — "okay / I see"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The little word that keeps every Bengali conversation moving.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-ACHCHHA-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

New: **আ** — the **independent** vowel *ā*, the shape a word uses when it *starts*
with a vowel. (A built-in vowel rides a consonant; a standalone one gets its own
letter.) Then the doubled **চ্ছ** (**চ** *ch* + **ছ** *chh*). Read **আ·চ্ছা**,
*ā-chchhā*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-ACHCHHA-01, BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

**আচ্ছা** (*āchchhā*) is the all-purpose "okay, alright, I see, go on" — a true
workhorse of spoken Bengali, from Sanskrit *acchā* ("good, clear"). It is the
word a Bengali speaker peppers through speech the way an English speaker says
"right… right," a nod made audible.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-ACHCHHA-01, BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

Note it opens with **আ**, a *standalone* vowel-letter — your first. This is the
other half of how the script handles vowels: attached to a consonant they are
little signs (**া**), but on their own they stand as full letters (**আ**). Same
sound, two shapes, chosen by position.
