# admitir — let it through

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05, ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **meter** and **aceptar**. The word coming next is a cousin of
the first and a rival of the second.

## You'll want to know: admitir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02] -->

**admitir** — to allow in, to accept as valid, and to own up to.

> No se **admiten** solicitudes fuera de plazo.

*No se admiten perros* — no dogs. *Admitir un error* — to admit a mistake.
*¿Admiten tarjeta?* — do you take cards, on a sign or a form.

**La admisión** is the noun, and *admitido* is the word beside your name on a
list when you are in.

## Grammar Lens: admitir, aceptar, entregar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03, ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-03, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02] -->

Three verbs about a thing crossing a line, sorted by who is doing what:

| the move | the verb |
|---|---|
| you agree to take what is offered | **aceptar** |
| an institution lets it through | **admitir** |
| you hand over what was due | **entregar** |

You *entregas* the form; the office *admite* it or not; a shop *acepta* your
card. Notice who is behind *admitir*: nearly every time, an institution. That is
why it turns up in the *se* form on notices — *no se admiten* — with nobody
named.

The other sense follows from the same picture: to *admit* a mistake is to let
it in rather than keep it outside.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05] -->

Latin **admittere**: **ad-**, towards, plus **mittere**, **to send**.

You have met *mittere* three times already, and this makes four:

| word | what the prefix does |
|---|---|
| **meter** | the bare verb, worn down: put in |
| **el permiso** | *per-*, sent **through** |
| **permitir** | the verb of that noun, let through |
| **admitir** | *ad-*, sent **towards** — let in |

So the family is a set of sendings, and the prefix does all the steering.
English holds the same shelf: **admit**, **permit**, **submit**, **transmit**,
**commit**, **mission** — every one a sending in some direction.

*Meter* is the one that came down through speech, which is why it looks least
like the others and is the only one you would use about a box.
