# (reading) — six characters, nothing said first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-REN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have written every one of these. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody is going to say them first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-SKILL-READ-CHARACTERS]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-YOU-01, ZH-LEX-HE-01] -->

> 人
> 书
> 看
> 有
> 和
> 十

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once. Do not count strokes — look at the whole
character and let it arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to take
apart. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-NUM-SHI, ZH-SKILL-READ-CHARACTERS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] In an alphabet a single letter is almost never a word. Here every
one of these six is.

[PAUSE 2s] That changes what reading is. There is no sounding out: a character
either lands whole or it does not land, and the only thing that moves it from
the second to the first is having met it before.

[PAUSE 2s] Six is enough for one sitting, because six is already six words.

## Wrap-up Recall
