## Grammar Lens: an impression is not a record
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-04] -->

This chapter is about the moment a thing stops being an impression and becomes
a record.

| impression | record |
|---|---|
| *hace calor* | *la temperatura: 24* |
| *llueve* | *lluvia: 12 mm* |

*Hace calor* and *llueve* are both true and neither can be compared with
yesterday. The nouns can. That is why the chapter's verb is **anotar** rather
than **apuntar**: a note you jot is for you, and a note you enter is for
whoever reads the book next.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-01, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03] -->

Two of the four hide something that is not about writing or weather at all.

| word | what is underneath |
|---|---|
| el cuaderno | **four** — *quaternum*, four sheets in one fold |
| la temperatura | **mixing** — *temperare*, to hold in proportion |

The other two say what they are. *Anotar* is *nota*, a mark, with a prefix
aiming it. And *la lluvia* is *pluvia*, which had been passed under your nose
several times — under *llueve*, *el llanto*, *lleno* and *planta* — before you
needed it.
