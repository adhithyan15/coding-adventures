## Script: the four, and what each one stands next to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-157, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-158, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-159, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-111, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-113] -->

> బ · ష · శ · ఫ

Not one of these four arrived on its own. Each stands beside a character you
had already been reading for chapters, and what it adds is a single change you
can hear:

| already yours | new | what changed |
|---|---|---|
| ప *pa* | బ *ba* | the voice switched on |
| ప *pa* | ఫ *pha* | a puff of air came after it |
| స *sa* | ష *ṣa* | the tongue curled back |
| స *sa* | శ *śa* | the tongue went flat against the front of the roof |

**Two of the four hang off ప and two off స.** A reader who holds those two
anchors in mind has four fewer shapes to memorise cold, because each of the
four is one adjustment away from something already known.
