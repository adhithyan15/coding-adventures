Gujarati, chapter 226: Breath nose and throat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ધ્રૂજવું (dhrūjvũ) — to shiver, to tremble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to yawn, then the Gujarati for sweat.

You'll want to know: ધ્રૂજવું.
ધ્રૂજવું — dhrūjvũ — "to shiver, to tremble".
શું તમે ઠંડીથી ધ્રૂજો છો? — śũ tame ṭhanḍīthī dhrūjo cho? — "Are you shivering with cold?"

The word, taken apart.
ધ્રૂજવું (dhrūjvũ) is probably inherited. CDIAL lists a Gujarati dhujvũ, "to tremble, be shaken", under Sanskrit dhūyate, "is shaken" (CDIAL 6863); the r of dhrūjvũ is not explained there, so the link is uncertain.

Lesson 2 of 5.
હાંફવું (hā̃phvũ) — to pant, to be out of breath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for sweat, then the Gujarati for to shiver.

You'll want to know: હાંફવું.
હાંફવું — hā̃phvũ — "to pant, to be out of breath".
દાદા હવે નથી હાંફતા. — dādā have nathī hā̃phtā — "Grandfather is not out of breath now."

The word, taken apart.
હાંફવું (hā̃phvũ) comes from hamph, "pant", an imitative reconstructed form; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, glossed "to pant" (CDIAL 13973).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ગળવું (gaḷvũ) — to swallow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to pant, then the Gujarati for to sniff.

You'll want to know: ગળવું.
ગળવું — gaḷvũ — "to swallow".
શું બાળક દવા ગળે છે? — śũ bāḷak davā gaḷe che? — "Does the child swallow the medicine?"

The word, taken apart.
ગળવું (gaḷvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from galati, "swallows", beside gilati (CDIAL 4075).
