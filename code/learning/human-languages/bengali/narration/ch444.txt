Bengali, chapter 444: Sulking and Making Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অভিমান (ôbhimān) — hurt pride; a loving sulk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to seethe, then the Bengali for to grumble.

You'll want to know: অভিমান.
অভিমান — ôbhimān — "hurt pride; a loving sulk".
বোনের জন্মদিনে আমি ফোন করলাম না, তাই তার অভিমান হল। boner jônmôdine āmi phon kôrlām nā, tāi tār ôbhimān hôlô. I didn't phone on my sister's birthday, so she was hurt.
অভিমান (ôbhimān) is felt between people who love each other: the sulk of a child, a sister or a wife, waiting to be coaxed round. অভিমানী, quick to feel it.

The word, taken apart.
অভিমান (ôbhimān) is a tatsama, Sanskrit abhimāna, "a high opinion of oneself, pride" (Monier-Williams); Bengali turned it into the wounded pride of someone slighted by a person they love.

Lesson 2 of 5.
অনুযোগ (ônujog) — a gentle complaint; a reproach (to someone close).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to grumble, then the Bengali for hurt pride.

You'll want to know: অনুযোগ.
অনুযোগ — ônujog — "a gentle complaint; a reproach (to someone close)".
মীরার অনুযোগ, অরুণ সবসময় দেরি করে আসে। mīrār ônujog, ôrun sôbsômôy deri kôre āse. Mira's complaint is that Arun always turns up late.
অনুযোগ (ônujog) করা, to complain to someone close. অভিযোগ is a formal complaint or a charge; অনুযোগ (ônujog) stays inside the family and among friends.

The word, taken apart.
অনুযোগ (ônujog) is a tatsama, Sanskrit anuyoga, which Monier-Williams gives for questioning and also for reproof; Bengali uses it for the milder complaint made to someone you are close to.

Lesson 3 of 5.
মুখ ভার করা (mukh bhār kôrā) — to sulk; to put on a sullen face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for hurt pride, then the Bengali for a gentle complaint.

You'll want to know: মুখ ভার করা.
মুখ ভার করা — mukh bhār kôrā — "to sulk; to put on a sullen face".
বকা খেয়ে ছোট বোন মুখ ভার করে বসে আছে। bôkā kheye chhoṭo bon mukh bhār kôre bôse āchhe. After being scolded, my little sister is sitting there sulking.
Past (he/she …): তিনি মুখ ভার করলেন, tini mukh bhār kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
মুখ ভার করা (mukh bhār kôrā) is মুখ (taught), the face, with bhār, "weight", from Sanskrit bhāra, "burden, weight" (CDIAL 9459), and kôrā, "to do" (CDIAL 2814): to make the face heavy.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ক্ষমা করা (khômā kôrā) — to forgive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a gentle complaint, then the Bengali for to sulk.

You'll want to know: ক্ষমা করা.
ক্ষমা করা — khômā kôrā — "to forgive".
আমার ভুলের জন্য বন্ধু আমাকে ক্ষমা করল। āmār bhuler jônyô bôndhu āmāke khômā kôrlô. My friend forgave me for my mistake.
Past (he/she …): তিনি ছেলেকে ক্ষমা করলেন, tini chheleke khômā kôrlen.
ক্ষমা চাওয়া, to ask forgiveness: আমি ক্ষমা চাইলাম.

The word, taken apart.
ক্ষমা করা (khômā kôrā) is Sanskrit kṣamā, "patience, forbearance" (Monier-Williams), with kôrā, "to do" (CDIAL 2814).

You'll want to know: মনোমালিন্য.

জমি নিয়ে দুই ভাইয়ের মনোমালিন্য হল। jômi niye dui bhāiyer mônomālinyô hôlô. The two brothers fell out over land.

The word, taken apart.
মনোমালিন্য (mônomālinyô) joins Sanskrit manas, "mind" (CDIAL 9822), in its compound form mano-, and mālinya, "dirtiness, darkness" (Monier-Williams): a mind clouded against someone.
