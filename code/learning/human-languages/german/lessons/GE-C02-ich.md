# ich — "I," the same ancient word as Latin *ego*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] To introduce yourself, first "I." German's is a three-letter word
hiding a very deep root.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-SOUND-ICH-LAUT-01]; assesses=[] -->

- **ich** = *ikh* — the *ch* here is **soft**: a light hiss at the front of the
  mouth (the "ich-sound"), *not* the throat-scrape of *Nacht*. German has both;
  after *i* and *e* it's the soft one.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ICH-02, GE-ETYMON-ICH-03]; assesses=[] -->

**ich** — "I." Root: Proto-Germanic **\*ik**, from Proto-Indo-European
**\*eǵ** — the *same* deep root that became:

- Latin **ego** (and English **ego**), Greek *egō*,
- and, worn down hardest of all, English **I**.

So German *ich*, Latin *ego*, and English *I* are three survivals of **one**
ancient word for "I" — English filed it down to a single letter, German kept a
bit more.
