# নেওয়া (neowā) — "to take," and the verb that finishes other verbs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Bengali rarely leaves a verb standing alone. This is the verb it
props most of them up with — and the tail it wears will look familiar.

## You'll want to know: নেওয়া
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C09-NEOWA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **নেওয়া** — *neowā* — **to take**

আমি নিই (*āmi nii*), "I take." আপনি নেন (*āpni nen*), respectfully.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**নেওয়া** is **নে** (*ne*) + **ওয়া** (*-owā*). **ন** is a dental *n*, tongue on
the teeth. The **ওয়া** tail is the one every dictionary-form verb of this kind
wears — you have it already on হওয়া, যাওয়া and খাওয়া.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**নেওয়া** is Sanskrit **√নী** (*√nī*), "to lead, to carry away." It is a
well-attested Indo-Iranian root — Avestan *nayeiti*, "he leads"; Sanskrit *netṛ*,
"a leader," which is why a politician in Kolkata or Delhi is a *netā*. Beyond
that branch the trail thins, and **English kept nothing from it**. No cousin will
be manufactured for you here; some roots stayed home.

The lesson worth more than a cousin is what Bengali *does* with the verb.

## Grammar Lens: the verb that ends a compound
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-GRAMMAR-C09-NEOWA-02]; assesses=[] -->

Put a verb into its **-এ** form (*likhe*, "having written"; *niye*, "having
taken") and hang a second verb behind it. The second verb stops meaning what it
means and starts colouring the first:

| compound | what it does |
|---|---|
| লিখে নেওয়া (*likhe neowā*) | write it **down** — take the writing for yourself |
| নিয়ে আসা (*niye āsā*) | **bring** — come, having taken |
| নিয়ে যাওয়া (*niye jāwā*) | **take away** — go, having taken |

Bengali has no separate word for "bring." It has "come having taken." And নেওয়া
is a noun as readily as লেখা was: **দেওয়া-নেওয়া** (*deowā-neowā*),
"giving-and-taking," is the everyday word for dealings between people.

"come having taken"; and "take away" is নিয়ে যাওয়া.) Does English share নেওয়া's
root? (**No** — √nī stayed in Indo-Iranian, and that is the honest answer.) Name
two more Bengali verbs that are also nouns. (**লেখা** "a piece of writing" and
**দেখা** "a seeing" — every **-া** form does it.)
