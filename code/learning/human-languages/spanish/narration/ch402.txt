Spanish, chapter 402: Five Words in a Clothes Shop.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
costar — when the price stands settled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the euro and the price. (El euro, el precio.)

You'll want to know first.
el precio — the number this verb asks about.
el euro — one unit the answer can use.

Sounds you'll need.
cos-TAR: keep the first s clear. Costar differs from contar only in that middle consonant: cos-tar, not con-tar.

The word, taken apart.
costar — "to cost."
It descends from Latin constare, "to stand together" and then "to be settled or established." A settled price is what an item costs. English cost reached the same Latin family through French.
Use the third-person question for one item: ¿Cuánto cuesta? — "How much does it cost?" The answer can be Cuesta diez euros.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: costar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Cuánto cuesta?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Cuesta diez euros.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ask how much it costs. (¿Cuánto cuesta?)
Source: Diccionario de la lengua española: costar.


Lesson 2 of 7.
la camisa — a garment with a long family.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask how much it costs. (¿Cuánto cuesta?)

Sounds you'll need.
ca-MI-sa: three open syllables, with the weight on mi.

The word, taken apart.
la camisa — "the shirt."
Spanish continues Late Latin camisia, a garment worn close to the body. English chemise and camisole travelled by another route from the same old garment word, so their opening sound changed while Spanish kept camisa.
In the shop: ¿Cuánto cuesta la camisa?

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: la camisa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Cuánto cuesta la camisa?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: La camisa cuesta diez euros.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the shirt, then ask its cost. (La camisa. ¿Cuánto cuesta?)
Source: Diccionario de la lengua española: camisa.


Lesson 3 of 7.
la camiseta — the smaller shirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the shirt and ask what it costs. (La camisa. ¿Cuánto cuesta?)

Sounds you'll need.
ca-mi-SE-ta: keep all four vowels; the stress moves to se.

The word, taken apart.
la camiseta — "the T-shirt."
The build is visible: camisa plus -eta, a suffix that can make something smaller. The lighter, smaller shirt became the ordinary T-shirt. Keep the two shop labels distinct: camisa for a shirt, camiseta for a T-shirt.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: la camiseta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Cuánto cuesta la camiseta?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Give the two garment words, larger category first. (La camisa, la camiseta.)
Source: Diccionario de la lengua española: camiseta.


Lesson 4 of 7.
la talla — the cut that became a size.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name a shirt and a T-shirt. (Una camisa, una camiseta.)

Sounds you'll need.
TA-ya for most speakers: the written ll commonly has the same sound as y. see pronunciation reference.

The word, taken apart.
la talla — "the size," especially a clothing size.
The noun belongs to tallar, "to cut or shape." A garment is cut to a measure, and that cut becomes its talla. In a shop, ¿Qué talla? asks "What size?"

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Qué talla?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Qué talla tiene la camiseta?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Cuánto cuesta la camisa?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ask "What size?" (¿Qué talla?)
Source: Diccionario de la lengua española: talla.


Lesson 5 of 7.
el gorro — the cap with no invented origin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask the size of the T-shirt, then ask its cost. (¿Qué talla tiene la camiseta? ¿Cuánto cuesta?)

Sounds you'll need.
GO-rro: hold the rolled rr clear. Contrast gorro, with rr, and gordo, with rd. The new word does not contain a d.

The word, taken apart.
el gorro — "the brimless cap," often round or close-fitting.
There is no secure etymology to turn into a story here. The Academy marks the origin of gorra as uncertain and derives gorro from it. Learn the shape instead: a gorra normally has a peak; a gorro is round or brimless. They are not masculine and feminine forms of one object.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el gorro]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: gorro, no gordo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Qué talla tiene el gorro?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ¿Cuánto cuesta el gorro?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the brimless cap and ask its cost. (El gorro. ¿Cuánto cuesta?)
Sources: DLE: gorro and DLE: gorra.
