## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C304-WELL-05]; assesses=[] -->

**triste** — "sad."

Latin **tristis** was wider than the English word. It covered *grim*, *harsh*,
*severe* — a *tristis* judge was a stern one — and only later settled onto
sorrow. French carried the softer end into *tristesse*, which English borrowed
without translating.

Here is the thing worth noticing, and it is a rule you have been shown four
times now without it being named: *tranquilo* becomes *tranquila*, *contento*
becomes *contenta*, *ocupado* becomes *ocupada*. **Triste does not move.**

Words ending in *-e* have one form for everybody. That is not an exception to
the *-o*/*-a* switch; it is the other half of it, and it costs you nothing to
learn because there is nothing to change.
