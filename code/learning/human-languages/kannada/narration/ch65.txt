Kannada, chapter 65: More Good Manners.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti) — a request.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new run: what did bēga mean, and what did hāgādare mean?

You'll want to know: ವಿನಂತಿ.
ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti) — "a request".
ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti) is the naming-word for the thing ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು has been doing for you since the please-chapter. ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು is how you make one; ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti) is what you have made.
Sanskrit, and its shape says a bending — the asker lowers himself a little, and that is exactly the difference between a ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti) and an instruction.

What you've built.
The first of five courtesy words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಅನುಮತಿ (anumati) — permission.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did vinanti mean?

You'll want to know: ಅನುಮತಿ.
ಅನುಮತಿ (anumati) — "permission".
ಅನುಮತಿ (anumati) is Sanskrit for a mind that goes along with you — agreement given rather than merely not refused.
It belongs with ಹೊರಡು from the leave-taking chapter. In a Kannada house you do not simply set out; you ask ಅನುಮತಿ (anumati) of whoever is host, and only then do you say ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ. The farewell you learned early has this word standing in front of it.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಸಂಕೋಚ (saṅkōca) — hesitancy, holding back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did anumati mean?

You'll want to know: ಸಂಕೋಚ.
ಸಂಕೋಚ (saṅkōca) — "hesitancy, holding back".
ಸಂಕೋಚ (saṅkōca) is Sanskrit for a drawing-in, a shrinking — and in Kannada it names the polite reluctance that stops you accepting what you actually want.
It is the one word in this chapter you will hear said to you rather than by you. A host feeding an atithi says ಸಂಕೋಚ (saṅkōca) ಬೇಡ, using the refusal-word from the replies chapter to refuse your refusal. Hearing it is the signal to eat.

What you've built.
Three.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ನಂಬಿಕೆ (nambike) — trust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did saṅkōca mean?

You'll want to know: ನಂಬಿಕೆ.
ನಂಬಿಕೆ (nambike) — "trust".
ನಂಬಿಕೆ (nambike) is native, made out of the everyday word for believing, and it covers both trust in a person and belief in a thing being so.
Set it beside ಗೌರವ from the courtesy chapter. ಗೌರವ can be given to a stranger at once, because it is owed to a position. ನಂಬಿಕೆ (nambike) has to be collected slowly, and no amount of the first turns into the second.

What you've built.
Four.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಸೌಜನ್ಯ (saujanya) — courtesy, good manners.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nambike mean?

You'll want to know: ಸೌಜನ್ಯ.
ಸೌಜನ್ಯ (saujanya) — "courtesy, good manners".
ಸೌಜನ್ಯ (saujanya) is Sanskrit, and underneath it is a good person — courtesy named as a quality of the one behaving rather than as a list of rules being followed. That is a real difference, and Kannada means it.
It is the word for everything this book has been quietly teaching alongside the vocabulary: ಧನ್ಯವಾದ, ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ, ಗೌರವ, ವಂದನೆ, and now the four words in front of this one. ಸೌಜನ್ಯ (saujanya) is the name of the whole habit.

What you've built.
Five: ವಿನಂತಿ (vinanti), ಅನುಮತಿ (anumati), ಸಂಕೋಚ (saṅkōca), ನಂಬಿಕೆ (nambike), ಸೌಜನ್ಯ (saujanya). Enough to name what you have been doing since the thank-you word.
