# 早 — sun on top, one more time

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-SCRIPT-RI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write 日. Four strokes. Middle bar before the bottom bar.

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-SCRIPT-ZAO]; assesses=[ZH-SCRIPT-RI] -->

> 早

**Six strokes**, and you can already guess how it starts.

**早 is 日 on top of 十.** You have written the sun before. The piece
underneath is **十**, which on its own is the character for **ten** — you have
not been taught it and you do not need it today. Treat it as two strokes: one
across, one down through it.

1-4. **日, complete.** Left side; top and right in one turning stroke; the middle
bar; the bottom bar closing it.
5. **十's horizontal**, left to right
6. **十's vertical**, coming down *through* that horizontal

Five pen lifts.

**Predict the rule before you read it: does 日 get finished first?** It does —
the same habit that made 是 manageable, and by now it should be doing the work
for you rather than the instruction.

A word about what 早 means, offered as a memory hook and labelled as one: the sun
sitting above a short line is easy to read as **the sun just up over the
horizon**, and 早 means **early**. That is a picture worth keeping. It is not a
proof, and the honest position is that the lower piece's history is disputed.
