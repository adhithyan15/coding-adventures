Lesson 1 of 6.
disponible — available, free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the probability, then the French for even if it means.

You'll want to know: disponible.
disponible — "available, free".
Je suis disponible mardi après-midi. I am free on Tuesday afternoon.
Of people and things: ce modèle n'est plus disponible.

The word, taken apart.
disponible is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin dispōnibilis (EtymDB), from Latin dispōnere "to set in order, to arrange", built on pōnere "to put" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
engager — to commit (mostly reflexive, s'engager); to hire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for even if it means, then the French for available.

You'll want to know: engager.
engager — "to commit (mostly reflexive, s'engager); to hire".
Il s'est engagé à finir le travail avant vendredi. He committed to finishing the work before Friday.
S'engager à + infinitive — to commit to doing. Engager quelqu'un — to hire someone. Nous engageons.

The word, taken apart.
engager is built from en- and gage "a pledge". gage is usually traced to a Frankish word for a pledge (EtymDB lists Germanic relatives such as Gothic wadi).

Lesson 5 of 6.
envisager — to consider, to plan on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the commitment, then the French for flexible.

You'll want to know: envisager.
envisager — "to consider, to plan on".
Nous envisageons de déménager l'année prochaine. We are considering moving next year.
Envisager de + infinitive. Nous envisageons.

The word, taken apart.
envisager is built from en- and visage "face": to look something in the face. visage rests on Latin vīsus "sight", from vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).
