# cenar — the meal as a verb

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-COMER, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *comer*. You can say you eat. Say that you eat **in the
evening**, and notice how many words English needs.

## You'll want to know: cenar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-COMER] -->

**cenar** — to have dinner, to dine. **la cena** is the meal itself.

*Cenamos a las nueve* — we have dinner at nine.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-05, ES-LEX-COMER] -->

Spanish makes a **verb out of each meal**, where English has to say *have* plus
a noun:

| Spanish verb | English needs |
|---|---|
| desayunar | to have breakfast |
| comer | to have lunch |
| cenar | to have dinner |

**comer** is the trap in that list. You already hold it as *to eat*, and in
Spain it also means specifically **to have lunch** — *¿has comido?* at four in
the afternoon is asking whether you have had lunch, not whether you have eaten
anything at all today.

So the three verbs are a set, and *comer* belongs to it as well as to the
general sense.
