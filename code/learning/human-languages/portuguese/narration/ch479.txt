Portuguese, chapter 479: Mágoas e perdão.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
magoar — to hurt (someone's feelings or body).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for lonely, then the Portuguese for grief.

You'll want to know: magoar.
magoar — "to hurt (someone's feelings or body)".
As palavras dele magoaram-me muito. His words hurt me a lot.
Also physical: magoei o pé — I hurt my foot. Magoar-se — to get hurt.

The word, taken apart.
magoar is usually traced to Latin maculāre "to spot, to stain" (Lewis & Short), built on macula "a spot, a stain": a hurt leaves a mark.

The word, taken apart.
rancor comes from Late Latin rancor "rancidness, a grudge" (EtymDB), built on rancēre "to be rotten".

Lesson 4 of 5.
perdoar — to forgive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for hurt, then the Portuguese for grudge.

You'll want to know: perdoar.
perdoar — "to forgive".
A minha irmã perdoou-me o atraso. My sister forgave me for being late.
Perdoar alguma coisa a alguém — to forgive someone for something.

The word, taken apart.
perdoar is inherited from Old Portuguese perdõar "to forgive" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Late Latin perdōnāre, built on dōnāre "to give".
