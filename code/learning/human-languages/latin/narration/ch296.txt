Latin, chapter 296: Initium et exitus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
initium, initiī — a beginning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for early, then the Latin for a little while ago.

You'll want to know: initium, initiī.
initium, initiī — "a beginning".
Initium fābulae laetum erat, fīnis trīstis. The beginning of the story was happy, the end sad.
Neuter, 2nd declension. Initiō: at the start. English initial.

The word, taken apart.
initium is built on ineō, "to go in, to begin", from in and eō, "to go" (EtymDB): the going-in. English initial and initiate come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
exitus, exitūs — a way out; an outcome, an end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a beginning, then the Latin for a start.

You'll want to know: exitus, exitūs.
exitus, exitūs — "a way out; an outcome, an end".
Nēmō exitum reī prōvidēre poterat. Nobody could foresee the outcome of the affair.
Masculine, 4th declension, from exeō, 'to go out'. English exit.

The word, taken apart.
exitus is built on exeō, "to go out", from ex and eō, "to go" (EtymDB): a going out, and so the way a thing comes out. English exit is from the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
intervallum, intervallī — a gap, an interval.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a start, then the Latin for a way out.

You'll want to know: intervallum, intervallī.
intervallum, intervallī — "a gap, an interval".
Post breve intervallum nūntius alter vēnit. After a short interval a second messenger came.
Neuter. First the space between two stakes of a rampart, then any gap in space or time.

The word, taken apart.
intervallum joins inter, "between", and vallum, "rampart, palisade" (EtymDB): the space between the stakes. English interval comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mōmentum, mōmentī — a moment; weight, importance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a way out, then the Latin for a gap.

You'll want to know: mōmentum, mōmentī.
mōmentum, mōmentī — "a moment; weight, importance".
Mōmentō temporis omnia mūtāta sunt. In a moment of time everything was changed.
Neuter. A slight movement that tips the scales, then an instant, and also 'weight, importance'. English moment, momentum.

The word, taken apart.
mōmentum is built on moveō, "to move" (EtymDB): a movement, the tilt of a balance, and so an instant. English moment and momentum come from it.
