Portuguese, chapter 524: Versos e rimas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a poesia — poetry; poem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to reread, then the Portuguese for bookmark.

You'll want to know: a poesia.
a poesia — "poetry; poem".
A minha avó sabe muita poesia de cor. My grandmother knows a lot of poetry by heart.
Uma poesia can also mean one poem.

The word, taken apart.
poesia comes from Latin poēsis (EtymDB), from Greek poíēsis "a making, a composing", built on poieîn "to make".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o verso — line (of poetry), verse; back (of a page).

You'll want to know: o verso.
o verso — "line (of poetry), verse; back (of a page)".
Este verso é o meu preferido. This line is my favourite.
Also the back of a sheet: no verso da folha.

The word, taken apart.
verso is inherited from Latin versus "a turning, a furrow, a line of writing" (EtymDB), from vertere "to turn".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a rima — rhyme.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for poet, then the Portuguese for line.

You'll want to know: a rima.
a rima — "rhyme".
As canções infantis têm muitas rimas. Children's songs have lots of rhymes.
The verb is rimar: amor rima com dor.

The word, taken apart.
rima goes back to Medieval Latin rithmus (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Greek rhythmós "measured flow, rhythm".

Lesson 5 of 5.
declamar — to recite (aloud).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for line, then the Portuguese for rhyme.

You'll want to know: declamar.
declamar — "to recite (aloud)".
No fim da festa, as crianças declamaram poemas. At the end of the party, the children recited poems.
More solemn than ler em voz alta.

The word, taken apart.
declamar is a learned borrowing from Latin dēclāmāre "to practise speaking aloud" (Lewis & Short), built on clāmāre "to shout".
