Lesson 1 of 5.
placenta, placentae — a flat cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a chicken, then the Latin for pepper.

You'll want to know: placenta, placentae.
placenta, placentae — "a flat cake".

The word, taken apart.
placenta was borrowed from Greek plakous, plakountos, "a flat cake" (Lewis & Short). Anatomists later gave the name to a flat organ of the womb.

The word, taken apart.
cervīsia came from Gaulish, the Celtic language of Gaul: Lewis & Short mark cervīsia as Gallic, a Celtic word for beer.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sūcus, sūcī — juice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a flat cake, then the Latin for beer.

You'll want to know: sūcus, sūcī.
sūcus, sūcī — "juice".

The word, taken apart.
sūcus belongs with sūgō, "to suck" (Lewis & Short), and both are usually traced to Proto-Indo-European sug-, "to suck" (EtymDB 2.1). English suck is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
rosa, rosae — a rose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for beer, then the Latin for juice.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say amō, I love, and then amīcum amō.

You'll want to know: rosa, rosae.
rosa, rosae — "a rose".

The word, taken apart.
rosa is related to Greek rhodon, "rose" (Lewis & Short), but it is not a direct borrowing: it probably reached Latin through another language of Italy. The word is often traced further back to Iranian.
