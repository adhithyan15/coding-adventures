# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-SKILL-READ-A1-PASSAGE-01, ZH-LEX-C86-NAR-01, ZH-LEX-C91-XIANGYAO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

A friend has sent you a message:

> 你在哪儿 你想要什么

[YOU READ: the message aloud — *nǐ zài nǎr, nǐ xiǎngyào shénme*]

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-MINGZI, ZH-LEX-C78-AGE78-01, ZH-LEX-JIA-01, ZH-SCRIPT-NA-WHERE-01, ZH-LEX-XIHUAN-01, ZH-LEX-C91-XIANGYAO-01, ZH-SCRIPT-YAO-01, ZH-LEX-C85-ZAI-01, ZH-LEX-C96-YINWEI-01, ZH-SCRIPT-YIN-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What is official, and what is not.** This book's A1 target is project-defined: aligned to HSK 3.0 Level 1, a match this book makes, not one the HSK publishes. Because the current trial pairs no speaking paper with Level 1, the complete A1 target adds independently scored speaking and productive writing, and at A1 the candidate writes a small form and a short message. No HSK timing is given here; the clock below is **this book's own**, not a published one.

[PAUSE 2s] Set a **three-minute timer**. Fill five fields first. Every label is written in characters you write:

- 名字
- 多大
- 家在哪儿
- 喜欢什么
- 想要什么

[PAUSE 2s] Then, before the timer rings, write back to your friend: **20 to 30 characters**, saying where you are, what you want, and one reason with 因为. This task allows pinyin for a word whose characters you have not written; it goes inside the message, and only characters count toward the 20 to 30. Do not look a character up.

[PAUSE 2s] Speed is where stroke order shows. A character written in the order you learned keeps its shape when you hurry. When the timer rings, **stop**, even mid-character.

[PAUSE 2s] Spend **at most one minute** checking, and do not rewrite the timed answer. Did your reason start with 因为? Did every field get an answer?

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson. In this book, writing is its own paper, scored out of 100, with 60 to pass, and it is not averaged against reading, listening or speaking. A high reading score cannot make up for writing that is not there.
