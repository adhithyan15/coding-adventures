## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-CHAUTHA-01]; assesses=[PA-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE, PA-SCRIPT-THA-01] -->

> **ਚੌਥਾ** — *chauthā* — **fourth**

**ਚਾਰ** (*chār*) gives **ਚੌ-** (*chau-*), and then the word ends in **-ਥਾ**.

The **ਥ** in it is the letter you wrote five lessons ago for **ਕਿੱਥੇ**. It is
doing the same shape here in a word that has nothing to do with *where*.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-CHAUTHA-01, PA-LEX-DUJA-01, PA-LEX-TIJA-01, PA-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-SET-01] -->

Three ordinals now, and two endings:

| how many | which one in the line | ending |
|---|---|---|
| **ਦੋ** | **ਦੂਜਾ** | **-ਜਾ** |
| **ਤਿੰਨ** | **ਤੀਜਾ** | **-ਜਾ** |
| **ਚਾਰ** | **ਚੌਥਾ** | **-ਥਾ** |

So **-ਜਾ** was a resemblance and not a rule, exactly as the last lesson refused
to promise. Wiktionary says why: **ਦੂਜਾ** and **ਤੀਜਾ** come from Sanskrit
*dvitīya* and *tṛtīya*, and **ਚੌਥਾ** comes from Sanskrit *caturtha* — a
different Sanskrit ending, worn down its own way.

These are not built words. They are **inherited** ones, each worn separately,
which is why no rule joins them.
