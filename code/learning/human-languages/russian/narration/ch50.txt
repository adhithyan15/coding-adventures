Lesson 1 of 5.
стена (stená) — a wall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a lamp, then the Russian for a key.

You'll want to know: стена.
стена (stená) — "a wall".

The word, taken apart.
стена (stená) from Proto-Slavic stěna — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Gothic stains, "stone", so English stone is a cousin, and he holds that a loan from Germanic cannot be proved.

Lesson 2 of 5.
крыша (krýsha) — a roof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a key, then the Russian for a wall.

You'll want to know: крыша.
крыша (krýsha) — "a roof".

The word, taken apart.
крыша (krýsha) is built on крыть, "to cover", with an -s- that became ш (Vasmer): the roof is the cover. The same verb is in открывать, "to open", and закрывать, "to close".

Lesson 3 of 5.
сад (sad) — a garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a wall, then the Russian for a roof.

You'll want to know: сад.
сад (sad) — "a garden".

The word, taken apart.
сад (sad) from Proto-Slavic sadŭ, "a planting" — inherited, from садить, "to plant", literally "to make sit", the partner of сидеть, "to sit" (Vasmer). Old Russian sadŭ also meant "a tree, a grove".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ванная (vánnaya) — the bathroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a roof, then the Russian for a garden.

You'll want to know: ванная.
ванная (vánnaya) — "the bathroom".

The word, taken apart.
ванная (vánnaya) is short for ванная (vánnaya) комната, "the bath room": an adjective from ванна, "a bathtub", standing alone. ванна came from German Wanne or French vanne (Vasmer); Trubachev adds that it is recorded by 1697.

Lesson 5 of 5.
часы (chasý) — a clock, a watch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a garden, then the Russian for the bathroom.

You'll want to know: часы.
часы (chasý) — "a clock, a watch". Always plural.

The word, taken apart.
часы (chasý) is the plural of час, "an hour": a clock is "the hours". час from Proto-Slavic časŭ, "time, a while", a cousin of Old Prussian kīsman, "time", and Albanian kohë, "time, weather" (Vasmer).
