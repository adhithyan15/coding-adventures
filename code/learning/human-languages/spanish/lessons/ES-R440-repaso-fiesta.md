## Grammar Lens: three ways a word hides its pieces
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-02, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-04] -->

You now have a small collection of them, and they fail differently.

| the word | what is hidden |
|---|---|
| **boda** | a **plural** the language forgot |
| **cumpleaños** | a whole **phrase**, fused |
| **disfrutar** | a real join whose **meaning** misleads |

*Boda* cannot be recovered from modern Spanish at all — only Latin says why it
is one word. *Cumpleaños* can: say it slowly and *cumple* and *años* fall apart
in your mouth. *Disfrutar* comes apart cleanly and then lies about what the
prefix is doing.

The piece-test tells you **whether** there is a join. It never tells you what the
join means, and *disfrutar* is the word that proves it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-04, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-05, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-03] -->

**You can now be invited to something, say you went, and say how it was.** That
is the whole social round-trip, and it is what a message from a friend actually
contains.

You also have the pair that will keep paying: **celebrarse** on a notice means
*is held*, not *is enjoyed*, and **el humor** is one word where English keeps
two.
