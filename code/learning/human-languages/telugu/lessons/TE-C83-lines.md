# (reading) — six lines, and no word for *is*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words. These put them into sentences.

[PAUSE 1s] Nothing here is new.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SKILL-READ-LINES]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-01, TE-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-03] -->

> ఇది పుస్తకం.
> ఇది తలుపు.
> కుర్చీ ఇక్కడ.
> పుస్తకం సులభం.
> నిజం.
> ఇవాళ.

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and look for the verb in the first four lines.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C76-JUDGE-04, TE-LEX-C75-WHEN-01, TE-SKILL-READ-LINES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] There is none. **ఇది పుస్తకం** is *this book* with nothing between
them, and it is a complete sentence: *this is a book*.

[PAUSE 2s] Telugu does not say *is*. Two words placed together are already a
statement, and a reader has to stop waiting for the word that joins them.

[PAUSE 2s] The last two lines are one word each and still complete. That is the
same rule taken as far as it goes.
