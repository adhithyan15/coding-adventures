# Review — one question, three answers

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-NOT-TIME] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new. Before the table: do the two past tenses differ in
**when**?

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-NOT-TIME, ES-GRAMMAR-BACKDROP-AND-EVENT, ES-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-CHANGES-MEANING, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

Three chapters, one question, three ways it gets answered.

| what you are doing | tense | example |
|---|---|---|
| reporting an event, with edges | **preterite** | *hablé* — I spoke |
| setting a scene, no edges | **imperfect** | *hablaba* — I was speaking |
| naming a state | **imperfect** | *tenía* — I had |
| naming the moment a state began | **preterite** | *tuve* — I got |

Rows one and two are the same event seen from two places. Rows three and four
are not: there the tense changes what happened.

## Grammar Lens: the question to actually ask
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-BACKDROP-AND-EVENT, ES-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-CHANGES-MEANING, ES-LEX-CUANDO, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-ONGOING-HABITUAL] -->

Not *"which tense is this?"* — that has no answer without you.

> **Am I telling you what happened, or what things were like?**

*What happened* has edges: preterite. *What things were like* does not:
imperfect. A story is mostly the second with the first dropped into it, which
is what *cuando* let you build:

> *Estudiaba cuando habló Ana.*

And when the verb names a state rather than an action, the same question still
works — you are simply more likely to be describing what things were like, so
the imperfect is the ordinary case and the preterite is the special one.

There is no list to memorise here, and that is deliberate. Lists of "preterite
trigger words" exist in most courses and they fail exactly where the choice is
interesting, because the same word can sit in either sentence. The question
does not fail, because the question is the thing the language is actually
asking.
