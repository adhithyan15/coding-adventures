Lesson 1 of 6.
shīzōng — to go missing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a honeymoon, then the Mandarin for a white wedding dress.

You'll want to know: shīzōng.
shīzōng — "to go missing".
nàge háizi shīzōng le — That child has gone missing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shīzōng is built from shī 'to lose' and zōng 'a footprint', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syit and tsjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a mistake' and 'to let go' and the second 'tracks': the trail is lost.

Lesson 2 of 6.
自从 (zìcóng) — since; ever since.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a white wedding dress, then the Mandarin for to go missing.

You'll want to know: 自从.
自从 — zìcóng — "since; ever since".
自从那天以后我天天写日记 zìcóng nà tiān yǐhòu, wǒ tiāntiān xiě rìjì — Since that day, I've kept a diary every day.
The new character, in pieces: 自 (zì) — six strokes: a short sweep down to the left on top, then a tall frame: a vertical, a bar that turns down the right side, two bars inside, and the bar that closes the foot, like 日 grown taller. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
自从 (zìcóng) is built from 自 (zì) 'from' and 从 'from', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzijH and dzjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'from' and 'by way of' and the second 'to go along with'.

Script — a short sweep over a tall frame.
自 (zì).
Two pieces: a short sweep on top, a tall frame under it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The sweep, one stroke: short, down to the left, at the top middle.
The frame, five strokes, built like 日: a vertical down the left; a bar across the top that turns down the right side; two bars inside; then the bar that closes the foot.
1 + 5 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 自 (zì), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 自 (zì)? (Six.) What comes first? (The short sweep on top.) How many bars inside the frame? (Two.)

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go missing, then the Mandarin for ever since.

You'll want to know: 来自.
来自 — láizì — "to come from".
来自中国的新生经常来我家 láizì Zhōngguó de xīnshēng jīngcháng lái wǒ jiā — The new students from China often come to our house.

The word, taken apart.
来自 (láizì) is built from 来 'to come' and 自 (zì) 'from', both inherited from Middle Chinese, loj and dzijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to arrive'.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pàichūsuǒ is built from pài, 出 'out' and 所 'a place', all inherited from Middle Chinese, pheaH, tsyhwit and srjoX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a branch of a river'; its sense 'to send' is not given there. The place that sends officers out.
