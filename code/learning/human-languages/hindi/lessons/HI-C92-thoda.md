# थोड़ा — the honest yes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Somebody asks whether you speak Hindi. You can say yes and you can
say no. Try to say **a little**, which is the true answer, and find you cannot.

## You'll want to know: थोड़ा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01]; assesses=[] -->

**थोड़ा** (*thoṛā*) — a little, a bit, some.

**हाँ, थोड़ी।** — Yes, a little. That two-word answer is a listening item on a
practice paper.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

It ends in **-आ**, so it bends like every other word that does:

| what is being hedged | the form |
|---|---|
| a masculine thing | थोड़ा |
| a feminine thing | थोड़ी |

That is why the paper's answer is **थोड़ी** rather than **थोड़ा** — the question
was about **हिंदी**, and *hindī* is feminine. The hedge agrees with what it
hedges, not with the person speaking.

This catches English speakers out, because *a little* in English is a fixed
lump that never changes. Here it is an adjective, and adjectives agree.

## Grammar Lens: where it stands
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| मैं थोड़ी हिंदी बोलता हूँ। | I speak a little Hindi. |
| हाँ, थोड़ी। | Yes, a little. |

In front of the noun, like any adjective. And on its own as a complete answer,
where the noun it agrees with is the one the question named.

A hedge is worth more at this level than it looks. **It is the difference
between claiming fluency and being believed**, and an examiner hears the
difference.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01] -->

hedge agrees with what it hedges.) Does English change *a little* the same way?
(No — it is a fixed lump there.)
