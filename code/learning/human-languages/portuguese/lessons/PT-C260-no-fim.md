# primeiro, depois, no fim — the events, in order

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-PRIMEIRO-02, PT-LEX-C71-QUANDO2-03, PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "first", the ordinal from chapter twenty-seven. (*Primeiro.*)
Then "afterwards". (*Depois.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the same words go to work on a story. The scene is painted;
the events need a running order.

## Grammar Lens: three places in a story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-DEPOIS-ORDEM-01, PT-GRAMMAR-NO-FIM-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C54-COISAS3-04, PT-LEX-C47-REFEICOES-04, PT-LEX-C150-TEMPO150-02] -->

> **Primeiro** comprei o bilhete. — First I bought the ticket.
> **Depois** esperei o comboio. — Then I waited for the train.
> **No fim**, voltei para casa. — In the end, I went back home.

**Depois** is the plain "then, after that". It stands between two events and
points from the first to the second, and you can use it as many times as the
story has events. **A seguir**, which you met as "next", does the same job.

**Depois** can also take a noun, with **de**: *depois do jantar*, "after
dinner", where *de* and *o* weld into **do**. *A seguir* takes *a*
instead, *a seguir ao jantar*, and often stands alone.

**No fim** is "in the end": the last event, and usually the outcome. You have
**finalmente** too, "finally, at last", and it carries relief: something you
were waiting for has come. *No fim* simply closes the story. *Fazia frio e
chovia, e no fim voltei para casa contente.*

Notice what these words do **not** touch: the scene. *Fazia frio* needs no
*depois*. Order belongs to the events.

## The word, taken apart: after, and the end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-DEPOIS-03]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C212-TEMPO212-01] -->

**depois** is usually traced to Latin *dē* and a spoken Latin *postius*, a
form of *post*, "after, behind" (Lewis & Short, under *post*); EtymDB 2.1
follows it only to Old Portuguese *depois*.
English keeps *post* in **postpone** and **postscript**.

**o fim**, "the end", has no parent in EtymDB 2.1. It is usually traced to
Latin *fīnis*, "boundary, end" (Lewis & Short), which English kept in
**final** and **finish**, and which also gave **finalmente**.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which of *depois* and *a seguir* takes a noun with *de*? (***Depois***
— *depois do jantar*.) Which closes a story, and which says "at last"? (***No

*depois*? (***Post***, "after".)
