Latin, chapter 511: Cruelty and gentleness.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
crūdēlitās, crūdēlitātis — cruelty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for out of one's mind, then the Latin for to be delirious.

You'll want to know: crūdēlitās, crūdēlitātis.
crūdēlitās, crūdēlitātis — "cruelty".
Crūdēlitās dominī servōs terret. The master's cruelty frightens the slaves.
Feminine, third declension, from crūdēlis. English cruelty.

The word, taken apart.
crūdēlitās is built on crūdēlis "cruel", itself related to crūdus "raw" (Lewis & Short). English cruelty comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

saevitia, saevitiae — "fury, savagery".
Saevitia tempestātis nautās terret. The fury of the storm frightens the sailors.
Feminine, first declension, from saevus — fierce. Of beasts, storms and harsh masters.

The word, taken apart.
saevitia is built on saevus "fierce, savage" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB); the origin of saevus is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
laedō, laedere — to hurt, harm; to offend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for cruelty, then the Latin for fury.

You'll want to know: laedō, laedere.
laedō, laedere — "to hurt, harm; to offend".
Verba tua amīcum laedunt. Your words hurt your friend.
laedō, laedere, laesī, laesum. Of the body and of feelings. English lesion.

The word, taken apart.
laedō is a native Latin verb; Lewis & Short offers a guess at its root, but the origin is not settled. English lesion comes from its participle laesus, and collide from the compound collīdō.

Vīnum dolōrem mītigat. Wine eases pain.
mītigō, mītigāre, mītigāvī, mītigātum: mītis 'mild' + agō 'to make'. English mitigate.

The word, taken apart.
mītigō is built on mītis "mild" and agō "to drive, make" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English mitigate comes from it.

mānsuētus, mānsuēta, mānsuētum — tame; gentle.

Leō in amphitheātrō mānsuētus est. The lion in the amphitheatre is tame.
The participle of mānsuēscō: manus 'hand' + suēscō 'to get used to' — used to the hand.

The word, taken apart.
mānsuētus is the participle of mānsuēscō "to grow tame", built on manus "hand" and suēscō "to become used to" (Lewis & Short): used to the hand.
