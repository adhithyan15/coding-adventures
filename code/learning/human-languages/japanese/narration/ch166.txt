Japanese, chapter 166: Mornings, Noons and Nights Ahead.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ごぜん (gozen) — a.m.; the morning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the twentieth of the month, then the Japanese for a birthday.

You'll want to know: ごぜん.
ごぜん — gozen — "a.m.; the morning".
あしたの ごぜんは はたらきます。 — ashita no gozen wa hatarakimasu — "Tomorrow morning I will work."

The word, taken apart.
ごぜん (gozen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are go, "noon" (Middle Chinese nguX) and zen, "before, in front" (dzen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ひる (hiru) — noon; the daytime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a birthday, then the Japanese for a.m..

You'll want to know: ひる.
ひる — hiru — "noon; the daytime".
あしたは ひるに ともだちと あいます。 — ashita wa hiru ni tomodachi to aimasu — "Tomorrow I will meet a friend at noon."

The word, taken apart.
ひる (hiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
