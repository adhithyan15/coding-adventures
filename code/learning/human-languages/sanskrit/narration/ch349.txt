Sanskrit, chapter 349: In Court.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
न्यायालयः (nyāyālayaḥ) — a court of law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for partiality, then the Sanskrit for breaks (a rule).

You'll want to know: न्यायालयः.
न्यायालयः — nyāyālayaḥ — "a court of law".
अधिवक्ता प्रातः न्यायालयं गच्छति। adhivaktā prātaḥ nyāyālayaṃ gacchati. The lawyer goes to court in the morning.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
न्यायालयः (nyāyālayaḥ) is a modern word built from nyāya, "rule, right manner", and ālaya, "a house, an abode"; MW records the parts but not the compound (MW).

प्रतिवादी (prativādī) — a defendant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for breaks (a rule), then the Sanskrit for a court of law.

You'll want to know: प्रतिवादी.
प्रतिवादी — prativādī — "a defendant".
प्रतिवादी न्यायाधीशाय सत्यं वदति। prativādī nyāyādhīśāya satyaṃ vadati. The defendant tells the judge the truth.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिवादी (prativādī) is built from prati, "against", and vādin, "speaking", from the root √vad; MW gives prati-vādin "an opponent" and "a defendant, a respondent" (MW).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a court of law, then the Sanskrit for a defendant.

You'll want to know: अपराध्यति.
अपराध्यति — aparādhyati — "does wrong; offends".
यः अपराध्यति, सः दण्डं प्राप्नोति। yaḥ aparādhyati, saḥ daṇḍaṃ prāpnoti. Whoever does wrong is punished.
Past (he …): सः अपराद्धवान्, saḥ aparāddhavān.

The word, taken apart.
अपराध्यति (aparādhyati) is built on apa, "away", and the root √rādh, "to succeed"; MW gives apa-√rādh "to wrong, to offend against, to sin" (MW).

You'll want to know: निगृह्णाति.
निगृह्णाति — nigṛhṇāti — "restrains; punishes".
राजा चौरान् निगृह्णाति। rājā caurān nigṛhṇāti. The king restrains thieves.
Past (he …): सः चौरं निगृहीतवान्, saḥ cauraṃ nigṛhītavān.

The word, taken apart.
निगृह्णाति (nigṛhṇāti) is built on ni, "down", and the root √grah, "to seize"; MW gives ni-√grah "to hold back, restrain, curb, punish" (MW).
