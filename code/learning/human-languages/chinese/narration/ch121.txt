Mandarin Chinese, chapter 121: Teaching and Practising.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiāo — to teach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pack food to take away, then the Mandarin for dim sum.

You'll want to know: jiāo.
jiāo — "to teach".
tā jiāo wǒ hànyǔ — She teaches me Chinese.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiāo is inherited from Middle Chinese kaewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to teach'; the dictionary also lists the character with a level tone, kaew, glossed 'to imitate', and the level jiāo of the verb matches that one.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Lesson 2 of 5.
liànxí — to practise.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: liànxí.
liànxí — "to practise".
wǒ měitiān liànxí shuō hànyǔ — I practise speaking Chinese every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liànxí is built from liàn and xí, both inherited from Middle Chinese, lenH and zip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as white boiled silk and the second 'to learn', explaining it as flying again and again.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fùxí — to revise; to go over again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to practise, then the Mandarin for to answer.

You'll want to know: fùxí.
fùxí — "to revise; to go over again".
wǒ wǎnshang fùxí hànyǔ — I go over my Chinese in the evening.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fùxí is built from fù 'again' and xí 'to learn', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjuwk and zip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to learn again. The dictionary glosses the first 'to return, to repeat' and lists it with a departing tone too, bjuwH, glossed 'again': fù continues that one, since the regular change from bjuwk gives fú.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cuò — wrong; mistaken.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to answer, then the Mandarin for to revise.

You'll want to know: cuò.
cuò — "wrong; mistaken".
zhège hànzì cuò le — This character is wrong.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cuò is inherited from Middle Chinese tshak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'mixed' and 'crossing slantwise'; how it came to mean wrong is not settled.
