# ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ) — a buzz, a hum

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-03, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Imagine a bee circling close enough that its sound fills the air.
You already know **క**, the dot-like **ం**, and the long **ా** sign. One rare
first letter will turn those familiar pieces into a name for that sound.

## You'll want to know: ఝంకారం
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C152-BUZZ-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ఝంకారం** — *jhaṅkāraṁ* — "a buzz" or "a hum".

Say it in three pieces: *jhaṅ · kā · raṁ*. The first sound is an aspirated
**jh**, with a small breath after the voice. Read the word as **ఝం-కా-రం**.

This is a learned, literary word derived from Sanskrit. Older dictionaries
also give it a wider sense of a loud resonant noise. Keep today's image narrow:
the steady buzzing or humming sound, especially the sound of a bee.
