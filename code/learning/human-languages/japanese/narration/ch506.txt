Japanese, chapter 506: Winning and Losing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きょうぎ (kyōgi) — a sport; a competition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bow (for arrows), then the Japanese for to dodge.

You'll want to know: きょうぎ.
きょうぎ — kyōgi — "a sport; a competition".
どの きょうぎが すきですか。 — dono kyōgi ga suki desu ka — "Which sport do you like?"

The word, taken apart.
きょうぎ (kyōgi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kyō, "emulate" (Middle Chinese gjaengH) and gi, "skill" (gjeX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
まかす (makasu) — to defeat; to beat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a victory, then the Japanese for a defeat.

You'll want to know: まかす.
まかす — makasu — "to defeat; to beat".
おとうとが しょうぎで ちちを まかしました。 — otōto ga shōgi de chichi o makashimashita — "My younger brother beat my father at shogi."
Polite まかします, makashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): いもうとが あにを まかしました。, imōto ga ani o makashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
まかす (makasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
