A 64-year-old male presented with severe acute-onset myalgia affecting his hips and shoulders, three months after a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes. He described proximal myalgia and stiffness affecting the upper limbs more than the lower limbs, with pain disturbing sleep and no diurnal variation. Systemic examination was normal with no overt neurological deficit, showing nonspecific myalgia and arthralgia.

Laboratory testing showed C-reactive protein elevated to a peak of 103 mg/L (reference less than 5) and an erythrocyte sedimentation rate of 45 mm/hr. Creatine kinase was 811 IU/L (reference 30 to 200), and on a later occasion rose to 3473 IU/L following strenuous activity. Antinuclear antibody, extractable nuclear antigen, and a myositis autoantibody panel were all negative. Immunoglobulins, renal function, and vitamin D were normal. MRI of the spine was normal. Upper-limb muscle MRI showed nonspecific atrophy without edema and no diagnostic findings.

He was treated with prednisolone 30 mg daily for five days with significant improvement, then commenced on a tapering regimen. On detailed questioning he reported episodic short-lived myalgia that was longstanding and normal for him. He also recalled muscle spasms when walking long distances, and as a child having to be carried off a running track after a 50-yard sprint.
