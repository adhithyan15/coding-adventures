French, chapter 339: Les preuves.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la preuve — the proof, the evidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for absurd, then the French for debatable.

You'll want to know: la preuve.
la preuve — "the proof, the evidence".
Tu dis que c'est lui, mais tu n'as aucune preuve. You say it was him, but you have no proof.
Faire preuve de — to show a quality: faire preuve de courage.

The word, taken apart.
preuve is usually traced to Old French prueve, a noun formed on prouver "to prove", which is inherited from Latin probāre "to test, to examine" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
évident — obvious, evident.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for debatable, then the French for the proof.

You'll want to know: évident.
évident — "obvious, evident".
C'est évident: il est fatigué. It is obvious: he is tired.
Ce n'est pas évident also means it is not easy.

The word, taken apart.
évident is a learned borrowing from Latin ēvidēns "apparent, visible, clear" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from ē "out" and vidēre "to see".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'évidence — the obvious, what is plain to see.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the proof, then the French for obvious.

You'll want to know: l'évidence.
l'évidence — "the obvious, what is plain to see".
Il faut se rendre à l'évidence: nous sommes perdus. We have to face the facts: we are lost.
A false friend: l'évidence is what is plain to see; evidence in court is la preuve. Se rendre à l'évidence — to face the facts.

The word, taken apart.
évidence is a learned borrowing from Latin ēvidentia "clearness, distinctness" (Lewis & Short), from ēvidēns "clear", built on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
incontestable — indisputable, unquestionable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for obvious, then the French for the obvious.

You'll want to know: incontestable.
incontestable — "indisputable, unquestionable".
Son talent est incontestable. Her talent is indisputable.
Literally that no one can contest. One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
incontestable is built from in- "not", contester and -able. contester comes from Latin contestārī "to call to witness" (Lewis & Short), built on testis "witness".

Lesson 5 of 5.
manifeste — clear, obvious, manifest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the obvious, then the French for indisputable.

You'll want to know: manifeste.
manifeste — "clear, obvious, manifest".
Il y a une erreur manifeste dans la facture. There is a clear mistake in the bill.
Formal. As a noun, un manifeste is a manifesto.

The word, taken apart.
manifeste is a learned borrowing from Latin manifestus "palpable, plain, evident" (Lewis & Short), which the dictionary links to manus "hand": so clear you could touch it.
