Arabic, chapter 533: Looking after loved ones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اعتنى (iʿtanā) — to look after.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to lead a life, then the Arabic for to die.

You'll want to know: اعتنى.
اعتنى — iʿtanā — "to look after".
تعتني أختي بجدتي المريضة. taʿtanī ukhtī bi-jaddatī al-marīḍa. My sister looks after my sick grandmother.
Present: يعتني, yaʿtanī.
It takes بـ for the person.

The word, taken apart.
اعتنى (iʿtanā) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-n-y, "to concern, to occupy" (Lane). ʿĀnā, to suffer, is from the same root; Form VIII iʿtanā is to make a person your concern.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

Lesson 2 of 5.
دلل (dallala) — to pamper; to spoil (a child).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to die, then the Arabic for to look after.

You'll want to know: دلل.
دلل — dallala — "to pamper; to spoil (a child)".
يدلل الجد حفيده الصغير. yudallilu al-jadd ḥafīdahu aṣ-ṣaghīr. The grandfather spoils his little grandson.
Present: يدلل, yudallil.

The word, taken apart.
دلل (dallala) is built on the native Arabic root d-l-l, "to direct; to be coquettish" (Lane). Form II dallala is to pamper. Dalla, to show the way, which you know, has the same letters.

You'll want to know: حضن.
حضن — ḥaḍana — "to cradle; to hold in one's arms".
حضنت الأم طفلها الباكي. ḥaḍanat al-umm ṭiflahā al-bākī. The mother held her crying child in her arms.
Present: يحضن, yaḥḍun.
It also means to foster a child.

The word, taken apart.
حضن (ḥaḍana) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-ḍ-n, "to hold to the bosom; to rear" (Lane). Lane gives yaḥḍunu as its present.

حبيب (ḥabīb) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-b-b, "to love" (Lane). Ḥubb, love, and aḥabba, to love, which you know, share the root.
