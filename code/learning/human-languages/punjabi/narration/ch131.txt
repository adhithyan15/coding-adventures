Punjabi, chapter 131: Duck, Lizard, Turtle, Crocodile, Mosquito.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਬੱਤਖ਼ (battakh) — a duck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a wolf, then the Punjabi for a pigeon.

You'll want to know: ਬੱਤਖ਼.
ਬੱਤਖ਼ — battakh — "a duck".

The word, taken apart.
ਬੱਤਖ਼ (battakh) is usually traced to Persian baṭ, "duck". Lane calls the matching Arabic baṭṭ a Persian word, or an imitation of the bird's cry.

ਕੱਛੂ — kacchū — "a turtle".

The word, taken apart.
ਕੱਛੂ (kacchū) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kacchapa, "turtle, tortoise" (CDIAL 2619).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਮਗਰਮੱਛ (magarmacch) — a crocodile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a lizard, then the Punjabi for a turtle.

You'll want to know: ਮਗਰਮੱਛ.
ਮਗਰਮੱਛ — magarmacch — "a crocodile".

The word, taken apart.
ਮਗਰਮੱਛ (magarmacch) is "crocodile-fish": magar from Sanskrit makara, "crocodile", through Prakrit magara (CDIAL 9692), and macch, "fish", from matsya (CDIAL 9758).

The word, taken apart.
ਮੱਛਰ (macchar) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from matsara, "mosquito, fly" (CDIAL 9757). CDIAL calls a Prakrit source for the Sanskrit word possible, though unsupported.
