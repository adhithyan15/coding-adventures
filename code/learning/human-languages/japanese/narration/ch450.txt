Japanese, chapter 450: At the Clinic.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ないか (naika) — internal medicine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a scar, then the Japanese for to wipe away.

You'll want to know: ないか.
ないか — naika — "internal medicine".
ねつが あるので、ないかに いきます。 — netsu ga aru node, naika ni ikimasu — "I have a fever, so I am going to internal medicine."

The word, taken apart.
ないか (naika) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nai, "inside" (Middle Chinese nwojH) and ka, "department" (khwa) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

めが かゆいので、めぐすりを つかいました。 — me ga kayui node, megusuri o tsukaimashita — "My eyes itched, so I used eye drops."

Lesson 5 of 5.
かんびょうする (kanbyō suru) — to nurse the sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for nutrition, then the Japanese for eye drops.

You'll want to know: かんびょうする.
かんびょうする — kanbyō suru — "to nurse the sick".
ははが ちちを かんびょうしています。 — haha ga chichi o kanbyō shite imasu — "My mother is nursing my father."
Polite かんびょうします, kanbyōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): むすめが そぼを かんびょうしました。, musume ga sobo o kanbyō shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かんびょうする (kanbyō suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango) kanbyō with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kan, "watch over" (Middle Chinese khan) and byō, "sick" (bjaengH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
