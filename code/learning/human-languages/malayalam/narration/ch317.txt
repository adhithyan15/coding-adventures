Malayalam, chapter 317: Alone, Anyway, For no reason, Not at all, Barely.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
തനിയെ (taniye) — alone, by oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a village council, then the Malayalam for the countryside.

You'll want to know: തനിയെ.
തനിയെ — taniye — "alone, by oneself".

The word, taken apart.
തനിയെ (taniye) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3196 lists tani, "by itself, alone", under tān, "self", with Tamil tāṉ.

Lesson 2 of 5.
എന്തായാലും (entāyāluṁ) — anyway, in any case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the countryside, then the Malayalam for alone.

You'll want to know: എന്തായാലും.
എന്തായാലും — entāyāluṁ — "anyway, in any case".

The word, taken apart.
എന്തായാലും (entāyāluṁ) is native Dravidian, "whatever it may become": entŭ, "what" (DEDR 5151), āyāl, "if it becomes" (DEDR 333), and -uṁ, "even". DEDR 333 compares Sanskrit ām.

Lesson 5 of 5.
കഷ്ടിച്ച് (kaṣṭiccŭ) — barely, hardly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for for no reason, then the Malayalam for not at all.

You'll want to know: കഷ്ടിച്ച്.
കഷ്ടിച്ച് — kaṣṭiccŭ — "barely, hardly".

The word, taken apart.
കഷ്ടിച്ച് (kaṣṭiccŭ) is a Malayalam verb form built on Sanskrit kaṣṭa, "bad, difficult" (CDIAL 2978): "with difficulty".
