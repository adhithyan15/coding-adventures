Lesson 1 of 6.
le dégât — the damage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to bite, then the French for the bite.

You'll want to know: le dégât.
le dégât — "the damage".
L'orage a fait beaucoup de dégâts. The storm did a lot of damage.
Usually plural: les dégâts.

The word, taken apart.
dégât is built on Old French degaster "to lay waste", usually traced to Latin dēvastāre (EtymDB lists no parent), built on vastus "empty, waste" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
la rayure — the scratch; a stripe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the insurance, then the French for the disaster victim.

You'll want to know: la rayure.
la rayure — "the scratch; a stripe".
Il y a une longue rayure sur la voiture. There is a long scratch on the car.
Also a stripe: une chemise à rayures.

The word, taken apart.
rayure is built on rayer "to scratch, to stripe", from raie "a line", which EtymDB traces through Old French roie to Gaulish rica "a furrow"; the details are not fully settled (EtymDB lists no parent).
