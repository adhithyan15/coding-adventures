Japanese, chapter 378: A Crowded Carriage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
まんいん (man'in) — full (of people).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a direction (of travel), then the Japanese for to move away.

You'll want to know: まんいん.
まんいん — man'in — "full (of people)".
あさの でんしゃは いつも まんいんです。 — asa no densha wa itsumo man'in desu — "The morning trains are always full."

The word, taken apart.
まんいん (man'in) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are man, "full" (Middle Chinese manX) and in, "member" (hjwen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

ぎゅうぎゅう — gyūgyū — "packed tight; squeezed in".
でんしゃは ぎゅうぎゅうでした。 — densha wa gyūgyū deshita — "The train was packed tight."

The word, taken apart.
ぎゅうぎゅう (gyūgyū) is a native mimetic word that pictures people or things pressed tightly together: an imitative Japanese form, inherited rather than borrowed, whose older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.
つめこむ (tsumekomu) — to cram in; to stuff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for congestion, then the Japanese for packed tight.

You'll want to know: つめこむ.
つめこむ — tsumekomu — "to cram in; to stuff".
かばんに にもつを つめこみました。 — kaban ni nimotsu o tsumekomimashita — "I crammed my things into the bag."
Polite つめこみます, tsumekomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あねが かばんに ふくを つめこみました。, ane ga kaban ni fuku o tsumekomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
つめこむ (tsumekomu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tsume, "packing" (from tsumeru) and komu, "to go in"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
