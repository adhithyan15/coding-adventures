Russian, chapter 32: Pain, Illness, Medicine, Doctor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
боль (bol) — pain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the face, then the Russian for blood.

You'll want to know: боль.
боль (bol) — "pain".

The word, taken apart.
боль (bol) from Proto-Slavic bolĭ — inherited, with the verb болеть, "to ache, to be ill". Vasmer sets it beside Gothic balwjan, "to torment", and Old English bealu, "evil", the bale of baleful, but notes that Berneker rejected the link.

Lesson 3 of 5.
лекарство (lekárstvo) — medicine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for pain, then the Russian for an illness.

You'll want to know: лекарство.
лекарство (lekárstvo) — "medicine".

The word, taken apart.
лекарство (lekárstvo) is лекарь, an old word for "doctor", with the noun suffix -ство. The root лек-, "cure", came into Slavic from Germanic; Vasmer calls the loan certain and compares Gothic lēkeis, "doctor". English leech, an old word for "physician", is from that Germanic word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
врач (vrach) — a doctor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an illness, then the Russian for medicine.

You'll want to know: врач.
врач (vrach) — "a doctor".

The word, taken apart.
врач (vrach) — a Church Slavonic word, Old Church Slavonic vračĭ; its -ра-, where East Slavic would say -оро-, marks it. Vasmer derives it from the root of врать, "to talk nonsense", and ворчать, "to grumble", so the first sense was "charmer, healer by spells"; Bulgarian врач (vrach) still means "sorcerer".

Lesson 5 of 5.
больница (bolnítsa) — a hospital.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for medicine, then the Russian for a doctor.

You'll want to know: больница.
больница (bolnítsa) — "a hospital". Built on боль (bol) as well.

The word, taken apart.
больница (bolnítsa) is больной, "sick; a patient", with -ница, a suffix that names places: a place for the sick. The root is that of боль (bol), "pain".
