Portuguese, chapter 378: Antes do exame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rever — to revise, to go over; to see again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the master's degree, then the Portuguese for the doctorate.

You'll want to know: rever.
rever — "to revise, to go over; to see again".
Revi a matéria toda no fim de semana. I revised all the material at the weekend.
Conjugated like ver: revejo, revês, revê. In Portugal it is the usual word for revising for exams.

The word, taken apart.
rever is built from re- "again" and ver "to see", inherited from Latin vidēre "to see"; compare Latin revidēre "to see again, to go to see again" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o manual — the textbook; the manual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to revise, then the Portuguese for the class notes.

You'll want to know: o manual.
o manual — "the textbook; the manual".
O manual de matemática pesa muito. The maths textbook is very heavy.
In Portugal, o manual escolar is the school textbook. As an adjective, by hand: trabalho manual.

The word, taken apart.
manual is a learned borrowing from Latin manuālis "of the hand, that fills the hand" (EtymDB), built on manus "hand": a book you hold.

Lesson 4 of 5.
decorar — to learn by heart, to memorise; to decorate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the class notes, then the Portuguese for the textbook.

You'll want to know: decorar.
decorar — "to learn by heart, to memorise; to decorate".
Decorei o poema para a aula. I learnt the poem by heart for the class.
In Portugal decorar very often means to memorise; it also means to decorate a room.

The word, taken apart.
decorar "to decorate" comes from Latin decorāre "to adorn" (EtymDB); the sense "to learn by heart" is usually linked with de cor "by heart", on Latin cor "heart" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o teste — the test.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the textbook, then the Portuguese for to learn by heart.

You'll want to know: o teste.
o teste — "the test".
Amanhã temos um teste de português. Tomorrow we have a Portuguese test.
At school, a written test during the term; o exame is bigger.

The word, taken apart.
teste was borrowed from English test (EtymDB), which goes back through Old French test "pot for melting metal" to Latin testum "an earthen vessel" (Lewis & Short): metals were tested in such pots.
