## You'll want to know: lǐbian
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C85-LIBIAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

*lǐbian* — "inside; in".

> *shū zài bāo lǐbian* — The book is in the bag.

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*lǐbian* is built from lǐ 'inside', the 里 you write, and bian 'side', the syllable that ends *zuǒbian* and *pángbiān*. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *liX* and *pen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the inside of a garment' and 'an edge; a side'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C85-ZAI-01] -->

*lǐbian* comes after the thing whose inside it names: *bāo lǐbian*, inside the bag. Put 在 in front and it is a place: *zài bāo lǐbian*.

里 on its own does the same job in a quicker sentence, as in *shān lǐ*. *lǐbian* can also stand alone: *zài lǐbian*, inside.
