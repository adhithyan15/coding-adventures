# el taller — where things get mended

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C363-WHERE-41, ES-LEX-TRABAJO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el coche*. Say *el trabajo*. The car has stopped working. Name
the place it goes.

## You'll want to know: el taller
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C363-WHERE-41] -->

**el taller** — the workshop, the garage in the sense of *repair shop*.

Careful with that English word: **el garaje** is where you park; **el taller**
is where it gets fixed. English uses *garage* for both and Spanish does not.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01] -->

**el taller** also means a **workshop** in the other sense — a short practical
class. *Un taller de cocina*, *un taller de reparación de bicicletas*.

| the phrase | what it is |
|---|---|
| llevar el coche al taller | the repair shop |
| un taller de cocina | a hands-on class |

The two meanings share a core that English keeps as well: a place where people
work with their hands, rather than sit and listen. If a notice offers you a
*taller*, expect to do something, not to watch.
