# sois — the last of the six

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SER-PRESENT-3PL, ES-LEX-VOSOTROS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "they are." (*Son*.) And which region uses *vosotros*? (*Spain*.)

## Grammar Lens: one syllable, and the paradigm closes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-SER-PRESENT-2PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-2PL, ES-CULTURE-VOSOTROS-USTEDES-SPLIT] -->

> **vosotros sois** — *you all are*, in Spain.

One syllable, and with it the six forms of *ser* are complete: *soy*, *eres*,
*es*, *somos*, *sois*, *son*.

As before, this is the form you need in order to **follow** Spain rather than to
speak everywhere. In the Americas, *ustedes son* does the same job.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SER-PRESENT-2PL] -->

Latin had **estis**. Spanish wore away the *e-* at the front and the *-t-* in
the middle, and *sois* is what was left — a word reduced almost to nothing but
still doing its whole job.

That is the pattern with this verb throughout. *Ser* is short everywhere because
it is said constantly, and constant use grinds words down. The commonest verb in
a language is almost always its most eroded one.
