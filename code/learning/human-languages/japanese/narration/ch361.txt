Japanese, chapter 361: Profit and Loss.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
もうける (mōkeru) — to make a profit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be saved up, then the Japanese for to keep down.

You'll want to know: もうける.
もうける — mōkeru — "to make a profit".
よく うれたら、みせは もうけるでしょう。 — yoku uretara, mise wa mōkeru deshō — "If it sells well, the shop will make a profit."
Polite もうけます, mōkemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): みせが もうけました。, mise ga mōkemashita.

The word, taken apart.
もうける (mōkeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 6.
りえき (rieki) — a profit; gains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to make a profit, then the Japanese for to be profitable.

You'll want to know: りえき.
りえき — rieki — "a profit; gains".
りえきが あったら、みんなで わけます。 — rieki ga attara, minna de wakemasu — "If there is a profit, we share it among us."

The word, taken apart.
りえき (rieki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ri, "advantage" (Middle Chinese lijH) and eki, "benefit" ('jiek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
