# ঁ, from above the line

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 5s] Say *yes* and *no*.

## Guided Practice — the distant band
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01, BN-SCRIPT-CHANDRABINDU-NASAL, BN-SCRIPT-CHANDRABINDU-01] -->

- [YOU SAY: *yes* — *hyã*, through the nose]
- [YOU SAY: *no* — *nā*, not through the nose]
- [YOU SAY: the number five — *pā̃ch*, and notice the same nasal]
- [YOU SAY: where on the line the mark that writes it sits (**above**)]

Three words in this whole book carry that crescent, and they are spread across
three chapters. That is exactly the shape of thing a reader loses: rare enough to
go, common enough to matter.

Say *hyã* and *nā* one after another until the difference is in the nose and not
in the effort.

## Writing — from sound
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-CHANDRABINDU-01, BN-SCRIPT-NA-01] -->

Cover every model. Write the crescent-with-a-dot, then put it on **না**.

- [YOU WRITE: ঁ]
- [YOU WRITE: নাঁ]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-CHANDRABINDU-01, BN-CONCEPT-C01-HYANNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Is **ঁ** a consonant? (**No**.) What does it change? (**The vowel** —
it goes through the nose.) And is it the only mark above the head-line?
(**Yes**.)
