Sanskrit, chapter 435: Old Coins and Storehouses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कपर्दकः (kapardakaḥ) — a cowrie shell (as money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a deposit, then the Sanskrit for pays back.

You'll want to know: कपर्दकः.
कपर्दकः — kapardakaḥ — "a cowrie shell (as money)".
पूर्वं जनाः कपर्दकैः धान्यं क्रीणन्ति स्म। pūrvaṃ janāḥ kapardakaiḥ dhānyaṃ krīṇanti sma. Long ago people used to buy grain with cowrie shells.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कपर्दकः (kapardakaḥ) is built on kaparda, "a cowrie shell", with the ending -ka; MW gives kapardaka as the cowrie shell, used as a small coin, but no derivation for kaparda, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

राजा कवये शतं निष्कान् ददाति। rājā kavaye śataṃ niṣkān dadāti. The king gives the poet a hundred gold pieces.

The word, taken apart.
निष्कः (niṣkaḥ) is the word MW gives for a gold ornament for the neck and a coin or weight of gold; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
दीनारः (dīnāraḥ) — a dinar (a gold coin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cowrie shell, then the Sanskrit for a gold piece.

You'll want to know: दीनारः.
दीनारः — dīnāraḥ — "a dinar (a gold coin)".
धनिकस्य स्यूते दीनाराः सन्ति। dhanikasya syūte dīnārāḥ santi. There are dinars in the rich man's bag.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दीनारः (dīnāraḥ) is borrowed from Greek dēnarion, the Roman denarius, as MW derives it; MW gives dīnāra as a gold ornament and a gold coin (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
भाण्डागारम् (bhāṇḍāgāram) — a storehouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a dinar, then the Sanskrit for barter.

You'll want to know: भाण्डागारम्.
भाण्डागारम् — bhāṇḍāgāram — "a storehouse".
नगरस्य भाण्डागारे बहु धान्यम् अस्ति। nagarasya bhāṇḍāgāre bahu dhānyam asti. There is a lot of grain in the city's storehouse.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
भाण्डागारम् (bhāṇḍāgāram) is built from bhāṇḍa, "a vessel, wares", and āgāra, "a house, a room"; MW gives the compound as a treasury, a storehouse (MW).
