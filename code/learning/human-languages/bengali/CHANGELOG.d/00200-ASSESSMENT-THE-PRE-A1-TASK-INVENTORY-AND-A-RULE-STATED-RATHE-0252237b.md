## Assessment: the pre-A1 task inventory, and a rule stated rather than borrowed

Bengali's pre-A1 four-skill task inventory is checked in, with the assessment
specification it cites. `reading-reach` now measures the track — the chapter-40
passage's 36 words against a 1-word ceiling, **3/3 parts in reach**, and a floor
committed so the number cannot quietly fall. Before this the track was not in
the table at all: a passage could be written or deleted and the report would
have read identically.

**No `assessment.json` yet, on purpose.** The contract names its artifacts by
path and the artifact gate treats a path leading nowhere as a hard error. The
contract is written after the inventories, mocks, rubrics and answer keys.

**Why the pass rule is the project's own.** The University of Dhaka's Institute
of Modern Languages teaches fourteen languages in 120-hour courses, and Bangla
is **the one of the fourteen reserved for foreign students** — which makes it
the closest institution in the world to this book's purpose. It is measured in
course hours and publishes no per-skill examination structure, pass rule or
rubric. A certificate for completing 120 hours certifies study, not four skills
each reaching a standard. So there is no real rule here to depart from or to
match, and the inventory says so; an unstated absence would read as an oversight
rather than as a finding.

**What the writing paper scores** is the মাত্রা, the headline stroke. Bengali
letters hang from a horizontal line drawn across the top, and that line is what
a reader's eye follows to find where one word stops and the next starts — so a
hand that omits it or breaks it mid-word has removed the thing that groups the
letters, and the READING part scores it too, not only the writing parts. Beside
it: the **hasanta** ্, written 284 times in the corpus, which cancels the
inherent vowel and builds every conjunct; and **vowel-sign side**, since ি goes
to the left of its consonant while ী goes to the right.

**The writing ramp, measured.** Bengali has 74 script lessons and **not one
writing-stage directive** in any of them. Observe/trace, guided copy, delayed
copy and dictation are unproved — not partly proved, unproved — while the pre-A1
paper requires delayed recall and dictation. The spec names the gap, because the
alternative is a contract that reads as satisfied by seventy-four lessons that
do not stage anything.
