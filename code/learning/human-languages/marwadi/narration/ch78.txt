Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 78: Day, Night, Morning, Month, Year.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दिन (din) — a day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for brown, then the Marwadi for sweet.

You'll want to know: दिन.
दिन — din — "a day".

The word, taken apart.
दिन (din) is Sanskrit dina, "day". CDIAL lists Hindi and Gujarati din under it and thinks the Gujarati and Marathi forms were taken back from Sanskrit (CDIAL 6328); it gives no Marwari form.

रात (rāt) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from rātrī, "night". CDIAL lists Old Marwari rāti under it (CDIAL 10702).

Lesson 3 of 5.
सवेरो (savero) — morning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a day, then the Marwadi for night.

You'll want to know: सवेरो.
सवेरो — savero — "morning".

The word, taken apart.
सवेरो (savero) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from savela, "in time, early", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 13291); the match is through Hindi sawerā and Gujarati saverā. It is built on sa-, "with", and velā, "time".

Lesson 5 of 5.
बरस (baras) — a year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for morning, then the Marwadi for a month.

You'll want to know: बरस.
बरस — baras — "a year".

The word, taken apart.
बरस (baras) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from varṣa, "rain; year". CDIAL lists Old Marwari barasa under it (CDIAL 11392). The sense moved from "rain, the rainy season" to "year".
