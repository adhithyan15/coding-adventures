Latin, chapter 443: Sandbank, Billow, Shallows, Strait, To sail past.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
syrtis, syrtis — a sandbank, a shoal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of the sea, then the Latin for coastal.

You'll want to know: syrtis, syrtis.
syrtis, syrtis — "a sandbank, a shoal".
Gubernātor syrtēs vītat et nāvem in altum dūcit. The helmsman avoids the sandbanks and steers the ship into deep water.
Third declension, feminine: syrtis, syrtis f. Any sandbank, and above all the two dangerous sandy gulfs off the African coast.

The word, taken apart.
syrtis was borrowed from Greek syrtis 'sandbank' (Lewis & Short), above all the two gulfs off Africa full of shoals. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
flūctus, flūctūs — a billow, a wave; a surge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for coastal, then the Latin for a sandbank.

You'll want to know: flūctus, flūctūs.
flūctus, flūctūs — "a billow, a wave; a surge".
Magnī flūctūs in lītus cadunt. Great waves fall on the shore.
Fourth declension, masculine: flūctus, flūctūs m. A bigger word than unda: the wave as it rolls and breaks.

The word, taken apart.
flūctus is built on fluō 'to flow' with the noun suffix -tus (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a flowing, a surge of water.

Lesson 3 of 5.
vadum, vadī — a shallow, a shoal; a ford.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sandbank, then the Latin for a billow.

You'll want to know: vadum, vadī.
vadum, vadī — "a shallow, a shoal; a ford".
Equī flūmen per vadum trānseunt. The horses cross the river by the ford.
Second declension, neuter: vadum, vadī n. A place where water is shallow enough to walk through, or to run aground on.

The word, taken apart.
vadum is inherited from Proto-Italic waðom 'shallow, ford', from PIE wh₂dʰóm (EtymDB). Lewis & Short link it with vādō 'to go': a place where you can walk through water.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fretum, fretī — a strait, a narrow channel; (in poetry) the sea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a billow, then the Latin for a shallow.

You'll want to know: fretum, fretī.
fretum, fretī — "a strait, a narrow channel; (in poetry) the sea".
Fretum angustum est, sed flūctūs magnī sunt. The strait is narrow, but the waves are big.
Second declension, neuter: fretum, fretī n. A narrow sea between two coasts, where the water runs hard.

The word, taken apart.
fretum is inherited. Lewis & Short connect it with a root for restless, seething movement, seen in ferveō 'to boil', since water churns in a strait; EtymDB gives no parent, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 5 of 5.
praetervehor, praetervehī — to sail past, to ride past, to be carried past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a shallow, then the Latin for a strait.

You'll want to know: praetervehor, praetervehī.
praetervehor, praetervehī — "to sail past, to ride past, to be carried past".
Nāvis multa oppida praetervehitur. The ship sails past many towns.
Deponent in form: praetervehor, praetervehī, praetervectus sum, with an accusative of the place passed.

The word, taken apart.
praetervehor is built from praeter 'past, beyond' and vehor, the passive of vehō 'to carry' (Lewis & Short): to be carried past a place by ship or carriage.
