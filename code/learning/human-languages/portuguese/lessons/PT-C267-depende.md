# depende — the condition you do not have to finish

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-SE-PRESENTE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if it rains, I don't go out". (*Se chove, não saio.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That sentence names its condition. Sometimes you want the
condition without saying what it is.

## You'll want to know: depende
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-DEPENDE-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C234-VERBOS234-04, PT-LEX-C115-LAZER115-04] -->

**depende** — "it depends".

> — *Vais à festa amanhã?* — *Depende.*
> — Are you going to the party tomorrow? — It depends.

You have **depender**, "to depend". *Depende* is its "it" form, and on its
own it is a complete answer: it says a condition exists and declines to say
which. That is exactly why it is so useful for a question you are not ready to
answer.

## Grammar Lens: depende de
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-DEPENDE-DE-02]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-SE-01, PT-LEX-C53-COISAS2-03, PT-LEX-C92-VIAGEM92-05] -->

Add **de** and you can name the condition after all:

> **Depende de** ti. — It depends on you.
> **Depende do** comboio. — It depends on the train.
> **Depende do** dinheiro. — It depends on the money.

*De* and *o* weld into **do**, and *de* and *a* into **da**: *depende da
hora*. English says *depend **on***; Portuguese says *depende **de***, "hangs
**from**", which is the older picture.

Or name it with a whole *se*: *Depende. Se chove, não vou.* The *depende*
announces a condition; the *se* sentence states it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**depender** comes from Latin *dēpendēre*, "to hang from, to
hang down" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under *dependeo*): *dē*, "down from",
and *pendēre*, "to hang". To depend on something is to **hang from** it, and
the answer swings until the condition settles. Chapter nineteen hung **pensar**
from the same family, weighing by hanging; English **pendant** and **pending**
belong to it too.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-DEPENDE-01, PT-GRAMMAR-DEPENDE-DE-02, PT-LEX-C234-VERBOS234-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What does *depende* say, and what does it not say? (That there **is**
a condition — not **which**.) What do you add to name it? (***De***: *depende
de ti*.) What is hanging, in *dēpendēre*? (**The answer**, from the condition.)
