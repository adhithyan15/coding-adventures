Mandarin Chinese, chapter 298: A Fire.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
huǒzāi — big fire; a fire that destroys buildings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for what comes of it, then the Mandarin for to deal with a problem.

You'll want to know: huǒzāi.
huǒzāi — "big fire; a fire that destroys buildings".
huǒzāi de yuányīn shì shénme — What caused the fire?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huǒzāi is built from 火 'fire' and zāi 'a disaster', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwaX and tsoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the second as a spelling of the word for fire sent from heaven.

Lesson 2 of 6.
救火 (jiùhuǒ) — to fight a fire; to put out a fire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to deal with a problem, then the Mandarin for a fire that destroys buildings.

You'll want to know: 救火.
救火 — jiùhuǒ — "to fight a fire; to put out a fire".
大家都来救火了 dàjiā dōu lái jiùhuǒ le — Everyone came to fight the fire.
The new character, in pieces: 救 (jiù) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left seven strokes, a bar, a vertical with a hook, a dot and a rising stroke on its left, a short sweep and a dot on its right, and a dot at the top right; on the right the striking piece of 故, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
救火 (jiùhuǒ) is built from 救 (jiù) 'to save' and 火 'fire', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjuwH and xwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to protect' and 'to stop': to stop a fire.

Script — a hooked post with small strokes, then the striking piece.
救 (jiù).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, seven strokes. A bar. A vertical down through it that hooks to the left at the foot. A dot on its left, then a stroke rising to the right below the dot. A short sweep down to the left on its right, then a short stroke falling to the right under that. Last, a dot high on the right, above the end of the bar.
The right piece, four strokes. The striking piece of 故: a sweep to the left, a bar, a long sweep to the lower left and a long stroke falling to the right.
7 + 4 = eleven.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 救 (jiù), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 救 (jiù)? (Eleven.) Which stroke of the left piece comes last? (The dot high on the right.) Where else have you made the right piece? (In 故 and 做.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
xiāofángyuán — firefighter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a fire that destroys buildings, then the Mandarin for to put out a fire.

You'll want to know: xiāofángyuán.
xiāofángyuán — "firefighter".
xiāofángyuán hěn kuài jiù lái le — The firefighters were there in no time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiāofángyuán is built from xiāo 'to put out', fáng 'to guard against' and yuán 'a member', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sjew, bjang and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to put out', the second 'to keep off' and the third 'a count of things': one of those who put out fires.

hǎn — "to shout; to call out".
wǒ tīngdào yǒu rén zài hǎn — I heard someone shouting.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǎn is inherited from Middle Chinese xamX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'a sound'; the Guangyun also lists it under two other readings, heamX and xeamX, both glossed as a sound or a cry.
