Mandarin Chinese, chapter 75: Celebrating.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
快乐 (kuàilè) — joyful; happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for welcome, then the Mandarin for to introduce.

You'll want to know: 快乐.
快乐 — kuàilè — "joyful; happy".
我们很快乐 wǒmen hěn kuàilè — We're very happy.
The new character, in pieces: 乐 (lè) — a nearly flat fall, a turn, and then 小 with its middle hooked. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
快乐 (kuàilè) is built from 快 and 乐 (lè), both inherited from Middle Chinese, khwaejH and lak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as 'glad' and the second as 'joy': gladness twice over.

What you've built.
One more word for meeting people.

Lesson 2 of 6.
乐 (lè) — a fall, a turn, and 小.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a fall, a turn, and 小 and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say kuàilè — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
小 快.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xiǎo, kuài]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a fall, a turn, and 小.
乐 (lè).
One piece, five strokes.
A left-falling stroke across the top, from right to left, nearly flat.
A vertical that turns right, under the left end of the first stroke.
Then the three strokes of 小: the vertical down the middle that hooks to the left, a dot falling left, a dot falling right.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 乐 (lè), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 乐 (lè)? (Five.) Which character you know are its last three? (小.) Which way does the first stroke run? (From right to left, nearly flat.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 乐 (lè), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Lesson 4 of 6.
dàngāo — cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for joyful, then the Mandarin for birthday.

You'll want to know: dàngāo.
dàngāo — "cake".
māma de dàngāo hěn hǎochī — Mum's cake is delicious.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dàngāo is built from dàn 'egg', the dàn of jīdàn, and gāo 'cake'. No dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled; the second is probably the kaw that the Guangyun writes with another form of the character (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed with a word for gruel.
