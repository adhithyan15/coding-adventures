## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-APARCAMIENTO]; assesses=[] -->

**el aparcamiento** — "parking" or "the car park."

The pieces are transparent once you see them. **Aparcar** is "to park," and
**-miento** turns an action into its result or place. So *aparcamiento* can be
the act of parking or the place set aside for it.

The tempting origin story is wrong in a useful way. Spanish *aparcar* was not
lifted directly from English **park** when cars arrived. The Academy derives it
from Spanish **parque**. The verb first meant placing vehicles or equipment in
a designated park or camp; the modern car sense grew from that Spanish family.

You will also see **parking** on signs, and across the Americas
**estacionamiento**, **parqueadero**, and **parqueo** are common. This lesson
teaches *aparcamiento* because that is the exam's form, not because it is the
only Spanish word in use.
