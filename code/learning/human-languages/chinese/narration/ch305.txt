Mandarin Chinese, chapter 305: Getting Engaged.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tán liàn'ài — to go out with someone; to be in a relationship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for school tuition fees, then the Mandarin for to commit to memory.

You'll want to know: tán liàn'ài.
tán liàn'ài — "to go out with someone; to be in a relationship".
tāmen zài tán liàn'ài — They're going out together.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tán liàn'ài is built from tán 'to talk', liàn 'to long for' and ài 'love', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dam, ljwenH and 'ojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to talk', the second 'to long for' and the third 'to cherish': to talk love.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

The word, taken apart.
xīnláng is built from 新 'new' and láng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, sin and lang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the title of an official; its use for a young man is not given there.
