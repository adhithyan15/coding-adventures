Italian, chapter 479: Ridere di gusto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'allegria — cheerfulness, joy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for envy, then the Italian for to envy.

You'll want to know: l'allegria.
l'allegria — "cheerfulness, joy".
La musica porta allegria alla festa. Music brings cheer to the party.
Built on allegro "cheerful".

The word, taken apart.
allegria is built on allegro "cheerful", usually traced to Vulgar Latin alecrus, from Latin alacer "lively, quick" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: la risata.

Abbiamo fatto una bella risata. We had a good laugh.
Fare una risata — to have a laugh.

The word, taken apart.
risata is built on riso "laughter", which is inherited from Latin rīsus (Lewis & Short), from rīdēre "to laugh"; -ata makes one burst of it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'entusiasmo — enthusiasm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for laugh, then the Italian for to giggle.

You'll want to know: l'entusiasmo.
l'entusiasmo — "enthusiasm".
Ha cominciato il nuovo lavoro con entusiasmo. She started the new job with enthusiasm.
The adjective is entusiasta.

The word, taken apart.
entusiasmo is a learned borrowing, usually traced through Late Latin enthusiasmus to Greek enthousiasmós "inspiration by a god", from éntheos "possessed by a god" (EtymDB, under French enthousiasme).

Lesson 5 of 5.
commuoversi — to be moved, to be touched.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to giggle, then the Italian for enthusiasm.

You'll want to know: commuoversi.
commuoversi — "to be moved, to be touched".
Mi commuovo sempre ai matrimoni degli amici. I always get emotional at my friends' weddings.
The past participle is commosso: ero commosso — I was moved.

The word, taken apart.
commuoversi is the reflexive of commuovere "to move, to touch", inherited from Latin commovēre "to stir up" (Lewis & Short), from movēre "to move".
