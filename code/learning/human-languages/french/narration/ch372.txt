French, chapter 372: Prendre un risque.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le risque — the risk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for final, then the French for to schedule.

You'll want to know: le risque.
le risque — "the risk".
Il y a un risque de pluie demain. There is a chance of rain tomorrow.
Prendre un risque — to take a risk; the verb is risquer.

The word, taken apart.
risque comes from Old Italian risco "danger" (EtymDB). The further origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hasardeux — risky, chancy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to schedule, then the French for the risk.

You'll want to know: hasardeux.
hasardeux — "risky, chancy".
C'est un projet hasardeux, mais il veut essayer. It is a risky project, but he wants to try.
Feminine hasardeuse. Formal; in speech, risqué.

The word, taken apart.
hasardeux is built from an old French word for chance and the suffix -eux. That word is usually traced, through Spanish, to Arabic az-zahr "the die"; the details are not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
improbable — improbable, unlikely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the risk, then the French for risky.

You'll want to know: improbable.
improbable — "improbable, unlikely".
Il est improbable qu'il vienne ce soir. It is unlikely that he will come tonight.
Il est improbable que takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
improbable is a learned borrowing from Latin improbābilis "not to be approved" (Lewis & Short), built from in- "not" and probābilis, from probāre "to test".

Lesson 5 of 5.
quitte à — even if it means.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for improbable, then the French for the probability.

You'll want to know: quitte à.
quitte à — "even if it means".
Je pars tôt, quitte à attendre une heure sur le quai. I am leaving early, even if it means waiting an hour on the platform.
infinitive: you accept the cost. Être quitte — to be even, owe nothing.

The word, taken apart.
quitte comes from Old French quitte "free, released", taken from Latin quiētus "at rest" (EtymDB), from quiēs "rest" (Lewis & Short); a Medieval Latin form quittus is usually placed in between.
