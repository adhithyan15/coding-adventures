Mandarin Chinese, chapter 529: Good Luck, Bad Luck.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
dǎoméi — to be out of luck; to have bad luck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to make a bet, then the Mandarin for happening by chance.

You'll want to know: dǎoméi.
dǎoméi — "to be out of luck; to have bad luck".
jīntiān zhēn dǎoméi wǒ de qiánbāo bú jiàn le — What bad luck today: my wallet's gone.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎoméi is built from dǎo 'to fall over' and méi 'mould'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to fall flat'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
运气 (yùnqi) — a person's luck (good or bad).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for happening by chance, then the Mandarin for to be out of luck.

You'll want to know: 运气.
运气 — yùnqi — "a person's luck (good or bad)".
运气好的话我们就能买到票 yùnqi hǎo dehuà wǒmen jiù néng mǎi dào piào — With luck, we'll be able to get tickets.
The new character, in pieces: 运 (yùn) — seven strokes in two pieces: 云 first, four strokes; then the walking piece, three strokes, as in 这, along the left and the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
运气 (yùnqi) is built from 运 (yùn) and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjunH and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'far', 'to move' and 'to carry'. The sense 'luck' is not given there; the second is said light.

Script — 云 first, then the walking piece.
运 (yùn).
Two pieces, and the walking piece comes last, though it runs up the left and along the foot. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The inside piece, 云, four strokes, as you wrote it: a short bar, a longer bar, a stroke that falls to the left and turns right along the bottom, then a dot.
The walking piece, three strokes, as in 这: a dot, a stroke that zigzags down, then a long sweep along the foot to the right.
4 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
走运 (zǒuyùn) — to be in luck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be out of luck, then the Mandarin for a person's luck.

You'll want to know: 走运.
走运 — zǒuyùn — "to be in luck".
如果你走运就能找到好工作 rúguǒ nǐ zǒuyùn jiù néng zhǎo dào hǎo gōngzuò — If you're in luck, you'll find a good job.

The word, taken apart.
走运 (zǒuyùn) is built from 走 'to go' and 运 (yùn) 'luck', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsuwX and hjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hurry'; the sense 'luck' of the second is not given there.

You'll want to know: xìngyùn.
xìngyùn — "lucky; fortunate".
wǒ hěn xìngyùn bùrán wǒ jiù chídào le — I was lucky, or I'd have been late.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xìngyùn is built from xìng and 运 (yùn) 'luck', both inherited from Middle Chinese, heangX and hjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first, after an older book, as having good fortune and escaping harm.
