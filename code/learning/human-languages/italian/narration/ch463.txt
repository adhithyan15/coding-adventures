Italian, chapter 463: Sugli scogli.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lo scoglio — the rock (in the sea).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the cave, then the Italian for to climb.

You'll want to know: lo scoglio.
lo scoglio — "the rock (in the sea)".
Prendiamo il sole sugli scogli. We sunbathe on the rocks.
Figuratively, an obstacle.

The word, taken apart.
scoglio is inherited from Vulgar Latin scoclus, from Latin scopulus 'rock, crag' (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Italian for to climb, then the Italian for the rock.

You'll want to know: abbronzarsi.
abbronzarsi — "to get a tan".
Mi abbronzo subito al mare. I tan quickly at the seaside.
L'abbronzatura — the tan.

The word, taken apart.
abbronzarsi is built from a- and bronzo 'bronze': turning the colour of bronze. The origin of bronzo is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la conchiglia.
la conchiglia — "the seashell".
I bambini raccolgono conchiglie sulla spiaggia. The children pick up shells on the beach.
Le conchiglie are also a pasta shape.

The word, taken apart.
conchiglia is inherited from Latin conchylium 'shellfish' (Lewis & Short), from Greek konkhúlion, a little kónkhē 'mussel'.

Con l'alta marea la spiaggia sparisce. At high tide the beach disappears.
L'alta marea, la bassa marea; also a flood of people: una marea di gente.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tuffarsi — to dive, to plunge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the seashell, then the Italian for the tide.

You'll want to know: tuffarsi.
tuffarsi — "to dive, to plunge".
Ci tuffiamo dallo scoglio più alto. We dive off the highest rock.
Tuffarsi in un libro — to lose yourself in a book.

The word, taken apart.
tuffarsi is the reflexive of tuffare 'to plunge', which EtymDB traces to Lombardic taufan 'to dip, to immerse', a Germanic word borrowed into Italian.
