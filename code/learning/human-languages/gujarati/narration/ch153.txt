Gujarati, chapter 153: Rooms, roof and veranda.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઓરડો (orḍo) — room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to believe, then the Gujarati for to change.

You'll want to know: ઓરડો.
ઓરડો — orḍo — "room".
આ મારો ઓરડો છે. — ā māro orḍo che — "This is my room."

The word, taken apart.
ઓરડો (orḍo) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from apavaraka, "inner apartment", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "principal room" (CDIAL 450).

Lesson 2 of 5.
રસોડું (rasoḍũ) — kitchen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to change, then the Gujarati for room.

You'll want to know: રસોડું.
રસોડું — rasoḍũ — "kitchen".
માતા રસોડામાં રાંધે છે. — mātā rasoḍāmā̃ rā̃dhe che — "Mother cooks in the kitchen."

The word, taken apart.
રસોડું (rasoḍũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from rasavāṭa, "cooking enclosure", a reconstructed form built from rasa, "juice, flavour", and vāṭa, "enclosure"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "kitchen" (CDIAL 10658).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ઓસરી (osrī) — veranda.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for kitchen, then the Gujarati for terrace.

You'll want to know: ઓસરી.
ઓસરી — osrī — "veranda".
દાદા ઓસરીમાં બેસે છે. — dādā osrīmā̃ bese che — "Grandfather sits on the veranda."

The word, taken apart.
ઓસરી (osrī) is inherited, but its source is not settled. CDIAL glosses it "front portion of a house" and lists it twice: under avaśara, "protection, shelter", and under Sanskrit upasara, "approach" (CDIAL 848, 2258).

Lesson 5 of 5.
દાદર (dādar) — stairs, staircase.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for terrace, then the Gujarati for veranda.

You'll want to know: દાદર.
દાદર — dādar — "stairs, staircase".
તે દાદર ચઢે છે. — te dādar caḍhe che — "He climbs the stairs."

The word, taken apart.
દાદર (dādar) is counted as inherited. CDIAL lists Gujarati dādar, "ladder", under Sanskrit dardara, "split, broken", without saying how the sense moved (CDIAL 6196).
