German, chapter 432: Geld zurück.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Geschäftsführer — the manager, the managing director.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to grumble, then the German for dissatisfied.

You'll want to know: der Geschäftsführer.
der Geschäftsführer — "the manager, the managing director".
Der Gast möchte mit dem Geschäftsführer sprechen. The guest would like to speak to the manager.
Same form in the plural. A woman: die Geschäftsführerin.

The word, taken apart.
Geschäftsführer is built from Geschäft 'business', inherited from Old High German giscaft (EtymDB), and Führer 'leader', from führen 'to lead'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: die Unannehmlichkeit.
die Unannehmlichkeit — "the inconvenience, the trouble".
Wir entschuldigen uns für die Unannehmlichkeiten. We apologise for the inconvenience.
Mostly plural: die Unannehmlichkeiten, in a polite set phrase.

The word, taken apart.
Unannehmlichkeit is built from un-, annehmlich 'agreeable', from annehmen 'to accept', and -keit; nehmen is inherited from Proto-Germanic nemaną.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the manager, then the German for the inconvenience.

You'll want to know: die Anzahlung.
die Anzahlung — "the deposit, the down payment".
Für das Zimmer zahlen wir eine Anzahlung von hundert Euro. We pay a deposit of a hundred euros for the room.
Plural: die Anzahlungen. The rest is paid later.

The word, taken apart.
Anzahlung is built from anzahlen 'to pay part of', from zahlen 'to pay', which comes from Zahl 'number', inherited from Old High German zala (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
entschädigen — to compensate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the inconvenience, then the German for the deposit.

You'll want to know: entschädigen.
entschädigen — "to compensate".
Das Hotel entschädigt uns mit einer Nacht gratis. The hotel compensates us with a free night.
Jemanden für etwas entschädigen — to compensate someone for something.

The word, taken apart.
entschädigen is built from ent- 'away' and Schaden 'damage', an inherited Germanic word (EtymDB lists both): to take the damage away.
