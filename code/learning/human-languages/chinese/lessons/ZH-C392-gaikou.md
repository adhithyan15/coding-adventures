## You'll want to know: 改口
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C392-NEWPLAN392-05]; assesses=[] -->

**改口** — *gǎikǒu* — "to change one's tune; to take back what one said".

> *tā zuótiān shuō bú qù jīntiān yòu gǎikǒu le* — Yesterday he said he wasn't going; today he's changed his tune.

Two third tones meet in this word: gǎi is said with a rising tone before kǒu, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**改口** (*gǎikǒu*) is built from 改 'to alter' and 口 'the mouth', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kojX* and *khuwX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'what a person speaks and eats with': altering what comes out of the mouth.
