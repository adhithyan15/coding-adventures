Hindi, chapter 523: Peel, Pound, Strain.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चाकू (cākū) — a knife, a pocket knife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to seethe, then the Hindi for to drain off (liquid).

You'll want to know: चाकू.
चाकू — cākū — "a knife, a pocket knife".
सब्ज़ी काटने के लिए एक तेज़ चाकू दीजिए। Sabzī kāṭne ke liye ek tez cākū dījiye. Please give me a sharp knife to cut the vegetables.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
चाकू (cākū) is usually traced to Persian chāqū, "a knife".

Lesson 2 of 5.
छीलना (chīlnā) — to peel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to drain off (liquid), then the Hindi for a pocket knife.

You'll want to know: छीलना.
छीलना — chīlnā — "to peel".
मीरा आलू छील रही है। Mīrā ālū chīl rahī hai. Mira is peeling potatoes.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने केला छीला, usne kelā chīlā.

The word, taken apart.
छीलना (chīlnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from chilla, "torn, cut", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5051). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to peel".

The word, taken apart.
दलना (dalnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit dalati, "bursts, splits" (CDIAL 6216). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to grind coarsely".
