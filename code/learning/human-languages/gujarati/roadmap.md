# Gujarati Roadmap

This roadmap distinguishes what the book actually teaches from the path to a
book-only pre-A1-through-C2 course. The framework is [HL00](../../../specs/HL00-human-language-curriculum-framework.md),
the exact authored order is the [session map](session-map.md), and the exam
destination is defined by [assessment-spec.md](assessment-spec.md) and
[assessment.json](assessment.json).

Every lesson remains five minutes or less. Writing, listening, speaking,
reading, grammar, vocabulary, retrieval, and assessment grow together in small
steps; later-level labels are not readiness evidence by themselves.

## Authored now

- **Chapters 1-2:** meaning-first greeting, one-form-at-a-time script runway,
  courtesy, yes/no, and a first independent answer written from sound.
- **Chapters 3-7:** all remaining Gujarati forms shown by the current book are
  explicitly taught before later first use.
- **Chapters 8-10:** introductions, wellbeing, and farewells.
- **Chapters 11-18:** first verbs, numbers one to five, thought and literacy,
  requests, food and drink, family, and body words.
- **Chapters 19-23:** real R3/R4 retrieval for early forms and conversations.
- **Chapters 24-27:** a first concrete place/hand/money/map vocabulary runway.
- **Chapter 28:** eight everyday time words acquired ear-first, with only two of
  them written, plus a distant return of the map words.
- **Chapter 29:** the six time words chapter 28 left oral reach the page, each
  spelled entirely from signs taught before the first name exchange.
- **Chapter 30:** the fifth-return slab. Nine zero-new-atom lessons return the
  first five numbers and the fifteen core verbs at R4 distance.
- **Chapters 31-34:** twenty more ear-first headwords — food, the house, the sky
  and the river, and people — at one new word per lesson, one written word per
  chapter, and a named distant band inside every return.

The authored boundary is **34 chapters / 228 lessons**. It has zero duration
violations, zero never-taught Gujarati glyphs, zero script-closure findings, and
zero headwords without a romanization, but it remains pre-A1 work in progress.
The current gentle-ramp snapshot records 283 missed retrieval windows (down from
339 on a track half again as long), one atom never revisited, three atom spikes,
and four measurement-blind lessons.

## Dependency-ordered backlog

### 1. Stabilize the measured foundation

1. Service the earliest R1-R4 misses without creating chapter atom spikes.
2. Revisit the six orphan atoms and migrate the measurement-blind lesson.
3. Split the two lesson spikes and one chapter spike into smaller lessons.
4. ~~Service the core-verb chapters (13-16) at R4.~~ **Done** in chapter 30:
   nine zero-new-atom lessons place the numbers and the fifteen core verbs 98 to
   104 positions after their introductions. R4 misses fell 101 to 43. What is
   left at R4 is the opening greeting-and-courtesy material and the twenty-odd
   script forms of chapters 1-7, which needs its own slab.

These items unblock trustworthy expansion because new chapters should not pile
onto known continuity debt.

### 2. Complete pre-A1 instruction and assessment

1. Grow high-frequency concrete vocabulary in tiny listening-first runways.
2. Add location, time, possession, simple requests, repair, negation, and basic
   question patterns with explicit Gujarati agreement and word order.
   **Repair, negation, joining and the question family are done** — chapters
   35-41 (HL-C316) take the A1 joining column from 0 of 11 to 10 and add the
   apology the book had no word for. Location, time and possession remain.
3. Expand handwriting from single forms and short words to short dictated
   messages, while continuing guided copy, delayed copy, and free recall.
4. Add connected mini-dialogues, short readings, speaker audio, and cumulative
   mixed-skill reviews.
5. Build both timed pre-A1 mocks, answer keys, rubrics, examiner prompts, and
   calibration samples; then run a book-only learner validation.

### 3. Build A1 and A2

For each rung, first create the machine-readable task inventory, then author the
curriculum required to pass it, and only then publish two complete timed mocks.
Cover everyday transactions, family and work, directions and travel, past and
future reference, paragraph reading, connected listening, short correspondence,
and increasingly independent handwriting. Require separate reading, listening,
writing, and speaking passes.

### 4. Build B1 and B2

Extend into sustained narration, reasons and opinions, reported information,
condition, longer travel and service encounters, multi-paragraph writing,
authentic-speed listening, extended prose, argument, register choice, and
regional variation. Preserve short lesson size by decomposing long exam tasks
into micro-skills, then recombining them only in cumulative practice and mocks.

### 5. Build C1 and C2

Teach implicit meaning, abstract discussion, source synthesis, precise register
shifts, extended structured writing, literary and classical reading, cultural
weight, fine semantic shades, and robust comprehension across regional voices.
Finish both level inventories and two timed mocks per level, then obtain expert
review and book-only learner pass evidence for all four skills.

### 6. Release readiness

At every milestone, regenerate canonical artifacts, narration, progress, and the
book; run the full corpus and gentle-ramp gates; compile the PDF in strict mode;
inspect rendered Gujarati pages; and record independent language-expert review.
The track is complete only when a learner who used the whole book can pass the
project-defined C2 examination, not when every level merely has content.
