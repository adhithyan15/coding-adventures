French, chapter 392: À la banque.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
les espèces — the cash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the investment, then the French for the rate.

You'll want to know: les espèces.
les espèces — "the cash".
Ce petit café n'accepte que les espèces. This small café only takes cash.
Payer en espèces — to pay cash. Québec: payer comptant. In the singular, une espèce is a species or a kind.

The word, taken apart.
espèces is the plural of espèce "kind", from Latin speciēs "appearance, kind" (Lewis & Short), from specere "to look": money in a visible form.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: le chéquier.
le chéquier — "the chequebook".
La banque m'a envoyé un nouveau chéquier. The bank sent me a new chequebook.
From chèque. Fewer and fewer people use one.

The word, taken apart.
chéquier is built from chèque and the suffix -ier. chèque is usually traced to English cheque, check, linked to check "a control".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le virement — the bank transfer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cheque, then the French for the chequebook.

You'll want to know: le virement.
le virement — "the bank transfer".
J'ai fait un virement pour payer le loyer. I made a transfer to pay the rent.
Un virement bancaire — a bank transfer.

The word, taken apart.
virement is built from virer "to turn, to transfer" and -ment (EtymDB). The origin of virer is usually traced to a Latin form linked to vibrāre "to shake"; it is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
retirer — to take out, to withdraw.

You'll want to know: retirer.
retirer — "to take out, to withdraw".
J'ai retiré cinquante euros au distributeur. I took fifty euros out of the cash machine.
Also to take off: retirer son manteau.

The word, taken apart.
retirer is built from re- "back" and tirer "to pull" (EtymDB). The origin of tirer is not settled.
