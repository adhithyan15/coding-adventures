# adiós — "to God," the goodbye that's also a blessing

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You learned that *buenos días* hides a blessing to God. *Adiós*
hides the same thing in plain sight — it literally *says* God. Once you see
it, you can't unsee it, and it unlocks a French word too.

## You'll want to know first

- [ES-C01-L02 (buenos días)](./ES-C01-L02-buenos-dias.md) — the "hidden
  blessing to God" pattern; *adiós* is the same idea, undisguised.

## Sounds you'll need

- `accent-mark` — the written accent on **ó** forces the stress onto the last
  syllable: *a-DYOS*. (Without it, the default rule would stress *a-*.)
- The **d** between vowels is soft, toward English *th*: *a-THYOS*-ish.

## The word, taken apart

**adiós** is two pieces fused: **a** + **Dios**.

**a** — to, toward. Root: Latin **ad** ("to, toward"). This *ad-* is one of
the most productive prefixes in English — it means "toward" and it's glued
onto hundreds of words: **ad**vance, **ad**here, **ad**apt, **ad**mire. (It
often disguises itself by absorbing into the next letter — *ad* + *cuire* →
*ac*quire, *ad* + *tract* → *at*tract — a shape-shift worth watching for.)

**Dios** — God. Root: Latin **Deus** ("god"). The family:

- **deit**y, **div**ine, **deif**y — all the "god" words.
- and the goodbye itself: French **adieu** is the *exact same construction* —
  *à* ("to") + *Dieu* ("God"). Spanish *adiós*, French *adieu*, Italian
  *addio* — three languages, one identical little prayer.

So **adiós = "to God."**

## Why it's said this way

This is a fossil like *buenos días*, but you can still see the whole skeleton.
*Adiós* is shortened from **"a Dios seas"** / **"a Dios vayas"** — *"may you
go to God,"* i.e. "I commend you to God's care until we meet again." It was a
real blessing for someone leaving on a journey (when travel was dangerous and
a goodbye might be the last).

That's why *adiós* carries a faint weight of finality — traditionally it
suited longer partings. For "see you later," everyday Spanish leans on
lighter words like *hasta luego* ("until later") or *chao*. But every *adiós*
still quietly means "go with God."

> The "blessing" origin is well-established here; the exact elided phrase
> (*seas* vs. *vayas*) varied by region and era.
