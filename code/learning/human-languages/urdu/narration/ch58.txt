Urdu, chapter 58: Window, Chair, Wall, Roof, Bed.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
کھڑکی (khiṛkī) — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for coffee, then the Urdu for a house.

You'll want to know: کھڑکی.
کھڑکی — khiṛkī — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
کھڑکی (khiṛkī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from khaṭakkikā, "side door" (CDIAL 3770). The Sanskrit word is known only from the old word-lists.

Lesson 2 of 5.
کرسی (kursī) — a chair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a house, then the Urdu for a window.

You'll want to know: کرسی.
کرسی — kursī — "a chair".

The word, taken apart.
کرسی (kursī) is a loan from Arabic kursī, "chair, throne", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is k-r-s (Lane). Arabic itself most likely took it from Aramaic.

Lesson 4 of 5.
چھت (chhat) — a roof; a ceiling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a chair, then the Urdu for a wall.

You'll want to know: چھت.
چھت — chhat — "a roof; a ceiling".

The word, taken apart.
چھت (chhat) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from chatti, "covering", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4971). It is built on chad-, "to cover", the root of chattra, "parasol".
