# स्वागतम् (svāgatam) — "welcome"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] One word that is *cognate* with English "welcome" — not just a
translation of it.

## The letters in this word

New: the conjunct **स्व** (**स्** + **व** va) = *sva*, **ग** ga, and the *halant*
**म्** m closing the word with no vowel. Read **स्वा·ग·तम्**, *svā-ga-tam*.

## The word, taken apart

**स्वागतम्** = *su* ("well, good") + *āgata* ("come," from *ā* "toward" + the root
√gam "to go/come") — "**well come**," which is exactly what English *welcome*
means too. Two deep Indo-European threads surface:
- *su-* "good" is the twin of **Greek *eu-*** — the *eu* of *euphoria*, *eulogy*,
  *euphemism*. Sanskrit *su-* and Greek *eu-* are the same prefix.
- the root √gam "to go/come" is PIE *gʷem-* — the very root of English **come**
  (and of Latin *venīre*, hence *venue*, *convene*).

So *svāgatam* and *welcome* are not just parallel ideas but partly **cognate**
ones.

## Why it's said this way

*Su* + *āgata* fuses into *svāgata* by **sandhi** — Sanskrit's rule that *u*
before a vowel becomes the glide *v*. You will see sandhi everywhere; it is why
Sanskrit words seem to melt into one another. The neuter ending *-am* makes it
"a welcome / it is welcomed."
