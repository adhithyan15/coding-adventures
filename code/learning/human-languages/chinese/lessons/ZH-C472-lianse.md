## You'll want to know: 脸色
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C472-FACE472-02]; assesses=[] -->

**脸色** — *liǎnsè* — "complexion; the colour of one's face".

> **你脸色不好是不是病了** *nǐ liǎnsè bù hǎo shì bu shì bìng le* — You don't look well. Are you ill?

The new character, in pieces: 脸 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left 月 *yuè*, four strokes; on the right seven, as on the right of 检 *jiǎn*: a long stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right, a short bar, two dots, a short stroke falling to the left and a long bar. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**脸色** (*liǎnsè*) is built from 脸 'face' and 色 'colour', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *leamX* and *srik* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under two readings, *tshjem* and *leamX*, and glosses *leamX* as a kind of stew; neither gloss is the face, so how it came to mean face is not settled. It glosses the second 'colour of the face'.
