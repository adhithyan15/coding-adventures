Japanese, chapter 272: A Trick over the Phone.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
さぎ (sagi) — a fraud; a swindle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hide (oneself), then the Japanese for to catch.

You'll want to know: さぎ.
さぎ — sagi — "a fraud; a swindle".
年じゅう さぎの でんわが あります。 — nenjū sagi no denwa ga arimasu — "Swindlers phone all year round."

The word, taken apart.
さぎ (sagi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sa, "deceive" (Middle Chinese tsraeH) and gi, "cheat" (khi) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
だます (damasu) — to trick; to deceive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to catch, then the Japanese for a fraud.

You'll want to know: だます.
だます — damasu — "to trick; to deceive".
その おとこは ははを だましました。 — sono otoko wa haha o damashimashita — "That man tricked my mother."
Polite だまします, damashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おとこが ちちを だましました。, otoko ga chichi o damashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
だます (damasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

はんにんは おかねを やまに かくしました。 — hannin wa okane o yama ni kakushimashita — "The culprit hid the money in the hills."
Polite かくします, kakushimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): どろぼうが おかねを かくしました。, dorobō ga okane o kakushimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かくす (kakusu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the transitive partner of kakureru, "to hide oneself".

You'll want to know: ほんとう.
ほんとう — hontō — "true; the truth".
それは ほんとうですか。 — sore wa hontō desu ka — "Is that really so?"
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ほんとう (hontō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hon, "true" (Middle Chinese pwonX) and tō, "right" (tang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
