# el nivel — the level

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CURSO, ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el curso** and **demasiado**. A class has one of these, and
when it is wrong the second word is how you say so.

## You'll want to know: el nivel
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01] -->

**el nivel** — the level.

> El **nivel** era demasiado alto para ella.

*Nivel* is what a class, a language exam or a game has. *¿Qué nivel tienes?*
is the ordinary way to ask how far along somebody is. Note the stress: *ni-VEL*,
on the end, and the plural is **los niveles**.

It is also a level in the physical sense — a floor of a building, the surface
of water, the tool a builder uses.

## Grammar Lens: the phrase the question will use
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-02, ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01] -->

Two ways of saying the same complaint, and you need to recognise both.

| about the class | about its level |
|---|---|
| el curso era **demasiado avanzado** | el **nivel** era **demasiado alto** |

A speaker will usually say the first. Somebody reporting it afterwards — a
summary, a question, an exam option — will often reach for the second, because
*nivel* lets you talk about the difficulty without naming the class again.

*Alto* and *bajo* are what *nivel* takes, not *grande* or *mucho*. A level is
a **height**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-02, ES-LEX-C359-COUNT-19] -->

[El litro](./ES-C359-litro.md) already told you about Latin **libra**, the
balance — hiding in *lb*, in **Libra** the scales in the sky, in
**deliberate** and **equilibrium**.

*Nivel* is the same word, made small. Latin **libella** is a *little libra*, a
builder's level: a tiny balance that tells you when a surface is true. It
reached Spanish through French, and on the way the first **l** turned into an
**n** — *livel* became *nivel* — because two *l* sounds so close together pull
apart.

English took *libella* too, without that swap, and got **level**.

So *nivel* and *litro* are cousins, and a level is not a measure of height so
much as an instrument for finding **balance**.
