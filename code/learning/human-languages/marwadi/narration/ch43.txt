Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 43: The Letter That Ends Ram.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
म (ma) — the letter that ends राम.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: म and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write अ and उ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say राम (rām), "Ram". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: म.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
म — ma, the sound m.
It ends राम (rām), the name in the greeting राम-राम सा, the first words in this book. The first chapter built राम from र, ा and म (ma), and gave र and ा lessons of their own. म (ma) gets its own lesson now.

Writing: म.
1. start here: descend the left stem, curl left and clockwise around the lower loop, and continue right through the crossbar without lifting.
2. without lifting, climb up the right stem to the headline.
3. descend the right stem top-to-bottom.
4. lift, then draw the shirorekha left-to-right — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
The source shows three movements; most native writers draw म (ma) in two strokes (80% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples). This path keeps every movement in order and direction, and lifts only before the headline.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: JackPotte, ‘Devanagari m म (ma).gif’, strokes 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 29 March 2009.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at राम and point at म (ma) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: म (ma) three times, saying m as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: उ, then म (ma), from memory]
[once you have stopped driving — read: राम aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — म? (ma, m.) Which word did it come from? (राम, rām, "Ram".)
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write the letter you took out of rām (Ram).


Lesson 2 of 3.
(one letter) (ek akhar) — From the letter to the word — a first pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 3 seconds]
Say राम (rām), then write the letter it gave you.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: म. name: ma. the word it came from: राम rām.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter in the table from memory, then check it]
[your turn — say: the word each one came from]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: each word in the table aloud, and point at its letter]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word did each letter come from? (म (ma) from राम.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Write the letter from each word in the table, in the table's order.


Lesson 3 of 3.
(one letter) (ek akhar) — From the word back to the letter — a second pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 3 seconds]
Write म (ma) without looking, then say the word it came from.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: म. name: ma. the word it came from: राम rām.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: each word in the table, then write the letter it gave you without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter once more, slowly, larger than it is printed]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say each word in the table once more, and name the letter you took from it.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Write the letter from each word in the table, in the table's order.
