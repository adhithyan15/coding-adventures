## You'll want to know: subiungō, subiungere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C430-TEMO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**subiungō, subiungere** — "to yoke, to harness; to put under".

> Agricola mūlōs plaustrō subiungit.
>
> *The farmer harnesses the mules to the wagon.*

*subiungō, subiungere, subiūnxī, subiūnctum*, often with a dative: *equōs currū subiungere*. English *subjunctive* comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*subiungō* is built from *sub-* 'under' and *iungō* 'to join, to yoke' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *subjunctive* comes from its participle *subiūnctus*.
