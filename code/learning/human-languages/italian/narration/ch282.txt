Italian, chapter 282: Il giornale del mattino.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la cronaca — the news pages, the local news; the chronicle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for last night, then the Italian for the whole morning.

You'll want to know: la cronaca.
la cronaca — "the news pages, the local news; the chronicle".
Leggo sempre la cronaca della mia città. I always read the local news for my town.
La cronaca nera is crime news; la cronaca rosa is celebrity gossip.

The word, taken apart.
cronaca is inherited from Latin chronica "a chronicle" (EtymDB), from Greek khronikós "of time", built on khrónos "time".

Lesson 2 of 5.
il quotidiano — the daily newspaper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the whole morning, then the Italian for the news pages.

You'll want to know: il quotidiano.
il quotidiano — "the daily newspaper".
Compro un quotidiano ogni mattina. I buy a daily paper every morning.
As an adjective, quotidiano means daily, everyday: la vita quotidiana.

The word, taken apart.
quotidiano is a learned borrowing from Latin quotīdiānus "daily" (EtymDB), built on quot "as many as" and diēs "day".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il titolo — the headline; the title.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the daily newspaper, then the Italian for the weekly.

You'll want to know: il titolo.
il titolo — "the headline; the title".
Il titolo in prima pagina è enorme. The headline on the front page is huge.
In a paper i titoli are the headlines; in a book, il titolo is the title.

The word, taken apart.
titolo is inherited from Latin titulus "an inscription, a label, a title" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'articolo — the article (in a paper).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the weekly, then the Italian for the headline.

You'll want to know: l'articolo.
l'articolo — "the article (in a paper)".
Ho letto un articolo interessante sul treno. I read an interesting article on the train.
In grammar, l'articolo is the article too: il, la, un.

The word, taken apart.
articolo is a learned borrowing from Latin articulus "a small joint, a part" (Lewis & Short), a little artus "joint".
