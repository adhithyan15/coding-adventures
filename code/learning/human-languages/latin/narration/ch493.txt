Latin, chapter 493: Anger and indignation.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
īrāscor, īrāscī — to grow angry, be angry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for gentle, then the Latin for to soothe.

You'll want to know: īrāscor, īrāscī.
īrāscor, īrāscī — "to grow angry, be angry".
Dominus servō īrāscitur, quod cēnam nōn parāvit. The master grows angry with the slave because he has not prepared dinner.
Deponent: īrāscor, īrāscī, īrātus sum, with the dative of the person. Īrātus, already known, is its participle.

The word, taken apart.
īrāscor is built on īra "anger" with -scor, an ending for a state that begins (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English irascible comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
īrācundus, īrācunda, īrācundum — hot-tempered, quick to anger.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to soothe, then the Latin for to grow angry.

You'll want to know: īrācundus, īrācunda, īrācundum.
īrācundus, īrācunda, īrācundum — "hot-tempered, quick to anger".
Senex īrācundus semper clāmat. The hot-tempered old man is always shouting.
Built on īra — anger. A trait of character, where īrātus is a passing mood.

The word, taken apart.
īrācundus is built on īra "anger" with the adjective ending -cundus, as in verēcundus (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
indignus, indigna, indignum — unworthy, undeserving; shameful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for hot-tempered, then the Latin for a hot temper.

You'll want to know: indignus, indigna, indignum.
indignus, indigna, indignum — "unworthy, undeserving; shameful".
Hoc verbum tē indignum est. This word is unworthy of you.
With the ablative: indignus honōre — unworthy of honour. The opposite of dignus.

The word, taken apart.
indignus is built on in- "not" and dignus "worthy" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB traces dignus to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to take, accept"). English indignity comes from its noun indignitās.
