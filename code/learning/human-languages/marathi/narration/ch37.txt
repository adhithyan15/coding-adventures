Marathi, chapter 37: Who, and How Many.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
कोण (koṇ) — who.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You own one asking word: काय (kāy), what. It asks about a thing. Nothing you own yet asks about a person.

You'll want to know: कोण.
कोण — koṇ — who.
तो कोण आहे? — to koṇ āhe? — who is that?
कोण (koṇ) asks for a person the way काय asks for a thing. Both begin with क, and that is not luck: Marathi builds its asking words on one inherited stem. काय, कसा, कोण (koṇ) — the क- family. Meet a new word starting with क in a question and you can guess its job before you know its meaning.
कोण (koṇ) comes down from Sanskrit कः (kaḥ, "who"), by way of the older Marathi कवण. English who and Latin quis grew from the same Indo-European stem, which is why the family feels oddly familiar.
Point it at somebody you have already named: मीरा कोण आहे? — Mīrā koṇ āhe? — who is Mira?

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: koṇ — who]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mitra koṇ āhe? — who is the friend?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which letter every Marathi asking word so far begins with — क]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Ask who your friend is.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word asks for a thing, and which for a person? (काय; कोण (koṇ).) What do they share? (The क- stem.)
Sources: Wiktionary: कोण (koṇ).


Lesson 2 of 3.
किती (kitī) — how many.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can count एक दोन तीन चार पाच. You met कोण (koṇ) one lesson ago. Neither of them lets you ask somebody for a number.

You'll want to know: किती.
किती — kitī — how many, how much.
तुम्हाला किती भाऊ आहेत? — tumhālā kitī bhāū āhet? — how many brothers do you have?
Another क- word, and the answer to it is always a number. That is what makes it worth spending a lesson on so early: five numbers with no question to trigger them are five words nobody asks you for.
किती (kitī) is Sanskrit कति (kati, "how many") barely changed. Latin quot and English how many carry the same ancient stem.
It also does duty for how much of something uncountable — Marathi does not split the two the way English does.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kitī — how many]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī bhāū? — how many brothers?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say dusrā — the other one — then answer kitī? with don]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Ask how many sisters, then answer with two.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What kind of answer does किती (kitī) always want? (A number.) And कोण (koṇ)? (A person.)
Sources: Wiktionary: किती (kitī).


Lesson 3 of 3.
Sort the answers, not the questions — two asking words, two shapes of answer — a person and a number.

Warm-up.
[pause 4 seconds]
Three asking words are now yours. Name them in the order you met them, oldest first.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kāy āhe? — and answer with a thing]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: koṇ āhe? — and answer with a person]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī? — and answer with a numeral between ek and pāch]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: read the form label आवडती कृती and say why it ends in -ती]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Match each asking word to the shape of its answer: a thing, a person, a number.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
All three begin with the same letter. Which? (क.) So what does a strange new क- word at the front of a sentence probably want from you? (An answer — it is a question.)
