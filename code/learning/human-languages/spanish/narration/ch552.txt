Spanish, chapter 552: Lejos de casa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el inmigrante — the immigrant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to emigrate, then the Spanish for to adapt.

You'll want to know: el inmigrante.
el inmigrante — "the immigrant".
Muchos inmigrantes trabajan en el campo. Many immigrants work on the land.
The same form for a woman: la inmigrante.

The word, taken apart.
inmigrante is built on inmigrar, a learned word from Latin immigrāre 'to move into' (Lewis & Short), built on in and migrāre 'to move'.

You'll want to know: la frontera.
la frontera — "the border, the frontier".
Cruzaron la frontera de noche. They crossed the border at night.

The word, taken apart.
frontera is usually traced to an old word for 'facing, opposite', from Latin frōns, frontem 'forehead, front' (EtymDB). The border is the line where two countries face each other.

Lesson 5 of 5.
independizarse — to become independent; to leave home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the refugee, then the Spanish for the border.

You'll want to know: independizarse.
independizarse — "to become independent; to leave home".
Me independicé a los veintidós años. I left home at twenty-two.
For a young person it means moving out of the parents' house. It takes c before e: me independicé.

The word, taken apart.
independizarse is built on independiente with the ending -izar. The root is depender, from Latin dēpendēre 'to hang down from', built on pendēre 'to hang' (Lewis & Short).
