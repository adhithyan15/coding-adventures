5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સસરો (sasro) — a father-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to recall, then the Gujarati for sympathy.

You'll want to know: સસરો.
સસરો — sasro — "a father-in-law".
મારા સસરા રોજ સવારે મંદિરે જાય છે. mārā sasrā roj savāre mandire jāy che. My father-in-law goes to the temple every morning.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સસરો (sasro) is inherited from Sanskrit śvaśura, "husband's father"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 12753). One's own father-in-law is spoken of in the respectful plural, મારા સસરા (mārā sasrā).

જેઠાણી રોજ મને રસોડામાં મદદ કરે છે. jeṭhāṇī roj mane rasoḍāmā̃ madad kare che. My husband's elder brother's wife helps me in the kitchen every day.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
જેઠાણી (jeṭhāṇī) goes back to a reconstructed jyēṣṭhajāni-, "senior wife, elder brother's wife", built from jyēṣṭha, "eldest", and jāni, "wife"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 5287).
