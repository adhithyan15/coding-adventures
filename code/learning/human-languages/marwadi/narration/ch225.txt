Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 225: Packing for the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गठड़ी (gaṭhṛī) — a bundle (tied in cloth).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to leap up, then the Marwadi for a kettledrum.

You'll want to know: गठड़ी.
गठड़ी — gaṭhṛī — "a bundle (tied in cloth)".
म्हारी गठड़ी भारी है। mhārī gaṭhṛī bhārī hai. My bundle is heavy.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गठड़ी (gaṭhṛī) is inherited from Sanskrit grathna, "a bunch", from the root granth, "to tie"; CDIAL lists Hindi gaṭhṛī, "a bundle". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 4348).

Lesson 3 of 5.
मसक (masak) — a water-skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a bundle, then the Marwadi for a cloth bag.

You'll want to know: मसक.
मसक — masak — "a water-skin".
हूं मसक मांय पाणी भरसूं। hū̃ masak mā̃y pāṇī bharsū̃. I'll fill the water-skin.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
मसक (masak) is usually traced to Persian mašk, "a leather bag for water"; Marwadi breaks up its final -šk with a vowel.
