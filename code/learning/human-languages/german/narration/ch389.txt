German, chapter 389: Sich etwas leisten.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
leisten — to afford (sich leisten); to achieve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to pay back, then the German for to borrow.

You'll want to know: leisten.
leisten — "to afford (sich leisten); to achieve".
Ein neues Auto können wir uns im Moment nicht leisten. We cannot afford a new car at the moment.
Sich etwas leisten — to afford something. Also to achieve: Sie hat viel geleistet. Die Leistung — the achievement.

The word, taken apart.
leisten is inherited from Old High German leisten and Proto-Germanic laistijaną (EtymDB), usually explained as first "to follow a track", then to carry out what you owe; EtymDB links it to English last.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lohnen — to be worth it (sich lohnen); to reward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to borrow, then the German for to afford.

You'll want to know: lohnen.
lohnen — "to be worth it (sich lohnen); to reward".
Der lange Weg hat sich gelohnt: Die Aussicht ist fantastisch. The long walk was worth it: the view is fantastic.
Mostly reflexive: Das lohnt sich — it is worth it. Der Lohn — the wage, the reward.

The word, taken apart.
lohnen is inherited from Middle High German lōnen and Old High German lōnōn "to pay, to reward" (EtymDB), from Lohn "reward", Proto-Germanic launą.

Lesson 3 of 5.
finanzieren — to finance, to pay for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to afford, then the German for to be worth it.

You'll want to know: finanzieren.
finanzieren — "to finance, to pay for".
Sie finanziert ihr Studium mit einem Nebenjob. She pays for her studies with a part-time job.
Perfekt: hat finanziert. Die Finanzen — finances.

The word, taken apart.
finanzieren was borrowed from French financer (EtymDB), from finance "payment", built on Old French finer "to settle a debt", from fin "end", Latin fīnis (Lewis & Short): you bring a debt to an end.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Ausgabe — the expense; the edition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to be worth it, then the German for to finance.

You'll want to know: die Ausgabe.
die Ausgabe — "the expense; the edition".
Unsere größte Ausgabe ist die Miete. Our biggest expense is the rent.
Plural: die Ausgaben. Also an edition of a newspaper or a book: die neue Ausgabe.

The word, taken apart.
Ausgabe is inherited from Middle High German ūzgābe (EtymDB), of the family of ausgeben "to spend, to give out", built on geben "to give", Proto-Germanic gebaną.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Einnahme — the income, the takings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to finance, then the German for the expense.

You'll want to know: die Einnahme.
die Einnahme — "the income, the takings".
Die Einnahmen des Cafés sind im Sommer viel höher. The café's takings are much higher in summer.
Mostly plural: die Einnahmen. Also the taking of medicine: die Einnahme von Tabletten.

The word, taken apart.
Einnahme is the noun of einnehmen "to take in", built on nehmen "to take", Old High German neman, Proto-Germanic nemaną (EtymDB).
