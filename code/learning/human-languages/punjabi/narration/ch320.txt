Punjabi, chapter 320: At the Clinic.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਡਾਕਟਰ (ḍākṭar) — a doctor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for healthy, then the Punjabi for well again after an illness.

You'll want to know: ਡਾਕਟਰ.
ਡਾਕਟਰ — ḍākṭar — "a doctor".
ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਮੈਂ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਕੋਲ ਗਿਆ। kall maiṁ ḍākṭar kol giā. Yesterday I went to the doctor.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
ਡਾਕਟਰ (ḍākṭar) is borrowed from English doctor.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ਮਰੀਜ਼.

ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਹਸਪਤਾਲ ਵਿੱਚ ਬਹੁਤ ਮਰੀਜ਼ ਸਨ। kall haspatāl vich bahut marīz san. There were a lot of patients in the hospital yesterday.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
ਮਰੀਜ਼ (marīz) is a loan, usually traced through Persian to Arabic marīḍ, "sick".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਟੀਕਾ (ṭīkā) — an injection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for medical treatment, then the Punjabi for the pulse.

You'll want to know: ਟੀਕਾ.
ਟੀਕਾ — ṭīkā — "an injection".
ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਹਸਪਤਾਲ ਵਿੱਚ ਮੈਂ ਟੀਕਾ ਲਿਆ। kall haspatāl vich maiṁ ṭīkā liā. Yesterday I had an injection at the hospital.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਟੀਕਾ (ṭīkā) goes back to a reconstructed ṭikka, "a mark, a spot"; CDIAL lists Punjabi ṭikkā, "mark", and Hindi ṭīkā, "caste-mark", under it (CDIAL 5458). How it came to mean an injection is not given there.
