## You'll want to know: crēbrēscō, crēbrēscere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C301-SUSURRUS-04]; assesses=[] -->

**crēbrēscō, crēbrēscere** — "to grow stronger, to spread (of a rumour)".

> Fāma dē victōriā in diēs crēbrēscēbat.
>
> *The report of the victory grew stronger day by day.*

*crēbrēscō, crēbrēscere, crēbruī*, from *crēber* (taught). Tacitus uses it of a rumour that keeps growing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**crēbrēscō** is built on *crēber*, "frequent, close-packed", with the ending *-ēscō* for something that is becoming so (Lewis & Short): to become frequent.
