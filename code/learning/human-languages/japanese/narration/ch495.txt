Japanese, chapter 495: Smell and Flavour.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
におい (nioi) — a smell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bamboo shoot, then the Japanese for a shiitake mushroom.

You'll want to know: におい.
におい — nioi — "a smell".
だいどころから いい においが します。 — daidokoro kara ii nioi ga shimasu — "A good smell is coming from the kitchen."

The word, taken apart.
におい (nioi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ふうみ (fūmi) — flavour; taste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a smell, then the Japanese for a fragrance.

You'll want to know: ふうみ.
ふうみ — fūmi — "flavour; taste".
この みそしるは ふうみが いいです。 — kono misoshiru wa fūmi ga ii desu — "This miso soup has a good flavour."

The word, taken apart.
ふうみ (fūmi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are fū, "wind" (Middle Chinese pjuwng) and mi, "flavor" (mj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

しんせん — shinsen — "fresh".
いちばの さかなは しんせんです。 — ichiba no sakana wa shinsen desu — "The fish at the market is fresh."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
しんせん (shinsen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shin, "new" (Middle Chinese sin) and sen, "fresh" (sjen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
