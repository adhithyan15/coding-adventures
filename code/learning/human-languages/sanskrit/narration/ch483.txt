Sanskrit, chapter 483: Medicines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गुटिका (guṭikā) — a pill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for medical treatment, then the Sanskrit for a splinter.

You'll want to know: गुटिका.
गुटिका — guṭikā — "a pill".
प्रतिदिनं द्वे गुटिके खादतु। pratidinaṃ dve guṭike khādatu. Take two pills every day.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गुटिका (guṭikā) MW's first sense is "a small globe or ball" and then "a pill"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

The word, taken apart.
चूर्णम् (cūrṇam) is traced to the root √carv, "to grind with the teeth", by MW, which gives "powder, flour" (MW).

माता व्रणे लेपं स्थापयति। mātā vraṇe lepaṃ sthāpayati. Mother puts ointment on the wound.

वैद्यः व्रणे लेपं लिम्पति। vaidyaḥ vraṇe lepaṃ limpati. The physician smears ointment on the wound.
Past (he …): सः व्रणे लेपं लिप्तवान्, saḥ vraṇe lepaṃ liptavān.

The word, taken apart.
लिम्पति (limpati) comes from the root √lip, "to smear, to anoint", whose present stem takes a nasal (li-m-pa) (MW).
