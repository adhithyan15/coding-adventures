Japanese, chapter 235: Baseball and Judo.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
なげる (nageru) — to throw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a player, then the Japanese for cheering.

You'll want to know: なげる.
なげる — nageru — "to throw".
こうえんで いぬに ぼうを なげました。 — kōen de inu ni bō o nagemashita — "In the park I threw a stick for the dog."
Polite なげます, nagemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
なげる (nageru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to throw, then the Japanese for to kick.

You'll want to know: やきゅう.
やきゅう — yakyū — "baseball".
きのう ともだちと やきゅうを しました。 — kinō tomodachi to yakyū o shimashita — "Yesterday I played baseball with friends."

The word, taken apart.
やきゅう (yakyū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ya, "field" (Middle Chinese yaeX) and kyū, "ball" (gjuw) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
