Latin, chapter 77: Send, Stay, Fall, Put, Find.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mittō, mittere — to send.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to swim, then the Latin for to call.

You'll want to know: mittō, mittere.
mittō, mittere — "to send". English mission.

The word, taken apart.
mittō has no secure origin; EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed Proto-Indo-European meyth2-, "to exchange, to remove", and Lewis & Short call it doubtful. English admit comes from it too.

Lesson 2 of 5.
maneō, manēre — to stay, to remain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to call, then the Latin for to send.

You'll want to know: maneō, manēre.
maneō, manēre — "to stay, to remain".

The word, taken apart.
maneō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European men-, "to stay" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek menō, "to stay", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). English remain and mansion come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pōnō, pōnere — to put, to place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to stay, then the Latin for to fall.

You'll want to know: pōnō, pōnere.
pōnō, pōnere — "to put, to place". English position.

The word, taken apart.
pōnō is contracted from an older posinō (Lewis & Short), made of a prefix po-, "off, away", and sinō, "to let, to leave": to let something down in its place.

Lesson 5 of 5.
inveniō, invenīre — to find, to come upon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to fall, then the Latin for to put.

You'll want to know: inveniō, invenīre.
inveniō, invenīre — "to find, to come upon". English invent.

The word, taken apart.
inveniō is built from in, "on, upon", and veniō, "to come": to come upon a thing (Lewis & Short). English invent first meant "to find".
