# мечтать (mechtát) — to dream of, to long to

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-RAD-SHORT-01, RU-GRAMMAR-MNE-BYLO-01, RU-LEX-C138-FUT138-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was glad" and "I had fun". (*Ya byl rad. Mne býlo
véselo.*) Then "to hope". (*Nadéyatsya.*) A trip told in the past leaves
something for the future: a hope, a dream.

## You'll want to know: мечтать
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-MECHTAT-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C135-ACT135-05, RU-LEX-C56-NATURE2-05, RU-LEX-C180-ZDOROV180-02, RU-GRAMMAR-INFINITIVE-AFTER-VERB-01] -->

**мечтать** — *mechtát* — "to dream of, to long to". It takes an
infinitive, as *хотеть* does:

> **Я мечтаю путешествовать.** — *ya mechtáyu puteshéstvovat* — I dream of
> travelling.

Or **о** and a thing, with the thing's ending changed:

> **Я мечтаю о море.** — *ya mechtáyu o móre* — I dream of the sea.

This is a waking dream, a wish. A dream at night is **сон**, the word you
have for sleep.

## Grammar Lens: я надеюсь, что …
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-NADEYUS-CHTO-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C138-FUT138-01, RU-LEX-C59-WEATHER-04, RU-LEX-C64-COLOUR2-03, RU-GRAMMAR-CHTO-COMPLEMENTISER-01, RU-LEX-BYT] -->

A dream may never come; a hope is about what may well happen. **Я
надеюсь, что …** takes a whole clause, and a hope about tomorrow puts it
in the future:

> **Я надеюсь, что погода будет хорошая.** — *ya nadéyus, shto pogóda
> búdet khoróshaya* — I hope the weather will be good.

**Будет**, "will be", is the future of *быть*: the *буду* inside
*будущий*. Short and spoken: **Надеюсь!** — "I hope so!"

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-MECHTAT-01] -->

**мечтать** is inherited from Proto-Slavic *\*mĭčĭtati*, and the noun
**мечта**, "a dream, a wish", from *\*mĭčĭta*, "dream" (EtymDB 2.1).
EtymDB derives that noun from a Proto-Slavic verb *\*mikati* and gives it
no meaning; by the usual account it is the verb of blinking and
flickering, so a dream was first a flicker before the eyes.
