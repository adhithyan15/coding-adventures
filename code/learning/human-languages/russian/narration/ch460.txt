Russian, chapter 460: Streams and ponds.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ручей (ruchéy) — a stream, a brook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to scramble, then the Russian for to stumble.

You'll want to know: ручей.
ручей (ruchéy) — "a stream, a brook".
В лесу мы пили воду из ручья. V lesú my píli vódu iz ruchyá. In the forest we drank water from a stream.
Masculine; the е drops out: ручья, ручьи.

The word, taken apart.
ручей (ruchéy) is inherited from Proto-Slavic ručajĭ 'a loudly flowing stream' (EtymDB), which EtymDB builds on a verb ručati 'to roar, to hum'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
болото (bolóto) — a swamp, a marsh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to stumble, then the Russian for a stream.

You'll want to know: болото.
болото (bolóto) — "a swamp, a marsh".
За деревней большое болото. Za derévney bolshóye bolóto. Beyond the village there is a big swamp.
Neuter. The native -оло- shape; Church Slavonic has блато.

The word, taken apart.
болото (bolóto) is inherited through Old East Slavic boloto from Proto-Slavic bolto 'a swamp' (EtymDB); its -оло- is the native East Slavic shape.

Lesson 3 of 5.
родник (rodník) — a spring (of water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a stream, then the Russian for a swamp.

You'll want to know: родник.
родник (rodník) — "a spring (of water)".
Вода в роднике холодная и чистая. Vodá v rodniké kholódnaya i chístaya. The water in the spring is cold and clean.
Masculine; the stress moves: родника (rodniká).

The word, taken apart.
родник (rodník) is usually derived from род- 'birth', as in родить 'to give birth': water born from the earth. EtymDB lists the word without a source, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 4 of 5.
пруд (prud) — a pond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a swamp, then the Russian for a spring.

You'll want to know: пруд.
пруд (prud) — "a pond".
В пруду плавают утки. V prudú plávayut útki. Ducks are swimming in the pond.
Masculine; в пруду — in the pond.

The word, taken apart.
пруд (prud) is usually counted an inherited Slavic word; EtymDB gives no source but lists Polish prąd and Slovak prúd 'a current' as its cousins.

Lesson 5 of 5.
журчать (zhurchát) — to babble, to murmur (of water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a spring, then the Russian for a pond.

You'll want to know: журчать.
журчать (zhurchát) — "to babble, to murmur (of water)".
Под мостом тихо журчит ручей. Pod mostóm tíkho zhurchít ruchéy. Under the bridge a stream babbles quietly.
Imperfective. Present журчит, журчат.

The word, taken apart.
журчать (zhurchát) is usually taken to imitate the sound of running water; EtymDB lists it without a source.
