Urdu, chapter 423: Rates and Deals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سودا (saudā) — a deal; groceries.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to collect (money owed), then the Urdu for to give back.

You'll want to know: سودا.
سودا — saudā — "a deal; groceries".
بازار میں ہم نے سودا خریدا۔ bāzār meṅ ham ne saudā kharīdā. We bought our groceries at the market.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
سودا (saudā) is usually traced to Persian saudā, "trade, a bargain"; in Urdu it is a deal, and also the day's shopping.

Lesson 3 of 5.
دام (dām) — the asking price; a price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

دام — dām — "the asking price; a price".
دکاندار نے کم دام مانگے۔ dukāndār ne kam dām māṅge. The shopkeeper asked a low price.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
دام (dām) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin dām, "a copper coin, money, a price", under Sanskrit dramma, "a coin", which was itself taken from Greek drachmē (CDIAL 6622).

Lesson 4 of 5.
رعایت (riāyat) — a discount; a concession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the going rate, then the Urdu for the asking price.

You'll want to know: رعایت.
رعایت — riāyat — "a discount; a concession".
دکاندار نے ہمیں رعایت دی۔ dukāndār ne hameṅ riāyat dī. The shopkeeper gave us a discount.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رعایت (riāyat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic riʿāya, "regard, consideration"; in a shop it is the price taken off.
