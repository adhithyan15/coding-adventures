# (el informe mensual del huerto) — read the figures, then judge them

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01, ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **producir** and **el objetivo**. A sheet with both on it is
asking one question: did the first reach the second?

## Reading: the sheet on the shed door
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02, ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03] -->

> **HUERTO COMUNITARIO — MARZO**
>
> **Objetivo** del mes: **alimentar** a 50 familias.
>
> **Producido**: 380 kg.
> Familias atendidas: 41.
>
> No fue **suficiente**. En abril vamos a plantar más patata (papa), que
> **produce** más kilos por metro cuadrado.

## Grammar Lens: the sheet answers its own question
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02, ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04] -->

Four numbers and one verdict, and the verdict is the word this chapter was
for.

| what the sheet gives | what it settles |
|---|---|
| 50 against 41 | the target was missed |
| *no fue suficiente* | and by how the garden judges it |

It says *suficiente*, not *bastante*, and that is deliberate — though both
would be understood. *No fue bastante* is what you would say out loud. *No fue
suficiente* is what a sheet writes, because it names the requirement rather
than the quantity, and the 380 kg is being measured against the 50 families.

Note **producido** too: the participle, used as a plain label for the amount.
A sheet does not need a whole sentence to report a quantity.
