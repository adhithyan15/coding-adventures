Kannada, chapter 62: Things People Make.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ನೂಲು (nūlu) — thread, yarn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new run: what did bella mean, and what did sakkare mean?

You'll want to know: ನೂಲು.
ನೂಲು (nūlu) — "thread, yarn".
ನೂಲು (nūlu) is inherited, and it is the stuff ಬಟ್ಟೆ is made of. Everything worn in the cloth chapter began here.
The same shape does double duty: ನೂಲು (nūlu) is the thread and it is equally the spinning of it, so one word covers the material and the making. Kannada does that often, and you will start to expect it.

What you've built.
The first of five things a household makes or keeps.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಸೂಜಿ (sūji) — a needle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nūlu mean?

You'll want to know: ಸೂಜಿ.
ಸೂಜಿ (sūji) — "a needle".
ಸೂಜಿ (sūji) is what the ನೂಲು (nūlu) is put through. It is a borrowing from the north, and it arrived early enough that nothing about it feels foreign now.
It is also the hand of a clock. A ಗಂಟೆ has two ಸೂಜಿ (sūji) on its face, and Kannada saw the resemblance before anybody thought to give the hands a separate name.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಹಗ್ಗ (hagga) — a rope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did sūji mean?

You'll want to know: ಹಗ್ಗ.
ಹಗ್ಗ (hagga) — "a rope".
ಹಗ್ಗ (hagga) is ನೂಲು (nūlu) grown up: strands laid together until the result will hold weight. Native, with nothing borrowed anywhere in it.
In a ಹಳ್ಳಿ yard it is the thing at the end of which the ಹಸು stands. Twist it thinner and it ties a ಬುಟ್ಟಿ shut; twist it thicker and it draws water out of a ಕೆರೆ.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಪೊರಕೆ (porake) — a broom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did hagga mean?

You'll want to know: ಪೊರಕೆ.
ಪೊರಕೆ (porake) — "a broom".
ಪೊರಕೆ (porake) is a bundle of stiff ribs tied at one end — no handle, so it is used bent over, in short strokes.
It is the first object of the day. The ground outside the ಬಾಗಿಲು is swept with a ಪೊರಕೆ (porake) before the ರಂಗೋಲಿ is set out on it, because the pattern needs clean ground to sit on. Sweeping, then drawing: that order is fixed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಕೊಡೆ (koḍe) — an umbrella.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did porake mean?

You'll want to know: ಕೊಡೆ.
ಕೊಡೆ (koḍe) — "an umbrella".
ಕೊಡೆ (koḍe) works twice over in Karnataka: it keeps the ಮಳೆಗಾಲ off you, and it keeps the ಬಿಸಿಲು off you in the months on either side of it. In a Kannada street both uses are ordinary.
Read it carefully. ಕೊಡೆ (koḍe) and the ಕೊಡು you already know start with the same two letters, and they are separate items — one is a thing, the other is what you do with it, and joining them would be inventing a story. Kannada gives you plenty of real ones; this is not one.
Put ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು in front of any of this chapter's five and you have asked for it properly.

What you've built.
Five: ನೂಲು (nūlu), ಸೂಜಿ (sūji), ಹಗ್ಗ (hagga), ಪೊರಕೆ (porake), ಕೊಡೆ (koḍe). Enough to ask a household politely for the thing you need.
