# Chapter 14 checkpoint — six seasons, and ऋ from memory

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new here. The seasons out loud first, then a mark and two
letters on the page.

## Guided Practice — the six seasons
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C14-RITU-01, HI-CONCEPT-C14-RITU-02] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: the six seasons, in order]

(*Vasant, grīṣma, varṣā, śarad, hemant, śiśir*.)

- Which one has no Western match? (*Varṣā*, the monsoon.)
- Is this a four-season frame with two extras? (No: six seasons is a different
  way of dividing the year, with the cold split into *hemant* and *śiśir*.)

## Guided Practice — one more word from this chapter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-ANCHOR-GHEE] -->

- What is "ghee, clarified butter"? (*Ghī*.)

## Script — ◌ँ, घ and ऋ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-118, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-136, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-125, HI-LEX-ANCHOR-GHEE] -->

- What does **◌ँ** do in **पाँच**? (The vowel under it is said through the
  nose.)
- Which letter opens **घी**, and what sound does it carry? (**घ**, *gha*, with
  a puff of breath.)
- **ऋ** opens **ऋतु**. Is it the consonant **र**? (No: it is an independent
  vowel, written *ṛ* and said *ri* in Hindi.)

[YOU READ: **घी** and **ऋतु** aloud, and point to the घ and the ऋ]

## Writing — from memory
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-125, HI-SCRIPT-WRITE-125, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-136, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-118] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: dictation-transcription -->

[YOU COVER: the Script block above, so no model stays in view]

[YOU WRITE: the first letter of *ritu*, then the letter for *gha*]

[YOU WRITE: the mark that sends a vowel through the nose]

[YOU CHECK: against the key — **ऋ**, **घ**, **◌ँ**]

[YOU WRITE: one repaired stroke, then stop]

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which season follows the monsoon, and which two split the cold?
(*Śarad*; *hemant* and *śiśir*.) That is the chapter: six seasons, and the vowel
letter that opens their name.
