You'll want to know: 哪儿.
哪儿 — nǎr — "where?".
你在哪儿 nǐ zài nǎr — Where are you?
我在那儿 wǒ zài nàr — I'm over there.
The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
哪儿 (nǎr) is built from 哪 'which', the nǎ of nǐ shì nǎ guó rén, and the r ending 儿. 哪 has no reading in the Guangyun: no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
The answer goes where the question word was, and nothing else moves: 你在哪儿 — 我在那儿 (nǐ zài nǎr — wǒ zài nàr). Only the tone tells the two words apart: nǎr, with the dip, asks; nàr, with the fall, answers.

Script — 口 beside the whole of 那.
哪 (nǎ).
Two characters you own, side by side. Nothing in it is new.
口, three strokes, small, on the left: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the bottom bar.
那, six strokes, whole, as you write it alone: the hooked turn across the top and down the right, the upper bar, the lower bar, the left-falling stroke through them, then the ear on the right, across, back, round and hooking, and its long vertical.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 哪 (nǎ), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 哪? (Nine.) Which two characters sit side by side? (口 and 那.) What tells 哪 from 那 when you say them? (The tone: nǎ dips, nà falls.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 哪 (nǎ), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: shàngbian.
shàngbian — "on top; above".
shū zài zhuōzi shàngbian — The book is on the table.
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shàngbian is built from shàng 'above', the 上 you write, and bian 'side'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangH and pen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun glosses dzyangH as 'ruler', not 'above', so the plain sense 'on top' is assumed here rather than quoted.

What you've built.
A thing, then the place word after it: zhuōzi shàngbian, on top of the table. With 在 in front it answers 哪儿 (nǎr).

You'll want to know: xǐshǒujiān.
xǐshǒujiān — "toilet; washroom".
qǐngwèn, xǐshǒujiān zài nǎr — Excuse me, where is the toilet?
Its first and last characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xǐshǒujiān is built from xǐ 'to wash', shǒu 'hand', the 手 you write, and jiān 'room', the jiān of fángjiān. All three are inherited from Middle Chinese, sejX, syuwX and kean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun glosses sejX 'to wash' and kean 'a gap; between'; 'a room' is a later sense of jiān.

What you've built.
qǐngwèn, xǐshǒujiān zài nǎr may be the most useful sentence in the chapter: a polite opener, the thing, 在, and the question word in the place slot.

The exchange.
A: 我的手机呢 wǒ de shǒujī ne — Where's my phone? B: zài zhuōzi shàngbian — On the table. A: bú zài zhuōzi shàngbian — It isn't on the table. B: zài yǐzi xiàbian — Under the chair, then. A: 对 谢谢 duì, xièxie — Yes. Thanks. A: qǐngwèn, xǐshǒujiān zài nǎr — Excuse me, where's the toilet? B: 在那儿 zài nàr — Over there. A: 你在哪儿 nǐ zài nǎr — And where will you be? B: wǒ zài wàibian — Outside.
Every answer starts at 在, or drops even that. The thing being looked for was named once, and the rest of the exchange leaves it out.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
