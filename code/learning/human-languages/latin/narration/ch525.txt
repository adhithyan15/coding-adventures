Latin, chapter 525: Coughs and sore eyes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tussiō, tussīre — to cough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sore, then the Latin for to swell.

You'll want to know: tussiō, tussīre.
tussiō, tussīre — "to cough".
Senex tōtam noctem tussit. The old man coughs all night.
tussiō, tussīre, from tussis — a cough, already known.

The word, taken apart.
tussiō is built on tussis "a cough" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), which EtymDB traces to a Proto-Indo-European root for coughing. English pertussis, whooping cough, comes from the noun.

Lesson 2 of 5.
vomō, vomere — to vomit, be sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to swell, then the Latin for to cough.

You'll want to know: vomō, vomere.
vomō, vomere — "to vomit, be sick".
Nauta in nāve vomit. The sailor is sick on the ship.
vomō, vomere, vomuī, vomitum. Also of a volcano spewing fire. English vomit.

The word, taken apart.
vomō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to vomit", and Greek emeo is a cousin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English vomit comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lippus, lippa, lippum — bleary-eyed, with sore eyes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to vomit, then the Latin for to be thirsty.

You'll want to know: lippus, lippa, lippum.
lippus, lippa, lippum — "bleary-eyed, with sore eyes".
Servus lippus librum legere nōn potest. The bleary-eyed slave cannot read the book.
Watery, inflamed eyes, a common complaint in Rome.

The word, taken apart.
lippus is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short compares a root meaning "to smear", but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lippitūdō, lippitūdinis — inflammation of the eyes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be thirsty, then the Latin for bleary-eyed.

You'll want to know: lippitūdō, lippitūdinis.
lippitūdō, lippitūdinis — "inflammation of the eyes".
Propter lippitūdinem epistulās nōn scrībō. Because of sore eyes I am not writing letters.
Feminine, third declension, from lippus. A complaint of busy letter-writers.

The word, taken apart.
lippitūdō is built on lippus "bleary-eyed" with -tūdō (Lewis & Short).
