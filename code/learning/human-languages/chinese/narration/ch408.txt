6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhànpái — bus stop sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the end of the line, then the Mandarin for a D-class bullet train.

You'll want to know: zhànpái.
zhànpái — "bus stop sign".
wǒ zài zhànpái xiàbian děng nǐ — I'll wait for you under the bus stop sign.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhànpái is built from 站 'a stop' and pái 'a board', both inherited from Middle Chinese, treamH and bea (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a signboard.

Lesson 2 of 6.
公交 (gōngjiāo) — public transport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a D-class bullet train, then the Mandarin for a bus stop sign.

You'll want to know: 公交.
公交 — gōngjiāo — "public transport".
我每天坐公交去学校 wǒ měitiān zuò gōngjiāo qù xuéxiào — I take public transport to school every day.
The new character, in pieces: 交 (jiāo) — six strokes: a dot and a bar on top; under them a short stroke falling to the left and a short dot falling to the right; then a long stroke falling to the left, crossed by a long stroke falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
公交 (gōngjiāo) is built from 公 'public' and 交 (jiāo) 'to cross', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and kaew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'shared' and the second 'to join'. It is a short form of a longer name for public transport; the city bus is gōngjiāochē.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Script — a lid, then two strokes crossing.
交 (jiāo).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The lid, two strokes. A dot, then a long bar across, as on top of 文.
The lower piece, four strokes. A short stroke falling to the left and a short dot falling to the right, apart, like two feet. Then a long stroke falling to the left, and across it a long stroke falling to the right to the foot.
2 + 4 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 交 (jiāo)? (Six.) What sits on top? (A dot and a bar, as on 文.) What are the last two strokes? (A long fall to the left, crossed by a long fall to the right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
tóubì — to drop coins in the box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bus stop sign, then the Mandarin for public transport.

You'll want to know: tóubì.
tóubì — "to drop coins in the box".
shàng chē yǐhòu qǐng tóubì — Please put your coins in once you're on.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tóubì is built from tóu 'to throw' and bì 'money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duw and bjiejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as gifts of silk; the sense 'coin' is not given there.

Lesson 6 of 6.
起点 (qǐdiǎn) — starting point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to drop coins in the box, then the Mandarin for a long-distance coach.

You'll want to know: 起点.
起点 — qǐdiǎn — "starting point".
公交的起点在火车站 gōngjiāo de qǐdiǎn zài huǒchēzhàn — The bus route starts at the railway station.
Two third tones meet in this word: qǐ is said with a rising tone before diǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
起点 (qǐdiǎn) is built from 起 'to rise, to start' and 点 'a point', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khiX and temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and the second as a dot.
