Latin, chapter 335: Lūdī circēnsēs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aurīga, aurīgae — a charioteer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to vow, then the Latin for a vow.

You'll want to know: aurīga, aurīgae.
aurīga, aurīgae — "a charioteer".
Aurīga equōs celeriter agēbat. The charioteer was driving the horses fast.
Masculine, 1st declension, though it ends in -a, like nauta. Famous charioteers grew very rich.

The word, taken apart.
aurīga is explained by Lewis & Short as built on an old word for the reins and agō, "to drive": he who handles the reins. The details are not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
quadrīgae, quadrīgārum — a four-horse chariot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a vow, then the Latin for a charioteer.

You'll want to know: quadrīgae, quadrīgārum.
quadrīgae, quadrīgārum — "a four-horse chariot".
Quadrīgae per circum volābant. The four-horse chariots flew round the circus.
Feminine, plural: four horses under one yoke.

The word, taken apart.
quadrīgae is a shortened quadriiugae, from quattuor, "four", and iugum, "yoke" (Lewis & Short): four together.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mēta, mētae — a turning post; a goal, a limit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a charioteer, then the Latin for a four-horse chariot.

You'll want to know: mēta, mētae.
mēta, mētae — "a turning post; a goal, a limit".
Aurīga prope mētam cecidit. The charioteer fell near the turning post.
Feminine. The cone-shaped posts at each end of the track, where crashes happened. Also any limit or goal.

The word, taken apart.
mēta is inherited from Proto-Italic mētā, "boundary" (EtymDB): a marker at the limit.

Lesson 4 of 5.
factiō, factiōnis — a racing team; a party, a faction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a four-horse chariot, then the Latin for a turning post.

You'll want to know: factiō, factiōnis.
factiō, factiōnis — "a racing team; a party, a faction".
Puer factiōnem viridem amābat. The boy loved the Green team.
Feminine, from faciō. The racing teams wore colours: white, red, green and blue. English faction.

The word, taken apart.
factiō is built on faciō, "to make, to do" (EtymDB): a doing together, and so a group that acts together. English faction comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
circēnsis, circēnse — of the circus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a turning post, then the Latin for a racing team.

You'll want to know: circēnsis, circēnse.
circēnsis, circēnse — "of the circus".
Lūdī circēnsēs per quīnque diēs fīēbant. The races in the circus went on for five days.
Two endings. Lūdī circēnsēs: the races. A satirist said the people wanted only bread and races.

The word, taken apart.
circēnsis is built on circus, "ring, racecourse" (EtymDB), with the ending -ēnsis, "belonging to".
