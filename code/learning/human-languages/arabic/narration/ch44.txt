Lesson 1 of 1.
The same writing, with a clock — a timed writing test that changes as you answer it, and what that changes about practising.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.
[pause 1 second]
A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

Writing — timed production.
What the target publishes. The writing section runs 20 to 25 minutes and holds three prompts. Those numbers come out of task-shapes/a1.json.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now the part that makes this different from every other timed paper, and it is worth knowing before the clock starts rather than during it: the test is adaptive. It chooses your next prompt from how you answered the last one. Two candidates sitting the same test do not see the same questions.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three things follow, and they change how to practise rather than how to write:
[pause 2 seconds]
One — there is no paper to rehearse. Memorising an answer buys nothing, because the question it answered may never be asked of you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two — a weak answer makes the next question easier, and an easier question caps the level you can be reported at. Guessing short to save time is not neutral here; it lowers the ceiling of everything after it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three — the result is not a pass mark. This test reports a separate benchmark level per skill and publishes no pass or fail. The A1 claim in this book is the project's own mapping, stated in the contract, not something the test awards.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write for twenty minutes to one prompt of your choosing, at the top of what you can actually do. Stop when the time is up.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Did you write at your ceiling, or somewhere comfortably below it?
[pause 2 seconds]
On a fixed paper the difference costs you a few marks. On an adaptive one it costs you the questions you never got to see.
