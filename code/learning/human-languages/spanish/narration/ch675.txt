Spanish, chapter 675: Las partes de la ciudad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
las afueras — the outskirts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to travel around, then the Spanish for the route.

You'll want to know: las afueras.
las afueras — "the outskirts".
Vivimos en las afueras de la ciudad. We live on the outskirts of the city.
Always plural: las afueras.

The word, taken apart.
afueras is built on afuera and fuera "outside", which come from Latin forās "out of doors" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la cuadra — the block (of a street) (Latin America); the stable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the route, then the Spanish for the outskirts.

You'll want to know: la cuadra.
la cuadra — "the block (of a street) (Latin America); the stable".
El hotel está a dos cuadras. The hotel is two blocks away.
Spain says la manzana for a block; there la cuadra is a stable.

The word, taken apart.
cuadra comes from Latin quadra "a square" (Lewis & Short): a city block is a square of streets.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el soportal — the arcade (covered walkway).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the block, then the Spanish for the alley.

You'll want to know: el soportal.
el soportal — "the arcade (covered walkway)".
Con la lluvia caminamos bajo los soportales. In the rain we walk under the arcades.
Often plural: los soportales of a main square.

The word, taken apart.
soportal is built from so- "under", from Latin sub, and portal "doorway", from Latin porta "gate".

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: el adoquín.
el adoquín — "the paving stone, the cobblestone".
Las calles del centro son de adoquines. The streets in the centre are cobbled.
Hard work for a suitcase on wheels.

The word, taken apart.
adoquín comes from Andalusian Arabic ad-dukkán "stone bench", from Arabic dukkān (EtymDB).
