# do vartamān — the habit and the moment

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say both forms of *to do* that you now have — the habit and the one
in progress — and try to say in English what separates them.

## You'll want to know: the pair
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05]; assesses=[] -->

| Hindi | English |
|---|---|
| मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ। | I speak Hindi. |
| मैं हिंदी बोल रहा हूँ। | I am speaking Hindi. |

The first is a **fact about you**. The second is a **report about this minute**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

This distinction is worth pausing on, because English speakers have it and most
European learners of Hindi do not. French, German and Spanish all translate both
of those lines with a single present tense; Hindi and English each keep two.

So the good news is that **your instinct is already right**. Where English says
*I am doing*, Hindi says *कर रहा हूँ*. Where English says *I do*, Hindi says
*करता हूँ*. Translate the shape, not the words.

One caution. Hindi leans on the habit form in a place English does not: for
something happening in the near future that is already arranged, Hindi is happy
with the habitual, where English reaches for *I am*.

## Grammar Lens: which word in the sentence tells you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-LEX-C49-LEAVE-01] -->

| form | the shape of the verb |
|---|---|
| habit | stem **+ -ता**, then the copula |
| in progress | bare stem, then **रहा**, then the copula |

The copula is in both, so it tells you nothing. What decides it is whether the
stem carries **-ता** or stands bare with **रहा** behind it.

**अभी** — the word for *right now* — sits comfortably with the second and
strangely with the first, which is the quickest way to hear the difference.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which form states a fact about you? (The habit, with **-ता**.) Which
reports this minute? (The one with **रहा**.) Does the copula help you tell them
apart? (No — it is in both.)
