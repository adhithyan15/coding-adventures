# del — and then the list stops

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-AL, ES-LEX-DE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *A* + *el* gave you *al*. Now take *de*, which you have had since
chapter forty-eight.

[PAUSE 1s] Say *de el* quickly, and let the same thing happen.

## Grammar Lens: the second squeeze
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-DEL]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DE] -->

> *de* + *el* → ***del***

> *el libro* ***del*** *profesor* — the teacher's book

[PAUSE 2s] Same rule, same force. And notice what *del* is doing in that
phrase: Spanish has no apostrophe-s, so *of the* is how it says whose something
is. *Del profesor* — of the teacher.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-ONLY-TWO]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-OBLIGATORY] -->

Now the part worth celebrating.

[PAUSE 2s] **That is the complete list.** Spanish has exactly **two**
obligatory contractions — *al* and *del* — and no others. Not *de la*, not *a
la*, not *de los*. Those stay two words forever.

| | |
|---|---|
| *a* + *el* | ***al*** |
| *de* + *el* | ***del*** |
| *a la*, *de la*, *a los*, *de las* | unchanged |

[PAUSE 2s] It is rare to be handed a grammar rule and told the exceptions are
finished. Learn these two and you will never wonder about a third.
