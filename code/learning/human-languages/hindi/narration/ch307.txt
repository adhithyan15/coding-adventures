Hindi, chapter 307: Trapped.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
फँसना (phãsnā) — to be trapped; to be stuck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an epidemic, then the Hindi for cholera.

You'll want to know: फँसना.
फँसना — phãsnā — "to be trapped; to be stuck".
बाढ़ में कई लोग छतों पर फँस गए। bāṛh meṁ kaī log chatoṁ par phãs gae. In the flood many people were stranded on rooftops.
Past (he …): वह आदमी फँस गया, vah ādmī phãs gayā.

The word, taken apart.
फँसना (phãsnā) is set by CDIAL under Sanskrit spāśayati (CDIAL 13814), of the family of pāśa, a noose. You फँसते हो as in a net: in a flood, in traffic, or in trouble.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for cholera, then the Hindi for to be trapped.

You'll want to know: पुकारना.
पुकारना — pukārnā — "to call out (to)".
छत से एक बच्चा मदद के लिए पुकार रहा था। chat se ek baccā madad ke liye pukār rahā thā. From a rooftop a child was calling out for help.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे पुकारा, usne mujhe pukārā.

The word, taken apart.
पुकारना (pukārnā) is inherited from pukkār, a shout, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 8246). It is a call meant to be heard far off: मदद के लिए पुकारना, to call for help.

You'll want to know: सन्नाटा.
सन्नाटा — sannāṭā — "a dead silence".
रात को गाँव में सन्नाटा था। rāt ko gā̃v meṁ sannāṭā thā. At night a dead silence lay over the village.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सन्नाटा (sannāṭā) is set by CDIAL under Sanskrit saṁnaṣṭa (CDIAL 12925). It is not a calm quiet but a silence that frightens: सन्नाटा छा गया, a hush fell.

Lesson 4 of 5.
निकालना (nikālnā) — to take out; to pull out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to call out (to), then the Hindi for a dead silence.

You'll want to know: निकालना.
निकालना — nikālnā — "to take out; to pull out".
लोगों ने बच्चे को पानी से निकाला। logoṁ ne bacce ko pānī se nikālā. People pulled the child out of the water.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को निकाला, usne bacce ko nikālā.

The word, taken apart.
निकालना (nikālnā) goes back to Sanskrit niṣkālayati, "drives out" (CDIAL 7484). It is the transitive partner of निकलना: you निकलते हो from a house, and you निकालते हो something from a bag.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बेहाल (behāl) — in a sorry state.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a dead silence, then the Hindi for to take out.

You'll want to know: बेहाल.
बेहाल — behāl — "in a sorry state".
दो दिन बाद लोग भूख से बेहाल थे। do din bād log bhūkh se behāl the. Two days later people were in a sorry state from hunger.

The word, taken apart.
बेहाल (behāl) is usually traced to Persian bē, "without", with ḥāl, "a state, a condition", an Arabic word Persian took over: without a good state, at the end of one's strength.
