# la temperatura — the temperature

## You'll want to know: la temperatura
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02] -->

**la temperatura** — the temperature.

> Anote la **temperatura** a las ocho y a las ocho de la tarde.

*Tomar la temperatura* — to take somebody's temperature. *Tener temperatura*
is to be running a fever, with no number said out loud.

Note what *calor* and *frío* cannot do: they tell you which way, never how
much. *Hace calor* is a judgement. *La temperatura* is a measurement, and the
whole reason a notebook wants one is that a measurement can be compared with
yesterday's.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03] -->

Latin **temperare** did not mean heat at all. It meant **to mix in the right
proportion** — to hold two things in balance so that neither wins.

Follow that one idea and a very odd family falls into line:

| English | what is being balanced |
|---|---|
| to **temper** steel | heat against hardness |
| a **temperate** climate | summer against winter |

A person's **temper** is the mix of their humours, and losing it is the mix
coming apart. **Tempera** paint is pigment held in the right proportion of
egg. And a **temperature** is the point a mixture has reached on the scale
between hot and cold.

Spanish keeps the verb as **templar**, to temper or to tune an instrument, and
*templado* is the word for weather that is neither one thing nor the other.

## Grammar Lens: -ura on an adjective
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02] -->

*La factura* gave you **-ura** as the thing an action produces, with a verb
hiding inside the noun. *Temperatura* is built the same way and on the same
instruction: the verb inside it is *temperare*, and what the action produces is
a reading.

The ending has a second family, though, and it does not take verbs at all:

| base | noun |
|---|---|
| alto | la alt**ura** |
| ancho | la anch**ura** |

Those sit on adjectives, and they name how much of the quality there is —
*altura* is how tall, *anchura* is how wide. So when you meet a new **-ura**
noun, look for a verb first, as *factura* told you to, and look for an
adjective second.
