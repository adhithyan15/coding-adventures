## You'll want to know: jīngjìcāng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C414-CABIN414-03]; assesses=[] -->

*jīngjìcāng* — "economy class".

> *wǒmen zuò jīngjìcāng qù Běijīng* — We're flying to Beijing in economy.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jīngjìcāng* is built from *jīngjì* 'economy' and *cāng* 'a cabin'. The first two parts are inherited from Middle Chinese, *keng* and *tsejH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'constant' and 'to cross a river'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the third, so its history is not settled.
