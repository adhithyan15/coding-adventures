# wenn ich Zeit hätte — if things were different

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-WENN-PRAESENS-01, GE-GRAMMAR-WUERDE-INFINITIV-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if I have time, I'll come". (*Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme
ich.*) That condition may well come true. Now one that is not true at all.

## Grammar Lens: the Konjunktiv II in both halves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-WENN-IRREAL-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-HAETTE-GERN-01, GE-LEX-ZEIT-01, GE-LEX-LESEN-07, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-KOMMA-VERB-02] -->

When the condition is not real, both halves go into the Konjunktiv II:

> *Wenn ich Zeit* **hätte**, **würde** *ich mehr lesen.* — If I had time, I
> would read more.

I do not have time; that is the point. Set the two side by side:

> *Wenn ich Zeit habe, lese ich.* — real: it may happen.
> *Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich lesen.* — not real: it is not happening.

The word order has not changed: the verb at the end of the *wenn* clause,
then verb, comma, verb: *hätte, würde*.

One short version is worth having on its own: *Wenn ich du wäre …*, if I
were you.

## Why it's said this way: ich könnte
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ICH-KOENNTE-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KONNTE-01, GE-LEX-C81-SCHULE-05, GE-ETYMON-KONJUNKTIV-UMLAUT-02] -->

**ich könnte** is "I could", in the sense of "I would be able to". It is
*konnte* with an umlaut, and like *würde* it puts the infinitive last:

> *Wenn ich Geld hätte, könnte ich nach Japan reisen.* — If I had money, I
> could travel to Japan.

It also makes a suggestion soft: *Wir könnten morgen fahren*, we could go
tomorrow.

Listen for the vowel. *Ich konnte* is "I was able to", over and done. *Ich
könnte* is "I could", if things were different.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KONNTE-01] -->

**könnte** is **konnte** with the umlaut of the Konjunktiv II. EtymDB 2.1
traces *können* through Old High German *kunnan* to Proto-Germanic
*\*kunnaną*; you met its link to English *could*, and its old sense "to
know how", with *konnte* in chapter twenty-five. **Zeit** goes back to
Proto-Germanic *\*tīdiz*, "time, period" (EtymDB 2.1), the same word as
English *tide*.
