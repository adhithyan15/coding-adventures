# அவர்கள் — they, and an ending hiding in a word you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C02-NII-NIINGAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the polite word for *you*. Look at its last syllable.

[PAUSE 2s] You have been carrying a plural ending around since chapter two
without being told it was one.

## You'll want to know: அவர்கள்
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C85-AVARGAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **அவர்கள்** — *avarkaḷ* — **they**

It is **அவர்** with **-கள்** on the end, and **-கள்** is Tamil's plural. The
same piece is sitting on the end of **நீங்கள்**, which is why that word does
double duty as a polite singular *you* and a plain plural *you*.

| one | more than one |
|---|---|
| நீ | நீங்கள் |
| அவர் | அவர்கள் |

**The ending you have been pronouncing for eighty chapters turns out to be a
word-building piece**, and now it builds something for you rather than arriving
pre-attached.

## Grammar Lens: the same double duty
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C85-AVARGAL-01, TA-LEX-C85-AVAN-AVAL-01] -->

**அவர்கள்** carries the same ambiguity as நீங்கள். About several people it is
plainly *they*; about one person it is a step more respectful still than அவர் —
the way some languages use a plural to raise the register.

Context settles which, and context usually settles it easily: a room with three
people in it needs no disambiguation.
