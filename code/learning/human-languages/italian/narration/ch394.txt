Italian, chapter 394: I conti di casa.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
il bilancio — the budget, the balance sheet; the assessment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the punishment, then the Italian for the sanction.

You'll want to know: il bilancio.
il bilancio — "the budget, the balance sheet; the assessment".
Se spendiamo così, il bilancio di casa non regge. If we spend like this, the household budget will not hold up.
Fare un bilancio — to take stock. A company's yearly accounts are il bilancio.

The word, taken apart.
bilancio is built on bilanciare "to balance", from bilancia "scales", inherited from Vulgar Latin bilancia, from Latin bilanx "having two scale pans", built from bi- and lanx "a dish" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Stress on the first syllable: rèddito. La dichiarazione dei redditi — the tax return.

The word, taken apart.
reddito is a learned borrowing from Latin redditus "given back, returned" (Lewis & Short), past participle of reddere "to give back", built on dare "to give".

Lesson 3 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la bolletta.
la bolletta — "the utility bill".
Se non paghi la bolletta, ti tagliano la luce. If you do not pay the bill, they cut off your electricity.
For electricity, gas, water or phone; at a restaurant it is il conto.

The word, taken apart.
bolletta is a little bolla "seal, stamped paper", inherited from Latin bulla "a bubble, a knob, a seal" (EtymDB): first a stamped receipt.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: il canone.
il canone — "the regular fee, the rent; the canon".
Il canone d'affitto aumenta ogni anno. The rent goes up every year.
Stress on the first syllable: cànone. Il canone of the state TV is a yearly licence fee.

The word, taken apart.
canone is a learned borrowing from Latin canōn, from Greek kanṓn "a measuring rod, a standard" (EtymDB): a fixed rule, then a fixed payment.

Lesson 5 of 6.
il mutuo — the mortgage, the (bank) loan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the utility bill, then the Italian for the regular fee.

You'll want to know: il mutuo.
il mutuo — "the mortgage, the (bank) loan".
Se la banca ci dà il mutuo, compriamo la casa. If the bank gives us the mortgage, we will buy the house.
Pagare il mutuo — to pay the mortgage. As an adjective it means mutual.

The word, taken apart.
mutuo is a learned borrowing from Latin mūtuum "a loan" (Lewis & Short), from mūtuus "borrowed, lent; in return, mutual".
