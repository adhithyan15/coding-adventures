# espero voltar — saying what you are reaching for

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-ESPERAR-HOPE-04, PT-GRAMMAR-SENTIMENTO-ESTAVA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I hope so". (*Espero que sim.*) Then "I was pleased".
(*Estava contente.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a past and a future. Last lesson was how it felt;
this one is what you want next.

## You'll want to know: espero voltar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-ESPERO-INFINITIVO-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-ESPERAR-02, PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-03, PT-LEX-C42-VERBOS3-04] -->

**espero** + infinitive — "I hope to".

> **Espero voltar** a Portugal. — I hope to come back to Portugal.
> **Espero aprender** bem português. — I hope to learn Portuguese well.

You have **esperar**, which waits and hopes; **espero** is its "I" form, and
an infinitive straight after it settles the meaning: hope. Nothing goes in
between. Spanish says the same, *espero volver*; Italian needs a small word,
*spero di*; Portuguese does not.

When the person who hopes is not the one who will do it, the frame is the one
you already have, *espero que…*, and the verb after it bends into a form a
later chapter builds. For now, keep to hopes about yourself.

## Grammar Lens: o meu sonho é
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-O-MEU-SONHO-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C149-SAUDE149-01, PT-LEX-C43-VERBOS4-05, PT-LEX-C83-QUERER83-02, PT-GRAMMAR-GOSTAR-DE-04, PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-05] -->

An ambition is bigger than a hope, and it has its own frame:

> **O meu sonho é** viver em Lisboa. — My dream is to live in Lisbon.
> **O meu sonho é** viajar muito. — My dream is to travel a lot.

**o sonho**, "the dream", you have. The article stays in front of *meu*, as
it does all through European Portuguese; in Brazil it is often dropped,
*meu sonho é*. After *é* the infinitive follows bare: the dream **is** the
doing.

Three strengths, then, for the future: *gostaria de viajar*, I'd like to;
*espero viajar*, I hope to; *o meu sonho é viajar*, it is my dream. Only the
first needs a *de*.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**esperar** is inherited from Latin *spērāre*, "to hope, to expect" (EtymDB
2.1; Lewis & Short, under *spero*). English **desperate** is the same verb with
*dē-* in front, "hope taken away", and **a esperança**, "hope", is its noun.
**o sonho** is inherited from Latin *somnium*, "a dream" (EtymDB 2.1), which is
built on *somnus*, "sleep": a dream is what sleep brings.
