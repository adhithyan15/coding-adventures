Lesson 1 of 6.
le verglas — the black ice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to rumble, then the French for the gust.

You'll want to know: le verglas.
le verglas — "the black ice".
Avec le verglas, les voitures roulent très lentement. Because of the black ice, cars are going very slowly.
Une route verglacée — an icy road.

The word, taken apart.
verglas is built from verre 'glass', from Latin vitrum, and glace 'ice', from Latin glaciēs (EtymDB): ice as smooth as glass.

What you've built.

The origin of givre is not settled; it may come from a language spoken in the region before Latin. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 3 of 6.
geler — to freeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the black ice, then the French for the frost.

You'll want to know: geler.
geler — "to freeze".
Cette nuit, il a gelé et le lac est blanc. Last night it froze and the lake is white.
Il gèle — it is freezing; je suis gelé — I am frozen.

The word, taken apart.
geler is inherited through Old French from Latin gelāre 'to freeze', from gelū 'frost' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
grelotter — to shiver (with cold).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to freeze, then the French for to thaw.

You'll want to know: grelotter.
grelotter — "to shiver (with cold)".
Sans manteau, le garçon grelotte à l'arrêt de bus. Without a coat the boy is shivering at the bus stop.
Only from cold.

The word, taken apart.
grelotter is built on grelot 'small round bell' (EtymDB), usually explained by an old phrase about teeth that rattle like a little bell.
