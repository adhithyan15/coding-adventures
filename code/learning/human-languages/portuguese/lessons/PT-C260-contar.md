# contar uma história — to tell a story, which is to count it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-DEPOIS-ORDEM-01, PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01, PT-LEX-C84-PORQUE84-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can hold a scene still, put events in order, and say what a
reason led to with *por isso*. This lesson adds one small word, then puts it
all together.

## Grammar Lens: mas, the turn in a story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-MAS-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C130-NUMEROS130-05] -->

**mas** means "but". It joins two facts that pull against each other:

> *Estava sol, **mas** fazia frio.* — The sun was out, but it was cold.
> *Esperei muito, **mas** o comboio não chegou.* — I waited a long time, but
> the train did not come.

In a story it marks the turn. *E* adds; *mas* turns.

## Grammar Lens: the backbone of a told story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-HISTORIA-EM-ORDEM-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C133-VERBOS133-04, PT-LEX-C230-TRAB230-03, PT-LEX-C73-CIDADE1-04, PT-LEX-ENCONTRAR-02, PT-LEX-FAZIA-FRIO-01] -->

**contar uma história** is "to tell a story", with a *contar* you already
have. Here is what it tells, with both pasts to work with:

> **Era** sábado. **Fazia** calor e **estava** sol. **Primeiro fui** ao
> mercado. **Depois encontrei** um amigo, e **falámos** muito. **Mas no fim
> começou** a chover, **por isso voltei** para casa.
>
> *It was Saturday. It was hot and the sun was out. First I went to the market.
> Then I met a friend, and we talked a lot. But in the end it started to rain,
> so I went back home.*

*Era*, *fazia*, *estava*: the scene, held still. *Fui*, *encontrei*,
*falámos*, *começou*, *voltei*: the events, put in order by *primeiro*,
*depois* and *no fim*. *Mas* marks the turn; *por isso* says what the rain led
to.

## The word, taken apart: counting it out
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-CONTAR-03]; assesses=[] -->

**contar** is inherited from Latin *computāre*, "to reckon up" (EtymDB 2.1),
which is *com-*, "together", plus *putāre*, "to settle an account" (Lewis &
Short). Portuguese kept one verb for both jobs: *contar até dez*, to count to
ten, and *contar uma história*. English **recount** has the same picture.

**mas** is inherited from Latin *magis*, "more" (EtymDB 2.1), the same Latin
word that gave **mais**. "More" became "rather", and "rather" became "but".
