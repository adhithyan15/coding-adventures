Russian, chapter 294: Guilt and punishment.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
виновный (vinóvnyy) — guilty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a prison, then the Russian for to accuse.

You'll want to know: виновный.
виновный (vinóvnyy) — "guilty".
Суд признал его виновным. Sud priznál yevó vinóvnym. The court found him guilty.
Виновный в + prepositional. In speech, of a small fault: я виноват — it is my fault.

The word, taken apart.
виновный (vinóvnyy) is built on вина "guilt", an inherited Slavic word, with the adjective ending -ный; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to accuse, then the Russian for guilty.

You'll want to know: наказание.
наказание (nakazániye) — "a punishment".
Наказание было справедливым. Nakazániye býlo spravedlívym. The punishment was fair.
Neuter.

The word, taken apart.
наказание (nakazániye) is built on наказать, from на- and казать "to show, to tell", inherited from Proto-Slavic kazati (EtymDB): first "an instruction", then a punishment.

Before the new one: say the Russian for a punishment, then the Russian for to punish.

You'll want to know: признаваться.
признаваться (priznavátsya) — "to confess, to admit".
Он признался, что взял деньги. On priználsya, shto vzyal déngi. He admitted that he had taken the money.
Imperfective; perfective признаться (priznátsya). Present признаюсь, признаёшься (priznayóshsya). Признаться в любви — to declare one's love.

The word, taken apart.
признаваться (priznavátsya) is built on при- and знать "to know", which is inherited from Proto-Slavic znati, from PIE ǵneh₃- "to know" (EtymDB): to make known.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to punish, then the Russian for to confess.

You'll want to know: оправдывать.
оправдывать (oprávdyvat) — "to acquit; to justify".
Суд оправдал молодого человека. Sud opravdál molodóvo chelovéka. The court acquitted the young man.
Imperfective; the perfective is оправдать (opravdát). Also оправдать надежды — to live up to hopes.

The word, taken apart.
оправдывать (oprávdyvat) is built on о- and правда "truth", from правый "right, fair", an inherited Slavic word: to show someone is in the right. EtymDB lists no parent.
