Russian, chapter 282: Sources and rumours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
источник (istóchnik) — a source; a spring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the air, then the Russian for a TV channel.

You'll want to know: источник.
источник (istóchnik) — "a source; a spring".
Журналист не назвал свой источник. Zhurnalíst ne nazvál svoy istóchnik. The journalist did not name his source.
First sense: a spring of water. Источник информации — a source of information.

The word, taken apart.
источник (istóchnik) is built on истечь "to flow out", from из- "out" and the Slavic verb течь "to flow": first a spring, then any source. EtymDB lists no parent.

Очевидец рассказал, что видел. Ochevídets rasskazál, shto vídel. The eyewitness told what he had seen.
Genitive очевидца.

The word, taken apart.
очевидец (ochevídets) is built on очи "eyes", plural of око, Proto-Slavic oko, and видеть "to see", Proto-Slavic viděti (EtymDB): one who saw with his own eyes.

Lesson 3 of 5.
слух (slukh) — a rumour; hearing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a source, then the Russian for an eyewitness.

You'll want to know: слух.
слух (slukh) — "a rumour; hearing".
По городу ходил странный слух. Po górodu khodíl stránnyy slukh. A strange rumour was going round the town.
Ходят слухи — rumours are going round. Also an ear for music: у неё хороший слух (u neyó khoróshiy slukh).

The word, taken apart.
слух (slukh) is inherited from Proto-Slavic sluxŭ "hearing", from PIE ḱlew- "to hear" (EtymDB): what people have heard.

Lesson 5 of 5.
факт (fakt) — a fact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a rumour, then the Russian for a detail.

You'll want to know: факт.
факт (fakt) — "a fact".
Это известный факт. Éto izvéstnyy fakt. This is a well-known fact.
Masculine. На самом деле or фактически — in fact.

The word, taken apart.
факт (fakt) goes back to Latin factum "a deed, a thing done", from facere "to do" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the Russian word with no parent.
