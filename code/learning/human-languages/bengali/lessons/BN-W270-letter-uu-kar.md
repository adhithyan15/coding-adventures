# ূ (-ū) — the sign under the দ of দূর

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C270-DUR-01, BN-SCRIPT-U-MATRA-01, BN-SCRIPT-C269-U-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say **দূর**, "far". Then write **তু** and **উ**: the *u* sign and the *u* letter you know.

## Script you'll notice: ূ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-C270-UU-KAR-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-U-MATRA-01, BN-LEX-C270-DUR-01, BN-SCRIPT-C269-U-01] -->

**ূ** — *-ū*, the long *u*, written under its consonant: **দূর**. Bengali says it the same as **ু**; the long sign is a spelling, mostly in words from Sanskrit, as **ী** is beside **ি**.

Set the print of **দূ** beside **দু**. Under **দু** hangs a small closed loop with a short slanting tail at its lower right. Under **দূ** hangs a rounded hook that is open on the right, like a C, with a short slanting tail at its lower end.

## Writing: ূ — copy the printed shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-C270-UU-KAR-01] -->

Put your pen beside **দূ** and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large: the **দ**, then the open hook under it, then its tail. Check the copy against the print: open, not closed like **ু**.

> This book does not yet tell you where to start ূ or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.
