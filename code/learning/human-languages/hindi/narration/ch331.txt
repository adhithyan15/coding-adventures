Hindi, chapter 331: Fasting and Offerings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
व्रत (vrat) — a fast; a vow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a festive procession, then the Hindi for a devotee.

You'll want to know: व्रत.
व्रत — vrat — "a fast; a vow".
दादी ने पूरे दिन व्रत रखा। dādī ne pūre din vrat rakhā. Grandmother fasted all day.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
व्रत (vrat) is Sanskrit vrata, "a vow, a rule of conduct" (Monier-Williams). व्रत रखना is to keep a fast, a vow to go without food.

Lesson 2 of 5.
आरती (ārtī) — an arati, worship with a lamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a devotee, then the Hindi for a fast.

You'll want to know: आरती.
आरती — ārtī — "an arati, worship with a lamp".
शाम को मंदिर में आरती हुई। śām ko mandir meṁ ārtī huī. In the evening there was an arati at the temple.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
आरती (ārtī) goes back to Sanskrit ārātrika (CDIAL 1315): a lamp waved in circles before a god while a song is sung.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an arati, worship with a lamp, then the Hindi for a hymn.

You'll want to know: प्रसाद.
प्रसाद — prasād — "a blessed offering (of food)".
पुजारी ने सबको प्रसाद बाँटा। pujārī ne sabko prasād bā̃ṭā. The priest handed out the blessed offering to everyone.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
प्रसाद (prasād) is Sanskrit prasāda, "grace, favour" (Monier-Williams): food first offered to a god and then shared out as a blessing.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a hymn, then the Hindi for a blessed offering (of food).

You'll want to know: पकवान.
पकवान — pakvān — "a festive dish; a delicacy".
माँ ने त्योहार के लिए कई पकवान बनाए। māṁ ne tyohār ke liye kaī pakvān banāe. Mother made several festive dishes for the festival.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
पकवान (pakvān) is Sanskrit pakvānna, pakva, cooked, and anna, food (Monier-Williams); in Hindi it is the rich, fried food of a feast day.
