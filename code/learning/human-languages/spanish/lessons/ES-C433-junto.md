# junto — together, or beside

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C302-AWAY-01, ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la calle*. You can put a thing **in** a street. Say that two
people did something **together** and see whether you have the word.

## You'll want to know: junto
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-05]; assesses=[] -->

**juntos** — together. **junto a** — next to, beside.

Same word, two jobs, and the little **a** is what separates them.

## Grammar Lens: the a decides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-05, ES-LEX-C302-AWAY-01] -->

| you say | it means |
|---|---|
| cenamos juntos | we had dinner together |
| junto a la puerta | next to the door |

Without **a**, it describes the people and agrees with them: *juntos*, *juntas*.
With **a**, it stops describing anybody and becomes a position — and then it
stops agreeing, staying **junto** whatever follows.

That is a small thing to watch, and it is the sort of small thing a reader
trips on: *las cajas, juntas* is a comment about the boxes; *junto a las cajas*
is where you are standing.
