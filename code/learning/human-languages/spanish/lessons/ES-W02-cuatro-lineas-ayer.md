# Four lines to one person — where the second needs the first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-WRITING-CONTROLLED-COMPOSITION] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last writing lesson asked for lines that were **correct**. This
one asks for lines that **hold together**, which is a different thing and a
harder one.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read this. A real one. You will need them in
a moment.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-3PL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can be
connected, and one long line can fail to be.

[PAUSE 2s] What makes writing connected is that a sentence stops standing on its
own — line two is about line one, and a reader who skipped line one would not
follow it.

[PAUSE 2s] Spanish gives you exactly two words for that at this point, and they
do opposite jobs. **y** adds one thing to another. **porque** puts one thing
*under* another and says it is the reason.

[PAUSE 2s] That is the whole difference between a list and a paragraph. A list
can be read in any order. A paragraph cannot.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-WRITING-CONNECTED-COMPOSITION]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-PLURAL, ES-WRITING-CONTROLLED-COMPOSITION] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** about yesterday, addressed to the person you
picked. Greet them, tell them two things you did, and close.

At least one line must use **y**, and at least one must use **porque**.

A worked example, from words you already hold:

> Hola.
> Ayer fui a la tienda y comí pan.
> Hablé con mi amiga porque tuve tiempo.
> Hasta luego.

[PAUSE 3s] Now write your own four. Do not copy the example — it is there to
show the **shape**, and its second and third lines are the only ones doing the
work.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-WRITING-CONNECTED-COMPOSITION] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Cover your first line and read the second on its own. Does it still
make sense without the first?

[PAUSE 2s] If it does, you wrote a **list**. That is not a failure — it is the
stage you were at one lesson ago — but go back and make one line depend on
another before you stop.

[PAUSE 2s] And read it as the person you named would. Writing for a reader you
can picture is the part that makes this a real task rather than an exercise, and
it is the part the exam will keep asking for at every level above this one.
