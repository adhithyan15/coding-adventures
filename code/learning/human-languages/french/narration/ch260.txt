French, chapter 260: What I Think, and So — je pense que, donc.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
je pense que — giving a view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to think". (Penser.) Then "I think". (Je pense.)
[pause 1 second]
A story says what happened. Giving reasons starts somewhere else: with what you think about it.

You'll want to know: je pense que.
je pense que — "I think that". It is the everyday opinion opener.
Je pense que le train est cher. — I think the train is expensive. Je pense que oui. — I think so., Je pense que non. — I don't think so.
The que cannot be dropped, as English drops its that. After it, the verb stays plain: je pense que c'est cher. Only a denial, je ne pense pas que…, makes careful French bend the verb that follows into a form a later chapter builds.
Two near neighbours do the same job. Je crois que, from croire, sounds a shade less sure. Je trouve que, from trouver, is "I find that", for a judgement you have made yourself: je trouve que le film est ennuyeux.
To hand the opinion back to a friend: Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu en penses? — "And you, what do you think of it?" The little en means "of it"; keep the whole question as one piece for now.

Grammar Lens: à mon avis.
You have l'avis, "a notice, an opinion". À mon avis is "in my opinion", and it puts your standpoint first:
À mon avis, le train est cher, mais le petit musée est sympathique.
Relaxed speech says pour moi for the same thing. Je pense que puts the opinion first; à mon avis says whose it is before you give it.

The word, taken apart.
penser is inherited, through Old French penser, from Latin pēnsāre, "to weigh carefully" (EtymDB 2.1), so a thought is a thing weighed. avis goes back to Old French avis, built on vis, "a view", which is inherited from Latin vīsus, "a look, an appearance" (EtymDB 2.1). À mon avis is "at my view": how the matter looks from where I stand.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: À mon avis, le film est ennuyeux.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "I don't think so". (Je pense que non.) Can the que be dropped? (No.) Which opener says whose view it is first? (À mon avis.) And what did Latin pēnsāre mean? (To weigh.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
donc, alors — the arrow that points forward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Give an opinion with a reason: "I think it's going to rain, because there's no sun." (Je pense qu'il va pleuvoir, parce qu'il n'y a pas de soleil.)
[pause 1 second]
Parce que runs from the claim back to its reason. This lesson runs the other way.

Grammar Lens: the two arrows.
The same two facts, in opposite orders:
J'ai pris le métro parce qu'il pleuvait. — I took the metro because it was raining. Il pleuvait, donc j'ai pris le métro. — It was raining, so I took the metro.
Parce que points back, from what happened to its cause. Donc points forward, from the cause to what followed, which is why the cause has to come first. Which one you reach for depends on which fact you want to say first: a choice about emphasis, not about grammar.
The tenses keep their jobs. Il pleuvait is the scene, the reason; j'ai pris is the event, the consequence.

Grammar Lens: alors, the chatty one.
alors can stand in the same place: Il pleuvait, alors j'ai pris le métro. It is lighter and more conversational, the "so" of a friend telling you about their day. Donc is the neutral one, as at home in a letter as in speech.
chatty: alors, neutral: donc.
Both point forward. Alors also keeps its first meaning, "then, at that time", so in a story it can do both jobs at once. And when donc opens a clause, its c is usually said: Donc, je reste.

The word, taken apart.
alors is à plus Old French lors, "then", which goes back to Latin illā hōrā, "at that hour" (EtymDB 2.1): a word about time that became a word about consequence, as English "then" did. donc is usually traced to Late Latin dunc, "then, so", whose own making is debated; EtymDB 2.1 follows it only as far as Middle French donc.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: back — J'ai pris le métro parce qu'il pleuvait.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: forward — Il pleuvait, donc j'ai pris le métro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, to a friend — Il pleuvait, alors j'ai pris le métro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Il faisait froid, alors je suis rentré.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: À mon avis, le train est cher, donc je prends le métro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which way does parce que point, and which way donc? (Parce que back to the cause; donc forward to the consequence.) Which of the two forward words is the chatty one? (Alors.) What did Latin illā hōrā mean? (At that hour.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — a view, and the arrows behind it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think so". (Je pense que oui.) Then "in my opinion". (À mon avis.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and the start of every argument you will make in French. An opinion, with je pense que, je crois que or à mon avis. A reason pointing back, with parce que. A consequence pointing forward, with donc or alors.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here is one view, built both ways:
Je prends le métro, parce que je pense que le train est cher. Je pense que le train est cher, donc je prends le métro.
Same facts, same view. The first starts from the decision; the second starts from the opinion that caused it.
