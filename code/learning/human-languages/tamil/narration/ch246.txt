Lesson 1 of 5.
கொண்டாட்டம் (koṇṭāṭṭam) — celebration, festivities.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a man, then the Tamil for a woman.

You'll want to know: கொண்டாட்டம்.
கொண்டாட்டம் — koṇṭāṭṭam — "celebration, festivities".

The word, taken apart.
கொண்டாட்டம் (koṇṭāṭṭam) is the noun of koṇṭāṭu, "to celebrate", usually analysed as native koṇṭu, "having taken", from koḷ (DEDR 2151), and āṭu, "to dance, to play" (DEDR 347).

Lesson 3 of 5.
கலாச்சாரம் (kalāccāram) — culture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for celebration, then the Tamil for firecrackers.

You'll want to know: கலாச்சாரம்.
கலாச்சாரம் — kalāccāram — "culture".

The word, taken apart.
கலாச்சாரம் (kalāccāram) is a modern coinage from Sanskrit kalā, "art" (CDIAL 2927), and ācāra, "conduct, custom" (CDIAL 1071). Which way kalā travelled is debated: CDIAL takes it from Dravidian, while DEDR files the native Tamil word for "art" under the verb "to learn" (DEDR 1297) and only compares kalā.

Lesson 4 of 5.
பழக்கம் (paḻakkam) — a habit, a custom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for firecrackers, then the Tamil for culture.

You'll want to know: பழக்கம்.
பழக்கம் — paḻakkam — "a habit, a custom".

The word, taken apart.
பழக்கம் (paḻakkam) is native Dravidian. DEDR 4000 lists Malayalam paḻakuka, "to be accustomed". It is the noun of paḻaku, "to become used to".
