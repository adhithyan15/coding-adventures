Portuguese, chapter 388: Pagar pelos erros.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
punir — to punish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the defence, then the Portuguese for the court sentence.

You'll want to know: punir.
punir — "to punish".
A lei pune quem conduz depois de beber. The law punishes people who drive after drinking.
Formal; ser punido — to be punished.

The word, taken apart.
punir is a learned borrowing from Latin pūnīre "to punish" (EtymDB), built on poena "penalty, punishment" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a pena — the penalty, the sentence served; the pity; the feather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the court sentence, then the Portuguese for to punish.

You'll want to know: a pena.
a pena — "the penalty, the sentence served; the pity; the feather".
Ele cumpriu uma pena de dois anos. He served a two-year sentence.
Que pena! — what a pity; vale a pena — it is worth it. Uma pena is also a feather.

The word, taken apart.
pena "penalty, pity" is inherited from Latin poena "penalty, punishment" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from Greek poinḗ "blood money, fine"; pena "feather" is a separate word, from Latin penna.

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to punish, then the Portuguese for the penalty.

You'll want to know: a prisão.
a prisão — "the prison; the arrest".
Ele passou cinco anos na prisão. He spent five years in prison.
Also the arrest: ordem de prisão — arrest warrant.

The word, taken apart.
prisão was borrowed from French prison (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin prehēnsiō "a seizing" (EtymDB), on prehendere "to seize" (Lewis & Short).

Condenar — to convict, to condemn.

The word, taken apart.
condenação is a learned borrowing from Latin condemnātiō "a condemning" (EtymDB), on condemnāre "to condemn", built on damnum "loss, damage" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o castigo — the punishment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the prison, then the Portuguese for the conviction.

You'll want to know: o castigo.
o castigo — "the punishment".
O castigo foi ficar sem telemóvel uma semana. The punishment was a week without a mobile phone.
For children: ficar de castigo — to be grounded.

The word, taken apart.
castigo is built on castigar "to punish" (EtymDB), inherited from Latin castīgāre "to correct, to punish", on castus "pure" (Lewis & Short).
