# ਕਰਨਾ (karnā) — "to do," the verb whose root you met in your first greeting

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-NAMASTE-01, PA-GRAMMAR-VERB-STEM-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The third verb of the chapter — and the one whose root has been
sitting inside a word you have said since your very first greeting.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-KA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-RA-01] -->

**ਕ** (*ka*) + **ਰ** (*ra*) + **ਨਾ** (*nā*) → **ਕਰਨਾ** (*karnā*). Two of the three
pieces are already yours to read.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-KARNA-05, PA-ETYMON-KR-DO-05]; assesses=[] -->

**ਕਰਨਾ** (*karnā*, "to do, to make") is from the Sanskrit root *√kṛ* ("to
do"), from PIE **\*kʷer-** — one of Sanskrit's most productive roots. You met its
family in Chapter 1's **ਨਮਸਤੇ**/**ਨਮਸਕਾਰ** (*namaskār* = *namas* + *kār*, "the
**making** of a bow") and in *karma* ("a thing **done**"). Strip *-nā* and the
stem is *kar-*, exactly as *bol-* and *rahi-* came out of their infinitives.
