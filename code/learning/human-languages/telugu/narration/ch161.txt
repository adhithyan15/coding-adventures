Telugu, chapter 161: To dance, To celebrate, To greet, To feel shy, To be surprised.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
నాట్యం చేయు (nāṭyaṁ cēyu) — to dance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to remember, then the Telugu for to agree.

You'll want to know: నాట్యం చేయు.
నాట్యం చేయు — nāṭyaṁ cēyu — "to dance".

The word, taken apart.
నాట్యం చేయు (nāṭyaṁ cēyu) pairs Sanskrit nāṭya, "dancing, dramatic art" (CDIAL 7046), with the Telugu ending -ṁ, and native cēyu, "to do" (DEDR 1957).

Lesson 2 of 5.
జరుపుకో (jarupukō) — to celebrate (a festival or occasion).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to agree, then the Telugu for to dance.

You'll want to know: జరుపుకో.
జరుపుకో — jarupukō — "to celebrate (a festival or occasion)".

The word, taken apart.
జరుపుకో (jarupukō) is native Dravidian. DEDR 2360 lists jarapu, "to spend or pass (time)", beside jarugu, "to happen, move on", with Kannada sari, "to move, go". The ending -kō means "for oneself" (DEDR 2151).

You'll want to know: పలకరించు.
పలకరించు — palakariñcu — "to greet".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of పలకరించు (palakariñcu) is uncertain. DEDR 3887 lists paluku, "to sound, answer, say", which it resembles, but not this verb itself.
