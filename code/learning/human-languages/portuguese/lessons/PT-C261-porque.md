# porque — because, at last

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-ACHO-QUE-01, PT-LEX-C84-PORQUE84-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I think so". (*Acho que sim.*) Then join two facts with the
joiner from the writing chapters: "it was cold, that's why I didn't go out".
(*Fazia frio, por isso não saí.*)

[PAUSE 1s] When you wrote four lines, this book had no word for *because*.
Here it is.

## You'll want to know: porque
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-PORQUE-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-02, PT-LEX-C143-VERBOS143-02, PT-LEX-C92-VIAGEM92-04] -->

**porque** — "because". It goes in front of the reason:

> *Não saí **porque** chovia.* — I didn't go out because it was raining.
> *Apanhei o autocarro **porque** fazia frio.* — I took the bus because it was
> cold.

Notice the tenses keep their jobs. *Não saí* and *apanhei* are events, in the
perfeito; the reasons, *chovia* and *fazia frio*, are scene, in the
imperfeito. A reason in a story is very often part of the scene.

## Grammar Lens: porque é que…?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-PORQUE-E-QUE-02]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-GOSTAR-DE-04] -->

The same word asks the question, and European Portuguese likes to ask it with
**é que** after it, "is it that":

> — ***Porque é que*** estudas português? — ***Porque*** gosto de Portugal.
> — Why are you learning Portuguese? — Because I like Portugal.

When the word stands alone, or ends the sentence, it takes an accent and the
stress moves: *Não vou.* — ***Porquê?*** — "I'm not going." — "Why?"

Brazil writes the question as two words, *por que*, and the lone one as *por
quê*; the answer is *porque* everywhere.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**porque** is two words grown together: **por**, "for", and **que**, "what,
that" (EtymDB 2.1 traces it to Old Portuguese *porque*, with Italian *perché* as its cousin). **que**
is inherited from Latin *quid*, "what" (EtymDB 2.1), and **por** is usually
traced to Latin *prō*, "for, on behalf of". So the question and the answer
are the same two words: "for what", and "for this".
