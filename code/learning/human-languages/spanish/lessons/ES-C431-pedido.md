# el pedido — what you asked for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-COMPRAR-07, ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say *comprar* and *la tienda*. Now say the **thing itself** —
the parcel that has not arrived yet — and see whether you have a word for it.

## You'll want to know: el pedido
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**el pedido** — the order.

Not the act of ordering: the **thing ordered**. The box on its way to you, the
line on the restaurant's docket, the number you quote on the phone.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

**el pedido** is the past participle of **pedir**, to ask for, used as a noun.
What was asked for. Spanish does this constantly, and once you see it you will
see it everywhere:

| verb | the noun made from it |
|---|---|
| pedir | el pedido |
| llamar | la llamada |
| entrar | la entrada |

You already hold **la entrada** for a ticket. It is *the entered thing* by the
same move. A participle that hardens into a noun is one of the cheapest ways
this language makes new words, and it costs you no new ending.
