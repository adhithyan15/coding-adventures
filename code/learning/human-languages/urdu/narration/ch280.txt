Urdu, chapter 280: Robbers on the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.

ڈاکو (ḍākū) — a bandit.

رات کو سڑک پر ڈاکو تھے۔ rāt ko saṛak par ḍākū the. There were bandits on the road at night.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بندوق (bandūq) — a gun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a bandit, then the Urdu for an armed robbery.

You'll want to know: بندوق.
بندوق — bandūq — "a gun".
ڈاکو کے پاس بندوق تھی۔ ḍākū ke pās bandūq thī. The bandit had a gun.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
بندوق (bandūq) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic bunduq, "a hazelnut", and so a pellet; the sense of a firearm is thought to have grown from the pellets it fired. It is feminine: بندوق تھی.

Lesson 4 of 5.
باندھنا (bāndhnā) — to tie up; to bind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an armed robbery, then the Urdu for a gun.

You'll want to know: باندھنا.
باندھنا — bāndhnā — "to tie up; to bind".
ڈاکوؤں نے چوکیدار کو باندھا۔ ḍākuoṅ ne chaukīdār ko bāndhā. The bandits tied up the watchman.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے رسی باندھی, us ne rassī bāndhī.

The word, taken apart.
باندھنا (bāndhnā) is inherited from Sanskrit bandhati, "binds" (CDIAL 9139). A person tied up takes کو and the verb stays masculine, چوکیدار کو باندھا; a thing tied agrees, رسی باندھی.

Lesson 5 of 5.
لٹیرا (luṭerā) — a robber; a looter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a gun, then the Urdu for to tie up.

You'll want to know: لٹیرا.
لٹیرا — luṭerā — "a robber; a looter".
اگر لٹیرے آئے تو ہم بھاگیں گے۔ agar luṭere āe to ham bhāgeṅ ge. If the robbers come, we will run.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
لٹیرا (luṭerā) is built on lūṭnā, "to plunder", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under luṭṭati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 11078), with the ending -erā for the one who does it.
