Sanskrit, chapter 482: At the Physician's.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चिकित्सति (cikitsati) — treats a patient.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lame, then the Sanskrit for hunchbacked.

You'll want to know: चिकित्सति.
चिकित्सति — cikitsati — "treats a patient".
वैद्यः रुग्णं बालं चिकित्सति। vaidyaḥ rugṇaṃ bālaṃ cikitsati. The physician treats the sick child.
Past (he …): सः रुग्णं बालं चिकित्सितवान्, saḥ rugṇaṃ bālaṃ cikitsitavān.

The word, taken apart.
चिकित्सति (cikitsati) is built on the desiderative of the root √cit, "to perceive, to care for", the word behind चिकित्सकः; MW gives "to treat medically, cure" (MW).

आतुरः (āturaḥ) MW divides it ā-tura and gives "suffering, sick (in body or mind)"; it names no root, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
निदानम् (nidānam) — the cause of an illness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for treats a patient, then the Sanskrit for a patient.

You'll want to know: निदानम्.
निदानम् — nidānam — "the cause of an illness".
वैद्यः रोगस्य निदानं जानाति। vaidyaḥ rogasya nidānaṃ jānāti. The physician knows the cause of the illness.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
निदानम् (nidānam) is built from ni and the root √dā, "to bind": MW's first sense is "a band, a halter", then "a first cause" and "the cause of a disease and enquiry into it, pathology" (MW).

The word, taken apart.
उपचारः (upacāraḥ) is built from upa, "near", and the root √car, "to move"; MW gives "attendance, service" and "attendance on a patient, medical practice" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
शल्यम् (śalyam) — a splinter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the cause of an illness, then the Sanskrit for medical treatment.

You'll want to know: शल्यम्.
शल्यम् — śalyam — "a splinter".
पादे शल्यम् अस्ति, अतः सः शनैः चलति। pāde śalyam asti, ataḥ saḥ śanaiḥ calati. There is a splinter in his foot, so he walks slowly.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
शल्यम् (śalyam) MW's first sense is "a dart, a lance", then "anything causing pain" and, in medicine, "anything lodged in the body, a splinter"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
