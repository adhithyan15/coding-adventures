# आप कैसे हैं? (āp kaise haiṁ?) — "how are you?"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C03-KAISE-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three words you already own, assembled into the first thing you ask
after hello.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-CONCEPT-C03-AAPKAISEHAIN-01]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C03-KAISE-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01] -->

**हैं** is **है** (*hai*, "is") + the *anusvāra* dot **ं** → **हैं** (*haiṁ*),
the nasalised plural. Everything else — **आप** (*āp*), **कैसे** (*kaise*) — you
have met.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-AAPKAISEHAIN-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-KAISE-01] -->

**आप कैसे हैं?** = **आप** (*āp*, respectful "you") + **कैसे** (*kaise*, "how") +
**हैं** (*haiṁ*, "are") — literally "**you how are?**" Hindi keeps the verb
**last**, exactly as in Chapter 2's *merā nām … hai*. And because *āp* is
grammatically plural (that is how Hindi shows respect), its verb is the plural
**haiṁ**, not the singular *hai*.

## Grammar Lens: respect is plural
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-AAPKAISEHAIN-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-KAISE-01] -->

To an elder or a stranger: **आप कैसे हैं?** To a friend (*tum*): **तुम कैसे हो?**
(*tum kaise ho?* — *ho* is the familiar "are"). To no one in particular you would
never drop to *tū*. Match the "you" to the verb: *āp … haiṁ*, *tum … ho*.
