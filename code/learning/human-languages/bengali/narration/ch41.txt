Bengali, chapter 41: Letters You Have Read All Along.
7 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ং (anusvar) — the ng in বাংলা.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ং and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write থ and ঞ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say বাংলা (bānglā), "Bengali". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ং.
ং — anusvar, the sound ng.
It is inside বাংলা (bānglā), the name of the language itself. The small mark right after বা closes that syllable with ng.

Writing: ং.
Put your pen on ং (anusvar) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at বাংলা and point at ং (anusvar) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ং (anusvar) three times, saying ng as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ঞ, then ং (anusvar), from memory]
[your turn — read: বাংলা aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ং? (anusvar, ng.) Which word did it come from? (বাংলা, bānglā, "Bengali".)

It ends মাফ (maf), "pardon", in মাফ করবেন, "excuse me". The word came from Persian, and there ফ (pha) is read as f.
