# el fin de semana — the week's end

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PREPARAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Spanish builds “weekend” as a transparent phrase: **el fin de
semana**, literally “the end of the week.” It gives a time for a plan: **voy a
preparar el postre este fin de semana**.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-FIN-DE-SEMANA]; assesses=[] -->

**Fin** continues Latin **fīnis**, “boundary” or “end.” **Semana** continues
Late Latin **septimāna**, the seven-day period. The little **de** joins them:
**fin de semana**. Learn the three words as one planning block.

## Grammar Lens: one weekend, many weekends
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-FIN-DE-SEMANA] -->

For one, say **el fin de semana**. In the plural, only the first noun changes:
**los fines de semana**. For a regular habit, **trabajo los fines de semana**;
for one upcoming period, **este fin de semana**.
