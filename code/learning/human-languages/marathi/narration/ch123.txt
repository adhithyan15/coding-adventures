Marathi, chapter 123: Need, Hope, Dream, To ask for, To decide.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गरज (garaj) — a need.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for wanted, then the Marathi for a wish.

You'll want to know: गरज.
गरज — garaj — "a need".

The word, taken apart.
गरज (garaj) is a loan from Arabic gharaḍ, "aim, purpose, need", which reached Marathi through Persian. Its Arabic root is gh-r-ḍ (Lane). Persian and Hindustani say gharaz.

Lesson 4 of 5.
मागणे (māgṇe) — to ask for.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for hope, then the Marathi for a dream.

You'll want to know: मागणे.
मागणे — māgṇe — "to ask for".

The word, taken apart.
मागणे (māgṇe) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mārgati, "seeks, requests" (CDIAL 10074). It is built on mārga, "track, path".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ठरवणे (ṭharavṇe) — to decide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a dream, then the Marathi for to ask for.

You'll want to know: ठरवणे.
ठरवणे — ṭharavṇe — "to decide".

The word, taken apart.
ठरवणे (ṭharavṇe) is the "make" form of Marathi ṭharṇẽ, "to be settled". CDIAL derives that verb from a reconstructed stabhira, "fixed, firm", rather than stharati, "is firm", and marks it as taken from Hindi (CDIAL 13680, 13742).
