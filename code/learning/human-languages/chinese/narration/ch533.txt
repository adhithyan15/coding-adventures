Lesson 1 of 6.
xiàngwǎng — to yearn for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for as one would wish it, then the Mandarin for to count on someone.

You'll want to know: xiàngwǎng.
xiàngwǎng — "to yearn for".
rúguǒ yǒu jīhuì wǒ jiù qù wǒ xiàngwǎng de dìfang — If I get the chance, I'll go to the place I've always longed for.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàngwǎng is built from xiàng 'towards' and 往 'to go', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xjangH and hjwangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which gives the first as the same word as its usual spelling and glosses the second 'to go to'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
希望 (xīwàng) — to hope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to count on someone, then the Mandarin for to yearn for.

You'll want to know: 希望.
希望 — xīwàng — "to hope".
我希望明天不下雨 wǒ xīwàng míngtiān bú xiàyǔ — I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.
The new character, in pieces: 希 (xī) — seven strokes in three pieces: a small cross of two strokes on top; a long bar with a long stroke falling to the left through it; and at the foot three strokes, a short vertical, a bar that turns down and hooks, and a long vertical through the middle. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
希望 (xīwàng) is built from 希 (xī) and 望 'to look out for', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xj+j and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to stop', 'to scatter' and, after an older dictionary, 'rare', and also with the second: 'to hope'.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
希 (xī) — a cross, a bar and a long stroke, then three at the foot.

Script — a cross, a bar and a long stroke, then three at the foot.
希 (xī).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, two strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, then a short stroke falling to the right across it, a small cross.
The middle piece, two strokes: a long bar, then a long stroke falling to the left from above the bar down through it.
The bottom piece, three strokes: a short vertical on the left, a bar that turns down the right side and hooks to the left, then a long vertical down through the middle, past the foot.
2 + 2 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 希 (xī), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 希 (xī) have? (Seven.) What is the last stroke? (A long vertical through the middle of the foot.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 希 (xī), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: kěwàng.
kěwàng — "to thirst for; to crave".
tā hěn kěwàng dāng yīshēng — He longs to be a doctor.

The word, taken apart.
kěwàng is built from kě 'thirsty' and 望 'to look out for', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khat and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'hunger and thirst'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
心想事成 (xīnxiǎng shìchéng) — may your wishes all come true.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 心想事成.
心想事成 — xīnxiǎng shìchéng — "may your wishes all come true".
祝你们好运心想事成 zhù nǐmen hǎoyùn xīnxiǎng shìchéng — Good luck to you all, and may all your wishes come true.

The word, taken apart.
心想事成 (xīnxiǎng shìchéng) is built from 心 'the heart', 想 'to wish', 事 'a matter' and 成 'done', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sim, sjangX, dzriH and dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): what the heart thinks of, may it be done.
