French, chapter 428: Une fuite dans la salle de bains.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la fuite — the leak; the escape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pillowcase, then the French for the bathrobe.

You'll want to know: la fuite.
la fuite — "the leak; the escape".
Il y a une fuite sous la douche. There is a leak under the shower.
Une fuite d'eau — a water leak; prendre la fuite — to run away.

The word, taken apart.
fuite goes back to Vulgar Latin fugita 'a fleeing', from Latin fugere 'to flee' (Lewis & Short): the water escapes.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: le robinet.
le robinet — "the tap, the faucet".
Le robinet d'eau chaude ne ferme plus. The hot tap will not turn off any more.
Ouvrir le robinet — to turn on the tap.

The word, taken apart.
robinet is usually traced to an old pet name for a sheep: early taps were often shaped like a sheep's head. EtymDB lists the word without a source.

The word, taken apart.
lavabo is a learned borrowing from Latin lavābō 'I will wash', from lavāre 'to wash' (EtymDB), first a word in a church prayer.

Lesson 5 of 5.
déboucher — to unblock; to uncork; to come out (onto).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the washbasin, then the French for to drip.

You'll want to know: déboucher.
déboucher — "to unblock; to uncork; to come out (onto)".
Le plombier a débouché la douche en dix minutes. The plumber unblocked the shower in ten minutes.
Déboucher une bouteille — to open a bottle; la rue débouche sur la place — the street comes out onto the square.

The word, taken apart.
déboucher is built from dé- and boucher 'to stop up' (EtymDB), which comes from Middle French bousche 'bundle of straw', the old way to plug a hole.
