Spanish, chapter 725: Amigos fieles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fiel — faithful, loyal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for sincere, then the Spanish for honesty.

You'll want to know: fiel.
fiel — "faithful, loyal".
Mi perro es muy fiel. My dog is very faithful.
Plural fieles; los fieles are the faithful in a church.

The word, taken apart.
fiel is inherited from Latin fidēlis "faithful" (EtymDB), built on fidēs "faith, trust".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: leal.
leal — "loyal".
Siempre ha sido leal a sus amigos. He has always been loyal to his friends.
Leal a — loyal to.

The word, taken apart.
leal is usually traced to Latin lēgālis "of the law", built on lēx "law"; English loyal and legal are the same Latin word by two roads.

Lesson 4 of 5.
reconciliarse — to make up, to be reconciled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for loyal, then the Spanish for loyalty.

You'll want to know: reconciliarse.
reconciliarse — "to make up, to be reconciled".
Después de la pelea, nos reconciliamos. After the fight, we made up.
Reconciliarse con alguien. Colloquially hacer las paces.

The word, taken apart.
reconciliarse is usually described as a learned borrowing from Latin reconciliāre "to bring together again", built on conciliāre "to unite".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: fingir.
fingir — "to pretend, to fake".
Fingió que no me veía. He pretended not to see me.
Fingir que + indicative. Spelling: finjo.

The word, taken apart.
fingir comes from Latin fingere "to shape, to invent, to feign" (EtymDB); English fiction and feign share it.
