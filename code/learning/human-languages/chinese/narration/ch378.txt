Mandarin Chinese, chapter 378: Noise Next Door.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zàoyīn — unwanted noise; din.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the area around (a place), then the Mandarin for a home near a good school.

You'll want to know: zàoyīn.
zàoyīn — "unwanted noise; din".
wǎnshang wàibian de zàoyīn tài dà le — The noise outside is far too loud at night.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zàoyīn is built from zào 'clamour' and 音 'a sound', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sawH and 'im (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a crowd shouting'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
生人 (shēngrén) — stranger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to grumble about, then the Mandarin for soundproofing (keeping sound out).

You'll want to know: 生人.
生人 — shēngrén — "stranger".
晚上有生人来你最好不要开门 wǎnshang yǒu shēngrén lái nǐ zuìhǎo bú yào kāimén — If a stranger comes at night, you'd better not open the door.

The word, taken apart.
生人 (shēngrén) is built from 生 'raw, new' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to grow'. The sense 'unfamiliar' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
wùyè — property management; the management office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for soundproofing (keeping sound out), then the Mandarin for a stranger.

You'll want to know: wùyè.
wùyè — "property management; the management office".
dēng huài le nǐ yīnggāi zhǎo wùyè — If the light's broken, you should call property management.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wùyè is built from wù 'a thing' and yè 'an undertaking', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjut and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the ten thousand things' and the second 'an affair'. 'Property', and the office that looks after it, are modern senses.
