Gujarati, chapter 230: Toothache and tears.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
દાઢ (dāḍh) — molar, back tooth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for heel, then the Gujarati for rib.

You'll want to know: દાઢ.
દાઢ — dāḍh — "molar, back tooth".
મારી દાઢ હવે નથી દુખતી. — mārī dāḍh have nathī dukhtī — "My back tooth doesn't hurt any more."
Not દાઢી, a beard.

The word, taken apart.
દાઢ (dāḍh) is a tadbhava. It comes from dāṁṣṭra, "fang, tusk", beside Sanskrit daṁṣṭra, "tusk, fang"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "molar" (CDIAL 6250).

Lesson 3 of 6.
આંસુ (ā̃su) — tear, teardrop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for molar, then the Gujarati for jaw.

You'll want to know: આંસુ.
આંસુ — ā̃su — "tear, teardrop".
તેની આંખમાં આંસુ નથી. — tenī ā̃khmā̃ ā̃su nathī — "There are no tears in her eyes."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
આંસુ (ā̃su) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from aśru, "tear", which picked up its nasal early, as the word for eye did (CDIAL 919).

શું દર્દી પીડાય છે? — śũ dardī pīḍāy che? — "Is the patient in pain?"

Lesson 5 of 6.
બેભાન થવું (bebhān thavũ) — to faint, to pass out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for tear, then the Gujarati for to suffer.

You'll want to know: બેભાન થવું.
બેભાન થવું — bebhān thavũ — "to faint, to pass out".
શું તે બેભાન થયો? — śũ te bebhān thayo? — "Did he faint?"
Word for word, to become senseless: બેભાન, bebhān, and થવું, thavũ.

The word, taken apart.
બેભાન થવું (bebhān thavũ) is built from bebhān, "senseless", and thavũ, "to become". Bebhān is usually explained as the Persian prefix be-, "without", joined to bhān, "awareness", usually traced to Sanskrit bhāna, "appearance" (CDIAL 9451); CDIAL lists no Gujarati form of the whole.
