Italian, chapter 341: Non sono d'accordo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il disaccordo — the disagreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for convincing, then the Italian for to persuade.

You'll want to know: il disaccordo.
il disaccordo — "the disagreement".
Siamo in disaccordo su quasi tutto. We disagree about almost everything.
Essere in disaccordo — to disagree. The opposite is essere d'accordo.

The word, taken apart.
disaccordo is built from dis- and accordo "agreement", which comes from accordare, a verb usually traced to Vulgar Latin accordāre, built on Latin cor, cordis "heart" (EtymDB): hearts that do not beat together.

Lesson 2 of 5.
obiettare — to object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to persuade, then the Italian for the disagreement.

You'll want to know: obiettare.
obiettare — "to object".
Posso obiettare una cosa? Il prezzo è troppo alto. May I object to one thing? The price is too high.
Obiettare che — to object that. Formal; in speech many people say non sono d'accordo.

The word, taken apart.
obiettare is a learned borrowing from Latin obiectāre "to throw against, to set before, to reproach" (Lewis & Short), formed on obicere, built from ob "against" and iacere "to throw".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ribattere — to answer back, to retort.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for to object, then the Italian for the objection.

You'll want to know: ribattere.
ribattere — "to answer back, to retort".
Lei ha subito ribattuto che non era vero. She immediately retorted that it was not true.
Literally to beat back: you hit the ball back over the net.

The word, taken apart.
ribattere is built from ri- "back, again" and battere "to beat", which is inherited from Latin battuere "to beat" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
contraddire — to contradict.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the objection, then the Italian for to answer back.

You'll want to know: contraddire.
contraddire — "to contradict".
Non mi piace quando mi contraddici davanti a tutti. I do not like it when you contradict me in front of everyone.
Conjugated like dire: contraddico, contraddici, contraddice. Contraddirsi is to contradict yourself.

The word, taken apart.
contraddire comes from Latin contrādīcere "to speak against" (EtymDB), built from contrā "against" and dīcere "to say"; Italian reshaped it on dire.
