## Grammar Lens: the fourth phrase to give up a word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04] -->

| what you already said | the word inside it |
|---|---|
| *interesante* | **interesar** |
| *mira* | **mirar** |
| *el punto* | **apuntar** |
| *sí, claro* | **claro** |

And **la tienda** joins them from the other direction: a shop turned out to be
this chapter's verb, a stretched awning.

**Claro** is the strongest case yet, because *sí, claro* is among the first
things anybody learns to say in Spanish and among the commonest things anybody
says. You were told back then that it means *bright* — and still never once
used it to describe a room, because agreeing with somebody asks nothing of the
colour sense.

The habit to keep: when a new word resembles a phrase you already use, look
for the connection before learning it twice.

## Grammar Lens: two verbs that both mean put
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01] -->

| the picture | the verb |
|---|---|
| into an inside | **meter** — *meter en la caja* |
| onto or at a place | **poner** — *poner en la mesa* |

*Meter* comes with **en** because "in" is the verb's whole meaning. If nothing
encloses the thing, *poner* is what you want.

The same test sorts *pintar de verde*, where **de** marks a resulting colour.
Spanish tells you a great deal through its prepositions, and each of these
verbs carries one it will not give up.
