# sambandh kī tālikā — relation, on one page

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-01, HI-LEX-C93-REL-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the word for *with* and the word for *in front of*, and say
what the two phrases have in the middle.

## Grammar Lens: the six
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-01, HI-LEX-C93-REL-02, HI-LEX-C93-REL-03] -->

| slot 3 | English | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| साथ | with | company |
| बिना | without | absence |
| पास | near | distance |

## Grammar Lens: and the other three
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C93-REL-05, HI-LEX-C93-REL-06] -->

| slot 3 | English | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| सामने | in front of | facing side |
| पीछे | behind | far side |
| लिए | for | benefit or purpose |

Six words, and **not one of them is a new piece of grammar**. Each is a noun
sitting in a possessive phrase. **दोस्त के साथ** is *the friend's company* and
**घर के पास** is *the house's nearness*, and Hindi is content to leave it at
that.

## Grammar Lens: the one word that never changes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01, HI-LEX-C93-REL-07] -->

Slot 2 is **के** every single time, whatever stands on either side of it.

| slot 1 | slot 2 | slot 3 |
|---|---|---|
| दोस्त | के | साथ |
| घर | के | पास |
| करने | के | लिए |

It is **के** rather than **का** or **की** because there is a further word
coming, and a possessive in front of a further word goes oblique. That is the
same rule the noun chapter taught and the same rule the possessive chapter
taught, applied a third time.

When slot 1 is a pronoun, slot 2 disappears into it: **मेरे पास**, **मेरे लिए**.
The **के** is still there — it is the **-ए** you can hear on **मेरे**.
