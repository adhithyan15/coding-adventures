## You'll want to know: 有钱
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C188-MONEY188-01]; assesses=[] -->

**有钱** — *yǒuqián* — "rich; well-off".

> **他很有钱** *tā hěn yǒuqián* — He's very well off.

The new character, in pieces: 钱 — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left the metal piece, five strokes; on the right two short bars, a long slanting hook, a stroke falling to the left across it, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**有钱** (*yǒuqián*) is built from 有 'to have' and 钱 'money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *hjuwX* and *dzjen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the second, after an older commentary, as money named after a spring, because it flows everywhere.
