## You'll want to know: नाश्ता
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C73-EATING-02]; assesses=[] -->

**नाश्ता** (*nāshtā*) — "breakfast".

Persian ناشتا (*nāshtā*) means "not having eaten, on an empty stomach". The meal
took its name from the emptiness it ends.

English did the identical thing and nobody notices any more: *breakfast* is the
**breaking** of the **fast**. Two languages, no contact, the same picture — the
night is a fast and the morning meal is what ends it.

The middle of the word stacks श on त with the vowel-killer ्, the same stack you
have written in **नमस्ते** and read in **स्टेशन**.

**नाश्ता** is also a doing word's companion: **नाश्ता करना** is "to have
breakfast", built the way **आराम करना** was.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

A place to eat, and the first meal of the day to eat there.
