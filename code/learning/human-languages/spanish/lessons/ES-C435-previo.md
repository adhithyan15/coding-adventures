## You'll want to know: previo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-03] -->

**previo / previa** — previous, prior, earlier.

**experiencia previa** — previous experience. **sin previo aviso** — without
prior warning, which is the other place you will meet it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-04] -->

**pre-** before, plus **-vio** from *via*, way. What lies on the way **before**.

The position is worth watching, because Spanish adjectives usually follow the
noun and this one does both:

| phrase | where it sits |
|---|---|
| experiencia previa | after |
| sin previo aviso | before |

*Sin previo aviso* is a fixed phrase and keeps the older order. Fixed phrases
preserve arrangements that ordinary speech has left behind, which is why they
so often look like exceptions — they are the older rule, kept whole.
