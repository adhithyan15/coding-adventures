Spanish, chapter 646: Llenar el depósito.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el surtidor — the petrol pump; the jet (of a fountain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tyre, then the Spanish for to get a puncture.

You'll want to know: el surtidor.
el surtidor — "the petrol pump; the jet (of a fountain)".
Pare junto al surtidor número tres. Stop next to pump number three.
At a petrol station each pump is un surtidor.

The word, taken apart.
surtidor is built on surtir "to supply; to spout". The origin of surtir is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
repostar — to refuel, to fill up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get a puncture, then the Spanish for the petrol pump.

You'll want to know: repostar.
repostar — "to refuel, to fill up".
Tenemos que repostar antes de salir. We have to fill up before we leave.
A Spain word; in Latin America people say cargar gasolina or echar gasolina.

The word, taken apart.
repostar is usually traced to reposte "store-room, larder", from Latin repositum "something put away", built on pōnere "to put"; it first meant taking on fresh supplies.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el depósito — the tank; the deposit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the petrol pump, then the Spanish for to refuel.

You'll want to know: el depósito.
el depósito — "the tank; the deposit".
Llene el depósito, por favor. Fill the tank, please.
At a bank el depósito is a deposit.

The word, taken apart.
depósito is a learned borrowing from Latin dēpositum "something laid down, entrusted", from dēpōnere "to put down" (Lewis & Short), built on pōnere "to put".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el combustible — the fuel.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for to refuel, then the Spanish for the tank.

You'll want to know: el combustible.
el combustible — "the fuel".
El combustible está muy caro. Fuel is very expensive.
As an adjective it means something that can burn.

The word, taken apart.
combustible is a learned borrowing built on Latin combūrere "to burn up, to consume" (Lewis & Short), through its past form combustus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el kilómetro — the kilometre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tank, then the Spanish for the fuel.

You'll want to know: el kilómetro.
el kilómetro — "the kilometre".
Faltan diez kilómetros. There are ten kilometres to go.
Often written km. El kilómetro cero is the point distances are measured from.

The word, taken apart.
kilómetro was borrowed from French kilomètre, a word made from Greek khílioi "a thousand" and métron "measure".
