Marathi, chapter 104: Hot, Cold, Slowly, Fast, Expensive.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गरम (garam) — hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for old (things), then the Marathi for bad.

You'll want to know: गरम.
गरम — garam — "hot".

The word, taken apart.
गरम (garam) is a loan, usually traced to Persian garm, "warm, hot".

Lesson 2 of 5.
थंड (thaṇḍ) — cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for bad, then the Marathi for hot.

You'll want to know: थंड.
थंड — thaṇḍ — "cold".

The word, taken apart.
थंड (thaṇḍ) is inherited. CDIAL lists it under Sanskrit stabdha, "stiff; solidified (as water)", through a form thaṇḍha, which it says was influenced by another word (CDIAL 13676). DEDR compares Tamil taṇ, "cold, cool" (DEDR 3045).

हळू — haḷū — "slowly".

The word, taken apart.
हळू (haḷū) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit laghu, "light; slight, quick", with its consonants swapped round (CDIAL 10896).

Lesson 5 of 5.
महाग (mahāg) — expensive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for slowly, then the Marathi for fast.

You'll want to know: महाग.
महाग — mahāg — "expensive".

The word, taken apart.
महाग (mahāg) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mahārgha, "high-priced" (CDIAL 9954). It is mahā, "great", and argha, "price".
