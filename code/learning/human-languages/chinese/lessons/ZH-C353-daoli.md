## You'll want to know: 道理
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C353-POINT353-02]; assesses=[] -->

**道理** — *dàoli* — "sense; the reason something makes sense".

> **你说得很有道理** *nǐ shuō de hěn yǒu dàoli* — You've got a point there.

The new character, in pieces: 道 — twelve strokes in two pieces: first a head piece of nine, two short strokes, a bar, a short left-falling stroke and a narrow box with two bars inside; then the walking piece of 这 and 过, three strokes, written last. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**道理** (*dàoli*) is built from 道 'a way' and 理 'order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *dawX* and *liX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a principle' and 'a road', and the second 'to put in order'. The second is said light here.
