French, chapter 50: Neck, Shoulder, Knee, Face, Blood.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le cou — the neck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a finger, then the French for a tooth.

You'll want to know: le cou.
le cou — "the neck".

The word, taken apart.
cou from Latin collum, "neck" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1) Old French said col; the l later melted into a vowel.

The word, taken apart.
épaule from Old French espalle from Late Latin spatula, "a flat piece, a shoulder blade" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1) The é- stands where an s once was: espaule, then épaule.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: le visage.
le visage — "the face".

The word, taken apart.
visage — built in Old French from vis, "face", with the ending -age. vis from Latin visus, "a look, an appearance", from videre, "to see". (EtymDB 2.1).
