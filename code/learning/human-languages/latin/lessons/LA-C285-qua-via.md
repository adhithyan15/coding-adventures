# quā viā — asking the way

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C52-ASK-01, LA-LEX-C107-URBS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask "where is the forum?" (*Ubi est forum?*)

[PAUSE 1s] That asks for a place. A traveller standing at a crossroads
needs something else: not where it is, but which way to go.

## You'll want to know: via, viae
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C285-VIA-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C238-LOC238-05] -->

**via, viae** — "a road, a way, a street". It is a feminine noun like
*porta*.

[PAUSE 2s] You own **quā**, "by which way", and its own lesson said that
*viā*, "road", was understood inside it. Now say it out loud:

> **Quā viā ad forum?** — By which road to the forum?

*Viā* is the ablative, "by road": the road you travel along.

## Grammar Lens: asking politely
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C285-QUA-VIA-02]; assesses=[LA-PRAGMATICS-QUAESO-02, LA-LEX-DICO-01, LA-LEX-C244-RES244-04] -->

A stranger deserves a softer opening. Put two pieces in front:

> **Quaesō, dīc mihi: quā viā ad thermās?**
> — Please, tell me: which way to the baths.

[PAUSE 2s] **Dīc** is "tell!", the command of *dīcō*, and it is short: no
ending at all. *Mihi*, "to me", you know from *ignōsce mihi*.

[PAUSE 1s] *Ad* plus the accusative is where you are heading: *ad forum*,
*ad thermās*.

## The word, taken apart: via
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C156-ACT156-03] -->

**via** is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European *\*wegh-*
(EtymDB 2.1), the root the *vehō* lesson named, "to carry, to convey";
Lewis & Short put the two words together. A road is where things are
carried. English *way* is usually given as a cousin, and English *via*, "by
way of", is this word's ablative, taken over whole.
