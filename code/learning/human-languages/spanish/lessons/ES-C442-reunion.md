# la reunión — made one again

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SALA, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-03, ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-03] -->

time and you know what a notice calls holding an event. Name the event itself.

## You'll want to know: la reunión
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SALA] -->

**la reunión** — the meeting.

*Una reunión de vecinos* is a residents' meeting, the one whose notice goes up
in the entrance hall. *La sala de reuniones* is the meeting room. The verb is
**reunirse**: *nos reunimos el jueves* — we are meeting on Thursday.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-04] -->

**re-** again, plus **unir** from Latin **ūnus**, one, plus **-ión**.

A *reunión* is **a making-one-again**. The word assumes the people were together
before and have scattered, which is why *reunirse* also covers a family
gathering and why English **reunion** sounds warmer than **meeting**.

Spanish uses the one word for both. *Una reunión de trabajo* and *una reunión
familiar* are the same noun, and only the adjective says which.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-04, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-03] -->

Put this next to **celebrarse**, which you met on a notice meaning *is held*.

> La reunión de vecinos se **celebrará** el jueves.

Both halves of that sentence are about a crowd. **Celebrar** is built on
*celeber*, thronged; **reunión** is built on making people one again. The
sentence says, twice over, that people are being brought together — and neither
word mentions anybody doing the bringing.

That doubling is ordinary in official Spanish. Once you can hear it, a notice
stops being a wall of formal vocabulary and becomes two or three plain ideas
said in their dressed-up clothes.
