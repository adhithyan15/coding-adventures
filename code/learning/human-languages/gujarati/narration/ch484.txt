Gujarati, chapter 484: Finding a Cure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઇલાજ (ilāj) — a cure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to writhe, then the Gujarati for a burning feeling.

You'll want to know: ઇલાજ.
ઇલાજ — ilāj — "a cure".
આ રોગનો ઇલાજ છે, ચિંતા ન કરો. ā rogno ilāj che, cintā na karo. There is a cure for this illness; don't worry.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઇલાજ (ilāj) is usually traced to Arabic ʿilāj, "treatment, cure", which reached Gujarati through Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉપચાર (upcār) — a remedy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a burning feeling, then the Gujarati for a cure.

You'll want to know: ઉપચાર.
ઉપચાર — upcār — "a remedy".
દાદી શરદી માટે આદુનો ઉપચાર કરે છે. dādī śardī māṭe āduno upcār kare che. Grandmother treats a cold with ginger.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઉપચાર (upcār) is a tatsama, Sanskrit upacāra, "approach, service, attendance", as Monier-Williams records it; the medical sense grew from attending on the sick.

Lesson 3 of 5.
નિદાન (nidān) — a diagnosis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a cure, then the Gujarati for a remedy.

You'll want to know: નિદાન.
નિદાન — nidān — "a diagnosis".
દાક્તરે રોગનું સાચું નિદાન કર્યું. dāktare rognũ sācũ nidān karyũ. The doctor made the right diagnosis.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
નિદાન (nidān) is a tatsama, Sanskrit nidāna, which Monier-Williams gives as "a band" and as a first cause; the old medicine used it for finding the cause of an illness.

Lesson 4 of 5.
મટાડવું (maṭāḍvũ) — to cure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a remedy, then the Gujarati for a diagnosis.

You'll want to know: મટાડવું.
મટાડવું — maṭāḍvũ — "to cure".
આ દવા તાવ મટાડે છે. ā davā tāv maṭāḍe che. This medicine cures fever.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે રોગ મટાડ્યો, teṇe rog maṭāḍyo.

The word, taken apart.
મટાડવું (maṭāḍvũ) is the causative of મટવું (maṭvũ), "to be cured", which CDIAL lists, "to be rubbed out", under Sanskrit mṛṣṭa, "rubbed" (CDIAL 10299).
