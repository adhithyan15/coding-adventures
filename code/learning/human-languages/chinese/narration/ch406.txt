Mandarin Chinese, chapter 406: A Busy Platform.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yōngjǐ — crowded.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the last bus or train, then the Mandarin for to call at a station.

You'll want to know: yōngjǐ.
yōngjǐ — "crowded".
dìtiě tài yōngjǐ le wǒmen zuò chūzūchē qù — The metro's too crowded: we'll take a taxi.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yōngjǐ is built from yōng 'to hold close' and jǐ 'to squeeze', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jowngX and tsej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hold in the arms' and the second 'to push aside'. Those forms would regularly give yǒng and jī: the tones of yōngjǐ are irregular.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
来回 (láihuí) — round trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to call at a station, then the Mandarin for crowded.

You'll want to know: 来回.
来回 — láihuí — "round trip".
cóng zhèr dào jīchǎng yí ge láihuí yào liǎng ge xiǎoshí — A round trip from here to the airport takes two hours.

The word, taken apart.
来回 (láihuí) is built from 来 'to come' and 回 'to go back', both inherited from Middle Chinese, loj and hwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to arrive' and the second 'to turn'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
gǎiqiān — to change a booking; to change a ticket to another train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for crowded, then the Mandarin for a round trip.

You'll want to know: gǎiqiān.
gǎiqiān — "to change a booking; to change a ticket to another train".
wǒ xiǎng gǎiqiān míngtiān de huǒchē — I'd like to change to tomorrow's train.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǎiqiān is built from gǎi 'to alter' and qiān 'to sign'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese kojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to change'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
xínglijià — luggage rack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to change a booking, then the Mandarin for a motor car.

You'll want to know: xínglijià.
xínglijià — "luggage rack".
zhège xínglijià hěn gāo — This luggage rack is very high.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xínglijià is built from xíngli 'luggage' and jià 'a frame', all inherited from Middle Chinese, haeng, liX and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last as the frame of a house.
