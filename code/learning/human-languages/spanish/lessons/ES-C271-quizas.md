# quizás — saying it without claiming it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] An argument needs one more thing: a way to put an idea forward while admitting you might be wrong.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-QUIZAS]; assesses=[] -->

**quizás** — "maybe, perhaps."

It is a fossilised question. Latin **qui sapit** — *"who knows?"* — said often
enough that it stopped being a question and became a word meaning *perhaps*. The
*sapit* is the same root as **savoir**, **sapient** and, at some distance,
**sage**: knowing.

English did the identical thing twice over. *Perhaps* is *per* + *hap*, "by
chance"; *maybe* is *may be*. Every language seems to build its hedges by wearing
down a phrase that once admitted ignorance out loud.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SEMANTIC-HEDGE-EPISTEMIC]; assesses=[] -->

This is a **hedge**: it lowers your commitment without lowering your meaning.
*Es difícil* claims. *Quizás es difícil* offers.

Set beside what you already have, you now hold three settings on the same dial —
*en mi opinión* marks a claim as yours, *quizás* marks it as uncertain, and a
bare statement claims outright.
