Lesson 2 of 5.
cēna, cēnae — dinner, the main meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to rejoice, then the Latin for food.

You'll want to know: cēna, cēnae.
cēna, cēnae — "dinner, the main meal".

The word, taken apart.
cēna is inherited. An older spelling was caesna (Lewis & Short), and it is usually traced through Proto-Italic kertsnā, "a portion", to Proto-Indo-European (s)ker-, "to cut" (EtymDB 2.1): the meal as one's share.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prandium, prandiī — lunch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for food, then the Latin for dinner.

You'll want to know: prandium, prandiī.
prandium, prandiī — "lunch".

The word, taken apart.
prandium probably began as "the early meal". Its first part is an old word for "early", which Lewis & Short compare with Doric Greek prān, "early". The second part is often taken as the root of edō, "to eat", but that is not certain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ientāculum, ientāculī — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dinner, then the Latin for lunch.

You'll want to know: ientāculum, ientāculī.
ientāculum, ientāculī — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
ientāculum is built on ientō, "to have breakfast" (Lewis & Short), with -culum, an ending for a thing. Where ientō comes from is uncertain.
