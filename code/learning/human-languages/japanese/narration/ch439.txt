Japanese, chapter 439: Brave and Timid.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おくびょう (okubyō) — timid; cowardly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for leisurely, then the Japanese for to act big.

You'll want to know: おくびょう.
おくびょう — okubyō — "timid; cowardly".
うちの いぬは おくびょうです。 — uchi no inu wa okubyō desu — "Our dog is timid."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
おくびょう (okubyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are oku, "timidity" (Middle Chinese 'ik) and byō, "sick" (bjaengH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to act big, then the Japanese for timid.

You'll want to know: よわむし.
よわむし — yowamushi — "a weakling".
よわむしと 言わないで ください。 — yowamushi to iwanaide kudasai — "Please do not call me a weakling."

The word, taken apart.
よわむし (yowamushi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: yowa, "weak (yowai)" and mushi, "insect" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
