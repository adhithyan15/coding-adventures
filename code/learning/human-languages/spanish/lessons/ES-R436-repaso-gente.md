## Grammar Lens: the cycle is now complete
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02, ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-05] -->

| step | verb |
|---|---|
| I hand it over | **presto** |
| you go and get it | **recoges** |
| you give it back | **devuelves** |

Six chapters ago you could buy a thing and pay for it. You can now describe its
whole life: ordered, delayed, collected, lent, returned.

**prestar** is the lender's verb only. The borrower says **pedir prestado**,
which contains the *pedir* under **el pedido** from the first chapter of this
run — so the cycle closes on the word it opened with.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-01, ES-LEX-PAN-01, ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-03] -->

**Two of these you could have guessed and did not need to be told.**
**compañero** was fully explained when you learned *pan*, years of pages ago in
book time — *com-* plus *pānis*, one you share bread with — and all this chapter
did was hand you the word the story was about. **ofrecer** unfolds into *carry
toward*.

The other three are plainer, and that is the honest proportion: some words open,
some do not, and the only way to know is to try.
