French, chapter 486: Nuits agitées.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'insomnie — insomnia, sleeplessness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for convalescence, then the French for to look after.

You'll want to know: l'insomnie.
l'insomnie — "insomnia, sleeplessness".
Quand je suis stressé, je souffre d'insomnie. When I am stressed, I suffer from insomnia.
Feminine. Often plural: avoir des insomnies.

The word, taken apart.
l'insomnie is a learned borrowing from Latin insomnia "sleeplessness" (EtymDB), built on somnus "sleep" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: ronfler.
ronfler — "to snore".
Mon colocataire ronfle très fort. My flatmate snores very loudly.
Also of an engine: le moteur ronfle.

The word, taken apart.
ronfler goes back to Old French ronfler (EtymDB); it is usually taken to imitate the sound, perhaps with help from souffler "to blow".

Lesson 3 of 5.
le cauchemar — nightmare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for insomnia, then the French for to snore.

You'll want to know: le cauchemar.
le cauchemar — "nightmare".
Cette nuit, j'ai fait un cauchemar terrible. Last night I had a terrible nightmare.
Faire un cauchemar — to have a nightmare.

The word, taken apart.
le cauchemar comes from Old French cauquemare (EtymDB), built from caucher "to press", from Latin calcāre "to tread", and Middle Dutch mare "a night ghost", a Germanic word for a spirit that sits on the sleeper.

Lesson 4 of 5.
bâiller — to yawn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to snore, then the French for nightmare.

You'll want to know: bâiller.
bâiller — "to yawn".
Les élèves bâillent pendant le cours de l'après-midi. The pupils yawn during the afternoon lesson.
Do not mix it up with bailler, an old word for giving.

The word, taken apart.
bâiller is inherited from Old French baaillier, from Vulgar Latin bataclāre (EtymDB), built on batāre "to gape".

The word, taken apart.
la sieste was borrowed from Spanish siesta (EtymDB), from Latin sexta (hōra) "the sixth hour", that is noon.
