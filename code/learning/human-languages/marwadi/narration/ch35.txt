Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 35: The Ten Digit Signs.
10 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 10.
० १ २ (0 1 2) — the first three digits.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the first three figures until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Recall the numbered-price payoff, then write ten, twenty and a hundred in words.

Script — the first three figures.
Everything this chapter has written so far has been a number in words. A Rajasthani stall does not label anything in words. It writes figures, and Devanagari has its own ten.
० — zero १ — one २ — two.
Look at where the ink sits. Each of these hangs from a stroke along the top, the same head-line that carries क and न and every other letter in this book. That is the quickest way to tell a Devanagari digit from a Latin one on a mixed sign.

Guided Practice.
Trace ०, १, २ three times each. Say the Marwadi word for the second and third as you write them.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Unicode Devanagari chart; Omniglot, Numbers in Rajasthani.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]

Lesson 3 of 10.
३ ४ ५ (pāṅch) (3 4 5) — three digits, two of them traps.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — three more figures until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Write very, write three in words, then write the digit for one.

Script — three more figures.
३ — three ४ — four ५ (pāṅch) — five.
Take them one at a time and set each beside the Latin figure a reader of this book already knows.
३ gives little trouble. ४ and ५ (pāṅch) give a great deal: a reader who glances at ४ sees an eight, and a reader who glances at ५ (pāṅch) sees a four. Neither guess is right, and both are wrong in the direction that matters at a counter.
Read them slowly for now. Speed on these two is worth nothing.

Guided Practice.
Trace ३, ४, ५ three times each, saying tīn, chār, pāṅch as you go. Then write ४ and ५ side by side and say aloud which is which without looking at this page.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Unicode Devanagari chart; Omniglot, Numbers in Rajasthani.

Write the digit for five, then the digit for four.

Lesson 5 of 10.
६ ७ ८ (6 7 8) — the last two traps.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the last three of the traps until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Write the too-expensive answer, then write the digits for four and five.

Script — the last three of the traps.
६ — six ७ — seven ८ — eight.
६ is close enough to its Latin cousin that a reader guesses it correctly. The other two are not.
७ carries a hook that pulls the eye towards a nine. ८ is closed and round and looks more like a letter than a figure. Both stand next to ६ here on purpose: a trap is easiest to learn beside something safe.

Guided Practice.

Lesson 7 of 10.
९ (9) — the tenth digit.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the tenth figure until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Say a little, then write the digits for seven and eight.

Script — the tenth figure.
९ (9) — nine — no.
Like ३ and ६, this one a Latin eye can very nearly guess, and unlike ७ the guess is right.
With ९ (9) the set is closed. Ten digit signs, taught across four lessons, and the eye can now read any figure a stall prints as far as the words go.
Write ९ (9) and नो side by side. The figure and the word for nine share nothing at all — which is the whole reason the figures had to be taught separately.

Guided Practice.
Trace ९ (9) three times. Then write the whole run ० १ २ (0 1 2) ३ ४ ५ (pāṅch) (3 4 5) ६ ७ ८ (6 7 8) ९ (9) (the ten Devanagari digits) once, slowly, saying the Marwadi word for each from one onward.

Wrap-up Recall.
