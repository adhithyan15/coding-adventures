Mandarin Chinese, chapter 327: Heroes and Emperors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
名人 (míngrén) — famous person; well-known figure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to found something new, then the Mandarin for an ancient civilization.

You'll want to know: 名人.
名人 — míngrén — "famous person; well-known figure".
他是一个世界名人 tā shì yí ge shìjiè míngrén — He's famous all over the world.

The word, taken apart.
名人 (míngrén) is built from 名 'a name' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjieng and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a name'.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yīngxióng is built from yīng and xióng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jaeng and hjuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a flower' and 'outstanding', and the second 'male' as against female.

You'll want to know: huángdì.
huángdì — "emperor".
tā shí suì de shíhou dāng le huángdì — He became emperor when he was ten.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huángdì is built from huáng and dì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwang and tejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a sovereign' and 'great', and calls the second the title of one who rules all under heaven.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎzhàng — to fight a war; to go into battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a hero, then the Mandarin for an emperor.

You'll want to know: dǎzhàng.
dǎzhàng — "to fight a war; to go into battle".
gǔdài de shíhou zhè liǎng ge guójiā chángcháng dǎzhàng — In ancient times these two countries were often at war.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎzhàng is built from 打 'to strike' and zhàng 'weapons', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and drjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hit' and the second 'arms'. Middle Chinese taengX would not regularly give dǎ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zhànzhēng — war.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an emperor, then the Mandarin for to go into battle.

You'll want to know: zhànzhēng.
zhànzhēng — "war".
zhànzhēng de shíhou yéye hái shì yí ge shàonián — During the war my grandpa was still a teenager.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhànzhēng is built from zhàn and zhēng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyenH and tsreang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to fear' and the second 'to compete'. The sense 'to fight' for the first is not given there.
