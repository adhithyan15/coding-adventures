German, chapter 256: Finding the Way — wie komme ich zum Bahnhof.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
wie komme ich zum Bahnhof — asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
To get a stranger's attention in the street, say Entschuldigung!

You'll want to know: wie komme ich zum Bahnhof.
Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof? — "Excuse me, how do I get to the station?"
German asks with kommen: literally "how do I come to the station?" English says "get"; German keeps the plain verb of coming.
Two short follow-ups help:
Ist das weit? — Is it far? Danke schön! — Thank you.
To a stranger, keep to Sie: Können Sie mir helfen?

Grammar Lens: zum or zur.
zu, "to", takes the dative, and it fuses with the article:
zum = zu dem, for der and das words: zum Bahnhof, zum Museum zur = zu der, for die words: zur Post, zur Apotheke, zur Haltestelle.
So the gender you learned with each noun decides the little word in front of it. Der Bahnhof, zum Bahnhof. Die Post, zur Post.
In Switzerland you will hear Exgüsi in dialect for "excuse me", from French excusez; Entschuldigung is understood everywhere.

The word, taken apart.
kommen from Old High German kweman from Proto-Germanic kwemaną, "to come" (EtymDB 2.1): the same verb as English come. zu is usually traced to Old High German zuo, a cousin of English to; EtymDB lists zu without a parent, so that is given as the usual account.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Wie komme ich zur Post? Then zum Museum, zur Haltestelle]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the follow-up: Ist das weit?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"How do I get to the station?" (Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?) Zum is short for what? (Zu dem.) "To the post office"? (Zur Post, because it is die Post.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
Gehen Sie geradeaus — following the answer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask the way to the station. (Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?)
[pause 1 second]
Now the stranger answers, and you have to follow.

You'll want to know: links.
You have geradeaus, straight on, and rechts, right. The missing one is links, "left": links, with a short i.
geradeaus — straight on rechts — right links — left.
German speakers often add nach in front: nach links, nach rechts, "to the left, to the right". Both ways are standard.

Grammar Lens: the Sie command, verb first.
A stranger will tell you the way with Sie. The polite command puts the verb first, in its Sie form, and Sie straight after it:
Gehen Sie geradeaus. — Go straight on. Gehen Sie bis zur Ampel, dann links. — Go as far as the lights, then left. Nehmen Sie die U-Bahn. — Take the underground.
Bis zur Ampel is bis + zur: as far as the lights. Bis zur Kreuzung, as far as the crossroads.
The answer may end with the place itself: Der Bahnhof ist gleich gegenüber, the station is right opposite.
With a friend, the du command you met in chapter forty-six drops the pronoun: Geh geradeaus! With a stranger, keep the Sie.

The word, taken apart.
links comes from Middle High German linc, "left", and EtymDB 2.1 sets Old High German linka, "the left hand", beside it; further back its origin is not settled. rechts is built on recht, which EtymDB traces through Old High German reht to Proto-Germanic rehtaz, "right, direct", the parent of English right. The -s at the end of both is usually explained as an old adverb ending, the one that also sits in abends, "in the evening".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: geradeaus, rechts, links]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Gehen Sie geradeaus.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Gehen Sie bis zur Ampel, dann links.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Nehmen Sie die U-Bahn.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: to a friend: Geh geradeaus, dann rechts!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Left"? (Links.) "Go straight on", to a stranger? (Gehen Sie geradeaus.) Where does the verb stand in a command? (First.) "As far as the lights"? (Bis zur Ampel.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — the way there.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask the way to the pharmacy. (Wie komme ich zur Apotheke?) Die Apotheke, so zur.

What you've built.
Two lessons, and a whole exchange in the street. You ask with wie komme ich, and the gender of the place picks zum or zur. The answer comes back as a Sie command with the verb first, and three short words do much of the work: geradeaus, rechts, links.
[pause 2 seconds]
One exchange, both halves:
Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Museum? Gehen Sie geradeaus bis zur Kreuzung, dann rechts. Das Museum ist gleich gegenüber.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
