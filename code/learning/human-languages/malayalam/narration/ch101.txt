Malayalam, chapter 101: Build and Height.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
കുറിയ (kuṟiya) — short, of a person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say small. Then say he.

You'll want to know.
കുറിയ — kuṟiya — short, said of a person's height.
അവൻ കുറിയ കുട്ടിയാണ്. — avan kuṟiya kuṭṭiyāṇŭ — "He is a short child."

The word, taken apart.
Put it beside the word you already have and the shape is the same.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: ചെറിയ. romanized: ceṟiya.
word: കുറിയ. romanized: kuṟiya.
Same റ, same -ിയ ending, same job in the sentence: in front of the noun, unchanged, exactly as the adjective chapter said.
What differs is what each one may describe. ചെറിയ is small — a small house, a small cup. കുറിയ (kuṟiya) is short in the sense of not tall, and it is a person's height it measures.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 3 seconds]
What is short, of a person? (കുറിയ (kuṟiya).) Which word you already had is built the same way? (ചെറിയ.) And where does it stand? (In front of the noun, unchanged.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
മെലിഞ്ഞ (meliñña) — thin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say short, of a person. Where does it stand?

You'll want to know.
മെലിഞ്ഞ — meliñña — thin, said of a person's build.
മെലിഞ്ഞ കുട്ടി — meliñña kuṭṭi — "a thin child."

The word, taken apart.
The ending you have met; the shape before it you have not.
വലിയ, നല്ല, പുതിയ, പഴയ, ചെറിയ, and now കുറിയ (kuṟiya) — every adjective the book has put in front of a noun ends in -a, and so does this one. That is the whole requirement, and മെലിഞ്ഞ (meliñña) meets it.
What is different is what comes in front of the -a. The others end -ിയ; this one ends in a doubled ഞ്ഞ. That changes nothing about how it is used — it still stands before the noun and still does not change:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
കുറിയ കുട്ടി, kuṟiya kuṭṭi.
മെലിഞ്ഞ കുട്ടി, meliñña kuṭṭi.

Lesson 3 of 5.
തടിച്ച (taṭicca) — stout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say a thin child.

You'll want to know.
തടിച്ച — taṭicca — stout, heavily built.
തടിച്ച കുട്ടി — taṭicca kuṭṭi — "a stout child."

The word, taken apart.
This is the far end of the last word, and the book has done that before: one quality, then its opposite, so a single axis costs you two words and not four.
Three shapes, one ending, one behaviour:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
adjective: കുറിയ (kuṟiya). ends: -ിയ.
adjective: മെലിഞ്ഞ (meliñña). ends: -ഞ്ഞ.
adjective: തടിച്ച (taṭicca). ends: -ച്ച.
Every one of them closes on -a, stands in front of the noun, and does not change. The shape in front of the -a varies and never matters.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ഉയരം (uyaraṁ) — height.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say short, of a person. Then say the word for there is.

You'll want to know.
ഉയരം — uyaraṁ — height.
എനിക്ക് ഉയരമുണ്ട്. — enikku uyaramuṇṭŭ — "To me, height exists" — I am tall.

The word, taken apart.
There is no single word for tall in this book, and this is how you say it anyway.
കുറിയ (kuṟiya) was an adjective, so it went in front of a noun. ഉയരം (uyaraṁ) is not an adjective — it is a noun, height — so it takes the other machine you already own: the one where a thing exists to someone.
എനിക്ക് ഉയരമുണ്ട്. — enikku uyaramuṇṭŭ — to-me height there-is — I am tall.
That frame is not new. The chapter that gave you ഉണ്ട് used it to say that someone has a thing, and the chapter on എനിക്ക് gave you the person the having happens to. All this chapter does is put a new noun into it.
And the ം does what it always does when something follows it — it takes the full letter so it can carry what comes next: ഉയരം (uyaraṁ) + ഉണ്ട് becomes ഉയരമുണ്ട്.

Lesson 5 of 5.
(short, thin, stout, and height) (uyaravuṁ kuṟiyayuṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say short, thin, stout, and height.

Grammar Lens: three of them go in front, and one cannot.
Three of this chapter's four words are adjectives, and all three behave the way every adjective in the book has:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
adjective: കുറിയ. before the noun: കുറിയ കുട്ടി (kuṟiya kuṭṭi).
adjective: മെലിഞ്ഞ. before the noun: മെലിഞ്ഞ കുട്ടി (meliñña kuṭṭi).
adjective: തടിച്ച. before the noun: തടിച്ച കുട്ടി (taṭicca kuṭṭi).
They end -ിയ, -ഞ്ഞ and -ച്ച — three different shapes, all closing on -a, none of them changing for the noun behind it.
ഉയരം (uyaraṁ) is the odd one, and not because it is irregular. It is a noun, so there is nothing for it to stand in front of. It uses the frame you were given for having:
എനിക്ക് ഉയരമുണ്ട്. — enikku uyaramuṇṭŭ — to-me height there-is — I am tall.

What you've built.
The book still has no single word for tall. It has a noun for height and a way to say that someone has it, and between them that is the whole meaning.
That is worth noticing rather than papering over: ചെറിയ and കുറിയ (kuṟiya) are almost the same word and do not describe the same thing — one is small, said of objects, the other short, said of people — and tall is not the mirror of either. You reach it by a different route.
