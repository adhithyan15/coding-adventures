## Grammar Lens: the guess, and the one that failed
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-04, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-04] -->

| word | comes apart? |
|---|---|
| **el cargador** | yes — cargar + **-dor** |
| **la batería** | no — built in French, not here |

Four chapters have now handed you **-dor**, **-ería** and **-ero**, and
*batería* is the first word that wore an ending and did not come apart. Do not
read that as a warning against guessing. A guess that fails costs nothing; not
guessing costs every word you could have read without help.

**el taller** and **el garaje** are the pair to keep straight: English calls
both *garage*, Spanish does not. You park in one and your car is repaired in
the other.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-01, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-03, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-04] -->

**You could name machines and could not say anything about one that had
stopped.** *La lavadora* and *el ordenador* were yours; the place they go, the
general word for the kind, the part that is missing, the battery that is flat
and the charger you lent somebody were not.

That is most of what a person actually says about a device — and almost none of
it is in a beginner's dialogue, because beginners' dialogues are about things
that work.
