Latin, chapter 130: Wave, Ice, Hail, Thunder, Lightning.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
unda, undae — a wave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rock, then the Latin for a river bank.

You'll want to know: unda, undae.
unda, undae — "a wave".

The word, taken apart.
unda is inherited, usually traced to the Proto-Indo-European root wed-, "water", in a form with an n. Greek hydōr, "water", and English water are cousins (Lewis & Short).

The word, taken apart.
glaciēs is related to gelū, "frost", and both are usually traced to Proto-Indo-European gel-, "cold, to freeze" (EtymDB 2.1). English cold is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 7.
fulgur, fulguris — lightning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for hail, then the Latin for thunder.

You'll want to know: fulgur, fulguris.
fulgur, fulguris — "lightning".

The word, taken apart.
fulgur is built on fulgeō, "to flash, to lighten" (Lewis & Short), which is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European bhleg-, "to shine, to burn". fulmen, "a thunderbolt", is from the same root.
