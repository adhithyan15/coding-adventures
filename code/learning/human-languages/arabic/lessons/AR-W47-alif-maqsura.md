# ى (alif maqṣūra) — the letter that ends إلى

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C46-DAL-01, AR-SCRIPT-C47-ALIF-HAMZA-BELOW-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write د and إ once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **إلى اللقاء** (*ilā l-liqāʾ*), "see you".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ى
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SCRIPT-C47-ALIF-MAQSURA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ى** — *alif maqṣūra*, a long *ā* at the very end of a word.

It ends **إلى** (*ilā*) in **إلى اللقاء**. It looks like **ي** with its two dots
taken away, and that is the whole trick: no dots, no *y*. It only ever stands at
the end of a word, and there it is read as a long *ā*.

## Writing: ى
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C47-ALIF-MAQSURA-01] -->

- **1.** start here: curve from above the baseline through the upper half of the S
- **2.** without lifting, continue through the wide flat lower curve and finish near the baseline — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 0.** The pen never leaves the paper.

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Arabic Language Learning Notes, Learn How to Write Arabic Letters — Basic Naskh: Alif Maqsura ى, isolated form and stroke order (updated 2025-01-29; accessed 2026-08-23).
