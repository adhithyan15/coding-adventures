Lesson 1 of 6.
gōnggòng — public; shared by everyone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to put a limit on, then the Mandarin for morality.

You'll want to know: gōnggòng.
gōnggòng — "public; shared by everyone".
zhèr yǒu gōnggòng cèsuǒ ma — Is there a public toilet here?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gōnggòng is built from 公 and gòng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and gjowngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'shared' and 'upright', and the second 'together' and 'all'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
必要 (bìyào) — necessary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for morality, then the Mandarin for shared by everyone.

You'll want to know: 必要.
必要 — bìyào — "necessary".
我觉得没有必要问这个问题 wǒ juéde méiyǒu bìyào wèn zhège wèntí — I don't think there's any need to ask that.
The new character, in pieces: 必 (bì) — five strokes: a dot on the left, the long curve along the foot that hooks up, as in 心, a dot on top, a long left-falling stroke down through the curve, and a dot on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
必要 (bìyào) is built from bì and 要 'to need', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjit and 'jiewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'certain' and 'so', and the second 'to bind'.

Script — the strokes of the heart, with a sweep through them.
必 (bì).
One piece, five strokes. It looks like 心 crossed by a long sweep; the order is its own.
A dot on the left, falling down to the left.
The long curve along the foot, sweeping right and hooking up, as in 心.
A dot on top, falling to the right.
A long left-falling stroke from the top right down through the curve to the bottom left. Only then:
the dot on the right, last.
Five strokes, and the sweep comes before the last dot.

Lesson 4 of 6.
不必 (búbì) — need not; there's no need to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for shared by everyone, then the Mandarin for necessary.

You'll want to know: 不必.
不必 — búbì — "need not; there's no need to".
你不必说对不起你没有错 nǐ búbì shuō duìbuqǐ nǐ méiyǒu cuò — You needn't apologize; you did nothing wrong.
The tone of bù changes here: before a falling tone it is said bú, and the spelling writes the change.

The word, taken apart.
不必 (búbì) is built from 不 'not' and 必 (bì) 'surely', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut and pjit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'certain' and 'so'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
chǎnghé — occasion; setting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for necessary, then the Mandarin for there's no need to.

You'll want to know: chǎnghé.
chǎnghé — "occasion; setting".
nǐ yīnggāi kàn chǎnghé shuōhuà — You should suit what you say to the occasion.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chǎnghé is built from chǎng and hé, both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjang and hop (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a place of offering' and 'a threshing floor', and the second 'to join'. Middle Chinese drjang would regularly give cháng; the dipping tone of chǎng is irregular.
