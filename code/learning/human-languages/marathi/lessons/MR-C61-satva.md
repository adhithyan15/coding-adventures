## You'll want to know: सातवा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-SATVA]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-VA, MR-LEX-SAAT] -->

> **सातवा** — *sātvā* — **seventh**

**सात** + **वा**. Say it before you read it.

One small thing in the sound. **सहा** ends in a vowel, so **सहावा** comes out in
three beats — *sa-hā-vā*. **सात** ends in a consonant, so **सातवा** comes out in
two — *sāt-vā*. The ending did not change; the number it landed on did.

## Grammar Lens: the dictionaries agree with you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-SATVA, MR-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-VA, MR-LEX-PAHILA] -->

Look up **पहिला** in Molesworth's 1857 dictionary and it is there, with its own
definition. Look up **तिसरा**, and **चौथा** — both there, each with its own
entry.

Look up **सातवा** and there is nothing. The dictionary has no such word.

That is not an oversight. A dictionary records what a reader cannot work out,
and by 1857 nobody thought **सातवा** needed recording, because anybody with
**सात** and the ending already had it. **The seam you were shown last chapter is
visible in the dictionary's own table of contents.**
