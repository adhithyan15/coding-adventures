Japanese, chapter 194: Straight On and Turn Right.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
みぎ (migi) — right; right-hand side.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for art museum, then the Japanese for to be visible.

You'll want to know: みぎ.
みぎ — migi — "right; right-hand side".
みぎに ぎんこうが あります。 — migi ni ginkō ga arimasu — "There is a bank on the right."
The other side is ひだり, "left".

The word, taken apart.
みぎ (migi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
まっすぐ (massugu) — straight ahead; straight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be visible, then the Japanese for right.

You'll want to know: まっすぐ.
まっすぐ — massugu — "straight ahead; straight".
この みちを まっすぐ あるきます。 — kono michi o massugu arukimasu — "I walk straight along this road."

The word, taken apart.
まっすぐ (massugu) is native Japanese, built from ma-, "true, pure", and sugu, "straight", both inherited rather than borrowed; JMdict marks it as usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
はくぶつかん (hakubutsukan) — museum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to turn, then the Japanese for to pass through.

You'll want to know: はくぶつかん.
はくぶつかん — hakubutsukan — "museum".
はくぶつかんは こうえんの なかに あります。 — hakubutsukan wa kōen no naka ni arimasu — "The museum is inside the park."

The word, taken apart.
はくぶつかん (hakubutsukan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are haku, which the Guangyun glosses "broad" (Middle Chinese pak), butsu, "thing" (mjut) and kan, "building" (kwanH) (KANJIDIC2; Guangyun and Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription, via tshet-uinh).
