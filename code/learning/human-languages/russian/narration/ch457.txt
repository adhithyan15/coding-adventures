Russian, chapter 457: Packing and delivery.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
упаковывать (upakóvyvat) — to pack, to wrap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for faulty, then the Russian for to save.

You'll want to know: упаковывать.
упаковывать (upakóvyvat) — "to pack, to wrap".
Продавец упаковывает подарок в красивую бумагу. Prodavéts upakóvyvayet podárok v krasívuyu bumágu. The seller wraps the present in nice paper.
Imperfective; perfective упаковать (упакую, упакуешь).

The word, taken apart.
упаковывать (upakóvyvat) is built from у- and паковать 'to pack', which is usually traced to German packen. EtymDB does not list the verb, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 2 of 5.
заворачивать (zavoráchivat) — to wrap (up); to turn (into).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to save, then the Russian for to pack.

You'll want to know: заворачивать.
заворачивать (zavoráchivat) — "to wrap (up); to turn (into)".
Продавщица заворачивает рыбу в бумагу. Prodavshchítsa zavoráchivayet rýbu v bumágu. The shop assistant wraps the fish in paper.
Imperfective; perfective завернуть. Also to turn: завернуть за угол — to turn the corner.

The word, taken apart.
заворачивать (zavoráchivat) is built from за- and -ворачивать, on the root of вертеть 'to turn': to turn something round in paper. EtymDB traces вертеть to Proto-Slavic vĭrtěti.

Lesson 3 of 5.
доставка (dostávka) — delivery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to pack, then the Russian for to wrap.

You'll want to know: доставка.
доставка (dostávka) — "delivery".
Доставка по городу бесплатная. Dostávka po górodu besplátnaya. Delivery within the town is free.
Feminine. Доставка на дом — home delivery.

The word, taken apart.
доставка (dostávka) is built on доставить 'to deliver', from до- 'up to' and ставить 'to put, to place', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic staviti (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
курьер (kuryér) — a courier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for delivery, then the Russian for to deliver.

You'll want to know: курьер.
курьер (kuryér) — "a courier".
Курьер привёз посылку утром. Kuryér privyóz posýlku útrom. The courier brought the parcel in the morning.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
курьер (kuryér) is usually traced to French courrier 'a messenger', which EtymDB takes from Italian corriere, a word usually linked with Latin currere 'to run' (Lewis & Short).
