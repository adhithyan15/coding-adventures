Lesson 1 of 5.
prius — earlier, sooner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for suddenly, then the Latin for until; while.

You'll want to know: prius.
prius — "earlier, sooner".

The word, taken apart.
prius is the neuter of prior, "former, earlier" (Lewis & Short), used as an adverb. prior is a comparative built on the root of prō, "before".

Lesson 4 of 5.
semel — once, a single time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for every year, then the Latin for now and then.

You'll want to know: semel.
semel — "once, a single time".

The word, taken apart.
semel is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European sm-meh1lom, "one time" (EtymDB 2.1), built on the root for "one"; simplex, "simple", and singulus, "single", share the root (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
bis — twice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for now and then, then the Latin for once.

You'll want to know: bis.
bis — "twice".

The word, taken apart.
bis comes from older duis, "two times" (Lewis & Short), built on duo, "two"; Latin turned an initial dw into b, as in bellum, "war", from older duellum.
