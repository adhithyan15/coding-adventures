Mandarin Chinese, chapter 415: The Terminal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hángzhànlóu — airport terminal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get airsick, then the Mandarin for a seat belt.

You'll want to know: hángzhànlóu.
hángzhànlóu — "airport terminal".
wǒmen de hángbān zài èr hào hángzhànlóu — Our flight goes from terminal two.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hángzhànlóu is built from háng 'to sail, to fly', 站 'a station' and lóu 'a building', all inherited from Middle Chinese, hang, treamH and luw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a boat' and the third as a building of several storeys.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: tuōyùn.
tuōyùn — "to check in a suitcase; to send luggage in the hold".
zhège xiāngzi yào tuōyùn — This suitcase needs to be checked in.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuōyùn is built from tuō 'to entrust' and yùn 'to carry', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thak and hjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the first under another spelling, 'to entrust', and glosses the second 'to move, to carry'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
安检 (ānjiǎn) — security check.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to check in a suitcase, then the Mandarin for a boarding pass.

You'll want to know: 安检.
安检 — ānjiǎn — "security check".
我们先去安检 wǒmen xiān qù ānjiǎn — We'll go through security first.

The word, taken apart.
安检 (ānjiǎn) is built from ān 'safe' and 检 'to check', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'an and kjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); it is a short form of a longer name, a safety check.
