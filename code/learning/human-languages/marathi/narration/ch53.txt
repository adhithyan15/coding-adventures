Marathi, chapter 53: Very, a Little, and Saying It Twice.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
खूप (khūp) — very, a lot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Nine adjectives, and every one of them is an on-or-off switch. You can say a house is big and not that it is very big.
[your turn — recall: say uṁch, then kaṭhīṇ, and name the class they share]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: खूप.
खूप — khūp — very, a lot, much.
खूप मोठं घर — khūp moṭhaṁ ghar — a very big house.
खूप (khūp) stands in front of the adjective, and the adjective goes on doing exactly what it did before: मोठा still agrees, सुंदर still does not. Nothing about the ending changes. One small word, and every adjective you own gains a second strength.
It works on a verb and a noun too, where English would need a lot or much:
खूप (khūp) पाणी — a lot of water. खूप (khūp) धन्यवाद — many thanks.
That last one is worth having by heart. खूप (khūp) धन्यवाद is the ordinary warm thank you very much, and you have owned धन्यवाद since chapter two with no way to strengthen it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: khūp moṭhaṁ ghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: khūp sundar khoolī — and hear that सुंदर still did not move]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: khūp dhanyavād — thank you very much]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'a very big house' and 'a very beautiful room'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does खूप (khūp) stand? (In front of the adjective.) What does it do to the adjective's ending? (Nothing at all.)
Sources: Wiktionary: खूप (khūp).


Lesson 2 of 4.
जरा (jarā) — a little.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
खूप (khūp) turns an adjective up. Nothing yet turns one down.

You'll want to know: जरा.
जरा — jarā — a little, slightly, just a bit.
जरा कठीण — jarā kaṭhīṇ — a little difficult.
The same slot as खूप (khūp), the opposite direction, and again the adjective's ending is untouched. Between the two of them every adjective you own now has three settings.
जरा (jarā) has a second use that matters more in a real conversation than the first. Put it in front of a request and the request becomes polite — not give, but give a little, which is how Marathi softens an instruction without adding a whole formula:
जरा पाणी द्या. — jarā pāṇī dyā. — a little water, please.
The word is shared with Hindi's zarā and with Urdu, and it does the same softening job in all three. It is the sort of word you will hear far more often than you read.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: jarā kaṭhīṇ, then khūp kaṭhīṇ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jarā pāṇī dyā — and hear the request soften]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say काळजी घ्या and add जरा (jarā) in front of it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'a little difficult' and then 'very difficult'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does जरा (jarā) do to a request? (Softens it — a little.) Which two words now share the slot in front of an adjective? (खूप (khūp) and जरा (jarā).)
Sources: Wiktionary: जरा (jarā).


Lesson 3 of 4.
खूप खूप (khūp khūp) — say it twice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two intensifiers, and one more setting still missing: the one above खूप (khūp).

Grammar Lens: repetition is a degree.
Marathi strengthens a word by saying it twice. No new vocabulary, no ending, no extra word — the same word, twice.
खूप खूप धन्यवाद. — khūp khūp dhanyavād. — thank you very, very much.
That is the warm form, the one somebody uses when the thanks is real, and it is built out of two words you already own.
It works on an adjective as well as on खूप (khūp). मोठं मोठं is great big; लहान लहान is ever so small; जरा (jarā) जरा (jarā) is little by little. In each one the word is unchanged and only said again.
English does the same trick and pretends not to — a long, long way, many, many thanks — so this is a rule you can hear rather than one you have to memorise.
It is worth keeping because it is free. Any adjective you learn from here on arrives with its own intensifier already attached.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: khūp khūp dhanyavād]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jarā, khūp, khūp khūp — three settings, quietest first]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say mājhyākaḍe khūp pāṇī āhe, then double the khūp]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Thank somebody warmly, then say something is very very difficult.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How does Marathi make a word stronger without adding anything? (By saying it twice.) What is the warm form of thank you? (खूप खूप (khūp khūp) धन्यवाद.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Turn the same adjective up and down — three settings on every adjective you own, and none of them cost a new word.
