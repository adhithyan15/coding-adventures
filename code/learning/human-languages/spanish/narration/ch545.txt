Spanish, chapter 545: De niño.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
criar — to bring up, to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pregnancy, then the Spanish for the newborn baby.

You'll want to know: criar.
criar — "to bring up, to raise".
Mi abuela me crió en el pueblo. My grandmother brought me up in the village.
Criarse is to grow up: Me crié en Valencia. It takes an accent: crío.

The word, taken apart.
criar is inherited from Latin creāre 'to make, to bring forth' (EtymDB). Spanish kept the everyday sense of rearing children; crear 'to create' is the same verb taken again from books.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la niñez — childhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the newborn baby, then the Spanish for to bring up.

You'll want to know: la niñez.
la niñez — "childhood".
Tuve una niñez muy feliz. I had a very happy childhood.
La infancia means the same.

The word, taken apart.
niñez is built on niño 'child' with the ending -ez. Niño is usually traced to a baby-talk word, ninno, not to any Latin noun.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: la juventud.
la juventud — "youth; young people".
En mi juventud viajé mucho. In my youth I travelled a lot.
La juventud is also young people as a group.

The word, taken apart.
juventud comes from Latin iuventūs, iuventūtis 'youth', built on iuvenis 'young' (Lewis & Short). English juvenile is from the same root.
