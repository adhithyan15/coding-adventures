Italian, chapter 359: Lo sciopero.

Lesson 1 of 5.
il sindacato — the trade union.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the employer, then the Italian for the employee.

You'll want to know: il sindacato.
il sindacato — "the trade union".
Il sindacato chiede stipendi più alti. The union is asking for higher wages.
A union member is un iscritto al sindacato.

The word, taken apart.
sindacato is built on sindaco, a learned borrowing from Late Latin syndicus "a representative of a body" (Lewis & Short), from Greek sýndikos "one who helps in court", built on díkē "justice".

Lesson 2 of 5.
lo sciopero — the strike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the employee, then the Italian for the trade union.

You'll want to know: lo sciopero.
lo sciopero — "the strike".
Domani c'è lo sciopero dei treni. Tomorrow there is a train strike.
Essere in sciopero — to be on strike. Strikes are announced in advance, so check before you travel.

The word, taken apart.
sciopero is built on scioperare, usually traced to a Vulgar Latin exoperāre "to be out of work", from ex- "out of" and operāre "to work", built on opus, operis "work" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
il turno — the shift; the turn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the strike, then the Italian for to go on strike.

You'll want to know: il turno.
il turno — "the shift; the turn".
Questa settimana faccio il turno di notte. This week I am on the night shift.
A turno — taking turns. È il tuo turno — it is your turn.

The word, taken apart.
turno came into Italian from French, from the family of tourner "to turn" (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin tornāre "to turn on a lathe" (Lewis & Short), from tornus "a lathe", a Greek word: work goes round in turns.

Lesson 5 of 5.
gli straordinari — the overtime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to go on strike, then the Italian for the shift.

You'll want to know: gli straordinari.
gli straordinari — "the overtime".
Questo mese ho fatto molti straordinari. This month I did a lot of overtime.
Short for le ore straordinarie. Straordinario also means extraordinary.

The word, taken apart.
straordinari is the plural of straordinario, a learned borrowing from Latin extraordinārius "out of the usual order" (Lewis & Short), built from extrā "outside" and ōrdō "order".
