Arabic, chapter 475: Wild animals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ضبع (ḍabʿ) — a hyena.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a feather, then the Arabic for to float.

You'll want to know: ضبع.
ضبع — ḍabʿ — "a hyena".
سمعنا صوت ضبع في الليل. samiʿnā ṣawt ḍabʿ fī al-layl. We heard a hyena at night.

The word, taken apart.
ضبع (ḍabʿ) was inherited from Proto-Semitic (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 5 of 5.
خفاش (khuffāsh) — a bat (the animal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hedgehog, then the Arabic for a squirrel.

You'll want to know: خفاش.
خفاش — khuffāsh — "a bat (the animal)".
يطير الخفاش في الليل. yaṭīru al-khuffāsh fī al-layl. The bat flies at night.

The word, taken apart.
خفاش (khuffāsh) is built on the native Arabic root kh-f-sh, "to have weak eyes" (Lane). EtymDB also derives it from this root: the bat was the animal that sees poorly by day.
