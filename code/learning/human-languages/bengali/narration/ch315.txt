Bengali, chapter 315: Swords and Cannons.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
অস্ত্র (ôstrô) — a weapon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a raid, then the Bengali for to occupy.

You'll want to know: অস্ত্র.
অস্ত্র — ôstrô — "a weapon".
সৈন্যদের হাতে অনেক অস্ত্র ছিল। soinyôder hāte ônek ôstrô chhilô. The soldiers carried many weapons.

The word, taken apart.
অস্ত্র (ôstrô) is Sanskrit astra, a missile weapon, an arrow, and then any weapon (Monier-Williams).

You'll want to know: তলোয়ার.
তলোয়ার — tôloyār — "a sword".
রাজকুমার তলোয়ার নিয়ে শত্রুর সঙ্গে লড়লেন। rājkumār tôloyār niye shôtrur sôngge lôṛlen. The prince took up his sword and fought the enemy.

The word, taken apart.
তলোয়ার (tôloyār) is usually traced to Sanskrit taravāri, 'a one-edged sword' (CDIAL 5706; Monier-Williams), which Monier-Williams wonders may stand for tala-v-.

ঢাল (ḍhāl) comes down from Sanskrit ḍhāla, 'a shield' (CDIAL 5583, which lists Bengali ḍhāl). Another ঢাল, a slope, is a different word.

পুলিশের হাতে বন্দুক ছিল। pulisher hāte bônduk chhilô. The police were carrying guns.

বন্দুক (bônduk) is usually traced through Persian to Arabic bunduq, a hazelnut and then a pellet; the name passed from the shot to the gun.
