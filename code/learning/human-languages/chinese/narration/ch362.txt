Lesson 1 of 6.
dàshēng — loudly; in a loud voice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to propose a toast, then the Mandarin for to look down on someone.

You'll want to know: dàshēng.
dàshēng — "loudly; in a loud voice".
qǐng búyào dàshēng shuōhuà — Please don't talk loudly.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dàshēng is built from 大 'big' and shēng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dajH and syeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'big' and the second 'a sound'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
必须 (bìxū) — must; it is required that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to look down on someone, then the Mandarin for in a loud voice.

You'll want to know: 必须.
必须 — bìxū — "must; it is required that".
上课的时候必须关手机 shàngkè de shíhou bìxū guān shǒujī — You have to switch your phone off in class.
The new character, in pieces: 须 (xū) — nine strokes in two pieces: on the left three left-falling strokes, one under another; on the right the page piece of 题, six strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
必须 (bìxū) is built from 必 'surely' and xū, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjit and sju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'certain' and the second 'what the mind wants' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'the hair of the face'.

Script — three sweeps, then the page piece.
须 (xū).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, three strokes: three short strokes falling to the left, one under another, the last the longest.
The right piece, the page piece, six strokes, as on the right of 题: a horizontal, a short left-falling stroke, a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, a left-falling stroke inside, then a dot at the foot.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 须 (xū), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 须 (xū)? (Two.) Where have you written the right piece? (On the right of 题.) How many strokes on the left? (Three, all falling to the left.)

公正 (gōngzhèng) is built from 公 and 正 'proper', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and tsyengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'upright' and 'selfless', and the second 'proper'.
