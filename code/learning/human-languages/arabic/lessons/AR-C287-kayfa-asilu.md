# كيف أصل إلى (kayfa aṣilu ilā) — how do I get to …?

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C286-FUTURE-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-SUBJUNCTIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I will travel tomorrow". (*Sa-usāfiru ghadan*.) Then "I
want to go to the market". (*Urīdu an adhhaba ilā as-sūq*.)

[PAUSE 1s] You are in a city you do not know. This is what you say to the
first person who stops.

## You'll want to know: كيف أصل إلى
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C287-TARIQ287-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-01, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-03, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-01, AR-LEX-C91-TOWN91-03] -->

**كيف أصل إلى** — *kayfa aṣilu ilā* — "how do I get to", word for word "how
do I arrive at".

> **من فضلك، كيف أصل إلى المحطة؟** — *min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā
> al-maḥaṭṭa?* — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?

*Aṣilu*, I arrive, is the present of *waṣala*, and the root's *w* has
dropped, as it did in *yajibu*. To a woman, say *min faḍliki*.

## Grammar Lens: the questions around it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C287-TARIQ287-01, AR-LEX-C204-MAKAN204-05, AR-GRAMMAR-C39-HAL-01, AR-LEX-C88-TIME88-03, AR-LEX-C88-TIME88-04, AR-LEX-C235-MAKAN235-01] -->

Two shorter questions often come with it. **أين** — *ayna*, "where": *ayna
al-matḥaf?*, where is the museum? And **هل** with *baʿīd* or *qarīb*: *hal
al-matḥaf baʿīd?*, is the museum far? The answer may be *qarīb min hunā*,
near here, or *baʿīd jiddan*, very far.

The answer to *kayfa aṣilu* is a set of orders, which the next lesson
builds.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**أصل** (*aṣilu*) is built on the native Arabic root *w-ṣ-l*, "to reach, to
join" (Lane), the root of *waṣala*, "he arrived", and of *ittaṣala*, "he
phoned", a call that joins two people. **كيف** (*kayfa*) and **إلى** (*ilā*)
are small particles, not verbs or nouns built on a three-letter root.
