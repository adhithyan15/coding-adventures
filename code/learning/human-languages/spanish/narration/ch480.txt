Spanish, chapter 480: Lo que dice la gente.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
afirmar — to state, to say that something is so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for in advance, then the Spanish for to replace.

You'll want to know: afirmar.
afirmar — "to state, to say that something is so".
El director afirma que no hay problema. The director states that there is no problem.
More formal than decir; news reports use it a lot.

The word, taken apart.
afirmar from Latin affirmare, "to make firm, to assert" (EtymDB 2.1), built on firmus, "firm" — the firmus inside firmar, "to sign".

Lesson 2 of 5.
comunicar — to communicate, to inform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to replace, then the Spanish for to state.

You'll want to know: comunicar.
comunicar — "to communicate, to inform".
Le comunicamos que su pedido ha llegado. We inform you that your order has arrived.
In Spain, está comunicando on the phone means the line is busy; elsewhere people say está ocupado.

The word, taken apart.
comunicar from Latin communicare, "to share, to make common" (EtymDB 2.1), from communis, "common" — a learned borrowing: the -c- between vowels did not become -g-.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: interrumpir.
interrumpir — "to interrupt".
Perdona que te interrumpa. Sorry to interrupt you.
Sin interrumpir — without interrupting.

The word, taken apart.
interrumpir from Latin interrumpere, "to break off, to break apart" (EtymDB 2.1): inter, "between", and rumpere, "to break" — a learned word; romper, "to break", is the inherited one.

prometer from Latin promittere, "to send forward; to promise" (EtymDB 2.1) — inherited, through Old Spanish prometer: the short i became e, as in meter, "to put", from mittere.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: quejarse.
quejarse — "to complain".
Los vecinos se quejan del ruido. The neighbours complain about the noise.
It goes with de: quejarse de algo.

The word, taken apart.
quejarse comes from spoken Latin quassiare, built on quassare, "to shake, to toss violently" (Lewis & Short) — inherited: the -ssi- became j.
