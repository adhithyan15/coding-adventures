# आणि (āṇi) — and

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-CHAHA, MR-LEX-DUDH, MR-LEX-AVADNE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say two sentences you already own: **मला चहा आवडतो**, and
**मला दूध आवडतं**. Two sentences, two breaths. This chapter makes them one.

## You'll want to know: आणि
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-JOIN-AANI]; assesses=[MR-LEX-AVADNE] -->

> **आणि** — *āṇi* — **and**

> **मला चहा आवडतो आणि दूध आवडतं.**
> — *malā chahā āvaḍto āṇi dūdh āvaḍtaṁ.* — **I like tea and I like milk.**

**आणि** sits BETWEEN the two pieces and changes nothing on either side. Each
half keeps its own agreement: **चहा** is masculine and keeps **आवडतो**,
**दूध** is neuter and keeps **आवडतं**. The joining word does not reach inside.

It joins two nouns just as happily: **भाऊ आणि बहीण**, brother and sister.

The word is old and slightly surprising. It comes from Sanskrit **अन्य**
(*anya*), "**other**." A word for *the other one* wore down into a word for
*one more*. English does the same trick from the other side: *another* is
*an other*.
