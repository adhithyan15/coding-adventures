Persian, chapter 32: Upset, Doctor, Grandfather, Grandmother, Uncle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ناراحت (nârâhat) — upset, sad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for hungry, then the Persian for happy.

You'll want to know: ناراحت.
ناراحت — nârâhat — "upset, sad".

The word, taken apart.
nârâhat is nâ-, "not", with râhat, "ease, comfort": "not at ease". nâ- is the native Persian negative prefix; râhat is borrowed from Arabic rāḥa, "rest, ease" (root r-w-ḥ, Lane).

Lesson 3 of 5.
پدربزرگ (pedarbozorg) — a grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for upset, then the Persian for a doctor.

You'll want to know: پدربزرگ.
پدربزرگ — pedarbozorg — "a grandfather".

The word, taken apart.
pedarbozorg is built from two inherited Persian words: pedar, "father" (Middle Persian pidar), and bozorg, "big, great" (Middle Persian wuzurg, EtymDB). The "great father" is the grandfather.

Lesson 5 of 5.
دایی (dâyi) — an uncle (mother's brother).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a grandfather, then the Persian for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: دایی.
دایی — dâyi — "an uncle (mother's brother)".

The word, taken apart.
dâyi is usually traced to Turkic, to the word behind Turkish dayı, "maternal uncle"; EtymDB instead connects it with Persian dâ, "mother". The origin is not settled.
