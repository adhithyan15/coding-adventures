Spanish, chapter 101: Conseguir --- Getting Is Following Until You Have It.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
conseguir — "to get", by following a thing until you have it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Traigo carries a thing that is already yours. Coger grabs what is in front of you. This verb is for the getting that took some work.

Sounds you'll need.
g-hard — the gu in conseguir is one hard g; the u is silent.
s-clear — a clean hissing s: con-se-GEER.

The word, taken apart: conseguir.
conseguir ("to get, to obtain, to manage to") from Latin cōnsequī: com- ("along with") + sequī, "to follow". You got a thing by following it until it was yours.
Sequī is one of Latin's great English suppliers: sequence, sequel, consequence, subsequent, consecutive; second, from secundus, "the one following"; execute and prosecute; obsequious, following someone about; and through French, pursue, ensue and suit.
Spanish also keeps the formal partner obtener from obtinēre, "to hold onto" — English obtain. It behaves exactly like tener, so its yo-form is obtengo. Conseguir is the everyday one.

Grammar Lens: the third crack, and a silent u that leaves.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
person: yo. form: consigo.
person: tú. form: consigues.
person: él/ella/usted. form: consigue.
person: nosotros. form: conseguimos.
person: ellos/ustedes. form: consiguen.
You have met dormir breaking o becomes ue and pido quietly breaking e becomes i. Here is that third pattern in full: stress on the stem turns e into i, and conseguimos stays plain because the stress moved to the ending.
Now the spelling. Spanish writes gu only to keep a g hard in front of e and i. Once the e becomes i, there is nothing in front to guard against, so the u has no job and goes: consigo, not consiguo. Two changes, one form, and only one of them is a sound.
