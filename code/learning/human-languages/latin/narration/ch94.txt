Latin, chapter 94: New, Old, Light, Fast, Slow.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
novus, nova, novum — new.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for short, then the Latin for high; deep.

You'll want to know: novus, nova, novum.
novus, nova, novum — "new". English novel.

The word, taken apart.
novus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European newos, "new" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek neos, "new", and English new are cousins (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
vetus, veteris — old.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for high; deep, then the Latin for new.

You'll want to know: vetus, veteris.
vetus, veteris — "old". English veteran.

The word, taken apart.
vetus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European wetos, "year" (EtymDB 2.1): old meant "full of years". Greek etos, "year", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
levis, leve — light (not heavy).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for new, then the Latin for old.

You'll want to know: levis, leve.
levis, leve — "light (not heavy)". English levity.

The word, taken apart.
levis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h1lengwh-, "light in weight" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek elakhys, "small", and English light are cousins (Lewis & Short). It is a different word from lēvis, "smooth".

Lesson 5 of 5.
lentus, lenta, lentum — slow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for light, then the Latin for fast.

You'll want to know: lentus, lenta, lentum.
lentus, lenta, lentum — "slow".

The word, taken apart.
lentus is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European lentos, "pliant, gentle" (EtymDB 2.1); its first sense was "pliant, supple". English lithe is often given as a cousin.
