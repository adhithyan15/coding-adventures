Lesson 1 of 6.
il sospettato — the suspect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the clue, then the Italian for the footprint.

You'll want to know: il sospettato.
il sospettato — "the suspect".
Il sospettato ha quarant'anni. The suspect is forty.
From sospettare, ‘to suspect’.

The word, taken apart.
sospettato is the past participle of sospettare "to suspect", built on sospetto, from Latin suspectus "looked at from below", from suspicere (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
il movente — the motive (for a crime).

You'll want to know: il movente.
il movente — "the motive (for a crime)".
Il movente del delitto è il denaro. The motive for the crime is money.
For crimes; in daily life a reason is il motivo.

The word, taken apart.
movente is the present participle of muovere "to move", from Latin movēre (Lewis & Short): what moves someone to act.

Lesson 4 of 6.
interrogare — to question; to test orally (at school).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the motive, then the Italian for the alibi.

You'll want to know: interrogare.
interrogare — "to question; to test orally (at school)".
La polizia ha interrogato il sospettato per ore. The police questioned the suspect for hours.
At school, interrogare is to test a pupil out loud.

The word, taken apart.
interrogare is a learned borrowing from Latin interrogāre "to ask, to question" (Lewis & Short), from inter and rogāre "to ask".
