Spanish, chapter 755: Calentar y trotar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el calentamiento — the warm-up; warming.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for gardening, then the Spanish for to sew.

You'll want to know: el calentamiento.
el calentamiento — "the warm-up; warming".
Antes de correr hago diez minutos de calentamiento. Before running I do a ten-minute warm-up.
El calentamiento global — global warming.

The word, taken apart.
calentamiento is built on calentar "to warm", made from caliente "hot" (EtymDB), which comes from Latin calēns, from calēre "to be warm".

Lesson 2 of 5.
trotar — to jog, to trot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to sew, then the Spanish for the warm-up.

You'll want to know: trotar.
trotar — "to jog, to trot".
Troto en el parque cada mañana. I jog in the park every morning.
Common in Latin America; in Spain people mostly say salir a correr.

The word, taken apart.
trotar was borrowed from French trotter "to trot", which is usually traced to Frankish trottōn "to tread".

You'll want to know: el atleta.
el atleta — "the athlete".
Es una atleta muy rápida. She is a very fast athlete.
Same form for men and women: el atleta, la atleta.

The word, taken apart.
atleta comes from Latin āthlēta, from Greek athlētḗs "one who competes for a prize" (EtymDB), from áthlos "contest".

You'll want to know: lesionarse.
lesionarse — "to get injured".
Se lesionó la rodilla en el partido. He injured his knee in the match.

The word, taken apart.
lesionarse is built on lesión "injury", a learned borrowing from Latin laesiō "injury" (EtymDB), from laedere "to hurt".
