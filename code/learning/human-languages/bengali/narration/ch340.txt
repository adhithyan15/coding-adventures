Bengali, chapter 340: What the Law Allows.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
বৈধ (boidhô) — lawful; valid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to defend, then the Bengali for to enjoy a right.

You'll want to know: বৈধ.
বৈধ — boidhô — "lawful; valid".
তোমার ভিসা কি বৈধ? tomār bhisā ki boidhô? Is your visa valid?

The word, taken apart.
বৈধ (boidhô) is Sanskrit vaidha, 'according to rule', legal (Monier-Williams), from vidhi 'a rule'. A paper or an act can be বৈধ.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ধারা (dhārā) — a section (of a law); a clause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for lawful, then the Bengali for banned.

You'll want to know: ধারা.
ধারা — dhārā — "a section (of a law); a clause".
আইনের এই ধারাটা জটিল। āiner ei dhārāṭā jôṭil. This section of the law is complicated.

The word, taken apart.
ধারা (dhārā) is Sanskrit dhārā, a stream or flow (Monier-Williams). The sense 'a section of a law', one strand in the flow of the text, is a later legal use.

You'll want to know: পালন করা.
পালন করা — pālôn kôrā — "to observe (a rule, a duty); to carry out".
আমরা সব নিয়ম পালন করি। āmrā sôb niyôm pālôn kôri. We follow all the rules.
Past (he/she …): তিনি তাঁর কর্তব্য পালন করলেন, tini tā̃r kôrtôbyô pālôn kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
পালন করা (pālôn kôrā) is Sanskrit pālana, guarding and keeping (Monier-Williams), with করা: to keep a rule, a duty or a festival day.
