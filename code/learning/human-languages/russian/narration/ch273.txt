Russian, chapter 273: Happening and going on.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
происходить (proiskhodít) — to happen, to take place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for soon after, then the Russian for later.

You'll want to know: происходить.
происходить (proiskhodít) — "to happen, to take place".
Это происходит каждую зиму. Éto proiskhódit kázhduyu zímu. This happens every winter.
Imperfective; the perfective is произойти (proizoytí), past произошёл (proizoshyól): что произошло? — what happened?

The word, taken apart.
происходить (proiskhodít) is built on про-, из- and ходить "to go", which is inherited from Proto-Slavic xoditi (EtymDB): to come out, to come about.

Lesson 2 of 5.
продолжаться (prodolzhátsya) — to go on, to continue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for later, then the Russian for to happen.

You'll want to know: продолжаться.
продолжаться (prodolzhátsya) — "to go on, to continue".
Дождь продолжался три дня. Dozhd prodolzhálsya tri dnya. The rain went on for three days.
Imperfective; perfective продолжиться (prodólzhitsya). Without -ся, продолжать takes an object: to continue something.

The word, taken apart.
продолжаться (prodolzhátsya) is built on про- and долгий "long", which is inherited from Proto-Slavic dĭlgŭ "long" (EtymDB): to be made longer.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: длиться.
длиться (dlítsya) — "to last".
Фильм длился два часа. Film dlílsya dva chasá. The film lasted two hours.
Imperfective; perfective продлиться (prodlítsya). The time is in the accusative: урок длится час.

The word, taken apart.
длиться (dlítsya) is built on длина "length", from the same Slavic root as долгий "long", Proto-Slavic dĭlgŭ (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
прекращаться (prekrashchátsya) — to stop, to cease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to go on, then the Russian for to last.

You'll want to know: прекращаться.
прекращаться (prekrashchátsya) — "to stop, to cease".
Музыка не прекращалась всю ночь. Múzyka ne prekrashchálas vsyu noch. The music did not stop all night.
Imperfective; perfective прекратиться (prekratítsya). Without -ся, прекращать stops something else.

The word, taken apart.
прекращаться (prekrashchátsya) is built on пре- and кратить "to shorten", from краткий "short", usually explained as the Church Slavonic shape of native короткий, Proto-Slavic kortŭkŭ (EtymDB): to cut short.

Lesson 5 of 5.
наступать (nastupát) — to come, to set in (of a time); to step on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to last, then the Russian for to stop.

You'll want to know: наступать.
наступать (nastupát) — "to come, to set in (of a time); to step on".
Наступает вечер. Nastupáyet vécher. Evening is coming on.
Imperfective; perfective наступить (nastupít): наступила зима — winter came. Also наступить на ногу — to step on a foot.

The word, taken apart.
наступать (nastupát) is built on на- and ступать "to step", the root of ступень "a step", inherited from Proto-Slavic stǫpenĭ (EtymDB): to step onto.
