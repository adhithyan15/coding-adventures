Mandarin Chinese, chapter 222: Under the Weather.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zháoliáng — to catch a chill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for below zero, then the Mandarin for gloves.

You'll want to know: zháoliáng.
zháoliáng — "to catch a chill".
zuótiān wǒ zháoliáng le — I caught a chill yesterday.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zháoliáng is built from zháo 'to be touched by' and liáng 'cool', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjak and ljang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to attach to' and the second 'thin' and 'cold'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
késou — to cough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for gloves, then the Mandarin for to catch a chill.

You'll want to know: késou.
késou — "to cough".
wǒ yǒudiǎnr késou — I've got a bit of a cough.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
késou is built from ké and sou. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese suwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), listed with a character for coughing; the dictionary reads the first only as a small child's laugh, so how it came to mean coughing is not settled.

sǎngzi — "throat; voice".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sǎngzi is built from sǎng 'throat' and the noun ending 子, inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). No dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎ pēntì — to sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to cough, then the Mandarin for throat.

You'll want to know: dǎ pēntì.
dǎ pēntì — "to sneeze".
gǎnmào de shíhou wǒ chángcháng dǎ pēntì — When I've got a cold I sneeze a lot.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎ pēntì is built from 打 'to strike, to do' and pēntì 'a sneeze', all inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX, phwonH and tejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to breathe out' and the third 'breath from the nose'. Neither dǎ nor the level tone of pēn is the regular outcome of the form listed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
nánshòu — miserable; unwell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for throat, then the Mandarin for to sneeze.

You'll want to know: nánshòu.
nánshòu — "miserable; unwell".
yīnwèi gǎnmào le wǒ hěn nánshòu — I feel awful because I've got a cold.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nánshòu is built from nán 'hard' and shòu 'to bear', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nan and dzyuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'difficult' and the second 'to hold' and 'to receive'.
