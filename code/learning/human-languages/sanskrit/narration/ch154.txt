Sanskrit, chapter 154: If, Provided that, Even so, Although, Or else.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
यदि (yadi) — if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for all around, then the Sanskrit for on all sides.

You'll want to know: यदि.
यदि — yadi — "if".

The word, taken apart.
yadi "if" is built on the relative stem ya- "which" (CDIAL 10410), already in the Rigveda (MW). MW compares the relative ya- with Greek hos "who". It lives on as Punjabi je and Marathi jai "if" (CDIAL 10410).

Lesson 2 of 5.
चेत् (cet) — provided that, if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for on all sides, then the Sanskrit for if.

You'll want to know: चेत्.
चेत् — cet — "provided that, if".

The word, taken apart.
cet is ced, built from ca "and" + id, an emphatic particle, as the Vedic word-by-word text splits it (MW). It first meant "and" and "when", and later "if"; like ca, it follows the first word of its clause.

Lesson 3 of 5.
तथापि (tathāpi) — even so, still.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for if, then the Sanskrit for provided that.

You'll want to know: तथापि.
तथापि — tathāpi — "even so, still".

The word, taken apart.
tathāpi is built from tathā "so, thus", on the pronoun base ta- "that", and api "even, also" (CDIAL 5647): "even so, nevertheless". CDIAL 5647 traces Punjabi te "and" to it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
यद्यपि (yadyapi) — although.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for provided that, then the Sanskrit for even so.

You'll want to know: यद्यपि.
यद्यपि — yadyapi — "although".

The word, taken apart.
yadyapi is built from yadi "if" and api "even, also", with i turning to y before the vowel (MW): "even if", so "although". It pairs with tathāpi "even so".

Lesson 5 of 5.
अथवा (athavā) — or else, or.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for even so, then the Sanskrit for although.

You'll want to know: अथवा.
अथवा — athavā — "or else, or".

The word, taken apart.
athavā is built from atha "then, now" and vā "or" (CDIAL 231): "or else, or rather". MW compares vā with Latin -ve "or", which also follows the word it joins.
