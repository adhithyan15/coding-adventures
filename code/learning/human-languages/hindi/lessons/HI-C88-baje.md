# पाँच बजे — reading the hour, and arranging to meet at it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C18-GHANTA-01, HI-CONCEPT-C06-PAANCH-NASAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say what the clock is doing — the verb that strikes. Now try
to say *at five*, and notice that telling the time and fixing a time are not
the same job.

## You'll want to know: पाँच बजे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01]; assesses=[] -->

**पाँच बजे** (*pā̃c baje*) — "at five o'clock."

**मैं पाँच बजे आऊँगा।** — I will come at five.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01, HI-CONCEPT-C18-GHANTA-02] -->

**बजे** comes from the verb you already have — the one meaning *to strike, to
toll*, because clocks and towns once marked the hour by striking a bell.

What is new is the **shape** of it. You learned it as something the clock is
doing right now. This form is not doing anything: it sits beside a number and
turns it into a **time to meet at**.

| what it does | the form |
|---|---|
| tells you the hour | the striking form you know |
| fixes a time to act | **बजे** |

There is no separate word for *at*. The number plus **बजे** is the whole of it.

## Grammar Lens: where it stands in the sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| मैं पाँच बजे आऊँगा। | I will come at five. |
| वह चार बजे आएगी। | She will come at four. |

The time phrase goes **after the person and before the verb**, in the same seat
the place phrase took. Hindi keeps the verb at the end and fills the middle with
whatever the sentence needs.

Both practice papers ask for exactly this pairing — a number, **बजे**, and a
future verb — in the first scored part.
