Mandarin Chinese, chapter 457: Thanks for the Help.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
láojià — may I trouble you (asking a favour).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the owner of a lost item, then the Mandarin for to claim lost property.

You'll want to know: láojià.
láojià — "may I trouble you (asking a favour)".
láojià qù huǒchēzhàn zěnme zǒu — Excuse me, how do I get to the railway station?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
láojià is built from láo 'to tire' and jià 'a carriage', both inherited from Middle Chinese, law and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'weary' and the second 'to ride'. The pair is usually explained as an old polite way of asking someone to trouble their carriage.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 3 of 7.
多谢 (duōxiè) — many thanks; thanks a lot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for may I trouble you, then the Mandarin for to ask a favour of someone.

You'll want to know: 多谢.
多谢 — duōxiè — "many thanks; thanks a lot".
多谢你我找到地图了 duōxiè nǐ wǒ zhǎodào dìtú le — Thanks so much: I've found my map.

The word, taken apart.
多谢 (duōxiè) is built from 多 'many' and 谢 'to thank', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ta and zjaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to decline'; the sense 'to thank' is not given there.

You'll want to know: gǎnjī.
gǎnjī — "to feel deeply grateful".
nǐ bāng le wǒ hěn duō wǒ hěn gǎnjī — You've helped me so much; I'm very grateful.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnjī is built from gǎn 'to be moved' and jī 'to surge', both inherited from Middle Chinese, komX and kek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to move' and the second as a rushing wave.

Lesson 5 of 7.
方法 (fāngfǎ) — a way to solve a problem; means.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for thanks a lot, then the Mandarin for to feel deeply grateful.

You'll want to know: 方法.
方法 — fāngfǎ — "a way to solve a problem; means".
找不到路的时候你有什么好方法 zhǎo bu dào lù de shíhou nǐ yǒu shénme hǎo fāngfǎ — When you can't find the way, what do you do?

The word, taken apart.
方法 (fāngfǎ) is built from 方 'a technique' and 法 'a rule', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjang and pjop (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, among other senses, as an art or technique and the second 'a rule'.
