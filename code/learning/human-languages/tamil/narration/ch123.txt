Tamil, chapter 123: Vegetables, Banana, Mango, Coconut, Onion.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
காய்கறி (kāykaṟi) — vegetables.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a fish, then the Tamil for meat.

You'll want to know: காய்கறி.
காய்கறி — kāykaṟi — "vegetables".

The word, taken apart.
காய்கறி (kāykaṟi) is native: kāy, "unripe fruit" (DEDR 1459, which compares Indo-Aryan kacca, "raw"), and kaṟi, "vegetables, curry" (DEDR 1391; Malayalam kaṟi).

Lesson 3 of 5.
மாம்பழம் (māmpaḻam) — a mango.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for vegetables, then the Tamil for a banana.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say எனக்குத் தமிழ் பிடிக்கும், then the plain verb for wanting, விரும்பு.

You'll want to know: மாம்பழம்.
மாம்பழம் — māmpaḻam — "a mango".

The word, taken apart.
மாம்பழம் (māmpaḻam) is native: mā, "mango" (DEDR 4782; Malayalam mā), and paḻam, "fruit" (DEDR 4004). DEDR compares Sanskrit mākanda, "mango tree", and phala, "fruit". English mango came, through Portuguese, from the Malayalam form māṅṅa.

Lesson 4 of 5.
தேங்காய் (tēṅkāy) — a coconut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a banana, then the Tamil for a mango.

You'll want to know: தேங்காய்.
தேங்காய் — tēṅkāy — "a coconut".

The word, taken apart.
தேங்காய் (tēṅkāy) is native Dravidian. DEDR 3408 lists Malayalam teṅṅu, "coconut tree", Kannada teṅgu and Telugu teṅkāya. It is teṅ, "coconut palm", with kāy, "fruit".

Lesson 5 of 5.
வெங்காயம் (veṅkāyam) — an onion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a mango, then the Tamil for a coconut.

You'll want to know: வெங்காயம்.
வெங்காயம் — veṅkāyam — "an onion".

The word, taken apart.
வெங்காயம் (veṅkāyam) begins with native veṇ, "white" (DEDR 5496). The second part is not settled, so the origin of the whole word is uncertain.
