## Fixed — chapter 42's வா practises chapter 32's atom, and எடு no longer claims ettu as a proven cousin

- **TA-C42-come no longer introduces a second atom for வா.** TA-C32-vaa already teaches *vā*, "to come", as `TA-LEX-VAA-01`. TA-C42-come taught the same word in the same sense, and introduced `TA-LEX-COME` for it, so the track counted one word as two pieces of knowledge. TA-C42-come now introduces nothing. It requires and practises `TA-LEX-VAA-01` and names TA-C32-vaa in `reviews_of`. Its input block now says the learner has already met the word, beside its partner *varu-*, without naming a chapter number. The old text called *vā* "the bare stem, with nothing added", which contradicts what Chapter 32 teaches: the endings attach to *varu-*. That line now says the bare command takes no endings and repeats the earlier warning. Everything that practised `TA-LEX-COME` now practises `TA-LEX-VAA-01`: TA-C42-sit, TA-C44-fruit, and the chapter 42 payoff in `chapters.d/0042.json`.
- **TA-C34-edu: Kannada and Telugu *ettu* are no longer called "the same old root" as Tamil *eḍu*.** The Dravidian Etymological Dictionary (Burrow & Emeneau, DEDR) files Tamil *eṭu* "to take up, raise" and Malayalam *eṭukka* in **DEDR 851**, the *er̤u* "to rise" entry. It files Kannada *ettu* "to lift, raise, take up" and Telugu *ettu* "to lift up, raise" in a separate entry, **DEDR 796**, together with Kota *et-*, Tulu *ettuni*, Kolami *et-* and the Gondi, Konda, Pengo, Manda and Kui child-carrying verbs. 796 has no Tamil or Malayalam member. The two entries only point at each other: 796 ends "Cf. 851 (Ta. er̤u)", and 851's Brahui line ends "Cf. 796 Ko. et-". The text now says that the etymological dictionary files Malayalam *eṭukkuka* together with *eḍu*. It says that Kannada/Telugu *ettu* looks close but is filed under a separate root: a likely cousin, not a proven one. This is the same caution the lesson already gives for *iḍu* and *eḍu*. The `etymology_hook` and the Guided Practice cue are corrected to match. The paragraph was kept short so the lesson stays under the 300-second duration threshold: the estimate is 296s, against 290s before.
