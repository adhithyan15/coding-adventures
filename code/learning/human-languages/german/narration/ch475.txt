German, chapter 475: Angeber und Bescheidene.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
prahlen — to boast, to brag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for curiosity, then the German for speechless.

You'll want to know: prahlen.
prahlen — "to boast, to brag".
Er prahlt gern mit seinem teuren Auto. He likes to brag about his expensive car.
Prahlen mit + dative.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of prahlen is not settled; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
angeben — to show off; to state.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for speechless, then the German for to boast.

You'll want to know: angeben.
angeben — "to show off; to state".
Mein Cousin gibt immer mit seinen Noten an. My cousin is forever showing off about his marks.
Separable: er gibt an, hat angegeben. On forms it means to state: das Alter angeben — to state your age.

The word, taken apart.
angeben is built on an "on" and geben "to give" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Old High German geban, from Proto-Germanic gebaną.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Angeber — show-off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: der Angeber.
der Angeber — "show-off".
Der neue Kollege ist ein echter Angeber. The new colleague is a real show-off.
Plural: die Angeber. A woman is die Angeberin.

The word, taken apart.
Angeber is built on angeben "to show off" with -er; geben is inherited from Old High German geban, from Proto-Germanic gebaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
bescheiden — modest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to show off, then the German for show-off.

You'll want to know: bescheiden.
bescheiden — "modest".
Trotz ihres Erfolgs ist die Sängerin bescheiden geblieben. Despite her success, the singer has stayed modest.
Of things it means modest, small: ein bescheidenes Gehalt.

The word, taken apart.
bescheiden is usually traced to the past participle of Middle High German bescheiden "to decide, to allot", built on scheiden "to separate"; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
eitel — vain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for show-off, then the German for modest.

You'll want to know: eitel.
eitel — "vain".
Er ist so eitel, dass er ständig in den Spiegel schaut. He is so vain that he keeps looking in the mirror.
Old meaning: pure, mere — eitel Sonnenschein, nothing but sunshine.

The word, taken apart.
eitel is inherited from Middle High German ītel "empty, vain" and Old High German ītal, from Proto-Germanic īdalaz "empty" (EtymDB); English idle is a cousin.
