Lesson 1 of 6.
la nappe — tablecloth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sponge, then the French for floor cloth.

You'll want to know: la nappe.
la nappe — "tablecloth".
Pour la fête, ma mère sort la belle nappe blanche. For the party my mother gets out the nice white tablecloth.
Also a sheet of water or fog: une nappe de brouillard.

The word, taken apart.
la nappe is inherited from Latin mappa "a napkin, a cloth" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with its first sound changed.

Lesson 2 of 6.
le couvert — place setting; cutlery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for floor cloth, then the French for tablecloth.

You'll want to know: le couvert.
le couvert — "place setting; cutlery".
Les enfants mettent le couvert avant le dîner. The children set out the plates and cutlery before dinner.
Mettre le couvert — to set out plates and cutlery for a meal. Les couverts — the cutlery.

The word, taken apart.
le couvert is the participle of couvrir "to cover" (EtymDB), inherited from Latin cooperīre "to cover completely" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
débarrasser — to clear away (after a meal); to get rid of.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for tablecloth, then the French for place setting.

You'll want to know: débarrasser.
débarrasser — "to clear away (after a meal); to get rid of".
Après le repas, mon père débarrasse la table. After the meal my father clears the dinner table.
Se débarrasser de — to get rid of.

The word, taken apart.
débarrasser is the opposite of embarrasser "to hinder". The origin of this root is not settled; a link with barre "bar" is often proposed.

Lesson 4 of 6.
la louche — ladle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for place setting, then the French for to clear away.

You'll want to know: la louche.
la louche — "ladle".
Elle sert la soupe avec une louche. She serves the soup with a ladle.
As an adjective, louche means shady: une affaire louche. That is a different word.

The word, taken apart.
This louche "a ladle" is usually traced to Frankish lōtja, a Germanic word. The adjective louche "shady" is a different word, from Latin luscus "one-eyed" (EtymDB).

le couvercle — "lid".
Avec le couvercle sur la casserole, ça cuit plus vite. With the lid on the pan, it cooks faster.
Built on couvrir "to cover".

The word, taken apart.
le couvercle is inherited from Latin cooperculum "a lid", from cooperīre "to cover completely" (Lewis & Short).
