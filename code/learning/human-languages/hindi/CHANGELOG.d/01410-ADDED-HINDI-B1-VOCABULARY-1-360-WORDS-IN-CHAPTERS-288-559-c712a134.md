## Added — Hindi B1 vocabulary, 1,360 words in chapters 288–559

- Added 272 chapters of five words each (1,360 headwords, 368 of them
  verbs), one word per lesson, with eight reviews in each of five tranches:
  - 288–342: narrating (theft and the police, accidents and fire,
    earthquakes, storms and floods, rescue and relief, the news, kings and
    wars, festivals, legends and books);
  - 343–397: reasons and conditions (causes and consequences, argument and
    proof, rights and the courts, government and citizens, research, then
    possibility, conditions, effort and success);
  - 398–451: travel (trains, roads, airports and customs, a place to stay,
    the way, the town, money and the market, the weather, nature, the farm);
  - 452–505: experience (feelings and character, the body and health, the
    family);
  - 506–559: work and crafts, repairs and the kitchen, free time, the arts
    and sport, then plans, means, obstacles, luck and choices.
- Every verb shows its past with ने and the right agreement (उसने बटुआ
  चुराया; वह थैले पर झपटा); every noun's gender is checked against every
  word that agrees with it. Examples use only words taught earlier and only
  the grammar the core teaches: no subjunctive, no passive, कहना rather than
  बोलना in the perfective.
- The first words of chapters 288–295 practise the core's last grammar
  points again, two words each; chapters 302 and 319 bring back ऐ, 305 and
  322 ः.
- Every word has a cited origin: CDIAL, Monier-Williams, or a named Persian,
  Arabic or English donor hedged as the usual tracing; no etymology is
  invented. Root slugs reuse the corpus spelling of each etymon.
- Echo words, a taught word plus a light verb, plain derivations of taught
  words, near-synonym clusters beyond two, and homographs of inflected forms
  were replaced during review.
