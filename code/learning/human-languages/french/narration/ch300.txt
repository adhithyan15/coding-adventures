French, chapter 300: Les secours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
les secours — the emergency services, help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the haematoma, then the French for the wound.

You'll want to know: les secours.
les secours — "the emergency services, help".
Les secours sont arrivés en dix minutes. The emergency services arrived in ten minutes.
Au secours! — help! Les premiers secours — first aid.

The word, taken apart.
secours is inherited from Latin succursus "help" (EtymDB), from succurrere "to run to help".

Lesson 2 of 5.
secourir — to rescue, to help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the wound, then the French for the emergency services.

You'll want to know: secourir.
secourir — "to rescue, to help".
Des voisins ont secouru la vieille dame. Neighbours helped the old lady.
Conjugated like courir: il a secouru.

The word, taken apart.
secourir is inherited from Latin succurrere "to run to help", from sub and currere "to run" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
réanimer — to resuscitate, to revive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the emergency services, then the French for to rescue.

You'll want to know: réanimer.
réanimer — "to resuscitate, to revive".
Les pompiers ont réanimé l'homme. The firefighters revived the man.
Le service de réanimation — intensive care.

The word, taken apart.
réanimer is built on animer, a learned borrowing from Latin animāre "to give life", from anima "breath, life" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le garrot — the tourniquet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to rescue, then the French for to resuscitate.

You'll want to know: le garrot.
le garrot — "the tourniquet".
Un médecin a posé un garrot sur sa jambe. A doctor put a tourniquet on his leg.
Also the withers of a horse.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of garrot is not settled; it is sometimes linked to Provençal garra "leg" (EtymDB).
