French, chapter 516: Pinceaux et couleurs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le pinceau — paintbrush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for actor, then the French for applause.

You'll want to know: le pinceau.
le pinceau — "paintbrush".
L'artiste nettoie ses pinceaux après le travail. The artist cleans her brushes after work.
Plural les pinceaux. For walls, a bigger brush is une brosse.

The word, taken apart.
le pinceau comes from Old French pincel (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin pēnicellus, a form of Latin pēniculus "a brush" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: la toile.
la toile — "canvas; cloth; web".
Le peintre a vendu sa plus grande toile. The painter sold his biggest canvas.
Une toile d'araignée — a spider's web. La Toile — the Web.

The word, taken apart.
la toile is inherited from Latin tēla "a web, a cloth on the loom" (EtymDB), related to texere "to weave" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'aquarelle — watercolour.

You'll want to know: l'aquarelle.
l'aquarelle — "watercolour".
Elle peint des paysages à l'aquarelle. She paints landscapes in watercolour.
Feminine. Both the technique and a painting.

The word, taken apart.
l'aquarelle was borrowed from Italian acquarella, built on acqua "water", inherited from Latin aqua (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: le chevalet.
le chevalet — "easel".
Le peintre installe son chevalet au bord de la rivière. The painter sets up his easel by the river.
Literally a little horse.

The word, taken apart.
le chevalet is built on cheval "horse" with the small ending -et (EtymDB); cheval is inherited from Latin caballus "a horse" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
esquisser — to sketch, to outline.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for watercolour, then the French for easel.

You'll want to know: esquisser.
esquisser — "to sketch, to outline".
L'architecte a esquissé la maison en quelques minutes. The architect sketched the house in a few minutes.
Une esquisse — a sketch. Esquisser un sourire — to give a faint smile.

The word, taken apart.
esquisser is built on esquisse "a sketch", borrowed from Italian schizzo (EtymDB), whose origin is usually traced to a Latin word for a sudden improvised poem.
