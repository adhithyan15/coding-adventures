Japanese, chapter 195: North, Opposite and Past.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
きた (kita) — north.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pass through, then the Japanese for museum.

You'll want to know: きた.
きた — kita — "north".
えきの きたに こうえんが あります。 — eki no kita ni kōen ga arimasu — "There is a park north of the station."

The word, taken apart.
きた (kita) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 6.
つきあたり (tsukiatari) — end of the street; dead end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for museum, then the Japanese for north.

You'll want to know: つきあたり.
つきあたり — tsukiatari — "end of the street; dead end".
この みちの つきあたりに ゆうびんきょくが あります。 — kono michi no tsukiatari ni yūbinkyoku ga arimasu — "The post office is at the end of this road."

The word, taken apart.
つきあたり (tsukiatari) is native Japanese, built from tsuki, "thrusting", and atari, "hitting", from ataru, "to hit": the place where a street runs into something (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for end of the street, then the Japanese for across the street.

You'll want to know: すぎる.
すぎる — sugiru — "to go past; to pass, to exceed".
あの しんごうを すぎます。 — ano shingō o sugimasu — "We go past that traffic light."
Polite すぎます, sugimasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
すぎる (sugiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi; JMdict marks at least one of its senses as usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
もどる (modoru) — to go back; to return.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for across the street, then the Japanese for to go past.

You'll want to know: もどる.
もどる — modoru — "to go back; to return".
ちょっと えきに もどります。 — chotto eki ni modorimasu — "I am going back to the station for a moment."
Polite もどります, modorimasu; a godan verb. Its transitive partner もどす is taught already.

The word, taken apart.
もどる (modoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it shares its stem with modosu, "to put back" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
