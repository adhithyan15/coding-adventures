Gujarati, chapter 334: The Hidden Treasure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ખજાનો (khajāno) — a treasure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for vanished, then the Gujarati for to appear.

You'll want to know: ખજાનો.
ખજાનો — khajāno — "a treasure".
ગુફામાં એક જૂનો ખજાનો હતો. guphāmā̃ ek jūno khajāno hato. There was an old treasure in the cave.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ખજાનો (khajāno) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic khizāna, "a storehouse, a treasury". It is masculine: જૂનો ખજાનો.

Lesson 2 of 5.

અજગર — ajgar — "a python".
ખજાના પાસે એક મોટો અજગર બેઠો હતો. khajānā pāse ek moṭo ajgar beṭho hato. A huge python lay beside the treasure.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
અજગર (ajgar) is a tatsama, Sanskrit ajagara, "a goat-swallower", a large snake (Monier-Williams). In stories it guards treasure.

Lesson 4 of 5.
લલચાવવું (lalcāvvũ) — to tempt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a python, then the Gujarati for greed.

You'll want to know: લલચાવવું.
લલચાવવું — lalcāvvũ — "to tempt".
ખજાનાએ ચોરને લલચાવ્યો. khajānāe corne lalcāvyo. The treasure tempted the thief.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બાળકને લલચાવ્યું, teṇe bāḷakne lalcāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
લલચાવવું (lalcāvvũ) is probably built on lālac, a longing: CDIAL sets the Hindi cousins lālac, "cupidity", and lalcānā, "to lust after", under Sanskrit lālitya, "amorousness" (CDIAL 11029). To tempt is to make someone long for something. The one tempted takes ને.

ચોર ઘણા દિવસ જંગલમાં ભટક્યો. cor ghaṇā divas jangalmā̃ bhaṭakyo. The thief wandered in the forest for many days.
Past (he …): તે જંગલમાં ભટક્યો, te jangalmā̃ bhaṭakyo.

The word, taken apart.
is listed by CDIAL under bhaṭ, "sudden movement or noise", glossed "to blaze, start" (CDIAL 9365), while CDIAL files the Hindi cousin bhaṭaknā, "to wander", under Sanskrit bhraṣṭá, "fallen" (CDIAL 9655); which line the wandering sense belongs to is not settled. રખડવું is to roam; ભટકવું (bhaṭakvũ) is to wander without finding the way.
