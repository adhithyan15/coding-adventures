# Herz — and the last few this book showed you exactly once

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-HERZ-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The heart. Say it, and let the **z** land as *ts* rather than as an
English *z*.

## Across the family
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-HERZ-03, GE-LEX-HAUPT-02, GE-CULTURE-HEAD-CONTAINERS-05] -->

**Herz** and English **heart** are the same word, separated by one regular
sound change: the **k** of the older form became **h** in the Germanic line, the
same swap already heard between *hören* and *hear*, and between *Hund* and
*hound*. Once you have seen it three times it stops being a curiosity and
becomes something you can use to guess.

**Haupt** and **head** are that same pair — and this is the one place the guess
would mislead you, because the word it predicts is not the word Germans say.
For the thing on your shoulders they reach for the old cup-word instead. A
sound law tells you which words are cousins. It does not tell you which cousin
people chose.

## Guided Practice: the apology, and the two warm thank-yous
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ENTSCHULDIGUNG-02, GE-PRAGMATICS-SORRY-04, GE-LEX-DANKE-SCHOEN-02, GE-LEX-VIELEN-DANK-02] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: bump into someone — *Entschuldigung*]
- [YOU SAY: stop a stranger to ask something — and notice it is the same word]
- [YOU SAY: the thank-you with the adjective — *danke schön*]
- [YOU SAY: the warmest everyday one — *vielen Dank*]

English splits *sorry* from *excuse me*; German does not, and your voice carries
the difference the vocabulary declines to.
