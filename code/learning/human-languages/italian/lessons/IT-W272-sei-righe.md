# Six lines about a journey

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter two hundred and sixty-two asked for four lines that held
together. This one asks for six, and they tell a story.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it: *un amico*, *un'amica*, someone
in your family.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-STORIA-IN-ORDINE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-SE-PRESENTE-01, IT-LEX-SAREBBE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A told story is not a list of events. It has a **scene**, in the
imperfetto: *era*, *c'era*, *faceva*, *ero contento*. It has **events**, in the
passato prossimo, put in order with *prima*, *poi* and *alla fine*. It has at
least one **reason**, pointing back with *perché* or forward with *quindi*.

[PAUSE 2s] And a good one ends by looking forward: *spero di …*, or *sarebbe
bello …*, or a real condition, *se …*, with the present in the *se* half.

[PAUSE 2s] This book's B1 writing paper asks for a connected text of 130 to
170 words. Six lines is the first step towards it, not the whole of it.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01, IT-LEX-ERA-01, IT-LEX-C-ERA-02, IT-LEX-FACEVA-01, IT-GRAMMAR-POI-01, IT-GRAMMAR-ALLA-FINE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-COSI-QUINDI-02, IT-GRAMMAR-SENTIMENTO-PASSATO-01, IT-LEX-SPERO-DI-01, IT-GRAMMAR-CONDIZIONALE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-SAREBBE-BELLO-02, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-SPIEGAZIONE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-PER-DURATA-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] On paper, write **six lines** to your reader about a journey,
real or invented, one line for each of these:

1. the scene: when it was, and the weather;
2. where you were, who was with you, and how long you stayed, with *per*;
3. the first thing that happened, with *prima*;
4. the next thing, with *poi*, and a reason, with *perché* or *quindi*;
5. how it ended and how you felt, with *alla fine* and *ero*;
6. one line forward, with *spero di*, *sarebbe bello* or *se*.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing
the right past for each line, and joining them.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01, IT-GRAMMAR-STORIA-IN-ORDINE-01, IT-LEX-ESSERE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-SE-PRESENTE-01, IT-SKILL-READ-NARRATIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now read your lines as you read the journey in chapter two hundred
and sixty-four. Mark every verb **S** for scene or **E** for event. Lines one and five should be mostly **S**; three
and four mostly **E**; in line two, a *per* stretch is **E**. A line about the weather marked **E** is the commonest
slip: change *ha piovuto* back to *pioveva*.

[PAUSE 2s] Then two spelling checks. A longer word stressed on its last
vowel carries the accent there, **perché**, **città**, and so do a few short
words you write all the time: **è**, **più**, **sì**. And
every double consonant is held: *aspettato*, *abbiamo*, *raccontava*.

[PAUSE 2s] Last, read line six alone. If it has a *se*, is the verb after it in
the plain present? If it has *sarebbe*, is it your verdict on something that
has not happened yet? Then it looks forward, and the story is finished.
