Mandarin Chinese, chapter 176: A Knock at the Door.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
qiāomén — to knock at the door.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a cupboard, then the Mandarin for a bookshelf.

You'll want to know: qiāomén.
qiāomén — "to knock at the door".
zuótiān wǎnshang yǒu rén qiāomén — Somebody knocked at the door last night.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qiāomén is built from qiāo 'to strike' and 门 'door', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khaew and mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Lesson 2 of 5.
línjū — neighbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bookshelf, then the Mandarin for to knock at the door.

You'll want to know: línjū.
línjū — "neighbour".
qiāomén de rén shì wǒ de línjū — The one knocking was my neighbour.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
línjū is built from lín 'near' and jū 'to dwell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lin and kjo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the one who dwells near. After an older book, the dictionary also counts five households together as one lín.

Lesson 3 of 5.
chǎojià — to quarrel; to have a row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to knock at the door, then the Mandarin for a neighbour.

You'll want to know: chǎojià.
chǎojià — "to quarrel; to have a row".
tā chángcháng gēn línjū chǎojià — He's forever quarrelling with the neighbours.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chǎojià is built from 吵 'noisy' and jià. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsrhaewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a sound'; the second is the word for a frame, and how it came to mean a fight is not settled.

What you've built.
