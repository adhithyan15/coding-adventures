# ಬಾಯಿ (bāyi) — "mouth," a word you've already met twice

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C32-BAA-01, KA-ETYMON-C33-BARE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Bā*, come; *bare*, write. Both chapters used one more word in
passing, to prove the same sound law, and neither one told you what it
meant.

## You'll want to know: ಬಾಯಿ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C37-BAAYI-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C37-MOOGU-01] -->

> **ಬಾಯಿ** — *bāyi* — **mouth**

> **ನನ್ನ ಬಾಯಿ.** — *nanna bāyi.* — "My mouth."

Four body words now: *tale*, *kai*, *kaṇṇu*, *kivi*, *mūgu*, *bāyi* — six.

## The word, taken apart: the third proof of a law you already know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C37-BAAYI-02]; assesses=[] -->

**ಬಾಯಿ** answers Tamil **வாய்** (*vāy*), "mouth," through the exact law
Chapter 32 gave you: word-initial Tamil *v-* becomes Kannada *b-*. You have
already seen this pair once, sitting quietly inside Chapter 32's etymology
note for **ಬಾ**, and again inside Chapter 33's for **ಬರೆ** — both times as a
worked example, neither time explained. Here is what it means:

| Tamil | Kannada | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *vā* | *bā* | come |
| *varai* | *bare* | write, draw |
| *vāy* | *bāyi* | mouth |

Three clean proofs of one law, and this lesson finally cashes in the third.
