# demasiado — too much

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BASTANTE, ES-LEX-C317-ANSW-20] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **bastante** and **poco**. You have a scale with a bottom and a
middle. Today it gets its top.

## You'll want to know: demasiado
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE] -->

**demasiado** — too much, too many, too.

> El curso era **demasiado** difícil para mí.

Before an adjective it does not change: *demasiado difícil*, *demasiado
avanzada*. Before a noun it agrees like any adjective: *demasiada gente*,
*demasiados libros*.

## Grammar Lens: the word that says it went past the point
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01, ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE] -->

[Bastante](./ES-C60-bastante.md) built you a scale of degree. This is the
member above the top of it, and the step matters.

| the word | where on the scale |
|---|---|
| poco | below what is wanted |
| bastante | enough — the point itself |
| **demasiado** | past the point |

*Muy difícil* is a judgement about the thing. **Demasiado difícil** is a
judgement about a **fit**: too hard *for something*, usually for you. That is
why it so often arrives with *para* — *demasiado avanzado para mí*.

English blurs this, because *too* and *very* sit close together in speech.
Spanish does not: say *muy* when you mean intense, and *demasiado* when you
mean it has gone past what works.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C471-CURSO-01] -->

Underneath is **de más** — *of more*, in excess — which became *demasía*, an
excess, and then this adjective. The *más* in it is the *más* you already use,
from Latin **magis**, more.

So the word is built out of an admission: there is **more** here than the
situation has room for. Not more than exists, more than **fits**.

Keep that and the grammar follows. *Demasiado* always implies a measure it has
overrun, even when nobody says what the measure was.
