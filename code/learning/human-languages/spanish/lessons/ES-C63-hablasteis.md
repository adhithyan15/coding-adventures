# hablasteis — the form you were promised

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-3PL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The *-ar* past, as far as you have it: *hablé*, *hablaste*, *habló*,
*hablamos*, *hablaron*.

[PAUSE 1s] Five of six. The review that closed the preterite told you the sixth
was still owed. Here it is.

## Grammar Lens: -steis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-2PL]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VOSOTROS] -->

> *vosotros* ***hablasteis*** — you all spoke

[PAUSE 2s] Look at it beside the *you* form you already know:

| | |
|---|---|
| *tú* | *habla* ***ste*** |
| *vosotros* | *habla* ***steis*** |

[PAUSE 2s] The same *-ste*, with *-is* stacked on the end. That *-is* is the
plural marker you met back in the present, in *habláis*. Nothing here is new;
it is two pieces you own, in a row.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRETERITE-AR-COMPLETE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] And with that, the *-ar* preterite is **complete**:

*hablé · hablaste · habló · hablamos · hablasteis · hablaron*

[PAUSE 2s] Six forms, no gaps. It is the first tense in this book you can say
straight through without a promissory note at the end.

[PAUSE 2s] As with every *vosotros* form, you will hear it in Spain and rarely
elsewhere — but you will **read** it everywhere, which is reason enough to know
it on sight.
