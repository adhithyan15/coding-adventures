Portuguese, chapter 318: Vamos festejar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
festejar — to celebrate (with a party).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for emigration, then the Portuguese for the emigrant.

You'll want to know: festejar.
festejar — "to celebrate (with a party)".
Festejámos o aniversário dela num restaurante. We celebrated her birthday in a restaurant.
Livelier than celebrar: music, food, friends.

The word, taken apart.
festejar is built on festa "a party", from Latin fēsta "feast days" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists it beside Occitan festejar and French festoyer.

Lesson 2 of 5.
comemorar — to commemorate, to mark (a date).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the emigrant, then the Portuguese for to celebrate.

You'll want to know: comemorar.
comemorar — "to commemorate, to mark (a date)".
A cidade comemora o fim da guerra todos os anos. The city marks the end of the war every year.
In Brazil it is also the everyday word for ‘to celebrate’.

The word, taken apart.
comemorar is a learned borrowing from Latin commemorāre "to call to mind" (EtymDB), built on memor "mindful".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o brinde — the toast (with drinks); a free gift.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to commemorate, then the Portuguese for the commemoration.

You'll want to know: o brinde.
o brinde — "the toast (with drinks); a free gift".
No fim do jantar, fizemos um brinde aos noivos. At the end of dinner we drank a toast to the bride and groom.
In shops, um brinde is a free gift.

The word, taken apart.
brinde goes back to a German toast, ich bring dir’s "I bring it to you" (EtymDB), which reached Portuguese through Spanish and Italian.
