Portuguese, chapter 384: Documentos em dia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
válido — valid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the offence, then the Portuguese for to transgress.

You'll want to know: válido.
válido — "valid".
O meu passaporte ainda é válido. My passport is still valid.
Válido até — valid until.

The word, taken apart.
válido is a learned borrowing from Latin validus "strong, healthy, in good order" (EtymDB), from valēre "to be strong" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
caducar — to expire, to lapse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to transgress, then the Portuguese for valid.

You'll want to know: caducar.
caducar — "to expire, to lapse".
O cartão caducou no mês passado. The card expired last month.
European Portuguese, of documents; caducado — expired.

The word, taken apart.
caducar is built on caduco "falling, worn out" (EtymDB), from Latin cadūcus "falling, perishable", on cadere "to fall" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
expirar — to run out, to expire; to breathe out.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for valid, then the Portuguese for to expire.

You'll want to know: expirar.
expirar — "to run out, to expire; to breathe out".
O prazo expira amanhã. The deadline runs out tomorrow.
Also to breathe out: inspirar e expirar.

The word, taken apart.
expirar is a learned borrowing from Latin exspīrāre "to breathe out, to expire" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from ex and spīrāre "to breathe".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o certificado — the certificate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to run out, then the Portuguese for to renew.

You'll want to know: o certificado.
o certificado — "the certificate".
Recebi o certificado no fim do curso. I received the certificate at the end of my course.
Certificado de habilitações — certificate of qualifications.

The word, taken apart.
certificado is the participle of certificar, which is usually traced to Late Latin certificāre "to make certain", on certus "sure" (Lewis & Short).
