Bengali, chapter 44: Ear, Fever, Hospital, Pain, Hunger.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
কান (kān) — an ear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for the tongue, then the Bengali for the head.

You'll want to know: কান.
কান — kān — "an ear".

The word, taken apart.
কান (kān) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karṇa, "ear" (CDIAL 2830).

You'll want to know: জ্বর.
জ্বর — jbôr — "a fever".

The word, taken apart.
জ্বর (jbôr) is Sanskrit jvara, "fever", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 5303). The word inherited from it is jar, which CDIAL lists.

খিদে — khide — "hunger".

খিদে (khide) is usually traced to Sanskrit kṣudhā, "hunger". CDIAL has that entry but lists no Bengali form under it (CDIAL 3716), so the line of descent is not confirmed there.
