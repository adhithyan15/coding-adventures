Bengali, chapter 332: One Thing Leads to Another.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
সুতরাং (sutôrāng) — therefore; and so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a financial loss, then the Bengali for useful.

You'll want to know: সুতরাং.
সুতরাং — sutôrāng — "therefore; and so".
বাস নেই, সুতরাং আমরা হেঁটে যাব। bās nei, sutôrāng āmrā hẽṭe jābô. There is no bus, so we will walk.

The word, taken apart.
সুতরাং (sutôrāng) is Sanskrit sutarām, 'all the more' (Monier-Williams), with its final m heard as ng. The sense 'therefore' is not one the dictionary gives; in Bengali the word draws the conclusion from what was said before.

You'll want to know: তাছাড়া.
তাছাড়া — tāchhāṛā — "besides; apart from that".
আজ আমি ক্লান্ত, তাছাড়া আমার অনেক কাজ আছে। āj āmi klāntô, tāchhāṛā āmār ônek kāj āchhe. I'm tired today, and besides, I have a lot of work.

The word, taken apart.
তাছাড়া (tāchhāṛā) is তা 'that' with ছাড়া 'apart from' (CDIAL 5612 and 4998 list Bengali tā and chāṛā): 'apart from that'. It adds a second reason to the first.

Lesson 3 of 5.
অথচ (ôthôch) — and yet; even so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for therefore, then the Bengali for besides.

You'll want to know: অথচ.
অথচ — ôthôch — "and yet; even so".
মীরা অনেক পড়ল, অথচ পাস করল না। mīrā ônek pôṛlô, ôthôch pās kôrlô nā. Mira studied a lot, and yet she did not pass.

The word, taken apart.
অথচ (ôthôch) is Sanskrit atha ca, 'and moreover' (Monier-Williams). The Bengali sense, 'and yet', which links a result you would not expect, is not one the dictionary gives.

Lesson 4 of 5.
অবশ্য (ôbôshyô) — of course; mind you.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for besides, then the Bengali for and yet.

You'll want to know: অবশ্য.
অবশ্য — ôbôshyô — "of course; mind you".
হোটেলটা দামি, অবশ্য খাবার দুর্দান্ত। hoṭelṭā dāmi, ôbôshyô khābār durdāntô. The hotel is expensive, though mind you, the food is excellent.

The word, taken apart.
অবশ্য (ôbôshyô) is Sanskrit avaśyam, 'necessarily' (Monier-Williams). Bengali uses it to grant a point while keeping your own: 'of course', 'mind you'.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: খাটা.
খাটা — khāṭā — "to hold good; to apply (of a rule); to toil".
এই নিয়মটা এখানে খাটে না। ei niyômṭā ekhāne khāṭe nā. This rule doesn't apply here.
Past (he/she …): সেই নিয়মটা খাটল না, sei niyômṭā khāṭlô nā.

The word, taken apart.
খাটা (khāṭā) is listed by CDIAL 3779 (khaṭṭáyati) with the senses 'to work' and 'to apply'. A person খাটে when working hard; a rule or a reason খাটে when it holds in a case.
