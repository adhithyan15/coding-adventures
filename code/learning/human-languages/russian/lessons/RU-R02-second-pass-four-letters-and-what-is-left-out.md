# с н б д — four letters, two kinds of trouble

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-PRAGMATICS-GREETING-REGISTER] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The long polite greeting, from memory, and who you would say it to.
Then say who you would not.

## Script: two that lie, two that are only new
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-S-01, RU-SCRIPT-N-01, RU-SCRIPT-B-01, RU-SCRIPT-D-01] -->

| letter | the trouble |
|---|---|
| **с** | looks like a Latin *c*, says **s** |
| **н** | looks like a Latin *n*, says **n** — but the shape is not the one you expect |
| **б** | a genuinely new shape, says **b** |
| **д** | a genuinely new shape, says **d** |

Two kinds of difficulty, needing different handling. **с** and **н** are false
friends: your eye reads those shapes already, and reads them wrong, so the work
is **unlearning**. **б** and **д** arrive with nothing attached, so the work is
only **learning**. The second kind is easier, and mixing the two up is why the
first kind keeps catching people out.

## Grammar Lens: the verb that is not there
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-ZERO-COPULA-01, RU-DRILL-PERSON-SHAPES-01] -->

Russian leaves out the present tense of *to be*. Where English needs *am*, *is*
or *are*, Russian sets the two halves of the sentence side by side and stops.

The person words are the other half of that. Subject and object forms differ,
and with no verb to carry the person, **the pronoun is doing more work than its
English counterpart** — which is why the shapes are worth drilling rather than
recognising.

[PAUSE 3s] Which two of the four are false friends, and why is that harder? (**с** and **н** — you have to **unlearn** a reading, not only learn one.) What does Russian leave out of a present-tense sentence? (**The verb *to be***.) And what does that put on the pronoun? (**More work** — it carries the person alone.)
