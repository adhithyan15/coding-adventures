# el sofá — the one you could not guess

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SILLA, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la silla*. You can name one seat. Name the long soft one that
three people share.

## You'll want to know: el sofá
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SILLA] -->

**el sofá** — the sofa.

Stressed on the last syllable, *so-FÁ*, and the written accent is there to say
so.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-05, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-04] -->

**This one was not built from anything you hold.** Four words in this chapter
came apart into a piece plus an ending; this one did not, and pretending
otherwise would waste your time.

It came into Spanish from Arabic, where the word named a raised, cushioned
bench along a wall. Spanish took hundreds of words from Arabic over several
centuries, and they tend to arrive **whole** like this rather than as pieces you
can recombine.

So the honest lesson of the chapter is two-sided:

| when a word comes apart | when it does not |
|---|---|
| look for the ending first | learn it whole, and move on |

Knowing which kind you are looking at is the skill. Trying to decompose *sofá*
would cost you minutes and teach you nothing.
