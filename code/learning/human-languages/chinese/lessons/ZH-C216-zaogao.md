## You'll want to know: zāogāo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C216-WRONG216-01]; assesses=[] -->

*zāogāo* — "oh no; what a mess".

> *zāogāo wǒ de qiánbāo diū le* — Oh no, I've lost my wallet!

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*zāogāo* is built from *zāo* 'dregs' and *gāo* 'cake', both inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): *tsaw*, glossed 'dregs', and probably the *kaw* that the dictionary writes with another form of the character, glossed with a word for gruel. How the pair came to mean a mess is not settled.
