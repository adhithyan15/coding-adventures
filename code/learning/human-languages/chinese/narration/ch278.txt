You'll want to know: 最后.
最后 — zuìhòu — "last; finally; in the end".
最后我们回家了 zuìhòu wǒmen huí jiā le — In the end we went home.
今天是最后一天 jīntiān shì zuìhòu yì tiān — Today is the last day.
The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. It is the zuì you already say for most.

The word, taken apart.
最后 (zuìhòu) is built from 最 (zuì) 'most' and 后 'after', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tswajH and huwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'utmost' and lists the second, under its traditional form, with 'before and after'; 'last' for the pair is the word's own sense.

What you've built.
最后 opens the last step of a story: 最后我们回家了 zuìhòu wǒmen huí jiā le. Before a noun it picks out the last one: 最后一天 zuìhòu yì tiān.
The 了 at the end is the 了 you know: it happened.

日 对.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rì, duì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 日, six strokes, then 又.
最 (zuì).
Three pieces, and you write two of them already.
日, four strokes, wide and flat on top: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the middle bar, the bottom bar.
Six strokes under it: a long horizontal across the whole width; then, on the left, two verticals down from it, the left one first; two short horizontals between them, the upper one first; and last a short stroke at the foot that rises to the right.
又, two strokes, on the right, as on the left of 对: a horizontal that turns and falls to the lower left, then a long right-falling stroke.
4 + 6 + 2 = twelve.

Grammar Lens: 先, 然后, 最后 — first, then, last.
To tell things in order, put 先 before the first verb, 然后 at the front of the next step, and 最后 (zuìhòu) at the front of the last.
我先吃饭 然后看书 最后回家 wǒ xiān chī fàn, ránhòu kàn shū, zuìhòu huí jiā — First I eat, then I read, and last I go home.
然后 is the ránhòu you have said since the chapter on the past. Now it prints: the next lesson puts 然 (rán) in your hand.
先 sits after the person, right before the verb: 我先吃饭. 然后 and 最后 (zuìhòu) open their clause, and the person need not be said again.
他先去学校 然后去打工 tā xiān qù xuéxiào, ránhòu qù dǎgōng — He goes to school first, then to his part-time job.
The order of the clauses is the order of the events.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — dà, diǎn, rè]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a four-stroke piece, 大 with a dot, and four dots.
然 (rán).
Three pieces, and you write the last two already.
The upper left, four strokes: a left-falling stroke; a horizontal that turns and falls to the lower left; then two dots inside it, the upper one first. It is not 月: there is no hooked frame and no inner bar.
大 with a dot, four strokes, on the upper right: the bar, the left-falling stroke from above it, the right-falling stroke, and last a dot at its upper right.
Four dots along the foot, from left to right, as at the foot of 点 and 热.
4 + 4 + 4 = twelve.

Lesson 5 of 8.
shǒuxiān — first of all.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: shǒuxiān.
shǒuxiān — "first of all; to begin with".
shǒuxiān wǒ xiǎng shuō xièxie — First of all, I'd like to say thank you.
shǒuxiān wǒmen qù mǎi dōngxi — To begin with, we go shopping.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shǒuxiān is built from shǒu 'the head' and 先 'first', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuwX and sen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'the head' and 'the beginning', and the second 'before and after'.

What you've built.
先 sits inside the clause, after the person: 我先吃饭. shǒuxiān opens the sentence, before the person, and it sounds planned: the first of several points.
然后 and 最后 follow it as usual: shǒuxiān …, ránhòu …, zuìhòu ….

Lesson 6 of 8.
jiēzhe — next; straight after that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, and the pattern it opens.
[your turn — recall: say shǒuxiān, first of all — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: 先 … 然后 … 最后, aloud — xiān … ránhòu … zuìhòu]

You'll want to know: jiēzhe.
jiēzhe — "next; straight after that".
wǒ xiān xǐ yīfu, jiēzhe zuòfàn — First I wash the clothes, and straight after that I cook.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiēzhe is built from jiē 'to join on' and a light ending, zhe. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsjep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to join' and 'to hold'. The second is written with a character the dictionary lists under five readings, drjo, trjoX, trjoH, trjak and drjak, and it glosses none of them as this ending, so the history of that part is not settled.

What you've built.
ránhòu leaves a little room between the steps; jiēzhe says the next one follows on at once.
Like 然后, it opens its clause: wǒ xiān xǐ yīfu, jiēzhe zuòfàn.

Grammar Lens: 了 … 以后, after doing something.
To say one thing comes after another is done, put 了 after the first verb and 以后 at the end of its clause; the next thing follows.
吃了饭以后 我们去买东西 chī le fàn yǐhòu, wǒmen qù mǎi dōngxi — After we've eaten, we'll go shopping. 下了课以后 他去打工 xià le kè yǐhòu, tā qù dǎgōng — After class he goes to his part-time job.
了 here is the 了 of a finished action, the one in 我看了书: the first event is complete before the second starts. It works for what happened and for what is planned; the rest of the sentence tells you which.
下课 opens up to take 了 in the middle: 下了课.

The exchange.
A: 你早上做什么 nǐ zǎoshang zuò shénme — What do you do in the morning? B: 我先洗手 然后吃早饭 wǒ xiān xǐ shǒu, ránhòu chī zǎofàn — I wash my hands first, then have breakfast. A: 吃了早饭以后呢 chī le zǎofàn yǐhòu ne — And after breakfast? B: jiēzhe qù xuéxiào — Straight off to school. A: 下了课以后呢 xià le kè yǐhòu ne — And after class? B: 先去打工 最后回家 xiān qù dǎgōng, zuìhòu huí jiā — First my part-time job, and last home.
The order of the clauses is the order of the day. B never says 我 after the first line: the person is clear, so Mandarin leaves it out.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Where does B go straight after breakfast?

What you've built — telling it in order.
Five ways to keep a story in order, all at the front of a clause or right before the verb:
先 xiān — first, before the verb.
shǒuxiān — first of all, at the very front.
然后 ránhòu and jiēzhe — then, and straight after.
最后 zuìhòu — last.
吃了饭以后 chī le fàn yǐhòu — after doing something.
Listen. Nǐ zuótiān xiān zuò le shénme?
Speak. Tell your morning in four steps: 先, 然后, jiēzhe, 最后 (zuìhòu).
