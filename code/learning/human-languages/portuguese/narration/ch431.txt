Portuguese, chapter 431: Calor e frio no quarto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o radiador — the radiator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to unblock, then the Portuguese for the toilet cistern.

You'll want to know: o radiador.
o radiador — "the radiator".
O radiador do quarto está frio. The radiator in the room is cold.
A car has um radiador too.

The word, taken apart.
radiador is built on radiar 'to give off rays', a learned borrowing from Latin radiāre, on radius 'ray, spoke' (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: o esquentador.
o esquentador — "the water heater (gas)".
O esquentador avariou e não há água quente. The water heater broke down and there is no hot water.
In Brazil o aquecedor covers both. Esquentar — to heat up.

The word, taken apart.
esquentador is built on esquentar 'to heat', from es- and quente 'hot' (EtymDB); quente is inherited from Latin calēns 'warm'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o termóstato — the thermostat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the heater, then the Portuguese for the water heater.

You'll want to know: o termóstato.
o termóstato — "the thermostat".
Baixei o termóstato para vinte graus. I turned the thermostat down to twenty degrees.
In Brazil: o termostato.

The word, taken apart.
termóstato is a learned coinage from Greek thermós 'hot' and statós 'standing still' (Lewis & Short has Latin thermae 'hot baths', from the same Greek word): it keeps the heat steady.
