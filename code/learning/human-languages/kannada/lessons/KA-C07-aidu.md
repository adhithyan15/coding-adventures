# ಐದು — five, and a rule you have now seen twice

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C07-NAALKU-01, KA-LEX-C07-ERADU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two lessons ago Tamil's **ṇṭ** softened into Kannada's **ḍ**. Hold
that in mind.

## You'll want to know: ಐದು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C07-AIDU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ಐದು** (*aidu*) — "**five**."

Tamil says *aintu*. Kannada says *aidu*. The hard **nt** in the middle has worn
down to a soft **d** — the same wearing-down you met in *iraṇṭu* → *eraḍu*.

Twice is enough to call it a pattern rather than a coincidence. When a Tamil
word you know has a hard cluster in the middle, guess that Kannada softened it,
and you will be right more often than not.

**ಐ** is an independent vowel: the shape *ai* takes when it opens a word.
