# la actriz — the ending that did not follow the usual rule

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01] -->

on, and expect to be wrong — the obvious guess is not what Spanish does here.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [el actor](./ES-C421-actor.md) — the doer, from *agere*, with the Latin

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la actriz** — "the actress." Not *la actora*, which is what the ordinary
Spanish habit of swapping **-o** for **-a** would have produced.

Latin had its own feminine for *-tor* nouns: **-trix**. *Actor* / **actrix**.
Spanish inherited that pair intact, turned the Latin **-x** into **-z**, and
kept it.

The ending is nearly extinct. Spanish has only a small handful of *-triz*
words left, and they are worth seeing together because the list is short enough
to hold in your head:

| masculine | feminine | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| el actor | la **actriz** | the actor |
| el emperador | la empera**triz** | the emperor |
| el institutor | la institu**triz** | the governess |

English kept the same Latin ending in **aviatrix** and **dominatrix**, and in
the mathematical **matrix** — originally *mater* plus *-trix*, "the womb", the
thing from which other things are produced.

Everywhere else in Spanish, the feminine of a person-noun is the ordinary
swap: *el camarero* / *la camarera*. **-triz** is the exception, and *actriz*
is the one you will meet most.

## Grammar Lens: two words, one root, three endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01] -->

Everything on this row came out of one Latin verb:

| word | ending | what the ending does |
|---|---|---|
| **act**o | — | the deed itself |
| el **act**or | -or | the man who does it |
| la **act**riz | -riz | the woman who does it |

Strip the endings and the same three letters are left standing: **act-**. The
root does the meaning; the ending only says *who*.

Note the article changes with the person, not with the building: *el actor del
teatro*, *la actriz del teatro*. The theatre stays masculine either way.
