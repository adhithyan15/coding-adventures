## Grammar Lens: pieces that are real, and pieces that are not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-01, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-05] -->

Three of this chapter's words look built. Two of them are.

| word | the front piece |
|---|---|
| **polideportivo** | real — Greek *polýs*, many |
| **descansar** | real — *des-*, the undoing |
| **reír** | **not a piece at all** |

The test is not how the word looks; it is whether the rest of the word survives
without the piece. Take *poli-* off and *deportivo* stands. Take *des-* off and
*cansar* stands. Take *re-* off *reír* and nothing is left, which is the proof
that it was never a prefix.

Carry that test forward. It is the one move that keeps morphology from turning
into guesswork, and it costs you a second per word.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-03, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-05, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-02] -->

**You can now say where you exercise, what goes wrong there, what you have to do
about it, and how you feel about the whole business.** That is a complete small
episode, and it is the shape a clinic conversation and a message to a friend
both take.

You also have a second doublet. **nadar** and **la natación** are one Latin verb
arriving twice, once worn down by mouths and once lifted off the page — and the
learned twin is the one on the timetable.
