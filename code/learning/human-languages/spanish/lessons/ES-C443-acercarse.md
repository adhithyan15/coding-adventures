# acercarse — making it near

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01, ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-02, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **cerca** and **lejos**. Say **interesar**. You can say where
something is. Name the act of closing the gap.

## You'll want to know: acercarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01] -->

**acercar** — to bring something nearer. **acercarse** — to come closer
yourself.

*Acércate* — come here, come closer. *Me acerqué a la ventana* — I went over to
the window. *Se acerca la fecha* — the date is getting close, which a Spanish
speaker says about an exam or a deadline the way English says *it's coming up*.

It takes **a**: *acercarse **a** algo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03, ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01] -->

**a-** plus **cerca** plus the **-ar** ending, and every piece is one you own.

| piece | what it does |
|---|---|
| a- | towards |
| cerc- | **cerca**, near |
| -ar | makes it a verb |

That is Spanish building a verb out of a preposition, and it does it constantly:
*acercar* from *cerca*, *alejar* from *lejos*, *aclarar* from *claro*. The
pattern is **a-** plus a word plus **-ar**, meaning *to make it that*.

Underneath, *cerca* is Latin **circā**, around — the same root English has in
**circa** on a date and in **circle**. Something near you was something *round
about* you.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03] -->

The reflexive is doing real work. **Acercar** something is an act with an
object; **acercarse** turns it back on the subject.

But notice the third example above. *Se acerca la fecha* — the date does not
move, and nobody moves it. Spanish still uses the reflexive, because what it
marks is not *doing it to yourself* so much as **nobody else did it**: the
change happened, and the sentence declines to name an agent.

That is the same reflexive you met on *equivocarse*, where the mistake befalls
the person. One form, one job: take the doer out.
