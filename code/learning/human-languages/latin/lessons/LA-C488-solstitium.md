## You'll want to know: sōlstitium, sōlstitiī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C488-BRUMA-02]; assesses=[] -->

**sōlstitium, sōlstitiī** — "the summer solstice; midsummer heat".

> Sōlstitiō diēs longissimus est.
>
> *At the summer solstice the day is longest.*

Second declension, neuter: *sōlstitium, sōlstitiī n.* For Romans mostly the summer one; midwinter was *brūma*. English *solstice* comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*sōlstitium* is built from *sōl* 'sun' and *sistō* 'to stand still' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): the time when the sun seems to stand still. English *solstice* comes from it.
