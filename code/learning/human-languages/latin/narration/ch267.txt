Latin, chapter 267: Fat, Clever, Special, Former, Nearest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pinguis, pingue — fat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fair, then the Latin for unjust.

You'll want to know: pinguis, pingue.
pinguis, pingue — "fat".

The word, taken apart.
pinguis has no sure origin; it is often linked with a Proto-Indo-European root peyH-, "fat, to swell" (EtymDB 2.1), or with Greek pakhys, "thick" (Lewis & Short), but neither link is settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
callidus, callida, callidum — clever, cunning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for unjust, then the Latin for fat.

You'll want to know: callidus, callida, callidum.
callidus, callida, callidum — "clever, cunning".

The word, taken apart.
callidus is built on calleō, "to be thick-skinned, to be practised" (Lewis & Short), from callum, "hard skin": hardened by experience, so shrewd. English callous comes from the same family.

prīstinus, prīstina, prīstinum — "former, of old".

The word, taken apart.
prīstinus belongs with prīscus, "ancient" (Lewis & Short), and with prior, "earlier", all built on what is usually reconstructed as pri-, "before".

Lesson 5 of 5.
proximus, proxima, proximum — nearest, next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for special, then the Latin for former.

You'll want to know: proximus, proxima, proximum.
proximus, proxima, proximum — "nearest, next".

The word, taken apart.
proximus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European prokwismmos, "nearest" (EtymDB 2.1), the superlative belonging to prope, "near"; Lewis & Short list it under propior, "nearer".
