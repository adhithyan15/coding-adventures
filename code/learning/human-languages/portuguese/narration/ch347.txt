Portuguese, chapter 347: Um tema polémico.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a polémica — the controversy, the public row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the conclusion, then the Portuguese for the criterion.

You'll want to know: a polémica.
a polémica — "the controversy, the public row".
O novo horário criou uma grande polémica na escola. The new timetable caused a big controversy at the school.
European polémica; Brazil polêmica.

The word, taken apart.
polémica is usually traced to French polémique "polemic", which EtymDB derives from Greek polemikós "of war", built on pólemos "war" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
contestar — to dispute, to challenge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the controversy, then the Portuguese for controversial.

You'll want to know: contestar.
contestar — "to dispute, to challenge".
Os vizinhos contestaram a decisão da câmara. The neighbours challenged the council's decision.
Contestar uma multa — to dispute a fine.

The word, taken apart.
contestar is a learned borrowing from Latin contestārī "to call to witness" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from con- and testārī "to bear witness", on testis "witness".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rebater — to rebut, to counter; to hit back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for controversial, then the Portuguese for to dispute.

You'll want to know: rebater.
rebater — "to rebut, to counter; to hit back".
Ela rebateu as críticas com calma. She rebutted the criticism calmly.
Literally to beat back, as with a ball.

The word, taken apart.
rebater is built from re- "back" and bater "to beat", which is inherited from Latin battuere "to beat, to strike" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
relevante — relevant, important.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to dispute, then the Portuguese for to rebut.

You'll want to know: relevante.
relevante — "relevant, important".
Essa informação não é relevante agora. That information is not relevant now.
The opposite is irrelevante.

The word, taken apart.
relevante is a learned borrowing from Latin relevāns (EtymDB), the present participle of relevāre "to lift up, to raise" (Lewis & Short), built on levāre "to raise": what is relevant stands out.
