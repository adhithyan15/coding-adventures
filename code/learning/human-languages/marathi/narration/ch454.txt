Marathi, chapter 454: Sulking and Making Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
समेट (sameṭ) — a reconciliation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to snap at, then the Marathi for to grumble.

You'll want to know: समेट.
समेट — sameṭ — "a reconciliation".
दोन मित्रांमध्ये शेवटी समेट झाला. don mitrāmmadhye śevṭī sameṭ jhālā. In the end the two friends made up.
Masculine.
समेट (sameṭ) होणे, to be reconciled; समेट (sameṭ) घडवणे, to bring two sides together.

The word, taken apart.
समेट (sameṭ) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Marathi verb sameṭṇẽ, "to roll up", under Sanskrit saṁveṣṭayati, "wraps up" (CDIAL 13026); the noun, a quarrel wound up and settled, is usually connected with that verb.

Lesson 2 of 5.
गैरसमज (gairsamaj) — a misunderstanding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to grumble, then the Marathi for a reconciliation.

You'll want to know: गैरसमज.
गैरसमज — gairsamaj — "a misunderstanding".
एका लहान गैरसमजामुळे दोन मित्र भांडले. ekā lahān gairsamjāmuḷe don mitra bhāṇḍle. Two friends quarrelled over a small misunderstanding.
Masculine.
गैरसमज (gairsamaj) होणे, to misunderstand: माझा गैरसमज (gairsamaj) झाला, I got it wrong.

The word, taken apart.
गैरसमज (gairsamaj) is a Marathi compound built from gair-, "not, other", a loan from Persian ghair usually traced to Arabic, and समज, "understanding", the noun of समजणे.

Lesson 3 of 5.
रुसणे (rusṇe) — to sulk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a reconciliation, then the Marathi for a misunderstanding.

You'll want to know: रुसणे.
रुसणे — rusṇe — "to sulk".
खेळणं मिळालं नाही म्हणून बाळ रुसलं. kheḷṇaṁ miḷālaṁ nāhī mhaṇūn bāḷ ruslaṁ. The baby sulked because it did not get the toy.
Past (he …): तो रुसला, to ruslā.
The noun रुसवा, a sulk; रुसवा काढणे, to coax someone out of a sulk.

The word, taken apart.
रुसणे (rusṇe) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit ruṣyati, "is angry"; CDIAL lists Marathi rusṇẽ, "to be offended" (CDIAL 10794).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मनवणे (manavṇe) — to coax round; to win over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a misunderstanding, then the Marathi for to sulk.

You'll want to know: मनवणे.
मनवणे — manavṇe — "to coax round; to win over".
रुसलेल्या बहिणीला त्याने गोड बोलून मनवलं. ruslelyā bahiṇīlā tyāne goḍ bolūn manavlaṁ. He coaxed his sulking sister round with sweet words.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने तिला मनवलं, tyāne tilā manavlaṁ.
The person coaxed takes -ला: त्याने बहिणीला मनवलं.

The word, taken apart.
मनवणे (manavṇe) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi mānaviṇẽ, "to persuade", under Sanskrit manyate, "thinks, honours" (CDIAL 9857); मनवणे is the everyday form.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to sulk, then the Marathi for to coax round.

You'll want to know: हिरमुसणे.
हिरमुसणे — hirmusṇe — "to be crestfallen".
परीक्षेत कमी गुण मिळाले म्हणून ती हिरमुसली. parīkṣet kamī guṇ miḷāle mhaṇūn tī hirmuslī. She was crestfallen because she got low marks in the exam.
Past (he …): तो हिरमुसला, to hirmuslā.
Of a disappointed child or player: हिरमुसलेला चेहरा, a crestfallen face.

The word, taken apart.
हिरमुसणे (hirmusṇe) is a Marathi verb whose origin is not settled.
