French, chapter 419: Décollage et atterrissage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
décoller — to take off; to unstick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the porthole, then the French for the aisle.

You'll want to know: décoller.
décoller — "to take off; to unstick".
L'avion a décollé avec une heure de retard. The plane took off an hour late.
Also décoller une étiquette — to peel off a label.

The word, taken apart.
décoller is built from dé- 'un-' and coller 'to stick', from colle 'glue', which goes back to Greek kólla (EtymDB): the plane comes unstuck from the ground.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'atterrissage — the landing (of a plane).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: l'atterrissage.
l'atterrissage — "the landing (of a plane)".
L'atterrissage a été un peu brusque. The landing was a bit rough.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
atterrissage is built on atterrir 'to land' with -age; atterrir comes from terre 'land', from Latin terra (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the noun.

Lesson 4 of 5.
atterrir — to land.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for the take-off, then the French for the landing.

You'll want to know: atterrir.
atterrir — "to land".
Nous avons atterri sous la pluie. We landed in the rain.
Conjugated like finir: nous atterrissons.

The word, taken apart.
atterrir is built from à and terre 'land', which is inherited from Latin terra (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the verb without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
survoler — to fly over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the landing, then the French for to land.

You'll want to know: survoler.
survoler — "to fly over".
Pendant le vol, nous avons survolé les montagnes. During the flight we flew over the mountains.
Figurative: survoler un texte — to skim a text.

The word, taken apart.
survoler is built from sur 'over' and voler 'to fly', which is inherited from Latin volāre (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the compound.
