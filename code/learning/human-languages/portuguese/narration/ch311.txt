Portuguese, chapter 311: As idades da vida.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a infância — childhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the godmother, then the Portuguese for to breastfeed.

You'll want to know: a infância.
a infância — "childhood".
Passei a infância numa aldeia. I spent my childhood in a village.
Amigo de infância — childhood friend.

The word, taken apart.
infância is a learned borrowing from Latin īnfantia "inability to speak, childhood", from īnfāns "not speaking", built on fārī "to speak" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a velhice — old age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for adolescence, then the Portuguese for youth.

You'll want to know: a velhice.
a velhice — "old age".
A minha avó passou a velhice com a família. My grandmother spent her old age with the family.
Built on velho. A gentler word is a terceira idade.

The word, taken apart.
velhice is built on velho "old", which is inherited from Latin vetulus "a little old", from vetus "old" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a geração — the generation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for youth, then the Portuguese for old age.

You'll want to know: a geração.
a geração — "the generation".
Na minha geração, poucas pessoas tinham telemóvel. In my generation few people had a mobile.
De geração em geração — from one generation to the next.

The word, taken apart.
geração comes down from Old Portuguese geeraçon (EtymDB), from Latin generātiō "a begetting", built on genus "birth, kind".
