French, chapter 435: Les panneaux.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le panneau — the road sign; the panel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the landmark, then the French for to mislay.

You'll want to know: le panneau.
le panneau — "the road sign; the panel".
Le panneau indique le centre-ville à deux kilomètres. The sign shows the town centre two kilometres away.
Plural les panneaux. Tomber dans le panneau — to fall for it.

The word, taken apart.
panneau comes from Old French panel (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin pannellus, a small pannus 'piece of cloth'.

Au croisement, nous tournons à droite. At the crossroads we turn right.
Close to le carrefour.

The word, taken apart.
croisement is built on croiser 'to cross' with -ment; croiser comes from croix, inherited from Latin crux (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the noun.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la borne — the marker post, the boundary stone; the charging point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the arrow, then the French for the crossroads.

You'll want to know: la borne.
la borne — "the marker post, the boundary stone; the charging point".
Une vieille borne au bord de la route indique le village. An old marker stone by the road points to the village.
Une borne kilométrique — a kilometre stone; une borne de recharge — a charging point.

The word, taken apart.
borne comes from an Old French word found as bodne and bontie (EtymDB gives bontie), which is usually traced to a Gaulish word for a boundary mark; the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'angle — the corner; the angle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the crossroads, then the French for the marker post.

You'll want to know: l'angle.
l'angle — "the corner; the angle".
La pharmacie est à l'angle de la rue. The chemist's is on the corner of the street.
A masculine noun. À l'angle de — on the corner of.

The word, taken apart.
angle came through Old French from Latin angulus 'corner, angle' (EtymDB).
