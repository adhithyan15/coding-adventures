Portuguese, chapter 263: Desculpe, Como Se Vai Para --- Asking the Way.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
desculpe, como se vai para… — the question that finds the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to go". (Ir.) Say "the station". (A estação.)
[pause 1 second]
You are in a town you do not know, and the station is somewhere.

You'll want to know: desculpe.
desculpe — "excuse me", said to someone you do not know.
It is the polite form of desculpar, "to excuse", the one that goes with o senhor, a senhora and você, which a stranger in the street is owed. The same word apologises: bump into someone and you say desculpe! In Brazil the usual way to stop a stranger is com licença, "with permission"; in Portugal you hear that mostly when someone needs to get past.

Grammar Lens: como se vai para…?
Desculpe, como se vai para a estação? — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?
Word for word, "how does one go to the station". The se is the one in como se chama: it leaves the person out, so the question is about the way, not about you. para plus the place, with its article:
para a estação, para o museu, para o hotel.
A second way to ask uses fica, the verb that tells where a place is: onde fica a estação, where the station is. Como se vai para asks for the way; onde fica asks for the place. Either can be followed by a short question that saves a walk, é longe, is it far.

The phrase, taken apart.
desculpar is built on Latin culpa, "fault, blame" (Lewis & Short), which Portuguese also inherited as a word of its own (EtymDB 2.1), with des-, "away", in front: to excuse someone is to take the fault away from them. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for desculpar itself, so that is the usual reading of its parts. English culpable shares the culpa.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Desculpe, como se vai para a estação? — and let the voice rise]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, to the museum, then to the hotel]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask for the place instead, and how far — Desculpe, onde fica a estação? É longe?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Who gets desculpe? (A stranger, anyone you call o senhor, a senhora or você.) Ask the way to the hotel. (Desculpe, como se vai para o hotel?) Which verb asks for the place rather than the way. (Fica — onde fica.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
à direita, à esquerda — following the answer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Stop a stranger and ask the way to the station. (Desculpe, como se vai para a estação?)
[pause 1 second]
Asking was the easy half. Now the answer comes back, fast.

You'll want to know: à direita, à esquerda.
à direita — "on the right, to the right". à esquerda — "on the left, to the left". With em frente, "straight ahead", which you already have, that is every direction a street can send you.
A estação é à direita. — The station is on the right. A primeira à esquerda. — The first on the left.
That second one is a whole answer in four words: a primeira agrees with a rua, the street it leaves unsaid. The accent on à marks two words in one, a, "to", and a, "the", run together.

Grammar Lens: siga em frente, vire à direita.
A stranger answering a desculpe answers politely, and the directions are instructions:
Siga em frente e depois vire à direita no semáforo. — Go straight on, and then turn right at the traffic lights.
vire is virar, "to turn", made a polite instruction. siga is the same for seguir, "to go on", the verb inside your a seguir. You need to understand these two, not build them yet.
A long answer comes in the order you will walk it, depois marking each piece.

The phrase, taken apart.
direita has no parent in EtymDB 2.1. It is usually traced to Latin dīrēctus, "straight, set in a straight line", the participle of dīrigere (Lewis & Short, under dirigo): the straight hand, the proper one.
esquerda is the surprise. It comes from Basque ezker, "left" (EtymDB 2.1), a word older than Latin's arrival in Iberia, as is Spanish izquierda. Latin's own word for the left hand, sinistra, lives on in English sinister.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: à direita, à esquerda, em frente]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: A primeira à esquerda. — then A segunda à direita.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the stranger's answer — Siga em frente e depois vire à direita no semáforo.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say the answer back to check it — Em frente, depois à direita no semáforo?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word is "left", and where does it come from? (Esquerda — from Basque.) What is vire, and from which verb? (A polite "turn", from virar.) What does a primeira à direita leave unsaid? (The street — a primeira agrees with it.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — ask, listen, check, thank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "on the left", then "on the right". (À esquerda, à direita.)

What you've built.
Asking the way has four steps, and you now have Portuguese for every one.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask: Desculpe, como se vai para a estação? Listen: Siga em frente e depois vire à esquerda no cruzamento. Check, by saying the answer back as a question: Em frente, depois à esquerda? Thank: Obrigado!, or Obrigada!
The check is the step travellers skip, and it is the one that saves the walk. A stranger who hears their own directions said back will correct the one piece you got wrong. And every depois in the answer is Latin post, "after": one step, then the next.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ask — Desculpe, como se vai para o museu?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hear the answer and say it back — Siga em frente e vire à direita. … Em frente, depois à direita?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: thank — Obrigado! or Obrigada!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: now be the stranger: send someone to the station — straight ahead, then the first on the left]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the four steps? (Ask, listen, check, thank.) Which one do travellers skip? (The check.) If a friend is lost, advise them to ask, and explain why. (Acho que devias perguntar, porque a estação é longe.) And what does explicar do, word for word? (It unfolds.)
