Portuguese, chapter 425: Onde dormir.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o albergue — the hostel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the flight attendant, then the Portuguese for the crew.

You'll want to know: o albergue.
o albergue — "the hostel".
Ficámos num albergue para jovens. We stayed in a hostel for young people.
Pilgrims on the long walks to the north sleep in albergues.

The word, taken apart.
albergue came through Occitan from Gothic haribairgō 'shelter for an army' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
a estalagem — the roadside inn, the inn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hostel, then the Portuguese for the country inn.

You'll want to know: a estalagem.
a estalagem — "the roadside inn, the inn".
A estalagem fica à beira da estrada. The inn is by the side of the road.
An old word; today often the name of a small hotel.

The word, taken apart.
estalagem came from Occitan ostalatge 'lodging', built on ostal 'house', from Late Latin hospitāle 'guest house' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a residencial — the guesthouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the country inn, then the Portuguese for the roadside inn.

You'll want to know: a residencial.
a residencial — "the guesthouse".
A residencial não é cara e fica perto da estação. The guesthouse is not expensive and is close to the station.
Also an adjective: um bairro residencial.

The word, taken apart.
residencial is built on residência 'residence', a learned word that goes back to Latin residēre 'to stay behind, to settle', on sedēre 'to sit' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the roadside inn, then the Portuguese for the guesthouse.

You'll want to know: o campismo.
o campismo — "camping".
O parque de campismo tem duche e piscina. The campsite has showers and a pool.
O parque de campismo — the campsite. In Brazil: o camping.

The word, taken apart.
campismo is built on campo 'field' with -ismo; campo is inherited from Latin campus 'field, open plain' (EtymDB).
