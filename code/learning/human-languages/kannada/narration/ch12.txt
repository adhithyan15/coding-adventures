Kannada, chapter 12: Family.
7 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ಅಪ್ಪ, ಅಮ್ಮ, and four sibling words — the same system as Tamil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Kannada asks the same question Tamil does about any sibling: older, or younger? — and the words for answering it are almost identical.

The word, taken apart: Parents — native, and matching Tamil.
ಅಪ್ಪ (appa) = "father" — matches Tamil appā closely.
ಅಮ್ಮ (amma) = "mother" — matches Tamil ammā closely.
Both are native Dravidian words (DEDR 156, 183).

The word, taken apart: Four sibling words, age before gender.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
Kannada: ಅಣ್ಣ (aṇṇa). meaning: older brother. compare Tamil: aṇṇaṉ — near match.
Kannada: ತಮ್ಮ (tamma). meaning: younger brother. compare Tamil: tambi — near match.
Kannada: ಅಕ್ಕ (akka). meaning: older sister. compare Tamil: akkā — near match.
Kannada: ತಂಗಿ (tangi). meaning: younger sister. compare Tamil: tangai — near match.
All four are native Dravidian, each with its own entry (DEDR 131, 3085, 23, 3015).
Just like Tamil, Kannada has no single word that simply means "brother" or "sister" — you name the sibling's relative age every time, not just their gender. This age-first system runs across the whole Dravidian family, not just one language.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "appa, amma" — father, mother]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "aṇṇa, tamma" — older/younger brother]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "akka, tangi" — older/younger sister]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say dayaviṭṭu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many Kannada sibling words are there, and what do they split on? (Four — ಅಣ್ಣ/ತಮ್ಮ, ಅಕ್ಕ/ತಂಗಿ — split by relative age, not just gender.) How closely do Kannada's words match Tamil's? (Very closely — nearly the same forms across the whole set.)


Lesson 2 of 7.
ಅವನು, ಅವಳು, ಅದು, ಅವರು — people and things outside the conversation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ನಾನು for “I”, then name father, mother, older brother and older sister. Now point away from “I” and “you”: this lesson gives those people their pronouns.

Grammar Lens: four third-person forms.
ಅವನು (avanu) — he.
ಅವಳು (avaḷu) — she.
ಅದು (adu) — it, that non-human thing.
ಅವರು (avaru) — they; also respectful “he” or “she”.
Use ಅವರು for one respected adult as well as for several people. The sentence around it tells you whether it means respectful singular or plural.

Grammar Lens: the first split is human or non-human.
Kannada does not make every noun carry masculine or feminine marking. In the third person, the useful first question is human or non-human: ಅವನು / ಅವಳು / ಅವರು point to people; ಅದು points to a non-human thing. That split will matter again when verbs and plurals change shape.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: father, then “he” — ಅಪ್ಪ, ಅವನು]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mother, then “she” — ಅಮ್ಮ, ಅವಳು]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ಅವರು once for “they”, then once for one respected adult]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — identify: which one is non-human? — ಅದು]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say he, she, it and they/respectful he or she. Which three forms point to people, and which one points to a non-human thing?


Lesson 3 of 7.
ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾನೆ, ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾಳೆ, ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾರೆ — let the ending name the person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “I work”: nānu kelasa māḍuttēne. Then say he, she and they/respectful he or she. Keep māḍutt- still and change only the ending.

Grammar Lens: the ending follows the human pronoun.
avanu kelasa māḍuttāne. — He works.
avaḷu kelasa māḍuttāḷe. — She works.
avaru kelasa māḍuttāre. — They work; or one respected person works.
The stem and -utt- stay still. The endings -tāne, -tāḷe, -tāre tell you which human pronoun fits. Keep the non-human present ending for a later lesson; for now, recognise adu as the non-human member of the pronoun set.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “He works” — avanu kelasa māḍuttāne]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “She works” — avaḷu kelasa māḍuttāḷe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “They work” — avaru kelasa māḍuttāre]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — identify: which ending can also belong to one respected person? — -tāre]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Hold māḍutt- still and give the three human endings: he, she, and they/respectful singular.


Lesson 4 of 7.
ಸ (sa) — one character, met inside words you already say.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ಸ and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: ಹ — what does it do?
[pause 2 seconds]
One character this time. Just one — and you have been saying it for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ಸ.
ಸ — sa.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not s, it is sa.
You already say these, and every one of them has ಸ (sa) somewhere inside it:
ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ namaskāra — hello / greetings (namaskāra — "a making of a bow").
ಸರಿ sari — okay / alright / correct (sari).
ಹೆಸರು hesaru — name.
ಸಂತೋಷ santōṣa — joy / pleased to meet you.

Writing: ಸ.
1. start at the tail and climb round the left curve.
2. without lifting, slant down to the right into the base.
3. without lifting, climb the right side and curve in at the top.
4. lift, then draw the top bar from left to right.
5. without lifting, curl up into the hook.
6. lift, then set the dot in the middle.
Pen lifts: 2. The body is one run, from the tail at the bottom left to the curl at the top right; then comes the curled bar, and last the dot in the middle, each after a lift.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna A, 'Kannada-alphabet-sa.gif', 40 frames, Wikimedia Commons, 25 May 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at these words, and find ಸ (sa) in the ones that have it]
ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ಸರಿ, ಧನ್ಯವಾದ.
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ಸ three times, saying sa as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — look: back at any page of this chapter and find ಸ (sa) once more]

Lesson 5 of 7.
ಅವನ, ಅವಳ, ಅವರ — whose family member?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say he, she and they/respectful he or she. Then say father, mother, older brother and older sister. Now join each person to a family word.

Grammar Lens: remove the final pronoun ending.
ಅವನ ಅಪ್ಪ (avana appa) — his father.
ಅವಳ ಅಮ್ಮ (avaḷa amma) — her mother.
ಅವರ ಅಕ್ಕ (avara akka) — their older sister, or a respected person's older sister.
For this small set, hear the pair: ಅವನು becomes ಅವನ, ಅವಳು becomes ಅವಳ, ಅವರು becomes ಅವರ. This lesson teaches those three possessive forms as a set; it does not yet turn the ending into a rule for every noun.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “his father” — ಅವನ ಅಪ್ಪ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “her mother” — ಅವಳ ಅಮ್ಮ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “their older sister” — ಅವರ ಅಕ್ಕ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — identify: which possessive can also belong to one respected person? — ಅವರ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Change he, she and they/respectful singular into his, her and their/respectful singular possessive.

[pause 2 seconds]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ೪.
೪ — 4, the digit for ನಾಲ್ಕು (nālku), "four".
You have now met four of the ten. The words came first, when you learned to count; the shapes are arriving one at a time, on top of words you can already say.

Writing: ೪ — follow the numbered strip.
Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ೪ (4) yourself — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
The strip shows where to start the character and which way to travel, in one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school, so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the only one.

Lesson 7 of 7.
ಹೆಸರುಗಳು, ಕೆಲಸಗಳು — more than one non-human thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ಹೆಸರು, “name”, then ಕೆಲಸ, “work”. These are non-human nouns, so their ordinary plural can use one ending.

Grammar Lens: add -ಗಳು.
ಹೆಸರು (hesaru, name) becomes ಹೆಸರುಗಳು (hesarugaḷu, names).
ಕೆಲಸ (kelasa, work) becomes ಕೆಲಸಗಳು (kelasagaḷu, kinds of work or tasks).
The ending is -ಗಳು (-gaḷu). Use it here for ordinary non-human nouns. Do not force it onto the family words: human plurals have their own patterns, and avaru already gives you “they” for people.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “names” — ಹೆಸರುಗಳು]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “kinds of work” — ಕೆಲಸಗಳು]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Her mother works” — avaḷa amma kelasa māḍuttāḷe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — identify: which pronoun means people in the plural? — avaru]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Make “name” and “work” plural. Then say “Her mother works.” Which pronoun gives you “they” for people?
