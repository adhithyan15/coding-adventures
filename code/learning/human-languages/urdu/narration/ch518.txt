Urdu, chapter 518: Learning to Sing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
آواز (āvāz) — a voice; a sound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a dialogue, then the Urdu for to charm.

You'll want to know: آواز.
آواز — āvāz — "a voice; a sound".
اس گلوکار کی آواز میٹھی ہے۔ is gulūkār kī āvāz mīṭhī hai. This singer has a sweet voice.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
آواز (āvāz) is usually traced to Persian āvāz, "a voice, a sound, a song".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ریاض (riyāz) — practice (in music).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for melodious, then the Urdu for a raga.

You'll want to know: ریاض.
ریاض — riyāz — "practice (in music)".
سارا ہر صبح دو گھنٹے ریاض کرتی ہے۔ Sārā har subah do ghanṭe riyāz kartī hai. Sara practises for two hours every morning.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ریاض (riyāz) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic riyāḍa, "exercise, training"; Urdu uses it for a musician's daily practice.

Lesson 5 of 5.
گنگنانا (gungunānā) — to hum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a raga, then the Urdu for practice (in music).

You'll want to know: گنگنانا.
گنگنانا — gungunānā — "to hum".
سارا باورچی خانے میں ایک گیت گنگنا رہی تھی۔ Sārā bāvarchī khāne meṅ ek gīt gungunā rahī thī. Sara was humming a song in the kitchen.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے ایک گیت گنگنایا, us ne ek gīt gungunāyā.

The word, taken apart.
گنگنانا (gungunānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin gungunānā, "to murmur", under ganagana, a form imitating the sound (CDIAL 4013).
