Latin, chapter 204: To state firmly, To hurt, To decorate, To throw together, To cease.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
affirmō, affirmāre — to state firmly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to annoy, then the Latin for to forget.

You'll want to know: affirmō, affirmāre.
affirmō, affirmāre — "to state firmly".

The word, taken apart.
affirmō is ad, "to", with firmō, "to make firm" (EtymDB 2.1), from firmus, "firm": to make a statement firm.

Lesson 4 of 5.
coniciō, conicere — to throw together; to guess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to hurt, then the Latin for to decorate.

You'll want to know: coniciō, conicere.
coniciō, conicere — "to throw together; to guess".

The word, taken apart.
coniciō is con-, "together", with iaciō, "to throw" (EtymDB 2.1): to throw together, then to put facts together, to guess. English conjecture comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēsinō, dēsinere — to cease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to decorate, then the Latin for to throw together; to guess.

You'll want to know: dēsinō, dēsinere.
dēsinō, dēsinere — "to cease".

The word, taken apart.
dēsinō is dē, "off, away", with sinō, "to let, to leave": to leave off. Sinō is inherited, but its deeper origin is uncertain: EtymDB 2.1 records two different roots.
