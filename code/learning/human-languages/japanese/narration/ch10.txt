Japanese, chapter 10: A Little More Slowly.
12 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 12.
せ — close the opener.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Say sumimasen, then write known す, み, ま, ん separately. Only the fourth sign is still missing.

Script — the shape.
せ — se.
Three strokes: a short horizontal, a longer crossing horizontal with a small hook, then a vertical curve through the right side. Keep the two horizontals separate.

Guided Practice.
Copy せ (se) once.
Hide it and write it after ten seconds.
Join the five known blocks: す | み | ま | せ (se) | ん.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read すみません once without romanization.
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart; stroke order per Hitsujun Shido no Tebiki (Japanese Ministry of Education, 1958).
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write the hiragana sign for se.

Lesson 3 of 12.
ね (ne) — finish the polite request.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Write お, then known が, い, し (shi), ま, す. Say the whole request ending once. One written mora remains between お and が.

Script — the shape.
ね — ne.
Two strokes. First a short vertical. Second, cross it from the upper right, hook left, sweep down, and finish with a small loop at the lower right.

Guided Practice.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 4 of 12.
すこし (sukoshi) — ask for a small change.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Ask for one repetition, then write known す and こ.

You'll want to know.
すこし (sukoshi) — su-ko-shi — a little.
Three morae. This word makes a request smaller and gentler: not a completely different explanation, only a little adjustment. The last sign is exposure for one lesson; its own writing step comes next.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: sukoshi becomes choose a little]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: su | ko | shi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[once you have stopped driving — point: known す, known こ, supported final sign]

Wrap-up Recall.
Keep it to three even beats.

Lesson 5 of 12.
し (shi) — one falling curve.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say sukoshi. Write す, こ, and review み.

Script — the shape.
し — shi.
One stroke. Begin high, descend almost vertically, then curve gently up and to the right. Do not add a second stroke or close the curve.

Guided Practice.

Hide it and write the single curve.
Join す | こ | し (shi) and read the three morae.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read すこし (sukoshi) once without support.

Lesson 6 of 12.
ゆっくり (yukkuri) — slowly, with a held beat.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Read すこし (sukoshi), then write known く, り, and し.

You'll want to know.
ゆっくり (yukkuri) — yu-k-ku-ri — slowly.
Do not squeeze the middle. The small っ holds one beat of closure before ku. The spelling is exposure today; this lesson earns the sound and practical job without pretending the two new shapes are already readable.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: yukkuri becomes choose slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — tap: yu | hold | ku | ri]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yukkuri slowly, without adding a vowel in the held beat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Meaning and timing are enough. The new opening sign comes next.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 1–3.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Say slowly and mark the held beat in yukkuri.

Lesson 8 of 12.
いってください (itte kudasai) — please say it.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Say yukkuri. Retrieve onegaishimasu, then write い, く, さ.

You'll want to know.
いってください。 — itte kudasai — please say it.
The first part is the request-linking form of iu, “to say.” Kudasai makes the requested action polite. Learn the whole spoken frame now. Its small っ, て, and voiced だ receive separate writing steps later.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: itte kudasai becomes choose please say it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it-te ku-da-sa-i]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — join: yukkuri itte kudasai — please say it slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Do not write the supported signs from memory yet.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 1–3.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Ask politely: please say it.


Lesson 9 of 12.
もうすこし (mō sukoshi) — a little more.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Write もう and すこし (sukoshi) separately. Read both.

You'll want to know.
もうすこし — mō sukoshi — a little more.
No new grammar is hiding here. もう points beyond the present amount; すこし (sukoshi) keeps the requested change small. Every sign is already earned.

Guided Practice.
Read もう | すこし (sukoshi).
Say mō sukoshi, keeping the extra beat in もう.
Hear the phrase, wait ten seconds, and write it from memory.

Wrap-up Recall.
Circle う, the second beat of mō.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 1–3.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]

Lesson 10 of 12.
わかりました (wakarimashita) — the repair worked.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Say “I do not understand,” then read もうすこし (mō sukoshi). Write み and し.

You'll want to know.
わかりました。 — wakarimashita — I understand; understood.
The familiar wakari- stem returns. Polite -mashita presents the change as achieved: the explanation landed. The final た is supported exposure until its own sign lesson.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: wakarimasen becomes not understood]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — hear: wakarimashita becomes understood now]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wakarimashita after a successful repetition]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Keep -masen and -mashita distinct; one reports a problem, the other success.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 2–3.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]

Lesson 11 of 12.
ここ (koko) — here, with one known sign twice.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Write こ, say wakarimashita, then retrieve sumimasen.

You'll want to know.
ここ (koko) — ko-ko — here; this place.
One owned sign appears twice. The word belongs to a larger pointing family that will later contrast near me, near you, and over there. Today, keep only the speaker-near place: here.

Guided Practice.
Point beside yourself and say koko.
Read こ | こ.
Hear ko-ko, wait ten seconds, and write both signs.

Wrap-up Recall.
No new shape was needed; the payoff is recombination.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, p. 2.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write here in hiragana using the same known sign twice.


Lesson 12 of 12.
もうすこし、ゆっくりいってください (mō sukoshi, yukkuri itte kudasai) — slower, please.

Warm-up.
[pause 30 seconds]
Write せ, お, ね, し, ゆ. Read sumimasen and onegaishimasu. Retrieve the repetition request, then the three new spoken pieces mō sukoshi, yukkuri, and itte kudasai.

You'll want to know.
もうすこし、ゆっくりいってください。 mō sukoshi, yukkuri itte kudasai — please say it a little more slowly.
No new rule arrives here. The first block is independently readable and writable. The second and third remain sound-first exposure where their small っ, て, and だ have not yet received individual writing lessons.

Guided Practice.
Listen: choose repetition or slower speech from the request you hear.
Speak: sumimasen + the slower request.
Read/write: independently retrieve only もうすこし (mō sukoshi).
Close: after the repeated line, say wakarimashita.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate repair and one wakarimashita.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 1–3.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Ask politely for the line a little more slowly.
