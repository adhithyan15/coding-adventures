Lesson 1 of 5.
काष्ठम् (kāṣṭham) — firewood; a piece of wood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pile, then the Sanskrit for a thorough cleaning.

You'll want to know: काष्ठम्.
काष्ठम् — kāṣṭham — "firewood; a piece of wood".
माता चुल्ल्यां काष्ठं स्थापयति। mātā cullyāṃ kāṣṭhaṃ sthāpayati. Mother puts firewood in the stove.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
काष्ठम् (kāṣṭham) is the word MW records for a piece of wood, timber, a stick; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
तापयति (tāpayati) — makes hot; heats up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a thorough cleaning, then the Sanskrit for firewood.

You'll want to know: तापयति.
तापयति — tāpayati — "makes hot; heats up".
सा स्थाल्यां तैलं तापयति। sā sthālyāṃ tailaṃ tāpayati. She heats oil in the pan.
Past (she …): सा जलं तापितवती, sā jalaṃ tāpitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
तापयति (tāpayati) comes from the causative of the root √tap, "to be hot, to give out heat", which also gives tapati: to make a thing hot (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
उत्तारयति (uttārayati) — takes down; lifts off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for firewood, then the Sanskrit for makes hot.

You'll want to know: उत्तारयति.
उत्तारयति — uttārayati — "takes down; lifts off".
माता चुल्ल्याः पात्रम् उत्तारयति। mātā cullyāḥ pātram uttārayati. Mother takes the pot off the stove.
Past (she …): सा पात्रम् उत्तारितवती, sā pātram uttāritavatī.

The word, taken apart.
उत्तारयति (uttārayati) is built on ud, "up, out", and the causative of the root √tṝ, "to cross over", which also gives tarati; MW gives the causative uttārayati as to make come out, to take down, to take off (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
भृज्जति (bhṛjjati) — roasts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for takes down, then the Sanskrit for cooking.

You'll want to know: भृज्जति.
भृज्जति — bhṛjjati — "roasts".
सः चणकान् भृज्जति। saḥ caṇakān bhṛjjati. He roasts chickpeas.
Past (he …): सः चणकान् भृष्टवान्, saḥ caṇakān bhṛṣṭavān.

The word, taken apart.
भृज्जति (bhṛjjati) comes from the root √bhrajj, "to roast, to parch, to fry", whose present is bhṛjjati; its past participle is bhṛṣṭa, and MW compares Greek phrygō and Latin frīgere (MW).
