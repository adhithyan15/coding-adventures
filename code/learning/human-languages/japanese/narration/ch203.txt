Japanese, chapter 203: Planes, Airports and Seats.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ひこうき (hikōki) — a plane; an aeroplane.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to halt, then the Japanese for a car park.

You'll want to know: ひこうき.
ひこうき — hikōki — "a plane; an aeroplane".
ひこうきで しまに いきます。 — hikōki de shima ni ikimasu — "I go to the island by plane."

The word, taken apart.
ひこうき (hikōki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hi, "to fly" (Middle Chinese pj+j), kō, "to go" (haeng) and ki, "machine" (kj+j) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.
とぶ (tobu) — to fly through the air; to fly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a plane, then the Japanese for an airport.

You'll want to know: とぶ.
とぶ — tobu — "to fly through the air; to fly".
ひこうきが うみの うえを とびます。 — hikōki ga umi no ue o tobimasu — "The plane flies over the sea."
Polite とびます, tobimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
とぶ (tobu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
