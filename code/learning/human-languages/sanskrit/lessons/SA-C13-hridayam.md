# हृदयम् (hṛdayam) — "heart," a root barely disguised anywhere

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NASIKA-NOSE, SA-ETYMON-NEHAS-NOSE, SA-LEX-AKSHI-EYE, SA-LEX-MUKHAM-MOUTH] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "nose," "eye," and "mouth." (*Nāsikā*, *akṣi*, *mukham*.) One
last word, and it names what this whole chapter has been checking on.

## You'll want to know: हृदयम्
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-HRIDAYAM-HEART]; assesses=[] -->

| Sanskrit | sound | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| हृदयम् | *hṛdayam* | **heart** |
| मम हृदयम् | *mama hṛdayam* | **my heart** |

Neuter — the family this whole chapter keeps returning to.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**हृ** is *hṛ*: **ह** *ha* carrying the vocalic **ऋ**, the same buzzing
vowel you first sounded out in **गृह्णाति**. **द** is *da*, **य** is *ya*,
**म्** closes it neuter: *hṛ-da-ya-m*.

## The word, taken apart — a root barely disguised anywhere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-KERD-HEART]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NASIKA-NOSE] -->

**हृदयम्** is PIE **kerd-**, and almost nothing about it has changed shape on
the way anywhere: English **heart**, Latin **cor**, genitive **cordis**
(→ *cordial*, *courage*, *accord*), Greek **kardia** (→ *cardiac*) — one
root, four languages, all still recognisable. The same root anchors Italian
**cuore**, Portuguese **coração**, and Russian *serdtse*, each an
independent inheritance of the identical PIE ancestor rather than a loan
from any of the others.

Eastward, Bengali *hridoy* keeps हृदयम् whole, a तत्सम; its worn-down twin
*hiya* survives only in poetry and song, not everyday speech — the rarer
pairing, where the carried-whole word is the common one and the worn-down
word is the rare one.

## What you've built: twelve words, three rooms
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PANIYAM-WATER, SA-LEX-KSHIRAM-MILK, SA-LEX-ANNAM-FOOD, SA-LEX-MITRAM-FRIEND, SA-LEX-BHRATA-BROTHER, SA-LEX-BHAGINI-SISTER, SA-GRAMMAR-NOUN-GENDER-THREE] -->

Three groups, twelve nouns: what you would ask for (*pānīyam*, *kṣīram*,
*annam*), who you would introduce (*mitram*, *kuṭumbam*, *bhrātā*,
*bhaginī*), and your own face (*akṣi*, *karṇaḥ*, *mukham*, *nāsikā*,
*hṛdayam*). Almost every one of them ended in the neuter **-म्** you first
named beside **अन्नम्** — and almost every one pointed at a word some other
language in this project already teaches as its own.
