## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la exposición** — "the exhibition." Latin *expositio*: **ex-** ("out") plus
**ponere** ("to place"). An exhibition is literally **a putting-out**.

*ponere* is one of the most productive verbs Latin ever handed on. Once you see
it, it is everywhere:

- ex**pos**e, ex**pos**ition — placed out.
- com**pos**e — placed together. de**pos**it — placed down.
- **pos**ition, post**pone**, com**pon**ent, op**pon**ent.

Spanish has the verb itself, *poner*, "to put" — so *exposición* is transparent
to a Spanish speaker in a way that *exhibition* is not to an English one.

## Grammar Lens: the ending that tells you the gender
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-02] -->

> Nouns ending in **-ción** are feminine, without exception worth worrying
> about.

*la exposición*, *la habitación*, *la nación*. The ending comes from Latin
*-tio*, which was feminine, and Spanish kept the gender along with the sound.

That makes **-ción** one of the few endings you can trust on sight — most noun
gender has to be learned with the word, and this one does not.
