# गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi) — I will go

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C139-FUT139-05, SA-GRAMMAR-C262-OBJECT-PERSON-NUMBER-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Close the last chapter with one quick choice: several people
including the speaker are the object, so say **अस्मान्** (*asmān*). Now say
**गमिष्यति** (*gamiṣyati*), “will go.” That known form is the bridge to one
new speaker form.

## You'll want to know: गमिष्यामि

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-VERB-C263-GAMISYAMI-01]; assesses=[] -->

**गमिष्यामि** — *gamiṣyāmi* — means “I will go.” Hear the long **-āmi** at
the end. For now, learn the complete form as one usable promise.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-VERB-C263-GAMISYAMI-01] -->

**गमिष्यामि** comes from the root **गम्** (*gam*), “go,” with the ordinary future
formation **-इष्य-** (*-iṣya-*), and ends in first-person **-आमि** (*-āmi*).
Whitney describes this Sanskrit s-future and its **-iṣya- / -sya-** formation
in *Sanskrit Grammar* §§932–935. This lesson makes no claim beyond that
transparent grammatical history.
