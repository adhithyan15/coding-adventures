Lesson 1 of 5.
こな (kona) — powder, flour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for east, then the Japanese for south.

You'll want to know: こな.
こな — kona — "powder, flour".

The word, taken apart.
こな (kona) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ごま (goma) — sesame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for south, then the Japanese for powder.

You'll want to know: ごま.
ごま — goma — "sesame".

The word, taken apart.
ごま (goma) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are go, "foreign" (Middle Chinese hu) and ma, "hemp" (mae) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart). JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.
