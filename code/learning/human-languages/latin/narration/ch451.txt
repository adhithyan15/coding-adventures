Latin, chapter 451: To inquire, To ask around, To ask after, To show, To make known.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
percontor, percontārī — to inquire, to ask questions.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for grasping, then the Latin for dirt.

You'll want to know: percontor, percontārī.
percontor, percontārī — "to inquire, to ask questions".
Viātor ā pastōre percontātur ubi oppidum sit. The traveller asks a shepherd where the town is.
Deponent: percontor, percontārī, percontātus sum, with ā or ex and the person asked. An indirect question follows in the subjunctive: ubi sit.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of percontor is not settled. Lewis & Short build it on per and a root they connect with hesitating; the older spelling is percunctor. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
scīscitor, scīscitārī — to ask around, to try to find out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dirt, then the Latin for to inquire.

You'll want to know: scīscitor, scīscitārī.
scīscitor, scīscitārī — "to ask around, to try to find out".
Mercātor in forō dē viā scīscitātur. The merchant asks around in the forum about the road.
Deponent: scīscitor, scīscitārī, scīscitātus sum. A frequentative: asking again and again.

The word, taken apart.
scīscitor is built on scīscō 'to seek to know' with the frequentative ending -itor (EtymDB), and scīscō comes from sciō 'to know' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
requīrō, requīrere — to ask after, to look for; to need.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to inquire, then the Latin for to ask around.

You'll want to know: requīrō, requīrere.
requīrō, requīrere — "to ask after, to look for; to need".
Hospes caupōnam bonam requīrit. The visitor asks after a good inn.
requīrō, requīrere, requīsīvī, requīsītum. English require and request come from it.

The word, taken apart.
requīrō is built from re- 'again' and quaerō 'to seek, to ask' (Lewis & Short), with the vowel weakened in the compound. English require and request come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mōnstrō, mōnstrāre — to show, to point out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to ask around, then the Latin for to ask after.

You'll want to know: mōnstrō, mōnstrāre.
mōnstrō, mōnstrāre — "to show, to point out".
Agricola viātōribus viam ad flūmen mōnstrat. The farmer shows the travellers the way to the river.
mōnstrō, mōnstrāre, mōnstrāvī, mōnstrātum, with a dative of the person shown. English demonstrate comes from the compound, and muster comes through French.

The word, taken apart.
mōnstrō is built on mōnstrum 'a sign, a portent' (EtymDB), which Lewis & Short derive from moneō 'to warn, to remind'. English demonstrate comes from dēmōnstrāre, and muster comes from mōnstrāre through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
indicō, indicāre — to make known, to indicate, to show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to ask after, then the Latin for to show.

You'll want to know: indicō, indicāre.
indicō, indicāre — "to make known, to indicate, to show".
Mīliārium indicat urbem decem mīlia passuum abesse. The milestone shows that the city is ten miles away.
indicō, indicāre, indicāvī, indicātum, first conjugation. Not the same as indīcō, indīcere 'to proclaim'. English indicate comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
indicō is built from in- and the root of dīcō 'to say, to show' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); index 'a pointer' belongs to the same family. English indicate comes from it.
