Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 289: Tools and Machines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मसीन (masīn) — a machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a trade, then the Marwadi for a labourer.

You'll want to know: मसीन.
मसीन — masīn — "a machine".
मसीन नुवी है। masīn nuvī hai. The machine is new.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
मसीन (masīn) is borrowed from English machine. It is feminine: मसीन नुवी है.

Lesson 2 of 5.
चलावणो (chalāvṇo) — to drive; to run (a machine).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a labourer, then the Marwadi for a machine.

You'll want to know: चलावणो.
चलावणो — chalāvṇo — "to drive; to run (a machine)".
भाई मसीन चलावै है। bhāī masīn chalāvai hai. My brother runs the machine.
Past (I …): म्हैं गाड़ी चलाई, mhaĩ gāṛī chalāī.

The word, taken apart.
चलावणो (chalāvṇo) is built on चालणो, to walk, as its causative: "to make go". CDIAL lists Old Marwari calaï, "goes", under Sanskrit calati (CDIAL 4715).

Lesson 3 of 5.
घिसणो (ghisṇo) — to rub smooth; to rub.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a machine, then the Marwadi for to drive.

You'll want to know: घिसणो.
घिसणो — ghisṇo — "to rub smooth; to rub".
काको लकड़ी घिसै है। kāko lakṛī ghisai hai. My uncle rubs the wood smooth.
Past (I …): म्हैं लकड़ी घिसी, mhaĩ lakṛī ghisī.

The word, taken apart.
घिसणो (ghisṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit gharṣati, "rubs"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi ghisnā, "to rub", and Gujarati ghasvũ, "to rub, polish" (CDIAL 4450).

Lesson 5 of 5.
चीतरणो (chītarṇo) — to paint a picture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to rub smooth, then the Marwadi for a carpenter's plane.

You'll want to know: चीतरणो.
चीतरणो — chītarṇo — "to paint a picture".
छोरी भींत माथै मोर चीतरै है। chhorī bhī̃t māthai mor chītrai hai. The girl paints a peacock on the wall.
Past (I …): म्हैं मोर चीतर्यो, mhaĩ mor chītaryo.

The word, taken apart.
चीतरणो (chītarṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit citrakāra, "a painter"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi citārnā, "to paint" (CDIAL 4807).
