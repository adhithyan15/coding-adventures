Mandarin Chinese, chapter 189: Forms and Dates.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
号 (hào) — day of the month; number (in a series).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to spend less, then the Mandarin for enough.

You'll want to know: 号.
号 — hào — "day of the month; number (in a series)".
今天是十月五号 jīntiān shì shíyuè wǔ hào — Today is the fifth of October.
The new character, in pieces: 号 (hào) — five strokes: 口 on top, then a long bar, and under it a stroke that goes down, turns right, turns down again and hooks to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
号 (hào) is inherited from Middle Chinese hawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to call', 'a command' and 'a title'. Numbering days and things is not given there, so how it came to that sense is not settled.

Script — 口 on top, then a bar and a hook.
号 (hào).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, 口, three strokes, the same three as in 叫 and 吃: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, then the bar that closes the foot.
The bottom piece, two strokes. A long bar across the middle, wider than 口. Then, from under the bar, a short stroke down that turns right, turns down again and hooks to the left at its foot.
3 + 2 = five.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 号 (hào), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 号 (hào)? (Five.) Which piece comes first? (口, on top.) Where does the last stroke hook? (To the left, at its foot.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
biǎogé — a form to fill in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for enough, then the Mandarin for day of the month.

You'll want to know: biǎogé.
biǎogé — "a form to fill in".
qǐng gěi wǒ yì zhāng biǎogé — Could I have a form, please?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biǎogé is built from biǎo and gé, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjewX and kaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to make clear' and the second 'a pattern' and 'a measure'; how the pair came to mean a form to fill in is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
tiánxiě — to fill in (a form).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for day of the month, then the Mandarin for a form to fill in.

You'll want to know: tiánxiě.
tiánxiě — "to fill in (a form)".
qǐng tiánxiě zhè zhāng biǎogé — Please fill in this form.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiánxiě is built from tián 'to fill up' and xiě 'to write', both inherited from Middle Chinese, den and sjaeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to stop up' and 'full', and the second, among other senses, 'to copy out a book'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
shēnfènzhèng — identity card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to fill in, then the Mandarin for to sign your name.

You'll want to know: shēnfènzhèng.
shēnfènzhèng — "identity card".
nǐ dài shēnfènzhèng le ma — Have you brought your ID card?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shēnfènzhèng is built from shēn 'oneself', fèn 'a share, a standing' and zhèng 'proof'. The first and the last are inherited from Middle Chinese, syin and tsyingH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'oneself' and 'to verify'; the dictionary gives the middle character another sound and sense, so its history is not settled.
