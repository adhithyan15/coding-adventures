# Unit 16: Definite & Indefinite Articles

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You've been seeing *(masc.)*/*(fem.)* tags on every noun since
Chapter 1's *el lunes*. This chapter makes that pattern fully explicit,
starting with the words that actually show it: articles.

## New Material

**el / la** — "the" (masc. singular / fem. singular)
**los / las** — "the" (masc. plural / fem. plural)
> ← Latin demonstrative *ille/illa* — the same root that gave you *él/ella*
> in Chapter 1. Articles and third-person pronouns share one Latin
> ancestor across the Romance languages.

**un / una** — "a/an" (masc. / fem.)
> ← Latin *unus/una* — the same root as the number *uno*. Spanish uses one
> word for both "a" and "one" (*un libro* = "a book" or "one book,"
> context decides) — English keeps these visibly separate.

## Grammar Lens

An **article** is the small word attached to a noun marking it as
"definite" (a specific, known thing — "the book") or "indefinite" (any
one of a kind — "a book"). English has this too (*the* / *a*), but
English's articles never change form. Spanish articles **must match the
noun's gender and number** — *el libro* (masc. sing.), *la casa* (fem.
sing.), *los libros* (masc. pl.), *las casas* (fem. pl.). This is the
first hard evidence that gender isn't just a label on nouns — it's a
requirement that ripples outward to everything describing that noun,
starting with the article in front of it.
