Lesson 1 of 5.
дерево (dérevo) — a tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a ticket, then the Russian for a map.

You'll want to know: дерево.
дерево (dérevo) — "a tree".

The word, taken apart.
дерево (dérevo) from Proto-Slavic dervo — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian dervà, "pine", Greek dóry, "wood, spear", drŷs, "oak", and Sanskrit dāru, "wood"; Trubachev adds Gothic triu, so English tree is a cousin, from PIE dóru, "tree, wood".

The word, taken apart.
цветок (tsvetók) is цвет, "bloom; colour", with the diminutive -ок. цвет from Proto-Slavic květŭ, "blossom", which Vasmer relates to Latvian kvitêt, "to shimmer".

Lesson 3 of 5.
лист (list) — a leaf; a sheet of paper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tree, then the Russian for a flower.

You'll want to know: лист.
лист (list) — "a leaf; a sheet of paper".

The word, taken apart.
лист (list) from Proto-Slavic listŭ — inherited, related to Lithuanian laĩškas, "leaf", and Latvian laiska, "a leaf of flax" (Vasmer). Paper took the same word, as in English a leaf of a book.

Lesson 4 of 5.
трава (travá) — grass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a flower, then the Russian for a leaf; a sheet of paper.

You'll want to know: трава.
трава (travá) — "grass".

The word, taken apart.
трава (travá) from Proto-Slavic trava — inherited, from the root of Old Russian трути, "to use up, to consume" (Vasmer): grass as the fodder that is eaten. The same root is in травить, "to poison".

Lesson 5 of 5.
деревня (derévnya) — a village.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a leaf; a sheet of paper, then the Russian for grass.

You'll want to know: деревня.
деревня (derévnya) — "a village".

The word, taken apart.
деревня (derévnya) first meant "a ploughed field", then "a farmstead with its land", then "a village" (Vasmer). It goes back to dĭrvĭnja, and Vasmer reports the usual link with Lithuanian dirvà, "a field".
