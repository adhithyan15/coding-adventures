# വന്നിട്ട് — having come

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-VARUKA-02, ML-GRAMMAR-C90-PAST-PARTICIPLE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *he came*.

[PAUSE 2s] Then say the describing form you made from it — *the teacher who
came*.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-VARUKA-02] -->

**വന്നിട്ട്** (*vanniṭṭŭ*) — **having come**.

It is the past form with **-ഇട്ട്** put on the end.

| | |
|---|---|
| **വന്നു** | he came |
| **വന്നിട്ട്** | having come |

## Grammar Lens: one past form, two directions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C90-PAST-PARTICIPLE-01] -->

**You have now made two things out of the same past form, and they were made in
opposite ways.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **വന്ന** | the **ു** taken **off** — describes a noun |
| **വന്നിട്ട്** | **-ഇട്ട്** put **on** — leans toward a verb |

The describing form was made by taking a vowel sign away. This one is made by
adding, and what it reaches for is different: **വന്ന** wants a noun to sit in
front of, while **വന്നിട്ട്** wants something else to follow — another verb, or
the word that closes the sentence.

**On its own it feels unfinished, and that is the point.** Saying *vanniṭṭŭ* and
stopping leaves a listener waiting, the way *having come* does in English. The
next page gives it the word it is waiting for.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What is added to a past form to make this one? (**-ഇട്ട്**.) What

after it? (**Another word to finish the sentence.**)
