Italian, chapter 476: Una grande delusione.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
deludere — to disappoint, to let down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for relief, then the Italian for to calm down.

You'll want to know: deludere.
deludere — "to disappoint, to let down".
Il film mi ha deluso: mi aspettavo di più. The film disappointed me: I expected more.
A false friend: it means to disappoint, not to delude. The past participle is deluso.

The word, taken apart.
deludere is a learned borrowing from Latin dēlūdere "to make fun of, to deceive" (Lewis & Short), built on lūdere "to play".

Lesson 2 of 5.
la delusione — disappointment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to calm down, then the Italian for to disappoint.

You'll want to know: la delusione.
la delusione — "disappointment".
Non passare l'esame è stata una grande delusione. Failing the exam was a big disappointment.
Una delusione d'amore — a heartbreak.

The word, taken apart.
delusione is a learned borrowing from Late Latin dēlūsiō "a deceiving" (Lewis & Short), from dēlūdere; in Italian it came to mean the feeling when hopes are let down.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il pianto — crying, tears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for disappointment, then the Italian for to comfort.

You'll want to know: il pianto.
il pianto — "crying, tears".
Dalla stanza si sentiva il pianto di un bambino. From the room you could hear a child crying.
The noun for piangere; scoppiare in un pianto — to burst into tears.

The word, taken apart.
pianto is inherited from Latin planctus "a beating of the breast in grief, a lament" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from plangere.

Lesson 5 of 5.
singhiozzare — to sob; to have hiccups.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: singhiozzare.
singhiozzare — "to sob; to have hiccups".
Il bambino singhiozzava e non riusciva a parlare. The child was sobbing and could not speak.
Il singhiozzo is a sob, and also the hiccups: ho il singhiozzo.

The word, taken apart.
singhiozzare is inherited from Vulgar Latin singlutiāre (EtymDB), from Latin singultus "a sob, a hiccup" (Lewis & Short).
