Japanese, chapter 108: Shellfish, Sea bream, Shark, Squirrel, Wild boar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かい (kai) — a shellfish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dew; the rainy season, then the Japanese for a slope.

You'll want to know: かい.
かい — kai — "a shellfish".

The word, taken apart.
かい (kai) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
りす (risu) — a squirrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sea bream, then the Japanese for a shark.

You'll want to know: りす.
りす — risu — "a squirrel".

The word, taken apart.
りす (risu) is usually explained as Sino-Japanese: the on'yomi of two characters, "chestnut" (Middle Chinese lit) and "rat" (Middle Chinese syoX), borrowed from Chinese and worn down. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.
