Sanskrit, chapter 462: Honest and False.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शठः (śaṭhaḥ) — deceitful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for heartless, then the Sanskrit for bears malice.

You'll want to know: शठः.
शठः — śaṭhaḥ — "deceitful".
शठः वणिक् मिथ्या वदति। śaṭhaḥ vaṇik mithyā vadati. The deceitful merchant tells lies.

The word, taken apart.
शठः (śaṭhaḥ) MW gives "false, deceitful, fraudulent" and "a cheat, a rogue"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

अपलपति — apalapati — "explains away; conceals".
चौरः सत्यम् अपलपति। cauraḥ satyam apalapati. The thief explains away the truth.
Past (he …): सः सत्यम् अपलपितवान्, saḥ satyam apalapitavān.

The word, taken apart.
अपलपति (apalapati) is built from apa, "away", and the root √lap, "to talk": to talk a thing away; MW gives "to explain away, deny, conceal" (MW).
