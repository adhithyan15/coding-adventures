## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C282-MEET-05]; assesses=[] -->

**el invitado** — "a guest."

From **invitar**, Latin **invitare**, to invite. *Invitado* is its participle,
"invited" — so Spanish names the guest by what was done to him, exactly as
*bienvenido* names a welcome by what is wished.

Put the two side by side. **venido** in the first of these five and **invitado**
in the last are the same piece of grammar doing the same job: an action turned
into a description of a person.

English **guest** is a different word altogether, Germanic *gastiz* — which is
also the source of Latin *hostis*, a stranger and then an enemy. Two peoples had
one word for the man at the door and could not agree whether he was a visitor or
a threat.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Five, and the run is closed: *bienvenido*, *el saludo*, *el abrazo*, *la sonrisa*,
*el invitado*.

Two of the five are participles, and that is the pattern worth carrying forward.
Spanish is unusually willing to name a person or a thing by an action already
finished — the one invited, the one welcomed — and once you notice it you will
find it everywhere.
