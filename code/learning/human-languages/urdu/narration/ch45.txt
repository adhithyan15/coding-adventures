Urdu, chapter 45: Boy, Girl, Man, Neighbour, Guest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
لڑکا (laṛkā) — a boy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a wife, then the Urdu for a child.

You'll want to know: لڑکا.
لڑکا — laṛkā — "a boy".

The word, taken apart.
لڑکا (laṛkā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from laḍikka, "child", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 10924). CDIAL calls the group a "defective" word for children and notes similar formations in Munda.

Lesson 3 of 5.
آدمی (ādmī) — a man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a boy, then the Urdu for a girl.

You'll want to know: آدمی.
آدمی — ādmī — "a man".

The word, taken apart.
آدمی (ādmī) is a loan from Arabic ādamī, "human, of Adam", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is ʾ-d-m (Lane). The ending -ī means "belonging to", so the word is "one of Adam's".
