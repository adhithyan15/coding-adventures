Latin, chapter 452: To stray, Off the road, Without roads, Roundabout way, To wander about.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aberrō, aberrāre — to stray, to wander from the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to show, then the Latin for to make known.

You'll want to know: aberrō, aberrāre.
aberrō, aberrāre — "to stray, to wander from the way".
In nebulā viātōrēs ā viā aberrant. In the fog the travellers stray from the road.
aberrō, aberrāre, aberrāvī, aberrātum, with ā or ab. English aberration comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
aberrō is built from ab 'away, from' and errō 'to wander, to go astray' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English aberration comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dēvius, dēvia, dēvium — off the road, out of the way; remote.

You'll want to know: dēvius, dēvia, dēvium.
dēvius, dēvia, dēvium — "off the road, out of the way; remote".
Vīlla dēvia est; nēmō eam facile invenit. The country house is out of the way; nobody finds it easily.
An adjective of three endings, from dē 'off' and via 'road'. English devious first meant 'off the main road'.

The word, taken apart.
dēvius is built from dē 'off, away from' and via 'road' (Lewis & Short). English devious comes from it, first in the sense 'out of the way'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ambāgēs, ambāgum — a roundabout way, a winding path; long-winded talk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for off the road, then the Latin for without roads.

You'll want to know: ambāgēs, ambāgum.
ambāgēs, ambāgum — "a roundabout way, a winding path; long-winded talk".
Pastor nōn rēctā viā sed per ambāgēs nōs dūcit. The shepherd leads us not by the direct road but by a roundabout way.
Third declension, feminine, mostly plural: ambāgēs, ambāgum f.; the singular appears only in the ablative ambāge. Also talk that goes round the point.

The word, taken apart.
ambāgēs is built from ambi- 'around' and agō 'to drive, to go' (EtymDB): a going round. Lewis & Short give a roundabout way, then roundabout speech.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vagor, vagārī — to wander about, to roam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for without roads, then the Latin for a roundabout way.

You'll want to know: vagor, vagārī.
vagor, vagārī — "to wander about, to roam".
Canis sine dominō per viās oppidī vagātur. A dog without a master roams the streets of the town.
Deponent: vagor, vagārī, vagātus sum. English vagabond comes from vagābundus, built on it.

The word, taken apart.
vagor is built on the adjective vagus 'wandering, roving' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English vague comes from vagus, and vagabond from vagābundus, built on the verb.
