French, chapter 401: Au volant.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
accélérer — to speed up, to accelerate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the slip road, then the French for to turn off.

You'll want to know: accélérer.
accélérer — "to speed up, to accelerate".
Sur l'autoroute, mon père accélère un peu trop. On the motorway my father speeds up a little too much.
L'accélérateur is the accelerator pedal.

The word, taken apart.
accélérer is a learned borrowing from Latin accelerāre 'to hasten', built on celer 'swift' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
braquer — to turn the wheel (hard); to aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to turn off, then the French for to speed up.

You'll want to know: braquer.
braquer — "to turn the wheel (hard); to aim".
Elle a braqué à gauche pour éviter le chat. She turned the wheel hard to the left to avoid the cat.
Braquer une banque is slang for to hold up a bank.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of braquer is not fully settled. It is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin verb built on Latin bracchium 'arm' (Lewis & Short): first to point or turn something with the arm.

Lesson 3 of 5.
déraper — to skid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to speed up, then the French for to turn the wheel.

You'll want to know: déraper.
déraper — "to skid".
Avec la pluie, la voiture a dérapé dans la descente. Because of the rain the car skidded on the way down.
Also figurative: la conversation a dérapé — the talk got out of hand.

The word, taken apart.
déraper is usually traced to Provençal derapar, first said of an anchor pulled loose from the sea bed, built on a Germanic verb for seizing. EtymDB links it with Germanic cousins such as Old Norse hreppa 'to grasp' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
caler — to stall (an engine); to wedge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to turn the wheel, then the French for to skid.

You'll want to know: caler.
caler — "to stall (an engine); to wedge".
Au feu rouge, j'ai calé deux fois. At the red light I stalled twice.
Je cale also means I give up, I cannot eat any more.

The word, taken apart.
caler in the sense 'to stall' was borrowed from Provençal calar 'to lower a sail', which goes back to Greek khalân 'to slacken' (EtymDB). A boat that lowers its sail stops, and so does an engine.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la pédale — the pedal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to skid, then the French for to stall.

You'll want to know: la pédale.
la pédale — "the pedal".
Le pied droit reste près de la pédale. The right foot stays near the pedal.
Pédaler — to pedal, on a bike.

The word, taken apart.
pédale was borrowed from Italian pedale, which is inherited from Latin pedālis 'of the foot', from pēs 'foot' (EtymDB).
