Japanese, chapter 482: Nails, Saws and Wire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
くぎ (kugi) — a nail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for wisdom, then the Japanese for to devise.

You'll want to know: くぎ.
くぎ — kugi — "a nail".
いたに くぎを うちます。 — ita ni kugi o uchimasu — "I drive a nail into the board."

The word, taken apart.
くぎ (kugi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for a nail, then the Japanese for a saw.

You'll want to know: はりがね.
はりがね — harigane — "wire".
はりがねで はこを むすびます。 — harigane de hako o musubimasu — "I tie the box up with wire."

The word, taken apart.
はりがね (harigane) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from hari, "needle" and gane, "metal" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
もくぞう (mokuzō) — made of wood; wooden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a saw, then the Japanese for wire.

You'll want to know: もくぞう.
もくぞう — mokuzō — "made of wood; wooden".
この うちは もくぞうです。 — kono uchi wa mokuzō desu — "This house is made of wood."

The word, taken apart.
もくぞう (mokuzō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are moku, "tree" (Middle Chinese muwk) and zō, "create" (dzawX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ささる (sasaru) — to stick into; to pierce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for wire, then the Japanese for made of wood.

You'll want to know: ささる.
ささる — sasaru — "to stick into; to pierce".
いたの くぎが てに ささりました。 — ita no kugi ga te ni sasarimashita — "A nail in the board went into my hand."
Polite ささります, sasarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): くぎが ゆびに ささりました。, kugi ga yubi ni sasarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ささる (sasaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
