German, chapter 514: Melodie und Rhythmus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Melodie — melody, tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for drums, then the German for to drum.

You'll want to know: die Melodie.
die Melodie — "melody, tune".
Die Melodie von diesem Lied ist sehr schön. The tune of this song is very beautiful.
Plural: die Melodien.

The word, taken apart.
Melodie was borrowed, through Late Latin melōdia, from Greek melōidía "singing, a song"; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to drum, then the German for melody.

You'll want to know: der Rhythmus.
der Rhythmus — "rhythm".
Die Kinder tanzen im Rhythmus der Musik. The children dance to the rhythm of the music.
Plural: die Rhythmen.

The word, taken apart.
Rhythmus is a learned borrowing from Latin rhythmus, from Greek rhythmós "measured flow, time" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Takt — beat, time (in music); tact.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for melody, then the German for rhythm.

You'll want to know: der Takt.
der Takt — "beat, time (in music); tact".
Der Schlagzeuger hält den Takt. The drummer keeps the beat.
Plural: die Takte. Also tact: Er hat keinen Takt.

The word, taken apart.
Takt comes from Latin tāctus "touch" (EtymDB), from tangere "to touch".

Lesson 5 of 5.
summen — to hum; to buzz.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for beat, then the German for verse.

You'll want to know: summen.
summen — "to hum; to buzz".
Beim Kochen summt meine Mutter ein Lied. My mother hums a song while she cooks.
Also of bees: Die Bienen summen.

The word, taken apart.
summen is inherited from Middle High German summen (EtymDB), an imitative word for a humming sound.
