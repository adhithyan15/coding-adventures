Malayalam, chapter 99: Songs and Stories.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ) — a song.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know.
പാട്ട് — pāṭṭŭ — a song.
ഇത് നല്ല പാട്ടാണ്. — itŭ nalla pāṭṭāṇŭ — "This is a good song."

The word, taken apart.
Listen to the join and you will hear nothing arrive. പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ) ends in the breathed half-u, and the chapter before last told you what those do when an ending comes: they step aside and let it land.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: നല്ലത്. joined to ആണ്: നല്ലതാണ് (nallathāṇŭ).
word: പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ). joined to ആണ്: പാട്ടാണ് (pāṭṭāṇŭ).
You have done this before without a new word to do it on. നല്ലതാണ് has been in the book since the opinion chapter. പാട്ടാണ് is the same event on a word you met a moment ago — no യ, no വ, nothing inserted.

Lesson 2 of 5.
പാടുക (pāṭuka) — to sing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say a song. Then say to play.

You'll want to know.
പാടുക — pāṭuka — to sing.
ഞാൻ പാടുന്നു. — ñān pāṭunnu — "I sing."

The word, taken apart.
Last chapter the noun sat inside the verb. This pair is not built that way.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
noun: കളി. verb: കളിക്കുക — the noun is there whole.
noun: പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ). verb: പാടുക (pāṭuka) — the ട്ട thins to a single ട.
Say them one after the other and you will hear it: പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ) holds the ṭ long, പാടുക (pāṭuka) does not.
Two words, one pattern between them — and that is all it is. Do not go looking for a doubled letter in every noun that sits beside a verb. These two are the pair this chapter gives you, and the book has not shown you a third.

Lesson 3 of 5.
നൃത്തം (nṛtthaṁ) — dance.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know.
നൃത്തം — nṛtthaṁ — dance.
ഇത് നല്ല നൃത്തമാണ്. — itŭ nalla nṛtthamāṇŭ — "This is a good dance."

The word, taken apart.
This is the third join, and the book has had it longest. നൃത്തം (nṛtthaṁ) ends in the ം, and you have known since the good-and-bad chapter what that sign does when ആണ് arrives: it is already the m, and it takes the full letter മ so it can carry the vowel behind it.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word ends in: the half-u. joined to ആണ്: പാട്ടാണ് (pāṭṭāṇŭ).
word ends in: ം. joined to ആണ്: നൃത്തമാണ് (nṛtthamāṇŭ).
Two words, two different endings, and neither one gains a sound. The first gives one up; the second only changes how its m is written.

Lesson 4 of 5.
കഥ (katha) — a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say a film. Then say dance.

You'll want to know.
കഥ — katha — a story.
ഇത് നല്ല കഥയാണ്. — itŭ nalla kathayāṇŭ — "This is a good story."

The word, taken apart.
You have a word that ends exactly as this one does. കഥ (katha)'s last letter is ഥ, carrying its own അ — and സിനിമ ends in അ too. So the same യ slides in:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ends in: അ. word: സിനിമ. joined to ആണ്: സിനിമയാണ് (sinimayāṇŭ).
ends in: അ. word: കഥ. joined to ആണ്: കഥയാണ് (kathayāṇŭ).
That is the whole chapter in three words. പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ) gives a sound up, നൃത്തം (nṛtthaṁ) re-spells one, and കഥ (katha) takes a letter in — three endings, three outcomes, and you were told each one before you met the word it lands on.

Lesson 5 of 5.
(a song, singing, dance, a story) (pāṭṭuṁ kathayuṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say a song, to sing, dance, a story.

Grammar Lens: three endings, three different events.
Four words met ആണ് in this chapter and the one before, and no two of the three endings did the same thing:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
word: പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ). joined to ആണ്: പാട്ടാണ് (pāṭṭāṇŭ). what happened: ends in the half-u — a sound is given up.
word: നൃത്തം (nṛtthaṁ). joined to ആണ്: നൃത്തമാണ് (nṛtthamāṇŭ). what happened: ends in ം — the m is re-spelled.
word: കഥ. joined to ആണ്: കഥയാണ് (kathayāṇŭ). what happened: ends in അ — a യ is added.
word: സിനിമ. joined to ആണ്: സിനിമയാണ് (sinimayāṇŭ). what happened: ends in അ — a യ is added.
Given up, re-spelled, added — three different events, and the only thing that tells you which you are looking at is how the word ended.

What you've built.
പാട്ട് (pāṭṭŭ) is the song; പാടുക (pāṭuka) is doing it. Unlike കളി and കളിക്കുക, the noun is not sitting whole inside the verb — the long ṭṭ thins to a single ṭ:
ഞാൻ പാടുന്നു. — ñān pāṭunnu — I sing.
