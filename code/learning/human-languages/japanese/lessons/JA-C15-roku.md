## You'll Want to Know — hear and say
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-ROKU]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-RO-01, JA-SCRIPT-KU-01, JA-LEX-KU] -->

> **ろく** — *roku* — **six**

**ろ** arrived last lesson; **く** you have had since chapter nine, where it also
became the number **nine**. So the sign **く** does two jobs in this run: it is
*nine* on its own and the second beat of *six*.

Six has one everyday reading. No native word stands beside it.

## What you've built — why six waited
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-ROKU, JA-LEX-NANA, JA-LEX-HACHI, JA-LEX-KU, JA-LEX-ICHI, JA-LEX-NI, JA-LEX-SAN, JA-LEX-YON, JA-LEX-GO] -->

You have been counting round a gap for three lessons. Here is what the gap was:
of the ten numbers in this run, **exactly two need a sign this book had not
taught**, and six is the first of them.

That is why the chapter did not start here. Everything that cost nothing came
first, so that when a lesson finally spends a sign you can see exactly what it
bought.

Say the run: *ichi, ni, san, yon, go, roku, nana, hachi, ku*. Nine of ten, and
only one gap left.
