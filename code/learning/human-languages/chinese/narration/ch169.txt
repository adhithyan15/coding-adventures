Lesson 1 of 7.
一大早 (yídàzǎo) — first thing in the morning; at the crack of dawn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to boil water, then the Mandarin for sugar.

You'll want to know: 一大早.
一大早 — yídàzǎo — "first thing in the morning; at the crack of dawn".
一大早妈妈去买大米了 yídàzǎo māma qù mǎi dàmǐ le — First thing this morning my mum went out to buy rice.
The tone of 一 changes: yī is said yí before the fourth tone of dà.

The word, taken apart.
一大早 (yídàzǎo) is built from 一 'one', 大 'big' and 早 'early', all three inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit, daH and tsawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'morning'.

Lesson 2 of 7.
午觉 (wǔjiào) — afternoon nap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sugar, then the Mandarin for first thing in the morning.

You'll want to know: 午觉.
午觉 — wǔjiào — "afternoon nap".
午觉以后我们喝茶 wǔjiào yǐhòu wǒmen hē chá — After the nap we'll have tea.
The new character, in pieces: 觉 (jiào) — nine strokes: three short strokes on top, the cover piece of 学 under them, and 见 at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
午觉 (wǔjiào) is built from 午 'noon' and 觉 (jiào) 'sleep', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nguX and kaewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second with the word for sleeping. The same character read jué, as in 觉得, is its other reading, glossed 'to wake' and 'to know'.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — jiàn, xué]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — three strokes, a cover, then 见.
觉 (jiào).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
Three short strokes on top: a dot on the left, a dot in the middle, and a short stroke on the right falling to the left.
Then the cover piece of 学, two strokes: a short stroke falling down on the left, then a horizontal that turns down and hooks.
Then 见, four strokes, as you write it, under the cover.
3 + 2 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 觉 (jiào), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 觉 (jiào)? (Nine.) What sits under the cover? (见.) Which word holds the cover too? (学.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
bàngwǎn — dusk; towards evening.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for first thing in the morning, then the Mandarin for an afternoon nap.

You'll want to know: bàngwǎn.
bàngwǎn — "dusk; towards evening".
wǒmen bàngwǎn qù sànbù — We go for a walk around dusk.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàngwǎn is built from bàng 'near' and 晚 'evening', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bangH and mjonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary gives the sense 'near' under the level-tone reading of the first; today's falling tone goes with bangH, which it leaves without a gloss.

Lesson 5 of 7.
áoyè — to stay up late; to stay up all night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an afternoon nap, then the Mandarin for towards evening.

You'll want to know: áoyè.
áoyè — "to stay up late; to stay up all night".
wǒ zuótiān áoyè le jīntiān hěn lèi — I stayed up late last night, and I'm tired today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
áoyè is built from áo 'to simmer' and yè 'night', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngaw and yaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to simmer'. How the pair came to mean staying up is not settled.
