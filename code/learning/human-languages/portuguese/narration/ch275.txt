Portuguese, chapter 275: Ao fim do dia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o amanhecer — the dawn, daybreak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for gradually, then the Portuguese for little by little.

You'll want to know: o amanhecer.
o amanhecer — "the dawn, daybreak".
Saímos de casa ao amanhecer. We left home at dawn.
Also a verb: amanhece cedo no verão — it gets light early in summer.

The word, taken apart.
amanhecer is built on manhã "morning" (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin māneāna, from Latin māne "in the morning".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o crepúsculo — the twilight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the late afternoon, then the Portuguese for nightfall.

You'll want to know: o crepúsculo.
o crepúsculo — "the twilight".
No crepúsculo, o céu fica cor de laranja. At twilight the sky turns orange.
A literary word for the half-light after sunset.

The word, taken apart.
crepúsculo is a learned borrowing from Latin crepusculum "twilight" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a véspera — the eve, the day before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for nightfall, then the Portuguese for the twilight.

You'll want to know: a véspera.
a véspera — "the eve, the day before".
Na véspera do exame, não dormi nada. The day before the exam I did not sleep at all.
Na véspera da partida — the day before leaving.

The word, taken apart.
véspera is inherited from Latin vespera "evening" (EtymDB): the evening before a feast became the day before it.
