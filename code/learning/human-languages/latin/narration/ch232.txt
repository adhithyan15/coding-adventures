Latin, chapter 232: To steal, To uncover, To seem, To serve, To suffer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fūror, fūrārī — to steal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to complain, then the Latin for to be missing.

You'll want to know: fūror, fūrārī.
fūror, fūrārī — "to steal".

The word, taken apart.
fūror is built on fūr, "a thief" (Lewis & Short), usually traced to Proto-Indo-European bhōr, "thief" (EtymDB 2.1), from the root of ferō, "to carry": one who carries off. It is a different word from furor, "rage".

Lesson 3 of 5.
videor, vidērī — to seem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to steal, then the Latin for to uncover.

You'll want to know: videor, vidērī.
videor, vidērī — "to seem".

The word, taken apart.
videor is the passive of videō, "to see": to be seen, so to seem. Videō is inherited from Proto-Indo-European weyd-, "to see, to know" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
serviō, servīre — to serve, to be a slave.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to uncover, then the Latin for to seem.

You'll want to know: serviō, servīre.
serviō, servīre — "to serve, to be a slave".

The word, taken apart.
serviō is the verb made from servus, "a slave" (EtymDB 2.1): to be a slave, to serve. English serve comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
patior, patī — to suffer, to allow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to seem, then the Latin for to serve.

You'll want to know: patior, patī.
patior, patī — "to suffer, to allow".

The word, taken apart.
patior has no sure origin. Lewis & Short compare Greek penthos, "grief", and EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed root pe(i)-, "to hurt", but neither link is settled. English passion and patient come from it.
