## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-HAIXING-01]; assesses=[] -->

*hái xíng* — **two rising tones.**

*hái* is "still"; *xíng* is "OK, it works". Together: "still OK" — not bad. Its
characters come later; this chapter keeps the word to the ear.

> *hái xíng, nǐ ne* — Not bad. And you?

## Why it's said this way — the middle answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-HAIXING-01] -->

很好 says things are good. *hái xíng* says they are all right, and it is the
answer people give when they are not going to complain. Said with a shrug, it can mean
"could be better".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*hái xíng* is built from *hái* "still" and *xíng* "all right". Both characters
are in the Guangyun (Baxter's transcription): the one for *hái* as *hwaen*,
"return", and the one for *xíng* as *haeng*, "walk, go". How they came to mean
"still" and "all right" is not settled.
