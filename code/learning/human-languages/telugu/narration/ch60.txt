Telugu, chapter 60: Animals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
గేదె (gēde) — a buffalo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nippu mean?

You'll want to know: గేదె.
గేదె (gēde) — "a buffalo".
గేదె (gēde) is the water buffalo — black, unhurried, and the animal most of the పాలు in a Telugu household actually come from. The cow gets the songs; the గేదె (gēde) fills the pot.
The word is inherited, and the animal is so ordinary that arriving at a village house often means being met by one before anybody comes out to say నమస్కారం.
A గేదె (gēde) standing in a చెరువు with only its nose above the water is the picture the word carries. That is where it spends the hottest part of the day.

The first animal.

Lesson 2 of 5.
మేక (mēka) — a goat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did gēde mean?

You'll want to know: మేక.
మేక (mēka) — "a goat".
మేక (mēka) is the goat: small, loud, and kept by households that cannot keep a గేదె (gēde). Where the buffalo wants water and grazing, the మేక (mēka) wants almost nothing and will eat what is left.
The word is inherited and it is old. Its shape sits close to the Tamil and Kannada words for the same animal, which is what happens to the names of animals people have kept for a very long time.
A మేక (mēka) will strip a bush bare and move on, which is why a గ్రామం keeps them at its edge rather than in its lanes.

What you've built.
Two: one for milk, one for anything.

Lesson 3 of 5.
కోడి (kōḍi) — a hen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mēka mean?

You'll want to know: కోడి.
కోడి (kōḍi) — "a hen".
కోడి (kōḍi) is the domestic fowl, hen or cock alike; the word does not divide them, and when the difference matters a second word goes in front.
It is inherited and it is everywhere in the family, close in shape to the Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam words for the same bird — one of those words that travelled with the animal and has not moved since.
The కోడి (kōḍi) is also the village clock. It wakes a house before ఉదయం is properly under way, which is why nobody there has ever needed a గంట.

Lesson 4 of 5.
చీమ (cīma) — an ant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kōḍi mean?

You'll want to know: చీమ.
చీమ (cīma) — "an ant".
చీమ (cīma) is the ant. After a గేదె (gēde) and a కోడి (kōḍi) it is a jump in scale worth making, because the same plain word-making covers whatever lives in a house, however small it is.
The word is inherited. Telugu has a stock comparison about a చీమ (cīma) carrying many times its own weight, and it is the ordinary way to praise somebody small who works hard.
A line of చీమలు across a floor usually ends at a పండు somebody left out.

What you've built.
Four: the biggest and the smallest so far.

Lesson 5 of 5.
పక్షి (pakṣi) — a bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did cīma mean?

You'll want to know: పక్షి.
పక్షి (pakṣi) — "a bird".
పక్షి (pakṣi) is the general one: any bird at all, the కోడి (kōḍi) included. It came in from Sanskrit, and beside it Telugu keeps its own పిట్ట for the small ones about a garden.
That is a split this book has met before — an inherited word doing the everyday work and a borrowed one carrying the wider idea, the same shape as నిప్పు beside అగ్ని. Once you start expecting it, half the vocabulary sorts itself.
Five animals now, from the గేదె (gēde) at the gate to the చీమ (cīma) under it, and a నమస్కారం for whoever keeps them.

What you've built.
Five: గేదె (gēde), మేక (mēka), కోడి (kōḍi), చీమ (cīma), పక్షి (pakṣi). Enough to greet a house and name what lives in it.
