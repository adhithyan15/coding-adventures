Latin, chapter 365: For, Of Course, Namely.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
quandōquidem — since, seeing that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to indicate, then the Latin for a suspicion.

You'll want to know: quandōquidem.
quandōquidem — "since, seeing that".
Quandōquidem tū ades, incipiāmus. Since you are here, let us begin.
Quandōquidem takes the indicative: the reason is a plain fact both sides accept. It is built from quandō and quidem, indeed.

The word, taken apart.
quandōquidem is built from quandō "when, since" and the particle quidem "indeed" (Lewis & Short): since, as we both know.

Lesson 2 of 5.
quippe — of course, naturally; for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a suspicion, then the Latin for since.

You'll want to know: quippe.
quippe — "of course, naturally; for".
Nōn vēnit, quippe quī aeger esset. He did not come, since of course he was ill.
Quippe gives a reason as something obvious, sometimes with irony. Quippe quī + subjunctive, seeing that he, is the classical use; Plautus and Livy also put the indicative after it.

The word, taken apart.
quippe is built from the old adverb quī "how" and the particle -pe (Lewis & Short), the same ending that is in nempe.

Lesson 3 of 5.
etenim — for indeed, and in fact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for since, then the Latin for of course.

You'll want to know: etenim.
etenim — "for indeed, and in fact".
Domī mānsī; etenim pluēbat. I stayed at home: for it was raining.
Etenim (= et + enim) opens a sentence that gives the reason for the last one. Like enim and nam, it takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
etenim is built from et "and" and enim "for, you see" (Lewis & Short): and the reason is.

Lesson 4 of 5.
nempe — surely, of course; namely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of course, then the Latin for for indeed.

You'll want to know: nempe.
nempe — "surely, of course; namely".
Quis hoc fēcit? Nempe tū. Who did this? You, of course.
Nempe appeals to what the listener must grant: surely, of course. It takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
nempe is built from nam "for" and the particle -pe (EtymDB), the same ending that is in quippe.

Lesson 5 of 5.
scīlicet — to be sure, of course.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for for indeed, then the Latin for surely.

You'll want to know: scīlicet.
scīlicet — "to be sure, of course".
Tū scīlicet omnia scīs! You know everything, to be sure!
Scīlicet = scīre licet, one may know. Often ironic: to be sure! English scilicet, short sc., means namely.

The word, taken apart.
scīlicet is contracted from scīre licet "one may know" (Lewis & Short): it is plain to know. English scilicet "namely" was borrowed from it.
