# వచ్చి (vacci) — having come, and then

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-GRAMMAR-C317-FIRST-THEN-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C317-CIVARIKI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "First I drank tea. After that I went to school." (*modaṭa nēnu ṭī tāgānu. ā taruvāta baḍiki veḷḷānu.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Two sentences, two full verbs. Telugu can fold them into one.

## You'll want to know: the -ఇ form
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C317-CONJUNCTIVE-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C90-LEAVE-03, TE-LEX-C40-BHOJANAM-01, TE-LEX-C72-MARKET-01, TE-LEX-C46-ASK-05] -->

> **నేను ఇంటికి వచ్చి భోజనం చేశాను.**
>
> *nēnu iṇṭiki vacci bhōjanaṁ cēśānu.* — "I came home and had a meal."

The first verb ends in **-ఇ** and carries no person: **వచ్చి**, "having come". Only the last verb says who and
when. So a chain of **-ఇ** forms runs on to one full verb at the end:

> **అతను అంగడికి వెళ్ళి, ఒక పుస్తకం కొని, ఇంటికి వచ్చాడు.**
>
> *atanu aṅgaḍiki veḷḷi, oka pustakaṁ koni, iṇṭiki vaccāḍu.* — "He went to the shop, bought a book and came home."

**కొని** (*koni*, "having bought") is the **-ఇ** form of **కొను**, "to buy". Many of these sit close to the past
stem: **వెళ్ళి**, **చదివి**, **తాగి**; and **తిను** → **తిని**, **చూడు** → **చూసి**,
**చేయు** → **చేసి**.

## You'll want to know: -ఇన తరువాత, after doing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C317-AFTER-DOING-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C64-JOIN-04, TE-LEX-C143-QUAL143-04] -->

> **భోజనం చేసిన తరువాత నేను నిద్రపోయాను.**
>
> *bhōjanaṁ cēsina taruvāta nēnu nidrapōyānu.* — "After having the meal, I slept."

**చేసిన** is "that was done", and before **తరువాత** it gives "after doing": **వచ్చిన తరువాత**, "after
coming"; **చూసిన తరువాత**, "after seeing". Where **-ఇ** ties two actions tightly, **-ఇన తరువాత** marks a
clear step in time.
