Portuguese, chapter 351: As repercussões.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o impacto — the impact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to give rise to, then the Portuguese for thanks to.

You'll want to know: o impacto.
o impacto — "the impact".
A nova fábrica teve um grande impacto na aldeia. The new factory had a big impact on the village.
Ter impacto em — to have an impact on.

The word, taken apart.
impacto is a learned borrowing from Latin impāctus (EtymDB), the participle of impingere "to push, to strike or drive into" (Lewis & Short), built from in and pangere "to fix".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a repercussão — the repercussion, the knock-on effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for thanks to, then the Portuguese for the impact.

You'll want to know: a repercussão.
a repercussão — "the repercussion, the knock-on effect".
A decisão teve repercussões em todo o país. The decision had repercussions across the whole country.
Mostly plural: as repercussões.

The word, taken apart.
repercussão is a learned borrowing from Latin repercussiō "a rebounding, a reflection" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from repercutere "to strike back", built on quatere "to shake, to strike".

Lesson 3 of 5.
implicar — to involve, to entail; to imply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the impact, then the Portuguese for the repercussion.

You'll want to know: implicar.
implicar — "to involve, to entail; to imply".
Mudar de casa implica muito trabalho. Moving house involves a lot of work.
Informally, implicar com alguém — to pick on someone.

The word, taken apart.
implicar is a learned borrowing from Latin implicāre "to fold into, to entangle" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from in and plicāre "to fold"; the inherited form of the same Latin verb is empregar.

Lesson 4 of 5.
acarretar — to entail, to bring about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the repercussion, then the Portuguese for to involve.

You'll want to know: acarretar.
acarretar — "to entail, to bring about".
Esse erro acarretou muitos problemas à empresa. That mistake brought many problems to the company.
Formal; close to implicar. Acarretar custos — to entail costs.

The word, taken apart.
acarretar first meant "to carry by cart": it is built on carreta "small cart", from carro, inherited from Latin carrus "a wagon" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
desencadear — to trigger, to unleash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to involve, then the Portuguese for to entail.

You'll want to know: desencadear.
desencadear — "to trigger, to unleash".
Uma pequena discussão desencadeou uma grande zanga. A small argument triggered a big row.
Literally to unchain.

The word, taken apart.
desencadear is built from des- and encadear "to chain", on cadeia "chain", inherited from Latin catēna "a chain" (Lewis & Short): to let loose from the chain.
