Spanish, chapter 771: Por el sabor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el sabor — the taste, the flavour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the butcher's, then the Spanish for the greengrocer's.

You'll want to know: el sabor.
el sabor — "the taste, the flavour".
Me gusta por el sabor, no por el precio. I like it for the taste, not the price.
¿De qué sabor? — which flavour? Saber a is to taste of: Sabe a limón.

The word, taken apart.
sabor is inherited from Latin sapōrem, from sapor 'a taste, a flavour' (EtymDB), built on sapere 'to taste', the verb behind saber.

Lesson 2 of 5.
picante — spicy, hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the greengrocer's, then the Spanish for the taste.

You'll want to know: picante.
picante — "spicy, hot".
No lo como porque es demasiado picante. I don't eat it because it is too spicy.
Picante is spicy-hot; hot in temperature is caliente.

The word, taken apart.
picante is built on picar 'to sting, to prick'. Picar is usually traced to spoken Latin pīccāre 'to sting, to strike', the form EtymDB gives for its French cousin piquer. Spicy food stings the tongue.

Lesson 3 of 5.
salado — salty; savoury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the taste, then the Spanish for spicy.

You'll want to know: salado.
salado — "salty; savoury".
No me gusta la sopa porque está muy salada. I don't like the soup because it is very salty.
In Spain salado can also mean witty or charming; in parts of Latin America, unlucky.

The word, taken apart.
salado is built on the participle of salar 'to salt', from sal, which is inherited from Latin sāl 'salt' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
crudo — raw; uncooked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for spicy, then the Spanish for salty.

You'll want to know: crudo.
crudo — "raw; uncooked".
No como sushi porque no me gusta el pescado crudo. I don't eat sushi because I don't like raw fish.
El petróleo crudo — crude oil. Lo tiene crudo (Spain, informal) — he's got it tough.

The word, taken apart.
crudo is inherited from Latin crūdus 'raw, uncooked, bloody' (EtymDB), through old Spanish crudo.

Lesson 5 of 5.
oler — to smell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for salty, then the Spanish for raw.

You'll want to know: oler.
oler — "to smell".
No la como porque huele mal. I don't eat it because it smells bad.
An h is added where it changes: huelo, hueles. Oler a — to smell of.

The word, taken apart.
oler is inherited from Latin olēre 'to give off a smell' (EtymDB). The stressed o became ue, and Spanish writes an h in front: huele.
