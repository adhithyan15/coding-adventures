# ತ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ◌ಿ — what does it do?

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. Just one — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ತ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-05]; assesses=[] -->

**ತ** — *ta*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *t*, it is **ta**.

You already say these, and every one of them has ತ somewhere inside it:

- **ಸಂತೋಷ** *santōṣa* — joy / pleased to meet you
- **ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ** *hōgi baruttēne* — goodbye (lit. "I'll go and come back")
- **ಮತ್ತೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ** *matte sigōṇa* — we'll meet again
- **ಮಾತನಾಡು** *mātanāḍu* — to speak

## Writing: ತ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-05] -->

- **1.** start at the upper-left end and sweep down around the broad bowl and up its right side
- **2.** without lifting, turn left over the top and curl down into the inner loop
- **3.** without lifting, close the small loop and rise to the top bar
- **4.** lift, then draw the top bar from left to right
- **5.** without lifting, curl up into the hook

**Pen lifts: 1.** The broad bowl, the arc over its top and the small loop
inside are one run; the curled bar on top comes after the only lift, as it
does for ನ.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna
> A, 'Kannada-alphabet-tha.gif', 43 frames, Wikimedia Commons, 25 May
> 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0.

> The animation is filed as 'tha': that series spells dental ತ with an *h*
> and keeps 'ta' for a different letter, ಟ.
