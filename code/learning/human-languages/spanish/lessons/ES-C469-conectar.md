# conectar — to connect

## You'll want to know: conectar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02] -->

**conectar** — to connect, to join one thing to another.

> No puedo **conectar** la impresora al ordenador.

The noun is **la conexión**, and it is worth a second look: the verb has a
**c**, the noun has an **x**. Say *co-ne-XIÓN*, weight on the last syllable.

## Grammar Lens: conectar and conectarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02, ES-LEX-INTERNET] -->

The same verb does two different jobs depending on whether the thing being
joined is an object out in front of you or yourself.

| what you mean | what you say |
|---|---|
| join a thing to something | **conectar** la impresora |
| get yourself online | **conectarse** a internet |

So *no me puedo conectar* means *I can't get connected* — you, not the
printer. The pronoun is doing the whole of that work, and dropping it turns
the sentence into a report about some object you never named.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03] -->

The Academy's dictionary derives **conectar** from English **to connect** —
and English had built that out of Latin **conectere**: **con-**, together,
plus **nectere**, to **tie**.

So the Latin is real, and the route is not the one it looks like. The word did
not sit in Spanish since Rome waiting for wires. It came in from English in
the modern era, carrying Latin parts back to a Latin language.

You have now met that happening three ways in a row:

- **el ordenador** — an old Spanish word that borrowed a new *meaning* from
  French;
- **internet** — an English word borrowed whole, with only the beat changed;
- **conectar** — an English word borrowed as a shape, its pieces Latin all
  along.

*Nectere* is a knot-word. English keeps it in **nexus**, a tying-together, and
in **annex**, tied on. A *nexus* is not a vague link — it is the place where
the threads are actually knotted.
