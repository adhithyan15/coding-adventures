French, chapter 142: Opening Hours, a Notice, the Rent, an Advert, a Bill.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
l'horaire — opening hours, a timetable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to foresee, then the French for to organise.

You'll want to know: l'horaire.
l'horaire — "opening hours, a timetable".
[once you have stopped driving — read: the door — Horaire: lundi – vendredi, 9 h – 18 h.]
Open on weekdays from nine to six: not on samedi.

What you've built.
One more word for this chapter.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: l'horaire]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: l'horaire, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say l'horaire]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the French for to foresee, then the French for to organise, then say l'horaire again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does l'horaire mean? ("Opening hours, a timetable".) Say it once more.

Lesson 6 of 7.
Saying no and looking back — a first pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the words for an advert and a bill.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: nothing, none, neither, which one, anything at all]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: already, recently, the day before yesterday, formerly, to happen]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Nothing? (rien.) None? (aucun / aucune.) Neither? (non plus.) Which one? (lequel / laquelle.) Anything at all? (n'importe quoi.) Already? (déjà.) Recently? (récemment.) The day before yesterday? (avant-hier.) Formerly? (autrefois.) To happen? (se produire.)
