Sanskrit, chapter 282: How I Feel, and What I Hope For.
4 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
अहं प्रसन्नः अस्मि (ahaṃ prasannaḥ asmi) — how I feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I am ill". (ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi.) And a woman? (ahaṃ rugṇā asmi.) Feelings work the same way.

You'll want to know: the feeling follows the speaker.
अहं प्रसन्नः अस्मि। ahaṃ prasannaḥ asmi. — "I am pleased."
अहं प्रसन्ना अस्मि। ahaṃ prasannā asmi. — "I am pleased."
The first is a man speaking, the second a woman: the word for the feeling takes the speaker's gender, -aḥ or -ā.
मीरा भीता अस्ति। mīrā bhītā asti. — "Mira is afraid."
अरुणः श्रान्तः अस्ति। aruṇaḥ śrāntaḥ asti. — "Arun is tired."
ते कुपिताः सन्ति। te kupitāḥ santi. — "They are angry."
सन्ति (santi) is "are", for several.

You'll want to know: happy, and what I like.
सुखी (sukhī), "happy", and दुःखी (duḥkhī), "unhappy", end in -ī for a man.
अद्य अहं सुखी अस्मि। adya ahaṃ sukhī asmi. — "Today I am happy."
What you like keeps the frame you know, with मह्यम् (mahyam), "to me":
मह्यं संगीतं रोचते। mahyaṃ saṅgītaṃ rocate. — "I like music."
A feeling with its reason:
अहं प्रसन्नः अस्मि, ahaṃ prasannaḥ asmi, यतः अरुणः आगतवान्। yataḥ aruṇaḥ āgatavān. — "I am pleased, because Arun has come."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I am afraid" — ahaṃ bhītaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I am tired" — ahaṃ śrāntaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I am angry" — ahaṃ kupitaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that you are tired today, then that Mira is pleased because Arun has come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I am pleased", said by a woman? (अहं प्रसन्ना अस्मि.) "Mira is afraid"? (मीरा भीता अस्ति.) "I like music"? (मह्यं संगीतं रोचते.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
भवितुम् इच्छामि (bhavitum icchāmi) — what I want to become.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I am pleased". (ahaṃ prasannaḥ asmi.) Then "I want to go". (ahaṃ gantum icchāmi.) Now say what you want to become.

You'll want to know: भवितुम् इच्छामि.
अहं वैद्यः भवितुम् इच्छामि। ahaṃ vaidyaḥ bhavitum icchāmi. — "I want to become a doctor."
भवितुम् (bhavitum) is the -tum form of भवति: "to become". What you become stays in the same case as you: वैद्यः, like अहम्.
सः अध्यापकः भवितुम् इच्छति। saḥ adhyāpakaḥ bhavitum icchati. — "He wants to become a teacher."
अहं नगरं गन्तुम् इच्छामि। ahaṃ nagaraṃ gantum icchāmi. — "I want to go to the city."

You'll want to know: मम स्वप्नः अस्ति.
मम स्वप्नः अस्ति (mama svapnaḥ asti), "my dream is", opens a hope; यत् gives what it is:
मम स्वप्नः अस्ति, mama svapnaḥ asti, यत् अहं वैद्यः भविष्यामि। yat ahaṃ vaidyaḥ bhaviṣyāmi. — "My dream is that I will become a doctor."
मीरायाः स्वप्नः अस्ति, mīrāyāḥ svapnaḥ asti, यत् सा नगरं गमिष्यति। yat sā nagaraṃ gamiṣyati. — "Mira's dream is that she will go to the city."
मीरायाः (mīrāyāḥ) is "Mira's".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I want to become a doctor" — ahaṃ vaidyaḥ bhavitum icchāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I want to become a teacher" — aham adhyāpakaḥ bhavitum icchāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I want to become a student" — ahaṃ chātraḥ bhavitum icchāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that you want to become a teacher, then that your dream is that you will go to the city.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"To become"? (भवितुम्.) "He wants to become a teacher"? (सः अध्यापकः भवितुम् इच्छति.) "My dream is that …"? (मम स्वप्नः अस्ति, यत् …)


Lesson 3 of 4.
उत्सुकः (utsukaḥ) — eager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I want to become a doctor". (ahaṃ vaidyaḥ bhavitum icchāmi.)

You'll want to know: उत्सुकः.
उत्सुकः — utsukaḥ — "eager". Like every feeling, it takes the gender of the one who feels it: उत्सुका (utsukā) for a woman.
अहम् उत्सुकः अस्मि। aham utsukaḥ asmi. — "I am eager."
मीरा उत्सुका अस्ति। mīrā utsukā asti. — "Mira is eager."
अहं नगरं द्रष्टुम् उत्सुकः अस्मि। ahaṃ nagaraṃ draṣṭum utsukaḥ asmi. — "I am eager to see the city."
द्रष्टुम् (draṣṭum), "to see", is the -tum form of पश्यति, from the root √dṛś of द्रक्ष्यति.

The word, taken apart.
उत्सुकः (utsukaḥ) is built from उत् (ut), "apart", and सु (su), "well", with the ending -ka, as Monier-Williams takes it apart; he gives its senses as restless and uneasy, then eager and longing for something.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: utsukaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The children are eager" — bālāḥ utsukāḥ santi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I am eager to see the city" — ahaṃ nagaraṃ draṣṭum utsukaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does उत्सुकः (utsukaḥ) mean? ("Eager".) "Mira is eager"? (मीरा उत्सुका अस्ति.)
