# One mark apart

## Script: three pairs the eye has to separate
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-QAMIZ-LETTERS, UR-SCRIPT-TE-VS-TTE, UR-SCRIPT-GAF-LETTER] -->

> ق · ک  ·  ت · ٹ  ·  گ · ک

Urdu spends very little ink on these differences, and each one changes the word:

| the pair | what separates them |
|---|---|
| **ق** *qāf* · **ک** *kāf* | the throat — *qāf* is drawn further back |
| **ت** *te* · **ٹ** *ṭe* | two dots above, against a small raised loop |
| **گ** *gāf* · **ک** *kāf* | one extra stroke laid across the top |

**ق** and **ک** are the pair to be honest about: relaxed speech often merges
them, and careful or formal speech keeps them apart, so the letters stay
distinct on the page whatever the mouth does. **ت** and **ٹ** never merge —
the dots mean the plain dental *t*, the loop means the retroflex the tongue
curls back for, and the two make different words.

## Wrap-up Recall
