6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
a nuca — nape (of the neck).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for nostril, then the Portuguese for gum.

You'll want to know: a nuca.
a nuca — "nape (of the neck)".
O sol queimou-me a nuca. The sun burned the back of my neck.
The back of the neck; the front is a garganta.

The word, taken apart.
nuca comes from Medieval Latin nucha, which is usually traced to an Arabic word for the spinal marrow.

Lesson 2 of 6.
a coxa — thigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for gum, then the Portuguese for nape.

You'll want to know: a coxa.
a coxa — "thigh".
Depois da corrida, doem-me as coxas. After the run, my thighs ache.
Of a chicken: a coxa de frango — a chicken leg.

The word, taken apart.
coxa is inherited, through Old Portuguese coixa (EtymDB), from Latin coxa "hip" (Lewis & Short); the meaning moved down to the thigh.

Lesson 4 of 6.
o calcanhar — heel.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for thigh, then the Portuguese for hip.

You'll want to know: o calcanhar.
o calcanhar — "heel".
Os sapatos novos fizeram-me uma ferida no calcanhar. The new shoes gave me a sore on my heel.
The heel of a shoe is o salto.

The word, taken apart.
calcanhar is usually traced to Vulgar Latin forms built on Latin calcāneum "heel" (Lewis & Short), from calx "heel".
