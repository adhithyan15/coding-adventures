# না … না … — neither, nor

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NA-VERB-01, BN-LEX-C17-BA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter twenty-seven was firm about this: **না** goes after. There is
exactly one construction in which it goes first, and it is the one that denies
both halves of a choice.

- [YOU RECALL: the word that offers the choice — *bā*]

## You'll want to know: না … না …
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-GRAMMAR-C17-NA-NA-01]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01, BN-LEX-C10-DUDH-01] -->

> **না চা, না দুধ** — *nā chā, nā dudh* — **neither tea nor milk.**

> **আমার না চা আছে, না দুধ** — *āmār nā chā āchhe, nā dudh* — **I have neither tea nor milk.**

One **না** in front of each thing refused. The pattern is the mirror image of
**আর**: where *and* stood once, between two nouns, *neither* stands twice, before
each of them.

## Grammar Lens: three ways to refuse two things
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C17-NA-NA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C17-O-CLITIC-01] -->

| shape | how it refuses |
|---|---|
| **আমার চা আর দুধ নেই** | one negator at the end covers both |
| **চা-ও নেই, দুধ-ও নেই** | each thing marked with **-ও**, each denied |
| **না চা, না দুধ** | the flat refusal, and the emphatic one |

All three are correct Bengali. The first is what you say without thinking about
it; the third is what you say when you want the refusal heard.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C17-NA-NA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NA-VERB-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Where does **না** stand in an ordinary negative sentence, and where
in this one? (**After the verb** ordinarily; **in front of each refused thing**
here.) Which joiner does the pattern mirror? (**আর**, one *and* between two
becoming one *no* before each.)
