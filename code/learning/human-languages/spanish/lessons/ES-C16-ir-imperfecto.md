# iba — ir returns to its inherited past

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-IR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-VERB-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-SER-IR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Present **voy** and completed-past **fui** came from different
historical sets. For an open or habitual past, **ir** gives you **iba** — “I was
going” or “I used to go.”

## Grammar Lens: the singular ir row
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-IR-VERB-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-IR, ES-LEX-A] -->

| person | open or habitual past |
|---|---|
| yo | **iba** |
| tú | **ibas** |
| él / ella / usted | **iba** |

Say **iba · ibas · iba**. Reuse the destination frame: **Iba a Madrid** — “I
was going to Madrid” or “I used to go to Madrid.” Plural persons wait.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ETYMON-IBA]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-IRE-VADERE, ES-ETYMON-FUI, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-VERB-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

**Iba** continues Latin **ībam**, from **īre**, the inherited verb behind the
Spanish infinitive **ir**. Present **voy** contains material associated with
**vādere**, while preterite **fui** comes from the Latin “be” perfect family.
These sources help explain the three learned sets; they do not mean that every
form in every tense can be assigned to one source by a three-box rule.
