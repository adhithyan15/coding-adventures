## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C288-LEAVE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**el viaje** — "the journey."

Latin **viaticum**, from **via**, a road: not the journey at all, but the money
and food you packed for one. Spanish moved the word off the supplies and onto the
trip.

English took the same *viaticum* through French and got **voyage**. It also has
*via* on its own: a **viaduct** carries a road; something **trivial** is *tri-via*,
the place where three roads meet, and what gets said at a crossroads is not
important.

The shift is the interesting part. A word for the preparation became the word
for the thing prepared for. That happens constantly, and it is why so many
ordinary words look sideways at their own meaning.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Two of the five, and both were something else first.

A jump became a way out; a packed bag became a journey. Neither word describes
what it now names.
