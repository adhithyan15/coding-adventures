Bengali, chapter 352: Fair for Everyone.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
বৈষম্য (boishômyô) — discrimination; inequality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for esteem, then the Bengali for to treat with regard.

You'll want to know: বৈষম্য.
বৈষম্য — boishômyô — "discrimination; inequality".
মেয়েদের সঙ্গে বৈষম্য করা অন্যায়। meyeder sôngge boishômyô kôrā ônyāy. Discriminating against girls is wrong.

The word, taken apart.
বৈষম্য (boishômyô) is Sanskrit vaiṣamya, unevenness (Monier-Williams), from viṣama 'uneven'. Bengali uses it for unequal treatment; it is done with -এর সঙ্গে.

You'll want to know: মর্যাদা.
মর্যাদা — môrjādā — "dignity; standing".
সব মানুষের মর্যাদা সমান। sôb mānusher môrjādā sômān. Every person has equal dignity.

The word, taken apart.
মর্যাদা (môrjādā) is Sanskrit maryādā, a boundary and so the proper limits of conduct (Monier-Williams). Bengali uses it for the dignity or standing those limits protect.

বঞ্চিত (bônchitô) — deprived (of).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for dignity, then the Bengali for sympathy.

You'll want to know: বঞ্চিত.
বঞ্চিত — bônchitô — "deprived (of)".
গ্রামের মানুষ ভালো হাসপাতাল থেকে বঞ্চিত। grāmer mānush bhālo hāspātāl theke bônchitô. Villagers are deprived of a good hospital.

The word, taken apart.
বঞ্চিত (bônchitô) is Sanskrit vañcita, deceived or cheated (Monier-Williams). The Bengali sense, deprived of something, is not one the dictionary gives; what is missing takes থেকে.

Lesson 5 of 5.
অন্তর্ভুক্ত করা (ôntôrbhuktô kôrā) — to include.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for sympathy, then the Bengali for deprived.

You'll want to know: অন্তর্ভুক্ত করা.
অন্তর্ভুক্ত করা — ôntôrbhuktô kôrā — "to include".
আমাদের দলে মীরাকেও অন্তর্ভুক্ত করো। āmāder dôle mīrākeo ôntôrbhuktô kôro. Include Mira in our team too.
Past (he/she …): তিনি আমাকে দলে অন্তর্ভুক্ত করলেন, tini āmāke dôle ôntôrbhuktô kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
অন্তর্ভুক্ত করা (ôntôrbhuktô kôrā) is a learned compound of Sanskrit antar, within, with bhukta, eaten or enjoyed (Monier-Williams), so 'taken in', and করা: to take someone or something inside a group. The compound itself is not in the dictionary.
