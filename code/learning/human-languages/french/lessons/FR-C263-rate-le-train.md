# j'ai raté le train — when it goes wrong

## You'll want to know: j'ai raté le train
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-RATE-LE-TRAIN-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C237-VERBES237-01, FR-LEX-C63-VERBES15-05, FR-LEX-C80-CHOSES32-02] -->

**j'ai raté le train** — "I've missed the train".

**rater** is a regular *-er* verb, the everyday one for anything that leaves
without you:

> **J'ai raté le train.** — I've missed the train.
> **J'ai raté le métro.** — I've missed the metro.

You also have **manquer**, which says the same thing a little more
carefully: *j'ai manqué le train*. In Québec, *manquer* is the more usual
verb for it. And for a thing that is gone, not a train that has left, the verb is
*perdre*: *j'ai perdu ma valise*, I've lost my suitcase.

English hesitates between "I missed" and "I've missed". French uses the
passé composé for both: one finished event.

## Grammar Lens: reporting a problem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-SIGNALER-PROBLEME-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C127-ECOLE79-05, FR-LEX-EXCUSEZ-MOI-01, FR-LEX-C132-QUALITES84-03] -->

A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:

> **Excusez-moi, j'ai un problème.** — a courtesy.
> **J'ai raté le train.** — what went wrong.
> **Le prochain train part à quelle heure ?** — what you need.

Or the fault is not yours: *le train est en retard*, "the train is late".
Say what happened, then ask the question that gets you moving again.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**rater** has no parent in EtymDB 2.1, which lists among its senses a gun
that misfires. Where the word itself comes from is not settled by either
source, so it is left open here. **manquer** was borrowed from
Italian *mancare*, "to be lacking", which goes back to Latin *mancus*,
"maimed, powerless" (EtymDB 2.1). **retard** comes from the verb *retarder*,
which goes back to Latin *retardāre*, "to keep back, to delay" (Lewis &
Short, under *retardo*).
