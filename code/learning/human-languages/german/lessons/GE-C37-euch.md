# euch — "you all," and the word English promoted

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-IHR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-IHR-T-02, GE-LEX-UNS-01, GE-LEX-DICH-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Ihr** is *you all*. It needs a done-to shape, and the shape it has
is the most quietly famous word in this chapter.

## You'll want to know: euch
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-EUCH-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-IHR-01, GE-LEX-UNS-01, GE-LEX-SEHEN-01] -->

> **euch** — "you all", when you are the ones being done to. It also covers *to
> you all*, exactly as *uns* covers both jobs.

Said *oykh*: the **eu** is the *oy* of English *boy*, and the **ch** is the soft
one.

> **Ich sehe euch.** — "I see you all."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-EUCH-01, GE-LEX-IHR-01, GE-LEX-DU-SIE-02, GE-LEX-UNS-01] -->

English had this word. Old English **ēow** was the done-to shape of **ġē**
("ye"), and *ēow* is exactly what German's *euch* is.

Then English did something strange. It let *ēow* — the **object** form — take
over the subject job as well, so *ye* died out and **you** became the only word
left for the second person, singular or plural, doer or done-to. Modern English
addresses everybody with what was once a plural object pronoun.

German still has the whole set standing: **du, dich, dir** for one person,
**ihr, euch** for several, **Sie, Ihnen** for a stranger. English boiled all of
that down to **you**, and *y'all* is the attempt to build the plural back.
