Italian, chapter 291: Mi hanno preso la borsa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lo scippo — the bag-snatching.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the victim, then the Italian for to commit.

You'll want to know: lo scippo.
lo scippo — "the bag-snatching".
Alla stazione c'è stato uno scippo. There was a bag-snatching at the station.
A theft from the hand, often by someone on a moped; common on the local news.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of scippo is not settled; it is usually linked with southern Italian dialect words for snatching (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il borseggiatore — the pickpocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bag-snatching, then the Italian for to snatch.

You'll want to know: il borseggiatore.
il borseggiatore — "the pickpocket".
Attenzione ai borseggiatori sull'autobus! Watch out for pickpockets on the bus!
Built on borsa, ‘bag’: someone who goes through your bag.

The word, taken apart.
borseggiatore is built on borseggiare "to pick pockets" (EtymDB), from borsa "bag", which is usually traced to Late Latin bursa "a purse".

Lesson 4 of 5.
derubare — to rob someone, to steal from.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to snatch, then the Italian for the pickpocket.

You'll want to know: derubare.
derubare — "to rob someone, to steal from".
In vacanza ci hanno derubato. We were robbed on holiday.
Derubare takes the person, rubare the thing: mi hanno derubato, mi hanno rubato il portafoglio.

The word, taken apart.
derubare is built on rubare "to steal"; EtymDB links it with French dérober "to steal", from Old French desrober. Rubare goes back, through Medieval Latin, to a Frankish word for plunder (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la refurtiva — the stolen goods.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the pickpocket, then the Italian for to rob someone.

You'll want to know: la refurtiva.
la refurtiva — "the stolen goods".
La polizia ha trovato la refurtiva in un garage. The police found the stolen goods in a garage.
A police and news word, singular only.

The word, taken apart.
refurtiva is usually traced to Latin rēs fūrtīva "a stolen thing", built on fūrtīvus "stolen", from fūrtum "theft" and fūr "thief" (Lewis & Short).
