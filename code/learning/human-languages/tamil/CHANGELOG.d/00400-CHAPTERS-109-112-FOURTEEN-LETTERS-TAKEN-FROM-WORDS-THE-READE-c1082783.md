## Chapters 109-112: fourteen letters taken from words the reader already says (HL-C443)

HL-C443's rule for gentle writing: teach each letter from a word the reader
already knows, and keep going until the reader has written every letter.

`measureLetterAnchoring` found fourteen characters Tamil readers had been
reading in words since chapter 1 and had never written on their own. Each now
gets one lesson, and each lesson names the word it came from:

| chapter | node | letters, and the words they come from |
|---|---|---|
| 109 | MEET-GREET | இ and ◌ை from இல்லை; ஸ from நமஸ்காரம் |
| 110 | EXCHANGE-NAMES | ங and ள from நீங்கள்; ◌ீ from நீ; ◌ெ from பெயர் |
| 111 | RESPOND-BASIC | ஐ from ஐந்து; ◌ூ from மூன்று; ஏ from ஏழு |
| 112 | COURTESY-THANK | ஞ from ஞாயிறு; ஊ from ஊர்; ஒ from ஒரு; ஷ from சந்தோஷம் |

Nine of the fourteen letters have a cited stroke order in `script-ductus`.
Those lessons give numbered pen movements, the number of pen lifts, and the
source, and the book prints each one's filmstrip. The four vowel signs and ஸ
have no cited order yet. Their lessons say "copy what you see", as the other
uncited Tamil letter lessons do.

Each lesson practises the two letters before it. Chapter 112 closes with two
reviews that cover all fourteen letters, so every new atom gets at least two
later revisits.
