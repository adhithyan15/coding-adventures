# el proveedor — the one further up the line

## You'll want to know: el proveedor
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**el proveedor** — the supplier.

The company that supplies the shop. You rarely deal with one; you hear about one
when something has gone wrong: *un problema con nuestro proveedor*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-04] -->

**proveer**, to provide, plus **-dor**, the ending that makes a doer.

That ending is worth more to you than this one word, because it is completely
regular and it turns verbs you already hold into people and machines:

| verb | the doer |
|---|---|
| proveer | el proveedor |
| trabajar | el trabajador |
| vender | el vendedor |

Once you notice **-dor**, a long unfamiliar noun often decomposes into a verb
you know plus *the one who does it*. That is a reading strategy, not a word.
