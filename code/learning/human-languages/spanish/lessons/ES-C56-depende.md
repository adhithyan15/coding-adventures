# depende — the condition you do not have to finish

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have spent two chapters building conditions with both halves:
*si* plus a situation, then what follows. This is the word for when you want the
condition without saying what it is.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [supposing out loud](./ES-C56-sintesis-condiciones.md) — the real and unreal
  conditions this word stands in for.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- Three syllables, stress in the middle: *de-PEN-de*. →
  [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-DEPENDE]; assesses=[] -->

**depende** — "it depends." Latin *de-* ("down from") plus *pendere* ("to hang").
To depend on something is to **hang from** it, and the metaphor is still doing
work: the answer is suspended from a condition, and until the condition settles
the answer swings.

That *pendere* is a generous root. It gave English **pendant** (a thing that
hangs), **pending** (still hanging), **suspend**, and — by way of a hanging scale
pan — **pensive** and even **pound**, because things were weighed by hanging them.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SEMANTIC-CONDITION-UNSTATED]; assesses=[] -->

Every condition you have built so far names its own *si*. *Depende* is the one
that does not: it asserts that a condition exists and declines to state it,
which is why it is such a useful answer to a question you are not ready to
answer.

Add *de* and you can name it after all — *depende de ti*, "it depends on you."
The condition stops being a mystery and becomes a thing you point at.
