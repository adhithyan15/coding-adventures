Italian, chapter 417: Alla fermata.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la banchina — the platform; the quay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the parking meter, then the Italian for to stop briefly.

You'll want to know: la banchina.
la banchina — "the platform; the quay".
Aspettiamo sulla banchina del binario tre. We wait on the platform of track three.
Il binario is the track and its number; la banchina is where you stand. At a port it is the quay.

The word, taken apart.
banchina is a little banco 'bench, counter', which was borrowed from a Germanic word for a bench (EtymDB).

What you've built.

You'll want to know: la pensilina.
la pensilina — "the bus shelter, the platform canopy".
Ci siamo riparati sotto la pensilina. We took shelter under the bus shelter.
The roof over a bus stop or a platform.

The word, taken apart.
pensilina is built on pensile 'hanging', a learned borrowing from Latin pēnsilis 'hanging' (EtymDB), from pendēre 'to hang': a roof that hangs out over you.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: il capolinea.
il capolinea — "the terminus, the end of the line".
Scendo al capolinea. I get off at the terminus.
Plural i capolinea. Figuratively, siamo al capolinea — this is the end.

The word, taken apart.
capolinea is built from capo 'head, end', inherited from Latin caput 'head', and linea 'line', from Latin līnea 'linen thread' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
il pullman — the long-distance bus, the coach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bus shelter, then the Italian for the terminus.

You'll want to know: il pullman.
il pullman — "the long-distance bus, the coach".
Il pullman per il mare parte alle otto. The coach to the seaside leaves at eight.
For trips between towns; in town you take l'autobus.

The word, taken apart.
pullman was borrowed from English pullman, first the name of a kind of comfortable railway carriage; in Italian it moved to the road.

Lesson 5 of 5.
transitare — to pass through, to transit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the terminus, then the Italian for the long-distance bus.

You'll want to know: transitare.
transitare — "to pass through, to transit".
Il treno non ferma qui, transita soltanto. The train does not stop here, it only passes through.
On station boards: treno in transito — a train passing through.

The word, taken apart.
transitare is built on transito, a learned borrowing from Latin trānsitus 'a passing over', from trānsīre 'to go across', from trāns and īre 'to go' (Lewis & Short).
