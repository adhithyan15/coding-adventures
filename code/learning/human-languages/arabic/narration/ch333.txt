Arabic, chapter 333: Sailors and pirates.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
غرق (ghariqa) — to go under; to drown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be displaced, then the Arabic for to bid farewell.

You'll want to know: غرق.
غرق — ghariqa — "to go under; to drown".
غرقت السفينة قرب الجزيرة. ghariqat as-safīna qurba al-jazīra. The ship sank near the island.
Present: يغرق, yaghraq.

The word, taken apart.
غرق (ghariqa) is built on the native Arabic root gh-r-q (Lane), "to sink in water, to drown"; gharīq is one who has drowned.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to bid farewell, then the Arabic for to go under.

You'll want to know: بحار.
بحار — baḥḥār — "a sailor".
عمل جدي بحارا ثلاثين سنة. ʿamila jaddī baḥḥāran thalāthīn sana. My grandfather worked as a sailor for thirty years.
Plural: بحارة, baḥḥāra.

The word, taken apart.
بحار (baḥḥār) is built on the native Arabic root b-ḥ-r (Lane), the root of the taught baḥr, the sea. The faʿʿāl pattern names a trade: "the man of the sea".

Plural: قراصنة, qarāṣina.
Today also a computer hacker: قرصان (qurṣān) إنترنت.

The word, taken apart.
قرصان (qurṣān) is usually traced to Italian corsaro, "a corsair", the raider of the Mediterranean, from Latin cursus, a running.

Lesson 5 of 5.
أسر (asara) — to capture, to take prisoner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a fleet, then the Arabic for a pirate.

You'll want to know: أسر.
أسر — asara — "to capture, to take prisoner".
أسر القراصنة البحارة. asara al-qarāṣina al-baḥḥāra. The pirates took the sailors prisoner.
Present: يأسر, yaʾsir.
A prisoner of war is أسير. Of beauty: أسر (asara) قلبه, it captivated him.

The word, taken apart.
أسر (asara) is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-s-r (Lane), "to bind with a thong". Lane links the taught usra, a family, to it: the kin who bind a man and stand with him.
