Lesson 1 of 6.
huòshèng — to win a contest; to be victorious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do training (for a sport), then the Mandarin for to pass the ball.

You'll want to know: huòshèng.
huòshèng — "to win a contest; to be victorious".
wǒmen de qiúduì huòshèng le wǒ gǎnjué tèbié gāoxìng — Our team won, and I'm over the moon.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huòshèng is built from huò 'to get' and shèng 'victory', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hweak and syingH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to get' and the second 'victory and defeat' and 'to overcome'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
球赛 (qiúsài) — a ball game (a football or basketball match).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pass the ball, then the Mandarin for to win a contest.

You'll want to know: 球赛.
球赛 — qiúsài — "a ball game (a football or basketball match)".
这是我看过的最好的一场球赛 zhè shì wǒ kàn guo de zuì hǎo de yì chǎng qiúsài — This is the best match I've ever seen.
The new character, in pieces: 赛 (sài) — fourteen strokes in three pieces: the roof of 家 jiā on top, three strokes; in the middle seven strokes, two short bars, two verticals, a long bar and two strokes spreading left and right; at the foot four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
球赛 (qiúsài) is built from 球 'a ball' and 赛 (sài) 'a contest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjuw and sojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to repay'; the sense 'a contest' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 6.
赛 (sài) — a roof, a middle of seven strokes, a foot of four.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a roof, a middle of seven strokes, a foot of four and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — jiā, bǐ]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a roof, a middle of seven strokes, a foot of four.
赛 (sài).
Three pieces, top to bottom.
The roof, three strokes, as on top of 家 and 安: a dot, a short stroke down on the left, then a bar that turns down at the right end and hooks.
The middle, seven strokes. Two short bars, one under the other. Two verticals down through them. A long bar across. Then a stroke falling to the left and a stroke falling to the right, spreading out like legs.
The foot, four strokes: a vertical, a bar that turns down, a stroke falling to the left inside, and a dot.
3 + 7 + 4 = fourteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 赛 (sài), all fourteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 赛 (sài) have? (Three.) What is on top? (The roof of 家.) How many strokes at the foot? (Four.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 赛 (sài), pinned stroke paths and medians.
