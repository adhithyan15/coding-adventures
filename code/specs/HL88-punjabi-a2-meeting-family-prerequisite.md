# HL88 — Punjabi A2 meeting-family prerequisite

Issue: #17985

The Punjabi A2 mock uses `ਮਿਲਣਾ`, `ਮਿਲਦੇ`, and `ਮਿਲਿਆ` eleven times across
the learner paper and confidential examiner master. None has a direct owner in
the authored course. An earlier etymology note mentions `ਮਿਲਣਾ` while explaining
`ਮਿਲਾਂਗੇ`, but explanatory prose is not an instructional introduction.

Chapter 188 closes that dependency gap without editing either mock. The ramp
retrieves the familiar future phrase `ਫਿਰ ਮਿਲਾਂਗੇ`, directly teaches the
infinitive `ਮਿਲਣਾ`, and gives it a retrieval step. It then introduces habitual
plural `ਮਿਲਦੇ` in the familiar present frame `ਅਸੀਂ ਮਿਲਦੇ ਹਾਂ`, contrasts the
two forms, and retrieves the habitual form. Finally it introduces masculine
singular perfective `ਮਿਲਿਆ` in `ਮੈਂ ਅਮਨ ਨੂੰ ਮਿਲਿਆ`, contrasts all three forms,
and provides another retrieval before a four-skill payoff.

Each form has one direct owner and at least two later retrieval lessons. All
supporting Punjabi must occur before the chapter. The broader perfective family
(`ਕੀਤਾ`, `ਗਏ`, `ਦੱਸਿਆ`, and `ਬਦਲਿਆ`) remains out of scope, as do unrelated
high-frequency mock words. This is a prerequisite tranche, not mock-readiness
or attainment evidence. Authored and generated ownership remains sharded by
language, chapter, lesson, or test case; no shared aggregate is introduced.
