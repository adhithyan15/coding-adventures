# las siglas — no points, and a plural made by doubling

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the rule you learned for abbreviations: every one ends in a
point. This lesson gives you a kind of short form where that rule is reversed.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C426-05]; assesses=[] -->

**una sigla** — a set of initials standing for a name. *ONU*, *DNI*, *RENFE*.

Unlike an abbreviation, **a sigla takes no points at all**:

| correct | wrong |
|---|---|
| DNI | D.N.I. |
| ONU | O.N.U. |

The two you will actually meet on a form are **DNI** — *Documento Nacional de
Identidad*, the Spanish identity card — and **UE**, the European Union.

## Grammar Lens: the plural, which is the strange part
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-05, ES-ORTH-C426-03] -->

A sigla does **not** take an **-s** in writing. It is made plural by **doubling
its letters**, with a space in the middle:

| singular | plural | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| EU | **EE. UU.** | the United States |
| CC | **CC. OO.** | a trade union confederation |

*EE. UU.* is *Estados Unidos* — two words, so two doubled letters. Said aloud it
is the full name; the doubling exists only on the page.

One more distinction the names record. A **sigla** is spelled out letter by
letter (*DNI* is *de-ene-i*). An **acrónimo** is said as a word (*RENFE*, *ONU*).
Which one a set of initials becomes depends on nothing but whether the result is
pronounceable.
