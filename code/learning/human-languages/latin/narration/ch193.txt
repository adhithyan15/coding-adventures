Lesson 1 of 5.
tympanum, tympanī — a drum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an owl, then the Latin for a peacock.

You'll want to know: tympanum, tympanī.
tympanum, tympanī — "a drum".

The word, taken apart.
tympanum was borrowed from Greek tympanon, "a drum" (Lewis & Short), from typtō, "to strike, to beat" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
tālus, tālī — a knucklebone; an ankle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a peacock, then the Latin for a drum.

You'll want to know: tālus, tālī.
tālus, tālī — "a knucklebone; an ankle".

The word, taken apart.
tālus comes from an older taxlus (Lewis & Short), as its diminutive taxillus, "a small die", shows; EtymDB 2.1 gives Proto-Italic tākslos, "knucklebone", but the deeper origin is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tessera, tesserae — a gaming die; a token.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a drum, then the Latin for a knucklebone; an ankle.

You'll want to know: tessera, tesserae.
tessera, tesserae — "a gaming die; a token".

The word, taken apart.
tessera comes from Greek tesseres, "four" (Lewis & Short): a square piece, with four corners. English tessellated, "made of small square tiles", comes from its diminutive.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a knucklebone; an ankle, then the Latin for a gaming die; a token.

You'll want to know: armilla, armillae.
armilla, armillae — "a bracelet".

The word, taken apart.
armilla is built on armus, "the upper arm, the shoulder" (Lewis & Short), with -illa, a diminutive ending: a band for the arm.

Lesson 5 of 5.
monīle, monīlis — a necklace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a gaming die; a token, then the Latin for a bracelet.

You'll want to know: monīle, monīlis.
monīle, monīlis — "a necklace".

The word, taken apart.
monīle is usually linked with a Proto-Indo-European word mon-, "neck", seen in Old Irish muin, "neck"; English mane is a cousin.
