Telugu, chapter 61: In the Kitchen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
పెరుగు (perugu) — curd.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did pakṣi mean?

You'll want to know: పెరుగు.
పెరుగు (perugu) — "curd".
పెరుగు (perugu) is set curd: పాలు left overnight with a spoonful of yesterday's పెరుగు (perugu) stirred in. It closes almost every meal in a Telugu house, and a bhōjanaṁ without it is thought unfinished.
The word is inherited, and Telugu uses the same shape for what curd does and for what anything does when it grows. Curd is milk that grew.
A గేదె's పాలు set thicker than a cow's, which is one reason a village that keeps buffaloes is in no hurry to change.

What you've built.
The first thing in the kitchen.

Lesson 2 of 5.
నెయ్యి (neyyi) — ghee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did perugu mean?

You'll want to know: నెయ్యి.
నెయ్యి (neyyi) — "ghee".
నెయ్యి (neyyi) is clarified butter, and it is what పెరుగు (perugu) becomes after churning and heating. Three things out of one: పాలు, పెరుగు (perugu), నెయ్యి (neyyi).
The word is inherited and very old. Its root runs across the whole family for the same fat, which suits a food that has been made the same way for longer than any of these languages have been separate.
A spoonful of నెయ్యి (neyyi) over rice is both a taste and a courtesy. It is what a house puts in front of an atithi first.

What you've built.
Two, and the second is made out of the first.

Lesson 3 of 5.
నూనె (nūne) — oil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did neyyi mean?

You'll want to know: నూనె.
నూనె (nūne) — "oil".
నూనె (nūne) is oil pressed from seeds — groundnut, sesame, sunflower — and it is what a kitchen cooks with on the days it is not cooking with నెయ్యి (neyyi).
The word is inherited, and Telugu keeps it firmly apart from నెయ్యి (neyyi). One comes from an animal and one comes from a విత్తనం, and no cook mixes the two up.
నూనె (nūne) also works outside the kitchen. It is what goes into a దీపం, and a house that has run out of it sits in the dark.

What you've built.
Three.

Lesson 4 of 5.
చక్కెర (cakkera) — sugar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nūne mean?

You'll want to know: చక్కెర.
చక్కెర (cakkera) — "sugar".
చక్కెర (cakkera) is sugar, and the word is a traveller. Sanskrit śarkarā meant "grit, gravel" — the stuff arrived in grains — and the same word went west through Persian and Arabic and Latin until English got "sugar" out of it. The Telugu shape and the English one are cousins with a very long road between them.
Telugu also keeps బెల్లం, the unrefined block of palm or cane sugar, for everything traditional. చక్కెర (cakkera) is the white refined thing that came later.
A pinch of చక్కెర (cakkera) goes into dishes an English kitchen would leave savoury, and it goes in to round off the salt rather than to make anything sweet.

What you've built.
Four.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మిరప (mirapa) — a chilli.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did cakkera mean?

You'll want to know: మిరప.
మిరప (mirapa) — "a chilli".
మిరప (mirapa) is the chilli, and it is the newest thing in this kitchen by several thousand years. The plant crossed from the Americas on Portuguese ships and reached the coast about four hundred years ago.
The word is older than the plant it names. It belonged to black pepper first, and when the new pod arrived and took over the job of making food hot, the name went with the job rather than staying with the plant.
Andhra cooking is built on it now, and a kitchen there measures itself by how much మిరప (mirapa) it can carry. Five things, and the person at the stove will ask your పేరు before the meal is over.

What you've built.
Five: పెరుగు (perugu), నెయ్యి (neyyi), నూనె (nūne), చక్కెర (cakkera), మిరప (mirapa). Enough to name what a kitchen holds while somebody asks you your పేరు.
