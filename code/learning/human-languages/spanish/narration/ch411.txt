Spanish, chapter 411: Ice Cream.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
el helado — something frozen becomes a treat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall el hielo, ice. Its cold family leads to el helado, ice cream.

The word, taken apart.
Helado is the participle of helar, to freeze: its first sense is “frozen.” Used as a noun with el, it names ice cream. Hielo, helar and helado all continue the Latin cold-family already met in the ice lesson. The silent h links their modern spelling even though it makes no sound.

Grammar Lens: thing or description?
With an article, el helado is the food. After a noun, helado / helada can describe something frozen: agua helada. For the mock café message, listen for the noun un helado.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el helado]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un helado de chocolate]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: quiero un helado]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ice cream? (El helado.) One chocolate ice cream? (Un helado de chocolate.)
Source: DLE: helado.


Lesson 2 of 3.

Lesson 3 of 3.
helado recall two — complete the café exchange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Listen for the food after un: un helado.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
“What would you like?” — “A chocolate ice cream.”
[your turn — say: ¿Qué quieres? — Un helado de chocolate.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[your turn — say: Quiero un helado, por favor.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Give the café answer once more. (Un helado de chocolate.)
