# el césped — grass you look after

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C299-FARM-01, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el campo** and **plantar**. Somebody planted the grass on a
football pitch, and that is the whole difference.

## You'll want to know: el césped
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-03] -->

**el césped** — the lawn, and on a sports ground the pitch.

*Cortar el césped* — to mow the lawn. *No pisar el césped* — keep off the
grass, the wording on the sign in every park. *El césped está seco* — the
pitch is dry.

It has no everyday plural. Where English says *the lawns*, Spanish repeats the
singular or names the places instead.

## Grammar Lens: césped is not hierba
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-04, ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-03] -->

Both are grass. They answer different questions:

| the question | the word |
|---|---|
| what plant is that? | **la hierba** |
| what surface is that? | **el césped** |

*Hierba* is the thing growing; *césped* is the maintained expanse of it. So
grass coming up between the paving stones is **hierba**, and nobody would call
it *césped*. A mown lawn is **césped**, though it is made of *hierba*.

Sports tell the same story. *Jugar en el césped* is to play on the pitch, and
a pitch that has stopped being looked after has stopped being *césped*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-04, ES-LEX-C299-FARM-01] -->

Latin **caespes**, a cut sod of turf — a slab of earth with grass on top,
lifted with a spade for building banks and walls.

That is a good picture to hold, because it explains why *césped* is about the
surface rather than the plant. A *caespes* was a thing you could carry.

The written accent is doing real work. A Spanish word ending in a consonant
other than *n* or *s* takes its stress on the last syllable by default, so
*cesped* without a mark would be read **ces-PED**. The accent overrules the
default and pins the stress on **CÉS**-ped.
