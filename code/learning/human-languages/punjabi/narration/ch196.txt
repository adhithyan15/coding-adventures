Lesson 2 of 5.
One noun for a beginning or start — beginning or start.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve the familiar first and last words of the work instruction.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਸ਼ੁਰੂ (shuruu) — shurū — beginning; start.
Hear and say the whole word before reading it. Keep it as a whole word rather than inventing smaller Punjabi pieces. This lesson owns the word alone; the complete instruction comes only after a separate retrieval.

Across the family.
The Punjabi University dictionary records ਸ਼ੁਰੂ (shuruu) as a noun for a beginning or start. Its history reaches Punjabi from Arabic through Persian and Urdu. Keep that source history separate from present-day Punjabi use, and learn the Punjabi form as one whole. Sources: Punjabi University Punjabi-English Dictionary; Wiktionary: ਸ਼ੁਰੂ (shuruu).

[pause 3 seconds]
Produce the noun for “beginning” or “start” without looking.

Lesson 4 of 5.
Put the new word into one work instruction — start the work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve ਕੰਮ, then ਸ਼ੁਰੂ (shuruu), then ਕਰੋ.

Grammar Lens.
ਕੰਮ ਸ਼ੁਰੂ ਕਰੋ (kamm shuruu karo) — kamm shurū karo — start the work.
Keep the new noun whole between familiar ਕੰਮ and ਕਰੋ. Familiar ਕਰੋ carries the polite command in the already-owned noun-plus-ਕਰਨਾ pattern. Gill and Gleason describe such noun/adjective-plus-verb conjuncts as conventional combinations: this chapter teaches only this one instruction, not every possible combination, tense, or function. Source: Harjeet Singh Gill and Henry A. Gleason, Jr., A Reference Grammar of Punjabi (1969), pp. 122 and 124, conjunct verbs.
