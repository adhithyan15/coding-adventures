## Grammar Lens: three endings, three questions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-02, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-03] -->

| ending | the question it answers |
|---|---|
| **-dor** | who or what does it? |
| **-ería** | where is it kept? |
| **-ero** | whose job is it? |

All three land on **puerta** or a word like it and hand you a noun you never
learned. Do not read a rule into it — many words have no ending at all, and
*el garaje* and *la norma* in this very chapter came in whole from elsewhere.

What these three chapters have built is a **habit**: before treating a long word
as new, look for a piece you already hold.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-01, ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-04, ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-05] -->

**You could describe a home. You could not describe a building.** *La casa*,
*la puerta* and *el vecino* were yours; the hall, the person who cleans it, the
car park beneath it and the list of rules on its wall were not.

Those four are where written Spanish actually meets a resident — on a notice, in
a message from an administrator, in an instruction about a key. And **junto a**
is the small word that lets you say where any of it is.
