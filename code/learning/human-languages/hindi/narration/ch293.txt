Hindi, chapter 293: Cheats.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ठगना (ṭhagnā) — to cheat; to swindle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a gun, then the Hindi for to tie up.

You'll want to know: ठगना.
ठगना — ṭhagnā — "to cheat; to swindle".
अगर कोई तुम्हें ठग रहा है, तो पुलिस को बताओ। agar koī tumhẽ ṭhag rahā hai, to pulis ko batāo. If someone is cheating you, tell the police.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे ठगा, usne mujhe ṭhagā.

The word, taken apart.
ठगना (ṭhagnā) is inherited from ṭhagg, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5489), which also lists the noun ṭhag, a swindler. English thug was taken from that noun.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to tie up, then the Hindi for to cheat.

You'll want to know: धोखा.
धोखा — dhokhā — "a deception; a trick".
उस आदमी ने हमें धोखा दिया। us ādmī ne hamẽ dhokhā diyā. That man deceived us.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
धोखा (dhokhā) is inherited from dhrōkṣa, a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6894). धोखा देना is to deceive someone; धोखा खाना, to be taken in.

Lesson 3 of 5.
रिश्वत (riśvat) — a bribe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to cheat, then the Hindi for a deception.

You'll want to know: रिश्वत.
रिश्वत — riśvat — "a bribe".
अगर वह रिश्वत माँगेगा, तो मैं पुलिस को बताऊँगा। agar vah riśvat mā̃gegā, to maiṁ pulis ko batāūṅgā. If he asks for a bribe, I will tell the police.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
रिश्वत (riśvat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic rishwa, "a bribe". It is feminine: उसने रिश्वत माँगी, he asked for a bribe.

Lesson 4 of 5.
बदमाश (badmāś) — a crook; a rascal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a deception, then the Hindi for a bribe.

You'll want to know: बदमाश.
बदमाश — badmāś — "a crook; a rascal".
दो बदमाश बस में लोगों का पैसा छीन रहे थे। do badmāś bas meṁ logoṁ kā paisā chīn rahe the. Two crooks were snatching people's money on the bus.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
बदमाश (badmāś) is usually traced to Persian bad, "bad", and maʿāš, "a livelihood", a word Persian took from Arabic: someone who lives by bad means. Said fondly to a child, it means a little rascal.

Lesson 5 of 5.
नक़ली (naqlī) — fake; counterfeit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a bribe, then the Hindi for a crook.

You'll want to know: नक़ली.
नक़ली — naqlī — "fake; counterfeit".
उसने मुझे एक नक़ली नोट दिया। usne mujhe ek naqlī noṭ diyā. He gave me a fake banknote.

The word, taken apart.
नक़ली (naqlī) is naql, a copy, usually traced through Persian to Arabic naql, "a copying, a carrying over", with the adjective ending -ī: a copy passed off as the real thing.
