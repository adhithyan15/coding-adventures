Lesson 1 of 6.
zhìshǎo — at least.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an insight, then the Mandarin for ridiculous.

You'll want to know: zhìshǎo.
zhìshǎo — "at least".
nǐ zhìshǎo yīnggāi zhǔnshí lái — You should at least turn up on time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhìshǎo is built from zhì and 少 'few', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyijH and syewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to arrive' and the second 'not many'.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
máobìng is built from máo and 病 'an illness', both inherited from Middle Chinese, maw and bjaengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, quoting an older dictionary, 'the hair of the brows and the like', and the second 'worry' and 'pain'. How the pair came to mean a fault is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
了不起 (liǎobuqǐ) — amazing; remarkable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for at least, then the Mandarin for a bad habit.

You'll want to know: 了不起.
了不起 — liǎobuqǐ — "amazing; remarkable".
你真了不起 nǐ zhēn liǎobuqǐ — You're amazing.
The tone of bù is lost here: between the two parts it is said light, bu.

The word, taken apart.
了不起 (liǎobuqǐ) is built from 了, 不 'not' and 起 'to rise', all three inherited from Middle Chinese, lewX, pjut and khiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'clever' and 'finished' and the third 'to rise'. How the three came to mean remarkable is not settled.

What you've built.
