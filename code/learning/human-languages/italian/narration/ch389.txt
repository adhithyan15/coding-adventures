Italian, chapter 389: La burocrazia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la burocrazia — the bureaucracy, the red tape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the renewal, then the Italian for the card.

You'll want to know: la burocrazia.
la burocrazia — "the bureaucracy, the red tape".
Per aprire un negozio c'è troppa burocrazia. To open a shop there is too much red tape.
Stress on -zìa.

The word, taken apart.
burocrazia was borrowed from French bureaucratie, built from bureau "desk, office" and -cratie "rule of" (EtymDB), a Greek ending, from krátos "power": rule by the desk.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la pratica — the paperwork, the case (being handled); the practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the card, then the Italian for the bureaucracy.

You'll want to know: la pratica.
la pratica — "the paperwork, the case (being handled); the practice".
La mia pratica è ancora in comune. My paperwork is still at the town hall.
In pratica — in practice, basically. Fare pratica — to get experience.

The word, taken apart.
pratica is a learned borrowing from Late Latin practica, related to practicus "active" (Lewis & Short), from Greek praktikḗ "practical knowledge", from prássein "to do, to act"; the office sense is a matter being dealt with.

The word, taken apart.
procedura came from French procédure, built on procéder, from Latin prōcēdere "to go forward" (Lewis & Short), built on cēdere "to go".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la richiesta — the request, the application.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the paperwork, then the Italian for the procedure.

You'll want to know: la richiesta.
la richiesta — "the request, the application".
Ho fatto richiesta di un nuovo documento. I have applied for a new document.
Su richiesta — on request. Fare richiesta di — to apply for.

The word, taken apart.
richiesta is built on richiedere "to request", from ri- and chiedere "to ask", which is inherited from Latin quaerere "to seek, to ask" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il certificato — the certificate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the procedure, then the Italian for the request.

You'll want to know: il certificato.
il certificato — "the certificate".
Se sei malato, devi portare un certificato medico. If you are ill, you have to bring a medical certificate.
Il certificato di nascita — the birth certificate.

The word, taken apart.
certificato is built on certificare "to certify", a learned borrowing from Late Latin certificāre "to make certain", built from certus "sure" and facere "to make" (Lewis & Short).
