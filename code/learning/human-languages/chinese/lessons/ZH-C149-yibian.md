## You'll want to know: 一边
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C149-YIBIAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**一边** — *yìbiān* — "at the same time", said twice, before two verbs; also "one side".

> **他一边吃饭一边看书** *tā yìbiān chī fàn yìbiān kàn shū* — He reads while he eats.

一 falls before the level tone of biān: *yìbiān*, as in *yì tiān*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**一边** (*yìbiān*) is built from 一 'one' and 边 'side', the 边 you write. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *'jit* and *pen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), *pen* glossed 'an edge; a border'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-ZAI-PROGRESSIVE-01] -->

Said twice, each straight before its own verb, 一边 … 一边 … puts two actions side by side: 一边吃饭, 一边看书.

The person is said once, at the front: 他一边吃饭一边看书.
