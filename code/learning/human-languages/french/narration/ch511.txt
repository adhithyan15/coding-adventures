French, chapter 511: Instruments de musique.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la flûte — flute; recorder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for ice rink, then the French for slope.

You'll want to know: la flûte.
la flûte — "flute; recorder".
Au collège, tous les élèves apprennent la flûte. At secondary school all the pupils learn the recorder.
La flûte à bec — the recorder; la flûte traversière — the flute. Also a tall champagne glass.

The word, taken apart.
la flûte goes back to Old French flaüte or fleute, probably from Old Occitan flaüt (EtymDB). The origin is not settled; it may imitate the sound.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: le violoncelle.
le violoncelle — "cello".
Ma sœur joue du violoncelle depuis l'âge de huit ans. My sister has played the cello since she was eight.
Jouer du violoncelle. The player is le or la violoncelliste.

The word, taken apart.
le violoncelle was borrowed from Italian violoncello, a small form of violone "a large viol", itself built on viola.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'accordéon — accordion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for cello, then the French for trumpet.

You'll want to know: l'accordéon.
l'accordéon — "accordion".
Au bal du village, un vieil homme jouait de l'accordéon. At the village dance an old man was playing the accordion.
Masculine. A typical sound of the French bal musette.

The word, taken apart.
l'accordéon was borrowed from German Akkordion (EtymDB), built on Akkord "chord", which itself was borrowed from French accord; accord goes back to Latin cor "heart" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
accorder — to tune (an instrument); to grant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for trumpet, then the French for accordion.

You'll want to know: accorder.
accorder — "to tune (an instrument); to grant".
Avant le concert, le guitariste accorde sa guitare. Before the concert the guitarist tunes his guitar.
Also to grant: accorder une pause. S'accorder — to agree.

The word, taken apart.
accorder is inherited from Vulgar Latin accordāre "to bring into agreement" (EtymDB), built on Latin cor "heart" (Lewis & Short): to make hearts agree.
