Mandarin Chinese, chapter 125: Angry and Worried.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
生气 (shēngqì) — to get angry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for ugly, then the Mandarin for to resemble.

You'll want to know: 生气.
生气 — shēngqì — "to get angry".
因为我没有来他生气了 yīnwèi wǒ méiyǒu lái tā shēngqì le — He got angry because I didn't come.

The word, taken apart.
生气 (shēngqì) is built from 生 'to grow' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The sense angry is not given there, so how the pair came to it is not settled.

You'll want to know: dānxīn.
dānxīn — "to worry".
nǐ bú yào dānxīn — Don't you worry.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dānxīn is built from dān 'to carry on the shoulder' and xīn 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tam and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The pair is not given there; a load carried in the heart is the plain picture, but the step is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
fàngxīn — to stop worrying; to be at ease.

You'll want to know: fàngxīn.
fàngxīn — "to stop worrying; to be at ease".
nǐ fàngxīn wǒ hěn hǎo — Don't worry, I'm fine.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàngxīn is built from fàng, the word for putting things down, and xīn 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjangH and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to drive out' and 'to send away'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zháojí — anxious; in a hurry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to worry, then the Mandarin for to be at ease.

You'll want to know: zháojí.
zháojí — "anxious; in a hurry".
wǒ hěn zháojí yīnwèi wǒ yǒushì — I'm in a rush, because I've got something on.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zháojí is built from zháo and jí, both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjak and kip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to attach to' and the second 'urgent' and 'narrow'; how the pair came to mean anxious is not settled.

The word, taken apart.
nánguò is built from nán 'hard' and guò 'to pass', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nan and kwaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): hard to get through. The pair is not given there.
