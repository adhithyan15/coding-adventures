Latin, chapter 526: Healing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sānō, sānāre — to heal, cure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for bleary-eyed, then the Latin for inflammation of the eyes.

You'll want to know: sānō, sānāre.
sānō, sānāre — "to heal, cure".
Medicus vulnus mīlitis sānat. The doctor heals the soldier's wound.
sānō, sānāre, sānāvī, sānātum, from sānus. English sanatorium.

The word, taken apart.
sānō is built on sānus "sound, healthy" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English sanatorium comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
medeor, medērī — to remedy, be good for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for inflammation of the eyes, then the Latin for to heal.

You'll want to know: medeor, medērī.
medeor, medērī — "to remedy, be good for".
Haec herba morbō medētur. This herb is good for the disease.
Deponent, with the dative: morbō medērī. No perfect in use. Medicus, medicīna and remedium come from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
medeor is inherited through Proto-Italic from a Proto-Indo-European root med- "to measure, take care" (EtymDB). medicus, medicīna and remedium belong to it, and so English medicine and remedy.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: convalēscō, convalēscere.
convalēscō, convalēscere — "to recover, get well".
Post longum morbum avus convalēscit. After a long illness grandfather is getting well.
convalēscō, convalēscere, convaluī, built on valeō — to be well. English convalesce.

The word, taken apart.
convalēscō is built on con- and valēscō, the inceptive of valeō "to be well, strong" (Lewis & Short). English convalesce comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
recreō, recreāre — to refresh, restore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to remedy, then the Latin for to recover.

You'll want to know: recreō, recreāre.
recreō, recreāre — "to refresh, restore".
Somnus corpus fessum recreat. Sleep refreshes the tired body.
recreō, recreāre, recreāvī, recreātum: re- 'again' + creō 'to make'. English recreation.

The word, taken apart.
recreō is built on re- "again" and creō "to create" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): to make new again. English recreation comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pōtiō, pōtiōnis — a draught, drink; a potion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to recover, then the Latin for to refresh.

You'll want to know: pōtiō, pōtiōnis.
pōtiō, pōtiōnis — "a draught, drink; a potion".
Medicus aegrō pōtiōnem amāram dat. The doctor gives the patient a bitter draught.
Feminine, third declension, from the root of pōtō — to drink. English potion, and poison too.

The word, taken apart.
pōtiō is built on the root of pōtō "to drink" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English potion comes from it, and poison too, through Old French.
