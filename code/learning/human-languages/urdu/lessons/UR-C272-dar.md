# ڈر (ḍar) — fear

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C268-LAGNA-01, UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "I'm hungry". (*mujhe bhūk lagī hai*.) Fear is said the same way.

## You'll want to know: ڈر
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-C272-DAR-01]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C268-LAGNA-01, UR-LEX-C67-NATURE67-02, UR-GRAMMAR-C264-PAST-01] -->

**ڈر** — *ḍar* — "fear". It is masculine.

> **مجھے ڈر لگتا ہے۔**
>
> *mujhe ḍar lagtā hai.* — "I'm afraid."

Fear strikes you, as hunger does: **مجھے**, and **لگتا** (*lagtā*) for a masculine **ڈر**. What you fear takes **سے** (*se*):

> **مجھے کتوں سے ڈر لگتا ہے۔**
>
> *mujhe kuttoṅ se ḍar lagtā hai.* — "I'm afraid of dogs."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ڈر** (*ḍar*) is inherited. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit *dara*, "fear" (CDIAL 6186), and lists the Hindi form *ḍar*, with the curled-back *ḍ*. Its first letter is new to your hand; the next lesson takes it out.
