Lesson 1 of 7.
后天 (hòutiān) — the day after tomorrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A new chapter, and it opens with a day that has not come yet.
[your turn — recall: say píngshí, usually — R1, the chapter you have finished]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 明天 — míngtiān, tomorrow — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say hòu, after; behind — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 后天.
后天 — hòutiān — "the day after tomorrow".
后天我们去 hòutiān wǒmen qù — We're going the day after tomorrow.
今天 明天 后天 jīntiān, míngtiān, hòutiān — today, tomorrow, the day after.
The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. It is the hòu you already say for behind and after.

The word, taken apart.
后天 (hòutiān) is built from 后 (hòu) 'after' and 天 'day'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, huwX and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun lists huwX under the traditional form of the character and glosses it 'before and after'; it glosses then 'the heavens above', so 'day' is assumed here, not quoted.

What you've built.
后天 (hòutiān) takes its place in the row of days you already say: zuótiān, 今天, 明天, 后天 (hòutiān).
Like the others it goes early in the sentence, before the verb: 后天我们去 hòutiān wǒmen qù.

Script — a three-stroke frame, then 口.
后 (hòu).
Two pieces, and the second you already write.
The frame, three strokes: a short left-falling stroke along the top, from right to left; a long left-falling stroke down the left side, curving out to the lower left; then a horizontal across the middle, from the long stroke to the right.
口, three strokes, under that horizontal, on the right: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the bottom bar.
3 + 3 = six.

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 3 of 7.
明天会下雨 (míngtiān huì xiàyǔ) — 会 for what will happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A word you already know does a second job in this lesson.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 后天, aloud — hòutiān]
[your turn — recall: say 我会开车 — wǒ huì kāichē, I can drive]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 下雨 — xiàyǔ, to rain]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 会 before a verb, for what is going to happen.
会 before a verb can say what will probably happen, or what someone will do.
明天会下雨 míngtiān huì xiàyǔ — It'll rain tomorrow. 后天不会下雨 hòutiān bú huì xiàyǔ — It won't rain the day after tomorrow.
不 before the fall of 会 is bú, as in bú qù.
You met 会 as can, for something learned: 我会开车. It is the same character in a different use. The rain has learned nothing, so 明天会下雨 (míngtiān huì xiàyǔ) is a forecast, not a skill; a time word in front usually tells you which one you hear.
You have said the future without 会 for a long time: 明天我们去 míngtiān wǒmen qù needs no extra word. 会 adds a forecast, what you expect to happen:
明天我会去 míngtiān wǒ huì qù — I'll go tomorrow.

Lesson 4 of 7.
kěnéng — maybe; possibly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read the last lesson's sentence, then a word that shares a sound with this one.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 明天会下雨, aloud — míngtiān huì xiàyǔ]
[your turn — recall: say kěyǐ, may — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: kěnéng.
kěnéng — "maybe; possibly".
tā kěnéng bù lái — He may not come.
míngtiān kěnéng huì xiàyǔ — It might rain tomorrow.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kěnéng is built from kě 'may', the kě of kěyǐ, and 能 'can', the 能 you write. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, khaX and nong (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to permit' and 'skilled'. 'Maybe' is the pair's own sense, which neither gloss gives.

What you've built.
kěnéng goes before the verb, or before 会: kěnéng huì xiàyǔ, it might rain.
With 会 alone the forecast sounds sure; with kěnéng in front it is only a chance.

You'll want to know: yídìng.
yídìng — "certainly; definitely".
tā yídìng huì lái — He'll definitely come.
一 rises before the fall of dìng: yídìng, as in yí ge.
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yídìng is built from 一 'one' and dìng 'settled'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit and dengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), dengH glossed 'calm; settled'. 'Certainly' is the pair's own sense, not the gloss's.

What you've built.
kěnéng and yídìng take the same place, before 会 or the verb: a chance, then a certainty.
tā kěnéng huì lái — he may come.
tā yídìng huì lái — he will, for sure.

Lesson 6 of 7.
tiānqì yùbào — weather forecast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, then the weather itself.
[your turn — recall: say yídìng, certainly — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 天气 — tiānqì, the weather — R3, durable]

You'll want to know: tiānqì yùbào.
tiānqì yùbào — "weather forecast".
tiānqì yùbào shuō míngtiān huì xiàyǔ — The forecast says it'll rain tomorrow.
Its last two characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiānqì yùbào is built from 天气 'weather' and yùbào. yùbào is built from yù and bào, inherited from Middle Chinese yoH and pawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'beforehand' and 'to report'. A report given beforehand is the plain reading of the two; 'forecast' for the whole is the modern compound's own sense.

What you've built.
tiānqì yùbào shuō …, the forecast says, and then the forecast itself, with 会:
tiānqì yùbào shuō hòutiān bú huì xiàyǔ — The forecast says it won't rain the day after tomorrow.

Lesson 7 of 7.
明天会下雨吗 — 后天不会 (míngtiān huì xiàyǔ ma — hòutiān bú huì).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The chapter closes here, with two people planning around the rain.
[your turn — recall: say tiānqì yùbào, the weather forecast — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say yídìng, certainly — R1]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say kěnéng, maybe — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange.
A: 明天会下雨吗 míngtiān huì xiàyǔ ma — Will it rain tomorrow? B: huì, tiānqì yùbào shuō huì xiàyǔ — It will. The forecast says so. A: 后天呢 hòutiān ne — And the day after? B: 后天不会 hòutiān bú huì — Not the day after. A: 我们后天去 wǒmen hòutiān qù — Then we'll go the day after tomorrow. B: 他会来吗 tā huì lái ma — Will he come? A: tā kěnéng bù lái — He may not. B: wǒ yídìng lái — I'll come, for sure.
会 answers a question about 会 on its own: míngtiān huì xiàyǔ ma? — huì. The answer drops everything else, as 是 and 有 do.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which day will it not rain?

What you've built — a forecast, a chance, a certainty.
Three ways to talk about what is coming, all before the verb:
明天会下雨 míngtiān huì xiàyǔ — it will rain tomorrow.
míngtiān kěnéng huì xiàyǔ — it might.
tā yídìng huì lái — he certainly will come.
Listen. Hòutiān huì xiàyǔ ma?
Speak. Say what the weather will do tomorrow, then what you will do the day after.
