Japanese, chapter 303: The Dragon in the Marsh.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りゅう (ryū) — a dragon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a legend, then the Japanese for a god.

You'll want to know: りゅう.
りゅう — ryū — "a dragon".
むかし、りゅうが そらを とんでいました。 — mukashi, ryū ga sora o tonde imashita — "Long ago a dragon flew in the sky."

The word, taken apart.
りゅう (ryū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): one Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese. It is ryū, "dragon" (Middle Chinese ljowng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

The word, taken apart.
ぬま (numa) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

うかぶ — ukabu — "to float up; to come to mind".
