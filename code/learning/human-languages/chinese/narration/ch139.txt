Mandarin Chinese, chapter 139: Colours and Shapes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lǜ — green.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for every single, then the Mandarin for the others.

You'll want to know: lǜ.
lǜ — "green".
chūntiān de cǎo hěn lǜ — In spring the grass is very green.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lǜ is inherited from Middle Chinese ljowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the colour of blue and yellow'. The regular sound change would give lù; why the everyday word is lǜ is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 3 of 5.
huī — grey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for green, then the Mandarin for yellow.

You'll want to know: huī.
huī — "grey".
wǒ de māo shì huī de — My cat is a grey one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huī is inherited from Middle Chinese xwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older book: 'dead fire', that is, ash. The colour of ash is the likely step to grey, though it is not given there.

The word, taken apart.
yuán is inherited from Middle Chinese hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the form of heaven'. The plain sense round is not among the glosses, so the step is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fěnhóng — pink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for grey, then the Mandarin for round.

You'll want to know: fěnhóng.
fěnhóng — "pink".
tā de màozi shì fěnhóng de — Her hat is a pink one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fěnhóng is built from fěn 'powder' and hóng 'red', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjunX and huwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): red as pale as face powder. The dictionary glosses the first with a powder made by burning lead.
