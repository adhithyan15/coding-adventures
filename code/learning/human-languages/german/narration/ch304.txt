German, chapter 304: Die Trauer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Trauer — the grief, mourning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the coffin, then the German for the cemetery.

You'll want to know: die Trauer.
die Trauer — "the grief, mourning".
Die Trauer in der Familie war groß. The grief in the family was great.
No plural. Traurig, already known, is related.

The word, taken apart.
Trauer comes from Middle High German trūre "mourning", from Old High German trūrēn "to mourn" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
das Beileid — the condolences, sympathy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the grief, then the German for to mourn.

You'll want to know: das Beileid.
das Beileid — "the condolences, sympathy".
Wir sprachen der Familie unser Beileid aus. We offered our condolences to the family.
Herzliches Beileid — my sincere condolences.

The word, taken apart.
Beileid joins bei "with" and Leid "sorrow", an inherited Germanic word whose English cousin is loath (EtymDB): sorrow shared with someone.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Witwe — the widow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to mourn, then the German for the condolences.

You'll want to know: die Witwe.
die Witwe — "the widow".
Die Witwe wohnte noch viele Jahre im Haus. The widow lived in the house for many more years.
Plural: die Witwen. A man is der Witwer.

The word, taken apart.
Witwe is inherited from Old High German wituwa, from Proto-Germanic widuwōn "widow" (EtymDB), the same source as English widow.
