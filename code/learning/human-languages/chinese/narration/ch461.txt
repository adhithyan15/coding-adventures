Lesson 1 of 6.
pèifú — to think highly of; to hold in high regard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sympathize with, then the Mandarin for to envy.

You'll want to know: pèifú.
pèifú — "to think highly of; to hold in high regard".
wǒ zuì pèifú wǒ de lǎoshī — The person I admire most is my teacher.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pèifú is built from pèi 'to wear at the belt' and fú 'to serve', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bwojH and bjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as jade worn at the belt and the second 'to serve' and 'clothes'. The sense 'to admire' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
爱心 (àixīn) — compassion; love and care for others.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to envy, then the Mandarin for to hold in high regard.

You'll want to know: 爱心.
爱心 — àixīn — "compassion; love and care for others".
nàge lùrén hěn yǒu àixīn wǒ hěn gǎnjī tā — That passer-by was so kind, and I'm very grateful to him.
The new character, in pieces: 爱 (ài) — ten strokes in three pieces: at the top a short stroke falling to the left, two dots and a stroke falling to the left, like a hand reaching down; a cover of two strokes, a short stroke down and a bar that turns down with a hook; and under it a bar, a long stroke falling to the left, and 又 yòu. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
爱心 (àixīn) is built from ài 'to love' and 心 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'ojH and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to cherish'.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Script — a hand on top, a cover, and 又 under them.
爱 (ài).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top, four strokes. A short stroke from the right falling to the left, a dot, a second dot, then a short stroke falling to the left on the right: a hand reaching down.
The cover, two strokes. A short stroke down on the left, then a bar that runs right, turns down and hooks.
The bottom, four strokes. A bar across, a long stroke falling to the left from above the bar, then 又: a bar that turns into a stroke falling to the left, and a long stroke falling to the right.
4 + 2 + 4 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 爱 (ài), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 爱 (ài) have? (Three: four strokes, two and four.) Which written character ends it? (又.)

爱情 — àiqíng — "romantic love; love between a couple".
zhè shì wǒ kàn guo de zuì hǎo de àiqíng diànyǐng — This is the best love film I've ever seen.

Lesson 6 of 6.
pànwàng — to look forward to; to long for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for love between a couple, then the Mandarin for treasured.

You'll want to know: pànwàng.
pànwàng — "to look forward to; to long for".
háizi dōu pànwàng guònián — All the children are looking forward to the new year.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pànwàng is built from pàn and wàng 'to look toward', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pheanH and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as beautiful eyes and the second 'to look toward', after an older book a person far away hoping to come home; the sense 'to look forward to' for the first is not given there.
