French, chapter 413: Sur le quai.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la voie — the track, the platform number; the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the flat-rate pass, then the French for to validate.

You'll want to know: la voie.
la voie — "the track, the platform number; the way".
Le train de sept heures part de la voie six. The seven o'clock train leaves from platform six.
La voie is the track with its number; le quai is where you stand.

The word, taken apart.
voie is inherited through Old French voie from Latin via 'road, way' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
composter — to stamp (a ticket).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to validate, then the French for the track.

You'll want to know: composter.
composter — "to stamp (a ticket)".
Avant de monter, il a composté son billet. Before getting on, he stamped his ticket.
Paper tickets were stamped in a small machine on the platform; in Switzerland, oblitérer is also heard.

The word, taken apart.
composter is built on composteur, a stamping machine, which is usually traced to Italian compositore 'typesetter', from Latin compōnere 'to put together'. EtymDB lists the verb without a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le contrôleur — the ticket inspector.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the track, then the French for to stamp.

You'll want to know: le contrôleur.
le contrôleur — "the ticket inspector".
Le contrôleur passe dans le train et vérifie les billets. The inspector goes through the train and checks the tickets.
Feminine: la contrôleuse.

The word, taken apart.
contrôleur comes from Old French contrerollour (EtymDB), built on contrerole 'a second register for checking', from Medieval Latin contrārotulum.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le wagon — the carriage (of a train).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to stamp, then the French for the ticket inspector.

You'll want to know: le wagon.
le wagon — "the carriage (of a train)".
Notre place est dans le wagon numéro douze. Our seat is in carriage number twelve.
Rail staff often say la voiture; most people say le wagon. Le wagon-restaurant — the dining car.

The word, taken apart.
wagon was borrowed from English wagon, which came from Dutch wagen (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
desservir — to stop at, to serve (a place); to clear away (after a meal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the ticket inspector, then the French for the carriage.

You'll want to know: desservir.
desservir — "to stop at, to serve (a place); to clear away (after a meal)".
Ce train dessert tous les villages de la vallée. This train serves all the villages in the valley.
Also desservir after a meal — to clear away the dishes.

The word, taken apart.
desservir is built from des- and servir 'to serve', which is inherited from Latin servīre (EtymDB).
