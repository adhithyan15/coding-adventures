Spanish, chapter 531: La búsqueda.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
desaparecido — missing; disappeared.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the attack, then the Spanish for to attack.

You'll want to know: desaparecido.
desaparecido — "missing; disappeared".
El niño estuvo desaparecido dos días. The boy was missing for two days.
Un desaparecido is a missing person.

The word, taken apart.
desaparecido is built on the participle of desaparecer 'to disappear', from des- and aparecer, which comes from Latin appārēre 'to appear' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
localizar — to locate, to find; to reach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for missing, then the Spanish for the search.

You'll want to know: localizar.
localizar — "to locate, to find; to reach".
Por fin localizaron el coche. At last they located the car.
It also means to reach someone by phone: No te pude localizar.

The word, taken apart.
localizar is built on local, from Latin locālis 'of a place' (Lewis & Short), from locus 'a place', with the ending -izar.

Lesson 4 of 5.
hallar — to find.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the search, then the Spanish for to locate.

You'll want to know: hallar.
hallar — "to find".
Hallaron al niño en el bosque. They found the boy in the woods.
More formal than encontrar; common in the news.

The word, taken apart.
hallar is inherited from Latin afflāre 'to blow, to breathe on' (EtymDB), through old Spanish fallar, a hunting word: the dog sniffs and finds.
