French, chapter 441: Dans la rue.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le banc — the bench.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the police station, then the French for the barracks.

You'll want to know: le banc.
le banc — "the bench".
Deux vieux messieurs lisent sur un banc. Two old men are reading on a bench.
Un banc de poissons — a shoal of fish.

The word, taken apart.
banc comes from a Germanic word for 'bench' (EtymDB), and English bench is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
le lampadaire — the street lamp; the floor lamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the barracks, then the French for the bench.

You'll want to know: le lampadaire.
le lampadaire — "the street lamp; the floor lamp".
Les lampadaires s'allument à six heures. The street lamps come on at six o'clock.
At home, un lampadaire is a standing lamp.

The word, taken apart.
lampadaire is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin lampadārium 'candlestick', from lampas, from Greek lampás 'torch' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le réverbère — the lamp post (old-style).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the bench, then the French for the street lamp.

You'll want to know: le réverbère.
le réverbère — "the lamp post (old-style)".
Dans la vieille rue, il reste quelques réverbères. In the old street a few old lamp posts are still standing.
Older word, often for old-fashioned lamps.

The word, taken apart.
réverbère is built on réverbérer 'to reflect', a learned borrowing from Latin reverberāre 'to beat back', from verber 'whip' (Lewis & Short): the old lamps had a mirror that threw back the light.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le kiosque — the kiosk, the newsstand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the street lamp, then the French for the lamp post.

You'll want to know: le kiosque.
le kiosque — "the kiosk, the newsstand".
Il achète le journal au kiosque du coin. He buys the paper at the kiosk on the corner.
Un kiosque à musique — a bandstand.

The word, taken apart.
kiosque was borrowed from Turkish köşk 'pavilion', which came from Persian kušk 'palace, portico' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le pavé — the cobblestone; the paving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the lamp post, then the French for the kiosk.

You'll want to know: le pavé.
le pavé — "the cobblestone; the paving".
Les vélos roulent mal sur les pavés. Bikes do not ride well on the cobbles.
Un pavé is also a very thick book or a thick steak.

The word, taken apart.
pavé is the past participle of paver 'to pave' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin pavīre 'to beat the earth flat' (Lewis & Short).
