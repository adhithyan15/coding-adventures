### The fourteen split cleanly in two, so the lessons did too

**Eleven are the first meeting**: the m→v softening, *mājhe nāv āhe*, possessive
respect, *ānand*, plural-respect agreement, the adjective that takes the
speaker's gender, the copula's person forms, *mī barā āhe*, *kāhī harkat nāhī*,
the *we* that lives in the verb, and *udyā bheṭū*. Every one of them is a way the
same verb — **आहे** — reshapes itself around whoever is speaking. Chapters 2-4
taught them one at a time; nothing since had asked for them together.

So `MR-R11` retrieves them as **one exchange**, which is how a learner will
actually meet them: give your name, ask for a name, ask after someone, answer,
wave off the trouble, arrange tomorrow. The title is the claim the lesson tests
— that the verb, not the pronoun, is carrying the person.

**Three are script**: फ, the daṇḍa, and the independent औ, taught in chapters 64
and 65 near the end of the writing runways with too little book left behind them
to be asked for again. `MR-R65` is the cold retrieval that gives them their
second and third look. It also names the thing a writing lesson could not:
**फ's sound has drifted** away from the aspirated stop its row predicts, which
is exactly the kind of fact that survives only if it is retrieved.

### Two deferrals, bought on a misread title

Marathi was passed over twice in this programme on the strength of a test
**name**:

> `gives the five Chapter 14 family atoms genuine R1, R2, and R3 retrieval`

That reads like a position pin. The body is a set-emptiness assertion over five
named atoms — and a `review` lesson can only give those atoms *more* revisits,
so it can never break it. Marathi has no index pin and no whole-track missed
total; it was as cheap as bengali was. **Two rounds of deferral bought nothing.**
Read the body, not the title.

### What the chapter-size pin actually measures

Both chapters grow by one lesson (11: 6 → 7, 65: 5 → 6) in a test named *keeps
Marathi's opening script runways below the chapter atom budget*. The name is
about atoms; the assertion counts **lessons**. Both new lessons introduce none,
so the budget the test exists to defend is untouched — but the number it pins
moves anyway, which is worth a comment in the file rather than a silent edit.
