French, chapter 408: Contrôles sur la route.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la contravention — the fine (for a driving offence), the ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the parallel parking space, then the French for the car pound.

You'll want to know: la contravention.
la contravention — "the fine (for a driving offence), the ticket".
Il a reçu une contravention pour excès de vitesse. He got a ticket for speeding.
Informally un PV, short for procès-verbal.

The word, taken apart.
contravention is a learned formation on Late Latin contrāvenīre 'to oppose, to go against', from contrā 'against' and venīre 'to come' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le radar — the speed camera; the radar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the car pound, then the French for the fine.

You'll want to know: le radar.
le radar — "the speed camera; the radar".
Il y a un radar à l'entrée du village. There is a speed camera at the edge of the village.
Un radar fixe stays in one place; un radar mobile moves.

The word, taken apart.
radar was borrowed from English radar, made from the first letters of radio detection and ranging. EtymDB lists the word without a source line.

Lesson 3 of 5.
flasher — to flash (of a speed camera), to catch speeding.

Before the new one: say the French for the fine, then the French for the speed camera.

You'll want to know: flasher.
flasher — "to flash (of a speed camera), to catch speeding".
Je me suis fait flasher à quatre-vingt-dix. I got caught by a speed camera at ninety.
Informal; mostly se faire flasher.

The word, taken apart.
flasher is built on flash, which was borrowed from English flash (EtymDB): the camera flashes when you go too fast.

Lesson 4 of 5.
verbaliser — to fine, to write up (a driver).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for the speed camera, then the French for to flash.

You'll want to know: verbaliser.
verbaliser — "to fine, to write up (a driver)".
Le policier a verbalisé le conducteur. The police officer fined the driver.
Formal and official.

The word, taken apart.
verbaliser is built on verbal, as in procès-verbal 'official written report', a learned word from Late Latin verbālis, from verbum 'word' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
contrôler — to inspect, to check; to control.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to flash, then the French for to fine.

You'll want to know: contrôler.
contrôler — "to inspect, to check; to control".
La police contrôle les papiers des automobilistes. The police check the drivers' papers.
Un contrôle — a check.

The word, taken apart.
contrôler comes from Old French contrerole 'a second register kept to check the first', from Medieval Latin contrārotulum (EtymDB), built on rotulus 'roll', a small rota 'wheel'.
