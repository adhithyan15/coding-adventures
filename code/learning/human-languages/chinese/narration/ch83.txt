Mandarin Chinese, chapter 83: Running Late.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shíjiān — time; free time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the middle, then the Mandarin for opposite.

You'll want to know: shíjiān.
shíjiān — "time; free time".
nǐ míngtiān yǒu shíjiān ma — Are you free tomorrow?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shíjiān is built from shí 'time' and jiān 'a gap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyi and kean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first with another word for a time, and lists the second under an older form of the character.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 2 of 5.
早 (zǎo) — early.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for opposite, then the Mandarin for time to spare.

You'll want to know: 早.
早 — zǎo — "early".
明天的火车很早 míngtiān de huǒchē hěn zǎo — Tomorrow's train leaves early.

The word, taken apart.
早 (zǎo) is inherited from Middle Chinese tsawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'morning'. Early is not given there; from morning to early is the likely step, but it is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
马上 (mǎshàng) — at once; right away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for time to spare, then the Mandarin for early.

You'll want to know: 马上.
马上 — mǎshàng — "at once; right away".
家里人马上来 jiā lǐ rén mǎshàng lái — The family will be here any minute.

The word, taken apart.
马上 (mǎshàng) is built from 马 'horse' and 上 'on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, maeX and dzyangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'On horseback', so already on the way, is the usual explanation, but it is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
láibují — to run out of time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for early, then the Mandarin for right away.

You'll want to know: láibují.
láibují — "to run out of time".
xiànzài qù láibují le — It's too late to go now.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Its middle syllable loses its tone: láibují, with bu light between two full syllables.

The word, taken apart.
láibují is built from 来 'to come', 不 'not' and jí 'to reach', all inherited from Middle Chinese, loj, pjut and gip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): coming, but not reaching in time.
