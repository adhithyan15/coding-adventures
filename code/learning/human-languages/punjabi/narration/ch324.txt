Punjabi, chapter 324: Aching and Out of Breath.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਬਾਂਹ (bāṁh) — an arm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a rib, then the Punjabi for to ache.

You'll want to know: ਬਾਂਹ.
ਬਾਂਹ — bāṁh — "an arm".
ਭਾਰੀ ਥੈਲੇ ਤੋਂ ਮੇਰੀ ਬਾਂਹ ਦੁਖਦੀ ਹੈ। bhārī thaile ton merī bāṁh dukhdī hai. My arm aches from the heavy bag.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਬਾਂਹ (bāṁh) is inherited from Sanskrit bāhu, "an arm"; CDIAL lists Punjabi bā̃h, "arm", under it (CDIAL 9229).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ਮੋਢਾ.
ਮੋਢਾ — moḍhā — "a shoulder".
ਪਿਤਾ ਜੀ ਬੱਚੇ ਨੂੰ ਮੋਢੇ ਉੱਤੇ ਚੁੱਕਦੇ ਹਨ। pitā jī bachche nū̃ moḍhe utte chukkde han. Father carries the child on his shoulder.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਮੋਢਾ (moḍhā) goes back to a reconstructed maurdhaka, "pertaining to the head"; CDIAL gives no Punjabi form, but lists Lahnda moḍhā, "shoulder" (CDIAL 10372).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਚੱਕਰ (chakkar) — a dizzy spell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to pant, then the Punjabi for sweat.

You'll want to know: ਚੱਕਰ.
ਚੱਕਰ — chakkar — "a dizzy spell".
ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਗਰਮੀ ਵਿੱਚ ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚੱਕਰ ਆਇਆ। kall garmī vich mainū̃ chakkar āiā. Yesterday I felt dizzy in the heat.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਚੱਕਰ (chakkar) is inherited from Sanskrit cakra, "a wheel, a circle"; CDIAL lists Punjabi cakkar, "circuit, ring", under it (CDIAL 4538). The sense of a dizzy spell is not given there.
