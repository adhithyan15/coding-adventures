Japanese, chapter 305: A Letter from Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ゆうびん (yūbin) — the post; mail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to peep, then the Japanese for needy.

You'll want to know: ゆうびん.
ゆうびん — yūbin — "the post; mail".
けさ ゆうびんが きました。 — kesa yūbin ga kimashita — "The post came this morning."

The word, taken apart.
ゆうびん (yūbin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yū, "mail" (Middle Chinese hjuw) and bin, "letter" (bjienH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
あてさき (atesaki) — an address (on a letter).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: あてさき.
あてさき — atesaki — "an address (on a letter)".
あてさきを まちがえました。 — atesaki o machigaemashita — "I got the address wrong."

The word, taken apart.
あてさき (atesaki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ate, "aiming" (from ateru), and saki, "the far end" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

ともだちから ひさしぶりの てがみが きました。 — tomodachi kara hisashiburi no tegami ga kimashita — "A letter came from my friend, the first in ages."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ひさしぶり (hisashiburi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from hisashi, "long-lasting" (from hisashii), and buri, "after an interval" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
