## You'll want to know: 进入
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C405-GATES405-02]; assesses=[] -->

**进入** — *jìnrù* — "to go into".

> *wǎng qián zǒu jiù kěyǐ jìnrù chēzhàn* — Go straight on and you can get into the station.

The new character, in pieces: 入 — two strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right that starts higher, above the top of the first. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**进入** (*jìnrù*) is built from 进 'to go forward' and 入 'to go in', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsinH* and *nyip* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'forward' and the second 'within' and 'to take in'.
