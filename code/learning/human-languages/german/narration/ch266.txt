German, chapter 266: Auf einmal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
plötzlich — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for so far, then the German for long ago.

You'll want to know: plötzlich.
plötzlich — "suddenly".
Plötzlich ging das Licht aus. Suddenly the light went out.
Stories love it; auf einmal means the same. Also an adjective: ein plötzlicher Regen.

The word, taken apart.
plötzlich is usually traced to Middle High German plotz "a clap, a slap" (EtymDB), a word that imitates the sound of a thud; EtymDB lists no parent for plötzlich itself.

Lesson 2 of 5.
unerwartet — unexpectedly; unexpected.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the German for long ago, then the German for suddenly.

You'll want to know: unerwartet.
unerwartet — "unexpectedly; unexpected".
Mein Bruder kam unerwartet zu Besuch. My brother came to visit unexpectedly.
The opposite of erwartet, from erwarten 'to expect'.

The word, taken apart.
unerwartet joins un- "not" and erwartet "expected" (EtymDB). The base is warten, from Old High German wartēn "to watch, to look after" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
rechtzeitig — in time, in good time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for unexpectedly, then the German for gradually.

You'll want to know: rechtzeitig.
rechtzeitig — "in time, in good time".
Wir sind rechtzeitig am Bahnhof angekommen. We arrived at the station in time.
In time for something; pünktlich means exactly at the time.

The word, taken apart.
rechtzeitig joins recht "right" and Zeit "time", both inherited Germanic words (EtymDB), with the ending -ig: at the right time.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zugleich — at once, at the same moment; both.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for gradually, then the German for in time.

You'll want to know: zugleich.
zugleich — "at once, at the same moment; both".
Alle sprachen zugleich. Everyone spoke at once.
Close to gleichzeitig, and more written. Also 'both': Sie ist Ärztin und zugleich Mutter.

The word, taken apart.
zugleich joins zu and gleich "equal, alike", from Old High German gilīh (EtymDB), usually traced to Proto-Germanic galīkaz "alike, similar": in the same way, at the same time.
