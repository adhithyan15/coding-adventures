Sanskrit, chapter 260: Me and you as objects.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
माम् (mām) — me, as the object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say सः पश्यति (saḥ paśyati), “he sees”. Keep the seeing verb. Now make the person seen be the speaker.

You'll want to know: माम्.
माम् (mām) means “me” when I am the object of the action.
सः माम् पश्यति। saḥ mām paśyati. He sees me.
The verb still says who does the seeing. माम् (mām) names the person the seeing reaches.
Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar §491 lists माम् (mām) as the independent accusative singular of the first-person pronoun. “Accusative” is the case label; “the object” is the job you can hear in this sentence.

Taken Apart.
As a grammatical form, माम् (mām) comes from the first-person pronoun paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar §491 records it as the independent accusative singular. This lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.
Source: Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar (1889), §491.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “me” as the object — mām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees me” — saḥ mām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the person seen is the speaker — mām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Someone sees the speaker. Which one-word object do you use? (माम्, mām.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
त्वाम् (tvām) — you, one familiar person, as the object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “he sees me”: सः माम् पश्यति (saḥ mām paśyati). Keep the frame and turn the object toward one familiar person.

You'll want to know: त्वाम्.
त्वाम् (tvām) means “you” when one familiar addressee is the object.
सः त्वाम् पश्यति। saḥ tvām paśyati. He sees you.
The opening tv- also appears in familiar tvam and tava. Here the long ā and final m belong to the independent accusative singular. Whitney §491 places त्वाम् (tvām) beside माम् (mām) in the first- and second-person paradigms.

Taken Apart.
As a grammatical form, त्वाम् (tvām) comes from the second-person pronoun paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar §491 records it as the independent accusative singular. This lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.
Source: Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar (1889), §491.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “he sees me” — saḥ mām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees you” — saḥ tvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: speaker as object — mām; one addressee as object — tvām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
One familiar person is seen. Which object form addresses that person? (त्वाम्, tvām.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Is the one person me or you? — me or you, one person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Give the two singular objects: “me” and “you”. (Mām; tvām.)

Grammar Lens: choose by person.
There is one person in either sentence. Choose by who that person is:
the speaker as object: माम् (mām);
one familiar addressee as object: त्वाम् (tvām).
Both are independent full forms and both take the same place before पश्यति. The choice changes the person seen, not the verb.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: “he sees me” — saḥ mām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees you” — saḥ tvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the rule — choose mām for the speaker, tvām for one familiar addressee]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: mām; tvām; mām; tvām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say both sentences without looking back, then name what changes. (Saḥ mām paśyati; saḥ tvām paśyati; the person used as the object changes.)
