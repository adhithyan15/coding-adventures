开会 (kāihuì) — to have a meeting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A new chapter, and it opens on a word you will hear at work.
[your turn — recall: say 一本书十元 — yì běn shū shí yuán — R1, the chapter you have finished]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say guǎnggào, an advert — R1]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 开门 — kāimén, to open — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 开会.
开会 — kāihuì — "to have a meeting".
明天下午开会 míngtiān xiàwǔ kāihuì — There's a meeting tomorrow afternoon.
我们在开会 wǒmen zài kāihuì — We're in a meeting.

The word, taken apart.
开会 (kāihuì) is built from 开 'to open' and 会 'a gathering'. 会 is the character of 会 'can' and 会 'will', inherited from Middle Chinese hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to come together': here it keeps that sense.

What you've built.
开 starts many things you know: 开门, 开车, and now 开会 (kāihuì).
在 before it says the meeting is going on: 我们在开会.

Lesson 2 of 7.
tōngzhī — to notify; a notice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, then a word that ends the same way.

You'll want to know: tōngzhī.
tōngzhī — "to notify; a notice".
lǎoshī tōngzhī wǒmen míngtiān kāihuì — The teacher let us know there's a meeting tomorrow.
zhè shì yí ge tōngzhī — This is a notice.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tōngzhī is built from tōng 'to get through' and zhī 'to know', the zhī of zhīdào. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, thuwng and trje (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to reach' and 'to perceive': making something known.

What you've built.
As a verb, tōngzhī takes the people told, then the news: tōngzhī wǒmen míngtiān kāihuì.
As a noun it is the notice itself, pinned up or sent round.

Lesson 3 of 7.
xiāoxi — news; a message.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, then a word that ends on the same light xi.
[your turn — recall: say tōngzhī, a notice — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say xiūxi, to rest — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: xiāoxi.
xiāoxi — "news; a message".
wǒ yǒu yí ge hǎo xiāoxi — I've got some good news.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiāoxi is built from xiāo and xi, the xi of xiūxi. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, sjew and sik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to melt away; to wane' and 'to stop; to breed'. Neither gloss gives 'news', so how the pair came to mean it is not settled.

What you've built.
xiāoxi is any news, and the message that brings it: a text from a friend is a xiāoxi.
The xi at the end is light, as in xiūxi.

You'll want to know: huífù.
huífù — "to reply (to a message)".
qǐng huífù — Please reply.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huífù is built from huí 'to go back' and fù 'to return', the fù of fùxí. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, hwoj and bjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to turn round' and 'to return'. The regular change from bjuwk gives fú; fù continues the Guangyun's other reading, bjuwH, glossed 'again'.

What you've built.
huídá answers a question; huífù answers a message.
请 in front makes it the polite request at the foot of a notice: qǐng huífù.

Lesson 6 of 7.
A reply, and the lines that lean on each other — four to six connected lines answering a friend's message, held together by time words, one subject and 因为.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson ended on a message from a friend: they cannot come tomorrow, and they ask whether you are at home the afternoon after.
[pause 1 second]
This lesson answers it.
[your turn — recall: say huífù, to reply]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Connected writing is not longer writing. Four short lines can hang together; six long ones can fall apart. What makes lines connected is that each one leans on the one before it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three things do the leaning, and you own all three. A time word at the front of a line tells your reader where in the day they are: 后天下午, 两点以后. The subject said once: say 我 in the first line, and the lines after it can start on the time or the verb. 因为 ties a reason to the line before it, so your reader knows why.
[pause 2 seconds]
Your reader is the friend who sent the message. Your purpose is to settle the appointment: answer the question, then agree a time.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four to six lines back to your friend. No name at the top: write to them as you would in a message.
Line 1 answers the question: 后天下午我在家, or 后天下午我不在家.
Line 2 says what you will be doing then, with 会, 要 or 正在.
Line 3 gives one reason, with 因为.
Line 4 offers a time, with 从 … 到 …, or 两点以后.
The last line asks after their mother. 你妈妈 … needs only characters you write.
[pause 2 seconds]
A word whose characters you have not written goes in pinyin, inside the line, with its tone marks: kěyǐ, shūfu, huífù. Every character comes from the 163 you have learned, each in the order you learned it, each in the same size of square.
[pause 2 seconds]
No Chinese commas or full stops: leave a space between clauses, as this book prints them.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the reply. Then read it once as your friend would, top to bottom.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read your third line on its own. Does it stand up without the line above it? (It should not quite: 因为 leans on the line before.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Count the lines that start with 我. If every one does, you wrote a list, not a reply.
