Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 25: The Sweet and the Tea: One New Consonant.
8 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
Hear chūrmā — sweet crumbled wheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Recall the five-word food payoff, say hard wheat rolls, then write wind.

You'll want to know.
chūrmā — sweet crumbled wheat dessert.
Three beats, the middle one long. Hear it twice and say it once. The source names it beside dāl and bāṭī; the three are often served together, and this lesson teaches the word rather than the custom.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.

Lesson 2 of 8.
चूरमा (chūrmā) — the sweet on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Recall the five-word transport payoff, then say the sweet crumbled wheat dessert and cloud.

You'll want to know.
चू + र + मा becomes चूरमा — chūrmā — sweet crumbled wheat dessert.
The ू under च is the long ū met in मिलसू, and मा closes with the long ा used since the first greeting.

Guided Practice.
Look, cover, wait five seconds, and write चूरमा (chūrmā). Check that the ू hangs below च and not below र.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
From chūrmā, write the sweet crumbled wheat dessert.

chāy — tea.

Lesson 4 of 8.
य (ya) — the glide at the end of tea.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one sign until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Say tea and rain, then write hard wheat rolls.

Script — one sign.
य — ya.
A left loop, a short arm, and a vertical stem on the right. Set it beside भ of भाई: भ opens its left side into a hook that reaches the stem, while य (ya) closes its left side into a loop.

Guided Practice.
Trace य (ya) once. Copy it once. Write भ beside it and say which one closes its left side. Cover both, wait five seconds, and write य (ya) from the glide sound cue.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Unicode Devanagari chart.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write the Devanagari ya consonant.


Lesson 5 of 8.
चाय (chāy) — tea on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 18 seconds]
Say tea, then write य (ya) and rain.

You'll want to know.
चा + य (ya) becomes चाय — chāy — tea.
The य (ya) carries no vowel mark of its own here and closes the word.

Guided Practice.
Look, cover, wait five seconds, and write चाय (chāy). Check that the ा sits after च and that य (ya) ends the word bare.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.

Lesson 7 of 8.
Payoff — seven food words in four skills.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Recall the earlier five-word food payoff and the three-word weather payoff.

Guided Practice.
Identify seven heard words.
Produce seven words from meaning cues.
Match seven printed cards to meanings.
Write all seven heard words without a model.
Pass each skill separately. Seven food names are a vocabulary map; offering food, accepting it, asking for more, and refusing politely are all still untaught.
