Persian, chapter 316: Thieves.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دزد (dozd) — a thief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a demonstration, then the Persian for a slogan.

You'll want to know: دزد.
دزد (dozd) — "a thief".
دزد (dozd) از پنجره وارد خانه شد. Dozd az panjere vâred-e khâne shod. The thief came into the house through the window.
Plural dozd-hâ. The verb dozdidan "to steal" is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
dozd is a native Persian word, usually traced to Middle Persian duzd, "thief"; EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
دزدی (dozdi) — a theft, stealing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a slogan, then the Persian for a thief.

You'll want to know: دزدی.
دزدی (dozdi) — "a theft, stealing".
دزدی (dozdi) ساعت دو شب اتفاق افتاد. Dozdi sâ'at-e do-ye shab ettefâq oftâd. The theft happened at two in the morning.
dozd "thief" + -i. In speech dozdi kardan is "to steal".

The word, taken apart.
dozdi adds the native noun suffix -i to dozd "thief", a word usually traced to Middle Persian duzd.

Lesson 3 of 5.
سارق (sâreq) — a robber, a burglar (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a thief, then the Persian for a theft.

You'll want to know: سارق.
سارق (sâreq) — "a robber, a burglar (formal)".
پلیس به دنبال سارق است. Polis be donbâl-e sâreq ast. The police are looking for the robber.
Formal, in the news; in speech dozd. Plural sâreqân.

The word, taken apart.
sâreq is borrowed from Arabic sāriq (EtymDB lists the Arabic word), the active participle "one who steals".

Lesson 4 of 5.
جیب بر (jib bor) — a pickpocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a theft, then the Persian for a robber.

You'll want to know: جیب بر.
جیب بر (jib bor) — "a pickpocket".
در اتوبوس یک جیب بر گوشی مرا برد. Dar otobus yek jib bor gushi-ye marâ bord. On the bus a pickpocket took my phone.
jib "pocket" + bor, the present stem of boridan "to cut": a pocket-cutter.

The word, taken apart.
jib "pocket" is borrowed from Arabic jayb (EtymDB). bor- is the present stem of the native verb boridan "to cut".

Lesson 5 of 5.
کیف قاپ (kif qâp) — a bag snatcher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a robber, then the Persian for a pickpocket.

You'll want to know: کیف قاپ.
کیف قاپ (kif qâp) — "a bag snatcher".
کیف قاپ (kif qâp) کیف زن را گرفت و دوید. Kif qâp kif-e zan râ gereft va david. The bag snatcher grabbed the woman's bag and ran.
kif "bag" + qâp, from qâpidan "to snatch".

The word, taken apart.
kif qâp joins kif "bag", whose origin is not settled, and qâp-, the stem of qâpidan "to snatch", which is usually traced to Turkic qap- "to grab". EtymDB lists no entry.
