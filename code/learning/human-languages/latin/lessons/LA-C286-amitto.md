# āmittō — something went wrong

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C162-PAS162-05, LA-LEX-C286-NAULUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask "what happened?" (*Quid accidit?*) Then say "I paid the
fare". (*Naulum solvī.*)

[PAUSE 1s] On a journey, the answer to *quid accidit* is often bad news.

## You'll want to know: āmittō, āmittere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C286-AMITTO-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C254-RES254-01, LA-LEX-C157-ACT157-04, LA-LEX-C248-RES248-01] -->

**āmittō, āmittere** — "to lose, to let slip". Its perfect is **āmīsī**,
"I have lost".

> **Sarcinam āmīsī!** — I have lost my pack!
> **Occāsiōnem āmīsī.** — I let the chance slip.

[PAUSE 2s] You own **perdō**, "to lose", too, and the two are not the same.
*Perdō* is losing by wasting or wrecking: money thrown away, a thing
ruined. *Āmittō* is losing by letting go: it slipped away from you.

## Grammar Lens: reporting the problem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C286-QUID-ACCIDIT-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C160-ACT160-03, LA-LEX-C286-NAVIS-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01] -->

A problem on the road is an event, so it comes in the **perfect**:

> **— Quid accidit?** — What happened?
> **— Nāvis iam discessit!** — The ship has already left!

[PAUSE 2s] *Discessit* is the perfect of *discēdō*, "to go away", and *iam*,
"already", makes it the missed boat. Answer *quid accidit* with what
happened, then say what it means for you: *Sarcinam āmīsī; nummōs nōn
habeō.* I have lost my pack; I have no money.

## The word, taken apart: āmittō
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C77-VERBA3-01] -->

**āmittō** is built from *ab*, "away", and *mittō*, "to send" (EtymDB 2.1;
Lewis & Short): to send away from yourself. In early Latin it meant "to let
someone go", and from there "to let a thing slip", and so "to lose". Its
perfect *āmīsī* keeps the perfect of *mittō*, *mīsī*.
