Russian, chapter 480: Laughter and Tears.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
хохотать (khokhotát) — to roar with laughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to respect, then the Russian for sincere.

You'll want to know: хохотать.
хохотать (khokhotát) — "to roar with laughter".
Мы хохотали весь вечер. My khokhotáli ves vécher. We roared with laughter all evening.
Imperfective; present хохочу, хохочешь (т becomes ч). Perfective захохотать — to burst out laughing.

The word, taken apart.
хохотать (khokhotát) is inherited from Proto-Slavic xoxotati (EtymDB), an imitative word built on the sound of loud laughter.

Lesson 3 of 5.
рыдать (rydát) — to sob.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to roar with laughter, then the Russian for to giggle.

You'll want to know: рыдать.
рыдать (rydát) — "to sob".
После фильма она рыдала. Pósle fílma oná rydála. After the film she sobbed.
Imperfective; present рыдаю. Stronger than плакать.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB lists рыдать (rydát) without a source; it is usually traced to Proto-Slavic rydati 'to weep aloud'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
утешать (uteshát) — to comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: утешать.
утешать (uteshát) — "to comfort".
Мама утешает плачущего ребёнка. Máma utesháyet pláchushchego rebyónka. Mum is comforting the crying child.
Imperfective; perfective утешить.

The word, taken apart.
утешать (uteshát) is built on у- and тешить 'to amuse, to please'; EtymDB lists тешить without a source, and it is usually traced to Proto-Slavic těšiti.

Lesson 5 of 5.
утешение (uteshéniye) — consolation, comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to sob, then the Russian for to comfort.

You'll want to know: утешение.
утешение (uteshéniye) — "consolation, comfort".
Твои слова — для меня большое утешение. Tvoí slová — dlya menyá bolshóye uteshéniye. Your words are a great comfort to me.
Neuter.

The word, taken apart.
утешение (uteshéniye) is built on утешить 'to comfort' with -ение; the root is that of тешить 'to please', usually traced to Proto-Slavic těšiti.
