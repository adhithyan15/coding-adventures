# طائرة (ṭāʾira) — a plane

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C288-SAFAR288-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C288-COUNTER-QUESTIONS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask for a return ticket, then ask the price. (*Urīdu tadhkirat
dhahāb wa-iyāb*, *bi-kam at-tadhkira?*)

[PAUSE 1s] Say the Arabic for an airport and a pilot. (*Maṭār*, *ṭayyār*.)
They share a root, and so does the thing between them.

## You'll want to know: طائرة
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C288-SAFAR288-02]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C100-LOC100-04, AR-LEX-C170-WAQT170-04, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-03] -->

**طائرة** — *ṭāʾira* — "a plane, an aeroplane".

> **وصلت الطائرة متأخرة.** — *waṣalat aṭ-ṭāʾira mutaʾakhkhira* — The plane
> arrived late.

> **سافرنا بالطائرة.** — *sāfarnā bi-ṭ-ṭāʾira* — We travelled by plane.

The verb takes the *-at* of "she": *waṣalat*. A word ending in **ة** is
usually feminine, and so is the verb that goes with it.

Say it *ṭā-ʾi-ra*: the long *ā*, then the catch, then *ira*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W06-HAMZA-01] -->

**طائرة** (*ṭāʾira*) is built on the native Arabic root *ṭ-y-r*, "to fly"
(Lane), the root of *ṭāra*, "it flew", *maṭār*, "an airport", and
*ṭayyār*, "a pilot". Lane gives *ṭāʾir* as a flying thing, a bird, and notes
that the feminine *ṭāʾira* is seldom used of a female bird; the sense "a
plane" belongs to the age of planes. The catch in the middle is a hamza, on a
seat the next lesson puts in your hand.
