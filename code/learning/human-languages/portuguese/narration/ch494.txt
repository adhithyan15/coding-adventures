Portuguese, chapter 494: Fraldas e biberões.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a fralda — nappy, diaper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to adopt, then the Portuguese for adoption.

You'll want to know: a fralda.
a fralda — "nappy, diaper".
O pai mudou a fralda ao bebé. The father changed the baby's nappy.
Mudar a fralda — to change a nappy.

The word, taken apart.
fralda goes back to Old Portuguese faldra (EtymDB), from a Gothic word related to falþan "to fold".

chupeta is built on chupar "to suck" (EtymDB); the verb is usually taken to imitate the sound of sucking.

Lesson 4 of 5.
gatinhar — to crawl (like a baby).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for baby's bottle, then the Portuguese for dummy.

You'll want to know: gatinhar.
gatinhar — "to crawl (like a baby)".
Com oito meses, a bebé já gatinha pela casa. At eight months, the baby is already crawling round the house.
Built on gato: to move on all fours like a little cat.

The word, taken apart.
gatinhar is built on gatinho "kitten", from gato "cat", which is inherited from Late Latin cattus (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
embalar — to rock (a baby); to pack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for dummy, then the Portuguese for to crawl.

You'll want to know: embalar.
embalar — "to rock (a baby); to pack".
A mãe embalou o bebé até ele adormecer. The mother rocked the baby until he fell asleep.
It also means to pack goods: embalar as compras.

The word, taken apart.
In the sense "to pack", embalar comes from French emballer (EtymDB), built on balle "a bale"; the origin of the sense "to rock a baby" is not settled.
