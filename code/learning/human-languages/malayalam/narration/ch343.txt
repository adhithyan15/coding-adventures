Malayalam, chapter 343: Asking the Way.
4 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) — a road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for "a way, a path", then "a street", then "a station". (vaḻi, teruvŭ, sṟṟēṣan.)

You'll want to know: റോഡ്.
റോഡ് — ṟōḍŭ — "a road".
ഈ റോഡ് സ്റ്റേഷനിലേക്ക് പോകുന്നു. ī ṟōḍŭ sṟṟēṣanilēkkŭ pōkunnu. — "This road goes to the station."
വലിയ റോഡിൽ ഒരുപാട് ബസുകൾ ഉണ്ട്. valiya ṟōḍil orupāṭŭ basukaḷ uṇṭŭ. — "There are a lot of buses on the big road."
വഴി is the way or route you take; റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) is the road itself; തെരുവ് is a street lined with houses and shops. -ലേക്ക് on സ്റ്റേഷൻ says "towards, to".
Its last consonant is new. The next lesson takes it out of this word and puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) is borrowed from English road. Malayalam hears the English r as its own റ and the English d as its curled-back ഡ (ḍa), and closes the word with the half-u, ŭ, as it does with ടിക്കറ്റ് and സീറ്റ്.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) mean? (a road.) How is it different from വഴി? (വഴി is the way you take; റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) is the road itself.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ഡ.
ഡ — ḍa: the tongue curled back as for ട, and the voice on as for ദ. It is the voiced partner of ട.
In റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) it carries the half-u sign ്, so the word ends -ḍŭ. Native Malayalam words rarely use it; it is the letter Malayalam reaches for when an English word has a d.

Writing: ഡ.
Put your pen on ഡ (ḍa) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see, slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 4.
സ്റ്റേഷൻ എവിടെയാണ്? (sṟṟēṣan eviṭeyāṇŭ?) — where is the station?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "where?", then "on the left", "on the right", and "straight ahead". (eviṭe, iṭattŭ, valattŭ, nēre.)

Grammar Lens: asking.
Open with ക്ഷമിക്കണം, "excuse me", then ask where, or ask how:
ക്ഷമിക്കണം, സ്റ്റേഷൻ എവിടെയാണ്? kṣamikkaṇaṁ, sṟṟēṣan eviṭeyāṇŭ? — "Excuse me, where is the station?"
സ്റ്റേഷനിലേക്ക് എങ്ങനെ പോകണം? sṟṟēṣanilēkkŭ eṅṅane pōkaṇaṁ? — "How do I get to the station?"
The second is the -അണം of chapter 342: "how must one go". എത്ര ദൂരമുണ്ട്, etra dūramuṇṭŭ, asks how far.

Grammar Lens: following the answer.
ഇടത്ത് is "on the left"; add -ഓട്ട്, as in അങ്ങോട്ട്, and it is "to the left": ഇടത്തോട്ട്, iṭattōṭṭŭ. So വലത്തോട്ട്, "to the right".
ഈ റോഡിലൂടെ നേരെ പോകൂ. കവലയിൽ എത്തുമ്പോൾ ഇടത്തോട്ട് തിരിയൂ. സ്റ്റേഷൻ വലത്ത് ആണ്. ī ṟōḍilūṭe nēre pōkū. kavalayil ettumpōḷ iṭattōṭṭŭ tiriyū. sṟṟēṣan valattŭ āṇŭ. — "Go straight along this road. When you reach the junction, turn left. The station is on the right."
പോകൂ and തിരിയൂ are the polite command in long -ū that you know from ഓടൂ. -ലൂടെ is "along, through", and രണ്ടാമത്തെ റോഡ് (ṟōḍŭ) is "the second road".
