Latin, chapter 414: Right and Wrong.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iniūria, iniūriae — a wrong, an injustice, an insult.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to beg to be spared, then the Latin for defence in court.

You'll want to know: iniūria, iniūriae.
iniūria, iniūriae — "a wrong, an injustice, an insult".
Iniūriam accēpit, nōn fēcit. He suffered a wrong; he did not do one.
iniūria, iniūriae f., in- not + iūs: what is against right. Iniūriā, wrongly. English injury.

The word, taken apart.
iniūria is built from in- "not" and iūs, iūris "right, law" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): what goes against right. English injury comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: flāgitium, flāgitiī.
flāgitium, flāgitiī — "a disgraceful act, a scandal".
Hoc flāgitium tacēre nōn possum. I cannot keep quiet about this scandal.
flāgitium, flāgitiī n., a shameful deed, and the person who does it.

The word, taken apart.
flāgitium is built on flāgitō "to demand loudly" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): first a furious demand, then the shameful act that people cried out against.

Lesson 3 of 5.
fās — divine law; what is right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a wrong, then the Latin for a disgraceful act.

You'll want to know: fās.
fās — "divine law; what is right".
Fās nōn est hoc dīcere. It is not right to say this.
fās n., indeclinable. Fās est, it is right, it is allowed by the gods; it covers what human law leaves out.

The word, taken apart.
fās is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European noun meaning "a saying, an utterance" (EtymDB), and Lewis & Short link it with fārī "to speak": what the gods have declared.

Lesson 4 of 5.

nefās — "a crime against heaven, a wicked thing".
Nefās est hospitem violāre. It is wicked to wrong a guest.
nefās n., indeclinable: ne- not + fās. English nefarious comes from its family.

The word, taken apart.
nefās is built from ne- "not" and fās "divine law" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): what heaven forbids. English nefarious comes from its adjective.

Lesson 5 of 5.
inīquus, inīqua, inīquum — unfair, unjust; uneven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for divine law, then the Latin for a crime against heaven.

You'll want to know: inīquus, inīqua, inīquum.
inīquus, inīqua, inīquum — "unfair, unjust; uneven".
Lēx inīqua est. The law is unfair.
inīquus, inīqua, inīquum, in- not + aequus, level, fair. English iniquity.

The word, taken apart.
inīquus is built from in- "not" and aequus "level, fair" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the vowel changed inside the compound. English iniquity comes from it.
