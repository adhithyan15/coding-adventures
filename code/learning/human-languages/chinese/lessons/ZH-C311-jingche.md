## You'll want to know: 警车
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C311-POLICE311-02]; assesses=[] -->

**警车** — *jǐngchē* — "police car".

> **小偷看到警车就走了** *xiǎotōu kàndào jǐngchē, jiù zǒu le* — The thief saw the police car and left.

The new character, in pieces: 警 — nineteen strokes in three pieces: top left, the grass piece of 花, then a two-stroke wrap with 口 inside; top right, the striking piece of 救; across the foot a dot, three bars and 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**警车** (*jǐngchē*) is built from 警 'to warn' and 车 'a vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kjaengX* and *tsyhae* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wake' and 'to guard against'.
