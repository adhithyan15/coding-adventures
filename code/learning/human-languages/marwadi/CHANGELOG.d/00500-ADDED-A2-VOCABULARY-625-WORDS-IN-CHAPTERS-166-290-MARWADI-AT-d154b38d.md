## Added — A2 vocabulary, 625 words in chapters 166-290; Marwadi attains A2

- Added 650 generated lessons in 125 chapters: 625 word lessons, five words a
  chapter, and 25 reviews. Each word lesson teaches one headword with its
  romanization, an example built only from words the track already teaches and
  the A2 core's grammar as it landed, where the word comes from, spoken
  practice that revisits earlier words, and one objective activity, a typed
  recall of the word. 187 of the 625 are verbs.
- The five tranches, each on A2 or lower spine nodes only (none on a B1 node):
  - a, chapters 166-190 (50 verbs): the day's routine, washing and dressing,
    housework, the hearth, cooking and water for the house (SAY-WHAT-I-DO);
    tying, covering, tearing, grinding, churning and putting away
    (NAME-EVERYDAY-ACTIONS); the kitchen, spices, seats, rooms, the courtyard
    and clothes (NAME-EVERYDAY-THINGS).
  - b, chapters 191-215 (33 verbs): notices, money, the bank, the post, the
    phone, the office and weights (READ-PRACTICAL-TEXTS); the town, directions,
    distance and the bus stand (ASK-LOCATION); buying, sizes, amounts, prices,
    returning and wrapping (SAY-WHAT-I-WANT).
  - c, chapters 216-240 (35 verbs): journeys and plans, the bus and the train,
    the weather to come and the fair (TALK-ABOUT-FUTURE); the desert land, its
    sand hills, wells, heat, dust and light (DESCRIBE-QUALITIES); camel gear,
    the herd, crops and the harvest (NAME-EVERYDAY-THINGS).
  - d, chapters 241-265 (34 verbs): a fall, fire and water, a theft, a quarrel,
    a visit, a wedding and a festival (TALK-ABOUT-PAST); the body, illness, the
    healer and feelings (CHECK-WELLBEING); relatives by marriage and the
    village's people and trades (EXCHANGE-NAMES).
  - e, chapters 266-290 (35 verbs): asking, answering, refusing, agreeing, help
    and permission (NEGATE-AND-ASK); music, songs, games, sport and crafts
    (SAY-WHAT-I-LIKE); school, study, work and skills
    (SAY-WHAT-I-HAVE-AND-CAN-DO).
- Every tranche was read by an independent reviewer for agreement and gender,
  Marwari rather than Hindi forms, and its sources, and fixed in place. The
  reviews replaced अलगणी, झाग and पूंछणो with पसारणो, चीथड़ो and साड़ी (a);
  removed slug exceptions the builder no longer needed (b); respelled कुवो,
  टाळणो and भेंट as CDIAL's Marwari-row कूवो, टालणो and भेट, and replaced the
  Hindi twin धुंध with धूंवर (c); replaced भाजणो, हिचकी, मिलाप, लाय and पसीजणो
  with गमणो, खांसी, दायजो, ठीकरो and जीवणो (d); and replaced बिसवास, सतावणो,
  खिजावणो, सही, बूझणो, फेंकणो, अंक and जांचणो with मतलब, फोड़णो, खीजणो, खरो,
  मीचणो, सांधणो, नतीजो and आंकणो, and the Hindi-shaped बांसुरी with बांसळी (e).
- Sources: a CDIAL Marwari or Old Marwari row is cited wherever the entry has
  one (240 words). Otherwise the word cites the entry's Hindi, Gujarati or
  Sindhi row and the lesson says so, "CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this
  entry" (246). Tatsamas and later learned borrowings cite Monier-Williams or
  CDIAL as such (22). Loans from Persian, Arabic and English are hedged as
  "usually traced to" their donor (117). Root slugs reuse the corpus slug of
  the same etymon wherever one exists.
- The past stays within what the core teaches: an intransitive past with वो or
  हूं (72 examples), and a transitive past only with म्हैं as its agent,
  agreeing with the thing (115), since noun and third-person agents wait for a
  source and for B1.
- No new letter, no idiom, sense or culture claim. Letter anchoring holds at
  cold 0, buildsToward 49, unread ऋ ऐ औ ञ ष, unwritten 0; every chapter's first
  lesson is drivable by ear.
- With 1,265 headwords and 276 verbs at or below A2, and all five A2 spine
  nodes realized by the core, the level gate now records Marwadi as having
  attained A2. That is the gate's vocabulary-and-grammar measure, not exam
  readiness: no A2 mock, rubric or human validation exists yet.
