Russian, chapter 98: Frying pan, Bucket, Rope, Nail, Hammer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
сковорода (skovorodá) — a frying pan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a wardrobe, then the Russian for a saucepan.

You'll want to know: сковорода.
сковорода (skovorodá) — "a frying pan".

The word, taken apart.
сковорода (skovorodá) from Proto-Slavic skovorda — inherited, with Church Slavonic сковрада. Vasmer calls its further origin unclear; a comparison with Old High German scart-îsan, "a frying pan", has been made, and Szemerényi derived it from an Iranian word for "bowl".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a frying pan, then the Russian for a bucket.

You'll want to know: верёвка.
верёвка (veryóvka) — "a rope".

The word, taken apart.
верёвка (veryóvka) is built on Old Russian вьрвь, "a cord", with -ка. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian virvė, "rope", and vérti, "to thread", and Greek erýō, "I drag".

Lesson 4 of 5.
гвоздь (gvozd) — a nail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bucket, then the Russian for a rope.

You'll want to know: гвоздь.
гвоздь (gvozd) — "a nail".

The word, taken apart.
гвоздь from Proto-Slavic gvozdĭ — inherited. Vasmer will not separate it from old Slavic gvozd, "a forest", and compares German Quaste, "a tassel", and Old Norse kvistr, "a twig": a nail was first a wooden peg.

Lesson 5 of 5.
молоток (molotók) — a hammer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a rope, then the Russian for a nail.

You'll want to know: молоток.
молоток (molotók) — "a hammer".

The word, taken apart.
молоток (molotók) is молот, "a sledgehammer", with the diminutive -ок. Vasmer thinks молот probably comes from молоть, "to grind", so "the crusher", and compares Latin malleus, "a hammer".
