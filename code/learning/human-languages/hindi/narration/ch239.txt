Hindi, chapter 239: Shiny, Hazy, Deserted, Crowded, Inhabited.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चमकदार (camakdār) — shiny.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for sticky, then the Hindi for rough.

You'll want to know: चमकदार.
चमकदार — camakdār — "shiny".

The word, taken apart.
चमकदार (camakdār) is camak, "shine", from camaknā, "to glitter", which CDIAL lists under a reconstructed camakka, "sudden movement" (CDIAL 4676), with the Persian ending -dār, "holder".

Lesson 2 of 5.
धुंधला (dhũdhlā) — hazy, blurred.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for rough, then the Hindi for shiny.

You'll want to know: धुंधला.
धुंधला — dhũdhlā — "hazy, blurred".

The word, taken apart.
धुंधला (dhũdhlā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dhūmāndha, "dim-sighted", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6858). It is built from dhūma, "smoke", and andha, "blind".

Lesson 3 of 5.
सुनसान (sunsān) — deserted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for shiny, then the Hindi for hazy.

You'll want to know: सुनसान.
सुनसान — sunsān — "deserted".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of सुनसान (sunsān) is uncertain. It may be built on sūn, "empty", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit śūnya, "empty" (CDIAL 12567), but CDIAL does not list sunsān itself.
