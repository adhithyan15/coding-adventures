Mandarin Chinese, chapter 82: Behind, Left and Right.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hòu — after; behind; later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for abroad, then the Mandarin for beside.

You'll want to know: hòu.
hòu — "after; behind; later".
sān tiān hòu wǒmen qù Běijīng — In three days we're going to Beijing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hòu is inherited from Middle Chinese huwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed with the pair 'before and after'; the dictionary writes it with the traditional form of the character.

What you've built.
One more word for meeting people.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: hòu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hòu, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: sān tiān hòu wǒmen qù Běijīng — In three days we're going to Beijing.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for abroad, then the Mandarin for beside, then say hòu again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does hòu mean? ("After; behind; later".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zhōngjiān — the middle; between.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the left side, then the Mandarin for the right side.

You'll want to know: zhōngjiān.
zhōngjiān — "the middle; between".

The word, taken apart.
zhōngjiān is built from 中 'middle' and jiān 'a gap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjuwng and kean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the second under an older form of the character.

Lesson 5 of 5.
duìmiàn — opposite; across from.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the right side, then the Mandarin for the middle.

You'll want to know: duìmiàn.
duìmiàn — "opposite; across from".
wǒ jiā duìmiàn yǒu yí ge fàndiàn — There's a restaurant across from my home.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
duìmiàn is built from 对 'to face' and miàn 'face', both inherited from Middle Chinese, twojH and mjienH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): face to face.

What you've built.
