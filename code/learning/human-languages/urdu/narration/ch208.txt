You'll want to know: بلبل.
بلبل — bulbul — "a nightingale".

The word, taken apart.
بلبل (bulbul) is a loan, usually traced to Persian bulbul, "nightingale".

Lesson 2 of 5.
سارس (sāras) — a crane (bird).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for vinegar, then the Urdu for a nightingale.

You'll want to know: سارس.
سارس — sāras — "a crane (bird)".

The word, taken apart.
سارس (sāras) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from sārasa, "the crane" (CDIAL 13359). It is built on saras, "lake".

Lesson 4 of 5.
چمگادڑ (chamgādaṛ) — a bat (animal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a crane, then the Urdu for a vulture.

You'll want to know: چمگادڑ.
چمگادڑ — chamgādaṛ — "a bat (animal)".

The word, taken apart.
چمگادڑ (chamgādaṛ) goes back to Sanskrit carmacaṭaka, "bat", "the leather sparrow", built from carman, "skin, leather", and caṭaka, "sparrow". CDIAL lists Hindi cām-gīdaṛ, "bat", under it (CDIAL 4700).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: بچھو.
بچھو — bichchhū — "a scorpion".

The word, taken apart.
بچھو (bichchhū) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vṛścika, "scorpion" (CDIAL 12081). CDIAL asks whether the many shapes of this word come from a taboo on naming the scorpion.
