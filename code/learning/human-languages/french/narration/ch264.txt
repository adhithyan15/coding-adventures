French, chapter 264: How It Felt — j'étais content, j'espère.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
j'étais content — how it felt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the last line of the journey you read: "I was pleased". (J'étais content.)
[pause 1 second]
That sentence chose its past for a reason. This lesson is about why.

Grammar Lens: a feeling is scene.
You already own the feelings: content, heureux, triste, inquiet, nerveux, fier. A feeling has no edge. It was going on, like the weather, so in a story it is scene, and it takes the imparfait of être:
D'abord, j'étais nerveux. Ensuite, j'étais content. — At first I was nervous. Then I was pleased.
The adjective agrees with whoever felt it, and the ear hears some of that. Content ends in a silent t; contente sounds it. Inquiet becomes inquiète, nerveux becomes nerveuse, heureux becomes heureuse. Triste has one form for a man or a woman.
Ma mère était inquiète. — My mother was worried. Nous étions contents. — We were pleased.
The passé composé is possible too, and it changes the picture: it makes the feeling a reaction, an event at one moment. Quand j'ai vu mon ami, j'ai été content. — When I saw my friend, I was glad.

Grammar Lens: content de.
To say what you were pleased with, add de:
J'étais content du voyage. — I was pleased with the trip. Je suis content de partir. — I'm glad to be leaving.
De and le weld into du, and an infinitive can follow de directly. Fier works the same way: je suis fier de toi, I'm proud of you.

The word, taken apart.
content is usually traced to Latin contentus, "satisfied", the participle of continēre, "to hold together" (Lewis & Short, under contentus and contineo), itself con- plus tenēre, "to hold". EtymDB 2.1 follows it back through Old French content. To be content is to hold yourself in, wanting nothing further. inquiet is a borrowing from Latin inquiētus, "restless" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short): in-, "not", and quiētus, "quiet".

Lesson 2 of 3.
j'espère revenir — saying what you are reaching for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to hope". (Espérer.) Then "I was pleased". (J'étais content.)
[pause 1 second]
An experience has a past and a future. Last lesson was how it felt; this one is what you want next.

You'll want to know: j'espère + an infinitive.
j'espère + infinitive — "I hope to".
J'espère revenir à Paris. — I hope to come back to Paris. J'espère parler français un jour. — I hope to speak French one day.
espérer is an -er verb whose é opens to è where the ending goes quiet: j'espère, tu espères, but nous espérons. An infinitive straight after it, with nothing in between, makes the hope your own. Italian puts a small di there; French puts nothing.
When someone else is the one who will do it, the frame is j'espère que…, and the verb after it stays plain: J'espère que tu vas bien. — I hope you're well.

Grammar Lens: mon rêve, c'est de…
An ambition is bigger than a hope, and it has its own frame:
Mon rêve, c'est de vivre près de la mer. — My dream is to live by the sea.
You have le rêve, "the dream", and rêver, "to dream", which takes the same de: je rêve de partir, I dream of leaving.
Three strengths, then, for the future: je voudrais partir, I'd like to; j'espère partir, I hope to; mon rêve, c'est de partir, it is my dream. Only the last needs a de.

The phrase, taken apart.
espérer is inherited from Latin spērāre, "to hope, to expect" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under spero), with an e- that French grew in front of sp-, as it grew an é- in front of the sc- of écrire. English desperate is the same verb with dē- in front: hope taken away. rêver goes back to Old French resver, "to be delirious" (EtymDB 2.1), and further than that the sources do not reach.

Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — how it felt, and what comes next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "my mother was worried". (Ma mère était inquiète.) Then "I hope to come back". (J'espère revenir.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and an experience has a shape: what happened, how it felt, and what you want next. The feeling is scene, in the imparfait: j'étais content, j'étais nerveux. What it was about takes de: content du voyage. And the future comes in three strengths: je voudrais, j'espère, mon rêve, c'est de.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here is a journey told with all three:
D'abord, j'étais nerveux, parce que je ne connaissais pas la ville. Mais ensuite, j'étais content du voyage. J'espère revenir la semaine prochaine.
Je ne connaissais pas is connaître in the imparfait, on the nous connaissons stem: a not-knowing that went on, so it is scene.
