Russian, chapter 427: In the harbour.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
гавань (gávan) — a harbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an oar, then the Russian for to row.

You'll want to know: гавань.
гавань (gávan) — "a harbour".
Вечером в гавань вернулись все лодки. Vécherom v gávan vernúlis vse lódki. In the evening all the boats came back to the harbour.
Feminine, though it ends in -ь.

The word, taken apart.
гавань (gávan) was borrowed from Dutch haven 'a harbour' (EtymDB), from Middle Dutch havene.

Lesson 2 of 5.
маяк (mayák) — a lighthouse; a beacon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to row, then the Russian for a harbour.

You'll want to know: маяк.
маяк (mayák) — "a lighthouse; a beacon".
На скале стоит старый маяк. Na skalé stoít stáryy mayák. An old lighthouse stands on the rock.
Masculine; the stress moves: маяка (mayaká).

The word, taken apart.
маяк (mayák) is usually traced to a Turkic word for a pole or a marker. EtymDB lists the Russian word with the sense 'lighthouse' but no source, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
матрос (matrós) — a sailor, a seaman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a harbour, then the Russian for a lighthouse.

You'll want to know: матрос.
матрос (matrós) — "a sailor, a seaman".
Старый матрос рассказывал нам о море. Stáryy matrós rasskázyval nam o móre. The old sailor told us about the sea.
Masculine. Моряк is anyone who works at sea; матрос (matrós) is a deckhand or a junior rank.

The word, taken apart.
матрос (matrós) was borrowed from Dutch matroos 'a sailor, a seaman', which EtymDB traces to French matelot.

Lesson 4 of 5.
капитан (kapitán) — a captain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a lighthouse, then the Russian for a sailor.

You'll want to know: капитан.
капитан (kapitán) — "a captain".
Капитан сказал, что море спокойное. Kapitán skazál, chto móre spokóynoye. The captain said the sea was calm.
Masculine. Also the captain of a team.

The word, taken apart.
капитан (kapitán) is usually traced through Italian capitano or French capitaine to Late Latin capitāneus 'a chief', from caput 'head' (EtymDB lists those words; the Russian entry has no source).

Lesson 5 of 5.
тонуть (tonút) — to sink; to drown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a sailor, then the Russian for a captain.

You'll want to know: тонуть.
тонуть (tonút) — "to sink; to drown".
Старая лодка медленно тонет. Stáraya lódka médlenno tónet. The old boat is slowly sinking.
Imperfective; perfective утонуть (of a person) or затонуть (of a ship). Present тону, тонешь.

The word, taken apart.
тонуть (tonút) is usually traced to the same root as топить 'to drown, to sink', which EtymDB takes from Proto-Slavic topiti. EtymDB lists тонуть (tonút) itself without a source.
