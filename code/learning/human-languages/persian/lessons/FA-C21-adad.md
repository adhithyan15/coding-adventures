# (reading) — the figures

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SKILL-READ-ANSWERS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, then six answers. These are not words at all.

[PAUSE 1s] You drew every one of them. Reading them is the same ten signs
coming the other way.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[FA-PERFORMANCE-DIGITS-READ] -->

> ۰ ۱ ۲ ۳
> ۴ ۵ ۶
> ۷ ۸ ۹
> ۱۰
> ۲۰
> ۱۰۰

[PAUSE 3s] Read across each line once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and this time read the last three as amounts rather than as
signs. Ten. Twenty. A hundred.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING] -->

[PAUSE 2s] These ten signs are the only Persian most travellers read before
they read any word at all. A price, a floor, a platform, a phone number: none
of them waits for you to know the letters.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice also which way they run. The digits go left to right inside a
number even though the words around them run right to left, so **۱۰۰** is a one
and two zeros in the order you already expect.

[PAUSE 2s] That is the whole trick, and it is why a figure is the first thing a
reader can take from a Persian page.
