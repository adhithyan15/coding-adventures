Sanskrit, chapter 488: Sleep and Waking.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रतिबुध्यते (pratibudhyate) — awakes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a scented cosmetic, then the Sanskrit for puts on clothes.

You'll want to know: प्रतिबुध्यते.
प्रतिबुध्यते — pratibudhyate — "awakes".
अरुणः प्रातः शीघ्रं प्रतिबुध्यते। aruṇaḥ prātaḥ śīghraṃ pratibudhyate. Arun wakes early in the morning.
Past (he …): सः प्रातः प्रतिबुद्धवान्, saḥ prātaḥ pratibuddhavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिबुध्यते (pratibudhyate) is built from prati and the root √budh, "to wake, to know", the root of बुध्यते; MW gives "to awaken, awake, wake" (MW).

You'll want to know: तन्द्रा.
तन्द्रा — tandrā — "lassitude; drowsiness".
भोजनात् अनन्तरं मम तन्द्रा भवति। bhojanāt anantaraṃ mama tandrā bhavati. After a meal I feel drowsy.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तन्द्रा (tandrā) MW gives "lassitude, exhaustion, laziness"; "drowsiness" is the sense it has in modern use; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रबोधयति (prabodhayati) — wakes someone up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for awakes, then the Sanskrit for lassitude.

You'll want to know: प्रबोधयति.
प्रबोधयति — prabodhayati — "wakes someone up".
माता प्रातः पुत्रं प्रबोधयति। mātā prātaḥ putraṃ prabodhayati. Mother wakes her son in the morning.
Past (she …): सा पुत्रं प्रबोधितवती, sā putraṃ prabodhitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
प्रबोधयति (prabodhayati) is built on pra and the causative of the root √budh, "to wake, to know", the root of बुध्यते: to make someone wake; MW gives "to wake up, awaken (trans.)" (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lassitude, then the Sanskrit for wakes someone up.

You'll want to know: उन्निद्रः.
उन्निद्रः — unnidraḥ — "sleepless".
उद्वेगेन पिता रात्रौ उन्निद्रः भवति। udvegena pitā rātrau unnidraḥ bhavati. Father lies sleepless at night with worry.

The word, taken apart.
उन्निद्रः (unnidraḥ) is built from ud, "up, out", and निद्रा, "sleep"; MW gives "sleepless, awake" (MW).
