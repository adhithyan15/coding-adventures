## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-23]; assesses=[] -->

**la etapa** — "a stage," "a leg" of a journey or of anything else.

The word did not start as a distance. It started as a building.

Middle Dutch **stapel** was a heap — a market pile, a stack of goods for sale.
French borrowed it as *estaple* for a depot: the storehouse where a marching
army halted and drew its bread. Because the army stopped there, the word came to
mean the *march between two of them*, and once it meant that it could mean any
stretch of anything: a stage of a race, a stage of a plan, a stage of a life.

English borrowed the same Dutch pile and kept it a pile. A **staple** is a main
commodity, and a *staple town* held the legal right to be the place where a
commodity was heaped and sold.

And French did the usual thing to the *s* in *estaple*. It dropped it and put a
mark over the vowel: **étape**. The accent is the same headstone as the little
hat in *hôtel* — Spanish borrowed the word after the *s* had already gone.
