Mandarin Chinese, chapter 314: Saying Goodbye.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zànglǐ — funeral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to hate to leave behind, then the Mandarin for to adjust to new surroundings.

You'll want to know: zànglǐ.
zànglǐ — "funeral".
dàjiā dōu cānjiā le tā de zànglǐ — Everyone came to his funeral.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zànglǐ is built from zàng 'to bury' and lǐ 'a rite', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsangH and lejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hide away' and the second, quoting an older dictionary, 'what is done to serve the spirits'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
生前 (shēngqián) — in someone's lifetime; while they were alive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to adjust to new surroundings, then the Mandarin for a funeral.

You'll want to know: 生前.
生前 — shēngqián — "in someone's lifetime; while they were alive".
我还记得他生前很喜欢喝茶 wǒ hái jìde tā shēngqián hěn xǐhuan hē chá — I still remember how he loved his tea.

The word, taken apart.
生前 (shēngqián) is built from 生 'life' and 前 'before', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): before, in life, said of someone who has died.
