Lesson 1 of 5.
تلفاز (tilfāz) — a television.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a fan, then the Arabic for a computer.

You'll want to know: تلفاز.
تلفاز — tilfāz — "a television".

The word, taken apart.
تلفاز (tilfāz) is a modern word borrowed from European television and fitted to an Arabic pattern; it has no Arabic root.

Lesson 2 of 5.
جريدة (jarīda) — a newspaper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a computer, then the Arabic for a television.

You'll want to know: جريدة.
جريدة — jarīda — "a newspaper".

The word, taken apart.
جريدة (jarīda) is built on the native Arabic root j-r-d, "to strip" (Lane). Lane's jarīda is a palm stalk stripped of its leaves, and also a register; the newspaper is a modern sense.

Lesson 3 of 5.
مجلة (majalla) — a magazine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a television, then the Arabic for a newspaper.
[pause 1 second]
Say "the weather," then "It is hot." (Aṭ-ṭaqs; al-jaw ḥārr — no verb "to be.")

You'll want to know: مجلة.
مجلة — majalla — "a magazine".

The word, taken apart.
مجلة (majalla) is filed by Lane under j-l-l as a book or scroll, and one of his sources ties it to jill, "a book's cover". It is often compared with Aramaic megillā, "scroll", and may be borrowed from it; the magazine is a modern sense.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ظرف (ẓarf) — an envelope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a magazine, then the Arabic for a letter.

You'll want to know: ظرف.
ظرف — ẓarf — "an envelope".

The word, taken apart.
ظرف (ẓarf), "a vessel, a receptacle", is an old native noun, filed under the root ẓ-r-f (Lane), whose verb means "to be elegant, clever". Lane's ẓarf is a vessel; the envelope is a later sense.
