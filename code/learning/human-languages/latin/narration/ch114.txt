Latin, chapter 114: Reason, Because of that, Place, Right, Left.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ratiō, ratiōnis — a reason, a way of thinking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for and so, then the Latin for a reason.

You'll want to know: ratiō, ratiōnis.
ratiō, ratiōnis — "a reason, a way of thinking".

The word, taken apart.
ratiō is built on ratus, "reckoned", the participle of reor, "to reckon, to think" (Lewis & Short): a reckoning. English reason comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
locus comes from Old Latin stlocus, "place" (Lewis & Short), usually traced to Proto-Indo-European stel-, "to put, to place" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
dexter, dextra, dextrum — right (not left).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for because of that, then the Latin for a place.

You'll want to know: dexter, dextra, dextrum.
dexter, dextra, dextrum — "right (not left)".

The word, taken apart.
dexter is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European deks-, "right side" (EtymDB 2.1), with -ter, an ending that sets one of two sides against the other; Greek dexios, "on the right", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
sinister, sinistra, sinistrum — left (not right).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a place, then the Latin for right.

You'll want to know: sinister, sinistra, sinistrum.
sinister, sinistra, sinistrum — "left (not right)".

The word, taken apart.
sinister has no sure origin; Lewis & Short call it doubtful. It ends in -ter, an ending that sets one of two sides against the other, as in dexter, "right".
