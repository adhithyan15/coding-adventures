Portuguese, chapter 442: O centro histórico.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a muralha — the city wall, the rampart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the public fountain, then the Portuguese for the pillory.

You'll want to know: a muralha.
a muralha — "the city wall, the rampart".
A cidade velha fica dentro da muralha. The old town is inside the city wall.
An ordinary wall is o muro or a parede.

The word, taken apart.
muralha was borrowed from Italian muraglia 'wall', from Late Latin mūrālia (EtymDB), built on mūrus 'wall'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: o mosteiro.
o mosteiro — "the monastery".
Os monges viviam neste mosteiro. The monks lived in this monastery.
O convento is a similar house, often for nuns.

The word, taken apart.
mosteiro is inherited from Old Portuguese mõesteiro (EtymDB), from Latin monastērium, from Greek monastḗrion, on mónos 'alone'.

claustro is a learned borrowing from Latin claustrum 'bar, closed place' (EtymDB), from claudere 'to close'.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the monastery, then the Portuguese for the cloister.

You'll want to know: deambular.
deambular — "to wander, to stroll around".
Passámos a tarde a deambular pelas ruas antigas. We spent the afternoon wandering through the old streets.
A bookish word; it suits slow walks with no plan.

The word, taken apart.
deambular is a learned borrowing from Latin deambulāre 'to walk about' (EtymDB), built on ambulāre 'to walk'.
