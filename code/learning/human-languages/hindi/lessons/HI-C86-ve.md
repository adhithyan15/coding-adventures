# वे — they, and the respect that looks like a plural

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-02, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **वह** and **हम**. You can point at one person and you can
include yourself in a group. Say "they," and notice the gap.

## You'll want to know: वे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-02]; assesses=[] -->

**वे** (*ve*) — "they."

It is the plural of **वह**, and the relationship is visible: the same word with
a different vowel.

## Grammar Lens: the plural that is also a courtesy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-02, HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-02] -->

The same habit you met with **आप** appears here. Hindi shows respect by using a
**plural** form for one person:

| form | about |
|---|---|
| वह | one person, familiar or neutral |
| **वे** | several people — **or one person spoken of with respect** |

So *ve ā rahe haiṅ* can be "they are coming" or "he is coming," said of someone
senior. This is the third time the pattern has appeared: *āp* for a respected
listener, the **-iye** command for a respected listener, and now *ve* for a
respected third person.

In everyday speech many speakers say **वो** for both *vah* and *ve*. Recognise
it; write the forms above.
