Lesson 1 of 7.
मञ्चः (mañcaḥ) — a bed, a platform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for for that reason, then the Sanskrit for because.

You'll want to know: मञ्चः.
मञ्चः — mañcaḥ — "a bed, a platform".

The word, taken apart.
mañca "a stage, a platform, a bed" is of unclear origin: CDIAL 9715 suggests a root √mac with a query mark, and MW equates √mac with a twin √mañc that it calls probably artificial. It lives on as Punjabi mañjā "bed, cot" and Hindi māñcā "platform" (CDIAL 9715).

Lesson 2 of 7.
उपधानम् (upadhānam) — a pillow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for because, then the Sanskrit for a bed.

You'll want to know: उपधानम्.
उपधानम् — upadhānam — "a pillow".

The word, taken apart.
upadhāna comes from upa-√dhā "place upon": "that on which one rests", a pillow, already in the Atharvaveda (MW). The root √dhā "put, place" is one MW sets beside Greek tithēmi and English do.

कम्बलः — kambalaḥ — "a blanket".

The word, taken apart.
kambala "a woollen blanket" is already in the Atharvaveda (MW), but its source is uncertain: CDIAL 2771, after EWA, allows that it may be borrowed from Austroasiatic. Hindi kambal "blanket" comes from it (CDIAL 2771).

Lesson 4 of 7.
यवनिका (yavanikā) — a curtain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pillow, then the Sanskrit for a blanket.

You'll want to know: यवनिका.
यवनिका — yavanikā — "a curtain".

The word, taken apart.
MW lists yavanikā "a screen of cloth, a curtain" under yavana "Ionian, Greek", beside yavanikā "a Greek woman", which suggests "the Greek cloth". MW also records a form javanikā, so the link with the Greeks is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 7.
तालकम् (tālakam) — a lock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a blanket, then the Sanskrit for a curtain.

You'll want to know: तालकम्.
तालकम् — tālakam — "a lock".

The word, taken apart.
tālaka "a lock, a bolt" is known only from the old word lists (MW). CDIAL 5749 and DEDR 3179 compare it with a Dravidian family of words for a door bar or bolt, so it is perhaps borrowed from Dravidian. It lives on as Hindi tālā "lock" (CDIAL 5749).
