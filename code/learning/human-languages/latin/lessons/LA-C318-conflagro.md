## You'll want to know: cōnflagrō, cōnflagrāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C318-CREMO-05]; assesses=[] -->

**cōnflagrō, cōnflagrāre** — "to go up in flames".

> Tōtum aedificium ūnā nocte cōnflagrāvit.
>
> *The whole building went up in flames in a single night.*

*cōnflagrō, cōnflagrāre, cōnflagrāvī, cōnflagrātum*: *con-* 'wholly' + *flagrō*. English *conflagration*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**cōnflagrō** is built on *flagrō*, "to blaze", with *con-*, "wholly" (Lewis & Short): to burn up entirely. English *conflagration* comes from it.
