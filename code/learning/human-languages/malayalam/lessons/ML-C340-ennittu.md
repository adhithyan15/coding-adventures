# എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ) — and then

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "having come". (**വന്നിട്ട്** (*vanniṭṭŭ*).) Then the word for "then, after that".
(**പിന്നെ** (*pinne*).)

## Grammar Lens: one sentence, with -ഇട്ട്
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01, ML-CONCEPT-C39-CHAAYA-01, ML-LEX-C85-DRINK-01, ML-LEX-C126-ABOUTYOU-05] -->

Chapter 111 gave you the past form with **-ഇട്ട്** on it: "having done". Put it inside a sentence
and it says "did this, and then":

> **ഞാൻ ചായ കുടിച്ചിട്ട് ഓഫീസിൽ പോയി.**
>
> *ñān cāya kuṭicciṭṭŭ ōphīsil pōyi.* — "I drank tea and then went to the office."

Piece by piece: "I, tea, having drunk, to the office went". The first action is finished before
the second begins.

## Grammar Lens: two sentences, with എന്നിട്ട്
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C340-ENNITTU-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01, ML-LEX-C340-IYITE-01, ML-LEX-C98-CINEMA-01] -->

To keep the two actions as two sentences, start the second with **എന്നിട്ട്**:

> **ഞാൻ ചായ കുടിച്ചു. എന്നിട്ട് ഓഫീസിൽ പോയി.**
>
> *ñān cāya kuṭiccu. enniṭṭŭ ōphīsil pōyi.* — "I drank tea. And then I went to the office."

**എന്നിട്ട്** is usually explained as the **-ഇട്ട്** ending on the same root as **എന്ന്**: "having
said so", "that done". It ties the next action to the one before.

**പിന്നെ** is the looser, everyday "then, after that", and it can also mean "later". **എന്നിട്ട്**
ties the next action to the one just done.

A listener who wants the story to go on asks **എന്നിട്ടോ?** (*enniṭṭō?*), "and then?"

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C340-ENNITTU-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "And then", at the start of a sentence? (**എന്നിട്ട്**, *enniṭṭŭ*.) The same idea
inside one sentence? (The **-ഇട്ട്** form: ***kuṭicciṭṭŭ***.) How is **പിന്നെ** different? (**Looser:
"after that", and it can also mean "later".**)
