## Etymology: three passes, and the third one moved the most

**`quemar` — the dictionaries flatly disagree, and the lesson now teaches that.**
DLE prints one unhedged line, *Del lat. cremāre*. Corominas opens his entry by
saying phonetics **forbid** it: Portuguese *queimar* needs an older *ai* where
*cremare* offers *e* (which should have broken to *ie*, giving a *quiemar*
Spanish never had), and losing *cremare*'s *r* is *gravísimo*. He proposes a
remaking under a Greek word for a burn and concedes his own soft spot. Drafted as
settled; shipped as an open argument, with `roots: [quemar-disputed]`.

**`cruzar` is not from `cruciare`.** Corominas knows *cruciare* already meant "to
cross" by about AD 500 and rejects it anyway on phonetic grounds: *cruzar* is
denominal, built inside Spanish on *cruz*. And *cruz* is **semi-learned** — short
Latin *ŭ* should have given ***croz***, and the surviving *u* is the fingerprint,
the same one *Dios* carries. The lesson teaches the fingerprint. Root corrected
`cruciare-latin` → `crux-latin`.

**`lanzar` is the `buscar` pattern again.** The verb is settled. *Lancea* is not:
DLE says *voz de or. celtíbero* flat out, Corominas says only that Varro thought
it Spanish and *quizá* Celtiberian, and recent specialists argue for a different
ancient Iberian language. One dictionary printed a possibility as a fact, and the
lesson says so.

**`usar` carries a star.** DLE prints **no etymology at all**; the *usare* every
popular account cites is reconstructed. It does appear in ninth-century legal
Latin, which is too late to document the stage that matters — so neither
"attested" nor "unattested in any Latin text" is true, and the lesson says the
narrow thing.

**`gritar`: the Quirites story is folk etymology.** Roman writers explained
*quiritare* as calling on the citizens; Corominas suspects the ancient
lexicographers built that gloss out of the resemblance, and etymonline labels it
folk etymology outright. Taught as a story the Romans told about themselves,
labelled. English *cry* is genuinely the same word, and the live payoff is that
*cry* drifted from shouting to weeping and *gritar* never did.

**`empujar` hedges twice.** DLE writes *Quizá*, Corominas *probablemente*. What
is hard is the Old Spanish spelling **empuxar**: that *x* can only come from a
Latin *-ls-*, the same change behind *mucho*. And English **push** is from plain
*pulsare* without the *in-*, so it is a **cousin, not the same word** — the error
the draft made and the sources caught.

**`vender` gained a proof.** The perfect **vendidi** and the participle
**venditum** still show *dedi* and *datum* inside them, and the long *e* before
*-nd-* is anomalous unless two words contracted. Morphology, not just meaning.

Corrections against the draft, each from a source rather than a summary: *waste*
is **West Germanic** influence on *vastare*, not Frankish; *vast* is dropped
because Latin *vastus* probably merges two words; *cremate* is an 1851
back-formation from *cremation*; *volatilis* meant *winged* in Latin, and the
English senses arrive in the reverse order; *bureau*'s cloth is an unresolved
either/or, so the lesson teaches the desk and names the doubt; the `gyros` you
eat is the same Greek root by a **shorter, later road** — straight from modern
Greek in 1971, never through Latin — the mirror of the *murder*/*morir* trap; and
`servus`'s own origin is left open between "guardian" and an Etruscan loan.

False friends taught explicitly: `usar`≠*usher*, `tocar`≠*took*/*toque*/*tact*,
`vender`≠*vendetta*, `gastar`≠*gastric*, `quemar`≠*quench*, `cruzar`≠*cruel*,
`colgar`≠*cough*, `gritar`≠*grit*, `borrar`≠*borrow*, `lanzar`≠the boat called a
*launch*, and `empujar`≠the *pulse* you cook.

the point at which that stops being rhetorical. And `level-gate.test.ts`'s

**That last one removed the criterion's only red instance**, so a synthetic
fixture replaces it: a track that meets criterion 2 exactly and carries one verb
fewer than 2b asks for, plus the counterfactual that tagging that one lesson as a
verb attains the level. Without it, `verbVocabularyOf` could have been deleted
and the suite would still have passed.
