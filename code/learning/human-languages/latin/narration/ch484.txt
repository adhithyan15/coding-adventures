Latin, chapter 484: Beech, Fir, Elm, Ash, Shady.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fāgus, fāgī — a beech tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to trickle, then the Latin for to flood.

You'll want to know: fāgus, fāgī.
fāgus, fāgī — "a beech tree".
Pastor sub fāgō sedet et cantat. The shepherd sits under a beech tree and sings.
Second declension but feminine, like most tree names: fāgus, fāgī f. English beech is a Germanic cousin, not a borrowing.

The word, taken apart.
fāgus is inherited from Proto-Italic fāgos, from PIE bʰeh₂ǵos 'beech tree' (EtymDB). English beech and German Buche are Germanic cousins (Lewis & Short).

abiēs, abietis — "a fir tree; fir wood".
In monte altō abietēs crēscunt. Fir trees grow on the high mountain.
Third declension, feminine: abiēs, abietis f. Its wood was light and good for ships and oars.

The word, taken apart.
abiēs is inherited from Proto-Italic abiets 'fir' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short call its further origin uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a beech tree, then the Latin for a fir tree.

You'll want to know: ulmus, ulmī.
ulmus, ulmī — "an elm tree".
Agricolae vītēs ad ulmōs ligant. The farmers tie the vines to the elm trees.
Second declension but feminine: ulmus, ulmī f. Italian farmers trained vines up elms. English elm is a Germanic cousin.

The word, taken apart.
ulmus is inherited from PIE h₁élem 'mountain elm' (EtymDB); English elm is a Germanic cousin from the same root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
opācus, opāca, opācum — shady, shaded; dark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an elm tree, then the Latin for an ash tree.

You'll want to know: opācus, opāca, opācum.
opācus, opāca, opācum — "shady, shaded; dark".
Aestāte viātōrēs locum opācum quaerunt. In summer travellers look for a shady place.
An adjective of three endings. English opaque comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of opācus is not settled: Lewis & Short give no etymology and EtymDB gives no parent. English opaque comes from it through French.
