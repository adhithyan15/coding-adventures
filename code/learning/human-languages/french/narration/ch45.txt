French, chapter 45: Reading — Instructions, a Form, and a First Paragraph.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six things you would be told.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every line below you have heard and said. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them first.

Reading.
Parle! Écoute! On y va! Un peu Beaucoup Merci.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice that three of them end in an exclamation mark and three do not. French puts a space before it, which English does not.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Parle! and Écoute! are the same verbs you conjugate, with the ending taken off and nothing put in its place. That is the whole imperative.
[pause 2 seconds]
An instruction you can only recognise by ear is one somebody has to say to you. On a page it waits, which is why a classroom is the first place a beginner reads rather than listens.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and half of them are one word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which line tells you to listen?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What does French put before an exclamation mark? (A space.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines off a form.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you things somebody says. These are things somebody writes down.
[pause 1 second]
Nothing here is new.

Reading.
Nom: Marie Âge: vingt ans Lundi: le travail Mardi: un livre Une heure Dix minutes.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice the colon. French puts a space before that too, and the space is the clearest signal on the page that a label has ended.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
A form is the first thing a beginner can read end to end. It puts the label before the value every time, and it never asks for a verb.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why the last two lines are the hardest, not the first four. Une heure and Dix minutes have no label in front of them, so nothing tells you what they are for until you have read them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and four of them do half your work.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
How old is she?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two lines have no label? (Une heure and Dix minutes.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What does French put before a colon? (A space.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first paragraph.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six instructions, then six lines off a form. Now a whole paragraph.
[pause 1 second]
Sixty-three words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
Bonjour! Je m'appelle Marie. J'habite à Paris. J'ai un frère et une sœur. Je bois du café et je mange du pain. Le jour, je vais au travail. Je travaille beaucoup, mais j'aime ça. Le soir, je lis un livre. Le livre est très bon. Je ne comprends pas tout, mais ça va. Demain, je vais parler français. Merci et au revoir.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and notice how little work it took.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Look at what is holding it together, because it is not vocabulary.
[pause 2 seconds]
Et adds. Mais holds two true things apart, and it does it twice here — once about work and once about understanding. Each of those was a chapter, and none of them is longer than four letters.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice the tenth line especially. Je ne comprends pas tout, mais ça va. Somebody has admitted they did not follow everything, and the paragraph carries on. That is the most useful sentence on this page.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole paragraph the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the paragraph aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Where does she live?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that paragraph were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word appears twice and turns the sentence both times? (Mais.)
