## You'll want to know: caementārius, caementāriī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C377-FULLO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**caementārius, caementāriī** — "a mason, a builder in stone".

> Caementāriī mūrum novum aedificant.
>
> *The masons are building a new wall.*

*caementārius, caementāriī* m., built on *caementum*, rough-cut stone set in mortar. English *cement* comes from *caementum*. A late word, in Jerome.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*caementārius* is built on *caementum* "rough-cut stone" (Lewis & Short), which comes from *caedō* "to cut". English *cement* comes from *caementum* through French.
