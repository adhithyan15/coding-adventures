Hindi, chapter 172: To stop (someone or something), To run away, To climb, To get down, To stroll.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
रोकना (roknā) — to stop (someone or something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to hide, then the Hindi for to stop.

You'll want to know: रोकना.
रोकना — roknā — "to stop (someone or something)".

The word, taken apart.
रोकना (roknā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from rokk, "stop", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 10827). CDIAL calls its link with Sanskrit rudh-, "to obstruct", not clear.

Lesson 2 of 5.
भागना (bhāgnā) — to run away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to stop, then the Hindi for to stop.

You'll want to know: भागना.
भागना — bhāgnā — "to run away".

The word, taken apart.
भागना (bhāgnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bhagna, "broken, defeated", the past participle that CDIAL lists in its entry for bhajyate, "is broken" (CDIAL 9361). Bloch linked the sense "flee" with a different old verb; CDIAL finds that unnecessary, since "broken" could come to mean "routed, fled".

उतरना (utarnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from uttarati, "passes over; descends" (CDIAL 1770). CDIAL traces the sense from "crosses over" through "lands" to "gets down".
