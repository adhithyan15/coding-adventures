Telugu, chapter 449: Roads Blocked.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
అడ్డంకి (aḍḍaṅki) — an obstacle; a hindrance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to take a turn, then the Telugu for a shortcut.

You'll want to know: అడ్డంకి.
అడ్డంకి — aḍḍaṅki — "an obstacle; a hindrance".
రోడ్డు మీద పడిన చెట్టు పెద్ద అడ్డంకి అయింది. rōḍḍu mīda paḍina ceṭṭu pedda aḍḍaṅki ayindi. The tree that fell on the road became a big obstacle.
అడ్డు, a hindrance, + -ంకి. It goes with plans too: పనికి అడ్డంకి (aḍḍaṅki), an obstacle to the work.

The word, taken apart.
అడ్డంకి (aḍḍaṅki) is native Dravidian, built on అడ్డు, "to hinder; a hindrance, a cross-bar" (DEDR 83), with Tamil aṭai, "to block".

Lesson 2 of 5.
మళ్ళింపు (maḷḷimpu) — a diversion; a detour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a shortcut, then the Telugu for an obstacle.

You'll want to know: మళ్ళింపు.
మళ్ళింపు — maḷḷimpu — "a diversion; a detour".
వంతెన పనుల వల్ల రోడ్డు మీద మళ్ళింపు ఉంది. vantena panula valla rōḍḍu mīda maḷḷimpu undi. There is a diversion on the road because of work on the bridge.
The noun of మళ్ళించు, to turn something aside. మళ్ళీ, again, comes from the same root: to turn back.

The word, taken apart.
మళ్ళింపు (maḷḷimpu) is native Dravidian, the noun of మళ్ళించు, "to turn aside", from మళ్ళు, "to turn back, to return" (DEDR 4761), the root of మళ్ళీ, "again".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for an obstacle, then the Telugu for a diversion.

You'll want to know: నిలిచిపోవు.
నిలిచిపోవు — nilicipōvu — "to stop dead; to come to a halt".
వరద వల్ల రైళ్ళు నిలిచిపోయాయి. varada valla raiḷḷu nilicipōyāyi. Because of the flood the trains stopped running.
Past (I …): నిలిచిపోయాను, nilicipōyānu.
Said of traffic, a service or work that stops and stays stopped.

The word, taken apart.
నిలిచిపోవు (nilicipōvu) is native Dravidian: నిలుచు, "to stand, to stop, to stay" (DEDR 3675), with పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572), which marks a change that has set in.

Lesson 4 of 5.
స్తంభించు (stambhiñcu) — to seize up; to come to a standstill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a diversion, then the Telugu for to stop dead.

You'll want to know: స్తంభించు.
స్తంభించు — stambhiñcu — "to seize up; to come to a standstill".
భారీ వర్షంతో నగరంలో ట్రాఫిక్ స్తంభించింది. bhārī varṣantō nagaraṁlō ṭrāphik stambhiñcindi. With the heavy rain the traffic in the city came to a standstill.
Past (it …): స్తంభించింది, stambhiñcindi.
స్తంభం is a pillar: a road that stands still like a pillar. The news uses it of traffic and of normal life. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: స్తంభించింది, it came to a standstill.

The word, taken apart.
స్తంభించు (stambhiñcu) is built from Sanskrit stambh, "to become stiff, to be fixed", whose noun stambha is "a pillar, a post" (CDIAL 13682), with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మూసుకుపోవు (mūsukupōvu) — to get blocked; to close up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to stop dead, then the Telugu for to seize up.

You'll want to know: మూసుకుపోవు.
మూసుకుపోవు — mūsukupōvu — "to get blocked; to close up".
చెత్త వల్ల కాలువ మూసుకుపోయింది. cetta valla kāluva mūsukupōyindi. The canal got blocked with rubbish.
Past (it …): మూసుకుపోయింది, mūsukupōyindi.
మూయు, to close, + -కో + పోవు. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: మూసుకుపోయింది, it got blocked. A blocked nose is ముక్కు మూసుకుపోయింది.

The word, taken apart.
మూసుకుపోవు (mūsukupōvu) is native Dravidian: మూయు, "to cover, to shut" (DEDR 4915), with the self-directed -కో and పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572).
