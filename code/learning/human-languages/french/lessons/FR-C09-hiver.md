# l'hiver — winter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last season, and it shares a root with the deepest sleep in the
animal world.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-HIVER-05]; assesses=[] -->

- `silent-h` — the **h** is silent, exactly as in **heure**. And because a
  silent *h* is no obstacle, the article elides straight through it:
  **l'hiver**, *lee-VAIR*.
- The **r** at the end is the throat-**r**, and it **is** sounded.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-HIVER-HIBERNER-06]; assesses=[] -->

**hiver** is Latin **hībernum**, short for *hībernum tempus* — "the **wintry**
season." Like *printemps*, it is a *tempus* phrase with the *tempus* worn off.

Now follow that root sideways. English **hibernate** is *hībernāre*, and it means,
with no metaphor at all, "**to spend the winter**." A bear hibernating is a bear
*wintering*.

So the same Latin word gave French a season and English a kind of sleep — one
naming the time, the other naming what you do in it.
