Russian, chapter 224: Birch, Spruce, Pine, Bush, Sheep.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
берёза (beryóza) — a birch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a banana, then the Russian for grapes.

You'll want to know: берёза.
берёза (beryóza) — "a birch".

The word, taken apart.
берёза (beryóza) from Proto-Slavic berza — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian béržas, Sanskrit bhūrja-, Old Norse bjǫrk and Old High German birihha: English birch is a cousin, from a root "bright, white".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Russian for a birch, then the Russian for a spruce.

You'll want to know: сосна.
сосна (sosná) — "a pine".

The word, taken apart.
сосна (sosná) from Proto-Slavic sosna. Vasmer derives it most likely from a PIE ḱasnos, "grey", as in Latin cānus, "grey", and Old High German haso, "hare"; Trubachev, in a note, doubts this.

Lesson 5 of 5.
овца (ovtsá) — a sheep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pine, then the Russian for a bush.

You'll want to know: овца.
овца (ovtsá) — "a sheep".

The word, taken apart.
овца (ovtsá) from Proto-Slavic ovĭca, a diminutive of the old word for "sheep" — inherited. Vasmer lists Sanskrit ávi-, Lithuanian avìs, Greek óis and Latin ovis: English ewe is a cousin.
