French, chapter 316: Célébrer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
célébrer — to celebrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to inherit, then the French for the inheritance.

You'll want to know: célébrer.
célébrer — "to celebrate".
La ville a célébré la fin de la guerre. The town celebrated the end of the war.
More formal than fêter; also for a mass or a wedding: célébrer un mariage.

The word, taken apart.
célébrer is a learned borrowing from Latin celebrāre "to frequent, to celebrate, to honour" (EtymDB), built on celeber "crowded, famous".

You'll want to know: le cortège.
le cortège — "the procession".
Le cortège a traversé le centre-ville. The procession went through the town centre.
A wedding, funeral or protest procession.

The word, taken apart.
cortège was borrowed from Italian corteggio "a retinue" (EtymDB), built on corte "court", from Latin cohors "an enclosure, a company" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la fanfare — the brass band; a fanfare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the celebration, then the French for the procession.

You'll want to know: la fanfare.
la fanfare — "the brass band; a fanfare".
La fanfare jouait devant la mairie. The brass band was playing in front of the town hall.
En fanfare — with a lot of noise.

The word, taken apart.
fanfare is usually explained as imitating the sound of trumpets; the origin is not settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le jour férié — the public holiday, the bank holiday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the procession, then the French for the brass band.

You'll want to know: le jour férié.
le jour férié — "the public holiday, the bank holiday".
Le premier mai est un jour férié. The first of May is a public holiday.
Faire le pont — to take the day off between a holiday and the weekend.

The word, taken apart.
férié is a learned borrowing from Latin fēriātus "on holiday", from fēriae "days of rest, holidays" (Lewis & Short); jour comes from Latin diurnum.
