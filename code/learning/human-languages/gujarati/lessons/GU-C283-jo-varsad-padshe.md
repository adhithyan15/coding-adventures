# જો વરસાદ પડશે, તો … (jo varsād paḍśe, to …) — if it happens

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C283-JO-TO-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C149-ISH-01, GU-LEX-C204-TIMES204-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if you have a fever, then you should rest". (*jo tamne tāv che, to tamāre ārām karvo joīe*.) Then "we will stay". (*ame rahīśũ*.)

## Grammar Lens: the future after જો
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C283-IF-FUTURE-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-VARSAAD-01, GU-LEX-C214-RAIN214-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-RAHEVUN-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-NEGATIVE-FUTURE-01] -->

> **જો વરસાદ પડશે, તો અમે ઘરે રહીશું.**
>
> *jo varsād paḍśe, to ame ghare rahīśũ.* — "If it rains, we'll stay at home."

English says "if it rains"; Gujarati says "if it will rain": a condition about later takes the future, **પડશે**, and so does the result, **રહીશું**. "If it doesn't rain" is the **નહીં** form: **જો વરસાદ નહીં પડે, તો અમે જઈશું** (*jo varsād nahī̃ paḍe, to ame jaīśũ*).

## You'll want to know: the past after જો
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C283-IF-PERFECTIVE-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C85-TRAVEL-02, GU-LEX-C85-TRAVEL-01, GU-LEX-AAVVU, GU-LEX-C195-LOST195-01] -->

> **જો ટ્રેન છૂટી ગઈ, તો હું બસમાં આવીશ.**
>
> *jo ṭren chūṭī gaī, to hũ basmā̃ āvīś.* — "If I miss the train, I'll come by bus."

When the condition would be over before the result starts, it can take the plain past: "if the train has gone, then …". **જો મારું પાકીટ ખોવાઈ ગયું, તો હું પોલીસમાં ફરિયાદ કરીશ** (*jo mārũ pākīṭ khovāī gayũ, to hũ polīsmā̃ phariyād karīś*).
