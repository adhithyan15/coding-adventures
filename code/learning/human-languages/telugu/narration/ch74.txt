Telugu, chapter 74: And, But, Or.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
మరియు (mariyu) — and.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did vagaru mean?

You'll want to know: మరియు.
మరియు (mariyu) — "and".
Telugu's everyday speech often joins two things by simply saying them one after the other, or by hanging a small -ఉ on the end of each. మరియు (mariyu) is the written, spoken-carefully form, and it is the one a list on a page will use.
Put it between two nouns you already have: నీళ్ళు మరియు (mariyu) పాలు — "water and milk".

What you've built.
The first way you have had to put two things in one sentence.

Lesson 2 of 5.
కానీ (kānī) — but.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mariyu mean?

You'll want to know: కానీ.
కానీ (kānī) — "but".
కానీ (kānī) turns a sentence around. Where మరియు (mariyu) adds, కానీ (kānī) takes back: it warns the listener that what comes next does not match what came before.
నాకు టీ ఇష్టం, కానీ (kānī) పాలు వద్దు — "I like tea, but no milk." This one word is what lets you disagree politely instead of only saying లేదు.

What you've built.
Two clauses, and a way to set the second against the first.

Lesson 3 of 5.
లేదా (lēdā) — or.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kānī mean?

You'll want to know: లేదా.
లేదా (lēdā) — "or".
You have met its first half already: లే- is the same root behind లేదు, "there isn't". లేదా (lēdā) is literally the or-else of that root — "failing that".
టీ లేదా (lēdā) పాలు? — "tea or milk?" That is the whole of an offered choice, and you can now make one.

What you've built.
A choice you can put to somebody.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఎందుకు (enduku) — why?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did lēdā mean?

You'll want to know: ఎందుకు.
ఎందుకు (enduku) — "why?".
Another word off the ఎ- question row you already know: ఎక్కడ "where", ఎవరు "who", and now ఎందుకు (enduku) "why". The row keeps its shape and you keep getting the words free.
It goes where its answer goes, not at the front: మీరు ఎందుకు (enduku) వెళ్తారు? — "why do you go?"

What you've built.
The question the next word answers.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) — because.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did enduku mean?

You'll want to know: ఎందుకంటే.
ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) — "because".
Look at it: it is ఎందుకు (enduku) with అంటే glued on — "if you ask why". Telugu answers the question by repeating it and then continuing, which is the gluing habit of your very first lesson doing real work.
నేను వెళ్తాను, ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) పని ఉంది — "I'll go, because there is work."

Grammar Lens: the reason clause comes second.
Telugu can put the reason first, as పని ఉంది కాబట్టి నేను వెళ్తాను. With ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) it goes second, after the thing being explained, exactly where English puts it. Use ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) while that ordering is still the one you think in.

What you've built.
A reason, attached to the thing it explains.
