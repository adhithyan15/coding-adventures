5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
عصير (ʿaṣīr) — juice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for onions, then the Arabic for breakfast.

You'll want to know: عصير.
عصير — ʿaṣīr — "juice".

The word, taken apart.
عصير (ʿaṣīr) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-ṣ-r, "to press, to squeeze" (Lane). ʿAṣīr is what is pressed out: juice.

Lesson 4 of 5.
غرفة (ghurfa) — a room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for chicken, then the Arabic for a house.

You'll want to know: غرفة.
غرفة — ghurfa — "a room".

The word, taken apart.
غرفة (ghurfa) is built on the native Arabic root gh-r-f, "to ladle out water with the hand" (Lane). Lane gives ghurfa as a handful of water, and also as an upper chamber, the sense that became "a room".

Lesson 5 of 5.
نافذة (nāfidha) — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a house, then the Arabic for a room.

You'll want to know: نافذة.
نافذة — nāfidha — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
نافذة (nāfidha) is built on the native Arabic root n-f-dh, "to pass through, to pierce" (Lane). Nāfidha is the feminine active participle, "one that passes through"; the window sense is not among Lane's.
