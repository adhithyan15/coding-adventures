# (reading) — the third paragraph

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING] -->

[PAUSE 2s] This one is longer than both of the others. You are not going to
notice.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-INDICATIVE-COMPLETE] -->

> El sábado María y su hermana van a casa de su madre.
> Su madre es profesora y habla español muy bien.
> Por la mañana comen pan y beben café.
> Luego María lee un libro y su hermana escribe.
> Por la tarde caminan hasta la casa de su hermano.
> Ahora es tarde, pero están bien.
> Mañana es domingo, y María no trabaja.

[PAUSE 3s] Once through, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Now answer this before you read it again: how many people are in it?

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PERO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Four people, and Spanish named each one once. After that they are
*su madre*, *su hermana*, *su hermano* — held in place by a single small word
you learned a long time ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Count what you actually did here. Sixty-one words. Four people. Two
days. One page of grammar you already had.

[PAUSE 2s] The first paragraph in this chapter was forty-seven words and felt
like a wall. This one is longer and did not. Nothing was added in between
except two more paragraphs — which is the only thing that ever fixes this.
