# mucha agua · pocos libros — when the quantity word agrees

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MUCHO, ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-MUY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Mucho café*. And *muy grande*, where the short form appeared
because an adjective followed.

[PAUSE 1s] Now try *mucho* in front of a **feminine** noun and listen to what
your mouth wants.

## Grammar Lens: in front of a noun, it agrees
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-QUANTIFIER-AGREES-WITH-NOUN]; assesses=[ES-LEX-AGUA-01, ES-GRAMMAR-AGUA-ARTICLE-03] -->

> ***mucha*** *agua* · ***muchos*** *libros* · ***pocas*** *casas*

[PAUSE 2s] When *mucho* or *poco* sits in front of a noun it is behaving like an
adjective, so it does what every adjective does: it takes the gender and the
number of the noun.

[PAUSE 2s] And *agua* is the test case you already own. It takes *el* for a
sound reason while being feminine underneath — so it is ***mucha*** *agua*,
never *mucho agua*. The quantity word reports the real gender, exactly as the
object pronoun did.

## Grammar Lens: after a verb, it freezes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-QUANTIFIER-FROZEN-AFTER-VERB]; assesses=[ES-LEX-POCO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now move it behind a verb, where there is no noun for it to lean on:

> *Ella trabaja* ***mucho***. — She works a lot.
> *Ella come* ***poco***. — She eats little.

[PAUSE 2s] **No agreement.** Not *mucha* because *ella* is feminine — the word
is not describing *her*, it is describing the working. A verb has no gender and
no number, so there is nothing to agree with, and the word freezes in its
plain masculine-singular shape.

[PAUSE 2s] So one test decides it: **is it leaning on a noun, or on a verb?**
