You'll want to know: らいしゅう.
らいしゅう — raishū — "next week": せんしゅう, こんしゅう, らいしゅう, last week, this week, next week.
Japanese has no separate future tense. The polite present ます says what will happen too, and a time word places it:
らいしゅう はたらきます。 — raishū hatarakimasu — "I will work next week."

Lesson 4 of 5.
つもり (tsumori) — an intention, a plan (I mean to...)

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Three recalls before the new word.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: つもり.
つもり — tsumori — "an intention", "a plan": what you mean to do. It follows a verb in its dictionary form, and です closes the sentence.
らいねん うみに いく つもりです。 — rainen umi ni iku tsumori desu — "I plan to go to the sea next year."
Here the dictionary form matters: iku, not ikimasu, comes before つもり (tsumori). ます goes at the very end of a polite sentence, and です does that job here.

You'll want to know: でしょう.
でしょう — deshō — "probably", "it will..., I expect". It closes a sentence about something you think will happen but cannot be sure of, the way a weather forecast does.
あしたは すずしいでしょう。 — ashita wa suzushii deshō — "It will probably be cool tomorrow."
Now you can talk about what comes next: what will happen (ます with a time word), what you mean to do (つもり (tsumori)), and what will probably happen (でしょう (deshō)).
