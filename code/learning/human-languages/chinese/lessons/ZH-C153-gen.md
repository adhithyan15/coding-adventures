## You'll want to know: gēn
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C153-GEN-01]; assesses=[] -->

*gēn* — "with; and"; with *yíyàng* after it, "the same as".

> *wǒ gēn tā yíyàng gāo* — I'm as tall as he is.

> *wǒ gēn māma qù* — I'm going with Mum.

Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*gēn* is inherited from Middle Chinese *kon* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the heel'. How the heel became 'with', following at someone's heels, is a guess the gloss does not make, so the history is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-BI-COMPARE-01, ZH-LEX-C94-YIYANG-01] -->

*gēn* … *yíyàng*, then an adjective, says two things are the same: *wǒ gēn tā yíyàng gāo*.

比 says one is more; *gēn* … *yíyàng* says neither is.
