Portuguese, chapter 476: Uma grande desilusão.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a desilusão — disappointment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to calm, then the Portuguese for relief.

You'll want to know: a desilusão.
a desilusão — "disappointment".
O concerto foi uma desilusão. The concert was a disappointment.
Uma desilusão amorosa — a heartbreak.

The word, taken apart.
desilusão is built from des- and ilusão "illusion", a learned borrowing from Latin illūsiō "mockery, deceit" (Lewis & Short), from lūdere "to play".

Lesson 3 of 5.
desanimado — discouraged, down.

You'll want to know: desanimado.
desanimado — "discouraged, down".
Depois da derrota, a equipa estava desanimada. After the defeat, the team was down.
Animado is the opposite: in good spirits.

The word, taken apart.
desanimado is built from des- and animar "to cheer up", from Latin animāre "to give life to" (Lewis & Short), built on anima "breath, soul".

consolar comes from Latin cōnsōlārī "to comfort, to console" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
desabafar — to get something off your chest, to unburden yourself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for discouraged, then the Portuguese for to console.

You'll want to know: desabafar.
desabafar — "to get something off your chest, to unburden yourself".
Precisava de desabafar e telefonei à minha irmã. I needed to get it off my chest, so I phoned my sister.
Desabafar com alguém — to pour your heart out to someone.

The word, taken apart.
desabafar is usually explained as des- plus abafar "to smother, to cover": to uncover what was held in. EtymDB lists the word without a source (EtymDB), and the origin of abafar is not settled.
