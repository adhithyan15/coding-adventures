Gujarati, chapter 156: Jewellery, bindi and henna.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઘરેણું (ghareṇũ) — ornament, piece of jewellery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for perfume, then the Gujarati for to apply.

You'll want to know: ઘરેણું.
ઘરેણું — ghareṇũ — "ornament, piece of jewellery".
દાદી પાસે જૂનું ઘરેણું છે. — dādī pāse jūnũ ghareṇũ che — "Grandmother has an old piece of jewellery."

The word, taken apart.
ઘરેણું (ghareṇũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "jewelry", under Sanskrit grahaṇa, "seizing, holding", in its second sense, "putting on (clothes)"; the same entry gives a Gujarati ghareṇe, "in pawn", under the first sense (CDIAL 4364).

Lesson 3 of 5.
મેંદી (mẽdī) — henna.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for ornament, then the Gujarati for bindi.

You'll want to know: મેંદી.
મેંદી — mẽdī — "henna".
તહેવારમાં છોકરીઓ મેંદી લગાડે છે. — tahevārmā̃ chokrīo mẽdī lagāḍe che — "Girls put on henna at festivals."

The word, taken apart.
મેંદી (mẽdī) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit mēndhī, mēndhikā, the plant Lawsonia alba, which gives henna, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word with that sense (CDIAL 10328).

Lesson 5 of 5.
બાંધવું (bā̃dhvũ) — to tie, to bind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for henna, then the Gujarati for to decorate.

You'll want to know: બાંધવું.
બાંધવું — bā̃dhvũ — "to tie, to bind".
તે વાળ બાંધે છે. — te vāḷ bā̃dhe che — "She ties up her hair."

The word, taken apart.
બાંધવું (bā̃dhvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bandhati, "binds", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to bind, tie" (CDIAL 9139).
