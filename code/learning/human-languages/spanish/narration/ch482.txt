Lesson 1 of 5.
concreto — specific, particular.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to foresee, then the Spanish for to watch.

You'll want to know: concreto.
concreto — "specific, particular".
¿Buscas algo concreto? Are you looking for something specific?
En concreto — specifically.

The word, taken apart.
concreto from Latin concretus, "grown together, hardened" (EtymDB 2.1), the participle of concrescere: con- and crescere, "to grow" — a learned word. Something concrete has set solid.

Lesson 2 of 5.
parecido — similar, alike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to watch, then the Spanish for specific.

You'll want to know: parecido.
parecido — "similar, alike".
Tu bolso es parecido al mío. Your bag is similar to mine.
It goes with a: parecido a.

The word, taken apart.
parecido is the participle of parecer, "to seem, to look like", from spoken Latin parescere, built on parere, "to appear" (Lewis & Short). Things that are parecidos look like each other.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: justo.
justo — "fair; exactly; tight".
No es justo. It is not fair.
Justo a tiempo — right on time; me queda justo — it is a tight fit.

The word, taken apart.
justo from Latin iustus, "upright, righteous" (Lewis & Short), from ius, "right, law". "Exactly" and "tight" grew out of "fitting, right".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rápido — fast, quick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for similar, then the Spanish for fair.

You'll want to know: rápido.
rápido — "fast, quick".
El tren rápido llega antes. The fast train gets there sooner.
As an adverb it also means quickly: ven rápido.

The word, taken apart.
rápido from Latin rapidus, "snatching, swift" (EtymDB 2.1), from rapere, "to snatch" — a learned borrowing: the stress stays on the first syllable and the -p- is kept.
