Mandarin Chinese, chapter 194: Roads and Crossings.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
马路 (mǎlù) — road; a street for traffic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to haggle, then the Mandarin for quality.

You'll want to know: 马路.
马路 — mǎlù — "road; a street for traffic".
我家在马路对面 wǒ jiā zài mǎlù duìmiàn — My home is across the road.
The new character, in pieces: 路 (lù) — thirteen strokes in two halves: on the left the foot piece, 口 over four short strokes; on the right a short fall, a bar that turns and falls to the left, a long stroke falling to the right, and 口 under them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
马路 (mǎlù) is built from 马 'horse' and 路 (lù) 'road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, maeX and luH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Why a road for traffic is named after horses is not settled.

Script — the foot piece, then three strokes over 口.
路 (lù).
Two halves, left before right, and each has 口 in it.
The left half, the foot piece, seven strokes. 口 at the top, narrow. Under it a short vertical, a short bar from its middle to the right, a short vertical on the left, and a stroke along the foot that rises to the right.
The right half, six strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. A bar that turns and falls down to the left. A long stroke falling down to the right. Then 口 underneath.
7 + 6 = thirteen.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 路 (lù)? (Thirteen.) Where is 口 in the left half? (At the top.) And in the right half? (At the bottom.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
zǒulù — to walk; to go on foot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for quality, then the Mandarin for a street for traffic.

You'll want to know: zǒulù.
zǒulù — "to walk; to go on foot".
wǒ měitiān zǒulù qù shàngbān — I walk to work every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zǒulù is built from zǒu and 路 (lù) 'road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsuwX and luH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to hurry'; in Mandarin it has come to mean walking.

Lesson 4 of 6.
bānmǎxiàn — zebra crossing; pedestrian crossing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a street for traffic, then the Mandarin for to go on foot.

You'll want to know: bānmǎxiàn.
bānmǎxiàn — "zebra crossing; pedestrian crossing".
zhèr méiyǒu bānmǎxiàn — There's no zebra crossing here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bānmǎxiàn is built from bānmǎ 'zebra', literally a mottled horse, and xiàn 'a line'. 马 and xiàn are inherited from Middle Chinese, maeX and sjenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists bān as paen, glossed 'mottled' and 'patterned': a crossing striped like a zebra.

Lesson 5 of 6.
guǎngchǎng — public square.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go on foot, then the Mandarin for zebra crossing.

You'll want to know: guǎngchǎng.
guǎngchǎng — "public square".
huǒchēzhàn qiánbian yǒu yí ge guǎngchǎng — There's a square in front of the station.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: guǎng is said with a rising tone before chǎng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
guǎngchǎng is built from guǎng 'wide' and chǎng 'an open ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwangX and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'big' and 'broad'.
