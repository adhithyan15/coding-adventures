# ഃ (ḥ) — the two rings inside ദുഃഖം

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-120, ML-SCRIPT-C113-KHA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write **ം** and **ഖ** once each, and name them. (The anusvaram, *ṁ*,
and *kha*.)

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ദുഃഖം** (*duḥkhaṁ*), "grief". Both of those are in it. This lesson takes out
the one mark you have not written and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ഃ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-SCRIPT-C345-VISARGA-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C345-DUHKHAM-01] -->

**ഃ** — the visarga, *ḥ*: a short breath after a vowel. It is two small rings, one above the other,
written after its syllable: **ദു** + **ഃ** → **ദുഃ**, *duḥ*.

Do not confuse it with **ം**, one ring, which is *ṁ*. The visarga lives in words taken from
Sanskrit, and most of them are learned words.

## Writing: ഃ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-C345-VISARGA-01] -->

One recorded hand writes **ഃ** in two strokes: the upper ring first, then, after a lift, the lower
one. Each ring starts at its top and goes round anticlockwise, to the left first.

Write **ഃ** yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed: two rings the same size, one above the
other. Copy the printed shape and keep the
order above.
