## You'll want to know: பழம்
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C44-ASK-01]; assesses=[] -->

**பழம்** (*paḻam*) — "a fruit".

Underneath it is a root about ripening. Proto-Dravidian **\*paḻ**, 'to ripen, to grow old', handed this family both a fruit and an age, and Tamil kept the pair close enough to hear: *paḻaiya*, the "old" from the run of describing words, is that same root wearing a different ending. Ripe and old are one idea seen twice.

Malayalam says പഴം, very nearly the identical word — the ordinary state of affairs between the two closest sisters. Telugu went to పండు (*paṇḍu*) and Kannada to ಹಣ್ಣು (*haṇṇu*), and the Kannada form has lost its opening *p-* to a shift Tamil sat out entirely.

The ழ in the middle is the letter this book has already spent a writing lesson on, the one only these two sisters still write.
