Gujarati, chapter 498: The Bride Leaves Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વિદાય (vidāy) — a farewell; the bride's send-off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the groom's procession, then the Gujarati for to marry off.

You'll want to know: વિદાય.
વિદાય — vidāy — "a farewell; the bride's send-off".
દીકરીની વિદાય વખતે માતા રડી પડી. dīkrīnī vidāy vakhte mātā raḍī paḍī. At her daughter's send-off the mother burst into tears.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વિદાય (vidāy) is recorded by Monier-Williams as Sanskrit vidāya, "permission to go away, dismissal with good wishes", with a note that in this sense it is perhaps not a Sanskrit word at all and a comparison with Arabic wadāʿ, "farewell"; its history is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
કુંવારું (kunvārũ) — unmarried.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a farewell, then the Gujarati for a bride.

You'll want to know: કુંવારું.
કુંવારું — kunvārũ — "unmarried".
મારા મામા હજી કુંવારા છે. mārā māmā hajī kunvārā che. My uncle is still unmarried.

The word, taken apart.
કુંવારું (kunvārũ) is inherited from Sanskrit kumāra, "a boy, a youth"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "unmarried" (CDIAL 3303).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a bride, then the Gujarati for unmarried.

દહેજ — dahej — "a dowry".
દહેજ માગવું ખોટું છે. dahej māgvũ khoṭũ che. Demanding a dowry is wrong.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
દહેજ (dahej) is usually traced to Arabic jahāz, "equipment, a trousseau", which reached Gujarati through Persian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
છૂટાછેડા (chūṭācheḍā) — a divorce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for unmarried, then the Gujarati for a dowry.

You'll want to know: છૂટાછેડા.
છૂટાછેડા — chūṭācheḍā — "a divorce".
દસ વરસ પછી તેમના છૂટાછેડા થયા. das varas pachī temnā chūṭācheḍā thayā. After ten years they got divorced.
A masculine noun, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
છૂટાછેડા (chūṭācheḍā) is built from chūṭā, "loosened, apart", on the stem of છૂટવું (chūṭvũ), "to get loose" (CDIAL 3707), and cheḍā, "ends": the ends of cloth tied together at a wedding come apart.
