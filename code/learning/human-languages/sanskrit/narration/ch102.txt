Lesson 1 of 5.
स्थाली (sthālī) — a plate, a pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for glasses, then the Sanskrit for a bag.

You'll want to know: स्थाली.
स्थाली — sthālī — "a plate, a pot".

The word, taken apart.
sthālī "an earthen dish, a cooking pot" is already in the Atharvaveda, and MW gives no derivation for it, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi thālī and Marathi thāḷī, the plate a whole meal is served on (CDIAL 13766).

चषकः — caṣakaḥ — "a cup".

The word, taken apart.
caṣaka "a cup, a wine-glass" belongs to classical poetry (MW). CDIAL 4726a, after EWA, takes it as borrowed from Iranian, while EtymDB links it with a root √caṣ "eat", so its origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
चमसः (camasaḥ) — a spoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a plate, then the Sanskrit for a cup.

You'll want to know: चमसः.
चमसः — camasaḥ — "a spoon".

The word, taken apart.
camasa comes from the root √cam "sip, drink" (MW). In the Rigveda it is the wooden cup or ladle the Soma was drunk from; "spoon" is its everyday sense today.

Lesson 4 of 5.
चुल्ली (cullī) — a stove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cup, then the Sanskrit for a spoon.

You'll want to know: चुल्ली.
चुल्ली — cullī — "a stove".

The word, taken apart.
cullī "a fireplace, a hearth" is often taken to be borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 4879 says so after EWA, and DEDR 2709 sets it beside Dravidian words for a furnace or kiln. It lives on as Hindi cūlhā "stove" (CDIAL 4879).

Lesson 5 of 5.
वर्तिका (vartikā) — a candle, a wick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a spoon, then the Sanskrit for a stove.

You'll want to know: वर्तिका.
वर्तिका — vartikā — "a candle, a wick".

The word, taken apart.
vartikā is varti "wick" with the suffix -kā, from the root √vṛt "turn, roll" (CDIAL 11359): a wick is a rolled strip. It lives on as Hindi battī "wick, lamp" (CDIAL 11359); using it for a candle is modern.
