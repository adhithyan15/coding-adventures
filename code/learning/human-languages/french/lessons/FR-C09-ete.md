# l'été — summer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Spring was named for its **place** in the year. Summer is named for
what it **feels like**.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-ETE-03]; assesses=[] -->

- **été** = *ay-TAY*. Both **é** are the bright, narrow *ay* you met in
  **février**.
- `elision` — the article does not stay *le*. Before a vowel sound it drops its
  own vowel and clamps on: *le été* is impossible, so it becomes **l'été**,
  said as one word, *lay-TAY*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**été** is Latin **aestās**, and *aestās* means "**heat**."

Not "the third season" or "the warm months" — just *heat*. Summer, in Latin and
so in French, is named for the thing itself. English keeps the same root in
**estival**, "of summer," and in **aestivate**, which is what an animal does when
it sleeps through the hot season rather than the cold one.
