# ا and م — finish **سلام** without a cliff

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-C01-SALAM-01, AR-SCRIPT-SIN-LAM-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall **س** and **ل**. Add just two shapes, then the spoken word
*salām* becomes readable and writable.

## Script — observe two new shapes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SCRIPT-WRITE-SALAM-03, AR-SCRIPT-JOINING-FORMS-04]; assesses=[AR-C01-SALAM-01, AR-SCRIPT-SIN-LAM-02] -->

- **ا** is *alif*: one clean upright carrying the long *ā* here. From
  Phoenician **ʾālep** ("ox") — ancestor of Greek *alpha* and Latin **A**.
- **م** is *mīm*: a small round head and a tail; it gives the final *m*. From
  Phoenician **mem** ("water") — sibling of Greek *mu* and Latin **M**.

Arabic letters join — and a joined letter **changes shape**. The same letter
looks different at the start, the middle and the end of a joined run. Its
skeleton never changes (three teeth stay three teeth); only the connectors at
its edges do. This is the one habit that makes Arabic read like handwriting
rather than like print.

Watch all of it happen in one short word. In **سلام**, *sīn* reaches leftward
into *lām*; *lām* and *alif* fuse into the fixed pair **لا**; then *alif*, which
never joins leftward, forces a pen lift before final *mīm*.

## Writing — guided copy, then delayed copy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-C01-SALAM-01, AR-SCRIPT-SIN-LAM-02, AR-SCRIPT-WRITE-SALAM-03, AR-SCRIPT-JOINING-FORMS-04] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: delayed-copy -->

1. Point right to left: **س · ل · ا · م**. Say *s · l · ā · m*.
2. Trace **سلام** once: teeth, tall hook, upright, lift, round-headed *mīm*.
3. Copy **سلام** once while looking.
4. Cover it. Write it once from memory, then compare and correct one feature.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-C01-SALAM-01, AR-SCRIPT-SIN-LAM-02, AR-SCRIPT-WRITE-SALAM-03, AR-SCRIPT-JOINING-FORMS-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **سلام**. (**salām, peace.**) What changes when a letter joins,
and what stays the same? (**The connectors at its edges change; its skeleton
does not.**) Where does the pen lift? (**After alif.**) Write the word once
without looking. This is delayed recall, not penmanship perfection.
