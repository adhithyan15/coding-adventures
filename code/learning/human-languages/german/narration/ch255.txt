German, chapter 255: You Should — du solltest, and Explaining Why.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
du solltest — you should.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I can take the umbrella". (Ich kann den Regenschirm mitnehmen.) The modal second, the infinitive at the end.

You'll want to know: du solltest.
du solltest — "you should": doo ZOLL-test.
Du solltest den Regenschirm mitnehmen. — You should take the umbrella.
It is a modal, so it builds the same bracket as kann: solltest second, the infinitive last. Its forms: ich sollte, du solltest, er sollte, wir sollten, Sie sollten.
Solltest looks like a past, and it is spelled exactly like the Präteritum of sollen. Here it is the Konjunktiv II, the mood of "it would be good if". That is what makes it advice. The plain present, du sollst, is stronger: it passes on an order or a duty, "you are to".

Grammar Lens: man sollte, and two verbs at the end.
For advice to everybody, German uses man, "one, people":
Man sollte den Fahrplan lesen. — One should read the timetable.
Man takes the er form of the verb: man sollte, like er sollte.
After dass or weil, both verbs go to the end, and the modal comes last of all:
Ich finde, dass du den Regenschirm mitnehmen solltest.

The word, taken apart.
sollen from Old High German sculan from Proto-Germanic skulaną, "to owe" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB sets English shall and should beside it: should is the past of shall, as solltest is of sollen. You met the same root in Entschuldigung, inside Schuld, a debt or a fault: what you should do is what you owe.

Lesson 2 of 3.
erklären, warum — explaining why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to explain". (Erklären.) Then ask "why is the train late?" (Warum hat der Zug Verspätung?)

Grammar Lens: a question inside a sentence.
Put that question inside a request and its verb moves to the end:
Warum hat der Zug Verspätung? — Why is the train late? Können Sie mir erklären, warum der Zug Verspätung hat? — Can you explain to me why the train is late?
Inside a sentence, warum opens a clause the way weil and dass do. Wie, wo and wann behave the same way: Ich erkläre dir, wie das geht. Weißt du, wann der Zug kommt?
Erklären takes the person in the dative: mir, dir, Ihnen, "to me, to you".

Why it's said this way: an explanation in three steps.
A clear explanation in German moves the way it does in English: what, why, and what follows.
Ich komme heute nicht. — what. Ich komme nicht, weil ich Fieber habe. — why, with weil. Darum rufe ich dich an. — what follows, with darum.
Each step has its own word order: plain, verb last, verb second. Said in a row, the three hold together as one explanation.

The word, taken apart.
erklären is er- + klar, "clear": to make clear. EtymDB 2.1 traces klar through Middle High German klār to Latin clārus, "bright, clear". The noun die Erklärung, the explanation, is built on the same verb.

Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — what you should do, and why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you should read the timetable". (Du solltest den Fahrplan lesen.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, and the whole of giving reasons is in place. An opinion, with dass or meiner Meinung nach. A reason with weil, a consequence with darum. Advice with du solltest or man sollte. And a question folded into a sentence, with its verb at the end: erklären, warum …
[pause 2 seconds]
Here is a friend's problem, and an answer that uses three of those pieces:
Der Zug hat Verspätung. Was soll ich machen? Meiner Meinung nach solltest du ein Taxi nehmen, weil es regnet.
Listen to the answer: after meiner Meinung nach, the modal comes straight next, solltest du, and the infinitive nehmen waits at the end of its clause.
