Mandarin Chinese, chapter 254: Born and Married.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
出生 (chūshēng) — to be born.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sons and daughters, then the Mandarin for a relative.

You'll want to know: 出生.
出生 — chūshēng — "to be born".
他的孩子出生了 tā de háizi chūshēng le — His baby has been born.

The word, taken apart.
出生 (chūshēng) is built from 出 'to come out' and 生 'to grow', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and srjaeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to grow'.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get married, then the Mandarin for to graduate.

You'll want to know: hūnlǐ.
hūnlǐ — "wedding".
wǒmen qù cānjiā tāmen de hūnlǐ — We're going to their wedding.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hūnlǐ is built from hūn 'marriage' and lǐ 'a rite', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwon and lejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older dictionary for the second: what is done to serve the spirits.
