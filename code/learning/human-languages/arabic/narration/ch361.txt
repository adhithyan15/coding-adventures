Arabic, chapter 361: Issues and questions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قضية (qaḍiyya) — a public issue; a cause; a court case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to argue, then the Arabic for a controversy.

You'll want to know: قضية.
قضية — qaḍiyya — "a public issue; a cause; a court case".
تكلمنا عن قضية (qaḍiyya) الماء في القرية. takallamnā ʿan qaḍiyyat al-māʾ fī al-qarya. We talked about the issue of water in the village.
Plural قضايا, qaḍāyā. In a court it is a case.

The word, taken apart.
قضية (qaḍiyya) is built on the native Arabic root q-ḍ-y, "to decide, to settle" (Lane). A qaḍiyya is a matter to be decided: a case, a cause, an issue.

مسألة (masʾala) is built on the native Arabic root s-ʾ-l, "to ask" (Lane). A masʾala is a thing asked about: a question to settle, a matter.

سياق — siyāq — "a context".
فهمت معنى الكلمة من السياق. fahimtu maʿnā al-kalima min as-siyāq. I understood the meaning of the word from the context.

The word, taken apart.
سياق (siyāq) is built on the native Arabic root s-w-q, "to drive along" (Lane). A siyāq is a driving along in order; in modern use, the run of words around a word: its context.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أثار (athāra) — to stir up; to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a matter, then the Arabic for a context.

You'll want to know: أثار.
أثار — athāra — "to stir up; to raise".
أثار الخبر اهتمام الناس. athāra al-khabar ihtimām an-nās. The news stirred up people's interest.
Present: يثير, yuthīr.
أثار (athāra) مشكلة, raised a problem.

The word, taken apart.
أثار (athāra) is built on the native Arabic root th-w-r, "to rise up, to be stirred" (Lane). It is Form IV, to stir up: to raise dust, and so to raise feelings or a question.

تساءل (tasāʾala) is built on the native Arabic root s-ʾ-l, "to ask" (Lane). It is Form VI of saʾala, "to ask": to ask oneself, to wonder.
