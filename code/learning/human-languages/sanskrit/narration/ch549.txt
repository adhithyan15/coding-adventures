Lesson 1 of 5.
स्पृहयति (spṛhayati) — is eager for; longs for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for flustered, then the Sanskrit for a refuge.

You'll want to know: स्पृहयति.
स्पृहयति — spṛhayati — "is eager for; longs for".
यदि भवान् ज्ञानाय स्पृहयति, तर्हि प्रतिदिनं पठतु। yadi bhavān jñānāya spṛhayati, tarhi pratidinaṃ paṭhatu. If you long for knowledge, read every day.
Past (he …): सः ज्ञानाय स्पृहितवान्, saḥ jñānāya spṛhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
स्पृहयति (spṛhayati) comes from the root √spṛh, "to long for, to desire", which takes the dative; MW compares Greek sperchō and English spring (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
लालसा (lālasā) — a craving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a refuge, then the Sanskrit for is eager for.

You'll want to know: लालसा.
लालसा — lālasā — "a craving".
यदि मधुरे लालसा अस्ति, तर्हि एकं मोदकं खादतु। yadi madhure lālasā asti, tarhi ekaṃ modakaṃ khādatu. If you crave something sweet, eat a sweet.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
लालसा (lālasā) comes from the intensive of the root √las, "to play, to shine", as MW derives it; MW gives lālasa and lālasā as longing, ardent desire (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रतीक्षा (pratīkṣā) — a wait; expectation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for is eager for, then the Sanskrit for a craving.

You'll want to know: प्रतीक्षा.
प्रतीक्षा — pratīkṣā — "a wait; expectation".
यदि प्रतीक्षा कठिना अस्ति, तर्हि पुस्तकं पठतु। yadi pratīkṣā kaṭhinā asti, tarhi pustakaṃ paṭhatu. If the wait is hard, read a book.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतीक्षा (pratīkṣā) is built on prati, "towards", and the root √īkṣ, "to see", which also gives pratīkṣate, "waits"; MW gives pratīkṣā as expectation (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
दिदृक्षा (didṛkṣā) — a wish to see.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a craving, then the Sanskrit for a wait.

You'll want to know: दिदृक्षा.
दिदृक्षा — didṛkṣā — "a wish to see".
यदि नगरस्य दिदृक्षा अस्ति, तर्हि श्वः तत्र गच्छामः। yadi nagarasya didṛkṣā asti, tarhi śvaḥ tatra gacchāmaḥ. If you want to see the city, we will go there tomorrow.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दिदृक्षा (didṛkṣā) comes from the desiderative of the root √dṛś, "to see": MW gives didṛkṣā as the desire of seeing (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
समीहते (samīhate) — strives for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wait, then the Sanskrit for a wish to see.

You'll want to know: समीहते.
समीहते — samīhate — "strives for".
यदि सः सुखं समीहते, तर्हि सः अन्यस्य साहाय्यं करोतु। yadi saḥ sukhaṃ samīhate, tarhi saḥ anyasya sāhāyyaṃ karotu. If he strives for happiness, let him help others.
Past (he …): सः सुखं समीहितवान्, saḥ sukhaṃ samīhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
समीहते (samīhate) is built on sam and the root √īh, "to strive"; MW gives samīh as to strive after, to wish for, to endeavour to gain (MW).
