Lesson 1 of 6.
二手 (èrshǒu) — second-hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to attach importance to, then the Mandarin for a way of doing things.

You'll want to know: 二手.
二手 — èrshǒu — "second-hand".
wǒ mǎi le yí ge èrshǒu shǒujī yīnwèi hěn piányi — I bought a second-hand phone because it was cheap.

The word, taken apart.
二手 (èrshǒu) is built from 二 'two' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyijH and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): through a second pair of hands.

Lesson 2 of 6.
变成 (biànchéng) — to turn into.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a way of doing things, then the Mandarin for second-hand.

You'll want to know: 变成.
变成 — biànchéng — "to turn into".
那家饭店变成了书店 nà jiā fàndiàn biànchéng le shūdiàn — That restaurant has turned into a bookshop.
The new character, in pieces: 变 (biàn) — eight strokes in two pieces: on top a dot and a bar over four short strokes; at the foot 又, two strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
变成 (biànchéng) is built from 变 (biàn) 'to change' and 成 'to complete', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjenH and dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to transform'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
变 (biàn) — a dot and a bar over four short strokes, then 又.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. [once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a dot and a bar over four short strokes, then 又.
变 (biàn).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, six strokes. A dot, then a long bar under it, as at the top of 文. Two short verticals under the bar, the left one first. Then a short stroke falling to the left beside the left vertical, and a dot falling to the right beside the right one.
The bottom piece, 又, two strokes, as you wrote it: a bar that turns and falls to the lower left, then a long stroke falling to the right across it.
6 + 2 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 变 (biàn), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 变 (biàn)? (Eight.) How does it start? (A dot and a bar, as in 文.) What is the bottom piece? (又, two strokes.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
xiánzhì — unused; lying idle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for second-hand, then the Mandarin for to turn into.

You'll want to know: xiánzhì.
xiánzhì — "unused; lying idle".
jiā lǐ xiánzhì de dōngxi kěyǐ sòng gěi péngyou — Things you don't use at home can go to friends.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiánzhì is built from xián 'idle' and zhì 'to put', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hean and triH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'leisure' and the second 'to set down'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
huásuàn — good value; worth it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to turn into, then the Mandarin for sitting unused at home.

You'll want to know: huásuàn.
huásuàn — "good value; worth it".
wǒ juéde èrshǒu chē bǐ xīn chē huásuàn — I think a second-hand car is better value than a new one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huásuàn is built from huá and suàn 'to count'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese swanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the number of things'; the dictionary glosses the first 'to row a boat', and how it came into this word is not settled.
