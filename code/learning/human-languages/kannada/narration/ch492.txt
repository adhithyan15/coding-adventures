Kannada, chapter 492: Joy and Delight.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಉಲ್ಲಾಸ (ullāsa) — cheerfulness; high spirits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for regret, then the Kannada for remorse.

You'll want to know: ಉಲ್ಲಾಸ.
ಉಲ್ಲಾಸ — ullāsa — "cheerfulness; high spirits".
ಬೆಳಗಿನ ತಣ್ಣನೆಯ ಗಾಳಿಯಿಂದ ಮನಸ್ಸಿಗೆ ಉಲ್ಲಾಸ ಆಯಿತು. beḷagina taṇṇaneya gāḷiyinda manassige ullāsa āyitu. The cool morning breeze lifted my spirits.
ಉಲ್ಲಾಸದಿಂದ, cheerfully.

The word, taken apart.
ಉಲ್ಲಾಸ (ullāsa) is borrowed from Sanskrit ullāsa, "light, splendour; a coming forth; joy, merriness" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಆನಂದಿಸು (ānandisu) — to enjoy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for remorse, then the Kannada for cheerfulness.

You'll want to know: ಆನಂದಿಸು.
ಆನಂದಿಸು — ānandisu — "to enjoy".
ಮಕ್ಕಳು ಕಡಲತೀರದಲ್ಲಿ ಆಟವನ್ನು ಆನಂದಿಸಿದರು. makkaḷu kaḍalatīradalli āṭavannu ānandisidaru. The children enjoyed playing on the beach.
Past (I …): ಆನಂದಿಸಿದೆ, ānandiside.
What you enjoy takes -ಅನ್ನು. The ending -ಇಸು makes a verb of the Sanskrit noun ಆನಂದ, bliss.

The word, taken apart.
ಆನಂದಿಸು (ānandisu) is built from Sanskrit ānanda, "happiness" (CDIAL 1172), with the Kannada ending -isu that turns a Sanskrit noun into a verb.

ಹುಮ್ಮಸ್ಸು — hummassu — "zest; eagerness".
ಹೊಸ ಕೆಲಸ ಶುರು ಮಾಡಲು ಅವನಿಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಹುಮ್ಮಸ್ಸು ಇದೆ. hosa kelasa śuru māḍalu avanige tumbā hummassu ide. He is full of zest to start the new job.
Close to ಉತ್ಸಾಹ, but warmer and more everyday.

The word, taken apart.
ಹುಮ್ಮಸ್ಸು (hummassu) is an everyday Kannada word for eager energy; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಹರ್ಷ (harṣa) — delight; joy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to enjoy, then the Kannada for zest.

You'll want to know: ಹರ್ಷ.
ಹರ್ಷ — harṣa — "delight; joy".
ಮದುವೆಯ ಸುದ್ದಿಯಿಂದ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಹರ್ಷ ತುಂಬಿತು. maduveya suddiyinda maneyalli harṣa tumbitu. The wedding news filled the house with joy.
Written and formal; in talk people say ಸಂತೋಷ.

The word, taken apart.
ಹರ್ಷ (harṣa) is borrowed from Sanskrit harṣa, "bristling, a thrill" (CDIAL 13999): the hair standing on end with excitement, and so delight.

ಒಳ್ಳೆಯ ಊಟದ ನಂತರ ಅಜ್ಜನಿಗೆ ತೃಪ್ತಿ ಆಯಿತು. oḷḷeya ūṭada nantara ajjanige tṛpti āyitu. After a good meal, grandfather felt satisfied.

The word, taken apart.
ತೃಪ್ತಿ (tṛpti) is borrowed whole from Sanskrit tṛpti, "satisfaction, contentment" (Monier-Williams).
