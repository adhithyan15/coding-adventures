Russian, chapter 204: To try, To order, To pay, To cost, To spend.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
пробовать (próbovat) — to try, to taste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cash machine, then the Russian for a coin.

You'll want to know: пробовать.
пробовать (próbovat) — "to try, to taste".

The word, taken apart.
пробовать (próbovat) comes from проба, "a test", probably through Polish próba or German Probe, from Medieval Latin proba, from probāre, "to test" (Vasmer).

Before the new one: say the Russian for a coin, then the Russian for to try.

You'll want to know: заказывать.
заказывать (zakázyvat) — "to order (food, goods)".

The word, taken apart.
заказывать (zakázyvat) is за- with -казывать, from казать, "to show, to say": to order is to tell someone what you want ahead.

Lesson 4 of 5.
стоить (stóit) — to cost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to order, then the Russian for to pay.

You'll want to know: стоить.
стоить (stóit) — "to cost".

The word, taken apart.
стоить (stóit), "to cost", is perhaps borrowed from Polish stoi, "it costs"; the West Slavic sense copies Latin cōnstāre, "to stand firm; to cost", the source of German kosten (Vasmer). It goes with стоять, "to stand".

Lesson 5 of 5.
тратить (trátit) — to spend (money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to pay, then the Russian for to cost.

You'll want to know: тратить.
тратить (trátit) — "to spend (money)".

The word, taken apart.
тратить (trátit) from Proto-Slavic tratiti — inherited; Ukrainian тратити also means "to lose, to ruin". Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian trótinti, "to tease", and Latvian truots, "a whetstone", and reports a further link with тереть, "to rub".
