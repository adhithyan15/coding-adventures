Lesson 1 of 5.
ज़रूरी (zarūrī) — necessary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for cheap, then the Hindi for hard.

You'll want to know: ज़रूरी.
ज़रूरी — zarūrī — "necessary".

The word, taken apart.
ज़रूरी (zarūrī) is a loan from Arabic ḍarūrī, "necessary", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ḍ-r-r (Lane).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: सुरक्षित.
सुरक्षित — surakṣit — "safe".

The word, taken apart.
सुरक्षित (surakṣit) is Sanskrit surakṣita, "well protected", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama. It is built from su-, "well", and rakṣita, "guarded".

Lesson 4 of 5.
मीठा (mīṭhā) — sweet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for dangerous, then the Hindi for safe.

You'll want to know: मीठा.
मीठा — mīṭhā — "sweet".

The word, taken apart.
मीठा (mīṭhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from miṣṭa, "dainty, sweet" (CDIAL 10299). CDIAL lists it under mṛṣṭa, "pure; prepared, savoury", of which miṣṭa is an early variant.
