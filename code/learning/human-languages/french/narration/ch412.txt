French, chapter 412: Au guichet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le guichet — the ticket office, the counter window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cyclist, then the French for to note.

You'll want to know: le guichet.
le guichet — "the ticket office, the counter window".
Il y a beaucoup de monde au guichet. There are a lot of people at the ticket office.
Le guichet automatique is a cash machine in some places.

The word, taken apart.
guichet comes from Old French guischet 'small door in a big one'. It is usually traced to a Norse or Germanic word, but the origin is not settled; English wicket is a cousin (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: le trajet.
le trajet — "the ride, the journey (from A to B)".
Le trajet en train dure deux heures. The train journey takes two hours.
Le trajet is the trip itself; le voyage is the whole travel.

The word, taken apart.
trajet was borrowed from Italian tragetto 'crossing', which goes back to Latin trāiectus 'a crossing over', from trāicere 'to throw across', built on iacere 'to throw' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le forfait — the flat-rate pass, the package.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the fare, then the French for the ride.

You'll want to know: le forfait.
le forfait — "the flat-rate pass, the package".
Elle a acheté un forfait pour la semaine. She bought a pass for the week.
Un forfait de ski — a ski pass; un forfait mobile — a phone plan.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of forfait in this sense is not settled. It is usually explained as an old fur fait 'done at the agreed rate', reshaped after the older forfait 'misdeed'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
valider — to validate (a ticket); to approve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the ride, then the French for the flat-rate pass.

You'll want to know: valider.
valider — "to validate (a ticket); to approve".
Dans le bus, les passagers valident leur billet. On the bus the passengers validate their tickets.
A ticket that is not validated can mean a fine.

The word, taken apart.
valider is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin validāre 'to make valid', from validus 'strong', built on valēre 'to be strong' (Lewis & Short).
