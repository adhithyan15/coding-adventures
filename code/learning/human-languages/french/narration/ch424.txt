French, chapter 424: Où dormir ?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'auberge — the inn; the hostel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to capsize, then the French for to drift.

You'll want to know: l'auberge.
l'auberge — "the inn; the hostel".
Nous avons dormi dans une petite auberge de campagne. We slept in a little country inn.
A feminine noun. Une auberge de jeunesse — a youth hostel.

The word, taken apart.
auberge was borrowed from Provençal alberga, which is usually traced to a Germanic word meaning 'shelter for an army'. EtymDB lists it without a source.

loger — "to lodge, to stay; to put up".
Pendant le festival, nous logeons chez des amis. During the festival we are staying with friends.
Le logement — housing.

The word, taken apart.
loger comes from Old French logier (EtymDB), built on loge 'hut', which is usually traced to a Frankish word for a shelter of leaves.

Lesson 5 of 5.
séjourner — to stay for a while, to spend time (somewhere).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the overnight stay, then the French for to lodge.

You'll want to know: séjourner.
séjourner — "to stay for a while, to spend time (somewhere)".
Ils ont séjourné une semaine au bord du lac. They stayed a week by the lake.
More formal than rester.

The word, taken apart.
séjourner goes back to Vulgar Latin subdiurnāre 'to spend the day', built on Latin diurnus 'of the day', from diēs 'day' (Lewis & Short).
