# Review — what went on, and what got done

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-L-GENDER-01, RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-LI-PLURAL-01, RU-GRAMMAR-BYL-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was at the museum", as a man, then as a woman. (*Ya byl
v muzéye. Ya bylá v muzéye.*) Then "we were at the museum". (*My býli v
muzéye.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-IMPERFECTIVE-PROCESS-01, RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-PERFECTIVE-RESULT-01, RU-GRAMMAR-POKA-WHILE-01, RU-GRAMMAR-KOGDA-EVENT-01] -->

Three lessons, and Russian can now look back. The past swaps the
infinitive's *-ть* for *-л*, agreeing with who did it. Nearly every verb
in it shows one of two views: the **imperfective** for what went on, again and again or at all;
the **perfective** for one deed done, with a result. **Пока** lays two
scenes side by side; **когда** with a perfective marks the moment that
cut in, or the next step.

[PAUSE 2s] One evening, told both ways:

> **Пока мама ужинала, папа смотрел фильм.** — *poká máma úzhinala, pápa
> smotrél film* — While Mum was having dinner, Dad was watching a film.
> **Когда мама поужинала, она позвонила бабушке.** — *kogdá máma
> poúzhinala, oná pozvoníla bábushke* — When Mum had finished her dinner,
> she rang Grandma.

The first pair went on together. The second pair came one after the other,
each one finished: *поужинала* is the perfective of *ужинать*, made with
*по-*.
