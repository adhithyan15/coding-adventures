# खाणे (khāṇe) — "to eat," and Marathi's third gender

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-JANE, MR-LEX-YENE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two infinitives so far: *jāṇe*, *yeṇe*. Say them. This third one is
ordinary as a verb and quietly remarkable as a noun.

## You'll want to know: खाणे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-KHANE]; assesses=[] -->

> **खाणे** — *khāṇe* — **to eat**

Stem **खा-** *khā-*, present **खातो** (*khāto*) and **खाते** (*khāte*) on the
gendered pattern you already have.

Its root is Sanskrit **खादति** *khādati*, "chews, eats" — a solidly Indo-Aryan
word with no borrowing in its history. The *-d-* wore away on the road to
Marathi; the long *ā* stayed.

## Sounds you'll need: खा · णे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ख** is *kha* — a *k* with a genuine puff of breath after it, the sound in
English *kit* said hard, not the *k* of *skit*. Marathi treats that puff as a
different letter, not an accent. Add the long-*ā* stroke and the familiar **णे**:
*khā-ṇe*.

## Grammar Lens: the infinitive is a neuter noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-NE-NEUTER-NOUN]; assesses=[] -->

**खाणे** also simply means "eating" — the thing, not the act of doing it. Every
*-णे* infinitive works this way; *खाणंपिणं* (*khāṇaṁ-piṇaṁ*), "eating and
drinking," is how Marathi says food and drink.

And as a noun it carries a gender — **neuter**, the third one. Hindi has two
genders, masculine and feminine. Marathi kept all three that Sanskrit had, so
words agreeing with a noun come in three shapes:

| gender | "good" | said |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | चांगला | *chāṅglā* |
| feminine | चांगली | *chāṅglī* |
| neuter | चांगलं | *chāṅglaṁ* |

So "eating is good" is **खाणं चांगलं आहे** — *khāṇaṁ chāṅglaṁ āhe*, with the
neuter ending twice over and the verb, as always, last.
