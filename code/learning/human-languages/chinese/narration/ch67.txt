Mandarin Chinese, chapter 67: Animals.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
马 (mǎ) — horse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for snow, then the Mandarin for wind.

You'll want to know: 马.
马 — mǎ — "horse".
我喜欢白马 wǒ xǐhuan bái mǎ — I love white horses.
The new character, in pieces: 马 (mǎ) — a turn, a zigzag that hooks, and a bar along the bottom: the right-hand piece of 吗 and 妈. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
马 (mǎ) is inherited from Middle Chinese maeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary explains its old character as the shape of a head, a mane, a tail and four legs.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mǎ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ xǐhuan bái mǎ — I love white horses.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for snow, then the Mandarin for wind, then say mǎ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 马 (mǎ) mean? ("Horse".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 6.
马 (mǎ) — a turn, a zigzag, a base.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a turn, a zigzag, a base and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say mǎ — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
吗 妈.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — ma, mā]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a turn, a zigzag, a base.
马 (mǎ).
One piece, three strokes.
A horizontal that turns down at its right end.
A vertical that turns right, then turns down again and hooks to the left at the foot, all in one stroke.
A horizontal along the bottom, rising a little to the right.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 马 (mǎ), all three, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 马 (mǎ)? (Three.) Which stroke turns without lifting? (The second.) Which characters you know have it on the right? (吗 and 妈.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 马 (mǎ), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
niǎo is inherited from Middle Chinese tewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed, through an older book, as the name for all long-tailed birds. Why the modern word begins with n is not settled.
