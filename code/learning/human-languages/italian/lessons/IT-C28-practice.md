## You'll want to know: the three chapters, on one table
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-TASK-C28-ORDINE-02]; assesses=[] -->

You have ten ordinals, an ending that makes the rest, and *prima* and *dopo*. That
is enough to put things in sequence, and the easiest place to do it is a table.

- **Prima l'antipasto.** — The antipasto first.
- **Poi il primo.** — Then the first course.
- **Dopo il primo, il secondo.** — After the first, the second.
- **Alla fine, il dolce.** — At the end, the sweet.

Two ways of saying the same order, and they do different work. The **ordinals**
name each thing's place — *il primo* is the first, whatever comes near it.
**Prima** and **dopo** name a relation between two things — *dopo il primo* is
true of anything that follows it.

So: *Il caffè viene dopo il dolce* — the coffee comes after the sweet — and
*fra un quarto d'ora*, in a quarter of an hour, is how long you might wait for it.
Say the courses in order, then say them again with *prima* and *dopo*, and you have
used both systems on the same four things.
