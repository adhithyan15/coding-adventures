Lesson 1 of 5.
योजना (yojanā) — a plan, a scheme.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a discussion, then the Sanskrit for a dispute.

You'll want to know: योजना.
योजना — yojanā — "a plan, a scheme".

The word, taken apart.
yojanā is built on the root √yuj, "to yoke, join" (MW), the root of yuga and yoga. A plan is an arrangement of parts joined together. MW compares √yuj with Latin iungere, "to join", and English yoke.

Lesson 2 of 5.
लक्ष्यम् (lakṣyam) — a goal, a target.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a dispute, then the Sanskrit for a plan.

You'll want to know: लक्ष्यम्.
लक्ष्यम् — lakṣyam — "a goal, a target".

The word, taken apart.
lakṣya is built on lakṣa, "mark, sign, stake", from the root √lakṣ, "to mark, observe" (MW). The Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad already uses it for an aim or target (CDIAL 10881). Lakṣa also became "100,000", the source of Hindi lākh.

You'll want to know: उपायः.
उपायः — upāyaḥ — "a way, a means".

The word, taken apart.
upāya is built on upa-, "near", and the root √i, "to go" (MW): first "approach, coming near", then the way by which you reach your aim (CDIAL 2308).

Lesson 4 of 5.
समस्या (samasyā) — a problem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a goal, then the Sanskrit for a way.

You'll want to know: समस्या.
समस्या — samasyā — "a problem".

The word, taken apart.
samasyā is built on sam, "together", and the root √as, "to throw, put" (MW): first "joining together". MW gives a second sense, part of a verse handed to someone else to complete, and the puzzle to be completed became the modern sense "problem".

You'll want to know: समाधानम्.
समाधानम् — samādhānam — "a solution".

The word, taken apart.
samādhāna is built on sam-ā- and the root √dhā, "to put, place" (MW): "putting together, adjusting, settling" (CDIAL 13210). Settling a difficulty is solving it.
