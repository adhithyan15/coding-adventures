French, chapter 471: Une grande déception.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la déception — disappointment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for relief, then the French for to reassure.

You'll want to know: la déception.
la déception — "disappointment".
Le film a été une vraie déception. The film was a real disappointment.
A false friend: it does not mean deception in English, which is la tromperie.

The word, taken apart.
la déception is a learned borrowing from Late Latin dēceptiō "deceit", from dēcipere "to catch, to deceive", built on capere "to take" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
décevoir — to disappoint.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for to reassure, then the French for disappointment.

You'll want to know: décevoir.
décevoir — "to disappoint".
Le concert m'a beaucoup déçu. The concert disappointed me a lot.
Like recevoir: je déçois, j'ai été déçu. It does not mean to deceive.

The word, taken apart.
décevoir is inherited, through Old French deceveir, from Latin dēcipere "to deceive" (EtymDB), built on capere "to take" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le chagrin — sorrow, grief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for disappointment, then the French for to disappoint.

You'll want to know: le chagrin.
le chagrin — "sorrow, grief".
Elle a eu beaucoup de chagrin quand son chat est mort. She felt great sorrow when her cat died.
Avoir du chagrin — to be sad. Un chagrin d'amour — heartbreak.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of le chagrin "sorrow" is not settled. A link with chagrin, a grainy leather named from Turkish sağrı (EtymDB), is sometimes proposed.
