# Six lines about a trip

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SKILL-C279-READ-STORY-01, MR-LEX-C279-RTU-01, MR-LEX-C281-JNAN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The A1 message asked for a few practised lines to Meera. This task asks for six lines, and they tell a story: a trip, to a village or anywhere else.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one reader: Meera, or a friend, by name. Say **ऋतू** and **ज्ञान** aloud; both will go in.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-C279-FIRST-THEN-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C279-CONJUNCTIVE-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C280-MHANUN-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C281-REASON-ORDER-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-DATIVE-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-VERBS-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C287-WOULD-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C287-UNREAL-IF-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A told story is not a list of events. It has a **scene**, in **-त होतो** or **असताना**. It has **events** in the plain past, kept in order with **आधी**, **मग**, **त्यानंतर** and **शेवटी**, and joined with **-ऊन**. It has a **reason**, with **म्हणून** or **कारण**, and a **feeling**, with **मला … आला** or **वाटलं**.

[PAUSE 2s] A good one ends by looking at what might have been: **जर …, तर मी … गेलो असतो**.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C276-ERGATIVE-NE-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C276-OBJECT-AGREEMENT-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C278-ONGOING-PAST-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C278-ASTANA-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C278-HABITUAL-PAST-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C279-FIRST-THEN-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C279-CONJUNCTIVE-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C280-MHANUN-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C281-MULE-01, MR-LEX-C283-TIKIT-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C283-LOST-REPORT-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C283-MISSED-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-DATIVE-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-VERBS-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C286-JAR-TAR-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C287-WOULD-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C287-UNREAL-IF-01, MR-LEX-C279-RTU-01, MR-LEX-C281-JNAN-01, MR-SCRIPT-VOCALIC-R-01, MR-SCRIPT-NYA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] On paper, in Devanagari, write **six lines** to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:

1. the scene: which season it was, with **ऋतू**, and what you were doing as it began, with **-त होतो** or **… तेव्हा**;
2. the first thing that happened, with **आधी**, and buying a ticket, with **तिकीट**;
3. what happened next, with **मग** or a **-ऊन** form, and one thing that went wrong: **हरवली** or **चुकली**;
4. why, with **म्हणून**, **कारण** or **-मुळे**;
5. how it ended and how you felt, with **शेवटी** and **मला … आला**, **वाटलं** or **झालं**;
6. what might have been, with **… असतो**, and one thing the trip taught you, with **ज्ञान**.

[PAUSE 3s] There is no model in this lesson. Use only words you already write. The work is choosing the right past for each line, and joining them.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-C278-ONGOING-PAST-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C276-OBJECT-AGREEMENT-01, MR-GRAMMAR-C287-UNREAL-IF-01, MR-SCRIPT-VOCALIC-R-01, MR-SCRIPT-NYA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now read your lines as you read Arun's monsoon day. Mark every verb **s** for scene, **e** for event. Line one should be mostly **s**; lines two and three mostly **e**.

[PAUSE 2s] Then three checks. A verb with an object in the plain past follows the thing: **-ने** on he, she or a name. **ऋतू** starts with the letter **ऋ**, not **र**. **ज्ञान** starts with **ज्ञ**, with **ञ** inside it.

[PAUSE 2s] Last, read line six alone. Do both halves of the if wear **असता** or **असतो**?
