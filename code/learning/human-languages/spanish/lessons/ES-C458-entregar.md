## You'll want to know: entregar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-01] -->

**entregar** — to hand in, hand over, deliver.

*Entregar los papeles* — to hand the paperwork in. *Hay que entregarlo antes
del viernes* — it has to be in before Friday. *El paquete se entrega mañana* —
the parcel is delivered tomorrow.

**La entrega** is the noun: *la entrega a domicilio* is home delivery, and
*el plazo de entrega* is the delivery time — both of this chapter's first two
words in one phrase.

## Grammar Lens: entregar is not dar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-01] -->

| what is happening | the verb |
|---|---|
| passing something to somebody | **dar** |
| handing over what was owed or due | **entregar** |

*Dame el boli* — pass me the pen. *Entrega el boli* would sound as though the
pen were evidence.

*Entregar* carries an obligation with it: homework, a form, a parcel, a set of
keys. Something was due, and now it has gone across. That is why a courier
*entrega* and a friend *da*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02, ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO] -->

Latin **integrare**, to make whole — from **integer**, **untouched,
complete**, which is *in-* plus the root of *tangere*, to touch. An *integer*
is a thing nobody has broken into.

So *entregar* is to hand a thing over **entire** — the sense of a delivery
that arrives with nothing missing.

The same *integer* gave Spanish **entero**, whole, and **íntegro**, which is
the bookish twin and means upright about a person. English took the number
sense as **integer** and the character sense as **integrity** — and *integrity*
is exactly what *entregar* asks for: the thing handed over undiminished.
