Mandarin Chinese, chapter 310: Who Did It.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
huáiyí — to suspect; to doubt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a con man, then the Mandarin for on purpose.

You'll want to know: huáiyí.
huáiyí — "to suspect; to doubt".
jǐngchá huáiyí tā shì xiǎotōu — The police suspect he's the thief.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huáiyí is built from huái 'to hold close' and yí 'to doubt', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hweaj and ngi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to embrace' and 'to think of' and the second 'unsure' and 'to suspect': to hold a doubt.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

zhèngrén is built from zhèng 'proof' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyingH and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to verify'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
zhèngjù — evidence; proof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to suspect someone, then the Mandarin for a witness to a crime.

You'll want to know: zhèngjù.
zhèngjù — "evidence; proof".
jǐngchá zhǎodào le zhèngjù — The police found the evidence.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhèngjù is built from zhèng 'to verify' and jù 'to lean on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyingH and kjoH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to rely on' and 'to hold': what a proof can lean on.

Lesson 4 of 5.
shuōhuǎng — to lie; to tell a lie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a witness to a crime, then the Mandarin for proof of what happened.

You'll want to know: shuōhuǎng.
shuōhuǎng — "to lie; to tell a lie".
wǒ juéde tā zài shuōhuǎng — I think he's lying.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shuōhuǎng is built from 说 'to say' and huǎng 'a lie'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the Guangyun has no entry for the second, so its history is not settled.
