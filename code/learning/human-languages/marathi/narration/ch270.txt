[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a pickle, then the Marathi for almonds.

You'll want to know: मनुका.
मनुका — manukā — "raisins".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of मनुका (manukā) is not settled. Neither CDIAL nor DEDR has an entry for it. It may be the same word as Hindi munakkā, "large raisin", from Arabic munaqqā, "cleaned", but no source here confirms it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
गूळ (gūḷ) — jaggery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for almonds, then the Marathi for raisins.

You'll want to know: गूळ.
गूळ — gūḷ — "jaggery".

The word, taken apart.
गूळ (gūḷ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from guḍa, "boiled sugarcane juice, molasses" (CDIAL 4182).

Lesson 4 of 5.
धोतर (dhotar) — a dhoti.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for raisins, then the Marathi for jaggery.

You'll want to know: धोतर.
धोतर — dhotar — "a dhoti".

The word, taken apart.
धोतर (dhotar) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dhotta, "cloth", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6881). CDIAL marks the Marathi word as taken from Gujarati.
