Lesson 1 of 6.
લોખંડ (lokhanḍ) — iron.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a patch (on cloth), then the Gujarati for embroidery.

You'll want to know: લોખંડ.
લોખંડ — lokhanḍ — "iron".
આ દરવાજો લોખંડનો છે. ā darvājo lokhanḍno che. This gate is made of iron.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
લોખંડ (lokhanḍ) comes from lōhōpaskara, "iron tools", a form CDIAL builds from lōhá, "iron", and upaskara, "tool"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word as "tools, iron, ironware" (CDIAL 11171).

Lesson 2 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for embroidery, then the Gujarati for iron.

You'll want to know: લુહાર.
લુહાર — luhār — "a blacksmith".
લુહાર લોખંડમાંથી ઓજારો બનાવે છે. luhār lokhanḍmā̃thī ojāro banāve che. The blacksmith makes tools out of iron.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
લુહાર (luhār) goes back to Sanskrit lōhakāra, "an iron-worker", built from lōha, "iron", and kāra, "maker"; CDIAL gives the Gujarati form as lavār (CDIAL 11159).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ભઠ્ઠી (bhaṭhṭhī) — a furnace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for iron, then the Gujarati for a blacksmith.

You'll want to know: ભઠ્ઠી.
ભઠ્ઠી — bhaṭhṭhī — "a furnace".
લુહારની ભઠ્ઠીમાં આગ સળગે છે. luhārnī bhaṭhṭhīmā̃ āg saḷge che. A fire burns in the blacksmith's furnace.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ભઠ્ઠી (bhaṭhṭhī) is a furnace or a kiln. CDIAL lists Panjabi bhaṭṭhī, "a furnace", and Hindi bhaṭṭhā, "a kiln", under Sanskrit bhráṣṭra, "a frying pan, a gridiron" (CDIAL 9656); it gives no Gujarati form for this word, so its exact history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
તપાવવું (tapāvvũ) — to heat (something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a blacksmith, then the Gujarati for a furnace.

You'll want to know: તપાવવું.
તપાવવું — tapāvvũ — "to heat (something)".
લુહારે ભઠ્ઠીમાં લોખંડ તપાવ્યું. luhāre bhaṭhṭhīmā̃ lokhanḍ tapāvyũ. The blacksmith heated the iron in the furnace.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે લોખંડ તપાવ્યું, teṇe lokhanḍ tapāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
તપાવવું (tapāvvũ) is built from તપવું, "to be hot", with the causative -āv-: to make something hot. CDIAL files the family under Sanskrit tapyati, "is hot" (CDIAL 5684).
