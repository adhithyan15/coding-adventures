# आप क्या कर रहे हैं? — the question, and four ways to answer it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have **क्या** for *what*. Put it in front of the plural
continuous and you have the question this lesson is about.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02] -->

> — आप क्या कर रहे हैं?
> — मैं हिंदी बोल रहा हूँ।

Three things are at work. **क्या** asks. **रहे हैं** is the plural because the
question is to somebody addressed as **आप**. The answer drops back to **रहा हूँ**,
because the answer is about one man speaking for himself — a woman answers
**बोल रही हूँ**, and the question does not change, because the asker does not
know yet who will answer.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05] -->

Answer it four ways, out loud:

- you are speaking Hindi
- you are going home
- you are eating
- and then say what you do **in general**, rather than now

[PAUSE 4s]

The fourth one is the trap and the test. *What are you doing?* takes the
continuous; *what do you do?* takes the habit, and the two questions sound
nearly the same in English.

## Grammar Lens: what a listening paper does with this
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05] -->

| what you hear | what it tells you |
|---|---|
| ...रहा है | one man, right now |
| ...रही है | one woman, right now |
| ...रहे हैं | more than one, or **आप** |

The middle word is doing almost all the work, and it sits at the end of the
sentence where a listener hears it last. Train the ear on those three shapes and
a personal-account listening item becomes three decisions instead of one guess.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Why is the question **रहे हैं** and the answer **रहा हूँ**? (The
question is to **आप**; the answer is about one person.) Which word carries the
gender? (The middle one.) Which form answers *what do you do?* (The habit.)
