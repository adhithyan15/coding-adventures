# अस्माकम् (asmākam) — our, of us

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-MAMA, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **मम गृहम्** (*mama gṛham*), “my house”. One person possesses
the house. Keep **मम** ready.

## You'll want to know: one possessor, then several

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01]; assesses=[] -->

| possessors | Sanskrit | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| one: me | **मम** (*mama*) | my, of me |
| several: us | **अस्माकम्** (*asmākam*) | our, of us |

Say the new form slowly: *as-mā-kam*. The long **ā** holds the middle beat.

These are genitive forms: they answer “whose?” Sanskrit uses that relationship
where English often uses a possessive word.

## The form, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01] -->

Whitney's *Sanskrit Grammar* §493 lists **अस्माकम्** as the genitive plural in
the first-person paradigm. Historically, he explains it as a fixed case-form
of an older adjective stem: the form is derived from *asmāka*. The history is
unusual; the job in the sentence is simple: **अस्माकम्** answers “of us /
whose?” and stays fixed before the noun.

Source: William Dwight Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §§493 and 516.
