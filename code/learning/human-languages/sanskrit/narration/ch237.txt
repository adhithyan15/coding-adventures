Sanskrit, chapter 237: Potter, Goldsmith, Blacksmith, Weaver, Washerman.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कुम्भकारः (kumbhakāraḥ) — a potter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a young woman, then the Sanskrit for a person.

You'll want to know: कुम्भकारः.
कुम्भकारः — kumbhakāraḥ — "a potter".

The word, taken apart.
kumbhakāra is a compound of kumbha, "pot", which you have already met, and kāra, "maker", from the root √kṛ (MW). It lives on as Hindi kumhār, "potter" (CDIAL 3310).

Lesson 2 of 5.
स्वर्णकारः (svarṇakāraḥ) — a goldsmith.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a person, then the Sanskrit for a potter.

You'll want to know: स्वर्णकारः.
स्वर्णकारः — svarṇakāraḥ — "a goldsmith".

The word, taken apart.
svarṇakāra is a compound of svarṇa, "gold", which MW calls a contraction of suvarṇa, "of fine colour", and kāra, "maker" (MW). Its fuller form suvarṇakāra lives on as Hindi sonār, "goldsmith" (CDIAL 13520).

लोहकारः (lohakāraḥ) — a blacksmith.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a potter, then the Sanskrit for a goldsmith.

You'll want to know: लोहकारः.
लोहकारः — lohakāraḥ — "a blacksmith".

The word, taken apart.
lohakāra is a compound of loha, first "red, copper", later "iron" (CDIAL 11158), and kāra, "maker" (MW). MW derives loha from a root meaning "to be red". It lives on as Hindi lohār, "blacksmith" (CDIAL 11159).

Lesson 4 of 5.
तन्तुवायः (tantuvāyaḥ) — a weaver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a goldsmith, then the Sanskrit for a blacksmith.

You'll want to know: तन्तुवायः.
तन्तुवायः — tantuvāyaḥ — "a weaver".

The word, taken apart.
tantuvāya is a compound of tantu, "thread, warp", from the root √tan, "to stretch, spin out", and vāya, "weaver", from √ve, "to weave" (MW; CDIAL 5662). A weaver is a thread-weaver; the same word also names the spider.

Lesson 5 of 5.
रजकः (rajakaḥ) — a washerman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a blacksmith, then the Sanskrit for a weaver.

You'll want to know: रजकः.
रजकः — rajakaḥ — "a washerman".

The word, taken apart.
rajaka is built on the root √rañj, "to colour, dye" (MW; CDIAL 10575), the root of rakta, "red", which you have already met. MW explains the name from his cleaning or whitening clothes; dyeing and washing went together.
