# (el cuaderno de campo) — read it, then fill in a day

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-01, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el cuaderno** and **anotar**. Somebody else will read what you
write in it, which is the whole reason the instructions exist.

## Reading: the notebook
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-01, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03] -->

Taped inside the front cover of a shared notebook in a community garden:

> **CUADERNO DE CAMPO — CÓMO USARLO**
>
> **Anote** la temperatura dos veces al día: a las ocho de la mañana y a las
> ocho de la tarde.
>
> Si ha llovido, **anote** también la lluvia en milímetros. Si no ha llovido,
> escriba un cero. No deje la casilla en blanco.
>
> Una línea por día. Si olvida un día, déjelo vacío y siga: es peor **anotar**
> de memoria que no **anotar** nada.

## Grammar Lens: a zero is not a blank
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-04] -->

Four instructions, and the last one is the interesting one.

| the instruction | what it wants |
|---|---|
| *anote la temperatura* | twice a day, at fixed hours |
| *escriba un cero* | a zero, not an empty box |

The notice draws a line between **un cero** and **la casilla en blanco**. A
zero says it did not rain. A blank says nobody looked. Those are different
facts, and a record that cannot tell them apart is worth less than one that
can — which is why the last line would rather you left a gap than filled it in
from memory.

## Grammar Lens: anote, not anota
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02] -->

Every instruction here is **usted**: *anote*, *escriba*, *deje*, *siga*. A
notice usually takes the polite form, because it does not know who will read
it — though plenty of Spanish notices use a bare infinitive instead, as in *no
fumar*.

| to one person you know | on a notice |
|---|---|
| *anota* | **anote** |

You have had the mechanism since *hable usted*: an *usted* command **is** the
subjunctive, because *usted* is a third-person title. Not a borrowing — the
same form doing its own job. (The *tú* negative is subjunctive too: *no
anotes*.) It is why *reduzca* on the road sign looked the way it did.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-01, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Yesterday it rained in the morning and the afternoon was warm. Fill
in the line out loud: say the date, the two temperatures, and the rain.

[PAUSE 3s] Now say what you would write if it had not rained at all — and say
why that is not the same as leaving it blank.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-01, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-02, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The field notebook? (**El cuaderno de campo**.) The polite command
to note something? (**Anote**.) What goes in the box on a dry day? (**Un
cero** — never a blank.)
