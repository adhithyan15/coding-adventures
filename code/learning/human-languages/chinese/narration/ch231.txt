Mandarin Chinese, chapter 231: Seeing a Doctor.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
中医 (zhōngyī) — traditional Chinese medicine; a doctor of it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a nose, then the Mandarin for a leg.

You'll want to know: 中医.
中医 — zhōngyī — "traditional Chinese medicine; a doctor of it".
我想看中医 wǒ xiǎng kàn zhōngyī — I'd like to see a Chinese-medicine doctor.
The new character, in pieces: 医 (yī) — seven strokes: a bar on top, five strokes inside it (a short fall, a short bar, a long bar, a long fall and a dot), then a stroke down the left that turns right along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
中医 (zhōngyī) is built from 中, as in 中国, and 医 (yī) 'medicine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjuwng and 'i (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to heal'.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

Script — a box open on the right, with five strokes inside.
医 (yī).
Two pieces: a box open on the right, and what sits inside. The box takes two strokes, one first and one last.
First, the top bar of the box.
Inside, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left, then a short bar. Then three as at the foot of 头: a long bar, a long stroke falling to the left through it, and a dot falling to the right.
Last, the box closes: a vertical down the left that turns and runs right along the foot.
1 + 5 + 1 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 医 (yī), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 医 (yī)? (Seven.) Which stroke comes last? (The one down the left and along the foot.) How many strokes sit inside? (Five.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 西医.
西医 — xīyī — "western medicine; a doctor of it".
我身体不好头也疼想看西医 wǒ shēntǐ bù hǎo tóu yě téng xiǎng kàn xīyī — I'm not well and my head hurts too; I'd like to see a doctor of western medicine.
The new character, in pieces: 西 (xī) — six strokes: a bar on top, a box of two strokes, two strokes inside it that start above it, and a bar along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
西医 (xīyī) is built from 西 (xī) 'west' and 医 (yī) 'medicine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sej and 'i (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as a bird settling on its nest when the sun is in the west.

Script — a bar, a box and two strokes inside.
西 (xī).
One piece, six strokes.
A bar across the top.
The box, two strokes: a vertical down the whole left side, then a bar that runs right and turns down.
Inside, two strokes, each starting above the box: a stroke down that falls a little to the left, then a stroke down that turns right along the inside.
Last, a bar closes the foot.
1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = six.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: guàhào.
guàhào — "to register at a clinic; to register at a hospital".
kànbìng yǐqián yào guàhào — Before you see the doctor, you have to register.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guàhào is built from guà 'to hang up' and 号 'a number', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kweaH and hawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
