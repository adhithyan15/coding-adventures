# Review — the two that agree, and the p that becomes an f

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C317-ANSW-19] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "another book," then "another house." (***Otro*** libro;
***otra*** casa.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C317-ANSW-19, ES-LEX-C317-ANSW-20] -->

Of the five answering words, exactly two change their ending: **otro** and
**poco**. Both are measuring a thing, so both match the thing.

> *otro* / *otra* — another
> *poco* / *poca* — a little

[PAUSE 2s] *Algo*, *alguien* and *nadie* never move, because none of them is
describing a noun. They **are** the noun.

## Why it's said this way: the same road, a third time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C317-ANSW-20] -->

*Poco* is Latin **paucus**, and English **few** is the same root arriving by the
Germanic road, after *p* turned to *f*.

[PAUSE 2s] That shift is not a one-off. *Pie* and **foot**. *Diente* and
**tooth**. *Poco* and **few**. Three pairs, one sound law — and once you can hear
a Spanish *p* behind an English *f*, a large part of English stops looking
foreign.
