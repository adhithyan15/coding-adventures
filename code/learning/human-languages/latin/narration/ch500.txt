Latin, chapter 500: Desire and envy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cupīdō, cupīdinis — desire, longing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to amuse, then the Latin for to please.

You'll want to know: cupīdō, cupīdinis.
cupīdō, cupīdinis — "desire, longing".
Cupīdō pecūniae multōs hominēs malōs facit. Desire for money makes many people bad.
Feminine in prose, third declension; as the god of love it is masculine. Built on cupiō.

The word, taken apart.
cupīdō is built on cupiō "to desire" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); cupiditās, already known, comes from the same verb, and English cupidity from that.

Lesson 4 of 5.
invideō, invidēre — to envy, begrudge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for eager, then the Latin for greedy.

You'll want to know: invideō, invidēre.
invideō, invidēre — "to envy, begrudge".
Soror sorōrī novam stolam invidet. One sister envies the other her new dress.
invideō, invidēre, invīdī, invīsum, with the dative of the person: tibi invideō — I envy you. Invidia and invidus, already known, come from it.

The word, taken apart.
invideō is built on in- "upon, against" and videō "to see" (Lewis & Short), first "to cast an evil eye on". English envy comes from invidia through Old French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rīvālis, rīvālis — a rival (in love).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for greedy, then the Latin for to envy.

You'll want to know: rīvālis, rīvālis.
rīvālis, rīvālis — "a rival (in love)".
Duo iuvenēs eandem puellam amant; rīvālēs sunt. Two young men love the same girl; they are rivals.
Masculine, third declension. First someone who shares a stream with you, then a rival in love. English rival.

The word, taken apart.
rīvālis is built on rīvus "a stream" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): first those who used the same brook, then rivals in love. English rival comes from it.
