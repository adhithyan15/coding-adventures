Italian, chapter 504: Carte e scommesse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il mazzo — deck (of cards); bunch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for board game, then the Italian for to cheat.

You'll want to know: il mazzo.
il mazzo — "deck (of cards); bunch".
Il nonno mescola il mazzo e dà le carte. Grandad shuffles the deck and deals the cards.
Un mazzo di fiori — a bunch of flowers; un mazzo di chiavi — a bunch of keys.

The word, taken apart.
mazzo is usually traced, like mazza "a club", to Vulgar Latin mattea, from Latin mateola "a mallet" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
scommettere — to bet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for deck, then the Italian for to bluff.

You'll want to know: scommettere.
scommettere — "to bet".
Scommetto che domani piove. I bet it rains tomorrow.
The past participle is scommesso. Scommetto che… — I bet that…

The word, taken apart.
scommettere is built from s- and commettere (EtymDB), inherited from Latin committere "to bring together, to match", from mittere "to send".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la scommessa — bet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to bluff, then the Italian for to bet.

You'll want to know: la scommessa.
la scommessa — "bet".
Ho vinto la scommessa: mi devi un caffè. I won the bet: you owe me a coffee.
Fare una scommessa — to make a bet.

The word, taken apart.
scommessa is the feminine past participle of scommettere "to bet", turned into a noun; the verb goes back to Latin committere (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il cruciverba — crossword.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to bet, then the Italian for bet.

You'll want to know: il cruciverba.
il cruciverba — "crossword".
In treno faccio sempre il cruciverba. On the train I always do the crossword.
The same form in the plural: i cruciverba.

The word, taken apart.
cruciverba is a modern word built from Latin crux, crucis "a cross" and verba "words" (Lewis & Short).
