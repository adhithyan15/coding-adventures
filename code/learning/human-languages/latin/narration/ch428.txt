Latin, chapter 428: Departure, Return, Travel abroad, To travel abroad, To go forward.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
profectiō, profectiōnis — a departure, a setting out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for travel money, then the Latin for to set out on.

You'll want to know: profectiō, profectiōnis.
profectiō, profectiōnis — "a departure, a setting out".
Ante profectiōnem mercātor sarcinās parat. Before the departure the merchant gets his baggage ready.
Third declension, feminine: profectiō, profectiōnis f., the noun of proficīscor 'to set out'.

The word, taken apart.
profectiō is built on proficīscor 'to set out' (Lewis & Short), through its participle profectus. EtymDB does not list it.

Fourth declension, masculine: reditus, reditūs m. Also income, what comes back from land.

The word, taken apart.
reditus is built on redeō 'to go back' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), itself from red- 'back' and eō 'to go'.

peregrīnātiō, peregrīnātiōnis — "travel abroad, a stay in foreign parts".
Post longam peregrīnātiōnem senex domum redit. After a long time abroad the old man comes home.
Third declension, feminine: peregrīnātiō, peregrīnātiōnis f. English peregrination comes from it.

peregrīnātiō is built on peregrīnor 'to travel abroad' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from peregrīnus 'foreign'. English peregrination comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
peregrīnor, peregrīnārī — to travel abroad, to live in foreign parts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a return, then the Latin for travel abroad.

You'll want to know: peregrīnor, peregrīnārī.
peregrīnor, peregrīnārī — "to travel abroad, to live in foreign parts".
Mercātor multōs annōs in terrīs longinquīs peregrīnātur. The merchant travels abroad in distant lands for many years.
Deponent: peregrīnor, peregrīnārī, peregrīnātus sum. English pilgrim comes from the related adjective peregrīnus.

The word, taken apart.
peregrīnor is built on peregrīnus 'foreign' (EtymDB), which goes back to peregrē 'abroad', from per- 'through, beyond' and ager 'land' (Lewis & Short). English pilgrim comes from peregrīnus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
prōgredior, prōgredī — to go forward, to advance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for travel abroad, then the Latin for to travel abroad.

You'll want to know: prōgredior, prōgredī.
prōgredior, prōgredī — "to go forward, to advance".
Nox venit, sed viātōrēs ad oppidum prōgrediuntur. Night comes, but the travellers go on towards the town.
Deponent: prōgredior, prōgredī, prōgressus sum. English progress comes from its participle.

The word, taken apart.
prōgredior is built from prō- 'forward' and gradior 'to step' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English progress comes from its participle prōgressus.
