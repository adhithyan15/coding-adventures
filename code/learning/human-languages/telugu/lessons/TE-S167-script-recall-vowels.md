## Script: the five, and the one question behind them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-162, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-163, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-164, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-165, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-166] -->

> అ · ఆ · ఏ · ఐ · ఒ

Five letters, and **every one of them is on the page for the same reason**: a
vowel turned up at the front of a word with no consonant to sit inside.

| letter | sound | the word it opens |
|---|---|---|
| అ | *a* | అవును — yes |
| ఆ | *ā* | ఆరు — six |
| ఏ | *ē* | ఏమిటి — what? |
| ఐ | *ai* | ఐదు — five |
| ఒ | *o* | ఒకటి — one |

Look down the right-hand column. **Three of the five open a number you can
already count with, and the other two open two of the commonest words in the
book.** None of these is an exotic letter kept for later; they were on your
pages from chapter one, and only now do you have the shapes for them.

The question to ask of any vowel in Telugu is the same each time: *is a
consonant carrying it?* If yes, a sign. If no, a letter.
