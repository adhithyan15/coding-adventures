Latin, chapter 386: Living Together.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
societās, societātis — a partnership, an alliance; society.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for command, then the Latin for the first.

You'll want to know: societās, societātis.
societās, societātis — "a partnership, an alliance; society".
Cum frātre societātem fēcit. He went into partnership with his brother.
societās, societātis f., built on socius, a partner, an ally. A business partnership in Roman law. English society.

The word, taken apart.
societās is built on socius "a partner, an ally" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), a word kin to sequor "to follow": one who goes along with you. English society comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mōs, mōris — a custom, a way; (plural) character, morals.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for a partnership, then the Latin for human nature.

You'll want to know: mōs, mōris.
mōs, mōris — "a custom, a way; (plural) character, morals".
Hic mōs antīquus est. This is an old custom.
mōs, mōris m. The plural mōrēs is a person's ways, his character. Mōs maiōrum, the custom of the ancestors. English moral and morals.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of mōs is not settled; Lewis & Short give no sure account. English moral comes from its adjective mōrālis.

Lesson 4 of 5.
īnstitūtum, īnstitūtī — an institution, a custom; a purpose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for human nature, then the Latin for a custom.

You'll want to know: īnstitūtum, īnstitūtī.
īnstitūtum, īnstitūtī — "an institution, a custom; a purpose".
Īnstitūta patrum servāmus. We keep the institutions of our fathers.
īnstitūtum, īnstitūtī n.: a thing set up and kept, a custom. English institute and institution.

The word, taken apart.
īnstitūtum is the participle of īnstituō "to set up" (Lewis & Short), from in and statuō "to set, to place". English institute comes from it.

concordia, concordiae f., built on cor, cordis, heart: hearts together. English concord.

The word, taken apart.
