Latin, chapter 318: Ārdet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cremō, cremāre — to burn up, to cremate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a firebrand, then the Latin for burning heat.

You'll want to know: cremō, cremāre.
cremō, cremāre — "to burn up, to cremate".
Ignis tōtum vīcum cremāvit. The fire burned up the whole street.
cremō, cremāre, cremāvī, cremātum. Romans usually burned their dead, and English cremate comes from this verb.

The word, taken apart.
cremō is inherited; EtymDB traces it to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to burn", also seen in carbō, "coal". English cremate comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ūrō, ūrere — to burn; to scorch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for burning heat, then the Latin for to burn up.

You'll want to know: ūrō, ūrere.
ūrō, ūrere — "to burn; to scorch".
Sōl agrōs ūrēbat. The sun was scorching the fields.
ūrō, ūrere, ussī, ustum. It has many compounds; English combustion comes from combūrō.

The word, taken apart.
ūrō is an inherited verb; Lewis & Short compare Greek heuō, "to singe". English combustion comes from its compound combūrō.

Lesson 3 of 5.
flagrō, flagrāre — to blaze, to be on fire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to burn up, then the Latin for to burn.

You'll want to know: flagrō, flagrāre.
flagrō, flagrāre — "to blaze, to be on fire".
Domus flagrābat, et vīcīnī aquam portābant. The house was blazing, and the neighbours were carrying water.
flagrō, flagrāre, flagrāvī: to blaze, and of people, to burn with a feeling. English flagrant.

The word, taken apart.
flagrō is inherited from Proto-Italic flagros, "burning" (EtymDB), of the same family as flamma, "flame". English flagrant comes from it.

cōnflagrō, cōnflagrāre, cōnflagrāvī, cōnflagrātum: con- 'wholly' + flagrō. English conflagration.

The word, taken apart.
cōnflagrō is built on flagrō, "to blaze", with con-, "wholly" (Lewis & Short): to burn up entirely. English conflagration comes from it.
