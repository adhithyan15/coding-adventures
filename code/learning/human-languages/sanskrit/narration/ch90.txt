Sanskrit, chapter 90: Day, Early evening, Afternoon, Midnight, Dawn.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अहः (ahaḥ) — a day, daytime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hard, difficult, then the Sanskrit for sweet.

You'll want to know: अहः.
अहः — ahaḥ — "a day, daytime".

The word, taken apart.
Ahaḥ is a form of ahar, "day", which is already in the Rigveda (MW, CDIAL 993). MW gives it no root, and its origin is not settled. It is the -aha of saptāha, "week".

You'll want to know: अपराह्णः.
अपराह्णः — aparāhṇaḥ — "afternoon".

The word, taken apart.
Aparāhṇa is built from apara, "later, hinder", and ahna, a form of ahar, "day" (MW). Apara is inherited: MW compares Gothic afar, "after".

Madhyarātri is built from madhya, "middle", and rātri, "night" (MW). Madhya is inherited: MW compares Latin medius and English mid, as in midnight.

Lesson 5 of 5.
उषः (uṣaḥ) — dawn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for afternoon, then the Sanskrit for midnight.

You'll want to know: उषः.
उषः — uṣaḥ — "dawn".

The word, taken apart.
Uṣas is inherited from Proto-Indo-European: MW compares Greek ēōs and Latin aurōra, "dawn", and EtymDB links them to one ancestor. In the Rigveda the dawn is a goddess.
