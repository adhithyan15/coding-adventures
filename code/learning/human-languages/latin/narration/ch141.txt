Lesson 1 of 5.
linteum, linteī — a linen cloth, a towel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a thread, then the Latin for wool.

You'll want to know: linteum, linteī.
linteum, linteī — "a linen cloth, a towel".

The word, taken apart.
linteum comes from līnum, "flax" (Lewis & Short), through linteus, "made of linen". English linen and line belong to the same family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
sāpō, sāpōnis — soap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wool, then the Latin for a linen cloth.

You'll want to know: sāpō, sāpōnis.
sāpō, sāpōnis — "soap".

The word, taken apart.
sāpō was borrowed from Germanic, as Lewis & Short note. The Germanic word is usually reconstructed as Proto-Germanic saipōn, "soap, salve" (EtymDB 2.1); English soap comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
calix, calicis — a cup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sponge, then the Latin for a water jar.

You'll want to know: calix, calicis.
calix, calicis — "a cup".

The word, taken apart.
calix is related to Greek kylix, "a drinking cup" (EtymDB 2.1), though whether Latin borrowed it is uncertain. It is a different word from Greek kalyx, "a flower bud". English chalice comes from it through French.
