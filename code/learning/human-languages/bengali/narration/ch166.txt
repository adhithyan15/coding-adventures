5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
শকুন (shôkun) — a vulture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for sandesh, then the Bengali for a paratha.

You'll want to know: শকুন.
শকুন — shôkun — "a vulture".

The word, taken apart.
শকুন (shôkun) is Sanskrit śakuna, "bird", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 12245). In Bengali it has narrowed to "vulture".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a vulture, then the Bengali for a whale.

You'll want to know: কাঁকড়া.
কাঁকড়া — kā̃kṛā — "a crab".

The word, taken apart.
কাঁকড়া (kā̃kṛā) (kāṁkṛā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karkaṭa, "crab" (CDIAL 2816). CDIAL lists Bengali kāṁkṛā, "crab", and suspects that the Sanskrit word is itself of non-Aryan origin.
