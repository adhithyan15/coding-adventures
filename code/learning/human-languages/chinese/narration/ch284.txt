You'll want to know: zhàntái.
zhàntái — "a platform", at a station.
huǒchē zài èr hào zhàntái — The train is at platform two.
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhàntái is built from 站 and tái 'a raised platform'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese treamH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary gives as the everyday word for standing alone. tái is written with a character whose traditional form is doj, glossed as a raised square of earth; the simplified character is also an old one in its own right, with the readings yi and thoj and other senses.

What you've built.
zhàntái holds the 站 of 火车站: the station, and the platform you stand on in it.
A number comes before 号: èr hào zhàntái, platform two.

Grammar Lens: 坐 and a vehicle, then 去.
坐 (zuò) and a vehicle, then 去 or 来: that is how you travel.
我们坐火车去 wǒmen zuò huǒchē qù — We're going by train. 我们坐八点的火车 wǒmen zuò bā diǎn de huǒchē — We're taking the eight o'clock train.
坐 is the zuò you know, to sit and to travel by; the next lesson puts it in your hand. The vehicle comes between 坐 and the verb of going.
Tickets are counted with zhāng, as flat things are: mǎi liǎng zhāng qù Běijīng de piào, two tickets to Beijing.

Script — 人 twice, then 土.
坐 (zuò).
Three pieces, and you write all of them already.
人, twice, side by side, the left one first. Each is a left-falling stroke and then a dot: neither has the long right-falling stroke of 人 standing alone.
土, three strokes, under them, as at the lower right of 在: the short horizontal; then the vertical, from the top between the two 人, down through it; then the long horizontal along the foot.
2 + 2 + 3 = seven.

Lesson 4 of 9.
dānchéng — one way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: the train you are taking, and how you count its tickets.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 我们坐八点的火车, aloud — wǒmen zuò bā diǎn de huǒchē]

You'll want to know: dānchéng.
dānchéng — "one way", of a ticket or a trip.
yì zhāng dānchéng piào — a one-way ticket.
dānchéng háishi wǎngfǎn — One way or return?
wǎngfǎn, there and back, is the other choice. Its characters come later; for now dānchéng is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dānchéng is built from dān 'single' and chéng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tan and drjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'single, not double' and the second 'a term; a limit'; 'one way' for the pair, a single stage of a journey, is the word's own sense.

What you've built.
At the window you are asked dānchéng háishi wǎngfǎn?, one way or return. 还是 is the or of a question.
Answer with the word alone: dānchéng.

You'll want to know: zuòwèi.
zuòwèi — "a seat".
zhèr yǒu zuòwèi ma — Is there a seat here?
Its characters come later. The first is not the 坐 (zuò) you write but one that shares its sound and holds it under a roof.

The word, taken apart.
zuòwèi is built from zuò 'a seat' and wèi 'a place', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzwaH and hwijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the stand of a bed' and 'a position; a rank'.

What you've built.
坐 (zuò) is the verb, to sit; zuòwèi is the noun, the seat itself: wǒ de zuòwèi zài nǎr?, where's my seat?

You'll want to know: tuìpiào.
tuìpiào — "to return a ticket", for your money back.
wǒ xiǎng tuìpiào — I'd like to return my ticket.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuìpiào is built from tuì 'to give back' and 票 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese thwojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to draw back'; the dictionary gives no form for the second in its ticket sense, so that part's history is not settled.

What you've built.
You know tuìfáng, to give back a hotel room; tuìpiào gives back a ticket the same way.
It is a verb on its own, with the ticket inside it: wǒ xiǎng tuìpiào.

Grammar Lens: 在 after the verb, for where the action lands.
在 after a verb says where the action lands, or where it stays.
你坐在这儿 nǐ zuò zài zhèr — You sit here. tā zhù zài xuéxiào lǐ — He lives at the school.
Keep it apart from 在 before the verb: 我在家吃饭 says where the eating happens. After the verb, 在 marks where you end up sitting, living or putting something: fàng zài zhèr, put it here.

Grammar Lens: 家 for counting shops.
家 after a number, or after 这 or 那, counts shops, hotels and restaurants.
这家书店很好 zhè jiā shūdiàn hěn hǎo — This bookshop is very good. 那家饭店的菜很好吃 nà jiā fàndiàn de cài hěn hǎochī — The food at that restaurant is delicious.
一家书店 (yì jiā shūdiàn), a bookshop; liǎng jiā bīnguǎn, two hotels. It is the 家 of home, now counting places that do business. 个 does not count them.

The exchange.
A: wǒ yào liǎng zhāng qù Běijīng de piào — Two tickets to Beijing, please. B: dānchéng háishi wǎngfǎn — One way or return? A: dānchéng, wǒmen zuò bā diǎn de huǒchē — One way. We're taking the eight o'clock train. B: bā diǎn de huǒchē zài èr hào zhàntái — The eight o'clock is at platform two. A: yǒu zuòwèi ma — Are there seats? B: 有 yǒu — There are. A: 车站里有一家书店吗 chēzhàn lǐ yǒu yì jiā shūdiàn ma — Is there a bookshop in the station? B: yǒu, zài zhàntái qiánbian — There is, in front of the platform.
The answers are short: dānchéng, 有. The station's bookshop is counted with 家, and the platform has a number before 号.
