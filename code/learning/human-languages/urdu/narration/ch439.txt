Urdu, chapter 439: Night in the Fields.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
گیدڑ (gīdaṛ) — a jackal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to peck (at), then the Urdu for prey.

You'll want to know: گیدڑ.
گیدڑ — gīdaṛ — "a jackal".
رات کو کھیتوں میں گیدڑ بولتے ہیں۔ rāt ko khetoṅ meṅ gīdaṛ bolte haiṅ. At night jackals howl in the fields.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
گیدڑ (gīdaṛ) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin gīdaṛ, "a jackal", under gidda, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 4158).

Lesson 3 of 7.
مویشی (maveshī) — livestock; cattle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a jackal, then the Urdu for a cricket (insect).

You'll want to know: مویشی.
مویشی — maveshī — "livestock; cattle".
کسان کے مویشی کھیت میں ہیں۔ kisān ke maveshī khet meṅ haiṅ. The farmer's livestock are in the field.
Masculine, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
مویشی (maveshī) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic mawāshī, "livestock, herds".

چرنا — charnā — "to graze".
مویشی دن بھر میدان میں چرتے ہیں۔ maveshī din bhar maidān meṅ charte haiṅ. The cattle graze in the meadow all day.
Past (it …): وہ گائے دیر تک چری, voh gāe der tak charī.

چرنا (charnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin carnā, "to move about, to graze", under Sanskrit cárati, "moves" (CDIAL 4686).

Lesson 5 of 7.
جوتنا (jotnā) — to plough; to yoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for livestock, then the Urdu for to graze.

You'll want to know: جوتنا.
جوتنا — jotnā — "to plough; to yoke".
کسان صبح سویرے کھیت جوتتا ہے۔ kisān subah savere khet jottā hai. The farmer ploughs his field early in the morning.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے دو کھیت جوتے, us ne do khet jote.

The word, taken apart.
جوتنا (jotnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin jotnā, "to yoke, to plough", under Sanskrit yoktrayati, "fastens" (CDIAL 10524).
