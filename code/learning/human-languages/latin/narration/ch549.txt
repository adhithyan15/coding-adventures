Latin, chapter 549: Poems and readings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
poēma, poēmatis — a poem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an ornament, then the Latin for ugly.

You'll want to know: poēma, poēmatis.
poēma, poēmatis — "a poem".
Poēta novum poēma amīcīs legit. The poet reads his new poem to his friends.
Neuter, third declension, from Greek; dative and ablative plural poēmatīs. English poem.

The word, taken apart.
poēma was borrowed from Greek poiema "a thing made, a poem", from poieo "to make" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English poem comes from it.

epigramma, epigrammatis — "an epigram, a short poem".
Hoc epigramma breve et facētum est. This epigram is short and witty.
Neuter, third declension, from Greek: first an inscription, then a short, pointed poem. English epigram.

Lesson 3 of 5.
versiculus, versiculī — a little verse, a short line.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a poem, then the Latin for an epigram.

You'll want to know: versiculus, versiculī.
versiculus, versiculī — "a little verse, a short line".
Amīcus mihi paucōs versiculōs scrībit. My friend writes me a few little verses.
Masculine, a diminutive of versus — a line of verse.

The word, taken apart.
versiculus is a diminutive of versus "a line of writing", from vertō "to turn" (Lewis & Short): the turn at the end of a line.

Lesson 4 of 5.
recitātiō, recitātiōnis — a public reading.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an epigram, then the Latin for a little verse.

You'll want to know: recitātiō, recitātiōnis.
recitātiō, recitātiōnis — "a public reading".
Hodiē poēta recitātiōnem in ātriō facit. Today the poet gives a reading in the hall.
Feminine, third declension, from recitō — to read aloud. Authors read new work to invited listeners. English recitation.

The word, taken apart.
recitātiō is the action noun of recitō "to read aloud", built on re- and citō "to call" (Lewis & Short). English recitation comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
audītor, audītōris — a listener, hearer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a little verse, then the Latin for a public reading.

You'll want to know: audītor, audītōris.
audītor, audītōris — "a listener, hearer".
Audītōrēs poētae plaudunt. The listeners applaud the poet.
Masculine, third declension, from audiō; also a pupil. English auditor.

The word, taken apart.
audītor is built on audiō "to hear" with -tor (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English auditor and auditorium come from the family.
