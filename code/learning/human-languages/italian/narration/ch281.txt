Italian, chapter 281: Il giorno prima, il giorno dopo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la vigilia — the eve, the day before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for lately, then the Italian for previously.

You'll want to know: la vigilia.
la vigilia — "the eve, the day before".
La vigilia dell'esame non ho dormito. On the eve of the exam I did not sleep.
Above all the eve of a holiday or of a big day.

The word, taken apart.
vigilia is a learned borrowing from Latin vigilia "a staying awake, a night watch" (Lewis & Short), from vigil "awake": the night people stayed up before a feast.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'indomani — the next day, the day after.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for previously, then the Italian for the eve.

You'll want to know: l'indomani.
l'indomani — "the next day, the day after".
L'indomani siamo partiti presto. The next day we left early.
Narrative and written; in speech, il giorno dopo.

The word, taken apart.
indomani is in with domani "tomorrow", which is inherited from Late Latin dē māne "from the morning"; Latin māne meant "in the morning" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
stamattina — this morning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the eve, then the Italian for the next day.

You'll want to know: stamattina.
stamattina — "this morning".
Stamattina ho perso l'autobus. This morning I missed the bus.
Short for questa mattina; in Tuscany also stamani.

The word, taken apart.
stamattina fuses questa "this" and mattina "morning", which goes back to Latin mātūtīnus "of the early morning" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
stanotte — last night; tonight.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for the next day, then the Italian for this morning.

You'll want to know: stanotte.
stanotte — "last night; tonight".
Stanotte ha nevicato. It snowed last night.
With a past tense it is last night; with the present or the future, tonight.

The word, taken apart.
stanotte fuses questa "this" and notte "night", which is inherited from Latin nox, noctis "night" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la mattinata — the whole morning, the morning as a span.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for this morning, then the Italian for last night.

You'll want to know: la mattinata.
la mattinata — "the whole morning, the morning as a span".
Ho passato la mattinata in biblioteca. I spent the morning in the library.
The ending -ata stresses the span of time, as in la giornata.

The word, taken apart.
mattinata is mattina "morning" with -ata, the ending for a stretch of time; mattina goes back to Latin mātūtīnus "of the early morning" (Lewis & Short).
