# युष्माकम् (yuṣmākam) — your, of you all

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01, SA-PATTERN-WHATS-YOUR-NAME] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say “our” (**अस्माकम्**, *asmākam*). Then recall the first word of
**तव नाम किम्?** (*tava nāma kim?*): **तव**, “your” to one familiar person.

## You'll want to know: one addressee, then several

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02]; assesses=[] -->

| people addressed | Sanskrit | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| one | **तव** (*tava*) | your, of you |
| several | **युष्माकम्** (*yuṣmākam*) | your, of you all |

Say the new form slowly: *yuṣ-mā-kam*. The ending sounds like *asmākam*; the
opening tells you that the possessors are the people being addressed.

Like *tava*, **युष्माकम्** is a genitive. It answers “whose?” for several
people, and the noun it comes before does not have to change.

## The form, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02] -->

Whitney's *Sanskrit Grammar* §493 places **युष्माकम्** beside **अस्माकम्** as
the second-person genitive plural. It too is historically a fixed case-form of
an older adjective stem: the form is derived from *yuṣmāka*. For use here,
keep the functional contrast: **तव** addresses one person; **युष्माकम्**
addresses several.

Source: William Dwight Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §§493 and 516.
