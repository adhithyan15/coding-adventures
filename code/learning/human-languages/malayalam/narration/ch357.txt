Malayalam, chapter 357: Afterwards and Ever Since.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പിന്നീട് (pinnīṭŭ) — afterwards; later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to learn by hearsay, then the Malayalam for to fabricate.

You'll want to know: പിന്നീട്.
പിന്നീട് — pinnīṭŭ — "afterwards; later".
ആദ്യം അവൻ ഒരു ലേഖകൻ ആയിരുന്നു, പിന്നീട് പത്രാധിപരായി. ādyaṁ avan oru lēkhakan āyirunnu, pinnīṭŭ patrādhiparāyi. At first he was a correspondent, later he became an editor.
പിന്നെ is then, next; പിന്നീട് (pinnīṭŭ) is later on, at a later time.

The word, taken apart.
പിന്നീട് (pinnīṭŭ) is native Dravidian, built on പിൻ (pin), "behind, after" (DEDR 4205), the root of പിന്നെ, "then". It looks further ahead in time than പിന്നെ does.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ക്രമേണ (kramēṇa) — gradually.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to fabricate, then the Malayalam for afterwards.

You'll want to know: ക്രമേണ.
ക്രമേണ — kramēṇa — "gradually".
മഴ ക്രമേണ കുറഞ്ഞു. maḻa kramēṇa kuṟaññu. The rain gradually eased.
A Sanskrit form kept whole: it never changes its ending.

The word, taken apart.
ക്രമേണ (kramēṇa) is borrowed whole from Sanskrit krameṇa, "in regular course, by degrees" (CDIAL 3577), a form of krama, "a step, a series": things change step by step.

Lesson 3 of 5.
അതുവരെ (atuvare) — until then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for afterwards, then the Malayalam for gradually.

You'll want to know: അതുവരെ.
അതുവരെ — atuvare — "until then".
അച്ഛൻ ആറ് മണിക്ക് വന്നു, അതുവരെ ഞങ്ങൾ കാത്തിരുന്നു. acchan āṟŭ maṇikkŭ vannu, atuvare ñaṅṅaḷ kāttirunnu. Father came at six, and until then we waited.
Built like ഇതുവരെ, so far: അത്, that, + വരെ, up to.

The word, taken apart.
അതുവരെ (atuvare) is native Dravidian: അത് (atŭ), "that", and വരെ, "up to, until". It mirrors ഇതുവരെ, "until now", with the far "that" (DEDR 1) in place of the near "this".

Lesson 4 of 5.
അന്നുമുതൽ (annumutal) — ever since; from that day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for gradually, then the Malayalam for until then.

You'll want to know: അന്നുമുതൽ.
അന്നുമുതൽ — annumutal — "ever since; from that day".
അന്നുമുതൽ അവൾ രാവിലെ നടക്കുന്നു. annumutal avaḷ rāvile naṭakkunnu. Ever since that day she has walked in the mornings.
അന്ന്, that day, + മുതൽ, from: the time a change began.

The word, taken apart.
അന്നുമുതൽ (annumutal) is native Dravidian: അന്ന് (annŭ), "on that day", and മുതൽ (mutal), "from, beginning with", which DEDR 4950 sets beside Tamil mutal, "beginning, first".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: പൊടുന്നനെ.
പൊടുന്നനെ — poṭunnane — "all of a sudden".
പൊടുന്നനെ വിളക്കുകൾ എല്ലാം കെട്ടു. poṭunnane viḷakkukaḷ ellāṁ keṭṭu. All of a sudden all the lamps went out.
A story-telling word, stronger than പെട്ടെന്ന്.

The word, taken apart.
പൊടുന്നനെ (poṭunnane) is native Dravidian: DEDR 4483 lists Malayalam poṭunnanē, "suddenly, in a moment", beside a Tamil expression for suddenness.
