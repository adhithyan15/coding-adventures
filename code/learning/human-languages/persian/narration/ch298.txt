Persian, chapter 298: If It Rains.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
اگر … باشد (agar ... bâshad) (agar... bâshad) — if it is.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if", then "otherwise". (Agar, vagarna.) Then the subjunctive of budan for "she": "that she be". (Bâshad.)
[pause 1 second]
Agar has waited since chapter one hundred and fifty-eight. Now you hold the verb shape it asks for.

Grammar Lens: the condition first, in the subjunctive.
اگر, agar, opens the condition. The condition usually comes first, then a pause, then what follows from it:
اگر هوا خوب باشد، به پارک میرویم. — agar havâ khub bâshad, be pârk miravim — If the weather is good, we'll go to the park.
The condition is about later, and it may not come true, so its verb is in the subjunctive: bâshad, not ast. The second half is a plain present, which in Persian also covers the future.
اگر خسته باشی، بهتر است بخوابی. — agar khaste bâshi, behtar ast bekhâbi — If you're tired, you'd better sleep.
Agar can also sit after the person it is about: to agar khaste bâshi.... Either way, agar stays inside the condition half.

Grammar Lens: vagarna, the other branch.
Vagarna, "otherwise", is the condition turned round, "and if not":
زود بروید، وگرنه قطار را از دست میدهید. — zud beravid, vagarna qatâr râ az dast midahid — Leave early, otherwise you'll miss the train.

The word, taken apart.
agar continues Middle Persian agar, "if" (EtymDB 2.1). Vagarna is va, "and", agar, and na, "not", run together: the same "if" with "not" on the end.

Lesson 2 of 6.
ژاکت (zhâkat) — a cardigan, in case it turns cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if the weather is good". (Agar havâ khub bâshad.) Then a padded jacket and a pullover. (Kâpshan, poliver.)
[pause 1 second]
A condition is easiest to practise on the weather, and on what to wear for it.

You'll want to know: ژاکت.
ژاکت — zhâkat — "a cardigan", a knitted jacket that buttons down the front.
اگر هوا سرد باشد، ژاکت بپوشید. — agar havâ sard bâshad, zhâkat bepushid — If it's cold, put on a cardigan.
Bepushid is pushidan, "to put on, to wear", on its stem push-. A zhâkat is lighter than a kâpshan, the padded jacket from chapter one hundred and twenty, and it opens at the front where a poliver goes over the head.
Say zhâ-kat. The zh is the sound in the middle of English measure, and its letter is one Persian added to the Arabic alphabet. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB 2.1 has no entry for zhâkat. It is usually traced to French jaquette, a jacket. EtymDB takes English jacket back to Middle French jacquet, from the same French family.

Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ز and پ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ژاکت (zhâkat), "a cardigan". You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ژ.
ژ — zhe, the sound in the middle of English measure.
It opens ژاکت (zhâkat). It is ز with three dots in place of one, and like ز it takes nothing on its left: after ژ (zhe) the pen lifts, and ا stands on its own before کت.
Arabic has no zh, so the Arabic alphabet had no letter for it. Persian made four letters of its own from Arabic ones, with extra dots or an extra stroke: پ from ب, چ from ج, گ from ک, and ژ (zhe) from ز. You have now written all four.

Writing: ژ.
Put your pen on ژ (zhe) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 4 of 6.
اگر باران بیاید (agar bârân biyâyad) — if it rains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if it's cold, put on a cardigan". (Agar havâ sard bâshad, zhâkat bepushid.) Then "to come", and its present stem. (Âmadan, â-.)
[pause 1 second]
In everyday Persian, rain comes: bârân miâyad, "it's raining", is word for word "rain is coming". That puts âmadan into a rainy condition.

Grammar Lens: biyâyad, the subjunctive of âmadan.
Be- meets the stem â-, and a y slips in to keep the two vowels apart: بیاید, biyâyad, "that it come". So biyâyam, biyâyi, biyâyad.
اگر باران بیاید، در خانه میمانیم. — agar bârân biyâyad, dar khâne mimânim — If it rains, we'll stay at home.
Word for word, "if rain come, we stay in the house". Mimânim is mândan, "to stay", on its stem mân-.

Grammar Lens: three shapes for the result.
A real condition can come true, and its result takes one of three shapes.
What will happen, in the plain present:
اگر دیر برسی، قطار را از دست میدهی. — agar dir beresi, qatâr râ az dast midahi — If you arrive late, you'll miss the train.
What to do, as a polite instruction: agar bârân biyâyad, zhâkat bepushid, if it rains, put on a cardigan.
What must happen, with bâyad: agar bârân biyâyad, bâyad zud beravim, if it rains, we have to leave early.
The agar half keeps its subjunctive in all three. Only the result changes.

Lesson 5 of 6.
بستگی دارد (bastegi dârad) — it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if it rains, we'll stay at home". (Agar bârân biyâyad, dar khâne mimânim.) Then "to tie, to close". (Bastan.)
[pause 1 second]
Asked about a plan, you often cannot answer yes or no yet. The honest answer is "it depends", and then the condition.

You'll want to know: بستگی دارد.
بستگی دارد — bastegi dârad — "it depends".
— فردا به پارک میروید؟ — fardâ be pârk miravid? — Are you going to the park tomorrow? — بستگی دارد. اگر هوا خوب باشد، بله. — bastegi dârad. agar havâ khub bâshad, bale. — It depends. If the weather is good, yes.
To name what it depends on, add be, "to": بستگی دارد به هوا, bastegi dârad be havâ, "it depends on the weather". The be phrase can also come first: be havâ bastegi dârad. And be shomâ bastegi dârad, "it depends on you", is a polite "it's up to you".
The verb is dâshtan, so it takes no mi-: dârad.

The phrase, taken apart.
bastegi is baste, "bound, tied", with -gi, which makes a noun of it: "boundness". Baste is the participle of bastan, "to tie, to close", built the way gozashte was built from gozashtan. So bastegi dârad be havâ is "it has a binding to the weather".
EtymDB 2.1 takes bastan back to an Indo-European root meaning "to bind, to tie", the same root it gives for English bind.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on the weather"? (Bastegi dârad be havâ.) What is bastegi, taken apart? (Baste, "bound", with -gi: boundness.) Which English verb shares its root? (Bind.)


Lesson 6 of 6.
Review — a plan that depends on the weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if the weather is good", then "if it rains". (Agar havâ khub bâshad, agar bârân biyâyad.)

What you've built.
Four lessons, and a plan can now hang on something. Agar opens the condition and takes the subjunctive, bâshad or biyâyad. The result is a present, a polite instruction, or bâyad. And when you cannot say yet, bastegi dârad holds the answer open.
