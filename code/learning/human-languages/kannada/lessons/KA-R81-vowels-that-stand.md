# (ಇ ಈ ಉ ಊ ಓ) — five that need nothing to lean on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-115, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-126] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two full-size letters, not signs. Name the one that says *i* and the
one that says *u*.

## Script: the same vowel, two shapes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-115, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-130, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-126, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-127, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-125] -->

| standing letter | sound | when you use it |
|---|---|---|
| **ಇ** | *i* | the word opens on the vowel |
| **ಈ** | *ī* | the same, held long |
| **ಉ** | *u* | the word opens on the vowel |
| **ಊ** | *ū* | the same, held long |
| **ಓ** | *ō* | the word opens on the vowel |

Every one of these has a partner among the signs. The difference is not the
sound — it is **whether a consonant is there to carry it.** A vowel with nothing
in front of it must be written full-size; the moment a consonant arrives, the
same vowel shrinks to a sign hanging off it.

Two pairs make this visible: **ಇ** against **ಿ**, and **ಓ** against **ೋ**. Same
vowel, twice, and the shape tells you where in the word you are.
