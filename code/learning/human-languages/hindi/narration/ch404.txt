Hindi, chapter 404: Traffic Jams.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ट्रैफ़िक (ṭraifik) — traffic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to take a stroll, then the Hindi for to pant.

You'll want to know: ट्रैफ़िक.
ट्रैफ़िक — ṭraifik — "traffic".
शाम को सड़क पर बहुत ट्रैफ़िक होता है। śām ko saṛak par bahut ṭraifik hotā hai. There is a lot of traffic on the road in the evening.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ट्रैफ़िक (ṭraifik) is borrowed from English traffic, and like its source it takes no plural: बहुत ट्रैफ़िक, a lot of traffic.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: जाम.
जाम — jām — "a traffic jam".
पुल पर बड़ा जाम था। pul par baṛā jām thā. There was a big jam on the bridge.
Masculine.
जाम (jām) लगना, for a jam to build up: सड़क पर जाम (jām) लगा है.

The word, taken apart.
जाम (jām) is borrowed from English jam, a road packed so tight that nothing moves.

Lesson 4 of 5.
रेंगना (reṅgnā) — to crawl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a traffic jam, then the Hindi for a motorcycle.

You'll want to know: रेंगना.
रेंगना — reṅgnā — "to crawl".
जाम में गाड़ियाँ रेंग रही थीं। jām meṁ gāṛiyā̃ reṅg rahī thīṁ. The cars were crawling along in the jam.
Past (it …): वह साँप घास में रेंगा, vah sā̃p ghās meṁ reṅgā.

The word, taken apart.
रेंगना (reṅgnā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit riṅgati, "crawls" (CDIAL 10739), which lists Hindi rẽgnā. A baby, a snake and a jammed lane of traffic all crawl.

Lesson 5 of 5.
हाँकना (hā̃knā) — to drive (animals); to drive (a cart).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a motorcycle, then the Hindi for to crawl.

You'll want to know: हाँकना.
हाँकना — hā̃knā — "to drive (animals); to drive (a cart)".
एक किसान सड़क पर भैंसें हाँक रहा था। ek kisān saṛak par bhaĩsẽ hā̃k rahā thā. A farmer was driving buffaloes along the road.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने गायें हाँकीं, usne gāyẽ hā̃kīṁ.

The word, taken apart.
हाँकना (hā̃knā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit hakkayati, "calls to" (CDIAL 13939), which lists Hindi hā̃knā, "to cry out, drive off": cattle are driven by shouting at them.
