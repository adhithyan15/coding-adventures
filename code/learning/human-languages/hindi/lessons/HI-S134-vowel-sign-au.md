# ◌ौ — the same mark, one stroke more

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-133, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-129] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: **झ** — what sound does it carry?

[PAUSE 2s] Now look at **दो** and find the mark riding on the **द**. The new one
is that mark with a second stroke added.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ौ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-134]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ौ** — *au*.

It is a **vowel sign**, so it never stands alone: it attaches to a consonant and
replaces the *a* built into it.

Where it sits: the **ā bar on the right, plus a double stroke above**.

Worked through: **क** + **◌ौ** = **कौ** — *kau*.

## Script: the pair that differs by one stroke
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-134, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-129] -->

| mark | sound | above the bar |
|---|---|---|
| ◌ो | o | one stroke |
| ◌ौ | au | two strokes |

**The only difference is the count**, which makes this the easiest mark in the
script to misread and the easiest to fix: when you meet one, stop and count the
strokes above the headline.

You already say these, and every one has **◌ौ** somewhere inside it:

- **नौ** — nine
- **मौसम** — weather
- **चौदह** — fourteen

## Writing: ◌ौ — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-134] -->

Put your pen on **◌ौ** and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
