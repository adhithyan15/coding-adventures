# (invitar, el importe, descontar, el golpe) — from cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-01, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Four words, no prompts. What somebody does when they are paying,
what the bill says, what happens to it over twelve months, and what it would
have been if it had all come at once.

## You'll want to know: the four, cold
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-01, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-02, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-03, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-04] -->

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| to invite | **invitar** |
| the amount | **el importe** |
| to deduct | **descontar** |
| the blow | **el golpe** |

## Grammar Lens: three of the four were already in the room
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-01, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-03] -->

Only one of these arrived from nowhere.

| word | what it came out of |
|---|---|
| invitar | **el invitado**, its own participle |
| el importe | **importar**, its third sense |
| descontar | **contar**, with *des-* reversing |

*El golpe* is the outsider, and even it has a relative you know in English.

So the chapter was mostly a matter of being handed things the book had been
holding: *invitar* had been named inside *el invitado*, and *importar* had told
you two of its three jobs already.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-02, ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-04] -->

Two roots do most of the work here.

| root | what it gave |
|---|---|
| *portare*, to carry | **importe**, deporte, comportamiento |
| *computare*, to reckon | **descontar**, contar, English *count* |

And *golpe* is Latin **colaphus** from Greek **kólaphos**, a box on the ear —
the same blow English keeps in **coup**, and the one French built *beaucoup*
out of. *Golpe de estado* and *coup d'état* are the same two words said twice.
