## You'll want to know: 错过
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C356-MISS356-02]; assesses=[] -->

**错过** — *cuòguò* — "to miss a chance; to miss a train".

> **我错过了那个机会** *wǒ cuòguò le nàge jīhuì* — I missed that chance.

The new character, in pieces: 错 — thirteen strokes in two pieces: on the left the metal piece of 钱, five strokes; on the right a short bar with two verticals through it and a long bar under them, then 日 *rì*, eight strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**错过** (*cuòguò*) is built from 错 'wrong' and 过 'to pass', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshak* and *kwaH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a file' and 'mixed', and the second 'a mistake' and 'to pass over'. How the first came to mean wrong is not settled.
