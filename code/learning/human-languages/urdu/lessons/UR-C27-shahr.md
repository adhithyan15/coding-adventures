## You'll want to know: شہر
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-SHAHR]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-MEIN-POST, UR-LEX-PURANA] -->

> **اس شہر میں** — *is shahr meṅ* — **in this city.**

> **پرانا شہر** — *purānā shahr* — **the old city.**

Twenty-six chapters, and the largest place this book has named is a room. **شہر**
is the first word in it for somewhere you could be *from*.

**شہر** does not end in **-ا**, so it does not change shape before the
postposition — but **اس** does, exactly as it did with **کمرے**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-SHAHR, UR-LEX-KAMRA] -->

**شہر** is Persian, and behind it stands Old Persian *xsathra*, meaning
**realm**. It is the same word that ends the royal name *Ardashir*, and the same
root as Sanskrit *kshatra*, "dominion" — which is where *Kshatriya*, the warrior
caste, comes from.

So a **city** in Urdu is etymologically *the place somebody rules*, where
**کمرہ** came in by sea from Portugal. Two words for two kinds of space, from two
directions, a thousand years apart.
