## HL-C217 — Marathi shows seven Devanagari letters it never teaches

The pre-A1 verb tranche (chapters 22-25) was authored around a hard constraint
that is worth writing down, because the next Marathi tranche will hit it too.

Marathi's script closure measures 39 taught glyphs against 46 shown. The seven
shown-but-never-taught are **घ, ए, ँ, ख, ग, छ, ऊ**. Every one of them already
appears in load-bearing body prose somewhere in chapters 5-17 — **घ** inside
**घेणे** and **काळजी घ्या**, **ख** inside **खाणे**, **ग** inside **सांगणे**,
**ँ** inside Hindi contrasts such as **आँख**.

That constraint shaped this tranche in two visible ways, and both were choices
rather than accidents:

- Nine of the twelve new headwords were selected so that every glyph in their
  body prose was already taught, which is why the tranche adds zero new closure
  violations while adding nineteen lessons.
- **सांगणे** was authored **gloss-first on purpose**: its Devanagari appears
  only in the headword, where the romanization exemption makes it exposure, and
  its body prose is entirely romanized. The lesson says in as many words that
  its *ga* has not been taught and that the letter is owed. **म्हणणे** then
  arrives as the readable partner, so the learner meets the by-ear word and the
  writable word in the same chapter.

**The work this leaves.** Three writing lessons — **ग**, **घ**, **ख** — would
close never-taught glyphs from seven to four, make **सांगणे**, **घेणे** and
**खाणे** readable, and let a later tranche stop routing around three of the
commonest consonants in the language.

**Why they are not in this tranche.** The existing Marathi writing lessons each
cite a specific stroke-order animation on Wikimedia Commons (`Devanagari_b`,
`Devanagari_bʰ`, `Devanagari_v`, and a stroke-order SVG for **आ**). No such
citation was on hand for **ग**, **घ** or **ख**, and inventing a source URL to
match the house pattern would be worse than leaving the gap measured. Whoever
picks this up should confirm the Commons files exist before writing the
lessons, and note that adding pen lessons pushes the track's drivable share
back down — the same tranche should carry voice material to hold it.

**Also left.** The eight remaining Marathi headwords without romanization are
all `type: writing` lessons in chapter 21, where withholding the romanization
is the point of the exercise. They should stay as they are; the exposure
counter will read 8 rather than 0 for that reason and that is correct.
