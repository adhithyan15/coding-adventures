Lesson 1 of 6.
chocar — to crash, to collide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to survive, then the Spanish for to trap.

You'll want to know: chocar.
chocar — "to crash, to collide".
Dos coches chocaron en la esquina. Two cars crashed on the corner.
Chocar con or chocar contra — to crash into. ¡Chócala! — high five!

The word, taken apart.
The origin of chocar is not settled. It is usually treated as imitative, a word that copies the noise of a blow; French choquer is close beside it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
atropellar — to run over, to knock down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to crash, then the Spanish for the crash.

You'll want to know: atropellar.
atropellar — "to run over, to knock down".
Un coche atropelló a una señora en el paso de cebra. A car knocked down a woman on the zebra crossing.
Me atropelló un coche — I was hit by a car.

The word, taken apart.
atropellar is built on tropel 'a rushing crowd' (EtymDB), with a-. Tropel is usually said to have come into Spanish from Occitan or French, and its root is usually traced to Germanic.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ileso — unhurt, unharmed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the crash, then the Spanish for to run over.

You'll want to know: ileso.
ileso — "unhurt, unharmed".
Los pasajeros salieron ilesos del accidente. The passengers came out of the accident unhurt.
Salir ileso — to escape unhurt.

The word, taken apart.
ileso is a learned borrowing from Latin illaesus 'unhurt, unharmed' (EtymDB), built on in- 'not' and laesus 'hurt', from laedere 'to hurt'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
caer — to fall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to run over, then the Spanish for unhurt.

You'll want to know: caer.
caer — "to fall".
Un árbol cayó en medio de la calle. A tree fell in the middle of the street.
Caerse is to fall over: Me caí en la calle. Forms: caigo, cayó, cayeron.

The word, taken apart.
caer is inherited from Latin cadere 'to fall, to happen', through spoken Latin cadēre (EtymDB). The d between vowels was lost.
