Lesson 1 of 5.
めまい (memai) — dizziness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for gargling, then the Japanese for physical condition.

You'll want to know: めまい.
めまい — memai — "dizziness".
あさ おきた とき、めまいが しました。 — asa okita toki, memai ga shimashita — "When I got up this morning, I felt dizzy."

The word, taken apart.
めまい (memai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana and lists five kanji spellings for it, so no single spelling shows where it comes from (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ねぶそく (nebusoku) — lack of sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for sluggish, then the Japanese for worn out.

You'll want to know: ねぶそく.
ねぶそく — nebusoku — "lack of sleep".
ねぶそくで あたまが いたいです。 — nebusoku de atama ga itai desu — "My head aches from lack of sleep."

The word, taken apart.
ねぶそく (nebusoku) is a mixed word: native ne, "sleeping", from neru, joined to Sino-Japanese busoku, "shortage", from bu, "negative" (Middle Chinese pjuw), and soku, "be sufficient" (tsjowk), readings borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
