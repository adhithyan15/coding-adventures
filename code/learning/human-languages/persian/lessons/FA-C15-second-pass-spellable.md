# ممنون · زن · دوست — three words the letters have caught up with

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-MAMNUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say the word you have used for *thank you* since the first page. You
have had it in your mouth far longer than you have had it on the page.

## You'll want to know: the letters have caught up with the words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-MAMNUN-01, FA-LEX-ZAN, FA-LEX-DUST] -->

Three words you already say, now taken apart with letters you can name:

| word | | its letters |
|---|---|---|
| **ممنون** *mamnun* | thank you | م · م · ن · و · ن |
| **زن** *zan* | woman | ز · ن |
| **دوست** *dust* | friend | د · و · س · ت |

Look at what is **not** there. *Mamnun* has an *a* you say and no *a* you read,
because Persian leaves the short vowels off the page — the rule you were told on
the very first word, now visible in a word you can spell. *Dust* has the
opposite: the **و** is written, because a long *u* earns a letter.

**Short vowels are heard and not written; long ones are both.** That single
sentence is most of what makes Persian readable.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-MAMNUN-01, FA-LEX-ZAN, FA-LEX-DUST] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at **ممنون** and name each letter from the right]
- [YOU SAY: **zan** — woman; then name its two letters]
- [YOU SAY: **dust** — friend; then say which of its four letters is the long *u*]
- [YOU SAY: **mamnun** — and say where the *a* you hear has gone on the page]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-MAMNUN-01, FA-LEX-ZAN, FA-LEX-DUST] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which word means *thank you*, and which vowel of it is unwritten?
(**ممنون** — the short *a*.) Which two letters spell *woman*? (**ز ن**.) Which
letter of **دوست** carries the long *u*? (**و**.) And what is the rule the three
words share? (**Short vowels are not written; long ones are.**)
