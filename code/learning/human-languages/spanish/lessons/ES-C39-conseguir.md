# conseguir — "to get", by following a thing until you have it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TRAER-01, ES-LEX-COGER-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Traigo* carries a thing that is already yours. *Coger* grabs what is
in front of you. This verb is for the getting that took some work.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `g-hard` — the *gu* in *conseguir* is one hard *g*; the *u* is silent.
- `s-clear` — a clean hissing *s*: *con-se-GEER*.

## The word, taken apart: conseguir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-CONSEGUIR-04, ES-ETYMON-CONSEGUIR-05]; assesses=[] -->

**conseguir** ("**to get, to obtain, to manage to**") ← Latin **cōnsequī**:
*com-* ("along with") + **sequī**, "**to follow**". You got a thing by following
it until it was yours.

*Sequī* is one of Latin's great English suppliers: **sequence**, **sequel**,
**consequence**, **subsequent**, **consecutive**; **second**, from *secundus*,
"the one following"; **execute** and **prosecute**; **obsequious**, following
someone about; and through French, **pursue**, **ensue** and **suit**.

Spanish also keeps the formal partner **obtener** ← *obtinēre*, "to hold onto" —
English **obtain**. It behaves exactly like *tener*, so its *yo*-form is
*obtengo*. *Conseguir* is the everyday one.

## Grammar Lens: the third crack, and a silent u that leaves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-E-I-06]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CONSEGUIR-04, ES-GRAMMAR-ASK-SPLIT-04, ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-O-UE-06] -->

| person | form |
|---|---|
| yo | **consigo** |
| tú | **consigues** |
| él/ella/usted | **consigue** |
| nosotros | **conseguimos** |
| ellos/ustedes | **consiguen** |

You have met *dormir* breaking **o→ue** and *pido* quietly breaking **e→i**.
Here is that third pattern in full: stress on the stem turns *e* into *i*, and
*conseguimos* stays plain because the stress moved to the ending.

Now the spelling. Spanish writes *gu* only to keep a *g* hard in front of *e* and
*i*. Once the *e* becomes *i*, there is nothing in front to guard against, so the
*u* has no job and goes: **consigo**, not *consiguo*. Two changes, one form, and
only one of them is a sound.
