5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مرآة (mirʾāh) — a mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a bed, then the Arabic for the floor; the earth.

You'll want to know: مرآة.
مرآة — mirʾāh — "a mirror".

The word, taken apart.
مرآة (mirʾāh) is built on the native Arabic root r-ʾ-y, "to see" (Lane). Mirʾāh is an instrument noun, "a thing to see with".

Lesson 5 of 5.
سكين (sikkīn) — a knife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a key, then the Arabic for a plate.

You'll want to know: سكين.
سكين — sikkīn — "a knife".

The word, taken apart.
سكين (sikkīn) is usually traced to Aramaic sakkīnā, "knife" (EtymDB), with Hebrew sakkin as a cousin. Lane lists it under s-k-n, "to be still", and quotes the old explanation that a knife stills the animal it slaughters; that is a guess of the Arabic grammarians, a folk etymology.
