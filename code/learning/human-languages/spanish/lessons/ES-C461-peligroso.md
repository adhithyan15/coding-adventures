## You'll want to know: peligroso
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-03] -->

**peligroso** — dangerous.

> **Peligro.** No pasar.

**El peligro** is the noun and the single word on the sign; **peligroso** is
the adjective. *Una zona peligrosa* — a dangerous area. *Es peligroso dejarlo
ahí* — it's dangerous to leave it there.

*Estar en peligro* is to be in danger, and *fuera de peligro* — out of danger —
uses the frame you already have from *fuera de servicio*.

## Grammar Lens: -oso makes an adjective of a noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-01] -->

**-oso** means "full of" or "given to", and it turns nouns into adjectives all
over the language:

| noun | adjective |
|---|---|
| el peligro | peligr**oso** |
| el cuidado | cuidad**oso** — careful |
| la fama | fam**oso** — famous |

English has the same ending in *dangerous*, *famous* and *curious*, from the
same Latin *-osus*, so the pair usually translates straight across.

It agrees like any adjective — *peligrosa*, *peligrosos* — and the noun keeps
its own gender: *el peligro* is masculine, *una zona peligrosa* feminine.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02] -->

Latin **periculum**, a trial or a risk — from a root meaning **to try**, the
one also behind *experiri*, to test by going through.

So a *peligro* is something you have to **try your luck against**, and English
**peril** is the same word, kept closer to the Latin. **Experience** and
**experiment** are relatives too: all of them are attempts, and only some are
survived.

From *periculum* to *peligro* the **r** and the **l** swapped places — the
metathesis you met in *milagro* from *miraculum*. Both words came through
speech, and both got their letters shuffled on the way; a word that arrived by
book was never shuffled: **periclitar**, to be in danger, keeps *periculum*'s
letters in their Latin order, and stands beside the spoken *peligro* as the
same word at two speeds.
