Lesson 2 of 5.
कोथिंबीर (kothimbīr) — coriander leaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a wok, then the Marathi for mustard seed.

You'll want to know: कोथिंबीर.
कोथिंबीर — kothimbīr — "coriander leaves".

The word, taken apart.
कोथिंबीर (kothimbīr) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kustumbarī, "coriander" (CDIAL 3380). CDIAL calls the Sanskrit word non-Aryan.

Lesson 3 of 5.
कढीपत्ता (kaḍhīpattā) — curry leaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for mustard seed, then the Marathi for coriander leaves.

You'll want to know: कढीपत्ता.
कढीपत्ता — kaḍhīpattā — "curry leaves".

The word, taken apart.
कढीपत्ता (kaḍhīpattā) is "kaḍhī leaf": kaḍhī, a dish of flour boiled in buttermilk, from Sanskrit kvathita, "boiled" (CDIAL 3637), and pattā, "leaf", from Sanskrit pattra (CDIAL 7733).

शेंगदाणे (śeṅgdāṇe) — peanuts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for coriander leaves, then the Marathi for curry leaves.

You'll want to know: शेंगदाणे.
शेंगदाणे — śeṅgdāṇe — "peanuts".

The word, taken apart.
शेंगदाणे (śeṅgdāṇe) is "pod-grains": śeṅg, "pod", whose origin is not settled, and dāṇe, "grains", usually traced to Persian dāna, "grain, seed".
