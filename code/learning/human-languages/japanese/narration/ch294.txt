Lesson 1 of 5.
みこし (mikoshi) — a portable shrine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the night sky, then the Japanese for to bustle.

You'll want to know: みこし.
みこし — mikoshi — "a portable shrine".
まつりで みこしが まちを とおります。 — matsuri de mikoshi ga machi o tōrimasu — "At the festival a portable shrine goes through the town."

The word, taken apart.
みこし (mikoshi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ぎょうれつ (gyōretsu) — a procession; a queue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a portable shrine, then the Japanese for to carry on one's shoulders.

You'll want to know: ぎょうれつ.
ぎょうれつ — gyōretsu — "a procession; a queue".
ぎょうれつは ゆっくり すすみました。 — gyōretsu wa yukkuri susumimashita — "The procession moved slowly along."

The word, taken apart.
ぎょうれつ (gyōretsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gyō, "line" (Middle Chinese hang) and retsu, "row" (ljet) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to carry on one's shoulders, then the Japanese for a procession.

You'll want to know: もりあがる.
もりあがる — moriagaru — "to liven up; to get going".
ゆうがたに なって、まつりは もりあがりました。 — yūgata ni natte, matsuri wa moriagarimashita — "As evening came on, the festival really got going."
Polite もりあがります, moriagarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): まつりが もりあがりました。, matsuri ga moriagarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
もりあがる (moriagaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mori, "heaping" (from moru), and agaru, "to rise" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ぼんおどり (bon'odori) — a Bon festival dance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a procession, then the Japanese for to liven up.

You'll want to know: ぼんおどり.
ぼんおどり — bon'odori — "a Bon festival dance".
なつには 日本中で ぼんおどりが あります。 — natsu ni wa nihonjū de bon'odori ga arimasu — "In summer there is festival dancing all over Japan."

The word, taken apart.
ぼんおどり (bon'odori) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: bon, "lantern festival", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese bwon), and odori, "dance", is native Japanese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
