Russian, chapter 305: Bringing up a family.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
воспитывать (vospítyvat) — to bring up, to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for old age, then the Russian for to grow up.

You'll want to know: воспитывать.
воспитывать (vospítyvat) — "to bring up, to raise".
Бабушка воспитала троих внуков. Bábushka vospitála troíkh vnúkov. Grandma brought up three grandchildren.
Imperfective; perfective воспитать (vospitát).

The word, taken apart.
воспитывать (vospítyvat) is usually described as a Church Slavonic formation, from вос- "up" and питать "to feed, to nourish": to feed up. EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
усыновлять (usynovlyát) — to adopt (a child).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to grow up, then the Russian for to bring up.

You'll want to know: усыновлять.
усыновлять (usynovlyát) — "to adopt (a child)".
Они усыновили мальчика из детского дома. Oní usynovíli málchika iz détskovo dóma. They adopted a boy from a children's home.
Imperfective; the perfective is усыновить (usynovít). For a girl also удочерить.

The word, taken apart.
усыновлять (usynovlyát) is built on у- and сын "son", inherited from Proto-Slavic synŭ, from PIE suHnús (EtymDB): to make someone a son.

Lesson 3 of 5.
стареть (starét) — to grow old, to age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to bring up, then the Russian for to adopt.

You'll want to know: стареть.
стареть (starét) — "to grow old, to age".
Мой пёс стареет и много спит. Moy pyos staréyet i mnógo spit. My dog is getting old and sleeps a lot.
Imperfective; perfective постареть (postarét). Of things: устареть — to go out of date.

The word, taken apart.
стареть (starét) is built on старый "old", inherited from Proto-Slavic starŭ "old" (EtymDB), with the verb ending -еть for becoming.

Lesson 4 of 5.
выпускной (vypusknóy) — a graduation party, a prom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to adopt, then the Russian for to grow old.

You'll want to know: выпускной.
выпускной (vypusknóy) — "a graduation party, a prom".
На выпускном все танцевали до утра. Na vypusknóm vse tantseváli do utrá. At the graduation party everyone danced until morning.
Short for выпускной (vypusknóy) вечер; an adjective that serves as a noun.

The word, taken apart.
выпускной (vypusknóy) is built on выпуск "a graduating class", from выпускать "to let out", whose root is Proto-Slavic pustiti "to let go" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
поколение (pokoléniye) — a generation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to grow old, then the Russian for a graduation party.

You'll want to know: поколение.
поколение (pokoléniye) — "a generation".
Три поколения семьи жили в одном доме. Tri pokoléniya semí zhíli v odnóm dóme. Three generations of the family lived in one house.
Neuter.

The word, taken apart.
поколение (pokoléniye) is built on колено "knee", which in older Russian also meant "a branch of a family, a line of descent"; EtymDB lists no parent.
