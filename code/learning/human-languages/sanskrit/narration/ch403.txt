Sanskrit, chapter 403: The Long Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पन्थाः (panthāḥ) — a route; a path.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lies down to sleep, then the Sanskrit for water for washing the feet.

You'll want to know: पन्थाः.
पन्थाः — panthāḥ — "a route; a path".
पन्थाः वनस्य मध्ये गच्छति। panthāḥ vanasya madhye gacchati. The route runs through the middle of the forest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पन्थाः (panthāḥ) is the nominative of pathin, "a way, a road", whose strong stem is panthan; it comes from the same word as pātheya, "food for the journey"; MW gives pathin as a way, path, road or route (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for water for washing the feet, then the Sanskrit for a route.

You'll want to know: दीर्घः.
दीर्घः — dīrghaḥ — "long".
पर्वतं प्रति पन्थाः दीर्घः अस्ति। parvataṃ prati panthāḥ dīrghaḥ asti. The route to the mountain is long.

The word, taken apart.
दीर्घः (dīrghaḥ) is the adjective MW gives for long, in space and in time; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
दुर्गमः (durgamaḥ) — hard to get through; impassable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a route, then the Sanskrit for long.

You'll want to know: दुर्गमः.
दुर्गमः — durgamaḥ — "hard to get through; impassable".
वर्षायां पन्थाः दुर्गमः भवति। varṣāyāṃ panthāḥ durgamaḥ bhavati. In the rains the route becomes hard to get through.

The word, taken apart.
दुर्गमः (durgamaḥ) is built from dur, "hard, ill", and the root √gam, "to go": hard to go through; MW gives durgama as difficult to be traversed, impassable (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पदातिः (padātiḥ) — a traveller on foot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for long, then the Sanskrit for hard to get through.

You'll want to know: पदातिः.
पदातिः — padātiḥ — "a traveller on foot".
पदातिः मार्गस्य पार्श्वे चलति। padātiḥ mārgasya pārśve calati. The traveller on foot walks at the side of the road.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पदातिः (padātiḥ) is built on pada, "a foot, a step"; MW gives padāti as going on foot, a pedestrian, and divides it, with a query, as pada + āti (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
विचरति (vicarati) — roams; rambles about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hard to get through, then the Sanskrit for a traveller on foot.

You'll want to know: विचरति.
विचरति — vicarati — "roams; rambles about".
पथिकः वने विचरति। pathikaḥ vane vicarati. The wayfarer roams in the forest.
Past (he …): सः वने विचरितवान्, saḥ vane vicaritavān.

The word, taken apart.
विचरति (vicarati) is built from vi, "apart, here and there", and the root √car, "to move", which also gives carati, "moves about"; MW gives vi-car as to rove or ramble about (MW).
