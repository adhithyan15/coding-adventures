Latin, chapter 514: Scorn and displeasure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
contemnō, contemnere — to despise, scorn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for it shames, then the Latin for shyness.

You'll want to know: contemnō, contemnere.
contemnō, contemnere — "to despise, scorn".
Sapiēns pecūniam contemnit. The wise man despises money.
contemnō, contemnere, contempsī, contemptum, with the accusative. English contempt.

The word, taken apart.
contemnō is built on con- and temnō "to scorn" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English contempt and contemptible come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
spernō, spernere — to reject, spurn.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: spernō, spernere.
spernō, spernere — "to reject, spurn".
Puella amōrem iuvenis spernit. The girl rejects the young man's love.
spernō, spernere, sprēvī, sprētum. Stronger than contemnō: to push away.

The word, taken apart.
spernō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root sper- (EtymDB), and English spurn is a cousin from the same root (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
dēspiciō, dēspicere — to look down on, despise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to despise, then the Latin for to reject.

You'll want to know: dēspiciō, dēspicere.
dēspiciō, dēspicere — "to look down on, despise".
Dīves pauperēs dēspicit. The rich man looks down on the poor.
dēspiciō, dēspicere, dēspexī, dēspectum: dē 'down' + speciō 'to look'. English despise.

The word, taken apart.
dēspiciō is built on dē- "down" and speciō "to behold" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English despise comes from it through Old French.

indignātiō, indignātiōnis — "indignation, outrage".

Feminine, third declension, from indignor. English indignation.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
displiceō, displicēre — to displease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to look down on, then the Latin for indignation.

You'll want to know: displiceō, displicēre.
displiceō, displicēre — "to displease".
Haec verba mihi valdē displicent. These words displease me greatly.
displiceō, displicēre, displicuī, displicitum, with the dative; the opposite of placeō. English displease.

The word, taken apart.
displiceō is built on dis- "apart, not" and placeō "to please" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English displease comes from it through Old French.
