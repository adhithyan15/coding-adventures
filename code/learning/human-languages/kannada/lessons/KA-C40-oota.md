# ಊಟ (ūṭa) — "a meal," the word that closes this chapter's table

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C32-TINNU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter 32 taught you *tinnu*, "to eat," and then, in a single
line, reached for a different word for a **full** meal — without ever
teaching that word. Fourteen chapters later, here it is.

## You'll want to know: ಊಟ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C40-OOTA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ಊಟ** — *ūṭa* — **a meal**

> **ಊಟ ಮಾಡು.** — *ūṭa māḍu.* — "to have a meal" (literally, "meal-do")

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*(Skip this if you already read Kannada.)* **ಊ** is the **independent**
long-*ū* vowel — the shape a word uses when it *opens* with this sound,
the way **ಋ** opened *ṛtu* back in Chapter 14. Every other long-*ū* you have
read so far rode on a consonant as the small sign **ೂ**; here, with no
consonant in front of it to carry, the vowel stands alone in its full
independent form.

## The word, taken apart: the word behind a phrase you already knew
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C40-OOTA-02]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C39-HAALU-01, KA-ETYMON-C25-SHUBHA-RATRI-01, KA-ETYMON-C25-SHUBHA-RATRI-02, KA-ETYMON-C25-SHUBHA-RATRI-03, KA-ETYMON-C31-SHUBHA-MADHYAHNA-01, KA-PRAGMATICS-C31-SHUBHA-MADHYAHNA-02] -->

**ಊಟ** is native Dravidian, a root Tulu keeps as its own everyday word for
food, *ūṭa*. Chapter 32 used the phrase **ಊಟ ಮಾಡು** to mark a **full** meal,
set against everyday **ತಿನ್ನು** — the same verb Tamil narrowed down to
chewing and snacking — but the book left *ūṭa* itself unexplained until
today.

A meal keeps close company with greetings you already own. **ಶುಭ
ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ** is wished at the very hour of the midday *ūṭa*; **ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ**
is wished once the evening one is over. Two greetings that name the hour;
one word for what fills it.
