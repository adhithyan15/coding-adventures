Lesson 1 of 5.
neben — next to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for at the back, then the German for opposite.

You'll want to know: neben.
neben — "next to".

The word, taken apart.
neben from Middle High German eneben, "level with": in + the word for "even, level", which EtymDB 2.1 lists beside English even. Next to is "on a level with".

Lesson 2 of 5.
zwischen — between.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for opposite, then the German for next to.

You'll want to know: zwischen.
zwischen — "between".

The word, taken apart.
zwischen from Old High German in zwiskēn, "among the two", from zwiski, "twofold", built on twai, "two" (EtymDB 2.1). Between is in the middle of two.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die U-Bahn — the underground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for between, then the German for behind.

You'll want to know: die U-Bahn.
die U-Bahn — "the underground".

The word, taken apart.
U-Bahn is short for "underground railway": the U is the first letter of the word for "underground". Bahn from Old High German bana from Proto-Germanic banō, "path, cleared way" (EtymDB 2.1) — inherited.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Straßenbahn — a tram.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for behind, then the German for the underground.

You'll want to know: die Straßenbahn.
die Straßenbahn — "a tram".

The word, taken apart.
Straßenbahn is the word for "street" + Bahn, "way, railway": a street railway. The "street" word from Middle High German strāze (EtymDB 2.1), from Latin strāta, "paved road" — an early loan, like English street.
