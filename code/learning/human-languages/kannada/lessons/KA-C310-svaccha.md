## You'll want to know: ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C310-SVACCHA-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C173-STHALA173-04, KA-LEX-C284-KALA284-03, KA-LEX-C50-LEAVE-01, KA-ETYMON-C15-NIIRU-AKKI-01] -->

**ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ** — *svaccha* — "clean".

> **ನಿನ್ನೆ ನಾನು ನನ್ನ ಕೋಣೆ ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ ಮಾಡಿದೆ.**
>
> *ninne nānu nanna kōṇe svaccha māḍide.* — "Yesterday I cleaned my room."

> **ಈಗ ಕೋಣೆ ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾಗಿದೆ.**
>
> *īga kōṇe svacchavāgide.* — "Now the room is clean."

**ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ ಮಾಡು**, "to make clean", is how Kannada says "to clean", the way **ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡು** says "to work". To say something **is** clean, add **ಆಗಿದೆ**, "has become, is", joined by a **-ವ-**: **ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾಗಿದೆ**. In front of a noun it takes **-ವಾದ**: **ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾದ ನೀರು** (*svacchavāda nīru*, "clean water").

Say the middle slowly: *svac-cha*. Hold the *c*, then let a puff of breath out with the second one. That second consonant is a letter you have not met yet, written small under **ಚ**. The next lesson takes it out.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ** (*svaccha*) is Sanskrit, kept whole as a tatsama. Monier-Williams gives *svaccha* as "very transparent or clear", and also "pure", and writes it *sv-accha*: *su-*, "well, very", and *accha*, "clear". The Kannada sense, "clean", sits close to that "clear, pure": nothing in the way, nothing on it.
