# పాలు (pālu) — "milk," where Kannada is the odd one out, not Telugu

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C39-TII-01, TE-ETYMON-C39-TII-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Ṭī* showed Telugu going its own way against most of its
neighbours. This word shows the opposite: Telugu sits with the majority,
and one cousin stands alone.

## You'll want to know: పాలు
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C39-PAALU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **పాలు** — *pālu* — **milk**

> **దయచేసి పాలు.** — *dayacēsi pālu.* — "Milk, please."

## The word, taken apart - three sisters keep the p, one turns it to h
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-ETYMON-C39-PAALU-02]; assesses=[] -->

**పాలు** descends, unchanged, from Proto-Dravidian **\*pāl** — matching
Tamil **பால்** (*pāl*) and Malayalam **പാൽ** (*pāl*) exactly. Kannada alone
says **ಹಾಲು** (*hālu*), because Kannada carries a **regular sound law**
that turns a word-initial Dravidian *p-* into *h-*: the same law that turns
Tamil **பத்து** (*pattu*, "ten") into Kannada **ಹತ್ತು** (*hattu*). Telugu,
Tamil, and Malayalam all keep the original *p-*; only Kannada shifted it.

So the pattern this chapter has been building — Telugu sometimes matching
its neighbours, sometimes standing apart — flips one more time: here it is
**Kannada** standing apart, not Telugu.
