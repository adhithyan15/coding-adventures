# One verb inside another

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-SAKNA-01, HI-LEX-CHAHNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say both sentences from this chapter: **मैं हिंदी बोल सकता हूँ** and
**मैं हिंदी बोलना चाहता हूँ**. Something is going on in the second one that is
worth naming.

## Grammar Lens: the -ना form is a noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-INFINITIVE-OBJECT-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-CHAHNA-01, HI-LEX-LOOK, HI-GRAMMAR-VERB-FINAL-01] -->

In **मैं हिंदी बोलना चाहता हूँ**, the word **बोलना** is not doing verb work. It
is sitting where **किताब** sits in **मैं किताब देखता हूँ** — in the object slot.
The dictionary form of a Hindi verb is also a **noun**, and it can go anywhere a
noun can go:

- **मैं किताब देखता हूँ.** — **I look at the book.** — an ordinary noun object
- **मैं बोलना चाहता हूँ.** — **I want to speak.** — a VERB in the same slot

That is a bigger gift than it looks. You have sixty-nine chapters of verbs, and
every single one of their **-ना** forms is now also a thing you can like, want,
or talk about.

The order is unchanged, because it never changes: the inner verb comes first as
a noun, the outer verb last as the verb. **सकना** is the same construction with
one shortcut — it wears the **-ना** away and takes the bare stem — which is why
the two lessons before this one belonged together.
