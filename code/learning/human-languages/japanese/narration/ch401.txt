Lesson 1 of 6.
ちょうしょく (chōshoku) — breakfast (a formal word).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a toilet, then the Japanese for to shut.

You'll want to know: ちょうしょく.
ちょうしょく — chōshoku — "breakfast (a formal word)".
ちょうしょくは なんじからですか。 — chōshoku wa nanji kara desu ka — "What time is breakfast served from?"

The word, taken apart.
ちょうしょく (chōshoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are chō, "morning" (Middle Chinese trjew) and shoku, "eat" (zyik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

りょかんの ゆうしょくは さかなでした。 — ryokan no yūshoku wa sakana deshita — "Dinner at the inn was fish."

ゆうしょく (yūshoku) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the native Japanese (wago) yū, "evening", inherited rather than borrowed, and the Sino-Japanese shoku, "eat" (Middle Chinese zyik), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
きがえる (kigaeru) — to change one's clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dinner (a formal word), then the Japanese for lunch (a formal word).

You'll want to know: きがえる.
きがえる — kigaeru — "to change one's clothes".
おふろの あとで ゆかたに きがえました。 — ofuro no ato de yukata ni kigaemashita — "After the bath I changed into a cotton kimono."
Polite きがえます, kigaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あにが ゆかたに きがえました。, ani ga yukata ni kigaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
きがえる (kigaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ki, "wearing" (from kiru) and gaeru, "changing" (from kaeru); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
