French, chapter 409: Dans les virages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le clignotant — the indicator, the turn signal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to fine, then the French for to inspect.

You'll want to know: le clignotant.
le clignotant — "the indicator, the turn signal".
Il tourne sans mettre son clignotant. He turns without using his indicator.
Built on clignoter 'to blink on and off'.

The word, taken apart.
clignotant is built on clignoter 'to flash on and off', from cligner 'to blink'. The origin of cligner is not settled, and EtymDB does not list the word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
le phare — the headlight; the lighthouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to inspect, then the French for the indicator.

You'll want to know: le phare.
le phare — "the headlight; the lighthouse".
La nuit, les phares éclairent la route. At night the headlights light up the road.
Un phare is also a lighthouse.

The word, taken apart.
phare comes from Latin pharus 'lighthouse', from Greek pháros, first the name of an island whose lighthouse was one of the wonders of the ancient world (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le virage — the bend, the curve (in a road).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: le virage.
le virage — "the bend, the curve (in a road)".
Le bus ralentit dans le virage. The bus slows down on the bend.
Un virage dangereux — a dangerous bend.

The word, taken apart.
virage is built on virer 'to turn' with -age. The origin of virer is not settled; it is often linked to Latin gyrare 'to turn round'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la chaussée — the road surface, the carriageway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the headlight, then the French for the bend.

You'll want to know: la chaussée.
la chaussée — "the road surface, the carriageway".
La chaussée est glissante après la pluie. The road surface is slippery after the rain.
The part of the street for cars, not the pavement.

The word, taken apart.
chaussée is inherited from Late Latin calciāta 'paved with lime', from calx 'lime' (EtymDB). English causeway is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dévier — to turn aside, to divert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the bend, then the French for the road surface.

You'll want to know: dévier.
dévier — "to turn aside, to divert".
La voiture a dévié de sa route sur le pont. The car swerved off course on the bridge.
Also dévier la circulation — to divert the traffic.

The word, taken apart.
dévier is a learned borrowing from Late Latin dēviāre 'to leave the road', from via 'way' (EtymDB).
