Latin, chapter 523: Build and health.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rōbustus, rōbusta, rōbustum — sturdy, strong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for thin, then the Latin for leanness.

You'll want to know: rōbustus, rōbusta, rōbustum.
rōbustus, rōbusta, rōbustum — "sturdy, strong".
Agricola rōbustus saccum gravem portat. The sturdy farmer carries a heavy sack.
From rōbur — oak, hardwood: as strong as oak. English robust.

The word, taken apart.
rōbustus is built on rōbur "oak, hard wood, strength" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): oaken, then sturdy. English robust comes from it.

The past participle of obedō — to eat away; here, well fed. English obese.

Lesson 3 of 5.
gracilis, gracile — slender, slim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sturdy, then the Latin for fat.

You'll want to know: gracilis, gracile.
gracilis, gracile — "slender, slim".
Puella gracilis et alta est. The girl is slender and tall.
Two endings. Slim in a graceful way; macer is thin from want.

The word, taken apart.
gracilis is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short compares an old Latin word for "slender", but the origin is not settled. English gracile comes from it; grace does not.

Lesson 4 of 5.
sānitās, sānitātis — health, soundness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fat, then the Latin for slender.

You'll want to know: sānitās, sānitātis.
sānitās, sānitātis — "health, soundness".
Sānitās maius bonum est quam pecūnia. Health is a greater good than money.
Feminine, third declension, from sānus. Of the mind, good sense. English sanity.

The word, taken apart.
sānitās is built on sānus "sound, healthy" with -itās (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English sanity comes from it.

Two endings; an older masculine salūber also appears. Of places, food and air. English salubrious.

The word, taken apart.
salūbris is built on salūs "health, safety" (Lewis & Short). English salubrious comes from it.
