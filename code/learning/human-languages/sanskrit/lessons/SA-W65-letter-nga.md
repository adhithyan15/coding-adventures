# ङ (ṅa) — the nasal before ख in सङ्ख्या

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-224, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-225] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write इ and ऋ once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **सङ्ख्या** (*saṅkhyā*), "number".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ङ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-C65-NGA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ङ** — *ṅa*, the nasal made where *k* and *kh* are made.

It is inside **सङ्ख्या** (*saṅkhyā*), "number", from the chapter on numbers. Say
*saṅkhyā*: the *ṅ* sits at the back of the mouth, ready for the *kh* after it.
Sanskrit spells that nasal out as **ङ**; Hindi writes the same word **संख्या**,
with a dot above instead.

## Writing: ङ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-C65-NGA-01] -->

Put your pen on ङ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
