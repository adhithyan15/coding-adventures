German, chapter 332: Gründe nennen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
begründen — to give reasons for, to justify; to found.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to talk, then the German for to argue.

You'll want to know: begründen.
begründen — "to give reasons for, to justify; to found".
Der Lehrer hat seine Entscheidung gut begründet. The teacher gave good reasons for his decision.
Etwas begründen — to back it up with reasons. It also means to found: eine Schule begründen.

The word, taken apart.
begründen is inherited from Middle High German begründen "to found, to make solid" (EtymDB), built on Grund "ground, reason", from Proto-Germanic grunduz "ground": you put ground under your words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Begründung — the justification, the reasons given.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to argue, then the German for to give reasons for.

You'll want to know: die Begründung.
die Begründung — "the justification, the reasons given".
Ohne Begründung hat er die Einladung abgelehnt. He turned down the invitation without giving any reason.
Plural: die Begründungen. Mit der Begründung, dass … — on the grounds that …

The word, taken apart.
Begründung is the noun of begründen, formed with -ung. The verb is built on Grund "ground", inherited from Proto-Germanic grunduz (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
nämlich — you see (adding the reason); namely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to give reasons for, then the German for the justification.

You'll want to know: nämlich.
nämlich — "you see (adding the reason); namely".
Ich komme heute nicht. Ich bin nämlich krank. I am not coming today. I am ill, you see.
Nämlich adds the reason after the fact and stands after the verb, not at the start: Ich bin nämlich krank. It also means namely: zwei Farben, nämlich Rot und Blau.

The word, taken apart.
nämlich goes back to Middle High German nemelīch "by name, expressly", built on Name "name". EtymDB has no reliable entry for the adverb, so this follows the usual account: first you named the thing, later the reason.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zumal — especially since, all the more as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the justification, then the German for you see.

You'll want to know: zumal.
zumal — "especially since, all the more as".
Wir bleiben zu Hause, zumal es draußen regnet. We are staying at home, especially since it is raining outside.
Zumal starts a clause with the verb at the end, like weil. It adds a second, extra reason.

The word, taken apart.
zumal was built from zu "at" and Mal "time, occasion", from Old High German māl (EtymDB), and is usually said to have first meant "at the same time, all together", from which the sense "especially since" grew; EtymDB has no entry for the compound.

Lesson 5 of 5.
erläutern — to explain in detail, to clarify.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for you see, then the German for especially since.

You'll want to know: erläutern.
erläutern — "to explain in detail, to clarify".
Die Ärztin hat mir die Ergebnisse genau erläutert. The doctor explained the results to me in detail.
More formal than erklären. Inseparable: sie erläutert, sie hat erläutert.

The word, taken apart.
erläutern is built on lauter "clear, pure", inherited from Middle High German lūter, Old High German hlūttar and Proto-Germanic hlūtraz "clear, clean" (EtymDB): you make a thing clear.
