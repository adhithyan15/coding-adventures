Lesson 1 of 5.
il partito — the political party.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to respect, then the Italian for the integration.

You'll want to know: il partito.
il partito — "the political party".
Non sono iscritto a nessun partito. I am not a member of any party.
Also the past participle of partire: è partito.

The word, taken apart.
partito is the past participle of partire in its old sense "to divide", inherited from Latin partīrī "to divide", from pars, partis "a part" (Lewis & Short): one part of the people.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: il parlamento.
il parlamento — "the parliament".
Il parlamento discute la nuova legge. Parliament is debating the new law.
Stress: parlaménto. Its members are elected by the citizens.

The word, taken apart.
parlamento is built on parlare "to speak", inherited from Late Latin parabolāre (EtymDB), after Old French parlement "a speaking, a meeting": the place where people talk.

Lesson 3 of 5.
il ministro — the minister.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the political party, then the Italian for the parliament.

You'll want to know: il ministro.
il ministro — "the minister".
Il ministro della salute parla in televisione stasera. The health minister is speaking on television tonight.
Il primo ministro — the prime minister. A woman is also la ministra.

The word, taken apart.
ministro is a learned borrowing from Latin minister "servant, attendant" (Lewis & Short), built on minus "less": first the one who serves, then the one who serves the state.

Lesson 4 of 5.
eleggere — to elect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the parliament, then the Italian for the minister.

You'll want to know: eleggere.
eleggere — "to elect".
I cittadini eleggono il sindaco ogni cinque anni. The citizens elect the mayor every five years.
Conjugated like leggere; past participle eletto.

The word, taken apart.
eleggere comes from Latin ēligere "to pick out, to choose" (EtymDB), built from ē "out" and legere "to gather, to choose".
