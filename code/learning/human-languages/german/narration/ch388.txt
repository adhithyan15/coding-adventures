German, chapter 388: Geld leihen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Kredit — the bank loan, the credit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to debit, then the German for the amount.

You'll want to know: der Kredit.
der Kredit — "the bank loan, the credit".
Für das Haus haben sie einen Kredit bei der Bank aufgenommen. They took out a bank loan for the house.
Plural: die Kredite. Einen Kredit aufnehmen — to take out a loan.

The word, taken apart.
Kredit was borrowed from French crédit (EtymDB), which goes back through Italian credito to Latin crēditum "a loan", from crēdere "to trust, to believe" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Darlehen — the loan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the amount, then the German for the bank loan.

You'll want to know: das Darlehen.
das Darlehen — "the loan".
Meine Eltern haben mir ein Darlehen für das Studium gegeben. My parents gave me a loan for my studies.
Plural: die Darlehen. Formal and legal; in everyday speech der Kredit is more common.

The word, taken apart.
Darlehen joins dar "there, forth" to Lehen "a loan, a fief", of the family of leihen "to lend", Old High German līhan, Proto-Germanic līhwaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Schulden — the debts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bank loan, then the German for the loan.

You'll want to know: die Schulden.
die Schulden — "the debts".
Nach dem Autokauf hatte er hohe Schulden. After buying the car he had large debts.
A plural noun: Schulden haben — to be in debt. The singular die Schuld means guilt or blame.

The word, taken apart.
Schulden is the plural of Schuld "debt, guilt", inherited from Old High German sculd and Proto-Germanic skuldiz (EtymDB): what you owe.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zurückzahlen — to pay back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the loan, then the German for the debts.

You'll want to know: zurückzahlen.
zurückzahlen — "to pay back".
Ich zahle dir das Geld nächste Woche zurück. I will pay you the money back next week.
Separable: ich zahle zurück, ich habe zurückgezahlt.

The word, taken apart.
zurückzahlen is built from zurück "back" and zahlen "to pay", from Zahl "number", Old High German zala, Proto-Germanic talō (EtymDB): you count the money back.

Lesson 5 of 5.
borgen — to borrow (sich borgen); to lend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the debts, then the German for to pay back.

You'll want to know: borgen.
borgen — "to borrow (sich borgen); to lend".
Ich habe mir das Fahrrad von meinem Nachbarn geborgt. I borrowed the bike from my neighbour.
Sich etwas borgen — to borrow; jemandem etwas borgen — to lend. In Austria people also say ausborgen.

The word, taken apart.
borgen is inherited from Old High German borgēn and Proto-Germanic burgōną (EtymDB), usually explained as first "to look after, to give surety", then to lend and borrow.
