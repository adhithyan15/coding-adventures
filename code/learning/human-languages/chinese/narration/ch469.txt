Lesson 1 of 7.
chōngdòng — impulsive; rash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for quick to pitch in, then the Mandarin for thoughtful of others' needs.

You'll want to know: chōngdòng.
chōngdòng — "impulsive; rash".
tā tài chōngdòng le chángcháng shuō cuò huà — He's too impulsive and often says the wrong thing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōngdòng is built from chōng 'to dash against' and dòng 'to move', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhowng and duwngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rush at' and the second 'restless' and 'to move'.

温和 (wēnhé) — gentle in manner; soft-spoken.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for thoughtful of others' needs, then the Mandarin for impulsive.

You'll want to know: 温和.
温和 — wēnhé — "gentle in manner; soft-spoken".
他的个性比我温和 tā de gèxìng bǐ wǒ wēnhé — He has a gentler nature than I do.
The new character, in pieces: 温 (wēn) — twelve strokes in three pieces: on the left the water piece, a dot, a second dot and a rising stroke; at the top right 日 rì, four strokes; at the foot a dish of five strokes, a vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two short verticals and a bar. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
温和 (wēnhé) is built from 温 (wēn) 'warm' and 和 'harmony', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'won and hwa (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'mild', 'good', 'soft' and 'warm' and the second 'in tune'.

Script — the water piece, then 日 over a dish.
温 (wēn).
Three pieces: the left, then the top right, then the foot. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, three strokes: the water piece, as in 汉. A dot, a second dot under it, then a stroke that rises from the bottom left up to the right.
The top right, 日, four strokes. A vertical down the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, a bar inside, then the bar across the foot.
The foot, a dish, five strokes. A short vertical on the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two short verticals inside, then a long bar across the bottom, under everything.
3 + 4 + 5 = twelve.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 温 (wēn) have? (Three: three strokes, four and five.) Which written character sits at the top right? (日.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
正经 (zhèngjīng) — decent; serious and proper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for impulsive, then the Mandarin for gentle in manner.

You'll want to know: 正经.
正经 — zhèngjīng — "decent; serious and proper".
他是一个很正经的人 tā shì yí ge hěn zhèngjīng de rén — He's a very decent man.

The word, taken apart.
正经 (zhèngjīng) is built from 正 'straight' and 经 'the warp thread', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyengH and keng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'right' and 'proper' and the second 'constant' and as the warp of cloth. The sense 'decent' is not given there.
