# Three ways to say no, and one way to ask

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NA-VERB-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Three negators arrived in this chapter and each one owns a different
kind of sentence. Name them in the order you met them, and name what each one
denies.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NA-VERB-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-POLAR-01, BN-LEX-C07-JANA-01, BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01, BN-CONCEPT-C05-AMIBANGLABOLI-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: the sentence with a verb — *āmi jāni nā*]
- [YOU SAY: the sentence with none — *āmār nām rām nôy*]
- [YOU SAY: the thing that is missing — *āmār chā nei*]
- [YOU SAY: each of those three again with a rising end, and hear a question]
- [YOU RECALL: the sentence chapter eleven taught, then deny it — *āmi bānglā bôli nā*]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NEI-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-POLAR-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-BHALO-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-AMARNAAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which negator would you use to deny **আমার নাম রাম**, which for
**আমি জানি**, and which for **আমার চা আছে**? (**নয়**, then **না**, then
**নেই**.) What has to change to turn any of the three into a question?
(**The pitch at the end, and nothing else**.)
