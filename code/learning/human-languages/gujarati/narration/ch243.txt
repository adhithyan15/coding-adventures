Gujarati, chapter 243: Lessons and school bags.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પાઠ (pāṭh) — lesson, reading.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to believe, then the Gujarati for to change.

You'll want to know: પાઠ.
પાઠ — pāṭh — "lesson, reading".
આજે અમે નવો પાઠ ભણીએ છીએ. — āje ame navo pāṭh bhaṇīe chīe — "Today we study a new lesson."

The word, taken apart.
પાઠ (pāṭh) is Sanskrit pāṭha, "text of a book; study", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 8035).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ગોખવું (gokhvũ) — to learn by heart, to cram.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to change, then the Gujarati for lesson.

You'll want to know: ગોખવું.
ગોખવું — gokhvũ — "to learn by heart, to cram".
તે રોજ પાઠ ગોખે છે. — te roj pāṭh gokhe che — "He learns the lesson by heart every day."

The word, taken apart.
The origin of ગોખવું (gokhvũ) is not settled. CDIAL lists no Gujarati verb of this form with the meaning "to learn by rote", and our other sources do not trace it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
દફતર (daphtar) — school bag; office file.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to learn by heart, then the Gujarati for schoolbook.

You'll want to know: દફતર.
દફતર — daphtar — "school bag; office file".
તેનું દફતર ભારે છે. — tenũ daphtar bhāre che — "His school bag is heavy."

The word, taken apart.
દફતર (daphtar) is a loan from Arabic daftar, "register, account book", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Lane records the view that Arabic itself took the word from Persian (Lane).

Lesson 5 of 5.
હાજર (hājar) — present, in attendance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for schoolbook, then the Gujarati for school bag.

You'll want to know: હાજર.
હાજર — hājar — "present, in attendance".
બધા વિદ્યાર્થી વર્ગમાં હાજર છે. — badhā vidyārthī vargmā̃ hājar che — "All the students are present in class."

The word, taken apart.
હાજર (hājar) is a loan from Arabic ḥāḍir, "present, at hand", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is ḥ-ḍ-r, "to be present" (Lane).
