## Chapters 34-37: sixteen letters taken from words the reader already says (HL-C443)

HL-C443's rule for gentle writing: teach each letter from a word the reader
already knows, and keep going until the reader has written every letter.
`measureLetterAnchoring` found sixteen Urdu letters the reader had been
reading in words and had never written on their own. Each now gets one lesson,
and each lesson names the word the letter came from:

| chapter | node | letters, and the words they come from |
|---|---|---|
| 34 | MEET-GREET | ب بولنا; ت تم; ٹ ٹھیک (the boat letters) |
| 35 | CHECK-WELLBEING | ج جی; ح حافظ; خ خدا; چ سوچنا (one skeleton, four dot patterns) |
| 36 | EXCHANGE-NAMES | د خدا; ڑ پڑھنا; ھ ٹھیک; گ گرمی (letters with marks of their own) |
| 37 | TAKE-LEAVE | ف حافظ; ق قمیض; ض قمیض; ظ حافظ; ئ ہوئی |

Two Urdu-specific points:

- The small mark that turns ت into ٹ also turns ر into ڑ. It means the same
  thing both times: the tongue curls back.
- ھ almost never sounds on its own. It adds breath to the letter before it, so
  ٹ + ھ is *ṭh*.

Fifteen of the sixteen letters have a cited `script-ductus` stroke order, so
fifteen new filmstrips print. ئ has no cited order, so its lesson says "copy
what you see". Chapter 37 closes on two reviews of all sixteen. Every new
writing lesson declares `delivery: script`, as Urdu's writing lessons do.

This chapter also reaches the خ and د of خدا حافظ. Until now the reading rung
had to avoid that phrase.

The printed citation for خ writes the source's "Ḳhe" as "Khe", because the
Urdu book's fonts have no Ḳ. The glyph-coverage gate caught it.
