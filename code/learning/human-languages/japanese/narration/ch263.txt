Japanese, chapter 263: Calling for Help.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きゅうきゅうしゃ (kyūkyūsha) — an ambulance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for suddenly, then the Japanese for damage.

You'll want to know: きゅうきゅうしゃ.
きゅうきゅうしゃ — kyūkyūsha — "an ambulance".
じこが あって、きゅうきゅうしゃが きました。 — jiko ga atte, kyūkyūsha ga kimashita — "There was an accident, and an ambulance came."

The word, taken apart.
きゅうきゅうしゃ (kyūkyūsha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kyū, "rescue" (Middle Chinese kjuwH), kyū, "emergency" (kip) and sha, "car" (tsyhae) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
よぶ (yobu) — to call; to send for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for damage, then the Japanese for an ambulance.

You'll want to know: よぶ.
よぶ — yobu — "to call; to send for".
ちちが きゅうきゅうしゃを よびました。 — chichi ga kyūkyūsha o yobimashita — "My father phoned for an ambulance."
Polite よびます, yobimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが いしゃを よびました。, haha ga isha o yobimashita.

The word, taken apart.
よぶ (yobu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
