Arabic, chapter 409: Laws.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قانون (qānūn) — a law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for well-being, then the Arabic for care.

You'll want to know: قانون.
قانون — qānūn — "a law".
يمنع القانون التدخين في المطاعم. yamnaʿu al-qānūn at-tadkhīn fī al-maṭāʿim. The law bans smoking in restaurants.
Plural: قوانين, qawānīn.

The word, taken apart.
قانون (qānūn) was borrowed from Greek kanōn, "a measuring rod, a rule" (EtymDB). Arabic gave it the sense of a law.

Lesson 2 of 5.
تشريع (tashrīʿ) — legislation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for care, then the Arabic for a law.

You'll want to know: تشريع.
تشريع — tashrīʿ — "legislation".
تحتاج البلاد إلى تشريع جديد للعمل. taḥtāju al-bilād ilā tashrīʿ jadīd li-l-ʿamal. The country needs new labour legislation.

The word, taken apart.
تشريع (tashrīʿ) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-ʿ, "to lay down a way" (Lane). Tashrīʿ is the Form II verbal noun, laying down a way for others to follow: legislation.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بند (band) — a clause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a law, then the Arabic for legislation.

You'll want to know: بند.
بند — band — "a clause".
قرأ المحامي كل بند في العقد. qaraʾa al-muḥāmī kulla band fī al-ʿaqd. The lawyer read every clause in the contract.
Plural: بنود, bunūd.

The word, taken apart.
بند (band) was borrowed from Persian band, "a tie, a bond" (EtymDB). In Arabic it is an article or clause of a text.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نص (naṣṣa) — to stipulate in writing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for legislation, then the Arabic for a clause.

You'll want to know: نص.
نص — naṣṣa — "to stipulate in writing".
ينص القانون على حق التعليم. yanuṣṣu al-qānūn ʿalā ḥaqq at-taʿlīm. The law stipulates the right to education.
Present: ينص, yanuṣṣ.
It takes على: ينص على …. The noun نص (naṣṣa), naṣṣ, means a text.

The word, taken apart.
نص (naṣṣa) is built on the native Arabic root n-ṣ-ṣ, "to raise, to set up" (Lane). Naṣṣa is a Form I verb; from setting a thing up plainly comes stating it in writing: to stipulate.
