## You'll want to know: 父子
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C507-FATHER507-02]; assesses=[] -->

**父子** — *fùzǐ* — "father and son".

> **他们父子都是歌手** *tāmen fùzǐ dōu shì gēshǒu* — Father and son are both singers.

The new character, in pieces: 父 — four strokes: two short strokes on top, one falling left and a dot falling right, then two long strokes that cross, one falling left and one falling right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**父子** (*fùzǐ*) is built from 父 'a father' and 子 'a son', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *bjuX* and *tsiX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, as the head of a family who leads and teaches, and the second 'offspring'. The dictionary also lists 父 as *pjuX*, a title of respect for a man.
