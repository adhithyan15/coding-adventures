Latin, chapter 323: Īnfāns nātus est.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pariō, parere — to give birth to; to produce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to perish, then the Latin for a guard.

You'll want to know: pariō, parere.
pariō, parere — "to give birth to; to produce".
Uxor eius fīliam peperit. His wife gave birth to a daughter.
pariō, parere, peperī, partum, short a. Not pāreō, pārēre, 'to obey' (taught). English parent comes from its family.

The word, taken apart.
pariō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to bring forth" (EtymDB). English parent comes from its participle parēns.

Masculine, 4th declension, from pariō.

Lesson 3 of 5.
obstetrīx, obstetrīcis — a midwife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to give birth to, then the Latin for a birth.

You'll want to know: obstetrīx, obstetrīcis.
obstetrīx, obstetrīcis — "a midwife".
Obstetrīx māne ad domum vēnit. The midwife came to the house in the morning.
Feminine, 3rd declension: she stands by the mother. English obstetrics.

The word, taken apart.
obstetrīx is built on obstō, "to stand before" (EtymDB), with the feminine ending -trīx: she who stands by. English obstetrics comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for a birth, then the Latin for a midwife.

nūtriō, nūtrīre — "to suckle, to feed, to rear".
Māter ipsa īnfantem nūtrīvit. The mother fed the baby herself.
nūtriō, nūtrīre, nūtrīvī, nūtrītum. English nourish and nutrition come from it.

The word, taken apart.
nūtriō is an inherited verb meaning first "to suckle" (EtymDB). English nourish comes from it through French, and nutrition directly.
