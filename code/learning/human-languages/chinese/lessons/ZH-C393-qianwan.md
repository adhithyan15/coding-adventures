## You'll want to know: 千万
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C393-BESURE393-02]; assesses=[] -->

**千万** — *qiānwàn* — "be sure to (urging); whatever you do".

> **你一个人在外边千万要保重** *nǐ yí ge rén zài wàibian qiānwàn yào bǎozhòng* — Out there on your own, be sure to look after yourself.

The new character, in pieces: 万 — three strokes: a long bar, a stroke that runs right and turns down with a hook, and a long stroke falling to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**千万** (*qiānwàn*) is built from 千 'a thousand' and 万 'ten thousand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshen* and *mjonH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'ten hundreds'; for the second it names a dance and an insect, and the number is not given there. A thousand times, ten thousand times over: the urging sense is usually explained that way.
