## Chapter 64 — reading: words, lines, and a first passage

Sanskrit's reading rung, and the last of the twenty-three tracks to get one. No
task shapes are declared for this track, so it moves no number.

Three lessons, 6 -> 5 -> 30 tokens, no new word in any. The corpus pin moves
347 -> 350 lessons with idioms, senses and culture claims unchanged at 11 / 12 /
13, because the rung declares nothing but the three reading skills themselves.

The rung is placed after the joiners of Chapter 52 rather than beside the deixis
of Chapter 14. A passage that can only place things is a list; **च**, **वा** and
**किन्तु** are what turn fourteen lines into something held together.

- **Words**: six nouns, three closing on **-अः** and three on **-अम्**, which
  sort themselves into two families before they are read for meaning. An English
  word carries its job in WHERE IT STANDS; a Sanskrit word carries it in HOW IT
  ENDS -- so a Sanskrit reader's eye goes to the end of a word first. Chapter 16
  already named the two endings; this is the first lesson that asks the reader to
  USE them, cold.
- **Lines**: six lines that say two things. **ग्रामः अत्र अस्ति**, **अत्र ग्रामः
  अस्ति**, **अस्ति अत्र ग्रामः** are one sentence written three ways, and the
  point is that English cannot do this. Because the ending holds the job, the
  position does not have to.
- **Passage**: fourteen lines in one village. **च** adds and **वा** offers, both
  standing AFTER what they join and once for each thing, so a list reads as
  *the-flower-and the-garland-and*; **किन्तु** stands first, which is why its
  line opens unlike any other on the page. The passage closes on **शान्तिः**,
  where Sanskrit recitation has closed for a very long time.

Every word is printed with space around it and its ending whole. Running
Sanskrit does not keep them that separate, and the words lesson says so on the
page -- a first reader needs to see the joints while they are still learning
where they are.
