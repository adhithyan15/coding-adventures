Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 161: What I Did.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
म्हैं रोटी खाई — I ate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I eat roti and I drink water. Then mother went home.

Grammar Lens: म्हैं.
Eating, drinking and seeing are done to something. In their past, I is म्हैं, not हूं:
म्हैं रोटी खाई। mhaĩ roṭī khāī. — I ate a roti.
Old Marwari already had mẽ, "I", beside hũ (CDIAL 9691, 992).

Grammar Lens: the thing decides.
The ending follows the thing, not you. रोटी is feminine, so खाई; पाणी is masculine, so पीयो:
म्हैं पाणी पीयो। mhaĩ pāṇī pīyo. — I drank water.
A man and a woman say the same: the roti decides, and so does the water.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: म्हैं दूध पीयो। — mhaĩ dūdh pīyo. — I drank milk.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: म्हैं गाय देखी। — mhaĩ gāy dekhī. — I saw the cow.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: म्हैं घर देख्यो। — mhaĩ ghar dekhyo. — I saw the house.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
I, in the past of eating? (म्हैं.) Who decides खाई or खायो? (The thing eaten.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Say that you ate a roti.


Lesson 2 of 5.
कियो, लियो — did, took.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I ate a roti. Then to do and to take: करणो, लेणो.

Grammar Lens: two short pasts.
करणो and लेणो have short pasts: कियो and लियो. For a feminine thing, की and ली:
म्हैं पाणी गरम कियो। mhaĩ pāṇī garam kiyo. — I heated the water.
म्हैं बात की। mhaĩ bāt kī. — I talked.
म्हैं दूध लियो। mhaĩ dūdh liyo. — I took milk.
CDIAL lists Old Marwari kiyo, "made", and liyo, "takes" (CDIAL 2814, 10948).

Guided Practice.
The thing changes the ending; the speaker does not:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
who speaks: a man. the thing: रोटी. the verb: ली.
who speaks: a woman. the thing: रोटी. the verb: ली.
who speaks: a man. the thing: दूध. the verb: लियो.
who speaks: a woman. the thing: दूध. the verb: लियो.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: म्हैं रोटी ली। — mhaĩ roṭī lī. — I took a roti.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: म्हैं दूध लियो। — mhaĩ dūdh liyo. — I took milk.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: म्हैं पाणी गरम कियो। — mhaĩ pāṇī garam kiyo. — I heated the water.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Did, of water? (कियो.) Did, of a talk? (की.) Took, of a roti? (ली.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Say that you took milk.


Lesson 3 of 5.
काढणो (kāḍhṇo) — to take out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say to pull and to lift. (खींचणो, उठावणो.) Then water.

You'll want to know: काढणो.
काढणो — kāḍhṇo — "to take out; to draw (water)".
म्हैं पाणी काढ्यो। mhaĩ pāṇī kāḍhyo. — I drew water.
वो रोज पाणी काढै है। vo roj pāṇī kāḍhai hai. — He draws water every day.
It holds a letter you have not written yet: ḍh, a ड with a breath after it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
काढणो (kāḍhṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed verb, kaḍḍhati, "pulls, draws". CDIAL lists Old Marwari kāḍhaï, "to pull out" (CDIAL 2660).

What you've built.
Its stem काढ ends in a consonant: काढूं हूं, काढै है, and in the past, with म्हैं, काढ्यो.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kāḍhṇo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: म्हैं पाणी काढ्यो। — mhaĩ pāṇī kāḍhyo. — I drew water.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say to pull, then kāḍhṇo again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does काढणो (kāḍhṇo) mean? ("To take out; to draw water".) Say it once more.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Marwadi for 'to take out'.


Lesson 4 of 5.
ढ (ḍha) — the letter inside काढणो (kāḍhṇo).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ढ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ड once, and name it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say काढणो (kāḍhṇo), "to take out". Its middle is a ड with a breath after it: ḍh. This lesson puts that letter in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ढ.
ढ — ḍha, the breathed ḍ.
It sits inside काढणो (kāḍhṇo). It stands beside ड and ड़ as ध stands beside द. Its body is one stroke: a stem that runs into a broad outer bowl and a closed loop inside it, then the headline.

Writing: ढ.
1. start at the stem's headline junction, descend, turn left through the upper shoulder, sweep counterclockwise around the broad outer bowl, and continue counterclockwise around the closed inner loop without lifting.
2. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 1.
verified two-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ढ (ḍha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
