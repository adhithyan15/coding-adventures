Hindi, chapter 283: Tickets, Trains and Trouble.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अगला (aglā) — next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "last week". (पिछले हफ़्ते.) Then आगे, "ahead". This lesson's word is the partner of the first.

You'll want to know: अगला.
अगला — aglā — "next".
अगली ट्रेन कब आएगी? aglī ṭren kab āegī? — "When will the next train come?"
It agrees as पिछला does: अगला स्टेशन (aglā sṭeśan), "the next station", but अगली ट्रेन, because ट्रेन is feminine. As a time it takes the form of पिछले हफ़्ते: अगले हफ़्ते (agle hafte), "next week"; अगले साल (agle sāl), "next year".
अगले हफ़्ते हम गाँव जाएँगे। agle hafte ham gā̃v jāeṅge. — "Next week we will go to the village."

The word, taken apart.
अगला (aglā) has no row of its own in CDIAL. It is usually connected with आगे (āge), "ahead", which CDIAL traces to Sanskrit agra, "top, front" (CDIAL 68); under the same entry CDIAL lists a Hindi āgil, "foremost". पिछला and अगला make a pair: the one behind you and the one in front.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: aglā, then aglī ṭren]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When will the next train come?" — aglī ṭren kab āegī?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Last week I was at home; next week I will go to the village" — pichle hafte maiṁ ghar par thā; agle hafte maiṁ gā̃v jāūṅgā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does अगला (aglā) mean? ("Next".) "Next week"? (अगले हफ़्ते.) "The next train"? (अगली ट्रेन: ट्रेन is feminine.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
शहर के लिए दो टिकट दीजिए (śahar ke liye do ṭikaṭ dījiye) — at the ticket window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have asked for a ticket since you first learned the word: एक टिकट दीजिए. Say it, then "the next train". (aglī ṭren.) This lesson buys the right ticket for the right train.

You'll want to know: buying a ticket.
शहर के लिए दो टिकट दीजिए। śahar ke liye do ṭikaṭ dījiye. — "Two tickets for the city, please."
The place you are going takes के लिए, "for". टिकट is masculine, and it does not change for two.
एक टिकट कितने का है? (ek ṭikaṭ kitne kā hai?) — How much is one ticket?
सौ रुपये का है। (sau rupaye kā hai.) — It's a hundred rupees.
कितने का, "of how much", agrees with the ticket.

You'll want to know: when it leaves, and how long.
अगली ट्रेन कितने बजे चलेगी? aglī ṭren kitne baje calegī? — "What time does the next train leave?"
A train चलती है, "moves off", and चलेगी is its future. "At what time" is कितने बजे, and the answer is a time you know: पाँच बजे.
How long will it take? लगना again, with the time as the thing:
कितना समय लगेगा? (kitnā samay lagegā?) — How long will it take?
दो घंटे लगेंगे। (do ghaṇṭe lageṅge.) — It will take two hours.
लगेगा follows समय; लगेंगे follows दो घंटे.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It will take two hours" — do ghaṇṭe lageṅge]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Ask for two tickets for the city, then ask what time the next train leaves.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Two tickets for the city"? (शहर के लिए दो टिकट दीजिए (śahar ke liye do ṭikaṭ dījiye).) "How much is one ticket?" (एक टिकट कितने का है?) "What time does it leave?" (कितने बजे चलेगी?) "How long will it take?" (कितना समय लगेगा?)


Lesson 3 of 5.
खोना (khonā) — to lose, to be lost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say a bag and a key. (thailā, cābī.) Then "he fell asleep". (vah so gayā.) The new verb works like that last one.

You'll want to know: खोना.
खोना — khonā — "to lose; to be lost".
मेरा थैला खो गया। merā thailā kho gayā. — "My bag got lost."
With गया after it, as in सो गया, the lost thing is the subject, and the verb follows it: मेरी चाबी खो गई (merī cābī kho gaī), "my key got lost", because चाबी is feminine.
With दिया after it, you are the one who lost it, and ने comes in:
मैंने अपनी चाबी खो दी। maiṁne apnī cābī kho dī. — "I lost my key."
दी follows चाबी. अपनी is "my own", the अपना you use wherever the owner is also the doer.

The word, taken apart.
खोना (khonā) is a tadbhava. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit kṣapayati, "destroys; throws" (CDIAL 3651), and gives "to lose, waste, throw away" for Hindi.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: khonā, then kho gayā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My key got lost" — merī cābī kho gaī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I lost my key" — maiṁne apnī cābī kho dī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does खोना (khonā) mean? ("To lose; to be lost".) "My bag got lost"? (मेरा थैला खो गया.) Why खो गई for a key? (चाबी is feminine.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई (merī ṭren chūṭ gaī) — when something goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "my bag got lost". (merā thailā kho gayā.) Then छूटना, "to be left behind". (chūṭnā.)

You'll want to know: reporting a loss.
मेरा थैला खो गया। उसमें मेरा बटुआ था। merā thailā kho gayā. usmeṁ merā baṭuā thā. — "My bag got lost. My wallet was in it."
उसमें is उस with में: "in it". Then ask for help, and say whom you told:
मेरी मदद कीजिए। (merī madad kījiye.) — Please help me. मदद is feminine: मेरी.
मैंने पुलिस को बताया। (maiṁne pulis ko batāyā.) — I told the police.
थाना कहाँ है? (thānā kahā̃ hai?) — Where is the police station?

You'll want to know: missing a train.
मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई। merī ṭren chūṭ gaī. — "I missed my train."
Word for word, "my train got left behind". The train is the subject, so छूट गई follows it, and there is no मैंने. With a bus, the same: मेरी बस छूट गई (merī bas chūṭ gaī).
The cause goes first, with इसलिए:
मुझे देर हो गई, इसलिए मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई। mujhe der ho gaī, isliye merī ṭren chūṭ gaī. — "I was late, so I missed my train."
And what it cost you, with पड़ना: मुझे बस से जाना पड़ा (mujhe bas se jānā paṛā), "I had to go by bus".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Please help me" — merī madad kījiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I told the police" — maiṁne pulis ko batāyā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was late, so I missed my bus" — mujhe der ho gaī, isliye merī bas chūṭ gaī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that your bag got lost and your wallet was in it, then that you missed your train.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"My bag got lost"? (मेरा थैला खो गया.) "My wallet was in it"? (उसमें मेरा बटुआ था.) "I missed my train"? (मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई (merī ṭren chūṭ gaī).) Why no मैंने? (The train is the subject: it got left behind.)
