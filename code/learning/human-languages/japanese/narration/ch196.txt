Japanese, chapter 196: Addresses, Maps and Neighbours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
むかう (mukau) — to head for; to face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go past, then the Japanese for to go back.

You'll want to know: むかう.
むかう — mukau — "to head for; to face".
いま えきに むかいます。 — ima eki ni mukaimasu — "I am heading for the station now."
Polite むかいます, mukaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
むかう (mukau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
きんじょ (kinjo) — neighbourhood; vicinity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go back, then the Japanese for to head for.

You'll want to know: きんじょ.
きんじょ — kinjo — "neighbourhood; vicinity".
きんじょに いい みせが あります。 — kinjo ni ii mise ga arimasu — "There is a good shop in my neighbourhood."

The word, taken apart.
きんじょ (kinjo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kin, "near" (Middle Chinese gj+nX) and jo, "place" (srjoX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
