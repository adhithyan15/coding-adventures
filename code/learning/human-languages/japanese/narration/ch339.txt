Japanese, chapter 339: Sorting and Keeping.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぶんべつする (bunbetsu suru) — to sort rubbish; to separate waste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to burn something, then the Japanese for resources.

You'll want to know: ぶんべつする.
ぶんべつする — bunbetsu suru — "to sort rubbish; to separate waste".
きそくが あるので、ごみを ぶんべつします。 — kisoku ga aru node, gomi o bunbetsu shimasu — "There is a rule, so we sort our rubbish."
Polite ぶんべつします, bunbetsushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ははが ごみを ぶんべつしました。, haha ga gomi o bunbetsu shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぶんべつする (bunbetsu suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, bunbetsu, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: bun, "part" (Middle Chinese pjun) and betsu, "separate" (bjet) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
とりいれる (toriireru) — to adopt an idea; to bring in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to preserve, then the Japanese for to safeguard.

You'll want to know: とりいれる.
とりいれる — toriireru — "to adopt an idea; to bring in".
しみんの いけんを とりいれた ほうが いいです。 — shimin no iken o toriireta hō ga ii desu — "They should take the citizens' views on board."
Polite とりいれます, toriiremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): かいしゃが とりいれました。, kaisha ga toriiremashita.

The word, taken apart.
とりいれる (toriireru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from tori, "taking (toru)" and ireru, "to put in", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ばらばら.
ばらばら — barabara — "scattered; in pieces".
ふるいので、ほんが ばらばらです。 — furui node, hon ga barabara desu — "It is old, so the book is falling apart."

The word, taken apart.
ばらばら (barabara) is a native mimetic word, one of the Japanese words that picture a manner or a feeling in sound; JMdict marks it as mimetic. It is inherited, not borrowed, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).
