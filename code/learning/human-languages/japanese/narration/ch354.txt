Japanese, chapter 354: Weather Plans.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
どしゃぶり (doshaburi) — a downpour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a water outage, then the Japanese for tentatively.

You'll want to know: どしゃぶり.
どしゃぶり — doshaburi — "a downpour".
どしゃぶりに なったら、しあいは ちゅうしでしょう。 — doshaburi ni nattara, shiai wa chūshi deshō — "If it pours, the match will probably be called off."

The word, taken apart.
どしゃぶり (doshaburi) is built from the Sino-Japanese dosha, written with two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, do, "soil" (Middle Chinese thuX) and sha, "sand" (srae), and the native buri, from furu, "to fall", voiced from furi. JMdict marks that spelling as ateji, characters chosen for their sound, so where dosha came from is not settled (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: しっけ.
しっけ — shikke — "dampness; humidity".
つゆに なったら、しっけが おおく なるでしょう。 — tsuyu ni nattara, shikke ga ōku naru deshō — "When the rainy season comes, it will probably get damp."

The word, taken apart.
しっけ (shikke) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shitsu, "damp" (Middle Chinese syip) and ke, "spirit" (khj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as shik-ke.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ひがさ (higasa) — a parasol.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a cold snap, then the Japanese for dampness.

You'll want to know: ひがさ.
ひがさ — higasa — "a parasol".
はれたら、ひがさを もって ください。 — haretara, higasa o motte kudasai — "If it is sunny, please take a parasol."

The word, taken apart.
ひがさ (higasa) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from hi, "sun" and gasa, "umbrella (kasa)", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
