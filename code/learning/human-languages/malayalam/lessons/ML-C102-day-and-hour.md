# തിങ്കളാഴ്ച രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് — on Monday at two o'clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C102-BARE-DAY-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C102-HOUR-DATIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *on Monday*. Say *at two o'clock*. Now say them one after the
other, without stopping.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C102-BARE-DAY-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C102-HOUR-DATIVE-01] -->

You have two answers to **when**, and real arrangements usually need both:

> **തിങ്കളാഴ്ച രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് ഞാൻ പോകും.**
> *tiṅkaḷāḻca raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ ñān pōkuṁ*
> "On Monday at two o'clock I will go."

**Neither piece changed to make room for the other.** The day-name is the bare
word from the table where you met all seven. The hour is the dative you built at
the start of this chapter. They
stand side by side in the one slot at the front, and the sentence behind them is
untouched.

## Grammar Lens: wider first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C102-BARE-DAY-01, ML-CONCEPT-C94-THREE-DAYS-01] -->

The order is not free, and it is the order you would guess: **the wider
container goes first.** A Monday holds a two o'clock; a two o'clock does not
hold a Monday.

| | |
|---|---|
| day, then hour | **തിങ്കളാഴ്ച രണ്ട് മണിക്ക്** |
| what English does | *on Monday at two* |

English arranges it the same way, which here is a genuine convenience rather
than a trap — you can carry your instinct straight across.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PHRASE-C102-WHAT-TIME-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C102-HOUR-DATIVE-01, ML-CONCEPT-C102-BARE-DAY-01] -->

Count what this chapter actually added: **one ending, on one kind of word.**

Everything else was already yours — the word that names an hour, the table of
day-names, the question word you have asked ages and distances and prices with,
and the slot at the front that *today* and *tomorrow* stand in. What was missing
was never vocabulary. It was the joint, and the
joint is **ക്ക്** on the hour and nothing at all on the day.
