Japanese, chapter 208: The Weather Forecast.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はれ (hare) — fine weather; clear skies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to clear up, then the Japanese for to cloud over.

You'll want to know: はれ.
はれ — hare — "fine weather; clear skies".
あしたは はれでしょう。 — ashita wa hare deshō — "Tomorrow will probably be fine."

The word, taken apart.
はれ (hare) is native Japanese (wago), derived from the verb hareru, "to clear up", inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
てんきよほう (tenkiyohō) — a weather forecast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fine weather, then the Japanese for cloudy weather.

You'll want to know: てんきよほう.
てんきよほう — tenkiyohō — "a weather forecast".
あしたの てんきよほうは ゆきです。 — ashita no tenkiyohō wa yuki desu — "Tomorrow's forecast is snow."

The word, taken apart.
てんきよほう (tenkiyohō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): four Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ten, "sky" (Middle Chinese then), ki, "air" (khj+jH), yo, "beforehand" (yoH) and hō, "report" (pawH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: そら.
そら — sora — "the sky".
あしたは そらが あおいでしょう。 — ashita wa sora ga aoi deshō — "The sky will probably be blue tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
そら (sora) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たいふう (taifū) — a typhoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a weather forecast, then the Japanese for the sky.

You'll want to know: たいふう.
たいふう — taifū — "a typhoon".
たいふうは らいしゅうでしょう。 — taifū wa raishū deshō — "The typhoon will probably come next week."

The word, taken apart.
たいふう (taifū) is Sino-Japanese (kango), read with on'yomi borrowed from Chinese. Its second part is fū, "wind" (Middle Chinese pjuwng); how the first part, tai, came to name a great storm is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Baxter's transcription).
