# angō, angere — to distress, torment; to choke

## You'll want to know: angō, angere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C497-SOLL-02]; assesses=[] -->

**angō, angere** — "to distress, torment; to choke".

> Cūra dē fīliō patrem angit.
>
> *Worry about his son torments the father.*

*angō, angere, anxī*. First to squeeze the throat, then to torment the mind. *Anxius*, already known, comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**angō** is inherited from Proto-Italic *angō*, from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "narrow, tight" (EtymDB). English *anxious* and *anguish* come from its family.
