## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C373-REF-51]; assesses=[] -->

**el oro** — "gold."

Latin **aurum**, and behind *aurum* an ancient root meaning **to shine, to
dawn**. Gold was not named for its weight or its rarity or its price. It was
named for looking like the first light of the morning.

The same root, barely disguised, is **aurora** — the dawn, and the aurora
borealis, which is dawn where dawn has no business being.

And, far enough back to need reconstructing, English **east** belongs to it
too. The east is the shining place. Gold, the dawn and a compass direction all
come from one idea about light.

English took the Latin word by a couple of side doors, both through French:

- an **oriole** is *aureolus*, "a little golden one," named for the bird's
  colour;
- an **aureole** is the ring of gold light a painter puts round a head. Same
  Latin word, different job.

The shortest survival is on the periodic table. Gold's symbol is **Au** — the
first two letters of *aurum*, sitting in a chemistry textbook long after Latin
stopped being spoken. Chemistry kept the Roman name for a metal the Romans
measured their world in.
