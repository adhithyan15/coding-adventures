## You'll want to know: さんせいする
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C317-SANSEI317-01]; assesses=[] -->

**さんせいする** — *sansei suru* — "to agree; to be in favour".

> **あにの いけんは いいと おもったので、さんせいしました。** — *ani no iken wa ii to omotta node, sansei shimashita* — "I thought my brother's idea was good, so I agreed with it."

Polite **さんせいします**, *sanseishimasu*; a する verb. Past (polite): **ははは さんせいしました。**, *haha wa sansei shimashita*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**さんせいする** (*sansei suru*) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb *suru*, "to do". The noun, *sansei*, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: *san*, "approve" (Middle Chinese *tsanH*) and *sei*, "become" (*dzyeng*) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for giving reasons and opinions.
