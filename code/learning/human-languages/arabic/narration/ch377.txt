Arabic, chapter 377: Government.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حكومة (ḥukūma) — a government.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an expert, then the Arabic for academic.

You'll want to know: حكومة.
حكومة — ḥukūma — "a government".
قررت الحكومة فتح مستشفى جديد. qarrarat al-ḥukūma fatḥ mustashfā jadīd. The government decided to open a new hospital.

The word, taken apart.
حكومة (ḥukūma) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-m, "to judge, to rule" (Lane). Ḥukūma is a verbal noun, ruling, which came to mean the body that rules: a government.

Lesson 2 of 5.
وزير (wazīr) — a minister.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for academic, then the Arabic for a government.

You'll want to know: وزير.
وزير — wazīr — "a minister".
زار الوزير المدرسة أمس. zāra al-wazīr al-madrasa ams. The minister visited the school yesterday.
Plural: وزراء, wuzarāʾ.

The word, taken apart.
وزير (wazīr) is built on the native Arabic root w-z-r, "to bear a heavy load" (Lane): the one who carries the ruler's burden. EtymDB lists a Middle Persian cognate, and some trace the word to Persian, so its origin is disputed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
برلمان (barlamān) — a parliament.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a minister, then the Arabic for a ministry.

You'll want to know: برلمان.
برلمان — barlamān — "a parliament".
ناقش البرلمان خطة الحكومة. nāqasha al-barlamān khiṭṭat al-ḥukūma. Parliament discussed the government's plan.

The word, taken apart.
برلمان (barlamān) was borrowed from French parlement (EtymDB), a place of speaking. Arabic has no p, so the word begins with b.

Lesson 5 of 5.
دستور (dustūr) — a constitution.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a ministry, then the Arabic for a parliament.

You'll want to know: دستور.
دستور — dustūr — "a constitution".
كتب الشعب دستورا جديدا. kataba ash-shaʿb dustūran jadīdan. The people wrote a new constitution.
Plural: دساتير, dasātīr.

The word, taken apart.
دستور (dustūr) was borrowed from Persian dastūr (EtymDB); Lane gives it as an arabicized word for the register of a realm's rules (Lane). Modern Arabic made it the constitution.
