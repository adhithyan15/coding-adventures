## You'll want to know: 感觉
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C286-GANJUE-01]; assesses=[] -->

**感觉** — *gǎnjué* — "to feel", how something seems to you; "a feeling".

> **我感觉很好** *wǒ gǎnjué hěn hǎo* — I feel fine.

> *wǒ gǎnjué tā bù gāoxìng* — I have a feeling he's not happy.

The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. The second is the 觉 of *juéde*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**感觉** (*gǎnjué*) is built from 感 and 觉, both inherited from Middle Chinese. 感 is *komX*, glossed 'to move, to stir'. The Guangyun lists 觉 under two readings: *kaewH*, glossed as sleep, and *kaewk*, glossed 'to understand' and 'to wake'; jué is the regular modern sound of *kaewk* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'To feel' for the pair is the word's own sense.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C252-FEEL252-02, ZH-LEX-C144-MATTER144-05, ZH-GRAMMAR-JUEDE-01] -->

*gǎndào* and 感觉 both report a feeling; 感觉 is also how something seems to you: 我感觉很好.

*juéde*, as in 我觉得, is what you think about it.
