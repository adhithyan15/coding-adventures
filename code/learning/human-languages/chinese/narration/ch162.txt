Mandarin Chinese, chapter 162: How Often.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yìbān — generally; as a rule; ordinary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for beef, then the Mandarin for chicken meat.

You'll want to know: yìbān.
yìbān — "generally; as a rule; ordinary".
wǒ yìbān qī diǎn qǐchuáng — As a rule I get up at seven.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
The tone of 一 changes: yī is said yì before the first tone of bān.

The word, taken apart.
yìbān is built from 一 'one' and bān 'kind'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese 'jit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is listed there as pan, glossed 'to carry', and as ban, glossed 'joy'. The sense 'kind' is not given, so how the pair came to mean 'as a rule' is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jīngcháng — regularly; often.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for as a rule, then the Mandarin for every single time.

You'll want to know: jīngcháng.
jīngcháng — "regularly; often".
wǒ jīngcháng qù Běijīng — I go to Beijing regularly.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīngcháng is built from jīng and cháng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, keng and dzyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'constant', using the second as its gloss; the sense 'often' is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ǒu'ěr — now and then; occasionally.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for every single time, then the Mandarin for regularly.

You'll want to know: ǒu'ěr.
ǒu'ěr — "now and then; occasionally".
wǒ ǒu'ěr hē kāfēi — I drink coffee now and then.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: ǒu'ěr is said óu'ěr, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
ǒu'ěr is built from ǒu 'by chance' and the old ending 尔, both inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the first as nguwH, glossed 'unexpected', though today's third tone goes with its other reading, nguwX 'a pair'; the second as nyeX, glossed 'you', here an ending.

Lesson 5 of 5.

tōngcháng — "normally; usually".

The word, taken apart.
tōngcháng is built from tōng 'to go through' and cháng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, thuwng and dzyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): what holds right through. The sense 'normal' is not given there.
