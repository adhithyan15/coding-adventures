Lesson 1 of 6.
たから (takara) — a treasure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a curse, then the Japanese for sly.

You'll want to know: たから.
たから — takara — "a treasure".
むかし ここに たからが ありました。 — mukashi koko ni takara ga arimashita — "Long ago there was treasure here."

The word, taken apart.
たから (takara) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 6.
びんぼう (binbō) — poor; poverty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to dig, then the Japanese for to bury.

You'll want to know: びんぼう.
びんぼう — binbō — "poor; poverty".
その おじいさんは びんぼうでした。 — sono ojiisan wa binbō deshita — "The old man was poor."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
びんぼう (binbō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are bin, "poor" (Middle Chinese bin) and bō, "scarce" (bjop) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
よくばり (yokubari) — greedy; a greedy person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: よくばり.
よくばり — yokubari — "greedy; a greedy person".
となりの おじいさんは よくばりでした。 — tonari no ojiisan wa yokubari deshita — "The old man next door was greedy."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
よくばり (yokubari) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: yoku, "greed", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese yowk), and bari, "stretch", is native Japanese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
