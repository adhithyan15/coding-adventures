Japanese, chapter 204: Trips, Inns and Sights.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りょこう (ryokō) — a trip; travel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a seat, then the Japanese for to give up.

You'll want to know: りょこう.
りょこう — ryokō — "a trip; travel".
らいげつ りょこうに いきます。 — raigetsu ryokō ni ikimasu — "I am going on a trip next month."

The word, taken apart.
りょこう (ryokō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ryo, "journey" (Middle Chinese ljoX) and kō, "to go" (haeng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
よる (yoru) — to drop in; to stop by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a Japanese inn, then the Japanese for sightseeing.

You'll want to know: よる.
よる — yoru — "to drop in; to stop by".
えきの ちかくの ほんやに よります。 — eki no chikaku no hon'ya ni yorimasu — "I drop in at the bookshop near the station."
Polite よります, yorimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
よる (yoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
