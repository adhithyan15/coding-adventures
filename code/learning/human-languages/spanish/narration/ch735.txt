Lesson 1 of 6.
el esqueleto — the skeleton.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to bleed, then the Spanish for the scar.

You'll want to know: el esqueleto.
el esqueleto — "the skeleton".
El esqueleto humano tiene 206 huesos. The human skeleton has 206 bones.

The word, taken apart.
esqueleto comes from Greek skeletós "dried up, a dried body" (EtymDB), from skéllein "to dry up".

Lesson 5 of 6.
latir — to beat (of the heart).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the stomach, then the Spanish for the brain.

You'll want to know: latir.
latir — "to beat (of the heart)".
Me late muy rápido el corazón. My heart is beating very fast.
El latido is the heartbeat.

The word, taken apart.
latir comes from Medieval Latin glattīre "to yelp" (EtymDB). Old latir was a dog's yelping; the steady, repeated sound gave the heartbeat.
