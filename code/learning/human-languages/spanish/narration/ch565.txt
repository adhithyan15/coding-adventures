5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la trama — the plot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the legend, then the Spanish for the myth.

You'll want to know: la trama.
la trama — "the plot".
La trama de la novela es muy complicada. The plot of the novel is very complicated.

The word, taken apart.
trama is inherited from Latin trāma 'the weft, the threads woven across' (EtymDB). A plot is the threads of a story woven together.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el argumento — the storyline, the plot summary; the argument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the myth, then the Spanish for the plot.

You'll want to know: el argumento.
el argumento — "the storyline, the plot summary; the argument".
Me contó el argumento de la película. He told me the storyline of the film.
It also means a reason in a debate: un buen argumento.

The word, taken apart.
argumento is a learned borrowing from Latin argūmentum 'a proof, the subject of a story' (EtymDB), from arguere 'to make clear'.

The word, taken apart.
capítulo is a learned borrowing from Late Latin capitulum 'a little head, a heading' (EtymDB), from caput 'head'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el desenlace — the ending, the outcome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the chapter, then the Spanish for the episode.

You'll want to know: el desenlace.
el desenlace — "the ending, the outcome".
El desenlace de la novela me sorprendió. The ending of the novel surprised me.
Also the outcome of a real event: un desenlace feliz.

The word, taken apart.
desenlace is built on desenlazar 'to untie' (EtymDB), from des- and lazo 'a knot, a bow', which comes from Latin laqueus 'a noose'. The ending unties the knot of the story.
