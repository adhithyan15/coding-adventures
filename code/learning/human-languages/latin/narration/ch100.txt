Latin, chapter 100: Fight, Sail, Prepare, Touch, Pull.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pugnō, pugnāre — to fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to praise, then the Latin for to move.

You'll want to know: pugnō, pugnāre.
pugnō, pugnāre — "to fight".

The word, taken apart.
pugnō is built on pugnus, "a fist" (EtymDB 2.1), so it probably first meant to fight with fists. pugnus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European peug-, "to prick, to punch" (EtymDB 2.1), the root of pungō, "to prick" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
nāvigō, nāvigāre — to sail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to move, then the Latin for to fight.

You'll want to know: nāvigō, nāvigāre.
nāvigō, nāvigāre — "to sail". English navigate.

The word, taken apart.
nāvigō is built from nāvis, "a ship", and agō, "to drive" (Lewis & Short): to drive a ship. nāvis is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European neh2us, "boat" (EtymDB 2.1).

The word, taken apart.
parō is probably related to pariō, "to bring forth, to procure", and is traced to a Proto-Indo-European root per-, "to produce, to procure" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
tangō, tangere — to touch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sail, then the Latin for to prepare.

You'll want to know: tangō, tangere.
tangō, tangere — "to touch". English tangible.

The word, taken apart.
tangō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European tag-, "to touch" (EtymDB 2.1). The n inside the present stem is an old way of marking the verb and is missing from its perfect tetigī, "I touched".

trahō, trahere — "to pull, to drag". English tractor.

The word, taken apart.
trahō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European tragh-, "to draw, to drag" (EtymDB 2.1). English attract comes from it too.
