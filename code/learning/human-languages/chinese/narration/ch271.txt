Mandarin Chinese, chapter 271: Moods.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jīdòng — excited; worked up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to back someone up, then the Mandarin for to believe.

You'll want to know: jīdòng.
jīdòng — "excited; worked up".
tā hěn jīdòng — He's very excited.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīdòng is built from jī and dòng 'to move', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kek and duwngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a rushing wave' and the second 'to stir'.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 3 of 6.
chījīng — surprised; taken aback.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for worked up, then the Mandarin for to touch someone's heart.

You'll want to know: chījīng.
chījīng — "surprised; taken aback".
wǒ hěn chījīng — I'm surprised.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chījīng is built from 吃 'to eat' and jīng 'a fright'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese kjaeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to fear' and, after an older dictionary, 'a horse taking fright'; how eating a fright came to mean surprise is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
wúliáo — bored; boring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to touch someone's heart, then the Mandarin for taken aback.

You'll want to know: wúliáo.
wúliáo — "bored; boring".
zhège jiémù hěn wúliáo — This show is boring.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wúliáo is built from wú 'not to have' and liáo, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mju and lew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to have or not have' and calls the second a particle. How the pair came to mean bored is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
心事 (xīnshì) — something on your mind; a worry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for taken aback, then the Mandarin for bored.

You'll want to know: 心事.
心事 — xīnshì — "something on your mind; a worry".
他今天不笑有心事 tā jīntiān bú xiào yǒu xīnshì — He hasn't smiled today; something's on his mind.

The word, taken apart.
心事 (xīnshì) is built from 心 'the heart' and 事 'an affair', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sim and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
