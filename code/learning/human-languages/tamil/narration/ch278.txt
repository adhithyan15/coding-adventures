Lesson 2 of 5.
வெண்டைக்காய் (veṇṭaikkāy) — okra, ladies' finger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for an opinion, then the Tamil for sugarcane.

You'll want to know: வெண்டைக்காய்.
வெண்டைக்காய் — veṇṭaikkāy — "okra, ladies' finger".

The word, taken apart.
வெண்டைக்காய் (veṇṭaikkāy) is veṇṭai, the name of the plant, and native kāy, "unripe fruit, vegetable" (DEDR 1459). The origin of veṇṭai is uncertain; DEDR has no entry for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
நிலக்கடலை (nilakkaṭalai) — a peanut, a groundnut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for sugarcane, then the Tamil for okra.

You'll want to know: நிலக்கடலை.
நிலக்கடலை — nilakkaṭalai — "a peanut, a groundnut".

The word, taken apart.
நிலக்கடலை (nilakkaṭalai) is native: nilam, "ground" (DEDR 3676), and kaṭalai, "Bengal gram" (DEDR 1120, which lists Kannada kaḍale): "the ground gram", since the nut ripens underground.

Lesson 4 of 5.
தர்பூசணி (tarpūcaṇi) — a watermelon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for okra, then the Tamil for a peanut.

You'll want to know: தர்பூசணி.
தர்பூசணி — tarpūcaṇi — "a watermelon".

The word, taken apart.
தர்பூசணி (tarpūcaṇi) is usually explained as Persian tarbuz, "watermelon", which reached Tamil through Hindustani, blended with Tamil pūcaṇi, "pumpkin".

Lesson 5 of 5.
முறுக்கு (muṟukku) — murukku (a crunchy spiral snack).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a peanut, then the Tamil for a watermelon.

You'll want to know: முறுக்கு.
முறுக்கு — muṟukku — "murukku (a crunchy spiral snack)".

The word, taken apart.
முறுக்கு (muṟukku) is native Dravidian. DEDR 5012 lists Malayalam muṟukuka, "to be twisted" and Kannada muṟuku. The entry glosses muṟukku as "a twisted cake", from "to twist".
