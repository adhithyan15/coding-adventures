# Es ist zwei Uhr — telling the time

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-UHR-01, GE-LEX-ZWEI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have the numbers and you have the word. The frame that joins
them needs nothing new.

## Grammar Lens: es ist, a number, Uhr
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ES-IST-UHR-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-LEX-UHR-01] -->

> **es ist** + the number + **Uhr**

> *Es ist **ein** Uhr.* — "It is one o'clock."
> *Es ist **zwei** Uhr.* — "It is two o'clock."
> *Es ist **zehn** Uhr.* — "It is ten o'clock."

Word for word that is the English sentence with *o'clock* moved: *it is two
o'clock*. Nothing about the order has to be relearned.

One thing to notice: at one o'clock it is ***ein*** Uhr, not *eins* — the
counting form drops its *-s* here exactly as it does before a noun, which you
met with *ein* and *eine*.
