Mandarin Chinese, chapter 329: Once upon a Time.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tónghuà — fairy tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a dragon, then the Mandarin for to carry messages between people.

You'll want to know: tónghuà.
tónghuà — "fairy tale".
xiǎoshíhou māma měitiān wǎnshang gěi wǒ dú tónghuà — When I was little, my mum read me a fairy tale every evening.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tónghuà is built from tóng 'a child' and 话 'talk', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwng and hwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which says of the first that it means a child not yet married.

What you've built.

gōngzhǔ — "princess".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gōngzhǔ is built from 公 and zhǔ, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and tsyuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a father' and 'an official' and the second 'to be in charge' and 'a lord'. How the pair came to mean a princess is not settled.

xiāoshī — "to disappear; to vanish".
dì'èr tiān zǎoshang nàge xiānnǚ tūrán xiāoshī le — The next morning the fairy suddenly vanished.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiāoshī is built from xiāo and shī, both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjew and syit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to die out' and 'to be used up', and the second 'to err' and 'to let go'.

You'll want to know: jiéjú.
jiéjú — "ending".
kàn le jiéjú yǐhòu wǒ kū le — I cried after reading the ending.

The word, taken apart.
jiéjú is built from jié 'to tie' and jú, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ket and gjowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tie' and the second 'an office' and 'a portion'. How the pair came to mean an ending is not settled.
