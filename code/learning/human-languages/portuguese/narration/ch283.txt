Portuguese, chapter 283: Boatos e desmentidos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o boato — the rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the press release, then the Portuguese for the commentator.

You'll want to know: o boato.
o boato — "the rumour".
Corre um boato na escola: o diretor vai sair. There is a rumour going round the school: the head is leaving.
Corre um boato — a rumour is going round.

The word, taken apart.
boato is a learned borrowing from Latin boātus "a bellowing, a loud cry" (EtymDB), from boāre "to cry aloud".

What you've built.

o rumor — "the murmur, a low noise; a rumour".
Ouvia-se o rumor do mar. You could hear the murmur of the sea.
In news it can also mean a rumour, like o boato.

The word, taken apart.
rumor comes from Latin rūmor "a noise, common talk, a rumour" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
desmentir — to deny, to refute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the rumour, then the Portuguese for the murmur.

You'll want to know: desmentir.
desmentir — "to deny, to refute".
O ator desmentiu a notícia. The actor denied the news.
Present: desminto, desmentes, desmente.

The word, taken apart.
desmentir is built from des- "un-" and mentir "to lie" (EtymDB has the word), which comes from Latin mentīrī "to lie" (Lewis & Short): to show that a story is false.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o desmentido — the denial.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the murmur, then the Portuguese for to deny.

You'll want to know: o desmentido.
o desmentido — "the denial".
O clube respondeu com um desmentido no mesmo dia. The club replied with a denial the same day.
The past participle of desmentir used as a noun.

The word, taken apart.
desmentido is the past participle of desmentir "to deny" used as a noun; the verb is built on des- and a verb that goes back to Latin mentīrī "to lie" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o porta-voz — the spokesperson.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to deny, then the Portuguese for the denial.

You'll want to know: o porta-voz.
o porta-voz — "the spokesperson".
O porta-voz do governo falou à imprensa. The government spokesperson spoke to the press.
The same form for a woman: a porta-voz.

The word, taken apart.
porta-voz joins porta "carries", from Latin portāre "to carry", and voz "voice", from Latin vōx (Lewis & Short): the one who carries the voice.
