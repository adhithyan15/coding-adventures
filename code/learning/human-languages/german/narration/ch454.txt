German, chapter 454: Eine Wanderung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Wanderung — the hike, the walk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the slope, then the German for the gorge.

You'll want to know: die Wanderung.
die Wanderung — "the hike, the walk".
Die Wanderung zum See dauert drei Stunden. The hike to the lake takes three hours.
Plural: die Wanderungen.

The word, taken apart.
Wanderung is built on wandern 'to hike, to wander' (EtymDB), inherited from Middle High German wandern and Old High German wantarōn.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: besteigen.
besteigen — "to climb (a mountain); to board".
Im Sommer besteigen sie einen hohen Berg. In summer they climb a high mountain.
Inseparable, with an object: sie hat den Gipfel bestiegen.

The word, taken apart.
besteigen is built from be- and steigen 'to climb', inherited from Old High German stīgan (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
absteigen — to climb down; to get off (a bike); to go down (a league).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the trail, then the German for to climb.

You'll want to know: absteigen.
absteigen — "to climb down; to get off (a bike); to go down (a league)".
Am Nachmittag steigen wir langsam ins Tal ab. In the afternoon we slowly climb down into the valley.
Separable; Perfekt with sein: wir sind abgestiegen.

The word, taken apart.
absteigen is inherited from Middle High German abestīgen (EtymDB), built from ab 'down' and steigen 'to climb'; Dutch afstijgen is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rasten — to rest, to stop for a rest (on a walk).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to climb, then the German for to climb down.

You'll want to know: rasten.
rasten — "to rest, to stop for a rest (on a walk)".
Auf halbem Weg rasten wir an einer Hütte. Halfway we stop for a rest at a hut.
Die Rast — the rest. Rasten sounds a little old-fashioned; people also say eine Pause machen.

The word, taken apart.
rasten is built on Rast 'rest, a break' (EtymDB lists the verb 'to rest'), an old Germanic word; English rest is usually counted as a cousin.
