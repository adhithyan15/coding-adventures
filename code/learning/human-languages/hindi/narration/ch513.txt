Hindi, chapter 513: At the Tailor's.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
सिलना (silnā) — to be stitched; to get sewn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a loom, then the Hindi for a knot.

You'll want to know: सिलना.
सिलना — silnā — "to be stitched; to get sewn".
मेरी नई कमीज़ कल तक सिल जाएगी। Merī naī kamīz kal tak sil jāegī. My new shirt will be stitched by tomorrow.
Past (it …): वह कुर्ता सिल गया, vah kurtā sil gayā.

The word, taken apart.
सिलना (silnā) is the intransitive partner of sīnā, "to sew": CDIAL files both under Sanskrit sīvyati, "sews" (CDIAL 13444).

Lesson 2 of 6.
सिलवाना (silvānā) — to have (clothes) stitched.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a knot, then the Hindi for to be stitched.

You'll want to know: सिलवाना.
सिलवाना — silvānā — "to have (clothes) stitched".
मैंने दर्ज़ी से एक नया कुर्ता सिलवाया। Maiṁne darzī se ek nayā kurtā silvāyā. I had a new kurta stitched by the tailor.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने एक कमीज़ सिलवाई, usne ek kamīz silvāī.

The word, taken apart.
सिलवाना (silvānā) is the second causative of sīnā, "to sew": CDIAL files sīnā and its causative silānā under Sanskrit sīvyati, "sews" (CDIAL 13444). It means getting someone else to do the sewing.

Lesson 4 of 6.
पिरोना (pironā) — to thread (a needle); to string (beads).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to have (clothes) stitched, then the Hindi for to snip.

You'll want to know: पिरोना.
पिरोना — pironā — "to thread (a needle); to string (beads)".
दर्ज़ी ने सूई में धागा पिरोया। Darzī ne sūī meṁ dhāgā piroyā. The tailor threaded the needle.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने माला में फूल पिरोए, usne mālā meṁ phūl piroe.

The word, taken apart.
पिरोना (pironā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit parivayati, "weaves round" (CDIAL 7869). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to plait, string (pearls), thread a needle".

Lesson 5 of 6.
उधड़ना (udhaṛnā) — to come unstitched; to come apart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to snip, then the Hindi for to thread (a needle).

You'll want to know: उधड़ना.
उधड़ना — udhaṛnā — "to come unstitched; to come apart".
मेरी जेब उधड़ गई। Merī jeb udhaṛ gaī. My pocket has come unstitched.
Past (it …): वह कमीज़ उधड़ गई, vah kamīz udhaṛ gaī.

The word, taken apart.
उधड़ना (udhaṛnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit uddharati, "takes out" (CDIAL 2009). CDIAL gives both Hindi verbs as "to be taken out, be unravelled"; udhaṛnā is the one that happens by itself.
