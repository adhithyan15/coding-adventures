Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 40: Reading — Signs, a Counter, and the Whole Bargain.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) (cāy · roṭī · kapṛā · rikśā · paisā · bhāv) (ye kapṛā dikhāvo. ye kitṇe kū hai?) (ye kitṇe kū hai? · ye ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai.) — six words off a signboard.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have written all six of these. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody is going to say them first.

Reading.
चाय रोटी कपड़ा रिक्शा पैसा भाव.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once. Do not sound out each sign — look at the whole word and let it arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again. Notice which came at once and which you had to assemble. Both are fine; the difference is what practice removes.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every one of these is a word you would actually meet painted on something — a stall, a cart, a board above a counter.
[pause 2 seconds]
Writing a word and reading it are not the same skill. Writing gives you all the time you want and the letters in order. A signboard gives you one look, and the word has to arrive whole.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why six is enough for one sitting.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which sign is over the tea stall?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What was new? (Nobody said them first. You read them.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) (cāy · roṭī · kapṛā · rikśā · paisā · bhāv) (ye kapṛā dikhāvo. ye kitṇe kū hai?) (ye kitṇe kū hai? · ye ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai.) — six lines off a counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you single words. These give each word something to stand on.
[pause 1 second]
Still nothing new — the market lines you already say, on the page.

Reading.
ये कितणे कू है? ये घणो महंगो है। दस करो। कोनी। पैसा ले लो। पाछे मिलसू।.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time hear whose line each one is. Three belong to you and three to the person behind the counter.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
कोनी। One word, and it is a whole turn in the conversation. A notice does not have to be long to be complete.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read in order, these six are not six notices at all — they are one bargain, from the first question to the walk-away. You have practised every one of them out loud; this is the first time you have seen them written down in the order they actually happen.
[pause 2 seconds]
That order is the thing to carry away. A market exchange has a shape, and knowing the shape is most of reading it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which line refuses the offer?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six lines were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What did reading them in order show you? (They are one bargain, not six notices.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) (cāy · roṭī · kapṛā · rikśā · paisā · bhāv) (ye kapṛā dikhāvo. ye kitṇe kū hai?) (ye kitṇe kū hai? · ye ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai.) — the whole bargain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now the whole thing, start to finish.
[pause 1 second]
It is longer than anything you have read. Nothing in it is new.

Reading.
ये कपड़ा दिखावो। ये कितणे कू है? बीस। ये घणो महंगो है। दस करो। कोनी। पंदरा करो। ठीक। पैसा ले लो। पाछे मिलसू।.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it through once without stopping. Do not translate as you go.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now again, and watch the number move: बीस, दस, पंदरा. That is the whole negotiation, and it is carried by three words you learned as counting.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
कोनी is the hinge. Before it, the price is falling because you pushed. After it, the price settles because they pushed back.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice what the passage never says. Nobody explains that पंदरा is between दस and बीस — the bargain assumes you know, and you do.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first time the book has asked you to hold a whole exchange at once, and the reason it can is that every line was paid for, one chapter at a time.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the whole exchange aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which single word turns the bargain around?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that exchange were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word turned it around? (कोनी.)
