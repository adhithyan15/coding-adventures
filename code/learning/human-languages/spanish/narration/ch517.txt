Spanish, chapter 517: El testigo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el testigo — the witness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to refute, then the Spanish for the version.

You'll want to know: el testigo.
el testigo — "the witness".
Un testigo llamó a la policía. A witness called the police.
The same form for a woman: la testigo. Ser testigo de algo is to witness it.

The word, taken apart.
testigo comes from old Spanish testiguar 'to bear witness' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin testificārī, built on testis 'a witness'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
presenciar — to witness, to be present at.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the version, then the Spanish for the witness.

You'll want to know: presenciar.
presenciar — "to witness, to be present at".
Presencié el accidente desde la ventana. I witnessed the accident from the window.
It takes a direct object: presenciar un partido — to watch a match in person.

The word, taken apart.
presenciar is built on presencia 'presence', which comes from Latin praesentia 'a being at hand' (Lewis & Short), from praesēns 'present'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
observar — to observe, to watch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the witness, then the Spanish for to witness.

You'll want to know: observar.
observar — "to observe, to watch".
La vecina lo observaba todo desde la ventana. The neighbour was watching everything from the window.
It also means to remark: Observó que era tarde.

The word, taken apart.
observar is a learned borrowing from Latin observāre 'to watch, to heed' (EtymDB), built on ob- and servāre 'to keep, to guard'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
percatarse — to notice, to realise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to witness, then the Spanish for to observe.

You'll want to know: percatarse.
percatarse — "to notice, to realise".
No me percaté de nada aquella noche. I didn't notice anything that night.
Percatarse de algo — to notice something. A little formal; in speech, darse cuenta.

The word, taken apart.
percatarse is built on old Spanish catar 'to see, to examine' with per-. Catar is usually traced to Latin captāre 'to try to catch', from capere 'to take'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el detalle — the detail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to observe, then the Spanish for to notice.

You'll want to know: el detalle.
el detalle — "the detail".
Recuerdo cada detalle de aquel día. I remember every detail of that day.
¡Qué detalle! — how thoughtful! — said about a small kind gesture.

The word, taken apart.
detalle was borrowed from French détail, which is usually traced to tailler 'to cut', from Late Latin taliāre. A detail is a small piece cut off from the whole.
