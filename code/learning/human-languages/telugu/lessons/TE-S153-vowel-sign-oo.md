# ో — a sign, not a letter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-08] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ◌ి — what does it do to the consonant it sits on?

[PAUSE 2s] This one does the same job with a different vowel.

## Script you'll notice: ో
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-153]; assesses=[] -->

**ో** — *ō*.

It is a **sign**, not a letter. A Telugu consonant arrives with an *a* already
inside it, and a sign is how you swap that *a* for something else. On its own
it says nothing; hung on a consonant it turns that consonant's vowel into a
long *ō*.

- **సంతోషం** *santōṣam* — happiness, gladness (santōṣam — "a being well pleased")

Look at the middle of that word: the **త** carries the sign, and *ta* becomes
*tō*.

## Writing: ో — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-153] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ో yourself —
slowly, and larger than it is printed. Then write it again attached to a
consonant, the way it actually appears.

> The strip shows **where to start the character and which way to travel**, in
> one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school,
> so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the
> only one.
