Arabic, chapter 341: Knights and treaties.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
فارس (fāris) — a knight, a horseman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a royal court, then the Arabic for to take office.

You'll want to know: فارس.
فارس — fāris — "a knight, a horseman".
ركب الفارس حصانه الأبيض. rakiba al-fāris ḥiṣānahu al-abyaḍ. The knight rode his white horse.
Plural: فرسان, fursān.
فارس (fāris) الأحلام: Prince Charming.

The word, taken apart.
فارس (fāris) is built on the native Arabic root f-r-s (Lane), the root of faras, a horse. Fāris is the man on the horse; furūsiyya is horsemanship and knighthood.

Lesson 3 of 5.
هدنة (hudna) — a truce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a knight, then the Arabic for a helmet.

You'll want to know: هدنة.
هدنة — hudna — "a truce".
وقع الطرفان هدنة لمدة شهر. waqqaʿa aṭ-ṭarafān hudna li-muddat shahr. The two sides signed a truce for a month.

The word, taken apart.
هدنة (hudna) is a native Arabic word built on the root h-d-n, recorded in hudna ʿalā dakhan, an illusory truce (Lane). The root is usually explained as "to be still, to be quiet": a time when the fighting is still.

Lesson 4 of 5.
معاهدة (muʿāhada) — a treaty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a helmet, then the Arabic for a truce.

You'll want to know: معاهدة.
معاهدة — muʿāhada — "a treaty".
وقع البلدان معاهدة (muʿāhada) سلام. waqqaʿa al-baladān muʿāhadat salām. The two countries signed a peace treaty.

The word, taken apart.
معاهدة (muʿāhada) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-h-d (Lane), where ʿahd is a pledge. Muʿāhada is the verbal noun of Form III, "pledging to one another".

Lesson 5 of 5.
خان (khāna) — to betray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a truce, then the Arabic for a treaty.

You'll want to know: خان.
خان — khāna — "to betray".
خان الوزير الملك في القصة. khāna al-wazīr al-malik fī al-qiṣṣa. In the story the minister betrayed the king.
Present: يخون, yakhūn.
Not the noun خان (khāna), an inn, which has another source.

The word, taken apart.
خان (khāna) is built on the native Arabic root kh-w-n (Lane), "to be unfaithful to a trust"; khiyāna is treachery.
