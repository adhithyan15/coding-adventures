Gujarati, chapter 273: Who did it, and what has happened.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
અરુણે ચા પીધી (Aruṇe cā pīdhī) — a name as the doer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I drank tea" and "he drank water". (mẽ cā pīdhī, teṇe pāṇī pīdhũ.) Both times the doer was a pronoun. This lesson puts a name in its place.

Grammar Lens: the doer takes -એ.
અરુણે ચા પીધી. Aruṇe cā pīdhī. — "Arun drank tea."
With a verb that has an object, a name or a noun that does it takes -એ (-e), the way હું becomes મેં:
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
the doer: અરુણ. with -એ: અરુણે (Aruṇe).
the doer: મીરા. with -એ: મીરાએ (Mīrāe).
the doer: બહેન. with -એ: બહેને (bahene).
the doer: પોલીસ. with -એ: પોલીસે (polīse).
the doer: છોકરો. with -એ: છોકરાએ (chokrāe).
After a consonant it is the sign ે; after a vowel, the letter એ. છોકરો first turns its -ો into -ા, as it does before -ને. A plural keeps its -ઓ and adds એ: છોકરાઓએ (chokrāoe), "the boys".

You'll want to know: the verb follows the thing.
મીરાએ પત્ર લખ્યો. Mīrāe patra lakhyo. — "Mira wrote a letter."
પત્ર is masculine, so લખ્યો. The doer leaves the ending alone; the thing sets it: મીરાએ વાર્તા લખી (Mīrāe vārtā lakhī), for a feminine story, and અરુણે પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું (Aruṇe pustak vā̃cyũ), for a neuter book.
When the thing is a person, it takes -ને, and in Gujarati the verb follows that person too:
અરુણે મીરાને જોઈ. Aruṇe Mīrāne joī. — "Arun saw Mira."
જોઈ is feminine, for Mira. Turn it round, મીરાએ અરુણને જોયો (Mīrāe Aruṇne joyo): masculine, for Arun. Some speakers say જોયું in both; this book follows the person.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "The boy ate rice" — chokrāe bhāt khādho]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My sister wrote a story" — mārī bahene vārtā lakhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Mira saw Arun" — Mīrāe Aruṇne joyo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that Mira drank tea, then that Arun read a book.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Arun drank tea"? (અરુણે ચા પીધી (Aruṇe cā pīdhī).) Why પીધી? (ચા is feminine.) "Mira wrote a letter"? (મીરાએ પત્ર લખ્યો.) Who sets the ending, Mira or the letter? (The letter.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
ગયો છે, ગયો હતો (gayo che, gayo hato) — has gone, had gone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "he went" and "she came". (te gayo, te āvī.) Then "he was". (te hato.) Put a past and a copula side by side, and a new past appears.

Grammar Lens: has gone.
અરુણ બજાર ગયો છે. Aruṇ bajār gayo che. — "Arun has gone to the market."
ગયો says he went. ગયો છે says he went and is still away: the going matters now. The છે follows the person, as in the present: હું હમણાં આવ્યો છું (hũ hamṇā̃ āvyo chũ), "I have come this moment"; અમે આવ્યા છીએ (ame āvyā chīe), "we have come".
A verb with an object keeps its doer form and follows the thing: મેં કામ કર્યું છે (mẽ kām karyũ che), "I have done the work". There the copula is છે, whoever the doer.

You'll want to know: had gone.
હું સ્ટેશન પહોંચ્યો, પણ ટ્રેન ઊપડી ગઈ હતી. hũ sṭeśan pahõcyo, paṇ ṭren ūpaḍī gaī hatī. — "I reached the station, but the train had left."
હતી puts the leaving further back, before another past. It follows the way હતો, હતી, હતું, હતા do:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
he went. has (now): તે ગયો છે. had (before): તે ગયો હતો.
she went. has (now): તે ગઈ છે. had (before): તે ગઈ હતી.
I did the work. has (now): મેં કામ કર્યું છે. had (before): મેં કામ કર્યું હતું.
Mira drank tea. has (now): મીરાએ ચા પીધી છે. had (before): મીરાએ ચા પીધી હતી.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "We have come" — ame āvyā chīe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Arun had drunk the tea" — Aruṇe cā pīdhī hatī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that Mira has gone home, then that you had done the work.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"He has gone"? (તે ગયો છે.) "She had gone"? (તે ગઈ હતી.) "I have done the work"? (મેં કામ કર્યું છે.) In મીરાએ ચા પીધી હતી, why હતી? (It follows ચા.)
