## You'll want to know: kěnéng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C147-KENENG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*kěnéng* — "maybe; possibly".

> *tā kěnéng bù lái* — He may not come.

> *míngtiān kěnéng huì xiàyǔ* — It might rain tomorrow.

Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*kěnéng* is built from kě 'may', the kě of *kěyǐ*, and 能 'can', the 能 you write. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *khaX* and *nong* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to permit' and 'skilled'. 'Maybe' is the pair's own sense, which neither gloss gives.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-HUI-FUTURE-01] -->

*kěnéng* goes before the verb, or before 会: *kěnéng huì xiàyǔ*, it might rain.

With 会 alone the forecast sounds sure; with *kěnéng* in front it is only a chance.
