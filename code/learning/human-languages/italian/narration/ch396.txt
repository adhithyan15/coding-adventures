Italian, chapter 396: In banca.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
i contanti — the cash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the rate, then the Italian for the inflation.

You'll want to know: i contanti.
i contanti — "the cash".
Posso pagare in contanti? Can I pay in cash?
Usually plural: pagare in contanti. The opposite is con la carta.

The word, taken apart.
contanti is the plural of contante "counted out", from contare "to count", inherited from Latin computāre "to reckon" (Lewis & Short): money you can count in your hand.

Lesson 2 of 5.
il saldo — the balance (of an account); the final payment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the inflation, then the Italian for the cash.

You'll want to know: il saldo.
il saldo — "the balance (of an account); the final payment".
Controllo il saldo prima di fare acquisti. I check my balance before I go shopping.
Il saldo is also what is left to pay: pagare il saldo. I saldi are the sales.

The word, taken apart.
saldo is built on saldare "to weld, to settle an account", from the adjective saldo "firm", inherited from Latin solidus "solid, whole" (Lewis & Short): the account made whole.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'assegno — the cheque.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for the cash, then the Italian for the balance.

You'll want to know: l'assegno.
l'assegno — "the cheque".
Oggi pochi pagano con l'assegno. Few people pay by cheque today.
Un assegno a vuoto — a cheque that bounces.

The word, taken apart.
assegno is built on assegnare "to assign", a learned borrowing from Latin assignāre "to allot, to mark out" (Lewis & Short), built on signum "a mark, a seal".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il versamento — the payment (into an account), the deposit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the balance, then the Italian for the cheque.

You'll want to know: il versamento.
il versamento — "the payment (into an account), the deposit".
Ho fatto un versamento sul conto di mio figlio. I made a payment into my son's account.
Built on versare "to pour; to pay in".

The word, taken apart.
versamento is built on versare "to pour, to pay in", inherited from Latin versāre "to turn often" (Lewis & Short), built on vertere "to turn".

Lesson 5 of 5.
il deposito — the deposit; the storeroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the cheque, then the Italian for the payment.

You'll want to know: il deposito.
il deposito — "the deposit; the storeroom".
Per l'appartamento devo lasciare un deposito di due mesi. For the flat I have to leave a two-month deposit.
Il deposito bagagli — the left-luggage office.

The word, taken apart.
deposito is a learned borrowing from Latin dēpositum "a thing laid down for safekeeping" (Lewis & Short), from dēpōnere "to lay down", built on pōnere "to put".
