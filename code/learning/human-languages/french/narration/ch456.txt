French, chapter 456: Ruisseaux et marais.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le ruisseau — the stream, the brook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to climb, then the French for to stumble.

You'll want to know: le ruisseau.
le ruisseau — "the stream, the brook".
Un petit ruisseau traverse le jardin. A little stream runs across the garden.
Plural les ruisseaux.

The word, taken apart.
ruisseau goes back to Vulgar Latin rīvuscellus (EtymDB), a small rīvus 'stream'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'étang — the pond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to stumble, then the French for the stream.

You'll want to know: l'étang.
l'étang — "the pond".
Des canards nagent sur l'étang. Some ducks are swimming on the pond.
A masculine noun. Bigger than a mare, smaller than a lac.

The word, taken apart.
étang comes from Old French estanc (EtymDB lists it), built on estanchier 'to stop the flow of water', which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin verb; the details are not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
patauger — to splash about, to wade; to flounder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pond, then the French for the marsh.

You'll want to know: patauger.
patauger — "to splash about, to wade; to flounder".
Les enfants pataugent dans le ruisseau. The children are splashing about in the stream.
Figurative: je patauge — I am getting nowhere.

The word, taken apart.
patauger is usually linked to patte 'paw, foot', but the origin is not settled. EtymDB lists it without a source.

ruisseler is built on ruissel, an old form of ruisseau 'stream' (EtymDB lists it), from Latin rīvus.
