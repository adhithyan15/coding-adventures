# douze — twelve

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One welded number could be an accident. Two of them is a pattern, and
this one lets you hear the join.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-DOUZE-02]; assesses=[] -->

- `vowel-ou` — **ou** is a single pure **oo**, as in English *food*. Never two
  sounds.
- `z-voiced` — the same buzzed **z** as **onze**.
- Together: **douze** = *DOOZ*, one syllable.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**douze** is Latin **duodecim** — *duo* ("two") plus *decem* ("ten").

And this time you can see both halves survive into the French. Say **deux**, then
say **douze**, and listen to the front of each: *deu-*, *dou-*. That is the same
digit, and it did not disappear — it was only worn down.

The back half is doing the same thing. **onze** and **douze** both end in
**-ze**, and both come from a Latin word ending in *-decim*. Two words, one
shared ending, and a strong hint about where that ending came from.
