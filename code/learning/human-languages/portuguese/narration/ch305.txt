Portuguese, chapter 305: Chegou a ambulância.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a ambulância — the ambulance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the landslide, then the Portuguese for to bury under earth or rubble.

You'll want to know: a ambulância.
a ambulância — "the ambulance".
A ambulância chegou em dez minutos. The ambulance arrived in ten minutes.
Chamar uma ambulância — to call an ambulance.

The word, taken apart.
ambulância came from French ambulance (EtymDB), first a field hospital that moved with an army, built on ambulant "walking", from Latin ambulāre "to walk".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: a sirene.
a sirene — "the siren".
Ouvi a sirene de uma ambulância. I heard an ambulance siren.
Also a sirena.

The word, taken apart.
sirene was borrowed through French sirène from Latin sīrēn, from Greek seirḗn, the singing creature of old stories (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
socorrer — to help (someone in danger), to come to the aid of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the ambulance, then the Portuguese for the siren.

You'll want to know: socorrer.
socorrer — "to help (someone in danger), to come to the aid of".
Um rapaz socorreu a senhora que caiu. A young man helped the lady who fell.
Socorro! is the cry for help.

The word, taken apart.
socorrer is inherited from Latin succurrere "to run to help" (EtymDB), from sub and currere "to run".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o socorrista — the first-aider, the paramedic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the siren, then the Portuguese for to help.

You'll want to know: o socorrista.
o socorrista — "the first-aider, the paramedic".
O socorrista falou com calma ao rapaz. The paramedic spoke calmly to the young man.
The same form for a woman: a socorrista.

The word, taken apart.
socorrista is built on socorro "help" with -ista for the person; socorro goes back to Latin succurrere "to run to help" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a maca.
a maca — "the stretcher".
Levaram o homem numa maca. They carried the man on a stretcher.
Not to be confused with a maçã, the apple.

The word, taken apart.
maca came from Spanish hamaca "a hammock" (EtymDB), a word taken from Taíno hamaka.
