# ع ('eyn) — the catch inside تعریف

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-TE-01, FA-SCRIPT-C22-RE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ت and ر once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **تعریف کردن** (*ta'rif kardan*), "to tell". You met it a
moment ago. This lesson takes the catch out of it and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ع
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-C291-EYN-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C291-TARIF-KARDAN-01] -->

**ع** — *'eyn*, the small catch written *'* in this book's romanization.

It is the second letter of **تعریف**, straight after **ت**. In Persian it is a
light stop in the throat, *ta'-rif*, or no more than a slightly longer vowel
before it. Arabic says it deeper in the throat; Persian does not.

It changes shape more than most letters. At the start of a word it is an open
hook, **عـ**. In the middle it closes into a small flat loop, **ـعـ**, which is
the shape inside **تعریف**. Standing alone, **ع**, the hook sits on a tail that
curls down below the line.

Almost every Persian word with **ع** came from Arabic, and Persian has
thousands of them.

## Writing: ع
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C291-EYN-01] -->

Put your pen on ع and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
