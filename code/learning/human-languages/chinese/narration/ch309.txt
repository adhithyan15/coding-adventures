Mandarin Chinese, chapter 309: A Thief.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
qiǎngjié — to rob by force.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to commit a crime, then the Mandarin for the local police station.

You'll want to know: qiǎngjié.
qiǎngjié — "to rob by force".
yǒu rén qiǎngjié le zhè jiā yínháng — Someone robbed this bank.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qiǎngjié is built from qiǎng 'to snatch' and jié 'to plunder', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjangX and kjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to rob by force'; the sense 'to snatch' for the first is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

Lesson 2 of 6.
小偷 (xiǎotōu) — thief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the local police station, then the Mandarin for to rob by force.

You'll want to know: 小偷.
小偷 — xiǎotōu — "thief".
小偷偷了我的钱包 xiǎotōu tōu le wǒ de qiánbāo — A thief stole my wallet.
The new character, in pieces: 偷 (tōu) — eleven strokes in two pieces: the person piece of 你 on the left, two strokes; on the right a roof of two strokes with a short bar under it, then 月 on the left and the two strokes on the right of 到. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
小偷 (xiǎotōu) is built from 小 'small' and 偷 (tōu) 'to steal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX and thuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to rob'.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Script — the person piece, then a roof over 月 and two posts.
偷 (tōu).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, two strokes. The person piece of 你: a short sweep down to the left, then a vertical.
The right piece, nine strokes, top to bottom. A roof: a sweep down to the left and a long stroke falling to the right. A short bar under it. Then, side by side, 月 on the left, four strokes, and on the right the two strokes on the right of 到: a short vertical and a long vertical that hooks to the left.
2 + 9 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 偷 (tōu), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 偷 (tōu)? (Two.) What is under the roof and the bar? (月, and the two strokes on the right of 到.) Which stroke is last? (The long vertical that hooks to the left.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
piànzi — swindler; con man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a thief, then the Mandarin for to con someone.

You'll want to know: piànzi.
piànzi — "swindler; con man".
nàge rén shì piànzi — That man is a con man.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
piànzi is built from piàn 'to con' and the noun ending zi. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the Guangyun has no entry for the first, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
故意 (gùyì) — on purpose; deliberately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to con someone, then the Mandarin for a con man.

You'll want to know: 故意.
故意 — gùyì — "on purpose; deliberately".
他故意不说话 tā gùyì bù shuōhuà — He's keeping quiet on purpose.

The word, taken apart.
故意 (gùyì) is built from 故 and 意 'an intention', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuH and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'old' and 'a matter' and the second 'will'; how the pair came to mean 'on purpose' is not settled.
