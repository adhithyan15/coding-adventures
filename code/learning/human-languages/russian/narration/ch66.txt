Russian, chapter 66: Hot, Cold, Warm, Fast, Slow.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
горячий (goryáchiy) — hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for long, then the Russian for short.

You'll want to know: горячий.
горячий (goryáchiy) — "hot".

The word, taken apart.
горячий (goryáchiy) is built on гореть, "to burn", which Vasmer relates to Lithuanian garėti, "to flare up", Sanskrit háras, "heat", and Greek thérmos, "warm", from PIE gʷʰer-, "hot".

Lesson 2 of 5.
холодный (kholódnyy) — cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for short, then the Russian for hot.

You'll want to know: холодный.
холодный (kholódnyy) — "cold".

The word, taken apart.
холодный (kholódnyy) is холод, "cold", with -ный. холод from Proto-Slavic xoldŭ; Vasmer thinks it is probably a variant of the root of Gothic kalds, "cold", and Latin gelidus, "icy", so English cold would be a cousin. Church Slavonic has хлад, "coolness".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for hot, then the Russian for cold.

You'll want to know: тёплый.
тёплый (tyóplyy) — "warm".

The word, taken apart.
тёплый (tyóplyy) from Proto-Slavic teplŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Sanskrit tápati, "heats", and Persian tāftan, "to burn"; Latin tepidus, "lukewarm", comes from the same PIE root tep-, "to be warm".

Lesson 4 of 5.
быстрый (býstryy) — fast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for cold, then the Russian for warm.

You'll want to know: быстрый.
быстрый (býstryy) — "fast".

The word, taken apart.
быстрый (býstryy) from Proto-Slavic bystrŭ — inherited; Serbo-Croatian бистар means "clear". Vasmer compares Old Norse bysja, "to gush out", and Norwegian buse, "to rush forward", but other links are proposed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
медленный (médlennyy) — slow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for warm, then the Russian for fast.

You'll want to know: медленный.
медленный (médlennyy) — "slow". The adjective beside медленно, slowly.

The word, taken apart.
медленный (médlennyy) from Old Russian mĭdĭlĭnŭ, Proto-Slavic mŭdĭlĭnŭ — inherited, with медлить, "to delay". Vasmer connects it with the Slavic verb muditi, "to linger".
