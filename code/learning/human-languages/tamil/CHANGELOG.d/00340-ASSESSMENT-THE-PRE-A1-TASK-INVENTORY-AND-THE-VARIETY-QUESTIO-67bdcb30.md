## Assessment: the pre-A1 task inventory, and the variety question answered

Tamil's pre-A1 four-skill task inventory is checked in, together with the
assessment specification it cites. The track is now measurable by
`reading-reach`, which could not see it at all before: with no
`task-shapes/pre-a1.json` there was no row in the table, so a reading passage
could be written or deleted and the report read identically. Chapter 84's
passage landed while this was in review, so the measurement is now a real one:
**30 words against a 1-word ceiling, 3/3 parts in reach**, with a floor
committed so the number cannot quietly fall.

**No `assessment.json` yet, on purpose.** The machine-readable contract names
its artifacts by path and the artifact gate treats a path that leads nowhere as
an error. Probed directly: a contract copied onto this track produces **28 new
dangling references** and a hard failure. The contract is written after the
inventories, mocks, rubrics and answer keys, not before them.

**The variety question.** Tamil is diglossic and a four-skill exam cannot score
a speaking paper without saying so. Reading and writing are assessed in
contemporary standard written Tamil; speaking and listening accept standard
spoken Tamil, and a candidate is penalised for neither register. Mixing them
inside one turn is not an error at pre-A1, where the learner has memorised
chunks rather than internalised a register; from A2 the task states which
register it expects.

**Why the pass rule differs from the real one.** The Tamil Virtual Academy, a
Government of Tamil Nadu institution, runs genuine graded Tamil certificates at
Basic, Intermediate and Advanced, examined each June and December, and requires
an average of not less than 40% across its online and written papers. This
contract cites that rule *because it refuses it*: it is a school-year ladder
mapped to classes 1-12 with aggregate compensation, and a ladder whose whole
claim is four independent skills cannot let a strong reader carry a silent
writer. Each paper must independently reach 60/100.

**What the writing paper scores** is the two things Tamil orthography actually
requires: the **pulli**, the dot above a consonant that carries no vowel — its
absence changes the syllable, and the corpus writes it 3,954 times — and
**vowel-sign side**, because Tamil writes ெ ே ை to the left of their consonant,
ா ி ீ ு ூ to the right, and ொ ோ ௌ on both sides at once. A sign on the wrong
side is the commonest error a learner makes and nothing else would catch it.
