\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This is the Hindi volume of the same project as the Spanish, French, German,
and Arabic ones. It is written for someone starting from zero --- who may not
read a single Devanagari letter --- so the script is taught \emph{inside the
word lessons themselves}: each word introduces exactly the letters it needs,
so you learn to read \hi{नमस्ते} \emph{and} learn that it means ``I bow to
you'' in the same few minutes. There is no alphabet drill to sit through
first. (A reader who already knows Devanagari simply skims the ``letters in
this word'' notes.)

Hindi's depth comes from a double inheritance. Much of its core vocabulary is
\textbf{Sanskrit} --- \hi{नमस्ते} and \hi{धन्यवाद} sit on ancient roots
(\emph{nam}, ``to bow''; \emph{dhanya}, ``worthy''). But centuries of Persian
as the language of courts layered on a second vocabulary, Persian and (through
it) Arabic --- so ``thanks'' is also \hi{शुक्रिया} (\emph{shukriyā}, the same
Semitic root as Arabic \emph{shukran}), and ``farewell'' is \hi{अलविदा}
(\emph{alvidā}, ``the farewell,'' carrying the Arabic article \emph{al-}). The
book keeps both streams in view.

Same three commitments as the other volumes: one word per lesson, gone deep;
the widest honest web of connections; the cultural reason, not just the rule.
