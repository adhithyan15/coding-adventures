French, chapter 334: Mon point de vue.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le point de vue — the point of view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a team, then the French for a birthday.

You'll want to know: le point de vue.
le point de vue — "the point of view".
De mon point de vue, c'est une bonne idée. From my point of view, it is a good idea.
The plural is les points de vue. À mon avis says much the same thing.

The word, taken apart.
point is inherited from Latin punctum "a prick, a point" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), and vue is the noun made from voir "to see", inherited from Latin vidēre: the spot you look from.

Lesson 2 of 5.
soutenir — to maintain, to claim; to support.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a birthday, then the French for the point of view.

You'll want to know: soutenir.
soutenir — "to maintain, to claim; to support".
Il soutient que le train est plus confortable que l'avion. He maintains that the train is more comfortable than the plane.
Conjugated like tenir: je soutiens, nous soutenons; past participle soutenu. It also means to support a person: soutenir un ami.

The word, taken apart.
soutenir is inherited from Latin sustinēre "to hold up, to bear" (Lewis & Short), through Old French sustenir (EtymDB). The Latin is built from sub "under" and tenēre "to hold": you hold an idea up so it does not fall.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prétendre — to claim (with a hint of doubt).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the point of view, then the French for to maintain.

You'll want to know: prétendre.
prétendre — "to claim (with a hint of doubt)".
Il prétend qu'il n'a pas reçu mon message. He claims he did not get my message.
A false friend: prétendre is to claim, and the speaker often doubts the claim. Conjugated like rendre: il prétend.

The word, taken apart.
prétendre comes from Latin praetendere "to stretch forth, to hold out in front" (Lewis & Short), taken into Old French as a learned word (EtymDB). You hold a claim out in front of you.

Lesson 4 of 5.
affirmer — to state firmly, to assert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to maintain, then the French for to claim.

You'll want to know: affirmer.
affirmer — "to state firmly, to assert".
Elle affirme qu'elle était à la maison ce soir-là. She states that she was at home that evening.
Stronger than dire: you say it as a fact.

The word, taken apart.
affirmer is a learned borrowing from Latin affirmāre "to assert, to declare" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on firmus "firm": you make your words firm.

Lesson 5 of 5.
convaincant — convincing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to claim, then the French for to state firmly.

You'll want to know: convaincant.
convaincant — "convincing".
Sa réponse n'était pas très convaincante. His answer was not very convincing.
It agrees: un ton convaincant, une réponse convaincante. It comes from convaincre, to convince.

The word, taken apart.
convaincant is the present participle of convaincre, which comes from Latin convincere "to overcome, to prove wrong" (Lewis & Short), built on vincere "to conquer".
