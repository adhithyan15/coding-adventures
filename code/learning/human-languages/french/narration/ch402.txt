French, chapter 402: Bien voir la route.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le pare-brise — the windscreen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to stall, then the French for the pedal.

You'll want to know: le pare-brise.
le pare-brise — "the windscreen".
Un caillou a cassé le pare-brise. A stone broke the windscreen.
The plural does not change: des pare-brise.

The word, taken apart.
pare-brise is built from parer 'to ward off' and brise 'breeze' (EtymDB): the glass that keeps the wind out. Parer goes back to Latin parāre 'to prepare' (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: le klaxon.
le klaxon — "the horn (of a car)".
Dans les rues étroites, on entend souvent le klaxon des taxis. In the narrow streets you often hear the taxis' horns.
First a trade name; now an ordinary noun.

The word, taken apart.
klaxon was borrowed from English klaxon (EtymDB), first the trade name of an electric car horn.

Lesson 5 of 5.
embuer — to mist up (mostly reflexive: s’embuer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the horn, then the French for to honk.

You'll want to know: embuer.
embuer — "to mist up (mostly reflexive: s’embuer)".
Quand il fait froid, les vitres s'embuent. When it is cold, the windows mist up.
Mostly reflexive: les vitres s'embuent. Embué — misty.

The word, taken apart.
embuer is built on buée 'mist, condensation'. Buée is usually traced to an old verb buer 'to do the washing', of Germanic origin: the steam that rose from the washing tubs. EtymDB does not list the word.
