Portuguese, chapter 414: Os bilhetes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a bilheteira — the ticket office, the box office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the route, then the Portuguese for to get off.

You'll want to know: a bilheteira.
a bilheteira — "the ticket office, the box office".
A bilheteira da estação abre às seis. The station ticket office opens at six.
In Brazil: a bilheteria. Theatres and stadiums have one too.

The word, taken apart.
bilheteira is built on bilhete 'ticket' with -eira, a place; bilhete came from French billet 'note', from Old French billette (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: o passe.
o passe — "the travel pass, the season ticket; the pass (in football)".
Compro o passe todos os meses. I buy the travel pass every month.
O passe covers buses, trams and the metro for a month.

The word, taken apart.
passe is built on passar 'to pass' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Vulgar Latin passāre, on Latin passus 'step'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
validar — to validate (a ticket); to approve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the ticket office, then the Portuguese for the travel pass.

You'll want to know: validar.
validar — "to validate (a ticket); to approve".
Validamos o bilhete à entrada do metro. We validate the ticket at the metro entrance.
Machines at the gates validam the card.

The word, taken apart.
validar is a learned borrowing from Latin validāre 'to make strong', from validus 'strong' (EtymDB), on valēre 'to be strong'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o revisor — the ticket inspector.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the travel pass, then the Portuguese for to validate.

You'll want to know: o revisor.
o revisor — "the ticket inspector".
O revisor pediu os bilhetes a toda a gente. The inspector asked everyone for their tickets.
Also a proofreader of texts.

The word, taken apart.
revisor is a learned formation on Latin revīsere 'to come back to see, to look again at', built on vidēre 'to see' (Lewis & Short): the person who checks the tickets again.

Lesson 5 of 5.
a coima — the fine (official).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to validate, then the Portuguese for the ticket inspector.

You'll want to know: a coima.
a coima — "the fine (official)".
Quem viaja sem bilhete paga uma coima. Anyone travelling without a ticket pays a fine.
A coima is the official word on notices; people often say a multa.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of coima is not settled. It is an old Portuguese word for a penalty, and EtymDB has no entry for it.
