# (reading) — six things you would be told

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPERATIF-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every line below you have heard and said. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-SKILL-READ-INSTRUCTIONS]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPERATIF-01] -->

> Parle !
> Écoute !
> On y va !
> Un peu
> Beaucoup
> Merci

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and notice that three of them end in an exclamation mark and
three do not. French puts a space before it, which English does not.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-BEAUCOUP-01, FR-LEX-UNPEU-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Parle !** and **Écoute !** are the same verbs you conjugate, with
the ending taken off and nothing put in its place. That is the whole imperative.

[PAUSE 2s] An instruction you can only recognise by ear is one somebody has to
say to you. On a page it waits, which is why a classroom is the first place a
beginner reads rather than listens.

[PAUSE 2s] Six lines, and half of them are one word.
