Gujarati, chapter 465: Strict and Proud.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સખત (sakhat) — strict; severe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for composed, then the Gujarati for to heed.

You'll want to know: સખત.
સખત — sakhat — "strict; severe".
અમારા આચાર્ય ઘણા સખત છે. amārā ācārya ghaṇā sakhat che. Our principal is very strict.

The word, taken apart.
સખત (sakhat) is usually traced to Persian saxt, "hard, firm".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: ચીકણું.
ચીકણું — cīkṇũ — "fussy; sticky".
મારી સાસુ ઘરકામમાં ઘણી ચીકણી છે. mārī sāsu gharkāmmā̃ ghaṇī cīkṇī che. My mother-in-law is very fussy about the housework.

The word, taken apart.
ચીકણું (cīkṇũ) goes back to Sanskrit cikkaṇa, "slippery, unctuous"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "sticky" (CDIAL 4782), and Gujarati also uses it for a fussy person.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ઉદ્ધત (uddhat) — insolent; rude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for strict, then the Gujarati for fussy.

You'll want to know: ઉદ્ધત.
ઉદ્ધત — uddhat — "insolent; rude".
ઉદ્ધત છોકરાએ ગુરુને ખરાબ જવાબ આપ્યો. uddhat chokrāe gurune kharāb javāb āpyo. The insolent boy gave the teacher a rude answer.

The word, taken apart.
ઉદ્ધત (uddhat) is a tatsama, Sanskrit uddhata, "raised, lifted up", as Monier-Williams records it; the sense "haughty, insolent" grew from holding one's head too high.

અહંકાર — ahankār — "egotism; conceit".
પૈસાનો અહંકાર સારો નથી. paisāno ahankār sāro nathī. Pride in money is not good.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
અહંકાર (ahankār) is a tatsama, Sanskrit ahaṃkāra, the making of an "I", egotism, as Monier-Williams records it.

You'll want to know: ટેવાવું.
ટેવાવું — ṭevāvũ — "to get used to".
નવા ઘરમાં બાળકો જલદી ટેવાઈ ગયાં. navā gharmā̃ bāḷko jaldī ṭevāī gayā̃. The children quickly got used to the new house.
Past (he …): તે ટેવાઈ ગયો, te ṭevāī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
ટેવાવું (ṭevāvũ) is built on ટેવ (ṭev), "a habit", which CDIAL traces to a reconstructed ṭēv-, "appoint, fix" (CDIAL 5475), with the verb ending -āvũ.
