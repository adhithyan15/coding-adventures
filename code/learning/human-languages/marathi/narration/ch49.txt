Marathi, chapter 49: Big, and How an Adjective Agrees.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
मोठा (moṭhā) — big.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Forty-eight chapters in you can name a house, put a room in it, say who it belongs to and how to get there. You cannot say one thing about what it is like.
[your turn — recall: say mājhyākaḍe ghar āhe, then mī mitrālā pāṇī deto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: मोठा.
मोठा — moṭhā — big, large.
मोठं घर — moṭhaṁ ghar — a big house.
Start with the ending. The headword is मोठा (moṭhā), and the example says मोठं. Nothing is wrong: the adjective took the shape of the noun it sits in front of, and घर is neuter. The next lesson is about nothing but that.
मोठा (moṭhā) covers size in every direction — a big house, a big city, a big brother. Where लांब measured a distance, this measures a thing.
One warning worth having early. If you know Hindi you will meet मोटा (moṭā) there and reach for it: it means fat, not big, and the two words are a genuine trap between the languages. Marathi's मोठा (moṭhā) is the neutral, everyday word; nothing about it is about a body.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: moṭhā — big]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: moṭhaṁ ghar — a big house]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say झोपणे and then ask for a big room to do it in]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'a big house', then 'my house is big'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word measures a distance, and which measures a thing? (लांब; मोठा (moṭhā).) What does the Hindi look-alike मोटा mean? (Fat — not the same word.)

Lesson 2 of 4.
मोठा (moṭhā), मोठी, मोठं — three endings, one rule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
माझा, माझी, माझं from chapter nine. उजवा, उजवी, उजवं from chapter forty-four. The same three endings, twice, and never named.

Grammar Lens: the adjective takes the gender of its noun.
मोठा moṭhā — with a masculine noun मोठी moṭhī — with a feminine noun मोठं moṭhaṁ — with a neuter noun.
Marathi has three genders where most European languages have two, and an adjective that ends in -आ in its dictionary form changes to match all three. You have owned the machinery since chapter nine; what you did not have was permission to use it on a new word.
मोठा (moṭhā) मित्र — a big friend (masculine) मोठी खोली — a big room (feminine) मोठं घर — a big house (neuter).
Say those three aloud and hear that only the last syllable moves. That is what makes the rule cheap: learn one adjective and you have learned three forms, and every -आ adjective after this costs you nothing extra.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: moṭhā mitra, moṭhī khoolī, moṭhaṁ ghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same three endings on ujvā — and hear that nothing new happened]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say चालणे, and the chālel that means that will do]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Put moṭhā on a friend, a room and a house, changing only the ending.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which part of the adjective moves? (Only the last syllable.) How many genders does it have to match? (Three.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
मोठं … आहे (moṭhaṁ … āhe) — where the adjective stands.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every adjective this book has shown you — माझा, उजवा, डावा, मोठा (moṭhā) — has stood in the same place, and nobody has said so.

Grammar Lens: in front, always.
A Marathi adjective goes before its noun and never after it.
मोठं घर — a big house. Never घर मोठं.
That is worth saying out loud because it is the opposite of what a Romance language does: Spanish writes una casa grande and French une maison grande, noun first. English happens to agree with Marathi here, which makes this one of the few places an English speaker can trust the order they already have.
The second position is different, and you have used it since chapter ten without noticing. When the adjective is the whole point of the sentence rather than a label on a noun, it goes at the END, in front of आहे:
माझं घर मोठं आहे. — mājhaṁ ghar moṭhaṁ āhe. — my house is big.
Two positions, two jobs: in front describes a thing you are naming, at the end before आहे makes the description the claim. The ending agrees in both.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: moṭhaṁ ghar — the label]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mājhaṁ ghar moṭhaṁ āhe — the claim]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say mājhyākaḍe moṭhaṁ ghar āhe — I have a big house]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Name a big house, then claim that your house is big.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does an adjective stand when it labels a noun? (Before it.) And when it is the claim of the sentence? (At the end, before आहे.)
