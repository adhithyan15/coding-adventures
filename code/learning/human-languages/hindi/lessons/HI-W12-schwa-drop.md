# नाम — the vowel at the end of a word that you write and do not say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

position, virama and conjunct. What is left is shapes -- and one rule nobody
told you.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-SCHWA-DROP-01]; assesses=[] -->

You know that a bare consonant carries an inherent **a**. Here is the exception
that governs almost every Hindi word, and nobody tells a beginner until they have
already mispronounced a hundred of them.

**At the end of a word, that inherent vowel is not pronounced.**

Take the word for *name*. Written out piece by piece it is **न** + **ा** + **म**,
and reading each piece aloud gives *nā-ma*. But the word is **nām**. The final
**म** carries an inherent *a* that Hindi simply does not say.

This is called **schwa deletion**, and it is why:

- **नाम** is *nām*, not *nāma*
- **घर** is *ghar*, not *ghara*
- **राम** is *Rām*, not *Rāma*

**Sanskrit says the final vowel. Hindi drops it.** The spelling is inherited from
Sanskrit and never changed; the pronunciation moved on a thousand years ago. Both
are true, and the script keeps the older one — exactly as English keeps the *k* in
*knight*.

Two things follow, and the second is the useful one:

1. A word you sound out letter by letter will end one vowel too long. Trim it.
2. **This is not a special case to memorise per word.** It is the default. Say the
   final consonant bare unless something is written after it.
