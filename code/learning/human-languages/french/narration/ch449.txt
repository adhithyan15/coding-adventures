French, chapter 449: Fleurs, tabac et colis.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le fleuriste — the florist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the watchmaker, then the French for the jeweller.

You'll want to know: le fleuriste.
le fleuriste — "the florist".
Le fleuriste a fait un joli bouquet pour ma mère. The florist made a pretty bunch of flowers for my mother.
Same form for a woman: la fleuriste.

The word, taken apart.
fleuriste is built on fleur 'flower' with -iste; fleur is inherited from Latin flōs, flōris (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
affranchir — to put a stamp on, to frank; to free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the florist, then the French for the tobacconist.

You'll want to know: affranchir.
affranchir — "to put a stamp on, to frank; to free".
Elle a affranchi la lettre au tarif normal. She put a normal-rate stamp on the letter.
L'affranchissement — the postage. First 'to set free'.

The word, taken apart.
affranchir is built from a- and franc 'free', the word behind franchir (EtymDB links the two); franc comes from the name of the Franks: a letter with its stamp travels free.

Lesson 4 of 5.
expédier — to send off, to ship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tobacconist, then the French for to put a stamp on.

You'll want to know: expédier.
expédier — "to send off, to ship".
Nous avons expédié le colis mardi. We sent the parcel off on Tuesday.
L'expéditeur — the sender.

The word, taken apart.
expédier is built on expédient, from Latin expedīre 'to free the feet, to get things ready', from pēs 'foot' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ficeler — to tie up (with string).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to put a stamp on, then the French for to send off.

You'll want to know: ficeler.
ficeler — "to tie up (with string)".
Le vendeur a ficelé le paquet avec soin. The shop assistant tied up the parcel carefully.
La ficelle — string; also a thin baguette.

The word, taken apart.
ficeler is built on ficelle 'string' (EtymDB lists the verb), which is usually traced to Vulgar Latin fūnicella, a small fūnis 'rope'.
