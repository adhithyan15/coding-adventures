German, chapter 272: Im Radio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Sendung — the broadcast, the programme; the consignment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the headline, then the German for the article.

You'll want to know: die Sendung.
die Sendung — "the broadcast, the programme; the consignment".
Die Sendung beginnt um acht Uhr. The programme begins at eight.
Plural: die Sendungen. At the post office, a Sendung is a parcel or letter on its way.

The word, taken apart.
Sendung is built on senden "to send", a cousin of English send from Proto-Germanic sandijaną "to cause to go" (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Rundfunk — radio, broadcasting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the article, then the German for the broadcast.

You'll want to know: der Rundfunk.
der Rundfunk — "radio, broadcasting".
Sein Vater arbeitete viele Jahre beim Rundfunk. His father worked in radio for many years.
No plural; a little formal. In speech people say das Radio.

The word, taken apart.
Rundfunk joins rund "round", Middle High German runt (EtymDB), which came through French from Latin rotundus "round" (Lewis & Short), and Funk "radio", a by-form of Funke "spark", from Old High German funcho (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: die Presse.
die Presse — "the press; a squeezer".
Die Presse schrieb viel über den Fall. The press wrote a lot about the case.
Also a kitchen press: eine Knoblauchpresse.

The word, taken apart.
Presse is built on pressen "to press", Old High German pressōn (EtymDB), which came from Latin pressāre, built on premere "to press" (Lewis & Short): first the printing press.

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Journalist — the journalist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for radio, then the German for the press.

You'll want to know: der Journalist.
der Journalist — "the journalist".
Ein Journalist stellte viele Fragen. A journalist asked many questions.
Weak noun: den Journalisten. Feminine die Journalistin. The J sounds as in French.

The word, taken apart.
Journalist was borrowed from French journaliste (EtymDB), built on journal "daily paper", from Latin diurnālis "daily", from diurnus "of the day".

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Titelseite — the front page.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the press, then the German for the journalist.

You'll want to know: die Titelseite.
die Titelseite — "the front page".
Das Foto war auf der Titelseite. The photo was on the front page.
Plural: die Titelseiten.

The word, taken apart.
Titelseite joins Titel "title", from Latin titulus "an inscription, a title" (Lewis & Short), and the inherited Seite "side", Old High German sīta (EtymDB).
