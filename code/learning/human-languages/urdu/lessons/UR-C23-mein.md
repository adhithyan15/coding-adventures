# میں (meṅ) — in

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-KO, UR-LEX-MAIN, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **to the mother**. Then say **I am well**, and look at the first
word of it.

## You'll want to know: میں after a noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-MEIN-POST]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KAMRA, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE] -->

> **کمرے میں** — *kamre meṅ* — **in the room.**

> **پانی میں** — *pānī meṅ* — **in the water.**

Twenty-three chapters, and this is the first time anything in this book has been
**inside** anything else.

Notice the noun. **کمرہ** became **کمرے** again, because a postposition is
following it. That is the rule from two lessons ago, and it will be the rule
every time.

## Grammar Lens: the same spelling, two different words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-MEIN-POST, UR-LEX-MAIN, UR-LEX-HUN, UR-LEX-KAMRA] -->

This word is spelled **exactly** like the pronoun **میں** you have used since
chapter four, and they are unrelated. They are told apart by **position**:

| where it stands | what it is | said |
|---|---|---|
| at the head of a clause | **I** | *maiṅ* |
| after a noun | **in** | *meṅ* |

The pronoun comes from Sanskrit *maya*; the postposition from *madhye*, "in the
middle of". They fell together on the page because the Urdu script writes no
short vowels — which is exactly the difficulty that makes reading Urdu hard and
that this book will keep returning to.

Both in one sentence:

> **میں کمرے میں ہوں** — *maiṅ kamre meṅ hūṅ* — **I am in the room.**

First word, pronoun. Fourth word, postposition. Same three letters.
