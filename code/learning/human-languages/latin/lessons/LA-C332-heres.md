## You'll want to know: hērēs, hērēdis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C332-HERES-01]; assesses=[] -->

**hērēs, hērēdis** — "an heir".

> Fīlius ūnus hērēs patris erat.
>
> *The only son was his father's heir.*

Masculine or feminine, 3rd declension. *Hērēdem facere*: to name someone heir in a will. English *heir*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**hērēs** is inherited; EtymDB traces it to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to leave behind", while Lewis & Short compare a Sanskrit root meaning "to take", so the details are not settled. English *heir* comes from it through French.
