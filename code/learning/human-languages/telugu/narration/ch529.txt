Telugu, chapter 529: Uncles and Aunts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
మేనమామ (mēnamāma) — a maternal uncle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a husband's sister, then the Telugu for an elder brother's wife.

You'll want to know: మేనమామ.
మేనమామ — mēnamāma — "a maternal uncle".
సెలవుల్లో మేము మేనమామ ఇంటికి వెళ్ళాము. selavullō mēmu mēnamāma iṇṭiki veḷḷāmu. In the holidays we went to our maternal uncle's house.
మామ alone can mean father-in-law; మేన- makes it the mother's brother.

The word, taken apart.
మేనమామ (mēnamāma) joins native māma, "maternal uncle, father-in-law" (DEDR 4813), to mēna-, which DEDR places in its appendix of words taken from Indo-Aryan (DEDR App. 53), going back through Prakrit kinship words to Sanskrit maithuna.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మేనకోడలు (mēnakōḍalu) — a niece; a sister's daughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a father's elder brother, then the Telugu for a mother's elder sister.

You'll want to know: మేనకోడలు.
మేనకోడలు — mēnakōḍalu — "a niece; a sister's daughter".
నా మేనకోడలు ఇవాళ బడికి వెళ్ళింది. nā mēnakōḍalu ivāḷa baḍiki veḷḷindi. My niece went to school today.
A man's sister's daughter or a woman's brother's daughter; కోడలు alone is a daughter-in-law.

The word, taken apart.
మేనకోడలు (mēnakōḍalu) is built from mēna-, listed in DEDR's appendix of Indo-Aryan loans (DEDR App. 53) and కోడలు, "daughter-in-law".
