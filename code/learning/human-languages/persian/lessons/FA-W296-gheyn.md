# غ (gheyn) — the letter that opens غذا

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C291-EYN-01, FA-SCRIPT-C26-GHAF-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ع and ق once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **غذا** (*ghazâ*), "food". You met it a moment ago. This
lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: غ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-C296-GHEYN-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C296-GHAZA-01] -->

**غ** — *gheyn*, the sound *gh*, made far back, like a soft gargle.

It opens **غذا**, in its opening shape, **غـ**, joined to **ذ**. It is **ع**
with **one dot above**, and it changes shape the same way: an open hook at
the start, a small closed loop in the middle, a tail at the end.

In Tehran speech **غ** and **ق** sound alike: *ghazâ* and *qatâr* open on the
same sound. Spelling keeps them apart, and so does this book's romanization:
**ق** is written *q*, as in *qatâr*, and **غ** is written *gh*, as in *ghazâ*.
Each word has to be learned with its own letter.

## Writing: غ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C296-GHEYN-01] -->

Put your pen on غ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
