Telugu, chapter 546: Around the House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
చూరు (cūru) — the eaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to shove, then the Telugu for a bet.

You'll want to know: చూరు.
చూరు — cūru — "the eaves".
వర్షం నీళ్ళు చూరు నుంచి కారుతున్నాయి. varṣaṁ nīḷḷu cūru nuñci kārutunnāyi. Rainwater is dripping from the eaves.
The edge of a sloping roof.

The word, taken apart.
చూరు (cūru) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2729 lists Telugu cūru, "eaves, the edge of a roof", with Kannada cūru.

The low plank seat for eating or working on the floor.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
చెంబు (cembu) — a small water pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a low wooden stool, then the Telugu for a dug well.

You'll want to know: చెంబు.
చెంబు — cembu — "a small water pot".
తాతయ్య చెంబుతో నీళ్ళు తాగారు. tātayya cembutō nīḷḷu tāgāru. Grandpa drank water from a small pot.
A small round metal pot with a neck.

The word, taken apart.
చెంబు (cembu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2775 lists Telugu cembu, "goblet, pitcher", with Tamil cempu, "copper; a metal vessel"; DEDR also raises the possibility that Sanskrit tāmra, "copper", played a part.
