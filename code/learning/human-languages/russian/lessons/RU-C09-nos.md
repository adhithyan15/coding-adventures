# нос — "nose," a second straight cousin in a row

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-UKHO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One syllable, one gender back to consonant-final masculine, and
another word that never left the family.

## You'll want to know first — нос
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-NOS]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-NOUN-GENDER-ENDING, RU-LEX-UKHO] -->

> **нос** — *nos* — **nose**. **Masculine.**

Consonant-final, back to the first row of Chapter 6's table. Every letter is
one you have read since Chapter 1.

## The word, taken apart — the plainest cousin yet
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-NOS-NOSE]; assesses=[RU-LEX-UKHO, RU-ETYMON-UKHO-EAR] -->

**нос** is Indo-European \**nas-*, and it barely needs translating: English
**nose** is the same word, and so is Latin **nasus** (→ **nasal**,
**nasturtium**, the "nose-twisting" flower). Say *nos* next to *nose* and
*nasus* and the family resemblance needs no explaining at all.

Two body-part words in a row, *ухо* and *нос*, and both are the same kind of
cousin: no loan, no replacement, just the same ancient root, worn down along
three different paths to the same meaning.
