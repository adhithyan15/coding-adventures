## You'll want to know: இரண்டாவது
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C82-ORDINAL-03]; assesses=[TA-LEX-NUMBERS-1-5-01] -->

**இரண்டாவது** (*iraṇḍāvadu*) — "**second**".

**இரண்டு** answers *how many*. **இரண்டாவது** answers *which one in the line*.

**இரண்டாவது நாள்** (*iraṇḍāvadu nāḷ*), the second day. **இரண்டாவது கதவு**
(*iraṇḍāvadu kadavu*), the second door.

## Grammar Lens: one ending, and one word that does not take it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-GRAMMAR-C82-ORDINAL-04]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C82-ORDINAL-03, TA-LEX-C82-ORDINAL-01] -->

| cardinal | ordinal |
|---|---|
| **இரண்டு** (*iraṇḍu*) | **இரண்டாவது** (*iraṇḍāvadu*) |

Add **-ஆவது** (*-āvadu*) to a number and you have its ordinal. That is the rule,
and it works on every number Tamil has.

There is exactly **one** exception in the whole set, and you met it last lesson:
you do not say *oṉṟāvadu* for **first**. Tamil reaches for **முதல்** instead.

So the one word a learner would have to memorise here is the one word this book
did not have to teach you.
