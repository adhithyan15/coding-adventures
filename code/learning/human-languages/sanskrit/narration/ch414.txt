Sanskrit, chapter 414: Losing the Way.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दिग्भ्रमः (digbhramaḥ) — losing one's bearings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for delivers, then the Sanskrit for arranges.

You'll want to know: दिग्भ्रमः.
दिग्भ्रमः — digbhramaḥ — "losing one's bearings".
वने पथिकस्य दिग्भ्रमः भवति। vane pathikasya digbhramaḥ bhavati. In the forest the wayfarer loses his bearings.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दिग्भ्रमः (digbhramaḥ) is built from diś, "a direction", and bhrama, "wandering, confusion", from the root √bhram, "to wander"; MW gives the compound as confusion about the quarters of the compass, mistaking the way (MW).

You'll want to know: भ्रान्तः.
भ्रान्तः — bhrāntaḥ — "astray; wandering".
भ्रान्तः पथिकः ग्रामं न प्राप्नोति। bhrāntaḥ pathikaḥ grāmaṃ na prāpnoti. The wayfarer who has gone astray does not reach the village.

The word, taken apart.
भ्रान्तः (bhrāntaḥ) comes from the root √bhram, "to wander", which also gives bhramati, "wanders": it is the participle, "having wandered", and MW gives bhrānta as roaming about and as confused (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रतिपद्यते (pratipadyate) — finds the way; reaches.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for losing one's bearings, then the Sanskrit for astray.

You'll want to know: प्रतिपद्यते.
प्रतिपद्यते — pratipadyate — "finds the way; reaches".
पथिकः शनैः मार्गं प्रतिपद्यते। pathikaḥ śanaiḥ mārgaṃ pratipadyate. Slowly the wayfarer finds the road.
Past (he …): सः मार्गं प्रतिपन्नवान्, saḥ mārgaṃ pratipannavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिपद्यते (pratipadyate) is built from prati, "towards", and the root √pad, "to go, to step"; MW gives prati-pad as to set foot upon, arrive at, reach, with the participle pratipanna, "arrived, found" (MW).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: अपरिचितः.
अपरिचितः — aparicitaḥ — "unfamiliar; strange".
एतत् नगरं मम अपरिचितम् अस्ति। etat nagaraṃ mama aparicitam asti. This city is unfamiliar to me.

The word, taken apart.
अपरिचितः (aparicitaḥ) is built from a, "not", and paricita, "known, familiar", from pari and the root √ci, "to gather"; MW gives aparicita as unacquainted, unknown (MW).
