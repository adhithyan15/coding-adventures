Urdu, chapter 317: The Night Before Eid.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
عید (īd) — the festival of Eid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to slip away, then the Urdu for to crawl.

You'll want to know: عید.
عید — īd — "the festival of Eid".
کل عید ہے۔ kal īd hai. Tomorrow is Eid.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
عید (īd) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ʿīd, "a festival, a day that comes round again". It is feminine: عید آئی.

ہلال — hilāl — "the new crescent moon".
شام کو آسمان پر ہلال نظر آیا۔ shām ko āsmān par hilāl nazar āyā. In the evening the new moon was seen in the sky.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ہلال (hilāl) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic hilāl, "the new moon": the thin first crescent that tells people the month has begun.

Lesson 4 of 5.
جگمگانا (jagmagānā) — to glitter; to be lit up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the new crescent moon, then the Urdu for henna.

You'll want to know: جگمگانا.
جگمگانا — jagmagānā — "to glitter; to be lit up".
بازار روشنی سے جگمگا رہا تھا۔ bāzār raushnī se jagmagā rahā thā. The market was glittering with lights.
Past (it …): وہ شہر جگمگایا, voh shahr jagmagāyā.

The word, taken apart.
جگمگانا (jagmagānā) is built on jagmag, "glitter", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5076). The doubled jag-mag is the twinkle of many lights.

Lesson 5 of 5.
رونق (raunaq) — liveliness; festive bustle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: رونق.
رونق — raunaq — "liveliness; festive bustle".
چاند رات کو بازار میں رونق تھی۔ chā̃d rāt ko bāzār meṅ raunaq thī. On the night of the new moon the market was full of life.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رونق (raunaq) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic rawnaq, "brightness, beauty"; in Urdu it is the life a crowd gives a place.
