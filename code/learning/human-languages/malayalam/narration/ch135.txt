5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ചന്ത (canta) — a market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a spoon, then the Malayalam for a plate.

You'll want to know: ചന്ത.
ചന്ത (canta) — "a market", where മീൻ and പച്ചക്കറി are sold.

The word, taken apart.
ചന്ത (canta) is of uncertain origin; neither DEDR nor CDIAL lists it.

Where the shopping happens.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ക്ഷേത്രം (kṣētraṁ) — a temple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a plate, then the Malayalam for a market.

You'll want to know: ക്ഷേത്രം.
ക്ഷേത്രം (kṣētraṁ) — "a temple".

The word, taken apart.
ക്ഷേത്രം (kṣētraṁ) from Sanskrit kṣētra, "land, field; a sacred place", taken over whole as a tatsama, with Malayalam's noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 3735). Its first sense is "land, a field"; the holy ground became the temple.

What you've built.
A place of worship.

Lesson 3 of 5.
പള്ളി (paḷḷi) — a church, a mosque.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a market, then the Malayalam for a temple.

You'll want to know: പള്ളി.
പള്ളി (paḷḷi) — both "a church" and "a mosque"; the word is older than the difference. It is also in പള്ളിക്കൂടം, "school".

The word, taken apart.
പള്ളി (paḷḷi) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil paḷḷi, Kannada paḷḷi and Telugu palli (DEDR 4018). Its first senses are "hamlet, a public building"; it came to name the house of worship of more than one faith. DEDR 4018 compares Sanskrit pallī.

What you've built.
One word, two buildings.

Lesson 5 of 5.

ഗ്രാമം (grāmaṁ) from Sanskrit grāma, "troop, village", taken over whole as a tatsama, with Malayalam's noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 4368).
