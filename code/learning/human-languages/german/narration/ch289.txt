German, chapter 289: Ein Sturz.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ausrutschen — to slip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to have an accident, then the German for the damage.

You'll want to know: ausrutschen.
ausrutschen — "to slip".
Sie rutschte auf dem nassen Boden aus. She slipped on the wet floor.
Separable, Perfekt with sein: Ich bin ausgerutscht.

The word, taken apart.
ausrutschen joins aus and rutschen "to slide", which is usually traced to Middle High German rutschen; EtymDB lists no parent.

stolpern may be related to Middle High German stolzen "to limp", but EtymDB lists no parent and the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
stürzen — to fall (heavily); to rush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to slip, then the German for to trip.

You'll want to know: stürzen.
stürzen — "to fall (heavily); to rush".
Der alte Mann stürzte auf der Treppe. The old man fell on the stairs.
Perfekt with sein: Er ist gestürzt. Also 'to rush': Sie stürzte aus dem Zimmer.

The word, taken apart.
stürzen is inherited from Old High German sturzen "to hurl, to plunge, to turn upside down" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Sturz — the fall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to trip, then the German for to fall.

You'll want to know: der Sturz.
der Sturz — "the fall".
Nach dem Sturz konnte sie nicht mehr gehen. After the fall she could not walk any more.
Plural: die Stürze. Also the fall of a government.

The word, taken apart.
Sturz is built on the root of stürzen "to plunge", Old High German sturzen (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
bluten — to bleed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to fall, then the German for the fall.

You'll want to know: bluten.
bluten — "to bleed".
Sein Knie blutete stark. His knee bled heavily.
Built on das Blut. Regular: blutete, hat geblutet.

The word, taken apart.
bluten is inherited from Middle High German bluoten (EtymDB), built on Blut "blood", Proto-Germanic blōþą.
