まちかど (machikado) — a street corner.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: まちかど.
まちかど — machikado — "a street corner".

The word, taken apart.
まちかど (machikado) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: machi, "town, street", and kado, "corner", both kun'yomi of its kanji (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
しない (shinai) — within the city.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a region, then the Japanese for a street corner.

You'll want to know: しない.
しない — shinai — "within the city".

The word, taken apart.
しない (shinai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "market, city" (Middle Chinese dzyiX) and nai, "inside" (nwojH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
はんたいがわ (hantaigawa) — the opposite side.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for within the city, then the Japanese for the suburbs.

You'll want to know: はんたいがわ.
はんたいがわ — hantaigawa — "the opposite side".

The word, taken apart.
はんたいがわ (hantaigawa) mixes the two layers: hantai, "opposite", borrowed from Chinese, han, "reverse, opposite" (Middle Chinese pjonX), and tai, "facing" (Middle Chinese twojH), with native kawa, "side", voiced to gawa (JMdict; Unihan, KANJIDIC2).
