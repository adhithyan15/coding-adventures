Marathi, chapter 159: Recently, Formerly, Already, the Past, To Happen.
8 lessons. All 8 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 8.
अलीकडे (alīkaḍe) — recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to obtain, then the Marathi for to save.

You'll want to know: अलीकडे.
अलीकडे — alīkaḍe — "recently". Marathi's past ends in -ले, -लो or -ली: अलीकडे मी एक पुस्तक वाचले. — alīkaḍe mī ek pustak vācle — "Recently I read a book."

The word, taken apart.
अलीकडे (alīkaḍe) is alī-, "this side", with -कडे, "toward". The origin of alī- is not settled; CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 4 of 8.
भूतकाळ (bhūtkāḷ) — the past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for formerly, then the Marathi for already.

You'll want to know: भूतकाळ.
भूतकाळ — bhūtkāḷ — "the past". It is भूत, "what has been", with काळ, "time". Grammar books use it for the past tense too.

The word, taken apart.
भूतकाळ (bhūtkāḷ) is a Sanskrit compound, a tatsama: bhūta, "become, been, past" (CDIAL 9552), and kāla, "time" (CDIAL 3084), with Marathi ळ.

घडणे — ghaḍṇe — "to happen". काय घडले? — kāy ghaḍle — "What happened?" Its past is घडले.

घडणे (ghaḍṇe) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghaṭate, "is busy with; happens" (CDIAL 4407).

Lesson 6 of 8.
मी गेलो (mī gelo) — the past, one small ending at a time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say घडणे (ghaḍṇe), "to happen", then मी, "I", and जाणे, "to go".

You'll want to know: who did it?
काल मी पुण्याला गेलो. kāl mī puṇyālā gelo. — "Yesterday I went to Pune."
The past shows who did it. A man says मी गेलो (mī gelo); a woman says मी गेले. With तो / ती, use तो गेला / ती गेली. With respect or several people, use तुम्ही गेलात. Hear the same gender endings you already know from माझा / माझी / माझं, now after -ल-.

You'll want to know: a few useful stems.
Most verbs put -ल- onto the stem: बसणे becomes बसलो, झोपणे becomes झोपलो, पडणे becomes पडला. Two everyday verbs change more: येणे becomes आलो / आली and होणे becomes झाला / झाली. Put नाही after the completed form for "did not": मी गेलो (mī gelo) नाही.

Lesson 7 of 8.
त्याने काम केलं (tyāne kām kelaṁ) — who did it, and what was done.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I went", then the -ने that can mean "by", then करणे.

You'll want to know: -ने on a past doer.
त्याने काम केलं. tyāne kām kelaṁ. — "He did the work."
With a past action done to something, the third-person doer takes -ने: तो becomes त्याने, ती becomes तिने, मीरा becomes मीराने. The first and second person stay मी and तू in ordinary Marathi: मी काम केलं.

You'll want to know: the verb follows the object.
The ending now follows what was acted on: त्याने आंबा खाल्ला, because आंबा is masculine; मी पोळी खाल्ली, because पोळी is feminine; त्याने पुस्तक वाचलं, because पुस्तक is neuter. So त्याने चित्रपट पाहिला has पाहिला following masculine चित्रपट.

Lesson 8 of 8.
होता / होती / होते (hotā / hotī / hote) — was, were, was not.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I went", "he did the work", then the present आहे.

You'll want to know: होता, होती, होते.
तो घरी होता. ती घरी होती. ते घरी होते. to gharī hotā. tī gharī hotī. te gharī hote. — "He was, she was, they were at home."
The past of आहे agrees like an adjective: masculine होता, feminine होती, neuter or respectful/plural होते. With a place, say गावात एक नदी होती, "There was a river in the village."

You'll want to know: नव्हता.
"Was not" joins the negative into the past form: तो शाळेत नव्हता, ती शाळेत नव्हती, दुकान मोठं नव्हतं. Keep the same agreement ending.
