Italian, chapter 131: Weekend, Date, Century, Often, Sometimes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il fine settimana — the weekend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for heavy, then the Italian for thin.

You'll want to know: il fine settimana.
il fine settimana — "the weekend".

The phrase, taken apart.
fine settimana is "the end of the week": fine, "end" from Latin finis, and settimana, "week" from Late Latin septimana, from septimus, "seventh". (EtymDB 2.1).

The word, taken apart.
data from Late Latin data, "given" — a word from the Latin of letters, which were headed data, "given (at)", before the place and the day. (EtymDB 2.1 records the Latin word.)

Lesson 5 of 5.
qualche volta — sometimes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for a century, then the Italian for often.

You'll want to know: qualche volta.
qualche volta — "sometimes".

The phrase, taken apart.
qualche volta is "some turn". EtymDB 2.1 records qualche, "some", as built on che, "that, which", from Latin quid or quod. volta, "a turn, a time", goes back to Latin volvere, "to roll, to turn"; EtymDB 2.1 has no row for it.
