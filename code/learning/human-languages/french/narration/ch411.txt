French, chapter 411: Un petit accident.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
percuter — to crash into, to hit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the rest area, then the French for to overtake.

You'll want to know: percuter.
percuter — "to crash into, to hit".
Une moto a percuté l'arrière du bus. A motorbike crashed into the back of the bus.
A hard, violent hit.

The word, taken apart.
percuter is a learned borrowing from Latin percutere 'to strike hard' (EtymDB), from per- and quatere 'to shake'.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: heurter.
heurter — "to bump into, to knock against; to offend".
En reculant, il a heurté un poteau. While reversing he bumped into a post.

The word, taken apart.
heurter comes from Old French hurter 'to ram', which is built on Frankish hūrt 'battering ram' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
constater — to note, to record (what happened).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pedestrian, then the French for the cyclist.

You'll want to know: constater.
constater — "to note, to record (what happened)".
Les deux conducteurs ont constaté les dégâts ensemble. The two drivers noted the damage together.
Le constat amiable is the form drivers fill in together after an accident.

The word, taken apart.
constater is built on Latin constat 'it is certain', from constāre 'to stand firm', from con- and stāre 'to stand' (EtymDB).
