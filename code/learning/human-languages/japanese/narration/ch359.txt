Japanese, chapter 359: Borrowing and Lending.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しゃっきん (shakkin) — a debt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pay into an account, then the Japanese for a bank balance.

You'll want to know: しゃっきん.
しゃっきん — shakkin — "a debt".
しゃっきんが あったら、はやく かえした ほうが いいです。 — shakkin ga attara, hayaku kaeshita hō ga ii desu — "If you have a debt, you had better pay it back soon."

The word, taken apart.
しゃっきん (shakkin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shaku, "borrow" (Middle Chinese tsjek) and kin, "gold" (kim) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as shak-kin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
たてかえる (tatekaeru) — to pay for someone (to be paid back).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a debt, then the Japanese for interest on money.

You'll want to know: たてかえる.
たてかえる — tatekaeru — "to pay for someone (to be paid back)".
さいふを わすれたら、ともだちが たてかえるでしょう。 — saifu o wasuretara, tomodachi ga tatekaeru deshō — "If you forget your purse, a friend will probably pay for you."
Polite たてかえます, tatekaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あねが りょうきんを たてかえました。, ane ga ryōkin o tatekaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
たてかえる (tatekaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from tate, "setting up (tateru)" and kaeru, "to exchange", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
