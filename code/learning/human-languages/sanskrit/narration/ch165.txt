Lesson 1 of 5.
धूलिः (dhūliḥ) — dust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a playground, then the Sanskrit for an airport.

You'll want to know: धूलिः.
धूलिः — dhūliḥ — "dust".

The word, taken apart.
dhūli "dust, powder, pollen" is inherited from Indo-European, with Latin fūlīgō "soot" as a cousin (EtymDB); MW queries a link with √dhū "shake". It lives on as Hindi dhūl and Marathi dhūḷ "dust" (CDIAL 6835).

ज्वाला — jvālā — "a flame".

The word, taken apart.
jvālā comes from the root √jval "blaze, glow, burn brightly" (MW): "a flame, a blaze". jvalati "burns" is the verb of the same root.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: अंगारः.
अंगारः — aṅgāraḥ — "a live coal".

The word, taken apart.
aṅgāra "charcoal, a live coal" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-European: MW sets it beside Lithuanian anglis and Russian ugol' "coal", and EtymDB agrees. It lives on as Hindi aṅgār, aṅgārā "glowing charcoal" (CDIAL 125).

Lesson 5 of 5.
लौहम् (lauham) — iron.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a flame, then the Sanskrit for a live coal.

You'll want to know: लौहम्.
लौहम् — lauham — "iron".

The word, taken apart.
lauha is built from loha "red; copper; iron" with the first vowel strengthened (CDIAL 11172a): "made of metal", and so "iron". MW connects loha with a lost root meaning "be red", and CDIAL 11158 does too; loha lives on as Hindi lohā "iron".
