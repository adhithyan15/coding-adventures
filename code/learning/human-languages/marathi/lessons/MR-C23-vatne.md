# वाटणे (vāṭṇe) — to feel, to seem

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-AVADNE, MR-LEX-MAHIT-JANNE, MR-LEX-BASNE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two verbs you own put you in the dative rather than the nominative:
**मला मराठी आवडते** and **मला माहीत आहे**. A third one joins them now, and it
is the one you answer *how are you* with.

## You'll want to know: वाटणे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-VATNE]; assesses=[] -->

> **वाटणे** — *vāṭṇe* — **to feel, to seem**

> **मला बरं वाटतं.** — *malā baraṁ vāṭtaṁ.* — **I feel fine.**

## Grammar Lens: the third verb that puts you in the dative
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-FEELING]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-LIKING, MR-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-KNOWLEDGE, MR-LEX-BARAM-01] -->

Word for word, **मला बरं वाटतं** is *to-me fine seems*. You are not the one
doing the feeling; the feeling arrives at you, and **मला** *malā* is where it
lands. Three sentences now stand on that one frame:

- **मला माहीत आहे** — to me it is known
- **मला मराठी आवडते** — to me Marathi is pleasing
- **मला बरं वाटतं** — to me fine seems

Marathi keeps saying that what happens inside a person happens *to* them. Once
that click lands, a whole family of verbs opens at once.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**व**, **ा**, **ट** — the retroflex *ṭa*, tongue curled back — then **णे**.
**वा-ट-णे**. In **वाटतं** the final **तं** carries the neuter ending that
**बरं** also wears.
