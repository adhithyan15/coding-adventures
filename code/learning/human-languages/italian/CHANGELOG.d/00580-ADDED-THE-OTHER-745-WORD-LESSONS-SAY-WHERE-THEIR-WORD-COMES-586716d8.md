## Added — the other 745 word lessons say where their word comes from

The 745 word lessons that still lacked etymology after the first batch (IT-C111-infermiera through IT-C259-storia, in sequence order) now carry both an `etymology_hook` and a visible `## The word, taken apart` block (`## The phrase, taken apart` for the six multi-word headwords). Every Italian word lesson now says where its word comes from (human-language-data: Italian word lessons without a hook 745 → 0, without visible etymology 745 → 0).

- **Sources.** The same two as the first batch, read locally and never vendored: EtymDB 2.1 (Fourrier & Sagot 2020, LREC; CC BY-SA 4.0), and Lewis & Short, *A Latin Dictionary* (Perseus Digital Library `lexica`, CC BY-SA 4.0) for certain textbook Latin forms that EtymDB lacks. Each line names only the source that supports it: "(EtymDB 2.1)", "(Lewis & Short)", "(EtymDB 2.1 records the Latin word.)" where EtymDB has the etymon but labels a learned word inherited, or no tag at all where neither source has a row.
- **What the 745 say.** 319 are inherited from Latin (*cuoco* ← *coquus*, *fegato* ← Late Latin *ficatum*, short for *iecur ficatum* "fig-fattened liver"). 126 are learned borrowings from Latin or, through Latin, Greek (*lezione* ← *lectio*, *politica* ← *politica* ← Greek *politike*). 63 are borrowed from other languages: French and Old French (*leggero*, *busta*, *villaggio*, *frontiera*, *marciapiede*, *avvisare*), Old Provençal (*guidare*, *sembrare*, *coraggio*, *geloso*, *carriera*, *bandiera*), Frankish (*guerra*, *guardare*, *rubare*), Lombardic (*guancia*, *balcone*, *schermo*), Arabic (*chitarra*, *cotone*, *tamburo*), Spanish (*casco*, *azienda*, *regalare*), English (*film*, *pigiama*), Norwegian (*sci*), German (*benzina*) and Greek (*magari*). 221 are built from parts, most from an Italian word the reader already has (*infermiera* from the old word for "sick", *scoprire* from *coprire*, *galleggiare* from *galla*, an oak gall that floats). Two are named after something (*lavagna*, after the Ligurian town of slate; *vulcano*, after Vulcan). 14 say plainly that the origin is uncertain and, where there is one, give the usual account: *farfalla*, *gufo*, *marrone*, *buio*, *traffico*, *rischiare*, *accorgersi*, *scivolare*, *chiacchierare*, *bruciare*, *tirare*, *sprecare*, *liscio* and *mattone*. None says "not recorded" for an origin that is textbook.
- **Origins EtymDB lacks.** 61 lines trace the word through the sister words EtymDB derives from the same etymon, and say so ("EtymDB 2.1 has no row for *gomito*; it derives Old French *coute* and the older Italian form *gombito* from *cubitum*"). 51 cite Lewis & Short (*gridare* ← *quiritare*, *pericolo* ← *periculum*, *insieme* ← *insimul*, *fulmine* ← *fulmen*, *fulminis*). Well-established origins that neither source has carry no tag (*quindi* ← *eccum inde*, *mentre* ← *dum interim*, *dunque* ← Late Latin *dunc*, *scappare* ← *excappare*, *regalare* ← Spanish *regalar*); seven say "usually traced to" because the account is standard but not certain (*mancia*, *pilota*, *bagaglio*, *fango*, *bugia*, *spegnere* ← *expingere*, and *ancora* ← *hanc horam*, where some prefer *hinc ad horam*).
- **Rows rejected.** 41 EtymDB rows were not used: homographs (*scuro* ← Lombardic *skūra*, the word for a shutter; *torre* ← *tollere*; *palazzo* ← *palatum* "palate"; *tuono* ← Greek *tonos* "a tone"; *ancora* ← *ancora* "anchor"; *orto* ← *ortus* "a rising"; *filo* ← *phylum*), wrong parents (*rimanere* ← Old Norse *feta*, *servire* ← Proto-Slavic, *asciutto* ← Greek *angeion*, *piccare* ← English *peak*, *ritorno* ← French *retour*, *spendere* ← German *spenden*, *allegro* ← French *allègre*, *frontiera* ← Old Provençal, and *schermo* ← *schermire*, which runs the wrong way), unconfirmable links (*scivolare* ← *exsibilare*), and junk suffix rows (*ricordo*, *obbedire*, and several verbs linked to English *surf*). 19 loans labelled inherited are given as borrowed (*guancia*, *fresco*, *guerra*, *noioso*, *geloso*, *cotone*, *racchetta*, *zero*…). 42 Latinisms labelled inherited are given as learned, with the sound change or spoken twin that shows it where there is one (*classe* kept *cl-*; *subito* kept its *b*; *necessario* kept *-ario*, where speech gave *-aio*, as in *operaio*; *esempio* beside spoken *scempio*, *monumento* beside Old Italian *monimento*).
- **No forward references.** A word taught later is described in English, not named: *disegno* is "the noun of the verb for 'to draw'", *firma* "the noun of the verb for 'to sign'".
- **Hooks.** Each `etymology_hook` (≤120 characters) says the same as its block; an English cognate is added only where it is certain.
- **`roots:`** 606 lessons gained a Latin etymon slug, reusing the corpus's existing spelling wherever one exists (`gratia`, `heri`, `tonare-latin`); the rest are new `<lemma>-latin` slugs. `check:root-slug-splits` reports no new split.
- **Durations.** No `max_seconds` changed. The new blocks put these lessons at 74–109 computed seconds against their declared 200.
