Lesson 1 of 8.
下个月 (xià ge yuè) — next month.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

[your turn — recall: say 明天会下雨吗 — míngtiān huì xiàyǔ ma, and answer it — R1, the chapter you have finished]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say tiānqì yùbào, the weather forecast — R1]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say yuè, a month — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 明年 — míngnián, next year — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 下个月.
下个月 — xià ge yuè — "next month".
下个月我们去 xià ge yuè wǒmen qù — We're going next month.
The next lesson puts the last character in your hand. It is the yuè you already say for a month.

The word, taken apart.
下个月 (xià ge yuè) is built from 下 'below; after', 个, the measure word, and 月 'month'. All three are inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX, kaH and ngjwot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun glosses haeX with, among other things, 'after' and 'the bottom', and quotes an old saying about the moon under ngjwot; 'next' in a row of months is assumed from 'after', not quoted.

What you've built.
下 here is the month after this one. Like 明天 and 后天, 下个月 (xià ge yuè) goes early in the sentence: before the verb, or before the person.
明年 says next year with 明; the month and the week take 下.

Script — the moon piece, standing on its own.
月 (yuè).
Nothing in it is new. You have written it four times already: on the right of 明 and of 期, under the top of 前, and on the left of 能. Now it stands alone, a little taller.
A left-falling stroke down the left side, curving out to the lower left.
The hooked frame: a horizontal across the top that turns down the right side and hooks to the left at its foot.
Two inner bars, the upper one first, each from the left side across to the right.
1 + 1 + 2 = four.

Lesson 3 of 8.
火车要开了 (huǒchē yào kāi le) — 要 for what is coming.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A word you know, before a verb this time.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 下个月, aloud — xià ge yuè]
[your turn — recall: say 我要水 — wǒ yào shuǐ, I want some water]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 火车 — huǒchē, a train]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 要 before a verb, for what is coming.
要 before a thing says what you want. 要 before a verb says what you are going to do, and with 了 at the end, what is about to happen.
我要水 wǒ yào shuǐ — I want some water. 下个月我要去中国 xià ge yuè wǒ yào qù Zhōngguó — Next month I'm going to China.
The test is the word after 要: a thing, and you want it; a verb, and you are going to do it.
火车要开了 huǒchē yào kāi le — The train is about to leave.
The 了 at the end is the 了 of a change, as in 我是大学生了: the moment is nearly here. For any second now, put kuài in front: huǒchē kuàiyào kāi le.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 我要水 — wǒ yào shuǐ — I want some water.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: huǒchē kuàiyào kāi le — the train is leaving any second]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 要 mean before a thing? (Want.) Before a verb? (Going to.) What does 了 add to 火车要开了 (huǒchē yào kāi le)? (It is about to happen.)

You'll want to know: 下星期.
下星期 — xià xīngqī — "next week".
下星期我们去看书 xià xīngqī wǒmen qù kàn shū — Next week we're going to go and read.
下星期我要开车去 xià xīngqī wǒ yào kāichē qù — Next week I'm going to drive there.

The word, taken apart.
下星期 (xià xīngqī) is built from 下 'after' and 星期 'week'. 星 and 期 are inherited from Middle Chinese, seng and gi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the stars' and 'a meeting; a limit'. The regular sound change from gi would give a second tone, qí; why the week says qī is not settled.

What you've built.
下星期 (xià xīngqī) takes no 个; 下个月 (xià ge yuè) does. Both go early in the sentence, and both pair with 要 before a verb:
下个月我要去中国 xià ge yuè wǒ yào qù Zhōngguó — Next month I'm going to China.

You'll want to know: 上个月.
上个月 — shàng ge yuè — "last month".
上个月我去了中国 shàng ge yuè wǒ qù le Zhōngguó — Last month I went to China.

The word, taken apart.
上个月 (shàng ge yuè) is built from 上 'above; before', 个 and 月. 上 is inherited from Middle Chinese dzyangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the Guangyun glosses this reading 'ruler', so 'above', and 'last' in a row of months, are assumed here, not quoted.

What you've built.
上 and 下 point through time as through a list read from the top: the month above is the one before, the month below the one after.
上个月 (shàng ge yuè) looks back, so it takes 了 when you say what happened: 上个月 (shàng ge yuè), 我, 去了, 中国.

Lesson 6 of 8.
dǎsuàn — to plan to; to intend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, then a verb this one is built on.
[your turn — recall: say 上个月 — shàng ge yuè, last month — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say suàn, to work out — R2, a few chapters back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: dǎsuàn.
dǎsuàn — "to plan to; to intend".
wǒ dǎsuàn xià ge yuè qù Běijīng — I'm planning to go to Beijing next month.
nǐ dǎsuàn zuò shénme — What are you planning to do?
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎsuàn is built from 打, the 打 of 打电话, and suàn 'to work out', the word you know. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and swanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to strike' and 'the number of things'. Neither dǎ nor suàn is the sound those readings would regularly give, and how striking and counting came to mean planning is not settled.

What you've built.
dǎsuàn comes before a verb, as 要 does: wǒ dǎsuàn qù, I plan to go.
要 says it is going to happen; dǎsuàn says it is your plan, and a plan can change.

You'll want to know: jiānglái.
jiānglái — "in the future; some day".
jiānglái wǒ xiǎng zuò lǎoshī — Some day I'd like to be a teacher.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiānglái is built from jiāng and 来 'to come'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjang and loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to send off; to go' and 'to arrive'. 'The time to come' is the pair's own sense, assumed here, not quoted.

What you've built.
jiānglái reaches further than 下个月 (xià ge yuè) or 明年: not a date, but the years ahead.
It goes early, before the person, as the other time words do.

下个月我要去中国 (xià ge yuè wǒ yào qù Zhōngguó) — two speakers trade their plans for next week, next month and the years ahead.

Warm-up.

The exchange.
A: 下星期你要做什么 xià xīngqī nǐ yào zuò shénme — What are you going to do next week? B: 下星期我要开车去大学 xià xīngqī wǒ yào kāichē qù dàxué — Next week I'm going to drive to the university. A: 下个月呢 xià ge yuè ne — And next month? B: 下个月我要去中国 xià ge yuè wǒ yào qù Zhōngguó — Next month I'm going to China. A: 上个月你也去了中国吗 shàng ge yuè nǐ yě qù le Zhōngguó ma — Did you go to China last month too? B: 没有 上个月我在家 méiyǒu, shàng ge yuè wǒ zài jiā — No, last month I was at home. A: jiānglái nǐ dǎsuàn zuò shénme — What do you plan to do in the future? B: wǒ xiǎng zuò lǎoshī — I'd like to be a teacher.
要 before each verb says what is coming; 上个月 (shàng ge yuè) takes 了 because it is over. méiyǒu answers the question about the past, as 没 reports what did not happen.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Where is B going next month?

What you've built — next week, next month, some day.
Time words go first; 要 or dǎsuàn goes before the verb:
下星期我要开车去 xià xīngqī wǒ yào kāichē qù — next week I'm going to drive.
下个月我要去中国 xià ge yuè wǒ yào qù Zhōngguó — next month I'm going to China.
jiānglái wǒ xiǎng zuò lǎoshī — some day I'd like to teach.
Listen. Xià ge yuè nǐ yào qù nǎr?
