# Review — if, and what it depends on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C289-SI-01, LA-LEX-C178-CAUSA178-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if you like, we'll go for a walk". (*Sī vīs, ambulāmus.*)
Then "unless it rains". (*Nisi pluit.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C289-SI-REAL-01, LA-LEX-C289-PENDET-EX-01, LA-LEX-C289-IN-POTESTATE-02, LA-ETYMON-C289-SI-02] -->

Three lessons, and the real condition is yours. *Sī* or *nisi* first, then
the result, and ordinary verbs on both sides: present, perfect, or a
command. And when the answer is neither yes nor no: *tōtum ex tempestāte
pendet*, or, handing it back, *in tuā potestāte est*.

> *— Crās iter Rōmam facimus?*
> *— Ex tempestāte pendet. Sī pluit, in vīllā manēmus; nisi pluit, iter
> facimus.*

[PAUSE 2s] *Crās*, "tomorrow", with a present verb: Latin speech often
lets the present stand for a plan.
