Spanish, chapter 525: Hay heridos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la herida — the wound, the injury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the alarm, then the Spanish for to evacuate.

You'll want to know: la herida.
la herida — "the wound, the injury".
Tiene una herida en la pierna. He has a wound on his leg.

The word, taken apart.
herida is built on the participle of herir 'to wound', which is inherited from Latin ferīre 'to strike' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
herir — to wound, to injure; to hurt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to evacuate, then the Spanish for the wound.

You'll want to know: herir.
herir — "to wound, to injure; to hurt".
El accidente hirió a dos personas. The accident injured two people.
Stem change: hiero, hirió. It also hurts feelings: Me hirió lo que dijo.

The word, taken apart.
herir is inherited from Latin ferīre 'to strike', through old Spanish ferir (EtymDB). Latin f- became Spanish h-.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la ambulancia — the ambulance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the wound, then the Spanish for to wound.

You'll want to know: la ambulancia.
la ambulancia — "the ambulance".
La ambulancia llegó en cinco minutos. The ambulance arrived in five minutes.

The word, taken apart.
ambulancia was borrowed from French ambulance, a word usually explained as first naming a field hospital that moved with an army. It is built on Latin ambulāre 'to walk' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
socorrer — to help, to come to the aid of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to wound, then the Spanish for the ambulance.

You'll want to know: socorrer.
socorrer — "to help, to come to the aid of".
Unos vecinos socorrieron a los heridos. Some neighbours came to the aid of the injured.
¡Socorro! — help! Socorrer a alguien — to help someone in danger.

The word, taken apart.
socorrer is inherited from Latin succurrere 'to run up, to run to help' (EtymDB), built on sub and currere 'to run' (Lewis & Short).
