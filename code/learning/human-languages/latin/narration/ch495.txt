Latin, chapter 495: Wonder and amazement.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
admīrātiō, admīrātiōnis — admiration, wonder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for disgust, then the Latin for it wearies.

You'll want to know: admīrātiō, admīrātiōnis.
admīrātiō, admīrātiōnis — "admiration, wonder".
Hospitēs templum novum cum admīrātiōne spectant. The guests gaze at the new temple in wonder.
Feminine, third declension, from admīror, already known. English admiration.

The word, taken apart.
admīrātiō is the action noun of admīror "to wonder at", built on ad- and mīror "to wonder" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English admiration comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
mīror, mīrārī — to wonder, marvel at.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for it wearies, then the Latin for admiration.

You'll want to know: mīror, mīrārī.
mīror, mīrārī — "to wonder, marvel at".
Puer elephantum mīrātur. The boy marvels at the elephant.
Deponent: mīror, mīrārī, mīrātus sum. With the accusative, or with quod — I am surprised that. English miracle belongs to the same family.

The word, taken apart.
mīror is built on mīrus "wonderful", which EtymDB traces through Proto-Italic to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to smile". English admire and miracle belong to the same family.

Lesson 3 of 5.
stupeō, stupēre — to be stunned, be amazed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for admiration, then the Latin for to wonder.

You'll want to know: stupeō, stupēre.
stupeō, stupēre — "to be stunned, be amazed".
Agricola in urbe magnā stupet. The farmer is stunned in the great city.
stupeō, stupēre, stupuī. Struck dumb with wonder or fear. English stupor comes from it, and stupid from its adjective stupidus.

The word, taken apart.
stupeō is inherited and usually traced to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to strike, to stun" (EtymDB). English stupid comes from its adjective stupidus.

Lesson 4 of 5.
attonitus, attonita, attonitum — thunderstruck, astonished.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to wonder, then the Latin for to be stunned.

You'll want to know: attonitus, attonita, attonitum.
attonitus, attonita, attonitum — "thunderstruck, astonished".
Attonitī nūntium audīmus. We hear the news, thunderstruck.
The past participle of attonō — to strike with thunder.

The word, taken apart.
attonitus is the past participle of attonō "to strike with thunder", built on ad- and tonō "to thunder" (Lewis & Short). English astonish comes from a related compound of tonō.
