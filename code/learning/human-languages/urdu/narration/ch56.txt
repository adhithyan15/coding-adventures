Urdu, chapter 56: Chilli, Butter, Cheese, Honey, Grapes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مرچ (mirch) — chilli, pepper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for sugar, then the Urdu for salt.

You'll want to know: مرچ.
مرچ — mirch — "chilli, pepper".

The word, taken apart.
مرچ (mirch) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from maricca, "peppercorn", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9875). It is a variant CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit marīca, "peppercorn", which it takes from an Austroasiatic language. When the chilli came to India, it took the name of black pepper.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مکھن (makkhan) — butter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for salt, then the Urdu for chilli.

You'll want to know: مکھن.
مکھن — makkhan — "butter".

The word, taken apart.
مکھن (makkhan) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mrakṣaṇa, "rubbing in; ointment, oil" (CDIAL 10378). Butter was first "the thing rubbed in".

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for butter, then the Urdu for cheese.

You'll want to know: شہد.
شہد — shahd — "honey".

The word, taken apart.
شہد (shahd) is a loan from Arabic shahd, "honey", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is sh-h-d (Lane).
