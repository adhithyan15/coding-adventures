## You'll want to know: gèng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C153-GENG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*gèng* — "even more; still more".

> *tā gèng gāo* — He's even taller.

> *tā bǐ wǒ gèng gāo* — He's even taller than me.

Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*gèng* is inherited from Middle Chinese *kaengH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to change; to alter'. 'Even more' is a later sense, assumed here, not quoted.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-BI-COMPARE-01] -->

*gèng* goes straight before the adjective: *tā gèng gāo*.

With 比 it sharpens the comparison: both are tall, and he is taller still.
