Hindi, chapter 552: Even So.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
जबकि (jabki) — whereas; while.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for stubborn, then the Hindi for force, coercion.

You'll want to know: जबकि.
जबकि — jabki — "whereas; while".
मुझे चाय पसंद है, जबकि मीरा को दूध पसंद है। Mujhe cāy pasand hai, jabki Mīrā ko dūdh pasand hai. I like tea, whereas Mira likes milk.

The word, taken apart.
जबकि (jabki) joins jab, "when", and ki, "that", which is usually traced to Persian ki.

Lesson 2 of 6.
भले ही (bhale hī) — even though; even if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for force, coercion, then the Hindi for whereas.

You'll want to know: भले ही.
भले ही — bhale hī — "even though; even if".
भले ही (bhale hī) रास्ता लंबा है, फिर भी हम चलकर जाएँगे। Bhale hī rāstā lambā hai, phir bhī ham calkar jāeṅge. Even though the way is long, we will go on foot.

The word, taken apart.
भले ही (bhale hī) is bhale, a form of bhalā, "good", which CDIAL derives from Sanskrit bhalla, "auspicious" (CDIAL 9408), with the emphatic hī: "well and good, even so".

Lesson 5 of 6.
मगर (magar) — but (in a lighter register); yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for anyway, then the Hindi for well.

You'll want to know: मगर.
मगर — magar — "but (in a lighter register); yet".
मैं बहुत थका था, मगर मैंने खाना बनाया। Maiṁ bahut thakā thā, magar maiṁne khānā banāyā. I was very tired, but I cooked the meal.
Means the same as लेकिन; common in speech and in songs.

The word, taken apart.
मगर (magar) is usually traced to Persian magar, "but, unless".
