German, chapter 312: Das Volksfest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Volksfest — the public festival, the folk festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the giving of Christmas presents, then the German for the gingerbread.

You'll want to know: das Volksfest.
das Volksfest — "the public festival, the folk festival".
Im Herbst gibt es in unserer Stadt ein großes Volksfest. In autumn there is a big public festival in our town.
Plural: die Volksfeste.

The word, taken apart.
Volksfest joins Volk "people", Old High German folc (EtymDB), and Fest "festival", from Latin festum "feast".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Tracht — the traditional dress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the funfair, then the German for the merry-go-round.

You'll want to know: die Tracht.
die Tracht — "the traditional dress".
Viele Leute trugen Tracht. Many people wore traditional dress.
Plural: die Trachten. In the south and in Austria, the Dirndl and the Lederhose.

The word, taken apart.
Tracht is inherited from Old High German draht, built on the root of tragen "to carry, to wear", Proto-Germanic draganą (EtymDB): what one wears.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Kapelle — the band (of musicians); the chapel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the merry-go-round, then the German for the traditional dress.

You'll want to know: die Kapelle.
die Kapelle — "the band (of musicians); the chapel".
Eine Kapelle spielte alte Lieder. A band played old songs.
Plural: die Kapellen. Also a small church, a chapel.

The word, taken apart.
Kapelle comes from Middle High German kappelle (EtymDB), from Medieval Latin capella "a little cloak", then, as the story is usually told, the chapel where a famous cloak was kept, then its musicians.
