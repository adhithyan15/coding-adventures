Arabic, chapter 497: Nerves before a big day.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
متوتر (mutawattir) — tense, nervous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for at ease, then the Arabic for to feel relieved.

You'll want to know: متوتر.
متوتر — mutawattir — "tense, nervous".
كانت أختي متوترة قبل المقابلة. kānat ukhtī mutawattira qabla al-muqābala. My sister was nervous before the interview.
The noun توتر, stress, shares the root.

The word, taken apart.
متوتر (mutawattir) is built on the native Arabic root w-t-r, "a bowstring" (Lane). Mutawattir is the participle of Form V: drawn tight, like a bowstring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to feel relieved, then the Arabic for tense.

You'll want to know: ارتبك.
ارتبك — irtabaka — "to get flustered".
ارتبك الطالب ونسي الجواب. irtabaka aṭ-ṭālib wa-nasiya al-jawāb. The student got flustered and forgot the answer.
Present: يرتبك, yartabik.

The word, taken apart.
ارتبك (irtabaka) is built on the native Arabic root r-b-k, "to be mixed up, entangled" (Lane). Form VIII irtabaka is to become tangled: to lose one's thread.

مضطرب — muḍṭarib — "agitated, unsettled".
كان البحر مضطربا في الليل. kāna al-baḥr muḍṭariban fī al-layl. The sea was rough in the night.
It is said of a person, the sea or the weather.

The word, taken apart.
مضطرب (muḍṭarib) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-r-b, "to strike, to beat" (Lane). Muḍṭarib is the participle of Form VIII, where the t turns into ṭ: one beaten about, restless.

Lesson 5 of 5.
كتم (katama) — to hold back (a feeling).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for agitated, then the Arabic for hesitant.

You'll want to know: كتم.
كتم — katama — "to hold back (a feeling)".
كتمت البنت ضحكتها في الصف. katamat al-bint ḍaḥkatahā fī aṣ-ṣaff. The girl held back her laugh in class.
Present: يكتم, yaktum.
It also means to keep a secret: كتم (katama) السر.

The word, taken apart.
كتم (katama) is built on the native Arabic root k-t-m, "to conceal, to keep secret" (Lane). Lane gives yaktumu as its present.
