# ಏ — the end of the first question you learned to ask

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-156] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ಎ — what sound, and how many lifts?

[PAUSE 2s] Ask somebody their name in Kannada. The last word of that question
opens on today's character.

## Script you'll notice: ಏ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-157]; assesses=[] -->

**ಏ** — *ē*.

It is an **independent vowel** — the shape *ē* takes when a word begins with it, rather than the sign it becomes inside one.

You already say these:

- **ಏನು** *ēnu* — what?
- **ಏಳು** *ēḷu* — seven
- **ಏಕೆಂದರೆ** *ēkendare* — because

**ಏನು closes the first question this book taught you** — ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು?, back
in chapter two. Until now you could ask it out loud without being able to write
its last piece.

Set it beside the one from the page before:

| short | long |
|---|---|
| ಎ *e* | ಏ *ē* |

The long one carries **a small loop above** the body the short one ends with.
Your pen builds the same lower body, then adds that loop separately.

## Writing: ಏ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-157] -->

- **1.** turn clockwise around the compact left loop
- **2.** without lifting, sweep through the joined lower curves and climb the right side
- **3.** without lifting, carry the tall outer arch over and finish at the upper left
- **4.** lift, then draw the small upper loop from left to right

**Pen lifts: 1.** Write the joined lower body and tall arch in one run, then lift
once for the small upper loop.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna
