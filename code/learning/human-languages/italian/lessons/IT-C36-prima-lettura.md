# (reading) — the first passage

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-SKILL-READ-LINES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, then six lines. Now twelve of them together.

[PAUSE 1s] Forty-seven words, and not one of them is new.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IL-MIO-NOME-02, IT-LEX-OGGI-IERI-02] -->

> Ciao!
> Il mio nome è Marco.
> Questo è il mio amico.
> Lui è molto buono.
> Oggi ho un caffè.
> Ho anche il pane.
> Leggo un libro, ma non capisco.
> Non so perché.
> Il libro è abbastanza buono.
> Qui c'è l'acqua.
> Domani leggo il libro.
> Grazie e arrivederci!

[PAUSE 3s] Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the
sentences arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Now read it again, and look at the word **perché**.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-MA-02, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02, IT-LEX-MOLTO-POCO-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Perché** is both *why* and *because*. Italian does not give you
two words for those, so a reader decides which one from where it sits.

[PAUSE 2s] Here it sits after **Non so**, so it is *why*. Had it opened a
clause after a full sentence, it would have been *because*. You have done a
piece of reading with no equivalent in English, and nothing on the page marked
it.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice also **il mio nome**. Italian keeps the article in front of
the possessive, which Spanish does not, and it is the kind of small difference
that only becomes visible when you read rather than translate.

[PAUSE 2s] This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at
once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.
