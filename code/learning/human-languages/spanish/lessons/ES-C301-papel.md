## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C301-ASK-05]; assesses=[] -->

**el papel** — "the paper."

It is usually traced, after Corominas and the DLE, through Catalan **paper** to
Latin **papyrus**, the Egyptian reed whose pith was beaten flat and written on
(EtymDB 2.1 agrees). English **paper** is the same reed, and the
**papyrus** in a museum case is the same word, unworn.

Put that beside *el libro*, named after the inner bark of a tree. Two writing
surfaces, both named after a plant.

*Papel* also means the **part** an actor plays, because the part was handed
over written on a sheet. *Hacer el papel de* — to play the part of. English
keeps the same idea in **role**, which was a little roll of paper.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Five: *la cuerda*, *la aguja*, *el hilo*, *la cesta*, *el papel*.

Add *por favor* to any of them and you have a polite request, the shape you
have used since the soap and the towel.

And every one of the five is named after a material rather than a use: a gut, a
sharp, a thread, a box, a reed. The farm gave you the opposite rule; both are true.
