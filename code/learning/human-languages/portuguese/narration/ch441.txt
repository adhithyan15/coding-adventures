Portuguese, chapter 441: Na praça.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o campanário — the bell tower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the municipality, then the Portuguese for the entry phone.

You'll want to know: o campanário.
o campanário — "the bell tower".
Do campanário vê-se toda a cidade. From the bell tower you can see all of the town.
Built on Latin campāna, a bell.

The word, taken apart.
campanário is a learned formation on Late Latin campāna 'bell' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
a catedral — the cathedral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the entry phone, then the Portuguese for the bell tower.

You'll want to know: a catedral.
a catedral — "the cathedral".
A catedral é o edifício mais antigo da praça. The cathedral is the oldest building on the square.
In Portugal a cathedral is often called a Sé.

The word, taken apart.
catedral is a learned borrowing from Late Latin cathedrālis 'of the bishop's seat', built on cathedra 'chair', from Greek kathédra 'seat' (Lewis & Short has cathedra).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o chafariz — the public fountain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the cathedral, then the Portuguese for the arcades.

You'll want to know: o chafariz.
o chafariz — "the public fountain".
Antigamente, as pessoas iam buscar água ao chafariz. In the old days, people went to fetch water from the fountain.
A stone fountain where people once filled their jugs.

The word, taken apart.
chafariz comes from Arabic ṣihrīj 'cistern, water tank' (EtymDB), like Catalan safareig.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o pelourinho — the pillory (stone pillar).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the arcades, then the Portuguese for the public fountain.

You'll want to know: o pelourinho.
o pelourinho — "the pillory (stone pillar)".
No centro da praça há um pelourinho de pedra. In the middle of the square there is a stone pillory.
A carved stone pillar that showed a town had its own courts.

The word, taken apart.
pelourinho comes from French pilori 'pillory' (EtymDB), reshaped with the small-size ending -inho; the French word's origin is not settled.
