## HL-C186 — दीजिए moves into the Hindi tea-request headword

After **का**, the next high-occurrence learner-facing Hindi candidate was
**दीजिए**. The script-fragment rows above it were already dispositioned in
HL-C180, while **ज़** is the consonant-plus-nuqta chunk inside several declared
words rather than a standalone lexical target.

Chapter 37 does explicitly teach **दीजिए**: it defines the form as polite “give”
for **आप**, contrasts spoken **दीजिए** with written **कृपया**, practises **एक
चाय दीजिए** and the same frame with water, and assesses where the politeness
lives. The old `word` metadata named only **चाय**, concealing the lesson's main
communicative payoff.

The lesson is now a `phrase` with headword **एक चाय दीजिए**. That keeps **चाय**
declared while exposing the assessed request form to reports and downstream
cards. Rerun the ranked report next; after explicitly dispositioned fragments,
the weather lesson's everyday **बारिश** is the clearest remaining lexical
candidate for promotion review.
