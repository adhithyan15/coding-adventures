Sanskrit, chapter 439: Winds and Storms.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पवनः (pavanaḥ) — a breeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sweat, then the Sanskrit for blazes.

You'll want to know: पवनः.
पवनः — pavanaḥ — "a breeze".
सायं शीतलः पवनः वहति। sāyaṃ śītalaḥ pavanaḥ vahati. In the evening a cool breeze blows.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पवनः (pavanaḥ) comes from the root √pū, "to purify": MW gives pavana, "the purifier", as wind, breeze, air; it lives on as Punjabi pauṇ, "wind" (MW; CDIAL 7978).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: प्रभञ्जनः.
प्रभञ्जनः — prabhañjanaḥ — "a tempest".
रात्रौ प्रभञ्जनेन वृक्षाः पतन्ति। rātrau prabhañjanena vṛkṣāḥ patanti. At night trees fall in the tempest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रभञ्जनः (prabhañjanaḥ) is built from pra and the root √bhañj, "to break": the breaker; MW gives prabhañjana as wind, storm, tempest, hurricane (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
आन्दोलयति (āndolayati) — sets swaying; swings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a tempest, then the Sanskrit for violent.

You'll want to know: आन्दोलयति.
आन्दोलयति — āndolayati — "sets swaying; swings".
पवनः शाखाम् आन्दोलयति। pavanaḥ śākhām āndolayati. The breeze sets the branch swaying.
Past (it …): पवनः शाखाम् आन्दोलितवान्, pavanaḥ śākhām āndolitavān.

The word, taken apart.
आन्दोलयति (āndolayati) is built on āndola, "swinging, a swing", for which MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain; MW gives āndolayati as to swing or agitate, with the participle āndolita, "shaken, swung" (MW).

Warm-up.

मन्दानिलः — mandānilaḥ — "a gentle breeze".
सायं नदीतीरे मन्दानिलः वहति। sāyaṃ nadītīre mandānilaḥ vahati. In the evening a gentle breeze blows on the riverbank.

The word, taken apart.
मन्दानिलः (mandānilaḥ) is built from manda, "slow, gentle", and anila, "wind, air"; MW gives the compound as a gentle breeze, a zephyr (MW).
