Marathi, chapter 452: A Sudden Fright.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
धक्का (dhakkā) — a shock; a push.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for displeased, then the Marathi for to get fed up.

You'll want to know: धक्का.
धक्का — dhakkā — "a shock; a push".
ती बातमी ऐकून आम्हाला मोठा धक्का बसला. tī bātmī aikūn āmhālā moṭhā dhakkā baslā. Hearing that news gave us a big shock.
Masculine.
धक्का (dhakkā) बसणे, to get a shock, with the dative; धक्का (dhakkā) देणे, to push.

The word, taken apart.
धक्का (dhakkā) is inherited from the expressive root dhakk, "push, strike" (CDIAL 6701), under which CDIAL lists Marathi dhakā, "push".

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 5.
बिथरणे (bitharṇe) — to panic; to run wild.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a shiver, then the Marathi for to take fright.

You'll want to know: बिथरणे.
बिथरणे — bitharṇe — "to panic; to run wild".
फटाक्याचा आवाज ऐकून घोडा बिथरला. phaṭākyācā āvāj aikūn ghoḍā bitharlā. The horse panicked at the sound of the firecracker.
Past (he …): तो बिथरला, to bitharlā.
Of animals and people who lose their heads in fright: घोडा बिथरला, the horse bolted.

The word, taken apart.
बिथरणे (bitharṇe) is inherited by CDIAL's account: it lists Marathi bitharṇẽ, "to misconduct oneself", beside itharṇẽ, "to run wild", under Sanskrit itvara, "walking; mean, cruel" (CDIAL 1566). How the b- arose is not explained there, so the link is not certain.
