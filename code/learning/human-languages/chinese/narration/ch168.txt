Mandarin Chinese, chapter 168: Steamed or Fried.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zhēng — to steam (food).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a power cut, then the Mandarin for a sound.

You'll want to know: zhēng.
zhēng — "to steam (food)".
māma zài zhēng bāozi — Mum is steaming buns.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhēng is inherited from Middle Chinese tsying (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Under this character the dictionary gives 'fine firewood'; written another way, the same syllable is glossed 'the breath of a fire rising', which is the steaming.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhá — to deep-fry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a sound, then the Mandarin for to steam.

You'll want to know: zhá.
zhá — "to deep-fry".
jīntiān wǎnshang wǒmen zhá yú — We're deep-frying fish tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhá is probably the word the Guangyun writes another way as dzreap, glossed 'to scald in hot water' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which would regularly give zhá; but no dictionary we use joins that word to this character, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dùn — to stew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to steam, then the Mandarin for to deep-fry.

You'll want to know: dùn.
dùn — "to stew".
tā zài dùn ròu — He's stewing some meat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dùn has no older form of this word in any dictionary we use. The Guangyun lists the character as dwon, glossed 'a blazing fire' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), but neither that sense nor that sound gives today's word, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
shāoshuǐ — to boil water.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to deep-fry, then the Mandarin for to stew.

You'll want to know: shāoshuǐ.
shāoshuǐ — "to boil water".
wǒ qù shāoshuǐ nǐmen yào hē chá ma — I'll put the kettle on: who wants tea?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shāoshuǐ is built from shāo 'to burn' and 水 'water', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syew and sywijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'fire' and 'to set alight'.
