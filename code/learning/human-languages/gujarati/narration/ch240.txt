Gujarati, chapter 240: In the garden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
છોડ (choḍ) — plant, shrub.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to sting, then the Gujarati for to slip away.

You'll want to know: છોડ.
છોડ — choḍ — "plant, shrub".
મેં છોડને પાણી આપ્યું. — mẽ choḍne pāṇī āpyũ — "I gave the plant water."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of છોડ (choḍ), "plant", is not settled: CDIAL lists no Gujarati form like it among its words for plants.

Lesson 2 of 5.
બીજ (bīj) — seed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to slip away, then the Gujarati for plant.

You'll want to know: બીજ.
બીજ — bīj — "seed".
મેં ખેતરમાં બીજ વાવ્યાં. — mẽ khetarmā̃ bīj vāvyā̃ — "I sowed seeds in the field."
Not બીજું, second.

The word, taken apart.
બીજ (bīj) is Sanskrit bīja, "seed", taken over almost whole, a tatsama. The inherited forms of the same word are Gujarati bī and bīyũ, "seed" (CDIAL 9250).

Lesson 3 of 5.
રોપવું (ropvũ) — to plant, to set out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for plant, then the Gujarati for seed.

You'll want to know: રોપવું.
રોપવું — ropvũ — "to plant, to set out".
તેણે બગીચામાં છોડ રોપ્યો. — teṇe bagīcāmā̃ choḍ ropyo — "She planted a plant in the garden."
વાવવું is to sow seed; રોપવું (ropvũ) is to put a young plant in the ground.

The word, taken apart.
રોપવું (ropvũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to plant", under ropyate, "is planted", the passive of Sanskrit ropayati, "plants", in its entry for rupati, "pierces, makes a hole, plants" (CDIAL 10783).

Lesson 4 of 5.
સીંચવું (sī̃cvũ) — to irrigate, to water.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for seed, then the Gujarati for to plant.

You'll want to know: સીંચવું.
સીંચવું — sī̃cvũ — "to irrigate, to water".
મેં સાંજે છોડ સીંચ્યો. — mẽ sā̃je choḍ sī̃cyo — "I watered the plant in the evening."

The word, taken apart.
સીંચવું (sī̃cvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit siñcati, "pours out, sprinkles", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to irrigate" (CDIAL 13394).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ગુલાબ (gulāb) — rose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to plant, then the Gujarati for to irrigate.

You'll want to know: ગુલાબ.
ગુલાબ — gulāb — "rose".
મેં મારી માતાને ગુલાબ આપ્યું. — mẽ mārī mātāne gulāb āpyũ — "I gave my mother a rose."

The word, taken apart.
ગુલાબ (gulāb) is a loan, usually traced to Persian gulāb, "rose water", built from gul, "rose", and āb, "water".
