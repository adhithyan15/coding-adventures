Russian, chapter 486: A Fever and a Sore Throat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
жар (zhar) — a fever, a high temperature; heat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a faint, then the Russian for to have the shivers.

You'll want to know: жар.
жар (zhar) — "a fever, a high temperature; heat".
У ребёнка жар, надо вызвать врача. U rebyónka zhar, nádo výzvat vrachá. The child has a fever; we need to call the doctor.
Masculine. У него жар (zhar) — he has a high temperature.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB lists жар (zhar) without a source; it is usually traced to Proto-Slavic žarъ 'heat', related to гореть 'to burn' and жарить 'to fry'.

Он громко сморкается в платок. On grómko smorkáyetsya v platók. He blows his nose loudly into a handkerchief.
Imperfective; perfective высморкаться.

The word, taken apart.
сморкаться (smorkátsya) is the reflexive of сморкать 'to blow one's nose' (EtymDB), the same root as насморк 'a runny nose'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
охрипнуть (okhrípnut) — to go hoarse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for chills, then the Russian for to blow one's nose.

You'll want to know: охрипнуть.
охрипнуть (okhrípnut) — "to go hoarse".
После концерта певец охрип. Pósle kontsérta pevéts okhríp. After the concert the singer went hoarse.
Perfective; past охрип, охрипла. Imperfective хрипнуть.

The word, taken apart.
охрипнуть (okhrípnut) is built on о- and хрипнуть, from хрип 'a wheeze'; EtymDB lists хрип and хрипнуть without a source, and the root is usually taken as imitative.
