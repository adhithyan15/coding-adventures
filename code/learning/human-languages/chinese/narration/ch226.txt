Mandarin Chinese, chapter 226: Grass and Lakes.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
草地 (cǎodì) — lawn; grassy ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pick fruit, then the Mandarin for to camp out.

You'll want to know: 草地.
草地 — cǎodì — "lawn; grassy ground".
gōngyuán lǐ yǒu yí kuài dà cǎodì — There's a big lawn in the park.
The new character, in pieces: 草 (cǎo) — nine strokes: the grass piece of 菜 on top, then 早 under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
草地 (cǎodì) is built from 草 (cǎo) 'grass' and 地 'ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshawX and dijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'all the plants'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
草 (cǎo) — the grass piece over 早.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the grass piece over 早 and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say cǎodì — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
早 花.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — zǎo, huā]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the grass piece over 早.
草 (cǎo).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The grass piece, three strokes, as in 菜 and 花: a bar across, then two short verticals.
Under it, 早, six strokes, as in 早上: 日, four strokes, then a long bar across and a vertical down through its middle.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 草 (cǎo), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 草 (cǎo)? (Nine.) What sits under the grass piece? (早.) What comes last? (The long vertical through the middle.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 草 (cǎo), pinned stroke paths and medians.

sēnlín — "forest".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sēnlín is built from sēn 'dense with trees' and lín 'woods'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese lim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'trees'; the dictionary describes the first, srim there, as how tall trees look.

The word, taken apart.
fēngjǐng is built from 风 'wind' and jǐng 'a view', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjuwng and kjaengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'bright' and 'light'; the sense 'a view' is not given there.
