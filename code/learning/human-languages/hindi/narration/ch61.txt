Hindi, chapter 61: At the Cooking Fire.
6 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
बर्तन (bartan) — a cooking vessel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new run of words: what did mor mean, and what did bhaĩs mean?

You'll want to know: बर्तन.
बर्तन (bartan) — "a cooking vessel".
बर्तन (bartan) is the word that goes with बरतना (baratnā), 'to handle, to make use of', and behind both stands Sanskrit वृत् (vṛt-), 'to turn, to carry on'. A बर्तन (bartan) is, quite literally, a thing in use.
That is why one word covers the whole shelf. A pot, a pan, a tray, a lid — each has its own name, and all of them together are बर्तन (bartan). Ask politely for बर्तन (bartan) and you have asked for whatever the cooking needs.

What you've built.
The first of five things at the fire.

Lesson 2 of 6.
ज़ फ़ ख़ क़ (za fa xa qa) — the same dot, a different job.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ज़ फ़ ख़ क़, Script: words you already say and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
The mark from before: where does it sit, and what does it change?
[pause 2 seconds]
On ड and ढ it made a flap. On these four it does something else — it lets the letter carry a sound that came into Hindi from outside it.

Script you'll notice: ज़ फ़ ख़ क़.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
letter: ज. with the dot: ज़. sound: za.
letter: फ. with the dot: फ़. sound: fa.
letter: ख. with the dot: ख़. sound: xa.
letter: क. with the dot: क़. sound: qa.
ज़ is the z of English zoo. फ़ is the f of far. ख़ is made further back than ख, at the soft palate. क़ is made further back than क, at the very top of the throat.
Many speakers say the plain letter for all four and are understood. The dot is still printed, so a reader has to know it.

Script: words you already say.
ज़रूर — certainly, मेज़ — table, दरवाज़ा — door.
सफ़ेद — white, सफ़र — a journey, तोहफ़ा — a gift.
ख़ुशी — happiness, ख़ुशबू — fragrance.
मुलाक़ात — a meeting.
One pair you have met already. खाना and ख़ाना were taught together, and the dot between them was named there as a fact about those two words. It is the same dot, and it is doing the same job in every word on this page. That lesson also told you the truth about it: the dot is often left off in print, and a reader sorts the two out from context.

Writing: the dotted four — copy what you see.
The letter is drawn exactly as you draw it bare. One dot goes below, clear of the line.
Put your pen on ज़ and follow it, then फ़. Copy slowly and larger than it is printed.
This book does not tell you where to start the character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source.

Lesson 3 of 6.
चम्मच (chammach) — a spoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did bartan mean?

You'll want to know: चम्मच.
चम्मच (chammach) — "a spoon".
Sanskrit had चमस (camasa), a wooden ladle lifted at sacrifices, and चम्मच (chammach) sits close enough to it that the two are often printed together. The road between them is not clean, though, so this book will show you the pair and stop there.
What is certain is the shape of the modern word: a doubled m in the middle, and the stress landing forward. Say it twice and you will hear that it is built to be said quickly, the way the names of small everyday objects usually are.

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 6.
गिलास (gilās) — a tumbler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did chammach mean?

You'll want to know: गिलास.
गिलास (gilās) — "a tumbler".
This one is English glass, taken whole. It arrived with the object, the way कैमरा arrived with the camera, and Hindi gave it a vowel in the middle so that a Hindi mouth could get through the gl- at the front.
Watch what happened to the meaning on the way in. A Hindi गिलास (gilās) is a drinking tumbler and is very often made of steel, so the English word for the material now names an object in a language where the object is usually not made of it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
तवा (tavā) — a flat iron griddle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did gilās mean?

You'll want to know: तवा.
तवा (tavā) — "a flat iron griddle".
Usually traced to a Sanskrit word on the root तप् (tap-), 'to heat, to burn' — the heater. The same root is inside तपस्, the heat of hard practice, so the kitchen and the hermitage turn out to share a syllable.
A तवा (tavā) is a flat iron disc over the fire, and it is what रोटी is made on. No तवा (tavā), no रोटी: the bread you met early on is defined by the surface it is cooked on as much as by the flour.

Lesson 6 of 6.
चूल्हा (chūlhā) — a cooking fire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the 4 words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: चूल्हा.
चूल्हा (chūlhā) — "a cooking fire".
From Sanskrit चुल्ली (cullī), 'fireplace, hearth'. The oldest चूल्हा (chūlhā) is a horseshoe of packed clay on the floor, fed with wood at the front, with the तवा (tavā) sitting on top of it.
It also counts households. To say two brothers have separate चूल्हे is to say they no longer eat as one family — the fire, not the roof, is what marks where one household ends. A gas ring in a city flat inherits the word and the meaning both.

What you've built.
Five: बर्तन (bartan), चम्मच (chammach), गिलास (gilās), तवा (tavā), चूल्हा (chūlhā). Enough to ask for what the cooking needs.
