# A connected Kannada message about your familiar day

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C64-JOIN-01, KA-LEX-C67-LINK-03, KA-GRAMMAR-C67-LINK-04, KA-LEX-C70-TELL-04, KA-GRAMMAR-C70-TELL-05, KA-ETYMON-C26-BELAGGE-01, KA-ETYMON-C27-SANJE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A connected message is not a pile of vocabulary and not one long
sentence. It is a short route for one reader. Before writing, draw eight empty
boxes and give them these jobs:

1. reader and greeting;
2. reason for writing;
3. where or when your day begins;
4. two familiar actions;
5. what happens afterwards and a second time anchor;
6. one contrast or reason;
7. one direct question about tomorrow;
8. a close.

Write only one or two English cue words in each box. Do not draft Kannada yet.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C01-NAMASKARA-01, KA-LEX-C04-NAALE-SIGONA-01, KA-LEX-C05-MAADU-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C05-MAADU-02, KA-ETYMON-C26-BELAGGE-01, KA-ETYMON-C27-SANJE-01, KA-LEX-C32-HOOGU-01, KA-LEX-C32-TINNU-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C32-TINNU-02, KA-LEX-C33-OODU-01, KA-LEX-C64-JOIN-01, KA-LEX-C67-LINK-03, KA-GRAMMAR-C67-LINK-04, KA-LEX-C70-TELL-04, KA-GRAMMAR-C70-TELL-05, KA-GRAMMAR-C69-ASK-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

There is no Kannada model, sentence bank, word bank, romanization, or copyable
answer on this page.

**Reader:** your friend Anu. **Purpose:** help Anu understand your familiar day
and choose a good time to visit you tomorrow.

Write **70–90 words in Kannada script**. Use every planning box. Include at
least four familiar actions, two time anchors, one sequence link, one contrast
or reason, and one direct question for Anu. Make every detail help with
tomorrow's visit. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose only
words and forms this book has taught, and connect them yourself.

This first connected attempt is untimed. Put away dictionaries, grammar
references, translators, generative assistants, and spell-checkers. Do not
copy from an earlier lesson. Ordinary legible Kannada handwriting is enough.

When the whole message exists, count its words once. If it is short, add one
useful taught detail to a planning box. If it is long, remove a detail that
does not help Anu. Do not replace the message with a memorised model.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C64-JOIN-01, KA-LEX-C67-LINK-03, KA-GRAMMAR-C67-LINK-04, KA-LEX-C70-TELL-04, KA-GRAMMAR-C70-TELL-05, KA-GRAMMAR-C69-ASK-02] -->

Review the same attempt in four separate passes:

1. **Task fulfilment and relevant content** — Anu, the familiar day,
   tomorrow's visit, and every requested content point are present.
2. **Organisation and cohesion** — the eight jobs form a route rather than a
   list of disconnected sentences.
3. **Kannada vocabulary and grammar** — every form and connector comes from
   the taught standard-colloquial Kannada track; a simpler familiar sentence
   is better than an invented structure.
4. **Kannada-script control** — letter shapes, vowel signs, conjuncts, word
   boundaries, and punctuation remain readable.

[PAUSE 2s] Mark one strength and one next step. Repair only the next step; keep
the original attempt so the next 70–90-word message can show real growth.

This is one untimed curriculum practice attempt, **not the complete 30-minute
A2 writing paper** and not evidence that either timed mock has been passed.
Closing the machine-measured connected-composition stage does not prove A2
attainment or readiness, learner performance, independent review, standard
setting, mock passage, calibration, or human validation.
