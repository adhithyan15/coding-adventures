Mandarin Chinese, chapter 195: At the Bank.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cúnqián — to put money by; to put money in the bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for public square, then the Mandarin for traffic lights.

You'll want to know: cúnqián.
cúnqián — "to put money by; to put money in the bank".
wǒ měi ge yuè dōu cúnqián — I put money aside every month.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cúnqián is built from cún 'to keep' and 钱 'money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzwon and dzjen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to be present' and 'to care for'; putting money away is not given there.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
xìnyòngkǎ — credit card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to put money by, then the Mandarin for to take out money.

You'll want to know: xìnyòngkǎ.
xìnyòngkǎ — "credit card".
zhèr kěyǐ yòng xìnyòngkǎ ma — Can I pay by credit card here?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xìnyòngkǎ is built from xìnyòng 'trust, credit' and kǎ 'a card'. The first two parts are inherited from Middle Chinese, sinH and yowngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'true' and 'to use'; kǎ is usually traced to English card.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mìmǎ — password; PIN.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take out money, then the Mandarin for credit card.

You'll want to know: mìmǎ.
mìmǎ — "password; PIN".
wǒ wàngle mìmǎ — I've forgotten my PIN.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mìmǎ is built from mì 'secret' and mǎ 'a numeral', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mit and maeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a ridge' and 'quiet', and the second as a stone like jade; how they came to mean secret and a number sign is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zhànghù — bank account.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for credit card, then the Mandarin for password.

You'll want to know: zhànghù.
zhànghù — "bank account".
nǐ yǒu yínháng zhànghù ma — Do you have a bank account?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhànghù is built from zhàng 'accounts' and hù 'a household'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese huX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as a door, half of a gate; no dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.
