## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C56-ORDINAL-07]; assesses=[] -->

**septimus, septima, septimum** — "**seventh**."

*Septem* is **seven**. Put the ending on and the vowel in the middle goes quiet:
*septem* → *septimus*, with *e* worn down to *i* because nothing is leaning on
it. Latin does that to unstressed middles constantly, and once you have heard it
here you will hear it everywhere.

**September** is this number in a name, and the calendar lesson explained why it
sits in ninth place: two months were pushed in at the front and the names never
moved.

The prettier descendant is the week. *Septimāna* was "a set of seven" — seven
days — and it went into Spanish as *semana* and French as *semaine*. So in two
of Latin's daughters the ordinary word for a week is a number word, and the
number is this one.
