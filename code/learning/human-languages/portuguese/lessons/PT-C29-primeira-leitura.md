# (reading) — the first passage

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-SKILL-READ-LINES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, then six lines. Now twelve of them together.

[PAUSE 1s] Forty-two words, and not one of them is new.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[PT-LEX-PRIMEIRO-02] -->

> Olá!
> Bom dia.
> Eu tenho um livro.
> O livro é muito bom.
> Eu leio e falo.
> Tenho um amigo.
> O amigo é o primeiro.
> A família é boa.
> Hoje eu como pão.
> Tenho água e café.
> Amanhã leio o livro.
> Obrigado. Adeus!

[PAUSE 3s] Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the
sentences arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Now read it again, and count the joining words.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One. **E**, twice.

[PAUSE 2s] There is no *but* and no *because* here, and that is not a
simplification — the book has not taught either yet. What holds the paragraph
together instead is the order of the sentences: a thing, then what it is like;
today, then tomorrow.

[PAUSE 2s] That is worth knowing before the joiners arrive. A paragraph can be
held together by sequence alone, and every language lets you do it.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice also that **eu** appears and then stops. Portuguese keeps the
person in the verb, so **Tenho** on its own is already *I have*, and the
paragraph drops the pronoun once it is established.

[PAUSE 2s] This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at
once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.
