Portuguese, chapter 508: O árbitro e o apito.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o árbitro — referee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for (long) walk, then the Portuguese for mountaineering.

You'll want to know: o árbitro.
o árbitro — "referee".
O árbitro mostrou um cartão amarelo. The referee showed a yellow card.
A woman is a árbitra.

The word, taken apart.
árbitro is a learned borrowing from Latin arbiter "a judge, an umpire" (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
apitar — to blow the whistle; to referee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for referee, then the Portuguese for whistle.

You'll want to know: apitar.
apitar — "to blow the whistle; to referee".
O árbitro apitou o fim do jogo. The referee blew the whistle for the end of the match.
Of a machine: to beep. O micro-ondas apitou.

The word, taken apart.
apitar is built on apito "whistle", which is probably imitative; the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
a bancada — stand (in a stadium).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for whistle, then the Portuguese for to blow the whistle.

You'll want to know: a bancada.
a bancada — "stand (in a stadium)".
Os adeptos cantavam na bancada. The fans were singing in the stand.
Also a kitchen worktop: a bancada da cozinha.

The word, taken apart.
bancada is built on banco "bench", which goes back to a Germanic word for a bench (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
