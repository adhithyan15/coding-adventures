Spanish, chapter 734: Quemaduras y cortes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
quemarse — to burn oneself; to get sunburnt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to infect, then the Spanish for to sneeze.

You'll want to know: quemarse.
quemarse — "to burn oneself; to get sunburnt".
Me quemé la mano con la sartén. I burned my hand on the frying pan.
Quemarse + body part. Quemarse al sol — to get sunburnt.

The word, taken apart.
quemarse is the reflexive of quemar, which is inherited from Latin cremāre "to burn" (EtymDB); English cremate is the same Latin verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la venda — the bandage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to burn oneself, then the Spanish for the burn.

You'll want to know: la venda.
la venda — "the bandage".
La enfermera me puso una venda. The nurse put a bandage on me.
A small sticking plaster is una tirita (Spain) or una curita (Latin America).

The word, taken apart.
venda is usually traced to Germanic binda "band, strip of cloth", from the root that gives English bind (EtymDB gives the Indo-European root bʰendʰ- "to bind").

Lesson 4 of 5.
sangrar — to bleed.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the burn, then the Spanish for the bandage.

You'll want to know: sangrar.
sangrar — "to bleed".
Me sangra la nariz. My nose is bleeding.
Works like doler: me sangra la rodilla.

The word, taken apart.
sangrar is inherited from Latin sanguināre "to bleed" (EtymDB), built on sanguis "blood".
