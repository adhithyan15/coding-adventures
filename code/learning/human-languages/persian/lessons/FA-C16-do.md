# دو — two, and a letter doing its third job

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-YEK, FA-ETYMON-YEK] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **yek**, and the English word that is the same root.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-DO]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-VAV-01] -->

> **دو** — *do* — **two**

Two letters. The second is **و**, the hard-working letter from the script chapter that
can be *v*, *u* or *o*; here it is **o**, and the word tells you so.

## The word, taken apart — the same word as English two
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-ETYMON-DO]; assesses=[FA-LEX-DO] -->

**دو** continues Middle Persian **dō**, from Proto-Iranian \**duwā*, from
Indo-European \**dwoh*, "two." English **two**, Latin **duo**, Greek **dýo**
and Sanskrit **dvá** are the same word.

Say *do* and *two* one after the other. Nothing has to be learned there; it has
to be recognised.
