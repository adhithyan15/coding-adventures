Hindi, chapter 375: Hopes and Fears.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आशा (āśā) — a hope, an expectation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for usually, then the Hindi for to make out.

You'll want to know: आशा.
आशा — āśā — "a hope, an expectation".
मुझे आशा है कि अगर मौसम अच्छा रहेगा तो फ़सल अच्छी होगी। mujhe āśā hai ki agar mausam acchā rahegā to fasal acchī hogī. I hope that if the weather stays good, the harvest will be good.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
आशा (āśā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit āśā, "a wish, a hope" (Monier-Williams).

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 5.
इशारा (iśārā) — a hint, a signal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an apprehension, then the Hindi for an indication.

You'll want to know: इशारा.
इशारा — iśārā — "a hint, a signal".
अगर मैं इशारा करूँगा तो अरुण दरवाज़ा खोलेगा। agar maiṁ iśārā karūṅgā to Aruṇ darvāzā kholegā. If I give a signal, Arun will open the door.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
इशारा (iśārā) is usually traced to Arabic ishāra, "a sign, a gesture", which reached Hindi through Persian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
रिझाना (rijhānā) — to charm, to win over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an indication, then the Hindi for a hint.

You'll want to know: रिझाना.
रिझाना — rijhānā — "to charm, to win over".
अगर तुम अच्छा गाना गाओगे तो लोगों को रिझा लोगे। agar tum acchā gānā gāoge to logoṁ ko rijhā loge. If you sing a good song, you'll win people over.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने लोगों को रिझाया, usne logoṁ ko rijhāyā.

The word, taken apart.
रिझाना (rijhānā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit ṛdhati, "thrives" (CDIAL 2457). CDIAL glosses the Hindi rijhānā "to enchant".
