## Unreleased — reading: six signs, six notices, and a first paragraph

Chapter 85 is Hindi's reading rung, and the first connected Devanagari the track
has ever asked a learner to read.

- Three lessons, ramping 6 → 17 → 78 words: single-word signs (**प्रवेश**,
  **निकास**, **खुला**, **बंद**), then notices with the verb restored
  (**दुकान बंद है।**), then a paragraph about Arun's day.
- **No new word in any of them.** Every Devanagari token was checked to occur in
  a lesson with a lower sequence number, so the lessons' claim is verified rather
  than asserted. One draft used **उसका**, which the check found untaught, and it
  was rewritten rather than waved through as an obvious inflection.
- Hindi already taught all four signs as vocabulary (`HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-01..04`);
  what it had never done was ask anyone to *read* one. That is the difference
  between knowing a word and recognising it before you have finished looking.
- Measured against Hindi's own task shapes, this moves reading from **0/3 to 3/3
  at pre-A1** and **0/3 to 1/3 at A1** — the 78-word paragraph clears the 70–90
  word `a1-reading-signs-and-forms` part. The two longer A1 parts (90–120 and
  90–140 words) remain, as does the whole ladder from A2 up to C2's 950–1050.
- Three lessons rather than one, because an atom introduced once and never
  revisited revokes the track's level on the reinforcement criterion.
