Spanish, chapter 578: No estoy de acuerdo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el desacuerdo — the disagreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for reasonable, then the Spanish for logical.

You'll want to know: el desacuerdo.
el desacuerdo — "the disagreement".
Estoy en desacuerdo con esa idea. I disagree with that idea.
Estar en desacuerdo con is the opposite of estar de acuerdo con.

The word, taken apart.
desacuerdo is built on acuerdo with the prefix des-; acuerdo comes from acordar, inherited from spoken Latin accordare, built on cor 'heart' (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
discrepar — to disagree, to differ.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for logical, then the Spanish for the disagreement.

You'll want to know: discrepar.
discrepar — "to disagree, to differ".
Discrepo un poco de tu opinión. I disagree a little with your opinion.
A polite, formal verb: discrepar de someone or something.

The word, taken apart.
discrepar is a learned borrowing from Latin discrepare 'to be out of tune, to differ', built from dis- 'apart' and crepare 'to rattle, to creak' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
oponerse — to oppose, to be against.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the disagreement, then the Spanish for to disagree.

You'll want to know: oponerse.
oponerse — "to oppose, to be against".
Me opongo a ese cambio. I am against that change.
Like poner: me opongo. Always with a: oponerse a algo.

The word, taken apart.
oponerse is a learned borrowing from Latin opponere 'to set against', ob + ponere 'to put', rebuilt on poner; EtymDB does not list it, but the Latin gloss is in Lewis & Short.

Lesson 4 of 5.
partidario — in favour; supporter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to disagree, then the Spanish for to oppose.

You'll want to know: partidario.
partidario — "in favour; supporter".
Soy partidario de trabajar en casa. I am in favour of working from home.
Ser partidario de + noun or infinitive. As a noun: los partidarios del plan.

The word, taken apart.
partidario is built on partido 'a side, a party', from partir 'to divide', which is inherited from Latin partire, built on pars 'part' (Lewis & Short).
