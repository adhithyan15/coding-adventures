Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 170: Wash Day.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
धोणो (dhoṇo) — to wash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to fry, then the Marwadi for to taste.

You'll want to know: धोणो.
धोणो — dhoṇo — "to wash".
हूं रोज कपड़ा धोऊं हूं। hū̃ roj kapṛā dhoū̃ hū̃. I wash clothes every day.
Past (I …): म्हैं कपड़ा धोया, mhaĩ kapṛā dhoyā.

The word, taken apart.
धोणो (dhoṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed dhauvati, "washes"; CDIAL lists Marwari dhoṇo under it (CDIAL 6886).

Lesson 2 of 5.
निचोवणो (nichovṇo) — to wring out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to taste, then the Marwadi for to wash.

You'll want to know: निचोवणो.
निचोवणो — nichovṇo — "to wring out".
मां कपड़ा निचोवै है। mā̃ kapṛā nichovai hai. Mother wrings out the clothes.
Past (I …): म्हैं कपड़ा निचोया, mhaĩ kapṛā nichoyā.

The word, taken apart.
निचोवणो (nichovṇo) comes from a reconstructed niścyotayati, "causes to trickle out"; CDIAL lists Hindi niconā, "to wring out", and Gujarati nicovvũ (CDIAL 7451). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

You'll want to know: पसारणो.
पसारणो — pasārṇo — "to spread out to dry; to stretch out".
हूं छात माथै कपड़ा पसारूं हूं। hū̃ chhāt māthai kapṛā pasārū̃ hū̃. I spread the clothes out on the roof.
Past (I …): म्हैं कपड़ा पसार्या, mhaĩ kapṛā pasāryā.

The word, taken apart.
पसारणो (pasārṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit prasārayati, "stretches out, extends"; CDIAL lists Marwari pasārṇo, "to spread out" (CDIAL 8838).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सुकावणो (sukāvṇo) — to dry (clothes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to wring out, then the Marwadi for to spread out to dry.

You'll want to know: सुकावणो.
सुकावणो — sukāvṇo — "to dry (clothes)".
हूं धूप मांय कपड़ा सुकाऊं हूं। hū̃ dhūp mā̃y kapṛā sukāū̃ hū̃. I dry the clothes in the sun.
Past (I …): म्हैं कपड़ा सुकाया, mhaĩ kapṛā sukāyā.

The word, taken apart.
सुकावणो (sukāvṇo) is built on a reconstructed śuṣkati, "becomes dry"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari sūkaï, "dries up", and a Gujarati verb sukāvũ, "to dry" (CDIAL 12552). The adjective सूको, dry, is its relative.
