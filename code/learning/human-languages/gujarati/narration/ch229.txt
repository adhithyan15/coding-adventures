Gujarati, chapter 229: From the wrist to the heel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કાંડું (kā̃ḍũ) — wrist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for skin, then the Gujarati for blister.

You'll want to know: કાંડું.
કાંડું — kā̃ḍũ — "wrist".
શું તમારું કાંડું દુખે છે? — śũ tamārũ kā̃ḍũ dukhe che? — "Does your wrist hurt?"

The word, taken apart.
કાંડું (kā̃ḍũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "wrist", under Sanskrit kāṇḍa, "single joint of a plant", a word it thinks probably came from Dravidian, so the wrist is a joint (CDIAL 3023).

Lesson 2 of 5.
અંગૂઠો (angūṭho) — thumb, big toe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for blister, then the Gujarati for wrist.

You'll want to know: અંગૂઠો.
અંગૂઠો — angūṭho — "thumb, big toe".
મારો અંગૂઠો નથી દુખતો. — māro angūṭho nathī dukhto — "My thumb does not hurt."

The word, taken apart.
અંગૂઠો (angūṭho) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from aṅguṣṭha, "thumb; big toe" (CDIAL 137).

ઘૂંટી — ghū̃ṭī — "ankle".
શું તમારી ઘૂંટી સૂજે છે? — śũ tamārī ghū̃ṭī sūje che? — "Is your ankle swelling?"
Not ઘૂંટણ, the knee.

The word, taken apart.
ઘૂંટી (ghū̃ṭī) is inherited. CDIAL lists it, glossed "ankle joint", under the lexical Sanskrit ghuṭa, "ankle", which it takes from Dravidian, after T. Burrow (CDIAL 4479).

Lesson 4 of 5.
એડી (eḍī) — heel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for thumb, then the Gujarati for ankle.

You'll want to know: એડી.

મારી એડી નથી દુખતી. — mārī eḍī nathī dukhtī — "My heel does not hurt."

The word, taken apart.
એડી (eḍī) comes from aḍḍi, "heel", a reconstructed form; CDIAL notes J. Bloch's view that it was borrowed from Dravidian, as in Tamil aḍi (CDIAL 191).
