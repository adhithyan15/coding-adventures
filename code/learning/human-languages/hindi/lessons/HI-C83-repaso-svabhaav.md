# सात शब्द स्वभाव के — seven words, three endings, and one pair of opposites

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name as many of the seven as you can before reading the table. Aim
for five.

## Grammar Lens: the ending decides, and the meaning never does
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-01, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-05, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-03] -->

All seven, sorted by what their last letters allow:

| word | meaning | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| *acchā* | good | *acchī* |
| बुरा | bad | बुरी |
| शर्मीला | shy | शर्मीली |
| होशियार | clever | होशियार |
| मिलनसार | sociable | मिलनसार |
| गंभीर | serious | गंभीर |
| मेहनती | hard-working | मेहनती |

Read the third column against the first. Where the two differ, the word moves
for a woman; where they are identical, it does not. Three move and four stay.

## Grammar Lens: where each word came from
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-05, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

The split is not random, and seeing why makes the endings predictable:

| source | the words | ends in |
|---|---|---|
| inherited from Sanskrit | *acchā*, बुरा, गंभीर | mostly **-आ** |
| Persian | होशियार, मिलनसार, शर्मीला | mostly a consonant |
| Arabic through Persian | मेहनती | **-ई** |

Inherited words are the ones that kept the **-आ** ending, because they are the
ones that went through the whole sound history of the language. Borrowed words
arrived later, in whatever shape the lending language had them, and mostly that
shape ended in a consonant.

**गंभीर** is the exception that proves it is a tendency rather than a law: it is
inherited from Sanskrit and still ends in a consonant. So the origin explains
the pattern — the **ending** is what you act on.
