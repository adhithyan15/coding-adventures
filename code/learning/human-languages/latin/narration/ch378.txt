Latin, chapter 378: Making Things by Hand.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
exerceō, exercēre — to train, to practise; to keep busy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a mason, then the Latin for a sculptor of statues.

You'll want to know: exerceō, exercēre.
exerceō, exercēre — "to train, to practise; to keep busy".
Pistor artem suam multōs annōs exercuit. The baker practised his trade for many years.
exerceō, exercēre, exercuī, exercitum. Artem exercēre, to practise a craft; sē exercēre, to train. English exercise.

The word, taken apart.
exerceō is built from ex and arceō "to shut in, to keep off" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to drive animals out to work, then to keep anyone busy. English exercise comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fabricor, fabricārī — to make, to build, to forge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sculptor of statues, then the Latin for to train.

You'll want to know: fabricor, fabricārī.
fabricor, fabricārī — "to make, to build, to forge".
Faber gladium fabricātur. The smith is forging a sword.
Deponent: fabricor, fabricārī, fabricātus sum, built on fabrica, a workshop, and faber, a smith. English fabricate.

The word, taken apart.
fabricor is built on fabrica "a workshop, a craft" (Lewis & Short), which comes from faber "a smith, a craftsman". English fabricate comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
texō, texere — to weave; to build.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to train, then the Latin for to make.

You'll want to know: texō, texere.
texō, texere — "to weave; to build".
Fēmina vestem ē lānā texit. The woman weaves a garment from wool.
texō, texere, texuī, textum. A speech too can be woven. English text, textile and texture.

The word, taken apart.
texō is inherited from Proto-Italic teksō (EtymDB), from a root meaning "to fashion, to build" that Greek tektōn "a builder" shares. English text and textile come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
suō, suere — to sew, to stitch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to make, then the Latin for to weave.

You'll want to know: suō, suere.
suō, suere — "to sew, to stitch".
Sūtor calceōs suit. The shoemaker stitches shoes.
suō, suere, suī, sūtum. Sūtor, a shoemaker, is the man who sews. English suture comes from it; English sew is a native cousin, not a loan.

The word, taken apart.
suō is inherited from Proto-Italic sūō (EtymDB), from the same Proto-Indo-European root as English sew, which came down through Old English, not from Latin.

Statuārius nōmen in saxō sculpsit. The sculptor carved the name in stone.

The word, taken apart.
