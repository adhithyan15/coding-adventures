## You'll want to know: لأن
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C40-LIANNA-01]; assesses=[AR-FUNC-C37-LA-AFHAM-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C37-PRESENT-I-01] -->

> **لا أكتب لأنني لا أسمع** — *lā aktub li-annanī lā asmaʿ* — **I am not writing because I do not hear.**

Look at the first letter. *limādhā* opens with **لـ**, *for*, and so does this
word — with **أنّ**, *that*, behind it instead of a question word. Arabic's *why*
and *because* are one particle apart, and both are visibly built out of pieces
rather than borrowed whole.

**لأنّ** needs a subject after it, so for *I* it becomes **لأنني**, *li-annanī*.
That is one ending on one word, and it is the only thing the construction asks
of you.

## Grammar Lens: the claim, then the reason
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C40-LIANNA-01, AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01, AR-LEX-C40-LIMADHA-01] -->

| head word | what follows it |
|---|---|
| **لكن** | an objection |
| **لأن** | a reason |

Both stand at the head of the clause they govern, and both come **after** the
claim they attach to. Put them in one sentence and you have the shape of an
argument.
