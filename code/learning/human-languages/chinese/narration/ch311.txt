Mandarin Chinese, chapter 311: The Police Car.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
diàochá — to investigate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to tell a lie, then the Mandarin for to own up to something.

You'll want to know: diàochá.
diàochá — "to investigate".
jǐngchá zhèngzài diàochá zhège shìgù — The police are investigating the accident.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
diàochá is built from diào 'to choose' and chá, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dewH and dzrae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to select' and the second 'wood floating in water'; how the pair came to mean 'to investigate' is not settled.

One more word for telling what happened.

Lesson 2 of 6.
警车 (jǐngchē) — police car.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to own up to something, then the Mandarin for to investigate a case.

You'll want to know: 警车.
警车 — jǐngchē — "police car".
小偷看到警车就走了 xiǎotōu kàndào jǐngchē, jiù zǒu le — The thief saw the police car and left.
The new character, in pieces: 警 (jǐng) — nineteen strokes in three pieces: top left, the grass piece of 花, then a two-stroke wrap with 口 inside; top right, the striking piece of 救; across the foot a dot, three bars and 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
警车 (jǐngchē) is built from 警 (jǐng) 'to warn' and 车 'a vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaengX and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wake' and 'to guard against'.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
警 (jǐng) — three pieces: top left, top right and the foot.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. 花 救.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — huā, jiù]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — three pieces: top left, top right and the foot.
警 (jǐng).
Three pieces: two side by side on top, one across the foot. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
Top left, eight strokes. The grass piece of 花: a bar and two short verticals. Under it a short sweep to the left and a stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks back. Inside, 口.
Top right, four strokes. The striking piece of 救: a sweep to the left, a bar, a long sweep to the lower left and a long stroke falling to the right.
The foot, seven strokes. A dot. A long bar across, then two shorter bars. Then 口 under them.
8 + 4 + 7 = nineteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 警 (jǐng), all nineteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 警 (jǐng)? (Three.) Which piece sits across the foot? (A dot, three bars and 口.) Where have you made the top right piece? (In 救 and 故.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 警 (jǐng), pinned stroke paths and medians.

The word, taken apart.
