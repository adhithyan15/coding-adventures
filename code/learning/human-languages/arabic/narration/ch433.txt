Arabic, chapter 433: A flat tyre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
إطار (iṭār) — a tyre; a frame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for brakes, then the Arabic for to supply.

You'll want to know: إطار.
إطار — iṭār — "a tyre; a frame".
كان إطار الدراجة قديما. kāna iṭār ad-darrāja qadīman. The bicycle's tyre was old.

The word, taken apart.
إطار (iṭār) is usually traced to the native Arabic root ʾ-ṭ-r, "to bend round". An iṭār is a ring or rim that goes round a thing.

Lesson 2 of 5.
عجلة (ʿajala) — a wheel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to supply, then the Arabic for a tyre.

You'll want to know: عجلة.
عجلة — ʿajala — "a wheel".
غير أبي عجلة (ʿajala) السيارة على الطريق. ghayyara abī ʿajalat as-sayyāra ʿalā aṭ-ṭarīq. My father changed the car's wheel on the road.
The same word means haste: fī ʿajala, "in a hurry".

The word, taken apart.
عجلة (ʿajala) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-j-l, "to hasten" (Lane). It is the root of mustaʿjil, "in a hurry"; Lane lists ʿajala as a cart drawn by an ox, said to be named for its quick going, and modern Arabic uses it for a wheel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
جر (jarra) — to tow; to drag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hole, then the Arabic for a pump.

You'll want to know: جر.
جر — jarra — "to tow; to drag".
جر رجل سيارتنا إلى القرية. jarra rajul sayyāratanā ilā al-qarya. A man towed our car to the village.
Present: يجر, yajurr.
Grammarians call the genitive case al-jarr, "the dragging".

The word, taken apart.
جر (jarra) is built on the native Arabic root j-r-r, "to drag, to pull" (Lane). It is the plain verb of a doubled root.
