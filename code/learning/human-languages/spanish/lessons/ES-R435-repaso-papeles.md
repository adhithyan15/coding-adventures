## Grammar Lens: the endings that read themselves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-02, ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-03, ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-05] -->

| ending | what it does | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ción** | action becomes a thing | la documentación |
| **-encia** | pairs with English *-ence* | la experiencia |
| participle | becomes a thing or a person | jubilado |

The participle row is the one to keep: **el pedido** in the first chapter of
this run was a thing, **jubilado** here is a person, and neither needed an extra
word to become one.

Five chapters have now handed you **-dor**, **-ería**, **-ero**, **-ción** and
**-encia**. Do not read that as a rule that every word yields — *el currículum*
in this very chapter kept a Latin ending and yields nothing. The habit is to
look, not to expect.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-01, ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-03, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-06] -->

**You could say you had a job, and nothing about getting one.** *El trabajo*,
*la empresa* and *trabajar* were yours; the CV you send, the papers you bring,
the experience an advert demands and the word for somebody who has stopped
working were not.

A job advert is four lines long and uses all five. That is the shape of A2
reading: short, ordinary, administrative, and impossible without exactly these
nouns.
