# d'abord — the events, in order

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C96-TEMPS48-03, FR-LEX-C156-TEMPS156-02, FR-LEX-C156-TEMPS156-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "then". (*Puis.*) Then "afterwards". (*Ensuite.*) Then
"finally". (*Enfin.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Three words for the middle and the end of a story. The order
still needs a first word.

## You'll want to know: d'abord
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-DABORD-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C80-CHOSES32-03, FR-LEX-C64-VERBES16-03] -->

**d'abord** — "first", "at first".

> **D'abord**, j'ai acheté le billet. — First I bought the ticket.
> **Ensuite**, j'ai attendu le train. — Next I waited for the train.
> **Puis** le train est arrivé. — Then the train came.
> **Enfin**, je suis rentré à la maison. — Finally I went back home.

Say it as one word: "da-BOR". The *d* is *de* with its vowel dropped, and the
final *d* of *abord* is silent.

## Grammar Lens: four places in a story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-RECIT-ORDRE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C55-SENTIR7-02, FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-LEX-MAIS-03] -->

**Ensuite** and **puis** both mean "then, next", and a story can use them as
many times as it has events. *Puis* is the lighter of the two, and speech
often doubles it up as *et puis*.

**Enfin** closes the story, and it often carries relief: something you were
waiting for has come at last. In speech it has a second life as a
hesitation, *enfin…*, "well, I mean…", which you will hear constantly.

**D'abord** can also mean "at first", setting up a change:

> *D'abord, j'avais peur, mais ensuite, c'était bien.* — At first I was
> scared, but then it was fine.

Notice what these words do **not** touch: the scene. *Il faisait froid*
needs no *d'abord*. Order belongs to the events.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**d'abord** is *de* plus **abord**, "the approach, the first coming
alongside". *Abord* is built on **bord**, "the edge, the side of a ship", a
Germanic word: EtymDB 2.1 sets it beside Old English *bord*, "side of a
ship", and Old Norse *borð*. The usual account runs through the verb
*aborder*, to bring a ship alongside: *d'abord* is "from the very first
approach".
