## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C350-REF-09]; assesses=[] -->

**distinto** — "different," "a different one."

You have **otro** for *another*. *Distinto* is sharper: not merely a second
one, but one that is unlike the first.

Latin **distinguere** is **dis-**, *apart*, plus an old verb **stinguere**, *to
prick*. Marking things apart meant putting a pinprick beside them — a tally,
a dot, a small wound in the wax.

So a **distinct** thing is one somebody has pricked apart from its neighbours,
and to **distinguish** is to do the pricking.

The family is odd and worth having. To **extinguish** is to prick a flame
out. An **instinct** is an urge that pricks you from inside. Something
**distinguished** has been marked off from the crowd.

In use, remember that *distinto* usually goes after its noun — **un libro
distinto** — while *otro* goes in front.
