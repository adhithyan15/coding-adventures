## Added — every Marwadi word lesson says where its word comes from

Every one of the track's 718 word lessons now has an `etymology_hook`, and every one says in its body where its word comes from. Before this change 552 had no hook and 716 had no visible origin (human-language-data `ETYMOLOGY_GAP_PINS.marwadi`, now 0 / 0).

- **New "taken apart" blocks (717).** Each sits after the section that introduces the word, opens with `<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->`, and agrees with the lesson's new hook. Phrase lessons get "The phrase, taken apart". The heard-first lessons and the script-ramp words got short blocks too. MW-C05-hear-naam, whose section already had a block, gained one origin sentence instead.
- **Sources.** Inherited words and Sanskrit loans cite Turner's *Comparative Dictionary of the Indo-Aryan Languages* (CDIAL) beside the fact, read from the moli-mandala data files: 1,237 citations of 462 entries in 578 lessons, each checked by script for the entry number, the Sanskrit or reconstructed form and the forms quoted from it. Where CDIAL has a Marwari or Old Marwari row, the block names it (*tāto*, *khāro*, *jāṇṇo*, Old Marwari *āpaṇau*, *bharaï*, *gaharau*), with its meaning when that differs (Old Marwari *gailo* "road" for *gelo*, *ghaṛī* "water-clock" for *ghaṛo*). Where it has none, the block says so and makes the match through the Hindi or Gujarati row. CDIAL's own hedges are carried: "probably", doubtful further sources, Dravidian, Munda and Austroasiatic origins, imitative words, and reconstructed forms, which are starred. DEDR is cited for *ṭhaṇḍo* and *ṭhaṇḍī* (DEDR 3045, which itself cites CDIAL 13676) and for *chappal* (DEDR 1963, "Cf. H. cappal"). Arabic loans name their root, checked against Lane's *Arabic-English Lexicon* (44 lessons), and reach Marwadi through Persian. Persian loans are written "usually traced to Persian …", with the Turkic or Mongolic word behind *bahādur* and *naukrī* named. English and Portuguese loans name the donor.
- **Rough mix.** About 485 inherited lessons (tadbhavas and words CDIAL lists from reconstructed forms), 35 tatsamas and semi-tatsamas, about 70 Persian and 45 Arabic loans, 19 English and 5 Portuguese loans, and 63 lessons (52 words) whose origin is not settled or only partly known. Those say so, after a search of CDIAL by form and by meaning and of DEDR; where CDIAL has a near lead it is given as a lead (*lū̃kṛī* and Hindi *lomṛī*, *het* and *hita*, *bā̃chṇo* and *vācayati*, *sapno* and *svapna*, *kyū̃kai*'s *kai* and Hindi *ki*).
- **Older hooks (166).** All were rewritten. Most were spelling, script or pacing notes rather than origins. Two described sound changes wrongly: *igyārā* keeps more of "one" than its opening vowel, and *aṭhārā* does not keep both consonants of "eight".
- **Font.** Long vowels with a nasal keep the track's macron-and-tilde spelling (*mā̃y*, *bā̃chṇo*), which the book's `nasal-macron.tex` draws. CDIAL's *ṁ* is written *ṃ* (*padāṃśa*, *bāṃṭaï*), because the book font has no *ṁ*.
- **Book contents.** The book now runs to 1,241 pages, so its last contents entries carry four-digit page numbers. `book/preamble.tex` widens the contents page-number box to 2.4em and the title clearance to 3.4em, and gives wrapped chapter titles a ragged right edge, as the Punjabi book does.
- **Durations.** Three lessons are raised to their computed length, all under 300 s: MW-C04-hear-paani 150 → 172, MW-C05-hear-kain 150 → 174, MW-C06-hear-question 150 → 197. All three were already over their limit before this change (160, 156, 176 s). Every other lesson stays within its declared time.
- **Roots.** Each lesson's `roots:` gained the slugs for its sources, reusing the slugs of the Hindi and Gujarati lessons for shared etyma; homographs get separate slugs (`sanskrit-nava-nine` beside `sanskrit-nava`, `sanskrit-nasta-nose` beside `sanskrit-nashta`). A lead that CDIAL does not confirm gets no slug. `check:root-slug-splits` reports nothing new.
