Gujarati, chapter 159: Scouring, drying and spills.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
માંજવું (mā̃jvũ) — to scour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for floor cloth, then the Gujarati for stain.

You'll want to know: માંજવું.
માંજવું — mā̃jvũ — "to scour".
તે રાત્રે વાસણ માંજે છે. — te rātre vāsaṇ mā̃je che — "She scours the pots at night."

The word, taken apart.
માંજવું (mā̃jvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit mārjati, "wipes off", on the root that gives mṛjanti, "they rub, clean"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to scour" (CDIAL 10080).

Lesson 2 of 5.
સૂકવવું (sūkavvũ) — to dry out, to put out to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for stain, then the Gujarati for to scour.

You'll want to know: સૂકવવું.
સૂકવવું — sūkavvũ — "to dry out, to put out to dry".
માતા અગાસીમાં કપડાં સૂકવે છે. — mātā agāsīmā̃ kapḍā̃ sūkave che — "Mother dries the clothes on the terrace."
Related to સૂકું, sūkũ, dry.

The word, taken apart.
સૂકવવું (sūkavvũ) is a tadbhava, the causative of an inherited verb for drying. CDIAL lists Gujarati sukvũ, "to dry up", and its causative sukavvũ under śuṣkati, "becomes dry" (CDIAL 12552).

Lesson 3 of 5.
નળ (naḷ) — water tap, pipe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to scour, then the Gujarati for to dry out.

You'll want to know: નળ.
નળ — naḷ — "water tap, pipe".
રસોડામાં નળ છે. — rasoḍāmā̃ naḷ che — "There is a tap in the kitchen."

The word, taken apart.
નળ (naḷ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit naḍa, naḷa, "a kind of reed", a hollow stem; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "pipe, drain" (CDIAL 6936).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ઢોળવું (ḍhoḷvũ) — to spill, to pour out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to dry out, then the Gujarati for water tap.

You'll want to know: ઢોળવું.
ઢોળવું — ḍhoḷvũ — "to spill, to pour out".
બાળક દૂધ ઢોળે છે. — bāḷak dūdh ḍhoḷe che — "The child spills the milk."

The word, taken apart.
ઢોળવું (ḍhoḷvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from ḍhōlayati, the causative of ḍhulati, "bends, falls, flows", a reconstructed form; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to pour out" (CDIAL 5593).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ભોંય (bhõy) — floor, ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for water tap, then the Gujarati for to spill.

You'll want to know: ભોંય.
ભોંય — bhõy — "floor, ground".
ભોંય ભીની છે. — bhõy bhīnī che — "The floor is wet."

The word, taken apart.
ભોંય (bhõy) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit bhūmi, "earth, ground", and CDIAL lists Gujarati bhõ and bhõy, "ground", marking bhõy as the Kathiawar form (CDIAL 9557).
