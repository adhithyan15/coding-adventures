German, chapter 359: Gerecht oder nicht?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Gerechtigkeit — justice, fairness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the vote, then the German for to contribute.

You'll want to know: die Gerechtigkeit.
die Gerechtigkeit — "justice, fairness".
Viele junge Leute kämpfen für mehr Gerechtigkeit. Many young people fight for more justice.
No plural in everyday use. From gerecht — fair.

The word, taken apart.
Gerechtigkeit is formed from gerecht "fair, righteous", Old High German gireht (EtymDB), with -igkeit; the adjective is built on the word for "right", Proto-Germanic rehtaz.

Plural: die Ungleichheiten. From ungleich — unequal.

The word, taken apart.
Ungleichheit is built from un- "not", gleich "equal", inherited from Old High German gilīh and Proto-Germanic galīkaz "alike" (EtymDB), and the ending -heit.

die Armut — "poverty".
In vielen Ländern leben Kinder in großer Armut. In many countries children live in great poverty.
No plural. Built on arm — poor.

The word, taken apart.
Armut is inherited from Middle High German armuot (EtymDB), built on arm "poor" with an old noun ending; EtymDB lists Dutch armoede as a cousin.

Sein Reichtum hat ihn nicht glücklich gemacht. His wealth did not make him happy.

Reichtum is inherited from Middle High German rīchtuom and Old High German rīhhituom (EtymDB), built on reich "rich" with -tum; EtymDB lists Dutch rijkdom and English richdom as cousins.

Lesson 5 of 5.
benachteiligen — to put at a disadvantage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for poverty, then the German for wealth.

You'll want to know: benachteiligen.
benachteiligen — "to put at a disadvantage".
Das neue Gesetz benachteiligt Familien mit wenig Geld. The new law puts families with little money at a disadvantage.
Inseparable: es benachteiligt, es hat benachteiligt. Benachteiligt sein — to be at a disadvantage.

The word, taken apart.
benachteiligen is built with be- on Nachteil "disadvantage", from nach "after" and Teil "part", Proto-Germanic dailą (EtymDB): you leave someone the worse part.
