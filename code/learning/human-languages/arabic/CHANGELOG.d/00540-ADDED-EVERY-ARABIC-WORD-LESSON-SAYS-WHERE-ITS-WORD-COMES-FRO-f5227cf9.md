## Added — every Arabic word lesson says where its word comes from

HL24 §9 asks every lesson that introduces a word to say where the word comes from, from a cited source. Each Arabic word lesson now carries an `etymology_hook` and an origin in its body (human-language-data: Arabic etymology gaps 1,187 / 1,195 → 0 / 0, empty "taken apart" blocks 19 → 0).

- **New blocks.** 1,197 lessons gained a "The word, taken apart" block; 20 older blocks gained an origin sentence, among them the 19 that took a word apart without saying where it came from.
- **Native words (about 1,080).** Each names its root and the sense Lane's *Arabic-English Lexicon* gives it, with the pattern where it is plain: *maktab*, "a place of writing", on *k-t-b*; *miftāḥ*, "an instrument of opening", on *f-t-ḥ*. Every root and form was checked by script against Lane's articles. Hebrew, Aramaic and Akkadian cousins appear only where standard and hedged. Two roots Lane's later volumes do not reach (*n-w-m*, *h-t-f*) say so.
- **Loans.** The donor comes from Lane's own notes or EtymDB 2.1: Persian 26 (*jawrab*, *sirwāl*, *filfil*, *bustān*, *shiṭranj*), modern European 24 (*bank*, *ṭāwila*, *sīnimā*), Greek 17 (*funduq*, *mūsīqā*, *daftar* through Persian), Aramaic or Syriac 11 (*kursī*, *sikkīn*, *ṣalāh*), Latin 4, Coptic 2 (*timsāḥ*, *ṭūb*), Turkish 1. Compounds and phrases (43) are taken apart piece by piece, and 15 old particles and pronouns are called inherited.
- **Not settled (33).** Among them *ʿīd*, *maydān*, *mīnāʾ*, *tārīkh*, *barīd*, *dīn*, *zarāfa* and the Arabic *qiṭṭ*, each with the competing accounts named.
- **Folk etymologies flagged.** *Sikkīn* "because it stills the slaughtered animal", *shiṭranj* as "a hundred stratagems" (it goes back to Sanskrit *caturaṅga*), and English *sheriff* beside *sharīf* in AR-C02-tasharrafna (*sheriff* is the Old English shire-reeve).
- **Hooks.** All 75 older hooks were cut to 120 characters or replaced. Twelve said nothing about origin or said something false: AR-C20 set Arabic *ʿashar* beside English *-teen*, AR-C08 called the weekday names ordinals, and AR-C16 printed an Arabic letter with Hebrew vowel points as an Aramaic word (fixed in its body too). AR-C18 now says *ṭaqs* is usually traced through Syriac to Greek *taxis*.
- **Root slugs.** Each block's root or donor became a `roots:` slug, reusing the slugs the track already had; the Arabic root ledger grows from 107 to 943 roots.
- **Durations.** No `max_seconds` value was raised. AR-C17-kam-umruka's new block was kept short so the lesson stays under 300 seconds.

- **Review.** Two independent reviewers read all 1,262 changed lessons against Lane (including its supplement files) and EtymDB. About 228 lessons (18%) needed a content fix, all made. The main fault came from the generating template, which had put the noun's own meaning where the root's sense belongs ("root *s-m-k*, 'fish'"); each block now gives the root's sense as Lane gives it ("*s-m-k*, 'to rise, to be high'"), or says the noun is an old native noun filed under its root. Claims Lane does not make were removed (*naḥla*, *kura*, *nādī*, *waraqa*), modern senses are now marked as later (*darrāja*, *sifāra*, *mawqiʿ*, *thaqāfa*, *khuṭṭa*), loans called native are corrected (*tawābil* from Aramaic *tablā*, *salla* from Syriac, *miqlama* on Greek-derived *qalam*), and speculative claims were softened (no shared Afroasiatic root for *qiṭṭ* and Latin *cattus*; Nubian is not Afroasiatic; *yawm* has no established link to an Egyptian word for "sun"). Several "not settled" origins now give a hedged account (*barīd*, *ward*, *shāsha*, *warsha*, *bunnī*, *laysa*, *lākin*).
