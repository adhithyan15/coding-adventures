## You'll want to know: shātān
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C227-SEA227-04]; assesses=[] -->

*shātān* — "sandy beach".

> *nàr de shātān hěn gānjìng* — The beach there was really clean.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*shātān* is built from *shā* 'sand' and *tān* 'a shore', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *srae* and *than* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as stones scattered by water, and glosses the second as shallows in a river, so how it came to mean a beach is not settled.
