Lesson 1 of 7.
ものがたり (monogatari) — a story; a tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for here and there, then the Japanese for to keep watch.

You'll want to know: ものがたり.
ものがたり — monogatari — "a story; a tale".
これは むかしの ものがたりです。 — kore wa mukashi no monogatari desu — "This is a story from long ago."

The word, taken apart.
ものがたり (monogatari) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mono, "thing", and gatari, "telling" (from kataru) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 7.
でんとう (dentō) — a tradition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to keep watch, then the Japanese for a story.

You'll want to know: でんとう.
でんとう — dentō — "a tradition".
この まちには ふるい でんとうが あります。 — kono machi ni wa furui dentō ga arimasu — "This town has old traditions."

The word, taken apart.
でんとう (dentō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are den, "transmit" (Middle Chinese drjwen) and tō, "relationship" (thowngH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 7.
かんじる (kanjiru) — to feel; to sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a tradition, then the Japanese for almost.

You'll want to know: かんじる.
かんじる — kanjiru — "to feel; to sense".
いなかで あきを かんじました。 — inaka de aki o kanjimashita — "In the countryside I could feel the autumn."
Polite かんじます, kanjimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ははが あきを かんじました。, haha ga aki o kanjimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かんじる (kanjiru) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: kan, "feeling", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese komX), and jiru, "a native verb ending", is native Japanese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
