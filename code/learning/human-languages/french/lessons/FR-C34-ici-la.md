# ici, là, là-bas — three distances

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-OU-03, FR-GRAMMAR-INTONATION-01, FR-GRAM-CEST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can ask **où ?**. You have had no way at all to answer it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-ICI-01, FR-LEX-LA-ADV-02, FR-LEX-LABAS-03]; assesses=[FR-LEX-OU-03, FR-GRAM-CEST-01] -->

> **ici** — here. · **là** — there. · **là-bas** — over there, further off.

> **Où est le café ? — Ici.**

**ici** is Latin **ecce hic**, "behold here" — the same **ecce**, "behold,"
that is buried inside **c'est**. French said its pointing word so often that it
fused into several separate everyday words and then disappeared inside each of
them; two more of them arrive in the next lesson.

**là-bas** is **là** plus **bas**, "low" — *there below*. The picture is of
something further down a road, and it marks the far end of a three-step scale
English can only manage with two words and a gesture.

The accent on **là** is doing real work: it separates the adverb from **la**, the
article. Same letter, same sound, and the grave mark is the only difference on
the page.
