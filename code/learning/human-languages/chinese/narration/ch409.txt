Mandarin Chinese, chapter 409: Heavy Traffic.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gāofēng — peak (of traffic); rush hour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a long-distance coach, then the Mandarin for a starting point.

You'll want to know: gāofēng.
gāofēng — "peak (of traffic); rush hour".
gāofēng de shíhou zài chē lǐ zhǎo bu dào zuòwèi — At rush hour you can't find a seat on the bus.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gāofēng is built from 高 'high' and fēng 'a peak', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and phjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the second under another spelling, glossed as a mountain peak.

Lesson 2 of 6.
交通 (jiāotōng) — traffic; transport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a starting point, then the Mandarin for the rush hour.

You'll want to know: 交通.
交通 — jiāotōng — "traffic; transport".
zhèr de jiāotōng hěn fāngbiàn — It's easy to get around here.
The new character, in pieces: 通 (tōng) — ten strokes in two pieces: the right piece first, seven strokes, a short bar that turns down to the left with a dot under it, then a frame with two short bars and a vertical through them; the walking piece of 这, three strokes, sits under it and is written last. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
交通 (jiāotōng) is built from 交 'to cross' and 通 (tōng) 'to go through', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaew and thuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to reach through'.

Script — the right piece first, then the walking piece under it.
通 (tōng).
Two pieces, and the walking piece comes last. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The right piece, seven strokes. A short bar that turns down to the left, and a dot under its end. A vertical down the left. A bar across the top that turns down the right side and hooks to the left at the foot. Two short bars inside. Then a vertical down through the middle.
The walking piece, three strokes, as in 这: a dot, a stroke that zigzags down, then a long sweep along the foot under the whole character.
7 + 3 = ten.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 通 (tōng)? (Ten.) Which piece comes last? (The walking piece, as in 这.) What runs down through the middle of the right piece? (A vertical.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
通车 (tōngchē) — to open to traffic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the rush hour, then the Mandarin for the traffic on the roads.

You'll want to know: 通车.
通车 — tōngchē — "to open to traffic".
zhèr de dìtiě míngnián tōngchē — The metro here opens next year.

The word, taken apart.
通车 (tōngchē) is built from 通 (tōng) 'to go through' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thuwng and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): vehicles can go through.

shòupiàoyuán — "ticket seller; bus conductor".
shòupiàoyuán zuò zài ménkǒu — The conductor sits by the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shòupiàoyuán is built from shòu 'to sell', 票 'a ticket' and yuán 'a member of staff'. The first and the last are inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyuwH and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
打车 (dǎchē) — to take a taxi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to open to traffic, then the Mandarin for a ticket seller.

You'll want to know: 打车.
打车 — dǎchē — "to take a taxi".
下雨的时候不好打车 xià yǔ de shíhou bù hǎo dǎchē — When it rains it's hard to get a taxi.

The word, taken apart.
打车 (dǎchē) is built from 打 and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike'. The sense 'to hire' in this word is not given there, and taengX would regularly give a different final, so dǎ is irregular.
