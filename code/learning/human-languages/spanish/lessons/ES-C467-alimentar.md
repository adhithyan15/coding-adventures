## You'll want to know: alimentar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02] -->

**alimentar** — to feed, to nourish.

> Lo que producimos **alimenta** a unas cuarenta familias.

*Alimentar a un niño* — to feed a child; the **a** is there because the thing
being fed is a person. **El alimento** is the food itself, and **la
alimentación** is the feeding, or a person's diet.

*Alimentarse de algo* is what an animal or a person lives on: *se alimentan de
fruta*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03] -->

Latin **alere** was wider than *to feed*. It meant to nourish, and by that to
**rear** — to bring a thing on, to make it bigger. Feeding is the sense that
stuck in Spanish; the others are worth having:

| English | how the root shows |
|---|---|
| **adult** | somebody who has finished growing |
| **alumnus** | somebody being brought up |

An **alumnus** is literally *one being nourished* — a foster-child, then a
pupil, then a graduate. **Coalesce** is *co-* plus *alescere*, to grow
together.

So *alimentar* is not about hunger. It is about making something bigger, and
food is the means.

## Grammar Lens: -mento, and the noun it builds here
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03, ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-01] -->

*El medicamento* gave you **-mento**, the formal twin of *-miento*, arriving
by the other road. Latin *-mentum* often named the **means** of a thing, and
both of these are that kind:

| noun | the means of |
|---|---|
| el medica**mento** | healing |
| el ali**mento** | growing |

Watch the cut: it is *ali-* plus *-mento*, exactly as *medica-* plus *-mento*.
And the noun is older than the verb — **Latin** built *alimentum* off *alere*
first, and *alimentare* came afterwards. Spanish inherited both, which is why
the noun is *alimento* and not *alimentamento*.
