Arabic, chapter 373: Taking a stand.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
انحاز (inḥāza) — to take sides.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to fall short, then the Arabic for to manage to.

You'll want to know: انحاز.
انحاز — inḥāza — "to take sides".
انحاز الحكم إلى الفريق الآخر. inḥāza al-ḥakam ilā al-farīq al-ākhar. The referee took the side of the other team.
Present: ينحاز, yanḥāz.
It takes إلى: انحاز (inḥāza) إلى ….

The word, taken apart.
انحاز (inḥāza) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-w-z, "to draw together, to gather" (Lane). It is Form VII, to draw oneself aside to one party: to take sides.

تمسك — tamassaka — "to hold on to".
تمسك الرجل برأيه. tamassaka ar-rajul bi-raʾyih. The man held on to his opinion.
Present: يتمسك, yatamassak.
It takes بـ: تمسك (tamassaka) بـ ….

The word, taken apart.
تمسك (tamassaka) is built on the native Arabic root m-s-k, "to hold, to grasp" (Lane). It is Form V, to keep a firm hold: to cling to a thing. أمسك, to hold, is from the same root.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: حسم.
حسم — ḥasama — "to resolve; to settle".
حسم المدير المسألة في يوم واحد. ḥasama al-mudīr al-masʾala fī yawm wāḥid. The manager settled the matter in a single day.
Present: يحسم, yaḥsim.

The word, taken apart.
حسم (ḥasama) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-s-m, "to cut off" (Lane). Ḥasama is a Form I verb: to cut a question off, to settle it for good.
