You'll want to know: zéro.
zéro — "zero".

The word, taken apart.
zéro from Italian zero from Medieval Latin zephirum, from Arabic sifr, "empty" — borrowed. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
soixante-dix — seventy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for zero, then the French for sixty.

You'll want to know: soixante-dix.
soixante-dix — "seventy". Literally "sixty-ten".

The word, taken apart.
soixante-dix — "sixty-ten": soixante, "sixty", and dix, "ten". France counts the seventies on from sixty.

Lesson 4 of 5.
quatre-vingt-dix — ninety.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sixty, then the French for seventy.

You'll want to know: quatre-vingt-dix.
quatre-vingt-dix — "ninety". Literally "four-twenties-ten".

The word, taken apart.
quatre-vingt-dix — "four-twenties-ten": quatre, vingt and dix. Old French often counted in twenties.
