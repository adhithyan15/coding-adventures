## You'll want to know: yíyàng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C94-YIYANG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*yíyàng* — "the same; alike".

> *wǒmen bù yíyàng* — We're not the same.

> *wǒmen yíyàng dà* — We're the same age.

一 before the fall of yàng rises: *yí*. Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*yíyàng* is built from yī 'one' and yàng 'a pattern; a kind': one pattern. yī is inherited from Middle Chinese *'jit* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); yàng has no reading in the Guangyun, so no dictionary we use records an older form of it, and its history is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C92-YE-01, ZH-LEX-C94-DOU-01] -->

In *tāmen yě dōu hěn hǎo*, 也 comes before 都: too, and all of them. *yíyàng* + an adjective says how two things match: *yíyàng dà*, the same size or the same age.
