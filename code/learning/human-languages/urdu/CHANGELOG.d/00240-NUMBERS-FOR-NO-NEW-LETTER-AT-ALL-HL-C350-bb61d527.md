## Numbers, for no new letter at all (HL-C350)

Urdu taught **no numeral** across 134 lessons. **ایک** and **دو** both returned
zero occurrences as words — the two raw matches for **دو** were inside **دوست**,
"friend" — so a track with fourteen verbs, a weather chapter, a repair kit and
an oblique case could not count to one, and an A1 candidate's age, price and
telephone number were all out of reach. Chapters 28–32 add 25 lessons and close
it.

- **Twelve cardinals** — *ek, do, tīn, chār, pānch, chhe, sāt, āṭh, nau, das,
  bīs, sau* — one to a lesson, each with the Indo-European root that ties it to
  a word the reader already says.
- **It cost NO NEW LETTER.** A census of the track's Perso-Arabic characters
  before designing anything showed every sign in all twelve words, and in all
  four ordinals, already on the page. The largest lexical gap in the file was
  among the cheapest to close.
- **The numerals are where Urdu did NOT borrow from Persian**, and the track's
  own two-roads thread finally has a clean case: *garmī* and *sardī* came in
  from Persian, but Persian's *yek* was as available as Persian's *sard* and
  Urdu declined it. *sāt* against Persian *haft* is the proof, because Iranian
  turned every word-initial *s* into an *h* and Indo-Aryan did not.
- **The teens are NOT here, and the file says why.** *gyārah, bārah, terah,
  chaudah, pandrah, solah, satrah, aṭhārah, unnīs* wear the ten down
  differently in almost every one: nine words, not one rule — the exact
  opposite of Persian, whose teens the same campaign closed in a single chapter
  because they are a unit in front of *dah*. `UR-A1-Q-01`'s probe names
  `UR-GRAMMAR-TEENS-IRREGULAR` rather than nine lexical atoms it would
  otherwise be inventing.
- **The ten Urdu digits** (chapter 31), grouped by what actually trips a
  reader: the **direction** (a number runs LEFT TO RIGHT inside a right-to-left
  line — no earlier chapter has had to say this), the one-tooth pair **۲/۳**,
  the three shapes **۴ ۵ ۶** that Urdu draws differently from Arabic *even
  though the two scripts share an alphabet*, and the **۷/۸** mirror. The
  inventory's own note predicted this fix; the chapter is what it asked for.
- **Ordinals** (chapter 32): three irregular forms at the bottom — *pehlā*, not
  built from *ek* at all, then *dūsrā* and *tīsrā*, where the number comes back
  with **-rā** — and one ending, **-vāṅ**, for everything above four.
- **Exam coverage 130/234 (56%) → 134/234 (57%)**, closing `UR-A1-Q-01`,
  `UR-A1-Q-02`, `UR-A1-Q-03` and `UR-A1-FUN-53`. Both halves of that
  measurement were falsified before shipping: a fabricated atom id and a
  removed probe each break the suite.
- **Zero reinforcement debt created.** Every atom the tranche introduces meets
  R1, R2 and R3, verified atom by atom. The +67 reinforcement misses are all
  pre-existing debt that growing the track from 134 to 159 lessons made
  judgeable for the first time — R4 alone goes 96 → 143. Script-closure
  violations (4), never-taught glyphs (20), atoms never revisited (3), atom and
  glyph spikes, payoff surprises and duration violations are unchanged.
- **One forward reference created, and it is a real observation rather than a
  defect**: 3 → 4. Teaching **چھ**, six, makes the earlier emphasised **چھ**
  inside **پوچھنا** ("to ask") a forward reference, because that syllable is
  the number. Recorded rather than hidden.
