Spanish, chapter 457: La farmacia de guardia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el medicamento — what the doctor ordered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el médico and la farmacia. One writes it down, the other hands it over.

You'll want to know: el medicamento.
el medicamento — the medicine, the drug: the formal word, and the one on the packaging.
Un medicamento con receta — a prescription medicine. Este medicamento no lo tenemos — we don't have that one in.
In speech people say la medicina as readily, and for a single pill la pastilla. Medicamento is what the box, the leaflet and the pharmacist will use.

Grammar Lens: -mento and -miento are the same ending.
You know -miento from el seguimiento. -mento is the same Latin ending arriving by the other road:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
worn down by speech: el seguimiento. lifted from the page: el medicamento.
worn down by speech: el pensamiento. lifted from the page: el documento.
Both are masculine, so nothing is lost either way. What the spelling tells you is the register: a -miento noun is usually everyday, a -mento noun usually formal or technical. The pattern held for rueda and rotativo, and it holds here.

The word, taken apart.
Latin medicus, a doctor — and behind it medēri, to heal, to look after.
So el médico and el medicamento are one word: the healer, and the thing that does the healing. The family runs wide — la medicina, remedio, and English remedy, medical, meditate.
That last one is worth a second, and worth handling carefully. An older root does sit behind medeor, and modus, moderate and meditate are independent evidence for it. What you should not do is gloss that root as "measure" and then offer a doctor's judgement as the proof — that argues in a circle, as the lesson on el médico has already said. The cousins are real; the story about why is not established.

Lesson 2 of 5.
encargar — ask them to get it in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el cargo and el pedido. Put en- on the front of the first and you get a verb.

You'll want to know: encargar.
encargar — to order something in, to ask for a thing to be got.
¿Me lo puede encargar? — can you order it in for me? Lo tengo encargado — it's on order. Encargar una tarta — to order a cake.
With -se de it means to take charge of something: yo me encargo de la comida — I'll see to the food. De eso se encarga la empresa — the company handles that.

Grammar Lens: encargar is not pedir.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
what is happening: asking for what is there. the verb: pedir.
what is happening: asking them to get what is not. the verb: encargar.
In a restaurant you pides a salad, because it is on the menu. At a chemist's you encargas a medicine, because it has to be brought in. Pedir takes something off a list; encargar creates a job for somebody.
That is also why encargarse de means taking responsibility: the load has been put on you.

The word, taken apart.
en- plus cargar, and cargar is the carricare from the lesson on el cargo — to load a cart.
So encargar is to load a task onto somebody, and every sense follows from that one picture. Order a cake: the baker now carries it. Take charge: you are carrying it. El encargado — the person in charge — is the one with the load on.
English has the same metaphor in charge: you are in charge, you take charge, somebody is charged with a duty. Two languages reached for a cart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la guardia — the one that stays open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say guardar and la farmacia. Somebody is keeping watch tonight, and it is one chemist in the town.

You'll want to know: la guardia.
la guardia — duty, watch: the turn of being the one available.
Farmacia de guardia. Servicio nocturno.
La farmacia de guardia — the duty chemist, the one open when the rest are shut, posted on every pharmacy door. Estar de guardia — to be on call. El médico de guardia — the doctor on duty.
El guardia with the masculine article is a different thing: a police officer. The article carries the whole distinction.

Grammar Lens: one word, two genders, two meanings.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
la guardia means the duty, the watch.
el guardia means the officer keeping it.
Spanish does this with a small set of words, and the rule inside it is consistent: the feminine is the abstract thing, the masculine is the person who does it. La guía is the guidebook, el guía is the person showing you round.
So la farmacia de guardia is a chemist on watch, and un guardia en la puerta is a man standing at it.

The word, taken apart.
Germanic wardōn, to watch over — the same root as guardar, which you already have for keeping and putting away.
Spanish took a shelf of Germanic words from the Visigoths and from Frankish, and they are recognisable by the gu- where English has plain w-:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
guardar means ward.
guerra means war.
guante means (glove, from the same Frankish want).
English got guard back from French, so guard and ward are the same Germanic word arriving twice — once directly, once round by way of Paris.

Lesson 4 of 5.
(el medicamento, encargar, la guardia) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say all three: the medicine, to order in, the duty rota.

Grammar Lens: the article doing the work.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
feminine — the thing: la guardia — the watch. masculine — the person: el guardia — the officer.
feminine — the thing: la guía — the guidebook. masculine — the person: el guía — the guide.
feminine — the thing: la policía — the force. masculine — the person: el policía — the officer.
One pattern, and it is worth having in advance: the feminine names the abstract thing or the body, the masculine names a person doing it. A Spanish speaker hears the article and knows which you meant before the noun arrives.
So la farmacia de guardia has nothing to do with a man on a door.

Grammar Lens: two of the three were relatives.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
new word: el medicamento. what it was built on: el médico — healer and the thing that heals.
new word: encargar. what it was built on: el cargo, and el carrito under that.
new word: la guardia. what it was built on: guardar, from Germanic wardōn.
All three, in fact. Encargar is the clearest: a cart, then a load, then a charge on a bill, then a task loaded onto somebody. Four lessons apart and one picture throughout.

Lesson 5 of 5.
(en la farmacia) — read it, then do it yourself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la guardia and el medicamento. The door below tells you where to find one of them after hours.

Guided Practice.

1 — en la puerta.
Horario: 9:00 a 14:00 y 17:00 a 20:30. Fuera de ese horario, farmacia de guardia en la calle Mayor.
2 — en el mostrador.
Ese medicamento se nos ha terminado. Se lo encargo y lo tiene mañana por la tarde, si le parece.
[pause 3 seconds]
[your turn — say: where to go at nine in the evening]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what the pharmacist offers to do]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: when the medicine will be there]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Why it's said this way.
Se lo encargo is the sentence to keep whole. Encargo rather than pido, because the thing is not there to be asked for — it has to be brought in. And se lo is two pronouns stacked: lo is the medicine, se is you. Spanish puts the person first and the thing second, which is the reverse of English.
Si le parece — "if that suits you" — is how an offer is softened at a counter. It does the same work as the cómoda of the delivery card, and both leave the decision with the customer without asking a question.
Notice too that the door says fuera de ese horario — the same fuera de frame as fuera de servicio, but here with a determiner. Fuera de servicio is a fixed phrase and takes nothing; fuera de ese horario points back at the hours printed two lines above, and pointing needs ese.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines at a chemist's counter: ask for a medicine, hear that it has run out, ask them to order it in, and ask when it will be there. Then say where the duty chemist is.
Then check yourself: did you use encargar rather than pedir, and did the pronouns come out as se lo?
