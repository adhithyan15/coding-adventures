Portuguese, chapter 523: Capítulo a capítulo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o capítulo — chapter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sculpt, then the Portuguese for sculptor.

You'll want to know: o capítulo.
o capítulo — "chapter".
Leio um capítulo antes de dormir. I read a chapter before I go to sleep.
Also an episode of a soap: o último capítulo.

The word, taken apart.
capítulo is a learned borrowing from Late Latin capitulum "a chapter" (EtymDB), a little "head" of text, from caput "head".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: o romancista.
o romancista — "novelist".
O romancista apresentou o novo livro na feira. The novelist presented his new book at the fair.
Built on o romance "a novel". A woman is a romancista.

The word, taken apart.
romancista is built on romance "a novel", from Old Occitan romans (EtymDB), a tale told in the everyday Romance tongue rather than in Latin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
reler — to reread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for novelist, then the Portuguese for biography.

You'll want to know: reler.
reler — "to reread".
Gosto de reler os livros da minha infância. I like rereading the books of my childhood.
Conjugated like ler: releio, relês, relê.

The word, taken apart.
reler is built from re- "again" and ler "to read", which is inherited from Latin legere "to gather, to read" (Lewis & Short).
