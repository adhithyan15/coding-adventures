Latin, chapter 550: Readers and booksellers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
litterātus, litterāta, litterātum — learned, well-read.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a public reading, then the Latin for a listener.

You'll want to know: litterātus, litterāta, litterātum.
litterātus, litterāta, litterātum — "learned, well-read".
Vir litterātus multōs librōs habet. The learned man has many books.
From litterae — letters, learning. English literate.

The word, taken apart.
litterātus is built on littera "a letter" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): lettered, learned. English literate comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bibliopōla, bibliopōlae — a bookseller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a listener, then the Latin for learned.

You'll want to know: bibliopōla, bibliopōlae.
bibliopōla, bibliopōlae — "a bookseller".
Bibliopōla novōs librōs in tabernā vendit. The bookseller sells new books in his shop.
Masculine, first declension, from Greek biblion 'book' + pōlein 'to sell'.

The word, taken apart.
bibliopōla was borrowed from Greek bibliopoles, built on biblion "book" and poles "seller" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
lēctor, lēctōris — a reader.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for learned, then the Latin for a bookseller.

You'll want to know: lēctor, lēctōris.
lēctor, lēctōris — "a reader".
Lēctor dominō librum legit. The reader reads a book to his master.
Masculine, third declension, from legō. Rich houses kept a slave to read aloud.

The word, taken apart.
lēctor is built on legō "to read" with -tor (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English lecture comes from the related lēctūra.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ēvolvō, ēvolvere — to unroll; to read (a book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a reader, then the Latin for to read through.

You'll want to know: ēvolvō, ēvolvere.
ēvolvō, ēvolvere — "to unroll; to read (a book)".
Discipulus librum ēvolvit et legit. The pupil unrolls the book and reads.
ēvolvō, ēvolvere, ēvolvī, ēvolūtum. Books were rolls, so to read was to unroll. English evolve.

The word, taken apart.
ēvolvō is built on ē- "out" and volvō "to roll" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to unroll a book. English evolve comes from it.
