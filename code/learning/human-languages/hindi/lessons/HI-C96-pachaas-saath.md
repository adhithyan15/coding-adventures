# पचास साठ — where the pattern stops helping

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C96-TENS-01, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **बीस, तीस, चालीस**. Listen to the ending they share.

## You'll want to know: पचास, साठ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C96-TENS-03, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-04]; assesses=[] -->

**पचास** (*pacās*) — fifty.

**साठ** (*sāṭh*) — sixty.

## Grammar Lens: one keeps the ending, one drops it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C96-TENS-03, HI-LEX-C96-TENS-04] -->

| number | word | the shared ending |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | चालीस | yes |
| 50 | पचास | a worn version |
| 60 | साठ | gone |

**पचास** still leans on **पाँच**, the five you have counted since the opening
chapters, and it still carries a trace of that ending. **साठ** leans on **छह**
and keeps nothing else.

That is the shape of the rest of this chapter: the words get less helpful as
the numbers get bigger. Knowing that in advance is worth more than a rule that
would not hold.

**पचास** is the one to hold on to hardest. It is the price on a tea stall sign
and the age of somebody's father, and it is the most common of the four so far.
