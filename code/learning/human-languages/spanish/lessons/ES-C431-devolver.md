# devolver — to turn it back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01, ES-LEX-PAGAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el pedido*. Say *pagar*. The order came, you paid, and it is
the wrong size. Say what you do next.

## You'll want to know: devolver
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02]; assesses=[] -->

**devolver** — to give back, to return a thing.

A thing, not a place. You *devolver* a jumper, a book, a borrowed charger. For
going back somewhere yourself, Spanish uses **volver** on its own.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02] -->

**de-** back, plus **volver**, to turn. To turn a thing back.

That is the whole word, and it tells you the grammar too: **devolver** turns
exactly like **volver**, which you already say. The **o** becomes **ue** when
the stress lands on it:

| form | |
|---|---|
| yo devuelvo | I give back |
| nosotros devolvemos | we give back |

The stress moves off the **o** in *devolvemos*, and the **ue** goes with it.
This is the same pattern as *poder / puedo* and *dormir / duermo*; learning it
once buys you a whole family of verbs.

## Grammar Lens: the two ways back
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

| you mean | you say |
|---|---|
| I return the order | devuelvo el pedido |
| I return home | vuelvo a casa |

English uses *return* for both and lets context sort it out. Spanish splits
them, and the split is not a nuisance — it is the prefix doing exactly what it
says.
