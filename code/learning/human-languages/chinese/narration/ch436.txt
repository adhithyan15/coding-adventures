Lesson 1 of 6.
dìxíng — terrain; lie of the land.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for spectacular, then the Mandarin for to come down a mountain.

You'll want to know: dìxíng.
dìxíng — "terrain; lie of the land".
zhège dìqū de dìxíng hěn tèbié — The land in this region has an unusual shape.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dìxíng is built from 地 'the earth' and xíng 'a shape', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dijH and heng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the look of a thing.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to come down a mountain, then the Mandarin for the lie of the land.

You'll want to know: 南方.
南方 — nánfāng — "the south of the country; southern China".
dōngtiān wǒmen zuò huǒchē qù nánfāng — In winter we take the train down south.
The new character, in pieces: 方 (fāng) — four strokes: a dot, a long bar, a stroke that turns down and hooks, and a long stroke falling to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
南方 (nánfāng) is built from 南 'south' and 方 (fāng) 'a quarter', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nom and pjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the four quarters of the world.

Lesson 3 of 6.
方 (fāng) — a dot over a bar, a hook and a long stroke.

Script — a dot over a bar, a hook and a long stroke.
方 (fāng).
One piece, four strokes. Count the strokes this time: there is only one piece.
A dot on top, in the middle.
A long bar under it.
From under the bar, a stroke that runs a little to the right, turns down and hooks to the left at the foot. Then a long stroke falling to the left, also from under the bar. These last two are made as in 万.
1 + 1 + 2 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 方 (fāng), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 方 (fāng)? (Four.) What comes first? (A dot.) Which stroke hooks? (The third: it runs right, turns down and hooks.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
fāngyán — dialect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the south of the country, then the Mandarin for the north of the country.

You'll want to know: fāngyán.
fāngyán — "dialect".
zhèr de rén shuō fāngyán wǒ tīng bu dǒng — People here speak a dialect I can't understand.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fāngyán is built from 方 (fāng) 'a region' and yán 'speech', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjang and ngjon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'speech'. The Guangyun itself quotes an old book called the Fangyan, a record of the speech of the regions.
