# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-ESM-E-MAN-AST, FA-LEX-KHUBAM-REPLY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked. A task you can
only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

This page will not show you any copyable Persian answer. Put away the earlier
lessons, romanization, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers.

## You'll want to know — what the A1 paper is
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-ESM-E-MAN-AST, FA-LEX-KHUBAM-REPLY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] This book found no widely used international A1 writing paper for
Persian. **SAMFA**, the Iranian exam this book names as its far destination, is
one academic test: its writing section is two essays in one hour, and the
provider notice this book checked maps it to no CEFR level at all. So there is
no SAMFA A1 paper to rehearse.

[PAUSE 2s] This book's A1 writing paper is therefore its own, modelled on what
the Council of Europe's framework expects of A1 writing: a form with personal
details, and a short, simple message. It runs **20 minutes**: a practical form,
then a **30 to 40 word** Persian message to a named reader. Today you rehearse
the form, for three minutes.

## Writing — timed assessment production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-ESM-E-MAN-AST, FA-LEX-KHUBAM-REPLY, FA-LEX-MADAR, FA-LEX-CHAY, FA-LEX-KETAB, FA-LEX-KHODAHAFEZ] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**One practical form — three minutes**

Set a **three-minute timer**. Complete **all six fields in Persian script** for
a new neighbourhood reading group. Write one short answer in every field, right
to left. Start the timer when you begin the first field; leave any unfinished
field as evidence when it rings.

1. Your name
2. How you are today
3. One person in your family
4. One drink you would like at the meeting
5. One thing you will bring
6. A goodbye

When the three-minute timer rings, **stop**. Record the number of completed
fields. An honest short attempt is more useful than a perfect untimed one.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-ESM-E-MAN-AST, FA-LEX-KHODAHAFEZ] -->

Spend at most one minute looking at this first attempt. Check the dots and the
joins in one field, note one field to practise next, and do not rewrite the
timed answer. Keep the page as evidence.

[PAUSE 2s] One fact about the full assessment changes how you study. Its four
papers are each out of 100, and you need **60 on every one** in the same
sitting. Nothing averages: strong reading cannot carry weak writing. And one of
writing's four scored dimensions is Persian orthographic control, so a missing
dot costs marks.

This checkpoint is **not the complete A1 writing paper**, which still needs its
mocks, rubric, calibration, and human review. It does not establish A1
attainment, readiness, completeness, or human validation.
