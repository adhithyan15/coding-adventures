Spanish, chapter 659: Barcas y marineros.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la lancha — the launch, the motorboat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the quay, then the Spanish for to disembark.

You'll want to know: la lancha.
la lancha — "the launch, the motorboat".
Fuimos a la cueva en lancha. We went to the cave by motorboat.
La lancha has a motor; la barca is often rowed.

The word, taken apart.
lancha came through Portuguese lancha and is usually traced to a Malay word meaning "swift".

Lesson 2 of 5.
el velero — the sailing boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to disembark, then the Spanish for the launch.

You'll want to know: el velero.
el velero — "the sailing boat".
El velero sale del puerto con poco viento. The sailing boat leaves the harbour in a light wind.
Built on la vela, the sail.

The word, taken apart.
velero is built on vela "sail", which comes from Latin vēla, the plural of vēlum "a cloth, a covering" (Lewis & Short), used for sails.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el marinero — the sailor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sailing boat, then the Spanish for to row.

You'll want to know: el marinero.
el marinero — "the sailor".
Mi abuelo era marinero. My grandfather was a sailor.
As an adjective it means seafaring: un pueblo marinero.

The word, taken apart.
marinero is built on marina (EtymDB), and through it on mar "sea", inherited from Latin mare.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to row, then the Spanish for the sailor.

You'll want to know: el capitán.
el capitán — "the captain".
El capitán habla con los pasajeros. The captain is talking to the passengers.
Of a ship, a plane or a team.

The word, taken apart.
capitán comes from Late Latin capitāneus "chief" (EtymDB), built on caput "head".
