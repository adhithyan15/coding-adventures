Gujarati, chapter 348: A Case in Court.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ન્યાયાધીશ (nyāyādhīś) — a judge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for in force, then the Gujarati for to defy.

You'll want to know: ન્યાયાધીશ.
ન્યાયાધીશ — nyāyādhīś — "a judge".
ન્યાયાધીશે બંને બાજુની વાત ધ્યાનથી સાંભળી. nyāyādhīśe banne bājunī vāt dhyānthī sā̃bhḷī. The judge listened carefully to both sides.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
ન્યાયાધીશ (nyāyādhīś) is built from Sanskrit nyāya, "rule, justice", and adhīśa, "a lord, a master" (Monier-Williams): the master of justice.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ફટકારવું (phaṭkārvũ) — to hit hard; to impose (a fine or a sentence).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an appeal, then the Gujarati for a ruling.

You'll want to know: ફટકારવું.
ફટકારવું — phaṭkārvũ — "to hit hard; to impose (a fine or a sentence)".
છોકરાએ બેટથી દડો મેદાનની બહાર ફટકાર્યો. chokrāe beṭthī daḍo medānnī bahār phaṭkāryo. The boy whacked the ball out of the ground with the bat.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે દડો દૂર ફટકાર્યો, teṇe daḍo dūr phaṭkāryo.

The word, taken apart.
ફટકારવું (phaṭkārvũ) CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to slash", under phaṭ, "a sudden movement", a sound-word like "crack!" (CDIAL 9038); a court that "hits" someone with a fine uses the same verb.
