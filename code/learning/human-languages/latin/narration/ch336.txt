Latin, chapter 336: Mūnus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gladiātor, gladiātōris — a gladiator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a racing team, then the Latin for of the circus.

You'll want to know: gladiātor, gladiātōris.
gladiātor, gladiātōris — "a gladiator".
Gladiātōrēs in harēnā pugnābant. Gladiators were fighting in the arena.
Masculine, 3rd declension, from gladius, 'sword' (taught). Most were slaves or prisoners; a few were volunteers.

The word, taken apart.
gladiātor is built on gladius, "sword" (EtymDB): the swordsman. English gladiator is the same word.

Lanista novōs gladiātōrēs ēmit. The trainer bought new gladiators.
Masculine, 1st declension. He owned and trained a troop; in Cicero the word is an insult.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mūnus, mūneris — a public show; a gift; a duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a gladiator, then the Latin for a trainer of gladiators.

You'll want to know: mūnus, mūneris.
mūnus, mūneris — "a public show; a gift; a duty".
Ad mūnus tōta urbs vēnit. The whole city came to the show.
Neuter, 3rd declension. First a duty, then a gift, and so the gladiator show given to the people. English munificent.

The word, taken apart.
mūnus is inherited from Proto-Italic moinos, "service" (EtymDB): a duty, then a gift, then a show. English munificent comes from its family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
vēnātiō, vēnātiōnis — a hunt; a wild-beast show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a trainer of gladiators, then the Latin for a public show.

You'll want to know: vēnātiō, vēnātiōnis.
vēnātiō, vēnātiōnis — "a hunt; a wild-beast show".
Māne vēnātiō erat, post merīdiem gladiātōrēs. In the morning there was a wild-beast hunt, in the afternoon the gladiators.
Feminine, from vēnor, 'to hunt' (taught). In the arena, hunters fought lions and bears.

The word, taken apart.
vēnātiō is built on vēnor, "to hunt" (Lewis & Short): hunting, and the meat of the hunt. English venison comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bēstia, bēstiae — a beast, a wild animal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a public show, then the Latin for a hunt.

You'll want to know: bēstia, bēstiae.
bēstia, bēstiae — "a beast, a wild animal".
Bēstiae ingentēs in harēnam missae sunt. Huge beasts were sent into the arena.
Feminine. A creature without reason, as opposed to people. English beast, through French.

The word, taken apart.
bēstia is a Latin word whose origin is not settled; Lewis & Short suggest a link with fera, "wild beast". English beast comes from it through French.
