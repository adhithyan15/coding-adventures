German, chapter 308: Prost!.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
anstoßen — to clink glasses, to drink to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the speech, then the German for the toast.

You'll want to know: anstoßen.
anstoßen — "to clink glasses, to drink to".
Um Mitternacht stießen alle an. At midnight everyone clinked glasses.
Separable: wir stoßen an. Auf jemanden anstoßen — to drink to someone.

The word, taken apart.
anstoßen is built on stoßen "to push, to bump", from Proto-Germanic stautaną (EtymDB), with an: glass knocks on glass.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Sekt — the sparkling wine.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the German for the toast, then the German for to clink glasses.

You'll want to know: der Sekt.
der Sekt — "the sparkling wine".
Zur Feier gab es eine Flasche Sekt. For the celebration there was a bottle of sparkling wine.
German sparkling wine: ein Glas Sekt.

The word, taken apart.
Sekt is usually traced to French vin sec or Italian vino secco "dry wine", from Latin siccus "dry" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jubeln — to cheer, to rejoice.

You'll want to know: jubeln.
jubeln — "to cheer, to rejoice".
Die Leute jubelten, als die Mannschaft gewann. People cheered when the team won.
Regular: jubelte, hat gejubelt.

The word, taken apart.
jubeln is built on Jubel "rejoicing", from Middle High German jūbel, which came from Latin iūbilus "a shout of joy" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Beifall — the applause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the sparkling wine, then the German for to cheer.

You'll want to know: der Beifall.
der Beifall — "the applause".
Am Ende gab es langen Beifall. At the end there was long applause.
No plural. Viel Beifall bekommen — to get a lot of applause.

The word, taken apart.
Beifall joins bei "with" and Fall, from fallen "to fall", Old High German val (EtymDB): it first meant siding with someone, then applause.

Lesson 5 of 5.
applaudieren — to applaud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to cheer, then the German for the applause.

You'll want to know: applaudieren.
applaudieren — "to applaud".
Die Leute applaudierten zehn Minuten lang. People applauded for ten minutes.
With the dative: Sie applaudierten dem Sänger.

The word, taken apart.
applaudieren is a learned borrowing from Latin applaudere "to strike upon, to applaud", built on plaudere (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.
