Mandarin Chinese, chapter 440: A Mountain Village.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gǎnjí — to go to market day; to go to a country market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a duck, then the Mandarin for to take one's mind off things.

You'll want to know: gǎnjí.
gǎnjí — "to go to market day; to go to a country market".
nǎinai měi ge xīngqī qù gǎnjí — Grandma goes to market day every week.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnjí is built from gǎn 'to hurry to' and jí 'a gathering'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese dzip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to gather'; no dictionary we use records an older form of gǎn in this sense, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 山村.
山村 — shāncūn — "mountain village".
tā zhù zài yí ge xiǎo shāncūn — He lives in a little mountain village.
The new character, in pieces: 村 (cūn) — seven strokes in two pieces: on the left the tree piece of 机, four strokes; on the right the right piece of 对, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
山村 (shāncūn) is built from 山 'a mountain' and 村 (cūn) 'a village', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srean and tshwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a farmstead.

Lesson 3 of 6.
村 (cūn) — the tree piece, then the right piece of 对.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. 机 对.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — jī, duì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the tree piece, then the right piece of 对.
村 (cūn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the tree piece, four strokes, as on the left of 机: a bar, a vertical, a stroke falling to the left, and a short dot to the right.
The right piece, three strokes, as on the right of 对: a bar, a vertical with a hook at the foot, and a dot to the left of it.
4 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 村 (cūn), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 村 (cūn)? (Seven.) What is the left piece? (The tree piece, as in 机.) Where does the last dot go? (To the left of the hooked vertical.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 村 (cūn), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 4 of 6.
村长 (cūnzhǎng) — village head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go to market day, then the Mandarin for a mountain village.

You'll want to know: 村长.
村长 — cūnzhǎng — "village head".
cūnzhǎng dài wǒmen qù tā jiā — The village head takes us to his house.

The word, taken apart.
村长 (cūnzhǎng) is built from 村 (cūn) 'a village' and 长 'the head', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshwon and trjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'big'; in this sense 长 is said zhǎng, not cháng.
