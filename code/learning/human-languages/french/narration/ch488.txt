French, chapter 488: Câlins et confidences.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le câlin — cuddle, hug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for tenderness, then the French for to stroke.

You'll want to know: le câlin.
le câlin — "cuddle, hug".
Avant de dormir, la petite veut un câlin. Before sleeping, the little girl wants a cuddle.
Faire un câlin à quelqu'un — to give someone a cuddle. In Québec also un colleux.

The word, taken apart.
le câlin is built on câliner. This is usually traced to a western dialect verb for resting in the shade or the warmth, which goes back to Latin calēre "to be warm" (Lewis & Short); the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la complicité — closeness, bond; complicity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for cuddle, then the French for to cuddle.

You'll want to know: la complicité.
la complicité — "closeness, bond; complicity".
Il y a une grande complicité entre les deux sœurs. There is a strong bond between the two sisters.
Usually warm: a close understanding. In law, complicity in a crime.

The word, taken apart.
la complicité is built on complice "partner, accomplice" (EtymDB), from Late Latin complex, complicis "closely joined", built on plicāre "to fold" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
chuchoter — to whisper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to cuddle, then the French for closeness.

You'll want to know: chuchoter.
chuchoter — "to whisper".
Les deux amies chuchotent au fond de la classe. The two friends are whispering at the back of the classroom.
Un chuchotement — a whisper. Murmurer is close.

The word, taken apart.
chuchoter is usually described as an imitative word: it copies the soft sound of whispering, and the origin is not settled beyond that.

Lesson 5 of 5.
confier — to entrust; (se confier) to confide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for closeness, then the French for to whisper.

You'll want to know: confier.
confier — "to entrust; (se confier) to confide".
Je me confie toujours à ma meilleure amie. I always confide in my best friend.
Confier un secret à quelqu'un; se confier à quelqu'un.

The word, taken apart.
confier goes back to Latin cōnfīdere "to trust fully", reshaped after fier; both are built on fīdere "to trust" (Lewis & Short).
