Gujarati, chapter 514: Something Has Come Loose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઢીલું (ḍhīlũ) — loose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for brass, then the Gujarati for scrap.

You'll want to know: ઢીલું.
ઢીલું — ḍhīlũ — "loose".
આ ખુરશીનો એક પગ ઢીલો છે. ā khurśīno ek pag ḍhīlo che. One leg of this chair is loose.

The word, taken apart.
ઢીલું (ḍhīlũ) comes from ḍhila, "slack", a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "loose, slack" (CDIAL 5590). It agrees with its noun: પગ ઢીલો, ગાંઠ ઢીલી.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

કસવું — kasvũ — "to tighten".
અરુણે ખુરશીના બધા સ્ક્રૂ કસ્યા. Aruṇe khurśīnā badhā skrū kasyā. Arun tightened all the screws of the chair.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે સ્ક્રૂ કસ્યો, teṇe skrū kasyo.

The word, taken apart.
કસવું (kasvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit kárṣati, "draws, pulls", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to tighten" (CDIAL 2908).

ખીંટી — khī̃ṭī — "a wall peg".
મારી છત્રી ખીંટી પર છે. mārī chatrī khī̃ṭī par che. My umbrella is on the peg.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ખીંટી (khī̃ṭī) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit kīla, "a stake, a peg", with the Gujarati word glossed "a stopper" (CDIAL 3202); on a wall it is a peg to hang things on.

મીરાએ થેલી ખીંટી પર લટકાવી. Mīrāe thelī khī̃ṭī par laṭkāvī. Mira hung the bag on the peg.

The word, taken apart.
લટકાવવું (laṭkāvvũ) is built from લટકવું, "to hang, to dangle", with the causative -āv-: to make something hang. CDIAL files the family under laṭṭa, "hanging" (CDIAL 10918).
