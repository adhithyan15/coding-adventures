# Gehen Sie geradeaus — following the answer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WIE-KOMME-ICH-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ZUM-ZUR-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask the way to the station. (*Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum
Bahnhof?*)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the stranger answers, and you have to follow.

## You'll want to know: links
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-LINKS-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C108-RICHTUNG108-05, GE-LEX-C95-WO-05] -->

You have **geradeaus**, straight on, and **rechts**, right. The missing
one is **links**, "left": *links*, with a short *i*.

> *geradeaus* — straight on
> *rechts* — right
> *links* — left

German speakers often add *nach* in front: *nach links*, *nach rechts*,
"to the left, to the right". Both ways are standard.

## Grammar Lens: the Sie command, verb first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SIE-IMPERATIV-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DU-SIE-02, GE-LEX-C107-ORT107-04, GE-LEX-C107-ORT107-03, GE-LEX-C109-RICHTUNG109-05, GE-LEX-C110-RICHTUNG110-04, GE-LEX-BIS-01, GE-LEX-DANN-01] -->

A stranger will tell you the way with *Sie*. The polite command puts the
verb first, in its *Sie* form, and *Sie* straight after it:

> *Gehen Sie geradeaus.* — Go straight on.
> *Gehen Sie bis zur Ampel, dann links.* — Go as far as the lights, then left.
> *Nehmen Sie die U-Bahn.* — Take the underground.

*Bis zur Ampel* is *bis* + *zur*: as far as the lights. *Bis zur Kreuzung*,
as far as the crossroads.

The answer may end with the place itself: *Der Bahnhof ist gleich
gegenüber*, the station is right opposite.

With a friend, the *du* command you met in chapter forty-six drops the
pronoun: *Geh geradeaus!* With a stranger, keep the *Sie*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C95-WO-05] -->

**links** comes from Middle High German *linc*, "left", and EtymDB 2.1
sets Old High German *linka*, "the left hand", beside it; further back its
origin is not settled. **rechts** is built on *recht*, which EtymDB traces
through Old High German *reht* to Proto-Germanic *\*rehtaz*, "right,
direct", the parent of English *right*. The *-s* at the end of both is
usually explained as an old adverb ending, the one that also sits in
*abends*, "in the evening".
