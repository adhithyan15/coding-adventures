# को — the word that was inside आपको

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **आपको**. Say it slowly, in two pieces, and you have already
said this lesson's word.

## You'll want to know: को
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05]; assesses=[] -->

**को** (*ko*) — "to," and the marker a definite or human object wears.

**आदमी को** — to the man, and also *the man* when he is the thing acted on.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04] -->

You met it fused. **आपको** is **आप + को**, and it was named as such when the
oblique pronouns arrived. Here it stands on its own, in front of a noun instead
of a pronoun, and it is the third postposition the track now has.

It does two jobs that English keeps apart:

| job | example |
|---|---|
| a recipient | आदमी को — *to* the man |
| a definite or human object | आदमी को — the man, as the thing acted on |

The second is the strange one to an English ear. English marks a direct object
by word order and nothing else. Hindi puts **को** on an object when it is a
**person** or a **particular** thing, and leaves it bare otherwise. Spanish does
the same with **a** before a human object, so the demand is not unique to Hindi
— only the marker is.

## Grammar Lens: three postpositions, one habit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01] -->

| postposition | what it marks |
|---|---|
| पर | at a point |
| से | from it, or by way of it |
| को | to it, or it as a definite object |

All three trail the noun. All three are one syllable. And all three do something
to the noun in front of them, which is what the next lesson is about.
