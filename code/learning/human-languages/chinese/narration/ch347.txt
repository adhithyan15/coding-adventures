Lesson 1 of 6.
zhǔyi — bright idea; idea of what to do.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to wrongly assume, then the Mandarin for to make a rough guess.

You'll want to know: zhǔyi.
zhǔyi — "bright idea; idea of what to do".
wǒ juéde zhège zhǔyi hěn hǎo — I think that's a good idea.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǔyi is built from zhǔ and 意 'intent', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyuX and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be in charge' and the second 'will'. The second is said light here.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 意思.
意思 — yìsi — "meaning; what something means".
这个字是什么意思 zhège zì shì shénme yìsi — What does this character mean?
The new character, in pieces: 思 (sī) — nine strokes in two pieces: on top a box with a cross inside it, five strokes; under it 心 xīn, the heart, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
意思 (yìsi) is built from 意 'intent' and 思 (sī) 'to think', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'iH and si (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'will' and the second 'to think of'. The second is said light here.

Script — a box with a cross, over the heart.
思 (sī).
Two pieces, top before bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, a box with a cross inside, five strokes. A short vertical down the left side. A horizontal along the top that turns down the right side. A short horizontal across the middle. A vertical down the middle. Then the bottom horizontal that closes the box.
The bottom piece, 心, four strokes, wider than the box: the left dot, the long curve along the foot that hooks up, the middle dot, the right dot.
5 + 4 = nine, and the last four your hand already knows.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

You'll want to know: 体会.
体会 — tǐhuì — "to know from experience; to come to understand".
wǒ néng tǐhuì nǐ de xīnqíng — I know how you feel.

The word, taken apart.
体会 (tǐhuì) is built from 体 'the body' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thejX and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the body' and the second 'to come together'. How the pair came to mean knowing from experience is not settled.
