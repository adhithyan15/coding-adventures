Mandarin Chinese, chapter 451: Specialities.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
chīsù — to eat no meat; to be vegetarian.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for authentic, then the Mandarin for a restaurant's signature dish.

You'll want to know: chīsù.
chīsù — "to eat no meat; to be vegetarian".
wǒ chīsù bù chī ròu — I'm vegetarian: I don't eat meat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chīsù is built from 吃 'to eat' and sù 'plain'. For the first the dictionary has khek, glossed 'to eat', under another way of writing it (there 吃 itself is kj+t, 'to stammer'), though khek would regularly give qì; the second is inherited from Middle Chinese suH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as plain white silk, and the sense 'meatless' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
特色 (tèsè) — special character; speciality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a restaurant's signature dish, then the Mandarin for to eat no meat.

You'll want to know: 特色.
特色 — tèsè — "special character; speciality".
zhè jiā fànguǎn de cài hěn yǒu tèsè — The food at this restaurant has a character all its own.
The new character, in pieces: 色 (sè) — six strokes in two pieces: on top a short stroke falling to the left and a small bar that turns down to the left; under them a bar that turns down, a short vertical, a bar, and a long stroke that runs down the left, along the foot and hooks up. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
特色 (tèsè) is built from 特 'single, special' and 色 (sè) 'a look', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dok and srik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the colour of the face.

Script — a small top piece, then a frame with a long hook.
色 (sè).
Two pieces, top before bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, two strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. Then a small bar that turns down to the left under it.
The bottom piece, four strokes. A bar that turns down on the right. A short vertical in the middle. A bar across under them. Last, a long stroke that runs down the left side, turns right along the foot and hooks up.
2 + 4 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 色 (sè), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 色 (sè)? (Six.) What is the last stroke? (A long stroke down the left that turns along the foot and hooks up.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
出色 (chūsè) — outstanding; excellent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to eat no meat, then the Mandarin for a special character of its own.

You'll want to know: 出色.
出色 — chūsè — "outstanding; excellent".
他的中文很出色 tā de Zhōngwén hěn chūsè — His spoken Chinese is outstanding.

The word, taken apart.
出色 (chūsè) is built from 出 'to stand out' and 色 (sè) 'a look', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and srik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); how the pair came to mean 'outstanding' is not settled.
