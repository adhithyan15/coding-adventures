# ahora · hoy — now and today

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MANANA, ES-LEX-HORA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Mañana* — tomorrow. *La hora* — the hour.

[PAUSE 1s] Two more time words, and both are hiding something you already know.

## The word, taken apart: ahora
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-AHORA, ES-ETYMON-AHORA-HAC-HORA]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HORA-01] -->

***Ahora*** — *now*. Look at what is sitting in the middle of it: ***hora***,
the word for *hour* you learned long ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Latin said ***hac hora*** — *at this hour*. Two words, worn together
into one, and the *h* in the middle went silent along the way. *Now* is
literally **this hour**.

## The word, taken apart: hoy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-HOY, ES-ETYMON-HOY-HODIE]; assesses=[] -->

***Hoy*** — *today*. Latin said ***hodie***, which was itself *hoc die*, *this
day*.

[PAUSE 2s] So the pair is symmetrical, and neither has parts any more: *this
hour* wore down to *ahora*, *this day* wore down to three letters. Spanish is
full of these — phrases that stopped being phrases.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PROGRESSIVE-ESTAR-GERUND, ES-GRAMMAR-NEAR-FUTURE-IR-A-INFINITIVE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] And now you can put a time on the tense you learned for the moment:

> ***Ahora*** *estoy comiendo.* — Right now, I'm eating.
> ***Hoy*** *voy a comer con ella.* — Today I'm going to eat with her.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice which tense each one takes. *Ahora* goes with the progressive,
because both mean *at this instant*. *Hoy* covers a whole day, so it takes the
ordinary forms — including the going-to future, which is how Spanish says the
thing English says with *I'm eating*.
