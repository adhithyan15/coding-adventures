Japanese, chapter 299: The King's Daughter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おうさま (ōsama) — a king.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a village, then the Japanese for a hard worker.

You'll want to know: おうさま.
おうさま — ōsama — "a king".
むかし、その くにに おうさまが いました。 — mukashi, sono kuni ni ōsama ga imashita — "Long ago that country had a king."

The word, taken apart.
おうさま (ōsama) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: ō, "king", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese hjwang), and sama, "polite suffix", is native Japanese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おひめさま (ohimesama) — a princess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a hard worker, then the Japanese for a king.

おひめさま — ohimesama — "a princess".
おうさまには うつくしい おひめさまが いました。 — ōsama ni wa utsukushii ohimesama ga imashita — "The king had a beautiful daughter."

The word, taken apart.
おひめさま (ohimesama) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from o-, "the polite prefix", hime, "princess", and sama, "the honorific" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
