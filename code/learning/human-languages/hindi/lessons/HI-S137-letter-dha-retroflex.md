# ढ — one lift, and the last one to come early

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-136] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: **घ** — how many pen lifts did it take?

[PAUSE 2s] This one takes fewer than any character in the book.

## Script you'll notice: ढ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-137]; assesses=[] -->

**ढ** — *ḍha*, curled back and breathy.

What it is made of:

- a descending stem joined to a broad outer bowl and closed inner loop
- the top shirorekhā

Two parts, where most characters have three or four.

## Script: the shortest build, and the last one early
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-137, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-136] -->

**One pen lift.** Everything below the headline is a single unbroken run —
stem, shoulder, outer bowl and inner loop without the pen leaving the paper —
and then the headline goes on top. Nothing else in this book is drawn in two
strokes.

It is also **the last letter you will meet before the words that use it**. From
here on, every remaining character will be one you have already been reading
without being shown, and each of those lessons will say so.

## Writing: ढ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-137] -->

- **1.** start at the stem's headline junction, descend, turn left through the upper shoulder, sweep counterclockwise around the broad outer bowl, and continue counterclockwise around the closed inner loop without lifting
- **2.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 1.** The pen comes up once.

> Verified two-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline; everyday handwriting may open the inner loop.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ढ-order.gif’, strokes 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
