Mandarin Chinese, chapter 185: Long Ago and Soon.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hǎojiǔ — a long time; ages.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for one's home town, then the Mandarin for to relax.

You'll want to know: hǎojiǔ.
hǎojiǔ — "a long time; ages".
wǒmen hǎojiǔ méi jiànmiàn le — We haven't seen each other for ages.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: hǎojiǔ is said háojiǔ, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
hǎojiǔ is built from 好 'good', here 'very', and jiǔ 'long-lasting', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawX and kjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'lasting long'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhīqián — before (a time); before (an event).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to relax, then the Mandarin for for ages.

You'll want to know: zhīqián.
zhīqián — "before (a time); before (an event)".
chī fàn zhīqián yào xǐ shǒu — Wash your hands before you eat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhīqián is built from zhī, the old word that links a thing to what it belongs to, and 前 'before', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyi and dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to go to'; the linking sense is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
早晚 (zǎowǎn) — morning and evening; sooner or later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for before (a time), then the Mandarin for after (a time).

You'll want to know: 早晚.
早晚 — zǎowǎn — "morning and evening; sooner or later".
这儿早晚都很冷 zhèr zǎowǎn dōu hěn lěng — Here it's cold morning and evening.
Two third tones: zǎowǎn is said záowǎn, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
早晚 (zǎowǎn) is built from 早 'early' and 晚 'late', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsawX and mjonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): early and late, and so sooner or later.

Lesson 5 of 5.
wǎngwǎng — more often than not; tends to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for after (a time), then the Mandarin for morning and evening.

You'll want to know: wǎngwǎng.
wǎngwǎng — "more often than not; tends to".
zhōumò wǒ wǎngwǎng shí diǎn qǐchuáng — At weekends I tend to get up at ten.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: wǎngwǎng is said wángwǎng, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
wǎngwǎng says one syllable twice. It is inherited from Middle Chinese hjwangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to go'; how the doubled word came to mean 'more often than not' is not settled.
