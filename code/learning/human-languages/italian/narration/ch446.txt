Italian, chapter 446: Il centro storico.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le mura — the walls (of a town).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the arcade, then the Italian for the loggia.

You'll want to know: le mura.
le mura — "the walls (of a town)".
Possiamo camminare sulle mura della città. We can walk on the city walls.
Il muro is a wall; the old plural le mura means the walls round a town.

The word, taken apart.
mura is the old plural of muro 'wall', which is inherited from Latin mūrus (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the loggia, then the Italian for the walls.

You'll want to know: la rocca.
la rocca — "the fortress, the stronghold".
La rocca domina il paese. The fortress looks down on the village.
Usually on a rock above the town.

The word, taken apart.
rocca goes back to Vulgar Latin rocca 'rock, crag'; the deeper origin is not settled, and it may be older than Latin.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the walls, then the Italian for the fortress.

You'll want to know: il borgo.
il borgo — "the village, the old quarter".
Il borgo ha solo trecento abitanti. The village has only three hundred inhabitants.
A small old town, often with walls.

The word, taken apart.
borgo comes from Late Latin burgus 'small fort' (EtymDB), which was borrowed from a Germanic word for a fort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: il chiostro.
il chiostro — "the cloister".
Nel chiostro c'è un pozzo antico. In the cloister there is an old well.
A square garden with arcades, inside a monastery.

The word, taken apart.
chiostro is inherited from Latin claustrum 'bar, enclosure' (EtymDB), from claudere 'to shut'.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: vagare.
vagare — "to wander, to roam".
Abbiamo vagato per il borgo tutto il pomeriggio. We wandered round the village all afternoon.
With no fixed goal; vago — vague.

The word, taken apart.
vagare is inherited from Latin vagārī 'to wander, to stroll about' (EtymDB), from vagus 'roaming'.
