Arabic, chapter 514: Worn out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أرق (araq) — insomnia.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to grow weak, then the Arabic for to bleed.

You'll want to know: أرق.
أرق — araq — "insomnia".
عانى أبي من الأرق قبل السفر. ʿānā abī min al-araq qabla as-safar. My father suffered from insomnia before the trip.
Do not mix it up with عرق, ʿaraq, sweat.

The word, taken apart.
أرق (araq) is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-r-q, "to be sleepless" (Lane). Araq is sleeplessness at night.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

إرهاق (irhāq) is built on the native Arabic root r-h-q, "to come upon, to overburden" (Lane). Irhāq is the verbal noun of Form IV, to overburden.

Lesson 5 of 5.
غفا (ghafā) — to doze off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to wear out, then the Arabic for to yawn.

You'll want to know: غفا.
غفا — ghafā — "to doze off".
غفا جدي أمام التلفاز. ghafā jaddī amāma at-tilfāz. My grandfather dozed off in front of the television.
Present: يغفو, yaghfū.

The word, taken apart.
غفا (ghafā) is built on the native Arabic root gh-f-w, "to doze, to sleep lightly" (Lane). Its last root letter w shows as alif in the past and as ū in the present.
