# Review — hasta pronto, and the word inside it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PRONTO, ES-LEX-HASTA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two words, and you have had both for a long time. What does *hasta*
mean? (**Until**.) And *pronto*? (**Soon** — and also "quickly", and "ready".)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PRONTO, ES-LEX-HASTA-PRONTO, ES-ETYMON-PROMPTUS] -->

Say it once, slowly: **hasta pronto**, *AHS-tah PRON-toh*. "Until soon."

[PAUSE 2s] Now take the second half on its own. **Pronto** came from Latin
**promptus**, "brought forth, ready at hand". Something *prompt* is brought out
without delay — and that is the whole distance between "ready" and "soon".

[PAUSE 2s] English has the same word, unchanged in the root: **prompt**,
**promptly**, and the computer **prompt** that sits there ready for you to type.
Italian took it too, and says ***Pronto!*** — "Ready!" — when it answers the
phone.
