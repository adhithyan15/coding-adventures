Gujarati, chapter 151: Reading a shop notice.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
નોટિસ (noṭis) — a posted notice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say perhaps she'll come, then I'm going to go to the market, as a plan.

You'll want to know: નોટિસ.
નોટિસ (noṭis) — "a posted notice, a notice".
A notice is a sheet put up for everyone: on a shop door, at a station, at work. Gujarati took the English word whole.
નોટિસ ક્યાં છે? — noṭis kyā̃ che? — Where is the notice? મેં નોટિસ વાંચી. — mẽ noṭis vā̃cī. — I read the notice.
વાંચી ends in -ી because નોટિસ (noṭis) is feminine.

The word, taken apart.
નોટિસ (noṭis) is a loan from English notice. English took it from French, and French from Latin notitia, "a being known", built on nōscere, "to come to know".

Lesson 2 of 5.
ખુલ્લું (khullũ) — not closed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say a posted notice, then I read the notice.

You'll want to know: ખુલ્લું.
ખુલ્લું (khullũ) — "not closed, open".
It describes a door, a window or a shop that stands open, and it bends like my:
દુકાન ખુલ્લી છે. — dukān khullī che. — The shop is open. દરવાજો ખુલ્લો છે. — darvājo khullo che. — The gate is open. બારી ખુલ્લી હતી. — bārī khullī hatī. — The window was open.
ખોલવું is the act, to open; ખુલ્લું (khullũ) is the state it leaves behind.

The word, taken apart.
ખુલ્લું (khullũ) is inherited. CDIAL sets ખોલવું kholvũ, "to open", and khulvũ, "to be open", under a reconstructed Indo-Aryan khōll- and khull- (CDIAL 3945); ખુલ્લું is the adjective of that second verb.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ફક્ત (phakt) — only.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the shop is open, then the window was open.

You'll want to know: ફક્ત.
ફક્ત (phakt) — "only, no more than".
Notices are full of limits, and ફક્ત (phakt) sets them:
દુકાન ફક્ત સવારે ખુલ્લી છે. — dukān phakt savāre khullī che. — The shop is open only in the morning. ફક્ત પાંચ રૂપિયા. — phakt pā̃c rūpiyā. — Only five rupees.
It stands right before the word it limits.

The word, taken apart.
ફક્ત (phakt) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic faqaṭ, "only, and that is enough". Lane treats faqaṭ under the root q-ṭ, whose qaṭ means "enough, sufficient" (Lane).

What you've built.
One more word for reading the notices on a street.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 4 of 5.
જાહેર (jāher) — public.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say only in the morning, then only five rupees.

You'll want to know: જાહેર.
જાહેર (jāher) — "public, open to all".
It marks what belongs to everyone, and on a notice it most often comes before રજા, a holiday:
આવતીકાલે જાહેર રજા છે. — āvtīkāle jāher rajā che. — Tomorrow is a public holiday.
A જાહેર (jāher) રજા closes offices, banks and many shops for everybody at once.

The word, taken apart.
જાહેર (jāher) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ẓāhir, "apparent, manifest", from the root ẓ-h-r, "to come out into the open, to become evident" (Lane). What is out in the open is public.

What you've built.
One more word for reading the notices on a street.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: jāher]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: āvtīkāle jāher rajā che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: શું આજે જાહેર (jāher) રજા છે? — is today a public holiday?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

What does જાહેર (jāher) mean? (Public, open to all.) Which word does it stand before on a notice? (રજા.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
(reading) (dukān roj savāre ane sānje khullī che.) — a notice on a shop door.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say tomorrow is a public holiday, then only in the morning.
[pause 1 second]
Nobody is talking to anybody on this page. A notice tells you what you may do, and when.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
નોટિસ (noṭis) દુકાન રોજ સવારે અને સાંજે ખુલ્લી છે. બપોરે દુકાન ખુલ્લી નથી. રવિવારે રજા છે. આવતીકાલે, સોમવારે, જાહેર (jāher) રજા છે. મંગળવારે દુકાન ફક્ત (phakt) સવારે ખુલ્લી હશે. બુધવારે દુકાન સવારે અને સાંજે ફરી ખુલ્લી હશે. આજે કેરી સસ્તી છે! સાંજે દૂધ અને દહીં પણ છે. આભાર.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the notice once, straight through, without translating]
[pause 3 seconds]

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Almost every line opens on a when: રોજ, બપોરે, then four days, each with the -ે of on. Two lines say ખુલ્લી હશે, will be open, about days still to come. The line with! is an advert, set into the notice to bring you in.

Wrap-up Recall.
