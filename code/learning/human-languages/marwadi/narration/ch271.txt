Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 271: Right or Wrong?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
खरो (kharo) — correct; genuine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to smash, then the Marwadi for to get cross.

You'll want to know: खरो.
खरो — kharo — "correct; genuine".
थारो जवाब खरो है। thāro javāb kharo hai. Your answer is correct.

The word, taken apart.
खरो (kharo) is inherited from Sanskrit khara, "hard, sharp"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari kharaü, "extreme", Sindhi kharo, "pure, genuine", and Gujarati kharũ, "real, good" (CDIAL 3819). How the sense "correct" grew out of "hard" is not settled. It agrees like any -ो adjective: खरो जवाब, खरी बात.

What you've built.
One more word for asking and answering.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 5.
गलत (galat) — wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to get cross, then the Marwadi for correct.

You'll want to know: गलत.
गलत — galat — "wrong".
यो सबद गलत है। yo sabad galat hai. This word is wrong.

The word, taken apart.
गलत (galat) is usually traced, through Persian ghalat, to Arabic ghalaṭ, "error". Like सही, it does not change.

साखी — sākhī — "a witness".
राम म्हारो साखी है। rām mhāro sākhī hai. Ram is my witness.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
साखी (sākhī) is inherited from Sanskrit sākṣin, "a witness"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari sākhī, "witness" (CDIAL 13321).

Lesson 5 of 5.
कसम (kasam) — an oath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for proof, then the Marwadi for a witness.

You'll want to know: कसम.
कसम — kasam — "an oath".
हूं कसम खाऊं हूं, यो साचो है। hū̃ kasam khāū̃ hū̃, yo sācho hai. I swear it: this is true.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कसम (kasam) is usually traced, through Persian qasam, to Arabic qasam, "an oath". You "eat" an oath: कसम खाऊं हूं, I swear.
