# ت and ن — the same bowl, dotted differently

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-BE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write ب. Bowl, then one dot below.

## Script — two dots above
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-TE-01]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-BE-01] -->

> ت

**The same bowl as be.** Two dots, and they sit **above** it.

That is the entire difference, and it is a whole different letter: **te**,
saying **t**.

## Script — one dot above, and a deeper bowl
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-NUN-01]; assesses=[] -->

> ن

**One dot above** — and a bowl that is noticeably **deeper and rounder** than
be's, curving well below the line before coming back up.

Its name is **nun** and it says **n**.

Standing alone, nun's deeper bowl helps you. In the middle of a joined run the
bowl gets trimmed like everyone else's, and then the dot is doing nearly all the
work: **one above** is nun, **two above** is te, **one below** is be.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-TE-01, FA-SCRIPT-NUN-01, FA-SCRIPT-BE-01] -->

Three letters, one shape:

> ب — one dot **below**
>
> ت — two dots **above**
>
> ن — one dot **above**

You learned one bowl and got three letters out of it. That is the trade Persian
keeps offering: fewer shapes than you feared, and dots you cannot afford to
skim.
