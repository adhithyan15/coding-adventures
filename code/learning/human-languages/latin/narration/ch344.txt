Latin, chapter 344: Maiōrēs nostrī.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
maiōrēs, maiōrum — ancestors, forefathers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a treaty, then the Latin for a triumph.

You'll want to know: maiōrēs, maiōrum.
maiōrēs, maiōrum — "ancestors, forefathers".
Maiōrēs nostrī hoc templum aedificāvērunt. Our ancestors built this temple.
Masculine, plural: the comparative of magnus as a noun, the 'greater' generations before us.

The word, taken apart.
maiōrēs is the plural of maior, "greater, elder", the comparative of magnus, "great" (Lewis & Short): the elders, the ancestors.

Lesson 2 of 5.
annālēs, annālium — annals, a year-by-year history.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a triumph, then the Latin for ancestors.

You'll want to know: annālēs, annālium.
annālēs, annālium — "annals, a year-by-year history".
In annālibus omnia bella scrīpta sunt. All the wars are written in the annals.
Masculine, plural, with librī understood. The priests kept a record of each year; later historians wrote annālēs.

The word, taken apart.
annālēs is built on annus, "year" (EtymDB): the yearly books. English annals comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
conditor, conditōris — a founder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for ancestors, then the Latin for annals.

You'll want to know: conditor, conditōris.
conditor, conditōris — "a founder".
Conditor urbis in forō sepultus esse dīcēbātur. The founder of the city was said to be buried in the forum.
Masculine, from condō, 'to found' (taught), short i. Do not confuse condītor, 'a seasoner'.

The word, taken apart.
conditor is built on condō, "to put together, to found" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor.

Lesson 4 of 5.
orīgō, orīginis — an origin, a beginning; ancestry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for annals, then the Latin for a founder.

You'll want to know: orīgō, orīginis.
orīgō, orīginis — "an origin, a beginning; ancestry".
Dē orīgine urbis multae fābulae nārrantur. Many stories are told about the origin of the city.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from orior, 'to rise'. English origin.

The word, taken apart.
orīgō is built on orior, "to rise, to arise" (Lewis & Short): the rising, the source. English origin comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vetustās, vetustātis — great age, antiquity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a founder, then the Latin for an origin.

You'll want to know: vetustās, vetustātis.
vetustās, vetustātis — "great age, antiquity".
Templum vetustāte collāpsum erat. The temple had fallen in from age.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from vetus (taught). Vetustāte: through long age.

The word, taken apart.
vetustās is built on vetustus, "aged", from vetus, "old" (Lewis & Short): long existence. English veteran belongs to the same family.
