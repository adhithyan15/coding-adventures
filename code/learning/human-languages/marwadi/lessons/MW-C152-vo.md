# वो — he, the man you talk about

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-HOON-01, MW-SCRIPT-THIK-01, MW-SCRIPT-WELLBEING-QUESTION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say how you are: **हूं ठीक हूं।** (*hū̃ ṭhīk hū̃.*) Then ask the polite
question: **आप कैसो हो?** (*āp kaiso ho?*)

So far you talk about yourself, **हूं**, and to the person in front of you, **आप**.
Now a third person: someone you talk *about*.

## Grammar Lens: वो
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-C152-VO-HE-01]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-HAI-01, MW-LEX-RAAM-01, MW-PHRASE-MY-NAME-IS-01] -->

> **वो राम है।**
>
> *vo rām hai.* — He is Ram.

**वो** (*vo*) is *he*: a man you are talking about, not to. He comes first, and
**है** ends the sentence, as it does in **म्हारो नाम राम है।**

> **वो बीमार है।**
>
> *vo bīmār hai.* — He is ill.

Old Marwari already had *vo*, "that": CDIAL lists it under Sanskrit *asau*, "that"
(CDIAL 972). This book keeps **वो** for a man, and names a woman by a word you
know: **मां**, **बहन**, **छोरी**.
