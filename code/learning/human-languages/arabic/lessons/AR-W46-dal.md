# د (dāl) — the letter that ends الحمد

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C46-SHIN-01, AR-SCRIPT-C46-SAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ش and ص once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **الحمد لله** (*al-ḥamdu lillāh*), "praise be to God".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: د
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SCRIPT-C46-DAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**د** — *dāl*, the sound *d*.

It closes **الحمد** in **الحمد لله** (*al-ḥamdu lillāh*), the everyday answer to
"how are you?". **د** is a **non-connector**, like **ا** and **ر**: it joins to the
letter before it but never to the one after, so a small gap always follows it.

## Writing: د
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C46-DAL-01] -->

- **1.** start here: begin at the upper tip and descend diagonally down and right through the curved shoulder
- **2.** turn left along the baseline without lifting — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 0.** The pen never leaves the paper.

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: د ذ ر, independent Daal at 00:07.0–00:07.6 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C46-SHIN-01, AR-SCRIPT-C46-SAD-01, AR-SCRIPT-C46-DAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at **الحمد لله** and point at د inside it]
- [YOU TRACE: د three times, saying *d* as you finish each one]
- [YOU WRITE: ص, then د, from memory]
- [YOU READ: **الحمد لله** aloud once more]
