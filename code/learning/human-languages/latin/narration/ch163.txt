Latin, chapter 163: The Day after Tomorrow, Afterwards, a Plan, To Decide, To Foresee.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
perendiē — the day after tomorrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for before, then the Latin for to happen.

You'll want to know: perendiē.
perendiē — "the day after tomorrow". One day further on than crās: Perendiē Rōmam ībō. — "The day after tomorrow I will go to Rome." ībō is the future of eō.

You'll want to know: posteā.
posteā — "afterwards, later". It is post, "after", with eā. Posteā tē vidēbō. — "I will see you afterwards." vidēbō is the future you met in crās tē vidēbō.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cōnstituō, cōnstituere — to decide, to set up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for afterwards, then the Latin for a plan; advice.

You'll want to know: cōnstituō, cōnstituere.
cōnstituō, cōnstituere — "to decide, to set up". Third-conjugation verbs, with a short -ere, make their future with -am, -ēs, -et: Crās cōnstituam. — "I will decide tomorrow." English constitute and constitution come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
prōvideō, prōvidēre — to foresee, to provide for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plan; advice, then the Latin for to decide.

You'll want to know: prōvideō, prōvidēre.
prōvideō, prōvidēre — "to foresee, to provide for". It is videō with prō, "ahead": seeing a thing before it comes. Its future follows vidēbō: prōvidēbō. English provide and provident come from it.
