# Unit 23: *Tener* — "To Have"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say *hay un libro*. Today's verb answers a related but
different question: not "does it exist" but "do you possess it."

## New Material

**tengo / tienes / tiene / tenemos / tienen** — I have / you have /
he-she-it has / we have / they have
> ← Latin *tenere* ("to hold" — cognate with English *tenant*, *tenacious*,
> *contain*, *maintain*, *retain* — all "holding" words). The same root as
> the *ten-* family you'll spotlight properly in a future morphology unit.

## Grammar Lens

*Tener* is a **stem-changing verb** — notice *tengo* keeps a plain stem
but *tienes/tiene/tienen* insert an *-ie-* the infinitive *tener* doesn't
have. This kind of internal vowel shift happens in a whole class of common
Spanish verbs (you'll meet more later) and has no real English parallel —
English verbs conjugate by changing endings (*have/has*), never by
inserting a vowel into the middle of the word.

Spanish also uses *tener* for some things English expresses with "to be"
— *"tengo hambre"* ("I'm hungry," literally "I have hunger"), *"tengo
frío"* ("I'm cold," literally "I have cold"). Where English reaches for
"is/am," Spanish sometimes reaches for "has" instead — a real,
non-obvious mapping worth flagging directly rather than translating word
for word.
