# du warst — "you were"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-LEX-DU-SIE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have *ich war*. The familiar "you" wants the ending it always
wants.

## You'll want to know: du warst
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-WARST-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-ENDINGS-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-BIST-01] -->

> **du warst** — "you were."

*War* plus **-st**, with nothing else moved. The same ending that made *bist*
out of *bin*, and *gehst* out of *gehen*.

So the past tense of this verb is better behaved than its present: you take one
stem, *war-*, and add ordinary endings to it. Everything difficult about *sein*
happens in the present.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-WARST-01] -->

- `u-long` — *du* keeps its long rounded **oo**.
- `final-t` — and *warst* ends on the fully released **t** German always gives
  you, after the *s*. Say the *s* and the *t* both: *varst*.
