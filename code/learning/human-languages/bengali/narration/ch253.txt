Bengali, chapter 253: Lie, Mystery, Joke, Smile, Friendship.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মিথ্যে (mitthe) — a lie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a granddaughter, then the Bengali for a father-in-law.

You'll want to know: মিথ্যে.
মিথ্যে — mitthe — "a lie".

The word, taken apart.
মিথ্যে (mitthe) is a semi-tatsama: Sanskrit mithyā, "wrongly, falsely", borrowed after the old sound changes had run their course and then lightly reshaped (CDIAL 10130). The word inherited from it is michā, "untrue", which CDIAL lists.

Lesson 4 of 5.
হাসি (hāsi) — a smile, laughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a mystery, then the Bengali for a joke.

You'll want to know: হাসি.
হাসি — hāsi — "a smile, laughter".

The word, taken apart.
হাসি (hāsi) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from hāsya, "laughable; laughter" (CDIAL 14071). CDIAL lists Bengali hāsi, "laughter, smile".
