Mandarin Chinese, chapter 541: If It Weren't for You.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
要不是 (yàobúshì) — if it were not for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the whole chain of causes, then the Mandarin for what is going on here.

You'll want to know: 要不是.
要不是 — yàobúshì — "if it were not for".
要不是你我们就找不到这儿了 yàobúshì nǐ wǒmen jiù zhǎo bu dào zhèr le — If it weren't for you, we'd never have found this place.
Tone: 不 is said bú before the falling tone of shì, and the spelling shows it.

The word, taken apart.
要不是 (yàobúshì) is built from 要 'if', 不 'not' and 是 'to be', all inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jiewH, pjut and dzyeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to bind by agreement', and its use for 'if' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Lesson 2 of 7.
duōkuī — it is thanks to; luckily for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for what is going on here, then the Mandarin for if it were not for.

You'll want to know: duōkuī.
duōkuī — "it is thanks to; luckily for".
duōkuī nǐ bāng wǒ bùrán wǒ jiù zuò bu wán — Thanks to your help I finished; otherwise I'd never have managed.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
duōkuī is built from 多 'much' and kuī, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ta and khjwe (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'many' and the second 'to be lacking'. How the pair came to mean 'thanks to' is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 7.
要么 (yàome) — either one or the other; either … or.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to make conditions better, then the Mandarin for the main one.

You'll want to know: 要么.
要么 — yàome — "either one or the other; either … or".
要么你来要么我去 yàome nǐ lái yàome wǒ qù — Either you come here or I go there.

The word, taken apart.
要么 (yàome) is built from 要 and the light ending 么, as in 怎么. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese 'jiewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is said light, and its history in this use is not settled.
