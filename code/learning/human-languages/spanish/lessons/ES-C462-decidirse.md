# decidirse — to make your mind up

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **decidir** and **acercar**. You already know what putting
**-se** on the second one did. Do it to the first.

## You'll want to know: decidirse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-01] -->

**decidirse** — to make up your mind.

*No me decido* — I can't decide. *Decídete* — make your mind up. *Se decidió
por el azul* — she went for the blue one.

The **-se** version takes **por** for the thing chosen: *me decidí por el
piso pequeño*.

## Grammar Lens: what the -se adds here
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05] -->

You have met the *-se* switch three times, and this is a fourth use of it —
not a new meaning, but a new *shade*:

| without -se | with -se |
|---|---|
| *acercar* — bring it closer | *acercarse* — come closer |
| *decidir* — settle a matter | *decidirse* — settle **yourself** |

*He decidido vender el coche* states the decision. *Me he decidido a vender el
coche* says you struggled and got there. The reflexive adds the **effort**, the
sense of having had to bring yourself to it.

That is why *no me decido* is the natural way to say you are dithering, and
*no decido* would sound like a job description.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02] -->

Latin **decidere**: **de-**, off, plus **caedere**, **to cut**. To decide is
to **cut off** — the other options, the deliberating, the retreat.

That picture explains the English family exactly. A **decision** cuts things
off. Something **decisive** cuts cleanly. And *caedere* is also in **concise**
(cut short), **precise** (cut in front, trimmed to the edge), and — through the
same cutting — **homicide** and **suicide**.

Spanish has *decidir*, *la decisión*, *conciso* and *preciso* off the same
root. When you make your mind up, a Latin speaker heard you reaching for a
blade.
