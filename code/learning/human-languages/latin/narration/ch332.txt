Latin, chapter 332: Testāmentum.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hērēs, hērēdis — an heir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a memorial, then the Latin for offerings to the dead.

You'll want to know: hērēs, hērēdis.
hērēs, hērēdis — "an heir".
Fīlius ūnus hērēs patris erat. The only son was his father's heir.
Masculine or feminine, 3rd declension. Hērēdem facere: to name someone heir in a will. English heir.

The word, taken apart.
hērēs is inherited; EtymDB traces it to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to leave behind", while Lewis & Short compare a Sanskrit root meaning "to take", so the details are not settled. English heir comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hērēditās, hērēditātis — an inheritance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: hērēditās, hērēditātis.
hērēditās, hērēditātis — "an inheritance".
Magnam hērēditātem ab avunculō accēpit. He received a large inheritance from his uncle.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from hērēs. English heredity and heritage belong to its family.

The word, taken apart.
hērēditās is built on hērēs, "heir" (EtymDB), with the ending -tās: what comes to an heir. English heredity comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
testāmentum, testāmentī — a will, a testament.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an heir, then the Latin for an inheritance.

You'll want to know: testāmentum, testāmentī.
testāmentum, testāmentī — "a will, a testament".
Senex testāmentum cōram testibus scrīpsit. The old man wrote his will in front of witnesses.
Neuter, from a verb meaning 'to call to witness', and so from testis (taught). Seven witnesses sealed a Roman will.

The word, taken apart.
testāmentum is built on testor, "to call to witness", from testis, "witness" (EtymDB): a declaration made before witnesses. English testament comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
obsignō, obsignāre — to seal, to seal up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an inheritance, then the Latin for a will.

You'll want to know: obsignō, obsignāre.
obsignō, obsignāre — "to seal, to seal up".
Testēs testāmentum ānulīs suīs obsignāvērunt. The witnesses sealed the will with their rings.
obsignō, obsignāre, obsignāvī, obsignātum: ob + signō (taught). A seal of wax pressed with a signet ring.

The word, taken apart.
obsignō is built on signō, "to mark, to seal", with ob- (EtymDB): to put a seal over.

Lesson 5 of 5.
relinquō, relinquere — to leave behind; to leave (in a will).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a will, then the Latin for to seal.

You'll want to know: relinquō, relinquere.
relinquō, relinquere — "to leave behind; to leave (in a will)".
Pater fīliīs domum et agrōs relīquit. The father left the house and the fields to his sons.
relinquō, relinquere, relīquī, relictum. English relinquish and relic belong to its family.

The word, taken apart.
relinquō is built on linquō, "to leave", with re-, "back, behind" (EtymDB). English relinquish comes from it through French, and relic from reliquiae, "remains", of the same family.
