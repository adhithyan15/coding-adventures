Sanskrit, chapter 290: Robbers on the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दस्युः (dasyuḥ) — a robber; a bandit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a trace, then the Sanskrit for before one's eyes.

You'll want to know: दस्युः.
दस्युः — dasyuḥ — "a robber; a bandit".
वने दस्युः वसति। vane dasyuḥ vasati. A robber lives in the forest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दस्युः (dasyuḥ) comes from the root √das, "to lay waste"; MW's older sense is an enemy of the gods, and it also gives dasyu as a robber (MW).

लुण्ठति — luṇṭhati — "plunders".
यदि दस्युः आगच्छति, तर्हि ग्रामं लुण्ठति। yadi dasyuḥ āgacchati, tarhi grāmaṃ luṇṭhati. If a robber comes, then he plunders the village.
Past (he …): सः ग्रामं लुण्ठितवान्, saḥ grāmaṃ luṇṭhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
लुण्ठति (luṇṭhati) comes from the root √luṇṭh, which MW gives with several senses, among them to rob or plunder (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
आक्रमति (ākramati) — attacks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a robber, then the Sanskrit for plunders.

You'll want to know: आक्रमति.
आक्रमति — ākramati — "attacks".
दस्युः पथिकम् आक्रमति। dasyuḥ pathikam ākramati. The robber attacks the traveller.
Past (he …): सः पथिकम् आक्रान्तवान्, saḥ pathikam ākrāntavān.

The word, taken apart.
आक्रमति (ākramati) is built from ā, "towards", and the root √kram, "to step, stride"; MW gives ā-kram as to step near, seize and attack (MW).

आभरणम् (ābharaṇam) is built from ā and the root √bhṛ, "to bear, carry"; MW gives ābharaṇa as decorating and as an ornament such as jewels (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
बलात् (balāt) — by force.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for attacks, then the Sanskrit for a piece of jewellery.

You'll want to know: बलात्.
बलात् — balāt — "by force".
दस्युः बलात् धनं गृह्णाति। dasyuḥ balāt dhanaṃ gṛhṇāti. The robber takes the money by force.

The word, taken apart.
बलात् (balāt) is built on bala, "strength, force": MW records its ablative balāt as meaning forcibly, against one's will (MW).
