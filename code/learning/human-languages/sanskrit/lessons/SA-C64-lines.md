# (reading) — six lines, and only two of them say anything different

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words. These put them somewhere.

[PAUSE 1s] Six lines — and six is more than the number of things they say.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SKILL-READ-LINES]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C14-DEIXIS-03, SA-LEX-C14-DEIXIS-04] -->

> ग्रामः अत्र अस्ति।
> अत्र ग्रामः अस्ति।
> अस्ति अत्र ग्रामः।
> वनम् तत्र अस्ति।
> तत्र वनम् अस्ति।
> अस्ति तत्र वनम्।

[PAUSE 3s] Read the six once, straight down.

[PAUSE 3s] Now read the first three again and say what changed.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-ASTI, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-02, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing changed. The first three lines are one sentence written three
ways, and the second three are another. **The village is here. The forest is
there.** That is the whole content of the page.

[PAUSE 2s] Do the same thing in English and it breaks, or it turns into
something else. *The village is here* and *here is the village* and *is here the
village* are not one sentence three ways.

[PAUSE 2s] This is what the last lesson bought. Because **ग्रामः** announces its
own job with its ending, it does not need a position to hold that job for it —
so the position is free, and a writer can put the emphasis wherever it belongs.

[PAUSE 2s] The usual order still puts the verb last, and the passage in the next
lesson mostly keeps it there. The freedom is real; it is not disorder.

[PAUSE 2s] Read the ending, not the seat. Everything else in Sanskrit follows
from that one habit.
