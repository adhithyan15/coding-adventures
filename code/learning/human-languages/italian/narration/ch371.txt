Lesson 1 of 5.
rilevante — relevant, significant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the reform, then the Italian for the member of parliament.

You'll want to know: rilevante.
rilevante — "relevant, significant".
Questo dettaglio non è rilevante. This detail is not relevant.
It also means considerable: una somma rilevante.

The word, taken apart.
rilevante is the present participle of rilevare "to raise, to point out", from Latin relevāre "to lift up again" (Lewis & Short), built on levāre "to raise": what stands out.

essenziale — "essential".

The word, taken apart.
essenziale is a learned borrowing from Late Latin essentiālis "of the essence" (EtymDB), from essentia "being, essence", built on esse "to be".

fondamentale — "fundamental, vital".
Per questo lavoro l'inglese è fondamentale. For this job English is vital.

The word, taken apart.
fondamentale is built on fondamento "foundation", inherited from Latin fundāmentum "a foundation" (Lewis & Short), from fundus "bottom, ground".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la premessa — the premise; the preface, the introduction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for essential, then the Italian for fundamental.

You'll want to know: la premessa.
la premessa — "the premise; the preface, the introduction".
Faccio una premessa: non sono un esperto. Let me say first: I am not an expert.
Fare una premessa — to say something first. In a book it is the preface.

The word, taken apart.
premessa is the past participle of premettere "to put first", from Latin praemittere "to send ahead" (Lewis & Short), built from prae "before" and mittere "to send".
