Arabic, chapter 387: People and nations.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
هجرة (hijra) — migration.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a conflict, then the Arabic for a crisis.

You'll want to know: هجرة.
هجرة — hijra — "migration".
زادت الهجرة إلى المدن. zādat al-hijra ilā al-mudun. Migration to the cities increased.

The word, taken apart.
هجرة (hijra) is built on the native Arabic root h-j-r, "to leave, to cut off" (Lane). Hijra is leaving one's land for another: migration.

Lesson 2 of 5.
أمة (umma) — a nation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a crisis, then the Arabic for migration.

You'll want to know: أمة.
أمة — umma — "a nation".
تحتفل الأمة بعيدها الوطني. taḥtafilu al-umma bi-ʿīdihā al-waṭanī. The nation is celebrating its national day.
Plural: أمم, umam.

The word, taken apart.
أمة (umma) is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-m-m, "to aim at, to head for" (Lane). An umma is a people that head the same way: a nation. أم, mother, is from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
جنسية (jinsiyya) — nationality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for migration, then the Arabic for a nation.

You'll want to know: جنسية.
جنسية — jinsiyya — "nationality".
يحمل صديقي جنسيتين. yaḥmilu ṣadīqī jinsiyyatayn. My friend holds two nationalities.

The word, taken apart.
جنسية (jinsiyya) is built on the native Arabic root j-n-s, "a kind, a sort" (Lane). Jinsiyya is a modern abstract noun from jins, kind: the kind of citizen one is, nationality.
