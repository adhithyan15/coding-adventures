il quartiere — the neighbourhood, the district.

You'll want to know: il quartiere.
il quartiere — "the neighbourhood, the district".
Abitiamo in un quartiere tranquillo. We live in a quiet neighbourhood.
Il quartier generale — headquarters.

The word, taken apart.
quartiere was borrowed from French quartier (EtymDB), from Latin quartārius 'a fourth part': first a quarter of a town.

Lesson 2 of 6.
la periferia — the outskirts, the suburbs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the landmark, then the Italian for the neighbourhood.

You'll want to know: la periferia.
la periferia — "the outskirts, the suburbs".
La casa è in periferia, lontano dal centro. The house is on the outskirts, far from the centre.
Often less smart than the English suburbs.

The word, taken apart.
periferia is a learned borrowing from Late Latin peripherīa, from Greek periphéreia 'circumference', built on pherein 'to carry'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
rintracciare — to track down, to trace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the neighbourhood, then the Italian for the outskirts.

You'll want to know: rintracciare.
rintracciare — "to track down, to trace".
Non riesco a rintracciare il suo indirizzo. I cannot track down his address.
Essere rintracciabile — to be reachable.

The word, taken apart.
rintracciare is built on traccia 'trace, track', the noun of tracciare 'to trace', which is usually traced to Vulgar Latin tractiāre, built on trahere 'to drag' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
il numero civico — the house number, the street number.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the outskirts, then the Italian for to track down.

You'll want to know: il numero civico.
il numero civico — "the house number, the street number".
Qual è il numero civico? What is the house number?
Written after the street name: via del Mare, 12.

The word, taken apart.
numero civico is built from numero 'number', inherited from Latin numerus, and civico, a learned borrowing from Latin cīvicus 'of citizens', from cīvis 'citizen' (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: il citofono.
il citofono — "the intercom, the entry phone".
Suona al citofono, ti apro. Ring the intercom, I will let you in.
Citofonare — to buzz someone.

The word, taken apart.
citofono is a learned formation, usually explained as Latin citō 'quickly' (Lewis & Short) and Greek phōnḗ 'voice'.
