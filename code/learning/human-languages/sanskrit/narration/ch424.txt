Sanskrit, chapter 424: Sights Worth Seeing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पर्यटति (paryaṭati) — tours; travels around.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a gap, then the Sanskrit for a gatekeeper.

You'll want to know: पर्यटति.
पर्यटति — paryaṭati — "tours; travels around".
पर्यटकः नगरे पर्यटति। paryaṭakaḥ nagare paryaṭati. The tourist tours the city.
Past (he …): सः नगरे पर्यटितवान्, saḥ nagare paryaṭitavān.

The word, taken apart.
पर्यटति (paryaṭati) is built from pari, "around", and the root √aṭ, "to roam", which also gives paryaṭaka, "a tourist"; MW gives pary-aṭ as to roam about, travel over, with the participle paryaṭita (MW).

You'll want to know: दर्शनीयः.
दर्शनीयः — darśanīyaḥ — "worth seeing".
नगरस्य प्राचीनः प्रासादः दर्शनीयः अस्ति। nagarasya prācīnaḥ prāsādaḥ darśanīyaḥ asti. The city's old palace is worth seeing.

The word, taken apart.
दर्शनीयः (darśanīyaḥ) is built on the root √dṛś, "to see", with the ending -anīya, "to be done": to be seen; MW gives darśanīya as worthy of being seen, beautiful (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for worth seeing, then the Sanskrit for delightful.

You'll want to know: दृश्यम्.
दृश्यम् — dṛśyam — "a view; a sight".
पर्वतात् नगरस्य दृश्यं सुन्दरम् अस्ति। parvatāt nagarasya dṛśyaṃ sundaram asti. The view of the city from the mountain is beautiful.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
दृश्यम् (dṛśyam) is built on the root √dṛś, "to see", with the ending -ya: MW gives dṛśya as visible, worth seeing and, as a noun, any visible object; a view is that sense (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
विहरति (viharati) — strolls; walks for pleasure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for delightful, then the Sanskrit for a view.

You'll want to know: विहरति.
विहरति — viharati — "strolls; walks for pleasure".
सायं वयम् उद्याने विहरामः। sāyaṃ vayam udyāne viharāmaḥ. In the evening we stroll in the garden.
Past (he …): सः उद्याने विहृतवान्, saḥ udyāne vihṛtavān.

The word, taken apart.
विहरति (viharati) is built from vi, "apart", and the root √hṛ, "to carry", which also gives harati, "takes away"; MW gives vi-hṛ as to walk for pleasure, to roam, with the participle vihṛta (MW).
