# कियो, लियो — did, took

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-GRAMMAR-C161-MHAI-AGENT-01, MW-LEX-C58-VERBS58-02, MW-LEX-C53-VERBS53-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I ate a roti*. Then *to do* and *to take*: **करणो**, **लेणो**.

## Grammar Lens: two short pasts
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-C161-KIYO-LIYO-01]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C58-VERBS58-02, MW-LEX-C53-VERBS53-04, MW-GRAMMAR-C161-MHAI-AGENT-01, MW-GRAMMAR-C161-OBJ-AGREE-01] -->

**करणो** and **लेणो** have short pasts: **कियो** and **लियो**.

> **म्हैं पाणी गरम कियो।**
>
> *mhaĩ pāṇī garam kiyo.* — I heated the water.

> **म्हैं दूध लियो।**
>
> *mhaĩ dūdh liyo.* — I took milk.

For a feminine thing, **लियो** turns into **ली**:

> **म्हैं रोटी ली।**
>
> *mhaĩ roṭī lī.* — I took a roti.

CDIAL lists Old Marwari *kiyo*, "made", and *liyo*, "takes" (CDIAL 2814, 10948).
This book pairs **कियो** with a masculine thing only. **ली** is central Marwari as
this book writes it; other districts differ.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-GRAMMAR-C161-KIYO-LIYO-01, MW-GRAMMAR-C161-OBJ-AGREE-01, MW-GRAMMAR-C161-MHAI-AGENT-01, MW-SCRIPT-ROTI-01, MW-LEX-C155-FOOD155-01] -->

The thing changes the ending; the speaker does not:

| who speaks | the thing | the verb |
|---|---|---|
| a man | **रोटी** | **ली** |
| a woman | **रोटी** | **ली** |
| a man | **दूध** | **लियो** |
| a woman | **दूध** | **लियो** |

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: **म्हैं रोटी ली।** — *mhaĩ roṭī lī.* — I took a roti.]
- [YOU SAY: **म्हैं दूध लियो।** — *mhaĩ dūdh liyo.* — I took milk.]
- [YOU SAY: **म्हैं पाणी गरम कियो।** — *mhaĩ pāṇī garam kiyo.* — I heated the water.]
