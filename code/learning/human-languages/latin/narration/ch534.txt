Latin, chapter 534: Kisses and hugs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ōsculum, ōsculī — a kiss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to hold dear, then the Latin for lovable.

You'll want to know: ōsculum, ōsculī.
ōsculum, ōsculī — "a kiss".
Māter fīliō ōsculum dat. The mother gives her son a kiss.
Neuter, a diminutive of ōs — mouth: a little mouth. Ōsculor, already known, is to kiss.

The word, taken apart.
ōsculum is a diminutive of ōs "mouth" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English osculate comes from the verb ōsculor.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bāsium, bāsiī — a loving kiss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for lovable, then the Latin for a kiss.

You'll want to know: bāsium, bāsiī.
bāsium, bāsiī — "a loving kiss".
Fīliola patrī bāsia mīlle dat. The little girl gives her father a thousand kisses.
Neuter, second declension. A warm word of love poetry. Spanish beso and Italian bacio come from it.

The word, taken apart.
bāsium is a word of uncertain origin; Lewis & Short compares English buss, and EtymDB gives a Proto-Indo-European root bu-, but it is not settled. Spanish beso and Italian bacio come from it.

Avia nepōtēs bāsiat. Grandmother kisses her grandchildren.
bāsiō, bāsiāre, bāsiāvī, bāsiātum, from bāsium.

The word, taken apart.
bāsiō is built on bāsium "a kiss" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). French baiser comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

suāvium, suāviī — "a kiss on the lips".
Puella iuvenī suāvium dat. The girl gives the young man a kiss on the lips.
Neuter, second declension; also spelt sāvium. A word of comedy and love letters.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
amplexus, amplexūs — an embrace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to kiss, then the Latin for a kiss on the lips.

You'll want to know: amplexus, amplexūs.
amplexus, amplexūs — "an embrace".
Māter fīliam in amplexū tenet. The mother holds her daughter in an embrace.
Masculine, fourth declension: arms wound around someone. In poetry also the coils of a snake.

The word, taken apart.
amplexus is the action noun of amplector "to embrace" (Lewis & Short).
