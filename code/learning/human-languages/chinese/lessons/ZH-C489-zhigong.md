## You'll want to know: 职工
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C489-STAFF489-02]; assesses=[] -->

**职工** — *zhígōng* — "member of staff; employee (of a firm or factory)".

> *zhè jiā gōngchǎng yǒu yìbǎi ge zhígōng* — This factory has a hundred staff.

The new character, in pieces: 职 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left an ear piece of six strokes, a bar, two verticals with two short bars between them and a stroke rising at the foot; on the right 只 *zhǐ*, five strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**职工** (*zhígōng*) is built from 职 'a post' and 工 'work', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsyik* and *kuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be in charge of' and, after an older dictionary, 'an occupation'.
