Japanese, chapter 345: When Plans Change.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
へんこうする (henkō suru) — to change a plan; to alter (a date).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a schedule, then the Japanese for going smoothly.

You'll want to know: へんこうする.
へんこうする — henkō suru — "to change a plan; to alter (a date)".
もし あめが ふったら、よていを へんこうします。 — moshi ame ga futtara, yotei o henkō shimasu — "If it rains, we will change the plan."
Polite へんこうします, henkōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): かいしゃが よていを へんこうしました。, kaisha ga yotei o henkō shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
へんこうする (henkō suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, henkō, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: hen, "change" (Middle Chinese pjenH) and kō, "renew" (kaeng) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
とりけす (torikesu) — to cancel; to take back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to change a plan, then the Japanese for to postpone.

You'll want to know: とりけす.
とりけす — torikesu — "to cancel; to take back".
びょうきに なったら、よやくを とりけして ください。 — byōki ni nattara, yoyaku o torikeshite kudasai — "If you fall ill, please cancel the booking."
Polite とりけします, torikeshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが よやくを とりけしました。, chichi ga yoyaku o torikeshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とりけす (torikesu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from tori, "taking (toru)" and kesu, "to put out", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
