Malayalam, chapter 340: Telling a Story in Order.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ഈയിടെ (īyiṭe) — recently, lately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for "meanwhile", then for "recently". (itiniṭe, aṭuttiṭe.) Then "once". (orikkal.)

You'll want to know: ഈയിടെ.
ഈയിടെ — īyiṭe — "recently, lately".
ഈയിടെ ഞാൻ ഒരു നല്ല സിനിമ കണ്ടു. īyiṭe ñān oru nalla sinima kaṇṭu. — "Recently I saw a good film."
With a past verb it means "recently, a short while back". With a present one it means "lately, these days":
ഈയിടെ മുത്തശ്ശിക്ക് സുഖമില്ല. īyiṭe muttaśśikkŭ sukhamilla. — "Lately Grandmother has not been well."
അടുത്തിടെ, which you know, says the same. You will hear both, and a story often opens on one of them.
Its first letter is one you have not written yet. The next lesson takes it out of this word and puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ഈയിടെ (īyiṭe) is native Dravidian, and it is read as "in this interval". ഈ, ī, is the "this" that stands before a noun, from the near demonstrative base (DEDR 410). ഇട, iṭa, is "interval, space between", with Tamil iṭai (DEDR 448). The -എ on the end places it in time. The same ഇട sits inside ഇതിനിടെ, "meanwhile".

Lesson 2 of 6.
ഈ (ī) — the letter that opens ഈയിടെ (īyiṭe).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. [once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ഇ and ീ once each, and name them. (Short i at the start of a word; long ī after a consonant, as in വീട്.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ഈയിടെ (īyiṭe), "recently". You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ഈ.
ഈ — ī, a long ī, at the start of a word.
After a consonant, long ī is the sign ീ: വീട്, vīṭŭ. At the start of a word there is no consonant to carry a sign, so the vowel stands as a letter of its own, ഈ.
It is ഇ with a tail added on the right: a small lobe, a notch, and a broader lobe that sweeps back along its foot. You have read the same tail on ഊ, which is ഉ with that tail.
On its own, ഈ (ī) is also a word: "this", in front of a noun. ഈ (ī) വീട് is "this house".

Writing: ഈ.
ഈ (ī) is two parts side by side: the whole of ഇ on the left, and the tail on the right. Write the ഇ you already know first, then add the tail beside it: a small lobe, a notch, and a broader lobe that sweeps back along its foot.
Write ഈ (ī) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Copy the printed shape.

Lesson 3 of 6.
എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ) — and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "having come". (വന്നിട്ട് (vanniṭṭŭ).) Then the word for "then, after that". (പിന്നെ (pinne).)

Grammar Lens: one sentence, with -ഇട്ട്.
You already know the past form with -ഇട്ട് on it: "having done". Put it inside a sentence and it says "did this, and then":
ഞാൻ ചായ കുടിച്ചിട്ട് ഓഫീസിൽ പോയി. ñān cāya kuṭicciṭṭŭ ōphīsil pōyi. — "I drank tea and then went to the office."
Piece by piece: "I, tea, having drunk, to the office went". The first action is finished before the second begins.

Grammar Lens: two sentences, with എന്നിട്ട്.
To keep the two actions as two sentences, start the second with എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ):
ഞാൻ ചായ കുടിച്ചു. എന്നിട്ട് ഓഫീസിൽ പോയി. ñān cāya kuṭiccu. enniṭṭŭ ōphīsil pōyi. — "I drank tea. And then I went to the office."
എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ) is usually explained as the -ഇട്ട് ending on the same root as എന്ന്: "having said so", "that done". It ties the next action to the one before.
പിന്നെ is the looser, everyday "then, after that", and it can also mean "later". എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ) ties the next action to the one done a moment before.
A listener who wants the story to go on asks എന്നിട്ടോ? (enniṭṭō?), "and then?"

[pause 3 seconds]
"And then", at the start of a sentence? (എന്നിട്ട്, enniṭṭŭ.) The same idea inside one sentence? (The -ഇട്ട് form: kuṭicciṭṭŭ.) How is പിന്നെ different? (Looser: "after that", and it can also mean "later".)


Lesson 4 of 6.
അതിനുശേഷം (atinuśēṣaṁ) — after that: a story in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "at first", "after", and "finally". (ādyaṁ, śēṣaṁ, oṭuvil.)

Grammar Lens: അതിനുശേഷം.
ശേഷം stands after the thing it comes after, and that thing takes -ന്. Make the thing അത്, "that", and the result is a sentence link:
അതിന് + ശേഷം becomes അതിനുശേഷം — atinuśēṣaṁ — after that.
A story then hangs on four posts: a time to open it, ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ or എന്നിട്ട് (enniṭṭŭ) or അതിനുശേഷം (atinuśēṣaṁ) in the middle, and ഒടുവിൽ to close.
കഴിഞ്ഞ ശനിയാഴ്ച ഞങ്ങൾ നഗരത്തിൽ പോയി. ആദ്യം ചന്തയിൽ പോയി. അതിനുശേഷം ഒരു ചെറിയ കടയിൽ ചായ കുടിച്ചു. ഒടുവിൽ രാത്രി വീട്ടിൽ എത്തി. kaḻiñña śaniyāḻca ñaṅṅaḷ nagarattil pōyi. ādyaṁ cantayil pōyi. atinuśēṣaṁ oru ceṟiya kaṭayil cāya kuṭiccu. oṭuvil rātri vīṭṭil etti. — "Last Saturday we went to the city. First we went to the market. After that we drank tea in a small shop. Finally we got home at night."

Grammar Lens: when it happened, -അപ്പോൾ.
You already know വരുമ്പോൾ, "when he comes". For "when he came", take the past form you know as വന്ന and add -പ്പോൾ: വന്നപ്പോൾ (vannappōḷ). In the same way എത്തി gives എത്തിയപ്പോൾ (ettiyappōḷ), "when (I) arrived", and കണ്ടു gives കണ്ടപ്പോൾ (kaṇṭappōḷ), "when (I) saw".
ഞാൻ എത്തിയപ്പോൾ മുത്തശ്ശി കാത്തിരിക്കുകയായിരുന്നു. ñān ettiyappōḷ muttaśśi kāttirikkukayāyirunnu. — "When I arrived, Grandmother was waiting."
The -പ്പോൾ clause sets the moment; the main verb says what was going on in it, or what happened next.

[your turn — say: "When I arrived, Grandmother was waiting."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"After that"? (അതിനുശേഷം (atinuśēṣaṁ): അതിന് + ശേഷം.) "When I arrived"? (എത്തിയപ്പോൾ, ettiyappōḷ.) Which word closes a story? (ഒടുവിൽ, oṭuvil.)
