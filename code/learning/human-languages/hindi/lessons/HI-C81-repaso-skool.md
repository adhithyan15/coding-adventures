# स्कूल or विद्यालय — the same building, two registers

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Both words mean school. Say each one, and decide which you would
write on an official form.

## Grammar Lens: borrowed whole, or built from parts
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-02, HI-LEX-C47-ROLE-01, HI-LEX-C47-ROLE-02] -->

This is the third time this pattern has turned up, and it is worth naming:

| meaning | borrowed whole | built from parts |
|---|---|---|
| a meal | नाश्ता | दोपहर का खाना |
| a school | स्कूल | विद्यालय |

Hindi keeps both kinds and uses them at different heights. The borrowed word is
what people say; the built word is what institutions write. Neither is more
correct, and a learner who only has one of them will sound wrong in half the
situations they meet.

Note which way round it falls here: the **borrowed** word is the casual one.
That is not a rule — it is what happened to these two.
