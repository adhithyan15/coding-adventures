# ਚਾਹ — the ਹ that is not a consonant

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-PAANI, PA-PHRASE-KIRPA-KARKE, PA-SOUND-PUNJABI-TONE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Pāṇī, kirpā karke* — water, please. The next drink uses the exact
same pattern, and its spelling hides a tone you have not met in a noun yet.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-CHA]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਚਾਹ** — *cāh* — **tea**

> **ਚਾਹ, ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ।** — *cāh, kirpā karke.* — **Tea, please.**

## Sounds you'll need — a high tone with nowhere left to fall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SOUND-TONE-HIGH-LEVEL]; assesses=[PA-SOUND-PUNJABI-TONE, PA-SOUND-TONE-FALLING] -->

**ਚਾਹ** is spelled with a final **ਹ**, but that **ਹ** is not said as a
consonant. Chapter 8 showed a *jh* losing its breath and leaving a **high,
falling** pitch on the vowel before it — falling, because more of the word
still followed. Here nothing follows: **ਚਾਹ** is one syllable, so the pitch
has nowhere to fall *to*. It simply stays **high**: *cā́h*, level and high
from the vowel to the end of the word.

Gurmukhi has no separate tone mark. A trailing **ਹ** that is not pronounced
is, in practice, how the script writes this tone — the same way **ਗ਼**'s dot
writes a borrowed sound. Say **ਖਾਣਾ** *khāṇā* again, flat, then **ਚਾਹ** *cā́h*,
high: two words that both end their vowel clean, one levelled and one not.

## The word, taken apart — the tea that went overland
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-CHA-CHINESE]; assesses=[PA-LEX-PAANI, PA-ETYMON-PAANI-DRINK] -->

**ਚਾਹ** is a loanword — unlike **pāṇī**, which Punjabi has owned since
Sanskrit. It came through Classical Persian **čā**, from Mandarin Chinese
**chá**: the **overland** route, out through Central Asia and Persia, the same
road that gave Hindi-Urdu *chāy*, Russian *chai*, and Turkish *çay*. Chinese
merchants on the coast spoke a different dialect, Hokkien, where the same word
is *tê* — carried by sea into Dutch and then English **tea**, French *thé*,
German *Tee*. One Chinese word, two pronunciations, sorted by whether a
language met it on a caravan route or a ship's deck.
