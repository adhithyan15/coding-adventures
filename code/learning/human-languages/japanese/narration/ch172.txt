Japanese, chapter 172: Mealtimes and Hunger.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょくじ (shokuji) — meal; dining.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for open for business, then the Japanese for not yet open.

You'll want to know: しょくじ.
しょくじ — shokuji — "meal; dining".
まいにち かぞくと しょくじを します。 — mainichi kazoku to shokuji o shimasu — "I have meals with my family every day."

The word, taken apart.
しょくじ (shokuji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shoku, "eat, food" (Middle Chinese zyik) and ji, "matter, thing" (dzriH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ひるごはん (hirugohan) — lunch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for not yet open, then the Japanese for meal.

You'll want to know: ひるごはん.
ひるごはん — hirugohan — "lunch".
ひるごはんに うどんを たべます。 — hirugohan ni udon o tabemasu — "I eat udon for lunch."

The word, taken apart.
ひるごはん (hirugohan) is built from native hiru, "midday", and Sino-Japanese gohan, "cooked rice, a meal", whose two parts are readings borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
すく (suku) — to get hungry; to become empty, to thin out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for meal, then the Japanese for lunch.

You'll want to know: すく.
すく — suku — "to get hungry; to become empty, to thin out".
ゆうがた おなかが すきます。 — yūgata onaka ga sukimasu — "I get hungry in the evening."
Polite すきます, sukimasu; a godan verb. The hungry one is the stomach: おなかが すきます.

The word, taken apart.
すく (suku) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for lunch, then the Japanese for to get hungry.

You'll want to know: ばんごはん.
ばんごはん — bangohan — "dinner; evening meal".
ばんごはんは いつも うちで たべます。 — bangohan wa itsumo uchi de tabemasu — "I always eat dinner at home."

The word, taken apart.
ばんごはん (bangohan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ban, "evening" (Middle Chinese mjonX), go, "honourable" (ngjoH) and han, "cooked rice" (bjonH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
