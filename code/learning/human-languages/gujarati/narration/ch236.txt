Gujarati, chapter 236: Birds in the neem tree.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
પંખી (pankhī) — bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to lick, then the Gujarati for tail.

You'll want to know: પંખી.
પંખી — pankhī — "bird".
સવારે એક પંખી આવ્યું. — savāre ek pankhī āvyũ — "A bird came in the morning."
Not પંખો, a fan.

The word, taken apart.
પંખી (pankhī) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit pakṣin, "winged; bird", built on pakṣa, "wing" (CDIAL 7636).

Lesson 2 of 6.
લીમડો (līmḍo) — neem tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for tail, then the Gujarati for bird.

You'll want to know: લીમડો.
લીમડો — līmḍo — "neem tree".
અમારા ઘર પાસે લીમડો હતો. — amārā ghar pāse līmḍo hato — "There was a neem tree near our house."

The word, taken apart.
લીમડો (līmḍo) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nimba, "the neem tree", where CDIAL lists Gujarati lĩbṛo (CDIAL 7245).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ડાળી (ḍāḷī) — branch, twig.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for bird, then the Gujarati for neem tree.

You'll want to know: ડાળી.
ડાળી — ḍāḷī — "branch, twig".
ગઈકાલે લીમડાની ડાળી પડી. — gaīkāle līmḍānī ḍāḷī paḍī — "Yesterday a branch of the neem tree fell."

The word, taken apart.
ડાળી (ḍāḷī) is inherited from the late Sanskrit ḍāla, "branch"; CDIAL lists Gujarati ḍāḷī, "branch", and notes that the many shapes of the word point to a non-Aryan, perhaps Munda, source (CDIAL 5546).

પોપટની ચાંચ લાલ હતી. — popaṭnī cā̃c lāl hatī — "The parrot's beak was red."

The word, taken apart.
ચાંચ (cā̃c) is inherited from Sanskrit cañcu, "beak", a word CDIAL takes from a Dravidian source; it lists Gujarati cā̃c, "beak" (CDIAL 4569).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ચણવું (caṇvũ) — to peck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for branch, then the Gujarati for beak.

You'll want to know: ચણવું.
ચણવું — caṇvũ — "to peck".
ચકલી આંગણામાં ચણી. — caklī ā̃gaṇāmā̃ caṇī — "The sparrow pecked in the courtyard."

The word, taken apart.
The origin of ચણવું (caṇvũ) in the sense "to peck" is not settled. CDIAL lists Gujarati caṇvũ only as "to build", under Sanskrit cinoti, "heaps up" (CDIAL 4814), and gives cugvũ as the word for a bird pecking (CDIAL 4852).
