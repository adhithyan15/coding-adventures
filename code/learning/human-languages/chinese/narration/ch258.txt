Mandarin Chinese, chapter 258: Warm-Hearted.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yǒuhǎo — friendly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a singer, then the Mandarin for to applaud.

You'll want to know: yǒuhǎo.
yǒuhǎo — "friendly".
zhèr de rén hěn yǒuhǎo — People here are very friendly.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: yǒu is said with a rising tone before hǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
yǒuhǎo is built from yǒu 'a friend' and 好 'good', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwX and xawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a friend'.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 2 of 6.
热心 (rèxīn) — warm-hearted; eager to help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to applaud, then the Mandarin for friendly.

You'll want to know: 热心.
热心 — rèxīn — "warm-hearted; eager to help".
我们的校长很热心 wǒmen de xiàozhǎng hěn rèxīn — Our head teacher is very warm-hearted.
The new character, in pieces: 热 (rè) — ten strokes: the hand piece of 打, three strokes; beside it a stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns down and hooks up, and a dot; then four dots along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
热心 (rèxīn) is built from 热 (rè) 'hot' and 心 'the heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyet and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as burning, as with fire.

Script — the hand piece and a hooked bend, over four dots.
热 (rè).
Three pieces: two side by side on top, and a row of dots under them.
The top left piece, three strokes, the hand piece of 打: a short bar, a vertical with a hook, then a stroke rising to the right across it.
The top right piece, three strokes. A stroke falling to the left. A bar that runs right, turns down, bends out to the right and hooks up. Then a dot inside.
The foot, four dots, left to right: the first leans to the left, the other three to the right.
3 + 3 + 4 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 热 (rè), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 热 (rè)? (Ten.) Which piece comes first? (The hand piece of 打.) What runs along the foot? (Four dots.)

The word, taken apart.
yōumò is usually traced to English humour, written with two characters whose sounds come close to it, yōu 'deep' and mò 'silent'; who first wrote it this way is not documented in what we can open.
