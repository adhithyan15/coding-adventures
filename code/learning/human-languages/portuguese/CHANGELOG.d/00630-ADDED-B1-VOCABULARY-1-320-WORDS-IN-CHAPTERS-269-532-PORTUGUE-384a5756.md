## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,320 words in chapters 269–532; Portuguese attains B1

- **264 new chapters (269–532) teach 1,320 new headwords in European Portuguese, one word per lesson.** Each lesson gives:
  - the word and its English;
  - a short example with its translation;
  - a usage note, which gives the Brazilian form where it differs;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 269–334, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (330 words): time and sequence, news, crime and justice, accidents and emergencies, life events, festivals (the *Santos Populares* among them), stories and history, and cooking step by step.
  - 335–400, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (330 words): opinion and argument, cause, work, society, conditions and plans, study, rules and the law, money, and technology. It includes *visto que*, *dado que*, *uma vez que*, *desde que*, *contanto que*, *caso*, *a menos que* and *a não ser que*, each shown with the mood it takes.
  - 401–466, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (330 words): the road and the car, stations, ports and airports, lodging and complaints, finding the way, the town, shops and services, nature, and weather.
  - 467–532, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (330 words): feelings and character, health and the body, relationships and family, hobbies, sport, music and the arts, reading and the screen, and life at home.
- **Portuguese now teaches 2,564 headwords at or below B1**, past the 2,500 the level gate asks for. With the B1 core (chapters 259–268), Portuguese attains B1.
- **European Portuguese throughout:** *comboio*, *autocarro*, *telemóvel*, *ficheiro*, *utilizador*, *estar a* + infinitive and enclisis, all in the AO90 orthography, as the CAPLE target requires.
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1 and Lewis & Short. Where neither supports a step, the lesson hedges it or says the origin is not settled, and learned borrowings are told apart from inherited words. An independent review of each tranche found and fixed errors in 249 of the 1,320 words (18.9%). Most were origin claims the sources do not support or wrong learned/inherited labels; the rest were Brazilian senses given as general, examples and root slugs. The review also replaced three headwords that only repeated taught words (*o lábio* against the taught *os lábios*, *os gémeos*, *a luva*).
- **No forward references.** Every candidate headword was screened against the text of every earlier lesson before drafting. Spanish's and Italian's B1 lessons showed this is the only way to keep the ratchet. Portuguese's forward-reference count stays at its pin.
