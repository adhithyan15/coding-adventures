## You'll want to know: गुरुः
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-C26-ROLE-01]; assesses=[] -->

**गुरुः** (*guruḥ*) — "a teacher, and the word for heavy".

**गुरुः** reached English on its own, so you already say it. What English lost on the way is the ordinary meaning: *guru* first of all means **heavy**. A heavy stone is *guru*; a teacher is *guru* because a teacher has weight.

The heavy-word travelled: Latin *gravis* — whence *grave*, *gravity*, *aggravate* — and Greek *barus*, whence *barometer*, the weight-measurer. So गुरुः, *gravity* and *barometer* all descend from one word for heaviness, and only Sanskrit made a person out of it.
