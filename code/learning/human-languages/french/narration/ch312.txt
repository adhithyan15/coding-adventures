French, chapter 312: Une nouvelle maison.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le déménagement — the move, the house move.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tiered wedding cake, then the French for the sugared almond.

You'll want to know: le déménagement.
le déménagement — "the move, the house move".
Le déménagement a pris toute la journée. The move took the whole day.
The verb is déménager.

The word, taken apart.
déménagement is built on ménage "household", which goes back to Vulgar Latin mansiōnāticum, built on Latin mānsiō "a dwelling", from manēre "to stay" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la crémaillère — the house-warming; a pot-hook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the move, then the French for to move in.

You'll want to know: la crémaillère.
la crémaillère — "the house-warming; a pot-hook".
Ils ont pendu la crémaillère samedi soir. They had their house-warming on Saturday night.
Pendre la crémaillère — to throw a house-warming party; once, hanging the pot-hook over the fire was the last step of moving in.

The word, taken apart.
crémaillère is usually traced to Late Latin cremaculus "a pot-hook", from Greek kremastḗr "a hanger"; the details are not settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

locataire is a learned word built on Latin locātus, from locāre "to place, to let out" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists Italian locatario beside it.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: le bail.
le bail — "the lease".
Ils ont signé un bail de trois ans. They signed a three-year lease.
Plural les baux. Informal: ça fait un bail! — it has been ages!

The word, taken apart.
bail is built on bailler "to hand over" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin bāiulāre "to carry a load" (Lewis & Short).
