# دس — ten

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-NAU, UR-ETYMON-NAU-NINE-PIE] -->

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-DAS]; assesses=[UR-LEX-DO] -->

> **دس** — *das* — **ten**

Two letters again, and the first is two's **د**.

## The word, taken apart — decem, ten, das
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-ETYMON-DAS-TEN-PIE]; assesses=[UR-LEX-DAS, UR-LEX-SAAT] -->

**دس** continues Sanskrit *daśa*, from Indo-European \**dekm*,
"ten." Latin **decem** — and through it **decimal** and **December** — English
**ten** and Greek **déka** are the same word.

*das* is the first number in this book that a shop is likely to say out loud.
Very little is priced below ten, which is why a course stopping at five could
name amounts nobody charges.
