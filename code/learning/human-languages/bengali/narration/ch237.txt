Bengali, chapter 237: To put out, To light, To bring to a halt, To cross, To go on an outing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
নেভানো (nebhāno) — to put out (a fire, a light).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an assembly, a meeting, then the Bengali for a journalist.

You'll want to know: নেভানো.
নেভানো — nebhāno — "to put out (a fire, a light)".

The word, taken apart.
নেভানো (nebhāno) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nirvāyati, "is extinguished" (CDIAL 7399). CDIAL lists Bengali nibāna, "to extinguish".

You'll want to know: পার হওয়া.
পার হওয়া — pār hôoyā — "to cross".

The phrase, taken apart.
পার হওয়া (pār hôoyā) is pār, "the far side, across", from Sanskrit pāra, "further bank" (CDIAL 8100), + hôoyā, "to be", from Sanskrit bhavati, "becomes, is" (CDIAL 9416).

Lesson 5 of 5.
বেড়ানো (beṛāno) — to go on an outing, to stroll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to bring to a halt, then the Bengali for to cross.

You'll want to know: বেড়ানো.
বেড়ানো — beṛāno — "to go on an outing, to stroll".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of বেড়ানো (beṛāno) is not settled. CDIAL lists Bengali beṛā, "to surround (with a fence)", under Sanskrit veṣṭayati, "wraps up, surrounds" (CDIAL 12132), but not the sense "to stroll".
