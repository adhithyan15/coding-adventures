# dēlīrō, dēlīrāre — to be delirious, talk nonsense

## You'll want to know: dēlīrō, dēlīrāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C510-INSA-05]; assesses=[] -->

**dēlīrō, dēlīrāre** — "to be delirious, talk nonsense".

> Aeger febrī dēlīrat.
>
> *The patient is delirious with fever.*

*dēlīrō, dēlīrāre, dēlīrāvī*: *dē* 'out of' + *līra* 'furrow' — to plough crooked, then to wander in mind. English *delirium*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**dēlīrō** is built on *dē-* "away from" and *līra* "a furrow" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): first to plough out of the line, then to wander in mind. English *delirious* and *delirium* come from it.
