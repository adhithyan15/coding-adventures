Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रच्छादयति (pracchādayati) — conceals, covers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for cleans, then the Sanskrit for fills.

You'll want to know: प्रच्छादयति.
प्रच्छादयति — pracchādayati — "conceals, covers".

The word, taken apart.
pracchādayati is pra- with chādayati, "covers, veils", built on the root √chad, "to cover" (MW). The simple verb lives on as Hindi chānā, "to thatch, spread over" (CDIAL 5018).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वितरति (vitarati) — distributes, hands out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for fills, then the Sanskrit for conceals.

You'll want to know: वितरति.
वितरति — vitarati — "distributes, hands out".

The word, taken apart.
vitarati is built on vi- "apart" and the root √tṝ, "to cross over" (MW). In the Rigveda it means "traverses"; in the Mahābhārata it already means "gives" (CDIAL 11719), the sense of handing out that you are learning here.

Lesson 3 of 5.
संगृह्णाति (saṅgṛhṇāti) — collects.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: संगृह्णाति.
संगृह्णाति — saṅgṛhṇāti — "collects".

The word, taken apart.
saṅgṛhṇāti is built on sam "together" and the root √grah, "to seize" (MW), the root of gṛhṇāti, "takes", which you have already met. To seize things together is to collect them.

Lesson 4 of 5.
विश्वसिति (viśvasiti) — trusts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for distributes, then the Sanskrit for collects.

You'll want to know: विश्वसिति.
विश्वसिति — viśvasiti — "trusts".

The word, taken apart.
viśvasiti is built on vi- and the root √śvas, "to breathe" (MW). MW's first gloss is "to draw breath freely, be free from fear", and from there "to trust". Its noun viśvāsa, "trust", went into Hindi as viśvās.

Lesson 5 of 5.
लज्जते (lajjate) — is ashamed, is shy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for collects, then the Sanskrit for trusts.

You'll want to know: लज्जते.
लज्जते — lajjate — "is ashamed, is shy".

The word, taken apart.
lajjate is the present of the root √lajj, "to be ashamed, blush" (MW). MW gives the root no older source, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi lājnā, "to blush, be ashamed" (CDIAL 10909).
