German, chapter 472: Sehnsucht und Heimweh.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sehnen — to long (sich sehnen nach).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to comfort, then the German for pity.

You'll want to know: sehnen.
sehnen — "to long (sich sehnen nach)".
Im Winter sehne ich mich nach der Sonne. In winter I long for the sun.
Reflexive: sich nach etwas sehnen.

The word, taken apart.
sehnen goes back to Middle High German senen "to long, to pine"; its older origin is not settled, and EtymDB lists the German verb without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Sehnsucht — longing, yearning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for pity, then the German for to long.

You'll want to know: die Sehnsucht.
die Sehnsucht — "longing, yearning".
Er hatte große Sehnsucht nach seiner Familie. He longed for his family.
Plural: die Sehnsüchte. Sehnsucht nach + dative.

The word, taken apart.
Sehnsucht is built on sehnen "to long" and Sucht "sickness, craving" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Old High German suht, from Proto-Germanic suhtiz.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Einsamkeit — loneliness; solitude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to long, then the German for longing.

You'll want to know: die Einsamkeit.
die Einsamkeit — "loneliness; solitude".
Nach dem Umzug litt sie unter Einsamkeit. After the move she suffered from loneliness.
No plural. Built on einsam — lonely.

The word, taken apart.
Einsamkeit is built on einsam "lonely" with -keit (EtymDB lists both); einsam joins ein "one", from Proto-Germanic ainaz, to the ending -sam.

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Heimweh — homesickness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for longing, then the German for loneliness.

You'll want to know: das Heimweh.
das Heimweh — "homesickness".
In der ersten Woche im Ferienlager hatte er Heimweh. In the first week at summer camp he was homesick.
No plural. The opposite is das Fernweh — the urge to travel.

The word, taken apart.
Heimweh is built on Heim "home" and Weh "pain, woe", which goes back to Middle High German we (EtymDB); the word is usually said to have spread from Swiss German.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vermissen — to miss (someone or something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for loneliness, then the German for homesickness.

You'll want to know: vermissen.
vermissen — "to miss (someone or something)".
Ich vermisse meine Freunde aus der Schule. I miss my friends from school.
Also to find something missing: Seit gestern vermisse ich meinen Schlüssel.

The word, taken apart.
vermissen is built on ver- and the older verb missen "to lack, to do without" (EtymDB), a Germanic word; English miss is a cousin.
