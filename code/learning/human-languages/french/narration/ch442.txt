French, chapter 442: Façades et vitrines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la façade — the façade, the front (of a building).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the kiosk, then the French for the cobblestone.

You'll want to know: la façade.
la façade — "the façade, the front (of a building)".
La façade de la mairie est toute blanche. The front of the town hall is all white.
Figurative: c'est de la façade — it is all for show.

The word, taken apart.
façade was borrowed from Italian facciata (EtymDB), built on faccia 'face', which goes back to Latin faciēs.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'enseigne — the shop sign; the brand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the façade, then the French for the shop window.

You'll want to know: l'enseigne.
l'enseigne — "the shop sign; the brand".
L'enseigne du café est en bois. The café's sign is made of wood.
A feminine noun. Une grande enseigne — a big chain of shops.

The word, taken apart.
enseigne is inherited from Latin īnsignia 'marks, badges', from signum 'mark' (Lewis & Short).

la gouttière — "the roof gutter, the rain gutter".
L'eau de pluie coule dans la gouttière. The rainwater runs into the gutter.
On the roof; the gutter in the street has another name.

gouttière is built on goutte 'drop', which is inherited from Latin gutta (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la halle — the covered market (hall).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the shop sign, then the French for the roof gutter.

You'll want to know: la halle.
la halle — "the covered market (hall)".
Le samedi, les paysans vendent leurs légumes sous la halle. On Saturdays the farmers sell their vegetables in the covered market.
Les halles — the covered market of a town.

The word, taken apart.
halle comes from Old French hale, from Frankish halla 'large room, hall' (EtymDB).
