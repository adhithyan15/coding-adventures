Bengali, chapter 469: Sweat and Skin.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ঘামা (ghāmā) — to sweat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a vein, then the Bengali for to thump.

You'll want to know: ঘামা.
ঘামা — ghāmā — "to sweat".
গরমে বাসে বসে আমি ঘামছিলাম। gôrôme bāse bôse āmi ghāmchhilām. Sitting on the bus in the heat, I was sweating.
Past (he/she …): তিনি রোদে ঘামলেন, tini rode ghāmlen.
The noun is ঘাম, sweat: ঘামে ভেজা জামা, a shirt wet with sweat.

The word, taken apart.
ঘামা (ghāmā) is the verb of ghām, "sweat", which CDIAL lists for Bengali under Sanskrit gharma, "heat" (CDIAL 4445).

মুখে ব্রণ হলে অরুণ লজ্জা পায়। mukhe brôn hôle ôrun lôjjā pāy. When he gets spots on his face, Arun feels shy.

The word, taken apart.
ব্রণ (brôn) is a tatsama, Sanskrit vraṇa, "an abscess, a boil, a scar" (CDIAL 12227); Bengali has narrowed it to a pimple.

তিল (til) is also the sesame seed; CDIAL thinks the two may be one word.

The word, taken apart.
তিল (til) is Sanskrit tila, "a mole on the skin" (CDIAL 5828), which CDIAL thinks may be the same word as tila, the sesame seed: a seed-sized dark spot.
