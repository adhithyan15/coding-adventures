## Grammar Lens: one, and the other
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C77-ORDER-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **एक … दूसरा** — "**one … the other**".
> **एक किताब नई है, दूसरी पुरानी।** — One book is new, the other old.
> **एक आदमी यहाँ है, दूसरा वहाँ।** — One man is here, the other there.

The second ordinal lesson said to hold both senses of **दूसरा** together, and
this is why. Hindi needs no third word to say "one … the other": it puts **एक**
in the first half and **दूसरा** in the second, and the second sense of *dūsrā*
does the rest.

Both halves agree with what they name, so a feminine noun gives **एक … दूसरी**.

That is a thing worth having. Sorting two of anything — two books, two men, two
roads — needs this and nothing else.
