## HL-C187 — बारिश expands the Hindi weather headword

After **दीजिए**, the clearest remaining high-occurrence Hindi candidate was
**बारिश**. Chapter 20 defines it as the everyday colloquial word for rain,
contrasts it with formal **वर्षा**, asks learners to produce both registers,
and assesses their likely false-friend relationship. It is therefore a real
lexical target, not etymological scenery.

The same audit exposed a broader metadata mismatch. Chapter 20's headword named
only **मौसम**, although its three assessed concepts also cover **गर्मी है**,
**ठंड है**, and **बारिश हो रही है**. The phrase headword now declares those
new weather forms, while **वर्षा** stays owned by the earlier season-set lesson;
redeclaring it here would move its continuity teaching point forward. This
promotes **बारिश** and also prevents the taught heat and cold forms from
remaining hidden behind the chapter label.

Rerun the ranked report next. Continue past the script, morphology, and
etymology fragments already evidenced in prior dispositions, and audit the
highest-occurrence remaining communicative form before changing metadata.
