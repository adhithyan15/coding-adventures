## Grammar Lens: three signs that stop you, and they are not the same
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-03, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-04, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-02] -->

English puts *closed* on all three of these. Spanish does not, and the word it
picks tells you what to do next.

| the sign | what it tells you |
|---|---|
| **CERRADO** | shut for now; come back later |
| **CORTADA** | the route is severed; go round |
| **TRASLADADO** | it is open, somewhere else |

A card reading *TRASLADADO* is the one worth recognising fastest, because it is
the only one of the three with an address underneath it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-01, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-02, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-03] -->

**You can now read the signs that decide where you are allowed to walk.** A
station, a shut shop, a severed street and an office that has gone somewhere
else are four different pieces of bad news, and you can tell them apart at a
glance instead of guessing from context.

You also met the limit of etymology. **el andén** and **tirar** have no
recoverable history, and the honest entry for both is *origin uncertain*. Every
other word in this chapter and the last came apart; these two did not, and saying
otherwise would mean making something up.
