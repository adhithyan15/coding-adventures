# വരുമ്പോൾ (varumpōḷ) — "when he comes"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C75-EPPOL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C32-POKUKA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the question word for time. Then say the future of *to go* and
the future of *to come*.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C75-UMBOL-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **അവൻ വരുമ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകും.**
>
> *avan varumpōḷ ñān pōkuṁ* — "**When he comes**, I will go."

English needs a word in front of the clause: *when* he comes. **Malayalam needs
no word at all.** It puts the whole idea on the end of the verb, and the clause
is one word long.

That is the thing to take from this lesson. Look for the join at the **end of a
verb** rather than in front of a clause, and a sentence that looked long stops
being long.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C75-UMBOL-01] -->

Write the future you already know, then put **പോൾ** after it:

| step | |
|---|---|
| the future | **വരും** *varuṁ* |
| plus പോൾ | **വരും + പോൾ** |
| written | **വരുമ്പോൾ** *varumpōḷ* |

The **ം** that ends വരും is an *m*. When a letter follows it, that *m* is
written joined to what comes next — **മ്പ**, the same shape you already read in
**ഒമ്പത്** (*ombatŭ*), "nine".

So the ending is not new material. It is a future form you have had since the
tense lesson, with the time-piece from the last lesson stuck on the back.

Tamil builds it the same way, from the same two parts: **வரும்போது**
(*varumpōtu*).

## Grammar Lens: it is not a question any more
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C75-UMBOL-01, ML-LEX-C75-EPPOL-01] -->

എപ്പോൾ **asks**. വരുമ്പോൾ **does not.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **എപ്പോൾ വരും?** | When will he come? |
| **വരുമ്പോൾ** | when he comes |

Both carry പോൾ and they are related. But one waits for an answer, and the other
fixes one event against another inside a sentence that keeps going. **If പോൾ is
on the end of a verb, nobody is being asked anything.**
