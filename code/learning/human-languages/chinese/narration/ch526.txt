Lesson 1 of 6.
chēng — to weigh on a scale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to make a formal complaint, then the Mandarin for to give the money back.

You'll want to know: chēng.
chēng — "to weigh on a scale".
qǐng nǐ bāng wǒ chēng zhèxiē píngguǒ — Could you weigh these apples for me?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chēng is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhing (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to know light from heavy'; the dictionary also lists tsyhingH, glossed 'fitting' and, in a common spelling, the balance that weighs things.

The word, taken apart.
gōngjīn is built from 公 'public, standard' and jīn, a weight, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and kj+n (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as sixteen ounces. The standard weight of a kilogram is a modern use.

Lesson 3 of 6.
重量 (zhòngliàng) — weight; how heavy something is.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to weigh on a scale, then the Mandarin for a kilogram.

You'll want to know: 重量.
重量 — zhòngliàng — "weight; how heavy something is".
zhège xiāngzi de zhòngliàng shì èrshí gōngjīn — This suitcase weighs twenty kilos.
The new character, in pieces: 量 (liàng) — twelve strokes in three pieces: 日 on top, four strokes; a long bar under it; and 里 at the foot, seven strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
重量 (zhòngliàng) is built from 重 'heavy' and liàng 'an amount', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjowngX and ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'much, thick' and gives the second as units of dry measure. The dictionary lists the second again as ljang, 'to measure', the liáng of the word for talking things over.

Script — 日, a long bar, then 里.
量 (liàng).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, 日, four strokes, small, as you wrote it: a vertical, a bar that turns down, a bar across the middle, a bar closing the foot.
One long bar across the middle, the widest stroke of the character.
The bottom piece, 里, seven strokes, as you wrote it: a vertical, a bar that turns down, a bar across the middle, a bar closing the box, a vertical down through the middle, a short bar, then the long bar at the foot.
4 + 1 + 7 = twelve.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 量 (liàng), all twelve, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 量 (liàng) have? (Three: 日, a long bar and 里.) How many strokes? (Twelve.)
