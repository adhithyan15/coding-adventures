Japanese, chapter 502: At the Theatre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
げきじょう (gekijō) — a theatre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a wind chime, then the Japanese for to scatter (something).

You'll want to know: げきじょう.
げきじょう — gekijō — "a theatre".
こんばん げきじょうで しばいを みます。 — konban gekijō de shibai o mimasu — "Tonight I am seeing a play at the theatre."

The word, taken apart.
げきじょう (gekijō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are geki, "drama" (Middle Chinese gjaek) and jō, "location" (drjang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
えんじる (enjiru) — to play (a role); to perform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a stage actor, then the Japanese for an actress.

You'll want to know: えんじる.
えんじる — enjiru — "to play (a role); to perform".
かれは えいがで いしゃを えんじます。 — kare wa eiga de isha o enjimasu — "He plays a doctor in the film."
Polite えんじます, enjimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): やくしゃが いしゃを えんじました。, yakusha ga isha o enjimashita.

The word, taken apart.
えんじる (enjiru) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese en, "performance" (Middle Chinese yenX), borrowed from Chinese and the native ending jiru (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
