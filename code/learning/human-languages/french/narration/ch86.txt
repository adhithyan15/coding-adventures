The word, taken apart.
ours from Old French urs from Latin ursus, "bear" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le renard — a fox.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a wolf, then the French for a bear.

You'll want to know: le renard.
le renard — "a fox".

The word, taken apart.
renard — named after Renart, the cunning fox of the medieval Roman de Renart; his name is the Germanic personal name Reginhard. It replaced the Old French word goupil, from Latin vulpecula, "little fox".

You'll want to know: le canard.
le canard — "a duck".

The word, taken apart.
canard — built from Old French cane, "a duck", with -ard (EtymDB 2.1). Where cane itself came from is uncertain; it is usually taken to imitate the bird's cackle.
