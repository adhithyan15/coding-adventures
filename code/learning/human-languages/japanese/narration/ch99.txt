さいふ (saifu) — a wallet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the fault, then the Japanese for a reason.

You'll want to know: さいふ.
さいふ — saifu — "a wallet".

The word, taken apart.
さいふ (saifu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sai, "wealth" (Middle Chinese dzoj) and fu, "cloth" (puH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 2 of 5.
つえ (tsue) — a walking stick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a reason, then the Japanese for a wallet.

You'll want to know: つえ.
つえ — tsue — "a walking stick".

The word, taken apart.
つえ (tsue) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

たわし — tawashi — "a scrubbing brush".

The word, taken apart.
たわし (tawashi) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. JMdict writes it with characters for "bundle" and "child", read as a whole, and marks it as usually written in kana. Its older origin is not settled.
