# दो भाषाएँ बोलती है — counting from one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Read this and say how many languages, before reading on.

> दो भाषाएँ बोलती है।

## Grammar Lens: the line a paper prints
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-04, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

That clause is lifted from a personal account in the second practice paper.
Until this chapter, **भाषाएँ** was an unreadable shape.

| word | what it does |
|---|---|
| दो | the number |
| भाषाएँ | the noun, carrying the plural nasal |
| बोलती है | the habit, and the speaker is a woman |

Two things say *plural* here and neither is the verb: the numeral and the
ending on the noun. **The verb agrees with the person, not with the count.**

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-07] -->

The other half of the chapter, in two lines that look almost alike:

> — बच्चे कहाँ हैं?
> — बच्चे को घर भेजा।

The first **बच्चे** is a plural and the second is one child in the oblique.
**Nothing but the postposition separates them**, and a reader who misses it
counts wrong.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-04] -->

Say each of these out loud, as a plural:

- one child, then two children
- one book, then two books
- one language, then two languages
- one man, then two men

[PAUSE 5s]

The fourth is the one that sounds wrong and is right. Say it twice.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Does the verb tell you how many? (No — it agrees with the person.)
What separates a plural **बच्चे** from an oblique one? (A postposition behind
it.) Which noun shape shows no plural at all? (A masculine noun not ending in
**-आ**.)
