Japanese, chapter 309: Presents in the Post.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
くれる (kureru) — to give to me; to give (to my family).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to speak to (someone), then the Japanese for busy.

You'll want to know: くれる.
くれる — kureru — "to give to me; to give (to my family)".
そぼが ゆかたを くれました。 — sobo ga yukata o kuremashita — "My grandmother gave me a summer kimono."
Polite くれます, kuremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ともだちが てがみを くれました。, tomodachi ga tegami o kuremashita.

The word, taken apart.
くれる (kureru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
おかえし (okaeshi) — a gift in return.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to give to me, then the Japanese for to give (to someone else).

You'll want to know: おかえし.
おかえし — okaeshi — "a gift in return".
おいわいの おかえしを おくりました。 — oiwai no okaeshi o okurimashita — "I sent a gift in return for the present."

The word, taken apart.
おかえし (okaeshi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from o-, "the polite prefix", and kaeshi, "returning" (from kaesu) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
