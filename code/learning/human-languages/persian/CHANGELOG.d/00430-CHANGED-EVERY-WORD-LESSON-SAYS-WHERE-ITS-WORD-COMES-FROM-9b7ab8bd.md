## Changed — every word lesson says where its word comes from

Every Persian word lesson now carries an `etymology_hook` and states its word's origin in the lesson body (HL24 §9). The etymology gap pins fall from 1,300 / 1,311 to 0 / 0, and the two empty "taken apart" blocks are filled (human-language-data: `persian.owner.ts`, `EMPTY_ETYMOLOGY_BLOCK_PINS.persian` 2 → 0).

- **Chapters 27-287.** The 1,300 vocabulary-wave lessons each gain a "The word, taken apart" block before "What you've built", and a matching hook. Arabic loans (319, 64 of them an Arabic noun with a Persian verb such as *kardan*) name the Arabic word, its sense and its root in Lane's Lexicon. Inherited words (about 440) give their Middle Persian form, cited to EtymDB 2.1 where its rows support it and otherwise hedged as "usually traced to". About 284 compounds and derivations are taken apart into their parts, each part with its origin. Loans name their donor: French (75), English (20), Turkic and Mongolian (21), Russian (5), Greek, Latin, Aramaic, Syriac and Akkadian (about 10), and Indian (5). Before review, 114 said the origin is not settled.
- **Corrections from the sources.** EtymDB's homograph links were rejected and said so where a learner could meet them (*sar* "head" is not Arabic *sirr* "secret"; *shâm* "dinner" is not *Shâm* "Syria"; *vali* "but" is not Arabic *walī*). *behtar* and *bad* say that English *better* and *bad* only look alike. Lane's notes that some Arabic words were themselves taken from Persian are carried (*qirmiz*, *qafaṣ*, *kibrīt*).
- **Hand-written chapters 1-20.** 14 hooks that described grammar or spelling now say where the word comes from; 12 lessons gain a short "taken apart" block (*bale*, *shomâ / to*, *chist*, *esm-e shomâ chist*, *hâl-e shomâ chetor ast*, *khubam*, *khodâ hâfez*, the teens *yâzdah*, *sizdah*, *pânzdah*, and the ordinals). *chetor*'s and *raftan*'s blocks now name their source. Older claims no source supports were corrected: *cheshm* continues Middle Persian *čašm* and is a cousin of Sanskrit *cakṣus*, not of English *spy* (its root slug `pie-spek` is removed); *setâre* continues Middle Persian *stārag*, not an Old Persian *star-*; *chahâr* continues Middle Persian *čahār*, not an Old Persian *chathwar*; *yek* continues Middle Persian *ēk*; the *kelid* hook no longer says Greek gave Latin *claudere*; *chây* "comes from" rather than "continues" Chinese *chá*.
- **Review.** Two independent reviewers read all 1,320 changed lessons against Lane, EtymDB and CDIAL. 54 lessons (4.1%) needed a content fix, all made. 33 "not settled" origins now give a hedged, sourced account: among them *bâdemjân* from Sanskrit *vātiṅgaṇa* (CDIAL 11503; Lane says Arabic *bāḏinjān* came from Persian), *limu* from Sanskrit *nimbū* (CDIAL 7247), *polow* from Sanskrit *pulāka* (CDIAL 8295), *kelisâ* from Greek *ekklēsia*, *jurâb* from Arabic *jawrab*, *kheyli* from Arabic *ḫayl*, *tanâb* from Arabic *ṭunub*, *âjor* from Akkadian *agurru*, *engelisi* from European *anglais* / *inglese*, *fâktor* from French *facture* and *ketri* from English *kettle*. About 60 words keep "not settled", most of them everyday words for which no account is published. Donors were corrected (*film* and *bâtri* from French, *sâbun* from Arabic *ṣābūn*), Middle Persian forms no source confirms were trimmed (*aknun*, *darvâze*, *kharboze*, *derow*), Arabic senses now follow Lane (*mashq*, *majalle*, *radd*), and root slugs now match the spellings the rest of the corpus uses (*arabic-khabar*, *persian-mihman*, *persian-ummed* and others).
- **No duration raised.** Every lesson stays within its declared `max_seconds`; the *bale* block was shortened to fit.
