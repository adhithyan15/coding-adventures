Lesson 1 of 5.
برسات (barsāt) — the rainy season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an ointment, then the Urdu for a pharmacy.

You'll want to know: برسات.
برسات — barsāt — "the rainy season".

The word, taken apart.
برسات (barsāt) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from varṣārātri, "the rainy season" (CDIAL 11398). It is built from varṣā, "rains", and rātri, "night".

The phrase, taken apart.
آج کل (āj kal) is "today-tomorrow": āj, "today", from Sanskrit adya (CDIAL 242), and kal, from kalya, "dawn, tomorrow" (CDIAL 3104).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for daily, then the Urdu for nowadays.

You'll want to know: آخر.
آخر — ākhir — "at last; the end".

The word, taken apart.
آخر (ākhir) is a loan from Arabic ākhir, "last, the end", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is ʾ-kh-r (Lane).

Lesson 5 of 5.
سالگرہ (sālgirah) — a birthday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for nowadays, then the Urdu for at last; the end.

You'll want to know: سالگرہ.
سالگرہ — sālgirah — "a birthday".

The word, taken apart.
سالگرہ (sālgirah) is "the year's knot", usually explained by the custom of tying a knot in a cord for each year of life. It joins Persian sāl, "year", and girah, "knot".
