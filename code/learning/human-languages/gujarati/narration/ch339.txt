Gujarati, chapter 339: Cause and Effect.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
અસર (asar) — an effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to pester, then the Gujarati for to hope.

You'll want to know: અસર.
અસર — asar — "an effect".
દવાની અસર બે કલાક પછી થઈ. davānī asar be kalāk pachī thaī. The medicine took effect two hours later.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
અસર (asar) is usually traced to Arabic athar, "a trace, a mark", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Gujarati uses it for what one thing does to another.

Lesson 2 of 5.
પ્રભાવ (prabhāv) — an influence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to hope, then the Gujarati for an effect.

You'll want to know: પ્રભાવ.
પ્રભાવ — prabhāv — "an influence".
ગુરુનો પ્રભાવ વિદ્યાર્થીઓ પર ઘણો છે. guruno prabhāv vidyārthīo par ghaṇo che. The teacher has a great deal of influence on the students.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પ્રભાવ (prabhāv) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit prabhāva, "might, power" (Monier-Williams). Gujarati uses it for the pull one person or thing has on another.

ગરમીને કારણે ઘણા રોગ નીપજે છે. garmīne kāraṇe ghaṇā rog nīpje che. Many illnesses come from the heat.
Past (it …): તે રોગ ગરમીથી નીપજ્યો, te rog garmīthī nīpajyo.

નીપજવું (nīpajvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit níṣpadyatē, "falls out, comes forth"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to be produced" (CDIAL 7511).

પૂર પછી ગામની પરિસ્થિતિ ખરાબ હતી. pūr pachī gāmnī paristhiti kharāb hatī. After the flood the village was in a bad way.

Lesson 5 of 5.
સંજોગ (sanjog) — a circumstance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to result, then the Gujarati for a situation.

You'll want to know: સંજોગ.
સંજોગ — sanjog — "a circumstance".
આવા સંજોગોમાં આપણે શું કરવું જોઈએ? āvā sanjogomā̃ āpaṇe śũ karvũ joīe? What should we do in circumstances like these?
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંજોગ (sanjog) goes back to Sanskrit saṃyoga, "a joining, a connection" (Monier-Williams), with y become j as in many words Gujarati inherited. "A circumstance" is its Gujarati sense: the way things happen to come together.
