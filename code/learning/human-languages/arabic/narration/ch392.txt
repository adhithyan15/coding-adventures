Arabic, chapter 392: Lecturers and supervisors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
محاضر (muḥāḍir) — a lecturer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an institute, then the Arabic for a questionnaire.

You'll want to know: محاضر.
محاضر — muḥāḍir — "a lecturer".
يشرح المحاضر الدرس بوضوح. yashraḥu al-muḥāḍir ad-dars bi-wuḍūḥ. The lecturer explains the lesson clearly.

The word, taken apart.
محاضر (muḥāḍir) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-ḍ-r, "to be present" (Lane). Muḥāḍir is the Form III active participle, one who speaks to those present: a lecturer.

Lesson 2 of 5.
أستاذ (ustādh) — a professor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a questionnaire, then the Arabic for a lecturer.

You'll want to know: أستاذ.
أستاذ — ustādh — "a professor".
أستاذ الأدب رجل لطيف. ustādh al-adab rajul laṭīf. The literature professor is a kind man.
Plural: أساتذة, asātidha.

The word, taken apart.
أستاذ (ustādh) was borrowed from Persian ustād, "a master, a teacher" (EtymDB). In Arabic it is both a polite title and a university professor.

Lesson 3 of 5.
أشرف (ashrafa) — to supervise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a lecturer, then the Arabic for a professor.

You'll want to know: أشرف.
أشرف — ashrafa — "to supervise".
أشرف المعلم على الرحلة. ashrafa al-muʿallim ʿalā ar-riḥla. The teacher supervised the trip.
Present: يشرف, yushrif.
It takes على: أشرف (ashrafa) على ….

The word, taken apart.
أشرف (ashrafa) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-f, "to be high, noble" (Lane). It is Form IV, to look down from a height: to oversee, to supervise.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ذاكر (dhākara) — to study for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to supervise, then the Arabic for a supervisor.

You'll want to know: ذاكر.
ذاكر — dhākara — "to study for".
ذاكر الطالب دروسه حتى منتصف الليل. dhākara aṭ-ṭālib durūsahu ḥattā muntaṣaf al-layl. The student studied his lessons until midnight.
Present: يذاكر, yudhākir.
Common in spoken Arabic; formal writing often says درس.

The word, taken apart.
ذاكر (dhākara) is built on the native Arabic root dh-k-r, usually traced to the sense "to remember, to mention". It is Form III, to go over a thing with oneself again: to study for a test.
