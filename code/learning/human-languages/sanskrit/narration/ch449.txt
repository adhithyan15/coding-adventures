Sanskrit, chapter 449: Amazed and Pleased.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आवेगः (āvegaḥ) — agitation; a flurry of feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for merriment, then the Sanskrit for distress.

You'll want to know: आवेगः.
आवेगः — āvegaḥ — "agitation; a flurry of feeling".
वार्तया मातुः आवेगः भवति। vārtayā mātuḥ āvegaḥ bhavati. The news throws mother into a flurry.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आवेगः (āvegaḥ) is built from ā and the root √vij, "to tremble, to start": a quick stirring of the mind; MW gives "hurry or haste produced by excitement" and "flurry, agitation" (MW).

विस्मयते — vismayate — "is amazed".
रात्रौ मीरा ताराः पश्यति, विस्मयते च। rātrau mīrā tārāḥ paśyati, vismayate ca. At night Mira sees the stars and is amazed.
Past (she …): सा विस्मितवती, sā vismitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
विस्मयते (vismayate) is built from vi, "apart", and the root √smi, "to smile", the verb behind विस्मयः; MW gives "to wonder, be surprised or astonished at" (MW).

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a flurry of feeling, then the Sanskrit for is amazed.

You'll want to know: आश्चर्यम्.
आश्चर्यम् — āścaryam — "a marvel".
अहो आश्चर्यम्! aho āścaryam! Oh, what a marvel!
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
आश्चर्यम् (āścaryam) MW reports that it is said to come from ā and the root √car, "to move", with an s inserted (Pāṇini 6.1.147); its first sense is "appearing rarely", and the history is not settled (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
तोषयति (toṣayati) — pleases; gratifies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for is amazed, then the Sanskrit for a marvel.

You'll want to know: तोषयति.
तोषयति — toṣayati — "pleases; gratifies".
पौत्रः मधुरेण गीतेन पितामहं तोषयति। pautraḥ madhureṇa gītena pitāmahaṃ toṣayati. The grandson pleases his grandfather with a sweet song.
Past (he …): सः पितामहं तोषितवान्, saḥ pitāmahaṃ toṣitavān.

The word, taken apart.
तोषयति (toṣayati) is built on the causative of the root √tuṣ, "to be satisfied, to be pleased", the root of तुष्यति and सन्तोषः: to make someone pleased; MW gives the causative the senses "to satisfy, please, appease" (MW).

रोमाञ्चः (romāñcaḥ) is built from roman, "the hair of the body", and añca, from the root √añc, "to bend, to rise"; MW gives "thrill of the hair" (MW).
