Arabic, chapter 81: Camel, Donkey, Hen, Big, Small.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جمل (jamal) — a camel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a snake, then the Arabic for a bear.

You'll want to know: جمل.
جمل — jamal — "a camel".

The word, taken apart.
جمل (jamal) is built on the native Arabic root j-m-l, "a camel" (Lane). Hebrew gamal is its cousin, and English camel came through Greek kamēlos from a Semitic word of this kind.

You'll want to know: حمار.
حمار — ḥimār — "a donkey".

حمار (ḥimār) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-m-r, "an ass; to be red" (Lane). Hebrew ḥamor, "donkey", is its cousin; Lane lists it under the same letters as aḥmar, "red".

دجاجة (dajāja) is built on the native Arabic root d-j-j, "the domestic fowl" (Lane). Dajāja is one bird of dajāj, "fowls": a hen.
