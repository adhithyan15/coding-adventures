Portuguese, chapter 322: Fogo de artifício.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o fogo de artifício — the fireworks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the disguise, then the Portuguese for to disguise.

You'll want to know: o fogo de artifício.
o fogo de artifício — "the fireworks".
À meia-noite houve fogo de artifício na praia. At midnight there were fireworks on the beach.
Singular in Portuguese: o fogo de artifício foi lindo.

The word, taken apart.
fogo is inherited from Latin focus "a hearth" (EtymDB); artifício is a learned borrowing from Latin artificium "a craft, a skill", built on ars "art" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: o foguete.
o foguete — "the rocket".
Um foguete subiu e explodiu no céu. A rocket went up and burst in the sky.
Also a space rocket. At village festivals, foguetes are fired to announce the party.

The word, taken apart.
foguete is a little fogo "fire" (EtymDB lists it), from Latin focus "a hearth", with the small-thing ending -ete.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: a fogueira.
a fogueira — "the bonfire".
Na praia, fizemos uma fogueira e cantámos. On the beach we made a bonfire and sang.
People jump over fogueiras on some June nights.

The word, taken apart.
fogueira comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB) and goes back to Vulgar Latin focāria, built on Latin focus "a hearth".

Lesson 4 of 5.
enfeitar — to decorate, to adorn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the rocket, then the Portuguese for the bonfire.

You'll want to know: enfeitar.
enfeitar — "to decorate, to adorn".
As crianças enfeitaram a sala com balões. The children decorated the room with balloons.
Enfeitar-se — to dress up, to make oneself pretty.

The word, taken apart.
enfeitar is usually traced to en- and feito "made", the past participle of fazer, from Latin factus (Lewis & Short); EtymDB has the word with no source.
