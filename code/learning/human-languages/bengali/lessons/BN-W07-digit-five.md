# ৫ — the Bengali five

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-DIGIT-ONE-01, BN-LEX-C25-AMAKE-01, BN-SCRIPT-TIN-READ-01] -->

- [YOU RECALL: read **১** — **R1**, one lesson back]
- [YOU RECALL: say *me*, the object form — **R3**, twenty lessons back]
- [YOU RECALL: read **তিন** off the page — **R4**, eighty lessons back]

That last recall is the one this chapter exists for. **তিন** is the **word**
*three*, spelled with three letters, and you have been able to read it for eighty
lessons. **৩** is the **digit** *3*, and until two lessons ago you could not read
it at all. A book can print either one, and they are not the same object.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-DIGIT-FIVE-01]; assesses=[BN-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE, BN-SCRIPT-DIGIT-THREE-01] -->

> ৫

That is the Bengali digit **five**, and you can say it: **পাঁচ**.

Another one that brings nothing: a hooked stroke over an open bowl, like a **C**
with a lid. **5** will not help you and no Bengali letter you know is close to it.

You can now read every number written out of **০ ১ ২ ৩ ৫**, which is most of a
page number and all of a small count.
