Persian, chapter 197: Condition, Law, Nevertheless, Although, Or.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شرط (shart) — a condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an edge, then the Persian for the ground floor.

You'll want to know: شرط.
شرط — shart — "a condition".

The word, taken apart.
shart is borrowed from Arabic šarṭ, "condition, stipulation". Its Arabic root is š-r-ṭ (Lane).

قانون — qânun — "a law, a rule".

qânun is borrowed from Arabic qānūn, "rule, law", which goes back to Greek kanōn, "rule, measuring rod" (EtymDB), the word behind English canon.

Lesson 3 of 5.
با این حال (bâ in hâl) — nevertheless, even so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a condition, then the Persian for a law.

You'll want to know: با این حال.
با این حال — bâ in hâl — "nevertheless, even so".

The word, taken apart.
bâ in hâl is "with this state": bâ, "with", and in, "this", are native; hâl, "state", is borrowed from Arabic ḥāl (root ḥ-w-l, Lane).

Lesson 4 of 5.
اگرچه (agarche) — although.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a law, then the Persian for nevertheless.

You'll want to know: اگرچه.
اگرچه — agarche — "although".

The word, taken apart.
agarche is agar, "if", with che, "what": "even if". Both are native; agar continues Middle Persian agar (EtymDB).
