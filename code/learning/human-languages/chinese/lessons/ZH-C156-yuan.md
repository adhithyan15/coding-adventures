## You'll want to know: 元
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C156-YUAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**元** — *yuán* — "yuan", the unit of money, as it is written.

> **一本书十元** *yì běn shū shí yuán* — A book costs ten yuan.

On a price list it is written 元; aloud, most people say *kuài*: *shí kuài*. The next lesson puts the character in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**元** (*yuán*) is inherited from Middle Chinese *ngjwon* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'great; the beginning; the head'. The unit of money is a modern sense, assumed here, not quoted.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C41-MONEY41-03, ZH-LEX-C88-BEN-01] -->

The number, then 元: 十元. A thing's price follows the thing: 一本书, 十元.

When you say it, swap in *kuài*: *yì běn shū shí kuài*. 一 falls before the dip of běn: *yì běn*.
