# सः गतवान् (saḥ gatavān) — the past that follows the doer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-PAST-AUGMENT-01, SA-LEX-C17-TIME-04, SA-GRAMMAR-C275-FUTURE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "the boy went", with the past you know. (*bālaḥ agacchat*.) Then say "yesterday". (*hyaḥ*.) This lesson gives a second past, the one the stories in this book are told in.

## You'll want to know: गतवान्, गतवती
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-TAVAT-01]; assesses=[SA-REL-SAH-01, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-01, SA-LEX-C17-TIME-04, SA-LEX-C74-MARKET-04, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-02] -->

> **सः गतवान्।**
>
> *saḥ gatavān.* — "He went."

> **सा गतवती।**
>
> *sā gatavatī.* — "She went."

**गतवान्** (*gatavān*), "went", is a describing word made from the verb: *-vān* for a man, *-vatī* for a woman.

> **सः ह्यः गृहं गतवान्।**
>
> *saḥ hyaḥ gṛhaṃ gatavān.* — "He went home yesterday."

> **मीरा ह्यः आपणं गतवती।**
>
> *mīrā hyaḥ āpaṇaṃ gatavatī.* — "Mira went to the shop yesterday."

For several people, **गतवन्तः** (*gatavantaḥ*); for two, **गतवन्तौ** (*gatavantau*):

> **ते ह्यः ग्रामं गतवन्तः।**
>
> *te hyaḥ grāmaṃ gatavantaḥ.* — "They went to the village yesterday."

It needs no **अस्मि**: a man says *ahaṃ gatavān*, "I went", a woman *ahaṃ gatavatī*.

## You'll want to know: the stem under it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-TAVAT-02]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PATHATI-READ, SA-LEX-KHADATI-EAT, SA-LEX-LIKHATI-WRITE, SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE, SA-LEX-AGACCHATI-COME, SA-LEX-ANNAM-FOOD, SA-LEX-C35-HOUSE-04] -->

The part before *-vat-* is the verb's *-ta* form, which describes on its own: **गतः** (*gataḥ*), "gone". Each verb has its own, given with it in the dictionary:

| the verb | the past (a man) |
|---|---|
| "does" | **कृतवान्** (*kṛtavān*) |
| **पठति** | **पठितवान्** (*paṭhitavān*) |
| **खादति** | **खादितवान्** (*khāditavān*) |
| **लिखति** | **लिखितवान्** (*likhitavān*) |
| **पश्यति** | **दृष्टवान्** (*dṛṣṭavān*) |
| **आगच्छति** | **आगतवान्** (*āgatavān*) |

The thing done stays as it was:

> **सः अन्नं खादितवान्।**
>
> *saḥ annaṃ khāditavān.* — "He ate the food."

> **मीरा पुस्तकं पठितवती।**
>
> *mīrā pustakaṃ paṭhitavatī.* — "Mira read the book."

"Said" comes from the other "say" root, √vac: **उक्तवान्** (*uktavān*; Monier-Williams gives *ukta* there).

> **सा किम् उक्तवती?**
>
> *sā kim uktavatī?* — "What did she say?"

The past you know still stands: *agacchat* and *gatavān* both say "went", and both are correct. This book tells its stories with *gatavān*, one shape for every verb (Pāṇini 1.1.26 names *-ta* and *-tavat* together).
