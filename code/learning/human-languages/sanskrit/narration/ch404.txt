Sanskrit, chapter 404: Lonely Roads and Wild Places.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रान्तरम् (prāntaram) — a long lonely road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a traveller on foot, then the Sanskrit for roams.

You'll want to know: प्रान्तरम्.
प्रान्तरम् — prāntaram — "a long lonely road".
प्रान्तरे कः अपि न अस्ति। prāntare kaḥ api na asti. There is nobody at all on the lonely road.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रान्तरम् (prāntaram) is built from pra, "forth", and antara, "an interval, the space between"; MW gives prāntara as a long desolate road and as the country between two villages (MW).

कान्तारे सिंहाः वृकाः च वसन्ति। kāntāre siṃhāḥ vṛkāḥ ca vasanti. Lions and wolves live in the wilderness.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कान्तारः (kāntāraḥ) is the word MW gives for a large wood or wilderness and for a difficult road through a forest; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
देशाटनम् (deśāṭanam) — travel through the country.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a long lonely road, then the Sanskrit for a wilderness.

You'll want to know: देशाटनम्.
देशाटनम् — deśāṭanam — "travel through the country".
देशाटनेन ज्ञानं वर्धते। deśāṭanena jñānaṃ vardhate. Knowledge grows by travelling through the country.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
देशाटनम् (deśāṭanam) is built from deśa, "a country", and aṭana, "roaming", from the root √aṭ, "to roam", which also gives paryaṭaka, "a tourist"; MW gives the compound as roaming through a land, travelling (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
निलीयते (nilīyate) — takes cover; hides oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wilderness, then the Sanskrit for travel through the country.

You'll want to know: निलीयते.
निलीयते — nilīyate — "takes cover; hides oneself".
पथिकः सिंहात् गुहायां निलीयते। pathikaḥ siṃhāt guhāyāṃ nilīyate. The wayfarer takes cover from the lion in a cave.
Past (he …): सः गुहायां निलीनवान्, saḥ guhāyāṃ nilīnavān.

The word, taken apart.
निलीयते (nilīyate) is built from ni, "down", and the root √lī, "to cling, to settle"; MW gives ni-lī as to settle down or alight and to hide oneself from something (in the ablative), and KRM gives the participle līna (MW).
