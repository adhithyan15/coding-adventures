German, chapter 463: Eisige Kälte.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Raureif — the hoarfrost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hail, then the German for to thunder.

You'll want to know: der Raureif.
der Raureif — "the hoarfrost".
Am Morgen sind die Bäume weiß vom Raureif. In the morning the trees are white with hoarfrost.
No plural. Also simply der Reif.

The word, taken apart.
Raureif is built from rau 'rough' and Reif 'hoarfrost', which EtymDB traces through Middle High German rīfe to Old High German rīfo. This Reif is a different word from Reif 'ring', though EtymDB lists both under one entry.

Lesson 2 of 5.
gefrieren — to freeze (solid).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: gefrieren.
gefrieren — "to freeze (solid)".
Bei minus zehn Grad gefriert sogar der See. At minus ten degrees even the lake freezes.
Perfekt with sein: der See ist gefroren.

The word, taken apart.
gefrieren is built from ge- and the inherited verb for freezing; EtymDB traces frieren through Old High German friosan to Proto-Germanic freusaną and lists Dutch vriezen and English freeze as cousins.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tauen — to thaw, to melt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the hoarfrost, then the German for to freeze.

You'll want to know: tauen.
tauen — "to thaw, to melt".
Im März taut der Schnee auf den Dächern. In March the snow on the roofs thaws.
With es for the weather: es taut. Perfekt with sein for snow and ice: der Schnee ist getaut.

The word, taken apart.
tauen 'to thaw' is an inherited Germanic verb (EtymDB lists 'to thaw'), and English thaw is a cousin. It is a different word from Tau 'dew'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bibbern — to shiver, to tremble (colloquial).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to thaw, then the German for the thaw.

You'll want to know: bibbern.
bibbern — "to shiver, to tremble (colloquial)".
An der Haltestelle bibbern die Kinder vor Kälte. At the bus stop the children shiver with cold.
Everyday word, with vor: vor Kälte bibbern.

The word, taken apart.
bibbern is an expressive form usually linked to beben 'to tremble'; EtymDB does not list it, so the line is hedged.
