Latin, chapter 435: Vessel, Little boat, Skiff, Punt, Raft.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nāvigium, nāvigiī — a vessel, a craft (any ship or boat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to neigh, then the Latin for a muleteer.

You'll want to know: nāvigium, nāvigiī.
nāvigium, nāvigiī — "a vessel, a craft (any ship or boat)".
In portū multa nāvigia sunt, magna et parva. In the harbour there are many vessels, large and small.
Second declension, neuter: nāvigium, nāvigiī n. A general word for anything that sails.

The word, taken apart.
nāvigium is built on nāvigō 'to sail' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from nāvis 'ship', inherited from PIE néh₂us 'boat'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nāvicula, nāviculae — a little boat, a small ship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a muleteer, then the Latin for a vessel.

You'll want to know: nāvicula, nāviculae.
nāvicula, nāviculae — "a little boat, a small ship".
Piscātor nāviculā in flūmine piscēs capit. The fisherman catches fish from a little boat on the river.
First declension, feminine: nāvicula, nāviculae f., a diminutive of nāvis. English navicular 'boat-shaped' comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
nāvicula is a diminutive of nāvis 'ship' (Lewis & Short), made with the ending -cula. nāvis is inherited from PIE néh₂us 'boat' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
scapha, scaphae — a skiff, a ship's boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a vessel, then the Latin for a little boat.

You'll want to know: scapha, scaphae.
scapha, scaphae — "a skiff, a ship's boat".
Nautae ē nāve in scapham dēscendunt et ad lītus remīs eunt. The sailors climb down from the ship into the skiff and row to the shore.
First declension, feminine: scapha, scaphae f. The small boat a ship carried or towed, for going ashore.

The word, taken apart.
scapha was borrowed from Greek skaphē 'light boat, skiff' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short compare scabō 'to scratch, to scrape', as of a hollowed-out boat.

Lesson 4 of 5.
linter, lintris — a punt, a small flat boat; a trough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a little boat, then the Latin for a skiff.

You'll want to know: linter, lintris.
linter, lintris — "a punt, a small flat boat; a trough".
Agricolae lintribus flūmen trānseunt. The farmers cross the river in punts.
Third declension, feminine: linter, lintris f. A flat river boat; also a wooden tub or trough.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of linter is not settled: Lewis & Short connect it, doubtfully, with Greek plynō 'to wash', a washtub before a boat, and EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ratis, ratis — a raft; (in poetry) a boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a skiff, then the Latin for a punt.

You'll want to know: ratis, ratis.
ratis, ratis — "a raft; (in poetry) a boat".
Mīlitēs ratēs ex arboribus faciunt et flūmen trānseunt. The soldiers make rafts out of trees and cross the river.
Third declension, feminine: ratis, ratis f. Logs tied together; poets use it for any ship.

The word, taken apart.
ratis is inherited. Lewis & Short connect it with rēmus 'oar' and Greek eretēs 'rower', but EtymDB gives no parent, so the link is hedged.
