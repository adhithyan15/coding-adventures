Portuguese, chapter 480: Culpa e vergonha.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
arrepender — to regret (arrepender-se de).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to forgive, then the Portuguese for forgiveness.

You'll want to know: arrepender.
arrepender — "to regret (arrepender-se de)".
Arrependi-me de não ter estudado mais. I regretted not having studied more.
Reflexive in practice: arrepender-se de + noun or infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
arrepender is inherited, through Old Portuguese arrepentir (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin repoenitēre, built on Latin paenitēre "to cause regret".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o remorso — remorse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to regret, then the Portuguese for regret.

You'll want to know: o remorso.
o remorso — "remorse".
Depois da mentira, ficou com remorsos. After the lie, he felt remorse.
Often plural: ter remorsos.

The word, taken apart.
remorso is a learned borrowing from Late Latin remorsus "a biting back", from Latin remordēre "to bite again" (Lewis & Short): a conscience that bites.

You'll want to know: envergonhar.
envergonhar — "to embarrass, to shame".
As notas más envergonharam o rapaz. The bad marks made the boy ashamed.
Envergonhar-se — to be ashamed.

The word, taken apart.
envergonhar is built on vergonha "shame", which is inherited from Latin verēcundia "modesty, shame" (Lewis & Short).
