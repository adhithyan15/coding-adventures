# السلام عليكم (as-salāmu ʿalaykum) — "peace be upon you"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The greeting, assembled from two pieces you now have — and one of
the most-spoken sentences on Earth.

## You'll want to know first

- [سلام (salām)](./AR-C01-salam.md) · [ال (al-)](./AR-C01-al.md)

## Sounds you'll need

- `ayn` — the **ع** (ʿayn) in *ʿalaykum* is a sound English has no equivalent
  for: a tightening deep in the throat. Approximate it for now; a native ear
  forgives.

## The word, taken apart — by assembling it

**السلام عليكم** = **as-salāmu** ("the peace," *al-* + *salām*, with the
sun-letter *s* assimilating just like last lesson) + **ʿalaykum** ("upon
you," where *-kum* = "you all"). Literally: **"the peace [be] upon you."**

Notice there's no word for "is/be" — Arabic simply places "the peace" and
"upon you" side by side, and the "is" is understood. (Spanish would need
*está*; Arabic drops it entirely in the present.)

## Why it's said this way

This is the traditional, universal Muslim greeting, and its reply is fixed and
lovely: **وعليكم السلام** (*wa-ʿalaykum as-salām*) — "**and** upon you [be]
the peace" — you return the peace, with a *wa-* ("and") in front. You give
peace; the other gives it back. It works any time of day, formal or informal,
and it carries real weight — you are not saying "hi," you are wishing someone
*peace*. (The lighter, secular *marḥaban* is always available too.)
