Portuguese, chapter 317: Uma nova etapa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aposentar — to retire (usually aposentar-se).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the will, then the Portuguese for the pension.

You'll want to know: aposentar.
aposentar — "to retire (usually aposentar-se)".
A professora aposentou-se aos sessenta e seis anos. The teacher retired at sixty-six.
Mostly aposentar-se, the usual word in Brazil; in Portugal reformar-se is more common, though civil servants such as teachers aposentam-se.

The word, taken apart.
aposentar comes down from Old Portuguese apousentar "to lodge, to give rest" (EtymDB), built on pousar "to rest", from Latin pausāre "to stop" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o pensionista — the pensioner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pension, then the Portuguese for to retire.

You'll want to know: o pensionista.
o pensionista — "the pensioner".
O meu vizinho é pensionista e passa o dia no jardim. My neighbour is a pensioner and spends the day in the garden.
Built on a pensão. The same form for a woman: a pensionista.

The word, taken apart.
pensionista is built on pensão "a pension" with -ista for the person; pensão comes from Latin pēnsiō "a payment" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
emigrar — to emigrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to retire, then the Portuguese for the pensioner.

You'll want to know: emigrar.
emigrar — "to emigrate".
Nos anos sessenta, o meu avô emigrou para o estrangeiro. In the sixties my grandfather emigrated abroad.
Emigrar is leaving; imigrar is arriving.

The word, taken apart.
emigrar is a learned borrowing from Latin ēmigrāre "to move out", built on migrāre "to move from place to place" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a emigração — emigration.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pensioner, then the Portuguese for to emigrate.

You'll want to know: a emigração.
a emigração — "emigration".
Nos anos sessenta, a emigração esvaziou muitas aldeias. In the sixties emigration emptied many villages.
Leaving the country; the opposite movement is immigration.

The word, taken apart.
emigração is a learned word from Late Latin ēmigrātiō "a moving out", built on migrāre "to move from place to place" (Lewis & Short).
