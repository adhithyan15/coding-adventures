Latin, chapter 352: Pānis domesticus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mola, molae — a millstone; a mill; salted meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a pestle, then the Latin for to grind.

You'll want to know: mola, molae.
mola, molae — "a millstone; a mill; salted meal".
Asinus molam in pistrīnō vertēbat. A donkey was turning the millstone in the bakery.
Feminine; the plural molae is a mill. Mola salsa, salted meal, was sprinkled on victims. English molar, the grinding tooth.

The word, taken apart.
mola is inherited from the Proto-Indo-European root melh₂-, "to grind" (EtymDB). English molar comes from it, and English meal, "flour", is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
crībrum, crībrī — a sieve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to grind, then the Latin for a millstone.

You'll want to know: crībrum, crībrī.
crībrum, crībrī — "a sieve".
Pistor farīnam crībrō pūrgābat. The baker was cleaning the flour with a sieve.
Neuter. Pouring water into a sieve was a Roman picture of a hopeless task.

The word, taken apart.
crībrum is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to sift" (EtymDB), also seen, says Lewis & Short, in cernō, "to sift, to separate".

Lesson 3 of 5.
subigō, subigere — to knead; to subdue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a millstone, then the Latin for a sieve.

You'll want to know: subigō, subigere.
subigō, subigere — "to knead; to subdue".
Pistor farīnam et aquam subēgit. The baker kneaded the flour with water.
subigō, subigere, subēgī, subāctum: sub + agō. Also to bring under one's power.

The word, taken apart.
subigō is built on agō, "to drive", with sub-, "under" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): to work something under, to knead, and to subdue.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fermentum, fermentī — leaven, yeast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sieve, then the Latin for to knead.

You'll want to know: fermentum, fermentī.
fermentum, fermentī — "leaven, yeast".
Sine fermentō pānis dūrus fit. Without leaven bread turns out hard.
Neuter, from a verb meaning 'to boil, to bubble'. English ferment.

The word, taken apart.
fermentum is built on ferveō, "to boil, to bubble" (EtymDB): what makes dough bubble. English ferment comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
clībanus, clībanī — a baking pot, a small oven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to knead, then the Latin for leaven.

You'll want to know: clībanus, clībanī.
clībanus, clībanī — "a baking pot, a small oven".
Pānem in clībanō coxit. He baked the bread in a baking pot.
Masculine, a Greek loan: a clay or iron pot heated with coals, wider at the bottom.

The word, taken apart.
clībanus was borrowed from Greek klibanos, "a baker's oven, a covered pan" (EtymDB).
