## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C325-PLACE-10]; assesses=[] -->

**el lugar** — "the place," a spot rather than a town.

Latin **locus** was a place. Spanish took it twice and let the two copies drift
apart until they stopped sounding related.

One copy stayed about space. *Locālis*, *belonging to a place*, wore through Old
Spanish *logar* into **lugar** — a spot, a position, somewhere something goes.
The other copy turned into time. *In locō*, *in that place*, became *luego*, and
what began as *there* slid into *then* — which is why you have been saying
**hasta luego** since almost the beginning without meeting the place inside it.

You have also been carrying the noun whole. **En primer lugar** — *in the first
place* — is this word, holding the same job it holds in English.

English kept *locus* untranslated for a point in space, and built **local**,
**locate**, **location**, **allocate** and **dislocate** on it. It also has the
French version: *lieu* is *locus* worn down, so a **lieutenant** is a
*place-holder*, the officer who stands in somebody else's spot — and *in lieu
of* is *in the place of*, still doing its Latin job in an English sentence.
