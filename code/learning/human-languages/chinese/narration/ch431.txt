解说 (jiěshuō) — to give a running commentary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take a selfie, then the Mandarin for the peak season.

You'll want to know: 解说.
解说 — jiěshuō — "to give a running commentary".
dǎoyóu zhèngzài gěi wǒmen jiěshuō — The guide is giving us a commentary.

The word, taken apart.
解说 (jiěshuō) is built from 解 'to explain' and 说 'to speak', both inherited from Middle Chinese, keaX and sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to explain' and the second 'to tell'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
春游 (chūnyóu) — spring outing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the peak season, then the Mandarin for to give a running commentary.

You'll want to know: 春游.
春游 — chūnyóu — "spring outing".
我们坐火车去春游 wǒmen zuò huǒchē qù chūnyóu — We're going on a spring outing by train.
The new character, in pieces: 游 (yóu) — twelve strokes in three pieces: on the left the water piece of 洗, three strokes; in the middle a dot, a bar, a stroke that turns down and hooks, and a stroke falling to the left; on the right a short stroke falling to the left and a bar, with 子 under them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
春游 (chūnyóu) is built from 春 'spring' and 游 (yóu) 'to roam', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwin and yuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to float' and 'to let loose'; the sense 'to tour' is usually explained as a widening of it.

Script — the water piece, a middle piece, then 子.
游 (yóu).
Three pieces, left to right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the water piece, three strokes, as in 洗: two dots, then a stroke that rises to the right.
The middle piece, four strokes. A dot on top. A bar under it. Under the bar, a stroke that runs a little to the right, turns down and hooks to the left at the foot. Then a stroke falling to the left, from under the bar.
The right piece, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left and a bar, like a small roof. Under them 子 zǐ: a bar that turns down to the left, a vertical with a hook, and a bar across.
3 + 4 + 5 = twelve.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 游 (yóu) have? (Three: three strokes, four and five.) Which written character sits at the bottom right? (子, three strokes.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
多 (duō) — many; much; a lot of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a spring outing, then the Mandarin for a pleasure boat.

You'll want to know: 多.
多 — duō — "many; much; a lot of".
春天这儿的游客很多 chūntiān zhèr de yóukè hěn duō — In spring there are a lot of visitors here.
Before a noun it is usually 很多 hěn duō: 很多人 'a lot of people'.

The word, taken apart.
多 (duō) is inherited from Middle Chinese ta (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'many'.

héyǐng is built from hé 'together' and yǐng 'a likeness', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hop and 'jaengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to join' and the second as a shape and its shadow; the sense 'a photograph' is modern.
