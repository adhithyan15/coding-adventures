## You'll want to know: むだ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C338-MUDA338-01]; assesses=[] -->

**むだ** — *muda* — "useless; a waste".

> **ひるに でんきを つかったら、むだです。** — *hiru ni denki o tsukattara, muda desu* — "Using the lights in the daytime is a waste."

A na-adjective: な before a noun.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**むだ** (*muda*) is written as Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, *mu*, "nothing" (Middle Chinese *mju*) and *da*, "burdensome". JMdict marks that spelling as ateji, characters chosen for their sound, so whether the word itself came from Chinese is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
