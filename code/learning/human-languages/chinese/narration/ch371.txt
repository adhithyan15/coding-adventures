Lesson 1 of 6.
shènzhì — even (going so far as).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the effect something has, then the Mandarin for in short.

You'll want to know: shènzhì.
shènzhì — "even (going so far as)".
tā hěn máng shènzhì méiyǒu shíjiān chī fàn — He's so busy he hasn't even got time to eat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shènzhì is built from shèn and zhì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyimX and tsyijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'excessive' and the second 'to arrive'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
虽然 (suīrán) — although.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for in short, then the Mandarin for even (going so far as).

You'll want to know: 虽然.
虽然 — suīrán — "although".
虽然下雨了但是我们还是去了 suīrán xiàyǔ le dànshì wǒmen háishi qù le — Although it rained, we still went.
The new character, in pieces: 虽 (suī) — nine strokes in two pieces: 口 kǒu on top, and under it an insect piece, six strokes: a small box with a vertical through it, a stroke rising along the foot, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
虽然 (suīrán) is built from suī and 然, both inherited from Middle Chinese, swij and nyen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a particle and the second as a particle and 'like this'.

What you've built.

虽 (suī) — a mouth over an insect.

Script — a mouth over an insect.
虽 (suī).
Two pieces, top before bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, 口, three strokes, small: a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, the bottom horizontal.
The bottom piece, the insect piece, six strokes: a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, a horizontal closing the small box, a vertical down through it, a stroke rising to the right along the foot, then a dot on the right.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 虽 (suī), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 虽 (suī)? (Two.) What is on top? (口.) What is the last stroke? (A dot on the right.)

yuánzé is built from yuán and zé, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwon and tsok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a broad plain' and the second 'a rule'. How the first came to mean a source is not settled.
