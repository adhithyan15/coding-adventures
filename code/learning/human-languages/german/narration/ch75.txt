Lesson 1 of 5.
die Sahne — cream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a salad; lettuce, then the German for noodles.

You'll want to know: die Sahne.
die Sahne — "cream".

The word, taken apart.
Sahne was borrowed into standard German from Low German and Dutch. Its origin is uncertain; EtymDB 2.1 has the word but no source for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Pfeffer — pepper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for noodles, then the German for cream.

You'll want to know: der Pfeffer.
der Pfeffer — "pepper".

The word, taken apart.
Pfeffer from Old High German pheffar, from Latin piper from Greek péperi (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed so early that its p shifted to pf, as in Apfel.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Nuss — a nut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for cream, then the German for pepper.

You'll want to know: die Nuss.
die Nuss — "a nut".

The word, taken apart.
Nuss from Old High German nuz from Proto-Germanic hnuts — inherited, the same word as English nut, with German ss where English kept t. (EtymDB 2.1).
