Mandarin Chinese, chapter 126: Often and Sometimes.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
chángcháng — often.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for anxious, then the Mandarin for upset.

You'll want to know: chángcháng.
chángcháng — "often".
wǒ chángcháng hē chá — I often drink tea.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chángcháng says cháng twice. It is inherited from Middle Chinese dzyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it as a length of two fathoms and as an official title. The sense 'usual' is not given there, so how it came to mean often is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
以前 (yǐqián) — before; in the past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for often, then the Mandarin for sometimes.

You'll want to know: 以前.
以前 — yǐqián — "before; in the past".
以前我不会说汉语 yǐqián wǒ bú huì shuō hànyǔ — I couldn't speak Chinese before.
The new character, in pieces: 以 (yǐ) — four strokes: a vertical that flicks up at its foot, a dot beside it, then a long stroke falling to the left and a dot at the lower right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
以前 (yǐqián) is built from 以 (yǐ), glossed 'to use' and 'with', and 前 'before', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yiX and dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Script — four strokes in two pairs.
以 (yǐ).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, two strokes. A vertical that runs down and flicks up to the right at its foot, then a short dot to the right of it, falling to the lower right.
The right piece, two strokes. A long left-falling stroke from the top right, sweeping down toward the foot of the first stroke, then a dot at the lower right, falling to the lower right.
2 + 2 = four.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 以 (yǐ)? (Four.) Which stroke flicks up? (The first, at its foot.) Which way does the long one fall? (Down to the left.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
gāngcái — a moment ago.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sometimes, then the Mandarin for in the past.

You'll want to know: gāngcái.
gāngcái — "a moment ago".
gāngcái wǒ zài xǐshǒujiān — A moment ago I was in the toilet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gāngcái is built from gāng and cái, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kang and dzoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'strong', and lists the second, under an older way of writing it, as 'barely', a different word from the cái of talent; how the pair came to mean a moment ago is not settled.
