Hindi, chapter 284: Feelings and Hopes.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ख़ुश (khuś) — happy, glad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the line from your first meeting. (आपसे मिलकर ख़ुशी हुई.) Its ख़ुशी is "happiness". Then "sad". (उदास, udās.)

You'll want to know: ख़ुश.
ख़ुश — khuś — "happy, glad".
आज मैं बहुत ख़ुश हूँ। āj maiṁ bahut khuś hūṁ. — "I am very happy today."
ख़ुश (khuś) never changes: वह ख़ुश (khuś) है for him and for her, हम ख़ुश (khuś) हैं for us. It is the word ख़ुशी is built on: ख़ुश (khuś), "happy", and ख़ुशी (khuśī), "happiness".
With होना in the past, it says the gladness came: वह बहुत ख़ुश हुई (vah bahut khuś huī), "she was very pleased". The one you are pleased with takes से: मैं तुमसे ख़ुश हूँ (maiṁ tumse khuś hūṁ), "I am pleased with you".

The word, taken apart.
ख़ुश (khuś) is usually traced to Persian khush, "happy, pleasant". ख़ुशी adds -ई, the ending of many Hindi nouns of feeling, and ख़ुशबू, "fragrance", opens with the same word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: khuś, then khuśī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I am very happy today" — āj maiṁ bahut khuś hūṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She was very pleased" — vah bahut khuś huī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ख़ुश (khuś) mean? ("Happy, glad".) Does it change for a woman? (No: वह ख़ुश (khuś) है.) "Happiness"? (ख़ुशी.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe ḍar lagtā hai) — saying how you feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'm hungry" and "I am happy". (mujhe bhūkh lagī hai, maiṁ khuś hūṁ.) Two ways of feeling, and this lesson sets them side by side.

You'll want to know: a feeling that comes to you.
मुझे कुत्तों से डर लगता है। mujhe kuttoṁ se ḍar lagtā hai. — "I'm afraid of dogs."
Some feelings come to you, and Hindi says so. The one who feels takes मुझे, the feeling is the subject of लगना, and what you fear takes से.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
the feeling: डर, masculine. the sentence: मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe ḍar lagtā hai) — I'm afraid.
the feeling: ठंड, feminine. the sentence: मुझे ठंड लग रही है (mujhe ṭhaṇḍ lag rahī hai) — I'm cold.
the feeling: how it seemed. the sentence: मुझे अच्छा लगा (mujhe acchā lagā) — I liked it; it felt good.
Anger comes with आना, "to come": मुझे गुस्सा आया (mujhe gussā āyā), "I got angry".

You'll want to know: a feeling you are in.
मीरा आज उदास है। Mīrā āj udās hai. — "Mira is sad today."
Other feelings are a state you are in, said with होना: मैं ख़ुश (khuś) हूँ; वह परेशान है (vah pareśān hai), "he is worried". ख़ुश (khuś), उदास and परेशान never change; अकेला agrees: वह अकेली है (vah akelī hai), "she is alone".
With हो गया, the feeling sets in: वह उदास हो गई (vah udās ho gaī), "she became sad". To ask: क्या हुआ? तुम परेशान क्यों हो? (kyā huā? tum pareśān kyoṁ ho?), "What happened? Why are you worried?"

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I'm cold" — mujhe ṭhaṇḍ lag rahī hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I liked the food" — mujhe khānā acchā lagā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Why are you worried?" — tum pareśān kyoṁ ho?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you are afraid of dogs, then that Mira is happy today.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I'm afraid"? (मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe ḍar lagtā hai).) "I got angry"? (मुझे गुस्सा आया.) "She is sad"? (वह उदास है.) "She became sad"? (वह उदास हो गई.) Which feelings take मुझे? (The ones that come to you: डर, ठंड, गुस्सा.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
मैं अध्यापक बनना चाहता हूँ (maiṁ adhyāpak bannā cāhtā hūṁ) — what I want to become.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want to go". (मैं जाना चाहता हूँ.) Then बनना, "to become", and सपना, "a dream".

You'll want to know: wanting to become.
मैं अध्यापक बनना चाहता हूँ। maiṁ adhyāpak bannā cāhtā hūṁ. — "I want to become a teacher."
चाहना takes a verb in -ना, as in मैं जाना चाहता हूँ. With बनना, "to become", it says what you want to be. A woman says चाहती हूँ: मैं डाक्टर बनना चाहती हूँ (maiṁ ḍākṭar bannā cāhtī hūṁ).
"When I grow up" is बड़ा होकर (baṛā hokar), the -कर of होना: बड़ा होकर मैं अध्यापक बनूँगा (baṛā hokar maiṁ adhyāpak banūṅgā), "when I grow up I will be a teacher". A girl says बड़ी होकर … बनूँगी.
To ask: तुम क्या बनना चाहते हो? (tum kyā bannā cāhte ho?), "What do you want to be?"

You'll want to know: hopes and dreams.
मेरा सपना है कि एक दिन मेरा अपना घर होगा। merā sapnā hai ki ek din merā apnā ghar hogā. — "My dream is that one day I will have a house of my own."
सपना is masculine: मेरा सपना. After कि, the dream is told in the future, with होगा. A dream can also be a verb in -ना: मेरा सपना अध्यापक बनना है (merā sapnā adhyāpak bannā hai), "my dream is to become a teacher".
Hope works the same way: मुझे उम्मीद है कि … (mujhe ummīd hai ki …), "I hope that …", with the future after it: मुझे उम्मीद है कि तुम आओगे (mujhe ummīd hai ki tum āoge), "I hope you will come".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "My dream is to become a doctor" — merā sapnā ḍākṭar bannā hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I hope it won't rain tomorrow" — mujhe ummīd hai ki kal bāriś nahīṁ hogī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you want to become a teacher, then that you hope Mira will come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I want to become a teacher"? (मैं अध्यापक बनना चाहता हूँ (maiṁ adhyāpak bannā cāhtā hūṁ), or चाहती हूँ.) "When I grow up"? (बड़ा होकर.) "I hope that you will come"? (मुझे उम्मीद है कि तुम आओगे.) Why मेरा सपना? (सपना is masculine.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — feelings and hopes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I am happy" and "I'm afraid". (maiṁ khuś hūṁ, mujhe ḍar lagtā hai.)

[pause 3 seconds]
"I liked it"? (मुझे अच्छा लगा.) "I got angry"? (मुझे गुस्सा आया.) "She is happy"? (वह ख़ुश (khuś) है: ख़ुश (khuś) does not change.) "I want to become …"? (मैं … बनना चाहता हूँ.) "I hope that …"? (मुझे उम्मीद है कि …, then the future.)
