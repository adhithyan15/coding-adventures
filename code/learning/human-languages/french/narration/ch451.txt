French, chapter 451: À la banque.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le retrait — the withdrawal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the card machine, then the French for cash.

You'll want to know: le retrait.
le retrait — "the withdrawal".
Il a fait un retrait de cinquante euros. He took out fifty euros.
Un retrait d'argent — a cash withdrawal.

The word, taken apart.
retrait is the old past participle of retraire 'to draw back', from Latin retrahere, built on trahere 'to draw' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
créditer — to credit, to pay into.

Before the new one: say the French for cash, then the French for the withdrawal.

You'll want to know: créditer.
créditer — "to credit, to pay into".
La banque a crédité mon compte ce matin. The bank paid the money into my account this morning.
The opposite of débiter.

The word, taken apart.
créditer is built on crédit, from Latin crēditum 'a loan', from crēdere 'to trust' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
régler — to settle (a bill), to pay; to adjust.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for the withdrawal, then the French for to credit.

You'll want to know: régler.
régler — "to settle (a bill), to pay; to adjust".
Nous avons réglé la note de l'hôtel par carte. We paid the hotel bill by card.
Régler un problème — to sort out a problem; régler la radio — to tune the radio.

The word, taken apart.
régler is built on règle 'rule, ruler', which goes back to Latin rēgula (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le découvert — the overdraft.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to credit, then the French for to settle.

You'll want to know: le découvert.
le découvert — "the overdraft".
À la fin du mois, il est souvent à découvert. At the end of the month he is often overdrawn.
Être à découvert — to be overdrawn.

The word, taken apart.
découvert is the past participle of découvrir 'to uncover' (EtymDB), from Late Latin discooperīre: an account left uncovered by money.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le relevé — the statement (from a bank); the reading.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to settle, then the French for the overdraft.

You'll want to know: le relevé.
le relevé — "the statement (from a bank); the reading".
Elle vérifie son relevé de compte chaque mois. She checks her bank statement every month.
Un relevé de compte; le relevé du compteur — the meter reading.

The word, taken apart.
relevé is the past participle of relever 'to lift; to note down' (EtymDB lists it), from Latin relevāre, built on levāre 'to raise'.
