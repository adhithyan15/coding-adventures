Hindi, chapter 320: Kings and Emperors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सम्राट (samrāṭ) — an emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a slap, then the Hindi for to twist.

You'll want to know: सम्राट.
सम्राट — samrāṭ — "an emperor".
सम्राट के पास बहुत बड़ी सेना थी। samrāṭ ke pās bahut baṛī senā thī. The emperor had a very large army.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सम्राट (samrāṭ) is Sanskrit samrāj, "a universal ruler" (Monier-Williams), whose nominative is samrāṭ: a king above kings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to twist, then the Hindi for an emperor.

You'll want to know: बादशाह.
बादशाह — bādśāh — "a sovereign; a king of kings".
बादशाह ने क़िले में एक बड़ी दावत दी। bādśāh ne qile meṁ ek baṛī dāvat dī. The emperor gave a great feast in the fort.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
बादशाह (bādśāh) is usually traced to Persian pādšāh, "a king", the title of the Mughal rulers. In stories it is the king of a Persian-style court.

Lesson 5 of 5.
दरबार (darbār) — a royal court.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

दरबार में कवि और गायक भी थे। darbār meṁ kavi aur gāyak bhī the. There were poets and singers at the royal court too.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
दरबार (darbār) is usually traced to Persian darbār, "a royal court", whose first part is dar, a door, as in दरवाज़ा: those who stand at the king's door.
