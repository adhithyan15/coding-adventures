Malayalam, chapter 496: Rivers and Springs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
നദി (nadi) — a large river.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a meadow, then the Malayalam for shade.

You'll want to know: നദി.
നദി — nadi — "a large river".
ആ നദി കടലിൽ ചേരുന്നു. ā nadi kaṭalil cērunnu. That river flows into the sea.
Plural: നദികൾ, nadikaḷ.
A formal word, used for the big rivers; in speech any river is a പുഴ.

The word, taken apart.
നദി (nadi) is borrowed from Sanskrit nadī, "a river" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
അരുവി (aruvi) — a brook; a stream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for shade, then the Malayalam for a large river.

You'll want to know: അരുവി.
അരുവി — aruvi — "a brook; a stream".
കാട്ടിലൂടെ ഒരു ചെറിയ അരുവി ഒഴുകുന്നു. kāṭṭilūṭe oru ceṟiya aruvi oḻukunnu. A little stream flows through the forest.
Smaller than a പുഴ: water that runs down from the hills.

The word, taken apart.
അരുവി (aruvi) is native Dravidian, from a root that means to spring out and flow down gently (DEDR 226).

മലയുടെ ചുവട്ടിൽ ഒരു തണുത്ത ഉറവ ഉണ്ട്. malayuṭe cuvaṭṭil oru taṇutta uṟava uṇṭŭ. There is a cold spring at the foot of the hill.
Where water comes up out of the ground; a well fills from its ഉറവ (uṟava).

The word, taken apart.
ഉറവ (uṟava) is native Dravidian, from a root that means to ooze or spring up (DEDR 761).

Lesson 4 of 5.
അണക്കെട്ട് (aṇakkeṭṭŭ) — a dam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a brook, then the Malayalam for a water spring.

You'll want to know: അണക്കെട്ട്.
അണക്കെട്ട് — aṇakkeṭṭŭ — "a dam".
മഴക്കാലത്ത് അണക്കെട്ടിൽ ധാരാളം വെള്ളം ഉണ്ടാകും. maḻakkālattŭ aṇakkeṭṭil dhārāḷaṁ veḷḷaṁ uṇṭākuṁ. In the rainy season the dam holds a lot of water.
അണ, a dam or bank, plus കെട്ട്, something built or tied. The shutters are opened when it is full.

The word, taken apart.
അണക്കെട്ട് (aṇakkeṭṭŭ) is a native Dravidian compound of അണ, a dam or embankment (DEDR 122), and കെട്ട്, a thing built or tied (DEDR 1147).

Lesson 5 of 5.
വറ്റുക (vaṟṟuka) — to dry up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a water spring, then the Malayalam for a dam.

You'll want to know: വറ്റുക.
വറ്റുക — vaṟṟuka — "to dry up".
വേനലിൽ ഗ്രാമത്തിലെ കിണറുകൾ വറ്റി. vēnalil grāmattile kiṇaṟukaḷ vaṟṟi. In the dry season the village wells dried up.
Past: വറ്റി, vaṟṟi.
Water dries up: a well, a river, a pond. Clothes and grain ഉണങ്ങുക instead.

The word, taken apart.
വറ്റുക (vaṟṟuka) is native Dravidian, "to dry up, to grow lean" (DEDR 5320).
