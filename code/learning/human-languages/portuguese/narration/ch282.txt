Portuguese, chapter 282: O que foi dito.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a declaração — the declaration, the public statement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the written report, then the Portuguese for to announce in the news.

You'll want to know: a declaração.
a declaração — "the declaration, the public statement".
O presidente fez uma declaração aos jornalistas. The president made a statement to the journalists.
Fazer uma declaração — to make a statement. Also a form you fill in.

The word, taken apart.
declaração is a learned borrowing from Latin dēclārātiō "a making clear" (EtymDB), from dēclārāre, built on clārus "clear".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o comunicado — the press release, the official notice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: o comunicado.
o comunicado — "the press release, the official notice".
O clube divulgou um comunicado no site. The club put out a press release on its website.
A short official text sent to the media.

The word, taken apart.
comunicado is the past participle of comunicar used as a noun; the verb comes from Latin commūnicāre "to share", built on commūnis "common" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o comentador — the commentator, the pundit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to confirm, then the Portuguese for the press release.

You'll want to know: o comentador.
o comentador — "the commentator, the pundit".
Na televisão, um comentador explicou os resultados. On television a pundit explained the results.
Portugal’s TV is full of comentadores of politics and football. A woman is a comentadora.

The word, taken apart.
comentador is built on comentar, a learned borrowing from Latin commentārī "to think over, to study"; Latin itself had commentātor "a deviser, an interpreter" (Lewis & Short).
