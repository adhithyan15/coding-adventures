Malayalam, chapter 106: Before and After.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
മുൻപ് (munpŭ) — before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say at two o'clock. Then say the word you have for then — the one that puts one turn after another.

You'll want to know.
മുൻപ് (munpŭ) — before.
It goes after. English puts before in front of what it is before; Malayalam puts it behind:
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ് — raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ — "before two o'clock."
Read that right to left and it comes out as English: before — two o'clock.

Why it's said this way.
Look at what it is standing on. മണിക്ക് — the hour with the dative on it, exactly as you built it. മുൻപ് (munpŭ) does not attach to the word and does not change it. It is a separate word that leans on an ending you already make.
The word itself is built on mun, the old Dravidian word for the front. What is in front of you in space became what is in front of you in time.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 2 of 4.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — after.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say before two o'clock. Then say the word for the meal.

You'll want to know.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — after.
It stands where മുൻപ് (munpŭ) stands, on the same kind of ending:
ഊണിന് ശേഷം — ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ — "after the meal."

Grammar Lens: the same case, wearing its other shape.
ഊണിന് is the dative again — and it is the other of the two shapes you were given. The hour took -ക്ക്; ഊണ് takes -ിന്, because the ending is chosen by the sound the word finishes on.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ്, raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ, before two o'clock.
ഊണിന് ശേഷം, ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ, after the meal.
Same case, two shapes, and both of these words lean on it. You are not learning a new piece of machinery — you are hanging two more words on a piece you have had since your first case ending.

Why it's said this way.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) is Sanskrit, and its plain meaning is the remainder — what is left over. After the meal is, in the word's own terms, what is left of the day once the meal is done.

Lesson 3 of 4.
ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, മുൻപ് (munpŭ), ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — two kinds of ordering.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say at first. Say then. You have had both for a long while.

You'll want to know.
You now have four words about order, and they split into two pairs that do different jobs.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, ādyaṁ, pinne, stand alone and order the telling.
മുൻപ്, ശേഷം, munpŭ, śēṣaṁ, lean on a word and order events.
ആദ്യം and പിന്നെ need nothing in front of them. They say first this, then that about the order you are saying things in.
മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) cannot stand alone. Each one needs a thing to be before or after, and that thing arrives wearing the dative.

What you've built.
Until this chapter you could order your own sentences and not the world. First … then … arranges what you say. Before two o'clock and after the meal arrange what happens, against something you name.
That is the whole of what the two new words add — and neither of them needed a new piece of grammar to do it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which two of the four stand alone? (ആദ്യം and പിന്നെ.) Which two need something to lean on? (മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ).) What does the thing they lean on wear? (The dative.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
(before, after) (munpuṁ śēṣavuṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say before and after.

Grammar Lens: both of them lean, and neither of them attaches.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ്, raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ, before two o'clock.
ഊണിന് ശേഷം, ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ, after the meal.
Neither word changed the word in front of it. The hour is the hour you already built; the meal is the meal with the dative's other shape on it. Both new words are separate, and both stand behind what they are about — which is the opposite of where English puts them.

What you've built.
The ending underneath both of them is your first case ending. It was glossed to and for; you put it on an hour to mean at; and now two more words lean on it. One ending, four jobs, and no new machinery in this chapter at all.
And you can now order two different things: ആദ്യം and പിന്നെ order the telling, മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) order the events.
