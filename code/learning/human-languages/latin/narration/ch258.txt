Latin, chapter 258: To dream, To push, To go past, To make no sound, To dread.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
somniō, somniāre — to dream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to watch, then the Latin for to stroll.

You'll want to know: somniō, somniāre.
somniō, somniāre — "to dream".

The word, taken apart.
somniō is the verb made from somnium, "a dream" (Lewis & Short), which is built on somnus, "sleep".

Lesson 2 of 5.
pellō, pellere — to push, to drive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to stroll, then the Latin for to dream.

You'll want to know: pellō, pellere.
pellō, pellere — "to push, to drive".

The word, taken apart.
pellō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pel-, "to drive, to thrust". Greek pallō, "to brandish, to shake", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). English repel and pulse come from its family.
