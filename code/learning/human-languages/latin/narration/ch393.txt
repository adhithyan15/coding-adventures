Latin, chapter 393: Making Up My Mind.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
statuō, statuere — to set up; to decide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for convenient, then the Latin for to take precautions.

You'll want to know: statuō, statuere.
statuō, statuere — "to set up; to decide".
Statuī domī manēre. I decided to stay at home.
statuō, statuere, statuī, statūtum. With an infinitive, to decide to; with the accusative and infinitive, to judge that. English statute.

The word, taken apart.
statuō is built on status "a standing, a position" (Lewis & Short), from the root of stō "to stand": to make a thing stand. English statute comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dēstinō, dēstināre — to fix, to intend; to appoint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to take precautions, then the Latin for to set up.

You'll want to know: dēstinō, dēstināre.
dēstinō, dēstināre — "to fix, to intend; to appoint".
Hunc diem nūptiīs dēstināvērunt. They fixed this day for the wedding.
dēstinō, dēstināre, dēstināvī, dēstinātum: to make fast, then to set aside for a purpose. English destine and destination.

The word, taken apart.
dēstinō is built from dē and an old verb meaning "to set, to make stand" (EtymDB), from the root of stō "to stand". English destine and destination come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prōpositum, prōpositī — a plan, a purpose, an aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to set up, then the Latin for to fix.

You'll want to know: prōpositum, prōpositī.
prōpositum, prōpositī — "a plan, a purpose, an aim".
Prōpositum meum est librum scrībere. My aim is to write a book.
prōpositum, prōpositī n., the participle of prōpōnō, to set before: what you have set before yourself. English purpose goes back to it through French.

The word, taken apart.
prōpositum is the participle of prōpōnō "to set before" (Lewis & Short), from prō "before" and pōnō "to put". English purpose goes back to the same verb through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.

inceptum, inceptī — "an undertaking, a venture".

inceptum, inceptī n., the participle of incipiō, to begin: a thing begun.

The word, taken apart.
inceptum is the participle of incipiō "to begin" (Lewis & Short), from in and capiō "to take": a thing taken in hand. English inception comes from its family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cōnor, cōnārī — to try, to attempt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plan, then the Latin for an undertaking.

You'll want to know: cōnor, cōnārī.
cōnor, cōnārī — "to try, to attempt".
Cōnābar dormīre, sed nōn poteram. I was trying to sleep, but I could not.
Deponent: cōnor, cōnārī, cōnātus sum + infinitive. Cōnātus, an attempt.

The word, taken apart.
cōnor is inherited from Proto-Italic kōnāō "to try, to exert oneself" (EtymDB); its deeper origin is not settled.
