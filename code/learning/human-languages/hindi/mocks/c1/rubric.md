# Hindi C1 mock rubric

Score either C1 mock against [`task-shapes/c1.json`](../../task-shapes/c1.json).
Reading, listening, writing, and speaking are independent 100-point papers.
Each must reach 60/100; no compensation across skills is permitted.

## Reading — 100 points

Dense public, professional, academic, and literary texts are worth 25 points
each. Divide each part equally among its nine keyed items and retain one decimal
place. Items may require argument and discourse structure, rhetorical purpose,
evidence qualification, implications, register effects, figurative meaning,
cultural inference, and precise textual support. Credit the best supported
answer, including when a distractor is generally plausible but not justified by
the source.

## Listening — 100 points

Unscripted discussion, lecture, interview, and narrative parts are worth 25
points each. Each recording plays once at 160–185 words per minute after an
unscored orienting preview. The seven items per part are equally weighted.
Natural overlap, self-correction, regional features, and implicit alignment may
appear, but acoustic difficulty must not replace language comprehension. No
transcript, Romanization, dictionary, translator, or learner-controlled replay
is allowed.

## Writing — 100 points

The 180–220 word source synthesis is worth 40 points: source accuracy 10,
selection/integration 8, organisation/cohesion 7, audience/register 5,
vocabulary/grammar 7, orthographic control 3. It must distinguish source claims
from the writer's additions. The 300–380 word extended response is worth 60
points: intellectual development 17, organisation/cohesion 12,
genre/audience/register 9, vocabulary/grammar 16, orthographic control 6. A
response outside the range loses only credit for evidence it fails to provide.
Romanized, translated, copied, or memorised model text earns no independent
writing credit.

## Speaking — 100 points

The five-minute source-based presentation is worth 35 points: source synthesis
9, argument 9, organisation 6, vocabulary/grammar 7, pronunciation/fluency 4.
Collaborative synthesis is worth 30: source integration 7,
interaction/repair 7, negotiation/conclusion 7, vocabulary/grammar 6,
pronunciation/fluency 3. Challenge and defence is worth 35: reasoned defence 9,
qualification/reformulation 7, interaction/repair 6, register 4,
vocabulary/grammar 6, pronunciation/fluency 3. Score only the learner's own
contribution. Paper keywords made during 15 minutes of preparation are allowed;
a written answer script is not.

## Readiness boundary

These are project-defined C1 targets, not an official CEFR qualification.
Readiness claims require timed administrations, calibrated double-scoring of
productive work, qualified human review of Hindi language and cultural content,
and recording review. Until those checks exist, report an executable target and
observed paper scores, not a proven C1 pass.
