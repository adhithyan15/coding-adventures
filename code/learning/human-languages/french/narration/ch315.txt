Lesson 1 of 6.
la tombe — the grave, the tomb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the funeral, then the French for the cemetery.

You'll want to know: la tombe.
la tombe — "the grave, the tomb".
Elle met des fleurs sur la tombe de sa mère. She puts flowers on her mother's grave.
Not to be confused with il tombe, he falls.

The word, taken apart.
tombe comes from Late Latin tumba "a tomb" (EtymDB), a borrowing from Greek týmbos "a burial mound".

Lesson 2 of 6.
le deuil — mourning, grief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cemetery, then the French for the grave.

You'll want to know: le deuil.
le deuil — "mourning, grief".
Toute la famille est en deuil. The whole family is in mourning.
Porter le deuil — to wear mourning; faire son deuil — to come to terms with a loss.

The word, taken apart.
deuil is usually traced to a Late Latin noun dolus "grief", built on dolēre "to suffer, to grieve" (Lewis & Short); it is not classical dolus "trickery", and EtymDB lists no parent.

Il a laissé sa maison à ses enfants dans son testament. He left his house to his children in his will.

The word, taken apart.
testament is a learned borrowing from Latin testāmentum "a will", from testārī "to bear witness", built on testis "witness" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.

You'll want to know: hériter.
hériter — "to inherit".
Elle a hérité de la maison de sa tante. She inherited her aunt's house.
Hériter de quelque chose — with de.

The word, taken apart.
hériter comes from Old French eriter (EtymDB), from Late Latin hērēditāre "to inherit", built on hērēs "heir" (Lewis & Short).
