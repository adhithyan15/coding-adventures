Telugu, chapter 495: Fear and Fright.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
భయపడు (bhayapaḍu) — to be afraid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to panic, then the Telugu for eagerness.

You'll want to know: భయపడు.
భయపడు — bhayapaḍu — "to be afraid".
చిన్నప్పుడు నేను చీకటికి భయపడేవాడిని. cinnappuḍu nēnu cīkaṭiki bhayapaḍēvāḍini. As a child I used to be afraid of the dark.
Past (I …): భయపడ్డాను, bhayapaḍḍānu.
What you fear takes -కి or -అంటే: కుక్కలంటే భయపడు (bhayapaḍu), to be afraid of dogs. The noun is భయం.

The word, taken apart.
భయపడు (bhayapaḍu) joins a Sanskrit noun to a native verb: bhaya, "fear, peril" (CDIAL 9390), borrowed as భయం, and the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853). A noun of feeling + -పడు makes many Telugu verbs.

Lesson 2 of 5.
భయపెట్టు (bhayapeṭṭu) — to frighten; to scare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for eagerness, then the Telugu for to be afraid.

You'll want to know: భయపెట్టు.
భయపెట్టు — bhayapeṭṭu — "to frighten; to scare".
ఆ కుక్క పిల్లల్ని భయపెట్టింది. ā kukka pillalni bhayapeṭṭindi. That dog frightened the children.
Past (I …): భయపెట్టాను, bhayapeṭṭānu.
-పెట్టు after a noun of feeling makes someone else feel it: భయపడు (bhayapaḍu), to be afraid; భయపెట్టు (bhayapeṭṭu), to frighten.

The word, taken apart.
భయపెట్టు (bhayapeṭṭu) joins Sanskrit bhaya, "fear" (CDIAL 9390), to the native verb peṭṭu, "to put, to place": to put fear into someone.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ఉలిక్కిపడు (ulikkipaḍu) — to be startled; to jump with fright.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be afraid, then the Telugu for to frighten.

You'll want to know: ఉలిక్కిపడు.
ఉలిక్కిపడు — ulikkipaḍu — "to be startled; to jump with fright".
తలుపు చప్పుడు విని ఆమె ఉలిక్కిపడింది. talupu cappuḍu vini āme ulikkipaḍindi. Hearing the knock at the door, she started.
Past (I …): ఉలిక్కిపడ్డాను, ulikkipaḍḍānu.
The cause often takes -కి: చప్పుడుకి ఉలిక్కిపడు (ulikkipaḍu), to start at a noise.

The word, taken apart.
ఉలిక్కిపడు (ulikkipaḍu) ends in the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853); the origin of the first part, ulikki, is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
పీడకల (pīḍakala) — a nightmare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to frighten, then the Telugu for to be startled.

You'll want to know: పీడకల.
పీడకల — pīḍakala — "a nightmare".
నిన్న రాత్రి నాకు ఒక పీడకల వచ్చింది. ninna rātri nāku oka pīḍakala vaccindi. Last night I had a nightmare.
A dream comes to you: నాకు కల వచ్చింది. Plural: పీడకలలు.

The word, taken apart.
పీడకల (pīḍakala) is a compound: Sanskrit pīḍā, "pain, damage" (CDIAL 8227), borrowed as పీడ, "trouble", and the Telugu word కల, "a dream": a dream that torments.

Lesson 5 of 5.
తల్లడిల్లు (tallaḍillu) — to be distraught.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be startled, then the Telugu for a nightmare.

You'll want to know: తల్లడిల్లు.
తల్లడిల్లు — tallaḍillu — "to be distraught".
బాబు కనిపించలేదు, అమ్మ తల్లడిల్లింది. bābu kanipiñcalēdu, amma tallaḍillindi. The little boy could not be found; his mother was distraught.
Past (I …): తల్లడిల్లాను, tallaḍillānu.
A strong verb for being torn with worry or grief.

The word, taken apart.
తల్లడిల్లు (tallaḍillu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3104 lists it, with tallaḍamu, "turmoil, anxiety", beside Kannada tallaṇa, "agitation, alarm, grief".
