Urdu, chapter 319: Kites in Spring.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بسنت (basant) — the spring kite festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for congratulations, then the Urdu for to cling.

You'll want to know: بسنت.
بسنت — basant — "the spring kite festival".
لاہور میں بسنت کا میلہ تھا۔ Lāhaur meṅ basant kā melā thā. There was a spring kite fair in Lahore.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
بسنت (basant) is inherited from Sanskrit vasanta, "spring" (Monier-Williams). In Lahore it names the day of kites at the end of winter.

Lesson 3 of 5.
چرخی (charkhī) — a spool (for kite string).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the spring kite festival, then the Urdu for a kite string.

You'll want to know: چرخی.
چرخی — charkhī — "a spool (for kite string)".
بھائی نے چرخی پکڑی۔ bhāī ne charkhī pakṛī. My brother held the spool.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
چرخی (charkhī) is usually traced to Persian charkh, "a wheel", with the small ending -ī: the little wheel the string winds on.

Lesson 4 of 5.
اڑانا (uṛānā) — to fly a kite; to make fly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a kite string, then the Urdu for a spool.

You'll want to know: اڑانا.
اڑانا — uṛānā — "to fly a kite; to make fly".
ہم نے چھت پر پتنگ اڑائی۔ ham ne chhat par patang uṛāī. We flew a kite on the roof.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے پتنگ اڑائی, us ne patang uṛāī.

The word, taken apart.
اڑانا (uṛānā) is the causing partner of اڑنا, to fly: CDIAL lists Hindi uṛānā, "to cause to fly", under Sanskrit uḍḍīyatē (CDIAL 1697).

Lesson 5 of 5.
کٹنا (kaṭnā) — to be cut; to be cut loose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a spool, then the Urdu for to fly a kite.

You'll want to know: کٹنا.
کٹنا — kaṭnā — "to be cut; to be cut loose".
شام کو میری پتنگ کٹ گئی۔ shām ko merī patang kaṭ gaī. In the evening my kite was cut loose.
Past (it …): وہ پتنگ کٹ گئی, voh patang kaṭ gaī.

The word, taken apart.
کٹنا (kaṭnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousins kaṭnā, "to be cut", and kāṭnā under Sanskrit kártati (CDIAL 2854). It is the partner of کاٹنا: you کاٹنا a string, and a kite کٹ گئی.
