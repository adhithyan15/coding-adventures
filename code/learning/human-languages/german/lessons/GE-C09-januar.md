# der Januar — "January"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MONAT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The first of the twelve borrowed names, and it is a doorway.

## You'll want to know: der Januar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-JANUAR-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MONAT-01] -->

> **der Januar** — "January."

Latin *Januarius*, the month of **Janus** — the Roman god of doors, gateways and
beginnings, shown with two faces because a doorway looks both ways at once.

A first month is exactly that: the year behind you and the year ahead, seen from
the same spot.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-JANUAR-01] -->

- `j-as-y` — German *j* is English *y*. *Januar* begins **ya-**, not **ja-**.
- `vowel-a-long` — and the *a* that follows is long and open.

*YA-nu-ar*, with the weight on the first syllable.
