Arabic, chapter 335: Fairy-tale creatures.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ساحر (sāḥir) — a magician, a sorcerer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to convict, then the Arabic for innocence.

You'll want to know: ساحر.
ساحر — sāḥir — "a magician, a sorcerer".
حول الساحر الأمير إلى ضفدع. ḥawwala as-sāḥir al-amīr ilā ḍifdaʿ. The magician turned the prince into a frog.
Plural: سحرة, saḥara.
Of a place or a smile, ساحر (sāḥir) is enchanting.

The word, taken apart.
ساحر (sāḥir) is built on the native Arabic root s-ḥ-r (Lane), "to bewitch, to enchant"; siḥr is magic and sāḥir the one who works it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
غول (ghūl) — an ogre, a ghoul.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for innocence, then the Arabic for a magician.

You'll want to know: غول.
غول — ghūl — "an ogre, a ghoul".
خاف الأطفال من الغول في الحكاية. khāfa al-aṭfāl min al-ghūl fī al-ḥikāya. The children were scared of the ogre in the tale.
Plural: غيلان, ghīlān.
English ghoul is usually traced to this word.

The word, taken apart.
غول (ghūl) is built on the native Arabic root gh-w-l (Lane), "to take and destroy without warning". Lane gives ghūl as a being of the desert believed to lead travellers astray.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تنين (tinnīn) — a dragon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: تنين.
تنين — tinnīn — "a dragon".
طار التنين فوق الجبل. ṭāra at-tinnīn fawqa al-jabal. The dragon flew over the mountain.

The word, taken apart.
تنين (tinnīn) is given by Lane, under t-n-n, as a huge serpent. It was borrowed from Aramaic tannīnā (EtymDB); Hebrew has tannīn, a sea monster.

Lesson 4 of 5.
عملاق (ʿimlāq) — a giant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an ogre, then the Arabic for a dragon.

You'll want to know: عملاق.
عملاق — ʿimlāq — "a giant".
في القصة عملاق يعيش في الغابة. fī al-qiṣṣa ʿimlāq yaʿīshu fī al-ghāba. In the story a giant lives in the forest.
Plural: عمالقة, ʿamāliqa.
Of a company or a team: عملاق (ʿimlāq), a giant.

The word, taken apart.
عملاق (ʿimlāq) is built on the four-letter root ʿ-m-l-q (Lane), where it is "very tall", and the ʿamāliqa are a people of old remembered as giants. Its origin before that is not settled.

ساعد الأقزام البنت في الغابة. sāʿada al-aqzām al-bint fī al-ghāba. The dwarfs helped the girl in the forest.
Plural: أقزام, aqzām.

The word, taken apart.
قزم (qazam) is usually traced to a native Arabic root q-z-m, where qazam is something small and mean; Lane's article on it is empty. It now names a dwarf.
