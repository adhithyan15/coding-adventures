Arabic, chapter 103: Beside, Left, Middle, Corner, North.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بجانب (bi-jānib) — beside, next to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for behind, then the Arabic for between.

You'll want to know: بجانب.
بجانب — bi-jānib — "beside, next to".

The word, taken apart.
بجانب (bi-jānib) is built from the preposition bi-, "at, by", and jānib, "side", the active participle of the root j-n-b (Lane): "at the side of".

Lesson 2 of 5.
يسار (yasār) — left.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for between, then the Arabic for beside.

You'll want to know: يسار.
يسار — yasār — "left".

The word, taken apart.
يسار (yasār) is built on the native Arabic root y-s-r, "to be easy, gentle" (Lane). Lane gives yasār as the left hand and also as ease, wealth.

Lesson 4 of 5.
زاوية (zāwiya) — a corner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for left, then the Arabic for the middle.
[pause 1 second]
Someone wishes you ṣabāḥ al-khayr. Reply. (Ṣabāḥ an-nūr, "morning of light.")

You'll want to know: زاوية.
زاوية — zāwiya — "a corner".

The word, taken apart.
زاوية (zāwiya) is built on the native Arabic root z-w-y, "to draw together, to gather" (Lane). Zāwiya is the feminine active participle, a corner where things gather.

Lesson 5 of 5.
شمال (shamāl) — north.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for the middle, then the Arabic for a corner.

You'll want to know: شمال.
شمال — shamāl — "north".

The word, taken apart.
شمال (shamāl) is a native noun, filed under the root sh-m-l (Lane). Lane gives shimāl as the left hand and shamāl as the north wind; facing the sunrise, north is on the left.
