# el grado — the degree

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03, ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la temperatura**, then the scale: *poco*, *bastante*, *muy*.
The word coming next is what that scale is a scale of, and you have never been
given it.

## You'll want to know: el grado
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C465-APUNTE-03] -->

**el grado** — the degree.

> Estamos a tres **grados**.

*Doce grados* — twelve degrees. *Bajo cero* — below zero. The everyday frame
with a number is **estar a**: *estamos a tres grados*, we are at three
degrees. You will also hear *hace tres grados* and *hay tres grados*; *estar
a* is the one to produce.

When you learned *la temperatura* you got the quantity without its unit. This
is the unit.

## Grammar Lens: the word you already had under another job
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02, ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE] -->

*Poco*, *bastante*, *muy* is a scale of **degree**, and *degree* is **grado**.
The review that taught that scale carries this word in its own filename and
never once put it in front of you. Here it is.

| use | what it counts |
|---|---|
| *tres grados* | steps up a thermometer |
| the *poco / bastante / muy* scale | steps up a quality |

Latin **gradus** is a **step** — the one you take walking. From there it went
to any step in a series: a step on a staircase, a step of rank, a step of
temperature. English keeps the walking sense in **gradual**, one step at a
time, and in **grade**, **degree** and **graduate**, who has climbed the
steps.

So a *grado* is not only a number. It is a position on a set of stairs, and a
thermometer is a staircase somebody agreed the height of.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-01] -->

*Gradus* also gives Spanish **el grado** for a university degree and for a
school year, and **la graduación**, which is both the ceremony and the
strength of an alcohol — another scale with steps on it.

**De buen grado** — willingly, gladly — is a different word, from *gratum*,
pleasing. Two *grado*s, and only this one has stairs in it.
