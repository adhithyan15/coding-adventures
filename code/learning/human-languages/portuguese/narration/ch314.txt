Portuguese, chapter 314: Uma perda na família.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o falecimento — the passing, the death (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the separation, then the Portuguese for the move.

You'll want to know: o falecimento.
o falecimento — "the passing, the death (formal)".
O falecimento do avô juntou a família toda. Grandfather’s passing brought the whole family together.
A respectful word, common in notices.

The word, taken apart.
falecimento is built on falecer "to pass away", with the ending -mento; the verb goes back to Vulgar Latin fallēscere (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o óbito — the death (in official language).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the passing, then the Portuguese for to pass away.

You'll want to know: o óbito.
o óbito — "the death (in official language)".
O médico confirmou o óbito às seis horas. The doctor confirmed the death at six o’clock.
An official word: certidão de óbito — death certificate.

The word, taken apart.
óbito is a learned borrowing from Latin obitus "a going down, death", from obīre "to go towards, to die", built on īre "to go" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o velório — the wake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the death, then the Portuguese for mourning.

You'll want to know: o velório.
o velório — "the wake".
Muitos vizinhos foram ao velório. Many neighbours went to the wake.
The night when people keep watch with the dead before the burial.

The word, taken apart.
velório is built on velar "to keep watch" with the ending -ório; velar comes from Latin vigilāre "to stay awake, to keep watch" (Lewis & Short).
