Latin, chapter 148: Lucky, Wretched, Angry, Quiet, Purple.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fēlīx, fēlīcis — lucky, happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for safe, then the Latin for dangerous.

You'll want to know: fēlīx, fēlīcis.
fēlīx, fēlīcis — "lucky, happy".

The word, taken apart.
fēlīx is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dheh1-, "to suck, to suckle" (EtymDB 2.1), the root of fēmina, "woman": it first meant "fruitful", then lucky.

Lesson 4 of 5.
quiētus, quiēta, quiētum — quiet, calm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wretched, then the Latin for angry.

You'll want to know: quiētus, quiēta, quiētum.
quiētus, quiēta, quiētum — "quiet, calm".

The word, taken apart.
quiētus is the participle of quiēscō, "to rest" (Lewis & Short), from quiēs, "rest", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kwyeh1-, "to rest" (EtymDB 2.1). English quiet and quit come from this family.
