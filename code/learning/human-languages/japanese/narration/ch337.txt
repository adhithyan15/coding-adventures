Japanese, chapter 337: Saving Energy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
せつやくする (setsuyaku suru) — to economize; to save (energy, money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for air, then the Japanese for pollution.

You'll want to know: せつやくする.
せつやくする — setsuyaku suru — "to economize; to save (energy, money)".
でんきが たかいので、せつやくしています。 — denki ga takai node, setsuyaku shite imasu — "Electricity is expensive, so we are cutting down."
Polite せつやくします, setsuyakushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ははが みずを せつやくしました。, haha ga mizu o setsuyaku shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
せつやくする (setsuyaku suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, setsuyaku, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: setsu, "joint" (Middle Chinese tset) and yaku, "shrink" ('jak) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
もったいない (mottainai) — wasteful; too good to waste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fuel, then the Japanese for petroleum.

You'll want to know: もったいない.
もったいない — mottainai — "wasteful; too good to waste".
もったいないから、すてないで ください。 — mottainai kara, sutenaide kudasai — "It's a waste, so please do not throw it away."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
もったいない (mottainai) is built from the Sino-Japanese mottai, two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, mochi, "not", shortened to mot-, and tai, "body" (Middle Chinese thejX), and the native nai, "not". JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
