Lesson 1 of 5.
ആൽമരം (ālmaraṁ) — a banyan tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a Vishu gift of money, then the Malayalam for a firecracker.

You'll want to know: ആൽമരം.
ആൽമരം — ālmaraṁ — "a banyan tree".

The word, taken apart.
ആൽമരം (ālmaraṁ) is native Dravidian: āl, "banyan" (DEDR 382, with Tamil āl and Kannada āl), and maram, "tree" (DEDR 4711).

You'll want to know: പ്ലാവ്.
പ്ലാവ് — plāvŭ — "a jackfruit tree".

The word, taken apart.
പ്ലാവ് (plāvŭ) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil palavu (DEDR 3988). Kannada and Telugu use panasa, the Sanskrit word for the same tree. DEDR 3988 compares Sanskrit panasa.

Lesson 3 of 5.
റോസാപ്പൂ (ṟōsāppū) — a rose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a banyan tree, then the Malayalam for a jackfruit tree.

You'll want to know: റോസാപ്പൂ.
റോസാപ്പൂ — ṟōsāppū — "a rose".

The word, taken apart.
റോസാപ്പൂ (ṟōsāppū) joins rōsa, from English rose or Portuguese rosa, to native pū, "flower" (DEDR 4345). DEDR 4345 compares Sanskrit puṣpa, with a query.

Lesson 5 of 5.
തുളസി (tuḷasi) — holy basil, tulsi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a rose, then the Malayalam for a hibiscus.

You'll want to know: തുളസി.
തുളസി — tuḷasi — "holy basil, tulsi".

The word, taken apart.
തുളസി (tuḷasi) is the holy basil's name across the south: DEDR 3357 sets it beside Tamil tuḻāy and Kannada toḷaci, and compares Sanskrit tulasī. Which way the word travelled is not settled.
