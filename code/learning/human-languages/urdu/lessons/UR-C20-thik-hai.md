# ٹھیک ہے — all right

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-THIK, UR-LEX-LEKIN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **ٹھیک** has meant *fine* since chapter four, and only ever about
your health. It has a second life, and it needs one more word.

- [YOU RECALL: the joiner from the lesson before — *lekin*]

## You'll want to know: ٹھیک ہے
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-FUNC-THIK-HAI]; assesses=[UR-LEX-THIK, UR-GRAMMAR-COPULA-FINAL] -->

> **ٹھیک ہے** — *ṭhīk hai* — **all right; that's right; agreed.**

> **ٹھیک ہے، لیکن …** — *ṭhīk hai, lekin …* — **all right, but …**

The copula of chapter two, **ہے**, does all the work. *ṭhīk hai* is what a
speaker says to accept a plan, to agree with a claim, and to end a phone call —
one phrase covering everything English spreads across *okay*, *fine*, *right*
and *deal*.

## Grammar Lens: agreeing, and taking it back
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-FUNC-THIK-HAI, UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB, UR-LEX-LEKIN, UR-LEX-NAHIN] -->

| said | means |
|---|---|
| *ṭhīk hai* | agreed |
| *ṭhīk nahīṅ hai* | that is not right |
| *ṭhīk hai, lekin …* | agreed as far as it goes |

The middle row is the negator of the last chapter dropped in front of the copula,
which is exactly where chapter nineteen said it goes. The third row is the shape
almost every real disagreement takes: accept first, then turn.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-FUNC-THIK-HAI, UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB, UR-SCRIPT-THIK] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What word does **ٹھیک** need to become an agreement? (**ہے**, the
copula from chapter two.) How do you disagree with it? (**Put نہیں in front of
the copula** — *ṭhīk nahīṅ hai*.) And what does *ṭhīk hai, lekin …* do that a
flat *nahīṅ* does not? (**Accepts first, and then turns**.)
