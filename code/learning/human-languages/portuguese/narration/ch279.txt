Portuguese, chapter 279: A imprensa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a imprensa — the press, the newspapers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for live, then the Portuguese for the broadcast.

You'll want to know: a imprensa.
a imprensa — "the press, the newspapers".
A imprensa falou muito do assunto. The press talked a lot about the matter.
It also names the printing press.

The word, taken apart.
imprensa goes back to Latin impressus "pressed in, printed" (EtymDB), from imprimere "to press into", built on premere "to press".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a reportagem — the report, the feature story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the broadcast, then the Portuguese for the press.

You'll want to know: a reportagem.
a reportagem — "the report, the feature story".
Vi uma reportagem sobre a vida no deserto. I saw a report about life in the desert.
A longer piece on TV or in a paper, with interviews and pictures.

The word, taken apart.
reportagem came from French reportage (EtymDB), built on reporter "to report", which goes back to Latin reportāre "to carry back", from portāre "to carry".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o correspondente — the correspondent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the report, then the Portuguese for the reporter.

You'll want to know: o correspondente.
o correspondente — "the correspondent".
O correspondente do jornal vive no estrangeiro. The newspaper’s correspondent lives abroad.
As an adjective it means ‘matching’: a resposta correspondente.

The word, taken apart.
correspondente is a learned word from Medieval Latin correspondēns "answering to each other", built on respondēre "to answer" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a manchete — the headline.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the reporter, then the Portuguese for the correspondent.

You'll want to know: a manchete.
a manchete — "the headline".
A manchete de hoje fala do governo. Today’s headline is about the government.
The big headline on the front page; o título is any heading.

The word, taken apart.
manchete was borrowed from French manchette "a cuff, a headline", a little manche "sleeve", which comes from Latin manica "a long sleeve", built on manus "hand" (Lewis & Short).
