## Fixed — Bengali root slugs name one etymon each, and কালো keeps "black" and "time" apart

- **BN-C14-kalo** moves from `kala-time-black-death` to `sanskrit-kala-black`, the slug the Gujarati, Hindi, Marathi, Marwadi, Punjabi and Urdu black lessons use. The old slug joined *kāla* "black" (CDIAL 3083) with *kāla* "time" (CDIAL 3084). The lesson called the two one word with a "still-debated" origin. CDIAL gives them separate entries and Monier-Williams lists them as two headwords (MW only guesses, with a query, that "black" shares the root of "time"). The gloss, title, warm-up, taken-apart block, practice line and recall now say this. The 508-character hook is now 107 characters and agrees with the block.
- **BN-C15-shari** moves from `shati-strip-of-cloth` to `sanskrit-shati`, the slug the Hindi, Marathi, Sanskrit and Urdu sari lessons use (*śāṭī*, CDIAL 12381, which lists Bengali *sāṛi*).
- **BN-C159-jehetu**, **BN-C218-jate** and **BN-C218-jmon** move from `sanskrit-ya` to `sanskrit-ya-relative`, the slug the Gujarati, Punjabi, Sanskrit and Urdu lessons use for the relative stem *ya-* (CDIAL 10391). Bare `sanskrit-ya` had also been used for *yā-* "go", and no lesson uses it any more.
- **BN-C15-shari**'s hook, 347 characters in a garbled ASCII spelling ("shATI shATI"), is now 105 characters in IAST: *shāṛi* ← *śāṭī* "strip of cloth" (CDIAL 12381), through Prakrit *sāḍī*, with English *sari* a modern loan, as the block says.
