Urdu, chapter 106: Thread, Towel, Comb, Glass, Pillow.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دھاگا (dhāgā) — thread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for scissors, then the Urdu for a needle.

You'll want to know: دھاگا.
دھاگا — dhāgā — "thread".

The word, taken apart.
دھاگا (dhāgā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dhāgga, "thread", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6770). CDIAL suggests, with a "possibly", that it crossed with dhara, "a flock of raw cotton".

You'll want to know: کنگھی.
کنگھی — kaṅghī — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
کنگھی (kaṅghī) goes back to Sanskrit kaṅkata, "comb" (CDIAL 2598). CDIAL marks the Hindi forms as taken over from Punjabi, and calls the Sanskrit word's Indo-European origin doubtful.

Lesson 4 of 5.
شیشہ (shīshā) — glass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a towel, then the Urdu for a comb.

You'll want to know: شیشہ.
شیشہ — shīshā — "glass".

The word, taken apart.
شیشہ (shīshā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian shīsha, "glass". It is a different word from sīsā, "lead", which comes from Sanskrit sīsa (CDIAL 13445).

تکیہ — takiyā — "a pillow".

The word, taken apart.
تکیہ (takiyā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian takya, "pillow, support", which Persian took from Arabic, on the root w-k-ʾ, "to lean" (Lane).
