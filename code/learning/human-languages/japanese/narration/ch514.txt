Japanese, chapter 514: Guesses and Forecasts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
すいそくする (suisoku suru) — to guess; to conjecture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to adjust, then the Japanese for arrangements.

You'll want to know: すいそくする.
すいそくする — suisoku suru — "to guess; to conjecture".
もし まよったら、すいそくしないで ください。 — moshi mayottara, suisokushinaide kudasai — "If you are unsure, please do not guess."
Polite すいそくします, suisokushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せんせいが こたえを すいそくしました。, sensei ga kotae o suisokushimashita.

The word, taken apart.
すいそくする (suisoku suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun suisoku with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "conjecture" (Middle Chinese tsyhwij) and soku, "fathom" (tsrhik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
みこみ (mikomi) — a prospect; a hope (of).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to guess, then the Japanese for to predict.

You'll want to know: みこみ.
みこみ — mikomi — "a prospect; a hope (of)".
みこみは ちりょうに よります。 — mikomi wa chiryō ni yorimasu — "The prospects depend on the treatment."

The word, taken apart.
みこみ (mikomi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from mi, "seeing" and komi, "putting in" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
よほう (yohō) — a forecast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to predict, then the Japanese for a prospect.

You'll want to know: よほう.
よほう — yohō — "a forecast".
もし よほうが あたったら、あしたは ゆきでしょう。 — moshi yohō ga atattara, ashita wa yuki deshō — "If the forecast is right, it will snow tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
よほう (yohō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yo, "beforehand" (Middle Chinese yoH) and hō, "report" (pawH), the same two as at the end of tenkiyohō, "the weather forecast" (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
