Mandarin Chinese, chapter 496: Levels.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
bèishū — to learn a text by heart; to recite from memory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to cheat in an exam, then the Mandarin for a hard problem.

You'll want to know: bèishū.
bèishū — "to learn a text by heart; to recite from memory".
kǎoshì yǐqián wǒ měi tiān zǎoshang bèishū — Before exams I learn my texts by heart every morning.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bèishū is built from bèi 'the back' and 书 'a book', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pwojH and syo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the back of the body' and also lists it as bwojH, 'to turn one's back on'. How the pair came to mean 'to recite from memory' is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
高级 (gāojí) — advanced; high-level.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a hard problem, then the Mandarin for to learn a text by heart.

You'll want to know: 高级.
高级 — gāojí — "advanced; high-level".
我学了三年汉语现在学高级汉语 wǒ xué le sān nián Hànyǔ xiànzài xué gāojí Hànyǔ — I've studied Chinese for three years, and now I'm doing advanced Chinese.
The new character, in pieces: 级 (jí) — six strokes in two pieces: on the left the silk piece of 红 hóng, three strokes; on the right three strokes, a long stroke falling left, a stroke that runs right and turns twice, and a long stroke falling right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
高级 (gāojí) is built from 高 'high' and 级 (jí) 'a grade', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and kip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a grade' and, after an older dictionary, 'the order of silk threads'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
级 (jí) — the silk piece, then three strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the silk piece, then three strokes and Guided Practice.

Script — the silk piece, then three strokes.
级 (jí).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, three strokes, as on the left of 红: a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, a second one like it below, then a short stroke rising to the right.
The right piece, three strokes: a long stroke falling to the left; then one stroke that runs right along the top, turns down and back, runs right again and falls away to the lower left; then a long stroke falling to the right.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 级 (jí), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 级 (jí) have? (Two.) Which characters you know share its left piece? (红, 经 or 约.) What is the last stroke? (A long stroke falling to the right.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 级 (jí), pinned stroke paths and medians.
