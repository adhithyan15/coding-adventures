Malayalam, chapter 402: Myths, Fables and Heroes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പുരാണം (purāṇaṁ) — a myth; an ancient legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to fight, then the Malayalam for to resist.

You'll want to know: പുരാണം.
പുരാണം — purāṇaṁ — "a myth; an ancient legend".
മുത്തശ്ശി ഞങ്ങൾക്ക് പുരാണങ്ങളിലെ കഥകൾ പറഞ്ഞുതന്നു. muttaśśi ñaṅṅaḷkkŭ purāṇaṅṅaḷile kathakaḷ paṟaññutannu. Grandmother told us stories from the old legends.
Plural: പുരാണങ്ങൾ, purāṇaṅṅaḷ.
Also the great old books of tales.

The word, taken apart.
പുരാണം (purāṇaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit purāṇa, "ancient, belonging to olden times" (CDIAL 8283): a tale from long ago.

Lesson 2 of 5.
കെട്ടുകഥ (keṭṭukatha) — a fable; a made-up story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to resist, then the Malayalam for a myth.

You'll want to know: കെട്ടുകഥ.
കെട്ടുകഥ — keṭṭukatha — "a fable; a made-up story".
അത് സത്യമല്ല, ഒരു കെട്ടുകഥ ആണ്. atŭ satyamalla, oru keṭṭukatha āṇŭ. That is not true, it is a made-up story.
കെട്ട്, a tying, + കഥ, a story: a story tied together.

The word, taken apart.
കെട്ടുകഥ (keṭṭukatha) joins the native കെട്ട്, "a tying, a bundle" (DEDR 1147), and കഥ (katha), "a story", borrowed from Sanskrit kathā (CDIAL 2704).

Lesson 3 of 5.
നാടോടിക്കഥ (nāṭōṭikkatha) — a folk tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a myth, then the Malayalam for a fable.

You'll want to know: നാടോടിക്കഥ.
നാടോടിക്കഥ — nāṭōṭikkatha — "a folk tale".
ഓരോ ഗ്രാമത്തിനും സ്വന്തം നാടോടിക്കഥകൾ ഉണ്ട്. ōrō grāmattinuṁ svantaṁ nāṭōṭikkathakaḷ uṇṭŭ. Every village has its own folk tales.
Plural: നാടോടിക്കഥകൾ, nāṭōṭikkathakaḷ.
നാടോടി, of the folk, roaming the land, + കഥ, a story.

The word, taken apart.
നാടോടിക്കഥ (nāṭōṭikkatha) is built from the native നാട്, "the country" (DEDR 3638), ഓടി, "running", from ഓടുക (DEDR 1041), and കഥ (katha), "a story": a tale that runs through the land.

Lesson 4 of 5.
നായകൻ (nāyakan) — a hero; a leader.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a fable, then the Malayalam for a folk tale.

You'll want to know: നായകൻ.
നായകൻ — nāyakan — "a hero; a leader".
കഥയിലെ നായകൻ ഒരു പാവം കർഷകൻ ആണ്. kathayile nāyakan oru pāvaṁ karṣakan āṇŭ. The hero of the story is a poor farmer.
A heroine is നായിക.

The word, taken apart.
നായകൻ (nāyakan) is borrowed from Sanskrit nāyaka, "a guide, a leader, a chief" (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam masculine ending -ൻ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ഗുണപാഠം (guṇapāṭhaṁ) — a moral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a folk tale, then the Malayalam for a hero.

You'll want to know: ഗുണപാഠം.
ഗുണപാഠം — guṇapāṭhaṁ — "a moral".
ആ കഥയുടെ ഗുണപാഠം എന്താണ്? ā kathayuṭe guṇapāṭhaṁ entāṇŭ? What is the moral of that story?
ഗുണം, a virtue, + പാഠം, a lesson: the good lesson a story teaches.

The word, taken apart.
ഗുണപാഠം (guṇapāṭhaṁ) is built from two Sanskrit words, guṇa, "a quality, a good quality" (CDIAL 4190), and pāṭha, "study, a text" (CDIAL 8035), the source of പാഠം (pāṭhaṁ), "a lesson".
