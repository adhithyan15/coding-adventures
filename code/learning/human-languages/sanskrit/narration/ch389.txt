Sanskrit, chapter 389: Can It Be Done?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शक्यः (śakyaḥ) — possible; able to be done.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for in vain, then the Sanskrit for an ideal.

You'll want to know: शक्यः.
शक्यः — śakyaḥ — "possible; able to be done".
यदि समयः अस्ति, तर्हि एतत् कार्यं शक्यम्। yadi samayaḥ asti, tarhi etat kāryaṃ śakyam. If there is time, this work can be done.

The word, taken apart.
शक्यः (śakyaḥ) comes from the root √śak, "to be able"; MW gives śakya "able, possible, practicable" (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कल्पयति (kalpayati) — imagines; supposes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for possible, then the Sanskrit for achievable.

You'll want to know: कल्पयति.
कल्पयति — kalpayati — "imagines; supposes".
यदि बालः एकाकी, तर्हि सः मित्राणि कल्पयति। yadi bālaḥ ekākī, tarhi saḥ mitrāṇi kalpayati. If the child is alone, he imagines friends.
Past (he …): सः नूतनं जगत् कल्पितवान्, saḥ nūtanaṃ jagat kalpitavān.

The word, taken apart.
कल्पयति (kalpayati) comes from the root √kḷp, "to be fit", in its causative form; MW gives the senses "to frame, to form, to invent" and "to imagine" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
कल्पना (kalpanā) — imagination; a fancy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for achievable, then the Sanskrit for imagines.

You'll want to know: कल्पना.
कल्पना — kalpanā — "imagination; a fancy".
यदि कल्पना न अभविष्यत्, तर्हि कविः काव्यं न अकरिष्यत्। yadi kalpanā na abhaviṣyat, tarhi kaviḥ kāvyaṃ na akariṣyat. Had there been no imagination, the poet would have made no poem.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कल्पना (kalpanā) comes from the root √kḷp, "to be fit, to form"; MW gives kalpanā "creating in the mind, assuming anything to be real, fiction" (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: यथार्थः.
यथार्थः — yathārthaḥ — "true to fact; real".
यदि वार्ता यथार्था, तर्हि सः श्वः गमिष्यति। yadi vārtā yathārthā, tarhi saḥ śvaḥ gamiṣyati. If the news is true, he will go tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
यथार्थः (yathārthaḥ) is built from yathā, "as", and artha, "meaning"; MW gives yathārtha "accordant with reality, conformable to truth, true, genuine" (MW).
