## Added — the A2 grammar core, chapters 145–152

Eight chapters, 48 lessons (sequences 7590–8060), two for each of the four A2 spine nodes the track had not reached. Before this change `SPINE-NEGATE-AND-ASK`, `SPINE-TALK-ABOUT-PAST`, `SPINE-TALK-ABOUT-FUTURE` and `SPINE-READ-PRACTICAL-TEXTS` had no path segment, and the track used only one future form (*maḷīśũ*) and one past form (*thayo*).

- **145, Saying not (`VERB-NEGATE`).** The present is completed person by person on the taught *hũ gujarātī bolũ chũ*: *te / tũ bole che*, *teo bole che*, *ame bolīe chīe*, *tame bolo cho*. Then *nathī* with the verb in *-to / -tī / -tā* (built on *samajto nathī*, both orders shown), *na* before a request (*cintā na karo*), *nahī̃* for one word, and *koī … nathī*, *kaī̃ … nathī*, *kyārey … nathī*.
- **146, Asking yes or no (`QUESTION-POLAR`).** *śũ* at the front, the rising question without it, the tags *… ke nahī̃?* and *…, kharũ?*, and answers with *hā / nā* and the verb again. The "to" pronouns (*mane … temne*) and the possessives (*māro / mārī / mārũ / mārā*, then *tārũ, tenũ, amārũ, āpṇũ, tamārũ, temnũ*) come here because the questions and answers need them.
- **147, What was and where I went (`VERB-PAST`).** *hato / hatī / hatũ / hatā*; the *-e* of *when* and *where* (*gaīkāle, āje, savāre, sānje, rātre, ravivāre, ghare*) and the two-way *kāle*; *gayo / gaī / gayũ / gayā*, *āvyo*, *thayo*; the word *moḍũ*.
- **148, What I did (`VERB-PAST`).** The object past: *mẽ cā pīdhī*, the doer forms *mẽ, tẽ, teṇe, ame, tame, temṇe*, the regular *-yũ* (*karyũ, āpyũ, lakhyũ, kahyũ, joyũ*) and the three *-dhũ* verbs (*līdhũ, khādhũ, pīdhũ*).
- **149, What will happen (`VERB-FUTURE`).** *-īś* and *-īśũ* (built on *maḷīśũ*), *-śe* and *-śo*; the short verbs *jaīś / jaśe*, *laīś / leśe*, *karīś*, and *thaśe*, *haśe*, *haśo*; the word *ūṅgh*. The familiar *tũ* takes *-īś* in the standard language; the lesson says so.
- **150, Plans and perhaps (`VERB-FUTURE`).** *-vāno / -vānī / -vānā* with the present copula, the question *śũ karvānā cho?*, the negative *nathī āvvāno*, and the word *kadāc*. A review closes chapters 145–148.
- **151, Reading a shop notice.** Four words (*noṭis*, *khullũ*, *phakt*, *jāher*) and a 45-word notice of opening days, a public holiday and an advert, with prompts that ask when to go.
- **152, Reading a note about a repair.** Three words (*ciṭṭhī*, *marāmat*, *bhāḍũ*) and a 52-word note from a landlord about a repair and the rent, with prompts that find its one question and plan the reply. Two reviews close the core.
- **Letters.** Three new letters, each written one lesson after a word that holds it: GU-W46-o (ઓ, from તેઓ), GU-W47-dda (ડ, from મોડું) and GU-W48-uu (ઊ, from ઊંઘ). The track now teaches 48 Gujarati code points. The filmstrip ledger in `data/ductus/filmstrip-geometry.d/gujarati.json` gains the three glyphs.
- **Function words stay out of word lessons.** *nathī*, *śũ*, *nā* and *che* already appear in earlier lessons, so they are taught inside `grammar` lessons, which register no forward-reference matcher. The eleven word headwords were each checked to appear nowhere earlier as a whole word.
- **Sources.** CDIAL for *teo* and *teṇe* (5612), *gayo* (4008), *thayo* (13768), *ūṅgh* (1633), *khullũ* (3945), *ciṭṭhī* (4832) and *bhāḍũ* (9468). Lane for *jāher* (root *ẓ-h-r*), *marāmat* (*r-m-m*) and *phakt* (*faqaṭ*, treated under *q-ṭ*). Hedged: *moḍũ* (origin not settled; CDIAL lists no Gujarati form), *kadāc* ("usually traced to" Sanskrit *kadācit*), and the future *-ś-* ("usually traced to" the Sanskrit *-iṣya-* future). *noṭis* is from English *notice*.
- **Romanization.** The new lessons use the track's recent scheme (chapters 92–143): *c* for ચ and *ch* for છ (*che*, *chũ*, *cintā*), *ś* for શ, and a tilde for a nasal vowel (*nahī̃*, *tyā̃*). Chapters 2–37 still write *chhe* and *pūchhvũ*; they are not changed here.
