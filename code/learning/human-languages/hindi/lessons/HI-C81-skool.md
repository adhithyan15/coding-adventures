# स्कूल (skūl) — the building the teacher and the student were always in

## You'll want to know: स्कूल
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**स्कूल** (*skūl*) — "school". Masculine.

Write it: स + ् + क gives the conjunct **स्क**, then ू above the ल... no: the
ू belongs to क, so it is **स्कू** and then **ल**. Three pieces, one of them a
conjunct you have seen before.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

This one is borrowed straight from English, and the English word has a joke
inside it that Hindi inherited without asking.

English *school* comes from Latin *schola*, from Greek **skholḗ** — which meant
**leisure**. Free time. The hours you did not have to work.

The Greeks thought study was what a person did with their leisure, so the word
for time off slid across to mean the place you spend it learning. Every language
that borrowed it borrowed the joke: Spanish *escuela*, German *Schule*, Russian
*shkola*, and now Hindi **स्कूल**.

So when you say *skūl*, you are saying a Greek word for "spare time" that has
travelled through Latin, English and into Devanagari without anyone noticing
what it used to mean.
