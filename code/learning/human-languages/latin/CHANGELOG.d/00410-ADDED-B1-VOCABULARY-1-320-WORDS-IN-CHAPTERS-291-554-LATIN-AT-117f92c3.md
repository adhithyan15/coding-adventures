## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,320 words in chapters 291–554; Latin attains B1

- **264 new chapters (291–554) teach 1,320 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 403 of them are verbs. Every headword is classical Latin attested in Lewis & Short, in the track's dictionary form with macrons (*nāvis, nāvis*; *narrō, narrāre*). Each lesson gives:
  - the word and its English;
  - a short Latin example with its translation;
  - a note with the gender, declension or principal parts, the case a verb takes, and English words that come from it;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 291–356, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (330 words): time and sequence, news and messengers, crime, trials and punishment, accidents and fire, birth to inheritance, festivals and games, war and history, myth, and the Roman kitchen.
  - 357–422, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (330 words): opinion and argument, the orator's words, cause and consequence, work and trades, society and politics, then conditions and plans, promises and agreements, school, laws and the courts, money and trade. The particles and conjunctions (*quandōquidem*, *quippe*, *cum* + subjunctive, *nē*, *quīn*, *dummodo*, *quamvīs*, *tametsī* and others) each say which mood they take.
  - 423–488, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (330 words): roads, carts and horses, ships and harbours, inns and complaints, finding the way, the town, the market and crafts, the farm, mountains and rivers, and weather.
  - 489–554, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (330 words): feelings, character, virtues and vices, the body and medicine, family and household, friendship and love, leisure and the baths, music, theatre and the arts, books and letters, and the home.
- **Latin now teaches 2,563 headwords at or below B1**, past the 2,500 the level gate asks for. With the B1 core (chapters 281–290), Latin attains B1.
- **No modern coinages.** Themes with no classical words (cars, phones, the internet) are covered with what Romans had: roads, carts and ships; letters and books; markets, baths and inns. Two late words are kept and labelled late in their notes (*caementārius*, *agrīmēnsor*).
- **Sources.** Every word and gloss was checked against Lewis & Short, and every PIE or Proto-Italic parent against EtymDB 2.1. Where they disagree or are silent, the lesson says so or hedges. An independent review of each tranche fixed errors in 165 of the 1,320 words (12.5%), mostly etymology attributions the sources do not carry.
- **No forward references and no hidden duplicates.** Every form of every candidate headword was screened against every earlier lesson's text, Latin forms spelled like English words were avoided or checked, and a macron-blind duplicate check found no word the track already teaches. Latin's forward-reference count stays at its pin of 29.
