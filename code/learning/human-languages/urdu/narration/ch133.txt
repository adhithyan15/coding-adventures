Urdu, chapter 133: Young, Old, Lovely, Soft, Hard.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جوان (javān) — young.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for strong, then the Urdu for weak.

You'll want to know: جوان.
جوان — javān — "young".

The word, taken apart.
جوان (javān) is usually traced to Persian javān, "young". CDIAL also lists a Hindi juwān, "a youth", under yuvāna, "young", the Indo-Aryan form of the same ancient word (CDIAL 10510).

Lesson 2 of 5.
بوڑھا (būṛhā) — old (of people).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for weak, then the Urdu for young.

You'll want to know: بوڑھا.
بوڑھا — būṛhā — "old (of people)".

The word, taken apart.
بوڑھا (būṛhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from buḍḍha, "old", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9271). CDIAL notes that the initial b- rules out direct descent from Sanskrit vṛddha, "grown old", though that word may have influenced it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
پیارا (pyārā) — lovely, dear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for young, then the Urdu for old.

You'll want to know: پیارا.
پیارا — pyārā — "lovely, dear".

The word, taken apart.
پیارا (pyārā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from priyakāra, "doing a kindness" (CDIAL 8975). It is built from priya, "dear", and kāra, "doing".
