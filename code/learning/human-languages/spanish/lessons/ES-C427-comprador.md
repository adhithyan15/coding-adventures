# el comprador — the other side of the counter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-01, ES-LEX-COMPRAR-07] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el vendedor*. Say *comprar*. Build the person who does the
second thing, using the machine from the first.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-02]; assesses=[] -->

**el comprador** — "the buyer."

*Comprar* minus **-ar**, plus **-dor**. No new rule, which is the point of
teaching the pair together: one ending buys both halves of a transaction.

| side | verb | person |
|---|---|---|
| sells | vender | el vendedor |
| buys | comprar | el comprador |

## Grammar Lens: the pair that names an economy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-02, ES-LEX-C427-AGENT-01] -->

These two words are how a form, a contract or a news item names the parties to
a sale. They are not shop vocabulary — *el vendedor* in a shop is the assistant,
but *vendedor* and *comprador* together are the **roles**, the way English says
*seller* and *buyer* rather than *shopkeeper* and *customer*.

That distinction is why the source lists them under commerce rather than under
shopping, and why knowing *comprar* was not enough to cover it.
