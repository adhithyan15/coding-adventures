Hindi, chapter 69: Ways of Going.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गाड़ी (gāṛī) — a vehicle; a train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two words back before anything new: what does bhāshā mean, and what is अंग्रेज़ी? The next five words are how you leave the शहर you live in.

You'll want to know: गाड़ी.
गाड़ी (gāṛī) — "a vehicle".
From Sanskrit गर्त (garta), a cart, through the Prakrits. It began as the thing oxen pulled and it has taken in everything since: a car is a गाड़ी (gāṛī), a train is a गाड़ी (gāṛī), a bullock cart is a गाड़ी (gāṛī).
That width is the reason to learn it first. बस you already own, and बस names one thing only. गाड़ी (gāṛī) names whatever is carrying you, so it is the word that never leaves you stuck — point at anything with wheels and गाड़ी (gāṛī) is right.
The ड़ in the middle is the flapped retroflex, made by flicking the tongue down off the roof of the mouth. It is ड with the nuqta underneath.

What you've built.
The first of five words for getting somewhere.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: say bhāshā, then read अंग्रेज़ी and say what it means]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — hear: gāṛī, slowly, with the flap in the middle]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: gāṛī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: gāṛī and bas, and say which one is wider]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does गाड़ी (gāṛī) mean? ("a vehicle".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
रेल (rel) — the railway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say gāṛī, then write गाड़ी: ग, then the ा stroke, then ड with its nuqta, then ी. Four pieces, all of them yours already.

You'll want to know: रेल.
रेल (rel) — "the railway".
English rail, borrowed whole in the nineteenth century and spelt with the े matra, which is where an English ay sound usually lands in Devanagari.
रेल (rel) on its own is the system: the network, the line, the railway as an institution. The train itself is रेलगाड़ी — रेल (rel) plus the गाड़ी (gāṛī) you learnt in the last lesson — and in speech people shorten that back to plain गाड़ी (gāṛī). Two words you now hold combine into the third without anything new.

What you've built.
गाड़ी (gāṛī) and रेल (rel), and the compound they make between them.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: read अंग्रेज़ी, then say it without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: rel]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: rel, then gāṛī, then relgāṛī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: गाड़ी (gāṛī) once more, without looking]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does रेल (rel) mean? ("the railway".) What is a रेलगाड़ी? ("a train".)


Lesson 3 of 5.
स्टेशन (sṭeshan) — a station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of your two words names the network, and which names the thing that moves along it?

You'll want to know: स्टेशन.
स्टेशन (sṭeshan) — "a station".
English station, and it shows its seams. Hindi does not open a word with two consonants stuck together, so the writing does it the Devanagari way instead: स with the vowel-killer ् underneath, then ट. That stack is the same virama you met inside नमस्ते.
The retroflex ट rather than dental त is deliberate. When Hindi borrows an English t, it hears the English tongue as curled back, so station becomes स्टेशन (sṭeshan) and not स्तेशन. Every English t you meet in a Hindi loan will do this.
The word is painted on every platform board in the country, in Devanagari and in Latin letters side by side.

What you've built.
Three: गाड़ी (gāṛī), रेल (rel), स्टेशन (sṭeshan). A vehicle, a network, and the place you wait.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: स्टेशन (sṭeshan), and find the ् that stacks the first two letters]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: sṭeshan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: rel sṭeshan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does स्टेशन (sṭeshan) mean? ("a station".) Say it once more.


Lesson 4 of 5.
टिकट (ṭikaṭ) — a ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write स्टेशन (sṭeshan) from memory. Start with the stacked स्, then ट, then े on top, then श, then न.

You'll want to know: टिकट.
टिकट (ṭikaṭ) — "a ticket".
English ticket, and both of its t sounds land on retroflex ट for the reason the last lesson gave. The ि in the middle is the short i matra, and it is written before the letter it is sounded after — you learnt that trick when you first wrote a name.
This is the word you actually say at a window. You already know how to ask for one thing politely: एक चाय दीजिए got you a cup of tea. एक टिकट (ṭikaṭ) दीजिए is the same frame with a new word dropped in, and it will get you onto a train.

What you've built.
Four, and a request you can already make with the fourth.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ṭikaṭ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ek ṭikaṭ dījie]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: टिकट (ṭikaṭ), putting the ि down before the ट it belongs to]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you ask for one ticket? (ek ṭikaṭ dījie.) Say it once more.


Lesson 5 of 5.
जाना (jānā) — to go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the four words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: जाना.
जाना (jānā) — "to go".
Sanskrit या (yā-), "to go", is the root; Hindi turned the initial y- into j-, which it does to many inherited words, and added the -ना ending that every Hindi doing word carries when you name it plain.
You already own three that end the same way: बोलना, देखना, सुनना. The ending is the pattern, not a coincidence, and जाना (jānā) now joins them.
जाना (jānā) is the one doing word a traveller cannot do without. मैं स्टेशन (sṭeshan) जाता हूँ uses the same shape as मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ, which you have been saying since the early chapters — the same frame, a different word inside it.

What you've built.
Five: गाड़ी (gāṛī), रेल (rel), स्टेशन (sṭeshan), टिकट (ṭikaṭ), जाना (jānā). A way to travel and a way to say you are going.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: jānā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṁ sṭeshan jātā hūṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: टिकट (ṭikaṭ) and then जाना (jānā), both from memory]
[your turn — say: all five in order]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does जाना (jānā) mean? ("to go".) Say that you go to the station.
