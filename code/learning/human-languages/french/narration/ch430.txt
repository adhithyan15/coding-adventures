French, chapter 430: Bestioles et volets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la punaise — the bedbug; the drawing pin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the fitted carpet, then the French for the stain.

You'll want to know: la punaise.
la punaise — "the bedbug; the drawing pin".
Elle a trouvé des punaises dans le matelas. She found bedbugs in the mattress.
Une punaise de lit — a bedbug; in an office, une punaise is a drawing pin.

The word, taken apart.
punaise is built on the old adjective punais 'stinking' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin putēre 'to stink'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
le cafard — the cockroach; the blues.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the stain, then the French for the bedbug.

You'll want to know: le cafard.
le cafard — "the cockroach; the blues".
Il y avait un cafard dans la salle de bains. There was a cockroach in the bathroom.
Avoir le cafard — to feel low.

The word, taken apart.
cafard is usually traced to Arabic kāfir 'unbeliever', first an insult for a two-faced person; the path to the insect is not settled. EtymDB lists the word without a source.

moustiquaire is built on moustique 'mosquito', from Spanish mosquito 'little fly', which goes back to Latin musca 'fly' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le volet — the shutter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cockroach, then the French for the mosquito net.

You'll want to know: le volet.
le volet — "the shutter".
Le matin, elle ouvre les volets de la chambre. In the morning she opens the bedroom shutters.
Fermer les volets — to close the shutters.

The word, taken apart.
volet is built on voler 'to fly' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin volāre: a panel that swings open like a wing.
