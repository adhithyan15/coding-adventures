# शुभ दुपार (śubh dupār) — "good afternoon"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-SHUBH, MR-PHRASE-SHUBH-SAKAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *good morning*. Now say the part of the day that comes after the
morning, and put the same word in front of it before you read on.

## You'll want to know: शुभ दुपार
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-PHRASE-SHUBH-DUPAR]; assesses=[MR-LEX-DUPAR] -->

> **शुभ दुपार.**
> — *śubh dupār.* — **good afternoon.**

If you built that yourself in the warm-up, the lesson has already done its job.
**शुभ** does not change for the word behind it, and the word behind it is one
you have had since the parts of the day.

| | |
|---|---|
| **शुभ सकाळ** | good morning |
| **शुभ दुपार** | good afternoon |

## Grammar Lens: a pattern you can finish alone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-PHRASE-SHUBH-DUPAR, MR-PHRASE-SHUBH-SAKAL, MR-LEX-SHUBH] -->

Two rows are enough to see the shape: **शुभ** plus a part of the day.

**You can already say the evening greeting**, and the next lesson will only
confirm what you worked out rather than teach it to you. That is what a system
is worth — the words stop arriving one at a time.

This one is also the **least used** of the set in speech, for the same reason
the last lesson gave: there is a greeting that covers the whole day and Marathi
speakers reach for it.
