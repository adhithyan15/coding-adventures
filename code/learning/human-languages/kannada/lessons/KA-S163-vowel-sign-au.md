# ◌ೌ — the mark in the third word you learned

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-162] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say yes in Kannada. That word has carried today's mark since chapter one.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ೌ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-163]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ೌ** — *au*.

It is a **vowel sign** — it cannot stand alone, it rides on a consonant and changes the vowel that consonant carries.

- **ಹ** plus **◌ೌ** gives **ಹೌ** — the first syllable of *haudu*

Like *ai*, this vowel **moves while you say it**: it starts low and slides
toward *u*. Two sounds in one mark, which is what the romanization's two letters
are showing.

Every vowel in this script has two forms — a letter for the front of a word, a
sign for inside one. This one's letter form is the single independent vowel
whose stroke order this project has no source for, so the chapter that taught
you to write the vowel letters stopped one short. **The sign is what a reader
actually meets on these pages**, and it is the form this book can hand over
honestly.

You already say these:

- **ಹೌದು** *haudu* — yes
- **ಗೌರವ** *gaurava* — respect
- **ಸೌಜನ್ಯ** *saujanya* — courtesy

## Writing: ◌ೌ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-163] -->

Put your pen on ◌ೌ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
