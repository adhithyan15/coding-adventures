Mandarin Chinese, chapter 412: Harbour and Deck.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gǎngkǒu — port; harbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a ferry across the water, then the Mandarin for a life jacket.

You'll want to know: gǎngkǒu.
gǎngkǒu — "port; harbour".
zhège gǎngkǒu hěn dà — This harbour is very big.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: gǎng is said with a rising tone before kǒu, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
gǎngkǒu is built from gǎng 'a harbour' and 口 'a mouth', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaewngX and khuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a branch of a river.

Lesson 2 of 5.
jiǎbǎn — ship's deck.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a life jacket, then the Mandarin for a harbour for ships.

You'll want to know: jiǎbǎn.
jiǎbǎn — "ship's deck".
wǒmen qù jiǎbǎn kàn dàhǎi — Let's go up on deck and look at the sea.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: jiǎ is said with a rising tone before bǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎbǎn is built from jiǎ 'a shell, armour' and bǎn 'a board', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaep and paenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as armour; how the pair came to mean a ship's deck is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
chuáncāng — ship's cabin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a harbour for ships, then the Mandarin for the ship's deck.

You'll want to know: chuáncāng.
chuáncāng — "ship's cabin".
nǐ de bāo fàng zài chuáncāng lǐ — Leave your bag in the cabin.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chuáncāng is built from 船 'a boat' and cāng 'a cabin'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese zywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
kào'àn — to come in to shore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the ship's deck, then the Mandarin for a ship's cabin.

You'll want to know: kào'àn.
kào'àn — "to come in to shore".
chuán mǎshàng yào kào'àn le — The boat is about to come in.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kào'àn is built from kào 'to lean against' and àn 'a shore', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khawH and nganH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the high bank of a river; the sense 'to lean on' for the first is not given there.
