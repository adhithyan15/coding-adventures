Japanese, chapter 462: Win, Lose or Tie.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょうぶ (shōbu) — a contest; a match.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for gymnastics, then the Japanese for a team manager.

You'll want to know: しょうぶ.
しょうぶ — shōbu — "a contest; a match".
あには おとうとと しょうぶを しました。 — ani wa otōto to shōbu o shimashita — "My elder brother had a contest with my younger brother."

The word, taken apart.
しょうぶ (shōbu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "victory" (Middle Chinese syingH) and bu, "defeat" (bjuwX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ひきわけ (hikiwake) — a tie (in a game).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a team manager, then the Japanese for a contest.

You'll want to know: ひきわけ.
ひきわけ — hikiwake — "a tie (in a game)".
しあいは ひきわけでした。 — shiai wa hikiwake deshita — "The match was a tie."

The word, taken apart.
ひきわけ (hikiwake) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: hiki, "pulling (hiku)" and wake, "dividing (wakeru)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
