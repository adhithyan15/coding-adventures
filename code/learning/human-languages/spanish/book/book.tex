\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This book exists because two ordinary language-learning facts turned out to
be more interesting than they first looked. First: a huge fraction of
Spanish vocabulary is not arbitrary --- it is Latin, lightly worn down by a
thousand years of use, and English borrowed from the same Latin source
often enough that the two languages are full of hidden cousins. Once you
see \emph{gracias} next to Latin \emph{gratia} next to English
\emph{grace}, \emph{gratitude}, and \emph{gratuity}, you stop memorizing
words and start recognizing them. Second: a word can be a compressed
phrase --- \emph{usted} is what is left of \emph{vuestra merced}, ``your
grace,'' worn down over centuries of use into a single pronoun. Watching
that compression happen, word by word, is its own kind of history lesson.

This book is organized the way a systematic grammar course is organized ---
present tense before past tense, one new grammatical idea at a time, small
pieces building on small pieces --- but every chapter also does two things
most grammar books don't: it traces vocabulary back to its Latin (and
occasionally Arabic) roots, and it explains every new grammatical concept
in plain language first, assuming no prior grammar terminology, because
that is exactly how this book's first reader learned English: by
immersion, not by formal instruction.

A companion set of practice units --- the same material broken into
5-minute, spaced-repetition-scheduled pieces meant to be worked through
during a daily commute --- lives alongside this book in the same repository.
This book is the continuous read; the units are the practice.

This is a living document. It grows one chapter at a time, and it is
released under CC~BY-SA~4.0 so that if it turns out useful to anyone else
learning Spanish this way, they are free to use it, adapt it, and pass it
on.
