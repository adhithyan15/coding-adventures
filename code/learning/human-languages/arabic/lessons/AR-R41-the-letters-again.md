# Why you have to ask for it back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W06-HARAKAT-01, AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Name the three short-vowel marks and say whether ordinary text prints
them. Then say what this chapter taught you to do when a word arrives without
enough of it to go on.

## Script: one bowl, four letters
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-01, AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-02, AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-03] -->

| letter | dots |
|---|---|
| **ب** *bāʾ* | one below |
| **ن** *nūn* | one above |
| **ت** *tāʾ* | two above |
| **ث** *thāʾ* | three above |

One shallow boat-bowl, four letters. The dots **are** the letter, so a smudge or a
bad line is not a small error — it is a different word. Two of the four build
**أنت**: hamza-seated *alif*, then *nūn*, then *tāʾ*.

## Script: the letter with three jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-01, AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-02, AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-03] -->

**ي** *yāʾ* is the same bowl with **two dots below** — a tooth when joined — and it
does three jobs at once:

- the consonant **y**
- the long vowel **ī**
- the suffix **ـي**, meaning **my**

- [YOU WRITE: **اسم**, then **اسمي** — *alif · sīn · mīm*, then the *yāʾ*]

That one added letter is the whole difference between *a name* and *my name*, and
it is the third job doing the work.

## Grammar Lens: one unwritten vowel, two different words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W06-HARAKAT-01, AR-CONCEPT-C36-MILAQA-02] -->

The **ḥarakāt** are three marks written around a consonant, not letters: *fatḥa*
above for *a*, *kasra* below for *i*, *ḍamma* above for *u*. Arabic is an abjad, so
everyday text leaves them out — and here is what that costs.

| shape | makes | example |
|---|---|---|
| **مَـ** *ma-* | a **place** | *maktab*, an office |
| **مِـ** *mi-* | a **tool** | **ملعقة** *milʿaqa*, a spoon |

A place and a tool are separated by **one mark that nobody prints**. **ملعقة** is
the licking-tool, from the root **ل-ع-ق**, and the only thing marking it as a tool
rather than a licking-place is a vowel you have to know.

This is the honest reason the repair kit exists. When the vowels are missing and
the context is thin, a reader guesses — and a listener asks.
