Marathi, chapter 278: Was Doing, While, Used To.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
मी वाचत होतो (mī vācat hoto) — what was going on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I won't come tomorrow". (mī udyā yeṇār nāhī.) Then "I was at home" and "I read a book". (mī gharī hoto, mī pustak vāclaṁ.)
[pause 1 second]
The last is an event: it happened and it was over. A story also needs what was going on at the time.

You'll want to know: -त होतो, 'was doing'.
काल रात्री नऊ वाजता मी पुस्तक वाचत होतो. kāl rātrī naū vājtā mī pustak vācat hoto. — "At nine last night I was reading a book."
Two pieces. वाचत is the bare -त shape you know from आवडत नाही; होतो, "I was", carries the person and the gender:
ती वाचत होती (vācat hotī) — she was reading.
तो काम करत होता (kām karat hotā) — he was working.
आम्ही बोलत होतो (bolat hoto) — we were talking.
पाऊस पडत होता (pāūs paḍat hotā) — it was raining.
This past follows the doer even with an object: ती पुस्तक वाचत होती. No -ने here.

Grammar Lens: the scene and the event.
मी वाचत होतो. तेव्हा मीरा आली. mī vācat hoto. tevhā mīrā ālī. — "I was reading. At that moment, Meera came."
The first sentence is the scene: it was going on. आली is the event: it happened once and cut into the scene. तेव्हा, the "then" of जेव्हा … तेव्हा, hooks one onto the other. A told story moves on this rhythm.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "She was reading" — tī vācat hotī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It was raining" — pāūs paḍat hotā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was reading. At that moment, Meera came." — mī vācat hoto. tevhā mīrā ālī.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that it was raining, then that you were reading when Meera came.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I was reading"? (मी वाचत होतो (mī vācat hoto).) "It was raining"? (पाऊस पडत होता.) Which past is the scene? (-त होतो.) Which is the event? (The plain past: आली.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
असताना (astānā) — while it was going on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was reading". (mī vācat hoto.) Then the two-part "when … then" frame. (jevhā … tevhā.)

You'll want to know: असताना, 'while'.
मी वाचत असताना मीरा आली. mī vācat astānā mīrā ālī. — "While I was reading, Meera came."
असताना is असणे, "to be", with -ताना, "while being". Put it after the -त shape, and the scene needs no second clause: मी वाचत असताना. It follows a place or a state too: मी घरी असताना (mī gharī astānā), "while I was at home"; लहान असताना (lahān astānā), "when (I was) small".
It does not change for person or gender. The event after it does: मीरा आली.

You'll want to know: जेव्हा … तेव्हा with the past.
मी घरी आलो, तेव्हा आई चहा करत होती. mī gharī ālo, tevhā āī cahā karat hotī. — "When I came home, Mother was making tea."
जेव्हा can stay or go: with the past, the first clause alone sets the time, and तेव्हा picks it up.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "While I was reading, Meera came" — mī vācat astānā mīrā ālī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "While I was at home, it was raining" — mī gharī astānā pāūs paḍat hotā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When I came home, Mother was making tea" — mī gharī ālo, tevhā āī cahā karat hotī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that Meera came while you were reading.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"While I was reading"? (मी वाचत असताना (astānā).) "When (I was) small"? (लहान असताना (astānā).) Does असताना (astānā) change for "she"? (No.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
मी जायचो (mī jāyco) — what used to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "when (I was) small". (lahān astānā.) Then "formerly". (pūrvī.)

You'll want to know: -ायचो, 'used to'.
लहान असताना मी रोज नदीवर जायचो. lahān astānā mī roj nadīvar jāyco. — "When I was small, I used to go to the river every day."
Take -णे off and put -ायच- on, then the endings of गेलो:
मी जायचो (mī jāyco) (jāyco) — I used to go; a woman says जायचे (jāyce).
तो जायचा (jāycā), ती जायची (jāycī) — he, she used to go.
आम्ही जायचो, ते जायचे — we, they used to go.
करणे becomes करायचो (karāyco); राहणे becomes राहायचो (rāhāyco): पूर्वी मी पुण्यात राहायचो (pūrvī mī puṇyāt rāhāyco), "I used to live in Pune". With an object it follows the doer: मी पुस्तक वाचायचो (mī pustak vācāyco).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "When I was small, I used to go to the river every day" — lahān astānā mī roj nadīvar jāyco]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Formerly she used to live in Pune" — pūrvī tī puṇyāt rāhāycī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We used to read books" — āmhī pustakaṁ vācāyco]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
पुस्तकं (pustakaṁ, "books").
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you used to go to the river every day when you were small.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I used to go"? (जायचो, or जायचे.) "She used to live"? (राहायची.) Which past is a habit? (-ायचो.)
