# स्वागतम् — the door, the seat, and the promise at the end

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-CULTURE-WELCOMING-A-GUEST, SA-CULTURE-SANSKRIT-ANSWERING] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The word you say to someone standing at your door. Then the one
syllable that agrees to whatever they ask next.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-ASMI-FIRST-PERSON, SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-THREE-PERSONS, SA-GRAMMAR-PRONOUN-DROPPING, SA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-OF-PURPOSE, SA-GRAMMAR-ONE-WORD-ONE-DAY] -->

[PAUSE 1s] A whole visit, in the order it happens. The nouns around it — the
door, the seat, the flower, the garland, the cupped hands — are this chapter's.
Everything you say is older.

- [YOU SAY: welcome them in — *svāgatam*]
- [YOU SAY: ask how they are, respectfully — *bhavān kathaṁ asti?*]
- [YOU SAY: answer for yourself, with no pronoun — *kuśalī asmi*]
- [YOU SAY: agree to something — *ām*]
- [YOU SAY: say when — *śvaḥ*]
- [YOU SAY: close it — *punar darśanāya*]

## Grammar Lens: three endings doing three jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-THREE-PERSONS, SA-GRAMMAR-PRONOUN-DROPPING, SA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-OF-PURPOSE] -->

| what you said | what the ending did |
|---|---|
| *asti* | put the sentence in the **third** person, because *bhavān* is a third-person word |
| *asmi* | carried **I** by itself, so the pronoun could go |
| *darśanāya* | turned *seeing* into **a purpose to act for** |

The last of those is the one with no English shape behind it. *Punar darśanāya*
is not a sentence and has no verb: it is *again* plus *seeing* wearing an ending
that means **for the sake of**. English needs a clause — *until we meet again* —
where Sanskrit needs an ending.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-OF-PURPOSE, SA-GRAMMAR-ONE-WORD-ONE-DAY, SA-CULTURE-WELCOMING-A-GUEST] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What does the ending on *darśanāya* add? (**For the sake of** —
purpose.) How many words does Sanskrit need for *tomorrow*? (**One** — *śvaḥ*.)
Is welcoming a guest in a pleasantry or a duty? (**A duty.**)
