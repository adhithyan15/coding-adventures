Sanskrit, chapter 250: Fort, Palace, Hut, Building, Lodging.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दुर्गम् (durgam) — a fort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a film, a painted cloth, then the Sanskrit for a game.

You'll want to know: दुर्गम्.
दुर्गम् — durgam — "a fort".

The word, taken apart.
durga is built on dus-, "hard", and -ga, "going", from the root √gam, "to go" (MW): "difficult of access", and so a fort. In the Rigveda it means "a difficult passage, danger" (CDIAL 6429). The goddess Durgā is "the hard to reach".

You'll want to know: प्रासादः.
प्रासादः — prāsādaḥ — "a palace".

The word, taken apart.
prāsāda is built on pra, "forward", and the root √sad, "to sit" (MW): "a lofty seat or terrace" where one sits in view, and later a lofty mansion or palace (CDIAL 8971, which marks the root with a question mark). It is a different word from prasāda, "favour".

You'll want to know: कुटीरम्.
कुटीरम् — kuṭīram — "a hut, a cottage".

The word, taken apart.
kuṭīra is built on kuṭī, "hut, cottage" (MW). CDIAL 3232 reports Mayrhofer's view that the word was borrowed from Dravidian. Marathi kuḍī, "hut", comes from kuṭī.

Lesson 4 of 5.
भवनम् (bhavanam) — a building, a house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a palace, then the Sanskrit for a hut.

You'll want to know: भवनम्.
भवनम् — bhavanam — "a building, a house".

The word, taken apart.
bhavana is built on the root √bhū, "to be, become" (MW), the root of bhavati: a house is the place where one is. Manu uses it for "a place of abode" (CDIAL 9417), and Pali has bhavana, "dwelling".

Lesson 5 of 5.
आवासः (āvāsaḥ) — lodging, a residence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a hut, then the Sanskrit for a building.

You'll want to know: आवासः.
आवासः — āvāsaḥ — "lodging, a residence".

The word, taken apart.
āvāsa is built on ā- and the root √vas, "to dwell" (MW), the root of vasāmi and nivāsa: an abode, a house (CDIAL 1432). Gujarati avās, "abode", comes from it.
