Japanese, chapter 189: Weighing and Handing Over.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
はかる (hakaru) — to weigh; to measure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to send, then the Japanese for registered mail.

You'll want to know: はかる.
はかる — hakaru — "to weigh; to measure".
ゆうびんきょくで にもつを はかります。 — yūbinkyoku de nimotsu o hakarimasu — "They weigh the package at the post office."
Polite はかります, hakarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
はかる (hakaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

You'll want to know: とどける.
とどける — todokeru — "to deliver; to report".
あした にもつを とどけます。 — ashita nimotsu o todokemasu — "I will deliver the package tomorrow."
Polite とどけます, todokemasu; an ichidan verb. Its intransitive partner とどく is taught already.

The word, taken apart.
とどける (todokeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it shares its stem with todoku, "to reach, to arrive" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
おみやげ (omiyage) — souvenir; gift brought back from a trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to accept, then the Japanese for to hand over.

You'll want to know: おみやげ.
おみやげ — omiyage — "souvenir; gift brought back from a trip".
ともだちに おみやげを かいます。 — tomodachi ni omiyage o kaimasu — "I buy a souvenir for a friend."

The word, taken apart.
おみやげ (omiyage) is built from polite o- and native miyage, written with two kanji for "local product" that are read as a whole; the older origin of miyage is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
