German, chapter 456: Im Wald.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Lichtung — the clearing (in a wood).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to splash, then the German for to flow.

You'll want to know: die Lichtung.
die Lichtung — "the clearing (in a wood)".
Auf der Lichtung machen wir ein Picknick. We have a picnic in the clearing.
Plural: die Lichtungen.

The word, taken apart.
Lichtung is built on lichten 'to thin out, to make clear', from licht 'light, open', inherited from Proto-Germanic leuhtą 'light' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Gebüsch — the bushes, the undergrowth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to flow, then the German for the clearing.

You'll want to know: das Gebüsch.
das Gebüsch — "the bushes, the undergrowth".
Ein Kaninchen versteckt sich im Gebüsch. A rabbit hides in the bushes.
Plural: die Gebüsche. One bush is der Busch.

The word, taken apart.
Gebüsch is built from ge-, which groups things together, and Busch 'bush', inherited from Old High German busk (EtymDB); English bush is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: der Farn.
der Farn — "the fern".
Unter den Bäumen wächst grüner Farn. Green ferns grow under the trees.
Plural: die Farne.

The word, taken apart.
Farn is an inherited Germanic word; EtymDB lists English fern and Dutch varen as cousins and links them to an old word for a feather or wing.

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Moos — the moss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bushes, then the German for the fern.

You'll want to know: das Moos.
das Moos — "the moss".
Die alten Steine sind voll mit Moos. The old stones are covered in moss.
Plural: die Moose. In slang, Moos also means money.

The word, taken apart.
Moos is inherited through Middle High German mos from Old High German mos (EtymDB), which also meant a bog; Old English mos 'bog, moss' is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rascheln — to rustle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: rascheln.
rascheln — "to rustle".
Im Herbst raschelt das Laub unter unseren Füßen. In autumn the leaves rustle under our feet.
Es raschelt im Gebüsch — something is rustling in the bushes.

The word, taken apart.
rascheln is a sound word that imitates the noise of dry leaves or paper (EtymDB lists the sense 'to rustle').
