Japanese, chapter 84: What kind of, Other, Everybody, This sort of, That sort of.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
どんな (donna) — what kind of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for this kind of, then the Japanese for that kind of.

You'll want to know: どんな.
どんな — donna — "what kind of".

The word, taken apart.
どんな (donna) is native, built on do-, the root of "which"; it is usually explained as a shortening of dono yō na, "of which kind". It is inherited rather than borrowed and has no kanji (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ほか (hoka) — other, another.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for that kind of, then the Japanese for what kind of.

You'll want to know: ほか.
ほか — hoka — "other, another".

The word, taken apart.
ほか (hoka) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: みんな.
みんな — minna — "everybody".

The word, taken apart.
みんな (minna) is native: a spoken form of mina, "all", with a nasal beat added; both are written with the same character (JMdict). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
こういう (kouiu) — this sort of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for other, then the Japanese for everybody.

You'll want to know: こういう.
こういう — kouiu — "this sort of".

The word, taken apart.
こういう (kouiu) (kōiu) is native: kō, "like this", built on ko-, with iu, "to say", so "that one would call like this" (JMdict). It is inherited rather than borrowed.
