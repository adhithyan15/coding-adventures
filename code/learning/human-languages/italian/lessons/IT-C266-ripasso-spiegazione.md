# Review — explain yourself

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-DOVREI-01, IT-ETYMON-DEBERE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "you should sleep" to a friend. (*Dovresti dormire.*) Which
English word for owing shares its Latin? (**Debt**, or **due**, or **duty**.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SPIEGAZIONE-01, IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01, IT-LEX-ERA-01, IT-LEX-FACEVA-01] -->

Two chapters, four moves. You can say what you think, why, what follows from
it, and what ought to be done. And because chapter two hundred and sixty-three
gave you the scene, your reasons can reach into the past:

> — Perché non sei andato al mercato?
> — Faceva freddo ed ero stanco, quindi sono restato a casa. Secondo me
> dovrei uscire di più.

The reason is scene, *faceva* and *ero*; the consequence is an event, *sono
restato*; the advice is about now. Three times in one answer.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SPIEGAZIONE-01, IT-ETYMON-SPIEGARE-02, IT-LEX-DOVREI-01, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] In *faceva freddo, quindi sono restato a casa*, which half is the
scene and which the event? (***Faceva freddo*** is the scene; ***sono
restato*** is the event.) What does *spiegare* do, literally? (It **unfolds**.)
And what is softer than *devo*? (***Dovrei.***)
