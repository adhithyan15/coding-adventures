Portuguese, chapter 270: Entretanto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
entretanto — meanwhile, in the meantime; however.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for later, then the Portuguese for finally.

You'll want to know: entretanto.
entretanto — "meanwhile, in the meantime; however".
Eu fiz o jantar; entretanto, o meu irmão pôs a mesa. I made dinner; meanwhile my brother set out the plates and glasses.
In formal writing it can also mean ‘however’; in Brazil that is the main sense.

The word, taken apart.
entretanto joins entre "between" and tanto "so much", from Latin inter "between" and tantum "so much" (Lewis & Short): the time in between.

You'll want to know: simultaneamente.
simultaneamente — "simultaneously, at the same time".
Os dois telefones tocaram simultaneamente. The two phones rang at the same time.
Built on simultâneo, ‘happening together’.

The word, taken apart.
simultaneamente is built on simultâneo, a learned word from Medieval Latin simultāneus, which rests on Latin simul "at the same time, together" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
assim que — as soon as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for meanwhile, then the Portuguese for simultaneously.

You'll want to know: assim que.
assim que — "as soon as".
Assim que cheguei a casa, começou a chover. As soon as I got home, it started to rain.
Joins two events that follow each other at once. About the future it takes the subjunctive: assim que puderes.

The word, taken apart.
assim is usually traced to Latin ad sīc "so, in this way"; EtymDB lists it beside Spanish así, from Latin sīc "so, thus" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
logo que — the moment that, as soon as (formal).

You'll want to know: logo que.
logo que — "the moment that, as soon as (formal)".
Logo que o filme acabou, as pessoas saíram. The moment the film ended, people left.
A little more formal than assim que.

The word, taken apart.
logo is inherited from Latin locō "in the place, on the spot" (EtymDB), from locus "place": on the spot, at once.

Lesson 5 of 5.
subitamente — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for as soon as, then the Portuguese for the moment that.

You'll want to know: subitamente.
subitamente — "suddenly".
Subitamente, as luzes apagaram-se. Suddenly the lights went out.
More formal than de repente; storytellers like it.

The word, taken apart.
subitamente is built on súbito "sudden", a learned borrowing from Latin subitus "sudden, unexpected" (Lewis & Short), with the adverb ending -mente.
