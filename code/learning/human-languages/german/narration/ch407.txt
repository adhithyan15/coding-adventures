German, chapter 407: Zu schnell.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rasen — to speed, to race along.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the multi-storey car park, then the German for the parking ticket.

You'll want to know: rasen.
rasen — "to speed, to race along".
Auf der Landstraße rast ein junger Mann an uns vorbei. On the country road a young man races past us.
Perfekt with sein when someone moves fast: er ist gerast.

The word, taken apart.
rasen is an old Germanic verb that first meant 'to rage, to be wild' (EtymDB lists 'to rush, to rage'); its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Blitzer — the speed camera (colloquial).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the parking ticket, then the German for to speed.

You'll want to know: der Blitzer.
der Blitzer — "the speed camera (colloquial)".
Vor der Schule steht ein Blitzer. There is a speed camera in front of the school.
Same form in the plural. Everyday speech; officially die Geschwindigkeitskontrolle.

The word, taken apart.
Blitzer is built on blitzen 'to flash', from Blitz 'lightning', inherited from Middle High German blitze (EtymDB): the camera flashes when a car is too fast.

Lesson 3 of 5.
erwischen — to catch (someone at something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to speed, then the German for the speed camera.

You'll want to know: erwischen.
erwischen — "to catch (someone at something)".
Der Blitzer hat ihn mit 80 erwischt. The speed camera caught him doing 80.
Everyday word. Den Bus gerade noch erwischen — to manage to catch the bus.

The word, taken apart.
erwischen is usually explained as built from er- and wischen 'to wipe, to make a quick movement', inherited from Middle High German wischen (EtymDB lists wischen): first a quick grab.

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Bußgeld — the fine (for a traffic offence).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the speed camera, then the German for to catch.

You'll want to know: das Bußgeld.
das Bußgeld — "the fine (for a traffic offence)".
Das Bußgeld für zu schnelles Fahren ist hoch. The fine for driving too fast is high.
Plural: die Bußgelder. Der Bußgeldbescheid is the letter that comes in the post.

The word, taken apart.
Bußgeld is built from Buße 'amends, penance', inherited from Old High German buoza (EtymDB), and Geld 'money', also an inherited Germanic word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Strafzettel — the penalty ticket, the fine slip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to catch, then the German for the fine.

You'll want to know: der Strafzettel.
der Strafzettel — "the penalty ticket, the fine slip".
Am Abend findet sie einen Strafzettel am Auto. In the evening she finds a ticket on the car.
Same form in the plural. In everyday speech also das Knöllchen.

The word, taken apart.
Strafzettel is built from Strafe 'punishment, fine', inherited from Middle High German strāfe (EtymDB), and Zettel 'slip of paper', which goes back to Medieval Latin cedula (EtymDB).
