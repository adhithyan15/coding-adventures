\chapter*{Devanagari for Marathi --- a reference}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Devanagari for Marathi (reference)}
\markboth{Devanagari}{Devanagari}

Not to be read before the lessons --- they teach reading through real words. This
page gathers the system, and flags what is special about \emph{Marathi's}
Devanagari.

\section*{The three facts}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Consonants carry a built-in ``a.''} \mr{क} ka, \mr{न} na,
    \mr{म} ma.
  \item \textbf{A vowel sign (\emph{m\=atr\=a}) changes it}: \mr{का} k\=a,
    \mr{कि} ki, \mr{की} k\=\i, \mr{कु} ku, \mr{के} ke, \mr{को} ko, \mr{कौ} kau.
  \item \textbf{A \emph{halant} removes it}, and the bare consonant joins the
    next as a conjunct: \mr{स्} $+$ \mr{का} $\to$ \mr{स्का}; \mr{न्} $+$ \mr{य}
    $\to$ \mr{न्य}. The \emph{anusv\=ara} dot \mr{ं} nasalises a vowel
    (\mr{बरं} \emph{bar\d{n}}).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What is special about Marathi's Devanagari}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The extra letter \mr{ळ}} (\emph{\d{l}a}) --- a \textbf{retroflex
    l}, made with the tongue curled back. Hindi does \emph{not} have it; Marathi
    shares it with the Dravidian languages to the south (Tamil \mr{}\emph{ḷ},
    Kannada, etc.), a mark of centuries of contact. It appears in common words
    like \emph{k\=a\d{l}} (``time'') and \emph{ph\=a\d{l}} (``fruit'').
  \item \textbf{The vowel \mr{ॲ} / \mr{ऍ}} for the English ``a'' of \emph{cat} ---
    added for English loanwords (\emph{b\d{n}k}, ``bank'').
  \item \textbf{Three genders.} Marathi keeps Sanskrit's masculine, feminine,
    \emph{and} neuter, where Hindi collapsed to two --- and it marks gender on
    verbs and adjectives more thoroughly than Hindi.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Marathi in the family}

Marathi is \textbf{Indo-Aryan} (like Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali --- descended from
Sanskrit), spoken in Maharashtra. Its Devanagari and much of its formal
vocabulary are shared with Hindi and Sanskrit; its sound system, its extra
letter \mr{ळ}, and parts of its grammar bear the imprint of long contact with
Dravidian. The lessons trace both threads.
