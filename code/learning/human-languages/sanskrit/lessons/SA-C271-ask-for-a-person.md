# Ask for a familiar person

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C14-DEIXIS-06, SA-LEX-ASTI, SA-LEX-C83-SCHOOL-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall three known pieces: **अध्यापकः**, “teacher”; **कुत्र**,
“where?”; and **अस्ति**, “is.”

## Grammar Lens: build the person request

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01]; assesses=[] -->

Put the familiar person first, then the familiar location question:

> **अध्यापकः कुत्र अस्ति?**
>
> *adhyāpakaḥ kutra asti?*
>
> “Where is the teacher?”

Nothing in this turn is a new noun or question word. The new skill is the
complete turn: asking where a familiar person is so you can speak with them.

## Guided Practice

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] You arrive at school and need the teacher. Ask where the teacher is.

## What you've built

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01] -->

**अध्यापकः कुत्र अस्ति?** (*adhyāpakaḥ kutra asti?*) is one bounded way to ask
for a familiar person. It does not establish a general service-counter script.
