## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C303-REPAIR-02]; assesses=[] -->

**el momento** — "a moment."

Latin **momentum** came from *movere*, "to move," and it did not mean time at
all. It meant the **smallest movement that tips a balance** — the last grain
that makes one pan drop.

That is a weight, not a duration. But the tipping happens in an instant, and the
word slid from the movement to the instant. English kept the original sense in
*momentum* and the borrowed sense in *moment*.

*Un momento, por favor* buys you that instant — and it is the politest thing you
can say when you need to think.
