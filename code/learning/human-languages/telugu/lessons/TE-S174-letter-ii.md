# ఈ (ī) — the letter that opens ఈలోగా

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-135, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, TE-LEX-C317-ILOGA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write **ఇ** and **ీ** once each, and name them. (Short *i* at the start of a word;
long *ī* after a consonant, as in **నీళ్ళు**.)

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ఈలోగా** (*īlōgā*), "in the meantime". You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes its first
letter out and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ఈ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-174]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C317-ILOGA-01] -->

**ఈ** — *ī*, a long *ī* at the start of a word.

After a consonant, long *ī* is the sign **ీ**: **నీళ్ళు**, *nīḷḷu*. At the start of a word there is no consonant
to carry a sign, so the vowel stands as a letter of its own, **ఈ**.

**ఈ** is not **ఇ** with a mark added: it has a shape of its own. In print it is a round body with a straight bar
across it, a short hook rising on the left, a long stroke sweeping up to the right, and a small curl standing
beside it on the right.

On its own, **ఈ** is also a word: "this", in front of a noun. **ఈ పుస్తకం** is "this book"; **ఆ పుస్తకం** is
"that book".

## Writing: ఈ — copy the printed shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-174] -->

Put your pen beside **ఈ** and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Keep the bar straight and the
little curl on the right separate from the body.

> This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.
