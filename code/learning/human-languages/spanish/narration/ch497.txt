Spanish, chapter 497: Una vida.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nacer — to be born.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to take away, then the Spanish for to fill.

You'll want to know: nacer.
nacer — "to be born".
Nací en Sevilla. I was born in Seville.
It takes a z in the first person present: nazco.

The word, taken apart.
nacer from Latin nasci, "to be born" (Lewis & Short) — inherited, and remade as an ordinary -er verb.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fallecer — to pass away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to fill, then the Spanish for to be born.

You'll want to know: fallecer.
fallecer — "to pass away".
Su abuelo falleció el año pasado. His grandfather passed away last year.
A gentler, more formal word than morir.

The word, taken apart.
fallecer comes from spoken Latin fallescere (EtymDB 2.1), built on fallere, "to deceive, to fail" — first "to run short", then "to die". Fallar, "to fail", is a relative.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cuidar — to look after, to take care of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: cuidar.
cuidar — "to look after, to take care of".
Cuido a mi hermano pequeño. I look after my little brother.
The verb behind el cuidado, care.

The word, taken apart.
cuidar from Latin cogitare, "to think" (EtymDB 2.1), through Old Spanish coidar, "to think, to be concerned" — inherited. Caring for someone began as thinking about them.
