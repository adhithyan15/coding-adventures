Arabic, chapter 292: Would, And Six Lines About A Trip.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اعتمد (iʿtamada) — to depend on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if it rains, we'll watch a film". (Idhā amṭarat, fa-sa-nushāhidu fīlman.)
[pause 1 second]
Sometimes you cannot give the result yet, because it hangs on something else. This is the verb for hanging on something.

You'll want to know: اعتمد.
اعتمد — iʿtamada — "he depended, he relied", with على, ʿalā, "on".
اعتمدت على صديقي. — iʿtamadtu ʿalā ṣadīqī — I relied on my friend.
The present is yaʿtamidu, he depends; aʿtamidu ʿalayka, I'm counting on you. It has the same shape as iʿtaqada, to believe, and its present aʿtaqidu: one letter apart.

The word, taken apart.
اعتمد (iʿtamada) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-m-d, "to prop up, to support" (Lane). Lane gives iʿtamadtu ʿalā ash-shayʾ as leaning or resting on a thing, and then relying on a person. To depend on something, in Arabic, is to lean your weight on it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: iʿtamada, then yaʿtamidu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I relied on my friend" — iʿtamadtu ʿalā ṣadīqī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'm counting on you" — aʿtamidu ʿalayka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We don't depend on money" — lā naʿtamidu ʿalā al-māl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does اعتمد (iʿtamada) mean? ("To depend on, to rely on".) Which little word goes with it? (ʿAlā, on.) Which verb has the same shape? (Iʿtaqada, to believe.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
يعتمد على (yaʿtamidu ʿalā) — it depends on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I relied on my friend". (Iʿtamadtu ʿalā ṣadīqī.) Then "if it rains, we won't go out". (Idhā amṭarat, fa-lan nakhruja.)
[pause 1 second]
Someone asks about your plan, and you do not know yet. That is an answer too.

Grammar Lens: it depends on.
— هل ستسافر غدا؟ — hal sa-tusāfiru ghadan? — Are you travelling tomorrow? — يعتمد على الطقس. — yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs — It depends on the weather.
"It" is not said: the ya- of yaʿtamidu carries it. Put the thing the plan hangs on after ʿalā: yaʿtamidu ʿalā al-waqt, it depends on the time; yaʿtamidu ʿalayka, it's up to you.
It answers the same need as a condition with idhā, but leaves the condition open: idhā kāna aṭ-ṭaqs jamīlan, fa-sa-usāfiru says which way it goes; yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs only says what decides.

Grammar Lens: ḥasaba, according to.
Ḥasaba, from chapter two hundred and sixty-eight, puts the deciding thing in front of a plain statement:
حسب الجدول، ينطلق القطار بعد عشر دقائق. — ḥasaba al-jadwal, yanṭaliqu al-qiṭār baʿda ʿashr daqāʾiq — According to the timetable, the train leaves in ten minutes.
Ḥasaba aṭ-ṭaqs is "depending on the weather": sa-nusāfiru bi-l-qiṭār aw bi-l-ḥāfila, ḥasaba aṭ-ṭaqs.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Are you travelling tomorrow?" … "It depends on the weather" — hal sa-tusāfiru ghadan? … yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It's up to you" — yaʿtamidu ʿalayka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "According to the timetable, the train leaves in ten minutes"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We'll travel by train or by bus, depending on the weather" — sa-nusāfiru bi-l-qiṭār aw bi-l-ḥāfila, ḥasaba aṭ-ṭaqs]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on the weather"? (Yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs.) "According to the timetable"? (Ḥasaba al-jadwal.) What does yaʿtamidu leave open that idhā closes? (Which way it goes.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
لو (law) — if it were so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it depends on the weather", then "according to the timetable". (Yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs, ḥasaba al-jadwal.)
[pause 1 second]
Idhā is for what may well happen. For what is not so, and you only imagine, Arabic has a third "if".

Grammar Lens: law, and la- on the result.
لو كان عندي وقت لسافرت. — law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu — If I had time, I would travel.
لو — law — takes the past, and so does the result. The result usually carries لـ — la- — written onto it: la-sāfartu, I would have travelled, I would travel. The meaning is "but I don't have time". That is the difference from idhā: idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, fa-sa-usāfiru leaves it open; law says it is not so.
You have said law since chapter six: law samaḥt, "if you would permit", the polite "please" that does not take the yes for granted.

Grammar Lens: if I were you, and if only.
لو كنت مكانك لذهبت إلى الطبيب. — law kuntu makānaka la-dhahabtu ilā aṭ-ṭabīb — If I were you, I'd go to the doctor.
Word for word, "if I were in your place". ليت — layta, "if only" — wishes without a result: laytanī kuntu ghaniyyan, if only I were rich; laytanī kuntu hunāka, if only I had been there.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: "If I had time, I would travel" — law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the open one, with idhā — idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, fa-sa-usāfiru]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I were you, I'd go to the doctor" — law kuntu makānaka la-dhahabtu ilā aṭ-ṭabīb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If only I were rich" — laytanī kuntu ghaniyyan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a wish of your own with law and la-, then one with atamannā an]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If I had time, I would travel"? (Law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu.) What goes on the result? (La-.) Which "if" says "but it is not so"? (Law.) "If only"? (Layta.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
Review — would, if only, and it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if it rains, we'll watch a film at home". (Idhā amṭarat, fa-sa-nushāhidu fīlman fī al-manzil.) Then "if you arrive early", with the other "if". (In waṣalta mubakkiran.)

What you've built.
Three lessons, and three distances from the facts. Idhā: it may well happen, idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, fa-sa-usāfiru. Yaʿtamidu ʿalā: it is still open, yaʿtamidu ʿalā al-waqt. Law: it is not so, law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu; and layta, the wish with no result.
[pause 2 seconds]
All three, on one question:
— hal sa-tusāfiru? — yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs. law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu al-usbūʿa al-qādim.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It depends on the person in charge" — yaʿtamidu ʿalā al-masʾūl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I were you, I would ask the way" — law kuntu makānaka la-saʾaltu: kayfa aṣilu ilā al-maḥaṭṭa?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You ought to ask the client" — yanbaghī an tasʾala al-ʿamīl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Take the first street on the right" — khudh ash-shāriʿa al-awwala ʿalā al-yamīn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If only the sentence were shorter" — layta al-jumlata kānat aqṣar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it were a new experience, I would try it" — law kānat tajribatan jadīdatan la-jarrabtuhā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one thing you would do law kāna ʿindī waqt, and one you will do idhā something happens]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which "if" says it is not so? (Law.) What goes on its result? (La-.) "It depends on the time"? (Yaʿtamidu ʿalā al-waqt.) "If only"? (Layta.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected Arabic lines to one reader about a trip — a scene, events in order, a reason, a feeling, and a last line with a condition or a wish.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter forty-five asked for four lines that held together. This one asks for six, and they tell a story.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list of events. It has a scene, in kāna and kāna with the present. It has events in the plain past, put in order with awwalan, thumma, ʿindamā and fī an-nihāya. It has at least one reason, with li-anna, bi-sabab or fa-, and a feeling, with kuntu and -an, or shaʿartu bi-.
[pause 2 seconds]
A good one ends by looking forward or wishing: atamannā an …, idhā … fa-sa- …, or law … la- ….
[pause 2 seconds]
At B1 a connected text runs longer than six lines. Six lines is the first step towards it, not the whole of it.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, right to left, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, with qabla or al-māḍī, and the weather, with kāna;
where you went and who was with you, with maʿa, and the ticket: tadhkirat dhahāb wa-iyāb or dhahāb faqaṭ, and from which platform;
the first thing that happened, with awwalan or ʿindamā;
the next thing, with thumma or baʿda an, and a reason, with li-anna, bi-sabab, as-sababu huwa anna or fa-, or what you had to do, with kāna yajibu an;
how it ended and how you felt, with fī an-nihāya and kuntu … -an or shaʿartu bi-;
one line forward: atamannā an, a real idhā, or law … la-.
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing the right past for each line, and joining them.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines as you read the trip to the sea in chapter two hundred and eighty-four. Mark every verb S for scene or E for event. Line one should be mostly S; lines three and four mostly E. A line about the weather marked E is the commonest slip: change amṭarat back to kāna aṭ-ṭaqs mumṭiran if you meant the scene.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then three spelling checks. Every wa- and fa- is joined to the next word, with no space: وكان, فذهبنا. Every -an after kāna on a word without ة has its ا: سعيدا. And each hamza sits on its seat: سؤال with ؤ, طائرة with ئ.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. If it has idhā, is the verb after it in the past? If it has law, does the result carry la-?
