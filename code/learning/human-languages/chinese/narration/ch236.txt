Lesson 1 of 6.
出现 (chūxiàn) — to appear; to turn up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have no choice but, then the Mandarin for no wonder.

You'll want to know: 出现.
出现 — chūxiàn — "to appear; to turn up".
草地里出现了一个人 cǎodì lǐ chūxiànle yí ge rén — Someone appeared on the lawn.
The new character, in pieces: 现 (xiàn) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left two bars, a vertical and a stroke rising to the right, then 见 on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
出现 (chūxiàn) is built from 出 'to come out' and 现 (xiàn) 'to show', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and henH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to reveal', noting the character as an everyday form.

What you've built.

Script — a narrow left piece, then 见.
现 (xiàn).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, four strokes, narrow: a bar, a second bar, a vertical down through both, then a short stroke rising to the right at the foot.
The right piece, 见, four strokes, as in 再见 and 看见: a vertical, a bar that runs right and turns down, a long stroke falling to the left, then a stroke that bends right along the foot and flicks up.
4 + 4 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 现 (xiàn), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 现 (xiàn)? (Eight.) What is the right piece? (见.) Which way does the last stroke of the left piece go? (It rises to the right.)

You'll want to know: fāxiàn.
fāxiàn — "to discover; to find out".
huí jiā yǐhòu wǒ fāxiàn qiánbāo diū le — When I got home, I found my wallet was gone.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fāxiàn is built from fā 'to send out' and 现 (xiàn) 'to show', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjot and henH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and 'to make clear'.
