Urdu, chapter 368: Getting Out of Trouble.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دقت (diqqat) — a difficulty; a hassle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a clause, then the Urdu for to come into force.

You'll want to know: دقت.
دقت — diqqat — "a difficulty; a hassle".
اگر کوئی دقت ہے تو مجھے بتائیے۔ agar koī diqqat hai to mujhe batāiye. If there is any difficulty, tell me.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
دقت (diqqat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic diqqa, "fineness, exactness"; Urdu uses it for a difficulty or a hassle, a sense our sources do not trace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to come into force, then the Urdu for a hassle.

You'll want to know: رکاوٹ.
رکاوٹ — rukāvaṭ — "an obstacle".
اگر رکاوٹ آئی تو ہم دوسرا راستہ لیں گے۔ agar rukāvaṭ āī to ham dūsrā rāstā leṅ ge. If something blocks us, we will take another route.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رکاوٹ (rukāvaṭ) is built on رکنا, "to stop", which CDIAL lists beside roknā under rokk, "stop", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 10827); -āvaṭ makes the noun: what stops you.

Before the new one: say the Urdu for an obstacle, then the Urdu for a solution.

You'll want to know: اٹکنا.
اٹکنا — aṭaknā — "to get stuck; to be held up".
اگر میں راستے میں اٹک گیا تو فون کروں گا۔ agar maiṅ rāste meṅ aṭak gayā to fon karūṅ gā. If I get stuck on the way, I will phone.
Past (it …): وہ ٹرک پل پر اٹک گیا, voh ṭrak pul par aṭak gayā.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
نبٹانا (nibṭānā) — to deal with; to settle (a matter).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a solution, then the Urdu for to get stuck.

You'll want to know: نبٹانا.
نبٹانا — nibṭānā — "to deal with; to settle (a matter)".
اگر آپ معاملہ نبٹائیں گے تو سب خوش ہوں گے۔ agar āp muāmalā nibṭāeṅ ge to sab khush hoṅ ge. If you deal with the matter, everyone will be happy.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کام نبٹایا, us ne kām nibṭāyā.

The word, taken apart.
نبٹانا (nibṭānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin nibṭānā, "finishes", beside nibaṭnā, "to be settled", under Sanskrit nírvartate (CDIAL 7395). To bring a matter to its end; Urdu also says nimṭānā.
