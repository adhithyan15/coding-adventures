Lesson 1 of 5.
portus, portūs — a harbour, a port.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to take across, then the Latin for to swim to safety.

You'll want to know: portus, portūs.
portus, portūs — "a harbour, a port".
Tandem nāvis in portum intrat, et nautae gaudent. At last the ship comes into the harbour, and the sailors are glad.
Fourth declension, masculine: portus, portūs m. Figurative too: a safe place after trouble. English port comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
portus is inherited from Proto-Italic portus, from PIE pértus 'crossing, passage' (EtymDB), the same root as porta 'gate'. English port comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nāvāle, nāvālis — a dockyard, a place where ships are kept.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to swim to safety, then the Latin for a harbour.

You'll want to know: nāvāle, nāvālis.
nāvāle, nāvālis — "a dockyard, a place where ships are kept".
In nāvālī fabrī nāvēs novās aedificant. In the dockyard the workmen build new ships.
Third declension, neuter: nāvāle, nāvālis n., often plural nāvālia. It is the neuter of the adjective nāvālis 'of ships'; English naval comes from that.

The word, taken apart.
nāvāle is the neuter of the adjective nāvālis 'of ships', built on nāvis 'ship' (Lewis & Short). English naval comes from the adjective.

Lesson 3 of 5.
aestuārium, aestuāriī — a tidal inlet, a tidal marsh; an estuary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a harbour, then the Latin for a dockyard.

You'll want to know: aestuārium, aestuāriī.
aestuārium, aestuāriī — "a tidal inlet, a tidal marsh; an estuary".
Ubi mare recēdit, in aestuāriō lutum est. When the sea goes back, there is mud in the tidal inlet.
Second declension, neuter: aestuārium, aestuāriī n. Land the tide covers and uncovers. English estuary comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
aestuārium is built on aestus 'heat, surge, tide' with the suffix -ārium (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English estuary comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pharus, pharī — a lighthouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dockyard, then the Latin for a tidal inlet.

You'll want to know: pharus, pharī.
pharus, pharī — "a lighthouse".
Nocte lūx pharī procul ardet, et nautae portum inveniunt. At night the light of the lighthouse burns far off, and the sailors find the harbour.
Second declension, feminine: pharus, pharī f. (also pharos). Named after a famous lighthouse island.

The word, taken apart.
pharus was borrowed from Greek pharos, the name of the island whose lighthouse became famous (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); the name then became the common word for any lighthouse.

Lesson 5 of 5.
prōmunturium, prōmunturiī — a headland, a promontory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tidal inlet, then the Latin for a lighthouse.

You'll want to know: prōmunturium, prōmunturiī.
prōmunturium, prōmunturiī — "a headland, a promontory".
Nāvis prope prōmunturium altum nāvigat. The ship sails near a high headland.
Second declension, neuter: prōmunturium, prōmunturiī n., also spelled prōmontōrium. English promontory comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
prōmunturium is usually traced to prō 'forward' and mineō 'to jut out' (Lewis & Short): land that juts forward into the sea. English promontory comes from it.
