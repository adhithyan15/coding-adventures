Sanskrit, chapter 291: Cheats.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
धूर्तः (dhūrtaḥ) — a rogue; a cheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a piece of jewellery, then the Sanskrit for by force.

You'll want to know: धूर्तः.
धूर्तः — dhūrtaḥ — "a rogue; a cheat".
सः नरः धूर्तः अस्ति। saḥ naraḥ dhūrtaḥ asti. That man is a rogue.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
धूर्तः (dhūrtaḥ) comes from the root √dhūrv, "to hurt" (MW also names √dhvṛ); MW gives dhūrta as cunning and, as a noun, a rogue or swindler (MW).

वञ्चयति — vañcayati — "cheats; deceives".
धूर्तः वृद्धं वञ्चयति। dhūrtaḥ vṛddhaṃ vañcayati. The rogue cheats the old man.
Past (he …): सः वृद्धं वञ्चितवान्, saḥ vṛddhaṃ vañcitavān.

The word, taken apart.
वञ्चयति (vañcayati) comes from the root √vañc, "to go crookedly"; MW gives its causative vañcayati as to avoid, to lead astray and to cheat (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
उत्कोचः (utkocaḥ) — a bribe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a rogue, then the Sanskrit for cheats.

You'll want to know: उत्कोचः.
उत्कोचः — utkocaḥ — "a bribe".
यदि सः उत्कोचं याचते, तर्हि धूर्तः। yadi saḥ utkocaṃ yācate, tarhi dhūrtaḥ. If he asks for a bribe, then he is a cheat.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
उत्कोचः (utkocaḥ) is built from ut and the root √kuc, "to bend, contract"; MW's senses run from unwinding to bribery and corruption (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
कपटम् (kapaṭam) — fraud; a trick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for cheats, then the Sanskrit for a bribe.

You'll want to know: कपटम्.
कपटम् — kapaṭam — "fraud; a trick".
एतत् सर्वं कपटम् अस्ति। etat sarvaṃ kapaṭam asti. All this is a fraud.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
कपटम् (kapaṭam) MW gives kapaṭa as fraud, deceit and cheating, and reports a commentator's derivation from √kamp, "to tremble", so its origin is uncertain (MW).
