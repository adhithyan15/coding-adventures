Japanese, chapter 200: Changing Trains and Waiting for Buses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
のりかえる (norikaeru) — to change trains; to transfer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to come to a stop, then the Japanese for an express.

You'll want to know: のりかえる.
のりかえる — norikaeru — "to change trains; to transfer".
ここで ちかてつに のりかえます。 — koko de chikatetsu ni norikaemasu — "I change to the subway here."
Polite のりかえます, norikaemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
のりかえる (norikaeru) is native Japanese (wago), built from nori, "riding", and kaeru, "to change", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
でる (deru) — to leave; to go out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an express, then the Japanese for to change trains.

You'll want to know: でる.
でる — deru — "to leave; to go out".
とっきゅうは もう でました。 — tokkyū wa mō demashita — "The limited express has already left."
Polite でます, demasu; an ichidan verb. Its partner だす, dasu, means "to take out".

The word, taken apart.
でる (deru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
てつどう (tetsudō) — a railway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to change trains, then the Japanese for to leave.

You'll want to know: てつどう.
てつどう — tetsudō — "a railway".
この てつどうは みなとまで いきます。 — kono tetsudō wa minato made ikimasu — "This railway goes as far as the harbour."

The word, taken apart.
てつどう (tetsudō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tetsu, "iron" (Middle Chinese thet) and dō, "way" (dawX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ていりゅうじょ (teiryūjo) — a bus stop; a tram stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a railway, then the Japanese for a boarding point.

You'll want to know: ていりゅうじょ.
ていりゅうじょ — teiryūjo — "a bus stop; a tram stop".
ていりゅうじょで ともだちを まちます。 — teiryūjo de tomodachi o machimasu — "I wait for my friend at the bus stop."

The word, taken apart.
ていりゅうじょ (teiryūjo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tei, "to halt" (Middle Chinese deng), ryū, "to stay" (ljuw) and jo, "place" (srjoX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The last is voiced from sho to jo.
