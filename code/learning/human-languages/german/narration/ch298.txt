German, chapter 298: Die Verlobung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
verloben — to get engaged; mostly reflexive: sich verloben.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the degree, then the German for the move.

You'll want to know: verloben.
verloben — "to get engaged; mostly reflexive: sich verloben".
Sie haben sich im Urlaub verlobt. They got engaged on holiday.
Sich mit jemandem verloben. Verlobt — engaged.

The word, taken apart.
verloben is built on loben, which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic lubōną "to praise, to vow": to make a promise.

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Bräutigam — the bridegroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the engagement, then the German for the bride.

You'll want to know: der Bräutigam.
der Bräutigam — "the bridegroom".
Der Bräutigam war sehr nervös. The bridegroom was very nervous.
Plural: die Bräutigame.

The word, taken apart.
Bräutigam is inherited from Old High German brūtigomo, from Proto-Germanic brūdigumô, built on the word for "bride" and gumô "man" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Brautpaar — the bride and groom, the bridal couple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bride, then the German for the bridegroom.

You'll want to know: das Brautpaar.
das Brautpaar — "the bride and groom, the bridal couple".
Alle Gäste warteten auf das Brautpaar. All the guests waited for the bride and groom.
Plural: die Brautpaare.

The word, taken apart.
Brautpaar joins the inherited Braut "bride" and Paar "pair, couple", which came from Latin pār "equal" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent for the compound.
