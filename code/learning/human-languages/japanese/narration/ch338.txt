Japanese, chapter 338: Less and More.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
むだ (muda) — useless; a waste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for petroleum, then the Japanese for too good to waste.

You'll want to know: むだ.
むだ — muda — "useless; a waste".
ひるに でんきを つかったら、むだです。 — hiru ni denki o tsukattara, muda desu — "Using the lights in the daytime is a waste."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
むだ (muda) is written as Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, mu, "nothing" (Middle Chinese mju) and da, "burdensome". JMdict marks that spelling as ateji, characters chosen for their sound, so whether the word itself came from Chinese is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
へらす (herasu) — to reduce; to cut down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for too good to waste, then the Japanese for useless.

You'll want to know: へらす.
へらす — herasu — "to reduce; to cut down".
かんきょうの ために、ごみを へらした ほうが いいです。 — kankyō no tame ni, gomi o herashita hō ga ii desu — "For the environment, we should reduce our rubbish."
Polite へらします, herashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): まちが ごみを へらしました。, machi ga gomi o herashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
へらす (herasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
