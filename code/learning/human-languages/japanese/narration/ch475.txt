Japanese, chapter 475: Kitchen and Table.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
れいぞうこ (reizōko) — a fridge; a refrigerator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to tear into pieces, then the Japanese for to dilute.

You'll want to know: れいぞうこ.
れいぞうこ — reizōko — "a fridge; a refrigerator".
ぎゅうにゅうは れいぞうこに あります。 — gyūnyū wa reizōko ni arimasu — "The milk is in the fridge."

The word, taken apart.
れいぞうこ (reizōko) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are rei, "cool" (Middle Chinese lengX), zō, "storehouse" (dzangH), and ko, "warehouse" (khuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
おおさじ (ōsaji) — a tablespoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a fridge, then the Japanese for tableware.

You'll want to know: おおさじ.
おおさじ — ōsaji — "a tablespoon".
おおさじで さとうを いれて ください。 — ōsaji de satō o irete kudasai — "Please put the sugar in with a tablespoon."

The word, taken apart.
おおさじ (ōsaji) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: ō, "big" and saji, "spoon" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
