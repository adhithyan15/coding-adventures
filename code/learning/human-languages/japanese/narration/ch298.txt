Japanese, chapter 298: Long, Long Ago.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
むかし (mukashi) — long ago; the old days.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to drive out, then the Japanese for rare.

You'll want to know: むかし.
むかし — mukashi — "long ago; the old days".
むかし、やまに おじいさんが すんでいました。 — mukashi, yama ni ojiisan ga sunde imashita — "Long ago an old man lived in the mountains."

The word, taken apart.
むかし (mukashi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Before the new one: say the Japanese for rare, then the Japanese for long ago.

You'll want to know: むかしばなし.
むかしばなし — mukashibanashi — "a folk tale; an old story".
そぼは いつも むかしばなしを しました。 — sobo wa itsumo mukashibanashi o shimashita — "Grandmother was always telling old stories."

The word, taken apart.
むかしばなし (mukashibanashi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mukashi, "long ago", and banashi, "talk" (from hanasu) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for long ago, then the Japanese for a folk tale.

かたる — kataru — "to tell (a story); to relate".
そふは ときどき むかしばなしを かたります。 — sofu wa tokidoki mukashibanashi o katarimasu — "Grandfather sometimes tells old stories."
