Telugu, chapter 91: Greetings People Actually Say.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
బాగున్నారా? (bāgunnārā?) — are you well?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for leave, then the Telugu for let's go.

You'll want to know: బాగున్నారా?
బాగున్నారా? (bāgunnārā?) — "are you well?".
It is the answer you already give, బాగున్నాను, turned into a question: the respectful ending -ారు and the question mark -ఆ you know from అవునా?

What you've built.
The greeting most people open with, built from your own answer.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for leave, then the Telugu for let's go, then say bāgunnārā? again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does బాగున్నారా? (bāgunnārā?) mean? ("Are you well?".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 7.
చాలా రోజులైంది (cālā rōjulaindi) — it has been a long time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for let's go, then the Telugu for are you well.

You'll want to know: చాలా రోజులైంది.
చాలా రోజులైంది (cālā rōjulaindi) — "it has been a long time", literally "many days have become".
చాలా and రోజు are both yours already; -లు makes the days plural.

What you've built.
The greeting for someone you have not seen in a while.

Lesson 3 of 7.
ఏంటి సంగతి? (ēṇṭi saṅgati?) — what's new?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for are you well, then the Telugu for it has been a long time.

You'll want to know: ఏంటి సంగతి?
ఏంటి సంగతి? (ēṇṭi saṅgati?) — "what's new?", literally "what's the matter?", but warm.
ఏంటి is the everyday spoken form of ఏమిటి. Use it with friends, not with a stranger.

What you've built.
A friendly opener, and the spoken shape of a word you learned formally.

Lesson 4 of 7.
ఎక్కడికి? (ekkaḍiki?) — where to?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for it has been a long time, then the Telugu for what's new.

You'll want to know: ఎక్కడికి?
ఎక్కడికి? (ekkaḍiki?) — "where to?".
It is ఎక్కడ, "where", with the -కి "to" ending. Asked of a neighbour on the street it is a greeting, not a demand; ఇంటికి, "home", is a full answer.

What you've built.
A street greeting, and its answer from the last chapter.

Lesson 5 of 7.
భోంచేశారా? (bhōñcēśārā?) — have you eaten?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for what's new, then the Telugu for where to.

You'll want to know: భోంచేశారా?
భోంచేశారా? (bhōñcēśārā?) — "have you eaten?".
It is భోజనం చేశారా?, "have you done the meal?", worn short in speech. Asked around a mealtime it is a greeting and a kindness; ఆ, అయింది — "yes, done" — is enough of an answer.

What you've built.
Five greetings people actually use, one of them about food.

Lesson 6 of 7.
Hello and goodbye, the everyday way — a second pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
You meet a neighbour you have not seen in a long time, around lunch. Say three things.

Grammar Lens: the ending -కి, "to".
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: ఇంటికి. built from: house + -కి.
word: ఎక్కడికి? (ekkaḍiki?) built from: where + -కి.
The same -కి as నాకు: a direction, to a place or to a person.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: ask a friend what's new, then ask where they are going]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: answer that you are going home]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: at the door, tell your host to take care and to come soon]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: take your leave formally, then hurry your group along]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Are you well? (బాగున్నారా? (bāgunnārā?)) Long time? (చాలా రోజులైంది (cālā rōjulaindi).) What's new? (ఏంటి సంగతి? (ēṇṭi saṅgati?)) Where to? (ఎక్కడికి? (ekkaḍiki?)) Have you eaten? (భోంచేశారా? (bhōñcēśārā?)) Take care? (జాగ్రత్త.) Soon? (త్వరగా.) Home? (ఇంటికి.) Leave? (సెలవు.) Let's go? (పదండి.)


Lesson 7 of 7.
After the name — a second pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
You have given your name. Ask the next two questions: the job, and the address.

Grammar Lens: one frame, many questions.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ask about you: మీ ఉద్యోగం ఏమిటి? ask what happened to you: మీకు పెళ్ళి అయిందా?
ask about you: మీ చిరునామా ఏమిటి? ask what happened to you: మీకు ఏ భాషలు వచ్చు?
మీ asks about something of yours; మీకు asks what is so, or what came, to you. The children question is the right-hand kind: మీకు పిల్లలు ఉన్నారా?

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: say you know English and a little Telugu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: introduce your son and your daughter]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say he is a good person, and our relative]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a neighbour asks where to, and whether you have eaten; answer both]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Children? (పిల్లలు.) A boy, a girl? (అబ్బాయి, అమ్మాయి.) A person? (మనిషి.) A relative? (బంధువు.) A job? (ఉద్యోగం.) A language? (భాష.) English? (ఇంగ్లీషు.) A wedding? (పెళ్ళి.) An address? (చిరునామా.)
