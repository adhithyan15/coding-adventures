6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yèshì — night market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a market held at dawn, then the Mandarin for to set up a street stall.

You'll want to know: yèshì.
yèshì — "night market".
wǎnshang wǒmen zuò gōngjiāo qù yèshì — In the evening we take the bus to the night market.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yèshì is built from yè 'night' and 市 'a market', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yaeH and dzyiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 6.
口味 (kǒuwèi) — one's taste in food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to set up a street stall, then the Mandarin for a night market.

You'll want to know: 口味.
口味 — kǒuwèi — "one's taste in food".
nánfāng rén hé běifāng rén de kǒuwèi bù yíyàng — People in the south and the north have different tastes in food.
The new character, in pieces: 味 (wèi) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left 口 kǒu, small; on the right a short bar, a longer bar under it, a vertical through both, and a stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
口味 (kǒuwèi) is built from 口 'the mouth' and 味 (wèi) 'a flavour', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khuwX and mj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the five flavours, sour, salty, sweet, bitter and pungent.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Script — a small 口, then two bars and a vertical.
味 (wèi).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 口 kǒu, three strokes, small and set high.
The right piece, five strokes. A short bar. A longer bar under it. A vertical down through both. Then a stroke falling to the left and a stroke falling to the right, from where the vertical crosses the long bar.
3 + 5 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 味 (wèi), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 味 (wèi)? (Eight.) Which bar is shorter, the top one or the one under it? (The top one.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
风味 (fēngwèi) — local flavour; distinctive taste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a night market, then the Mandarin for one's taste in food.

You'll want to know: 风味.
风味 — fēngwèi — "local flavour; distinctive taste".
我想吃这个岛的风味小吃 wǒ xiǎng chī zhège dǎo de fēngwèi xiǎochī — I'd like to try this island's local snacks.

The word, taken apart.
风味 (fēngwèi) is built from 风 'a custom' and 味 (wèi) 'a flavour', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjuwng and mj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the sense 'a local custom' for the first is not given there.
