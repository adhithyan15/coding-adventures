German, chapter 518: Pinsel und Leinwand.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Gemälde — painting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to applaud, then the German for applause.

You'll want to know: das Gemälde.
das Gemälde — "painting".
Im Museum hängt ein berühmtes Gemälde aus dem Mittelalter. A famous painting from the Middle Ages hangs in the museum.
Plural: die Gemälde. Built on malen — to paint.

The word, taken apart.
Gemälde is built on malen "to paint", from Old High German mālōn "to mark, to paint"; EtymDB lists the noun without a source.

der Pinsel — "paintbrush".
Für die kleinen Details nimmt sie einen dünnen Pinsel. For the small details she uses a thin brush.
Plural: die Pinsel.

The word, taken apart.
Pinsel comes from Middle High German bensel, pensel, from Old French pincel, from Latin pēniculus "little tail, brush" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Leinwand — canvas; screen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for painting, then the German for paintbrush.

You'll want to know: die Leinwand.
die Leinwand — "canvas; screen".
Der Maler stellt eine neue Leinwand auf. The painter sets up a new canvas.
Plural: die Leinwände. Also a cinema screen.

The word, taken apart.
Leinwand is inherited from Middle High German līnwāt "linen" (EtymDB), built on lein "flax" and wāt "cloth"; the second part was later reshaped like Wand "wall".

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Staffelei — easel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for paintbrush, then the German for canvas.

You'll want to know: die Staffelei.
die Staffelei — "easel".
Die Staffelei steht am Fenster, wo das Licht gut ist. The easel stands by the window, where the light is good.
Plural: die Staffeleien.

The word, taken apart.
Staffelei is built on Staffel "step, rung", which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic stapulaz "post"; the ending -ei makes a noun.
