Latin, chapter 233: To press on, To break off, To tie, To mix, To obey.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
īnstō, īnstāre — to press on, to insist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to serve, then the Latin for to suffer.

You'll want to know: īnstō, īnstāre.
īnstō, īnstāre — "to press on, to insist".

The word, taken apart.
īnstō is in, "on, upon", with stō, "to stand" (EtymDB 2.1): to stand upon, to press on. English instant comes from its participle.

Lesson 4 of 5.
misceō, miscēre — to mix.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to break off, then the Latin for to tie.

You'll want to know: misceō, miscēre.
misceō, miscēre — "to mix".

The word, taken apart.
misceō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European meyk-, "to mix" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek mignymi, "to mix", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). English mix comes from its participle mixtus, "mixed".

Lesson 5 of 5.
pāreō, pārēre — to obey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to tie, then the Latin for to mix.

You'll want to know: pāreō, pārēre.
pāreō, pārēre — "to obey".

The word, taken apart.
pāreō is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European peh2s-, "to watch, to see" (EtymDB 2.1): first to appear, to be at hand, then to be at someone's call, to obey.
