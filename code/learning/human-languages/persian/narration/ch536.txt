Persian, chapter 536: Tidying Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جمع کردن (jam' kardan) — to tidy up; to gather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a homemaker, then the Persian for a capable housewife.

You'll want to know: جمع کردن.
جمع کردن (jam' kardan) — "to tidy up; to gather".
بچه ها اتاقشان را جمع کردند. bachchehâ otâqashân râ jam' kardand. The children tidied their room.
jam' 'gathering' + kardan. Also 'to collect' and, in sums, 'to add'.

The word, taken apart.
jam' was borrowed from Arabic jamʿ, 'gathering' (EtymDB). kardan, 'to do', is inherited Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
خشک کردن (khoshk kardan) — to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a capable housewife, then the Persian for to tidy up.

You'll want to know: خشک کردن.
خشک کردن (khoshk kardan) — "to dry".
او موهایش را با حوله خشک کرد. u muhâyash râ bâ hule khoshk kard. She dried her hair with a towel.
khoshk 'dry' + kardan. khoshk shodan — to dry out.

The word, taken apart.
khoshk kardan is built on two inherited words: khoshk, 'dry', usually traced to Middle Persian hušk, and kardan, 'to do'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نامرتب (nâmorattab) — untidy, messy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to dry, then the Persian for to make a mess.

You'll want to know: نامرتب.
نامرتب (nâmorattab) — "untidy, messy".
اتاق برادرم همیشه نامرتب است. otâq-e barâdaram hamishe nâmorattab ast. My brother's room is always untidy.
nâ- 'not' + morattab 'tidy', as in morattab kardan.

The word, taken apart.
nâmorattab is built in Persian from the prefix nâ-, 'not', and morattab, 'in order', a loan usually traced to Arabic murattab, 'arranged'. EtymDB has no entry for it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to make a mess, then the Persian for untidy.

You'll want to know: آشغال.
آشغال (âshghâl) — "rubbish, trash".
پدرم شبها آشغالها را بیرون میبرد. pedaram shabhâ âshghâlhâ râ birun mibarad. My father takes the rubbish out at night.
Everyday; zobâle is more formal.

The word, taken apart.
âshghâl is the everyday word for rubbish, but EtymDB has no entry for it, and its origin is not settled.
