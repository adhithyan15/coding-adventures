# त्यानंतर (tyānantar) — first, then, after that, in the end

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say "then" and "after". (*mag*, *nantar*.) Then "I used to go". (*mī jāyco*.)

## You'll want to know: first, then, after that
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C279-FIRST-THEN-01]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C111-TIME111-03, MR-LEX-C171-VEL171-03, MR-LEX-CHAHA, MR-LEX-GHENE] -->

> **आधी मी चहा घेतला. मग मी पुस्तक वाचलं.**
>
> *ādhī mī cahā ghetlā. mag mī pustak vāclaṁ.* — "First I had tea. Then I read a book."

A story keeps its events in order with four words:

- **आधी** — first
- **मग** — then
- **त्यानंतर** (*tyānantar*) — after that: **त्या**, "that", and **नंतर**, "after"
- **शेवटी** — in the end

Each one opens its sentence.

## Grammar Lens: -नंतर after a noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C279-NANTAR-POSTP-01]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C94-SCHOOL94-01, MR-LEX-C111-TIME111-04] -->

> **शाळेनंतर मी घरी आलो.**
>
> *śāḷenantar mī gharī ālo.* — "After school I came home."

**नंतर** also stands after a noun, in its bent form: **शाळा** → **शाळेनंतर** (*śāḷenantar*), "after school". **त्यानंतर** is the same thing done to **तो**.
