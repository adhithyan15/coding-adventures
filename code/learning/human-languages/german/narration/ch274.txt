German, chapter 274: Ein Gerücht.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Gerücht — the rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to announce, then the German for to make public.

You'll want to know: das Gerücht.
das Gerücht — "the rumour".
Im Dorf gab es ein Gerücht über den neuen Nachbarn. In the village there was a rumour about the new neighbour.
Plural: die Gerüchte.

The word, taken apart.
Gerücht is usually traced to Middle Low German gerüchte "outcry, talk", built on the root of rufen "to call", from Proto-Germanic hrōpaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Quelle — the source; the spring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to make public, then the German for the rumour.

You'll want to know: die Quelle.
die Quelle — "the source; the spring".
Der Journalist nannte seine Quelle nicht. The journalist did not name his source.
Plural: die Quellen. The first sense is a spring of water.

The word, taken apart.
Quelle is inherited from Old High German quella, which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic kwellōn "well, spring": where water, and later news, comes from.

Lesson 3 of 5.
behaupten — to claim, to assert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the rumour, then the German for the source.

You'll want to know: behaupten.
behaupten — "to claim, to assert".
Er behauptete, dass er nichts gesehen hatte. He claimed that he had seen nothing.
Often with dass and the verb at the end. Reflexive, sich behaupten means to hold one's own.

The word, taken apart.
behaupten is usually traced to Middle High German behoubeten "to hold one's ground", built on Haupt "head", a cousin of English head (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
bestätigen — to confirm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to claim, then the German for the claim.

You'll want to know: bestätigen.
bestätigen — "to confirm".
Die Polizei bestätigte die Meldung. The police confirmed the news item.
Perfekt: hat bestätigt. Also of a booking: Das Hotel bestätigte die Reservierung.

The word, taken apart.
bestätigen is usually traced to Middle High German bestætigen "to make firm", built on stætec "steady", from the root of stehen "to stand"; EtymDB lists no parent.
