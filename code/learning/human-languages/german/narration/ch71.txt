German, chapter 71: Cake, Soup, Honey, Oil, Lunch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Kuchen — a cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for an onion, then the German for a carrot.

You'll want to know: der Kuchen.
der Kuchen — "a cake".

The word, taken apart.
Kuchen from Old High German kuohho from Proto-Germanic kōkô, "cake" — inherited; Dutch koek is the same word. (EtymDB 2.1) English cake is a cousin, through Norse.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Suppe — a soup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a carrot, then the German for a cake.

You'll want to know: die Suppe.
die Suppe — "a soup".

The word, taken apart.
Suppe came in from French soupe and Middle Low German suppe, sope; EtymDB 2.1 traces sope to Proto-Germanic supô, "sop". So German borrowed back a Germanic word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Mittagessen — lunch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for honey, then the German for oil.

You'll want to know: das Mittagessen.
das Mittagessen — "lunch".

The word, taken apart.
Mittagessen is Mittag + the word for "a meal", which is the verb "to eat" from Old High German ezzan from Proto-Germanic etaną, the same verb as English eat. (EtymDB 2.1).
