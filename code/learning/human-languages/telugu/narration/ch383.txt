Telugu, chapter 383: Making a Case.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
వాదన (vādana) — a line of reasoning; an argument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to regard as, then the Telugu for an angle.

You'll want to know: వాదన.
వాదన — vādana — "a line of reasoning; an argument".
అతని వాదనలో బలం లేదు. atani vādanalō balaṁ lēdu. There is no strength in his argument.
Plural: వాదనలు, vādanalu.
The case you make, not a quarrel. Its verb is వాదించు, to argue, which you know.

The word, taken apart.
వాదన (vādana) is built on Sanskrit vāda, "speech, statement" (CDIAL 11507), from the root vad, "to speak"; the verb వాదించు comes from the same word.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
నిరూపించు (nirūpiñcu) — to prove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a line of reasoning, then the Telugu for evidence.

You'll want to know: నిరూపించు.
నిరూపించు — nirūpiñcu — "to prove".
ఆమె తన మాట నిజమని నిరూపించింది. āme tana māṭa nijamani nirūpiñcindi. She proved that what she said was true.
Past (I …): నిరూపించాను, nirūpiñcānu.
What you prove is often a clause ending in అని:... నిజమని నిరూపించు (nirūpiñcu), to prove that... is true.

The word, taken apart.
నిరూపించు (nirūpiñcu) is built on Sanskrit nirūpaṇa, "stating, determining, defining", and also "examination" (Monier-Williams), with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.

Lesson 4 of 5.
తర్కం (tarkaṁ) — logic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for evidence, then the Telugu for to prove.

You'll want to know: తర్కం.
తర్కం — tarkaṁ — "logic".
అతని మాటల్లో తర్కం లేదు. atani māṭallō tarkaṁ lēdu. There is no logic in what he says.
Careful, step-by-step reasoning. The adjective is తార్కిక, logical.

The word, taken apart.
తర్కం (tarkaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit tarka, "inquiry" (CDIAL 5714), which Monier-Williams also glosses "reasoning, speculation".

Lesson 5 of 5.
సహేతుకమైన (sahētukamaina) — reasonable; well-grounded.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to prove, then the Telugu for logic.

You'll want to know: సహేతుకమైన.
సహేతుకమైన — sahētukamaina — "reasonable; well-grounded".
అది సహేతుకమైన ప్రశ్న. adi sahētukamaina praśna. That is a reasonable question.
A reason stands behind it: స-, with, + హేతు, a reason. The ending -మైన makes an adjective, as in కఠినమైన.

The word, taken apart.
సహేతుకమైన (sahētukamaina) is built on Sanskrit sahetuka, "together with a reason" (Monier-Williams), from sa-, "with", and hetu, "a cause, a reason"; Telugu adds -మైన to make an adjective.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons and opinions.
