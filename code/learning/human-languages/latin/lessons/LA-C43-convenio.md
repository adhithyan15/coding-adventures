# conveniō and occurrō — coming together, running into

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-RESPONDEO-01, LA-ETYMON-RESPONDEO-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Respondeō** answered by pledging back. This verb needs nothing
new from you: both halves of it are already yours.

## You'll want to know: conveniō — "I meet"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-CONVENIO-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-VENIO-01] -->

**Conveniō** means "**I meet**," "I come together with." Fourth
conjugation, because **veniō** is:

- **conveniō** — I meet
- **convenīs** — you meet, to one person
- **convenit** — he meets, she meets
- **convenīmus** — we meet
- **convenītis** — you meet, to several people
- **conveniunt** — they meet

## You'll want to know: occurrō — "I run into"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-OCCURRO-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-CURRO-01] -->

**Occurrō** is the other kind of meeting: not arranged, but stumbled into.
Third conjugation, like **currō**: **occurrō, occurris, occurrit,
occurrimus, occurritis, occurrunt**.

## The word, taken apart: two prefixes, two verbs you own
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-CONVENIO-OCCURRO-03]; assesses=[LA-ETYMON-VENIO-02, LA-ETYMON-CURRO-02, LA-LEX-COGITO-01, LA-ETYMON-COGITO-02, LA-LEX-CLAUDO-01, LA-ETYMON-CLAUDO-02, LA-PRAGMATICS-VOLUP-EST-CONVENISSE-01, LA-LEX-FERO-01, LA-GRAMMAR-FERO-SUPPLETION-02, LA-ETYMON-FERO-03] -->

*Convenīre* is *con-*, "together," plus *venīre*, "to come." To meet is to
**come together**. English has **convene**, **convention**, a **convent**
(those who came together), and **convenient**, which began as things
*coming together* well. A **covenant** is this verb's present participle,
picked up through Old French.

*Occurrere* is *ob-*, "toward, against," plus *currere*, "to run." To meet
by chance is to **run into** — the English idiom says the same thing, and
gives **occur**. A thought that *occurs* to you has run at you.

That *con-* is worth collecting. It sits on **cōgitō**, "I think" — *con-*
plus a verb of shaking, so thinking is shaking things together. It sits on
**claudō**: *conclūdere* shuts things in together, whence **conclude**. And
it sits on **ferō**, where *cōnferre* gives **confer** and **conference**,
people carrying their views together — a compound inherits its parent's
oddity, so *cōnferre*'s past is *contulī*, borrowing that second root
exactly as *ferō* does.

One more piece is already yours. Chapter 21 taught **volup est convēnisse**,
"it is a pleasure to have met." *Convēnisse* is this verb — you have been
saying *conveniō* for twenty-two chapters without knowing it.
