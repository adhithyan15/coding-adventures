Malayalam, chapter 346: Bigger, Best, and How Long.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
അതിനെക്കാൾ (atinekkāḷ) — than that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "big", "more", and "a good one". (valiya, kūṭutal, nallatŭ.)

Grammar Lens: X-നെക്കാൾ, "than X".
Malayalam has no "bigger". It says "than that, this is big". Put the thing you compare against in the object form, അവനെ, അതിനെ, എന്നെ, and add -ക്കാൾ:
ഇത് അതിനെക്കാൾ വലുതാണ്. itŭ atinekkāḷ valutāṇŭ. — "This is bigger than that."
ട്രെയിൻ ബസിനെക്കാൾ വേഗത്തിൽ പോകും. ṭreyin basinekkāḷ vēgattil pōkuṁ. — "The train goes faster than the bus."
എനിക്ക് ചായയെക്കാൾ കാപ്പിയാണ് ഇഷ്ടം. enikkŭ cāyayekkāḷ kāppiyāṇŭ iṣṭaṁ. — "I like coffee more than tea."
വലുത് is "a big one", made from വലിയ the way നല്ലത് is made from നല്ല. You will also meet the ending with a long vowel, -ഏക്കാൾ, as in അതിനേക്കാൾ (atinēkkāḷ); both are correct, and they mean the same.

Grammar Lens: ഏറ്റവും, "the most".
Put ഏറ്റവും before the quality, and it is the top of the scale:
ഇത് ഗ്രാമത്തിലെ ഏറ്റവും വലിയ വീടാണ്. itŭ grāmattile ēṟṟavuṁ valiya vīṭāṇŭ. — "This is the biggest house in the village."
-ലെ is "in, of" before a noun. ഏറ്റവും നല്ലത് is "the best one".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Than him"? (avanekkāḷ.) "Than me"? (ennekkāḷ.) "The biggest"? (ēṟṟavuṁ valiya.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ഋതു (ṛtu) — a season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "the rainy season", then "summer". (maḻakkālaṁ, vēnalkkālaṁ.) Then "the most". (ēṟṟavuṁ.)

You'll want to know: ഋതു.
ഋതു — ṛtu — "a season".
മഴക്കാലം എനിക്ക് ഏറ്റവും ഇഷ്ടമുള്ള ഋതുവാണ്. maḻakkālaṁ enikkŭ ēṟṟavuṁ iṣṭamuḷḷa ṛtuvāṇŭ. — "The rainy season is my favourite season."
പഴയ കണക്കിൽ ഒരു വർഷത്തിൽ ആറ് ഋതുക്കൾ ഉണ്ട്. paḻaya kaṇakkil oru varṣattil āṟŭ ṛtukkaḷ uṇṭŭ. — "In the old reckoning, a year has six seasons."
In everyday talk, -കാലം names each season, as in മഴക്കാലം. ഋതു (ṛtu) is the word for the season itself, in writing, songs and the calendar. Most speakers say it close to rutu.
Its first letter is new; the next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ഋതു (ṛtu) from Sanskrit ṛtu, "a fixed time; a season", taken over whole (Monier-Williams). The same dictionary records the later Sanskrit count of six seasons in a year, the count the old reckoning keeps.

[your turn — say: ṛtu, then the plural ṛtukkaḷ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The rainy season is my favourite season."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In the old reckoning, a year has six seasons."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ഋതു (ṛtu) mean? (a season.) Which ending names each season in everyday talk? (-കാലം.)

Now say ഋതു (ṛtu), "a season". This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ഋ.
ഋ — ṛ, the Sanskrit vowel r, at the start of a word. Malayalam says it ru, with a short u after the r: ഋതു (ṛtu) sounds close to rutu.
After a consonant it is the sign ൃ, as in കൃഷി and സുഹൃത്ത്. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own, ഋ (ṛ). Only a few learned words begin with it.
Its shape is two upper loops joined by a sagging waist, with an oval below.

Writing: ഋ.
One recorded hand writes ഋ (ṛ) in two strokes. First the top: from inside the upper left loop, round it anticlockwise, down and along the waist, and up round the upper right loop, anticlockwise again. Then, after one lift, the oval below, from its top centre, clockwise.
Write ഋ (ṛ) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Copy the printed shape and keep the order above.

Lesson 4 of 5.
മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി (mūnnŭ varṣamāyi) — for three years now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a year", "an hour", and "from now on". (varṣaṁ, maṇikkūr, ini mutal.)

Grammar Lens: a span with -ആയി, still running.
To say how long something has been going on, put -ആയി, "has become", on the span, and keep the verb in the present:
മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി ഞാൻ മലയാളം പഠിക്കുന്നു. mūnnŭ varṣamāyi ñān malayāḷaṁ paṭhikkunnu. — "I have been learning Malayalam for three years."
The question puts എത്ര in front: എത്ര വർഷമായി? (etra varṣamāyi?), "for how many years?"

Grammar Lens: since, and a finished span.
മുതൽ, which you know from ഇനി മുതൽ, says "since, from":
ഇന്നലെ മുതൽ എനിക്ക് പനിയാണ്. innale mutal enikkŭ paniyāṇŭ. — "I have had a fever since yesterday."
A span that is over takes no ending at all: the plain past says it lasted that long and stopped.
ഞാൻ രണ്ട് മണിക്കൂർ കാത്തിരുന്നു. ñān raṇṭŭ maṇikkūr kāttirunnu. — "I waited for two hours."
