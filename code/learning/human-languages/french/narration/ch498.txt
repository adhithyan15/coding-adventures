French, chapter 498: En plein air.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'escalade — climbing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to fish, then the French for fisherman.

You'll want to know: l'escalade.
l'escalade — "climbing".
Je fais de l'escalade en salle le mercredi. I go indoor climbing on Wednesdays.
Feminine. Faire de l'escalade — to climb (as a sport).

The word, taken apart.
l'escalade was borrowed from Italian scalata (EtymDB), from scalare "to climb", built on scala "ladder", inherited from Latin scāla (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
grimper — to climb.

Before the new one: say the French for fisherman, then the French for climbing.

You'll want to know: grimper.
grimper — "to climb".
Les enfants grimpent dans l'arbre. The children are climbing the tree.
Grimper à un arbre or dans un arbre. Also of prices: les prix grimpent.

The word, taken apart.
grimper is usually traced to gripper "to grab", a verb of Germanic origin, with an added nasal sound; the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la gourde — water bottle, flask.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for climbing, then the French for to climb.

You'll want to know: la gourde.
la gourde — "water bottle, flask".
Avant de partir, il a rempli sa gourde. Before leaving, he filled his water bottle.
First a dried gourd. Informally, une gourde is also a dimwit.

The word, taken apart.
la gourde goes back to Old French coorde (EtymDB), from Latin cucurbita "a gourd" (Lewis & Short): dried gourds once served as flasks.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la lampe de poche — torch, flashlight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to climb, then the French for water bottle.

You'll want to know: la lampe de poche.
la lampe de poche — "torch, flashlight".
Il cherche ses clés avec une lampe de poche. He is looking for his keys with a torch.
Also une lampe torche.

The word, taken apart.
la lampe de poche is "a pocket lamp": lampe comes from Latin lampas "a torch", a borrowing from Greek, and poche "pocket" is of Frankish origin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le feu de camp — campfire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for water bottle, then the French for torch.

You'll want to know: le feu de camp.
le feu de camp — "campfire".
Le soir, nous chantions autour du feu de camp. In the evening we used to sing round the campfire.
Faire un feu de camp — to make a campfire.

The word, taken apart.
le feu de camp joins feu, inherited from Latin focus "hearth", and camp, from Latin campus "a field" (Lewis & Short).
