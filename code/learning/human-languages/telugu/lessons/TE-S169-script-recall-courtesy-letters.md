# ఞ · ◌ౌ — the two you were saying before you could see them

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-132, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-133] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Cover the two words this chapter taught for *gratitude* and for
*respect*. From memory, say which character sits inside each one.

## Script: a letter you rarely see alone, and a sign that replaces what it lands on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-132, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-133] -->

> ఞ · ◌ౌ

These two are opposites in how they behave, which is the thing worth holding:

| character | how it behaves |
|---|---|
| ఞ *ña* | a consonant, almost never standing alone — it lives stacked under జ |
| ◌ౌ *au* | a vowel sign, never standing alone — it replaces the *a* inside a consonant |

In **కృతజ్ఞత** the stack **జ్ఞ** is spelled *jña* and comes out of the mouth
closer to *gnya*. In **గౌరవం** the **గ** stops being *ga* the moment the sign
lands on it and becomes *gau*.

One is a shape you will hardly ever see by itself; the other changes a shape you
see constantly. Neither adds a sound beside the letter — one hides inside a
block, the other overwrites what was already there.
