Japanese, chapter 221: Fever, Pain and Treatment.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
たいおん (taion) — body temperature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to lose weight, then the Japanese for an appetite.

You'll want to know: たいおん.
たいおん — taion — "body temperature".
たいおんは たかいですか。 — taion wa takai desu ka — "Is your temperature high?"

The word, taken apart.
たいおん (taion) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tai, "body" (Middle Chinese thejX) and on, "warm" ('won) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 6.
さがる (sagaru) — to go down; to drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an appetite, then the Japanese for body temperature.

You'll want to know: さがる.
さがる — sagaru — "to go down; to drop".
ねつは さがりましたか。 — netsu wa sagarimashita ka — "Has your fever gone down?"
Polite さがります, sagarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
さがる (sagaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
