# Three settings on one dial

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-QUIZAS, ES-LEX-SUPONGO-QUE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two hedges, and a bare statement between them. This is where you feel
the difference rather than read it.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-QUIZAS, ES-SEMANTIC-HEDGE-EPISTEMIC, ES-LEX-SUPONGO-QUE, ES-SEMANTIC-HEDGE-INFERENTIAL] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Take one idea of your own — anything you could argue about — and say
it three times.

- [YOU SAY: the bare claim. No hedge. Hear how much you have committed to.]
- [YOU SAY: the same claim with *quizás* in front. You have not changed the idea;
  you have changed how far you will defend it.]
- [YOU SAY: the same claim again with *supongo que*. Now you are not admitting
  ignorance — you are offering an inference, and inviting the listener to check
  your reasoning rather than your confidence.]

[PAUSE 2s]
- [YOU SAY: which of the three you would use with a stranger, and why]
- [YOU SAY: which one an argument is hardest to answer with, and why]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SEMANTIC-HEDGE-EPISTEMIC, ES-SEMANTIC-HEDGE-INFERENTIAL] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which hedge admits you do not know? (*Quizás*.) Which one claims a
reason you have not been given? (*Supongo que*.) What did the Latin behind each
one mean — "who knows?", and "to place underneath"?
