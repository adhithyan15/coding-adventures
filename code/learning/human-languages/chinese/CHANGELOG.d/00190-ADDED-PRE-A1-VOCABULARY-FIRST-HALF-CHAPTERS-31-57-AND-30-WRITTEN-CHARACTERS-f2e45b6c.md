## Added — the pre-A1 vocabulary, first half: chapters 31-57 and 30 written characters

- 27 chapters of five words each (135 words, 37 of them verbs), one word per lesson,
  with reviews closing chapters 36, 42, 47, 52 and 57 and a final review: family and
  names, who does what and where people come from, this and that, eating and
  drinking, paying and the shop, coming and going and places in town, today and
  tomorrow, the week, months and years, the time and the day, sleep, feelings, the
  body, speaking and hearing, learning, what you love, free time and home.
- 30 writing lessons, 他 们 妈 男 叫 来 这 那 吃 饭 喝 水 买 多 少 去 车 天 今 星 期 年 明 点 午 晚
  手 说 话 想, each directly after the word whose headword first shows the character
  (the 很 shape of chapter 30), with its stroke order from Hanzi Writer Data and a
  stroke-by-stroke strip. Each comes back in at least two later lessons.
- A word whose characters are not all written is taught by ear with a pinyin
  headword (94 of the 135); the other 41 are headed in characters the reader writes.
  Examples print hanzi only for written characters, pinyin otherwise.
- Origins cite the Guangyun (Baxter's transcription) and hedge every sense it does
  not give; where the regular sound change would not give the modern reading, the
  lesson says so, and a word with no recorded older form says it is not settled.
- The book's Chinese snippets may now break a line between two characters
  (`\XeTeXlinebreaklocale "zh"` on `\chinesefont`), so a run of hanzi is no longer
  one unbreakable box. `\hbadness`, `\hfuzz`, `\tolerance` and the warning baseline
  are untouched.
- The font subset gains every newly printed character.
