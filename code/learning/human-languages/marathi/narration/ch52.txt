Marathi, chapter 52: Easy, Hard, Tall.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
सोपा (sopā) — easy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four adjectives in a row have refused to agree. Here is one that does, so the ending muscle does not go cold.
[your turn — recall: say hushār, then kharāb, and name the class they share]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: सोपा.
सोपा — sopā — easy, simple.
सोपा, सोपी, सोपं — sopā, sopī, sopaṁ.
It ends in -आ, so all three forms are yours already. The third adjective to behave this way and the first that costs nothing to conjugate, which is the point of having had the rule named in chapter forty-nine.
The sentence to keep is the one about the language itself:
मराठी सोपी आहे. — marāṭhī sopī āhe. — Marathi is easy.
मराठी is feminine, so the adjective ends in -ी. Say it out loud; you have earned it, and it is true more often than a beginner expects.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: sopā, sopī, sopaṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: marāṭhī sopī āhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: join two judgements with आणि — lahān āṇi sopī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that Marathi is easy, then that the room is small and easy.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What gender is मराठी, and what does सोपा (sopā) become with it? (Feminine; सोपी.) Which class does सोपा (sopā) belong to? (The agreeing one.)
Sources: Wiktionary: सोपा (sopā).


Lesson 2 of 4.
कठीण (kaṭhīṇ) — hard, difficult.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
सोपा (sopā) without its opposite is an opinion nobody can disagree with.

You'll want to know: कठीण.
कठीण — kaṭhīṇ — hard, difficult.
मराठी कठीण नाही. — marāṭhī kaṭhīṇ nāhī. — Marathi is not difficult.
No ending, in any gender: the last letter is not -आ, so the class is settled before you look anything up. Its opposite सोपा (sopā) agrees and it does not, which is a useful pair to hold precisely because the two halves behave differently.
Sanskrit कठिन (kaṭhina) meant hard in the physical sense — a hard stone, hard ground. Marathi kept the word and moved it almost entirely onto tasks: कठीण (kaṭhīṇ) काम, hard work; कठीण (kaṭhīṇ) भाषा, a hard language. For a hard OBJECT a Marathi speaker will normally reach for another word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kaṭhīṇ — hard, difficult]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: marāṭhī kaṭhīṇ nāhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: sopī kiṁvā kaṭhīṇ? — and hear only one of the two move]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Ask whether Marathi is easy or difficult, then answer.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What did कठिन mean in Sanskrit? (Physically hard.) Does कठीण (kaṭhīṇ) agree? (No.)
Sources: Wiktionary: कठीण (kaṭhīṇ).


Lesson 3 of 4.
उंच (uṁch) — tall, high.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
मोठा and लहान measure a thing in every direction at once. A person has one direction that matters more than the others.

You'll want to know: उंच.
उंच — uṁch — tall, high.
माझा मित्र उंच आहे. — mājhā mitra uṁch āhe. — my friend is tall.
Invariable, like most of what you have met: no -आ, no ending. It works on a person, a building and a hill without changing, and it is the natural partner of the वर you learned in chapter forty-two — what is उंच (uṁch) is what is high वर.
The pair to hold is उंच (uṁch) against मोठा. A मोठा मित्र is a big or an elder friend; an उंच (uṁch) मित्र is a tall one. English big blurs the two and Marathi keeps them apart, which matters the moment somebody asks you to describe a person for a minute.
Its ancestor is Sanskrit उच्च (ucca), "high, lofty" — the same syllable that sits inside words for height across the Indo-Aryan languages.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: uṁch — tall]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mājhā mitra uṁch āhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: uṁch and moṭhā back to back, and name the difference]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say mitrālā and describe the friend you can now see]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that your friend is tall but not big.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word would you use for a tall friend, and which for a big one? (उंच (uṁch); मोठा.) Does उंच (uṁch) agree? (No.)
Sources: Wiktionary: उंच (uṁch).


Lesson 4 of 4.
