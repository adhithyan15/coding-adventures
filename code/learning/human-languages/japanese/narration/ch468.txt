Japanese, chapter 468: Works of Art.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
さくひん (sakuhin) — a work of art; a piece.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for embroidery, then the Japanese for to whittle.

You'll want to know: さくひん.
さくひん — sakuhin — "a work of art; a piece".
これは そふの さくひんです。 — kore wa sofu no sakuhin desu — "This is a work by my grandfather."

The word, taken apart.
さくひん (sakuhin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are saku, "make" (Middle Chinese tsak) and hin, "article" (phimX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
くみたてる (kumitateru) — to assemble; to put together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sculpture, then the Japanese for a masterpiece.

You'll want to know: くみたてる.
くみたてる — kumitateru — "to assemble; to put together".
ちちは たなを くみたてています。 — chichi wa tana o kumitatete imasu — "My father is putting a shelf together."
Polite くみたてます, kumitatemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): だいくが いすを くみたてました。, daiku ga isu o kumitatemashita.

The word, taken apart.
くみたてる (kumitateru) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: kumi, "putting together (kumu)" and tateru, "to set up" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
