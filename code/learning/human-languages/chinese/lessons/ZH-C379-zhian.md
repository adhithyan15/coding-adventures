## You'll want to know: zhì'ān
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C379-SAFE379-05]; assesses=[] -->

*zhì'ān* — "law and order; public order".

> *zhèr de zhì'ān hěn hǎo suǒyǐ wǒ hěn fàngxīn* — Law and order is good here, so I don't worry.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*zhì'ān* is built from *zhì* 'to govern' and 安 'calm', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *dri* and *'an* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to put in order' and also lists it twice in the departing tone, *drijH* and *driH*, with the same gloss. Mandarin zhì follows a departing reading.
