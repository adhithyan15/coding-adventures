## You'll want to know: dǎzhàng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C327-HERO327-04]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎzhàng* — "to fight a war; to go into battle".

> *gǔdài de shíhou zhè liǎng ge guójiā chángcháng dǎzhàng* — In ancient times these two countries were often at war.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎzhàng* is built from 打 'to strike' and *zhàng* 'weapons', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *drjangH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hit' and the second 'arms'. Middle Chinese *taengX* would not regularly give dǎ.
