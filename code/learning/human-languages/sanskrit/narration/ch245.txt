Lesson 1 of 5.
नालिका (nālikā) — a tube, a pipe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for other, then the Sanskrit for alone.

You'll want to know: नालिका.
नालिका — nālikā — "a tube, a pipe".

The word, taken apart.
nālikā is built on nāla, "hollow stalk, tube", which MW derives from nala or naḍa, "reed". CDIAL 7047 lists nālikā "stalk" under nāḍī, "tube", the word for the pulse: a pipe is a hollow reed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
तक्रम् (takram) — buttermilk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for alone, then the Sanskrit for a tube.

You'll want to know: तक्रम्.
तक्रम् — takram — "buttermilk".

The word, taken apart.
takra is buttermilk thinned with water (MW). MW and CDIAL 5617 both link it to the root √tañc, "to contract", as milk curdles, and CDIAL compares Persian talx, "bitter". It lives on as Marathi and Gujarati tāk, "buttermilk".

Lesson 3 of 5.
फलरसः (phalarasaḥ) — fruit juice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a tube, then the Sanskrit for buttermilk.

You'll want to know: फलरसः.
फलरसः — phalarasaḥ — "fruit juice".

The word, taken apart.
phalarasa is a compound of phala, "fruit", and rasa, "sap, juice" (MW). Rasa is a Rigvedic word for the juice of plants, later also "taste" (CDIAL 10650), and it lives on as Hindi ras, "juice".

Lesson 4 of 5.
लवंगम् (lavaṅgam) — a clove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for buttermilk, then the Sanskrit for fruit juice.

You'll want to know: लवंगम्.
लवंगम् — lavaṅgam — "a clove".

The word, taken apart.
lavaṅga names the clove tree and its buds, first in the poets (CDIAL 10977). MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi laũg, "clove".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for fruit juice, then the Sanskrit for a clove.

You'll want to know: एला.
एला — elā — "cardamom".

The word, taken apart.
elā is probably borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 2522 follows Mayrhofer in taking it from a Dravidian source. It lives on as Marathi eḷā, "cardamom".
