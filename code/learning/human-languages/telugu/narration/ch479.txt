Telugu, chapter 479: Thunder and Downpours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
పిడుగు (piḍugu) — a thunderbolt; a lightning strike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a rainstorm, then the Telugu for to lose strength.

You'll want to know: పిడుగు.
పిడుగు — piḍugu — "a thunderbolt; a lightning strike".
పొలంలో తాటిచెట్టు మీద పిడుగు పడింది. polaṁlō tāṭiceṭṭu mīda piḍugu paḍindi. Lightning struck a palm tree in the field.
పిడుగు (piḍugu) పడు, lightning strikes. Bad news that comes out of nowhere is also a పిడుగు (piḍugu).

The word, taken apart.
పిడుగు (piḍugu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 4150 lists Telugu piḍugu, "a thunderbolt, lightning", with Kannada piḍugu.

Lesson 2 of 5.
కుండపోత (kuṇḍapōta) — a torrential rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to lose strength, then the Telugu for a thunderbolt.

You'll want to know: కుండపోత.
కుండపోత — kuṇḍapōta — "a torrential rain".
సాయంత్రం కుండపోత వర్షం కురిసింది. sāyantraṁ kuṇḍapōta varṣaṁ kurisindi. In the evening it poured with rain.
కుండ, a pot, + పోత, pouring: rain as if poured from a pot. Mostly కుండపోత (kuṇḍapōta) వర్షం.

The word, taken apart.
కుండపోత (kuṇḍapōta) is a Telugu compound built from కుండ, "a pot", and పోత, "pouring", the noun of పోయు, "to pour" (DEDR 4407).

Lesson 3 of 5.
కమ్ము (kammu) — to close in; to envelop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a thunderbolt, then the Telugu for a torrential rain.

You'll want to know: కమ్ము.
కమ్ము — kammu — "to close in; to envelop".
ఆకాశంలో నల్లని మబ్బులు కమ్మాయి. ākāśaṁlō nallani mabbulu kammāyi. Dark clouds closed in across the sky.
Past (it …): కమ్మింది, kammindi.
Said of clouds, smoke or darkness that cover everything. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: కమ్మింది, it closed in.

The word, taken apart.
కమ్ము (kammu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1221 lists Telugu kammu, "to cover, to surround, to overspread", beside kappu, "to spread over in a thick covering, as clouds".

Lesson 4 of 5.
మసకబారు (masakabāru) — to grow dim; to blur.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a torrential rain, then the Telugu for to close in.

You'll want to know: మసకబారు.
మసకబారు — masakabāru — "to grow dim; to blur".
వర్షంలో దూరం అంతా మసకబారింది. varṣaṁlō dūraṁ antā masakabārindi. In the rain everything in the distance grew dim.
Past (it …): మసకబారింది, masakabārindi.
మసక, hazy, + బారు, to become. Old glasses and old eyes also మసకబారు (masakabāru). Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: మసకబారింది, it grew dim.

The word, taken apart.
మసకబారు (masakabāru) is a Telugu compound: మసక, "dim, hazy", native Dravidian (DEDR 4627), and -బారు, "to become", whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
తుంపర (tumpara) — spray; fine drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to close in, then the Telugu for to grow dim.

You'll want to know: తుంపర.
తుంపర — tumpara — "spray; fine drizzle".
జలపాతం దగ్గర తుంపర మా మీద పడింది. jalapātaṁ daggara tumpara mā mīda paḍindi. Near the waterfall the spray fell on us.
Fine drops of water: from a waterfall, a wave or a light rain.

The word, taken apart.
తుంపర (tumpara) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3398 lists Telugu tuppara, "a drop of water, a thin drizzle", with the variant tumpara.
