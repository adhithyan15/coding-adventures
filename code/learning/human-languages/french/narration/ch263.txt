French, chapter 263: At the Station — un aller-retour, j'ai raté le train.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
un aller-retour — there and back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket". (Un billet.) Say "the return". (Le retour.) Then ask a stranger for a coffee. (Je voudrais un café.)
[pause 1 second]
You are at the ticket window, and you know the polite opener already.

You'll want to know: un aller-retour, un aller simple.
un aller-retour — "a return ticket". un aller simple — "a one-way ticket".
Here aller is a noun: l'aller is the outward journey, and le retour is the way back. So an aller-retour is "a going and a coming back", and an aller simple is the going on its own. Both are masculine, un, and the word billet is usually left out.

Grammar Lens: at the window.
The place you are going to takes pour, "for":
Je voudrais un aller-retour pour Paris, s'il vous plaît. — Un aller simple pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît.
Then the three questions that get you onto the right train, said in one breath: À quelle heure part le train? De quel quai? C'est combien? — "What time does the train leave? From which platform? How much is it?"
And the answer you will hear back puts the hour and the platform side by side: Le train part à dix heures, quai trois.

The phrase, taken apart.
aller has three Latin parents, and its infinitive is the one usually traced to Latin ambulāre, "to walk". retour is formed from the verb for "to return", re- plus tourner, "to turn", which is inherited from Latin tornāre (EtymDB 2.1). simple is inherited from Latin simplex, "single, plain" (EtymDB 2.1). So un aller simple is, at root, "a single walk out", and the ticket home is a turning back.

[your turn — say: Un aller simple pour Lyon.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which ticket brings you home again? (Un aller-retour.) Which word comes before the city? (Pour.) What is l'aller, as a noun? (The outward journey, the going.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
j'ai raté le train — when it goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask for a one-way ticket to Paris. (Un aller simple pour Paris, s'il vous plaît.)
[pause 1 second]
Now the train has gone without you.

You'll want to know: j'ai raté le train.
j'ai raté le train — "I've missed the train".
rater is a regular -er verb, the everyday one for anything that leaves without you:
J'ai raté le train. — I've missed the train. J'ai raté le métro. — I've missed the metro.
You also have manquer, which says the same thing a little more carefully: j'ai manqué le train. In Québec, manquer is the more usual verb for it. And for a thing that is gone, not a train that has left, the verb is perdre: j'ai perdu ma valise, I've lost my suitcase.
English hesitates between "I missed" and "I've missed". French uses the passé composé for both: one finished event.

Grammar Lens: reporting a problem.
A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:
Excusez-moi, j'ai un problème. — a courtesy. J'ai raté le train. — what went wrong. Le prochain train part à quelle heure? — what you need.
Or the fault is not yours: le train est en retard, "the train is late". Say what happened, then ask the question that gets you moving again.

The phrase, taken apart.
rater has no parent in EtymDB 2.1, which lists among its senses a gun that misfires. Where the word itself comes from is not settled by either source, so it is left open here. manquer was borrowed from Italian mancare, "to be lacking", which goes back to Latin mancus, "maimed, powerless" (EtymDB 2.1). retard comes from the verb retarder, which goes back to Latin retardāre, "to keep back, to delay" (Lewis & Short, under retardo).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: J'ai raté le train. — then J'ai raté le métro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three parts — Excusez-moi, j'ai un problème. J'ai raté le train. Le prochain train part à quelle heure?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the fault that is not yours — Le train est en retard.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what you need, with this ticket — Je peux prendre le prochain train avec ce billet?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "I've missed the train". (J'ai raté le train.) Which verb says it more carefully, and is the more usual one in Québec? (Manquer.) Name the three parts of a complaint. (A courtesy, what went wrong, what you need.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — a journey that goes wrong, and is put right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a return ticket". (Un aller-retour.) Then "I've missed the train". (J'ai raté le train.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, and a whole journey you can handle alone. Find the way: excusez-moi, pour aller à…? Buy the ticket: un aller-retour pour…. And when it goes wrong, say so in three parts: a courtesy, what happened, what you need.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here is the whole of it, told afterwards as a story:
J'avais un aller-retour pour Paris. Quand je suis arrivé sur le quai, le train est parti. Donc j'ai demandé: « Excusez-moi, le prochain train part à quelle heure? ».
The scene in the imparfait, the events in the passé composé, and the question quoted whole. Est parti is partir in the passé composé, with être.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: at the window — Je voudrais un aller-retour pour Paris, s'il vous plaît.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: on the platform — Excusez-moi, j'ai un problème. J'ai raté le train.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the story, afterwards — Quand je suis arrivé sur le quai, le train est parti.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: directions to the platform — Allez tout droit, puis tournez à gauche.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the three parts of a complaint? (A courtesy, what went wrong, what you need.) Which ticket is one-way? (Un aller simple.) In the story, why is j'avais in the imparfait? (The ticket in your pocket is scene.)
