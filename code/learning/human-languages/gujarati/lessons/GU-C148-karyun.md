## Grammar Lens: -્યું on the stem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C148-YUN-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C05-KAAMKARVUN-01, GU-LEX-C55-HOUSEWORK-03, GU-LEX-JOVU, GU-LEX-C142-ACT142-01, GU-LEX-LAKHVU] -->

Most verbs build this past in one move: take the stem and add **-્યું**, a
virama and then **યું**, so the stem's last consonant joins the **ય**.

- **કર-** → **કર્યું** — *karyũ* — did (the **ર** rides on top as a small hook);
- **આપ-** → **આપ્યું** — *āpyũ* — gave;
- **લખ-** → **લખ્યું** — *lakhyũ* — wrote;
- **કહ-** → **કહ્યું** — *kahyũ* — said;
- **જો-** → **જોયું** — *joyũ* — saw (a stem ending in a vowel takes **યું** with no virama).

> **મેં કામ કર્યું.** — *mẽ kām karyũ.* — I did the work.
> **તેણે પત્ર લખ્યો.** — *teṇe patra lakhyo.* — She wrote a letter.
> **મેં તેને પૈસા આપ્યા.** — *mẽ tene paisā āpyā.* — I gave him the money.
> **તેણે મને પત્ર આપ્યો.** — *teṇe mane patra āpyo.* — She gave me a letter.
> **મેં બે ફોન કર્યા.** — *mẽ be phon karyā.* — I made two phone calls.

The four endings again: **-્યો**, **-ી**, **-્યું**, **-્યા**. **પત્ર** is
masculine, so **લખ્યો**; **પૈસા** is plural, so **આપ્યા**. In the feminine the
**ય** drops: **કરી**, **આપી**, **જોઈ**.
