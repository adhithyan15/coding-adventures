## You'll want to know: 开门
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C155-KAIMEN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**开门** — *kāimén* — "to open (for business); to open the door".

> **星期天不开门** *xīngqītiān bù kāimén* — It's closed on Sundays.

> **早上九点开门** *zǎoshang jiǔ diǎn kāimén* — It opens at nine in the morning.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**开门** (*kāimén*) is built from 开 'to open' and 门 'a door'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *khoj* and *mwon* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), *khoj* glossed 'to open; to undo'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-CONG-DAO-01, ZH-LEX-C46-WEEK46-05] -->

A shop that 开门 is open for business, not only its door. On a sign the hours come first: 早上九点到晚上八点开门.

不 stays *bù* before the level tone of kāi: 不开门.
