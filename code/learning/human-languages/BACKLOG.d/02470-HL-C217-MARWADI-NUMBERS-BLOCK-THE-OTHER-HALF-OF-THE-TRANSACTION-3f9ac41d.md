## HL-C217 — Marwadi can ask a price but can never hear one

Chapters 26--31 close the gap HL-C217 named. One five-turn counter exchange is
taught once and run unchanged over the shopping, transport, and food nouns the
track already had: **ये कपड़ा दिखावो। / ये कितणे कू है? / ये घणो महंगो है। /
थोड़ु सस्तो करो। / पैसा ले लो।**, plus a closing question and a food order.
The track goes from 53 to 71 distinct taught headwords, and the five asking
words **दिखावो, करो, लो, दो, लावो** close the HL09 3.1 verb-vocabulary
criterion at pre-A1, which had stood at 0 of 5. Vocabulary is now the only
blocker on the level.

What the exchange still cannot do, and what blocks it:

- **The seller's turn is missing entirely, and digits are why.** Every line in
  the tranche is a buyer's line. The learner can ask what something costs and
  can never understand the answer, because the Devanagari digits **०--९** and
  the number words have no lesson anywhere in the track. That is a single
  well-defined slice -- ten signs and the counting words -- and until it lands,
  no priced answer, no total, and no agreed final price can be taught. It is
  now the highest-value next slice, ahead of any further function.
- **Refusal has no words at all.** Accepting is covered by *lo*; refusing a
  final price and walking away is not, and neither is the contrasting *no*
  response that the track has owed since Chapter 3's **हां सा**.
- **The courtesy turns around the transaction are untaught.** Thanking somebody
  for doing what you asked, a you're-welcome response, okay, and asking again
  more softly all sit outside what these six chapters teach.

The script constraint moved but did not disappear. **ख** now has a lesson; the
untaught inventory is **झ, ञ, ढ, ध, फ, ष, ळ**, the independent vowels other
than **आ** and **ई**, and **ृ**. That is what kept **चाहिए** and **और** out of
this tranche despite both being printed in the source, and any candidate
sentence still has to be checked against the taught set before it is scheduled.

### A reusable pattern for the script-review gap

Corpus-wide a `SCRIPT-RECOG` atom is reviewed about twice against about
fourteen times for a `LEX` atom, against an owner directive of "reviewed
constantly". This tranche tried the cheapest fix that does not slow the ramp:
**every chapter ends in a script close that asks for its most-leaned-on signs
ALONE, on their own line, from a sound cue -- never inside a word** -- and one
lesson in the final chapter does nothing else. A sign that is only ever read
inside a word has been shown, not taught, and only an isolated retrieval tells
the two apart.

The cost is one lesson per chapter. The measured effect on Marwadi: `SCRIPT`
atoms moved from a mean of 7.59 reviews to 9.81 and now sit above the track's
`LEX` atoms at 9.64; the least-drilled single sign went from three reviews to
six, and 39 of 41 sign atoms are at seven or more. The tranche's own zeros were
unaffected. Any track can copy this without re-planning its content, because
the closes review material the chapter already taught.
