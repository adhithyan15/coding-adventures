# Review — what the surface does not tell you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PRONTO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] What does **pronto** mean on its own? (**Soon** — and "ready", the
sense English kept in *prompt*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PRONTO, ES-PRAGMATICS-SORRY-03, ES-GRAMMAR-PRONOUN-REVEALS-GENDER, ES-SOUND-HERMANO-04] -->

Four things you met separately, gathered because they share a shape: the part
you can see is not the part to trust.

[PAUSE 2s] **First.** *Pronto* is about **readiness**, from *promptus*; "soon"
is where readiness ended up.

[PAUSE 2s] **Second.** *Lo siento* and *perdón* both come out as English
"sorry". The English does not divide them; the Latin does. One names **your**
feeling, the other asks for **their** pardon.

[PAUSE 2s] **Third.** *El agua* looks masculine and is not. The article moved
for **sound**; the object pronoun did not, and says ***la***.

[PAUSE 2s] **Fourth.** The *h* on *hermano* looks like the *h* on *hijo*. One
stood where a Latin *f* was breathed aloud for centuries; the other went on the
page long after the sound had gone.

## Grammar Lens: the mark that was designed, not inherited
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-HISTORY-RAE-INVERTED-MARKS] -->

The **¿** belongs on that list from the other side. It is not a survival: the
Spanish Royal Academy made it official in **1754**, because Spanish leaves the
words of a question where they were and something had to warn the reader.

[PAUSE 2s] Four inherited surfaces that mislead, and one deliberate surface that
helps.
