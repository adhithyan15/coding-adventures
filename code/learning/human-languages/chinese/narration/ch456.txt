Mandarin Chinese, chapter 456: Lost and Found.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zǒusàn — to get separated (in a crowd).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for still valid, then the Mandarin for to apply for.

You'll want to know: zǒusàn.
zǒusàn — "to get separated (in a crowd)".
wǒ hé péngyou zài huǒchēzhàn zǒusàn le — My friend and I lost each other at the station.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zǒusàn is built from 走 'to walk' and sàn 'to scatter', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsuwX and sanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to run' and the second 'to break apart'.

我的地图不见了我问了一个路人 wǒ de dìtú bú jiàn le wǒ wèn le yí ge lùrén — My map had gone, so I asked a passer-by.

rènlǐng is built from rèn 'to recognise' and lǐng 'to receive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyinH and ljengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to know' and the second 'to take charge of'; the sense 'to receive' is not given there.
