Portuguese, chapter 502: Pescar e caçar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pescar — to fish; to catch (a fish).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for watering can, then the Portuguese for flowerpot.

You'll want to know: pescar.
pescar — "to fish; to catch (a fish)".
Ao domingo, o meu tio vai pescar para o rio. On Sundays, my uncle goes fishing on the river.
Ir pescar — to go fishing.

The word, taken apart.
pescar is inherited, through Vulgar Latin piscāre, from Latin piscārī "to fish" (EtymDB), built on piscis "fish".

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: o anzol.
o anzol — "fish hook".
O peixe ficou preso no anzol. The fish was caught on the hook.
Plural anzóis.

The word, taken apart.
anzol is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin diminutive built on Latin hāmus "a hook" (Lewis & Short); the exact form is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
caçar — to hunt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for bait, then the Portuguese for fish hook.

You'll want to know: caçar.
caçar — "to hunt".
Antigamente, os homens da aldeia caçavam coelhos. In the old days, the men of the village hunted rabbits.
Hunting is a caça; a hunter is um caçador.

The word, taken apart.
caçar is inherited, through Old Portuguese, from Late Latin captiāre "to try to catch" (EtymDB), built on capere "to take".
