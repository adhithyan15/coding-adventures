# ¿Vamos al teatro o al circo? — the five places in a real question

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A place you can name is not yet a place you can propose. Ask a friend
whether they want to go to the theatre or the circus, out loud, before reading
on.

## Grammar Lens: al and a la, doing the gender work for you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-03, ES-LEX-C354-WHERE-28] -->

Proposing an outing needs *a* ("to") in front of the place, and the article
comes along:

> *¿Vamos **al** teatro?* — masculine, so *a + el* squeezes into **al**.
> *¿Vamos **a la** exposición?* — feminine, so the two words stay apart.

Four of your five places take **al**. Only *la exposición* takes **a la**, and
you can predict which without remembering, because **-ción** told you the gender
when you met the word.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-03, ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-25, ES-LEX-C354-WHERE-28, ES-LEX-BAILAR] -->

Every word here is one you have met:

> —¿Vamos al cine?
> —No. Vamos al teatro.
> —¿Y el sábado?
> —El sábado, a la exposición del museo. Y el domingo, al circo.
> —¿Y después del circo?
> —Después, a bailar.

Four places, one short exchange, and the only thing you had to decide each time
was **al** or **a la**.

The last line is the exception that proves it. *Bailar* is a verb, not a place,
so it takes plain **a** and no article at all — the same reason *me gusta
bailar* needed none.
