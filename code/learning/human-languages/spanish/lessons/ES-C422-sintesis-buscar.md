# ¿Cuál es la página web? — the question a form actually asks you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C422-TECH-01, ES-LEX-CORREO-ELECTRONICO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la página web* and *el correo electrónico* one after the other.
Both are things a form will ask you for.

## Grammar Lens: the line on a form is the bare noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C422-TECH-01, ES-LEX-CORREO-ELECTRONICO] -->

A printed form drops the article; a person speaking puts it back:

| on the form | spoken aloud |
|---|---|
| Correo electrónico: | ¿Cuál es **el** correo electrónico? |
| Página web: | ¿Cuál es **la** página web? |

This is worth noticing because a reading paper shows you the left-hand column
and a listening paper plays you the right-hand one. The same word looks bare in
one and dressed in the other.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C422-TECH-01, ES-LEX-CORREO-ELECTRONICO, ES-LEX-INTERNET] -->

> **Formulario**
> Nombre:
> Correo electrónico:
> Página web:

Three lines, no articles, and you can now read every one of them.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C422-TECH-01, ES-LEX-CORREO-ELECTRONICO] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Why does a form print *Página web:* without *la*? (A form label is

you say it aloud? (*La*.)
