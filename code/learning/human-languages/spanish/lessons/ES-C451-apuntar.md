# apuntar — put it down

## You'll want to know: apuntar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C342-CLOCK-03] -->

**apuntar** — to note down, to jot.

*Apunta el número* — write the number down. *Voy a apuntarlo* — I'll make a
note of it.

And with **-se**, the meaning most useful at a counter: **apuntarse**, to put
your name down, to sign up.

*Me apunto a la excursión* — I'm putting my name down for the trip.
*¿Te apuntas?* — are you
in? That last one is an invitation to anything at all, and it is the phrase
you will hear when a group is deciding something.

## Grammar Lens: apuntar is not escribir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-02, ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-01] -->

| what you are doing | the verb |
|---|---|
| producing text | **escribir** |
| capturing something before it escapes | **apuntar** |

You *escribes* a letter, an essay, a message. You *apuntas* a phone number, an
address, a time somebody has said aloud. The difference is not length; it is
purpose. *Apuntar* is always about not losing the thing.

That is why *apuntarse* means signing up: you are getting your name captured
onto a list before the places run out.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-02, ES-LEX-C342-CLOCK-03] -->

**a-punt-ar**, built straight on **el punto**, and behind that Latin
**punctum**, a prick — from **pungere**, to pierce.

So *apuntar* is to put a point on something. All three of its senses are that
one action: to note a thing down is to mark a point on paper, to *apuntar* a
weapon is to aim its point, and to *apuntarse* is to put a point beside your
own name.

The family is wide. A **puncture** is a piercing, a **punctual** person hits
the point in time, and **puntiagudo** in Spanish is sharp-pointed. Every one
of them has a small sharp thing in it.
