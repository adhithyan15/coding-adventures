# (ಕ ಗ ಚ ಝ ಞ ಥ ಪ ಯ ಸ ಹ) — ten, gathered at last

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-116, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-112] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two that differ by voice alone. Read **ಕ**, then **ಗ**, and say what
your throat does on the second that it did not do on the first.

## Script: ten, sorted by where they are made
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-116, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-112, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-122, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-128, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-133, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-129, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-124] -->

These ten arrived one per chapter, scattered from the book's opening to well past
its middle, and no page ever put them side by side. Sorted by the mouth rather
than by the order you met them:

| where | letters |
|---|---|
| **throat** | **ಕ** *ka* · **ಗ** *ga* |
| **palate** | **ಚ** *ca* · **ಝ** *jha* · **ಞ** *ña* |
| **teeth** | **ಥ** *tha* |
| **lips** | **ಪ** *pa* |

Read down the middle row. **ಚ**, **ಝ** and **ಞ** are made in the same place, and
they differ only in voice, breath and nasality — which is the organising idea the
whole Kannada consonant grid is built on.

**ಞ** is the one to look at twice. It is the nasal of the palatal row and is rare
on the page; a reader who has not seen it since the chapter that taught it will
not place it at all.
