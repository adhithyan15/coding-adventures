# (reading) — six notices

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you single words. This one gives each word
something to stand on.

[PAUSE 1s] Still nothing new — the same signs, with **है** behind them.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-01, HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-02] -->

> दुकान बंद है।
> स्टेशन खुला है।
> यहाँ पानी है।
> टिकट यहाँ है।
> प्रवेश यहाँ है।
> कृपया धीरे।

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and this time notice you did not have to work out what was
shut. The notice said so.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C69-GOING-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **दुकान बंद है।** Three words, and the last one is the verb. Hindi
puts it there every time, so the end of a notice is where you find out whether
something *is* or *is not*.

[PAUSE 2s] That is worth more than it looks. It means you can read the first
two words of any notice, guess, and then have your guess confirmed or corrected
by the last word — which is exactly how a fluent reader reads.

[PAUSE 2s] The last line breaks the pattern: **कृपया धीरे।** No verb at all.
Signs drop the verb when nobody could mistake the meaning, and *please, slowly*
needs no help.
