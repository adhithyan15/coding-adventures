# Six lines about a trip

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-SCENE-EVENT-01, FA-SKILL-READ-NARRATIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter two hundred and eighty-nine asked for four lines that held
together. This one asks for six, and they tell a story.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-STORY-ORDER-01, FA-GRAMMAR-BAD-THEN-01, FA-GRAMMAR-AGAR-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-UNREAL-CONDITION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A told story is not a list of events. It has a **scene**, in *bud*
and the *mi-* past. It has **events** in the plain past, put in order with
*avval*, *ba'd*, *vaqti* and *dar âkhar*. It has at least one **reason**, with
*chon* or *barâ-ye hamin*, and a **feeling**, with *budam* or *shodam*.

[PAUSE 2s] A good one ends by looking forward or wishing: *omidvâram ke...*,
*agar ... bâshad, ...*, or *agar vaqt dâshtam, ... miraftam*.

[PAUSE 2s] The B1 writing paper asks for a connected text of 130 to 170
words. Six lines is the first step towards it, not the whole of it.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-PAST-CONTINUOUS-01, FA-GRAMMAR-SIMPLE-PAST-01, FA-LEX-C290-GOZASHTE-01, FA-GRAMMAR-DAR-AKHAR-01, FA-GRAMMAR-VAGHTI-01, FA-GRAMMAR-BA-WITH-01, FA-GRAMMAR-BARAYE-HAMIN-01, FA-LEX-C92-WHY92-03, FA-GRAMMAR-CAUSE-NOUN-OR-CLAUSE-01, FA-GRAMMAR-PISH-AGO-01, FA-GRAMMAR-FEELING-BUDAM-01, FA-GRAMMAR-FEELING-SHODAM-01, FA-GRAMMAR-DURATION-AST-KE-01, FA-GRAMMAR-REAL-CONDITION-01, FA-GRAMMAR-WOULD-MI-PAST-01, FA-GRAMMAR-DUST-DASHTAM-01, FA-GRAMMAR-KASH-01, FA-GRAMMAR-OMIDVARAM-KE-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] On paper, right to left, write **six lines** to your reader about a
trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:

1. the scene: when it was, with *gozashte* or *pish*, and the weather;
2. where you went, who was with you, with *bâ*, and for how long;
3. the first thing that happened, with *avval*;
4. the next thing, with *ba'd* or *vaqti*, and a reason, with *chon*, *be khâter-e* or *barâ-ye hamin*;
5. how it ended and how you felt, with *dar âkhar* and *budam* or *shodam*;
6. one line forward: *omidvâram ke*, a real *agar*, or *agar vaqt dâshtam*.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing
the right past for each line, and joining them.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-VAV-01, FA-SCRIPT-C296-GHEYN-01, FA-SCRIPT-C26-GHAF-01, FA-GRAMMAR-SCENE-EVENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now read your lines as you read the winter trip in chapter two
hundred and ninety-one. Mark every verb **S** for scene or **E** for event.
Line one should be mostly **S**; lines three and four mostly **E**. A line
about the weather marked **E** is the commonest slip: change *sard shod* back
to *sard bud*.

[PAUSE 2s] Then three spelling checks. Every *mi-* is joined to its verb,
**میرفتم**. Every **و** is followed by a break smaller than the space between
words. And each word keeps its own letter: **غذا** with **غ**, **قطار** with
**ق**, though the two sound alike.

[PAUSE 2s] Last, read line six alone. If it has *agar* and something that may
happen, is the verb after *agar* in the subjunctive? If it is a wish against
the facts, is it in the plain past, with the *mi-* past after it?
