# কালো (kalo) — "black," the same word as "time"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C14-NIL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Blue carried a false friend. This color hides something bigger
inside it: the very word Sanskrit uses for time itself, and for death.

## You'll want to know: কালো
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C14-KALO-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **কালো** — *kālo* — **black**

কালো চা (*kālo chā*) — black tea.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**কালো** is **ক** (*kô*) with the vowel sign **া** (*ā*), plus **ল** (*l*)
with the vowel sign **ো** (*o*): *kā-lo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**কালো** is built on Sanskrit **কাল** (*kāla*), a word that covers both
**"black, dark"** *and* **"time."** Sanskrit's own tradition ties the two
together poetically — time devours everything, and that devouring is
imagined as dark — and the same word names the fierce goddess **Kālī**
("the black/dark one") and stands as an epithet of **Yama**, the god of
death. Hindi's own *kālā* carries the identical root and the identical
story. Be honest, though: whether "black" and "time" are truly **one and
the same root**, or two separate ancient words that poetry and theology
later **fused** into a single idea, is a real, still-debated question among
linguists — the cultural connection is certain; the precise etymology
underneath it is not.
