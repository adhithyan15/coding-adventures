Bengali, chapter 421: Goods and Stock.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মাল (māl) — goods; merchandise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a village market, then the Bengali for to be sold.

You'll want to know: মাল.
মাল — māl — "goods; merchandise".
কুলিরা ট্রাক থেকে মাল নামাল। kulirā ṭrāk theke māl nāmālô. The porters unloaded the goods from the lorry.
মালপত্র, luggage, is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
মাল (māl) is usually traced to Arabic māl, "property, wealth, goods", which reached Bengali through Persian.

দোকানদার গুদামে চালের বস্তা রাখলেন। dokāndār gudāme chāler bôstā rākhlen. The shopkeeper stored the sacks of rice in the warehouse.

গুদাম (gudām) is usually traced to Malay gudang, "a storehouse", carried round the Indian Ocean by Portuguese traders; English godown is the same word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
পাইকারি (pāikāri) — wholesale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a warehouse, then the Bengali for an order (for goods or food).

You'll want to know: পাইকারি.
পাইকারি — pāikāri — "wholesale".
দোকানদার বড় বাজার থেকে পাইকারি দামে মাল কেনেন। dokāndār bôṛo bājār theke pāikāri dāme māl kenen. The shopkeeper buys goods at wholesale prices from the big market.
The opposite is খুচরো, retail, the same word as small change.

The word, taken apart.
পাইকারি (pāikāri) is usually traced to Persian paikār, "a trader, a retail dealer", with the Bengali ending -ই.

Lesson 5 of 5.
মজুত করা (môjut kôrā) — to stock up; to store.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an order (for goods or food), then the Bengali for wholesale.

You'll want to know: মজুত করা.
মজুত করা — môjut kôrā — "to stock up; to store".
শীতের আগে দোকানদার কম্বল মজুত করলেন। shīter āge dokāndār kômbôl môjut kôrlen. Before winter the shopkeeper stocked up on blankets.
Past (he/she …): তিনি চাল মজুত করলেন, tini chāl môjut kôrlen.
Also said of traders who hold back goods to push up the price.

The word, taken apart.
মজুত করা (môjut kôrā) joins মজুত, "in stock", usually traced to Arabic mawjūd, "existing, at hand", through Persian, to করা, to do.
