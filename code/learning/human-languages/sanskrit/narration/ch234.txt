Lesson 1 of 5.
शुष्यति (śuṣyati) — dries up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for follows, then the Sanskrit for goes out.

You'll want to know: शुष्यति.
शुष्यति — śuṣyati — "dries up".

The word, taken apart.
śuṣyati is the present of the root √śuṣ, "to dry, wither" (MW). MW thinks the root was first suṣ, and compares Greek auō, "I dry", and Lithuanian sausas, "dry", so it is inherited from Indo-European.

The word, taken apart.
plavate is the present of the root √plu, "to float, swim" (MW), a Rigvedic verb (CDIAL 9024). The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Greek pleō, "I sail", and Latin pluit, "it rains".

Lesson 3 of 5.
आरभते (ārabhate) — begins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dries up, then the Sanskrit for floats.

You'll want to know: आरभते.
आरभते — ārabhate — "begins".

The word, taken apart.
ārabhate is built on ā- and the root √rabh, "to take hold of" (MW), which MW connects with √labh, "to obtain". In the Rigveda it means "seizes", and from the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa "undertakes, begins" (CDIAL 1305): to begin is to take hold of a task.

Lesson 4 of 5.
समापयति (samāpayati) — finishes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for floats, then the Sanskrit for begins.

You'll want to know: समापयति.
समापयति — samāpayati — "finishes".

The word, taken apart.
samāpayati is the causative of sam-āp, "to obtain completely", from the root √āp, "to reach, obtain" (MW). It first meant "causes to gain completely" and then "finishes" (CDIAL 13216). MW compares √āp with Latin apīscor, "I reach", and aptus, "fitted".

Lesson 5 of 5.
प्रयच्छति (prayacchati) — hands over, gives.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for begins, then the Sanskrit for finishes.

You'll want to know: प्रयच्छति.
प्रयच्छति — prayacchati — "hands over, gives".

The word, taken apart.
prayacchati is built on pra, "forth", and the root √yam, whose present is yacchati, "holds" (MW). To hold something out towards someone is to hand it over, so MW gives both "stretches forth" and "offers, gives".
