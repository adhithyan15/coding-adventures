# descontar — to deduct

## Warm-up

## You'll want to know: descontar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CONTAR-08] -->

**descontar** — to deduct, to take off.

> Te lo **descuentan** en doce meses.

*El descuento* is the noun — a discount. *Con descuento* — at a reduced price.

Note the stem: *desc**ue**nto*, *desc**ue**ntan*. The **o** breaks into **ue**
under stress, exactly as it does in *contar* itself — *cuento*, *cuentas*.

## Grammar Lens: the des- that reverses, and the one that does not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-03, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04] -->

[Cubrir](./ES-C444-cubrir.md) set this out plainly: *des-* sometimes undoes the
verb and sometimes does nothing of the kind, and only the whole word tells you
which.

| word | what des- does |
|---|---|
| **descubrir** | takes the cover off — it reverses |
| *disfrutar* | nothing — the prefix does not reverse |
| **descontar** | **takes off the count — it reverses** |

This one is in the first camp, and cleanly. *Contar* adds up; *descontar*
takes away from the total. If you can count the thing, *des-* will subtract it.

*Descansar* is the same family — *des-* undoing *cansar*, to tire — and it is
where you first met the prefix doing real work.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C474-PAGO-03, ES-LEX-CONTAR-08] -->

*Contar* is Latin **computare**: **com-**, together, plus **putare**, to
reckon. So counting is a **reckoning-together**, and *descontar* is a reckoning
taken back out.

That *computare* is where English **compute** and **count** both come from —
the second worn down through French — so *contar* and *count* are the same
word wearing different amounts of Latin.

And *putare* first meant to **prune** a tree. Reckoning, in Latin, began as
cutting away what does not belong, which makes *descontar* an oddly exact
return to the root.
