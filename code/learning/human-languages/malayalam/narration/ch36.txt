Malayalam, chapter 36: Child, Son, Daughter.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
കുട്ടി (kuṭṭi) — "child, kid," the general word before the specific ones.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Suhṛttŭ named someone you choose. This chapter turns to someone you don't — the people a family is built around.

You'll want to know.
കുട്ടി — kuṭṭi — child, kid.
ഇത് എന്റെ കുട്ടിയാണ്. — ithu ente kuṭṭiyāṇŭ. — "This is my child."
കുട്ടി (kuṭṭi) is the general word — any child, before you say whose son or daughter.

The word, taken apart.
കുട്ടി is native Dravidian, matching Tamil's குட்டி (kuṭṭi) closely. Both languages let the same word cover two things: a human child, and the young of an animal — a പൂച്ചക്കുട്ടി (pūccakkuṭṭi) is a kitten, built on പൂച്ച, "cat," met earlier in this book, the same way kuṭṭi attaches to any animal name for its young. English does something similar with "kid," which covers both a child and a baby goat — one small word, carrying the same double duty in two unrelated language families.

Which English word carries the very same double duty? (Kid.) Name the six family people-words once more, and the group word none of them supplied. Lesson 2 of 3.
മകൻ (makan) — "son," a word Tamil hardly changed at all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Kuṭṭi was the general word. Now the two specific ones — starting with the son.

You'll want to know.
മകൻ — makan — son.
ഇത് എന്റെ മകനാണ്. — ithu ente makanāṇŭ. — "This is my son."

The word, taken apart.
മകൻ continues Proto-Dravidian makan, "son." Tamil keeps almost the identical shape, மகன் (makan) — same consonants, same vowels, same final -n. Kannada moved further: the -k- between vowels softened to -g-, and the final -n dropped, giving ಮಗ (maga). So on this word it is Malayalam and Tamil that hold the older, more conservative shape, and Kannada that innovated — the same closeness the very first greetings already showed in nandi, athe, illa and śari, this time in a family word rather than a greeting.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ithu ente makanāṇŭ" — this is my son]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the two cousins — "makan" (Malayalam, Tamil) … "maga" (Kannada)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: general then specific — "kuṭṭi … makan"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the friend-word, once more — "suhṛttŭ" — su plus hṛd]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "this is my son." (Ithu ente makanāṇŭ.) Which sister keeps almost the identical word? (Tamil — makan.) What did Kannada change? (Softened -k- to -g- and dropped the final -n, giving maga.) What does suhṛttŭ literally mean? ("Good-heart" — su plus hṛd.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
മകൾ (makaḷ) — "daughter," the closest match yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Makan matched Tamil closely. Its pair matches even closer — and gives you a reason to ask an old question again.

You'll want to know.
മകൾ — makaḷ — daughter.
ഇത് എന്റെ മകളാണ്. — ithu ente makaḷāṇŭ. — "This is my daughter."

The word, taken apart.
മകൾ continues Proto-Dravidian makaḷ, "daughter." Tamil's மகள் (makaḷ) is not just close — it is identical, sound for sound. Kannada shows the same softening you just met in maga: ಮಗಳು (magaḷu), -k- to -g-, plus a closing -u. So where makan/makan was already a close match, makaḷ/makaḷ is the closer one still — two sisters holding one Proto-Dravidian root letter for letter, four thousand years and a script apart.
Now that you have a son and a daughter, the age question comes back with someone new to ask it about: ninṟe makanŭ ethra vayassuṇṭŭ? — "how old is your son?" — the same dative-subject-plus-uṇṭŭ shape, unchanged.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ithu ente makaḷāṇŭ" — this is my daughter]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the identical pair — "makaḷ, makaḷ" — Malayalam, Tamil]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: son, daughter, both cousins — "makan … maga; makaḷ … magaḷu"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the age question, asked again — "ninṟe makanŭ ethra vayassuṇṭŭ?"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: general, then specific, twice over — "kuṭṭi … makan … makaḷ"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "this is my daughter." (Ithu ente makaḷāṇŭ.) How closely does Tamil match this word, compared with makan? (Even closer — makaḷ is identical sound for sound, where makan only nearly matched.) Ask "how old is your son?" using the age question's shape. (Ninṟe makanŭ ethra vayassuṇṭŭ? — "to your son, how much age exists?") What is the general word for a child, before makan or makaḷ? (കുട്ടി, kuṭṭi.)
