# (reading) — the first paragraph

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-SKILL-READ-DETAILS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six instructions, then six lines off a form. Now a whole paragraph.

[PAUSE 1s] Sixty-three words, and not one of them is new.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[FR-LEX-ET-01] -->

> Bonjour !
> Je m'appelle Marie.
> J'habite à Paris.
> J'ai un frère et une sœur.
> Je bois du café et je mange du pain.
> Le jour, je vais au travail.
> Je travaille beaucoup, mais j'aime ça.
> Le soir, je lis un livre.
> Le livre est très bon.
> Je ne comprends pas tout, mais ça va.
> Demain, je vais parler français.
> Merci et au revoir.

[PAUSE 3s] Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the
sentences arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Now read it again, and notice how little work it took.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-MAIS-03, FR-LEX-BEAUCOUP-01, FR-LEX-TRES-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Look at what is holding it together, because it is not vocabulary.

[PAUSE 2s] **Et** adds. **Mais** holds two true things apart, and it does it
twice here — once about work and once about understanding. Each of those was a
chapter, and none of them is longer than four letters.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice the tenth line especially. **Je ne comprends pas tout, mais
ça va.** Somebody has admitted they did not follow everything, and the
paragraph carries on. That is the most useful sentence on this page.

[PAUSE 2s] This is the first whole paragraph the book has asked you to hold at
once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.
