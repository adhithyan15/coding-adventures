# اول، بعد، در آخر (avval, ba'd, dar âkhar) — first, then, in the end

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-AVVAL, FA-LEX-C153-ZAMAN153-03, FA-LEX-C153-ZAMAN153-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "first", then "then, afterwards", then "at last". (*Avval*,
*sepas*, *bâlâkhare*.)

[PAUSE 1s] You hold the words already. This lesson strings them through a
run of events, and adds the one people use most in speech.

## Grammar Lens: three events, in order
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-BAD-THEN-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C43-FOOD43-04, FA-LEX-C88-LOC88-01, FA-LEX-C39-VERBS39-04, FA-LEX-C45-HOME45-01] -->

> **اول صبحانه خوردم. بعد به کتابخانه رفتم. در آخر به خانه برگشتم.**
>
> *avval sobhâne khordam. ba'd be ketâbkhâne raftam. dar âkhar be khâne
> bargashtam.*
>
> First I had breakfast. Then I went to the library. In the end I went back
> home.

**بعد**, *ba'd*, "then", opens the next event. It does the job *sepas* does,
and it is the one you hear at a kitchen table; *sepas* sounds written.

With *az* after it, *ba'd az* is "after": **بعد از صبحانه**, *ba'd az sobhâne*,
"after breakfast". *Pas az*, from chapter one hundred and fifty-two, means
the same and sounds more formal.

The events stay in the plain past, one after another, each with its edge.

## Grammar Lens: in the end, and at last
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-DAR-AKHAR-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C152-ZAMAN152-04, FA-LEX-C152-ZAMAN152-05] -->

**در آخر**, *dar âkhar*, is "in the end": *dar*, "in", and *âkhar*, "the end".
It closes the run.

*Bâlâkhare*, "at last", closes it too, with relief in it: the thing you were
waiting for has come. *Bâlâkhare residam*: I got there at last.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C291-EYN-01] -->

*ba'd* is borrowed from Arabic *baʿd*, "after" (EtymDB 2.1), and it carries
the catch you wrote one lesson ago: **ب**, then **ع**, then **د**. *Avval* and
*âkhar* are Arabic as well (EtymDB 2.1). In these three sentences the words
that order the events are Arabic, and the verbs are Persian.
