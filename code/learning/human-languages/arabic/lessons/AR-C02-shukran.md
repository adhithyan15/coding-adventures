# شكرا (shukran) — "thank you"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The word you'll use most after the greetings. Another clean root.

## Sounds you'll need

- `shin` — the **ش** (sh) = English *sh*. *SHUK-ran*. The final *-an* (ـًا)
  is a grammatical ending (the accusative) — for now just say *shukran*. →
  [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart

**شكرا** (*shukran*) — "thank you / thanks." Root: **ش–ك–ر** (**sh–k–r**), the
idea of **gratitude, thanks**:

- **shukr** — thanks, gratitude
- **shākir** — thankful (the *-ā-i-* pattern = "one who is ___ing" — the same
  pattern as *kātib* "writer" from k-t-b)
- **mashkūr** — thanked, deserving of thanks (the *ma---ū-* pattern =
  "___-ed", like *maktūb* "written")

Look what just happened: *shākir* and *mashkūr* sit in the **exact same
patterns** as *kātib* and *maktūb* from the root lesson. Learn a pattern once
and it works on every root. That's the whole engine.

> A warmer reply than "you're welcome": **عفوا** (*ʿafwan*), roughly "pardon,
> it's nothing," from a root meaning to pardon/efface.
