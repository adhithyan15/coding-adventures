# ◌ो — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-212, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-116, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-213] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one, the ones you have already met: ध · ट · ल. Say what each of them does.

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. One only — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ो
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-214]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ो** — *o*.

It is a **vowel sign**. It is not a letter and never stands alone: it attaches to a consonant and **replaces** the *a* built into it. Replaces, not adds — the *a* is gone.

Where it sits: the ā bar on the right plus a stroke above.

Worked through: **न** + **◌ो** = **नो** — *no*.

You already say these, and every one of them has ◌ो somewhere inside it:

- **करोमि** *karomi* — I do, I make
- **साहाय्यं करोति** *sāhāyyaṁ karoti* — he, she, or it helps — a noun plus "does," and help named as going along with someone

## Writing: ◌ो — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-214] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ◌ो yourself —
slowly, and larger than it is printed.
The short bar on top stays grey in the strip: in a word it is part of the
headline, which you draw last, once, across the whole word.

> The strip shows **where to start the character and which way to travel**, in
> one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school,
> so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the
> only one.
