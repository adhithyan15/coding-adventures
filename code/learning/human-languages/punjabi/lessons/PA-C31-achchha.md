# achchhā — the word that used to mean "clear water"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-JI, PA-LEX-HAAN-NAHIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **ਜੀ** once. Then say *hāṁ* and *nahīṁ*. Those three cover yes,
no, and respect. What they do not cover is the reply that accepts something
without agreeing or refusing — the sound an English speaker makes as *okay* or
*right*.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-ACHCHHA]; assesses=[] -->

> *achchhā* — **okay, all right, I see**

Say it slowly: *ach-chhā*. The middle is one *ch* held twice as long, and the
second half of that hold carries a puff of breath.

## The word, taken apart — from clear water to "fine"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-ACHCHHA-CLEAR]; assesses=[PA-LEX-ACHCHHA, PA-SCRIPT-ADDAK-DOUBLING] -->

The usual account takes *achchhā* back to Sanskrit *accha*, which
meant **clear, transparent** — said of water you could see the bottom through.
Clear became unclouded, unclouded became good, and good, in the mouth of
someone listening to you, became *fine, I follow you*.

Some dictionaries stop short of that and mark the origin unknown. Both accounts
are on the record, and a reader who wants the honest version should hold this
one loosely.

The held middle consonant does the same work here as the small mark **ੱ** you
met over **ਰੱਬ**: it doubles the sound that follows it. On the page this word
will need a letter you have not met, so it stays in sound for now.

Compare the three replies you now have by ear. *Hāṁ* agrees. *Jī* agrees
respectfully. *Achchhā* receives — it says the message landed, and leaves
agreement for later.
