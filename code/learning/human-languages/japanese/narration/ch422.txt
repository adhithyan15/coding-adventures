Japanese, chapter 422: Eating Out on the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
わしょく (washoku) — food in the Japanese style.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a price cut, then the Japanese for merchandise.

You'll want to know: わしょく.
わしょく — washoku — "food in the Japanese style".
たびさきで わしょくを たべました。 — tabisaki de washoku o tabemashita — "I ate Japanese food where I was staying."

The word, taken apart.
わしょく (washoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are wa, "Japanese style" (Middle Chinese hwa) and shoku, "food" (zyik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
さしみ (sashimi) — sliced raw fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for food in the Western style, then the Japanese for a set meal.

You'll want to know: さしみ.
さしみ — sashimi — "sliced raw fish".
みなとで さしみを たべました。 — minato de sashimi o tabemashita — "We ate sliced raw fish at the harbour."

The word, taken apart.
さしみ (sashimi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from sashi, "piercing" (from sasu) and mi, "flesh"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
