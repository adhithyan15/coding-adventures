German, chapter 491: Paten und Nachwuchs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Halbbruder — half-brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for twin, then the German for only child.

You'll want to know: der Halbbruder.
der Halbbruder — "half-brother".
Mein Halbbruder ist zehn Jahre jünger als ich. My half-brother is ten years younger than me.
Plural: die Halbbrüder. Likewise die Halbschwester.

The word, taken apart.
Halbbruder is built on halb "half", inherited from Old High German halb, from Proto-Germanic halbaz (EtymDB), and Bruder "brother".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does der Halbbruder mean? ("Half-brother".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
der Pate — godfather; godparent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for only child, then the German for half-brother.

You'll want to know: der Pate.
der Pate — "godfather; godparent".
Mein Onkel ist der Pate meiner Tochter. My uncle is my daughter's godfather.
Weak noun: den Paten. A godmother is die Patin or die Patentante.

The word, taken apart.
Pate comes from Middle High German pate (EtymDB), usually traced to Church Latin pater "father", as in pater spiritualis "spiritual father".

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Nachwuchs — offspring, new arrival; young talent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for godchild, then the German for spouse.

You'll want to know: der Nachwuchs.
der Nachwuchs — "offspring, new arrival; young talent".
Unsere Nachbarn haben Nachwuchs bekommen. Our neighbours have had a baby.
No plural. Also young talent: der Nachwuchs im Fußball.

The word, taken apart.
Nachwuchs is built on nach "after" and Wuchs "growth", from wachsen "to grow", inherited from Old High German wahsan, from Proto-Germanic wahsijaną (EtymDB).
