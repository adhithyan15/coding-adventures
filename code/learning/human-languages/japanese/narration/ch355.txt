Japanese, chapter 355: How Likely Is It?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
たぶん (tabun) — perhaps; probably.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a parasol, then the Japanese for in advance.

You'll want to know: たぶん.
たぶん — tabun — "perhaps; probably".
あめが ふったら、たぶん ちちは うちに いるでしょう。 — ame ga futtara, tabun chichi wa uchi ni iru deshō — "If it rains, my father will probably stay at home."

The word, taken apart.
たぶん (tabun) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ta, "many" (Middle Chinese ta) and bun, "part" (pjun) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
きっと (kitto) — surely; certainly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for in advance, then the Japanese for perhaps.

You'll want to know: きっと.
きっと — kitto — "surely; certainly".
まいにち れんしゅうしたら、きっと かつでしょう。 — mainichi renshū shitara, kitto katsu deshō — "If they practise every day, they will surely win."

The word, taken apart.
きっと (kitto) is a native mimetic word, one of the Japanese words that picture a manner or a feeling in sound; JMdict marks it as mimetic. It is inherited, not borrowed, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

かのうせい — kanōsei — "a possibility; a chance".
くもったら、あめの かのうせいが あるでしょう。 — kumottara, ame no kanōsei ga aru deshō — "If it clouds over, there will be a chance of rain."

The word, taken apart.
かのうせい (kanōsei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ka, "can" (Middle Chinese khaX), nō, "ability" (nong) and sei, "nature" (sjengH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
はずれる (hazureru) — to miss the mark; to turn out wrong; to come off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for very likely, then the Japanese for a possibility.

You'll want to know: はずれる.
はずれる — hazureru — "to miss the mark; to turn out wrong; to come off".
よそうが はずれたら、みんな こまるでしょう。 — yosō ga hazuretara, minna komaru deshō — "If the forecast turns out wrong, everyone will be in trouble."
Polite はずれます, hazuremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): よそうが はずれました。, yosō ga hazuremashita.

The word, taken apart.
はずれる (hazureru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the intransitive partner of hazusu, "to take off", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
