## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C50-POINT-02]; assesses=[] -->

**iste, ista, istud** — "**that**," the one near **you**.

The middle term, and the one with an attitude. *Iste* is literally "that of
yours," and Roman orators used it to keep a thing at arm's length. Cicero's
speeches are full of **iste** meaning "that man of yours" with a curl of the lip —
the word does the sneering, and no adjective is needed.

> **Iste liber.** — That book of yours.

Then the words moved. Spanish **este** ("this") is from *iste*, not from *hic* —
the middle term slid down into the near slot when *hic* dropped out, and *ipse*
slid in behind it to make *ese*. So Spanish still points three ways and does it
with a different three words, which is a good demonstration that a SYSTEM can
outlive every word in it.
