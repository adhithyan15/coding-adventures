# el currículum — a word that stayed Latin

## You'll want to know: el currículum
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TRABAJO] -->

**el currículum** — the CV, the résumé.

The full form is **currículum vítae**, and in speech people usually say only
**el currículum**, or shorten it further to **el currí**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-01] -->

Latin **curriculum** meant *a running*, a lap of a track — from *currere*, to
run. *Currículum vítae* is **the running of a life**.

Spanish kept the Latin **-um** ending instead of reshaping it, which marks the
word as a formal borrowing rather than an inherited one:

| inherited from Latin | borrowed from Latin |
|---|---|
| la puerta | el currículum |
| el agua | el álbum |

An inherited word has been worn down by centuries of speech; a borrowed one was
lifted from a book and still looks like it. That ending is the giveaway, and it
tells you the register before you know the meaning: a word ending **-um** in
Spanish is almost always formal, technical or administrative.

The same *currere* is under **el curso** and **la carrera**, both of which are
also runnings.
