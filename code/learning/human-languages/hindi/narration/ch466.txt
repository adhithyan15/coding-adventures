Hindi, chapter 466: Kind and Cruel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
निष्ठुर (niṣṭhur) — heartless; unfeeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a sigh, then the Hindi for wailing.

You'll want to know: निष्ठुर.
निष्ठुर — niṣṭhur — "heartless; unfeeling".
बच्चा रो रहा था, लेकिन वह निष्ठुर आदमी नहीं रुका। baccā ro rahā thā, lekin vah niṣṭhur ādmī nahīṃ rukā. The child was crying, but that heartless man didn't stop.

The word, taken apart.
निष्ठुर (niṣṭhur) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit niṣṭhura, "hard, rough, harsh, cruel", said especially of words (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सज्जन (sajjan) — a gentleman; a decent person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for cruel, then the Hindi for good-hearted.

You'll want to know: सज्जन.
सज्जन — sajjan — "a gentleman; a decent person".
दरवाज़े पर एक सज्जन आपसे मिलना चाहते हैं। darvāze par ek sajjan āpse milnā cāhte haiṁ. There's a gentleman at the door who would like to see you.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सज्जन (sajjan) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit saj-jana, "a good or virtuous man", built on sat, "good", and jana, "person" (Monier-Williams). It is the polite word for a man one does not know.

Lesson 5 of 5.
कठोर (kaṭhor) — harsh; stern.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for good-hearted, then the Hindi for a gentleman.

You'll want to know: कठोर.
कठोर — kaṭhor — "harsh; stern".
पिता की बात कठोर थी, लेकिन सही थी। pitā kī bāt kaṭhor thī, lekin sahī thī. Father's words were harsh, but they were right.

The word, taken apart.
कठोर (kaṭhor) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit kaṭhora, "hard, solid, stiff" (Monier-Williams). Hindi uses it for hard words and hard hearts.
