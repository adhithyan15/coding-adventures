Italian, chapter 520: Sul palco.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
recitare — to act; to recite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for gallery, then the Italian for applause.

You'll want to know: recitare.
recitare — "to act; to recite".
Recito in una compagnia di teatro amatoriale. I act in an amateur theatre company.
More than to recite: on stage it means to act.

The word, taken apart.
recitare is a learned borrowing from Latin recitāre "to read aloud" (EtymDB), from citāre "to call".

Lesson 2 of 5.
il copione — script.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for applause, then the Italian for to act.

You'll want to know: il copione.
il copione — "script".
Devo imparare il copione a memoria. I have to learn the script by heart.
Also a set pattern: seguire il copione.

The word, taken apart.
copione is built on copia "copy" (EtymDB), from Latin cōpia "plenty": the big copy each actor works from.

Lesson 3 of 5.
il ruolo — role, part.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to act, then the Italian for script.

You'll want to know: il ruolo.
il ruolo — "role, part".
Nello spettacolo ho il ruolo del padre. In the play I have the part of the father.
Also at work: il mio ruolo in azienda.

The word, taken apart.
ruolo was borrowed from French rôle "a roll, a list", from Medieval Latin rotulus "a roll of parchment", from rota "wheel" (Lewis & Short).

Ha debuttato a teatro a vent'anni. She made her stage debut at twenty.

The word, taken apart.
debuttare was borrowed from French débuter "to make the first move" (EtymDB), built on but "a target".

Lesson 5 of 5.
il regista — director.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for role, then the Italian for to make one's debut.

You'll want to know: il regista.
il regista — "director".
Il regista ha cambiato la fine dello spettacolo. The director changed the ending of the play.
The same form for a woman: la regista. The work is la regia.

The word, taken apart.
regista is built on regia "direction", usually traced to French régie "management", from régir "to govern", from Latin regere "to rule" (EtymDB, under French régie).
