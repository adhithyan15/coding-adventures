Urdu, chapter 275: It Depends, and What Would Have Been.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
منحصر (munhasir) — dependent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "on one condition". (ek shart par.) Then "the weather". (mausam.)

You'll want to know: منحصر.
منحصر — munhasir — "dependent".
یہ موسم پر منحصر ہے۔ yih mausam par munhasir hai. — "It depends on the weather."
What it depends on takes پر (par), "on", and ہے closes it.
سب کچھ آپ پر منحصر ہے۔ sab kuchh āp par munhasir hai. — "Everything depends on you."

The word, taken apart.
منحصر (munhasir) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic munḥaṣir, "confined, limited". Urdu uses it with پر for "dependent on".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: munhasir, then yih mausam par munhasir hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It depends on you" — yih āp par munhasir hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does منحصر mean? ("Dependent".) "It depends on the weather"? (یہ موسم پر منحصر ہے (par munhasir hai).)


Lesson 2 of 5.
پر منحصر ہے (par munhasir hai) — it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it depends on the weather". (yih mausam par munhasir hai.) Then "if it rains". (agar bārish huī.) A question often gets this answer.

You'll want to know: it depends on …
یہ منحصر ہے۔ yih munhasir hai. — "It depends."
کیا آپ کل آئیں گے؟ kyā āp kal āeṅ ge? — "Will you come tomorrow?"
یہ کام پر منحصر ہے۔ yih kām par munhasir hai. — "It depends on work."
یہ (yih), "this", is the plan in question; what decides it takes پر:
یہ پیسوں پر منحصر ہے۔ yih paisoṅ par munhasir hai. — "It depends on the money."

You'll want to know: it depends on whether …
When a whole question decides it, اس پر (is par) stands for it, and کہ (ki) brings the question:
یہ اس پر منحصر ہے، yih is par munhasir hai, کہ بارش ہو گی یا نہیں۔ ki bārish ho gī yā nahīṅ. — "It depends on whether it will rain or not."
یہ اس پر منحصر ہے، yih is par munhasir hai, کہ گاڑی کب جائے گی۔ ki gāṛī kab jāe gī. — "It depends on when the train leaves."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It depends on the weather" — yih mausam par munhasir hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It depends on the money" — yih paisoṅ par munhasir hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It depends on Sara" — yih Sārā par munhasir hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Someone asks if you'll come tomorrow. Say it depends on the weather, then that it depends on whether it will rain.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on work"? (یہ کام پر منحصر ہے (par munhasir hai).) And before a whole question? (یہ اس پر منحصر ہے (par munhasir hai) کہ …)


Lesson 3 of 5.
اگر وقت ہوتا (agar vaqt hotā) — what would have been.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it depends on the weather", then "it depends on whether it will rain". (yih mausam par munhasir hai, yih is par munhasir hai ki bārish ho gī yā nahīṅ.) Then "if it rains, we'll stay at home". (agar bārish huī, to ham ghar par raheṅ ge.) That may still happen. This lesson is for what did not.

You'll want to know: -تا in both halves.
میں آتا۔ maiṅ ātā. — "I would come."
Here, with no ہوں (hūṅ) after it and no نہیں before it, آتا (ātā) is not "I come": it is "I would come", or "I would have come".
اگر وقت ہوتا، agar vaqt hotā, تو میں آتا۔ to maiṅ ātā. — "If I had time, I would come."
Both halves take -تا with nothing after it, and each follows its own subject: ہوتا for وقت, آتا for me. The story around it says whether this is now or then.

You'll want to know: with نہ.
اگر بارش نہ ہوتی، agar bārish na hotī, تو ہم جاتے۔ to ham jāte. — "If it hadn't rained, we would have gone."
نہ (na), not نہیں, says "not" here, as in نہ … نہ … (na … na …).
اگر میں جلدی اٹھتا، agar maiṅ jaldī uṭhtā, تو گاڑی نہ چھوٹتی۔ to gāṛī na chhūṭtī. — "If I had got up early, I wouldn't have missed the train."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If I had time, I would come" — agar vaqt hotā, to maiṅ ātā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I had time, I would go" — agar vaqt hotā, to maiṅ jātā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I had time, I would read" — agar vaqt hotā, to maiṅ paṛhtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say: if I had time, I would come. Then: if it hadn't rained, we would have gone.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If I had time"? (اگر وقت ہوتا (agar vaqt hotā).) "I would come"? (میں آتا, or آتی.) Which "not" goes with ہوتی? (نہ.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip: what happened, a reason, a feeling, and what would have been.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You read Sara's trip to Lahore. Now you write one: a trip of your own, real or invented, for a friend to read.
[pause 1 second]
Say دوران (daurān) and بعد (bād) aloud; one of them will go in.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list. It has a scene, with رہا تھا or جب … تو …; events in the plain past, kept in order with پہلے, پھر, اس کے بعد, آخر میں, and joined with کر; a reason, with کیونکہ, اس لیے or کی وجہ سے; and a feeling, with مجھے … لگا or میں خوش ہوا. It can end on what might have been: اگر … ہوتا، تو ….

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, in Urdu script, write six lines, one for each frame. Each line is one short sentence of your own; the frame gives its job, not its words.
where you went, and when: کل میں … گیا (or گئی), or پچھلے ہفتے …;
the scene as you set off: … رہا تھا, or جب …، تو …;
what happened first and next: پہلے …، پھر …، or a کر form; one thing done with نے;
one thing that went wrong, why, and whom you told: کھو گیا, چھوٹ گئی or حادثہ, with کیونکہ or کی وجہ سے, and … کو بتایا;
how it ended and how you felt: آخر میں …، مجھے … لگا or میں خوش ہوا, or what you hope now: مجھے امید ہے کہ …;
what might have been: اگر … ہوتا، تو …, with one word that holds a letter you wrote in these chapters: ع, غ, ذ, ث, ڈ, ص or ؤ.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model in this lesson. Use only words you already write.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read your six lines aloud. Mark each past verb s for scene and e for event: line two mostly s, lines three and four mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then three checks. A plain past with an object has نے on its doer and follows the object: میں نے ٹکٹ لیا, میں نے چائے پی. A رہا تھا verb takes no نے. In line six, do both halves end in -تا?
