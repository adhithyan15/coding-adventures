Spanish, chapter 514: El reportaje.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el reportaje — the report, the feature story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the correspondent, then the Spanish for to publish.

You'll want to know: el reportaje.
el reportaje — "the report, the feature story".
Vimos un reportaje sobre la ciudad. We watched a report about the city.
A longer piece in a paper or on television. A short official report is un informe.

The word, taken apart.
reportaje was borrowed from French reportage, which is usually traced to reporter, from Latin reportāre 'to carry back' (Lewis & Short). A report carries the story back to you.

You'll want to know: el reportero.
el reportero — "the reporter".
El reportero llegó antes que la policía. The reporter arrived before the police.
A woman is la reportera.

The word, taken apart.
reportero is usually traced to English reporter, given the Spanish ending -ero. The English word comes through French from Latin reportāre 'to carry back' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
entrevistar — to interview.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the report, then the Spanish for the reporter.

You'll want to know: entrevistar.
entrevistar — "to interview".
Entrevistaron al alcalde en la radio. They interviewed the mayor on the radio.
La entrevista is the interview. It takes personal a with people: entrevistar a alguien.

The word, taken apart.
entrevistar is built on entrevista, made from entre 'between' and vista 'a sight', and usually said to follow French entrevue. Vista comes from Latin vidēre 'to see' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
difundir — to spread, to broadcast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the reporter, then the Spanish for to interview.

You'll want to know: difundir.
difundir — "to spread, to broadcast".
La radio difundió la noticia en pocos minutos. The radio spread the news in a few minutes.
Used for news, ideas and images: difundir una foto.

The word, taken apart.
difundir is a learned borrowing from Latin diffundere 'to pour out, to spread' (EtymDB), built on dis- 'apart' and fundere 'to pour'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
anunciar — to announce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to interview, then the Spanish for to spread.

You'll want to know: anunciar.
anunciar — "to announce".
El presidente anunció su viaje a Chile. The president announced his trip to Chile.
El anuncio is the announcement, and also an advert.

The word, taken apart.
anunciar is a learned borrowing from Latin annūntiāre 'to make known' (EtymDB), built on nūntius 'a messenger'. English announce is the same Latin verb through French.
