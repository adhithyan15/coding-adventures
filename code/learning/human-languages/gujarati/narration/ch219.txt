Gujarati, chapter 219: Thunder and hail.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ગાજવું (gājvũ) — to thunder, to rumble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for sunset, then the Gujarati for daylight.

You'll want to know: ગાજવું.
ગાજવું — gājvũ — "to thunder, to rumble".
રાત્રે વાદળ ગાજશે. — rātre vādaḷ gājśe — "The clouds will thunder at night."

The word, taken apart.
ગાજવું (gājvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from garjati, "thunders, roars" (CDIAL 4046).

Lesson 3 of 5.
છવાવું (chavāvũ) — to cloud over, to spread over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to thunder, then the Gujarati for to shine.

You'll want to know: છવાવું.
છવાવું — chavāvũ — "to cloud over, to spread over".
સાંજે આકાશમાં વાદળ છવાશે. — sā̃je ākāśmā̃ vādaḷ chavāśe — "Clouds will cover the sky in the evening."

The word, taken apart.
છવાવું (chavāvũ) is built on chāvũ, "to cover, to thatch", with the -ā- of a thing that happens by itself. CDIAL lists chāvũ, "to thatch", under Sanskrit chādayati, "covers, veils" (CDIAL 5018).

Lesson 4 of 5.
કરા (karā) — hailstones, hail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to shine, then the Gujarati for to cloud over.

You'll want to know: કરા.
કરા — karā — "hailstones, hail".
કાલે કરા પડશે. — kāle karā paḍśe — "Hail will fall tomorrow."
Masculine, and usually plural.

The word, taken apart.
કરા (karā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karaka, "hail" (CDIAL 2782).

Lesson 5 of 5.
માવઠું (māvṭhũ) — unseasonal rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to cloud over, then the Gujarati for hailstones.

You'll want to know: માવઠું.
માવઠું — māvṭhũ — "unseasonal rain".
આ વરસે શિયાળામાં માવઠું થશે. — ā varase śiyāḷāmā̃ māvṭhũ thaśe — "This year there will be rain out of season in winter."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
માવઠું (māvṭhũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from māghavṛṣṭi, "rain in the month of Māgha", a reconstructed form built on Sanskrit vṛṣṭi, "rain"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "shower of rain at an unseasonable time" (CDIAL 9994).
