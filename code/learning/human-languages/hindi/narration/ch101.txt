Hindi, chapter 101: Two Words Where English Has One.
9 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 9.
के साथ (ke sāth) — with, in two words.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say his and her in Hindi, and notice that both were the same word wearing different endings. Now try to say with my friend, and notice that English needs exactly one word to do it.

You'll want to know: के साथ.
मैं दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth) हूँ। — I am with a friend.
English spends one word on this. Hindi spends two, and only the second one is new.

The two words.
Take the phrase apart and something familiar falls out:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
piece: दोस्त. what it is: the noun.
piece: के. what it is: उसका's ending, standing on its own.
piece: साथ. what it is: the new word — company.
So दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth) is, piece by piece, the friend's company. Hindi is not reaching for a preposition at all. It is building a possessive and then naming the relation as a thing.
That is why the first word is के and never का: a possessive agrees with what follows it, and साथ is masculine and standing in front of a further word, so it takes the oblique ending you met on nouns one chapter ago.

Grammar Lens: a shape, not a word.
Write the shape down once, because the rest of this chapter is the same shape six more times:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot: 1. contents: the noun.
slot: 2. contents: के.
slot: 3. contents: the relation word.
अपने दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth) — with my own friend — puts अपना in slot 1's ordinary spot, bent to अपने because a postposition is coming. Everything in front of a postposition bends. That has now been true of possessives, of pronouns and of nouns, and it is true here.

Lesson 2 of 9.
के बिना (ke binā) — the opposite, for the price of one word.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say with a friend in Hindi. Now say it again and listen to how much of it you would have to change to mean the opposite.

You'll want to know: के बिना.
चाय के बिना (ke binā) — without tea.
One word changed. The shape did not move.

The two words.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
slot 3: साथ. the whole phrase: दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth). English: with a friend.
slot 3: बिना. the whole phrase: चाय के बिना (ke binā). English: without tea.
This is what the shape buys. Once slot 3 is a swappable position, every new relation costs exactly one word instead of a new piece of grammar — and this chapter is going to swap it five more times.
English does not work this way. With and without look related because one is built out of the other, but in front of and behind are unrelated words that share nothing. In Hindi all of them share के, and that is the part that is doing the grammatical work.

Grammar Lens: two ways to write it.
बिना is the one relation word in this chapter that is also allowed to stand in front of its noun — बिना चाय के is said too, and means the same thing.
Learn the slot-3 version. It is the one that matches everything else you are about to meet, and a reader who knows the ordinary order will still understand the other one when a paper prints it.

Lesson 3 of 9.
घर के पास (ghar ke pās) — the word that was hiding in I have.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I have in Hindi. Now look at the two words you said and ask what the second one actually means, because so far you have never been told.

You'll want to know: के पास.
दुकान घर के पास (ghar ke pās) है। — The shop is near the house.
पास means near. It has meant near the whole time.

The two words.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
phrase: मेरे पास. word for word: near me. the job it does: I have.
phrase: घर के पास (ghar ke pās). word for word: near the house. the job it does: it is nearby.
The same three slots, and the same slot-3 word. What changed is only what sits in slot 1 — a person or a place.
So Hindi says having by saying nearness, and the physical sense you are meeting now is the original one. A language with no verb for have reached for the nearest true thing: whatever is beside you is, for practical purposes, yours. The possessive chapter taught you the borrowed meaning first because it is the one a beginner needs first. This is the meaning it was borrowed from.

Grammar Lens: a place answer.
You have been able to ask where something is for sixty chapters. This is the first shape that lets you answer with anything more exact than a room:
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
question: दुकान कहाँ है? (dukān kahā̃ hai?) answer: घर के पास (ghar ke pās)।.
An A1 listening paper does this constantly, because near is how people actually give directions — not in metres, but relative to a landmark both speakers already know.

Lesson 4 of 9.
के सामने (ke sāmne) — in front of.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say near the house. Now say the word for a railway station. You are one word away from the sentence an A1 listening paper is most likely to set you.

You'll want to know: के सामने.
दुकान स्टेशन के सामने (ke sāmne) है। — The shop is in front of the station.

The two words.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
स्टेशन के पास means near the station.
स्टेशन के सामने (ke sāmne) means in front of the station.
English changed from one word to three. Hindi changed from one word to one.
Notice what सामने does not do. It ends in -ए, which on an adjective would be an ending that agrees with something — but this word never changes, whoever or whatever is in front of whatever. A slot-three word is fixed. The bending in this shape all happens in slot 2, and slot 2 is already done before slot 3 arrives.

Grammar Lens: front of what.
English in front of is ambiguous in a way Hindi's is not. In front of the station can mean the near side of the building, or it can mean a position further along the road. के सामने (ke sāmne) means the first: facing it, across from it, on the side you would stand to look at it.
That is the reading a direction-giver intends, and the reading an exam item expects.

Lesson 5 of 9.
के पीछे (ke pīche) — behind.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say in front of the station. Then say the word for a table, which you have had since the chapter on furniture.

You'll want to know: के पीछे.
कुर्सी मेज़ के पीछे (ke pīche) है। — The chair is behind the table.

The two words.
Three place words, and they do not all do the same kind of job:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot-three word: पास. what it tells you: how far — close, no direction.
slot-three word: सामने. what it tells you: which side — facing.
slot-three word: पीछे. what it tells you: which side — away.
पास answers a different question from the other two, and mixing them is the commonest way to get a direction item wrong. A shop can be near a station and still be behind it; it cannot be in front of it and behind it at once.

Grammar Lens: the one glyph worth pausing on.
पीछे carries छ, the breathy partner of च that the previous chapter taught. This is its first outing in an ordinary word rather than a number, and it is worth saying slowly: the breath belongs to the छ, not to the vowel either side of it.
Get that wrong and you say a different word. The pair च / छ does the same work here that त / थ does elsewhere, and both pairs punish the same inattention.

Lesson 6 of 9.
परिवार के लिए (parivār ke liye) — for, with a noun in front.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say for me in Hindi — you have had it since the possessive chapter. Then say the word for a family.

You'll want to know: के लिए.
यह परिवार के लिए (parivār ke liye) है। — This is for the family.

The two words.
मेरे लिए was taught as a phrase to memorise. It was never a phrase. It was this shape with a possessive in slot 1:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
slot 1: मेरे. slot 2: —. slot 3: लिए.
slot 1: परिवार. slot 2: के. slot 3: लिए.
The top row has no separate slot 2 because मेरे already is the possessive, bent. When slot 1 is a noun instead, the possessive has to be supplied, and के is what supplies it.
That is the whole difference between the two rows, and it is the same difference that runs through this chapter: a pronoun carries its own के inside itself, a noun does not.

Grammar Lens: what for covers.
के लिए covers two things English also puts under for:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
sense: who benefits. example: यह परिवार के लिए (parivār ke liye) है।.
sense: what something is meant to do. example: यह चाय के लिए है।.
The second sense is the one the next lesson builds on, because a purpose is usually an action rather than a thing, and an action needs one more change before it can stand in slot 1.

Lesson 7 of 9.
करने के लिए (karne ke liye) — in order to.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the infinitive to do. Now say for the family. To put the first inside the second, one of them has to bend — and by now you can guess which.

You'll want to know: करने के लिए.
मैं काम करने के लिए (karne ke liye) जाता हूँ। — I go in order to work.
करना became करने. Nothing else moved.

The word, taken apart.
Look at the ending that changed, and then look at where you have seen that change before:
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
kind of word: possessive. plain: मेरा. before a postposition: मेरे.
kind of word: demonstrative possessive. plain: उसका. before a postposition: उसके.
kind of word: own. plain: अपना. before a postposition: अपने.
kind of word: noun. plain: बच्चा. before a postposition: बच्चे.
kind of word: infinitive. plain: करना. before a postposition: करने.
Five kinds of word, one ending, one trigger. The infinitive is the last of the five, and it needs no new rule at all: -ना ends in -आ, so it bends exactly as बच्चा does.
The reason it can bend like a noun is that in Hindi it is one. करना is both to do and the doing, which is why it could already sit where a noun sits. A word that behaves like a noun bends like a noun.

Grammar Lens: where the purpose goes.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot: 1. contents: who.
slot: 2. contents: the purpose: काम करने के लिए (karne ke liye).
slot: 3. contents: the main verb, last.
English puts the purpose after the verb — I go to work. Hindi puts it before — because the verb goes last, and that has not changed since the first sentence you read.
So the order reverses, and the reversal is the only thing to practise. The pieces themselves are all old.

Lesson 8 of 9.
sambandh kī tālikā — relation, on one page.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the word for with and the word for in front of, and say what the two phrases have in the middle.

Grammar Lens: the six.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot 3: साथ. English: with. what it does: company.
slot 3: बिना. English: without. what it does: absence.
slot 3: पास. English: near. what it does: distance.

Grammar Lens: and the other three.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot 3: सामने. English: in front of. what it does: facing side.
slot 3: पीछे. English: behind. what it does: far side.
slot 3: लिए. English: for. what it does: benefit or purpose.
Six words, and not one of them is a new piece of grammar. Each is a noun sitting in a possessive phrase. दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth) is the friend's company and घर के पास (ghar ke pās) is the house's nearness, and Hindi is content to leave it at that.

Grammar Lens: the one word that never changes.
Slot 2 is के every single time, whatever stands on either side of it.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
slot 1: दोस्त. slot 2: के. slot 3: साथ.
slot 1: घर. slot 2: के. slot 3: पास.
slot 1: करने. slot 2: के. slot 3: लिए.
It is के rather than का or की because there is a further word coming, and a possessive in front of a further word goes oblique. That is the same rule the noun chapter taught and the same rule the possessive chapter taught, applied a third time.
When slot 1 is a pronoun, slot 2 disappears into it: मेरे पास, मेरे लिए. The के is still there — it is the -ए you can hear on मेरे.

Lesson 9 of 9.
दुकान कहाँ है? (dukān kahā̃ hai?) — answering with a landmark.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ask where the shop is. You have been able to ask this for a long time, and until this chapter you could not answer it.

The exchange.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
speaker: A. Hindi: दुकान कहाँ है? (dukān kahā̃ hai?)
speaker: B. Hindi: स्टेशन के सामने (ke sāmne) है।.
speaker: A. Hindi: स्टेशन के पास?
speaker: B. Hindi: हाँ, पास है।.
B's first answer gives a side; the second gives only a distance. B is not repeating — B is conceding the weaker claim, because A asked for less.

How to answer.
Find the landmark — the noun before के — then read the word after it, because that word alone carries the relation.
पास gives distance and no side. सामने and पीछे give a side and no distance. Do not upgrade पास into a side.

Grammar Lens: the chapter in one sentence.
Hindi has no prepositions. It has possessive phrases whose head noun names a relation.
मैं दोस्त के साथ (ke sāth) काम करने के लिए (karne ke liye) जाता हूँ। — Two relation phrases, one sentence, three slots each.
