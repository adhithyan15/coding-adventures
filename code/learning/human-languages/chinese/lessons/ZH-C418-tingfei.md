## You'll want to know: 停飞
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C418-CHECKIN418-03]; assesses=[] -->

**停飞** — *tíngfēi* — "to be grounded (of planes)".

> *fēijī tíngfēi le wǒmen zuò gāotiě qù Běijīng* — The planes are grounded: we're taking the high-speed train to Beijing.

The new character, in pieces: 停 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the person piece 亻; on the right a dot and a bar, a small 口, a short stroke down and a bar that hooks like a lid, a bar, and a vertical with a hook. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**停飞** (*tíngfēi*) is built from 停 'to stop' and 飞 'to fly', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *deng* and *pj+j* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rest, to stop' and the second 'to fly, to soar'.
