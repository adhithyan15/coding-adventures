Japanese, chapter 323: Saying No Politely.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
えんりょする (enryo suru) — to hold back; to decline politely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for by all means, then the Japanese for to come forward.

You'll want to know: えんりょする.
えんりょする — enryo suru — "to hold back; to decline politely".
あたまが いたいので、おさけは えんりょします。 — atama ga itai node, osake wa enryo shimasu — "My head hurts, so I will say no to the sake."
Polite えんりょします, enryoshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): きゃくは えんりょしました。, kyaku wa enryo shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
えんりょする (enryo suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, enryo, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: en, "far" (Middle Chinese hjwonX) and ryo, "thought" (ljoH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
こばむ (kobamu) — to reject; to refuse firmly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to come forward, then the Japanese for to hold back.

You'll want to know: こばむ.
こばむ — kobamu — "to reject; to refuse firmly".
あぶないと おもったので、かれは しごとを こばみました。 — abunai to omotta node, kare wa shigoto o kobamimashita — "He thought it was dangerous, so he refused the job."
Polite こばみます, kobamimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かれは こばみました。, kare wa kobamimashita.

The word, taken apart.
こばむ (kobamu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

You'll want to know: むり.
むり — muri — "impossible; unreasonable".
じかんが ないから、むりです。 — jikan ga nai kara, muri desu — "There is no time, so it is impossible."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
むり (muri) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are mu, "nothing" (Middle Chinese mju) and ri, "reason" (liX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

けっこう — kekkō — "no thank you; fine".

けっこう (kekkō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ketsu, "tie" (Middle Chinese ket) and kō, "build" (kuwH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as kek-kō.
