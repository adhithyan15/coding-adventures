Japanese, chapter 222: Medicine, Visits and Getting Well.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぬる (nuru) — to apply (cream); to spread on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for treatment, then the Japanese for prevention.

You'll want to know: ぬる.
ぬる — nuru — "to apply (cream); to spread on".
きずに くすりを ぬります。 — kizu ni kusuri o nurimasu — "I put ointment on the wound."
Polite ぬります, nurimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ぬる (nuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

おみまい — omimai — "a sick visit; calling on someone who is ill".
ともだちの おみまいに いきませんか。 — tomodachi no omimai ni ikimasen ka — "Shall we go and visit our sick friend?"

The word, taken apart.
おみまい (omimai) is native Japanese (wago), built from polite o- and mimai, "calling on someone", inherited rather than borrowed; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
かいふく (kaifuku) — recovery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to apply, then the Japanese for a sick visit.

You'll want to know: かいふく.
かいふく — kaifuku — "recovery".
かいふくは はやいですか。 — kaifuku wa hayai desu ka — "Is the recovery quick?"

The word, taken apart.
かいふく (kaifuku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "to revolve, go round" (Middle Chinese hwoj) and fuku, "to return" (bjuwk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
