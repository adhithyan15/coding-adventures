## Fewer pen lifts for ca and ya

ಚ (KA-S122) and ಯ (KA-S117) lifted the pen after every run of their source
animations, three times each. That staged the letter for the eye but
overstated how often writers lift: even Omniglot's 20 non-native copyists per
letter, who use more strokes than native writers wherever the two can be
compared, mostly lift less.

- **ಚ lifts once**, before the hooked upper bar. After the body the pen runs
  back along the lower bar, draws it rightward, and comes back to climb the
  short link: ten movements in two runs (55% of the copyists draw ಚ in two
  strokes, 15% in one).
- **ಯ lifts twice.** The middle arm runs back along the top bar and draws it
  into the hook: eight movements in three runs (45% of the copyists use three
  strokes, 40% two). One lift would mean going back over more than half the
  round bowl, or over the hook and the arm's whole right side, so the lifts
  before the arm and before the small right bowl stay.
- Both writing blocks list the new steps and pen-lift counts and say, in one
  sentence, that the animation lifts before each part while most people
  copying the letter lift less. Every animated movement is still drawn, in
  the same order and direction. KA-S122 stays under the 300s budget at a
  computed 290s; KA-S117 is at 270s.
