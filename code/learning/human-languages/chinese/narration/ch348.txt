Mandarin Chinese, chapter 348: Talking It Over.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tǎolùn — to discuss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the significance of something, then the Mandarin for to know from experience.

You'll want to know: tǎolùn.
tǎolùn — "to discuss".
wǒmen míngtiān kāihuì tǎolùn zhège wèntí — We'll discuss this at tomorrow's meeting.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tǎolùn is built from tǎo and lùn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, thawX and lwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to govern' and 'to punish', and the second 'to speak' and 'to discuss'; it also lists the second in the departing tone, the tone of lùn today.

The word, taken apart.
shāngliang is built from shāng and liáng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, syang and ljang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, among other senses, 'to deliberate', and the second 'to measure'. The second is said light here.

Lesson 5 of 5.
yízhì — all of one mind; in agreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to argue over a point, then the Mandarin for the other party.

You'll want to know: yízhì.
yízhì — "all of one mind; in agreement".
dàjiā de yìjiàn hěn yízhì — Everyone was of one mind.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
The tone of yī changes here: before a falling tone it is said yí, and the spelling writes the change.

The word, taken apart.
yízhì is built from 一 'one' and zhì 'to arrive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit and trijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the start of the numbers' and the second 'to arrive'. How the pair came to mean agreement is not settled.
