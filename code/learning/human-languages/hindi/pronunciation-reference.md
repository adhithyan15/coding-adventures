# Hindi / Devanagari — Reference

A **reference**, not a chapter — a place to look things up. You are *not* meant
to read this before the lessons: the lessons teach reading through real words,
introducing each letter as a word needs it. This page just gathers the system
in one spot.

## How Devanagari works (the three facts the lessons teach)

1. **Consonants carry a built-in "a".** क = "ka," न = "na," म = "ma." You never
   write the short *a* — it's already there.
2. **A vowel sign (*mātrā*) changes that built-in vowel.** Attached to a
   consonant, it swaps the *a* for another vowel:
   - **ा** = ā (का = kā) · **ि** = i (कि = ki, written *before*, read *after*)
     · **ी** = ī · **ु** = u · **ू** = ū · **े** = e (के = ke) · **ै** = ai ·
     **ो** = o · **ौ** = au.
3. **A *halant* (्) removes the built-in vowel.** क् = bare "k." A vowel-less
   consonant leans onto the next, forming a **conjunct**: स् + त → स्त ("st"),
   न् + य → न्य ("ny"), क् + र → क्र ("kr").

## Independent vowels (word-initial)

When a word *starts* with a vowel (no consonant to attach to), a full vowel
letter is used: **अ** a · **आ** ā · **इ** i · **ई** ī · **उ** u · **ऊ** ū ·
**ए** e · **ऐ** ai · **ओ** o · **औ** au.

## Sounds worth flagging

- **Aspirated vs. plain** consonants are different letters and change meaning:
  क k / ख kh, ग g / घ gh, त t / थ th, द d / ध dh, प p / फ ph, etc. The "h"
  is a real puff of breath.
- **Retroflex** consonants (ट ṭ, ड ḍ, ण ṇ, etc.) are made with the tongue
  curled back — distinct from the dental त/द (tongue on the teeth).
- **Nasalization**: a dot (anusvāra ं) or moon-dot (candrabindu ँ) nasalizes
  the vowel.
- **Nukta letters** (a dot under a letter) mark sounds borrowed mostly from
  Persian/Arabic/English: क़ q, ख़ x/kh, ग़ ġ, ज़ z, फ़ f, ड़/ढ़ retroflex flaps.

## The two vocabularies

Not pronunciation, but the key to Hindi: many everyday ideas have **two**
words — a **Sanskrit** (*tatsama*) one, often formal/literary, and a
**Perso-Arabic** one, often everyday: *dhanyavād* / *shukriyā* ("thanks"),
*namaste* / *alvidā*. The lessons trace which is which.
