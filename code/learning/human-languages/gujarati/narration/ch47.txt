Gujarati, chapter 47: Finger, Throat and Tongue.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
આંગળી (ā̃gaḷī) — a finger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a tooth, then the Gujarati for hair.

You'll want to know: આંગળી.
આંગળી (ā̃gaḷī) — "a finger". Five on each હાથ.

The word, taken apart.
આંગળી (ā̃gaḷī) (āṁgaḷī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from aṅguli, "finger, toe" (CDIAL 135).

What you've built.
One more word for finger, throat and tongue.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ā̃gaḷī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ā̃gaḷī, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say ā̃gaḷī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Gujarati for a tooth, then the Gujarati for hair, then say ā̃gaḷī again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does આંગળી (ā̃gaḷī) mean? ("A finger".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.
હોઠ (hoṭh) — a lip, the lips.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the throat, then the Gujarati for a shoulder.

You'll want to know: હોઠ.
હોઠ (hoṭh) — "a lip, the lips".

The word, taken apart.
હોઠ (hoṭh) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit oṣṭha, "lip" (CDIAL 2563). Its h- may come from a compound adhaoṣṭha, "lower lip", which K. R. Norman suggested and CDIAL records (CDIAL 245a).
