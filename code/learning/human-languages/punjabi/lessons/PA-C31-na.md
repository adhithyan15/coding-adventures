# ਨਾ — a negative you have been saying all along

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-HAAN-NAHIN-01, PA-ETYMON-NEGATIVE-NE-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **ਨਹੀਂ** once, then say *koī gall nahīṁ*. Now cut the word in
half and keep only its opening: *na*. That piece is a word in its own right.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-NA]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਨਾ** — *nā* — **no; don't**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-NA-01, PA-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01] -->

**ਨ** *n* and the upright **ਾ** that lengthens it: **ਨਾ**. Two shapes from
the first writing run, and nothing new.

## The word, taken apart — the oldest word in this book
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-NA-NE]; assesses=[PA-LEX-NA, PA-LEX-HAAN-NAHIN-01] -->

**ਨਾ** comes down from Sanskrit *na*, and *na* comes down from
Proto-Indo-European \**ne*. That particle has stayed recognisable for as long as
anything in this book: English **no**, **not** and the *un-* of *unhappy*, Latin
*ne-* and *nōn*, Greek *a-* in *atypical*. The wellbeing chapter met the same root inside
*nahīṁ*; here it stands on its own.

**ਨਹੀਂ** is this word with an old emphatic *hi* welded on — a strengthened no,
the one that refuses a statement. **ਨਾ** is the plain one: it tells someone not
to do a thing, and it can end a sentence as a soft question, the way English
tacks on *right?*

The difference to carry away is one of force, not of meaning. Both refuse; one
insists.

## Writing: assemble ਅੱਛਾ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-ACHCHHA-01]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-CHHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-INDEPENDENT-A-01, PA-SCRIPT-ADDAK-DOUBLING, PA-LEX-ACHCHHA] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Two sessions ago *achchhā* was sound only, because one of its letters had not
been taught. It has been taught now, so the word can be built.

Four pieces, left to right: **ਅ**, then the doubling mark **ੱ** above it, then
**ਛ**, then the upright **ਾ**. Written out: **ਅੱਛਾ**.

Copy it once with the model visible, say *achchhā* as you finish, then cover the
model and write it once more. Compare only whether the doubling mark sits above
the first letter.
