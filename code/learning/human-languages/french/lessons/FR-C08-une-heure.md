# une heure — one o'clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Eleven of the twelve hours behave identically. One does not, and the
reason is a rule you already met on a different word.

## Grammar Lens: one hour, two hours
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-HEURE-AGREEMENT-04]; assesses=[] -->

**heure** is a **feminine** noun, so "one" in front of it is **une**, not *un* —
the same gendered "one" you learned counting. And because the noun counts, it
takes a plural **-s** from two upwards:

- **Il est une heure.** — one o'clock. One hour, so no *-s*.
- **Il est deux heures.** — two o'clock. Two hours, so *-s*.

Now listen rather than read. That **-s** is **silent**, like every plural *-s*
in French. *Une heure* and *deux heures* end in the identical sound. The plural
is visible on the page and inaudible in the air — what your ear actually uses to
tell them apart is the **number in front**, which is why French never drops it.
