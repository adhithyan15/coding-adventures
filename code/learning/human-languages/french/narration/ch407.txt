6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
le stationnement — parking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the boot, then the French for the back seat.

You'll want to know: le stationnement.
le stationnement — "parking".
Le stationnement est interdit devant l'école. Parking is not allowed in front of the school.
Stationnement payant — pay parking.

The word, taken apart.
stationnement is built on stationner 'to park' with -ment. Stationner comes from station, a learned borrowing from Latin statiō 'a standing', from stāre 'to stand' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 6.
l'horodateur — the parking ticket machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for parking, then the French for to park.

You'll want to know: l'horodateur.
l'horodateur — "the parking ticket machine".
Elle paie à l'horodateur avec sa carte. She pays at the ticket machine with her card.
A masculine noun. Older single meters were parcmètres.

The word, taken apart.
horodateur is built from horo-, from Greek hṓra 'hour', and dateur 'something that dates', from date: it prints the time on the ticket. EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
le créneau — the parallel parking space; the time slot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to park, then the French for the parking ticket machine.

You'll want to know: le créneau.
le créneau — "the parallel parking space; the time slot".
Mon frère fait très bien les créneaux. My brother is very good at parallel parking.
Faire un créneau — to parallel park. Also a time slot: un créneau libre.

The word, taken apart.
créneau comes from Old French crenel, a small cren 'notch': first the gap at the top of a castle wall. The origin of cren is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
la fourrière — the car pound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the parking ticket machine, then the French for the parallel parking space.

You'll want to know: la fourrière.
la fourrière — "the car pound".
Sa voiture était mal garée et elle est partie à la fourrière. Her car was badly parked and it was taken to the pound.
Also the pound for stray dogs.

The word, taken apart.
fourrière is usually traced to Old French feurre 'fodder', of Germanic origin: first the place where seized animals were kept and fed. EtymDB does not list it.
