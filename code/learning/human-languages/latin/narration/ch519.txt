Latin, chapter 519: The senses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vīsus, vīsūs — sight, vision.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sole of the foot, then the Latin for navel.

You'll want to know: vīsus, vīsūs.
vīsus, vīsūs — "sight, vision".
Senex vīsum āmittit. The old man is losing his sight.
Masculine, fourth declension, from videō. One of the five senses.

The word, taken apart.
vīsus is the action noun of videō "to see" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English vision comes from the related vīsiō.

olfaciō, olfacere — "to smell, scent".
Canis cibum olfacit. The dog smells the food.
olfaciō, olfacere, olfēcī, olfactum: oleō 'to give off a smell' + faciō. English olfactory.

The word, taken apart.
olfaciō is built on oleō "to give off a smell" and faciō "to make" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English olfactory comes from it.
