Bengali, chapter 438: Mind and Feeling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মনোভাব (mônobhāb) — an attitude; a frame of mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a female singer, then the Bengali for a female dancer.

You'll want to know: মনোভাব.
মনোভাব — mônobhāb — "an attitude; a frame of mind".
নতুন কাজ নিয়ে অরুণের মনোভাব ভালো। nôtun kāj niye ôruner mônobhāb bhālo. Arun has a good attitude to the new work.

The word, taken apart.
মনোভাব (mônobhāb) joins Sanskrit manas, "mind" (CDIAL 9822), in its compound form mano-, and bhāva, "state, disposition" (CDIAL 9475): the state of the mind. Manas is the মনে of মনে রাখা (taught), to remember.

Lesson 2 of 5.
অনুভূতি (ônubhūti) — a feeling; a sensation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a female dancer, then the Bengali for an attitude.

You'll want to know: অনুভূতি.
অনুভূতি — ônubhūti — "a feeling; a sensation".
এই গানটা শুনে আমার একটা নতুন অনুভূতি হল। ei gānṭā shune āmār ekṭā nôtun ônubhūti hôlô. Listening to this song, I had a new feeling.

The word, taken apart.
অনুভূতি (ônubhūti) is a tatsama, Sanskrit anubhūti, "perception" (Monier-Williams); in Bengali it names a feeling or a sensation.

Lesson 3 of 5.
আবেগ (ābeg) — emotion; strong feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an attitude, then the Bengali for a feeling.

You'll want to know: আবেগ.
আবেগ — ābeg — "emotion; strong feeling".
বাবা আবেগে কেঁদে ফেললেন। bābā ābege kẽde phellen. Father was so moved that he burst into tears.
আবেগে, overcome with feeling.

The word, taken apart.
আবেগ (ābeg) is a tatsama, Sanskrit āvega, "haste born of excitement, agitation" (Monier-Williams); Bengali uses it for a strong feeling that carries you along.

Lesson 4 of 5.
অনুভব করা (ônubhôb kôrā) — to feel; to sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a feeling, then the Bengali for emotion.

You'll want to know: অনুভব করা.
অনুভব করা — ônubhôb kôrā — "to feel; to sense".
মীরা বন্ধুর ব্যথা অনুভব করল। mīrā bôndhur bæthā ônubhôb kôrlô. Mira felt her friend's pain.
Past (he/she …): তিনি ভয় অনুভব করলেন, tini bhôy ônubhôb kôrlen.
অনুভব alone is the noun: what you feel from the inside.

The word, taken apart.
অনুভব করা (ônubhôb kôrā) is Sanskrit anubhava, "perception, understanding" (Monier-Williams), with kôrā, "to do" (CDIAL 2814).

Lesson 5 of 5.
টের পাওয়া (ṭer pāoyā) — to become aware of; to sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: টের পাওয়া.
টের পাওয়া — ṭer pāoyā — "to become aware of; to sense".
রাতে আমি বাইরে একটা আওয়াজ টের পেলাম। rāte āmi bāire ekṭā āoyāj ṭer pelām. In the night I became aware of a noise outside.
Past (he/she …): তিনি বিপদ টের পেলেন, tini bipôd ṭer pelen.
টের lives only inside টের পাওয়া (ṭer pāoyā), often for something you notice without seeing it.

The word, taken apart.
টের পাওয়া (ṭer pāoyā) is ṭer, "notice", which CDIAL lists for Bengali under a reconstructed form (CDIAL 5473), with pāoyā, "to get" (CDIAL 8943).
