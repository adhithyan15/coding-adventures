Latin, chapter 446: Little bed, Straw bedding, Mat, Pillow, Blanket.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
lectulus, lectulī — a little bed, a small couch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a stable-keeper, then the Latin for hospitable.

You'll want to know: lectulus, lectulī.
lectulus, lectulī — "a little bed, a small couch".
In cubiculō parvō lectulus dūrus est. In the small bedroom there is a hard little bed.
Second declension, masculine: lectulus, lectulī m., a diminutive of lectus 'bed'.

The word, taken apart.
lectulus is a diminutive of lectus 'bed, couch' (Lewis & Short), which is usually traced to PIE legʰ- 'to lie down'.

strāmentum, strāmentī — "straw bedding, straw; a covering".
Pauper in strāmentō dormit, nōn in lectō. The poor man sleeps on straw, not in a bed.
Second declension, neuter: strāmentum, strāmentī n. Straw spread out for sleeping on, or for animals.

The word, taken apart.
strāmentum is built on sternō 'to spread' (Lewis & Short), from its participle strātus, with the suffix -mentum: something spread out to lie on.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for straw bedding, then the Latin for a mat.

You'll want to know: cervīcal, cervīcālis.
cervīcal, cervīcālis — "a pillow, a bolster".
Hospes cervīcal sub caput pōnit et dormit. The guest puts a pillow under his head and sleeps.
Third declension, neuter: cervīcal, cervīcālis n. Literally a thing for the neck.

The word, taken apart.
cervīcal is built on cervīx, cervīcis 'neck' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a support for the neck. English cervical comes from cervīx.

Lesson 5 of 6.
lōdīx, lōdīcis — a blanket, a coverlet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a mat, then the Latin for a pillow.

You'll want to know: lōdīx, lōdīcis.
lōdīx, lōdīcis — "a blanket, a coverlet".
Nox frīgida est, sed lōdīx tenuis est. The night is cold, but the blanket is thin.
Third declension, feminine: lōdīx, lōdīcis f. A wool blanket for a bed.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of lōdīx is not settled: Lewis & Short give the sense, a blanket or coverlet, but no source, and EtymDB does not list it.
