Mandarin Chinese, chapter 321: People of Long Ago.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
lìshǐ — history.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to bring up a subject, then the Mandarin for a voice message.

You'll want to know: lìshǐ.
lìshǐ — "history".
wǒ zài dàxué xué guo Zhōngguó de lìshǐ — I studied Chinese history at university.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lìshǐ is built from lì 'to pass through' and shǐ 'a record', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lek and sriX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go through' and calls the second 'one who records events'.

古人 — gǔrén — "the ancients; people of long ago".

The new character, in pieces: 古 (gǔ) — five strokes: 十 on top, and 口 under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

古人 (gǔrén) is built from 古 (gǔ) 'ancient' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'former'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
古 (gǔ) — 十 over 口.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 十 over 口 and Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
十 口.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shí, kǒu]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 十 over 口.
古 (gǔ).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The top piece, 十, two strokes. A long bar from left to right, then a vertical down through its middle.
The bottom piece, 口, three strokes. A short vertical on the left; a bar that runs right and turns down; then the bar that closes the foot.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 古 (gǔ), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 古 (gǔ)? (Five.) What is on top? (十.) What is under it? (口.)

古书 — gǔshū — "ancient book; old book".
我在书店里看到了一本古书 wǒ zài shūdiàn lǐ kàndào le yì běn gǔshū — I came across an old book in the bookshop.

The word, taken apart.
古书 (gǔshū) is built from 古 (gǔ) 'ancient' and 书 'a book', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and syo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'former' and says of the second that it sets things down.

Lesson 5 of 6.
古老 (gǔlǎo) — ancient; age-old.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the people of long ago, then the Mandarin for an old book from long ago.

You'll want to know: 古老.
古老 — gǔlǎo — "ancient; age-old".
他们家的房子很古老 tāmen jiā de fángzi hěn gǔlǎo — Their family's house is ancient.
Two third tones meet in this word: gǔ is said with a rising tone before lǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
古老 (gǔlǎo) is built from 古 (gǔ) 'ancient' and 老 'old', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and lawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'former' and the second 'aged'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
文字 (wénzì) — script; written language.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an old book from long ago, then the Mandarin for age-old.

You'll want to know: 文字.
文字 — wénzì — "script; written language".
汉字是很古老的文字 Hànzì shì hěn gǔlǎo de wénzì — Chinese characters are a very old script.

The word, taken apart.
文字 (wénzì) is built from 文 'writing' and 字 'a character', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjun and dziH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'writing' and 'beautiful'; for the second it gives 'an ornament' and 'to nurse', and the sense 'a written character' is not given there.
