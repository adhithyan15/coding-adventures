Hindi, chapter 158: Wide, Deep, High, Low, Empty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चौड़ा (cauṛā) — wide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for young, then the Hindi for old.

You'll want to know: चौड़ा.
चौड़ा — cauṛā — "wide".

The word, taken apart.
चौड़ा (cauṛā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ca-uḍa, "wide, open", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4534). CDIAL suggests, with a "possibly", that it was built on cau-, "four".

You'll want to know: ऊँचा.
ऊँचा — ū̃cā — "high, tall".

The word, taken apart.
ऊँचा (ū̃cā) (ūṁcā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ucca, "tall, lofty" (CDIAL 1634). CDIAL calls the influence of udañc, "turned upwards", on the nasal very doubtful.

नीचा — nīcā — "low".

The word, taken apart.
नीचा (nīcā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nīca, "low" (CDIAL 7540). CDIAL explains its -c- as shaped after its opposite, ucca, "high".

You'll want to know: ख़ाली.
ख़ाली — khālī — "empty".

The word, taken apart.
ख़ाली (khālī) is a loan from Arabic khālī, "empty", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is kh-l-w (Lane).
