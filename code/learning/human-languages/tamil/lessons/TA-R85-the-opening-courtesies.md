[PAUSE 3s] Greet someone. Then say the one word that means *fine*, *correct*,
and *all right* at the same time.

## You'll want to know: two facts about Tamil courtesy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-ROOT-C01-VANAKKAM-FAMILY-REGISTER-01, TA-ROOT-C01-NANDRI-FAMILY-REGISTER-01] -->

Both of the opening words carry a claim the book made once and never returned
to.

**வணக்கம்** is **native**. Neighbouring languages reached for a Sanskrit
borrowing for the same bow; Tamil kept its own word and uses it across every
setting, formal to casual. That is unusual enough in the region to be worth
knowing.

**நன்றி** is native too — built on the word for *goodness* — and it comes with a
pragmatic warning: among people who are close, saying thanks out loud can sound
**marked**, a small distance opening where none was wanted. The word is correct;
the question is whether the moment calls for it.

## Grammar Lens: answering, and how few words it takes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C01-ANSWERING-01, TA-LEX-C01-SARI-01, TA-LEX-C01-PRACTICE-01] -->

| you mean | you say |
|---|---|
| yes | **ஆம்** |
| no | **இல்லை** |
| fine, agreed, got it | **சரி** |

**சரி** is the one that does the most work, because it answers three different
questions — *is that right?*, *will you?*, *understood?* — with one syllable
pair and no change.

## The exchange — how are you, both sides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C03-EPPADI-01, TA-LEX-C03-EPPADI-IRUKKIRIRGAL-01, TA-LEX-C03-NALAM-01, TA-LEX-C03-PARAVAYILLAI-01, TA-LEX-C03-PRACTICE-01] -->

- **நீங்கள் எப்படி இருக்கிறீர்கள்?** — how are you, respectfully
- **நலம்** — the answer, and the whole reply is *nāṉ nalamāga irukkiṟēṉ*
- **எப்படி** on its own — *how*, the question word inside it
- **பரவாயில்லை** — *no problem*, and also the Tamil for *you're welcome*

That last one is the pair worth holding: English keeps *no problem* and
*you're welcome* apart, and Tamil hands you one phrase for both.
