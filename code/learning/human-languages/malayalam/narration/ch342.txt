Malayalam, chapter 342: Must, Ought To, and Because.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ഞാൻ പോകണം (ñān pōkaṇaṁ) — I must go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want tea". (enikkŭ cāya vēṇaṁ.) Then "no need, I don't want it". (vēṇṭa.)

Grammar Lens: -അണം on the verb.
Take a verb's dictionary form, drop -ഉക, and add -അണം:
പോകുക becomes പോകണം, pōkaṇaṁ, വരുക becomes വരണം, varaṇaṁ, പഠിക്കുക becomes പഠിക്കണം, paṭhikkaṇaṁ.
Grammars explain -അണം as വേണം itself, worn down and joined to the verb. Who stands in front decides what it means:
ഞാൻ നാളെ പോകണം. ñān nāḷe pōkaṇaṁ. — "I must go tomorrow."
എനിക്ക് നാളെ പോകണം. enikkŭ nāḷe pōkaṇaṁ. — "I want to go tomorrow."
With ഞാൻ it is a duty; with the dative എനിക്ക്, as with വേണം alone, it is a wish or a need.

Grammar Lens: ought to, need not, must not.
-ഏണ്ടതാണ് says what is right to do: "ought to".
നീ നേരത്തെ ഉറങ്ങേണ്ടതാണ്. nī nēratte uṟaṅṅēṇṭatāṇŭ. — "You ought to sleep early."
-ഏണ്ട, the വേണ്ട you know, says "need not": നീ വരേണ്ട (nī varēṇṭa), "you need not come". -അരുത് forbids: നീ വൈകരുത് (nī vaikarutŭ), "you must not be late".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "you must come", "I must study" — nī varaṇaṁ, ñān paṭhikkaṇaṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You need not come", then "you must not be late"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I must go"? (ñān pōkaṇaṁ.) What does enikkŭ pōkaṇaṁ say instead? (I want to go.) "Ought to sleep"? (uṟaṅṅēṇṭatāṇŭ.) "Need not come"? (varēṇṭa.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ഔദ്യോഗികമായ (audyōgikamāya) — official.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "an official, an officer", then "a document". (udyōgasthan, rēkha.) Then "I must go". (ñān pōkaṇaṁ.)

You'll want to know: ഔദ്യോഗികമായ.
ഔദ്യോഗികമായ — audyōgikamāya — "official": of an office, done formally.
ഇത് ഒരു ഔദ്യോഗികമായ രേഖയാണ്. itŭ oru audyōgikamāya rēkhayāṇŭ. — "This is an official document."
In set phrases it stands before its noun without -മായ:
മലയാളം കേരളത്തിന്റെ ഔദ്യോഗിക ഭാഷയാണ്. malayāḷaṁ kēraḷattinṟe audyōgika bhāṣayāṇŭ. — "Malayalam is the official language of Kerala."
With -ആയി it becomes "officially": ഔദ്യോഗികമായി (audyōgikamāyi). Its first letter is new; the next lesson takes it out and puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ഔദ്യോഗികമായ (audyōgikamāya) is a learned word built the Sanskrit way on udyoga, "undertaking, exertion" (Monier-Williams), which Malayalam took up for a post in an office: ഉദ്യോഗസ്ഥൻ, "an officer", is the same root. Lengthening the first vowel, u to au, and adding -ika makes "having to do with" it, so "of the office". The Malayalam -മായ then makes it an adjective.

[your turn — say: audyōgikamāya, in five beats: au-dyō-gi-ka-māya]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "This is an official document."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Malayalam is the official language of Kerala."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "officially" — audyōgikamāyi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Official"? (ഔദ്യോഗികമായ (audyōgikamāya).) "The official language"? (audyōgika bhāṣa.) Which word for a person shares its root? (ഉദ്യോഗസ്ഥൻ, "an officer".)

Script you'll notice: ഔ.
ഔ — au, at the start of a word. It is rare: a handful of learned words begin with it.
Its body is the shape of ഒ, which you write inside ഓ: an inner loop, a broad arch, a rounded lobe. On its right it carries the tail you have written on ഊ: a small lobe, a notch, and a broader lobe that sweeps back along its foot. Chapter 344 gives that tail a lesson of its own.

Writing: ഔ.
One recorded hand writes ഔ (au) in two strokes. First the body: from the inner tip, round the small loop, out over the broad arch, and down round the lower lobe to its foot. Then, after one lift, the tail: over the small lobe, into the notch, over the broader lobe and back along its foot.
Write ഔ (au) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Copy the printed shape and keep the order above.

Lesson 4 of 5.
വന്നതുകൊണ്ട് (vannatukoṇṭŭ) — because he came.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I had a fever. So I did not come." Then the same with kāraṇaṁ.

Grammar Lens: -അതുകൊണ്ട് on a verb.
To put the reason inside the sentence, turn its verb into "the …-ing" with -അത്, as in vaikiyatŭ, and add കൊണ്ട്, "with":
വന്നു becomes വന്നതുകൊണ്ട് — vannatukoṇṭŭ — because (he) came.
മഴ പെയ്തതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല. maḻa peytatukoṇṭŭ ñān vannilla. — "Because it rained, I did not come."
The present works the same way: വരുന്നു gives വരുന്നതുകൊണ്ട്. ഇല്ല gives ഇല്ലാത്തതുകൊണ്ട്:
സമയം ഇല്ലാത്തതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ ബസിൽ പോയി. samayaṁ illāttatukoṇṭŭ ñān basil pōyi. — "Because there was no time, I went by bus."

Grammar Lens: a plan and its reason.
A plan is in the future in -ഉം. Give its reason after കാരണം:
അടുത്ത വർഷം ഞാൻ കേരളത്തിൽ പോകും, കാരണം എനിക്ക് കൂടുതൽ മലയാളം പഠിക്കണം. aṭutta varṣaṁ ñān kēraḷattil pōkuṁ, kāraṇaṁ enikkŭ kūṭutal malayāḷaṁ paṭhikkaṇaṁ. — "Next year I will go to Kerala, because I want to learn more Malayalam."
