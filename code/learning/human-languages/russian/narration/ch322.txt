Russian, chapter 322: Knights.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
рыцарь (rýtsar) — a knight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a prince, then the Russian for a kingdom.

You'll want to know: рыцарь.
рыцарь (rýtsar) — "a knight".
Рыцарь сел на коня и уехал. Rýtsar sel na konyá i uyékhal. The knight got on his horse and rode off.
Genitive рыцаря.

The word, taken apart.
рыцарь (rýtsar) was borrowed through Polish rycerz from German Ritter "a rider, a knight" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
доспехи (dospékhi) — armour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a kingdom, then the Russian for a knight.

You'll want to know: доспехи.
доспехи (dospékhi) — "armour".
Доспехи рыцаря были очень тяжёлыми. Dospékhi rýtsarya býli óchen tyazhyólymi. The knight's armour was very heavy.
Plural; genitive доспехов.

The word, taken apart.
доспехи (dospékhi) is usually traced to доспеть "to get ready", from the root of спех "haste" and успех "success": the gear made ready. EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 4 of 5.
щит (shchit) — a shield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for armour, then the Russian for a sword.

You'll want to know: щит.
щит (shchit) — "a shield".
На щите был нарисован лев. Na shchité byl narisóvan lev. A lion was painted on the shield.
Genitive щита. Also a board: электрический щит (shchit) — a fuse box.

The word, taken apart.
щит (shchit) is inherited through Old East Slavic ščitŭ from Proto-Slavic ščitŭ "shield", which EtymDB traces to PIE skey- "to cut, to split": a split board.

Lesson 5 of 5.
трон (tron) — a throne.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a sword, then the Russian for a shield.

You'll want to know: трон.
трон (tron) — "a throne".
Король сидел на золотом троне. Koról sidél na zolotóm tróne. The king sat on a golden throne.
Взойти на трон (tron) — to come to the throne.

The word, taken apart.
трон (tron) came through Latin thronus "throne" from Greek thrónos "a chair, a throne" (EtymDB).
