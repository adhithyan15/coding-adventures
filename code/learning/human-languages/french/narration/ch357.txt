French, chapter 357: Le premier poste.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
le stage — the internship, the work placement; a training course.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to hire, then the French for to recruit.

You'll want to know: le stage.
le stage — "the internship, the work placement; a training course".
Cet été, je fais un stage dans un hôtel. This summer, I am doing an internship in a hotel.
A false friend: not a theatre stage. Un stage de cuisine — a cooking course.

The word, taken apart.
stage comes from Medieval Latin stagium "a period of residence" (EtymDB), usually linked to Latin stāre "to stand" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: l'apprenti.
l'apprenti — "the apprentice".
Le boulanger a un apprenti de seize ans. The baker has a sixteen-year-old apprentice.
Feminine apprentie.

The word, taken apart.
apprenti comes from Middle French apprentis (EtymDB), built on apprendre "to learn", which comes from Latin apprehendere "to seize, to grasp" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: l'employeur.
l'employeur — "the employer".
Mon employeur me paie à la fin du mois. My employer pays me at the end of the month.
The feminine employeuse is rare.

The word, taken apart.
employeur is built from employer and -eur. employer is inherited from Latin implicāre "to enfold, to involve" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
