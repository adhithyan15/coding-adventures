Bengali, chapter 512: Kitchen Tools.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
উনুন (unun) — a stove (of clay); an oven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to pour, then the Bengali for to dip.

You'll want to know: উনুন.
উনুন — unun — "a stove (of clay); an oven".
দিদিমা উনুনে ভাত রান্না করেন। Didimā unune bhāt rānnā kôren. My grandmother cooks rice on the clay stove.

The word, taken apart.
উনুন (unun) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Bengali unun, "oven, fireplace", under Sanskrit uṣṇayati, "heats" (CDIAL 2394).

Lesson 2 of 5.
খুন্তি (khunti) — a spatula; a flat cooking spoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to dip, then the Bengali for a stove.

You'll want to know: খুন্তি.
খুন্তি — khunti — "a spatula; a flat cooking spoon".
আমি খুন্তিটা কড়াইয়ের পাশে রাখলাম। Āmi khuntiṭā kôṛāiyer pāshe rākhlām. I put the spatula beside the wok.

The word, taken apart.
খুন্তি (khunti) is a tadbhava from Sanskrit khanitra, "a digging tool"; CDIAL lists Bengali khunti for a long-handled digging tool (CDIAL 3814). The kitchen sense, a spatula, is the modern language's own.
