German, chapter 505: Trainieren.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
trainieren — to train, to practise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for seed, then the German for harvest.

You'll want to know: trainieren.
trainieren — "to train, to practise".
Die Mannschaft trainiert dreimal pro Woche. The team trains three times a week.
Regular, Perfekt without ge-: hat trainiert.

The word, taken apart.
trainieren was borrowed from English to train (EtymDB lists the German verb), which came from French traîner "to drag", from Latin trahere "to pull".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: die Ausdauer.
die Ausdauer — "stamina, endurance".
Für einen Marathon braucht man viel Ausdauer. For a marathon you need a lot of stamina.
No plural.

The word, taken apart.
Ausdauer is built on aus "out" and dauern "to last", from Middle High German dūren, which came from Latin dūrāre "to last" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
der Muskelkater — sore muscles (after exercise).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for fitness, then the German for to stretch.

You'll want to know: der Muskelkater.
der Muskelkater — "sore muscles (after exercise)".
Nach dem ersten Training hatte ich drei Tage Muskelkater. After my first training session I had sore muscles for three days.
No plural. Literally a muscle tomcat; der Kater is also a hangover.

The word, taken apart.
Muskelkater is built on Muskel, from Latin mūsculus "muscle" (EtymDB), and Kater "tomcat", inherited from Old High German kataro; the joke behind the second part is not settled.
