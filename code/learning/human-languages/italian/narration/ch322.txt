Italian, chapter 322: Fuochi e falò.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
i fuochi d'artificio — the fireworks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to decorate, then the Italian for the tombola.

You'll want to know: i fuochi d'artificio.
i fuochi d'artificio — "the fireworks".
A mezzanotte ci sono i fuochi d'artificio. At midnight there are fireworks.
Artificio — a skilful device.

The word, taken apart.
fuochi is the plural of fuoco "fire", from Latin focus "hearth" (Lewis & Short); artificio comes from Latin artificium "craft, skill".

A fine anno si sentono botti dappertutto. At New Year you hear bangs everywhere.
Di botto — suddenly. I botti are firecrackers.

The word, taken apart.
botto is usually explained as a sound word that imitates a bang (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il falò — the bonfire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the fireworks, then the Italian for the firework bang.

You'll want to know: il falò.
il falò — "the bonfire".
Sulla spiaggia abbiamo acceso un falò. We lit a bonfire on the beach.
Stress on the last syllable; plural unchanged.

The word, taken apart.
falò comes from Greek phanós "a lantern, a torch" (EtymDB), probably through a Byzantine Greek form.
