Mandarin Chinese, chapter 445: By the Sea.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hǎilàng — sea wave; a wave of the sea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a lighthouse, then the Mandarin for sunscreen.

You'll want to know: hǎilàng.
hǎilàng — "sea wave; a wave of the sea".
hǎilàng tài dà le chuán kāi bu dào nàge dǎo — The waves are too big: the boat can't get to the island.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǎilàng is built from 海 'the sea' and làng 'a wave', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xojX and langH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'waves'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
yúchuán — fishing boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sunscreen, then the Mandarin for a wave of the sea.

You'll want to know: yúchuán.
yúchuán — "fishing boat".
měitiān zǎoshang hěn duō yúchuán chūhǎi — Every morning lots of fishing boats go out to sea.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yúchuán is built from yú 'fishing' and 船 'a boat', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjo and zywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, as catching fish. It is the syllable of yú 'a fish', written with the water piece.

Lesson 4 of 5.
bèiké — seashell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a fishing boat, then the Mandarin for to go ashore.

You'll want to know: bèiké.
bèiké — "seashell".
wǒ zhǎodào le hěn duō bèiké fàng zài bāo lǐ — I found lots of shells and put them in my bag.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bèiké is built from bèi 'a shellfish' and ké 'a hard shell'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese pajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as a creature of the sea in a shell; the second is in the Guangyun only under an older spelling, read khaewk and glossed 'a hard skin', and the everyday ké does not follow from that reading, so its history is not settled.

One more word for getting around.

xiàwǔ tuìcháo de shíhou wǒmen qù hǎibiān zhǎo bèiké — In the afternoon, when the tide goes out, we look for shells on the shore.
