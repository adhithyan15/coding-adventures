# du bist — "you are"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DU-SIE-02, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Du* is the familiar "you" — the one for a friend. Its verb ending
is **-st**, and for once *sein* uses it.

## You'll want to know: du bist
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-BIST-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-ENDINGS-01] -->

> **du bist** — "you are."

Take *bin*, drop the *n*, add the familiar **-st**: *bi-* plus *-st* is
*bist*. That is the same ending as *du gehst*, *du heißt*, *du siehst*.

Of the six present forms of *sein*, this is the only one that can be derived
from another one. Enjoy it; it does not happen again.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-BIST-01] -->

- `u-long` — *du* has a long, rounded **oo**, the vowel of English *do*, held.
- `final-t` — German releases a final **t** fully and audibly. *Bist* ends with
  a small puff of air; English tends to swallow it.
