Latin, chapter 384: Elections.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
suffrāgium, suffrāgiī — a vote; the right to vote.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a quaestor, then the Latin for a censor.

You'll want to know: suffrāgium, suffrāgiī.
suffrāgium, suffrāgiī — "a vote; the right to vote".
Cīvēs suffrāgia ferunt. The citizens cast their votes.
suffrāgium, suffrāgiī n. Suffrāgium ferre, to vote. English suffrage.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of suffrāgium is not settled: Lewis & Short link it, with a "perhaps", to suffrāgō "the hock of a leg", and EtymDB has no parent for it. English suffrage comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
comitia, comitiōrum — the voting assembly, the elections.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a censor, then the Latin for a vote.

You'll want to know: comitia, comitiōrum.
comitia, comitiōrum — "the voting assembly, the elections".
Comitia mox habēbuntur. The elections will be held soon.
comitia, comitiōrum n. pl.: the Roman people meeting to vote. The singular comitium is the meeting place.

The word, taken apart.
comitia is built from com- "together" and the root of eō "to go" (Lewis & Short): a going together.

Lesson 3 of 5.
candidātus, candidātī — a candidate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: candidātus, candidātī.
candidātus, candidātī — "a candidate".
Candidātus cīvēs in forō salūtābat. The candidate was greeting the citizens in the Forum.
candidātus, candidātī m., from candidus, shining white: a man seeking office wore a toga whitened with chalk. English candidate.

The word, taken apart.
candidātus is built on candidus "shining white" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): dressed in white. English candidate comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ambitiō, ambitiōnis — canvassing; ambition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the voting assembly, then the Latin for a candidate.

You'll want to know: ambitiō, ambitiōnis.
ambitiō, ambitiōnis — "canvassing; ambition".
Ambitiō eius omnibus nōta erat. His canvassing was known to all.
ambitiō, ambitiōnis f., built on ambiō, to go round: the candidate went round asking for votes. English ambition.

The word, taken apart.
ambitiō is the noun built on ambiō "to go round" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from amb- "around" and eō "to go". English ambition comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ēligō, ēligere — to pick out, to choose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a candidate, then the Latin for canvassing.

You'll want to know: ēligō, ēligere.
ēligō, ēligere — "to pick out, to choose".
Ex multīs hunc ēlēgimus. Out of many we chose this one.
ēligō, ēligere, ēlēgī, ēlēctum: ē + legō, to pick. English elect and election.

The word, taken apart.
ēligō is built from ē "out" and legō "to pick, to gather" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English elect comes from its participle ēlēctus.
