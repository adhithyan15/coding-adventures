Telugu, chapter 54: The Tree.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) — a tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mabbu mean?

You'll want to know: చెట్టు.
చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) — "a tree".
చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) is inherited and old. The Dravidian family kept the shape but shuffled the meaning: Tamil's ceṭi is a bush or a shrub, a small woody thing, while Telugu raised the same word to the full-grown tree and left the bush to other words.
A meaning that moves up or down the scale like that is the commonest kind of drift there is. The word holds its place in the family; what it points at slides.
మ్రాను is another Telugu word for a tree, older-sounding and now mostly literary. చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) is what anybody says.

What you've built.
The thing the rest of this chapter is made of.

Lesson 2 of 5.
కొమ్మ (komma) — a branch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ceṭṭu mean?

You'll want to know: కొమ్మ.
కొమ్మ (komma) — "a branch".
కొమ్మ (komma) is the branch, the arm the చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) puts out sideways.
It stands very close to కొమ్ము, the horn of an animal, and the closeness is not an accident: both are the thing that grows out and forks. A tree wearing horns is a fair description of a tree in winter.
కొమ్మ (komma) turns up in old poetry meaning a young woman, by way of the slenderness of a branch. That sense has gone quiet, but it is why the word looks odd inside a love poem.

What you've built.
Two: the tree and what it puts out.

Lesson 3 of 5.
వేరు (vēru) — a root.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did komma mean?

You'll want to know: వేరు.
వేరు (vēru) — "a root".
వేరు (vēru) is the root under the చెట్టు (ceṭṭu). It also means "separate, apart, other" — వేరు (vēru) as an answer means "that is a different thing".
One shape, two jobs, and they are not far apart when you think about what a root does: it is the part that is set aside, buried, kept out of the daylight and out of the reckoning.
Telugu is comfortable letting a word carry a solid thing and an abstract idea at once. You had one working two jobs a moment ago, with చుక్క.

What you've built.
Three: what is up, what is out, and what is under.

Lesson 4 of 5.
విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) — a seed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did vēru mean?

You'll want to know: విత్తనం.
విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) — "a seed".
విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) is the seed, and it is built on విత్తు, "to sow". The word for the thing comes out of the word for the act, which is the ordinary direction of travel: people had been sowing for a long time before anybody needed a name for what they held.
Sanskrit's bīja was borrowed in as bījaṁ, and it does the abstract work — the seed of an idea, the germ of a quarrel. విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) stays in the sack.
The whole chapter is inside this word. A విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) becomes a చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) with a వేరు (vēru) below and a కొమ్మ (komma) above.

What you've built.
Four.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మొక్క (mokka) — a young plant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did vittanaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: మొక్క.
మొక్క (mokka) — "a young plant".
మొక్క (mokka) is what the విత్తనం (vittanaṁ) becomes before it earns the name చెట్టు (ceṭṭu): a seedling, a sapling, anything small and green and rooted.
The line between మొక్క (mokka) and చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) is about woodiness rather than height. A tomato plant stays a మొక్క (mokka) at any size, because it stays soft. A చెట్టు (ceṭṭu) has bark.
Do not tie it to మొక్కు, "to bow, to make a vow", which sounds close. Telugu has a good many pairs like that, and a resemblance on its own settles nothing.

What you've built.
Five: చెట్టు (ceṭṭu), కొమ్మ (komma), వేరు (vēru), విత్తనం (vittanaṁ), మొక్క (mokka). A tree, its parts, and its beginning.
