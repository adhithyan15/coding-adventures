Mandarin Chinese, chapter 511: Close Family.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
亲人 (qīnrén) — one's close family; one's nearest and dearest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to dote on, then the Mandarin for to soothe a child.

You'll want to know: 亲人.
亲人 — qīnrén — "one's close family; one's nearest and dearest".
在国外的时候我最想我的亲人 zài guówài de shíhou wǒ zuì xiǎng wǒ de qīnrén — When I was abroad, I missed my family most of all.
The new character, in pieces: 亲 (qīn) — nine strokes in two pieces: on top the top of 意 yì, five strokes; under it four strokes, a bar, a vertical that hooks at the foot, a stroke falling left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
亲人 (qīnrén) is built from 亲 (qīn) 'close' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshin and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to love' and 'near'.

What you've built.

Script — the top of yì, then four strokes.
亲 (qīn).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top, five strokes, as on top of 意: a dot, a bar, a dot on the left and a short stroke falling to the left on the right, then a long bar.
The bottom, four strokes: a bar, a vertical down the middle that hooks to the left at the foot, a short stroke falling to the left, and a dot falling to the right.
5 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 亲 (qīn), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 亲 (qīn) have? (Two.) Which character you know has the same top? (意.) How does the middle vertical end? (With a hook to the left.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 亲 (qīn), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to soothe a child, then the Mandarin for one's nearest and dearest.

You'll want to know: 父亲.
父亲 — fùqīn — "father (the formal word)".
我父亲在大学教了三十年汉语 wǒ fùqīn zài dàxué jiāo le sānshí nián Hànyǔ — My father taught Chinese at a university for thirty years.
Many speakers make the second syllable light: fùqin.

The word, taken apart.
父亲 (fùqīn) is built from 父 'a father' and 亲 (qīn) 'close', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjuX and tshin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

妈妈亲自来学校看我 māma qīnzì lái xuéxiào kàn wǒ — Mum came to school to see me herself.

The word, taken apart.
亲自 (qīnzì) is built from 亲 (qīn) 'close' and 自 'self', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshin and dzijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'from'; the sense 'self' is not given there.
