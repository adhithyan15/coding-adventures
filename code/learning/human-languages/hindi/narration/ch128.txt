Hindi, chapter 128: Letters You Have Read All Along.
5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
◌ि (i kī mātrā) — the short i sign in शुक्रिया.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ◌ि and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ड and ़ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say शुक्रिया (shukriyā), "thanks". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ◌ि.
ि (i kī mātrā) — the short i sign.
It is inside शुक्रिया (shukriyā), "thanks", one of the first words in this book. Look at क्रि: the sign stands before the consonants and is said after them, kri. It is the one Hindi vowel sign written ahead of the sound it follows.

Writing: ◌ि.
Put your pen on ◌ि (i kī mātrā) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at शुक्रिया and point at ◌ि (i kī mātrā) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ◌ि (i kī mātrā) three times, saying i as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ़, then ◌ि (i kī mātrā), from memory]
[your turn — read: शुक्रिया aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ◌ि? (i kī mātrā, i.) Which word did it come from? (शुक्रिया, shukriyā, "thanks".)


Lesson 2 of 5.
इ (i) — the letter inside दिखाइए.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: इ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ़ and ◌ि (i kī mātrā) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say दिखाइए (dikhāie), "please show me". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: इ.
इ — the letter i.
It is inside दिखाइए (dikhāie), "please show me". After ा comes another vowel, and a vowel that follows a vowel has no consonant to ride on, so it is written as its own letter. ि (i kī mātrā), from the last lesson, is the same vowel as a sign.

Writing: इ.
1. start here: descend the upright from the headline.
2. turn left and curve around the upper bowl without lifting.
3. without lifting, sweep right through the waist and around the lower bowl.
4. finish down-right through the tail without lifting.
5. lift, then draw the shirorekha left-to-right — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari इ stroke order.svg’, panels 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at दिखाइए and point at इ inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: इ three times, saying i as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ◌ि (i kī mātrā), then इ (i), from memory]
[your turn — read: दिखाइए aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — इ? (i, i.) Which word did it come from? (दिखाइए, dikhāie, "please show me".)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ञ (ña) — the letter hidden inside ज्ञ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ञ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ◌ि (i kī mātrā) and इ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say संज्ञा (saṁjñā), "noun". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ञ.
ञ — ña, a nasal made where j is made.
It is hidden inside संज्ञा (saṁjñā), "noun". The joined letter ज्ञ is ज and ञ (ña) written as one, and Hindi says the pair gy. You have been reading ञ (ña) inside that join without seeing it on its own.

Writing: ञ.
1. start here: sweep clockwise around the open-left bowl.
2. lift, then sweep the shoulder right and rise to the headline.
3. lift, then descend the short lower stem.
4. lift, then draw the shirorekha left-to-right — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ञ (ña)-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at संज्ञा and point at ञ (ña) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ञ (ña) three times, saying ñ as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: इ (i), then ञ (ña), from memory]
[your turn — read: संज्ञा aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ञ? (ña, ñ.) Which word did it come from? (संज्ञा, saṁjñā, "noun".)


Lesson 4 of 5.

Guided Practice.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
