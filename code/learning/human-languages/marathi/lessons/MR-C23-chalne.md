# चालणे (chālṇe) — to walk, and the yes that comes out of it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-JHOPNE, MR-LEX-VICHAR-KARNE, MR-ETYMON-VICHAR-THINK-ASK] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **विचार** rested on a root meaning *to turn, to move about*. Its
close relative walks in here on its own two feet.

## You'll want to know: चालणे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-CHALNE]; assesses=[] -->

> **चालणे** — *chālṇe* — **to walk, to go along**

> **मी चालतो / चालते.** — *mī chālto / chālte.* — **I walk.**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**च** before **ा** lands closer to English *ts* than to *ch* in most Marathi
mouths, so **चा** is nearer *tsā* — the same thing that happens in *chār*.
Then **ल** and **णे**. **चा-ल-णे**.

## Grammar Lens: the yes that means it will walk
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-SENSE-CHALEL-OKAY]; assesses=[MR-LEX-CHALNE] -->

> **चालेल.** — *chālel.* — **That will do. / Fine by me.**

Formally this is the verb in the future: *it will walk*. In use it is one of
the commonest small yeses in the language — offered a time, a plan, a cup of
tea, you answer **चालेल** and the matter is settled. Beside **बरं**, which
agrees, **चालेल** accepts.

## The word, taken apart — moving, and thinking
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-CHALNE-CAL]; assesses=[MR-ETYMON-VICHAR-THINK-ASK] -->

**चालणे** continues Sanskrit **चलति** *calati*, "moves, stirs," on the root
**चल्** *cal-*, which is widely taken to be a variant of **चर्** *car-*, "to
move about" — the root you already met sitting inside **विचार**. Sanskrit
**चंचल** *cañcala*, "restless," is built on the same piece. So thinking and
walking, in this language, are two speeds of one old verb for moving.
