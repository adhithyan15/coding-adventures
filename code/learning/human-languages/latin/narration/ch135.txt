Lesson 1 of 5.
balaena, balaenae — a whale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a monkey, then the Latin for a dolphin.

You'll want to know: balaena, balaenae.
balaena, balaenae — "a whale".

The word, taken apart.
balaena was borrowed from Greek phalaina, "whale" (Lewis & Short), with b for the Greek ph.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dolphin, then the Latin for a whale.

You'll want to know: gallus, gallī.
gallus, gallī — "a cockerel".

The word, taken apart.
gallus is often traced to Proto-Italic galso-, "the crier" (EtymDB 2.1), after the bird's call, but this is uncertain. It is a different word from Gallus, "a Gaul".

Lesson 3 of 5.
labrum, labrī — a lip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a whale, then the Latin for a cockerel.

You'll want to know: labrum, labrī.
labrum, labrī — "a lip".

The word, taken apart.
labrum is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European leb-, "lip, to hang loosely" (EtymDB 2.1); English lip is a cousin. It is a different word from labrum, "a basin", contracted from lavābrum, "a bath" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
mentum, mentī — the chin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cockerel, then the Latin for a lip.

You'll want to know: mentum, mentī.
mentum, mentī — "the chin".

The word, taken apart.
mentum is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European men-, "to project, to stick out" (EtymDB 2.1): the chin as the part that juts. minae, "overhanging points, threats", is often put in the same family (Lewis & Short).
