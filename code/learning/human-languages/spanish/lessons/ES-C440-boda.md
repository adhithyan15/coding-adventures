# la boda — the vows, taken as one thing

## Warm-up

## You'll want to know: la boda
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C309-KIN-16] -->

**la boda** — the wedding.

*Vamos a una boda el sábado* — we are going to a wedding on Saturday. Spanish
uses the singular for the event and the plural **las bodas** for an anniversary
of it: *las bodas de plata* are a silver wedding, twenty-five years on.

The **b** is soft between vowels — *la BO-da*, with the lips barely closing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01] -->

**boda** looks like one solid piece and is not. It is Latin **vōta**, *the vows*
— the plural of **vōtum**, a vow.

A wedding was named for what is said at it, and the name is plural because more
than one vow is spoken. Then Spanish stopped hearing the plural and treated the
whole word as a single feminine noun, which is why you can now say *una boda*,
one-of-the-vows, without it sounding odd to anybody.

English kept the same root in **vote**, **votive** and **devotion**, all of them
about something pledged.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-01, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-03] -->

This is not a one-off. Latin had a third gender, **neuter**, whose plural ended
in **-a**, and Spanish lost that gender entirely. The plurals were left standing
with a feminine-looking ending and nothing to belong to, so the language took
them as feminine singulars.

| Latin, a neuter plural | Spanish, a feminine singular |
|---|---|
| vōta, the vows | **la boda** |
| folia, the leaves | la hoja |
| ligna, the firewoods | la leña |

Every one of those is a **countable plural read as a single mass or event**, and
that is the shape to look for: a Spanish feminine noun naming one thing made of
many. It is a third way for a word to hide its pieces — not a false ending, not
a false prefix, but a number the language forgot.

`cerrado` in the last chapter came apart the moment you looked. **boda** will
not, and no amount of looking at modern Spanish would tell you why.
