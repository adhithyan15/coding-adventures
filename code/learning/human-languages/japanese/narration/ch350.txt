Japanese, chapter 350: Taking Care.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ようじんする (yōjin suru) — to take precautions; to be on guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to miss a chance, then the Japanese for at great pains.

You'll want to know: ようじんする.
ようじんする — yōjin suru — "to take precautions; to be on guard".
ばんに でかけたら、ようじんして ください。 — ban ni dekaketara, yōjin shite kudasai — "If you go out in the evening, be on your guard."
Polite ようじんします, yōjinshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ちちは ようじんしました。, chichi wa yōjin shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ようじんする (yōjin suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, yōjin, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: yō, "utilize" (Middle Chinese yowngH) and jin, "heart" (sim) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
そなえる (sonaeru) — to prepare for; to be ready for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to take precautions, then the Japanese for carelessness.

You'll want to know: そなえる.
そなえる — sonaeru — "to prepare for; to be ready for".
たいふうが きたら こまるから、そなえた ほうが いいです。 — taifū ga kitara komaru kara, sonaeta hō ga ii desu — "A typhoon would cause trouble, so we should be ready for one."
Polite そなえます, sonaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): まちが たいふうに そなえました。, machi ga taifū ni sonaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
そなえる (sonaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
