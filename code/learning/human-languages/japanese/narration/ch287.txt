Japanese, chapter 287: Growing Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
よちよち (yochiyochi) — toddling; with tottering steps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for congratulations, then the Japanese for a grandchild.

You'll want to know: よちよち.
よちよち — yochiyochi — "toddling; with tottering steps".
あかちゃんが よちよち あるいています。 — akachan ga yochiyochi aruite imasu — "The baby is toddling along."

The word, taken apart.
よちよち (yochiyochi) is a native Japanese mimetic word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

はえる — haeru — "to sprout; to come through (of teeth)".
にわに くさが はえています。 — niwa ni kusa ga haete imasu — "Grass is coming up in the garden."
Polite はえます, haemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あかちゃんの はが はえました。, akachan no ha ga haemashita.

The word, taken apart.
はえる (haeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

おばあさん — obāsan — "a grandmother; an old woman".
おばあさんは まいにち さんぽします。 — obāsan wa mainichi sanpo shimasu — "Grandma goes for a walk every day."

The word, taken apart.
おばあさん (obāsan) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: bāsan, "an old woman", with the polite prefix o-. JMdict writes it with characters for "grandmother", read for their meaning (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to sprout, then the Japanese for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: しんせき.
しんせき — shinseki — "a relative".
しょうがつに しんせきが あつまります。 — shōgatsu ni shinseki ga atsumarimasu — "Our relatives gather at the new year."

The word, taken apart.
しんせき (shinseki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shin, "relative" (Middle Chinese tshin) and seki, "relatives" (tshek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
せいちょうする (seichō suru) — to grow up; to grow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a grandmother, then the Japanese for a relative.

You'll want to know: せいちょうする.
せいちょうする — seichō suru — "to grow up; to grow".
こどもは すぐ せいちょうします。 — kodomo wa sugu seichō shimasu — "Children grow up fast."
Polite せいちょうします, seichōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): こどもが せいちょうしました。, kodomo ga seichō shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
せいちょうする (seichō suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sei, "grow" (Middle Chinese dzyeng) and chō, "long" (trjangX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The native verb suru, "to do", makes it a verb.
