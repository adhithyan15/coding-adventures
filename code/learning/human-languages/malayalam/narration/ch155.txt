Malayalam, chapter 155: Cheek, Chin, Street, Library, Shirt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
കവിൾ (kavil) — a cheek.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for skin, then the Malayalam for a forehead.

You'll want to know: കവിൾ.
കവിൾ — kavil — "a cheek".

The word, taken apart.
കവിൾ (kavil) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil kavuḷ (DEDR 1337). DEDR 1337 compares Sanskrit kapola, with a query.

താടി (tāṭi) is of uncertain origin; DEDR, the dictionary of the Dravidian family, has no entry for it in this sense.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ഗ്രന്ഥശാല (granthaśāla) — a library.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a chin, then the Malayalam for a street.
[pause 1 second]
With enikkŭ in front, say "I know," "I understood" and "I like." (Aṟiyāṁ, manassilāyi, iṣṭamāṇŭ.)

You'll want to know: ഗ്രന്ഥശാല.
ഗ്രന്ഥശാല — granthaśāla — "a library".

The word, taken apart.
ഗ്രന്ഥശാല (granthaśāla) is built from Sanskrit parts: grantha, "a book", first "a knot" of bound leaves (CDIAL 4350), and śālā, "house, hall" (CDIAL 12414).
