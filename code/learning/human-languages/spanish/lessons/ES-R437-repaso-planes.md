## Grammar Lens: the reflexive marks what happens to you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-03, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-01, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-02] -->

| plain | reflexive | the difference |
|---|---|---|
| decido | me decido | choosing a thing vs making up your mind |
| equivoco | **me equivoco** | the error befalls you |

**equivocarse** is almost never plain, because a mistake is something that
happens **to** a person. The same shape covers *caerse*, *olvidarse* and
*perderse* — a family worth noticing together rather than one at a time.

**coincidir** takes no reflexive and still points at you: *me coincide* uses the
indirect pronoun, not the reflexive, because the clash is reported **to** you
rather than done by you.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-04, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-05, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-02, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-01] -->

**Seven chapters ago you could name things. You can now arrange an evening and
say what went wrong with it.** You can be offered two dates, decide, say the
other one clashes, admit a mistake, say how long something takes and name the
meal at the end of it.

**tardar** closes a debt: the chapter on delays mentioned it in passing and did
not teach it, and a word shown but never given is one a reader half-knows.
