Italian, chapter 344: Le prove.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la prova — the proof, the evidence; the test, the trial.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for absurd, then the Italian for questionable.

You'll want to know: la prova.
la prova — "the proof, the evidence; the test, the trial".
Non ci sono prove che sia stato lui. There is no proof that it was him.
Mettere alla prova — to put to the test. In a shop, la prova is also trying something on.

The word, taken apart.
prova is built on provare, inherited from Latin probāre "to test, to approve, to prove" (Lewis & Short), from probus "good, upright".

Lesson 2 of 5.
evidente — evident, clear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for questionable, then the Italian for the proof.

You'll want to know: evidente.
evidente — "evident, clear".
È evidente che non ha studiato. It is clear that he has not studied.
È evidente che takes the indicative, because the fact is certain.

The word, taken apart.
evidente is a learned borrowing from Latin ēvidēns "visible, apparent, clear" (EtymDB), built from ē "out" and vidēre "to see": something that stands out to the eye.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ovvio — obvious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the proof, then the Italian for evident.

You'll want to know: ovvio.
ovvio — "obvious".
Mi sembra ovvio: se piove, restiamo a casa. It seems obvious to me: if it rains, we stay at home.
Ovviamente — obviously. Plural ovvi, feminine ovvia.

The word, taken apart.
ovvio is a learned borrowing from Latin obvius "in the way, meeting, easy to reach" (Lewis & Short), from ob viam "towards the road": what you meet on the path.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: indiscutibile.
indiscutibile — "unquestionable, beyond question".
La sua bravura è indiscutibile. Her skill is beyond question.
Built on discutibile with in- "not".

The word, taken apart.
indiscutibile is built from in- "not" and discutibile, on discutere "to discuss", from Latin discutere "to strike apart" (Lewis & Short), built on quatere "to shake".

Lesson 5 of 5.
palese — plain, open (for all to see).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for obvious, then the Italian for unquestionable.

You'll want to know: palese.
palese — "plain, open (for all to see)".
La sua gelosia era palese. His jealousy was plain to see.
Formal; in speech evidente or chiaro are more frequent.

The word, taken apart.
palese is usually traced to Latin pālam "openly, in public" (Lewis & Short), through a Late Latin adjective of the type palēnsis; the exact path is not settled.
