Latin, chapter 407: Knowing and Not Knowing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
scientia, scientiae — knowledge, skill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for painstaking, then the Latin for careless.

You'll want to know: scientia, scientiae.
scientia, scientiae — "knowledge, skill".
Scientia rērum ūtilis est. Knowledge of things is useful.
scientia, scientiae f., built on sciēns, knowing. English science.

The word, taken apart.
scientia is built on sciēns "knowing" (EtymDB), the participle of sciō "to know" (Lewis & Short). English science comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
intellegentia, intellegentiae — understanding, intelligence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for careless, then the Latin for knowledge.

You'll want to know: intellegentia, intellegentiae.
intellegentia, intellegentiae — "understanding, intelligence".
Intellegentia eius omnēs superat. His understanding is greater than anyone's.
intellegentia, intellegentiae f., the noun of intellegō. English intelligence.

The word, taken apart.
intellegentia is the noun built on intellegō "to understand" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from inter "between" and legō "to pick": to choose between. English intelligence comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
imitor, imitārī — to imitate, to copy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for teaching, then the Latin for ignorance.

You'll want to know: imitor, imitārī.
imitor, imitārī — "to imitate, to copy".
Fīlius patrem imitātur. The son imitates his father.
Deponent: imitor, imitārī, imitātus sum, with the accusative. English imitate.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of imitor is not settled; Lewis & Short give no sure account, though it is often grouped with the word for an image. English imitate comes from it.
