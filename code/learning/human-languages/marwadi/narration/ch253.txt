Lesson 1 of 6.
सरदी (sardī) — a cold (the illness).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to chew, then the Marwadi for an ache.

You'll want to know: सरदी.
सरदी — sardī — "a cold (the illness)".
बेटो बीमार है, सरदी है। beṭo bīmār hai, sardī hai. My son is ill; he has a cold.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सरदी (sardī) is usually traced to Persian sardī, "coldness"; in Marwari it names the illness.

You'll want to know: खांसी.

रात मांय घणी खांसी आवै है। rāt mā̃y ghaṇī khā̃sī āvai hai. I cough a lot at night.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
खांसी (khā̃sī) is inherited from Sanskrit kāsā, "a cough". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi and Gujarati khā̃sī (CDIAL 3138).

You'll want to know: जीवणो.
जीवणो — jīvṇo — "to be alive; to live (a span of years)".
म्हारो काको अस्सी बरस जीयो। mhāro kāko assī baras jīyo. My father's brother lived to eighty.
Past (he …): वो सो बरस जीयो, vo so baras jīyo.

The word, taken apart.
जीवणो (jīvṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit jīvati, "is alive"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari jīvaï, "to live", under it (CDIAL 5241).

You'll want to know: धड़कणो.
धड़कणो — dhaṛakṇo — "to throb; to pound (of the heart)".
म्हारो दिल घणो धड़कै है। mhāro dil ghaṇo dhaṛakai hai. My heart is pounding hard.
Past (it …): वो दिल धड़क्यो, vo dil dhaṛakyo.

The word, taken apart.
धड़कणो (dhaṛakṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed dhaḍ, "throb, beat". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi dhaṛaknā, "to throb" (CDIAL 6711).
