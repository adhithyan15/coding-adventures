You'll want to know: でんわ.
でんわ — denwa — "a telephone", on a desk or in your pocket.
Three beats: de–n–wa. The last two signs are ん and わ, which you already write. The first one is て with the two short strokes of the voicing mark at its upper right, so te becomes de. The next lesson writes it.

Lesson 2 of 12.
で (de) — て, voiced.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Script — the change.

Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say a telephone — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say cloth — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say outside — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say thin, of tea or of a colour — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the change.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
て te + ゛ becomes で de.
Write て in full: one continuous stroke, across the top, back, then down and round to the right. Then add the two short strokes of the voicing mark at the upper right, the left one first. The base shape does not change.
The mark voices the opening consonant: t becomes d, and the vowel stays e. It is the change you already made on た for だ and on と for ど.
With ん and わ after it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: でんわ, denwa, a telephone.

Guided Practice.
Write て, then で, and say te, de.
Write た だ, と ど and て で (de) in pairs, and say each one.
Write でんわ from memory and say denwa.
