Urdu, chapter 295: Trapped.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پھنسنا (phansnā) — to be trapped; to be stuck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to rot, then the Urdu for cholera.

You'll want to know: پھنسنا.
پھنسنا — phansnā — "to be trapped; to be stuck".
تین آدمی ایک کمرے میں پھنس گئے۔ tīn ādmī ek kamre meṅ phans gae. Three men were trapped in a room.
Past (he …): وہ پھنس گیا, voh phans gayā.

The word, taken apart.
is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousins phā̃snā, "to ensnare", and phãsānā under Sanskrit spāśáyati (CDIAL 13814); پھنسنا (phansnā) is to be caught in the snare oneself.

Lesson 2 of 5.
پکارنا (pukārnā) — to call out (to).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for cholera, then the Urdu for to be trapped.

You'll want to know: پکارنا.
پکارنا — pukārnā — "to call out (to)".
اس نے مدد کے لیے پکارا۔ us ne madad ke liye pukārā. He called out for help.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے ماں کو پکارا, us ne māṅ ko pukārā.

The word, taken apart.
پکارنا (pukārnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin pukārnā, "to shout", under pukkār, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 8246). The person called takes کو: ماں کو پکارا.

You'll want to know: سناٹا.
سناٹا — sannāṭā — "a dead silence".
رات کو گلی میں سناٹا تھا۔ rāt ko galī meṅ sannāṭā thā. At night the lane was dead silent.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
سناٹا (sannāṭā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin sannāṭā, "a desolate silence", under Sanskrit saṃnaṣṭa, "vanished" (CDIAL 12925): the silence when every sound has gone.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نکالنا (nikālnā) — to take out; to pull out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to call out (to), then the Urdu for a dead silence.

You'll want to know: نکالنا.
نکالنا — nikālnā — "to take out; to pull out".
لوگوں نے انہیں باہر نکالا۔ logoṅ ne unheṅ bāhar nikālā. People pulled them out.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بچے کو نکالا, us ne bachche ko nikālā.

The word, taken apart.
is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin nikālnā, "to drive out, to take out", under Sanskrit niṣkālayati (CDIAL 7484). It is the partner of نکلنا: a thing نکلنا by itself, and you نکالنا it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بے حال (be hāl) — in a sorry state.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a dead silence, then the Urdu for to pull out.

You'll want to know: بے حال.
بے حال — be hāl — "in a sorry state".
وہ بھوک سے بے حال تھا۔ voh bhūk se be hāl thā. He was in a bad way from hunger.

The word, taken apart.
بے حال (be hāl) is usually traced to Persian bē, "without", and ḥāl, "a state", a word Persian took from Arabic: without a proper state, worn out.
