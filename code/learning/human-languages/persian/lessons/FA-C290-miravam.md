# میروم (miravam) — the present tense, built from the stem you stored

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-STEM, FA-STEM-RAV, FA-STEM-KON] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give the pair for "to go": the infinitive, then its present stem.
(*Raftan*, *rav-*.) Then the present stem of *kardan*. (*Kon-*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Chapter six asked you to store those stems before there was a tense
to run them on. This lesson is the tense.

## Grammar Lens: three pieces, in one order
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-MI-01]; assesses=[FA-STEM-KHAN, FA-STEM-DAN, FA-LEX-FEKR-KARDAN] -->

A Persian present is three pieces: **mi-**, then the present stem, then an
ending for the person.

> mi- + rav + -am → **میروم** — *miravam* — I go, I'm going

The endings are the six chapter seven named: *-am, -i, -ad, -im, -id, -and*.
So: *miravam*, I go; *miravi*, you go; *miravad*, he or she goes; *miravim*, we
go; *miravid*, you go, to several people or politely; *miravand*, they go.

The pronouns are *man*, *to*, *u* for "he" or "she", *mâ* for "we", *shomâ*
and *ânhâ*. The ending already says who, so **میروم** is a whole sentence.

Any stored stem works the same way: *mikonam*, I do; *mikhânam*, I read;
*midânam*, I know. A compound verb keeps its noun in front, **فکر میکنم**,
*fekr mikonam*, "I think".

One tense covers "I go", "I'm going" and often "I'll go":
**فردا میروم** — *fardâ miravam* — I'm going tomorrow.

Printed Persian often sets **می** off with a narrow break. This book writes
it joined to the verb, **میروم**, and it is read the same either way.

## Grammar Lens: ne-, and the verb that refuses mi-
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-NEGATIVE-NE-01]; assesses=[FA-STEM-DAR, FA-LEX-DUST-DASHTAN] -->

"Not" is **ne-**, in front of everything: **نمیروم**, *nemiravam*, I'm not
going; **نمیدانم**, *nemidânam*, I don't know.

*Dâshtan*, "to have", takes no *mi-* in this tense: **دارم**, *dâram*, I have,
and *dust dâram*, I like. Its "not" is *na-*: *nadâram*, *dust nadâram*. A few
compounds on *dâshtan* do take it, such as *negah dâshtan*, "to keep":
*negah midâram*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Histories of Persian usually trace *mi-* to an older adverb, *hamē*,
"continually", once a word of its own in front of the verb. EtymDB has no entry
for the prefix, so this is the usual account rather than a sourced one.
