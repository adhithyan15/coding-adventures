### Forty-four atoms, forty-five retrievals

Only **one** of the forty-four had no revisit at all; the other forty-three had
one and needed one more. That is the lowest ratio of any track in this
programme — Sanskrit's twenty-eight atoms cost forty-five retrievals, and
Malayalam's forty-four cost the same forty-five.

Eighteen of them are script-recognition atoms on a single path segment, taught
one per chapter from chapter 6 to chapter 39 and never gathered.

### The two script recalls sort by kind, not by when

**The first nine** are six letters, one killer and two hangers. The organising
fact is that a bare Malayalam letter already contains a short *a*: **്** takes
it away, **ി** and **ാ** swap it for another. Six letters, three marks, one
idea.

**The last nine** arrived late and thinly, and reading them together shows
something no chapter could show alone: **ഷ**, **ഭ** and **ഥ** are
Sanskrit-borrowing letters. A word built from Dravidian roots rarely needs them,
which is exactly why they were taught once and never asked for again.

**ഴ** gets its own moment in the first recall — the sound the language is named
for, and one a reader will not place after thirty chapters away from it.

### Three more, where the material lives

- **chapter 82** — the opening four chapters run cold. The claim worth holding
  is that **a question here is made by an ending, not by word order**:
  *sukhamāṇō* is the statement *you are well* with **-ō** on it. And the two
  farewells make a distinction English collapses — **പോയി വരാം** is *I will go
  and come back*, about the speaker; **വീണ്ടും കാണാം** is *we will meet again*,
  about both. Neither is a word for goodbye.
- **chapter 80** — a meal, the asking form, the agreement tag, and the ending
  that makes a person out of a language. **വേണോ** is not a separate word for *do
  you want*; it is the wanting word wearing a question ending taught long
  before.
- **chapter 92** — the last seven. **ഹോട്ടൽ** is the trap: a borrowed word that
  means an **eating place**, sitting beside **ബിൽ**, borrowed and meaning
  exactly what it looks like. The pair is the lesson — a loanword may keep its
  sense or take a new one, and the shape tells you nothing about which.

`review` lesson has a `headword:` field even though it introduces no headword.

**ശ** 18→19 from ശരി, **ബ** 5→6 from ബിൽ. No new glyph opens; `shown` holds at
