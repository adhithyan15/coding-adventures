Portuguese, chapter 310: O batizado.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o batizado — the christening, the baptism.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for pregnancy, then the Portuguese for the newborn baby.

You'll want to know: o batizado.
o batizado — "the christening, the baptism".
O batizado foi na igreja da aldeia. The christening was in the village church.
Before the 1990 spelling reform, baptizado.

The word, taken apart.
batizado is the past participle of batizar used as a noun; the verb goes back to Latin baptizāre, from Greek baptízein "to dip, to plunge" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
batizar — to baptise, to christen; to name.

You'll want to know: batizar.
batizar — "to baptise, to christen; to name".
Batizaram a menina em junho. They christened the little girl in June.
Also to give a name to anything: batizaram o barco com o nome da avó.

The word, taken apart.
batizar comes down from Old Portuguese bautizar (EtymDB), from Latin baptizāre, from Greek baptízein "to dip".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o padrinho — the godfather; the best man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the christening, then the Portuguese for to baptise.

You'll want to know: o padrinho.
o padrinho — "the godfather; the best man".
O meu tio é o meu padrinho. My uncle is my godfather.
At a wedding, os padrinhos are the witnesses.

The word, taken apart.
padrinho goes back to Vulgar Latin patrīnus "godfather" (EtymDB), from pater "father"; Italian and Spanish padrino are its cousins.

madrinha comes down from Old Portuguese madrỹa (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin matrīna, built on Latin māter "mother".
