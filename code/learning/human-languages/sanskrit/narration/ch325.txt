Lesson 1 of 6.
गदा (gadā) — a mace; a club.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for calls to mind, then the Sanskrit for tells of.

You'll want to know: गदा.
गदा — gadā — "a mace; a club".
वीरः हस्ते गदां धारयति। vīraḥ haste gadāṃ dhārayati. The hero holds a mace in his hand.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गदा (gadā) MW gives gadā as a mace, club or bludgeon, without a derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tells of, then the Sanskrit for a mace.

You'll want to know: शूलम्.
शूलम् — śūlam — "a spear; a trident".
सैनिकः शूलेन युध्यते। sainikaḥ śūlena yudhyate. The soldier fights with a spear.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
शूलम् (śūlam) MW gives śūla as a sharp iron pin or stake, and any pointed dart, lance, pike or spear, especially the trident of Śiva, without a derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: सारथिः.
सारथिः — sārathiḥ — "a charioteer".
सारथिः रथं वेगेन चालयति। sārathiḥ rathaṃ vegena cālayati. The charioteer drives the chariot fast.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सारथिः (sārathiḥ) is built on sa-ratha, "with a chariot", from ratha, "a chariot"; MW gives sārathi as a charioteer, a driver (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
विध्यति (vidhyati) — pierces.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a quiver, then the Sanskrit for a charioteer.

You'll want to know: विध्यति.
विध्यति — vidhyati — "pierces".
बाणः लक्ष्यं विध्यति। bāṇaḥ lakṣyaṃ vidhyati. The arrow pierces the target.
Past (he …): सः लक्ष्यं विद्धवान्, saḥ lakṣyaṃ viddhavān.

The word, taken apart.
विध्यति (vidhyati) comes from the root √vyadh, "to pierce, wound"; MW gives viddha as its past participle, pierced, stabbed, wounded (MW).
