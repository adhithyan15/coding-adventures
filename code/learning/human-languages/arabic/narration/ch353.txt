Arabic, chapter 353: Crowds in the street.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مظاهرة (muẓāhara) — a demonstration, a march.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a commotion, then the Arabic for a crowd.

You'll want to know: مظاهرة.
مظاهرة — muẓāhara — "a demonstration, a march".
سار الطلاب في مظاهرة سلمية. sāra aṭ-ṭullāb fī muẓāhara silmiyya. The students walked in a peaceful march.

The word, taken apart.
مظاهرة (muẓāhara) is built on the native Arabic root ẓ-h-r (Lane), the root of ẓahr, the back, and ẓahara, to appear. Form III ẓāhara is "to back someone up"; a public show of support is the modern sense.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: تجمع.
تجمع — tajammaʿa — "to gather, to assemble".
تجمع الناس حول السيارة. tajammaʿa an-nās ḥawla as-sayyāra. People gathered round the car.
Present: يتجمع, yatajammaʿ.

The word, taken apart.
تجمع (tajammaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root j-m-ʿ, "to gather" (Lane), the root of the taught ijtimāʿ, a meeting. It is Form V, "to come together".

Lesson 3 of 5.
هتف (hatafa) — to cheer, to chant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a demonstration, then the Arabic for to gather.

You'll want to know: هتف.
هتف — hatafa — "to cheer, to chant".
هتف الأطفال فرحا. hatafa al-aṭfāl faraḥan. The children cheered for joy.
Present: يهتف, yahtif.
The taught هاتف, a telephone, is the same root.

The word, taken apart.
هتف (hatafa) is usually traced to the native Arabic root h-t-f, "to call out", where hātif was a voice heard when the speaker is not seen; modern Arabic gave that word the telephone.
