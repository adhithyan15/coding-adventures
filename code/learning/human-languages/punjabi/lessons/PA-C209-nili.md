# One bounded feminine adjective form

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-C84-ADJ84-03, PA-GRAMMAR-ADJ-GENDER-AGREEMENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Retrieve familiar **ਨੀਲਾ**, “blue,” and the familiar rule that a
variable adjective agrees with a feminine noun.

## Grammar Lens: direct owner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-FORM-NILI-209]; assesses=[PA-LEX-C84-ADJ84-03, PA-GRAMMAR-ADJ-GENDER-AGREEMENT-01] -->

> **ਨੀਲਾ** — *nīlā* — blue, masculine singular direct
>
> **ਨੀਲੀ** — *nīlī* — blue, feminine singular direct

For this one form, change the final **-ਾ** ending of familiar **ਨੀਲਾ** to
feminine singular **-ੀ**. The result is **ਨੀਲੀ**. Keep the stem and meaning
steady; only the ending changes.

## Grammar Lens: source boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-NILI-209, PA-LEX-C84-ADJ84-03, PA-GRAMMAR-ADJ-GENDER-AGREEMENT-01] -->

Punjabi University Patiala's reference grammar says that inflectable
adjectives agree with their head noun in gender, number, and case. Its
noun-phrase examples contrast masculine **ਚੰਗਾ ਮੁੰਡਾ** with feminine
**ਚੰਗੀ ਕੁੜੀ**. Source: [Punjabi University reference grammar](https://pt.learnpunjabi.org/assets/a%20reference%20grammar_final.pdf).

That source supports this one feminine singular direct ending change. This
chapter does not teach a full agreement paradigm, plural or oblique forms, a
new colour, or a general contrast between attributive and predicative uses.
