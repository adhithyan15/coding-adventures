Mandarin Chinese, chapter 131: Four Seasons.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
春天 (chūntiān) — spring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sore, then the Mandarin for to see a doctor.

You'll want to know: 春天.
春天 — chūntiān — "spring".
我喜欢春天 wǒ xǐhuan chūntiān — I love spring.
The new character, in pieces: 春 (chūn) — the three bars of 三, a 人 whose two strokes spread across them, and 日 underneath. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
春天 (chūntiān) is built from 春 (chūn) 'spring' and 天 'sky, day', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwin and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'the head of the four seasons'.

What you've built.
One more word for times of day.

Lesson 2 of 6.
春 (chūn) — 三 and 人 over 日.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 三 and 人 over 日 and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say chūntiān — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
三 人 日.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — sān, rén, rì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 三 and 人 over 日.
春 (chūn).
Three pieces you know, top to bottom, the first two woven together.
The three bars of 三: two shorter bars, then a long third one.
人, two strokes, spread wide across the bars: the left-falling stroke from above the top bar down to the lower left, then the right-falling stroke from the middle out to the lower right.
日, four strokes, small, under the spread of 人.
3 + 2 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 春 (chūn), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 春 (chūn)? (Nine.) Which bar is the longest? (The third.) What sits at the bottom? (日.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 春 (chūn), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàtiān is built from xià 'summer' and 天 'sky', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeH and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the first under the words 'spring and summer'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
jìjié — season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for autumn, then the Mandarin for winter.

You'll want to know: jìjié.
jìjié — "season".
nǐ xǐhuan nǎge jìjié — Which season do you like?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìjié is built from jì and jié, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjwijH and tset (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'the youngest of brothers' and the second, after an older book, 'the joint of a bamboo'; how the pair came to mean a season is not settled.
