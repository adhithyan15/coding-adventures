Arabic, chapter 127: Curtain, Lock, Handkerchief, Bracelet, Earring.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ستارة (sitāra) — a curtain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a toy, then the Arabic for a fridge.

You'll want to know: ستارة.
ستارة — sitāra — "a curtain".

The word, taken apart.
ستارة (sitāra) is built on the native Arabic root s-t-r, "to veil, to cover" (Lane). Sitāra is a thing that covers.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — read: قال]
Which letter is the deep q? (ق, qāf, two dots above.)

You'll want to know: قفل.
قفل — qufl — "a lock".

The word, taken apart.
قفل (qufl) is built on the native Arabic root q-f-l. Lane does not list qufl itself, but under q-f-l he gives aqfala l-bāba, "he locked the door", and qāfila, "a company returning from a journey", that is, "a caravan".

Lesson 3 of 5.
منديل (mindīl) — a handkerchief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a curtain, then the Arabic for a lock.

You'll want to know: منديل.
منديل — mindīl — "a handkerchief".

The word, taken apart.
منديل (mindīl) is usually traced to Greek mandilion, "towel, napkin" (EtymDB), from Latin mantēle; Arabic grammarians placed it under n-d-l.
