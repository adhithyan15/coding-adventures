## You'll want to know: 他
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C31-PEOPLE31-02]; assesses=[] -->

**他** — *tā* — "he; him".

> **他是我的儿子** *tā shì wǒ de érzi* — He's my son.

The new character, in pieces: 他 — the person piece from 你 on the left, and on the right a three-stroke shape read yě. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**他** (*tā*) is inherited from Middle Chinese *tha* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses it 'not I', the other person, and calls this the everyday form of the character. From somebody other than me to he is the usual explanation, but the step itself is not settled.
