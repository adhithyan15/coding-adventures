Russian, chapter 77: City, Street, Shop, Market, School.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
город (górod) — a city, a town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a thousand, then the Russian for a country.

You'll want to know: город.
город (górod) — "a city, a town".

The word, taken apart.
город (górod) from Proto-Slavic gordŭ, "an enclosure, a fort" — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian gardas, "a fold", Gothic gards, "house", and Sanskrit gṛhá-, "house": English yard is a cousin. Church Slavonic has град, "city", as in Петроград, "Peter's city".

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a country, then the Russian for a city.

You'll want to know: улица.
улица (úlitsa) — "a street".

The word, taken apart.
улица (úlitsa) from Proto-Slavic ulica, built on ula, "a hollow, a channel" — inherited. Vasmer relates it to улей, "beehive", and Greek aulós, "pipe", aulṓn, "glen": a street as a passage between houses.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: магазин.
магазин (magazín) — "a shop".

The word, taken apart.
магазин (magazín) — first in Peter the Great's time; it came through German Magasin from French magasin, "a storehouse" (Vasmer). The French word goes back, through Italian, to Arabic makhāzin, "storehouses".

Lesson 4 of 5.
рынок (rýnok) — a market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a street, then the Russian for a shop.

You'll want to know: рынок.
рынок (rýnok) — "a market".

The word, taken apart.
рынок (rýnok) came through Polish rynek and Czech rynk, "town square", from Middle High German rinc, "a ring, a square" (Vasmer): the market sat in the ring of the square. German Ring is the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
школа (shkóla) — a school.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a shop, then the Russian for a market.

You'll want to know: школа.
школа (shkóla) — "a school". Where the учитель and the ученик meet.

The word, taken apart.
школа (shkóla) — recorded from 1388; it came through Polish szkoła from Latin schola, from Greek scholḗ, "leisure", then "lecture, school" (Vasmer).
