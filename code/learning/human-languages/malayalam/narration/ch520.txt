Malayalam, chapter 520: Compassion and Panic.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
അനുകമ്പ (anukampa) — compassion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for tender affection, then the Malayalam for to grumble.

You'll want to know: അനുകമ്പ.
അനുകമ്പ — anukampa — "compassion".
രോഗികളോട് ആ നഴ്സിന് വലിയ അനുകമ്പ ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നു. rōgikaḷōṭŭ ā naḻsinŭ valiya anukampa uṇṭāyirunnu. That nurse had great compassion for the patients.
A word of writing and speeches; the one pitied takes -ഓട്.

The word, taken apart.
അനുകമ്പ (anukampa) is borrowed from Sanskrit anukampā, "sympathy, compassion" (Monier-Williams), from anu-kamp, "to tremble along with someone".

Lesson 2 of 5.
സഹതാപം (sahatāpaṁ) — sympathy; pity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: സഹതാപം.
സഹതാപം — sahatāpaṁ — "sympathy; pity".
അയൽക്കാർ ഞങ്ങളോട് സഹതാപം കാണിച്ചു. ayalkkār ñaṅṅaḷōṭŭ sahatāpaṁ kāṇiccu. The neighbours showed us sympathy.
സഹ- with + താപം heat, pain: feeling the pain with someone.

The word, taken apart.
സഹതാപം (sahatāpaṁ) is a learned compound built from Sanskrit saha, "with", and tāpa, "heat, pain" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
വിങ്ങുക (viṅṅuka) — to throb; to swell with grief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for compassion, then the Malayalam for sympathy.

You'll want to know: വിങ്ങുക.
വിങ്ങുക — viṅṅuka — "to throb; to swell with grief".
സുഹൃത്ത് പോയപ്പോൾ അവളുടെ മനസ്സ് വിങ്ങി. suhṛttŭ pōyappōḷ avaḷuṭe manassŭ viṅṅi. When her friend left, her heart ached.
Past: വിങ്ങി, viṅṅi.
Of a wound it means to throb and swell; of the heart, to ache with held-back grief.

The word, taken apart.
വിങ്ങുക (viṅṅuka) is native Dravidian and seems to be a short-vowel form of വീങ്ങുക, "to swell": DEDR 5448 lists that verb with വീക്കം, a swelling, and the throbbing of a wound (DEDR 5448).

Lesson 4 of 5.
അസ്വസ്ഥത (asvasthata) — unease; restlessness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for sympathy, then the Malayalam for to throb.

You'll want to know: അസ്വസ്ഥത.
അസ്വസ്ഥത — asvasthata — "unease; restlessness".
ചൂട് കാരണം രാത്രി മുഴുവൻ എനിക്ക് അസ്വസ്ഥത തോന്നി. cūṭŭ kāraṇaṁ rātri muḻuvan enikkŭ asvasthata tōnni. Because of the heat I felt restless all night.
From അസ്വസ്ഥം, not at ease, with -ത; അ- turns a Sanskrit word into its opposite.

The word, taken apart.
അസ്വസ്ഥത (asvasthata) is borrowed from Sanskrit asvasthatā, "illness" (Monier-Williams), the opposite of svastha, "being in oneself, well".

Lesson 5 of 5.
വിതുമ്പുക (vitumpuka) — to sob; to choke up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to throb, then the Malayalam for unease.

You'll want to know: വിതുമ്പുക.
വിതുമ്പുക — vitumpuka — "to sob; to choke up".
അച്ഛൻ യാത്ര പറഞ്ഞപ്പോൾ കുട്ടി വിതുമ്പി. acchan yātra paṟaññappōḷ kuṭṭi vitumpi. When Father said goodbye, the child began to sob.
Past: വിതുമ്പി, vitumpi.
Crying held back, with trembling lips; louder crying is കരയുക.

The word, taken apart.
വിതുമ്പുക (vitumpuka) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5399 gives the Malayalam verb with the sense of starting to cry, as a child does (DEDR 5399).
