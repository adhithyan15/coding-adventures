Japanese, chapter 426: How I Feel Today.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きぶん (kibun) — one's mood; how one feels.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for serious, then the Japanese for selfish.

You'll want to know: きぶん.
きぶん — kibun — "one's mood; how one feels".
けさは きぶんが いいです。 — kesa wa kibun ga ii desu — "I am in a good mood this morning."

The word, taken apart.
きぶん (kibun) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "spirit" (Middle Chinese khj+jH) and bun, "part" (pjun) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

すっきり — sukkiri — "refreshed; clear and tidy".
よく ねたので、きぶんが すっきりしました。 — yoku neta node, kibun ga sukkiri shimashita — "I slept well, so my head feels clear."

The word, taken apart.
すっきり (sukkiri) is a native Japanese mimetic word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たえる (taeru) — to endure; to bear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for unbothered, then the Japanese for refreshed.

You'll want to know: たえる.
たえる — taeru — "to endure; to bear".
あには あつい なつに たえています。 — ani wa atsui natsu ni taete imasu — "My elder brother is putting up with the hot summer."
Polite たえます, taemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ははは いたみに たえました。, haha wa itami ni taemashita.

The word, taken apart.
たえる (taeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).
