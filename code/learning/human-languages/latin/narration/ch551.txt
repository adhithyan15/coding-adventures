Latin, chapter 551: Book boxes and tablets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
capsa, capsae — a book box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to read through, then the Latin for to unroll.

You'll want to know: capsa, capsae.
capsa, capsae — "a book box".
Puer librōs in capsā portat. The boy carries his books in a book box.
Feminine, first declension, from capiō — to hold. English case, a box, comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
capsa is built on capiō "to take, hold" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English case "box" comes from it through French, and capsule from its diminutive.

Lesson 2 of 5.
scrīnium, scrīniī — a writing case, chest for papers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to unroll, then the Latin for a book box.

You'll want to know: scrīnium, scrīniī.
scrīnium, scrīniī — "a writing case, chest for papers".
Epistulās in scrīniō servō. I keep the letters in a writing case.
Neuter, second declension. A round box for book-rolls and letters.

The word, taken apart.
scrīnium is a native Latin noun; Lewis & Short links it with scrībō "to write", but EtymDB traces it to a root meaning "to cut", so the origin is not settled. English shrine comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
membrāna, membrānae — parchment; skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a book box, then the Latin for a writing case.

You'll want to know: membrāna, membrānae.
membrāna, membrānae — "parchment; skin".
Poēta versūs in membrānā scrībit. The poet writes the verses on parchment.
Feminine, first declension, from membrum. Thin skin, then parchment for writing. English membrane.

The word, taken apart.
membrāna is built on membrum "limb" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): the skin over the limbs, then parchment. English membrane comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pugillārēs, pugillārium — writing tablets.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a writing case, then the Latin for parchment.

You'll want to know: pugillārēs, pugillārium.
pugillārēs, pugillārium — "writing tablets".
In pugillāribus pauca verba scrībit. He writes a few words on his writing tablets.
Masculine, third declension, plural. Small wax tablets that fitted in the hand, from pugillus — a little fist.

The word, taken apart.
pugillārēs is built on pugillus "a little fist, handful", from pugnus "fist" (Lewis & Short): tablets held in the hand.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rescrībō, rescrībere — to write back, reply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for parchment, then the Latin for writing tablets.

You'll want to know: rescrībō, rescrībere.
rescrībō, rescrībere — "to write back, reply".
Epistulam tuam accēpī et statim rescrībō. I have received your letter and I am writing back at once.
rescrībō, rescrībere, rescrīpsī, rescrīptum. An emperor's written answer was a rescrīptum.

The word, taken apart.
rescrībō is built on re- "back" and scrībō "to write" (Lewis & Short). English rescript comes from its participle.
