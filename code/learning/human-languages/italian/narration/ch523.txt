Italian, chapter 523: Una mostra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la mostra — exhibition, show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for clay, then the Italian for to model.

You'll want to know: la mostra.
la mostra — "exhibition, show".
Domenica andiamo a vedere una mostra di fotografia. On Sunday we are going to see a photography exhibition.
The same form is a verb: mi mostra la casa — he shows me the house.

The word, taken apart.
mostra is built on mostrare "to show", inherited from Latin mōnstrāre "to point out" (EtymDB).

Literally "head work": the best work.

The word, taken apart.
capolavoro is built from capo "head", from Latin caput (Lewis & Short), and lavoro "work", from Latin labor.

Lesson 3 of 5.
il ritratto — portrait.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for exhibition, then the Italian for masterpiece.

You'll want to know: il ritratto.
il ritratto — "portrait".
Nel salotto c'è un ritratto della nonna da giovane. In the living room there is a portrait of grandmother as a young woman.
Also a description: un ritratto della città.

The word, taken apart.
ritratto is the past participle of ritrarre "to portray", from Latin retrahere "to draw back" (EtymDB), built on trahere "to draw".

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'affresco — fresco.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for masterpiece, then the Italian for portrait.

You'll want to know: l'affresco.
l'affresco — "fresco".
Nella chiesa ci sono affreschi molto antichi. In the church there are very old frescoes.
A fresco: painted on wet plaster.

The word, taken apart.
affresco is built from a fresco "on the fresh (plaster)"; fresco comes from Medieval Latin friscus, from a Germanic word friskaz "fresh" (EtymDB).
