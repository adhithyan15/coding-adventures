German, chapter 311: Im Advent.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Weihnachtsmarkt — the Christmas market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to dress up, then the German for the custom.

You'll want to know: der Weihnachtsmarkt.
der Weihnachtsmarkt — "the Christmas market".
Auf dem Weihnachtsmarkt tranken wir heißen Tee. At the Christmas market we drank hot tea.
Plural: die Weihnachtsmärkte.

The word, taken apart.
Weihnachtsmarkt joins Weihnachten, Middle High German wīhenahten "the holy nights" (EtymDB), and Markt, which came from Latin mercātus "market" (EtymDB).

der Weihnachtsbaum — "the Christmas tree".
Der Weihnachtsbaum stand im Wohnzimmer. The Christmas tree stood in the living room.
Plural: die Weihnachtsbäume. In the south and in Austria also der Christbaum.

The word, taken apart.
Weihnachtsbaum joins Weihnachten "Christmas" and Baum "tree", Proto-Germanic baumaz, a cousin of English beam (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Bescherung — the giving of Christmas presents.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the Christmas tree, then the German for the Advent wreath.

You'll want to know: die Bescherung.
die Bescherung — "the giving of Christmas presents".
Die Bescherung war am Abend. The presents were given out in the evening.
In most families on the evening of the twenty-fourth.

The word, taken apart.
Bescherung is built on bescheren "to give, to allot", from Middle High German beschern "to destine, to allot" (EtymDB).

The word, taken apart.
Lebkuchen comes from Middle High German lebekuoche (EtymDB), with Kuchen "cake"; the origin of the first part is not settled.
