Persian, chapter 352: Ancient times.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
باستان (bâstân) — antiquity, ancient times.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to defeat, then the Persian for to conquer.

You'll want to know: باستان.
باستان (bâstân) — "antiquity, ancient times".
این بنا از زمان باستان مانده است. In banâ az zamân-e bâstân mânde ast. This building has survived from ancient times.
Bâstânshenâs "an archaeologist".

The word, taken apart.
bâstân is a native Persian word that EtymDB glosses "ancient era" without a parent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تمدن (tamaddon) — a civilisation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for antiquity, then the Persian for ancient.

You'll want to know: تمدن.
تمدن (tamaddon) — "a civilisation".
تمدن (tamaddon) در کنار رودخانه ها شروع شد. Tamaddon dar kenâr-e rudkhâne-hâ shoru' shod. Civilisation began on the banks of rivers.
Plural tamaddon-hâ. The Arabic word is linked with madina "city": living in cities.

The word, taken apart.
tamaddon is borrowed from Arabic tamaddun, "civilisation" (EtymDB), a word usually linked with madīna "city".

Lesson 4 of 5.
امپراتوری (emperâturi) — an empire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for ancient, then the Persian for a civilisation.

You'll want to know: امپراتوری.
امپراتوری (emperâturi) — "an empire".
این امپراتوری صدها سال دوام آورد. In emperâturi sad-hâ sâl davâm âvard. This empire lasted for hundreds of years.
Emperâtur "an emperor" + -i.

The word, taken apart.
emperâturi is built on emperâtur "emperor", which EtymDB gives as a borrowing of Latin imperator, "a commander-in-chief", later the title of the Roman emperors (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
سلسله (selsele) — a dynasty; a chain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a civilisation, then the Persian for an empire.

You'll want to know: سلسله.
سلسله (selsele) — "a dynasty; a chain".
این کاخ را پادشاهان یک سلسله قدیمی ساختند. In kâkh râ pâdeshâhân-e yek selsele-ye qadimi sâkhtand. The kings of an old dynasty built this palace.
Its first meaning is "a chain": selsele-ye kuh-hâ "a mountain range".

The word, taken apart.
selsele is borrowed from Arabic silsila, "chain" (EtymDB): a chain of kings.
