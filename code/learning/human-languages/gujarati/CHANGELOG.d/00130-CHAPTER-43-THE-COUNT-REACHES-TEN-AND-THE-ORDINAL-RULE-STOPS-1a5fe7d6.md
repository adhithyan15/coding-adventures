**THE ORDER IS THE COUNT, AND FOR ONCE THAT IS A CONSTRUCTION ARGUMENT RATHER
THAN A DEFAULT.** Six to ten have no morphology between them — all five come
down whole from Sanskrit — so HL-C354's rule sends the decision to cost, and
cost has a clear answer: છ, સાત, નવ and દસ need **no new sign at all**, and only
આઠ needs one. Cost-ordering would therefore put આઠ last. It was rejected,
because Wiktionary's **-મું** entry ends its exception list at **છ**: the reader
crosses the boundary between *exception* and *rule* exactly once, and in
counting order that crossing falls between the first lesson and the second,
where it is legible. Cost order would have bought nothing and left the reader
counting round a hole at eight for two lessons — which is what the Japanese
cardinal chapter's audible hole at six cost, on a track with no such boundary to
protect. The one sign is bought in the **third** lesson, which is where chapters
35-41 already put their writing lesson.

**WHAT WAS ALREADY OWNED, AND IT IS THE FIRST NUMBER.** **છ** is not a new

sign which sits seventh in the alphabet is the word for six, and the two numbers
have nothing to do with each other.

**ONE LETTER, AND IT COMPLETES A SQUARE THE READER ALREADY HOLDS THREE CORNERS
