# હા / ના (hā / nā) — "yes" and "no"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two tiny words no conversation survives without — and one is a cousin
of English.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-HAANAA-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

**હા** (*hā*) = **હ** (*ha*) + the long-*ā* sign. **ના** (*nā*) = **ન** (*na*) +
the long-*ā* sign.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-HAANAA-01, GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

**હા** (*hā*, "yes") and **ના** (*nā*, "no"). The "no" rides the ancient negative
**na-** — the very same Proto-Indo-European **\*ne** behind English **no**,
**not**, **none**, Latin *nōn*, Hindi *nahīṁ*, and Bengali *nā*. Of all the words
in this book, this "no" reaches furthest unchanged: one syllable spoken, in some
form, from Iceland to Gujarat for five thousand years.
