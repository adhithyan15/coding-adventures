## You'll want to know: crepīdō, crepīdinis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C425-AGGER-03]; assesses=[] -->

**crepīdō, crepīdinis** — "a raised edge; a kerb, a raised footway; a quay".

> Viātōrēs in crepīdine ambulant, plaustra in mediā viā eunt.
>
> *The travellers walk on the raised footway, the wagons go in the middle of the road.*

Third declension, feminine: *crepīdō, crepīdinis f.* Any raised edge: the kerb of a street, the base of a building, the stone edge of a quay.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*crepīdō* was borrowed from Greek *krēpis*, *krēpidos* 'foundation, base' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). The Greek word also meant a kind of boot, the source of Latin *crepida* 'sandal'.
