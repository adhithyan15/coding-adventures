## You'll want to know: sanciō, sancīre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C410-ROGATIO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**sanciō, sancīre** — "to make sacred and binding; to ratify".

> Lēgem iūre iūrandō sānxērunt.
>
> *They made the law binding by an oath.*

*sanciō, sancīre, sānxī, sānctum*: to make a thing sacred and binding. *Sānctus*, holy, is its participle. English *sanction* and *saint*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*sanciō* is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to make holy" (EtymDB), and *sānctus* "holy" is its participle (Lewis & Short). English *sanction* and *saint* come from it.
