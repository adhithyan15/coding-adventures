Gujarati, chapter 284: It depends, and what would have been.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
આધાર (ādhār) — a support; what something rests on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "or else". (nahī̃tar.) Then "to keep". (રાખવું.)

You'll want to know: આધાર.
આધાર — ādhār — "a support; what something rests on". It is masculine: મારો આધાર, "my support".
મારાં માતા-પિતા મારો આધાર છે. mārā̃ mātā-pitā māro ādhār che. — "My parents are my support."
A person can be a આધાર (ādhār), and so can a fact that a plan rests on. The next lesson builds "it depends" on that second sense.

The word, taken apart.
આધાર (ādhār) is a tatsama: Sanskrit ādhāra, "support, prop", and "the power of holding up" (Monier-Williams), from ā- and the root dhṛ-, "to hold".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ādhār, then māro ādhār]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My parents are my support" — mārā̃ mātā-pitā māro ādhār che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does આધાર (ādhār) mean? ("A support; what something rests on".) Its gender? (Masculine: મારો આધાર (ādhār).)


Lesson 2 of 5.
… પર આધાર રાખે છે (… par ādhār rākhe che) — it depends on …

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "my support". (māro ādhār.) Then "if it rains". (jo varsād paḍśe.)

Grammar Lens: it keeps its support on …
તે હવામાન પર આધાર રાખે છે. te havāmān par ādhār rākhe che. — "It depends on the weather."
Word for word: "it keeps its support on the weather". પર (par), "on", comes after its word, as સુધી does. The thing that depends is the subject of રાખે છે: બધું સમય પર આધાર રાખે છે (badhũ samay par ādhār rākhe che), "everything depends on the time". A person takes મારા, તમારા: તે તમારા પર આધાર રાખે છે (te tamārā par ādhār rākhe che), "it depends on you".

You'll want to know: "it depends", and then.
અમે જઈશું કે નહીં? તે વરસાદ પર આધાર રાખે છે. ame jaīśũ ke nahī̃? te varsād par ādhār rākhe che. — "Will we go or not? It depends on the rain."
"It depends" is seldom the end of an answer. Say on what, then follow with જો: જો વરસાદ નહીં પડે, તો અમે જઈશું (jo varsād nahī̃ paḍe, to ame jaīśũ).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Everything depends on the time" — badhũ samay par ādhār rākhe che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It depends on you" — te tamārā par ādhār rākhe che]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Someone asks if you'll go. Say it depends on the weather, and that if it doesn't rain, you'll go.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on the weather"? (તે હવામાન પર આધાર (ādhār) રાખે છે.) Where does પર go? (After its word.) "On you"? (તમારા પર.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
જો … હોત, તો … આવત (jo … hot, to … āvat) — what would have been.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it depends on the rain: if it doesn't rain, we'll go". (te varsād par ādhār rākhe che: jo varsād nahī̃ paḍe, to ame jaīśũ.) Then "I have time", the A2 way. (મારી પાસે સમય છે.)

Grammar Lens: the -ત form.
જો મારી પાસે સમય હોત, તો હું આવત. jo mārī pāse samay hot, to hũ āvat. — "If I had had time, I would have come."
The stem with -ત (-t) is "would": the condition did not hold, so the result did not happen. Both halves take it: હોત, from હોવું, and આવત, from આવવું. It can also be about now, a thing that is not so: "if I had time, I would come".
"If not" puts ન before it: જો ટ્રેન મોડી ન હોત, તો અમે વહેલા પહોંચત (jo ṭren moḍī na hot, to ame vahelā pahõcat), "if the train hadn't been late, we would have arrived early".

You'll want to know: one form for everyone.
-ત stays the same for every person, man or woman: હું જાત, તે જાત, અમે જાત, with the same jāt each time. It is no past, so a verb with an object keeps a plain doer, with no -એ:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
plain past: મેં કામ કર્યું. would have: હું કામ કરત.
plain past: તેણે કહ્યું. would have: તે કહેત.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If the train hadn't been late, we would have arrived early" — jo ṭren moḍī na hot, to ame vahelā pahõcat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I had time, I would do the work" — jo mārī pāse samay hot, to hũ kām karat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say: if I had had time, I would have come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I would have come"? (હું આવત.) Does it change for a woman? (No.) "I would do the work": મેં or હું? (હું કામ કરત.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip, with a scene, a reason, a feeling and a would-have-been.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The A2 composition was a practical note. This task asks for six lines that tell a story: a trip by train, bus or boat, real or invented.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: Arun, અરુણ, or Mira, મીરા. Mira's Sunday at the village is your model of order.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list. It has a scene, with -તો હતો or જ્યારે … ત્યારે, and events in the plain past, kept in order with પહેલાં, ત્યાર પછી and છેવટે and joined with -ઈને. It has a reason, with કારણ કે, -ને લીધે or એટલે, and a feeling, with મને બીક લાગી or હું ખુશ હતો.
[pause 2 seconds]
A good one ends on what might have been: જો …, તો … -ત.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, in Gujarati script, write six lines to your reader about the trip, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, and what was going on as it began, with -તો હતો or જ્યારે … ત્યારે;
the first thing that happened, with પહેલાં, and a -ઈને form;
what happened next, with ત્યાર પછી, and one thing that went wrong: ખોવાઈ ગઈ or છૂટી ગઈ;
why, with કારણ કે, -ને લીધે or એટલે;
how it ended and how you felt, with છેવટે and મને દુઃખ થયું or હું ખુશ હતો;
what might have been, with જો … હોત, તો … -ત, and one comparison, with કરતાં or સૌથી.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model in this lesson. Use only words you already write. The work is choosing the right past for each line, and joining them.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines as you read Mira's Sunday. Mark every verb s for scene, e for event. Line one should be mostly s; lines two and three mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then three checks. A verb with an object in the plain past needs -એ on a name: અરુણે, મીરાએ. દુઃખ has ઃ after દુ, and સૌથી has ૌ on its સ. ઝડપી opens on ઝ.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. Do both halves take -ત: હોત, then આવત or જાત?
