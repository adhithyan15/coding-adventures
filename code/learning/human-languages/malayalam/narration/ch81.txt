Malayalam, chapter 81: How Far Away.
4 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
അടുത്ത് (aṭuttŭ) — near.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say here. Say there. Then think about what neither of them tells you: how far away.

You'll want to know: അടുത്ത്.
അടുത്ത് — aṭuttŭ — near, close by.
ഇവിടെ and അവിടെ point. They split the world in two — where I am, and where I am not — and that is all they do. Something aviṭe might be across the room or across an ocean, and the word is the same either way.
അടുത്ത് (aṭuttŭ) measures. It is the first word in this track that says how much space is involved rather than which side of you it is on.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഇവിടെ / അവിടെ. does: point — this side or that.
അടുത്ത് (aṭuttŭ). does: measure — not much space.
That is a real division, and it is the reason a learner can have owned here and there for forty chapters and still be unable to say that the shop is close.

What you've built.
The near end of a scale you could previously only point along.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 2 of 4.
അകലെ (akale) — far away.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the word for near. Then say the three-way pattern the pointing words are built on.

You'll want to know: അകലെ.
അകലെ — akale — far away.
The scale now has both ends:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
അടുത്ത് aṭuttŭ, near.
അകലെ akale, far.
Between them you can place anything, and അവിടെ stops having to do a job it was never built for.

Why it's said this way: a shape that is not the pattern.
Look at അകലെ (akale) and അവിടെ side by side. Both open with അ, and the pointing pattern you learned says that അ- is the away one — aviṭe, that place, over there.
Resist that. The അ in akale is part of the word, not the pointing prefix stuck on the front of it. The pattern is real and this is not an instance of it; the two only look alike.
This is worth a page of its own because the habit it trains is the useful one. A pattern you have learned will start finding matches everywhere, including where there are none. The test is never how the word looks — it is whether the other members of the set exist. There is no ikale meaning near and no ekale meaning how far, so there is no set, so there is no pattern here.

Lesson 3 of 4.
എത്ര ദൂരം (ethra dūraṁ) — how far.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say far away. Then say the age question, the long one about what exists to you.

You'll want to know: ദൂരം, and എത്ര on its own.
ദൂരം — dūraṁ — distance.
aṭuttŭ and akale give you two rough settings. ദൂരം is the thing itself — the amount of space, which can be asked for and answered with a number.
And you already own the word for asking. എത്ര ethra — how much, how many — has been sitting inside the age question since you learned it:
നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എത്ര വയസ്സുണ്ട്? — to you, how much age exists?
It was a piece of a phrase then. Now take it out and use it.
എത്ര ദൂരം? — ethra dūraṁ? — how much distance? How far?
That is the whole question, and it is two words you were given separately. Lifting a word out of a phrase you already say is the cheapest vocabulary there is, and it is worth going looking for: a long sentence you learned whole usually has two or three words in it that work on their own.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
അടുത്ത് (aṭuttŭ), near — a setting.
അകലെ (akale), far — a setting.
എത്ര ദൂരം (ethra dūraṁ)?, how far — a question with a number for an answer.
dūraṁ is a borrowing from Sanskrit, where akale is inherited. Both are ordinary and neither is grander than the other; Malayalam keeps pairs like this all over, and having both is not a redundancy but a choice of texture.

Lesson 4 of 4.
(how far away) (ethra dūraṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say near, then say far.

Grammar Lens: pointing and measuring are different jobs.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
word: ഇവിടെ / അവിടെ. job: point — my side or not my side.
word: അടുത്ത് (aṭuttŭ) / അകലെ (akale). job: measure — a little space or a lot.
word: എത്ര ദൂരം (ethra dūraṁ)? job: ask — and expect a number back.
The pointing pair had been yours for a long time and could not answer how far, because splitting the world in two is not the same as measuring across it. Something aviṭe can be a step away or a country away.

What you've built.
A scale, and the question that reads it.
One of the four words cost nothing. എത്ര was already inside the age question you learned whole, doing exactly this job, and the chapter only took it out. A long sentence you learned whole usually holds two or three words that work on their own — worth going looking for, in this language and any other.
The chapter also asked you to refuse a pattern. അകലെ (akale) opens with അ and the pointing prefix is അ-, and they are not the same thing: there is no ikale and no ekale, so there is no set, so there is no pattern. A pattern you have learned will find matches where there are none, and the test is always whether the other members exist.
