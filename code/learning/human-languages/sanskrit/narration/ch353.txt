Sanskrit, chapter 353: Who Governs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शासनम् (śāsanam) — government; rule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for shows favour to, then the Sanskrit for forbearance.

You'll want to know: शासनम्.
शासनम् — śāsanam — "government; rule".
देशस्य शासनं कः करोति? deśasya śāsanaṃ kaḥ karoti? Who runs the country's government?
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
शासनम् (śāsanam) comes from the root √śās, "to rule, to order"; MW gives śāsana "government, dominion, rule" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for presides over, then the Sanskrit for a nation.

You'll want to know: शासकः.
शासकः — śāsakaḥ — "a ruler; a governor".
शासकः देशस्य सुखं चिन्तयेत्। śāsakaḥ deśasya sukhaṃ cintayet. A ruler should think of the country's well-being.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शासकः (śāsakaḥ) comes from the root √śās, "to rule", with the agent ending -aka; MW gives śāsaka "a chastiser, a teacher, a governor, a ruler" (MW).
