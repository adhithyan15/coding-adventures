Lesson 1 of 5.
messis, messis — the harvest, the crop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a ploughman, then the Latin for to harrow.

You'll want to know: messis, messis.
messis, messis — "the harvest, the crop".
Aestāte messis est, et omnēs in agrīs labōrant. In summer it is harvest time, and everyone works in the fields.
Third declension, feminine: messis, messis f., accusative often messem. From metō 'to reap'.

The word, taken apart.
messis is built on metō 'to reap, to mow' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from PIE h₂meh₁- 'to mow'. English mow and meadow are Germanic cousins.

Lesson 3 of 5.
falx, falcis — a sickle, a pruning hook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the harvest, then the Latin for a reaper.

You'll want to know: falx, falcis.
falx, falcis — "a sickle, a pruning hook".
Messor falce frūmentum metit. The reaper cuts the grain with a sickle.
Third declension, feminine: falx, falcis f. A curved blade for grain or for pruning vines.

The word, taken apart.
falx is inherited from PIE dʰalk- 'sickle, cutting tool' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short suggest a link with flectō 'to bend', for its curve, but that is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
spīca, spīcae — an ear of grain, a spike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a reaper, then the Latin for a sickle.

You'll want to know: spīca, spīcae.
spīca, spīcae — "an ear of grain, a spike".
Spīcae plēnae et flāvae sunt; messis bona erit. The ears of grain are full and golden; the harvest will be good.
First declension, feminine: spīca, spīcae f. The head of wheat or barley.

The word, taken apart.
spīca is usually traced to a root meaning 'to be sharp, pointed' (Lewis & Short), the root of spīna 'thorn'. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
trīticum, trīticī — wheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sickle, then the Latin for an ear of grain.

You'll want to know: trīticum, trīticī.
trīticum, trīticī — "wheat".
Agricola trīticum in agrō serit. The farmer sows wheat in the field.
Second declension, neuter: trīticum, trīticī n. The best grain for bread.

The word, taken apart.
trīticum is built on trītus, the participle of terō 'to rub, to thresh' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short, after an ancient explanation): the grain that is threshed.
