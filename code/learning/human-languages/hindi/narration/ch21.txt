Hindi, chapter 21: Numbers Six to Ten.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
छ (chha) — the puff again, on a letter you already have.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: छ, Script: the pattern, a second time and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: च — what sound does it carry?
[pause 2 seconds]
You have met this arrangement once before, with त and थ. It is about to happen again.

Script you'll notice: छ.
छ — chha.
It is the breathy partner of च, exactly as थ is the breathy partner of त. Say cha, then say it again with a puff of air behind it, and you have the difference.
What it is made of:
a continuous upper-left loop, lower bowl, outer-right rise and inner loop.
a short upper stem.
the top shirorekhā.

Script: the pattern, a second time.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
character: च. sound: ca. puff of breath: no.
character: छ. sound: chha. puff of breath: yes.
character: त. sound: ta. puff of breath: no.
character: थ. sound: tha. puff of breath: yes.
Four characters, two pairs, one distinction running through both. This script pairs its letters by breath, and once you have heard the pair once you can expect it everywhere — there are more pairs waiting, and each will cost you less than the last.
Unlike त and थ, the two shapes here look nothing alike. The sound is paired; the drawing is not.

Writing: छ.
1. start at the upper loop's open right tip, sweep counterclockwise around the upper-left loop, continue clockwise around the broad lower bowl, rise along the outer right side, and curl counterclockwise around the inner loop to its down-right tip without lifting.
2. lift and draw the short upper stem top-to-bottom.
3. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 2. The pen comes up 2 times and no more.
Verified three-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline; everyday handwriting may divide or simplify the loops differently.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-छ (chha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at च and छ (chha) side by side, and say which one carries the puff]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
च, छ (chha).
[once you have stopped driving — trace: छ three times, saying chha as you finish each one]
[your turn — say: the other pair in this script that works the same way]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is छ (chha) the breathy partner of? (च.) Which earlier pair works the same way? (त and थ.) Do the two shapes resemble each other? (No — the sound is paired, the drawing is not.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
छह, सात, आठ, नौ, दस — six to ten, the same erosion twice more.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You already watched trīṇi wear down to tīn in two careful steps. Two more numbers here do exactly the same thing.

You'll want to know — The five.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
6. Hindi: छह. said: chhah.
7. Hindi: सात. said: sāt.
8. Hindi: आठ. said: āṭh.
9. Hindi: नौ. said: nau.
10. Hindi: दस. said: das.

You'll want to know — sapta becomes satta becomes sāt, aṣṭa becomes aṭṭha becomes āṭh — the same mechanism again.
Put seven and eight beside their Sanskrit ancestors:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
Sanskrit: saptá. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: satta. Hindi: sāt.
Sanskrit: aṣṭá. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: aṭṭha. Hindi: āṭh.
This is the identical two-step process you already watched turn trīṇi into tīn:
The consonant cluster (-pt-, -ṣṭ-) simplifies into a doubled consonant (-tt-, -ṭṭh-) — the same weight, packed into one place.
The doubled consonant simplifies again, and the vowel lengthens to pay for what was lost — satta becomes sāt, aṭṭha becomes āṭh.
Same trade, same reason, two more numbers. This is exactly what the anchor lesson meant by "not decay in any bad sense" — it's the standard Prakrit weight-conservation move, and by now you've seen it three times.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "chhah, sāt, āṭh, nau, das"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the repeated mechanism — "saptá … satta … sāt" and "aṣṭá … aṭṭha … āṭh"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Count six to ten in Hindi. (Chhah, sāt, āṭh, nau, das.) What mechanism do sāt and āṭh share with lesson 6's tīn? (Cluster simplifies to a geminate, then the geminate simplifies again and the vowel lengthens — the same weight-conservation move, three numbers running.) Next: compare the three different histories inside six, nine, and ten.


Lesson 3 of 3.
छह, नौ, दस (chhah, nau, das) — three different paths.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Seven and eight repeated a familiar weight trade. Six, nine, and ten show that not every number took the same road.

You'll want to know — ṣaṣ becomes chhah — a sibilant moves forward.
छह (chhah) continues Sanskrit ṣaṣ, "six." The retroflex sibilant ṣ shifted forward into Hindi छ (ch). This is separate from cluster simplification, but it is still two thousand years of ordinary speech reshaping a Sanskrit word.

You'll want to know — nava becomes nau — a consonant dissolves.
नौ (nau) continues Sanskrit nava. The v between vowels weakened and disappeared; the vowels on either side contracted into the diphthong -ava- becomes -au-. Compare pāṁch: there a nasal moved into the vowel; here an intervocalic consonant dissolved between two vowels.

You'll want to know — daśa becomes das — the quiet path.
दस (das) continues Sanskrit daśa most plainly: the final vowel drops. There is no cluster to simplify and no middle consonant to lose.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ṣaṣ … chhah" — the sibilant shifts]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "nava … nau" — v dissolves into a diphthong]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "daśa … das" — only the final vowel drops]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What happened to v in nava becomes nau? (It dissolved between vowels, which contracted.) Which form changed least? (Das — only the final vowel dropped.) Count six to ten once more. (Chhah, sāt, āṭh, nau, das.)
