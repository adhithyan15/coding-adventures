Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 171: Washing Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बासण (bāsaṇ) — a utensil; a pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to dry (clothes), then the Marwadi for to gather up.

You'll want to know: बासण.
बासण — bāsaṇ — "a utensil; a pot".
रसोई मांय घणा बासण है। rasoī mā̃y ghaṇā bāsaṇ hai. There are a lot of pots in the kitchen.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
बासण (bāsaṇ) is inherited from Sanskrit vāsana, "a box, a casket"; CDIAL lists Hindi bāsan and Gujarati vāsaṇ, "a pot, a dish" (CDIAL 11599). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

You'll want to know: चीथड़ो.
चीथड़ो — chīthṛo — "a rag".
चीथड़ो मैलो है। chīthṛo mailo hai. The rag is dirty.

The word, taken apart.
चीथड़ो (chīthṛo) comes from a reconstructed citth, "pound, tear"; CDIAL lists Hindi cīthṛā and Sindhi cīthaṛo, both masculine, for a rag or old torn clothes (CDIAL 4802). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

Lesson 4 of 5.
साफ (sāph) — clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a rag, then the Marwadi for to scrub.

You'll want to know: साफ.
साफ — sāph — "clean".
म्हारी थाळी साफ है। mhārī thāḷī sāph hai. My plate is clean.

The word, taken apart.
साफ (sāph) is a loan, usually traced to Persian sāf, "clean", which Persian took from Arabic ṣāfī, "pure, clear". It does not change: साफ कपड़ा, साफ थाळी.
