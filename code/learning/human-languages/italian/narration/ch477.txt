Italian, chapter 477: Ricordi e rimpianti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la nostalgia — nostalgia, homesickness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for crying, then the Italian for to sob.

You'll want to know: la nostalgia.
la nostalgia — "nostalgia, homesickness".
Quando vivevo all'estero avevo nostalgia di casa. When I lived abroad I was homesick.
Avere nostalgia di — to miss (a place, a time).

The word, taken apart.
nostalgia comes from New Latin nostalgia (EtymDB), a modern coinage from Greek nóstos "return home" and álgos "pain".

malinconia comes from Late Latin melancholia (EtymDB), from Greek melankholía "black bile", which old medicine blamed for sadness.

Lesson 3 of 5.
rimpiangere — to miss, to regret (something lost).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for nostalgia, then the Italian for melancholy.

You'll want to know: rimpiangere.
rimpiangere — "to miss, to regret (something lost)".
Rimpiango gli anni dell'università. I miss my university years.
Built on piangere; it takes a direct object: rimpiango quei giorni.

The word, taken apart.
rimpiangere is built from ri-, in- and piangere "to weep", which is inherited from Latin plangere "to beat the breast in grief" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
il rimpianto — regret (for what is gone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for melancholy, then the Italian for to miss.

You'll want to know: il rimpianto.
il rimpianto — "regret (for what is gone)".
Non ho rimpianti: ho fatto le mie scelte. I have no regrets: I made my choices.
The past participle of rimpiangere, turned into a noun.

The word, taken apart.
rimpianto is the past participle of rimpiangere "to miss, to regret", turned into a noun; the verb is built on piangere, from Latin plangere (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il rimorso — remorse, guilt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to miss, then the Italian for regret.

You'll want to know: il rimorso.
il rimorso — "remorse, guilt".
Ho il rimorso di non averla chiamata. I feel guilty for not calling her.
Remorse for something you did; for something lost, say il rimpianto.

The word, taken apart.
rimorso is the past participle of rimordere "to bite again", from Latin remordēre "to vex, to torment" (Lewis & Short), built on mordēre "to bite": a conscience that bites.
