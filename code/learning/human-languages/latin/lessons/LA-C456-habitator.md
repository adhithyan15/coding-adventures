## You'll want to know: habitātor, habitātōris
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C456-INCOLA-05]; assesses=[] -->

**habitātor, habitātōris** — "an occupant, a dweller".

> Domus vetus est, sed habitātōrēs eam amant.
>
> *The house is old, but the people who live in it love it.*

Third declension, masculine: *habitātor, habitātōris m.*, from *habitō* 'to live'. English *habitat* comes from the same verb.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*habitātor* is built on *habitō* 'to live, to dwell' with the suffix *-tor* 'one who' (Lewis & Short). *habitō* itself is a frequentative of *habeō* 'to have, to hold'.
