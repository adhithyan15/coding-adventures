Spanish, chapter 570: En la calle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la manifestación — the demonstration, the protest march.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to defend, then the Spanish for the conflict.

You'll want to know: la manifestación.
la manifestación — "the demonstration, the protest march".
Miles de personas fueron a la manifestación. Thousands of people went to the demonstration.
In Spain people often say la mani.

The word, taken apart.
manifestación is a learned borrowing from Latin manifestātiō 'a showing' (Lewis & Short), from manifestus 'plain to see'. A demonstration shows what people want.

You'll want to know: el disturbio.
el disturbio — "the disturbance, the riot".
Hubo disturbios en el centro después del partido. There was rioting in the centre after the match.

The word, taken apart.
disturbio is built on disturbar (EtymDB), a learned word from Latin disturbāre 'to throw into disorder' (Lewis & Short), built on turba 'an uproar, a crowd'.

El escándalo salió en todos los periódicos. The scandal was in all the newspapers.

The word, taken apart.
escándalo is a learned borrowing from Church Latin scandalum, taken from Greek skándalon 'a stumbling block' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the disturbance, then the Spanish for the scandal.

You'll want to know: la polémica.
la polémica — "the controversy".
La nueva ley creó mucha polémica. The new law caused a lot of controversy.
As an adjective: una ley polémica — a controversial law.

The word, taken apart.
polémica comes from Greek polemikós 'warlike' (EtymDB), built on pólemos 'war'. A controversy is a war of words.
