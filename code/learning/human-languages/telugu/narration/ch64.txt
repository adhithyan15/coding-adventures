Telugu, chapter 64: Words That Join an Answer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
కూడా (kūḍā) — also.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ārōgyaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: కూడా.
కూడా (kūḍā) — "also".
కూడా (kūḍā) goes after the thing it is adding, not before it. నేను కూడా (kūḍā) is "me too"; పెరుగు కూడా (kūḍā) is "the curd as well".
English can drop "also" almost anywhere in a sentence and let the stress carry the meaning. Telugu puts కూడా (kūḍā) straight after whatever it includes, and that placement does the work the stress would have done.
It is a small word that softens a great deal. Adding కూడా (kūḍā) to an answer turns a flat అవును into agreement with the person who asked.

What you've built.
The first joining word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
మాత్రమే (mātramē) — only.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kūḍā mean?

You'll want to know: మాత్రమే.
మాత్రమే (mātramē) — "only".
మాత్రమే (mātramē) is the other half of కూడా (kūḍā). Where కూడా (kūḍā) opens an answer out, మాత్రమే (mātramē) shuts it: పెరుగు మాత్రమే (mātramē) is "curd and nothing else". It stands in the same place, straight after the thing it is about.
It is built on Sanskrit mātra, "a measure" — the same piece English carries in metre and in diameter. Saying మాత్రమే (mātramē) is saying "this much and no further".
With a small quantity in front it does polite work. కొంచెం మాత్రమే (mātramē), "only a little", accepts what is offered without taking much of it.

What you've built.
Two, and they pull opposite ways.

Lesson 3 of 5.
అయితే (ayitē) — in that case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mātramē mean?

You'll want to know: అయితే.
అయితే (ayitē) — "in that case".
అయితే (ayitē) picks up what the other person has just said and turns on it: సరే, అయితే (ayitē) వెళ్ళొస్తాను — "alright, in that case I'll be off".
It is put together out of the piece Telugu uses for "it happened" plus the little ending that means "if", so what it literally says is "if it is so". English "well then" and "in that case" both land in the same spot.
At the head of an answer, on its own, అయితే (ayitē) is one of the most useful things a learner can carry. It buys a moment, and it shows the other person you were listening.

What you've built.
Three.

Lesson 4 of 5.
తరువాత (taruvāta) — after that, later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ayitē mean?

You'll want to know: తరువాత.
తరువాత (taruvāta) — "after that, later".
తరువాత puts two things in order: bhōjanaṁ taruvāta, "after the meal". It goes after the thing it follows, exactly like కూడా (kūḍā) and మాత్రమే (mātramē) before it — a habit worth noticing, because it holds for a great many of these small words.
The word is inherited. Standing alone at the front of a sentence it means "later", and it is the gentle way to put something off: తరువాత (taruvāta), not now, and no reason offered.
తరువాత (taruvāta) is also how a story gets kept moving. A listener who says it is asking you to go on.

What you've built.
Four, and this one is about order.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఇంకా (iṅkā) — still, yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did taruvāta mean?

You'll want to know: ఇంకా.
ఇంకా (iṅkā) — "still, yet".
ఇంకా (iṅkā) says the thing is not finished. ఇంకా (iṅkā) ఉంది is "there is still some"; ఇంకా (iṅkā) రాలేదు is "she hasn't come yet".
It is the natural answer to అంతే. Where అంతే closes a list off, ఇంకా (iṅkā) says there is more of it coming, and the two make a pair the way కూడా (kūḍā) and మాత్రమే (mātramē) do.
Five small words, and not one of them names a thing. కూడా (kūḍā), మాత్రమే (mātramē), అయితే (ayitē), తరువాత (taruvāta), ఇంకా (iṅkā) are what turn a set of separate answers into a conversation.

What you've built.
Five: కూడా (kūḍā), మాత్రమే (mātramē), అయితే (ayitē), తరువాత (taruvāta), ఇంకా (iṅkā). Enough to add to an answer, limit it, order it, or keep it open.
