## Fixed — every taken-apart block now says where its word comes from

Thirty-one Latin word lessons had a "taken apart" block that listed English descendants, split a compound into Latin parts, or told the word's later history, and never said where the word itself came from (human-language-data `hasEmptyEtymologyBlock`, HL24 §9.4). Each block now says it. Sources: EtymDB 2.1 (Fourrier & Sagot 2020, CC BY-SA 4.0) and Lewis & Short (Perseus `lexica`, CC BY-SA 4.0), both read locally; every line is our own sentence. Empty etymology blocks 31 → 0.

- **New origin sentences (25).** Inherited words traced to Proto-Indo-European or Proto-Italic, or to a cousin in English, with EtymDB: *valēre*, the numbers six to ten, *pānis*, *malus*, *aut*, *hic*, *quis* (under *quia*, whose exact source is disputed), *mē* (under *meus*), *quattuor*, *quīnque*, *septem*, *octāvus*, *novem*, *decem*, *suādeō*, *sum*, *videō*, *brevis*. With Lewis & Short: *mūrus* (Old Latin *moerus*, set beside *moenia*), *sed* (probably an old ablative of the reflexive stem; uncertain), *ille* (Old Latin *olle*), *ibi* (the pronoun stem of *is*), *post* (compared with a Sanskrit word for "behind"). *iste* is *is* + *-te* (EtymDB). *sinō*'s deeper origin is uncertain.
- **Rewordings a signal can read (6).** LA-C11-menses says each month name comes from the god, festival or number beside it, *Aprīlis* least certain. LA-C24-propediem-te-videbo says "built from". LA-C53-schola says Latin "borrowed" the Greek word. LA-C87-ora says "of uncertain origin". LA-C53-magister and LA-C208-illico cite Lewis & Short for the analysis they already gave; the illico block notes Lewis & Short prefers the spelling *īlicō*.
- **Durations.** LA-C02-numbers-6-10 computes near 270 s; it dropped "(eight arms)" and a repeated "each two places off" clause so it stays under. No `max_seconds` was raised.
