Italian, chapter 339: Secondo me.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'opinione — the opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for exactly, then the Italian for almost.

You'll want to know: l'opinione.
l'opinione — "the opinion".
Ognuno ha la sua opinione. Everyone has their own opinion.
Secondo me opens an opinion; cambiare opinione is to change your mind.

The word, taken apart.
opinione is a learned borrowing from Latin opīniō, opīniōnis "belief, supposition, opinion" (Lewis & Short), built on the verb opīnārī "to suppose".

Lesson 2 of 5.
il parere — the view (someone holds), the considered opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for almost, then the Italian for the opinion.

You'll want to know: il parere.
il parere — "the view (someone holds), the considered opinion".
Vorrei sentire il tuo parere prima di decidere. I would like to hear your view before deciding.
Also the verb parere "to seem": mi pare giusto — it seems right to me. A mio parere is a little more formal than secondo me.

The word, taken apart.
il parere is the verb parere "to seem" used as a noun: what seems right to someone. The verb is inherited from Latin pārēre "to appear, to come into view" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
convinto — convinced, sure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the opinion, then the Italian for the view.

You'll want to know: convinto.
convinto — "convinced, sure".
Sono convinto che sia la scelta giusta. I am convinced it is the right choice.
After sono convinto che Italian uses the subjunctive: che sia. A woman says convinta.

The word, taken apart.
convinto is the past participle of convincere "to convince", which comes from Latin convincere "to overcome in argument, to prove" (Lewis & Short), built on vincere "to conquer".

Lesson 4 of 5.
sostenere — to maintain, to claim; to support.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the view, then the Italian for convinced.

You'll want to know: sostenere.
sostenere — "to maintain, to claim; to support".
Lui sostiene che il treno è più comodo dell'aereo. He maintains that the train is more comfortable than the plane.
Conjugated like tenere: sostengo, sostieni, sostiene. It can also mean to hold up or support a person or a weight.

The word, taken apart.
sostenere is inherited from Latin sustinēre "to hold up, to bear" (Lewis & Short), built from sub "under" and tenēre "to hold": you hold an idea up so it does not fall.

Lesson 5 of 5.
il punto di vista — the point of view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for convinced, then the Italian for to maintain.

You'll want to know: il punto di vista.
il punto di vista — "the point of view".
Dal mio punto di vista, è una buona idea. From my point of view, it is a good idea.
Dal mio punto di vista — from where I stand. The plural is i punti di vista.

The word, taken apart.
punto is inherited from Latin punctum "a prick, a point" (Lewis & Short), and vista is built on vedere, inherited from Latin vidēre "to see": the spot you look from.
