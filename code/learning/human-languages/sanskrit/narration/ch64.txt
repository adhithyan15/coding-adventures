Sanskrit, chapter 64: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Passage.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six words, and the end of each one.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have already met. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them to you first.

Reading.
ग्रामः मार्गः दीपः गृहम् वनम् पुस्तकम्.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time read the last letter of each one first.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
The six sorted themselves into two families before you read one of them for meaning. Three close on -अः, three close on -अम्, and you were told in Chapter 16 what that difference is.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the habit Sanskrit asks for and English never does. An English word carries its job in WHERE IT STANDS. A Sanskrit word carries its job in HOW IT ENDS.
[pause 2 seconds]
So a reader of Sanskrit reads the end of a word first. It is not a trick for beginners — it is what the writing system is built to be read with, and the next lesson shows what it buys you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice too that each word is printed with space around it and its ending whole. Running Sanskrit does not keep them that separate; a first reader needs to see the joints while they are still learning where they are.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which three words end in -अम्?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Where does an English word carry its job, and where does a Sanskrit one? (English in its position; Sanskrit in its ending.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines, and only two of them say anything different.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words. These put them somewhere.
[pause 1 second]
Six lines — and six is more than the number of things they say.

Reading.
ग्रामः अत्र अस्ति। अत्र ग्रामः अस्ति। अस्ति अत्र ग्रामः। वनम् तत्र अस्ति। तत्र वनम् अस्ति। अस्ति तत्र वनम्।.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read the six once, straight down.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read the first three again and say what changed.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Nothing changed. The first three lines are one sentence written three ways, and the second three are another. The village is here. The forest is there. That is the whole content of the page.
[pause 2 seconds]
Do the same thing in English and it breaks, or it turns into something else. The village is here and here is the village and is here the village are not one sentence three ways.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is what the last lesson bought. Because ग्रामः announces its own job with its ending, it does not need a position to hold that job for it — so the position is free, and a writer can put the emphasis wherever it belongs.
[pause 2 seconds]
The usual order still puts the verb last, and the passage in the next lesson mostly keeps it there. The freedom is real; it is not disorder.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read the ending, not the seat. Everything else in Sanskrit follows from that one habit.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines aloud, once, straight down]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Six lines. How many different things do they say?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Why can the words be shuffled? (The ending carries the job, so the position does not have to.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What does a Sanskrit reader look at first? (The end of the word.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now fourteen of them together.
[pause 1 second]
Still not one new word.

Reading.
एतत् गृहम् अस्ति। महत् गृहम् अस्ति। अत्र दीपः अस्ति। अत्र पुष्पम् च माला च। तत्र मार्गः अस्ति। तत्र वनम् च नदी च। पर्वतः तत्र अस्ति। किन्तु अत्र पर्वतः न अस्ति। अद्य बालः क्रीडति। बालः पुष्पम् वा फलम् वा गृह्णाति। धेनुः तृणम् खादति। वृद्धः अत्र तिष्ठति। नरः वनम् पश्यति। शान्तिः अत्र अस्ति।.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the lines arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and watch where अत्र turns into तत्र.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
The passage stands in one village. The first half of it looks around — अत्र for what is at hand, तत्र for what is past the road — and the second half watches what the village is doing.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three joiners carry all of it. च adds, वा offers, and किन्तु turns the page against itself: there is a mountain over there, BUT there is none here.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two of the three sit where English would never put them. पुष्पम् च माला च is the-flower-and the-garland-and — च and वा stand AFTER what they join, once for each thing, while किन्तु stands first, which is why that line opens differently from every other line on the page.
[pause 2 seconds]
The passage closes on शान्तिः. That is not a flourish added for an ending — it is the word Sanskrit recitation has closed on for a very long time, said three times over, and a reader meeting it at the foot of a page is meeting it where it has always been.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
What is there, but not here?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Where do च and वा stand? (After the thing they join, once for each.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which joiner stands first in its line? (किन्तु.)
