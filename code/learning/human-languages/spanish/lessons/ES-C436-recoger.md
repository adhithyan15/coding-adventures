# recoger — to gather it back up

## You'll want to know: recoger
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-05]; assesses=[] -->

**recoger** — to collect, to pick up, to fetch, to tidy away.

*Vengo a recoger unas fotos* — I have come to collect some photos. *Recojo a
los niños del colegio* — I pick the children up from school.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-05, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

**re-** back, plus **coger**, to take. To take back up what is there.

One Spanish verb covers what English splits four ways, and the split is
arbitrary in English rather than meaningful:

| what you recoger | English wants |
|---|---|
| un pedido | collect |
| a los niños | pick up |
| la mesa | clear |
| la ropa tendida | bring in |

Hold the Spanish idea — *gather back up* — and all four fall out of it. Trying
to remember four English translations is four times the work for less accuracy.

**Watch the spelling**: *recojo* with a **j** in the yo form, because *ge* would
be said differently from *go*. The sound stays put and the letter moves, which
is the reverse of what English spelling usually does to you.
