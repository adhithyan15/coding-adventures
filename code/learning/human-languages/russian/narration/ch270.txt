Russian, chapter 270: Would, and a Letter About a Trip.
4 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
я бы хотел — would, and the polite request.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want a coffee". (Ya khochú kófe.) Then "if I have time, I'll ring". (Yésli búdet vrémya, ya pozvonyú.) That was a real plan. Now a softer world: what would be.

Grammar Lens: бы and the -л form.
бы — by — is a small word that makes "would". It goes with the past form of the verb, the -л form, and here that form says nothing about the past:
Я бы хотел кофе. — ya by khotél kófe — I'd like a coffee. Я бы хотела чай. — ya by khotéla chay — I'd like tea; a woman says it. Я бы пошёл в кино. — ya by poshól v kinó — I'd go to the cinema.
Бы can also follow the verb: Я хотел бы кофе. It agrees with nothing; the -л form does the agreeing.

Grammar Lens: the polite request.
Я хочу is direct; я бы хотел is the polite way to ask for a thing. For asking someone to do something, Russian says "could you not …":
Вы не могли бы говорить медленно? — vy ne moglí by govorít médlenno? — Could you speak slowly? With повторить: Вы не могли бы повторить?
Могли is the -ли form of мочь, for polite вы; повторить is the partner of повторять. The не makes it gentler, not negative.

The word, taken apart.
бы is, by the usual account, a worn-down old form of быть, left over from a past tense Russian no longer has; EtymDB 2.1 lists бы without a parent. It is the same бы that sits inside чтобы, "in order to", a wish fastened to что.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Vy ne moglí by govorít médlenno?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: at a café the waiter asks Shto vy khotítye? Answer politely with ya by khotél or ya by khotéla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I'd like a coffee", a woman? (Я бы хотела кофе.) Which verb form goes with бы? (The -л form, but it is not past.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
если бы — the condition that is not so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'd like a coffee". (Ya by khotél kófe.) Then the real plan: "if I have time, I'll ring". (Yésli búdet vrémya, ya pozvonyú.)

Grammar Lens: у меня было, I had.
You have said у меня проблема, "at me, a problem". "I have time" is built the same way: у меня есть время. In the past, the thing you had is the subject, and было agrees with it: время is neuter, so у меня было время — u menyá býlo vrémya — I had time.

Grammar Lens: бы on both sides.
Если бы у меня было время, я бы позвонил. — yésli by u menyá býlo vrémya, ya by pozvoníl — If I had time, I would ring. Если бы я знал, я бы помог. — yésli by ya znal, ya by pomóg — If I had known, I would have helped.
Бы stands on both sides, each time with the past form: знал, позвонил, and помог, one of the few without an л. Russian marks no time here: the first can mean "if I had time now" or "if I had had time then", and the story around it decides. Помог, "helped", is the past of помочь, the partner of помогать.

Why it's said this way: real, or not real.
Если будет время, я позвоню. — It may well happen: a plan. Если бы было время, я бы позвонил. — It is not so: a wish, or a regret.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: U menyá býlo vrémya.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Yésli by u menyá býlo vrémya, ya by pozvoníl.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Yésli by ya znal, ya by pomóg, then a woman: Yésli by ya znála, ya by pomoglá.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one wish of your own: Yésli by u menyá býlo vrémya, ya by …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I had time"? (У меня было время.) A plan or a wish: если бы было время? (A wish: it is not so.) How many times does бы appear? (Twice, once on each side.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
Review — what will be, and what would be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask politely for a tea, as a woman. (Ya by khotéla chay.) Then ask someone to speak slowly. (Vy ne moglí by govorít médlenno?)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and Russian can now say what is not so. Бы with the -л form, whatever the time. Я бы хотел to ask for a thing, вы не могли бы to ask for a favour. And two kinds of если: the future for a plan, бы on both sides for a wish.
[pause 2 seconds]
The same dream, two ways:
Если у меня будут деньги, я буду путешествовать. — a plan. Если бы у меня были деньги, я бы путешествовал. — a wish.
Деньги, "money", is plural, so будут and были. Путешествовать is imperfective, so its future is буду and the infinitive.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Yésli by u menyá býli déngi, ya by puteshéstvoval.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Yésli by ya znála, ya by pomoglá.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: Ty búdesh puteshéstvovat létom? Éto zavísit ot déneg, with déneg, the "of" form of déngi; then one line with yésli]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Ya mechtáyu o móre. Yésli by u menyá býlo vrémya, ya by puteshéstvoval.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
A plan, or a wish: если бы у меня были деньги? (A wish.) "Could you …?", politely? (Вы не могли бы …?) "I had time"? (У меня было время.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines to one reader about a trip — the scene in the imperfective, the steps in order in the perfective, a feeling, and a last line with бы.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The four-line writing step held together with и, но, а and потому что. This time there are six, and they tell a story, as Anna's rainy Saturday did.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one person who will read it: друг, подруга, someone in your family.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told trip is more than a list of events. It has a scene, in the imperfective: было, шёл дождь, мы жили в гостинице. It has steps in order, each one finished, in the perfective. It has a feeling. And a good one ends by looking forward, or wishing.
[pause 2 seconds]
A past verb agrees with who did it: -л, -ла, -ли. A few, such as мог, drop the л only in the form for a man: мог, but могла, могли. A verb after бы takes the same forms.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when, with назад (год назад), and what it was like, in the imperfective;
the first step, with сначала, in the perfective;
the next step, with потом or затем, and a reason, with потому что, поэтому or так что; a problem works well here: я опоздал на поезд;
something that cut in, with когда or пока, and how it ended, with наконец;
how you felt, with я был рад, что …, я устал or мне было …, or what you thought, with по-моему and a comparison: лучше, чем …;
one line forward, with если бы у меня было время, я бы … (yésli by u menyá býlo vrémya, ya by …), or я бы хотел …, or a hope: я надеюсь, что …, я мечтаю ….
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. The exercise is the joining, and the aspect of each verb.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now go through your lines and find every verb. In line one, is it imperfective, a scene? In lines two, three and four, is each step perfective, one deed done? That is the slip to check first.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then the endings: -л for a man, -ла for a woman, -ли for more than one, after бы as well.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, the commas: one before что, потому что and чем, one before поэтому or так что when they join inside a sentence, and one at the seam after a когда, пока or если half. If line six uses если бы, does бы stand on both sides of the comma?
