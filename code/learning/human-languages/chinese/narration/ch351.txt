Lesson 1 of 7.
fēnxī — to analyse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to make a judgement, then the Mandarin for indeed.

You'll want to know: fēnxī.
fēnxī — "to analyse".
wǒmen yīnggāi xiān fēnxī wèntí — We should analyse the problem first.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fēnxī is built from fēn and xī, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjun and sek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to share out' and the second 'to split'.

Lesson 2 of 7.
有理 (yǒulǐ) — reasonable; in the right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for indeed, then the Mandarin for to analyse.

You'll want to know: 有理.
有理 — yǒulǐ — "reasonable; in the right".
我觉得你说得有理 wǒ juéde nǐ shuō de yǒulǐ — I think you've got a point.
The new character, in pieces: 理 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the jade piece, four strokes, three bars and a vertical, the last bar rising to the right; on the right 里 lǐ, seven strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
Two third tones meet in this word: yǒu is said with a rising tone before lǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
有理 (yǒulǐ) is built from 有 'to have' and 理 (lǐ) 'order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwX and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to put in order' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'to work jade'.

What you've built.

Script — the jade piece, then lǐ.
理 (lǐ).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the jade piece, four strokes, narrow: a horizontal, a second horizontal, a vertical down through them, then a bar at the foot that rises to the right.
The right piece, 里, seven strokes: a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, a horizontal across the middle, a horizontal closing the box, a vertical down through the middle, a short horizontal, then the long horizontal at the foot.
4 + 7 = eleven, and the last seven your hand already knows.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 理 (lǐ), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 理? (Two.) What is the right piece read? (lǐ, as in 里.) How does the jade piece end? (With a bar that rises to the right.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to analyse, then the Mandarin for in the right in an argument.

You'll want to know: jiǎnglǐ.
jiǎnglǐ — "to be reasonable; to listen to reason".
zhège rén bù jiǎnglǐ — This man won't listen to reason.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: jiǎng is said with a rising tone before lǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎnglǐ is built from jiǎng and 理 (lǐ) 'order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaewngX and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tell' and 'to discuss', and the second 'to put in order'.

You'll want to know: 理想.
理想 — lǐxiǎng — "ideal; aspiration".
我的理想是当医生 wǒ de lǐxiǎng shì dāng yīshēng — My dream is to be a doctor.
Two third tones meet in this word: lǐ is said with a rising tone before xiǎng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
理想 (lǐxiǎng) is built from 理 (lǐ) 'order' and 想 'to think', both inherited from Middle Chinese, liX and sjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to put in order' and the second 'to think'.
