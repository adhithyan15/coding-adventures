Spanish, chapter 253: Unos, Unas --- The Plural of One.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
unos, unas — the plural of "one".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Un libro, una casa. And you know where they came from: Latin unus, the number one.
[pause 1 second]
Which raises a question. What could a plural of one possibly mean?

Grammar Lens: it stops meaning one.
unos libros — some books, a few books unas casas — some houses.
[pause 2 seconds]
The moment it goes plural it stops being a number and becomes a vague quantity: a handful, several, a few. Not one, and not counted.
[pause 2 seconds]
English does the same trick with a and some and never notices, because it changes the word. Spanish keeps the word and changes only the ending.

Why it's said this way.
Now the useful part, because you already know the alternative.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
Compro libros., I buy books — books in general.
Compro unos libros., I buy some books — a few, this once.
[pause 2 seconds]
The bare plural you learned long ago talks about the kind of thing. Unos picks out a small, unspecified number of them.
[pause 2 seconds]
So they are not interchangeable, and the difference is one you can hear in English too — I buy books is a habit, I buy some books is an occasion.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What does unos mean, given that un means one? (Some — a few, uncounted.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And what does the bare plural mean instead? (The kind of thing, in general.)
