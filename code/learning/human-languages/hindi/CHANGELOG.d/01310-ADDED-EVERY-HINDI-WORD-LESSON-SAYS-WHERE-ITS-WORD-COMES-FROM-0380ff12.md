## Added — every Hindi word lesson says where its word comes from

Every one of the track's 1,200 word lessons now has an `etymology_hook`, and every one says in its body where its word comes from. Before this change 1,126 had no hook and 967 had no visible origin (human-language-data `ETYMOLOGY_GAP_PINS.hindi`, now 0 / 0).

- **New "taken apart" blocks (962).** Each sits after the section that introduces the word, opens with `<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->`, and agrees with the lesson's new hook. Phrase lessons get "The phrase, taken apart".
- **Sources.** Inherited words and Sanskrit loans cite Turner's *Comparative Dictionary of the Indo-Aryan Languages* (CDIAL) beside the fact, read from the moli-mandala data files: 1,492 citations of 596 entries, each checked by script for the entry number, the Sanskrit or reconstructed form, and the Hindi form. CDIAL's own hedges are carried: "probably", queried links, Dravidian, Munda and Austroasiatic sources, and forms CDIAL marks as taken from Punjabi or Gujarati. Reconstructed forms are starred. Arabic loans name their root, checked against Lane's *Arabic-English Lexicon*. Persian loans are written "usually traced to Persian …". English and Portuguese loans name the donor. DEDR is cited only for the three words with a Dravidian question (ठंडा, ठंड, चप्पल).
- **Rough mix.** About 590 tadbhavas, 110 tatsamas, 140 Persian, 100 Arabic, 55 English and 5 Portuguese loans, 90 words built inside Hindi from known parts, and 48 whose origin is uncertain or only partly known. Those say so; none is guessed.
- **Lessons that already had a block (82) or a hook (15).** Blocks that took apart only grammar gained one origin sentence: पर, था / थी / थे, the future -गा, रहा, से, को, तक, जानना, चाहिए and others. Hook-only lessons whose body was near its time limit gained one sentence in an existing section: ऋतु, शुभ दोपहर, सकना, चाहना, क्यों. The numerals and ordinals 1–10 gained blocks. Hooks that were not etymology, or ran past 120 characters, were rewritten.
- **Corrections to older text that contradicted CDIAL (15).** मोटा and नाटा belong to CDIAL's "defective" word groups (*\*muṭṭa*, *\*naṭṭa*), not to मूर्त and नत. पतला is *pattrala*, "leafy, leaf-like", not "fallen flat". ठीक comes, probably, from *sthita*, so the "no European cousin" line went. फिर is the "having turned" form of *\*phirati*. कल is *kalya*, "dawn, tomorrow", built on काल. मेरा is listed under the first-person base *ma-*, not *madīya*. तुम is CDIAL's *yuṣmad-* with its *t-* from *tvam*, and आप is *ātman*. लंबा no longer claims English *limb* came from Latin *limbus*. Six lessons (nose, now, day after tomorrow, back, roof, vehicle) now give CDIAL's source rather than a look-alike Sanskrit word.
- **Font.** Long vowels with a nasal are written with ṁ (*sāṁp*, *pāṁcvāṁ*), the track's own convention, because the book font has no long vowel with a tilde.
