Hindi, chapter 315: Claims and Denials.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दावा (dāvā) — a claim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to stir up, then the Hindi for a sensation.

You'll want to know: दावा.
दावा — dāvā — "a claim".
मंत्री ने दावा किया कि सब ठीक है। mantrī ne dāvā kiyā ki sab ṭhīk hai. The minister claimed that everything was fine.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
दावा (dāvā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic daʿwā, "a claim, a lawsuit". दावा करना is to claim; the claim is masculine: दावा किया.

Lesson 2 of 5.
जनता (jantā) — the public; the people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a sensation, then the Hindi for a claim.

You'll want to know: जनता.
जनता — jantā — "the public; the people".
जनता उस दावे से ख़ुश नहीं थी। jantā us dāve se khuś nahīṃ thī. The public was not happy with that claim.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
जनता (jantā) is Sanskrit janatā, "a number of people, a community" (Monier-Williams), from jana, a person. It is singular and feminine in Hindi: जनता ख़ुश नहीं थी.

Lesson 5 of 5.
टालना (ṭālnā) — to put off; to dodge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a declaration, then the Hindi for information.

You'll want to know: टालना.
टालना — ṭālnā — "to put off; to dodge".
मंत्री ने पत्रकारों का सवाल टाल दिया। mantrī ne patrakāroṁ kā savāl ṭāl diyā. The minister dodged the journalists' question.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बात टाल दी, usne bāt ṭāl dī.

The word, taken apart.
टालना (ṭālnā) is the transitive partner of टलना, from the same Sanskrit ṭalati (CDIAL 5450): to make something move away from you, a question, a visit or a danger.
