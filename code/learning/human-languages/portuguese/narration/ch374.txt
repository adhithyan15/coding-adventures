Lesson 1 of 6.
a intenção — the intention.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for unlikely, then the Portuguese for the probability.

You'll want to know: a intenção.
a intenção — "the intention".
Não tenho intenção de mudar de emprego. I have no intention of changing jobs.
Ter a intenção de + infinitive. Com boa intenção — meaning well.

The word, taken apart.
intenção is a learned borrowing from Latin intentiō "a stretching out, a straining", and later "purpose" (Lewis & Short), from intendere "to stretch towards", on tendere "to stretch".

Qual é o propósito desta reunião? What is the purpose of this meeting?

The word, taken apart.
propósito is a learned borrowing from Latin prōpositum "a plan, a purpose" (Lewis & Short), the participle of prōpōnere "to set before", on pōnere "to put".

Lesson 3 of 6.
a meta — the goal, the target; the finishing line.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the intention, then the Portuguese for the purpose.

You'll want to know: a meta.
a meta — "the goal, the target; the finishing line".
A minha meta é correr dez quilómetros. My goal is to run ten kilometres.
In a race, a meta is the finishing line.

The word, taken apart.
meta is a learned borrowing from Latin mēta "the turning post in a racecourse, a goal, a limit" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short).

disponível — "available; free (to meet)".
Estou disponível na quinta à tarde. I am free on Thursday afternoon.

The word, taken apart.
disponível is built on dispor "to arrange, to have at hand", from Latin dispōnere "to set in order", on pōnere "to put" (Lewis & Short), with -ível.

Lesson 5 of 6.
o compromisso — the commitment, the engagement; the compromise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the goal, then the Portuguese for available.

You'll want to know: o compromisso.
o compromisso — "the commitment, the engagement; the compromise".
Hoje não posso: tenho um compromisso às seis. I cannot today: I have an engagement at six.
In Portugal um compromisso is often an appointment or an obligation; it can also be a compromise.

The word, taken apart.
compromisso is a learned borrowing from Latin comprōmissum "a mutual promise to accept a judge's decision" (EtymDB), on prōmittere "to promise", built on mittere "to send" (Lewis & Short).
