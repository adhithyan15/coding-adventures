## You'll want to know: qiǎngjiù
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C299-RESCUE299-05]; assesses=[] -->

*qiǎngjiù* — "to fight to save a patient; to give emergency treatment".

> *yīshēng zhèngzài qiǎngjiù tā* — The doctors are fighting to save him.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*qiǎngjiù* is built from *qiǎng* 'to snatch' and 救 'to save', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshjangX* and *kjuwH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun gives the first no gloss in this reading and glosses it 'to resist' and 'to dash' in the level tone; the sense 'to snatch' is not given there.
