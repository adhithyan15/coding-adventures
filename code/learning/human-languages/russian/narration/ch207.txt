Russian, chapter 207: To be ill, To treat, To breathe, To cough, To sneeze.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
болеть (bolét) — to be ill; to ache.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to paint, then the Russian for to clean.

You'll want to know: болеть.
болеть (bolét) — "to be ill; to ache".

The word, taken apart.
болеть (bolét) from Proto-Slavic bolěti, with боль, "pain" — inherited (Vasmer). Vasmer sets it beside Old English bealu, "evil", a link Berneker rejected.

Lesson 2 of 5.
лечить (lechít) — to treat (an illness).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to clean, then the Russian for to be ill; to ache.

You'll want to know: лечить.
лечить (lechít) — "to treat (an illness)".

The word, taken apart.
лечить (lechít) comes from lěkŭ, "a cure", the root of лекарство, "medicine", which Vasmer calls a certain loan from Germanic, as in Gothic lēkeis, "a doctor".

Lesson 3 of 5.
дышать (dyshát) — to breathe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to be ill; to ache, then the Russian for to treat.

You'll want to know: дышать.
дышать (dyshát) — "to breathe".

The word, taken apart.
дышать (dyshát) from Proto-Slavic dyšati, with дух, "breath", and душа, "soul" — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian dūsėti, "to pant".
