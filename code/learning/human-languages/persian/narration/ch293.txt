Persian, chapter 293: Advice, And Why.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
بروم (beravam) — a going that is wanted, not stated.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'm going", then "I'm not going". (Miravam, nemiravam.)
[pause 1 second]
Both are statements. Advice, plans and wishes talk about a going that has not happened, and Persian gives that its own shape.

Grammar Lens: be- in place of mi-.
Swap mi- for be- and keep the present stem and the endings:
be- + rav + -am becomes بروم — beravam — that I go, for me to go.
So beravi, beravad, beravim, beravid, beravand. The same swap works on any stored stem: bekhânam, benevisam, begiram, and bekharam from kharidan. This is the subjunctive: the verb shape for something wanted, needed or possible rather than reported.
Three habits to know. A compound verb often leaves be- off, above all in writing: komak konam, fekr konam. Budan has a stem of its own here: bâsham, bâshi, bâshad. And "not" is na- in place of be-: naravam.

Grammar Lens: mikhâham, and the verb after it.
Khâstan, "to want", from chapter thirty-eight, has the present stem khâh-: mikhâham, I want; mikhâhi, you want. Whatever you want to do comes after it in the subjunctive:
میخواهم بروم. — mikhâham beravam — I want to go.
میخواهم کتاب بخوانم. — mikhâham ketâb bekhânam — I want to read a book.
میخواهی بروی؟ — mikhâhi beravi? — Do you want to go?
English says "want to go", with the plain verb. Persian gives the wanted verb its own person: "I want that I go".

The word, taken apart.
khâstan goes back, in EtymDB 2.1, to a Middle Persian verb written hwʾstn' in Pahlavi letters, xwāstan. EtymDB has no entry for the prefix be-, so no history is claimed for it here.

Lesson 2 of 6.
باید بروی (bâyad beravi) — you should go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want to go". (Mikhâham beravam.) Then the word for "must, should". (Bâyad.)
[pause 1 second]
Advice is a want that belongs to someone else. It takes the same verb shape.

Grammar Lens: bâyad, and the verb that carries the person.
باید does not change for the person. The verb after it does, in the subjunctive:
باید بروی. — bâyad beravi — You should go. You have to go.
باید بخوابم. — bâyad bekhâbam — I must sleep.
One word covers "must", "have to" and "should"; the situation and your tone decide how strong it is. With na- it forbids or warns:
نباید دیر برسی. — nabâyad dir beresi — You mustn't arrive late.

Grammar Lens: behtar ast, the softer one.
Behtar, "better", from chapter one hundred and sixty-four, with ast, "it is", and the subjunctive after it:
بهتر است زود بخوابی. — behtar ast zud bekhâbi — You'd better sleep early. It would be better to sleep early.
Behtar ast advises without pressing. With be nazar-e man in front it is gentler still.

The word, taken apart.
bâyad is usually traced to Middle Persian abāyēd, "it is needed". Its last sound is usually read as the -ad of "he, she, it": a verb frozen in its "it" form, "it is necessary". That fits what it does here. It takes no ending of its own, so the person travels to the next verb.

Lesson 3 of 6.
توضیح دادن (towzih dâdan) — to explain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you should go". (Bâyad beravi.) Then "to give". (Dâdan.)
[pause 1 second]
Advice is stronger with its reason. Before the reason, the verb for laying one out.

You'll want to know: توضیح دادن.
توضیح دادن — towzih dâdan — "to explain".
او مشکل را توضیح داد. — u moshkel râ towzih dâd — She explained the problem.
It runs on dâdan, "to give", whose present stem is deh-: towzih midaham, I explain; towzih bedahid, in the subjunctive, "that you explain". So باید توضیح بدهم, bâyad towzih bedaham, is "I have to explain".
You have sharh dâdan from chapter two hundred and twenty-three. It lays a thing out at length. Towzih dâdan is the one you reach for every day: make it clear.
Say tow-zih, two beats, with a breathy h at the end. Its third letter is a z you have not written yet, and the next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB 2.1 has no entry for towzih itself. It is the Arabic noun tawḍīḥ, usually explained as "making clear", on the root w-ḍ-ḥ of Arabic wāḍiḥ, "clear, obvious", which EtymDB does list.
Dâdan is Persian, and EtymDB takes it back to the Indo-European root for "to give" that also gave Latin dō, "I give". So the compound is "to give clarity".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I explain", then "I have to explain" — towzih midaham, bâyad towzih bedaham]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "to lay out at length" against "to make clear" — sharh dâdan, towzih dâdan]

Script you'll notice: ض.
ض — zâd, the sound z.
It is the third letter of توضیح. The و before it offers nothing to the left, so ض (zâd) starts a fresh run, ضیح, in its opening shape.
It is ص, from chapter twenty-five, with one dot above: the same wide oval body and the same trailing bowl. That makes four ways to write z: ز, ذ, ض (zâd) and ظ, and Persian says all four alike. Arabic keeps them apart in sound; Persian keeps them apart only in spelling, so each word has to be learned with its own z.

Writing: ض.
Put your pen on ض (zâd) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 5 of 6.
دلیل این است که (dalil in ast ke) — the reason is that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a reason", then "to explain". (Dalil, towzih dâdan.)
[pause 1 second]
An explanation is more than chon. Sometimes the reason needs a sentence to itself.

Grammar Lens: the reason, in a sentence of its own.
دیر رسیدم. دلیل این است که قطار دیر آمد. — dir residam. dalil in ast ke qatâr dir âmad — I arrived late. The reason is that the train came late.
Dalil, "the reason", in ast, "is this", and ke, "that", which opens the reason the way it opened the thought after fekr mikonam. The event comes first and stands alone; then the reason gets the floor.
Before a longer explanation, offer it: توضیح بدهم؟, towzih bedaham?, "shall I explain?". The subjunctive makes it a question about a thing not yet done.

Grammar Lens: a noun, or a sentence.
A cause that is one thing takes be khâter-e, with the ezafe joining the noun:
به خاطر باران دیر رسیدم. — be khâter-e bârân dir residam — I arrived late because of the rain.
A cause that is a whole happening takes chon, and a full sentence:
چون هوا سرد بود، دیر رسیدم. — chon havâ sard bud, dir residam — Because it was cold, I arrived late.
So: a noun after be khâter-e, a verb after chon. "Because of the rain", but "because it was cold".

Lesson 6 of 6.
Review — advice, and why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want to go", then "you should go". (Mikhâham beravam, bâyad beravi.)

What you've built.
Four lessons, and one new verb shape doing a great deal of work. Be- in place of mi- gives the subjunctive, and it follows mikhâham, bâyad and behtar ast: a thing wanted, needed or advised. The reason comes after, with chon and a sentence, be khâter-e and a noun, or dalil in ast ke when it needs a sentence of its own. Towzih dâdan names what you are doing.
[pause 2 seconds]
One piece of advice, built the whole way:
به نظر من بهتر است زود بخوابی. دلیل این است که فردا باید زود بروی. be nazar-e man behtar ast zud bekhâbi. dalil in ast ke fardâ bâyad zud beravi. If you ask me, you'd better sleep early. The reason is that tomorrow you have to leave early.
