Latin, chapter 508: Laziness and energy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ignāvia, ignāviae — cowardice; laziness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for insolent, then the Latin for boastfulness.

You'll want to know: ignāvia, ignāviae.
ignāvia, ignāviae — "cowardice; laziness".
Ignāvia mīlitum ducem īrātum facit. The soldiers' cowardice makes the general angry.
Feminine, first declension, from ignāvus — lazy, cowardly.

The word, taken apart.
ignāvia is built on ignāvus "idle, cowardly", from in- "not" and old gnāvus "busy, active" (Lewis & Short).

pigritia is built on piger "lazy, slow" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), which EtymDB links with pigeō "to feel annoyance".

Lesson 3 of 5.
segnis, segne — sluggish, slow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for cowardice, then the Latin for sloth.

You'll want to know: segnis, segne.
segnis, segne — "sluggish, slow".
Asinus segnis lentē ambulat. The sluggish donkey walks slowly.
Two endings. Nōn segniter — not slackly, with energy.

The word, taken apart.
segnis is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short reports that it is commonly linked with sequor "to follow", but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
strēnuus, strēnua, strēnuum — vigorous, active.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sloth, then the Latin for sluggish.

You'll want to know: strēnuus, strēnua, strēnuum.
strēnuus, strēnua, strēnuum — "vigorous, active".
Agricola strēnuus ā prīmā lūce labōrat. The vigorous farmer works from first light.
Brisk and energetic, in work or in war. English strenuous.

The word, taken apart.
strēnuus is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short and EtymDB compare Greek stereos "firm, hard", but the link is not settled. English strenuous comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
assiduus, assidua, assiduum — diligent, unremitting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sluggish, then the Latin for vigorous.

You'll want to know: assiduus, assidua, assiduum.
assiduus, assidua, assiduum — "diligent, unremitting".
Discipulus assiduus semper in scholā est. The diligent pupil is always at school.
From assideō — to sit beside: someone who stays at the task. English assiduous.

The word, taken apart.
assiduus is built on assideō "to sit beside", from ad- and sedeō "to sit" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English assiduous comes from it.
