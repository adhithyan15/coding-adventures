Hindi, chapter 104: Counting Past Twenty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तीस चालीस (tīs cālīs) — two rungs past where the counting stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count from ग्यारह to बीस. Then stop, because that is where your counting has ended until now.

You'll want to know: तीस, चालीस.
तीस (tīs) — thirty.
चालीस (cālīs) — forty.

Grammar Lens: the tens are the part worth learning.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
number: 20. word: बीस.
number: 30. word: तीस.
number: 40. word: चालीस.
Say those three in a row and the family resemblance is audible: –ीस at the end of all of them. It is the same ending, worn down from the same Sanskrit word for ten, and it will carry you as far as fifty.
तीन is three and तीस is thirty. चार is four and चालीस is forty. The openings are not identical to the small numbers, but they are close enough to hold on to, and that is the most help Hindi gives with numbers at this size.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: बीस, तीस, चालीस — three times, as a run]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the small number each of the two new words leans on]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the ending all three share]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which number is तीस? Which is चालीस? Which three of them so far end in the same sound?


Lesson 2 of 5.
पचास साठ (pacās sāṭh) — where the pattern stops helping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say बीस, तीस, चालीस. Listen to the ending they share.

You'll want to know: पचास, साठ.
पचास (pacās) — fifty.
साठ (sāṭh) — sixty.

Grammar Lens: one keeps the ending, one drops it.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
number: 40. word: चालीस. the shared ending: yes.
number: 50. word: पचास. the shared ending: a worn version.
number: 60. word: साठ. the shared ending: gone.
पचास still leans on पाँच, the five you have counted since the opening chapters, and it still carries a trace of that ending. साठ leans on छह and keeps nothing else.
That is the shape of the rest of this chapter: the words get less helpful as the numbers get bigger. Knowing that in advance is worth more than a rule that would not hold.
पचास is the one to hold on to hardest. It is the price on a tea stall sign and the age of somebody's father, and it is the most common of the four so far.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: बीस, तीस, चालीस, पचास, साठ — the whole run]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which of the five keeps least of the shared ending]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the small number पचास leans on]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which number is पचास? Which is साठ? Which of the two still sounds like the ending you have been hearing?


Lesson 3 of 5.
सत्तर अस्सी नब्बे (sattar assī nabbe) — the last three rungs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say पचास and साठ. One of them kept the old ending and one dropped it.

You'll want to know: सत्तर, अस्सी, नब्बे.
सत्तर (sattar) — seventy.
अस्सी (assī) — eighty.
नब्बे (nabbe) — ninety.

Grammar Lens: each one leans on a number you already count.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
small number: सात. ten times it: सत्तर.
small number: आठ. ten times it: अस्सी.
small number: नौ. ten times it: नब्बे.
Read that table down the left and you are counting seven, eight, nine — which you have done since early on. Read it down the right and you are counting seventy, eighty, ninety.
The opening consonant survives in all three, and that is the whole of the help on offer. Beyond the first sound, each word has been worn into its own shape and has to be met as a word.
Say the pairs across, one after another: sāt, sattar. āṭh, assī. nau, nabbe. Hearing them together is what makes them stick.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: सात — सत्तर, आठ — अस्सी, नौ — नब्बे, as three pairs]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the whole run from बीस to नब्बे]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what the three new words keep from their small numbers]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which number is सत्तर? Which is अस्सी? Which is नब्बे? Which small number does each one lean on?


Lesson 4 of 5.
गिनती की तालिका (gintī kī tālikā) — eight rungs, and what sits between them.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the last of the three words you met, the one for ninety.

Grammar Lens: the ladder.
[a table of 8 rows, read aloud]
number: 20. word: बीस.
number: 30. word: तीस.
number: 40. word: चालीस.
number: 50. word: पचास.
number: 60. word: साठ.
number: 70. word: सत्तर.
number: 80. word: अस्सी.
number: 90. word: नब्बे.
Eight words, on top of the hundred you already say. Every price, every age and every house number you are likely to meet sits on or between them.

Grammar Lens: what sits between them.
Here is the honest part, and it is better to hear it than to guess wrong.
Hindi puts the small number first. Twenty-five is five-twenty: पच्चीस (paccīs) opens with the sound of पाँच. Twenty-two is बाईस (bāīs) and opens with दो's vowel.
That order is the opposite of English, and it is the one thing about the in-between numbers a learner can rely on.
What a learner cannot rely on is reassembling them. पच्चीस is not पाँच and बीस stuck together in any way you could work out from the pieces — the two halves have worn into each other. Each of the numbers between the rungs is its own word, and they are met one at a time, over a long while.
So: recognise the shape, know the small number is in front, and learn the rungs cold. That is the part that pays.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the eight rungs in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: them backwards, from नब्बे down to बीस]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which end of पच्चीस holds the small number]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many rungs did this chapter add? Which comes first in पच्चीस, the five or the twenty? Can you build twenty-five out of its two pieces? (No — it has to be learned as a word.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
पचास रुपये (pacās rupaye) — a price, said out loud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the word for a rupee. You learned where its name came from — a piece of metal that has been given a shape.

Grammar Lens: put the two together.
पचास रुपये (pacās rupaye) — fifty rupees.
Number first, then the thing. That is the whole construction, and you have both halves already: the number arrived in this chapter and the rupee arrived a long way back.
रुपये rather than रुपया, because more than one is being counted. That bend is the same one the track has been making since the adjectives, and it costs nothing new here.

Grammar Lens: the prices you can now read.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
तीस रुपये means thirty rupees.
चालीस रुपये means forty rupees.
पचास रुपये (pacās rupaye) means fifty rupees.
सत्तर रुपये means seventy rupees.
सौ रुपये means a hundred rupees.
Five prices, and between them most of what a tea stall, a bus fare or a bag of vegetables will ask of you.
This is what the counting was for. A number on its own is a recitation; a number in front of a thing is a price, an age or an address, and that is where the nine rungs stop being a list and start being useful.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: पचास रुपये (pacās rupaye)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: तीस रुपये, चालीस रुपये, सत्तर रुपये, then सौ रुपये]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: why the last word is रुपये rather than रुपया]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — listen: for the number first and the thing second]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you say fifty rupees? Which comes first, the number or the thing? Which form does the rupee take when it is counted?
