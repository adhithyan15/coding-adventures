Lesson 1 of 5.
চামড়া (chāmṛā) — skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an eyebrow, then the Bengali for a bone.

You'll want to know: চামড়া.
চামড়া — chāmṛā — "skin".

The word, taken apart.
চামড়া (chāmṛā) is cām, "skin, leather", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit carman, "hide, skin" (CDIAL 4701), with the ending -ṛā.

Lesson 2 of 5.
জেলে (jele) — a fisherman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a bone, then the Bengali for skin.

You'll want to know: জেলে.
জেলে — jele — "a fisherman".

The word, taken apart.
জেলে (jele) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from jālika, "bird-catcher" (CDIAL 5217). CDIAL writes the Bengali form jāle, "fisherman"; it is built on jāla, "net".

কামার (kāmār) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karmāra, "blacksmith" (CDIAL 2898). CDIAL reports Chatterji's view that the word may be Dravidian.
