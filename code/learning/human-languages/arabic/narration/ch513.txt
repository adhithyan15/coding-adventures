Arabic, chapter 513: Long illnesses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سكري (sukkarī) — diabetes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for good health, then the Arabic for to be in pain.

You'll want to know: سكري.
سكري — sukkarī — "diabetes".
عند جدي مرض السكري. ʿinda jaddī maraḍ as-sukkarī. My grandfather has diabetes.
The full name is مرض السكري, the sugar illness.

The word, taken apart.
سكري (sukkarī) is built on sukkar, "sugar", with the ending -ī, "of": the sugar illness. Sukkar itself is usually traced through Persian to Sanskrit śarkarā.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ربو (rabw) — asthma.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be in pain, then the Arabic for diabetes.

You'll want to know: ربو.
ربو — rabw — "asthma".
يحمل أخي دواء الربو في حقيبته. yaḥmilu akhī dawāʾ ar-rabw fī ḥaqībatihi. My brother carries his asthma medicine in his bag.

The word, taken apart.
ربو (rabw) is built on the native Arabic root r-b-w, "to grow; to be out of breath" (Lane). Lane gives rabw as being out of breath, panting after running.

يعمل في الشركة موظف عنده إعاقة. yaʿmalu fī ash-sharika muwaẓẓaf ʿindahu iʿāqa. An employee with a disability works at the company.

The word, taken apart.
إعاقة (iʿāqa) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-w-q, "to hinder, to hold back" (Lane). Iʿāqa is the verbal noun of Form IV: a hindrance.

ضعف (ḍaʿufa) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-ʿ-f, "to be weak" (Lane). Ḍaʿīf, weak, which you know, is from the same root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
نزف (nazafa) — to bleed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a disability, then the Arabic for to grow weak.

You'll want to know: نزف.
نزف — nazafa — "to bleed".
نزف أنف اللاعب بعد الضربة. nazafa anf al-lāʿib baʿda aḍ-ḍarba. The player's nose bled after the blow.
Present: ينزف, yanzif.
The noun نزيف, bleeding, shares the root.

The word, taken apart.
نزف (nazafa) is built on the native Arabic root n-z-f, "to drain, to exhaust" (Lane). Lane gives nazafa as to drain a well dry; bleeding drains the blood.
