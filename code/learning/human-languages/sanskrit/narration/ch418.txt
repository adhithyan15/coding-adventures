Sanskrit, chapter 418: Nets, Shells and Pearls.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जालम् (jālam) — a net.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dry land, then the Sanskrit for the coastline.

You'll want to know: जालम्.
जालम् — jālam — "a net".
धीवरः नद्यां जालं क्षिपति। dhīvaraḥ nadyāṃ jālaṃ kṣipati. The fisherman casts a net into the river.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
जालम् (jālam) is the word MW gives for a net for catching birds or fish; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
शुक्तिः (śuktiḥ) — an oyster shell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a net, then the Sanskrit for a fishhook.

You'll want to know: शुक्तिः.
शुक्तिः — śuktiḥ — "an oyster shell".
समुद्रस्य तीरे एका शुक्तिः अस्ति। samudrasya tīre ekā śuktiḥ asti. There is a shell on the seashore.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शुक्तिः (śuktiḥ) probably comes from the root √śuc, "to shine", as MW suggests: MW gives śukti as a pearl-oyster or an oyster shell (MW).

शुक्तौ मौक्तिकं जायते। śuktau mauktikaṃ jāyate. A pearl forms inside an oyster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

The word, taken apart.
प्रवालम् (pravālam) probably comes from the root √val, "to turn", as MW suggests: MW gives pravāla as a young shoot and as coral (MW).
