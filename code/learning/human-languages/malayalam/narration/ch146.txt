Lesson 1 of 5.
ആവശ്യം (āvaśyaṁ) — a need.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for an aim, then the Malayalam for a plan.

You'll want to know: ആവശ്യം.
ആവശ്യം — āvaśyaṁ — "a need".

The word, taken apart.
ആവശ്യം (āvaśyaṁ) from Sanskrit āvaśyaka, "necessary", reshaped: Malayalam drops the -ka and adds its noun ending -ṁ.

The word, taken apart.
തീരുമാനം (tīrumānaṁ) is native Dravidian, built on the verb "to be finished, settled"; DEDR 3278 lists Malayalam tīrumānam itself, beside Tamil tīr and Kannada tīrumāna.

Lesson 3 of 5.
കട്ടിൽ (kaṭṭil) — a bed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a need, then the Malayalam for a decision.
[pause 1 second]
How can a word built on rāvŭ, "night," mean "morning"? (Probably "as the night ends" — a likely path, not a proven one.)

You'll want to know: കട്ടിൽ.
കട്ടിൽ — kaṭṭil — "a bed".

The word, taken apart.
കട്ടിൽ (kaṭṭil) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil kaṭṭil and Telugu kaṭli (DEDR 1145). DEDR 1145 compares Sanskrit khaṭvā, "cot" (CDIAL 3781).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ചീപ്പ് (cīppŭ) — a comb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a bed, then the Malayalam for a mirror.
[pause 1 second]
Is the reason rāvile means "morning" settled? (No: it is a plausible path, not a documented one.)

You'll want to know: ചീപ്പ്.
ചീപ്പ് — cīppŭ — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
ചീപ്പ് (cīppŭ) is of uncertain origin. DEDR 2622 lists a Malayalam cīppu, but with the sense "a bolt"; whether the comb is the same word is not known.
