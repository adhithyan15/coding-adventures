5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
だす (dasu) — to take out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to lend, then the Japanese for to give back.

You'll want to know: だす.
だす — dasu — "to take out".

The word, taken apart.
だす (dasu) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ごはん (gohan) — rice; a meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to fix, then the Japanese for to move.

You'll want to know: ごはん.
ごはん — gohan — "rice; a meal".

The word, taken apart.
ごはん (gohan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are go, "honourable; a polite prefix" (Middle Chinese ngjoH) and han, "cooked rice" (bjonH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The polite go- is the on'yomi of the character whose native reading gives o-.
