Italian, chapter 485: Una brutta storta.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
slogarsi — to sprain, to dislocate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to put in a plaster cast, then the Italian for to limp.

You'll want to know: slogarsi.
slogarsi — "to sprain, to dislocate".
Giocando a tennis mi sono slogato la caviglia. Playing tennis I sprained my ankle.
Slogarsi la caviglia — to sprain one's ankle; in speech also prendere una storta.

The word, taken apart.
slogarsi is built from s- "out of" and luogo "place", inherited from Latin locus (Lewis & Short): a joint put out of its place.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: gonfio.
gonfio — "swollen".
Ho la caviglia gonfia. My ankle is swollen.
The feminine is gonfia. The verb is gonfiarsi — to swell.

The word, taken apart.
gonfio is built on gonfiare "to blow up, to swell", inherited from Latin cōnflāre "to blow together" (Lewis & Short), from flāre "to blow".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la garza — gauze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for cramp, then the Italian for swollen.

You'll want to know: la garza.
la garza — "gauze".
Ho messo una garza pulita sulla ferita. I put a clean piece of gauze on the wound.
A thin cloth for wounds; the roll that holds it is la benda.

The word, taken apart.
garza comes from French gaze "gauze" (EtymDB), traditionally linked to a town on the eastern Mediterranean where the cloth was made.
