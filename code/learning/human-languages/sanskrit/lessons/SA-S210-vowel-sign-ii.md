# ◌ी — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-107, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-209, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-108] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one, the ones you have already met: त · ◌ं · स. Say what each of them does.

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. One only — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ी
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-210]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ी** — *ī*.

It is a **vowel sign**. It is not a letter and never stands alone: it attaches to a consonant and **replaces** the *a* built into it. Replaces, not adds — the *a* is gone.

Where it sits: a curl to the right with a tail over the top bar.

Worked through: **न** + **◌ी** = **नी** — *nī*.

You already say these, and every one of them has ◌ी somewhere inside it:

- **पानीयम्** *pānīyam* — water — "the thing to be drunk," and the exact word your neighbors' पानी grew from

## Writing: ◌ी — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-210] -->

Put your pen on ◌ी and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
