## Grammar Lens: a root you own predicts how a new word behaves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-05, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-03] -->

Two of these words turned out to be built on roots you had already, from
somewhere else entirely.

| new word | the word you already had | what the shared root buys |
|---|---|---|
| responsabilidad | **el esposo** | the promise inside *responsable* |
| repetir | **el pedido** | the **e→i** stem change |

*Repetir* is the sharper of the two. *Pedir* changes *pido*, *pides*, *pide*;
*repetir* changes *repito*, *repites*, *repite*. Nobody has to tell you that,
because they are one Latin verb with a prefix on one of them.

That is the return on etymology here — not that the stories are pleasant, but
that a root you own tells you how an unfamiliar word will behave before anybody
explains it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-01, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-03, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-04] -->

**You can now read a job advert and say what the work asks of you** — what it
demands, what you are answerable for, what you would be teaching or shown, and
when the person you are replacing is leaving.

You also have two endings that keep paying. **-idad** turns an adjective into
its quality and is regular, so *posible* gives *posibilidad*. And **-ente** or
**-ante** marks *the one doing the verb*, so an unfamiliar word in that shape
has a verb inside it worth looking for.
