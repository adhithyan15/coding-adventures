Bengali, chapter 71: Bear, Donkey, Butterfly, Ant, Hen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ভালুক (bhāluk) — a bear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a snake, then the Bengali for a crow.

You'll want to know: ভালুক.
ভালুক — bhāluk — "a bear".

The word, taken apart.
ভালুক (bhāluk) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bhallukka, "bear", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9415). It stands beside Sanskrit bhallūka, "bear", whose source is disputed: CDIAL reports both an Indo-European origin and Przyluski's Austroasiatic one.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a bear, then the Bengali for a donkey.

You'll want to know: প্রজাপতি.
প্রজাপতি — prôjāpôti — "a butterfly".

The word, taken apart.
প্রজাপতি (prôjāpôti) is Sanskrit prajāpati, "lord of creatures", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama. Bengali uses the creator god's name for the butterfly.
