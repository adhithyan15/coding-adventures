Lesson 1 of 6.
le moule — the baking tin, the mould.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to simmer, then the French for the casserole dish.

You'll want to know: le moule.
le moule — "the baking tin, the mould".
Elle verse la pâte dans le moule. She pours the mixture into the tin.
Masculine; la moule is a mussel.

The word, taken apart.
moule comes from Old French modle, inherited from Latin modulus "a small measure" (EtymDB), built on modus "measure".

Lesson 3 of 6.
la cuisson — the cooking, the cooking time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the baking tin, then the French for to put in the oven.

You'll want to know: la cuisson.
la cuisson — "the cooking, the cooking time".
La cuisson du gâteau dure quarante minutes. The cake takes forty minutes to cook.
For meat: quelle cuisson? — how would you like it cooked?

The word, taken apart.
cuisson is usually traced to Latin coctiō "a cooking", from coquere "to cook" (Lewis & Short), reshaped after cuire.

Le poulet dore lentement au four. The chicken is slowly turning golden in the oven.
Faire dorer les oignons — to brown the onions.

dorer is inherited through Old French dorer (EtymDB) from Latin deaurāre "to gild", built on aurum "gold" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
gratiner — to brown on top, to cook with a crust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cooking, then the French for to turn golden.

You'll want to know: gratiner.
gratiner — "to brown on top, to cook with a crust".
Elle fait gratiner les pâtes avec du fromage. She browns the pasta with cheese on top.
Un gratin — a dish browned in the oven, often with cheese.

The word, taken apart.
gratiner is built on gratin, the crust scraped from the dish, from the verb meaning "to scrape", which is usually traced to a Frankish word (EtymDB lists no parent).
