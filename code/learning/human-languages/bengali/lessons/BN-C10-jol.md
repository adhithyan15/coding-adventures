# জল (jôl) — "water," prised out of a phrase you already know

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C07-KHAOWA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter 7 taught you **জল খাওয়া**, "to drink water" — literally
"water-eat" — without ever teaching জল by itself. Here it is, on its own.

## You'll want to know: জল
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C10-JOL-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **জল** — *jôl* — **water**

**জল খান** — (please) drink some water, the same offer pattern as last lesson.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**জল** is **জ** (*jô*) + **ল** (*l*), two bare consonants with no vowel sign
between them. **জ** keeps its inherent vowel; **ল**, at the end of the word
with nothing written after it, is silent — so *jôl*, one syllable, not
*jô-lô*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**জল** is Sanskrit **জল** (*jala*), inherited straight through Prakrit. Past
Sanskrit its root is genuinely unsettled: one proposal traces it to PIE
**\*gʷelHo-**, "to gush forth, to spring" — but the same sources call a local,
non-Indo-European origin "unlikely though possible." No confident cousin is
claimed here; the honest answer is that nobody is sure.

Bengali has a second word for water, most common in Bangladesh: **পানি**
(*pani*). That one *is* on secure ground — Sanskrit **pānīya**, "drinkable,"
built directly on **√pā**, "to drink," the very root behind Chapter 7's
**পান করা** and English **potion**, **potable**. Two words for the same
thing, one of disputed origin and one you can trace all the way home.
