Lesson 1 of 5.
સંભાળવું (sambhāḷvũ) — to look after, to take care of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to persuade, then the Gujarati for advice.

You'll want to know: સંભાળવું.
સંભાળવું — sambhāḷvũ — "to look after, to take care of".
શું તમે તમારી દાદીને સંભાળો છો? — śũ tame tamārī dādīne sambhāḷo cho? — "Do you look after your grandmother?"
Not the taught સાંભળવું, sā̃bhaḷvũ, "to hear".

The word, taken apart.
સંભાળવું (sambhāḷvũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to guard, take care of", under Sanskrit sáṁbharati, "brings together", and its causative saṁbhārayati, "maintains" (CDIAL 12961).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉછેરવું (uchervũ) — to bring up, to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for advice, then the Gujarati for to look after.

You'll want to know: ઉછેરવું.
ઉછેરવું — uchervũ — "to bring up, to raise".
શું તમારી દાદીએ તમને ઉછેર્યા? — śũ tamārī dādīe tamne ucheryā? — "Did your grandmother bring you up?"

The word, taken apart.
ઉછેરવું (uchervũ) is inherited, the transitive partner of ucharvũ, "to be brought up". CDIAL derives ucharvũ from ut-śrīyatē, Sanskrit úcchrīyatē, "is raised" (CDIAL 1864).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to look after, then the Gujarati for to bring up.

You'll want to know: વખાણવું.
વખાણવું — vakhāṇvũ — "to praise".
સાહેબ ક્યારેય કોઈને નથી વખાણતા. — sāheb kyārey koīne nathī vakhāṇtā — "The boss never praises anyone."

The word, taken apart.
વખાણવું (vakhāṇvũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL derives it from Sanskrit vyākhyā́na, "comment, narration", by way of the noun vakhāṇ, "praise" (CDIAL 12188).

Lesson 4 of 5.
યાદ આવવી (yād āvvī) — to remember fondly, to miss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to bring up, then the Gujarati for to praise.

You'll want to know: યાદ આવવી.
યાદ આવવી — yād āvvī — "to remember fondly, to miss".
શું તમને ઘરની યાદ આવે છે? — śũ tamne gharnī yād āve che? — "Do you miss home?"
The one who misses takes -ને: મને તારી યાદ આવે છે, mane tārī yād āve che, "I miss you".

The word, taken apart.
યાદ આવવી (yād āvvī), for a memory to come, is built from yād, "memory", a loan usually traced to Persian yād, and the verb āvvī, "to come".
