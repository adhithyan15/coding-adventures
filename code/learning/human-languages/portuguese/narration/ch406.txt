Portuguese, chapter 406: A avaria.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a avaria — the breakdown, the fault.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the wheel rim, then the Portuguese for the warning triangle.

You'll want to know: a avaria.
a avaria — "the breakdown, the fault".
O carro teve uma avaria a meio da viagem. The car broke down halfway through the trip.
Estar avariado — to be out of order. In Brazil: o defeito, a pane.

The word, taken apart.
avaria came through Italian avaria from Arabic ʿawāriyya 'damaged goods', built on ʿawār 'fault, flaw' (EtymDB). It began as a word for damage to a ship's cargo.

Lesson 3 of 5.
rebocar — to tow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the breakdown, then the Portuguese for to break down.

You'll want to know: rebocar.
rebocar — "to tow".
Um camião rebocou o carro até à cidade. A truck towed the car to the town.
Rebocar also means to plaster a wall, a different word.

The word, taken apart.
rebocar 'to tow' is inherited from Vulgar Latin remulcāre, built on Latin remulcum 'towrope' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to tow, then the Portuguese for the tow truck.

You'll want to know: a oficina.
a oficina — "the repair garage; the workshop".
O mecânico da oficina disse que demora dois dias. The mechanic at the garage said it takes two days.
A garage to park in is a garagem; repairs happen at a oficina.

The word, taken apart.
oficina is a learned borrowing from Latin officīna 'workshop' (EtymDB), related to opifex 'worker', built on opus 'work'.
