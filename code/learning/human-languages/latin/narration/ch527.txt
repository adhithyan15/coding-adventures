Latin, chapter 527: Rest and sleep.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
requiēscō, requiēscere — to rest, take a rest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to refresh, then the Latin for a draught.

You'll want to know: requiēscō, requiēscere.
requiēscō, requiēscere — "to rest, take a rest".
Post labōrem in hortō requiēscimus. After work we rest in the garden.
requiēscō, requiēscere, requiēvī, requiētum. On tombs: requiēscat in pāce — may he rest in peace.

The word, taken apart.
requiēscō is built on re- and quiēscō "to rest" (Lewis & Short), from quiēs "quiet".

Lesson 2 of 5.
requiēs, requiētis — rest, repose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a draught, then the Latin for to rest.

You'll want to know: requiēs, requiētis.
requiēs, requiētis — "rest, repose".
Nautae nūllam requiem habent. The sailors have no rest.
Feminine, third declension; accusative requiem. English requiem, from the first word of a prayer for the dead.

The word, taken apart.
requiēs is built on re- and quiēs "rest, quiet" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which EtymDB traces to a Proto-Indo-European word for "rest". English requiem comes from its accusative.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dormītō, dormītāre — to doze, be sleepy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to rest, then the Latin for rest.

You'll want to know: dormītō, dormītāre.
dormītō, dormītāre — "to doze, be sleepy".
Avus post prandium in sellā dormītat. Grandfather dozes in his chair after lunch.
dormītō, dormītāre, dormītāvī: the frequentative of dormiō.

The word, taken apart.
dormītō is the frequentative of dormiō "to sleep" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English dormitory comes from dormītōrium, built on the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
īnsomnia, īnsomniae — sleeplessness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rest, then the Latin for to doze.

You'll want to know: īnsomnia, īnsomniae.
īnsomnia, īnsomniae — "sleeplessness".
Propter cūrās īnsomniā labōrō. Because of my worries I suffer from sleeplessness.
Feminine, first declension, from īnsomnis — sleepless. English insomnia.

The word, taken apart.
īnsomnia is built on īnsomnis "sleepless", from in- "not" and somnus "sleep" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English insomnia comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
recumbō, recumbere — to recline, lie down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

recumbō, recumbere — "to recline, lie down".
Hospitēs ad cēnam recumbunt. The guests recline at dinner.
recumbō, recumbere, recubuī. Romans lay on couches to eat. English recumbent.

The word, taken apart.
recumbō is built on re- "back" and -cumbō, a form of cubō "to lie down" (Lewis & Short). English recumbent comes from it.
