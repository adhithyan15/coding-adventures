5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'insonnia — insomnia, sleeplessness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for infection, then the Italian for to recover.

You'll want to know: l'insonnia.
l'insonnia — "insomnia, sleeplessness".
Quando sono stressato soffro d'insonnia. When I am stressed I suffer from insomnia.
Soffrire d'insonnia — to suffer from insomnia.

The word, taken apart.
insonnia is a learned borrowing from Latin insomnia "sleeplessness" (EtymDB), from in- "not" and somnus "sleep".

l'incubo — "nightmare".
Stanotte ho fatto un incubo terribile. Last night I had a terrible nightmare.
Fare un incubo — to have a nightmare. Also a bad situation: è stato un incubo.

The word, taken apart.
incubo comes from Late Latin incubus "a spirit that lies on the sleeper" (EtymDB), from incubāre "to lie upon" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
sbadigliare — to yawn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to snore, then the Italian for nightmare.

You'll want to know: sbadigliare.
sbadigliare — "to yawn".
Lo spettacolo era così noioso che tutti sbadigliavano. The show was so boring that everyone was yawning.
The noun is lo sbadiglio.

The word, taken apart.
sbadigliare is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin verb bataclāre "to gape", with s- in front, a form usually linked to batāre "to gape"; EtymDB lists it beside French bâiller and Occitan badalhar, which come from bataclāre.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sgranchirsi — to stretch (one's legs).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for nightmare, then the Italian for to yawn.

You'll want to know: sgranchirsi.
sgranchirsi — "to stretch (one's legs)".
Dopo tre ore in macchina scendo a sgranchirmi le gambe. After three hours in the car I get out to stretch my legs.
Sgranchirsi le gambe — to stretch one's legs.

The word, taken apart.
sgranchirsi is built from s- and granchio "crab, cramp", usually traced to Latin cancer "a crab" (Lewis & Short): to work the cramp out.
