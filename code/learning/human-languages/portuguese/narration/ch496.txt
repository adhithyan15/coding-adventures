Portuguese, chapter 496: Ternura e carinho.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a ternura — tenderness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for (monthly) pocket money, then the Portuguese for spoiled.

You'll want to know: a ternura.
a ternura — "tenderness".
A avó olhava para o neto com ternura. Grandma was looking at her grandson with tenderness.
Built on terno "tender".

The word, taken apart.
ternura is built on terno "tender", which is inherited from Latin tener "soft, tender" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

carinhoso is built on carinho "affection", which is usually traced to Spanish cariño (EtymDB); the deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: acariciar.
acariciar — "to stroke, to caress".
A menina acariciava o gato. The girl was stroking the cat.
A caress is uma carícia.

The word, taken apart.
acariciar is built on carícia "a caress", from Italian carezza, which goes back to Latin cārus "dear" (Lewis & Short).
