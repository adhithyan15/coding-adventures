Lesson 1 of 6.
مثال (misāl) — an example.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to own up to, then the Urdu for a misunderstanding.

You'll want to know: مثال.
مثال — misāl — "an example".
ایک مثال دیجیے۔ ek misāl dījiye. Give an example, please.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
مثال (misāl) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic mithāl, "a likeness, a model".

Lesson 4 of 6.
گنانا (ginānā) — to list; to enumerate (reasons, faults).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for that is to say, then the Urdu for clear in meaning.

You'll want to know: گنانا.
گنانا — ginānā — "to list; to enumerate (reasons, faults)".
اس نے تین سبب گنائے۔ us ne tīn sabab gināe. He listed three causes.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے سبب گنائے, us ne sabab gināe.

The word, taken apart.
گنانا (ginānā) is the causative of گننا, "to count", which is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin ginnā under Sanskrit gaṇáyati, "counts" (CDIAL 3993). To count things out one by one.

Lesson 5 of 6.
حوالہ (havālā) — a reference (to a source).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for clear in meaning, then the Urdu for to enumerate.

You'll want to know: حوالہ.
حوالہ — havālā — "a reference (to a source)".
اپنی بات کا حوالہ دیجیے۔ apnī bāt kā havālā dījiye. Give a source for your point, please.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
حوالہ (havālā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḥawāla, "a handing over"; Urdu uses it for pointing a reader on to a source, a sense our sources do not trace step by step.
