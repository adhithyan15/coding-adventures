Lesson 1 of 5.
むこう (mukou) — the other side.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for beside, then the Japanese for the very middle.

You'll want to know: むこう.
むこう — mukou — "the other side".

The word, taken apart.
むこう (mukou) (mukō) is native, from mukau, "to face, to head towards" (JMdict writes both with the same character). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

The word, taken apart.
てまえ (temae) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: te, "hand", and mae, "front": what is in front of your hand (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the other side, then the Japanese for this side of.

You'll want to know: かど.
かど — kado — "a corner".

The word, taken apart.
かど (kado) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かいだん (kaidan) — stairs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for this side of, then the Japanese for a corner.

You'll want to know: かいだん.
かいだん — kaidan — "stairs".

The word, taken apart.
かいだん (kaidan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "stairs, storey" (Middle Chinese keaj) and dan, "step, section" (dwanH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
