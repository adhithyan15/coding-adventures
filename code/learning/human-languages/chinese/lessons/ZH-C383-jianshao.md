## You'll want to know: jiǎnshǎo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C383-ENERGY383-04]; assesses=[] -->

*jiǎnshǎo* — "to reduce; to cut down".

> *zuò dìtiě kěyǐ jiǎnshǎo dǔchē* — Taking the underground can cut down on traffic jams.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: jiǎn is said with a rising tone before shǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jiǎnshǎo* is built from *jiǎn* 'to lessen' and 少 'few', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *keamX* and *syewX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to diminish' and the second 'not many'.
