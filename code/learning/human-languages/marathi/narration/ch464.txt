Marathi, chapter 464: Courage and Grit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हिंमत (himmat) — nerve; courage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to feel ashamed, then the Marathi for to feel sheepish.

You'll want to know: हिंमत.
हिंमत — himmat — "nerve; courage".
रात्री एकट्याने जायची त्याची हिंमत झाली नाही. rātrī ekṭyāne jāycī tyācī himmat jhālī nāhī. He did not have the nerve to go alone at night.
Feminine.
हिंमत (himmat) होणे, to dare, with the genitive: माझी हिंमत (himmat) झाली. हिंमत (himmat) हरणे, to lose heart.

The word, taken apart.
हिंमत (himmat) is a loan from Persian himmat, usually traced to Arabic himma, "resolve".

Lesson 5 of 5.
तग धरणे (tag dharṇe) — to hold out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for perseverance, then the Marathi for zest.

You'll want to know: तग धरणे.
तग धरणे — tag dharṇe — "to hold out".
दुष्काळातही हे झाड तग धरून उभं आहे. duṣkāḷātahī he jhāḍ tag dharūn ubhaṁ āhe. Even in the drought this tree has held out.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने तग धरला, tyāne tag dharlā.
To hold one's ground through hardship: तग धरून राहणे.

The word, taken apart.
तग धरणे (tag dharṇe) is a Marathi phrase built on तग, from the reconstructed tagg, "endure", under which CDIAL lists Marathi tagṇẽ, "to last" (CDIAL 5625), with धरणे, "to hold".
