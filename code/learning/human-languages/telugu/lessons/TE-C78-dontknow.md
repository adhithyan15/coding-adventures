# నాకు తెలియదు (nāku teliyadu) — I do not know

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: what did *telusā* mean?

## You'll want to know: నాకు తెలియదు
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C78-YESNO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**తెలియదు** (*teliyadu*) — "it is not known", which is Telugu for "I don't know".

This is the answer to the last word, and it is probably the sentence you will need most often of anything in this book.

It is not తెలుసు with లేదు after it. Telugu negates an ordinary verb from the inside, with **-అదు** on the stem: తెలియ- + -దు.

## Grammar Lens: how Telugu says a verb did not happen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C78-YESNO-03]; assesses=[] -->

The two negatives of your first lesson, లేదు and కాదు, deny **existence** and **identity**. An ordinary action is denied differently — with an ending on the verb itself: వెళ్తాను "I go" becomes **వెళ్ళను** "I don't go"; వస్తాను becomes **రాను**. The ending carries the negative and no separate word is used. తెలియదు is that pattern on తెలియ-.
