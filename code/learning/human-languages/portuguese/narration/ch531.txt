Portuguese, chapter 531: Pequenos arranjos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
consertar — to repair, to fix.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for pantry, then the Portuguese for damp.

You'll want to know: consertar.
consertar — "to repair, to fix".
O meu pai consertou a cadeira partida. My father fixed the broken chair.
Not concertar, which means to arrange or to agree.

The word, taken apart.
consertar is usually traced to Latin cōnsertus "joined together", from cōnserere "to join" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
pendurar — to hang (up).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for damp, then the Portuguese for to repair.

You'll want to know: pendurar.
pendurar — "to hang (up)".
Pendurei o quadro na parede da sala. I hung the picture on the living-room wall.
Pendurar o casaco — to hang up your coat.

The word, taken apart.
pendurar is already found in Old Portuguese (EtymDB) and is usually traced to Latin pendēre "to hang" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o berbequim — (electric) drill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to hang, then the Portuguese for to hammer.

You'll want to know: o berbequim.
o berbequim — "(electric) drill".
Fez um furo na parede com o berbequim. He drilled a hole in the wall with the drill.
In Brazil: a furadeira.

The word, taken apart.
berbequim is usually traced to French vilebrequin, a hand drill; the origin of the French word is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: o alicate.
o alicate — "pliers".
Usou o alicate para tirar o prego. He used the pliers to pull out the nail.
One tool in Portuguese: um alicate.

The word, taken apart.
alicate comes from Arabic laqqāṭ "pincers" (EtymDB), with the Arabic article al-.
