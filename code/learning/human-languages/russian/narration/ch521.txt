Russian, chapter 521: Brush and Canvas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
кисть (kist) — a paintbrush; the hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a viewer, then the Russian for to act in a film.

You'll want to know: кисть.
кисть (kist) — "a paintbrush; the hand".
Художник взял тонкую кисть. Khudózhnik vzyal tónkuyu kist. The artist picked up a fine brush.
Feminine. Also the hand from the wrist down, and a bunch of grapes.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB lists кисть (kist), glossed 'cluster, bunch', without a source, and the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
акварель (akvarél) — watercolour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a paintbrush, then the Russian for canvas.

You'll want to know: акварель.
акварель (akvarél) — "watercolour".
Она рисует акварелью. Oná risúyet akvarélyu. She paints in watercolour.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
акварель (akvarél) was borrowed from French aquarelle (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Italian acquerello, built on acqua 'water', from Latin aqua.

Lesson 5 of 5.
позировать (pozírovat) — to pose, to sit (for an artist).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for watercolour, then the Russian for a landscape.

You'll want to know: позировать.
позировать (pozírovat) — "to pose, to sit (for an artist)".
Сестра позировала художнику три часа. Sestrá pozírovala khudózhniku tri chasá. My sister sat for the artist for three hours.
Imperfective; present позирую. Takes the dative.

The word, taken apart.
позировать (pozírovat) is built on поза 'a pose', which EtymDB traces through German Pose to French pose, with -ировать; EtymDB has no entry for the verb.
