Mandarin Chinese, chapter 64: Asking for Help.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
对不起 (duìbuqǐ) — sorry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for identifying number, then the Mandarin for to look for.

You'll want to know: 对不起.
对不起 — duìbuqǐ — "sorry".
duìbuqǐ wǒ bù dǒng nǐ de huà — Sorry, I don't understand what you're saying.
The new character, in pieces: 起 (qǐ) — the walking piece read zǒu along the left and the bottom, carrying a three-stroke piece read jǐ, 'self', on its back: not the jǐ of 机. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
Its middle syllable loses its tone: duìbuqǐ, with bu light between two full syllables.

The word, taken apart.
对不起 (duìbuqǐ) is built from 对 'to answer, to match', 不 'not' and 起 (qǐ) 'to rise', all inherited from Middle Chinese, twojH, pjut and khiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'Not able to face you' is the usual explanation, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

Lesson 2 of 6.
起 (qǐ) — the walking piece, carrying jǐ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the walking piece, carrying jǐ and Guided Practice.

Script — the walking piece, carrying jǐ.
起 (qǐ).
Two pieces: the walking piece first, then the piece on its back.
The walking piece, seven strokes, read zǒu, as in the word for to walk: a short horizontal, a vertical down through it, a longer horizontal; then below, a short vertical with a short bar to its right, a left-falling stroke, and last a long stroke that sweeps right along the whole bottom.
The right piece, three strokes, read jǐ, 'self', not the jǐ of 机, sitting on the sweep: a horizontal that turns down, a short horizontal, then a vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up.
7 + 3 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 起 (qǐ), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 起 (qǐ)? (Two — the walking piece and jǐ.) Which stroke runs under the whole character? (The long sweep, the seventh.) Which character comes before it in 对不起 (duìbuqǐ)? (不.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
bāng — to help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to look for, then the Mandarin for sorry.

You'll want to know: bāng.
bāng — "to help".
nǐ bāng wǒ xiě hǎo bu hǎo — Will you help me write it?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāng is inherited from Middle Chinese pang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to mend shoes'. The sense 'to help' is not given there, so how it got it is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
bāngmáng — to help out; to do a favour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sorry, then the Mandarin for to help.

You'll want to know: bāngmáng.
bāngmáng — "to help out; to do a favour".
wǒ xiǎng bāngmáng — I'd like to help.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāngmáng is built from bāng 'to help' and máng 'busy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pang and mang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses mang as 'afraid', so the sense busy is not given there; helping with what keeps somebody busy is the likely picture, but it is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
huán — to give back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do a favour, then the Mandarin for to borrow.

You'll want to know: huán.
huán — "to give back".
wǒ míngtiān huán nǐ — I'll give it back tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
The same character is read hái in hái xíng; here, giving something back, it is huán.

The word, taken apart.
huán is inherited from Middle Chinese hwaen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to return' and 'to go back'.
