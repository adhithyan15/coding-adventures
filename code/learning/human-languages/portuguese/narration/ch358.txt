Lesson 1 of 6.
o funcionário — the employee, the official, the clerk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to employ, then the Portuguese for the employer.

You'll want to know: o funcionário.
o funcionário — "the employee, the official, the clerk".
O funcionário do banco foi muito simpático. The bank clerk was very nice.
Feminine a funcionária. Funcionário público — civil servant.

The word, taken apart.
funcionário was modelled on French fonctionnaire (EtymDB), built on função "function", from Latin fūnctiō "a performing, an execution" (Lewis & Short).

sindicato is built on síndico "representative", from Latin syndicus, from Greek súndikos "advocate"; French syndicat "trade union", from Medieval Latin syndicātus, shaped the modern sense (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 6.
a greve — the strike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the shift, then the Portuguese for the trade union.

You'll want to know: a greve.
a greve — "the strike".
Os comboios não circulam hoje por causa da greve. The trains are not running today because of the strike.
Fazer greve — to go on strike; estar em greve — to be on strike.

The word, taken apart.
greve was borrowed from French grève, which first meant "a sandy riverbank"; the sense "strike" is usually explained by a riverside square in Paris where men out of work gathered. EtymDB lists the Portuguese word but gives no source for it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
reivindicar — to lay claim to, to demand (as a right).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the trade union, then the Portuguese for the strike.

You'll want to know: reivindicar.
reivindicar — "to lay claim to, to demand (as a right)".
Os trabalhadores reivindicam salários mais altos. The workers are demanding higher wages.
Formal, of rights and pay; in speech pedir.

The word, taken apart.
reivindicar is formed from Latin rēs, reī "thing" and vindicāre "to lay claim to, to avenge" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): to claim a thing as yours.
