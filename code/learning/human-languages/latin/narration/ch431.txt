Latin, chapter 431: Rein, Leather strap, Bridle, To bridle, Whip.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
habēna, habēnae — a rein; a strap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to yoke, then the Latin for to unyoke.

You'll want to know: habēna, habēnae.
habēna, habēnae — "a rein; a strap".
Raedārius habēnās tenet et equī lentē eunt. The coachman holds the reins and the horses go slowly.
First declension, feminine: habēna, habēnae f., mostly plural: habēnās dare, to give a horse its head.

The word, taken apart.
habēna is built on habeō 'to have, to hold' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): the strap by which a horse is held.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lōrum, lōrī — a leather strap, a thong; (plural) reins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to unyoke, then the Latin for a rein.

You'll want to know: lōrum, lōrī.
lōrum, lōrī — "a leather strap, a thong; (plural) reins".
Servus sarcinās lōrīs ad mūlum ligat. The slave ties the bags to the mule with straps.
Second declension, neuter: lōrum, lōrī n. A strap of leather for tying, for reins, or for whipping.

The word, taken apart.
lōrum is inherited. Lewis & Short compare Greek eulēra 'reins' and suggest an older form vlōrum; EtymDB gives no parent, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
frēnum, frēnī — a bridle, a bit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rein, then the Latin for a leather strap.

You'll want to know: frēnum, frēnī.
frēnum, frēnī — "a bridle, a bit".
Equus ferōx frēnum nōn amat. A wild horse does not like the bridle.
Second declension, neuter: frēnum, frēnī n.; the plural is often frēnī m. Also spelled fraenum. English refrain 'to hold back' comes from refrēnāre 'to bridle back'.

The word, taken apart.
frēnum is inherited and is usually traced to PIE dʰer- 'to hold, to support' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English refrain 'to hold back' comes from Latin refrēnāre, built on it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
frēnō, frēnāre — to bridle; to hold back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a leather strap, then the Latin for a bridle.

You'll want to know: frēnō, frēnāre.
frēnō, frēnāre — "to bridle; to hold back".
Puer equum frēnat, deinde ascendit. The boy bridles the horse, then gets on.
frēnō, frēnāre, frēnāvī, frēnātum. Also figurative: īram frēnāre, to hold back anger.

The word, taken apart.
frēnō is built on frēnum 'bridle' (Lewis & Short), a regular first-conjugation verb made from the noun.

Lesson 5 of 5.
scutica, scuticae — a whip, a lash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bridle, then the Latin for to bridle.

You'll want to know: scutica, scuticae.
scutica, scuticae — "a whip, a lash".
Raedārius scuticam habet, sed equōs nōn tangit. The coachman has a whip, but he does not touch the horses.
First declension, feminine: scutica, scuticae f. A strap whip of leather.

The word, taken apart.
scutica was borrowed from Greek; EtymDB traces it to skytos 'leather, hide'. Lewis & Short gloss it as a lash or whip but give no origin.
