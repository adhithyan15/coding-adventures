Arabic, chapter 358: Arguments and proof.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حجة (ḥujja) — an argument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sentence, then the Arabic for a barber.

You'll want to know: حجة.
حجة — ḥujja — "an argument".
قدم الطالب حجة قوية في النقاش. qaddama aṭ-ṭālib ḥujja qawiyya fī an-niqāsh. The student put forward a strong argument in the discussion.
Plural: حجج, ḥujaj.

The word, taken apart.
حجة (ḥujja) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-j-j, "to make for; to overcome in argument" (Lane). A ḥujja is what wins a dispute: an argument, or a proof.

مبدأ — mabdaʾ — "a principle".
الصدق مبدأ مهم عند أبي. aṣ-ṣidq mabdaʾ muhimm ʿinda abī. Honesty is an important principle for my father.

The word, taken apart.
مبدأ (mabdaʾ) is built on the native Arabic root b-d-ʾ, "to begin" (Lane). Mabdaʾ is a noun of place, a starting point: the idea everything else starts from.

Lesson 3 of 5.
برهان (burhān) — a proof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an argument, then the Arabic for a principle.

You'll want to know: برهان.
برهان — burhān — "a proof".
هذه الصورة برهان على كلامه. hādhihi aṣ-ṣūra burhān ʿalā kalāmih. This photo is proof of what he said.
Plural: براهين, barāhīn.
It takes على: برهان (burhān) على …, proof of ….

The word, taken apart.
برهان (burhān) is a native Arabic word that Lane files under b-r-h-n, "to bring clear proof" (Lane). Some early grammarians built it on a three-letter root instead, so the shape of its root is disputed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أثبت (athbata) — to prove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a principle, then the Arabic for a proof.

You'll want to know: أثبت.
أثبت — athbata — "to prove".
أثبت المحامي أن الرجل كان في المنزل. athbata al-muḥāmī anna ar-rajul kāna fī al-manzil. The lawyer proved that the man had been at home.
Present: يثبت, yuthbit.
Followed by أن: أثبت (athbata) أن …, proved that ….

The word, taken apart.
أثبت (athbata) is built on the native Arabic root th-b-t, "to be firm, to stand fast" (Lane). It is Form IV, to make firm: to establish a thing, to prove it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
أقنع (aqnaʿa) — to convince.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a proof, then the Arabic for to prove.

You'll want to know: أقنع.
أقنع — aqnaʿa — "to convince".
أقنعت أختي أبي بشراء سيارة جديدة. aqnaʿat ukhtī abī bi-shirāʾ sayyāra jadīda. My sister convinced my father to buy a new car.
Present: يقنع, yuqniʿ.
What you convince someone of comes after بـ: أقنعه بـ ….

The word, taken apart.
أقنع (aqnaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root q-n-ʿ, "to be content, satisfied" (Lane). It is Form IV, to make content: to bring someone round to an idea.
