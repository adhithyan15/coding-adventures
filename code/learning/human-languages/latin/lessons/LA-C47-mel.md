## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C47-TABLE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**mel** — "**honey**."

Proto-Indo-European ***\*melit-***, and it is one of those roots that turns up in
languages that otherwise share very little — Greek *meli*, Hittite *milit*, and
Latin *mel*, all honey.

Something **mellifluous** is *mel* plus *fluere*, **flowing with honey** — used
of a voice. **Mellow** is probably from the same family, though the road is not
clean.

Two food words came in through Portuguese, which is why they look nothing alike.
**Marmalade** is *marmelada*, a quince paste — Portuguese *marmelo*, "quince,"
from Greek *melimēlon*, a **honey-apple**. And **molasses** is Portuguese
*melaço*, the honey-like residue left when sugar is refined.

English **honey** itself is Germanic and comes from a root meaning "golden."
Unrelated — a different language naming the same substance by a different
property.
