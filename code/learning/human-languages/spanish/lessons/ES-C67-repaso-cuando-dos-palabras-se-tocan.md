# Review — what happens where two words touch

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-UNO-APOCOPE, ES-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-APOCOPE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "One book." (*Un libro*.) "The first book." (*El primer libro*.)
Neither word is whole.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-UNO-APOCOPE, ES-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-APOCOPE, ES-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-BEFORE-NOUN, ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE, ES-LEX-BASTANTE] -->

**One: the word bites itself short.**

*Uno* → *un libro*. *Primero* → *primer libro*. *Mucho* → *muy*. *Malo* → *mal*.

[PAUSE 2s] And the biting has a condition: it happens **in front of the noun**
and nowhere else. Say *el libro primero* and the *o* comes straight back. That
position is also where an ordinal lives in the first place — *el primer libro*,
not *el libro primer*.

[PAUSE 2s] Your scale of degree is built out of exactly this. *Poco*,
***bastante***, *muy* — and the top of it, *muy*, is *mucho* with the end
chewed off.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-DEL, ES-GRAMMAR-CONTRACTION-ONLY-TWO, ES-GRAMMAR-O-BECOMES-U, ES-LEX-CON, ES-LEX-CONMIGO] -->

**Two: the two words fuse.**

> *a + el* → ***al***.  ·  *de + el* → ***del***.

And then the list stops. Two obligatory contractions, no others — a rare thing
to be able to say about any grammar.

[PAUSE 2s] **Three: the word changes shape to stay audible.** *O* is a single
letter, and in front of a word starting with *o-* a single letter would vanish.
So it becomes *u*: *siete **u** ocho*.

[PAUSE 2s] And the one that went furthest. Latin put *cum* on the **end** of the
pronoun — *mēcum*, "with me." Spanish stopped hearing that ending as a word and
put ***con*** back on the front. **Conmigo** therefore says *with* twice, and it
has been getting away with it for a thousand years.
