Malayalam, chapter 513: After the Storm.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ക്യാമ്പ് (kyāmpŭ) — a relief camp; a camp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to go under, then the Malayalam for to divert.

You'll want to know: ക്യാമ്പ്.
ക്യാമ്പ് — kyāmpŭ — "a relief camp; a camp".
വെള്ളപ്പൊക്കത്തിൽ ആയിരം പേർ ക്യാമ്പിൽ താമസിച്ചു. veḷḷappokkattil āyiraṁ pēr kyāmpil tāmasiccu. In the flood a thousand people stayed in the relief camp.
Plural: ക്യാമ്പുകൾ, kyāmpukaḷ.
Schools often become ക്യാമ്പുകൾ during a flood.

The word, taken apart.
ക്യാമ്പ് (kyāmpŭ) was borrowed from English camp, a place of tents or shelters.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ദുരിതാശ്വാസം (duritāśvāsaṁ) — disaster relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to divert, then the Malayalam for a relief camp.

You'll want to know: ദുരിതാശ്വാസം.
ദുരിതാശ്വാസം — duritāśvāsaṁ — "disaster relief".
നാട്ടുകാർ ദുരിതാശ്വാസത്തിനായി പണം നൽകി. nāṭṭukār duritāśvāsattināyi paṇaṁ nalki. The local people gave money for disaster relief.
ദുരിതം, distress, plus ആശ്വാസം, relief.

The word, taken apart.
ദുരിതാശ്വാസം (duritāśvāsaṁ) is a Malayalam compound built from two Sanskrit words, durita, "difficulty, distress" (Monier-Williams), and āśvāsa, "breathing freely" (CDIAL 1471).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ഇടിയുക (iṭiyuka) — to cave in; to crumble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a relief camp, then the Malayalam for disaster relief.

You'll want to know: ഇടിയുക.
ഇടിയുക — iṭiyuka — "to cave in; to crumble".
കനത്ത മഴയിൽ വഴിയുടെ ഒരു ഭാഗം ഇടിഞ്ഞു. kanatta maḻayil vaḻiyuṭe oru bhāgaṁ iṭiññu. In the heavy rain part of the road caved in.
Past: ഇടിഞ്ഞു, iṭiññu.
For earth, walls and banks: മണ്ണ് ഇടിഞ്ഞു, the earth gave way.

The word, taken apart.
ഇടിയുക (iṭiyuka) is native Dravidian, "to crumble, to fall to pieces" (DEDR 432).

Lesson 4 of 5.
കടപുഴകുക (kaṭapuḻakuka) — to be uprooted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for disaster relief, then the Malayalam for to cave in.

You'll want to know: കടപുഴകുക.
കടപുഴകുക — kaṭapuḻakuka — "to be uprooted".
കാറ്റിൽ വഴിയോരത്തെ ഒരു വലിയ മരം കടപുഴകി. kāṟṟil vaḻiyōratte oru valiya maraṁ kaṭapuḻaki. In the wind a big tree by the roadside was uprooted.
Past: കടപുഴകി, kaṭapuḻaki.
Only for trees torn out by the roots: മരം കടപുഴകി, the tree came down roots and all.

The word, taken apart.
കടപുഴകുക (kaṭapuḻakuka) is a Malayalam verb for a tree torn up by the roots; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
വലയുക (valayuka) — to be in distress; to be put to hardship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to cave in, then the Malayalam for to be uprooted.

You'll want to know: വലയുക.
വലയുക — valayuka — "to be in distress; to be put to hardship".
ബസ് ഇല്ലാതെ യാത്രക്കാർ വലഞ്ഞു. basŭ illāte yātrakkār valaññu. With no bus, the passengers were stranded and suffered.
Past: വലഞ്ഞു, valaññu.
Common in the news: യാത്രക്കാർ വലഞ്ഞു, the passengers had a hard time.

The word, taken apart.
വലയുക (valayuka) is native Dravidian, "to be straitened, pressed, distressed" (DEDR 5281).
