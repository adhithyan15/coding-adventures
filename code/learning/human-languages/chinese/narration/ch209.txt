Mandarin Chinese, chapter 209: Rules and Notices.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guīdìng — rule; regulation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tool, then the Mandarin for battery.

You'll want to know: guīdìng.
guīdìng — "rule; regulation".
zhè shì xuéxiào de guīdìng — That's the school's rule.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guīdìng is built from guī 'a pair of compasses, a rule' and dìng 'to settle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjwie and dengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'round' and the second 'calm'.

What you've built.
One more word for reading notices and messages.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: guīdìng]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: guīdìng, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhè shì xuéxiào de guīdìng — That's the school's rule.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for tool, then the Mandarin for battery, then say guīdìng again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does guīdìng mean? ("Rule; regulation".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎrǎo — to disturb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for safe, then the Mandarin for to keep up.

You'll want to know: dǎrǎo.
dǎrǎo — "to disturb".
qǐng bú yào dǎrǎo wǒ — Please don't disturb me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before rǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dǎrǎo is built from 打 'to strike' and rǎo 'to trouble', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and nyewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to throw into disorder' and 'to vex'.
