Lesson 1 of 5.
علامة (ʿalāma) — a mark, a grade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a colleague, then the Arabic for a company.

You'll want to know: علامة.
علامة — ʿalāma — "a mark, a grade".

The word, taken apart.
علامة (ʿalāma) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-l-m, "to know; a mark" (Lane). ʿAlāma is a mark, a sign.

You'll want to know: مادة.
مادة — mādda — "a school subject".

The word, taken apart.
مادة (mādda) is built on the native Arabic root m-d-d, "to stretch" (Lane). Mādda is matter, the stuff a thing is drawn from; the school subject is a modern sense.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بريد (barīd) — post, mail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a school subject, then the Arabic for fees.

You'll want to know: بريد.
بريد — barīd — "post, mail".

The word, taken apart.
بريد (barīd) is disputed. Lane's sources call it of Persian origin, from a phrase for the docked tails of post-mules; EtymDB traces it to Greek beredos, from Late Latin veredus, "post-horse". The origin is not settled.
