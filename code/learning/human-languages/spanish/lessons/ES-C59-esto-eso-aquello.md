# esto · eso · aquello — pointing at what has no name yet

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-THREE-DISTANCES, ES-GRAMMAR-NOUN-GENDER] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Este libro*, *esa casa*. Both pointing words agreed with a noun.

[PAUSE 1s] But what do you say when there **is** no noun — when you do not know
what the thing is, or when the thing is not a thing at all?

## Grammar Lens: the third set
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-NEUTER]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-ESTE] -->

There is one more form of each, ending in **-o**:

| | |
|---|---|
| ***esto*** | this, here, whatever it is |
| ***eso*** | that, by you, whatever it is |
| ***aquello*** | that, over there, whatever it is |

[PAUSE 2s] These never sit in front of a noun. They **are** the noun.

> *¿Qué es* ***esto****?* — What is this?

*Este* could not ask that: it needs a word to lean on, and the whole point of
the question is that you have not got one.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-NEUTER] -->

Spanish nouns are masculine or feminine — you learned that in chapter one, and
it has held ever since. So what gender is *esto*?

[PAUSE 2s] **Neither.** It is the one leftover of a third gender Latin had and
Spanish otherwise abandoned, kept for exactly this job: pointing at what has not
been sorted into a gender, **because it has not been named**.

Which is also how you point at a whole situation — *Eso está bien.* Not a book,
not a house; the thing just said. Nothing there has a gender, so the form that
agrees with nothing fits.

[PAUSE 2s] Name it, and agreement returns: *¿Qué es esto?* — *Es un libro.* —
*Ah, ese libro.*
