Japanese, chapter 191: Closing Time and Local Shops.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しまる (shimaru) — to close; to be shut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to enter, then the Japanese for to be open.

You'll want to know: しまる.
しまる — shimaru — "to close; to be shut".
ゆうびんきょくは ごじに しまります。 — yūbinkyoku wa goji ni shimarimasu — "The post office closes at five."
Polite しまります, shimarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
しまる (shimaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
やっきょく (yakkyoku) — pharmacy; chemist's.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for market, then the Japanese for liquor store.

You'll want to know: やっきょく.
やっきょく — yakkyoku — "pharmacy; chemist's".
やっきょくで くすりを かいます。 — yakkyoku de kusuri o kaimasu — "I buy medicine at the pharmacy."

The word, taken apart.
やっきょく (yakkyoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yak, "medicine" (Middle Chinese yak) and kyoku, "office, bureau" (gjowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

いちばで やすい やさいを みつけます。 — ichiba de yasui yasai o mitsukemasu — "I find cheap vegetables at the market."
