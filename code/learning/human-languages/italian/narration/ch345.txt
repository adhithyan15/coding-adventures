Italian, chapter 345: Dire la propria.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
esprimere — to express.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for unquestionable, then the Italian for plain.

You'll want to know: esprimere.
esprimere — "to express".
Non è facile esprimere quello che sento. It is not easy to express what I feel.
Past participle espresso — the same word as the coffee, pressed out. Esprimersi — to express yourself.

The word, taken apart.
esprimere is a learned borrowing from Latin exprimere "to press out, to wring" (EtymDB), built from ex- "out of" and premere "to press": the thought is squeezed out into words.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
affermare — to state, to assert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for plain, then the Italian for to express.

You'll want to know: affermare.
affermare — "to state, to assert".
Il sindaco afferma che le tasse non aumenteranno. The mayor states that taxes will not rise.
Stronger than dire. Affermarsi is to become established, to succeed.

The word, taken apart.
affermare is a learned borrowing from Latin affirmāre "to make firm, to assert" (Lewis & Short), built from ad "to" and firmāre "to strengthen", on firmus "firm".

affermazione is a learned borrowing from Latin affirmātiō, affirmātiōnis "an assertion" (EtymDB), from affirmāre "to make firm", built on firmus "firm".

Lesson 4 of 5.
attendibile — credible, trustworthy (of news or a source).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to state, then the Italian for the statement.

You'll want to know: attendibile.
attendibile — "credible, trustworthy (of news or a source)".
Questa notizia non è attendibile. This news is not reliable.
For information and sources: una notizia attendibile.

The word, taken apart.
attendibile is built on attendere in its old sense "to pay heed to", from Latin attendere "to stretch towards, to give heed" (Lewis & Short), built on tendere "to stretch": worth heeding.

Lesson 5 of 5.
affidabile — reliable, dependable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the statement, then the Italian for credible.

You'll want to know: affidabile.
affidabile — "reliable, dependable".
Mio fratello è una persona affidabile: puoi lasciargli le chiavi. My brother is a reliable person: you can leave him the keys.
Una macchina affidabile — a reliable car.

The word, taken apart.
affidabile is built on affidare "to entrust", which comes from Vulgar Latin fīdāre, a form of Latin fīdere "to trust" (Lewis & Short): someone you can trust things to.
