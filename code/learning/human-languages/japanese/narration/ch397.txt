Japanese, chapter 397: A Room for the Night.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
みんしゅく (minshuku) — a family-run guesthouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for strolling, then the Japanese for wandering restlessly.

You'll want to know: みんしゅく.
みんしゅく — minshuku — "a family-run guesthouse".
うみの ちかくに みんしゅくが あります。 — umi no chikaku ni minshuku ga arimasu — "There is a family-run guesthouse near the sea."

The word, taken apart.
みんしゅく (minshuku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are min, "people" (Middle Chinese mjin) and shuku, "inn" (sjuwk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

いっぱく — ippaku — "a one-night stay".
りょかんに いっぱくしました。 — ryokan ni ippaku shimashita — "I stayed one night at a Japanese inn."

The word, taken apart.
いっぱく (ippaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ichi, "one" (Middle Chinese 'jit) and haku, "overnight stay" (bak), joined as ippaku (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ひがえり (higaeri) — a day trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for accommodation, then the Japanese for a one-night stay.

You'll want to know: ひがえり.
ひがえり — higaeri — "a day trip".
やまに ひがえりで いきました。 — yama ni higaeri de ikimashita — "We went to the mountains on a day trip."

The word, taken apart.
ひがえり (higaeri) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from hi, "day" and gaeri, "returning" (from kaeru); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

You'll want to know: あずかる.
あずかる — azukaru — "to look after (something for someone)".
ともだちの にもつを あずかりました。 — tomodachi no nimotsu o azukarimashita — "I looked after my friend's luggage."
Polite あずかります, azukarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): りょかんが にもつを あずかりました。, ryokan ga nimotsu o azukarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
あずかる (azukaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the partner of azukeru, "to deposit" (JMdict).
