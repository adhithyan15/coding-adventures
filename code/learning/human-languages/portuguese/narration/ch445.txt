Portuguese, chapter 445: Obras na rua.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o andaime — the scaffolding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bollard, then the Portuguese for to obstruct.

You'll want to know: o andaime.
o andaime — "the scaffolding".
Há andaimes à frente do prédio. There is scaffolding in front of the building.
Usually plural: os andaimes.

The word, taken apart.
andaime is usually traced to Arabic ad-daʿā'im 'the supports', but the origin is not settled; EtymDB lists Spanish andamio as a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a grua — the crane (for building).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to obstruct, then the Portuguese for the scaffolding.

You'll want to know: a grua.
a grua — "the crane (for building)".
Uma grua enorme levanta os tijolos. A huge crane lifts the bricks.
The bird is o grou; the machine took its name from its long neck.

The word, taken apart.
grua goes back to Latin grūs 'crane, the bird' (EtymDB): the machine has the bird's long neck.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o estaleiro — the building site; the shipyard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the scaffolding, then the Portuguese for the crane.

You'll want to know: o estaleiro.
o estaleiro — "the building site; the shipyard".
O estaleiro fecha ao fim de semana. The building site is closed at the weekend.
Os estaleiros navais — the shipyards.

The word, taken apart.
estaleiro came from Spanish astillero 'shipyard', which came from Old French astelier 'workshop' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
desabar — to collapse, to fall down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the crane, then the Portuguese for the building site.

You'll want to know: desabar.
desabar — "to collapse, to fall down".
Com a chuva, um muro velho desabou. In the rain, an old wall collapsed.
Also of a storm: desabou uma tempestade.

The word, taken apart.
desabar is usually explained as des- and aba 'brim, edge', but the origin is not settled; EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
demolir — to knock down, to demolish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the building site, then the Portuguese for to collapse.

You'll want to know: demolir.
demolir — "to knock down, to demolish".
Vão demolir a fábrica antiga. They are going to knock down the old factory.
The noun is a demolição.

The word, taken apart.
demolir is a learned borrowing from Latin dēmōlīrī 'to pull down' (Lewis & Short), built on mōlēs 'mass, structure'.
