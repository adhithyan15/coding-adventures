## You'll want to know: രണ്ടാം
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C67-ORDINAL-03]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C67-ORDINAL-02, ML-CONCEPT-C07-NUMBERS-1-5-01] -->

**രണ്ടാം** (*raṇṭāṁ*) — "**second**". **രണ്ട്** loses its final **ŭ** and takes
**ആം**, exactly as **ഒന്ന്** did.

| cardinal | ordinal |
|---|---|
| **ഒന്ന്** (*onnŭ*) | **ഒന്നാം** (*onnāṁ*) |
| **രണ്ട്** (*raṇṭŭ*) | **രണ്ടാം** (*raṇṭāṁ*) |

**രണ്ടാം വാതിൽ** (*raṇṭāṁ vātil*), the second door.

## Across the family: the word Malayalam did not use
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-ETYMON-C67-ORDINAL-04]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C67-ORDINAL-01] -->

Two ordinals is enough to see the machine, and enough to see what it saved
Malayalam from. Its three closest relatives all have a *separate word* for
**first**, and it is the same word in all three:

| language | "one" | "first" |
|---|---|---|
| Malayalam | *onnŭ* | **ഒന്നാം** *onnāṁ*, straight off *onnŭ* |
| Tamil | *oṉṟu* | **முதல்** *mudal*, "a beginning" |
| Kannada | *ondu* | **ಮೊದಲನೆಯ** *modalaneya*, on *modalu* |
| Telugu | *okaṭi* | **మొదటి** *modaṭi*, on *modalu* |

Malayalam has that word too — **മുതൽ** (*mutal*) is the same Dravidian root — but
it drifted, and in Malayalam it means "**from**", the point a thing starts at
rather than the thing that is first in line. So the slot that the sisters filled
with a special word stayed empty here, and the ordinary ending filled it.
