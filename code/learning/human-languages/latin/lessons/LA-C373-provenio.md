## You'll want to know: prōveniō, prōvenīre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C373-EFFECTUS-03]; assesses=[] -->

**prōveniō, prōvenīre** — "to come forth, to grow; to turn out".

> Frūmentum hōc annō bene prōvēnit.
>
> *The grain grew well this year.*

*prōveniō, prōvenīre, prōvēnī, prōventum*. Of a harvest, to come up; of a plan, to turn out. English *provenance* comes from it through French.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*prōveniō* is built from *prō* "forth" and *veniō* "to come" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *provenance* comes from it through French.
