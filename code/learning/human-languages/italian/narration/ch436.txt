la lampadina — the light bulb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to cool down, then the Italian for the water heater.

You'll want to know: la lampadina.
la lampadina — "the light bulb".
In bagno serve una lampadina nuova. A new bulb is needed in the bathroom.
La lampada is a lamp.

The word, taken apart.
lampadina is a little lampada 'lamp', which is inherited from Latin lampas, from Greek lampás 'torch' (Lewis & Short).

L'interruttore è dietro la porta. The switch is behind the door.
Built on interrompere: it interrupts the current.

The word, taken apart.
interruttore is a learned borrowing from Latin interruptor 'an interrupter', from interrumpere 'to break off', built on rumpere 'to break' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
il fusibile — the fuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the light bulb, then the Italian for the switch.

You'll want to know: il fusibile.
il fusibile — "the fuse".
È saltato un fusibile. A fuse has blown.
Saltare is the verb: the fuse jumps.

The word, taken apart.
fusibile is a learned borrowing from Late Latin fūsibilis 'that can be melted', from fundere 'to pour, to melt' (Lewis & Short): the wire melts and cuts the current.

You'll want to know: ricaricare.
ricaricare — "to recharge; to reload".
Devo ricaricare il telefono. I need to charge my phone.
Il caricabatterie — the charger.

The word, taken apart.
ricaricare is built from ri- 'again' and caricare 'to load', which is inherited from Late Latin carricāre, built on carrus 'cart'.
