## You'll want to know: The word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-02]; assesses=[] -->

> **secondo** (masculine) · **seconda** (feminine) — **second**.
> **il secondo giorno** — the second day. **la seconda volta** — the second time.

There is no *due* in it. It comes from Latin ***sequī***, "**to follow**", so
second place is named for what it does — the place that **comes after** — and not
for the count it sits at. English **second**, **sequel**, **sequence** and
**consequence** are the same root.

## Why it's said this way: the two courses
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-NOTICE-SECONDO-03]; assesses=[] -->

An Italian menu is written with these two words used as nouns, and once you have
them you can read one:

| course | what arrives |
|---|---|
| **l'antipasto** | before the meal |
| **il primo** | pasta, rice or soup |
| **il secondo** | meat or fish |
| **il dolce** | the sweet |

*Il primo* and *il secondo* are not "starter" and "main". They are literally
**the first** and **the second**, and an Italian ordering both is ordering two
courses, not a starter and an entrée. Two ordinals, and half a meal is legible.
