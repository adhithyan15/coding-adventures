# wie — "how," cousin of English *how*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] German asks "what's your name?" as "**how** are you called?" — so
first, "how."

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-SOUND-WIE-01]; assesses=[] -->

- **wie** = *vee*. Two traps: German **w** is an English *v*, and **ie** is a
  long *ee*. So *wie* sounds like English "vee," never "wy."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-WIE-02, GE-ETYMON-WIE-03]; assesses=[] -->

**wie** — "how." Root: Proto-Germanic **\*hwī**, from the PIE interrogative root
**\*kʷo-** — the very root behind the English **wh-** words:

- **how**, **what**, **who**, **why** — and, through Latin, the **qu-** of
  *question*, *quantity*, and the *quo modo* that became French *comment*.

German *wie* and English *how* are the same question-word, lightly reshaped.

## Grammar Lens: German counts names as a matter of "how"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WIE-02] -->

Like French, German asks a name with "**how**," not "what": *wie heißen Sie?*
is literally "*how* are you called?" — about the *manner* of your being called.
Don't translate word for word; learn the logic: the name-question runs on
**wie**.
