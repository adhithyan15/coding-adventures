Japanese, chapter 156: Closets, Drawers and a Study.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おしいれ (oshiire) — a closet (for futons).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for furniture, then the Japanese for the front entrance.

You'll want to know: おしいれ.
おしいれ — oshiire — "a closet (for futons)".
ふとんを おしいれに しまいます。 — futon o oshiire ni shimaimasu — "I put the futon away in the closet."

The word, taken apart.
おしいれ (oshiire) is native Japanese, built from oshi, "pushing", the stem of osu, and ire, "putting in", the stem of ireru; both parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しょさい (shosai) — a home office; a study.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a closet, then the Japanese for a drawer.

You'll want to know: しょさい.
しょさい — shosai — "a home office; a study".
ちちは しょさいで ほんを よみます。 — chichi wa shosai de hon o yomimasu — "My father reads books in his study."

The word, taken apart.
しょさい (shosai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sho, "writing, books" (Middle Chinese syo) and sai, "room, purification" (tsreaj) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
いれる (ireru) — to put in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a drawer, then the Japanese for a home office.

You'll want to know: いれる.
いれる — ireru — "to put in".
かばんに ほんを いれます。 — kaban ni hon o iremasu — "I put a book in my bag."
Polite いれます, iremasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
いれる (ireru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as iru, "to go in"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
