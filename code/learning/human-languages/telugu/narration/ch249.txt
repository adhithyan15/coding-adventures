Telugu, chapter 249: To endure, To crawl, To bend down, To stretch out, To stack.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
భరించు (bhariñcu) — to endure, to put up with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to rob, then the Telugu for to hand over.

You'll want to know: భరించు.
భరించు — bhariñcu — "to endure, to put up with".

The word, taken apart.
భరించు (bhariñcu) is Sanskrit bhara, "carrying; weight" (CDIAL 9393), made into a verb with the Telugu ending -iñcu: "to bear".

Lesson 3 of 5.
వంగు (vaṅgu) — to bend down, to stoop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to endure, then the Telugu for to crawl.

You'll want to know: వంగు.
వంగు — vaṅgu — "to bend down, to stoop".

The word, taken apart.
వంగు (vaṅgu) is native Dravidian. DEDR 5335 lists vaṅgu, "to bend, stoop", with Tamil vāṅku, "to bend". DEDR compares Sanskrit vaṅka, "bent, crooked" (CDIAL 11191), which CDIAL ties to a probably non-Aryan word-group.

చాచు (cācu) is native Dravidian. DEDR 2433 lists a nasalised cāgu, "to be stretched", with Kannada cācu, "to stretch out", and sets beside them, with a query, Tamil cāṭu, "to stretch out".
