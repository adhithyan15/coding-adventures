# झ (jha) — the letter inside समझणो

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-JA-01, MW-LEX-C157-VERB157-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write **ज** once, and name it.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **समझणो** (*samajhṇo*), "to understand". Its middle is a **ज**
with a breath after it: *jh*. This lesson puts that letter in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: झ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-SCRIPT-C157-JHA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**झ** — *jha*, the breathed *j*.

It sits inside **समझणो**. It pairs with **ज** as **छ** pairs with **च**: the same
place in the mouth, with a breath. On the page it is one joined body, with a short
upper stem, two loops and a diagonal tail, then a middle crossbar, a right stem
and the headline.

## Writing: झ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-C157-JHA-01] -->

- **1.** descend the short upper stem, turn left and curve clockwise around the upper bowl, continue through the waist and clockwise around the lower loop, then finish down-right through the diagonal tail without lifting
- **2.** lift at the central junction and draw the middle crossbar left-to-right
- **3.** without lifting, climb up the right stem to the headline
- **4.** descend the right stem top-to-bottom
- **5.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 2.**

> The source shows four movements; native writers draw झ as three pen-down strokes (53% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 44 of 83). This ductus follows the four movements in order and direction, lifting only before the crossbar and the headline: the crossbar ends on the right stem, so the pen climbs the stem before descending it.

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-झ-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
