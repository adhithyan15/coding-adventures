# ض (ḍād) — the letter inside فضلك

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C47-FA-01, AR-SCRIPT-C47-QAF-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ف and ق once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **من فضلك** (*min faḍlik*), "please".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ض
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SCRIPT-C48-DAD-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ض** — *ḍād*, a heavy *d*.

It is inside **من فضلك** (*min faḍlik*), "please". It is **ص** (*ṣād*), which you
already write, with **one dot above**. Arabic is sometimes called *the language of
the ḍād*, because the sound is so rare in other languages.

## Writing: ض
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C48-DAD-01] -->

- **1.** start here: close the oval clockwise from its lower-left junction
- **2.** turn left and rise into the short shoulder without lifting
- **3.** lift, restart at the baseline junction, and sweep through the trailing bowl
- **4.** lift again, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 2.**

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: س ش ص ض, independent Daad at 00:43.1–00:46.3 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C47-FA-01, AR-SCRIPT-C47-QAF-01, AR-SCRIPT-C48-DAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at **من فضلك** and point at ض inside it]
- [YOU TRACE: ض three times, saying *ḍ* as you finish each one]
- [YOU WRITE: ق, then ض, from memory]
- [YOU READ: **من فضلك** aloud once more]

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter is this — ض? (**ḍād**, *ḍ*.) Which word did it
come from? (**من فضلك**, *min faḍlik*, "please".)
