Bengali, chapter 274: If, and Otherwise.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
যদি … তাহলে … (jôdi … tāhôle …) — if, then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "for two years". (dui bôchhôr dhôre.) Then যদি, "if", and তাহলে, "then, in that case": both of which you know.

You'll want to know: if, then.
যদি কাল ঝড় হয়, তাহলে আমরা যাব না। jôdi kāl jhôṛ hôy, tāhôle āmrā jābô nā. — "If there's a storm tomorrow, we won't go."
The যদি half takes the present, even about tomorrow: হয়, not হবে. The তাহলে half takes the future. তাহলে may be left out: যদি তুমি আসো, আমি রান্না করব (jôdi tumi āso, āmi rānnā kôrbô), "if you come, I'll cook".

You'll want to know: if not.
Inside a যদি half, না comes before the verb, not after it:
যদি ট্রেন সময়মতো না আসে, তাহলে আমি বাসে যাব। jôdi ṭren sômôymôto nā āse, tāhôle āmi bāse jābô. — "If the train doesn't come on time, I'll take the bus."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If there's a storm tomorrow, we won't go" — jôdi kāl jhôṛ hôy, tāhôle āmrā jābô nā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you come, I'll cook" — jôdi tumi āso, āmi rānnā kôrbô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If the train doesn't come on time, I'll take the bus" — jôdi ṭren sômôymôto nā āse, tāhôle āmi bāse jābô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that if there's a storm tomorrow you won't go, then that if the train doesn't come you'll take the bus.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which tense after যদি? (The present.) After তাহলে? (The future.) Where does না go in the যদি half? (Before the verb.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
বৃষ্টি হলে … (brishṭi hôle …) — the -le form, and otherwise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "If there's a storm tomorrow, we won't go". (jôdi kāl jhôṛ hôy, tāhôle āmrā jābô nā.) Then নাহলে, "otherwise".

You'll want to know: the -le form.
Bengali can say "if" with no যদি: the verb takes -লে, on the stem of the past, and stays the same for every person.
বৃষ্টি হলে আমরা বাড়িতে থাকব। brishṭi hôle āmrā bāṛite thākbô. — "If it rains, we'll stay at home."
বৃষ্টি (brishṭi) is "rain", and বৃষ্টি হওয়া is "to rain": "rain happens".
হওয়া becomes হলে, আসা becomes এলে, যাওয়া becomes গেলে, খাওয়া becomes খেলে, করা becomes করলে, কেনা becomes কিনলে: তুমি এলে আমি খুশি হব (tumi ele āmi khushi hôbô), "if you come, I'll be happy". The person is in the noun before it, never in the ending.

You'll want to know: otherwise.
নাহলে, "otherwise", which you know, is না with this same হলে: "if it is not so".
তাড়াতাড়ি যেতে হবে, নাহলে ট্রেন ছেড়ে যাবে। tāṛātāṛi jete hôbe, nāhôle ṭren chheṛe jābe. — "We have to go quickly, otherwise the train will leave."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, we'll stay at home" — brishṭi hôle āmrā bāṛite thākbô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you come, I'll be happy" — tumi ele āmi khushi hôbô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We have to go quickly, otherwise the train will leave" — tāṛātāṛi jete hôbe, nāhôle ṭren chheṛe jābe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that if you come, you'll be happy; then that we have to go quickly, otherwise the train will leave.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
আসা in the -লে form? (এলে.) Does -লে change for the person? (No.) "Otherwise"? (নাহলে: "if not so".)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ঢোকা (ḍhokā) — to enter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "If you come, I'll be happy". (tumi ele āmi khushi hôbô.)

You'll want to know: ঢোকা.
ঢোকা — ḍhokā — "to enter, to go in".
বাড়িতে ঢুকে আমি জুতো খুললাম। bāṛite ḍhuke āmi juto khullām. — "I came into the house and took my shoes off."
The place you enter takes -এ or -তে: বাড়িতে, দোকানে. The ও of ঢোকা (ḍhokā) rises to উ in the past, the -e form and the -লে form, as খোলা does: সে দোকানে ঢুকল (she dokāne ḍhuklô), "she went into the shop"; ঢুকে; ঢুকলে.

The word, taken apart.
ঢোকা (ḍhokā) is listed in CDIAL, as ḍhukā, "to enter", under a reconstructed ḍhukyati, "approaches" (CDIAL 5592): a form no text records, hence the star.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ḍhokā, then she ḍhuklô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I came into the house and took my shoes off" — bāṛite ḍhuke āmi juto khullām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She went into the shop" — she dokāne ḍhuklô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ঢোকা (ḍhokā) mean? ("To enter".) Its past? (ঢুকল, with উ.) Its -e form? (ঢুকে.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ঢ (ḍhô) — the letter at the start of ঢোকা (ḍhokā).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page, your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ঢ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say ঢোকা (ḍhokā), "to enter". Then read ট (ṭô) and ড (ḍô).

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ঢ.
ঢ — ḍhô: the ড sound with a breath after it, as ঠ is ট with a breath. It opens ঢোকা (ḍhokā).
In the print of this book, ঢ (ḍhô) is ট without its raised hook. It has a headline; a straight stem stands under the left part of it; the stem's foot rounds into a bowl, and the bowl's right side ends in a solid knob, tucked inside the curve under the right half of the headline. The right side does not reach the headline. Look at ট beside it: below the headline the two are alike, and ট has the hook above.

Writing: ঢ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ঢ (ḍhô) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the headline, the stem on the left, the round bottom, the solid knob, and the gap between the knob and the headline.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ঢ (ḍhô) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.
