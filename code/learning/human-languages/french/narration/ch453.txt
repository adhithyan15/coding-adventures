French, chapter 453: Emballer et envoyer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'emballage — the packaging, the wrapping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to sell off cheap, then the French for the street sale.

You'll want to know: l'emballage.
l'emballage — "the packaging, the wrapping".
Il y a trop d'emballage autour de ce petit objet. There is too much packaging round this small object.
A masculine noun. Un emballage cadeau — gift wrap.

The word, taken apart.
emballage is built on emballer 'to wrap up' with -age (EtymDB lists it). Emballer comes from balle 'bale', usually traced to a Frankish word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
emballer — to wrap (up), to pack; to thrill (informal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the street sale, then the French for the packaging.

You'll want to know: emballer.
emballer — "to wrap (up), to pack; to thrill (informal)".
La vendeuse emballe le cadeau dans du papier rouge. The shop assistant wraps the present in red paper.
Informal: ça m'emballe — I love the idea; s'emballer — to get carried away.

The word, taken apart.
emballer is built from en- and balle 'bale, bundle' (EtymDB lists the verb), and balle is usually traced to a Frankish word meaning 'ball'.

You'll want to know: le livreur.
le livreur — "the delivery man, the courier".
Le livreur a laissé le paquet chez la voisine. The delivery man left the parcel with the neighbour.
Feminine la livreuse.

The word, taken apart.
livreur is built on livrer 'to deliver' with -eur (EtymDB); livrer is inherited from Latin līberāre 'to set free', from līber 'free'.

le carton — "the cardboard box; cardboard".
Nous avons mis les livres dans des cartons. We put the books in cardboard boxes.
Un carton jaune — a yellow card, in sport.

The word, taken apart.
carton was borrowed from Italian cartone 'thick paper' (EtymDB), from carta, which goes back to Latin charta 'sheet of papyrus'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le recommandé — the registered letter, registered post.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the delivery man, then the French for the cardboard box.

You'll want to know: le recommandé.
le recommandé — "the registered letter, registered post".
La banque m'a envoyé un recommandé. The bank sent me a registered letter.
En recommandé — by registered post; une lettre recommandée.

The word, taken apart.
recommandé is the past participle of recommander 'to entrust', from re- and commander, which goes back to Latin mandāre 'to entrust' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the noun.
