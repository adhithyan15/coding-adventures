# अगर ट्रेन छूट गई, तो … (agar ṭren chūṭ gaī, to …) — if it happens, and otherwise

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I missed my train". (**मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई**.) Then "if it rains, we will stay at home". (**अगर बारिश होगी, तो हम घर पर रहेंगे**.)

## You'll want to know: the plain past after अगर
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C286-IF-PERFECTIVE-01]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-C286-AGAR-TO-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C283-MISSED-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-ERGATIVE-NE-01, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02, HI-LEX-C204-THG204-05, HI-CONCEPT-C07-HAAN-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-FUTURE-PERSON-01] -->

> **अगर मेरी ट्रेन छूट गई, तो मैं बस से आऊँगा।**
>
> *agar merī ṭren chūṭ gaī, to maiṁ bas se āūṅgā.* — "If I miss my train, I'll come by bus."

For one event that may or may not happen, Hindi can put it in the plain past after **अगर**, as if it had already happened: **छूट गई**. The result stays in the future. It is the past of a story, used to try a case out.

- **अगर आपको देर हो गई, तो मुझे फ़ोन कीजिए।** (*agar āpko der ho gaī, to mujhe fon kījiye.*) — If you're running late, phone me.
- **अगर उसने हाँ कहा, तो हम कल चलेंगे।** (*agar usne hā̃ kahā, to ham kal caleṅge.*) — If she says yes, we'll go tomorrow.

## You'll want to know: नहीं तो, otherwise
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C286-NAHIN-TO-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C64-MEASURE-02, HI-LEX-C122-LATE-02, HI-LEX-C63-STATE-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-FUTURE-PERSON-01, HI-LEX-C280-LAGNA-01] -->

> **जल्दी चलो, नहीं तो देर हो जाएगी।**
>
> *jaldī calo, nahīṃ to der ho jāegī.* — "Hurry up, otherwise we'll be late."

**नहीं तो** (*nahīṃ to*) is "if not, then": a warning about what follows if the first part does not happen. The first part is often a request. The warning is in the future: here **हो जाएगी**, the future of **हो गई**.

- **खाना खाओ, नहीं तो भूख लगेगी।** (*khānā khāo, nahīṃ to bhūkh lagegī.*) — Eat, or you'll get hungry.
