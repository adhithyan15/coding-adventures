Portuguese, chapter 511: Taças e medalhas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o torneio — tournament.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to defeat, then the Portuguese for to knock out.

You'll want to know: o torneio.
o torneio — "tournament".
A escola organiza um torneio de ténis. The school is organising a tennis tournament.
Medieval knights fought in torneios too.

The word, taken apart.
torneio came into Old Portuguese as torneyo from Old Occitan tornei (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin tornāre "to turn".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a taça — cup, trophy; bowl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to knock out, then the Portuguese for tournament.

You'll want to know: a taça.
a taça — "cup, trophy; bowl".
A equipa levantou a taça no fim do jogo. The team lifted the cup at the end of the match.
Also a wine glass or a bowl: uma taça de fruta.

The word, taken apart.
taça came through Spanish taza from Arabic ṭāsa "a bowl" (EtymDB), which goes back to Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for cup, then the Portuguese for medal.

You'll want to know: o pódio.
o pódio — "podium".
Três atletas subiram ao pódio. Three athletes went up on the podium.
Subir ao pódio — to finish in the top three.

The word, taken apart.
pódio is a learned borrowing from Latin podium (EtymDB), from Greek pódion "a base", built on poús "foot".
