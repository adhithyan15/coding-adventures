Urdu, chapter 328: The Prince in Disguise.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شہزادہ (shahzādā) — a prince.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to bite (of a snake), then the Urdu for a diamond.

You'll want to know: شہزادہ.
شہزادہ — shahzādā — "a prince".
شہزادہ رات کو شہر میں گھومتا تھا۔ shahzādā rāt ko shahr meṅ ghūmtā thā. The prince used to walk about the city at night.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
شہزادہ (shahzādā) is usually traced to Persian shāhzāda, "born of a king", built from shāh, a king, and zāda, "born".

Lesson 2 of 5.
بھیس (bhes) — a disguise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a diamond, then the Urdu for a prince.

You'll want to know: بھیس.
بھیس — bhes — "a disguise".
اس نے کسان کا بھیس بدلا۔ us ne kisān kā bhes badlā. He disguised himself as a farmer.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
بھیس (bhes) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin bhes, "dress, an assumed appearance", under Sanskrit vēṣa (CDIAL 12129). بھیس بدلنا is to put on a disguise.

Lesson 3 of 5.
آزمانا (āzmānā) — to put to the test.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a prince, then the Urdu for a disguise.

You'll want to know: آزمانا.
آزمانا — āzmānā — "to put to the test".
شہزادے نے لوگوں کو آزمایا۔ shahzāde ne logoṅ ko āzmāyā. The prince put the people to the test.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے دوست کو آزمایا, us ne dost ko āzmāyā.

The word, taken apart.
آزمانا (āzmānā) is usually traced to Persian āzmā-, the stem of āzmūdan, "to test", with the Urdu verb ending.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بہکانا (bahkānā) — to lead astray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to put to the test, then the Urdu for deceit.

You'll want to know: بہکانا.
بہکانا — bahkānā — "to lead astray".
وزیر نے بادشاہ کو بہکایا۔ vazīr ne bādshāh ko bahkāyā. The minister led the king astray.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بچے کو بہکایا, us ne bachche ko bahkāyā.

The word, taken apart.
بہکانا (bahkānā) is the causing form of bahaknā, "to be misled", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under Sanskrit vyáthatē (CDIAL 12164): to make someone lose the way.
