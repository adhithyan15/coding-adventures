Japanese, chapter 404: The View from the Top.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ふうけい (fūkei) — scenery; a landscape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a place visited on a trip, then the Japanese for to visit a place.

You'll want to know: ふうけい.
ふうけい — fūkei — "scenery; a landscape".
いなかの ふうけいは うつくしいです。 — inaka no fūkei wa utsukushii desu — "The countryside scenery is beautiful."

The word, taken apart.
ふうけい (fūkei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are fū, "style" (Middle Chinese pjuwng) and kei, "scenery" (kjaengX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ながめる (nagameru) — to gaze at; to admire (a view).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to visit a place, then the Japanese for scenery.

You'll want to know: ながめる.
ながめる — nagameru — "to gaze at; to admire (a view)".
まどから うみを ながめました。 — mado kara umi o nagamemashita — "I gazed at the sea from the window."
Polite ながめます, nagamemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ちちが うみを ながめました。, chichi ga umi o nagamemashita.

The word, taken apart.
ながめる (nagameru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
みわたす (miwatasu) — to look out over; to survey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for scenery, then the Japanese for to gaze at.

You'll want to know: みわたす.
みわたす — miwatasu — "to look out over; to survey".
やまの うえから まちを みわたしました。 — yama no ue kara machi o miwatashimashita — "From the top of the mountain I looked out over the town."
Polite みわたします, miwatashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが まちを みわたしました。, ani ga machi o miwatashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
みわたす (miwatasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mi, "seeing" (from miru) and watasu, "to carry across"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
