German, chapter 486: Müde und erschöpft.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Müdigkeit — tiredness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for ankle, then the German for hip.

You'll want to know: die Müdigkeit.
die Müdigkeit — "tiredness".
Nach dem Mittagessen kommt bei mir oft die Müdigkeit. After lunch I often get tired.
No plural. Built on müde — tired.

The word, taken apart.
Müdigkeit is built on müde "tired" with -keit; müde is inherited from Old High German muodi, from Proto-Germanic mōþijaz (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
erschöpft — exhausted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for hip, then the German for tiredness.

You'll want to know: erschöpft.
erschöpft — "exhausted".
Nach dem Umzug waren wir völlig erschöpft. After the move we were completely exhausted.
The participle of erschöpfen — to exhaust. The noun: die Erschöpfung.

The word, taken apart.
erschöpft is built on er- and schöpfen "to scoop, to draw water", from Middle High German schepfen and Old High German scaphan (EtymDB): scooped empty.

Lesson 4 of 5.
schnarchen — to snore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for exhausted, then the German for to yawn.

You'll want to know: schnarchen.
schnarchen — "to snore".
Mein Mann schnarcht so laut, dass ich nicht schlafen kann. My husband snores so loudly that I can't sleep.
Regular: hat geschnarcht.

The word, taken apart.
schnarchen is inherited from Middle High German snarchen (EtymDB), probably an imitative word; English snore and snort are of the same kind.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Albtraum — nightmare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to yawn, then the German for to snore.

You'll want to know: der Albtraum.
der Albtraum — "nightmare".
Nach dem Horrorfilm hatte ich einen Albtraum. After the horror film I had a nightmare.
Plural: die Albträume. Also spelt der Alptraum.

The word, taken apart.
Albtraum is built on Alb "elf, night spirit", inherited from Middle High German alb and Old High German alp, from Proto-Germanic albiz (EtymDB), and Traum "dream".
