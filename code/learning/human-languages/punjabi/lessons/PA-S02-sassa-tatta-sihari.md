# ਸ · ਤ · ਕ — three pieces met inside a word you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-MA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-RA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-LAVA-01, PA-LEX-HAI-02, PA-GRAMMAR-VERB-LAST-02, PA-PHRASE-MY-NAME-02, PA-LEX-YOU-REGISTER-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three more consonants — and with the two you met a moment ago, whole words start falling out.

First, say the last things you learned once more, out loud and from
memory:

- [YOU SAY: *hai* · *merā nāṁ … hai* · *tū̃ / tusī̃*]

## Script you'll notice: ਸ · ਤ · ਕ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-SA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-TA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-KA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> ਸ

**sassā** — *sa*. A consonant, a plain *s*. It is also the letter the whole script is often taught from first.

> ਤ

**tattā** — *ta*. A consonant, the **dental** *t*: tongue on the teeth, softer than any English *t*.

> ਕ

**kakkā** — *ka*. A consonant, the plain *k* of *skin* — no puff of breath, unlike the *k* of English *kin*.

Now put them where you have already heard them:

> **ਸ** + **ਾ** + **ਰ** + **ਾ** → **ਸਾਰਾ** (*sārā*, “whole”)
>
> **ਤ** + **ਾ** + **ਰ** + **ਾ** → **ਤਾਰਾ** (*tārā*, “star”)
>
> **ਕ** + **ਰ** → **ਕਰ** (*kar*, “do!”)
>
> **ਤਾਰਾ** — *tārā* — **star**

You are not being asked to write any of this. You are being asked to
**recognise** it the next time it goes past.

## Writing: copy what you can see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-SA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-TA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RECOG-KA-01] -->

Keep ਸ · ਤ · ਕ in front of you and do not cover any of them. Follow each shape once with a
finger, then copy it once with a pencil, larger than it is printed. Say its
sound as your hand stops.

> This is **observe-and-trace with the model visible**. Nothing in these
> chapters asks you to write a Punjabi word from memory; that ladder starts
> later, and it starts from these same shapes.
