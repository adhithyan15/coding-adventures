Bengali, chapter 310: Conch Shells and Prayers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
শঙ্খ (shôngkhô) — a conch shell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a new account book, then the Bengali for a custom.

You'll want to know: শঙ্খ.
শঙ্খ — shôngkhô — "a conch shell".
সন্ধ্যায় মন্দিরে কেউ শঙ্খ বাজাল। sôndhyāy mondire keu shôngkhô bājālô. In the evening someone blew a conch at the temple.

The word, taken apart.
শঙ্খ (shôngkhô) is Sanskrit śaṅkha, a shell, above all the conch (Monier-Williams). Its ঙ্খ is ngkh; everyday speech wears the word down to শাঁখ.

প্রার্থনা করা — prārthônā kôrā — "to pray".
বন্যার সময় সবাই মন্দিরে গিয়ে প্রার্থনা করল। bônyār sômôy sôbāi mondire giye prārthônā kôrlô. During the flood everyone went to the temple and prayed.
Past (he/she …): তিনি মন্দিরে প্রার্থনা করলেন, tini mondire prārthônā kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
প্রার্থনা করা (prārthônā kôrā) puts Sanskrit prārthanā, 'a request, a petition; a prayer' (Monier-Williams), before করা.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ভক্ত (bhôktô) — a devotee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a conch shell, then the Bengali for to pray.

You'll want to know: ভক্ত.
ভক্ত — bhôktô — "a devotee".
পুজোর দিন মন্দিরে অনেক ভক্ত এলেন। pujor din mondire ônek bhôktô elen. Many devotees came to the temple on the day of the puja.

The word, taken apart.
ভক্ত (bhôktô) is Sanskrit bhakta, first 'allotted, shared' and then 'devoted to, a worshipper' (Monier-Williams); ভাত, cooked rice, comes down from the same word (CDIAL 9331).

পবিত্র (pôbitrô) is Sanskrit pavitra, a means of purifying, a strainer, and so 'pure, holy' (Monier-Williams).
