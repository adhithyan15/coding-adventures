# বোন (bon) — "sister," and the root it shares with rice

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C11-BHAI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] ভাই (*bhāi*) turned out to be the very word "brother." This one does not —
and where it comes from instead is the surprise this chapter has been saving.

## You'll want to know: বোন
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C11-BON-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **বোন** — *bon* — **sister**

**আমার বোন** (*āmār bon*) — my sister.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-DUDH-01, BN-LEX-C10-BHAT-01, BN-SOUND-C10-DUDH-02] -->

**বোন** is **ব** with the vowel sign **ো** (*bo*) plus **ন** (*n*), silent at
the end — one plain consonant, no cluster, so the inherent vowel drops just as
it did on দুধ and ভাত: *bon*, not *bono*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

English "sister" descends from PIE **\*swésōr**. **বোন does not.** It is a
tadbhava through Magadhi Prakrit *\*bahiṇī, from Old Indo-Aryan *\*baghinī —
a doublet of Sanskrit's own **ভগিনী** (*bhaginī*), "one with a share," built
on **ভগ** (*bhaga*), "a share, a portion, good fortune." And *bhaga* is a noun
from the very same verb that named this chapter's rice two lessons ago:
**√ভজ্** (*√bhaj*, "to divide, to share out"), PIE **\*bʰeh₂g-**. ভাত is
"the portion given"; বোন is "the one with a portion." Bengali's word for
sister and Bengali's word for rice are, underneath, cousins. Its word for
sister and English's are not.

The same light touch as last lesson's দাদা applies here: an older woman may
be addressed as **দিদি** (*didi*, "elder sister") as a mark of respect,
family or not.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Is বোন related to English "sister"? (**No** — different root
entirely.) What is it related to instead? (**ভাত** — both trace to √bhaj,
"to share.") Say "one brother" and "two sisters." (**Ek bhāi. Dui bon.**)
Which of those two numbers keeps a vowel Hindi and Marathi lost? (**দুই** —
the *-i* after the old *dv-*.) And which number wears the chandrabindu?
(**পাঁচ**, five — not needed here, but still the only one that has it.)
