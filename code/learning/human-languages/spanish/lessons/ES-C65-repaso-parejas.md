[PAUSE 2s] Three places, in order. (*Aquí*, *ahí*, *allí*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-AHORA, ES-LEX-HOY, ES-GRAMMAR-INDEFINITE-PLURAL, ES-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-VUESTRO, ES-GRAMMAR-VER-PRETERITE] -->

Five chants, and each one finishes something.

[PAUSE 2s] **Places.** *Aquí*, ***ahí***, ***allí*** — one for each of *este*,
*ese*, *aquel*.

[PAUSE 2s] **Times.** ***Ahora***, ***hoy***, *mañana* — now, today, tomorrow.

[PAUSE 2s] **Articles.** *Un libro*, ***unos*** *libros* — one, then a few.

[PAUSE 2s] **Possessives.** *Nuestra casa*, ***vuestra*** *casa* — and with
that the set of six is closed.

[PAUSE 2s] **Two verbs.** ***Vi***, ***di*** — the shortest pasts in the
language.

## Grammar Lens: a gap in a set is hard to see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-COMPLETE, ES-ORTHOGRAPHY-ONE-SYLLABLE-NO-ACCENT, ES-LEX-DAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every one of these was a set you already partly had. You had *aquí*
without *ahí*. You had *mañana* without *hoy*. You had *un* without *unos*, and
*nuestro* without *vuestro*.

[PAUSE 2s] That is a particular kind of gap, and it is harder to notice than a
missing subject, because nothing feels absent — you simply use the half you
have and route around the rest.

[PAUSE 2s] And two small rules came out of it worth keeping. *Vi* and *di* take
**no accent**, because a one-syllable word has nowhere else the beat could
fall. And *dar*, though an *-ar* verb, takes the *-er* endings in the past.
