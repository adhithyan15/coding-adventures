Latin, chapter 200: To lift, To reach, To recognise, To refuse, To fly about.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tollō, tollere — to lift, to raise; to remove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to share, then the Latin for to avoid.

You'll want to know: tollō, tollere.
tollō, tollere — "to lift, to raise; to remove".

The word, taken apart.
tollō is inherited from Proto-Italic tolnō, "to lift up", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European tl-neh2-, "to lift, to bear" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek talanton, "a balance, a weight", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
volitō, volitāre — to fly about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to recognise, then the Latin for to refuse.

You'll want to know: volitō, volitāre.
volitō, volitāre — "to fly about".

The word, taken apart.
volitō is a frequentative of volō, "to fly" (Lewis & Short): to keep flying, to flit about. It is a different verb from volō, "to want".
