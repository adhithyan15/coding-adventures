German, chapter 52: Reading — Nouns, Sentences, and a First Paragraph.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six nouns, and a capital letter doing real work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have met. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them first.

Reading.
Brot Kaffee Wasser Buch Bruder Schwester.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice they all begin with a capital. Every one of them.

Grammar Lens: the capital is information.
[pause 2 seconds]
German capitalises every noun. Spoken, that rule is invisible and costs nothing. Written, it is the single most useful thing on a German page for a beginner.
[pause 2 seconds]
It means you can find the nouns in a sentence before you understand the sentence. In a language that puts its verb at the end and its cases on the articles, knowing which words are the things is most of the way to a reading.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the trade: a writer capitalises a hundred nouns so a reader can skip a hundred decisions.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which of the six would you drink?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What do all six have in common on the page? (A capital letter — and it tells you they are nouns.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six sentences, and a wait at the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you nouns. These are whole sentences.
[pause 1 second]
You have said every one of them.

Reading.
Ich kann Deutsch. Ich möchte lernen. Ich muss arbeiten. Wer ist das? Das ist mein Bruder. Danke!
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time notice where the second verb sits in the first three.

Grammar Lens: the bracket, from the outside.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have been building the bracket from the inside: choose the modal, park the verb at the end. Reading it, the bracket arrives the other way round — möchte comes early and then you wait.
[pause 2 seconds]
That wait is what German reading is. A reader holds the front of the sentence open until the last word closes it, and the modal at the front is the signal to start holding.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and three of them ask you to wait.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which line says something is necessary?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What does a modal at the front tell a reader? (To wait — the sentence is not finished until the last word.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first paragraph.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six nouns, then six sentences. Now a whole paragraph.
[pause 1 second]
Forty-eight words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
Guten Tag! Ich heiße Anna. Das ist mein Bruder. Er ist sehr gut. Wir trinken Kaffee und essen Brot. Ich lese ein Buch. Das Buch ist schön und alt. Ich kann Deutsch. Ich möchte viel lernen, weil Deutsch schön ist. Morgen muss ich arbeiten. Danke und auf Wiedersehen.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and find the one line where the verb is last.

Grammar Lens: the word that moves a verb.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ich möchte viel lernen, weil Deutsch schön ist. That ist at the end is not a mistake and not a style. Weil put it there.
[pause 2 seconds]
Und does not do that. Nor does aber. Four of the shortest words in German, and one of them rearranges the sentence after it while the others leave it alone.
[pause 2 seconds]
Reading is where that stops being a rule you apply and becomes something you see coming. The comma and the weil together are the warning: hold on, the verb is at the far end.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole paragraph the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the paragraph aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which word sends the verb to the end?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that paragraph were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which joiner moves the verb, and which two leave it alone? (Weil moves it; und and aber do not.)
