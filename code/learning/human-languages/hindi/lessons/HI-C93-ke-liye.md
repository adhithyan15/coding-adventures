# परिवार के लिए — for, with a noun in front

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-06, HI-LEX-FAMILY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *for me* in Hindi — you have had it since the possessive
chapter. Then say the word for a family.

## You'll want to know: के लिए
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-06]; assesses=[] -->

**यह परिवार के लिए है।** — This is for the family.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-06, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-06] -->

**मेरे लिए** was taught as a phrase to memorise. It was never a phrase. It was
this shape with a possessive in slot 1:

| slot 1 | slot 2 | slot 3 |
|---|---|---|
| मेरे | — | लिए |
| परिवार | के | लिए |

The top row has no separate slot 2 because **मेरे** already **is** the
possessive, bent. When slot 1 is a noun instead, the possessive has to be
supplied, and **के** is what supplies it.

That is the whole difference between the two rows, and it is the same
difference that runs through this chapter: a pronoun carries its own **के**
inside itself, a noun does not.

## Grammar Lens: what *for* covers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-06, HI-LEX-C93-REL-01] -->

**के लिए** covers two things English also puts under *for*:

| sense | example |
|---|---|
| who benefits | यह परिवार के लिए है। |
| what something is meant to do | यह चाय के लिए है। |

The second sense is the one the next lesson builds on, because a **purpose** is
usually an action rather than a thing, and an action needs one more change
before it can stand in slot 1.
