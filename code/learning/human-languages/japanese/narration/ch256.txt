Japanese, chapter 256: Which Way to the Station.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
どこですか / どうやって (doko desu ka / dō yatte) — asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "where" and "a station". (doko, eki.) Then the -て form of いく. (itte.)

You'll want to know: どこですか, どうやって.
えきは どこですか。 eki wa doko desu ka. — "Where is the station?"
To ask how to get somewhere, どうやって (dō yatte) asks "how": どう, "how", with やって, the -て form of やる, "to do".
くうこうまで どうやって いきますか。 kūkō made dō yatte ikimasu ka. — "How do I get to the airport?"
ちかてつで いきます。 chikatetsu de ikimasu. — "You go by subway."

You'll want to know: いって、まがって ください.
Directions are -て ください requests, one after another, joined with the -て form:
まっすぐ いって ください。 massugu itte kudasai. — "Please go straight on."
まっすぐ いって、みぎに まがって ください。 massugu itte, migi ni magatte kudasai. — "Go straight on, then turn right."
つぎの かどを ひだりに まがって ください。 tsugi no kado o hidari ni magatte kudasai. — "Turn left at the next corner."
ぎんこうの まえに あります。 ginkō no mae ni arimasu. — "It is in front of the bank."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Where is the station?" — eki wa doko desu ka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Where is the bank?" — ginkō wa doko desu ka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Where is the hospital?" — byōin wa doko desu ka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Ask how to get to the airport.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"How do I get there?" (どうやって いきますか.) "Please turn right"? (みぎに まがって ください.) "Go straight on, then turn left"? (まっすぐ いって、ひだりに まがって ください.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
まい (mai) — the counter for flat things.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "where is the station?" (eki wa doko desu ka.) Then "a ticket". (kippu.) People are counted with にん; flat things have a counter of their own.

You'll want to know: まい.
まい — mai — the counter for flat things: tickets, stamps, sheets, plates. The number goes in front: いちまい (ichimai), one; にまい (nimai), two; さんまい (sanmai), three.
きっぷを にまい ください。 kippu o nimai kudasai. — "Two tickets, please."
きってを さんまい ください。 kitte o sanmai kudasai. — "Three stamps, please."

The word, taken apart.
まい (mai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the on'yomi of a character KANJIDIC2 glosses "sheet of …" and "counter for flat thin objects or sheets", a reading borrowed from Chinese (KANJIDIC2). JMdict lists it as a counter, "for thin, flat objects (e.g. sheets of paper, plates, coins)" (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "One ticket, please" — kippu o ichimai kudasai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Two tickets, please" — kippu o nimai kudasai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Three tickets, please" — kippu o sanmai kudasai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Say 'two tickets, please'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two tickets? (にまい.) Three stamps? (さんまい.) Two people? (ふたり.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
きっぷを にまい ください (kippu o nimai kudasai) — at the ticket window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "two tickets, please". (kippu o nimai kudasai.) Then "a ticket window", "one way" and "a round trip". (madoguchi, katamichi, ōfuku.)

You'll want to know: 〜までの きっぷ.
くうこうまでの きっぷを ください。 kūkō made no kippu o kudasai. — "A ticket to the airport, please."
まで says how far; の joins it to the ticket: "a ticket as far as the airport". The number and まい (mai) go before ください:
くうこうまでの きっぷを にまい ください。 kūkō made no kippu o nimai kudasai. — "Two tickets to the airport, please."
かたみちですか。 katamichi desu ka. — "One way?"
いいえ、おうふくを にまい ください。 iie, ōfuku o nimai kudasai. — "No, two returns, please."

You'll want to know: なんじに でますか.
なんじ (nanji), "what time", is なん, "what", with じ (ji), "o'clock". でます (demasu) is what a train does when it leaves:
つぎの でんしゃは なんじに でますか。 tsugi no densha wa nanji ni demasu ka. — "What time does the next train leave?"
くじに でます。 kuji ni demasu. — "It leaves at nine."
のりばは どこですか。 noriba wa doko desu ka. — "Where does it leave from?"

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It leaves at nine" — kuji ni demasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It leaves at five" — goji ni demasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It leaves at seven" — shichiji ni demasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Ask what time the next train leaves.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"A ticket to the airport, please"? (くうこうまでの きっぷを ください.) "What time does it leave?" (なんじに でますか.) "Where does it leave from?" (のりばは どこですか.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
日本人 (nihonjin) — a Japanese person.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: You'll want to know: 日本人.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "the Japanese language". (nihongo.) Then "a friend". (tomodachi.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
You'll want to know: 日本人.
日本人 — nihonjin — "a Japanese person". It is 日本 (nihon), "Japan", the first part of 日本語, with じん (jin), "person", after it.
ともだちは 日本人です。 tomodachi wa nihonjin desu. — "My friend is Japanese."
You write the first two signs. The third is new, read じん (jin) here; the next lesson writes it.

The word, taken apart.
日本人 (nihonjin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ni, "sun" (Middle Chinese nyit), hon, "origin" (pwonX) and jin, "person" (nyin) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict glosses it "Japanese person" (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nihonjin]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My friend is Japanese" — tomodachi wa nihonjin desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My teacher is Japanese" — sensei wa nihonjin desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'a Japanese person'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"A Japanese person"? (日本人, nihonjin.) How is the new sign read here? (じん, jin.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
人 (jin) — a person, two strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say nihonjin, a Japanese person. You write 日 and 本. Then call up 出: the long stem first.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the shape.
人 — jin.
Its parts:
a stroke falling to the left.
a stroke falling to the right, leaning on it.
Two strokes, one lift:
draw the long falling stroke from the top, down to the left.
lift, start partway down the first stroke and draw the second down to the right.
The second stroke starts on the first, not at the top. Here it is read じん (jin), as in 日本人 (nihonjin); this lesson asks only for that reading.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
Find 人 (jin) in 日本人 and say nihonjin.
Copy 人 three times, saying jin at each one.
Find it once more, in ともだちは 日本人です (tomodachi wa nihonjin desu), and say the line.

Wrap-up Recall.
How many strokes? (Two.)
