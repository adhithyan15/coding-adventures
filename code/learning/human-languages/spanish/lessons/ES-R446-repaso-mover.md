## Grammar Lens: you owned four of these already
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-01, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-03, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-04] -->

Not one of these five was learned from nothing.

| new word | what you already had |
|---|---|
| **mirar** | *mira*, the word that opens a sentence |
| **acompañar** | *el compañero*, and the bread inside it |
| **directo** | *recto* |
| **el partido** | *la parte* |

*Mirar* is the sharpest case. You have been saying **mira** for chapters as a
way of starting a sentence, without being told it is an order from a verb.
Finding the verb behind a fixed phrase you already use is one of the cheapest
kinds of vocabulary there is, and it has happened three times now — *interesante*
gave *interesar*, *el concierto* gave *concertar*, and *mira* gives *mirar*.

When a new word looks like something you say already, check before you learn it
twice.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-01, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-02, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-04, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-05] -->

**You can now arrange to go somewhere with somebody and talk about what you
watched.** That is most of what a short message between friends is for.

You also have two distinctions a reader notices. **Ver** is the eyes receiving
and **mirar** is the eyes aimed. **Mover** is a thing you could push with your
hands and **trasladar** is an address changing. Both are register rather than
grammar, and getting them backwards is the kind of mistake that is understood
and still sounds wrong.
