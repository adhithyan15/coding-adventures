Latin, chapter 291: Prīmō, tunc, dēmum.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
prīmō — at first, at the beginning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a ditch, then the Latin for a rampart.

You'll want to know: prīmō.
prīmō — "at first, at the beginning".
Prīmō caelum serēnum erat; deinde pluit. At first the sky was clear; then it rained.
An adverb. Prīmō marks a first stage that later changes; prīmum (taught) counts the steps of a list.

The word, taken apart.
prīmō is the ablative of prīmus, "first", used as an adverb (Lewis & Short): at the first point in time. English prime and primary come from prīmus.

Lesson 2 of 5.
tunc — then, at that time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rampart, then the Latin for at first.

You'll want to know: tunc.
tunc — "then, at that time".
Tunc urbs parva erat, sed mūrī altī erant. At that time the city was small, but the walls were high.
A stronger tum (taught): at that very time. Livy and later writers use it freely in stories.

The word, taken apart.
tunc is built on tum, "then", with the pointing particle -ce that is also seen in hic (Lewis & Short): then, at that point.

Lesson 3 of 5.
antequam — before (joining two clauses).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for at first, then the Latin for then.

You'll want to know: antequam.
antequam — "before (joining two clauses)".
Antequam lūx vēnit, mīlitēs ē castrīs discessērunt. Before daylight came, the soldiers left the camp.
A conjunction, often split as ante … quam. With the perfect for a plain past event.

The word, taken apart.
antequam joins ante, "before", and quam, "than, as" (Lewis & Short): earlier than the moment when.

Lesson 4 of 5.
nōndum — not yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for then, then the Latin for before.

You'll want to know: nōndum.
nōndum — "not yet".
Nūntius nōndum vēnerat; itaque exspectābāmus. The messenger had not yet come, so we were waiting.
The opposite of iam (taught), 'already'. Nōndum + pluperfect sets a scene in which something is still awaited.

The word, taken apart.
nōndum joins nōn, "not", and dum, "yet, still" (Lewis & Short): not as yet.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēmum — at last, only then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for before, then the Latin for not yet.

You'll want to know: dēmum.
dēmum — "at last, only then".
Post multās hōrās dēmum ad urbem pervēnimus. After many hours we at last reached the city.
Tum dēmum: only then. It stresses that something came late, after waiting; close to tandem (taught).

The word, taken apart.
dēmum is explained by Lewis & Short as a superlative-like form built on dē, "down from": the lowest, last point. The details are not settled.
