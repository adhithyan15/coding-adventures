Japanese, chapter 423: A Treat and the Bill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ごちそう (gochisō) — a feast; a treat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for sliced raw fish, then the Japanese for grilled chicken on skewers.

You'll want to know: ごちそう.
ごちそう — gochisō — "a feast; a treat".
そふの うちで ごちそうを たべました。 — sofu no uchi de gochisō o tabemashita — "We had a feast at my grandfather's house."

The word, taken apart.
ごちそう (gochisō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the polite prefix go- (Middle Chinese ngjoH) and two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: chi, "gallop" (drje), and sō, "run" (tsuwX); JMdict gives the sense "a treat" without saying how the running came to mean it (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
わりかん (warikan) — splitting the bill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a feast, then the Japanese for paying the bill.

You'll want to know: わりかん.
わりかん — warikan — "splitting the bill".
ともだちと わりかんで はらいました。 — tomodachi to warikan de haraimashita — "My friend and I split the bill."

The word, taken apart.
わりかん (warikan) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the native Japanese (wago) wari, "splitting" (from waru), inherited rather than borrowed, and the Sino-Japanese kan, "check" (Middle Chinese khomH), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おごる (ogoru) — to treat someone; to buy someone a meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for paying the bill, then the Japanese for splitting the bill.

You'll want to know: おごる.
おごる — ogoru — "to treat someone; to buy someone a meal".
ともだちに ひるごはんを おごりました。 — tomodachi ni hirugohan o ogorimashita — "I treated my friend to lunch."
Polite おごります, ogorimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが ばんごはんを おごりました。, ani ga bangohan o ogorimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おごる (ogoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

まんぷく (manpuku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are man, "full" (Middle Chinese manX) and fuku, "belly" (pjuwk), joined as manpuku (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
