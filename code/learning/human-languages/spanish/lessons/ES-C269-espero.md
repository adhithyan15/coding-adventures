# espero viajar — saying what you are reaching for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ambitions are things you have not got yet. Spanish says them with a
verb you have already met wearing a different coat.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [feliz](./ES-C269-feliz.md) — the feeling this chapter opened with.
- [esperar](./ES-C40-esperar.md) — where you first met this verb, meaning *wait*.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- Three syllables, stress in the middle: *es-PE-ro*. →
  [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-ESPERAR-HOPE]; assesses=[] -->

**espero** — "I hope." The same *esperar* you learned as "to wait," and Spanish
sees no reason to separate them. To wait for something and to hope for it are one
act: you are standing in the present with your attention on a future you cannot
reach yet.

**Where it comes from.** Latin *sperare*, "to hope" — the root of English
**prosper** ("to hope forward") and, negated, **desperate**, which is literally
*de-sperare*, to have hope taken away. Spanish keeps the plain form; English kept
the compounds.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SEMANTIC-HOPE-AND-WAIT]; assesses=[] -->

This is your ambition frame. *Espero* plus an infinitive says what you are
reaching for: *espero viajar*, *espero aprender*, *espero vivir en España*. Every
one of those infinitives is a verb you already own, so the frame costs you
nothing new and turns your whole verb list into statements about your future.
