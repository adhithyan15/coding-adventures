Lesson 1 of 6.
fànguǎn — restaurant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bathroom, then the Mandarin for a curtain.

You'll want to know: fànguǎn.
fànguǎn — "restaurant".
wǒ jiā hòubian yǒu yí ge fànguǎn — There's a restaurant behind our place.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fànguǎn is built from 饭 'meal' and guǎn 'lodging house', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjonH and kwanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), though the falling tone of kwanH would regularly give guàn.

Lesson 2 of 6.
冷菜 (lěngcài) — cold starter; cold dishes (on a menu).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a curtain, then the Mandarin for a restaurant.

You'll want to know: 冷菜.
冷菜 — lěngcài — "cold starter; cold dishes (on a menu)".
我们要两个冷菜 wǒmen yào liǎng ge lěngcài — We'd like two cold starters.
The new character, in pieces: 冷 (lěng) — seven strokes: the ice piece on the left (a dot, then a stroke rising to the right), and on the right 今 as you write it, with one more dot under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
冷菜 (lěngcài) is built from 冷 (lěng) 'cold' and 菜 'dish', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lengX and tshojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'cold' and calls the second any plant fit to eat; 'a dish' is the later sense. The dishes a meal starts with, served cold.

Script — the ice piece, then 今 and a dot.
冷 (lěng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The ice piece, two strokes. A dot. Then, under it, a short stroke rising to the upper right.
The right piece, five strokes: 今, then a dot. 今 as you write it: a stroke falling to the left, one falling to the right, a dot under the roof, and a horizontal that turns and falls to the left. Last, one more dot under it.
2 + 4 + 1 = seven.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 冷 (lěng)? (Seven.) How many in the ice piece? (Two: a dot, then a rising stroke.) Which character makes most of the right-hand piece? (今, with one more dot.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
小吃 (xiǎochī) — snack; street food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a restaurant, then the Mandarin for a cold starter.

You'll want to know: 小吃.
小吃 — xiǎochī — "snack; street food".
中午没有时间我们吃小吃 zhōngwǔ méiyǒu shíjiān wǒmen chī xiǎochī — There's no time at lunch, so we'll grab a snack.

The word, taken apart.
小吃 (xiǎochī) is built from 小 'small' and 吃 'to eat': a small eat. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese sjewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); for the second the dictionary has khek, glossed 'to eat', under another way of writing it, though khek would regularly give qì.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 家常菜.
家常菜 — jiāchángcài — "home cooking; everyday dishes".
我们去对面吃家常菜 wǒmen qù duìmiàn chī jiāchángcài — Let's go across the road for some home cooking.

The word, taken apart.
家常菜 (jiāchángcài) is built from 家 'home', 常 'everyday' and 菜 'dish', all three inherited from Middle Chinese, kae, dzyang and tshojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the dishes a home makes every day. The everyday sense of the second is not given there.
