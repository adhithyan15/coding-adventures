Arabic, chapter 450: Town hall and consulate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بلدية (baladiyya) — a town hall; a municipality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to grumble, then the Arabic for to swap.

You'll want to know: بلدية.
بلدية — baladiyya — "a town hall; a municipality".
ذهبت إلى البلدية لدفع الرسوم. dhahabtu ilā al-baladiyya li-dafʿ ar-rusūm. I went to the town hall to pay the fees.

The word, taken apart.
بلدية (baladiyya) is built on the native Arabic root b-l-d, "a town, a country" (Lane). It is a noun in -iyya made from balad: the body that runs a town.

You'll want to know: قنصلية.
قنصلية — qunṣuliyya — "a consulate".
القنصلية مغلقة يوم الجمعة. al-qunṣuliyya mughlaqa yawm al-jumʿa. The consulate is closed on Friday.

The word, taken apart.
قنصلية (qunṣuliyya) is usually traced to the European word consul, from Latin, with the Arabic ending -iyya.

The word, taken apart.
دوام (dawām) is built on the native Arabic root d-w-m, "to last, to continue" (Lane). Working hours are the time one keeps at a job.

تقدم — taqaddama — "to apply (for); to step forward".
تقدمت أختي بطلب تأشيرة. taqaddamat ukhtī bi-ṭalab taʾshīra. My sister applied for a visa.
Present: يتقدم, yataqaddam.
taqaddama bi-ṭalab: to put in an application.

The word, taken apart.
تقدم (taqaddama) is built on the native Arabic root q-d-m, "to come forward, to precede" (Lane). It is Form V.
