## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C48-DENY-03]; assesses=[] -->

**malus** — "**bad**."

The adjective that **male** was cut from. It changes its ending to match its noun,
exactly as **bonus** does in *bonam noctem*: *malus* (m.), *mala* (f.), *malum* (n.).

Now the coincidence that changed a story. Latin also had **mālum**, "**apple**,"
borrowed from Greek *mēlon* — a different word, with a long *ā*, and in writing
without macrons the two look identical. Genesis names no fruit at all: it says
only "the fruit of the tree." When Latin translators wrote of the tree of *mālum*,
the pun with *malum*, "evil," was there for the taking, and medieval art took it.

**Treat that as the standard account rather than as settled fact**: the pun is
real and the timing is right, and no single document records anybody deciding it.
English **malevolent**, **malefactor** and **malcontent** are all *malus*.
