# ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, മുൻപ്, ശേഷം — ordering a run, and ordering against a point

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C68-ORDINAL-08, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *at first*. Say *then*. You have had both for a long while.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C68-ORDINAL-08, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01, ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01, ML-CONCEPT-C40-OON-01] -->

You now have four words about order, and they split into **two pairs that do
different jobs** — though not the job you might expect.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **ആദ്യം**, **പിന്നെ** | *ādyaṁ*, *pinne* | order a **run** of things |
| **മുൻപ്**, **ശേഷം** | *munpŭ*, *śēṣaṁ* | order one thing against a **named point** |

**Both pairs order real events.** You were shown **ആദ്യം ഊണ്, പിന്നെ ചായ** —
lunch first, then tea — and that is the world being ordered, not a story about
it.

**What the first pair cannot do is name what it is measuring against.** It lays
things out in a run: this, then that. **മുൻപ്** and **ശേഷം** do something the
other pair has no way to do — they take a **named** time or event and place
something relative to it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01] -->

**Until this chapter you could put events in a run and not anchor them.**
*First lunch, then tea* says which came first and says nothing about when either
happened. *Before two o'clock* and *after the meal* fix an event against a point
you have **named**.

That is the whole of what the two new words add — and neither of them needed a
new piece of grammar to do it.
