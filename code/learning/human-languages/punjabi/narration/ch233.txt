Punjabi, chapter 233: An Afternoon Rest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਆਰਾਮ (ārām) — rest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to come unstitched, then the Punjabi for to stuff.

You'll want to know: ਆਰਾਮ.
ਆਰਾਮ — ārām — "rest".
ਦੁਪਹਿਰ ਨੂੰ ਦਾਦਾ ਜੀ ਆਰਾਮ ਕਰਦੇ ਹਨ। dupahir nū̃ dādā jī ārām karde han. My grandfather rests in the afternoon.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਆਰਾਮ (ārām) is borrowed: it is usually traced to Persian ārām, "rest, quiet".

Lesson 2 of 5.
ਦਰੀ (darī) — a cotton rug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to stuff, then the Punjabi for rest.

You'll want to know: ਦਰੀ.
ਦਰੀ — darī — "a cotton rug".
ਫ਼ਰਸ਼ ਉੱਤੇ ਇੱਕ ਨੀਲੀ ਦਰੀ ਹੈ। farsh utte ikk nīlī darī hai. There is a blue rug on the floor.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਦਰੀ (darī) is inherited from a reconstructed darita, "split cane, mat"; CDIAL lists Punjabi darī, "carpet" (CDIAL 6194).

You'll want to know: ਵਿਛਾਉਣਾ.
ਵਿਛਾਉਣਾ — vichhāuṇā — "to spread out (a rug or bedding)".
ਮੈਂ ਫ਼ਰਸ਼ ਉੱਤੇ ਦਰੀ ਵਿਛਾਉਂਦੀ ਹਾਂ। maiṁ farsh utte darī vichhāundī hāṁ. I spread the rug on the floor.
Past (I …): ਮੈਂ ਚਾਦਰ ਵਿਛਾਈ, maiṁ chādar vichhāī.

The word, taken apart.
ਵਿਛਾਉਣਾ (vichhāuṇā) is inherited from a reconstructed vicchādayati, "spread out"; CDIAL lists Punjabi vichāuṇā, "to spread out (bedding &c.)" (CDIAL 11692).
