Japanese, chapter 417: Carrying the Luggage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
てにもつ (tenimotsu) — hand luggage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to turn very cold, then the Japanese for to shine on.

You'll want to know: てにもつ.
てにもつ — tenimotsu — "hand luggage".
てにもつを ひとつ もって、ひこうきに のりました。 — tenimotsu o hitotsu motte, hikōki ni norimashita — "I got on the plane with one piece of hand luggage."

The word, taken apart.
てにもつ (tenimotsu) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the native Japanese (wago) te, "hand", inherited rather than borrowed; the native Japanese (wago) ni, "load", inherited rather than borrowed; and the Sino-Japanese motsu, "thing" (Middle Chinese mjut), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

せおう — seou — "to carry on one's back".

せおう (seou) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from se, "back", and ou, "to carry on one's back"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
