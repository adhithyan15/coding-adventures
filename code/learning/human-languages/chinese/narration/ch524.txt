Mandarin Chinese, chapter 524: Half Price.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiézhàng — to settle the bill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the buyer (in a sale), then the Mandarin for to sell retail.

You'll want to know: jiézhàng.
jiézhàng — "to settle the bill".
wǒmen děng le èrshí fēnzhōng cái jiézhàng — We waited twenty minutes before we could settle the bill.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiézhàng is built from jié 'to tie' and zhàng 'an account'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese ket (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to tie'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
半价 (bànjià) — half price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sell retail, then the Mandarin for to settle the bill.

You'll want to know: 半价.
半价 — bànjià — "half price".
今天这儿的东西都半价 jīntiān zhèr de dōngxi dōu bànjià — Everything here is half price today.
The new character, in pieces: 价 (jià) — six strokes in three pieces: the person piece on the left, two strokes, as in 你; 人 on the right, two strokes; and under 人 a short stroke falling to the left and a vertical. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
半价 (bànjià) is built from 半 'half' and 价 'a price', the jià of the word for price, both inherited from Middle Chinese, panH and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a thing split in the middle and the second as the count of a price.

Script — the person piece, then 人 with two legs under it.
价 (jià).
Three pieces: the person piece, then 人, then what stands under it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the person piece, two strokes, as in 你: a stroke falling to the left, then a vertical down to the foot.
The top right piece, 人, two strokes, as you wrote it, spread wide like a roof: a long stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right.
Under it, two strokes: a short stroke that drops and falls to the left, then a vertical on the right.
2 + 2 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 价 (jià), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 价 (jià) have? (Six.) What is the first piece? (The person piece, on the left.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
要价 (yàojià) — to name one's price; to ask a price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to settle the bill, then the Mandarin for at half price.

You'll want to know: 要价.
要价 — yàojià — "to name one's price; to ask a price".
tā yàojià bǐ nà jiā shāngdiàn gāo — He's asking more than that shop.

The word, taken apart.
要价 (yàojià) is built from 要 'to want' and 价 (jià) 'a price', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jiewH and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to bind by agreement' and the second as the count of a price.

还价 — huánjià — "to offer a lower price back; to make a counter-offer".
在这儿买东西最好还价 zài zhèr mǎi dōngxi zuìhǎo huánjià — When you buy things here, it's best to offer less.
