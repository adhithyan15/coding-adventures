German, chapter 324: Rühren.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rühren — to stir; to move (the heart).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to grate, then the German for to halve.

You'll want to know: rühren.
rühren — "to stir; to move (the heart)".
Er rührte die Suppe langsam. He stirred the soup slowly.
Also to touch the heart: Das Lied rührte sie.

The word, taken apart.
rühren is inherited from Old High German ruoren, from Proto-Germanic hrōzijaną "to stir" (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
verquirlen — to whisk, to beat (eggs).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to stir, then the German for to give a stir.

You'll want to know: verquirlen.
verquirlen — "to whisk, to beat (eggs)".
Er verquirlte die Eier mit etwas Milch. He whisked the eggs with a little milk.
Built on der Quirl, a kitchen whisk.

The word, taken apart.
verquirlen is built on Quirl "a stirring stick, a whisk", usually traced to Old High German dweran "to turn"; EtymDB lists no parent for Quirl.

Lesson 4 of 5.
aufschlagen — to crack open (an egg); to open (a book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to give a stir, then the German for to whisk.

You'll want to know: aufschlagen.
aufschlagen — "to crack open (an egg); to open (a book)".
Sie schlug drei Eier auf. She cracked three eggs.
Separable: sie schlägt auf. Also to open a book: das Buch aufschlagen.

The word, taken apart.
aufschlagen joins auf "open" and schlagen "to strike", an inherited Germanic verb; EtymDB lists no parent for the compound.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Schneebesen — the whisk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to whisk, then the German for to crack open.

You'll want to know: der Schneebesen.
der Schneebesen — "the whisk".
Mit dem Schneebesen schlug er die Sahne steif. He whipped the cream stiff with the whisk.
Plural: die Schneebesen. Schnee 'snow' plus Besen 'broom': beaten egg white looks like snow.

The word, taken apart.
Schneebesen joins Schnee "snow" and Besen "broom", Old High German besamo, Proto-Germanic besamô (EtymDB).
