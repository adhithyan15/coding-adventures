Spanish, chapter 526: El rescate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rescatar — to rescue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to help, then the Spanish for the injured person.

You'll want to know: rescatar.
rescatar — "to rescue".
Rescataron a los niños del río. They rescued the children from the river.

The word, taken apart.
rescatar comes from spoken Latin reexcaptāre 'to take back' (EtymDB), built on captāre 'to try to catch', from capere 'to take'.

El médico le salvó la vida. The doctor saved his life.
To save money is ahorrar, not salvar.

The word, taken apart.
salvar is inherited from Late Latin salvāre 'to save' (EtymDB), built on salvus 'safe, unharmed'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
atrapar — to trap, to catch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to save, then the Spanish for to survive.

You'll want to know: atrapar.
atrapar — "to trap, to catch".
El humo atrapó a la familia en el piso. The smoke trapped the family in the flat.
Quedarse atrapado — to be trapped.

The word, taken apart.
atrapar is usually traced to French attraper 'to catch in a trap', built on trappe 'a trap', a word of Frankish origin. English trap comes from the same Germanic family.
