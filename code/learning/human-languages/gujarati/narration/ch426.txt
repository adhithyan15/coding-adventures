Lesson 1 of 5.
લાખ (lākh) — a lakh (one hundred thousand).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a pledge, then the Gujarati for paid in full.

You'll want to know: લાખ.
લાખ — lākh — "a lakh (one hundred thousand)".
આ ગાડીની કિંમત પાંચ લાખ રૂપિયા છે. ā gāḍīnī kimmat pā̃c lākh rūpiyā che. This car costs five lakh rupees.

The word, taken apart.
લાખ (lākh) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit lakṣa, "a mark; a hundred thousand"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 10881). Indian numbers count in lakhs and crores.

Lesson 3 of 5.
બાદબાકી (bādbākī) — subtraction.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lakh, then the Gujarati for a crore.

You'll want to know: બાદબાકી.
બાદબાકી — bādbākī — "subtraction".
બાળકો શાળામાં બાદબાકી શીખે છે. bāḷko śāḷāmā̃ bādbākī śīkhe che. Children learn subtraction at school.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
બાદબાકી (bādbākī) is built from બાદ, "after, taken away", usually traced to Arabic baʿd, "after", and બાકી, "what remains", usually traced to Arabic bāqī: you take away and look at what is left.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ગુણવું (guṇvũ) — to multiply (numbers).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a crore, then the Gujarati for subtraction.

You'll want to know: ગુણવું.
ગુણવું — guṇvũ — "to multiply (numbers)".
મીરાએ કાગળ પર બે મોટી રકમ ગુણી. Mīrāe kāgaḷ par be moṭī rakam guṇī. Mira multiplied two large numbers on paper.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે રકમ ગુણી, teṇe rakam guṇī.

The word, taken apart.
ગુણવું (guṇvũ) comes from Sanskrit guṇayati, "multiplies" (Monier-Williams), built on guṇa, a strand or a fold: a number folded over on itself.

Lesson 5 of 5.
વધારવું (vadhārvũ) — to increase; to raise (a price).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for subtraction, then the Gujarati for to multiply.

You'll want to know: વધારવું.
વધારવું — vadhārvũ — "to increase; to raise (a price)".
દુકાનદારે ભાવ વધાર્યો. dukāndāre bhāv vadhāryo. The shopkeeper put the price up.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ભાડું વધાર્યું, teṇe bhāḍũ vadhāryũ.

The word, taken apart.
વધારવું (vadhārvũ) is built on વધારે, more, a tadbhava of Sanskrit vṛddhatara, "larger" (CDIAL 12074): to make something more. વધવું, to go up, is its partner.
