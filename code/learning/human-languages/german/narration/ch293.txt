German, chapter 293: Es brennt.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
brennen — to burn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to evacuate, then the German for to survive.

You'll want to know: brennen.
brennen — "to burn".
Das Haus brannte die ganze Nacht. The house burned all night.
Mixed verb: brannte, hat gebrannt. Es brennt! — fire!

The word, taken apart.
brennen is an inherited Germanic verb: EtymDB traces Old High German brennen to Proto-Germanic brannijaną, built on brinnaną "to burn", and lists Old English birnan "to burn" among its cousins: the family of English burn.

Die Flammen waren schon hoch über dem Dach. The flames were already high above the roof.
Plural: die Flammen. In Flammen stehen — to be on fire.

The word, taken apart.
Flamme was borrowed from Latin flamma "a flame, a blaze" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB records the loan already in Old High German flamma.

Lesson 4 of 6.
löschen — to put out, to extinguish; to delete.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the smoke, then the German for the flame.

You'll want to know: löschen.
löschen — "to put out, to extinguish; to delete".
Die Feuerwehr löschte das Feuer in einer Stunde. The fire brigade put out the fire in an hour.
On a computer it means to delete: ein Foto löschen.

The word, taken apart.
löschen is inherited from Middle High German leschen, Old High German lescan (EtymDB). The old verb is usually glossed "to go out, to die down"; later it came to mean "to make go out".
