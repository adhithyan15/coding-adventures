# Synthesis — a verb you have never met

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-INDICATIVE-COMPLETE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new in this chapter, and nothing to memorise. Say the six
endings of a regular *-er* verb.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-INDICATIVE-COMPLETE, ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-CHANGE-STRESS-RULE, ES-GRAMMAR-YO-GO-PLURAL-REGULAR] -->

Everything you hold about the present tense now fits in three lines:

1. **Three families**, six endings each; `-er` and `-ir` differ only in
   *nosotros* and *vosotros*.
2. **A stem breaks exactly where it is stressed** — so *nosotros* and *vosotros*
   never break.
3. **A `-go` sits in the *yo* form and nowhere else.**

That is the whole system. Not a summary of it — the system.

And a system, unlike a table, works on words it has never seen.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-CHANGE-STRESS-RULE, ES-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-INDICATIVE-COMPLETE] -->

Here is a verb this book has not taught you: **beber**, "to drink" — you have
met the word, but never its full set.

Do not look anything up. It is a regular `-er` verb, so:

> **bebo** · **bebes** · **bebe** · **bebemos** · **bebéis** · **beben**

Six forms, and the only thing you needed was rule 1.

Now one that uses all three rules. **Salir** is an `-ir` verb, and it is in the
`-go` club. Rules 1 and 3 give you every form, and you can produce them before
reading on:

> **salgo** · **sales** · **sale** · **salimos** · **salís** · **salen**

One odd form, in the one slot rule 3 names. The other five are plain `-ir`.

Four broken, two whole, and the two whole ones are the two the stress leaves
alone. Every time.
