Lesson 2 of 5.
One person noun for a traveller — traveller or person who travels.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve the familiar first and last words of the identity frame.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਯਾਤਰੀ (yaatrii) — yātrī — traveller; person who travels.
Hear and say the whole word before reading it. Keep it as one whole person noun rather than inventing smaller Punjabi pieces. This lesson owns the word alone; the complete identity frame comes only after a separate retrieval.

The word, taken apart — where the trail runs out.
Current Punjab School Education Board Class 11 material gives this word as the meaning of another traveller term. PSEB's Class 5 book independently uses it for a person who travels beyond Earth. Together they support the bounded person-who-travels meaning in current educational Punjabi. Sources: PSEB Class 11 and PSEB Class 5.
The exact origin of modern written ਯਾਤਰੀ (yaatrii) remains unknown in the cited sources. Its origin remains unknown here. This lesson therefore makes no etymological claim. Keep the present-day noun whole rather than turning a spelling resemblance into a derivation.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: ਯਾਤਰੀ (yaatrii)]

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 5.
Put the new person noun into one identity frame — this person is a traveller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve ਇਹ, then the new person noun, then ਹੈ.

Grammar Lens.
ਇਹ ਯਾਤਰੀ ਹੈ (eh yaatrii hai) — eh yātrī hai — this person is a traveller.
Keep the new whole noun between familiar ਇਹ and ਹੈ. Familiar ਹੈ stays last in the already-owned identity frame. This chapter teaches only this one person label; it does not add a form rule or a wider construction.
