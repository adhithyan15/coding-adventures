You'll want to know: шляпа.
шляпа (shlyápa) — "a brimmed hat".

The word, taken apart.
шляпа (shlyápa) — recorded from 1589; it comes from Bavarian Schlappe, "a cap", Middle High German slappe, "a soft, drooping hat" (Vasmer).

The word, taken apart.
ремень (remén) from Proto-Slavic remy, remene — inherited. Vasmer holds that a loan from Old High German riumo, "a strap", is impossible on the evidence of Old Church Slavonic and old word-building.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: карман.
карман (karmán) — "a pocket".

The word, taken apart.
карман (karmán) — Old Russian already had the name Kormanŭ. Turkic karman, "a pocket, a purse", was once taken as the source; Radloff saw it as a loan from Russian, and Räsänen again argued for a Turkic origin (Vasmer).

Lesson 4 of 5.
пуговица (púgovitsa) — a button.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a belt, then the Russian for a pocket.

You'll want to know: пуговица.
пуговица (púgovitsa) — "a button".

The word, taken apart.
пуговица (púgovitsa) from Old Russian pugŭvĭ, пугы, "a button", with -ица. It is usually compared with Latvian puoga, "a button", and Sanskrit puñja-, "a lump" (Vasmer), though Persson separated the Sanskrit.

Lesson 5 of 5.
серьги (sérgi) — earrings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pocket, then the Russian for a button.

You'll want to know: серьги.
серьги (sérgi) — "earrings".

The word, taken apart.
серьги (sérgi) is the plural of серьга, "an earring". One view reshapes Old Russian усерязь, "an earring", but Vasmer finds the г hard to explain and prefers a loan from an Old Chuvash word for "a ring"; Räsänen, in Trubachev's note, derives it from Chagatai and other Turkic isirga, "ear pendants".
