Telugu, chapter 338: Little by Little.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
క్రమంగా (kramaṅgā) — gradually; step by step.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for shackles, then the Telugu for the accused.

You'll want to know: క్రమంగా.
క్రమంగా — kramaṅgā — "gradually; step by step".
క్రమంగా అతని ఆరోగ్యం బాగుపడింది. kramaṅgā atani ārōgyaṁ bāgupaḍindi. Gradually his health got better.
క్రమం, order or sequence, with -గా: in order, step by step.

The word, taken apart.
క్రమంగా (kramaṅgā) is built on Sanskrit krama, "a step, a series" (CDIAL 3577), with the Telugu adverb ending -gā; CDIAL notes that Sanskrit kramēṇa already meant "by degrees".

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: రానురాను.
రానురాను — rānurānu — "as time went by".
రానురాను ఆ పల్లె పట్టణంగా మారింది. rānurānu ā palle paṭṭaṇaṅgā mārindi. As time went by, that village grew into a town.
రాను, coming, said twice: as the days keep coming.

The word, taken apart.
రానురాను (rānurānu) is native Telugu: the verb rā, "to come" (DEDR 5270), said twice.

Lesson 3 of 5.
మునుపు (munupu) — previously; before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for gradually, then the Telugu for as time went by.

You'll want to know: మునుపు.
మునుపు — munupu — "previously; before".
మునుపు ఇక్కడ ఒక చెరువు ఉండేది. munupu ikkaḍa oka ceruvu uṇḍēdi. Previously there was a pond here.
Close to ముందు; మునుపటి means former: మునుపటి రాజు, the former king.

The word, taken apart.
మునుపు (munupu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5020 lists Telugu munupu, "former time; before", with Tamil muṉpu; ముందు comes from the same root.

Lesson 4 of 5.
గతం (gataṁ) — the past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for as time went by, then the Telugu for previously.

You'll want to know: గతం.
గతం — gataṁ — "the past".
ఆమె తరచుగా గతం గుర్తుచేసుకుంటుంది. āme taracugā gataṁ gurtucēsukuṇṭundi. She often recalls the past.
You know గత, last or past: గత వారం. గతం (gataṁ) is the noun.

The word, taken apart.
గతం (gataṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit gata, "gone, past" (CDIAL 4008); as a noun it is what has gone by.

Lesson 5 of 5.
అప్పట్లో (appaṭlō) — in those days.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for previously, then the Telugu for the past.

You'll want to know: అప్పట్లో.
అప్పట్లో — appaṭlō — "in those days".
అప్పట్లో ఇక్కడ బస్సులు లేవు. appaṭlō ikkaḍa bassulu lēvu. In those days there were no buses here.
A spoken form: అప్పట్లో (appaṭlō) looks back at a whole period, అప్పుడు at one moment.

The word, taken apart.
అప్పట్లో (appaṭlō) is native Telugu: appaṭi, "of that time", from appuḍu, "then" (DEDR 4559), with the locative -lō, "in".
