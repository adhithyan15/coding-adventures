Latin, chapter 368: The Orator.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ōrātor, ōrātōris — a speaker, an orator; an envoy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for that … not, then the Latin for as.

You'll want to know: ōrātor, ōrātōris.
ōrātor, ōrātōris — "a speaker, an orator; an envoy".
Ōrātor ā populō laudātus est. The speaker was praised by the people.
ōrātor, ōrātōris m. Built on ōrō, to speak, to plead. An envoy who speaks for his city is also an ōrātor. English orator.

The word, taken apart.
ōrātor is built on ōrō "to speak, to plead" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor of the one who does it. English orator comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ēloquentia, ēloquentiae — eloquence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for as, then the Latin for a speaker.

You'll want to know: ēloquentia, ēloquentiae.
ēloquentia, ēloquentiae — "eloquence".
Ēloquentia eius iūdicēs mōvit. His eloquence moved the judges.
ēloquentia, ēloquentiae f. Built on ēloquēns, speaking out well. English eloquence.

The word, taken apart.
ēloquentia is built on ēloquēns "speaking out well" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the participle of a verb made from ē "out" and loquor "to speak". English eloquence comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
disserō, disserere — to discuss, to hold forth (on).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for eloquence, then the Latin for a discussion.

You'll want to know: disserō, disserere.
disserō, disserere — "to discuss, to hold forth (on)".
Senex dē amīcitiā disseruit. The old man spoke at length about friendship.
disserō, disserere, disseruī, dissertum. Dē + ablative names the subject. It is a different verb from serō, to sow. English dissertation.

The word, taken apart.
disserō is built from dis- "apart" and serō, serere, seruī "to join, to string together" (Lewis & Short), a different verb from serō "to sow": to set ideas out in a row. English dissertation comes from it.

Deponent: ēloquor, ēloquī, ēlocūtus sum. Ē + loquor: to speak out. English eloquent.

The word, taken apart.
ēloquor is built from ē "out" and loquor "to speak" (Lewis & Short). English eloquent comes from its present participle.
