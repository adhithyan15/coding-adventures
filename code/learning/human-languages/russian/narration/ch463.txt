Russian, chapter 463: At the seaside.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
прилив (prilív) — a high tide, a flood tide; a surge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to light, then the Russian for to put out.

You'll want to know: прилив.
прилив (prilív) — "a high tide, a flood tide; a surge".
Во время прилива вода подходит к дороге. Vo vrémya prilíva vodá podkhódit k doróge. At high tide the water comes up to the road.
Masculine. Прилив сил — a surge of energy.

The word, taken apart.
прилив (prilív) is built from при- 'up to' and -лив 'a pouring', from лить 'to pour', which EtymDB takes from Proto-Slavic liti.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Дети собирают ракушки на пляже. Déti sobiráyut rakúshki na plyázhe. The children are collecting seashells on the beach.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ракушка (rakúshka) is built on раковина 'a shell' (EtymDB), a word that Old East Slavic used for mother of pearl.

Lesson 5 of 5.
нырять (nyryát) — to dive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bay, then the Russian for a seashell.

You'll want to know: нырять.
нырять (nyryát) — "to dive".
Мальчики ныряют с камня в море. Málchiki nyryáyut s kámnya v móre. The boys dive from a rock into the sea.
Imperfective; perfective нырнуть.

The word, taken apart.
нырять (nyryát) is usually linked with нора 'a burrow': to dive is to go down into a hole. EtymDB lists the word without a source, so the line is hedged.
