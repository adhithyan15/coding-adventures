Hindi, chapter 485: Feeling Unwell.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बदहज़मी (badhazmī) — indigestion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to rub in, then the Hindi for a razor.

You'll want to know: बदहज़मी.
बदहज़मी — badhazmī — "indigestion".
ज़्यादा मिठाई खाकर अरुण को बदहज़मी हो गई। zyādā miṭhāī khākar Aruṇ ko badhazmī ho gaī. Arun got indigestion from eating too many sweets.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
बदहज़मी (badhazmī) is usually traced to Persian bad-hazmī, "indigestion", which joins Persian bad, "bad", to Arabic haḍm, "digestion", said hazm in Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
मूर्छा (mūrchā) — a faint; a swoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a razor, then the Hindi for indigestion.

You'll want to know: मूर्छा.
मूर्छा — mūrchā — "a faint; a swoon".
गर्मी से एक आदमी को सड़क पर मूर्छा आ गई। garmī se ek ādmī ko saṛak par mūrchā ā gaī. A man fainted in the street from the heat.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
मूर्छा (mūrchā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit mūrchā, "fainting, a swoon, stupor" (Monier-Williams). mūrchā ānā is to faint; everyday speech often says behoś honā.

Lesson 3 of 5.
पेचिश (peciś) — dysentery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for indigestion, then the Hindi for a faint.

You'll want to know: पेचिश.
पेचिश — peciś — "dysentery".
गंदा पानी पीकर अरुण को पेचिश हो गई। gandā pānī pīkar Aruṇ ko peciś ho gaī. Arun got dysentery from drinking dirty water.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
पेचिश (peciś) is usually traced to Persian pēchish, "twisting, gripes", from pēch, "a twist": the illness is named for the cramps in the belly.

Lesson 4 of 5.
उबकना (ubaknā) — to retch; to throw up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a faint, then the Hindi for dysentery.

You'll want to know: उबकना.
उबकना — ubaknā — "to retch; to throw up".
बासी दूध पीकर बच्चा उबक गया। bāsī dūdh pīkar baccā ubak gayā. The child threw up after drinking stale milk.
Past (he …): वह उबक गया, vah ubak gayā.

The word, taken apart.
उबकना (ubaknā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi ubaknā, "to vomit", under ubbakka, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 2337). ulṭī, "vomiting", is the noun most people use.
