Arabic, chapter 284: A Story, In Order.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أولا، ثم، بعد ذلك (awwalan, thumma, baʿda dhālika) — first, then, after that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "we went", "we ate", "we came back". (Dhahabnā, akalnā, rajaʿnā.)
[pause 1 second]
Three events in a row are a list. A few small words turn them into a story, and you already own most of them.

Grammar Lens: first, then, after that.
Awwalan opens the first step. Thumma moves to the next, with a little time between. Baʿda dhālika, "after that", is baʿda with dhālika, "that", the same dhālika that sits inside li-dhālika.
أولا ذهبنا إلى السوق، ثم أكلنا في مطعم، وبعد ذلك رجعنا إلى الفندق. — awwalan dhahabnā ilā as-sūq, thumma akalnā fī maṭʿam, wa-baʿda dhālika rajaʿnā ilā al-funduq — First we went to the market, then we ate in a restaurant, and after that we went back to the hotel.
Each joiner stands at the head of its own step, and each step has its own past verb.

Grammar Lens: closing the story.
في النهاية — fī an-nihāya — "in the end". Nihāya is "an end", from the root of intahā, "to end".
في النهاية وجدت المفتاح. — fī an-nihāya wajadtu al-miftāḥ — In the end I found the key.
Akhīran, "at last", closes a story too, and it usually carries relief: the thing you were waiting for came.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "First we went to the market" — awwalan dhahabnā ilā as-sūq]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "then we ate in a restaurant" — thumma akalnā fī maṭʿam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "and after that we went back to the hotel" — wa-baʿda dhālika rajaʿnā ilā al-funduq]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"After that"? (Baʿda dhālika.) "In the end"? (Fī an-nihāya.) Which root does nihāya share with "to end"? (N-h-y, as in intahā.) Which closer often carries relief? (Akhīran, at last.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
حكى (ḥakā) — to tell a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "first", "then", "after that". (Awwalan, thumma, baʿda dhālika.) And the Arabic for a story. (Qiṣṣa.)
[pause 1 second]
You can put a story in order now. This is the verb for handing it on.

You'll want to know: حكى.
حكى — ḥakā — "he told (a story)", "to tell, to relate".
حكت جدتي قصة جميلة. — ḥakat jaddatī qiṣṣatan jamīla — My grandmother told a lovely story.
It ends like qaḍā, and it bends the same way: ḥakaytu, I told, as qaḍaytu, I spent. The present is yaḥkī, he tells, and with kāna in front, kānat taḥkī, she used to tell.
Qāla reports what somebody said; ḥakā passes on a whole story, a piece of news or something that happened.

The word, taken apart.
حكى (ḥakā) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-y, "to relate, to narrate; to imitate" (Lane). Lane gives both senses side by side: to tell a story is to say again what was said or done, and to imitate is to do again what someone did.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ḥakā, then ḥakaytu and ḥakat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My grandmother told a lovely story" — ḥakat jaddatī qiṣṣatan jamīla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She used to tell a story every day" — kānat taḥkī qiṣṣatan kulla yawm]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He said", then "he told a story" — qāla, ḥakā qiṣṣa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does حكى (ḥakā) mean? ("To tell a story".) "I told"? (Ḥakaytu, like qaḍaytu.) "He tells"? (Yaḥkī.) What else can the root mean, in Lane? (To imitate.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
عندما (ʿindamā) — when, in a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "she told a story". (Ḥakat qiṣṣa.) Then say ḥīnamā jāʾa, kataba, "when he came, he wrote".
[pause 1 second]
Ḥīnamā has a twin that you will hear far more often in a story.

Grammar Lens: ʿindamā and the past.
عندما — ʿindamā — "when". It is ʿinda, "at", with mā after it, as ḥīnamā is ḥīna, "time", with mā. Put a past verb after it, and the clause names the moment the next event hangs on:
عندما وصلنا إلى المطار، اتصلت بزوجتي. — ʿindamā waṣalnā ilā al-maṭār, ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī — When we arrived at the airport, I phoned my wife.
The ʿindamā clause can follow as well: ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī ʿindamā waṣalnā. Either way, the arrival comes first and the call second.

Grammar Lens: one event after another.
Baʿda means "after" before a noun: baʿda al-ʿashāʾ, after dinner. Before a whole clause it takes an, "that", and the past:
بعد أن أكلنا، خرجنا. — baʿda an akalnā, kharajnā — After we had eaten, we went out.
So a story can be ordered two ways: by joiners, awwalan … thumma, or by clauses, ʿindamā and baʿda an. Good stories mix them.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ʿindamā waṣalnā ilā al-maṭār, ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same two events the other way round — ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī ʿindamā waṣalnā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After dinner", then "after we had eaten" — baʿda al-ʿashāʾ, baʿda an akalnā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: baʿda an akalnā, kharajnā, wa-fī an-nihāya rajaʿnā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which two pieces make ʿindamā? (ʿInda and mā.) Its twin? (Ḥīnamā.) What does baʿda take before a clause? (An, then the past.) In ʿindamā waṣalnā, ittaṣaltu, which came first? (The arrival.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
(reading) — a trip to the sea, told in both pasts.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter forty-two gave you a whole encounter to read: a greeting, a choice and a goodbye. This passage is a story, eighty-two words long, and it happened last summer.
[pause 1 second]
Every word in it was taught before this lesson.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
في الصيف الماضي سافرت مع صديقي إلى قرية صغيرة قريبة من البحر. كان الطقس جميلا. عندما (ʿindamā) وصلنا، ذهبنا أولا إلى الفندق. ثم أكلنا السمك في مطعم صغير، وكان الطعام لذيذا. وبعد ذلك سبحنا في البحر. في المساء كنا نشرب الشاي، وكان صديقي يحكي قصة طويلة. كنت أستمع وأضحك. ذات مرة كنا نمشي في القرية عندما (ʿindamā) أمطرت. بعد أن رجعنا إلى الفندق، قرأنا وشربنا القهوة. في النهاية رجعنا بالقطار إلى المدينة. وعندما وصلت إلى المنزل، حكيت القصة لجدتي. كانت الرحلة قصيرة وجميلة جدا.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go; let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Then read it a second time and sort every verb into one of two piles: the scene, or what happened.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
The events: sāfartu, waṣalnā, dhahabnā, akalnā, sabaḥnā, amṭarat, rajaʿnā, qaraʾnā, ḥakaytu. Each one happened and was over, and awwalan, thumma, baʿda dhālika and fī an-nihāya keep them in order.
[pause 2 seconds]
The scene: the weather, the good food, the tea in the evening. Kāna paints it, and so does kāna with the present: kunnā nashrabu, kāna ṣadīqī yaḥkī, kuntu astamiʿu. After kāna an adjective takes -an: jamīlan, ladhīdhan. Chapter two hundred and eighty-nine comes back to that.
[pause 2 seconds]
In the rain, both pasts meet: kunnā namshī, the walk still running, and ʿindamā amṭarat, the rain that cut into it.

[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
How did they travel back to the city?
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]

[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]
Why is kunnā namshī built with kāna? (The walk was still going on when the rain came: it is scene.)
[pause 2 seconds]

Lesson 5 of 5.
Review — a story, in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was reading", then "I was at the office". (Kuntu aqraʾu, kuntu fī al-maktab.) And the Arabic for a page. (Ṣafḥa.)

What you've built.
Four lessons, and a story has its order. Joiners at the head of each step: awwalan, thumma, baʿda dhālika, fī an-nihāya. Clauses that name the moment: ʿindamā with the past, and baʿda an with the past. And ḥakā, to hand the whole thing on.
[pause 2 seconds]
One small story, in four steps:
أولا ذهبت إلى المكتب، ثم كتبت رسالة. عندما رجعت، حكيت القصة لصديقي. — awwalan dhahabtu ilā al-maktab, thumma katabtu risāla. ʿindamā rajaʿtu, ḥakaytu al-qiṣṣa li-ṣadīqī — First I went to the office, then I wrote a letter. When I came back, I told my friend the story.
