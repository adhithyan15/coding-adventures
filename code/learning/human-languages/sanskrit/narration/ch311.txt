Sanskrit, chapter 311: A Rumour Spreads.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
किंवदन्ती (kiṃvadantī) — a rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for publishes, then the Sanskrit for a detail.

You'll want to know: किंवदन्ती.
किंवदन्ती — kiṃvadantī — "a rumour".
नगरे एका किंवदन्ती प्रसरति। nagare ekā kiṃvadantī prasarati. A rumour is spreading in the city.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
किंवदन्ती (kiṃvadantī) is built from kim, "what", and a form of the root √vad, "to say": MW explains it as the question what do they say, common talk, rumour (MW).

आतंकः (ātaṅkaḥ) MW gives ātaṅka as disease, and also as affliction of mind, apprehension, fear, without a root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: उत्तेजयति.
उत्तेजयति — uttejayati — "stirs up; excites".
धूर्तः जनान् उत्तेजयति। dhūrtaḥ janān uttejayati. The rogue stirs up the people.
Past (he …): सः जनान् उत्तेजितवान्, saḥ janān uttejitavān.

The word, taken apart.
उत्तेजयति (uttejayati) is built from ud, "up", and the causative of the root √tij, "to be sharp"; MW gives ut-tejayati as to excite, stimulate, incite (MW).

The word, taken apart.
भ्रान्तिः (bhrāntiḥ) comes from the root √bhram, "to wander, roam", as in bhramati; MW gives bhrānti as wandering, and also as perplexity, error, false opinion (MW).
