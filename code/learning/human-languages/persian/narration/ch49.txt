Persian, chapter 49: Pencil, Pen, School, Bag, Money.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مداد (medâd) — a pencil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a glass, then the Persian for a notebook.

You'll want to know: مداد.
مداد — medâd — "a pencil".

The word, taken apart.
medâd is borrowed from Arabic midād, "ink". Its Arabic root is m-d-d (Lane). Persian moved the word from ink to the pencil.

Lesson 2 of 5.
خودکار (khodkâr) — a pen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a notebook, then the Persian for a pencil.

You'll want to know: خودکار.
خودکار — khodkâr — "a pen".

The word, taken apart.
khodkâr is built from khod, "self", and kâr, "work": "self-working", for a pen that carries its own ink. Both parts are native Persian; kâr is usually traced to Middle Persian kār.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
پول (pul) — money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a school, then the Persian for a bag.

You'll want to know: پول.
پول — pul — "money".

The word, taken apart.
pul first named a small copper coin. It is usually traced to a Greek coin name, though accounts differ on which: some give the old obolos, others the Byzantine follis, from Latin follis, "bag".
