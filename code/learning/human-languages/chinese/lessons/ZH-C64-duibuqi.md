## You'll want to know: 对不起
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C64-HELP64-01]; assesses=[] -->

**对不起** — *duìbuqǐ* — "sorry".

> *duìbuqǐ wǒ bù dǒng nǐ de huà* — Sorry, I don't understand what you're saying.

The new character, in pieces: 起 — the walking piece read zǒu along the left and the bottom, carrying a three-stroke piece read jǐ, 'self', on its back: not the jǐ of 机. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

Its middle syllable loses its tone: duìbuqǐ, with bu light between two full syllables.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**对不起** (*duìbuqǐ*) is built from 对 'to answer, to match', 不 'not' and 起 'to rise', all inherited from Middle Chinese, *twojH*, *pjut* and *khiX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'Not able to face you' is the usual explanation, but it is not settled.
