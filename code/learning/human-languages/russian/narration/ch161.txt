Lesson 1 of 5.
анкета (ankéta) — a form, a questionnaire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a drawer; a crate, then the Russian for a light bulb.

You'll want to know: анкета.
анкета (ankéta) — "a form, a questionnaire".

The word, taken apart.
анкета (ankéta) comes from French enquête, "an inquiry", which goes back, Vasmer says, to the Latin family of quaestiō, "a question".

Lesson 2 of 5.
конверт (konvért) — an envelope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a light bulb, then the Russian for a form.

You'll want to know: конверт.
конверт (konvért) — "an envelope".

The word, taken apart.
конверт (konvért) — from Peter the Great's time; it came through German Kuvert or from French couvert, "a cover", from couvrir, "to cover", Latin coopertum, "covered" (Vasmer). Vasmer calls its н unexplained.

Lesson 3 of 5.
марка (márka) — a postage stamp; a brand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a form, then the Russian for an envelope.

You'll want to know: марка.
марка (márka) — "a postage stamp; a brand".

The word, taken apart.
марка (márka) comes from German Marke, "a mark, a sign", Middle High German marc, from merken, "to notice" (Vasmer). The марка (márka) that names the old German coin is German Mark.

Lesson 4 of 5.
открытка (otkrýtka) — a postcard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an envelope, then the Russian for a postage stamp; a brand.

You'll want to know: открытка.
открытка (otkrýtka) — "a postcard".

The word, taken apart.
открытка (otkrýtka) is cut down from открытое письмо, "an open letter", with -ка: a postcard has no envelope. открытый is "opened", from открыть, "to open", on крыть, "to cover".
