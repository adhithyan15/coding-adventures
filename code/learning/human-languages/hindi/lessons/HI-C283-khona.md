# खोना (khonā) — to lose, to be lost

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C131-THINGS131-04, HI-LEX-C184-THG184-02, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-STEMS-01] -->

## You'll want to know: खोना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C283-KHONA-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C131-THINGS131-04, HI-LEX-C184-THG184-02, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-04, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-STEMS-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-ERGATIVE-NE-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-OBJECT-AGREEMENT-01] -->

**खोना** — *khonā* — "to lose; to be lost".

> **मेरा थैला खो गया।**
>
> *merā thailā kho gayā.* — "My bag got lost."

With **गया** after it, as in **सो गया**, the lost thing is the subject, and the verb follows it: **मेरी चाबी खो गई** (*merī cābī kho gaī*), "my key got lost", because **चाबी** is feminine.

With **दिया** after it, you are the one who lost it, and **ने** comes in:

> **मैंने अपनी चाबी खो दी।**
>
> *maiṁne apnī cābī kho dī.* — "I lost my key."

**दी** follows **चाबी**. **अपनी** is "my own", the **अपना** you use wherever the owner is also the doer.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**खोना** (*khonā*) is a tadbhava. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit *kṣapayati*, "destroys; throws" (CDIAL 3651), and gives "to lose, waste, throw away" for Hindi.
