Lesson 1 of 6.
भिक्षुः (bhikṣuḥ) — a mendicant monk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a chariot procession, then the Sanskrit for fetches.

You'll want to know: भिक्षुः.
भिक्षुः — bhikṣuḥ — "a mendicant monk".
भिक्षुः प्रातः ग्रामं गच्छति। bhikṣuḥ prātaḥ grāmaṃ gacchati. The monk goes to the village in the morning.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
भिक्षुः (bhikṣuḥ) comes from the root √bhikṣ, "to beg, to ask for alms"; MW gives bhikṣu as a beggar, a religious mendicant and a Buddhist monk (MW).

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for fetches, then the Sanskrit for a mendicant monk.

You'll want to know: विहारः.
विहारः — vihāraḥ — "a Buddhist monastery".
भिक्षुः विहारे वसति। bhikṣuḥ vihāre vasati. The monk lives in the monastery.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
विहारः (vihāraḥ) is built from vi, "apart", and the root √hṛ, "to carry": MW gives vihāra as walking for pleasure and, among Buddhists and Jainas, a monastery (MW).

Lesson 4 of 6.
उपासकः (upāsakaḥ) — a lay follower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a Buddhist monastery, then the Sanskrit for alms.

You'll want to know: उपासकः.
उपासकः — upāsakaḥ — "a lay follower".
उपासकः भिक्षवे भोजनं ददाति। upāsakaḥ bhikṣave bhojanaṃ dadāti. The lay follower gives food to the monk.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
उपासकः (upāsakaḥ) is built from upa, "near", and the root √ās, "to sit", which also gives āste, "sits": one who sits near and serves; MW gives upāsaka as a servant, a worshipper and a follower (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
चैत्यम् (caityam) — a memorial shrine; a sacred mound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for alms, then the Sanskrit for a lay follower.

You'll want to know: चैत्यम्.
चैत्यम् — caityam — "a memorial shrine; a sacred mound".
विहारस्य समीपे प्राचीनं चैत्यम् अस्ति। vihārasya samīpe prācīnaṃ caityam asti. Near the monastery there is an ancient shrine.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
चैत्यम् (caityam) is built on citā, "a funeral pile", or citi, "a heap", as MW gives it: MW gives caitya as a funeral monument or stūpa, a sacred tree on a mound, and a temple or place of worship (MW).
