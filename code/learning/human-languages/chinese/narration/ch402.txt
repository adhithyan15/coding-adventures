Mandarin Chinese, chapter 402: At the Station.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
chéngkè — passenger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get old, then the Mandarin for everything.

You'll want to know: chéngkè.
chéngkè — "passenger".
huǒchēzhàn lǐ yǒu yìxiē chéngkè — There are a few passengers in the station.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chéngkè is built from chéng 'to ride' and kè 'a guest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zying and khaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to drive', 'to mount' and 'to prevail'.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 2 of 5.
候车 (hòuchē) — to wait for a train or bus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for everything, then the Mandarin for a passenger.

You'll want to know: 候车.
候车 — hòuchē — "to wait for a train or bus".
我们在车站候车 wǒmen zài chēzhàn hòuchē — We're waiting for the train at the station.

The word, taken apart.
候车 (hòuchē) is built from 候 'to wait for' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huwH and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wait upon, to watch for'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
gāotiě — high-speed railway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a passenger, then the Mandarin for to wait for a train.

You'll want to know: gāotiě.
gāotiě — "high-speed railway".
wǒmen zuò gāotiě qù Běijīng — We're taking the high-speed train to Beijing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gāotiě is built from 高 'high' and tiě 'iron', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and thet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second, after an older dictionary, 'black metal'. It is a short form of a longer name meaning a high-speed iron road.
