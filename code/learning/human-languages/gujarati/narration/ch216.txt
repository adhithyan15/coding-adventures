Gujarati, chapter 216: The hot months.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
તપવું (tapvũ) — to blaze, to be hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for thick, then the Gujarati for lukewarm.

You'll want to know: તપવું.
તપવું — tapvũ — "to blaze, to be hot".
બપોરે સૂરજ તપશે. — bapore sūraj tapśe — "The sun will blaze at midday."

The word, taken apart.
તપવું (tapvũ) is usually traced to Sanskrit tapyati, "is hot". CDIAL does not list tapvũ itself, but it lists the related Gujarati tāpvũ, "to warm oneself", under the same entry (CDIAL 5684).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉકળાટ (ukaḷāṭ) — sultry heat, mugginess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for lukewarm, then the Gujarati for to blaze.

You'll want to know: ઉકળાટ.
ઉકળાટ — ukaḷāṭ — "sultry heat, mugginess".
બપોરે ઘણો ઉકળાટ હશે. — bapore ghaṇo ukaḷāṭ haśe — "It will be very muggy at midday."

The word, taken apart.
ઉકળાટ (ukaḷāṭ) is built on the verb ukaḷvũ, "to boil", so muggy air is air that seems to boil. CDIAL derives ukaḷvũ from utkalati, "goes out or up", a reconstructed form (CDIAL 1716).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

ચોમાસામાં ઘણો ભેજ હશે. — comāsāmā̃ ghaṇo bhej haśe — "There will be a lot of humidity in the monsoon."

The word, taken apart.
The origin of ભેજ (bhej) is not settled. CDIAL lists no Gujarati form for it, and none of its entries for damp or moisture holds the word.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for sultry heat, then the Gujarati for humidity.

You'll want to know: તાપમાન.
તાપમાન — tāpmān — "temperature".
શિયાળામાં તાપમાન નીચું હશે. — śiyāḷāmā̃ tāpmān nīcũ haśe — "The temperature will be low in winter."

The word, taken apart.
તાપમાન (tāpmān) is a learned word built from Sanskrit tāpa, "heat" (CDIAL 5767), and māna, "measure"; EtymDB lists it as borrowed from Sanskrit.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ડિગ્રી (ḍigrī) — degree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for humidity, then the Gujarati for temperature.

You'll want to know: ડિગ્રી.
ડિગ્રી — ḍigrī — "degree".
કાલે ચાળીસ ડિગ્રી હશે. — kāle cāḷīs ḍigrī haśe — "It will be forty degrees tomorrow."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ડિગ્રી (ḍigrī) is a loan from English degree, for heat and for a university degree alike.
