# ich hätte gern — I'd like, and that would be

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-HATTE-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I had" and "I was". (*Ich hatte. Ich war.*) Change the
*a* of each to *ä*, add an *e* to the second, and the past turns into
"would".

## You'll want to know: ich hätte gern
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ICH-HAETTE-GERN-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-GERN-11, GE-LEX-MOECHTEN-01, GE-LEX-C111-FAHRT111-02, GE-LEX-KAFFEE-02] -->

**ich hätte gern** — "I'd like", literally "I would gladly have": *ikh
HET-tuh gairn*.

> *Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee.* — I'd like a coffee.
> *Ich hätte gern eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin.* — I'd like a ticket to Berlin.

It sits beside *ich möchte*, and both are polite. At a counter or in a
café, *ich hätte gern* is a common way to order.

The forms: *ich hätte*, *du hättest*, *er hätte*, *wir hätten*, *Sie
hätten*.

## Grammar Lens: das wäre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-DAS-WAERE-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C132-EIGEN132-04, GE-LEX-DU-SOLLTEST-01] -->

**wäre** is "would be", made from *war*:

> *Das wäre schön.* — That would be lovely.
> *Das wäre nett.* — That would be kind.

The forms: *ich wäre*, *du wärst*, *er wäre*, *wir wären*, *Sie wären*.

This is the **Konjunktiv II**, the mood of what is not real, or not yet,
or said gently. You have used one form already: *du solltest*, which is
spelled like the Präteritum. *Hätte* and *wäre* add an umlaut to theirs.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-HATTE-01] -->

**hätte** is *hatte* and **wäre** is *war*, each with an umlaut. Neither
form is in EtymDB 2.1, so this is the usual account in histories of German:
the old subjunctive endings had an *i* in them, as in Old High German
*wārī*, and that *i* pulled the vowel forward, *a* to *ä*. The *i* is gone;
the umlaut it left is the mark of the mood. *Haben* itself EtymDB traces to
Proto-Germanic *\*habjaną*.
