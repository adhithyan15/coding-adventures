French, chapter 314: Les funérailles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
décéder — to pass away, to die (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to take into hospital, then the French for the death.

You'll want to know: décéder.
décéder — "to pass away, to die (formal)".
Il est décédé à l'âge de quatre-vingts ans. He passed away at the age of eighty.
Takes être. Official language; mourir in everyday speech.

The word, taken apart.
décéder is a learned borrowing from Latin dēcēdere "to go away, to die" (EtymDB), from dē and cēdere "to go".

Lesson 2 of 5.
enterrer — to bury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the death, then the French for to pass away.

You'll want to know: enterrer.
enterrer — "to bury".
Ils ont enterré leur grand-père près de l'église. They buried their grandfather near the church.
Figurative: enterrer un projet — to drop a plan.

The word, taken apart.
enterrer is inherited from Vulgar Latin interrāre "to put in the earth" (EtymDB), built on Latin terra "earth".

Lesson 4 of 5.
les funérailles — the funeral (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to bury, then the French for the burial.

You'll want to know: les funérailles.
les funérailles — "the funeral (formal)".
Les funérailles du chanteur ont eu lieu dans son village. The singer's funeral was held in his village.
Formal and plural only; the everyday word is l'enterrement.

The word, taken apart.
funérailles comes from Medieval Latin fūnerālia (EtymDB), built on fūnus "a funeral" (Lewis & Short).

cimetière comes from Church Latin coemētērium, a borrowing from Greek koimētḗrion "a sleeping place" (Lewis & Short).
