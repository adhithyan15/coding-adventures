Lesson 2 of 5.
ਪਿਤਾ (pitā) — a father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for happy, then the Punjabi for sad.

You'll want to know: ਪਿਤਾ.
ਪਿਤਾ — pitā — "a father".

The word, taken apart.
ਪਿਤਾ (pitā) is Sanskrit pitā, the nominative of pitṛ, "father", taken over whole, a tatsama. The inherited line survives in Punjabi piu, "father", and mā-pe, "mother and father", which CDIAL lists under pitṛ (CDIAL 8179).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਪੁੱਤਰ (puttar) — a son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a father, then the Punjabi for a mother.

You'll want to know: ਪੁੱਤਰ.
ਪੁੱਤਰ — puttar — "a son".

The word, taken apart.
ਪੁੱਤਰ (puttar) goes back to Sanskrit putra, "son". Its doubled tt is an inherited sound change, and it keeps the r, as Lahnda puttar does. CDIAL lists the shorter Punjabi form putt beside Lahnda puttar (CDIAL 8265).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਧੀ (dhī) — a daughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a mother, then the Punjabi for a son.

You'll want to know: ਧੀ.
ਧੀ — dhī — "a daughter".

The word, taken apart.
ਧੀ (dhī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from duhitṛ, "daughter" (CDIAL 6481).
