# desculpe, como se vai para… — the question that finds the way

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-IR-02, PT-LEX-C74-CIDADE2-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to go". (*Ir.*) Say "the station". (*A estação.*)

[PAUSE 1s] You are in a town you do not know, and the station is somewhere.

## You'll want to know: desculpe
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-DESCULPE-01]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-PORQUE-E-QUE-02] -->

**desculpe** — "excuse me", said to someone you do not know.

It is the polite form of **desculpar**, "to excuse", the one that goes with
*o senhor*, *a senhora* and *você*, which a stranger in the street is owed.
The same word apologises: bump into someone and you say *desculpe!* In
Brazil you will also hear *com licença*, "with permission", for this; in
Portugal you hear that mostly when someone needs to get past.

## Grammar Lens: como se vai para…?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-COMO-SE-VAI-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C75-CIDADE3-02, PT-LEX-C75-CIDADE3-04, PT-LEX-SER-ESTAR-02] -->

> **Desculpe, como se vai para a estação?** — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?

Word for word, "how does one go to the station". The **se** leaves the
person out, so the question is about the way, not about you. **para** plus the place, with its article:

> **para a** estação · **para o** museu · **para o** hotel

A second way to ask uses **fica**, the verb that tells where a place is:
*onde fica a estação*, where the station is. *Como se vai para* asks for the
**way**; *onde fica* asks for the **place**. Either can be followed by a short
question that saves a walk, *é longe*, is it far.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**desculpar** is built on Latin *culpa*, "fault, blame" (Lewis & Short), with
*des-*, "away", in front: to excuse someone is to take the fault away from them.
EtymDB 2.1 has no row for *desculpar* itself, so that is the usual reading of
its parts. English **culpable** shares the *culpa*.
