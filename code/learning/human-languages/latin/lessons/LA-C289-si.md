# sī — if

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-PRAGMATICS-QUAESO-02, LA-LEX-C178-CAUSA178-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Your very first politeness gave you **sīs**, a squeezed *sī
vīs*, "if you wish". And you own **nisi**, "unless".

[PAUSE 1s] Both have the word for "if" inside them. Now it stands on its
own.

## You'll want to know: sī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C289-SI-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-02, LA-LEX-AMBULO-01] -->

**sī** — "if". It opens the condition, and the condition usually comes
first:

> **Sī vīs, ambulāmus.** — If you like, we'll go for a walk.
> **Sī pluit, nōn ambulō.** — If it rains, I don't go walking.

[PAUSE 2s] **nisi**, "unless, if not", opens the same slot the other way
round:

> **Nisi pluit, ambulō.** — Unless it rains, I go walking.

[PAUSE 1s] *Sī nōn* also exists, and it puts the weight on the *nōn*.
*Nisi* is the everyday way to say "unless".

## The word, taken apart: sī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-C289-SI-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C178-CAUSA178-05] -->

**sī** is inherited, usually traced to a small Proto-Indo-European word
*\*swe* (EtymDB 2.1); Lewis & Short derive it from a pronoun stem and
record an older spelling, *seī*. The details are not settled. **nisi**, as
its own lesson said, is *ne*, "not", in front of *sī*.
