Lesson 1 of 6.
hángyè — line of business; industry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do an internship, then the Mandarin for pay and conditions.

You'll want to know: hángyè.
hángyè — "line of business; industry".
nǐ zài nǎge hángyè gōngzuò — What line of work are you in?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hángyè is built from háng 'a row' and yè 'a trade', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hang and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a file of soldiers' and the second 'affairs'.

You'll want to know: 事业.
事业 — shìyè — "career; life's work".
他觉得事业最重要 tā juéde shìyè zuì zhòngyào — He thinks his career matters most.
The new character, in pieces: 业 (yè) — five strokes and no piece you have written yet: two verticals side by side, a dot on the left and a short stroke falling left on the right, then a long bar along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
事业 (shìyè) is built from 事 'a matter' and 业 (yè) 'a trade', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzriH and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to employ' and the second 'affairs' and 'great'.

Script — two verticals, two short strokes, a long bar.
业 (yè).
Five strokes, in three steps.
Two verticals first, side by side in the middle: the left one, then the right one, which starts a little higher.
Two short strokes outside them: a dot on the left that falls to the right, then a short stroke on the right that falls to the left.
Last, a long bar along the foot, wider than everything above it.
2 + 2 + 1 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 业 (yè), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 业 (yè) have? (Five.) What comes first? (The two verticals.) And last? (The long bar along the foot.)

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a line of business, then the Mandarin for one's life's work.

You'll want to know: 就业.
就业 — jiùyè — "to find employment; to get a job".
xiànzài dàxuésheng jiùyè bù róngyì — It isn't easy for graduates to find jobs these days.

The word, taken apart.
就业 (jiùyè) is built from 就 'to go to' and 业 (yè) 'work', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjuwH and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to accomplish' and 'to go towards'.
