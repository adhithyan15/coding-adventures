Portuguese, chapter 362: O ordenado e a reforma.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o ordenado — the salary, the wages.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for efficient, then the Portuguese for the productivity.

You'll want to know: o ordenado.
o ordenado — "the salary, the wages".
O ordenado chega no fim do mês. The salary arrives at the end of the month.
In Portugal o ordenado and o salário are both common.

The word, taken apart.
ordenado is the participle of ordenar "to order, to arrange" used as a noun, through Old Portuguese ordenado (EtymDB); ordenar is inherited from Latin ōrdināre "to set in order", on ōrdō "order" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o subsídio — the allowance, the benefit; the subsidy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the productivity, then the Portuguese for the salary.

You'll want to know: o subsídio.
o subsídio — "the allowance, the benefit; the subsidy".
O subsídio de férias ajuda a pagar a viagem. The holiday allowance helps to pay for the trip.
In Portugal many workers get two extra months of pay a year: subsídio de férias and subsídio de Natal.

The word, taken apart.
subsídio is a learned borrowing from Latin subsidium "troops in reserve, help" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built on subsidere "to settle, to stay behind", on sedēre "to sit".

Lesson 3 of 5.
negociar — to negotiate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the salary, then the Portuguese for the allowance.

You'll want to know: negociar.
negociar — "to negotiate".
O sindicato negociou um ordenado melhor. The union negotiated a better salary.
Also to trade: negociar em vinho.

The word, taken apart.
negociar is a learned borrowing from Latin negōtiārī "to carry on business" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built on negōtium "business", from nec "not" and ōtium "leisure".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a reforma — the retirement, the pension; the reform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the allowance, then the Portuguese for to negotiate.

You'll want to know: a reforma.
a reforma — "the retirement, the pension; the reform".
O meu avô está na reforma há dez anos. My grandfather has been retired for ten years.
European Portuguese for retirement; in Brazil a aposentadoria. Also a reform: a reforma da escola.

The word, taken apart.
reforma is built on reformar "to reshape" (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin refōrmāre "to shape again, to transform" (Lewis & Short), on fōrma "shape".

reformado — "retired".
A minha mãe é reformada: era professora. My mother is retired: she was a teacher.
European Portuguese; in Brazil aposentado. Also a noun: os reformados.

The word, taken apart.
reformado is the participle of reformar, a learned borrowing from Latin refōrmāre "to shape again, to transform" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short).
