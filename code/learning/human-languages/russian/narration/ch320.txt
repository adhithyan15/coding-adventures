Russian, chapter 320: Fairy-tale creatures.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
волшебник (volshébnik) — a wizard, a magician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a storyteller, then the Russian for to make up.

You'll want to know: волшебник.
волшебник (volshébnik) — "a wizard, a magician".
В сказке волшебник помог бедной девушке. V skázke volshébnik pomóg bédnoy dévushke. In the tale a wizard helped a poor girl.
Волшебный — magic, magical.

The word, taken apart.
волшебник (volshébnik) is built on волшебный "magic" and -ик, from волшба "sorcery", Old East Slavic vŭlšĭba "magic, sorcery" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ведьма (védma) — a witch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to make up, then the Russian for a wizard.

You'll want to know: ведьма.
ведьма (védma) — "a witch".
Старая ведьма жила в лесу. Stáraya védma zhilá v lesú. An old witch lived in the forest.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ведьма (védma) is inherited through Old East Slavic vědĭma from Proto-Slavic vědĭma (EtymDB), built on the root of ведать "to know": a woman who knows.

Чудовище жило в глубокой пещере. Chudóvishche zhílo v glubókoy peshchére. The monster lived in a deep cave.
Neuter, though it ends in -е: страшное чудовище (chudóvishche).

The word, taken apart.
чудовище (chudóvishche) is built on чудо "a wonder, a marvel", an inherited Slavic word, with the ending -овище; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
превращаться (prevrashchátsya) — to turn into, to become.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a monster, then the Russian for a dragon.

You'll want to know: превращаться.
превращаться (prevrashchátsya) — "to turn into, to become".
Ночью ведьма превращалась в чёрную кошку. Nóchyu védma prevrashchálas v chyórnuyu kóshku. At night the witch turned into a black cat.
Imperfective; perfective превратиться (prevratítsya), превращусь. It takes в + accusative.

The word, taken apart.
превращаться (prevrashchátsya) is built on пре- and вращать "to turn", which EtymDB marks as borrowed from Church Slavonic vraščati, from Proto-Slavic vorťati; the native cousin is воротить, Proto-Slavic vortiti (EtymDB).
