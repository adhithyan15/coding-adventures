# சொ — one new sign in a familiar request

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-SOLLUNGAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *solluṅgaḷ*: **please tell me**. Its first sound is *so*. You
already know the sound and meaning; today only its written vowel sign is new.

## Script you'll notice: ொ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-SCRIPT-SHORT-O-01]; assesses=[] -->

The consonant **ச** begins with *ca*. Add the short-*o* sign **ொ**, and it reads
*so*:

> **ச + ொ → சொ**

The short-*o* sign reaches around its consonant. Part appears on the left and
part on the right, but together they are one vowel sign. Read the whole joined
shape as one syllable: **சொ**, *so*.

Do not invent a stroke order from the printed font. Follow the model as one
shape today.

## Writing — observe and trace
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-SHORT-O-01, TA-LEX-SOLLUNGAL-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: observe-trace -->

Trace **சொ** once with the model visible. Say *so* as your finger reaches the
vowel sign. Then point to the first syllable in **சொல்லுங்கள்** and say the
whole familiar request, *solluṅgaḷ*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-SHORT-O-01, TA-LEX-SOLLUNGAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **சொ**. (***So.***) What does the whole spoken request
*solluṅgaḷ* mean? (**Please tell me.**) One new sign is enough for today.
