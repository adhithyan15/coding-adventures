Lesson 1 of 7.
都 (dōu) — all; both.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

[your turn — recall: say 太大了 — tài dà le, too big]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 都.
都 — dōu — "all; both".
我们都是学生 wǒmen dōu shì xuésheng — We're all students.
他们都很好 tāmen dōu hěn hǎo — They're all fine.
The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
都 (dōu) is inherited from Middle Chinese tu (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'all together', and also 'a capital city', the sense said dū today. The regular change from tu gives dū; why the word for 'all' is said dōu is not settled.

What you've built.
都 (dōu) goes in front of the verb or the adjective and looks back at the people before it: 我们都, all of us. With two people it means both.

Script — 者, then the ear on the right.
都 (dōu).
Two pieces, left before right, and every stroke in them is one you have made.
者, eight strokes: first the foot of 在, a short bar, a vertical through it, then the long bar; then a long left-falling stroke from the upper right; then 日 under them.
The ear piece, two strokes, on the right, as 那 ends: across, back, round and hooking; then the long vertical.
8 + 2 = ten.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 都 (dōu), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 都 (dōu)? (Ten.) Which character ends the left piece? (日.) Where have you written the ear piece? (On the right of 那.)

Lesson 3 of 7.
yíyàng — the same.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read a line with two characters you have written in the last two chapters, then the tone of 一.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 他们也都很好, aloud — tāmen yě dōu hěn hǎo, they're all fine too]
[your turn — recall: say 一个 — yí ge, with 一 rising]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: yíyàng.
yíyàng — "the same; alike".
wǒmen bù yíyàng — We're not the same.
wǒmen yíyàng dà — We're the same age.
一 before the fall of yàng rises: yí. Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yíyàng is built from yī 'one' and yàng 'a pattern; a kind': one pattern. yī is inherited from Middle Chinese 'jit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); yàng has no reading in the Guangyun, so no dictionary we use records an older form of it, and its history is not settled.

What you've built.
In tāmen yě dōu hěn hǎo, 也 comes before 都: too, and all of them. yíyàng + an adjective says how two things match: yíyàng dà, the same size or the same age.

You'll want to know: niánqīng.
niánqīng — "young".
wǒ de lǎoshī hěn niánqīng — My teacher is very young.
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
niánqīng is built from nián 'year', the 年 you write, and qīng 'light', not heavy: light in years. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, nen and khjieng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), khjieng glossed 'light in weight'.

What you've built.
niánqīng is said of grown people; a child is xiǎo. lǎo, old, is its opposite.

You'll want to know: pàng.
pàng — "fat; plump".
māo tài pàng le — The cat's too fat.
Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
The character for pàng is in the Guangyun as phanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'half of a sacrificial animal'. That reading would give pàn, not pàng, and the sense is not 'fat', so the history of pàng is not settled.

What you've built.
pàng is a word for people and animals, and said of a person it is blunt: a cat can hear tài pàng le without offence.

The exchange.
A: 他们都是你的同学吗 tāmen dōu shì nǐ de tóngxué ma — Are they all your classmates? B: 对 我们都是学生 duì, wǒmen dōu shì xuésheng — Yes, we're all students. A: nǐmen dōu hěn niánqīng — You're all so young. B: wǒmen yíyàng dà — We're the same age. A: 那个男人呢 nàge nánrén ne — And that man? B: tā shì wǒmen de lǎoshī, tā hěn gāo, hěn shòu — He's our teacher. He's tall and thin. A: nǐ de māo hěn pàng — Your cat's fat. B: 也很大 yě hěn dà — And big.
都 (dōu) gathers everybody before it into one: 他们都, 我们都, 你们都. yíyàng matches them up; gāo, shòu and pàng tell them apart.

Reading — the exchange at once.
