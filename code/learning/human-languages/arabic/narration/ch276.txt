Lesson 1 of 5.
حيوان (ḥayawān) — an animal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a party, then the Arabic for a wedding.

You'll want to know: حيوان.
حيوان — ḥayawān — "an animal".

The word, taken apart.
حيوان (ḥayawān) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-y-y, "to live" (Lane). Ḥayawān is a living thing.

Lesson 4 of 5.
زرافة (zarāfa) — a giraffe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for birds, then the Arabic for a sparrow.

You'll want to know: زرافة.
زرافة — zarāfa — "a giraffe".

The word, taken apart.
زرافة (zarāfa) is listed by Lane under z-r-f, with an explanation linking it to a word for "a company", because it seems an assemblage of animals; that looks like a folk explanation. EtymDB derives it from Syriac zarīpā; the origin is not certain. English giraffe comes from zarāfa through Italian.
