Mandarin Chinese, chapter 396: Whatever Happens.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
bùguǎn — no matter (whether or how).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get (a job) done, then the Mandarin for a condition (that must be met).

You'll want to know: bùguǎn.
bùguǎn — "no matter (whether or how)".
bùguǎn míngtiān tiānqì zěnmeyàng wǒ dōu qù — Whatever the weather tomorrow, I'm going.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 不 keeps its own falling tone here, bù, because guǎn after it is a third tone, not a falling one.

The word, taken apart.
bùguǎn is built from 不 'not' and guǎn 'to be in charge', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut and kwanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a pipe' and 'to take charge': not caring which.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Lesson 5 of 6.
jíshǐ — even if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for unless, then the Mandarin for round the clock.

You'll want to know: jíshǐ.
jíshǐ — "even if".
jíshǐ xiàyǔ wǒmen yě yào qù — Even if it rains, we're still going.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jíshǐ is built from jí 'to go up to' and shǐ 'to make (someone do)', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsik and sriX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go to' and 'now' and the second 'to employ' and 'to order'. How the pair came to mean 'even if' is not settled.
