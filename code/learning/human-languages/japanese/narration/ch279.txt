Lesson 1 of 6.
かざん (kazan) — a volcano.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a tornado, then the Japanese for to lean.

You'll want to know: かざん.
かざん — kazan — "a volcano".
日本には かざんが おおいです。 — nihon ni wa kazan ga ōi desu — "Japan has many volcanoes."

The word, taken apart.
かざん (kazan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ka, "fire" (Middle Chinese xwaX) and zan, "mountain" (srean) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The second is voiced from san to zan.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ふせぐ (fusegu) — to prevent; to keep off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an eruption, then the Japanese for a disaster.

You'll want to know: ふせぐ.
ふせぐ — fusegu — "to prevent; to keep off".
みんなで じこを ふせいで ください。 — minna de jiko o fuseide kudasai — "Please all help to prevent accidents."
Polite ふせぎます, fusegimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かさが あめを ふせぎました。, kasa ga ame o fusegimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ふせぐ (fusegu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
