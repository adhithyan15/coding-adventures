German, chapter 371: Zusagen und absagen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zusagen — to accept (an invitation), to promise; to appeal to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to be true, then the German for to turn out.

You'll want to know: zusagen.
zusagen — "to accept (an invitation), to promise; to appeal to".
Sie hat für die Hochzeit zugesagt. She has accepted the invitation to the wedding.
Separable: sie sagt zu, sie hat zugesagt. Das sagt mir zu — I like that.

The word, taken apart.
zusagen is built from zu and sagen "to say", inherited from Proto-Germanic sagjaną (EtymDB), a cousin of English say: you say yes to something.

Lesson 3 of 5.
absagen — to call off, to cancel; to decline.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to accept, then the German for the acceptance.

You'll want to know: absagen.
absagen — "to call off, to cancel; to decline".
Wegen des schlechten Wetters wurde das Konzert abgesagt. Because of the bad weather the concert was called off.
Separable: er sagt ab, er hat abgesagt. Einen Termin absagen — to cancel an appointment.

The word, taken apart.
absagen is built from ab "off" and sagen "to say", Proto-Germanic sagjaną (EtymDB): you say a plan off.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Bestätigung — the confirmation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to call off, then the German for the rejection.

You'll want to know: die Bestätigung.
die Bestätigung — "the confirmation".
Die Bestätigung für das Hotel kommt per E-Mail. The confirmation for the hotel comes by email.
Plural: die Bestätigungen. From bestätigen — to confirm.

The word, taken apart.
Bestätigung is the noun of bestätigen "to confirm", which is usually traced to the old adjective stet "steady, firm", of the family of stehen "to stand". EtymDB has no entries for these, so the account is hedged.
