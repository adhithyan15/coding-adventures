# बच्चे को — the same ending, and this time it is not a plural

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You know **बच्चे** as *children*. Read **बच्चे को** and notice it
cannot mean *to the children* and *one child* at the same time — so which is it?

## You'll want to know: बच्चे को
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06]; assesses=[] -->

**बच्चे को** — **to the child**. One child.

A noun standing in front of a postposition changes shape, and for a masculine
**-आ** noun that shape is **-ए** — the same ending the plural uses.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01] -->

This is **not a new rule**. It is the one the possessives taught you, arriving on
a noun:

| word | plain | in front of a postposition |
|---|---|---|
| मेरा | मेरा | मेरे पास |
| उसका | उसका | उसके बाद |
| **बच्चा** | **बच्चा** | **बच्चे को** |

Hindi calls this shape the **oblique**. Every **-आ** word in the language wears
it — possessives, adjectives, and now nouns — and it is always the same **-ए**.

So the track has taught this bend four times on four kinds of word, and this is
the fourth and last kind.

## Grammar Lens: how to tell the two -ए apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01] -->

| what you see | what it is |
|---|---|
| बच्चे हैं | plural — nothing follows the noun |
| बच्चे को | singular oblique — a postposition follows |

**The postposition is the whole signal.** If one is standing behind the noun,
the **-ए** is oblique and the noun may well be singular. If nothing is, the
**-ए** is a plural.

A plural *in front of* a postposition takes yet another shape, which is one step
past A1 — recognise **बच्चों को** on a page and leave it there for now.
