Telugu, chapter 88: The People Around You.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
పిల్లలు (pillalu) — children.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for I see, then the Telugu for really.

You'll want to know: పిల్లలు.
పిల్లలు (pillalu) — "children".
మీకు పిల్లలు (pillalu) ఉన్నారా? — "do you have children?", literally "to you, are there children?". The -లు at the end is the plural you met in your very first lesson.

What you've built.
The question that follows a name in almost every first conversation.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: pillalu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: pillalu, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask mīku pillalu unnārā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for I see, then the Telugu for really, then say pillalu again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does పిల్లలు (pillalu) mean? ("Children".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
అబ్బాయి (abbāyi) — a boy; a son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for really, then the Telugu for children.

You'll want to know: అబ్బాయి.
అబ్బాయి (abbāyi) — "a boy", and in a family, "a son".
మా అబ్బాయి (abbāyi) — "our boy", which is how a parent says "my son" out loud.

What you've built.
A word for a boy that doubles as the everyday word for a son.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for really, then the Telugu for children, then say abbāyi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does అబ్బాయి (abbāyi) mean? ("A boy; a son".) Say it once more.


Lesson 3 of 5.
అమ్మాయి (ammāyi) — a girl; a daughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for children, then the Telugu for a boy.

You'll want to know: అమ్మాయి.
అమ్మాయి (ammāyi) — "a girl", and in a family, "a daughter".
It is built like అబ్బాయి (abbāyi), and it opens on the same sound as అమ్మ, "mother". మా అమ్మాయి (ammāyi) — "our girl", "my daughter".

What you've built.
Boy and girl as a pair, each also a word for a child of the family.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ammāyi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ammāyi, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say mā abbāyi, then mā ammāyi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for children, then the Telugu for a boy, then say ammāyi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does అమ్మాయి (ammāyi) mean? ("A girl; a daughter".) Say it once more.


Lesson 4 of 5.
మనిషి (maniṣi) — a person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a boy, then the Telugu for a girl.

You'll want to know: మనిషి.
మనిషి (maniṣi) — "a person, a human being".
మంచి మనిషి (maniṣi) — "a good person", with the మంచి you already have. The plural changes shape: మనుషులు (manuṣulu), "people".

What you've built.
A word for anyone at all, and your first plural that changes its stem.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: maniṣi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maniṣi, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say manci maniṣi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for a boy, then the Telugu for a girl, then say maniṣi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does మనిషి (maniṣi) mean? ("A person, a human being".) Say it once more.


Lesson 5 of 5.
బంధువు (bandhuvu) — a relative.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a girl, then the Telugu for a person.

You'll want to know: బంధువు.
బంధువు (bandhuvu) — "a relative".
అతను మా బంధువు (bandhuvu) — "he is our relative", with no word for is, the same way నా పేరు... needs none.

What you've built.
Children, a boy, a girl, a person, a relative: the people an introduction reaches for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: bandhuvu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: bandhuvu, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say atanu mā bandhuvu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for a girl, then the Telugu for a person, then say bandhuvu again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does బంధువు (bandhuvu) mean? ("A relative".) Say it once more.
