Lesson 1 of 6.
とまどう (tomadou) — to be bewildered; to be at a loss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for unpleasant, then the Japanese for to run wild.

You'll want to know: とまどう.
とまどう — tomadou — "to be bewildered; to be at a loss".
せんせいの しつもんに とまどいました。 — sensei no shitsumon ni tomadoimashita — "The teacher's question left me at a loss."
Polite とまどいます, tomadoimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): がくせいが とまどいました。, gakusei ga tomadoimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とまどう (tomadou) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: its second part is madou, "to be perplexed", and JMdict spells the first, to, with the kanji for "door" or for "route", so which to it is is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 6.
おどろかす (odorokasu) — to surprise someone; to startle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be startled, then the Japanese for to be dumbfounded.

You'll want to know: おどろかす.
おどろかす — odorokasu — "to surprise someone; to startle".
おとうとが うしろから ははを おどろかしました。 — otōto ga ushiro kara haha o odorokashimashita — "My younger brother startled my mother from behind."
Polite おどろかします, odorokashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ねこが こどもを おどろかしました。, neko ga kodomo o odorokashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おどろかす (odorokasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the transitive partner of odoroku, "to be surprised", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
