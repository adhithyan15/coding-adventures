Latin, chapter 317: Incendium.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
incendium, incendiī — a fire, a blaze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to run to help, then the Latin for help.

You'll want to know: incendium, incendiī.
incendium, incendiī — "a fire, a blaze".
Noctū magnum incendium in urbe ortum est. At night a great fire broke out in the city.
Neuter, from incendō, 'to set on fire' (taught). Fire was the great fear of a crowded city of wooden floors and oil lamps.

The word, taken apart.
incendium is built on incendō, "to set on fire" (EtymDB), from in and candeō, "to glow". English incendiary comes from its family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
aestus, aestūs — heat, glow; the tide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for help, then the Latin for a fire.

You'll want to know: aestus, aestūs.
aestus, aestūs — "heat, glow; the tide".
Propter aestum incendiī nēmō appropinquāre poterat. Because of the heat of the fire nobody could come near.
Masculine, 4th declension. The heat of fire or summer, the surge of the sea, and the tide. Related to aestās (taught).

The word, taken apart.
aestus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to burn" (EtymDB), and is a cousin of aestās, "summer".

Lesson 3 of 5.
favilla, favillae — glowing ash, embers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fire, then the Latin for heat.

You'll want to know: favilla, favillae.
favilla, favillae — "glowing ash, embers".
Ventus favillam in tēcta vīcīna ferēbat. The wind was carrying the glowing ash onto the roofs nearby.
Feminine, mostly poetic: the hot ash that drifts and starts new fires.

The word, taken apart.
favilla is linked by Lewis & Short with a root meaning "to shine", seen in Greek phōs, "light"; the link is not certain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
torris, torris — a firebrand, a burning log.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for heat, then the Latin for glowing ash.

You'll want to know: torris, torris.
torris, torris — "a firebrand, a burning log".
Servus torrem ē focō rapuit. A slave snatched a burning log from the hearth.
Masculine, 3rd declension: a piece of wood still burning.

The word, taken apart.
torris belongs with torreō, "to scorch, to roast" (Lewis & Short): the scorching log.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ārdor, ārdōris — burning heat; passion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for glowing ash, then the Latin for a firebrand.

You'll want to know: ārdor, ārdōris.
ārdor, ārdōris — "burning heat; passion".
Ārdor flammārum mūrōs nigrōs fēcit. The heat of the flames blackened the walls.
Masculine, 3rd declension, from ārdeō, 'to burn' (taught). Also a burning eagerness. English ardour.

The word, taken apart.
ārdor is built on ārdeō, "to burn" (EtymDB): burning, in fire and in feeling. English ardour comes from it through French.
