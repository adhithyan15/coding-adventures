# Review — what you think, why, and what follows

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01, IT-GRAMMAR-CREDO-DI-SI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Someone asks *Il museo è interessante?* Answer "I think so".
(*Credo di sì.*) Then ask them back. (*Secondo te?*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-COSI-QUINDI-02, IT-LEX-C125-QUALITA125-05, IT-LEX-C74-AGG3-05] -->

Three moves, and you own a word for each. An **opinion**: *secondo me*. A
**reason**, pointing back: *perché*. A **consequence**, pointing forward:
*quindi* or *così*.

> *Secondo me il museo è interessante, perché è molto antico.*
> *Il treno è caro, quindi prendo l'autobus.*

[PAUSE 2s] The first sentence runs claim, then cause. The second runs cause,
then consequence. Put them side by side and you can argue a small point in
either direction, which is most of what giving reasons is.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01, IT-GRAMMAR-CREDO-DI-SI-02, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01, IT-GRAMMAR-COSI-QUINDI-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the three moves and a word for each. (Opinion ***secondo me***,
reason ***perché***, consequence ***quindi*** or ***così***.) Say "I don't think
so". (***Credo di no.***) In *pioveva, quindi sono restato a casa*, which half is
the scene? (***Pioveva*** — the reason.)
