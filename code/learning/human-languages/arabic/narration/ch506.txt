Arabic, chapter 506: The face up close.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جفن (jafn) — an eyelid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hip, then the Arabic for a joint.

You'll want to know: جفن.
جفن — jafn — "an eyelid".
فتح المريض جفنيه ببطء. fataḥa al-marīḍ jafnayhi bi-buṭʾ. The patient slowly opened his eyelids.
Plural: جفون, jufūn.

The word, taken apart.
جفن (jafn) is built on the native Arabic root j-f-n, "a sheath, a covering" (Lane). The eyelid is the eye's cover.

Lesson 2 of 5.
رمش (rimsh) — an eyelash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a joint, then the Arabic for an eyelid.

You'll want to know: رمش.
رمش — rimsh — "an eyelash".
للطفلة رموش طويلة. li-ṭ-ṭifla rumūsh ṭawīla. The little girl has long eyelashes.
Plural: رموش, rumūsh.

The word, taken apart.
رمش (rimsh) is a native Arabic word for an eyelash, usually traced to the root r-m-sh; the origin of the sense is not settled. Its plural rumūsh is the common form.

Lesson 3 of 5.
لثة (litha) — the gums.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an eyelid, then the Arabic for an eyelash.

You'll want to know: لثة.
لثة — litha — "the gums".
قال طبيب الأسنان إن لثتي سليمة. qāla ṭabīb al-asnān inna lithatī salīma. The dentist said that my gums are healthy.
It is one word for all the gums.

The word, taken apart.
لثة (litha) is a native Arabic word for the gums. Lane notes that some file it under l-w-th, from the flesh wound round the roots of the teeth; its root is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
عصب (ʿaṣab) — a nerve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for the gums, then the Arabic for a chin.

You'll want to know: عصب.
عصب — ʿaṣab — "a nerve".
أصاب الحادث عصبا في يده. aṣāba al-ḥādith ʿaṣaban fī yadihi. The accident damaged a nerve in his hand.
Plural: أعصاب, aʿṣāb.
أعصاب also means nerves as in calm: أعصابه قوية.

The word, taken apart.
عصب (ʿaṣab) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-ṣ-b, "to bind, to tie tight" (Lane). In the old language ʿaṣab was a sinew, the cord that binds the body.
