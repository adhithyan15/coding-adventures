Mandarin Chinese, chapter 393: Be Sure To.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhǔfu — to exhort; to tell (someone) to be sure to do something.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to change the name (of), then the Mandarin for to change one's tune.

You'll want to know: zhǔfu.
zhǔfu — "to exhort; to tell (someone) to be sure to do something".
māma zhǔfu wǒ wǎnshang zǎo huí jiā — Mum told me to be sure to come home early tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǔfu is built from zhǔ 'to entrust' and fu. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to entrust to'; the dictionary has no entry for the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
千万 (qiānwàn) — be sure to (urging); whatever you do.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to change one's tune, then the Mandarin for to exhort (someone).

You'll want to know: 千万.
千万 — qiānwàn — "be sure to (urging); whatever you do".
你一个人在外边千万要保重 nǐ yí ge rén zài wàibian qiānwàn yào bǎozhòng — Out there on your own, be sure to look after yourself.
The new character, in pieces: 万 (wàn) — three strokes: a long bar, a stroke that runs right and turns down with a hook, and a long stroke falling to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
千万 (qiānwàn) is built from 千 'a thousand' and 万 (wàn) 'ten thousand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshen and mjonH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'ten hundreds'; for the second it names a dance and an insect, and the number is not given there. A thousand times, ten thousand times over: the urging sense is usually explained that way.

Script — a bar, a turning hook, a long fall.
万 (wàn).
Three strokes, top to bottom.
A long bar across the top.
A turning hook: from under the middle of the bar, a stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks to the left, as in 为 and 月.
A long stroke falling to the left, from the bar down to the lower left.
One, two, three.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 万 (wàn)? (Three.) Which stroke has the hook? (The second, the turning hook.) Which comes last? (The long fall to the left.)

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to exhort (someone), then the Mandarin for be sure to (urging).

You'll want to know: 万能.
万能 — wànnéng — "able to do everything; all-purpose".
méiyǒu wànnéng de bànfǎ — There's no way that works for everything.

The word, taken apart.
万能 (wànnéng) is built from 万 (wàn) 'ten thousand' and 能 'able', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjonH and nong (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'skilled': able in ten thousand things.
