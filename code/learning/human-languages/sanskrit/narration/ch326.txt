Sanskrit, chapter 326: A Night of Lights.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दीपावलिः (dīpāvaliḥ) — the festival of lamps; a row of lamps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a charioteer, then the Sanskrit for pierces.

You'll want to know: दीपावलिः.
दीपावलिः — dīpāvaliḥ — "the festival of lamps; a row of lamps".
दीपावल्यां सर्वत्र दीपाः ज्वलन्ति। dīpāvalyāṃ sarvatra dīpāḥ jvalanti. At Diwali lamps burn everywhere.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दीपावलिः (dīpāvaliḥ) is built from dīpa, "a lamp", and āvali, "a row"; MW gives dīpāvali as a row of lights, a night illumination, and the festival of Diwali is its modern use (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
दीपयति (dīpayati) — lights; kindles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for pierces, then the Sanskrit for the festival of lamps.

You'll want to know: दीपयति.
दीपयति — dīpayati — "lights; kindles".
बालिका गृहस्य द्वारे दीपं दीपयति। bālikā gṛhasya dvāre dīpaṃ dīpayati. The girl lights a lamp at the door of the house.
Past (she …): सा दीपं दीपितवती, sā dīpaṃ dīpitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
दीपयति (dīpayati) comes from the root √dīp, "to blaze, shine", as in dīpa, "a lamp", in its causative form: to set alight, kindle (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
मण्डयति (maṇḍayati) — decorates; adorns.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the festival of lamps, then the Sanskrit for lights.

You'll want to know: मण्डयति.
मण्डयति — maṇḍayati — "decorates; adorns".
माता गृहं पुष्पैः मण्डयति। mātā gṛhaṃ puṣpaiḥ maṇḍayati. Mother decorates the house with flowers.
Past (she …): सा गृहं मण्डितवती, sā gṛhaṃ maṇḍitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
मण्डयति (maṇḍayati) comes from the root √maṇḍ, "to deck, adorn"; MW gives its causative maṇḍayati as to adorn, and maṇḍita as adorned, decorated (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
कौतुकम् (kautukam) — festive excitement; eagerness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lights, then the Sanskrit for decorates.

You'll want to know: कौतुकम्.
कौतुकम् — kautukam — "festive excitement; eagerness".
उत्सवे बालस्य कौतुकं वर्धते। utsave bālasya kautukaṃ vardhate. At the festival the child's excitement grows.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
कौतुकम् (kautukam) is built on kutūhala, "curiosity" (MW abbreviates it), and MW gives kautuka as curiosity, eagerness, and festivity, a show (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
शोभा (śobhā) — splendour; beauty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for decorates, then the Sanskrit for festive excitement.

You'll want to know: शोभा.

दीपैः नगरस्य शोभा वर्धते। dīpaiḥ nagarasya śobhā vardhate. With the lamps the splendour of the city grows.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शोभा (śobhā) is usually traced to the root √śubh, "to shine", as in śobhate; MW gives śobhā as splendour, brilliance, beauty, grace (MW).
