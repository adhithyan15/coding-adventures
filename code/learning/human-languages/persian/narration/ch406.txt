Persian, chapter 406: In court.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قاضی (qâzi) — a judge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a punishment, then the Persian for a permit.

You'll want to know: قاضی.
قاضی (qâzi) — "a judge".
قاضی به حرفهای هر دو طرف گوش داد. qâzi be harfhâ-ye har do taraf gush dâd. The judge listened to both sides.
Plural qozât, an Arabic broken plural, or qâzihâ.

The word, taken apart.
قاضی (qâzi) comes from Arabic qāḍin "judge" (EtymDB); Persian says the Arabic ḍ as z.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: متهم.
متهم (mottaham) — "accused; a defendant".
متهم گفت که آن شب خانه بوده است. mottaham goft ke ân shab khâne bude ast. The accused said that he had been at home that night.
Plural mottahamân. Mottaham kardan — to accuse.

The word, taken apart.
متهم (mottaham) is usually traced to Arabic muttaham "accused", from the same Arabic family as ittihām "accusation".

Lesson 4 of 5.
حکم (hokm) — a verdict, a ruling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for accused, then the Persian for a witness.

You'll want to know: حکم.
حکم (hokm) — "a verdict, a ruling".
قاضی حکم را خواند. qâzi hokm râ khând. The judge read out the verdict.
Plural ahkâm, an Arabic broken plural. Also an order or a decree.

The word, taken apart.
حکم (hokm) is usually traced to Arabic ḥukm "judgement, ruling", from the same Arabic family as ḥukūma "government".

Warm-up.

محاکمه او سه ماه طول کشید. mohâkeme-ye u se mâh tul keshid. His trial lasted three months.

محاکمه (mohâkeme) is usually traced to Arabic muḥākama "trial, bringing to judgement", from the same Arabic family as ḥukm "judgement".
