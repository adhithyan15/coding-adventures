Japanese, chapter 466: The Traditional Arts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょどう (shodō) — calligraphy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a painter, then the Japanese for fine art.

You'll want to know: しょどう.
しょどう — shodō — "calligraphy".
しゅうまつに しょどうを ならっています。 — shūmatsu ni shodō o naratte imasu — "I take calligraphy lessons at the weekend."

The word, taken apart.
しょどう (shodō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sho, "write" (Middle Chinese syo) and dō, "way" (dawX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
いけばな (ikebana) — flower arranging.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fine art, then the Japanese for calligraphy.

You'll want to know: いけばな.
いけばな — ikebana — "flower arranging".
ははは いけばなが じょうずです。 — haha wa ikebana ga jōzu desu — "My mother is good at flower arranging."

The word, taken apart.
いけばな (ikebana) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: ike, "keeping alive (ikeru)" and bana, "flower (hana)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
