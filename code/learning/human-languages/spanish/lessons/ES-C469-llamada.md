## You'll want to know: la llamada
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

**la llamada** — the call.

You have seen this word once, in the middle of somebody else's lesson. *El
pedido* listed it as an example of a participle hardened into a noun, and then
moved on. Here it is as yours.

> Voy a hacer una **llamada**.

*Devolver la llamada* — to return the call. *Una llamada perdida* — a missed
call, the notice your phone leaves you.

## Grammar Lens: the vowel is a rule, the gender is not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-04, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

Which vowel the participle takes is decided for you, and
[the participle lesson](./ES-C49-participio.md) gave you that rule.

| the verb ends | the participle takes |
|---|---|
| **-ar** | -ado, -ada |
| **-er**, **-ir** | -ido, -ida |

*Llamar* is an *-ar* verb, so *-ada*. *Pedir* is an *-ir* verb, so *-ido*.
That much you can work out.

What you cannot work out is the **article**. *El pedido* is masculine. *La
llamada* and *la entrada* are feminine. Nothing in *pedir*, *llamar* or
*entrar* predicts which way each one went — the gender was settled by whatever
noun the participle used to lean on, and that noun is long gone.

So learn the article with the word, the way you learned *el impreso* and *la
impresora*. Working out the ending is free; the gender is not.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-04] -->

*Llamada* is *llamar* made into a thing, and *llamar* you have had since the
very beginning — [the lesson where you learned to say your
name](./ES-C03-llamar.md) traced it to Latin **clamare**, to cry out, and
showed you the *cl-* that became *ll-*.

What is worth adding is how literal the noun still is. A *llamada* is a
**crying-out**, and Spanish has not forgotten it: *una llamada de atención* is
a warning — a call for attention, not a phone call at all. The telephone
borrowed a word that was already doing the job.
