# concertar — agreeing, by way of a fight

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-22, ES-LEX-HORA-01, ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la cita** and **la hora**. Say **el envío**. You know the
appointment and you know the time. Name the act of fixing them together.

## You'll want to know: concertar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-22] -->

**concertar** — to arrange, to set up by agreement.

*Concertar una cita* — to make an appointment. It is the verb a clinic, a bank
or a council uses on its website, where a friend would say *quedar*.

It breaks its stem under stress, like *cerrar*: **concierto**, **conciertas**,
**concierta**, but *concertamos*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-03, ES-LEX-CONCIERTO] -->

Say the *yo* form aloud: **concierto**. You have met that word already, and it
meant a performance.

**el concierto** and **yo concierto** are the same word. Both are Latin
**concertāre**, and here is the part that surprises people: *concertāre* meant
**to contend, to fight it out** — *con-* together plus *certāre*, to compete.

| what it became | how the contest turned into it |
|---|---|
| **concertar** una cita | settle it by thrashing it out |
| **un concierto** | voices contending *with* each other |

The sense slid from fighting *against* to striving *together*, and both
descendants kept a piece of it. A negotiation and an orchestra are the same
activity seen from two moods.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-03] -->

English has the same pair and hides it every bit as well. A **concert** is the
performance; **to concert** an effort, and **in concert with**, are the
agreement sense, and **concerted** is what several people
fought out in advance.

So *concertar* is not a word to learn cold. You already had **el concierto**,
and the only new thing is that the noun has a verb standing behind it.

That is worth doing on purpose whenever a new verb looks like a noun you know.
Check whether they are the same word before you treat them as two.
