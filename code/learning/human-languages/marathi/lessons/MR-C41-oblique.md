# घरा- (gharā-) — the stem a noun takes before a postposition

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-GHAR, MR-LEX-KHOLI, MR-SCRIPT-I-INDEPENDENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **घर** became **घरात** last lesson, and **पुणे** became **पुण्यात**
back in chapter twelve. Neither one just took an ending. Both changed shape first.

## Grammar Lens: the noun bends before anything is glued to it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-POSTPOSITION-ON-NOUN, MR-LEX-RAHNE] -->

Marathi puts almost every place, time and direction relation on a **post**position
— a piece glued to the END of the noun. Chapter twelve showed you one. What it
did not say is that the noun does not stay as it is: it first takes an **oblique
stem**, and only then accepts the glue.

> **घर** → **घरा-** → **घरात**
>
> **पुणे** → **पुण्या-** → **पुण्यात**
>
> **खोली** → **खोली-** → **खोलीत**

Three classes, three answers, and the pattern is worth holding as a shape rather
than a list. A neuter noun ending in a consonant takes **-आ**. A noun ending in
**-ए** takes **-या**. A feminine noun in **-ी** does not change at all.

This is the single most load-bearing rule in the book so far, because nothing in
the next three chapters works without it. Every one of the five postpositions
they teach — *madhye*, *var*, *khālī*, *javaḷ* and *samor* — demands the oblique
before it, and a Marathi sentence that skips it is not merely clumsy: it is
wrong.
