# es — "it," the small word German leans on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-GEHEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have the verb: *geht*, "goes." But goes *what*? German needs a
subject, and for a state — the weather, your day — it reaches for one tiny word.

## You'll want to know: es
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ES-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **es** — "it."

Said *ess*: a short clean *e*, the vowel of English *bet*, and a crisp *s*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-ES-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

**es** is English **it**. The two look unalike only because of the same swap you
already met in *das* / *that*: where English keeps a **t**, German often shows an
**s**. Line them up and the pattern is one column wide:

| English | German |
|---|---|
| tha**t** | da**s** |
| i**t** | e**s** |
| wha**t** | wa**s** |

Same word, one consonant apart — and *das*, the neuter "the," is *es*'s close
relative, which is why both point at things rather than people.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-ETYMON-ES-02] -->

- [YOU SAY: "es" (*ess*) — "it"]
- [YOU SAY: "it" then "es"; "that" then "das" — the same t/s swap, twice]

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-ETYMON-ES-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What is *es*? ("It.") Which English consonant turns up as *s* in
German? (*T* — *it/es*, *that/das*, *what/was*.) Say "it goes." (*Es geht*.)
