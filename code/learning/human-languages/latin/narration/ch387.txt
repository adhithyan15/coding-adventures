Latin, chapter 387: Unrest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
discordia, discordiae — disagreement, discord.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an institution, then the Latin for harmony.

You'll want to know: discordia, discordiae.
discordia, discordiae — "disagreement, discord".
Discordia cīvium urbem perdidit. The discord of the citizens ruined the city.
discordia, discordiae f., dis- apart + cor: hearts apart, the opposite of concordia. English discord.

The word, taken apart.
discordia is built on discors "at odds" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from dis- "apart" and cor, cordis "heart". English discord comes from it.

In castrīs sēditiō orta est. A mutiny broke out in the camp.
sēditiō, sēditiōnis f., sēd- apart + itiō, a going: a going apart. English sedition.

sēditiō is built from sēd- "apart" and itiō "a going", from eō "to go" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): people going off on their own. English sedition comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
concitō, concitāre — to stir up, to rouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for seditious, then the Latin for stormy.

You'll want to know: concitō, concitāre.
concitō, concitāre — "to stir up, to rouse".
Ōrātor plēbem in senātum concitāvit. The speaker stirred up the common people against the senate.
concitō, concitāre, concitāvī, concitātum, the frequentative of concieō: con- + cieō, to set in motion.

The word, taken apart.
concitō is the frequentative of concieō "to stir up" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from con- and cieō "to set in motion": to set a crowd moving all together.
