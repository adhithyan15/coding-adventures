اترنا (utarnā) — to get off, to go down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to come out, then the Urdu for to climb.

You'll want to know: اترنا.
اترنا — utarnā — "to get off, to go down".

The word, taken apart.
اترنا (utarnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from uttarati, "passes over; descends" (CDIAL 1770). CDIAL traces the sense from "crosses over" through "lands" to "gets down".

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is a short independent attempt, not a model. You will write the answers, and this page will not show you any copyable Urdu answer.
Put away Roman Urdu, Devanagari, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers. Set a three-minute timer. Write in Urdu script; either approved Nastaliq or accessibility Naskh is fine. Ordinary legible handwriting is enough: imitation Nastaliq calligraphy is not scored.

Writing — timed assessment production.
One practical form — three minutes.
Complete all six fields in Urdu script for a new neighbourhood activity group. Write one short answer in every field. Start the timer when you begin the first field; leave any unfinished field as evidence when it rings.
Your name.
Your city or village.
A language you speak.
One place you like.
One drink or food you like.
One everyday action or kind of work you do.
When the three-minute timer rings, stop. Record the number of completed fields. An honest short attempt is more useful than a perfect untimed one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Spend at most one minute looking at this first attempt. Note one field you completed clearly and one field to practise next; do not rewrite the timed answer. Keep the page as evidence.
This checkpoint is not the complete A1 writing paper. On another day, close this lesson and open the separate A1 writing-only learner paper for the authentic form and named-reader message under one 20-minute assessment clock. That paper and its later rubric review are a separate assessment session, not work inside this under-five-minute lesson. It is writing practice only; it does not establish full A1 exam readiness.
