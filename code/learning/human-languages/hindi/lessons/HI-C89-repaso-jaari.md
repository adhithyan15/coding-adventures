# abhī kī tālikā — two dials, and two sentences you already knew

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 3s] Name the three pieces of a continuous sentence, and say which two of
them move, before you look at the table.

## Grammar Lens: the grid
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-04] -->

| subject | middle and last |
|---|---|
| मैं (m) | रहा हूँ |
| मैं (f) | रही हूँ |
| वह (m) | रहा है |
| वह (f) | रही है |
| हम, वे, आप | रहे हैं |
| तुम | रहे हो |

The stem in front of all of them is **bare**, every time. Six rows and not one
of them touches the verb.

## Grammar Lens: two sentences that were built this way all along
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C18-GHANTA-02, HI-CONCEPT-C20-MAUSAM-03, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03] -->

You have been saying this pattern for a long time without being given it:

| sentence | what it is |
|---|---|
| एक बज रहा है | *baj-* bare, **रहा**, **है** |
| बारिश हो रही है | *ho-* bare, **रही**, **है** |

Neither was a lump. The clock one is masculine and the rain one is feminine,
which is the only difference between them, and it is the middle word that shows
it — exactly as the grid above says it should.

## Grammar Lens: against the habit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-05, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01] -->

| form | the verb |
|---|---|
| habit | बोलता हूँ |
| in progress | बोल रहा हूँ |
| earlier habit | बोलता था |

Three tenses off one stem, and the only thing you have to look at is what sits
immediately after it.
