# tender — hang it out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06, ES-LEX-LIMPIO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la tienda** and **limpio**. A shop is about to turn out to
be a stretched thing.

## You'll want to know: tender
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-LIMPIO] -->

**tender** — to hang out, to spread, to stretch out.

*Voy a tender la ropa* — I'm going to hang the washing out. *El tendedero* —
the airer or the washing line. *Tender la mano* — to hold out your hand.

In a flat with no garden this is daily vocabulary, and the sign in a stairwell
saying *no tender ropa en la fachada* is asking you not to hang washing on the
front of the building.

## Grammar Lens: tender is an e-to-ie verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03] -->

The same break you have watched all chapter, one vowel over:

| stress on the stem | stress on the ending |
|---|---|
| t**ie**ndo, t**ie**ndes | tend**e**mos |
| t**ie**nde, t**ie**nden | tend**é**is |

And now say **tiendo** and **la tienda** together. They are the same shape,
because *la tienda* **is** this verb: a shop was a stretched awning, a cloth
tent pulled open over a market stall.

The shop chapter named that cloth when it taught *la tienda*; here is the
verb it was named from, so the noun and its verb finally sit together.
*Tienda de campaña* — a camping tent — kept the original object entirely.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04, ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06] -->

Latin **tendere**, to stretch.

The family in English is long: **tend**, **tent**, **tension**, **extend**,
**intend**, **pretend** — to stretch something out in front of you —
**attention**, a stretching towards.

Spanish has the whole set too: *tender*, *la tienda*, *extender*, *pretender*,
*la tensión*, *atender*. Every one of them is something being pulled taut,
either a cloth or a mind.
