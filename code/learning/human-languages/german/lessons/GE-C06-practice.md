# Practice — counting, and asking for one

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ZAEHLEN-1-5-01, GE-LEX-ZAEHLEN-6-10-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ten numbers, one article, and one fact about which words a language
keeps. Put them to work.

## Grammar Lens: the ten, and what each one cost
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-EINS-01, GE-LEX-SECHS-01, GE-LEX-ZEHN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SOUND-LAW-01] -->

| German | Said | English twin |
|---|---|---|
| *eins* | *eyns* | one |
| *zwei* | *tsvy* | two |
| *drei* | *dry* | three |
| *vier* | *feer* | four |
| *fünf* | *fewnf* | five |
| *sechs* | *zeks* | six |
| *sieben* | *ZEE-ben* | seven |
| *acht* | *akht* | eight |
| *neun* | *noyn* | nine |
| *zehn* | *tsayn* | ten |

Ten numbers and not one of them borrowed. Every one is the English word with a
predictable coat on, which is the whole reason this chapter was cheap.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-EIN-EINE-01, GE-GRAMMAR-COUNTING-S-DROPS-02, GE-LEX-BITTE-01, GE-LEX-DANKE-01, GE-LEX-TAG-02, GE-LEX-NACHT-02, GE-LEX-SEHR-01, GE-LEX-GUT-02, GE-LEX-UND-01] -->

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *Eins, zwei, drei …* | One, two, three… |
| *Und?* | And? |
| *… vier, fünf, sechs, sieben, acht, neun, zehn!* | …four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten! |
| *Sehr gut!* | Very good! |
| *Danke!* — *Bitte.* | Thanks! — You're welcome. |

Every word in that exchange is one you already own, and seven of them you
learned this chapter.

Then the article, on the two nouns *guten Tag* and *gute Nacht* are built from:

> *der Tag* → **ein Tag** · *die Nacht* → **eine Nacht**

*Eins* on its own keeps its **-s**, because nothing follows it. The moment a
naming word arrives, the *-s* comes off.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ZAEHLEN-1-5-01, GE-LEX-ZAEHLEN-6-10-01, GE-GRAMMAR-COUNTING-S-DROPS-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Count to ten. When does *eins* become *ein*? (In front of a naming
word.) Are the German numbers borrowed from Latin? (No — they are the English
numbers' twins.) Which German words *were* borrowed from Latin? (The month
names.)
