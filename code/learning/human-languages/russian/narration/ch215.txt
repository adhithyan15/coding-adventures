Russian, chapter 215: Of course, Probably, Maybe, Unfortunately, Fortunately.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
конечно (konéchno) — of course.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for up, then the Russian for down.

You'll want to know: конечно.
конечно (konéchno) — "of course".

The word, taken apart.
конечно (konéchno) is the adverb of конечный, "final", from конец, "end": "finally, surely", and so "of course".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: может быть.

The word, taken apart.
может быть (mózhet byt) is "it can be": может, "can", from мочь, with быть, "to be", which Vasmer relates to Lithuanian būti, "to be", and Latin fuī, "I was".

Lesson 4 of 5.
к сожалению (k sozhaléniyu) — unfortunately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for probably, then the Russian for maybe.

You'll want to know: к сожалению.
к сожалению (k sozhaléniyu) — "unfortunately".

The word, taken apart.
к сожалению (k sozhaléniyu) is "to (my) regret": к, "to", with сожалению, from сожаление, "regret", built on жалеть, "to pity". Vasmer relates жаль, "pity", to Lithuanian gėlà, "torment", and gélti, "to sting".

Lesson 5 of 5.
к счастью (k schástyu) — fortunately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for maybe, then the Russian for unfortunately.

You'll want to know: к счастью.
к счастью (k schástyu) — "fortunately".

The word, taken apart.
к счастью (k schástyu) is "to (my) luck": к, "to", with счастью, from счастье, "luck, happiness", which is usually explained as sŭ-, "good", with часть, "a share": "a good lot" (Vasmer).
