Latin, chapter 312: Poena et supplicium.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fateor, fatērī — to admit, to confess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for innocent, then the Latin for fault.

You'll want to know: fateor, fatērī.
fateor, fatērī — "to admit, to confess".
Tandem fūr fassus est sē crumēnam rapuisse. At last the thief admitted that he had snatched the purse.
fateor, fatērī, fassus sum, a deponent, with accusative and infinitive. English confess comes from a compound of it.

The word, taken apart.
fateor is inherited from Proto-Italic fatēōr (EtymDB), usually traced to a root meaning "to speak", also seen in fārī, "to speak". English confess comes from a compound.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pūniō, pūnīre — to punish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fault, then the Latin for to admit.

You'll want to know: pūniō, pūnīre.
pūniō, pūnīre — "to punish".
Magister discipulōs pigrōs pūniēbat. The teacher used to punish lazy pupils.
pūniō, pūnīre, pūnīvī, pūnītum, old form poeniō. From poena (taught). English punish.

The word, taken apart.
pūniō is built on poena, "penalty" (EtymDB), itself a loan from Greek poinē, "blood-money". English punish comes from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
supplicium, suppliciī — punishment, execution; a prayer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to admit, then the Latin for to punish.

You'll want to know: supplicium, suppliciī.
supplicium, suppliciī — "punishment, execution; a prayer".
Prōditōrēs ad supplicium ductī sunt. The traitors were led to execution.
Neuter. First a kneeling in prayer, then the kneeling of the condemned. Summum supplicium: the death penalty.

The word, taken apart.
supplicium is built on supplex, "kneeling, begging" (EtymDB): a kneeling down, in prayer or to receive punishment.

Lesson 4 of 5.
exsilium, exsiliī — exile, banishment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to punish, then the Latin for punishment.

You'll want to know: exsilium, exsiliī.
exsilium, exsiliī — "exile, banishment".
Cōnsul in exsilium īre coāctus est. The consul was forced to go into exile.
Neuter, also exilium. A Roman citizen could often choose exile before his trial ended. English exile.

The word, taken apart.
exsilium is built on exsul, "an exiled person" (EtymDB), which Lewis & Short connect either with solum, "soil", or with a root meaning "to go"; the origin is not settled. English exile comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vinculum, vinculī — a chain, a bond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for punishment, then the Latin for exile.

You'll want to know: vinculum, vinculī.
vinculum, vinculī — "a chain, a bond".
Latrōnēs in vinculīs tenēbantur. The bandits were kept in chains.
Neuter, also vinclum. In vincula conicere: to throw into prison.

The word, taken apart.
vinculum is built on vinciō, "to bind, to fetter", with the tool ending -ulum (EtymDB): that which binds.
