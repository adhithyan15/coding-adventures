Lesson 1 of 7.
あぶない (abunai) — dangerous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for all right, then the Japanese for endurance.

You'll want to know: あぶない.
あぶない — abunai — "dangerous".
この みちは あぶないですか。 — kono michi wa abunai desu ka — "Is this road dangerous?"

The word, taken apart.
あぶない (abunai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 7.
きけん (kiken) — danger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for endurance, then the Japanese for dangerous.

You'll want to know: きけん.
きけん — kiken — "danger".
いのちに きけんは ありません。 — inochi ni kiken wa arimasen — "There is no danger to life."

The word, taken apart.
きけん (kiken) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "danger" (Middle Chinese ngjwe) and ken, "steep, risky" (xjemX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 7.
いきる (ikiru) — to be alive; to live (on).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dangerous, then the Japanese for danger.

You'll want to know: いきる.
いきる — ikiru — "to be alive; to live (on)".
さかなは みずの なかで いきます。 — sakana wa mizu no naka de ikimasu — "Fish live in water."
Polite いきます, ikimasu; an ichidan verb. It sounds the same as the polite form of いく, iku.

The word, taken apart.
いきる (ikiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
