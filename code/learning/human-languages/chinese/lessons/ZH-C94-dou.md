## You'll want to know: 都
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C94-DOU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**都** — *dōu* — "all; both".

> **我们都是学生** *wǒmen dōu shì xuésheng* — We're all students.

> **他们都很好** *tāmen dōu hěn hǎo* — They're all fine.

The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**都** (*dōu*) is inherited from Middle Chinese *tu* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'all together', and also 'a capital city', the sense said dū today. The regular change from *tu* gives dū; why the word for 'all' is said dōu is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C31-PEOPLE31-04] -->

都 goes in front of the verb or the adjective and looks back at the people before it: 我们都, all of us. With two people it means both.
