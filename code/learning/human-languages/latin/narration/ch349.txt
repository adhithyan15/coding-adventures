Latin, chapter 349: Fābulae mīrae.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fābulor, fābulārī — to chat, to tell stories.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an omen, then the Latin for to prophesy.

You'll want to know: fābulor, fābulārī.
fābulor, fābulārī — "to chat, to tell stories".
Senēs in tabernā dē rēbus antīquīs fābulābantur. The old men were chatting in the inn about the old days.
fābulor, fābulārī, fābulātus sum, a deponent, from fābula (taught). In Spain it became hablar, 'to speak'.

The word, taken apart.
fābulor is built on fābula, "story, talk" (Lewis & Short), from fārī, "to speak". Spanish hablar, "to speak", comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
āiō — to say yes; to say.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to prophesy, then the Latin for to chat.

You'll want to know: āiō.
āiō — "to say yes; to say".
Nauta mōnstrum sē vīdisse ait. The sailor says he saw a monster.
Defective: mostly āiō, ais, ait, aiunt. Aiunt: they say. Its opposite is negō (taught), 'to say no'.

The word, taken apart.
āiō is inherited from Proto-Italic agjō (EtymDB), from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to say". Only a few of its forms are in use (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
mīrābilis, mīrābile — wonderful, astonishing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to chat, then the Latin for to say yes.

You'll want to know: mīrābilis, mīrābile.
mīrābilis, mīrābile — "wonderful, astonishing".
Fābula mīrābilis erat, sed vēra. The story was astonishing, but true.
Two endings, from a verb meaning 'to wonder at'. English admirable is a cousin.

The word, taken apart.
mīrābilis is built on mīror, "to wonder at", from mīrus, "wonderful" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -bilis. English marvel and miracle come from the same family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
incrēdibilis, incrēdibile — unbelievable, incredible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to say yes, then the Latin for wonderful.

You'll want to know: incrēdibilis, incrēdibile.
incrēdibilis, incrēdibile — "unbelievable, incredible".
Nauta rēs incrēdibilēs nārrābat. The sailor was telling unbelievable things.
Two endings: in- 'not' + crēdibilis 'believable', from crēdō (taught). English incredible.

The word, taken apart.
incrēdibilis joins in-, "not", and crēdibilis, "worthy of belief", from crēdō, "to believe" (EtymDB). English incredible comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
commentum, commentī — an invention, a made-up story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wonderful, then the Latin for unbelievable.

You'll want to know: commentum, commentī.
commentum, commentī — "an invention, a made-up story".
Tōta fābula commentum erat. The whole story was an invention.
Neuter, the participle of comminīscor, 'to think up'. Also a commentary, which gives English comment.

The word, taken apart.
commentum is the participle of comminīscor, "to devise, to invent" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from the root of mēns, "mind". English comment comes from it.
