Russian, chapter 23: One to Five, and an Answer to Skolko.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
два (dva) — two, and the question this book could already ask.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask сколько — how many. Then say один and the root it goes back to.

You'll want to know first — one word.
два — dva — two.
Twenty-two chapters in, this book can ask сколько? and could not understand any answer to it. один is in the corpus, but it arrived as half of the один … другой pattern — one … the other — which is a joining word wearing a number's clothes. два (dva) is the first number this track teaches as a number.

The word, taken apart — the same word as English two.
два (dva) continues Indo-European dwóh₁, "two." English two, Latin duo, Greek dýo and Sanskrit dvá are the same word, and Russian's form is among the least worn of them.
Say dva and two one after the other. That is not something to learn; it is something to recognise, and it will be true of nearly every number in this slice.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — say: два (dva) — two]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — connect: два (dva) from dwóh₁ becomes English two, Latin duo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: один, два (dva)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Wiktionary: два (dva); Omniglot, Numbers in Russian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Someone asks skolko. Answer: two.


Lesson 2 of 5.
три (tri) — three.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say два (dva) and its English cousin.

You'll want to know first — one word.
три — tri — three.
Three letters, and every one of them is a Cyrillic shape this book taught long ago. Say it and read it at the same time: т-р-и.

The word, taken apart — three, tres, tri.
три continues Indo-European tréyes, "three." English three, Latin trēs, Greek treîs and Sanskrit tri are the same word.
This is the plainest of the whole set. Nothing has shifted: the t, the r and the vowel are all where they were, which is why три (tri) and three sound like each other and сто and hundred, later in this slice, will not.

Lesson 3 of 5.
четыре (chetýre) — four, and one consonant's three fates.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say три (tri) and its Latin cousin, then два (dva).

You'll want to know first — one word.
четыре — chetýre — four.
Three syllables, stressed on the second, and the vowel in that stressed syllable is ы — the sound this book warned has no English equivalent. Hold it there and the word falls into place.

The word, taken apart — quattuor, four, chetyre.
четыре (chetýre) continues Indo-European kʷetwóres, "four."
The opening kʷ is the interesting part, because the branches split three ways over it. Latin kept it whole: quattuor. English lost the lip-rounding and softened the k to f: four. Slavic pushed it forward to a ch, which is what you are saying.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: два (dva), три (tri), четыре (chetýre)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 4 of 5.
пять (pyat) — five, and a cousin English kept whole.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say четыре (chetýre) and what three branches did with its first sound.

You'll want to know first — one word.
пять — pyat — five.
One syllable, ending in the soft sign that this book has been putting on the ends of words since its first chapters. It softens the т and is not a sound of its own.

The word, taken apart — pentagon.
пять (pyat) continues Indo-European pénkʷe, "five." Greek pénte, Latin quīnque and English five are the same word.
The Greek form is the one an English speaker already owns: pentagon, pentathlon, Pentecost — all of them carry five in a shape much closer to пять (pyat) than English five is. The number was in the reader's vocabulary before the lesson was.

Lesson 5 of 5.
Payoff — one to five, and an answer to сколько.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask сколько, say пять (pyat), then say what один goes back to.

Guided Practice.
Hear each of the five named singly and out of order, and say what it is.
Count backwards from пять (pyat) to один.
Read the five printed shuffled — пять (pyat), один, четыре (chetýre), три (tri), два (dva) — and say each.
Write all five from dictation. No new Cyrillic letter appears in any of them.
Hear сколько? and answer it with each of the five in turn.
Then say the English cousin of each: one, two, three, four, five. All five have one. That is not luck — Russian's numerals are among the least worn in the family, and this book's reader already owned most of them.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Omniglot, Numbers in Russian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 16 seconds]
Count backwards from five to one in Russian.
