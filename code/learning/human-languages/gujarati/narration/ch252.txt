Gujarati, chapter 252: Duties and responsibility.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ફરજ (pharaj) — duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to pay attention, then the Gujarati for to memorize.

You'll want to know: ફરજ.
ફરજ — pharaj — "duty".
આ મારી ફરજ છે. — ā mārī pharaj che — "This is my duty."

The word, taken apart.
ફરજ (pharaj) is a loan from Arabic farḍ, "obligation", which reached Gujarati through Persian farz (EtymDB). Its Arabic root is f-r-ḍ (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 5.
જવાબદારી (javābdārī) — responsibility.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to memorize, then the Gujarati for duty.

You'll want to know: જવાબદારી.
જવાબદારી — javābdārī — "responsibility".
ઘરની જવાબદારી મારા પિતા પાસે છે. — gharnī javābdārī mārā pitā pāse che — "My father has the responsibility for the house."

The word, taken apart.
જવાબદારી (javābdārī) is built on javābdār, "answerable", with -ī (EtymDB). Javāb, "answer", is a loan from Arabic jawāb, root j-w-b (Lane), and -dār, "holder", is usually traced to Persian.

Lesson 3 of 5.
આચાર્ય (ācārya) — principal, head teacher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for duty, then the Gujarati for responsibility.

You'll want to know: આચાર્ય.
આચાર્ય — ācārya — "principal, head teacher".
આચાર્ય દરેક વર્ગમાં જાય છે. — ācārya darek vargmā̃ jāy che — "The principal goes to every class."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
આચાર્ય (ācārya) is Sanskrit ācārya, "teacher", taken over whole as a tatsama. CDIAL has no entry for it, though it lists the related ācāra, "mode of action, conduct" (CDIAL 1071).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ગણવેશ (gaṇveś) — uniform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for responsibility, then the Gujarati for principal.

You'll want to know: ગણવેશ.
ગણવેશ — gaṇveś — "uniform".
શાળામાં બધા ગણવેશ પહેરે છે. — śāḷāmā̃ badhā gaṇveś pahere che — "Everyone wears a uniform at school."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગણવેશ (gaṇveś), the dress of a group, is a modern learned compound built from Sanskrit gaṇa, "group", and vēṣa, "dress"; CDIAL lists Old Gujarati vesa, "dress" (CDIAL 12129).
