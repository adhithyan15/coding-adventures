Italian, chapter 352: Le ripercussioni.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'impatto — the impact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to determine, then the Italian for to influence.

You'll want to know: l'impatto.
l'impatto — "the impact".
Il turismo ha un forte impatto sulla città. Tourism has a strong impact on the city.
Also a physical crash: l'impatto con il muro.

The word, taken apart.
impatto was borrowed from English impact (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin impāctus, past participle of impingere "to push or strike against" (Lewis & Short), built on pangere "to fix, to drive in".

Lesson 2 of 5.
la ripercussione — the repercussion, the knock-on effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to influence, then the Italian for the impact.

You'll want to know: la ripercussione.
la ripercussione — "the repercussion, the knock-on effect".
La crisi ha avuto ripercussioni su tutto il paese. The crisis had repercussions on the whole country.
Mostly plural: avere ripercussioni su.

The word, taken apart.
ripercussione is a learned borrowing from Latin repercussiō "a striking back, a rebound" (Lewis & Short), from repercutere "to strike back", built on percutere and quatere "to shake, to strike".

Lesson 3 of 5.
il risvolto — the implication, the side effect; the turn-up (of trousers).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the impact, then the Italian for the repercussion.

You'll want to know: il risvolto.
il risvolto — "the implication, the side effect; the turn-up (of trousers)".
La storia ha un risvolto che nessuno immaginava. The story has an implication that no one imagined.
The first sense is the folded-back part of a sleeve or of trousers; then the hidden side of a matter.

The word, taken apart.
risvolto is built from ri- "back" and svolto, a form of svolgere and volgere "to turn", which is inherited from Latin volvere "to roll, to turn" (Lewis & Short): the part turned back.

Lesson 4 of 5.
scatenare — to unleash, to spark off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the repercussion, then the Italian for the implication.

You'll want to know: scatenare.
scatenare — "to unleash, to spark off".
La sua frase ha scatenato una lunga discussione. His remark sparked off a long discussion.
Literally to unchain. Scatenarsi — to break loose; on a dance floor, to let yourself go.

The word, taken apart.
scatenare is built from s- "off" and catena "chain", which is inherited from Latin catēna "a chain" (Lewis & Short): to let something off the chain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
innescare — to trigger, to set off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the implication, then the Italian for to unleash.

You'll want to know: innescare.
innescare — "to trigger, to set off".
Un piccolo errore può innescare un grande problema. A small mistake can trigger a big problem.
Literally to bait a hook or to prime a charge.

The word, taken apart.
innescare is built from in- and esca "bait; tinder", which is inherited from Latin ēsca "food, bait" (Lewis & Short): to put the bait or the spark in place.
