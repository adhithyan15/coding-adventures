Japanese, chapter 487: Running a Business.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょうばい (shōbai) — business; trade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to fit in, then the Japanese for to peel off.

You'll want to know: しょうばい.
しょうばい — shōbai — "business; trade".
そふは しょうばいを しています。 — sofu wa shōbai o shiteimasu — "My grandfather runs a business."

The word, taken apart.
しょうばい (shōbai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "selling" (Middle Chinese syang) and bai, "sell" (meaH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
うりあげ (uriage) — sales; takings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a merchant, then the Japanese for a shop manager.

You'll want to know: うりあげ.
うりあげ — uriage — "sales; takings".
せんしゅうの うりあげは よかったです。 — senshū no uriage wa yokatta desu — "Last week's sales were good."

The word, taken apart.
うりあげ (uriage) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from uri, "selling" and age, "raising" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
