Malayalam, chapter 508: Getting Wet.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ചാറുക (cāṟuka) — to drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to stop raining, then the Malayalam for a light drizzle.

You'll want to know: ചാറുക.
ചാറുക — cāṟuka — "to drizzle".
പുറത്ത് മഴ ചാറുന്നു. puṟattŭ maḻa cāṟunnu. It is drizzling outside.
Past: ചാറി, cāṟi.
The subject is the rain: മഴ ചാറുന്നു, the rain drizzles.

The word, taken apart.
ചാറുക (cāṟuka) is native Dravidian, "to run off; to drizzle" (DEDR 2482).

Lesson 2 of 6.
പേമാരി (pēmāri) — a downpour; torrential rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a light drizzle, then the Malayalam for to drizzle.

You'll want to know: പേമാരി.
പേമാരി — pēmāri — "a downpour; torrential rain".
രാത്രി മുഴുവൻ പേമാരി ആയിരുന്നു. rātri muḻuvan pēmāri āyirunnu. There was torrential rain all night.
Stronger than മഴ: the rain of a storm.

The word, taken apart.
പേമാരി (pēmāri) is a Malayalam word for heavy rain whose second part is മാരി, heavy rain (DEDR 4819, which compares Sanskrit māri, rain); the origin of the first part is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
നനയുക (nanayuka) — to get wet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to drizzle, then the Malayalam for a downpour.

You'll want to know: നനയുക.
നനയുക — nanayuka — "to get wet".
കുട ഇല്ലാത്തതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ നനഞ്ഞു. kuṭa illāttatukoṇṭŭ ñān nanaññu. Because I had no umbrella, I got wet.
Past: നനഞ്ഞു, nanaññu.
The person who gets wet is the subject: ഞാൻ നനഞ്ഞു, I got wet.

The word, taken apart.
നനയുക (nanayuka) is native Dravidian, "to become wet, to be soaked" (DEDR 3630).

Lesson 4 of 6.
മഴക്കോട്ട് (maḻakkōṭṭŭ) — a raincoat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a downpour, then the Malayalam for to get wet.

You'll want to know: മഴക്കോട്ട്.
മഴക്കോട്ട് — maḻakkōṭṭŭ — "a raincoat".
അവൻ മഴക്കോട്ട് ഇട്ട് ബൈക്കിൽ പോയി. avan maḻakkōṭṭŭ iṭṭŭ baikkil pōyi. He put on a raincoat and went on his motorbike.
മഴ, rain, plus English coat.

The word, taken apart.
മഴക്കോട്ട് (maḻakkōṭṭŭ) is a Malayalam compound built from the native മഴ, rain (DEDR 4753), and കോട്ട്, which was borrowed from English coat.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ഉണക്കുക (uṇakkuka) — to dry out; to hang out to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to get wet, then the Malayalam for a raincoat.

You'll want to know: ഉണക്കുക.
ഉണക്കുക — uṇakkuka — "to dry out; to hang out to dry".
വെയിൽ വന്നപ്പോൾ അമ്മ തുണികൾ ഉണക്കി. veyil vannappōḷ amma tuṇikaḷ uṇakki. When the sun came out, mother dried the clothes.
Past: ഉണക്കി, uṇakki.
The transitive partner of ഉണങ്ങുക, to become dry.

The word, taken apart.
ഉണക്കുക (uṇakkuka) is native Dravidian, "to put to dry, to air" (DEDR 601).
