Spanish, chapter 611: Con una condición.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la condición — the condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the immigration, then the Spanish for the living together.

You'll want to know: la condición.
la condición — "the condition".
Te ayudo con una condición. I will help you on one condition.
Las condiciones de trabajo — working conditions.

The word, taken apart.
condición is a learned borrowing from Latin condicio 'agreement, terms', built on condicere 'to agree together', from dicere 'to say' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
siempre que — as long as; whenever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the living together, then the Spanish for the condition.

You'll want to know: siempre que.
siempre que — "as long as; whenever".
Puedes ir siempre que vuelvas pronto. You can go as long as you come back early.
With the subjunctive it sets a condition; with the indicative it means whenever.

The word, taken apart.
siempre que is built on siempre, inherited from Latin semper 'always', and que (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
con tal de que — provided that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for as long as, then the Spanish for unless.

You'll want to know: con tal de que.
con tal de que — "provided that".
Te lo presto con tal de que me lo devuelvas. I will lend it to you provided that you give it back.
Followed by the subjunctive; con tal de + infinitive also works.

The word, taken apart.
con tal de que is built on tal, inherited from Latin talis 'such, of that kind' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
en caso de que — in case; if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for unless, then the Spanish for provided that.

You'll want to know: en caso de que.
en caso de que — "in case; if".
En caso de que llegues tarde, llámame. If you are late, call me.
Followed by the subjunctive; en caso de + noun: en caso de incendio — in case of fire.

The word, taken apart.
en caso de que is built on caso, a learned borrowing from Latin casus 'a falling, an event', from cadere 'to fall' (EtymDB).
