French, chapter 272: Jadis et naguère.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jadis — long ago, in olden days.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for in the long run, then the French for sooner or later.

You'll want to know: jadis.
jadis — "long ago, in olden days".
Jadis, cette rue était un marché. Long ago, this street was a market.
Literary and a little old-fashioned; fairy tales like it.

The word, taken apart.
jadis comes from Old French ja a dis "it is days already" (EtymDB), with ja from Latin iam "already" and dis from Latin diēs "day".

Lesson 2 of 5.
naguère — not long ago; formerly (literary).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sooner or later, then the French for long ago.

You'll want to know: naguère.
naguère — "not long ago; formerly (literary)".
Naguère, ce village était très calme. Not long ago, this village was very quiet.
From n'a guère: it is hardly (long) ago. Many writers now use it for 'formerly'.

The word, taken apart.
naguère comes from n'a guère "it is hardly (long)". Guère is usually traced to a Frankish word meaning "much"; the origin is not fully settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 3 of 5.
auparavant — before, previously.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for long ago, then the French for not long ago.

You'll want to know: auparavant.
auparavant — "before, previously".
Elle est médecin; auparavant, elle était professeur. She is a doctor; before that, she was a teacher.
An adverb that stands alone. Before a noun, French uses avant.

The word, taken apart.
auparavant is built from au, par and avant; avant is inherited from Late Latin abante, built on Latin ante "before" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
jusqu'alors — until then, up to then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for not long ago, then the French for before.

You'll want to know: jusqu'alors.
jusqu'alors — "until then, up to then".
Jusqu'alors, personne ne connaissait son secret. Until then, nobody knew his secret.
Looks back to a moment in the past; from today, say jusqu'à maintenant.

The word, taken apart.
jusque is built on Latin ūsque "all the way" (Lewis & Short) with inde (EtymDB) or, in another view, dē; alors joins à and lors, which EtymDB traces to Latin illā hōrā "at that hour".

Lesson 5 of 5.
depuis lors — since then, ever since.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for before, then the French for until then.

You'll want to know: depuis lors.
depuis lors — "since then, ever since".
Il est parti en 2010; depuis lors, il n'écrit plus. He left in 2010; since then, he no longer writes.
Formal; in speech, depuis or depuis ce moment-là.

The word, taken apart.
depuis joins de and puis "then" (EtymDB); lors is traced in EtymDB to Latin illā hōrā "at that hour".
