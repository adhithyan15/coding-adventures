Urdu, chapter 33: Reading — Six Words, Six Lines, and a First Paragraph.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) (pānī · kām · shahr · kamra · mā̃ · lāhaur) (yih merī mā̃ hai. yih hamārā kamra hai.) (salām · āp kā nām kyā hai? · maiṅ yahāṅ hūṅ) — six words, nothing said first.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have written every letter in every one of these. Not one word is new, and not one shape inside them is new either.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them before you read them.

Reading.
پانی کام شہر کمرہ ماں لاہور.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once. Do not take each word apart — look at the whole shape and let it arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to unpick. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Urdu joins its letters, so a word is not five shapes standing in a row. It is one shape.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why reading a word cold is a different skill from writing it. Writing, you make the joins one at a time and know what is coming. Reading, the joins are already made and the whole word has to land at once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six is enough for one sitting.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which of the six is a city?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What was new? (Nobody said them first. You read them.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) (pānī · kām · shahr · kamra · mā̃ · lāhaur) (yih merī mā̃ hai. yih hamārā kamra hai.) (salām · āp kā nām kyā hai? · maiṅ yahāṅ hūṅ) — six lines, in the order they happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you single words. These are whole turns.
[pause 1 second]
You have said every one of them. None is new.

Reading.
سلام آپ کا نام کیا ہے؟ آپ کیسے ہیں؟ میں یہاں ہوں۔ شکریہ نہیں.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time hear that these are not six separate lines. They are the opening of a meeting, in the order it happens.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two of these are questions and you can see it before you read a word of them, because the question mark sits at the end where Urdu puts it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Spoken, a voice told you which turn was whose. On the page nothing does. You worked it out from where each line sits, which is most of what reading a conversation is.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and the order is the whole lesson.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
How many of the six lines are questions?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six lines were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What told you which were questions? (The mark at the end.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) (pānī · kām · shahr · kamra · mā̃ · lāhaur) (yih merī mā̃ hai. yih hamārā kamra hai.) (salām · āp kā nām kyā hai? · maiṅ yahāṅ hūṅ) — the first paragraph.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now a whole paragraph.
[pause 1 second]
Thirty-seven words, and every letter inside them is one you can already write.

Reading.
یہ میری ماں ہے۔ یہ ہمارا کمرہ ہے۔ کمرے میں پانی ہے۔ آسمان نیلا ہے۔ وہ کالا ہے۔ ہم لاہور میں ہیں۔ لاہور ایک شہر ہے۔ یہاں کام ہے۔ کیا یہ پرانا ہے؟ نہیں۔ سلام اور شکریہ۔.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and notice how little work it took.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Almost every line here is the same sentence: something is something. ہے ends nearly all of them.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is what makes a first paragraph possible. You know the shape of each line before you reach it, so your attention goes to the two or three words in the middle that change.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then one line breaks the pattern, and you can see it break before you read it: the ninth ends in a question mark, and the tenth answers it in one word.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole paragraph the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because every piece was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the paragraph aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which city are they in?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that paragraph were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word ends nearly every line? (ہے.)
