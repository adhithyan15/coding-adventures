Italian, chapter 301: Arrivano i soccorsi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il soccorso — emergency help, rescue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for unhurt, then the Italian for to come through all right.

You'll want to know: il soccorso.
il soccorso — "emergency help, rescue".
Il soccorso è arrivato dopo dieci minuti. Help arrived after ten minutes.
Chiamare i soccorsi — to call for help.

The word, taken apart.
soccorso is inherited from Vulgar Latin succursum (EtymDB), built on Latin succurrere "to run to help".

Lesson 2 of 5.
soccorrere — to come to the aid of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to come through all right, then the Italian for emergency help.

You'll want to know: soccorrere.
soccorrere — "to come to the aid of".
I passanti hanno soccorso il ciclista. Passers-by came to the cyclist's aid.
Conjugated like correre: ha soccorso.

The word, taken apart.
soccorrere is inherited from Latin succurrere "to run up to help" (EtymDB), from sub and currere "to run".

il soccorritore — "the rescuer, the paramedic".
I soccorritori hanno lavorato tutta la notte. The rescuers worked all night.
A woman is la soccorritrice.

The word, taken apart.
soccorritore is soccorrere with the agent ending -tore; the root is Latin currere "to run" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la barella — the stretcher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to come to the aid of, then the Italian for the rescuer.

You'll want to know: la barella.
la barella — "the stretcher".
Lo hanno messo sulla barella. They put him on the stretcher.
In barella — on a stretcher.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of barella is not settled; it is often linked with bara "a bier", which EtymDB traces to a Longobard word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la sirena — the siren.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the rescuer, then the Italian for the stretcher.

You'll want to know: la sirena.
la sirena — "the siren".
Si sente la sirena dell'ambulanza. You can hear the ambulance siren.
In stories, la sirena is also a mermaid.

The word, taken apart.
sirena comes through Late Latin sīrēna from Greek Seirḗn (EtymDB), the singing sea creatures of the old myths.
