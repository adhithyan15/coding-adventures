French, chapter 347: Puisque et vu que.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
puisque — since, as (a known reason).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to lead to, then the French for to follow.

You'll want to know: puisque.
puisque — "since, as (a known reason)".
Puisque tu es là, tu peux m'aider. Since you are here, you can help me.
Takes the indicative: puisque tu es là. The reason is one both people already know. Before il or elle: puisqu'il.

The word, taken apart.
puisque is built from puis "then, afterwards" and que. puis is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin form of post "after" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
étant donné que — given that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to follow, then the French for since.

You'll want to know: étant donné que.
étant donné que — "given that".
Étant donné que le magasin est fermé, nous rentrons. Given that the shop is closed, we are going home.
Takes the indicative: étant donné que le magasin est fermé. Careful style, frequent in writing.

The word, taken apart.
étant donné que is built from étant, the present participle of être, and donné "given". donner is inherited from Latin dōnāre "to give as a present" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
vu que — seeing that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for since, then the French for given that.

You'll want to know: vu que.
vu que — "seeing that".
Vu qu'il pleut, nous restons à la maison. Seeing that it is raining, we are staying at home.
Takes the indicative: vu qu'il pleut. A little more relaxed than étant donné que.

The word, taken apart.
vu que is built from vu, the past participle of voir "to see", which is inherited from Latin vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
par conséquent — consequently, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for given that, then the French for seeing that.

You'll want to know: par conséquent.
par conséquent — "consequently, therefore".
Il est malade; par conséquent, il ne vient pas. He is ill; consequently, he is not coming.
Written style; in speech, donc or alors.

The word, taken apart.
conséquent is a learned borrowing from Latin cōnsequēns "following, consistent" (EtymDB), the participle of cōnsequī "to follow after", built on sequī "to follow" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
c'est pourquoi — that is why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for seeing that, then the French for consequently.

You'll want to know: c'est pourquoi.
c'est pourquoi — "that is why".
Je n'ai pas d'argent; c'est pourquoi je reste ici. I have no money; that is why I am staying here.
It brings in the result, after the cause.

The word, taken apart.
pourquoi is built from pour "for", from Latin prō, and quoi "what", from Latin quid (Lewis & Short): for what.
