Portuguese, chapter 433: Barulho e sujidade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a algazarra — the racket, the din.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to recharge, then the Portuguese for the power cut.

You'll want to know: a algazarra.
a algazarra — "the racket, the din".
Os vizinhos fizeram uma algazarra até às duas da manhã. The neighbours made a racket until two in the morning.
Often of happy noise: children at play.

The word, taken apart.
algazarra comes from Arabic with the article al-; EtymDB traces it to ghazāra 'abundance', but the details are not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
a barata — the cockroach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the racket, then the Portuguese for to creak.

You'll want to know: a barata.
a barata — "the cockroach".
Encontrámos uma barata na cozinha. We found a cockroach in the kitchen.
Same spelling as barata 'cheap', the feminine of barato, but a different word.

The word, taken apart.
barata is inherited from Latin blatta 'cockroach, moth' (EtymDB).

Havia sujidade debaixo da cama. There was dirt under the bed.

The word, taken apart.
sujidade is built on sujo 'dirty', which is inherited from Latin sūcidus 'juicy, greasy' (EtymDB).
