Lesson 1 of 5.
छिनत्ति (chinatti) — cuts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for swims, then the Sanskrit for jumps.

You'll want to know: छिनत्ति.
छिनत्ति — chinatti — "cuts".

The word, taken apart.
chinatti comes from the root √chid "cut, split", with the nasal -na- that class-7 verbs push inside the root (MW). MW sets √chid beside Greek schizō "split", the source of English schism, and Latin scindo; Gujarati kept it as chiṇvũ "to slit" (CDIAL 5045).

The word, taken apart.
bhinatti comes from the root √bhid "split, break", with the class-7 infix -na- (MW). The root is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB): MW sets it beside Latin findo "split" and English bite.

Lesson 3 of 5.
स्थापयति (sthāpayati) — places, puts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for cuts, then the Sanskrit for breaks.

You'll want to know: स्थापयति.
स्थापयति — sthāpayati — "places, puts".

The word, taken apart.
sthāpayati is the causative of the root √sthā "stand": "makes stand, sets up, places" (CDIAL 13756). MW sets √sthā beside Latin stare and English stand; Bengali kept the causative as thuoyā "to put, keep" (CDIAL 13756).

Lesson 4 of 5.
प्रेषयति (preṣayati) — sends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for breaks, then the Sanskrit for places.

You'll want to know: प्रेषयति.
प्रेषयति — preṣayati — "sends".

The word, taken apart.
preṣayati comes from pra "forth" and the root √iṣ "set in motion, send" (MW; CDIAL 9003): "drives on, sends forth". Pali kept it as peseti "sends", especially with a message (CDIAL 9003).

anviṣyati comes from anu "after" and the root √iṣ "seek, wish" (MW's third √iṣ): "seeks after, searches". It is the same √iṣ as icchati "wishes".
