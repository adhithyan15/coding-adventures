## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-VUELO]; assesses=[] -->

**el vuelo** — "the flight."

The noun sits beside **volar**, "to fly," from Latin **volāre**. The oldest
meaning is the act of flying. Travel language narrows it neatly: the
dictionary's third sense is the route an aeroplane covers, with or without
stops, between its point of origin and its destination.

That definition gives you a useful frame rather than an isolated label:

- **el vuelo sale del aeropuerto** — the flight leaves the airport;
- **el destino del vuelo** — the flight's destination.

English **volatile** belongs to the same Latin family. Before it meant
chemically quick to evaporate or emotionally changeable, it described what
could fly away. The common picture is movement into the air.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

You can name the air journey itself: *el vuelo*.
