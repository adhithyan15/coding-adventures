Mandarin Chinese, chapter 246: Out in the Hills.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cūnzi — village.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for mood, then the Mandarin for to look after yourself.

You'll want to know: cūnzi.
cūnzi — "village".
zhège cūnzi hěn xiǎo — This village is tiny.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cūnzi is built from cūn 'village' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshwon and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
shítou — stone; rock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a village, then the Mandarin for a bridge.

You'll want to know: shítou.
shítou — "stone; rock".
nà kuài shítou hěn dà — That rock is huge.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shítou is built from shí 'stone' and 头, here a light noun ending, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyek and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as the body of a mountain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
chítáng — pond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bridge, then the Mandarin for a stone.

You'll want to know: chítáng.
chítáng — "pond".
cūnzi lǐ yǒu yí ge chítáng — There's a pond in the village.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chítáng is built from chí 'a pool' and táng 'a bank'. The dictionary (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription) explains the first, drje there, as standing water, and glosses the second, dang there, with a word for a dyked pond.

Lesson 5 of 5.
huáchuán — to row a boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a stone, then the Mandarin for a pond.

You'll want to know: huáchuán.
huáchuán — "to row a boat".
zhōumò wǒmen qù gōngyuán huáchuán — At weekends we go rowing in the park.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huáchuán is built from huá 'to pole along' and chuán 'boat', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwae and zywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to push a boat forward'.
