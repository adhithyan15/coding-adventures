Latin, chapter 506: Manners.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cōmis, cōme — courteous, friendly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for kindly, then the Latin for goodwill.

You'll want to know: cōmis, cōme.
cōmis, cōme — "courteous, friendly".
Hospes cōmis omnēs benignē salūtat. The courteous host greets everyone kindly.
Two endings. Warm, easy, approachable manners.

The word, taken apart.
cōmis comes from Old Latin cosmis (EtymDB), which EtymDB traces to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to smile"; Lewis & Short calls it doubtful, so the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.

cōmitās, cōmitātis — "friendliness, affability".
Cōmitāte suā multōs amīcōs habet. Through his friendliness he has many friends.
Feminine, third declension, from cōmis.

The word, taken apart.
cōmitās is built on cōmis "courteous" with -itās (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
urbānitās, urbānitātis — refinement, polished wit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for courteous, then the Latin for friendliness.

You'll want to know: urbānitās, urbānitātis.
urbānitās, urbānitātis — "refinement, polished wit".
Urbānitās eius in sermōne appāret. His refinement shows in his conversation.
Feminine, third declension, from urbānus: the polish of the city, urbs. English urbanity.

The word, taken apart.
urbānitās is built on urbānus "of the city", from urbs "city" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English urbanity comes from it.
