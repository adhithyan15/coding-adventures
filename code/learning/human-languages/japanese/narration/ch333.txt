Japanese, chapter 333: Young and Old.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
わかもの (wakamono) — young people; a young person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for discrimination, then the Japanese for all members.

You'll want to know: わかもの.
わかもの — wakamono — "young people; a young person".
いなかには わかものが すくないと おもいます。 — inaka ni wa wakamono ga sukunai to omoimasu — "I think there are few young people in the countryside."

The word, taken apart.
わかもの (wakamono) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from waka, "young (wakai)" and mono, "person", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

The word, taken apart.
こうれいしゃ (kōreisha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "high" (Middle Chinese kaw), rei, "age" (leng) and sha, "someone" (tsyaeX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かいごする (kaigo suru) — to give care; to look after the old or sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an elderly person, then the Japanese for population.

You'll want to know: かいごする.
かいごする — kaigo suru — "to give care; to look after the old or sick".
そぼが びょうきに なったので、ははが かいごしています。 — sobo ga byōki ni natta node, haha ga kaigo shite imasu — "My grandmother fell ill, so my mother is looking after her."
Polite かいごします, kaigoshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ははが そぼを かいごしました。, haha ga sobo o kaigo shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かいごする (kaigo suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, kaigo, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: kai, "mediate" (Middle Chinese keajH) and go, "protect" (huH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
