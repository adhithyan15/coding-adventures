Lesson 1 of 5.
штраф (shtraf) — a fine.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: штраф.
штраф (shtraf) — "a fine".

The word, taken apart.
штраф (shtraf) — from 1711, under Peter the Great; it comes from German Strafe, "punishment, a fine" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
человек (chelovék) — a person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for poems, poetry, then the Russian for a fine.

You'll want to know: человек.
человек (chelovék) — "a person".

The word, taken apart.
человек (chelovék) from Proto-Slavic čelověkŭ — inherited. Vasmer reports the usual reading: the first part goes with челядь, "household people", and Sanskrit kúla-, "family"; the second with Lithuanian vaĩkas, "child, boy". Other accounts he finds less likely.

Lesson 3 of 5.
мужчина (muzhchína) — a man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a fine, then the Russian for a person.

You'll want to know: мужчина.
мужчина (muzhchína) — "a man".

The word, taken apart.
мужчина (muzhchína) is built on муж, "a man, a husband", with -чина, a suffix for persons. муж is a cousin of English man (Vasmer).
