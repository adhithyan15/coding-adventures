Russian, chapter 165: Crow, Eagle, Parrot, Hamster, Hedgehog.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ворона (voróna) — a crow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for air, then the Russian for the earth; land.

You'll want to know: ворона.
ворона (voróna) — "a crow".

The word, taken apart.
ворона (voróna) from Proto-Slavic vorna — inherited, a cousin of Lithuanian várna and Old Prussian warne, "crow" (Vasmer); it goes with ворон, "raven". Church Slavonic has врана, "crow".

Lesson 2 of 5.
орёл (oryól) — an eagle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the earth; land, then the Russian for a crow.

You'll want to know: орёл.
орёл (oryól) — "an eagle".

The word, taken apart.
орёл (oryól) from Proto-Slavic orĭlŭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian erẽlis, Gothic ara and German Aar, "eagle", and Greek órnis, "bird"; English erne, "sea eagle", is a cousin.

You'll want to know: попугай.

The word, taken apart.
попугай (popugáy) — recorded from 1589; it probably came through Dutch papegaai and Low German papagoie from Old French papegai and Spanish papagayo, from Arabic babaghā, "parrot" (Vasmer).

хомяк (khomyák) — Old Russian хомекы; Vasmer thinks it is probably an old loan from Iranian, compare Avestan hamaēstar-, "a foe who throws to the ground".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ёж (yozh) — a hedgehog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a parrot, then the Russian for a hamster.

You'll want to know: ёж.
ёж (yozh) — "a hedgehog".

The word, taken apart.
ёж (yozh) from Proto-Slavic ežĭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian ežỹs, German Igel and Greek echînos, "hedgehog", and ties them to Greek échis, "snake": perhaps a taboo name, "the snake-eater".
