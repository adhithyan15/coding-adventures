Lesson 1 of 5.
おおあめ (ōame) — heavy rain; a downpour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to float, then the Japanese for once more.

You'll want to know: おおあめ.
おおあめ — ōame — "heavy rain; a downpour".
きのうは おおあめでした。 — kinō wa ōame deshita — "It poured yesterday."

The word, taken apart.
おおあめ (ōame) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ō, "big", and ame, "rain" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
あふれる (afureru) — to overflow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for heavy rain, then the Japanese for a flood.

You'll want to know: あふれる.
あふれる — afureru — "to overflow".
いけの みずが あふれています。 — ike no mizu ga afurete imasu — "The pond is overflowing."
Polite あふれます, afuremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): かわが あふれました。, kawa ga afuremashita.

The word, taken apart.
あふれる (afureru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たいへん (taihen) — awful; serious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to overflow, then the Japanese for a cliff.

You'll want to know: たいへん.
たいへん — taihen — "awful; serious".
おおあめで、きのうは たいへんでした。 — ōame de, kinō wa taihen deshita — "With the downpour, yesterday was awful."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
たいへん (taihen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tai, "big" (Middle Chinese dajH) and hen, "unusual" (pjenH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
