## You'll want to know: gōngzhǔ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C329-TALE329-02]; assesses=[] -->

*gōngzhǔ* — "princess".

> *cóngqián yǒu yí ge hěn piàoliang de gōngzhǔ* — Once upon a time there was a very beautiful princess.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*gōngzhǔ* is built from 公 and *zhǔ*, both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kuwng* and *tsyuX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a father' and 'an official' and the second 'to be in charge' and 'a lord'. How the pair came to mean a princess is not settled.
