Portuguese, chapter 475: Nervos e calma.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o nervosismo — nervousness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for tantrum, then the Portuguese for to whine.

You'll want to know: o nervosismo.
o nervosismo — "nervousness".
Antes do exame, o nervosismo não me deixou dormir. Before the exam, nerves kept me awake.
Built on nervoso.

The word, taken apart.
nervosismo is built on nervoso "nervous", from Latin nervōsus "sinewy, vigorous" (Lewis & Short), from nervus "a sinew".

What you've built.

impaciente is a learned borrowing from Latin impatiēns "unable to bear" (EtymDB), built on patī "to suffer, to bear".

Lesson 4 of 5.
acalmar — to calm (down).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to irritate, then the Portuguese for impatient.

You'll want to know: acalmar.
acalmar — "to calm (down)".
A mãe acalmou o bebé com uma canção. The mother calmed the baby with a song.
Acalmar-se — to calm yourself down: acalmei-me.

The word, taken apart.
acalmar is built on calma "calm" (EtymDB), from Late Latin cauma "the heat of the midday sun", from Greek kaûma: at noon everything goes still.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: o alívio.
o alívio — "relief".
Que alívio! O exame correu bem. What a relief! The exam went well.
Sentir alívio — to feel relieved.

The word, taken apart.
alívio comes from aliviar "to relieve" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin alleviāre "to lighten", built on levis "light".
