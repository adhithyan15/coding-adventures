You'll want to know: مستری.
مستری — mistrī — "a mechanic".

The word, taken apart.
مستری (mistrī) is usually traced to Portuguese mestre, "master, master craftsman".

Lesson 3 of 5.
چوکیدار (chaukīdār) — a watchman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a mechanic, then the Urdu for a waiter.

You'll want to know: چوکیدار.
چوکیدار — chaukīdār — "a watchman".

The word, taken apart.
چوکیدار (chaukīdār) is "holder of the post". chaukī, "a guard post", is listed by CDIAL under Sanskrit catuṣka, "set of four; a square courtyard" (CDIAL 4629), and -dār, "holder", is Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سپاہی (sipāhī) — a soldier; a constable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a waiter, then the Urdu for a watchman.

You'll want to know: سپاہی.
سپاہی — sipāhī — "a soldier; a constable".

The word, taken apart.
سپاہی (sipāhī) is a loan, usually traced to Persian sipāhī, "soldier". It is built on sipāh, "army". English took the word as sepoy.

Lesson 5 of 5.
فوج (fauj) — an army.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a watchman, then the Urdu for a soldier; a constable.

You'll want to know: فوج.
فوج — fauj — "an army".

The word, taken apart.
فوج (fauj) is a loan from Arabic fawj, "a troop, a company", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is f-w-j (Lane).
