Portuguese, chapter 291: Raptos e reféns.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o rapto — the kidnapping, the abduction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the threat, then the Portuguese for the murderer.

You'll want to know: o rapto.
o rapto — "the kidnapping, the abduction".
O rapto do empresário foi notícia em toda a cidade. The businessman’s kidnapping was news all over the city.
O rapto de uma criança — a child abduction.

The word, taken apart.
rapto is a learned borrowing from Latin raptus "a carrying off", from rapere "to seize, to snatch" (Lewis & Short).

o sequestro — the hostage-taking, the kidnapping; the hijacking.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: o sequestro.
o sequestro — "the hostage-taking, the kidnapping; the hijacking".
O sequestro do avião durou dez horas. The hijacking of the plane lasted ten hours.
In Brazil the usual word for a kidnapping.

The word, taken apart.
sequestro is a learned borrowing from Latin sequestrum "a deposit placed in trust", from sequester "a go-between, a trustee" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
sequestrar — to hold hostage, to kidnap; to hijack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to kidnap, then the Portuguese for the hostage-taking.

You'll want to know: sequestrar.
sequestrar — "to hold hostage, to kidnap; to hijack".
Sequestraram um autocarro com vinte pessoas. They hijacked a bus with twenty people on it.
In court language: sequestrar bens — to seize goods.

The word, taken apart.
sequestrar is a learned borrowing from Late Latin sequestrāre "to put aside, to hold in trust", from sequester "a trustee" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o refém — the hostage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hostage-taking, then the Portuguese for to hold hostage.

You'll want to know: o refém.
o refém — "the hostage".
O refém voltou para casa ao fim de dois dias. The hostage went back home after two days.
The same form for a woman: a refém. Plural reféns.

The word, taken apart.
refém comes from Arabic rahīn "a pledge, a hostage" (EtymDB).
