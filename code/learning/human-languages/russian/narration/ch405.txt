Russian, chapter 405: A flat tyre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
шина (shína) — a tyre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to start, then the Russian for to stall.

You'll want to know: шина.
шина (shína) — "a tyre".
Передняя шина совсем старая. Perédnyaya shína sovsém stáraya. The front tyre is quite old.
Feminine. In everyday speech people also say колесо — a wheel.

The word, taken apart.
шина (shína) was borrowed from German Schiene 'a rail, a strip' (EtymDB), from Old High German skina; the word probably named the iron band round a cartwheel before the rubber tyre.

Lesson 2 of 5.
прокол (prokól) — a puncture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to stall, then the Russian for a tyre.

You'll want to know: прокол.
прокол (prokól) — "a puncture".
Из-за прокола мы час стояли у дороги. Iz-za prokóla my chas stoyáli u dorógi. Because of the puncture we stood by the road for an hour.
Masculine. У меня прокол (prokól) — I have a puncture.

The word, taken apart.
прокол (prokól) is built on проколоть 'to pierce through', from про- 'through' and колоть 'to prick, to split'. EtymDB does not list прокол (prokól); its parts are ordinary Russian.

Lesson 3 of 5.
насос (nasós) — a pump.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tyre, then the Russian for a puncture.

You'll want to know: насос.
насос (nasós) — "a pump".
Хорошо, что у нас был с собой насос. Khoroshó, chto u nas byl s sobóy nasós. It was a good thing we had a pump with us.
Masculine. Велосипедный насос (nasós) — a bicycle pump.

The word, taken apart.
насос (nasós) is built from на- and the root of сосать 'to suck': a pump sucks up air or water. EtymDB lists the word without a source.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: накачивать.
накачивать (nakáchivat) — "to pump up, to inflate".
Каждую неделю он накачивает шины на велосипеде. Kázhduyu nedélyu on nakáchivayet shíny na velosipéde. Every week he pumps up the tyres on his bicycle.
Imperfective; perfective накачать.

The word, taken apart.
накачивать (nakáchivat) is built from на- and качать 'to swing, to pump'. EtymDB lists качать without a source, so its deeper origin is left open here.

Lesson 5 of 5.
буксовать (buksovát) — to spin (of wheels), to get stuck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pump, then the Russian for to pump up.

You'll want to know: буксовать.
буксовать (buksovát) — "to spin (of wheels), to get stuck".
В снегу колёса долго буксовали. V snegú kolyósa dólgo buksováli. In the snow the wheels spun for a long time.
Imperfective; perfective забуксовать. Present буксует. Also figurative: проект буксует — the project is stuck.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of буксовать (buksovát) is not settled. It is often linked with буксир 'a tug, a towrope', a sailors' word usually traced to Dutch; EtymDB does not list буксовать (buksovát).
