# discutir — to discuss, and to argue

## You'll want to know: discutir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C466-INFORME-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C466-INFORME-03] -->

**discutir** — to discuss, and also to argue.

> Podemos **discutir** el comportamiento en la reunión.

*Discutir un tema* — to discuss a topic. *Discutir con alguien* — to argue
with somebody. **La discusión** is both the discussion and the row.

## Grammar Lens: the preposition decides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C466-INFORME-04] -->

One verb covers what English splits between *discuss* and *argue*, and the
grammar around it does most of the sorting.

| what follows | what it means |
|---|---|
| a thing — *discutir el precio* | discuss it |
| **con** a person — *discutir con él* | argue with him |

A thing after the verb is a topic; a person after **con** is an opponent. It
is not watertight — *discutir el precio con el jefe* can be either, and tone
settles it — but the pattern is reliable enough to lean on, and mistaking the
two is how a polite sentence turns rude.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C466-INFORME-04, ES-LEX-C466-INFORME-01] -->

Latin **discutere**: **dis-**, apart, plus **quatere**, to shake.

To *discutere* was to **shake a thing to pieces** — to strike it until it came
apart and you could see what was inside. The Romans used it of smashing, and
only later of taking an argument apart.

Which makes the two Spanish senses one sense. Discussing a topic and arguing
with a person are the same act at two temperatures: something is being shaken
until it comes apart. English kept the violent end elsewhere — **concussion**
is *con-* plus the same *quatere*, a violent shaking of the head.

*Percusión* is a third member, a striking right through.
