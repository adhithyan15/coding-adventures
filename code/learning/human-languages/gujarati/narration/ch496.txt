Gujarati, chapter 496: A New Baby.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ધાવવું (dhāvvũ) — to suckle; to feed at the breast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to come into the world, then the Gujarati for to give birth to.

You'll want to know: ધાવવું.
ધાવવું — dhāvvũ — "to suckle; to feed at the breast".
બાળક માતાને ધાવે છે. bāḷak mātāne dhāve che. The baby is feeding at its mother's breast.
Past (it …): તે બાળક ધાવ્યું, te bāḷak dhāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
ધાવવું (dhāvvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit dhāpayatē, "suckles"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to suck at the breast" (CDIAL 6784).

દાદી ગીત ગાઈને બાળકને હિંચકામાં હીંચોળે છે. dādī gīt gāīne bāḷakne hinckāmā̃ hī̃coḷe che. Grandmother sings a song and rocks the baby on the swing.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બાળકને હીંચોળ્યું, teṇe bāḷakne hī̃coḷyũ.

The word, taken apart.
હીંચોળવું (hī̃coḷvũ) is built on the stem that CDIAL lists as Gujarati hĩcvũ, "to swing", under a reconstructed hicc- (CDIAL 14083); હિંચકો (hincko), "a swing", is its cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to suckle, then the Gujarati for to rock a cradle.

You'll want to know: નામકરણ.
નામકરણ — nāmkaraṇ — "a naming ceremony".
નામકરણમાં ફોઈએ બાળકનું નામ નક્કી કર્યું. nāmkaraṇmā̃ phoīe bāḷaknũ nām nakkī karyũ. At the naming ceremony the baby's paternal aunt chose its name.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
નામકરણ (nāmkaraṇ) is a tatsama, Sanskrit nāmakaraṇa, "the giving of a name", as Monier-Williams records it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
બાળોતિયું (bāḷotiyũ) — a baby's nappy; a cloth for a baby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a naming ceremony, then the Gujarati for to welcome with rites.

You'll want to know: બાળોતિયું.
બાળોતિયું — bāḷotiyũ — "a baby's nappy; a cloth for a baby".
માતાએ બાળકનું ભીનું બાળોતિયું બદલ્યું. mātāe bāḷaknũ bhīnũ bāḷotiyũ badalyũ. The mother changed the baby's wet nappy.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
બાળોતિયું (bāḷotiyũ) goes back to a reconstructed bāladhōtta-, "child's cloth", built from Sanskrit bāla-, "child", and dhōtta-, "cloth"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "cloth for a baby" (CDIAL 9219).
