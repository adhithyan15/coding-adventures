Latin, chapter 154: To join, To visit, To sing, To paint, To lead.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iungō, iungere — to join.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to climb, then the Latin for to go down.

You'll want to know: iungō, iungere.
iungō, iungere — "to join".

The word, taken apart.
iungō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European yewg-, "to join, to yoke" (EtymDB 2.1); the n inside the present stem is an old mark of the verb. Greek zeugnymi, "to yoke", and English yoke are cousins (Lewis & Short).

vīsitō, vīsitāre — "to visit".

The word, taken apart.
vīsitō is a frequentative of vīsō, "to go and see" (Lewis & Short), itself built on videō, "to see": to keep going to see.

Lesson 3 of 5.
cantō, cantāre — to sing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to join, then the Latin for to visit.

You'll want to know: cantō, cantāre.
cantō, cantāre — "to sing".

The word, taken apart.
cantō is a frequentative of canō, "to sing" (Lewis & Short), built on its stem cant-, and in everyday Latin it came to replace canō. canō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European keh2n-, "to sing" (EtymDB 2.1).

You'll want to know: dūcō, dūcere.
dūcō, dūcere — "to lead".

The word, taken apart.
dūcō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dewk-, "to lead, to pull" (EtymDB 2.1); Gothic tiuhan, "to lead, to draw", is a cousin (Lewis & Short), and so are English tug and tow.
