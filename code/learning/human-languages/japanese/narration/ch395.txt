Lesson 1 of 6.
こしょう (koshō) — a breakdown; a fault.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to descend, then the Japanese for to cross over.

You'll want to know: こしょう.
こしょう — koshō — "a breakdown; a fault".
くるまが こしょうしました。 — kuruma ga koshō shimashita — "The car broke down."

The word, taken apart.
こしょう (koshō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ko, "circumstances" (Middle Chinese kuH) and shō, "hinder" (tsyangH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ふさがる (fusagaru) — to be blocked; to be closed off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a breakdown, then the Japanese for a garage.

You'll want to know: ふさがる.
ふさがる — fusagaru — "to be blocked; to be closed off".
じこで みちが ふさがっています。 — jiko de michi ga fusagatte imasu — "The road is blocked because of an accident."
Polite ふさがります, fusagarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ゆきで みちが ふさがりました。, yuki de michi ga fusagarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ふさがる (fusagaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and JMdict marks it as usually written in kana (JMdict). It is the intransitive partner of fusagu, "to block" (JMdict).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be blocked, then the Japanese for to loosen.

You'll want to know: いきすぎる.
いきすぎる — ikisugiru — "to go past one's turning; to go too far".
しんごうを いきすぎて、みちに まよいました。 — shingō o ikisugite, michi ni mayoimashita — "I went past the traffic light and got lost."
Polite いきすぎます, ikisugimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ちちが かどを いきすぎました。, chichi ga kado o ikisugimashita.

The word, taken apart.
いきすぎる (ikisugiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from iki, "going" (from iku) and sugiru, "to go beyond"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
