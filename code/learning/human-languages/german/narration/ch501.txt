German, chapter 501: Brettspiele.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Brettspiel — board game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for riddle, then the German for crossword.

You'll want to know: das Brettspiel.
das Brettspiel — "board game".
Am Wochenende spielen wir gern Brettspiele. At the weekend we like playing board games.
Plural: die Brettspiele.

The word, taken apart.
Brettspiel is built on Brett "board, plank", inherited from Middle High German bret, and Spiel "game", from Old High German spil (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Schach — chess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for crossword, then the German for board game.

You'll want to know: das Schach.
das Schach — "chess".
Mein Sohn spielt jeden Dienstag Schach im Verein. My son plays chess at the club every Tuesday.
No plural. Schach! — check! Schachmatt — checkmate.

The word, taken apart.
Schach came through Medieval Latin scaccus from Arabic, which took it from Persian shāh "king" (EtymDB): the game is named after its key piece.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Würfel — dice; cube.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for board game, then the German for chess.

You'll want to know: der Würfel.
der Würfel — "dice; cube".
Wer mit dem Würfel eine Sechs wirft, darf anfangen. Whoever throws a six with the dice gets to start.
Plural: die Würfel. Also a cube: der Eiswürfel — an ice cube. The verb is würfeln — to throw dice.

The word, taken apart.
Würfel is inherited from Middle High German würfel and Old High German wurfil, from Proto-Germanic wurpilaz "die" (EtymDB), built on the verb that is today werfen "to throw".

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Runde — round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for dice, then the German for to cheat.

You'll want to know: die Runde.
die Runde — "round".
Nach drei Runden hatte meine Schwester gewonnen. After three rounds my sister had won.
Plural: die Runden. Eine Runde ausgeben — to buy a round of drinks.

The word, taken apart.
Runde is built on rund "round", which came through Middle High German runt from Old French ront (EtymDB), itself from Latin rotundus.
