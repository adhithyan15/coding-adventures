# focus · mare · somnus — hearth, sea, sleep

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C44-HOUSE-05, LA-LEX-C45-WORLD-05, LA-LEX-C46-NIGHT-05] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Three words, one from the end of each of the last three chapters:
the hearth, the sea, and sleep. Say all three before reading on.

## You'll want to know: each one is a shelf of English
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C44-HOUSE-05, LA-LEX-C45-WORLD-05, LA-LEX-C46-NIGHT-05] -->

| Latin | what it named | what English took |
|---|---|---|
| **focus** | the hearth | *focus* — the point a lens gathers to |
| **mare** | the sea | *marine*, *maritime*, *mariner*, *submarine* |
| **somnus** | sleep | *insomnia*, *somnolent* |

**focus** is the odd one. English did not borrow the fireplace; it borrowed the
idea of **the one point a house is arranged around** and handed it to optics.
Meanwhile spoken Latin took the other road and let *focus* drift from the
fireplace to the fire itself, which is where the Romance words for *fire* come
from.

**mare** and **terra** together make **mediterrāneum**, the sea in the middle of
the earth — two words you hold explaining a name most people never read. And
**somnus** goes back to a root that reached Sanskrit **svapna** and Old English
*sweven*, a word for a dream that Chaucer still used.
