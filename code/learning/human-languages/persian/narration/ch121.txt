Lesson 1 of 5.
گاومیش (gâvmish) — a buffalo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a spring, then the Persian for a salt desert.

You'll want to know: گاومیش.
گاومیش — gâvmish — "a buffalo".

The word, taken apart.
gâvmish is usually explained as gâv, "cow, ox" (inherited, Middle Persian gāw), with mish, "ewe, sheep": the buffalo named from two kinds of cattle.

Lesson 3 of 5.
کرم (kerm) — a worm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a buffalo, then the Persian for a beetle.

You'll want to know: کرم.
کرم — kerm — "a worm".

The word, taken apart.
kerm is a native Persian word, inherited rather than borrowed; it is usually traced to Middle Persian kirm, "worm". EtymDB takes it back to an Indo-European word for "worm", the word behind Sanskrit kṛmi. (It also lists French crème, a different word.)

Lesson 5 of 5.
زرافه (zarâfe) — a giraffe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a worm, then the Persian for an insect.

You'll want to know: زرافه.
زرافه — zarâfe — "a giraffe".

The word, taken apart.
zarâfe is borrowed from Arabic zarāfa, "giraffe" (EtymDB), and English giraffe comes from the same Arabic word. EtymDB traces the Arabic word itself back to a Persian compound, but that is not settled.
