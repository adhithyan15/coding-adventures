# One clause or two

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-JOIN-AUR-01, HI-JOIN-JAB-TAB-01, HI-GRAMMAR-VERB-FINAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 5s] You now have three ways to put two actions in one sentence: *aur*
between two clauses, the **जब … तब** frame, and **-कर** inside a single clause.
Only one of them conjugates just once.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-KAR-01, HI-JOIN-AUR-01, HI-LEX-LOOK, HI-LEX-COME] -->

<!-- hl-activity: {"id":"HI-R73-chain-run","kind":"text","assesses":["HI-GRAMMAR-KAR-01","HI-JOIN-AUR-01"],"prompt":"Say the same two actions twice: joined with aur, then chained with -kar.","answer":"maiṁ kitāb dekhtā hūṁ aur ātā hūṁ; maiṁ kitāb dekhkar ātā hūṁ","accepted":["मैं किताब देखकर आता हूँ"],"feedback":{"correct":"Two endings with aur, one with -kar.","incorrect":"With -kar the first verb keeps no ending at all."},"response_seconds":30} -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: *maiṁ kitāb dekhtā hūṁ aur ātā hūṁ* — two endings]
- [YOU SAY: *maiṁ kitāb dekhkar ātā hūṁ* — one ending]
- [YOU SAY: which of the two a Hindi speaker says more often — **the -कर one**]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-KAR-01, HI-JOIN-AUR-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] How many person endings does a **-कर** sentence carry? (**One**.)
And an *aur* sentence with two verbs? (**Two**.) Then stop.
