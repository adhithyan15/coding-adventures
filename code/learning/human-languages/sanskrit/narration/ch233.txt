Sanskrit, chapter 233: Rains, Sprouts, Waits, Follows, Goes out.

Lesson 1 of 5.
वर्षति (varṣati) — rains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for is lost, then the Sanskrit for gets angry.

You'll want to know: वर्षति.
वर्षति — varṣati — "rains".

The word, taken apart.
varṣati is the present of the root √vṛṣ, "to rain" (MW), a Rigvedic verb (CDIAL 11394). It lives on as Hindi barasnā, "to rain", and its noun varṣa, "rain, year", gives Hindi baras, "year" (CDIAL 11392).

You'll want to know: रोहति.
रोहति — rohati — "sprouts, grows".

The word, taken apart.
rohati is the present of the root √ruh, "to rise, grow, mount" (MW). In the Rigveda it means both "grows" and "climbs" (CDIAL 10862), and Hindi rohanā means "to mount, climb".

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रतीक्षते (pratīkṣate) — waits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for rains, then the Sanskrit for sprouts.

You'll want to know: प्रतीक्षते.
प्रतीक्षते — pratīkṣate — "waits".

The word, taken apart.
pratīkṣate is built on prati, "towards", and the root √īkṣ, "to look" (MW): to wait is to look out for someone. It means "beholds" in the Atharvaveda and "waits for" in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (CDIAL 8620); Marathi paikhṇẽ means "to wait for".

Lesson 4 of 5.
अनुगच्छति (anugacchati) — follows.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sprouts, then the Sanskrit for waits.

You'll want to know: अनुगच्छति.
अनुगच्छति — anugacchati — "follows".

The word, taken apart.
anugacchati is built on anu, "after", and gacchati, "goes", from the root √gam (MW). To follow is to go after; √gam is the root of gacchāmi, "I go", which you have already met.
