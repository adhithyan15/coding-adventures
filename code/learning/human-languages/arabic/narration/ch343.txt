Arabic, chapter 343: Adventures.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مغامرة (mughāmara) — an adventure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a title, then the Arabic for to invent.

You'll want to know: مغامرة.
مغامرة — mughāmara — "an adventure".
كانت الرحلة مغامرة كبيرة. kānat ar-riḥla mughāmara kabīra. The trip was a great adventure.

The word, taken apart.
مغامرة (mughāmara) is built on the native Arabic root gh-m-r (Lane), where ghamr is deep water that covers a man. Mughāmara is the verbal noun of Form III, "to plunge into".

Lesson 2 of 5.
مجهول (majhūl) — unknown; a stranger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to invent, then the Arabic for an adventure.

You'll want to know: مجهول.
مجهول — majhūl — "unknown; a stranger".
وصلت رسالة من شخص مجهول. waṣalat risāla min shakhṣ majhūl. A letter arrived from an unknown person.
In grammar, the passive is المبني للمجهول, built for the unknown doer.

The word, taken apart.
مجهول (majhūl) is usually traced to the native Arabic root j-h-l, "to be ignorant, not to know". Majhūl is the passive participle: "what is not known".

صدفة — ṣudfa — "a coincidence, chance".
التقيت بصديقي القديم صدفة (ṣudfa) في السوق. iltaqaytu bi-ṣadīqī al-qadīm ṣudfatan fī as-sūq. I met my old friend by chance in the market.

The word, taken apart.
صدفة (ṣudfa) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-d-f (Lane), where Form III ṣādafa is "to light upon someone without meaning to".

Lesson 5 of 5.
خاطر (khāṭara) — to take a risk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a surprise, then the Arabic for a coincidence.

You'll want to know: خاطر.
خاطر — khāṭara — "to take a risk".
خاطر الرجل بحياته لينقذ الطفل. khāṭara ar-rajul bi-ḥayātihi li-yunqidha aṭ-ṭifl. The man risked his life to save the child.
Present: يخاطر, yukhāṭir.
It takes بـ for what is risked.

The word, taken apart.
خاطر (khāṭara) is built on the native Arabic root kh-ṭ-r (Lane), the root of khaṭar, danger, and khaṭīr, serious. It is Form III, "to put a thing in danger".
