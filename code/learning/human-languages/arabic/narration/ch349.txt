Arabic, chapter 349: For the first time.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
للمرة الأولى (li-l-marra al-ūlā) — for the first time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to punish, then the Arabic for to inform the police.

You'll want to know: للمرة الأولى.
للمرة الأولى — li-l-marra al-ūlā — "for the first time".
رأيت البحر للمرة الأولى في حياتي. raʾaytu al-baḥr li-l-marra al-ūlā fī ḥayātī. I saw the sea for the first time in my life.
Also لأول مرة.

The word, taken apart.
للمرة الأولى (li-l-marra al-ūlā) is "for the first passing": marra is built on the native Arabic root m-r-r, "to pass" (Lane), and ūlā is the feminine of awwal, first.

أول أمس — awwal ams — "the day before yesterday".
وصل خالي أول أمس. waṣala khālī awwal ams. My uncle arrived the day before yesterday.

أول أمس (awwal ams) is "the first of yesterday": awwal, first, is built on the native Arabic root ʾ-w-l (Lane), before the taught ams, yesterday.

Lesson 4 of 5.
يوما ما (yawman mā) — someday, one day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for by mistake, then the Arabic for the day before yesterday.

You'll want to know: يوما ما.
يوما ما — yawman mā — "someday, one day".
سأزور تلك المدينة يوما ما. sa-azūru tilka al-madīna yawman mā. I will visit that city someday.
Of the future; ذات يوم opens a story in the past.

The word, taken apart.
يوما ما (yawman mā) is "on some day": yawm is usually traced to an inherited Semitic word for day, and mā after it means "some, any".

Lesson 5 of 5.
عقب (ʿaqiba) — right after, following.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for the day before yesterday, then the Arabic for someday.

You'll want to know: عقب.
عقب — ʿaqiba — "right after, following".
نام الطفل عقب العشاء مباشرة. nāma aṭ-ṭifl ʿaqiba al-ʿashāʾ mubāsharatan. The child fell asleep straight after dinner.
A written word; بعد is the everyday one.

The word, taken apart.
عقب (ʿaqiba) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-q-b (Lane), the root of ʿaqib, the heel. What comes ʿaqiba a thing comes at its heels.
