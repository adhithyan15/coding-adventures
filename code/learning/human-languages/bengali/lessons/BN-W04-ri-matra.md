# ৃ — the vowel that used to be an *r*

## Warm-up

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-RI-MATRA-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-HA-01, BN-SCRIPT-U-MATRA-01, BN-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01] -->

> ৃ

A hook hanging under its consonant, in the same country as **ু**. It is a
**vowel sign**, and it says **ri**.

The reason it is filed with the vowels and not with **র** is a piece of history
worth two minutes. Sanskrit had a sound that behaved like a vowel and was made
like an *r*: a syllable you could build a word around with no other vowel in it
at all. English does this without noticing — the second syllable of *butter* has
no vowel letter doing any work, only the *r*. Sanskrit wrote that syllable, and
counted it among its vowels.

Bengali kept the sign and gave up the sound. What a Bengali reader says today is
plain **ri**. So the mark is a vowel that no longer sounds like the thing it is
named after, and its position in the alphabet is a fossil.

> **হ** + **ৃ** → **হৃ**

Look at where it goes and what it does to the letter above it. On **হ** the hook
is tucked into the letter's own tail, which is why this combination looks like a
single new shape rather than a letter plus a mark. That is normal for this sign
and it is the main difficulty it presents: **ৃ** is a mark that hides.
