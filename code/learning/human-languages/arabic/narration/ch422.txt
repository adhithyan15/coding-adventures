Arabic, chapter 422: Being fair.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
موضوعي (mawḍūʿī) — objective.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for intelligence, then the Arabic for diligence.

You'll want to know: موضوعي.
موضوعي — mawḍūʿī — "objective".
كان الحكم موضوعيا. kāna al-ḥakam mawḍūʿiyyan. The referee was objective.

The word, taken apart.
موضوعي (mawḍūʿī) is built on the native Arabic root w-ḍ-ʿ, "to put, to lay down" (Lane). Mawḍūʿī is a modern adjective from mawḍūʿ, a subject: keeping to the thing itself, objective.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what happens if.

Lesson 2 of 7.
تسامح (tasāmuḥ) — tolerance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for diligence, then the Arabic for objective.

You'll want to know: تسامح.
تسامح — tasāmuḥ — "tolerance".
التسامح مهم بين الجيران. at-tasāmuḥ muhimm bayna al-jīrān. Tolerance is important between neighbours.

The word, taken apart.
تسامح (tasāmuḥ) is built on the native Arabic root s-m-ḥ, "to be generous, to grant" (Lane). Tasāmuḥ is the Form VI verbal noun, being generous with one another: tolerance. سامح, to forgive, is from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 7.
مبرر (mubarrir) — a justification; a pretext.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for objective, then the Arabic for tolerance.

You'll want to know: مبرر.
مبرر — mubarrir — "a justification; a pretext".
لا يوجد مبرر لهذا التأخير. lā yūjadu mubarrir li-hādhā at-taʾkhīr. There is no justification for this delay.

The word, taken apart.
مبرر (mubarrir) is built on the native Arabic root b-r-r, "to be true, to be good" (Lane). Mubarrir is the Form II active participle, what shows a thing to be right: a justification, a pretext.

Lesson 5 of 7.
استنكر (istankara) — to condemn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a justification, then the Arabic for absence.

You'll want to know: استنكر.
استنكر — istankara — "to condemn".
استنكر الناس العنف في الشارع. istankara an-nās al-ʿunf fī ash-shāriʿ. People condemned the violence in the street.
Present: يستنكر, yastankir.

The word, taken apart.
استنكر (istankara) is built on the native Arabic root n-k-r, "not to know, to deny" (Lane). It is Form X, to hold a thing to be wrong: to condemn.
