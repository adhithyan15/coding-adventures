German, chapter 257: At the Station — hin und zurück, den Zug verpasst.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
hin und zurück — there and back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the ticket". (Die Fahrkarte.) And "simple, easy". (Einfach.) At a ticket counter, einfach has a second job.

You'll want to know: hin und zurück.
hin und zurück — "there and back", a return: hin oont tsoo-RÜCK.
The counter question is short, and so is the answer:
Einfach oder hin und zurück? — One way or return? Hin und zurück, bitte. — A return, please. Was kostet das? — How much is that?
Einfach at the counter means a single, one way. Hin is "there, away from here", and zurück is "back". The return half of the journey is the word you already have: die Rückfahrt.

Grammar Lens: nach for a city, zum for a place in town.
A ticket goes nach a city, or a country that takes no article:
Eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin, bitte. — A ticket to Berlin, please. Einmal nach Hamburg, hin und zurück. — One return to Hamburg.
Inside the town, you went zum Bahnhof and zur Post. Between towns, you travel nach Berlin.
Then the two questions for the platform:
Wann fährt der Zug ab? — When does the train leave? Von welchem Gleis? — From which platform?
In Switzerland the ticket is often das Billett, and a return is retour: einmal Bern retour. In Austria, too, a return is often hin und retour. Fahrkarte is understood everywhere.

The word, taken apart.
zurück is usually explained as zu, "to", + Rück-, "back": in Middle High German, ze rucke, "to the back". The Rück- is the same as in der Rücken and die Rückfahrt; EtymDB 2.1 lists zurück beside Dutch terug, built the same way, but does not take it apart.
hin is listed by EtymDB beside English hence, "from here"; both point away from where the speaker stands.

Lesson 2 of 3.
ich habe den Zug verpasst — when something goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to miss", a train or a chance. (Verpassen.) Then "I said", in the Perfekt. (Ich habe gesagt.)

You'll want to know: ich habe den Zug verpasst.
Ich habe den Zug verpasst. — "I missed the train": ikh HAH-buh dehn tsook fer-PASST.
Three things in it matter. It is the Perfekt, because a ticket counter is a conversation, and conversation mostly runs in the Perfekt. The participle is verpasst, with no ge-: verbs that begin with ver- do without it. And der Zug becomes den Zug, because the train is the thing missed.

Grammar Lens: say what happened, then ask.
Reporting a problem goes in two moves. First the problem, in the Perfekt or the present:
Ich habe den Zug verpasst. Ich habe meine Fahrkarte verloren. — I've lost my ticket. Mein Koffer ist kaputt. — My suitcase is broken.
Then the question that asks for help:
Wann fährt der nächste Zug? — When is the next train? Was kann ich machen? — What can I do?
Der nächste Zug is the nächste of nächste Woche, next.

The word, taken apart.
der Zug from Middle High German zuc (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB sets it beside Old English tyge, which it traces to a Proto-Germanic word for "a tug, a pull". By the usual account it belongs to the family of ziehen, "to pull": a train is the thing the engine pulls.
verpassen, from chapter one hundred and ninety, is ver- + passen. EtymDB 2.1 traces passen to Dutch, and further back to French passer, "to pass": to let a thing pass you by.

"I missed the train"? (Ich habe den Zug verpasst.) Why no ge- in verpasst? (It starts with ver-.) Why den Zug? (The train is the thing missed.) "When is the next train?" (Wann fährt der nächste Zug?)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — a day at the station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask a stranger the way to the station. (Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?) And give the answer back. (Gehen Sie geradeaus.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, and a trip by train is something you can manage in German. You find the station, you buy eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin, einfach or hin und zurück, and when the day goes wrong you say what happened in the Perfekt and ask what to do.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here it is in one go:
Einmal nach Berlin, hin und zurück, bitte. Was kostet das? Ich habe den Zug verpasst. Wann fährt der nächste Zug?
