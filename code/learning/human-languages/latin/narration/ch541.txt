Latin, chapter 541: At the baths.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
balneātor, balneātōris — a bath-keeper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hand-ball game, then the Latin for swimming.

You'll want to know: balneātor, balneātōris.
balneātor, balneātōris — "a bath-keeper".
Balneātor aquam calefacit. The bath-keeper heats the water.
Masculine, third declension, from balneum: the man who runs a bath-house.

The word, taken apart.
balneātor is built on balneum "bath" with -tor (Lewis & Short); balneum was borrowed from Greek balaneion.

Lesson 2 of 5.
caldārium, caldāriī — the hot room (of the baths).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for swimming, then the Latin for a bath-keeper.

You'll want to know: caldārium, caldāriī.
caldārium, caldāriī — "the hot room (of the baths)".
In caldāriō aqua calida est. In the hot room the water is hot.
Neuter, second declension, from caldus — a short form of calidus, hot.

The word, taken apart.
caldārium is built on caldus, a shortened calidus "hot" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English cauldron comes from the related caldāria "cooking pot".

Lesson 4 of 5.
frīgidārium, frīgidāriī — the cold room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the hot room, then the Latin for the warm room.

You'll want to know: frīgidārium, frīgidāriī.
frīgidārium, frīgidāriī — "the cold room".
Post caldārium in frīgidāriō natāmus. After the hot room we swim in the cold room.
Neuter, second declension, from frīgidus — cold. It held a cold plunge pool.

The word, taken apart.
frīgidārium is built on frīgidus "cold" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB traces frīgus to a Proto-Indo-European word for "cold". English frigid comes from frīgidus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
piscīna, piscīnae — a pool; a fish-pond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the warm room, then the Latin for the cold room.

You'll want to know: piscīna, piscīnae.
piscīna, piscīnae — "a pool; a fish-pond".
In piscīnā thermārum puerī natant. Boys swim in the pool of the baths.
Feminine, first declension, from piscis — fish: first a fish-pond, then any pool for swimming.

The word, taken apart.
piscīna is built on piscis "fish" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): a fish-pond, then a swimming pool. French piscine "swimming pool" comes from it.
