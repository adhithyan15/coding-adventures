Lesson 1 of 1.
di, haz, ve, pon, ten, sal, sé, ven — the eight irregular commands — and every one of them is a single syllable, for a reason.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
By last chapter's rule, "say it" should be dice. It is not. There are exactly eight of these, and once you see them together you will not need to memorise the list.

Grammar Lens: eight, all one syllable.
[a table of 8 rows, read aloud]
verb: decir. command: di, say.
verb: hacer. command: haz, do, make.
verb: ir. command: ve, go.
verb: poner. command: pon, put.
verb: tener. command: ten, have, hold.
verb: salir. command: sal, leave.
verb: ser. command: sé, be.
verb: venir. command: ven, come.
Read the middle column down. Every single one is one syllable, and most are two or three letters. No other set of forms in Spanish is this short.
That is the fact worth carrying, and it has a cause. A command is the sentence you shout, the sentence you say in a hurry, the sentence you interrupt somebody with. Words used that way get worn down harder than words used calmly — and these eight are the commonest verbs in the language, so they had the most use to be worn by.
So the list is not eight arbitrary exceptions. It is what the eight most-shouted verbs look like after two thousand years of being shouted.
