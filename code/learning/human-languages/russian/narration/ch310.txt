Russian, chapter 310: Anniversaries and christenings.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
юбилей (yubiléy) — a jubilee, a big round birthday or anniversary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a widow, then the Russian for an heir.

You'll want to know: юбилей.
юбилей (yubiléy) — "a jubilee, a big round birthday or anniversary".
У дедушки скоро юбилей, семьдесят лет. U dédushki skóro yubiléy, sémdesyat let. Grandpa soon has a big birthday: seventy.
A round number: fifty, sixty, seventy years.

The word, taken apart.
юбилей (yubiléy) was borrowed from German Jubiläum (EtymDB), which comes from Late Latin iūbilaeus "the jubilee year", which is usually traced to a Hebrew word for the ram's horn that announced it.

годовщина (godovshchína) — "an anniversary".
Сегодня годовщина их свадьбы. Sevódnya godovshchína ikh svádby. Today is their wedding anniversary.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
годовщина (godovshchína) is built on год "year", inherited through Old East Slavic godŭ from Proto-Slavic godŭ "the right time" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 6.
новорождённый (novorozhdyónnyy) — a newborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a jubilee, then the Russian for an anniversary.

You'll want to know: новорождённый.
новорождённый (novorozhdyónnyy) — "a newborn".
Новорождённый весил три килограмма. Novorozhdyónnyy vésil tri kilográmma. The newborn weighed three kilograms.
An adjective that serves as a noun. A girl is новорождённая (novorozhdyónnaya).

The word, taken apart.
новорождённый (novorozhdyónnyy) joins новый "new", inherited from Proto-Slavic novŭ, PIE néwos, and рождённый "born", from родить, Proto-Slavic roditi (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 6.
крестины (krestíny) — a christening (party).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an anniversary, then the Russian for a newborn.

You'll want to know: крестины.
крестины (krestíny) — "a christening (party)".
На крестины пригласили всех родственников. Na krestíny priglasíli vsekh ródstvennikov. All the relatives were invited to the christening.
Plural only; genitive крестин.

The word, taken apart.
крестины (krestíny) is built on крест "a cross", from Church Slavonic krĭstŭ, which EtymDB traces ultimately to Greek Khristós "the anointed".

Lesson 5 of 6.
крестить (krestít) — to christen, to baptize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a newborn, then the Russian for a christening.

You'll want to know: крестить.
крестить (krestít) — "to christen, to baptize".
Ребёнка крестили в мае. Rebyónka krestíli v máye. The baby was christened in May.
Both aspects; also perfective окрестить (okrestít). Present крещу, крестишь.

The word, taken apart.
крестить (krestít) is inherited from Proto-Slavic krĭstiti, built on krĭstŭ "baptism" (EtymDB), a word that goes back to Greek Khristós.
