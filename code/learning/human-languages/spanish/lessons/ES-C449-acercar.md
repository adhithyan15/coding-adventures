# acercar — pass it here

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03, ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **cerca** and **acercarse**. Now take the *-se* off the second
one and see what is left.

## You'll want to know: acercar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03] -->

**acercar** — to bring something closer, and so, constantly, **to pass**.

*¿Me acercas el taladro?* — can you pass me the drill? *Acerca la silla* —
pull your chair up. *Te acerco a la estación* — I'll run you to the station.

That last one is worth keeping. Offering somebody a lift in Spanish is offering
to *acercar* them: you are bringing them nearer to where they are going.

## Grammar Lens: one verb, two directions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03] -->

You already own the *-se* version. Here they are side by side:

| form | who moves |
|---|---|
| **acercarse** | the subject — *me acerco a la puerta* |
| **acercar** | something else — *acerco la silla* |

Without the *-se*, the verb needs an object; the thing that comes closer is
whatever you name. With the *-se*, the subject is the thing that comes closer.
That is the same switch you saw in *mudar* and *mudarse*, and it runs through
hundreds of Spanish verbs.

So in a workshop, *acércate* means come over here, and *acércamelo* means send
it over here. One syllable apart, and two different people doing the moving.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05, ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01] -->

**a-cerc-ar**: the prefix *a-* meaning towards, the *cerca* you already say,
and the *-ar* of an ordinary verb. The middle is Latin **circa**, around, and
its ring is still visible in *círculo* and in English *circle* and *circa*.

The road from "around" to "near" is short: things arranged around you are the
things close to you. Spanish turned the circle into a distance, then built a
verb on it, and the verb now does the job of the English *pass*, *pull up*
and *give a lift* all at once.
