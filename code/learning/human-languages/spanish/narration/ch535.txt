Spanish, chapter 535: Tarde o temprano.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
en un principio — at first, in the beginning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for next, then the Spanish for abruptly.

You'll want to know: en un principio.
en un principio — "at first, in the beginning".
En un principio nadie creyó al niño. At first nobody believed the boy.
It often sets up a later change: En un principio..., pero luego...

The word, taken apart.
en un principio is built on principio, which comes from Latin prīncipium 'a beginning' (EtymDB), from prīnceps 'first' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
tarde o temprano — sooner or later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for at first, then the Spanish for in the long run.

You'll want to know: tarde o temprano.
tarde o temprano — "sooner or later".
Tarde o temprano vas a saber la verdad. Sooner or later you are going to find out the truth.
Spanish puts late first: tarde o temprano.

The word, taken apart.
tarde o temprano is built on tarde, from Latin tardē 'slowly, late', and temprano, which is inherited from spoken Latin temporānus, from temporāneus 'timely' (EtymDB), built on tempus 'time'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
finalmente — finally, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for in the long run, then the Spanish for sooner or later.

You'll want to know: finalmente.
finalmente — "finally, in the end".
Finalmente, la policía detuvo al ladrón. Finally, the police arrested the thief.
Common in written stories and in the news.

The word, taken apart.
finalmente is built on final and the ending -mente, from Latin mente 'with a mind'. Final comes from Latin fīnis 'end' (Lewis & Short).
