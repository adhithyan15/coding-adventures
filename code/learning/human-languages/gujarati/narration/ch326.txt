Gujarati, chapter 326: The Flag Flies High.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઝંડો (jhanḍo) — a flag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a campaign, then the Gujarati for a revolt.

You'll want to know: ઝંડો.
ઝંડો — jhanḍo — "a flag".
ચોકમાં એક મોટો ઝંડો હતો. cokmā̃ ek moṭo jhanḍo hato. There was a big flag in the square.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઝંડો (jhanḍo) comes from dhvajadaṇḍa, "a flagstaff", a form CDIAL reconstructs, which lists Gujarati jhuṇḍo, "a flagstaff, a flag" (CDIAL 6898). It is masculine: મોટો ઝંડો.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ફરકાવવું (pharkāvvũ) — to hoist (a flag).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a revolt, then the Gujarati for a flag.

You'll want to know: ફરકાવવું.
ફરકાવવું — pharkāvvũ — "to hoist (a flag)".
આઝાદીના દિવસે ગુરુએ શાળામાં ઝંડો ફરકાવ્યો. ājhādīnā divse gurue śāḷāmā̃ jhanḍo pharkāvyo. On the day of independence the teacher raised the flag at school.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ઝંડો ફરકાવ્યો, teṇe jhanḍo pharkāvyo.

The word, taken apart.
ફરકાવવું (pharkāvvũ) is the causative of pharakvũ, to flutter, which CDIAL lists under pharati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 9050): to make a flag fly. ઝંડો (jhanḍo) ફરકાવ્યો, raised the flag.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a flag, then the Gujarati for to hoist.

You'll want to know: શાંતિ.
શાંતિ — śānti — "peace".
યુદ્ધ પછી ગામમાં ફરી શાંતિ આવી. yuddh pachī gāmmā̃ pharī śānti āvī. After the war, peace came back to the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
શાંતિ (śānti) is a tatsama, Sanskrit śānti, "tranquillity, peace" (CDIAL 12391). It is the noun beside શાંત, calm: શાંતિ આવી, peace came.

Lesson 4 of 5.
સરહદ (sarhad) — a border; a frontier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to hoist, then the Gujarati for peace.

You'll want to know: સરહદ.
સરહદ — sarhad — "a border; a frontier".
સૈનિકો સરહદ પર રહે છે. sainiko sarhad par rahe che. Soldiers are posted on the border.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સરહદ (sarhad) is usually traced to Persian sarḥadd, "a frontier", built from sar, head, and Arabic ḥadd, a limit: the edge of a land. It is feminine: સરહદ પર, on the border.

રાજાએ નદી કિનારે નવું શહેર સ્થાપ્યું. rājāe nadī kināre navũ śaher sthāpyũ. The king founded a new city on the river bank.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે શહેર સ્થાપ્યું, teṇe śaher sthāpyũ.

The word, taken apart.
સ્થાપવું (sthāpvũ) goes back to Sanskrit sthāpáyati, "causes to stand, establishes" (CDIAL 13756): to set something up to last. A city, a school or a temple સ્થાપે છે.
