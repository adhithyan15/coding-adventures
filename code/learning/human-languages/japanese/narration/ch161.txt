Japanese, chapter 161: Washing, Spreading and Drying.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
せんたく (sentaku) — washing clothes; the washing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to make dirty, then the Japanese for dirty.

You'll want to know: せんたく.
せんたく — sentaku — "washing clothes; the washing".
せんたくは あにの しごとです。 — sentaku wa ani no shigoto desu — "The washing is my elder brother's job."

The word, taken apart.
せんたく (sentaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sen, "wash", whose sound matches the rhyme book's senX although it gives "wash" under sejX, and taku, "rinse" (Middle Chinese draewk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ぬれる (nureru) — to get wet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dirty, then the Japanese for washing clothes.

You'll want to know: ぬれる.
ぬれる — nureru — "to get wet".
あめで くつが ぬれます。 — ame de kutsu ga nuremasu — "My shoes get wet in the rain."
Polite ぬれます, nuremasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ぬれる (nureru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
かわかす (kawakasu) — to dry (something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to spread out, then the Japanese for to get dry.

You'll want to know: かわかす.
かわかす — kawakasu — "to dry (something)".
ふとんを かわかします。 — futon o kawakashimasu — "I dry the futon."
Polite かわかします, kawakashimasu; a godan verb. Its intransitive partner is かわく, kawaku.

The word, taken apart.
かわかす (kawakasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as kawaku, "to get dry"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
