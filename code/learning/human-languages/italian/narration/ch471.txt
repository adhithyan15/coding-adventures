Italian, chapter 471: Timidi e socievoli.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la timidezza — shyness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for exactly, then the Italian for almost.

You'll want to know: la timidezza.
la timidezza — "shyness".
Con il tempo ho superato la timidezza. With time I got over my shyness.
Built on timido "shy" with -ezza, the ending that names a quality, as in bellezza.

The word, taken apart.
timidezza is built on timido "shy", a learned borrowing from Latin timidus "fearful" (Lewis & Short), from timēre "to fear"; -ezza turns it into the name of a quality.

Lesson 2 of 5.
socievole — sociable, outgoing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for almost, then the Italian for shyness.

You'll want to know: socievole.
socievole — "sociable, outgoing".
Mia sorella è molto socievole: parla con tutti. My sister is very sociable: she talks to everyone.
The opposite is poco socievole; asociale is much stronger.

The word, taken apart.
socievole comes from Latin sociābilis "that can be joined together" (EtymDB), built on socius "a companion, a partner" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
riservato — reserved, private (of a person); booked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for shyness, then the Italian for sociable.

You'll want to know: riservato.
riservato — "reserved, private (of a person); booked".
Mio figlio è un ragazzo riservato e parla poco di sé. My son is a reserved boy and says little about himself.
Of a table or a seat it means booked: un tavolo riservato.

The word, taken apart.
riservato is the past participle of riservare "to keep back", a learned borrowing from Latin reservāre (Lewis & Short), built on servāre "to keep, to watch over".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il chiacchierone — chatterbox; talkative.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sociable, then the Italian for reserved.

You'll want to know: il chiacchierone.
il chiacchierone — "chatterbox; talkative".
Il mio vicino è un gran chiacchierone. My neighbour is a real chatterbox.
A woman is la chiacchierona. It is built on chiacchierare "to chat".

The word, taken apart.
chiacchierone is built on chiacchierare "to chat" with -one, the ending for someone who does a lot of something (EtymDB). The verb itself is usually taken to imitate the sound of chatter.

Lesson 5 of 5.
arrossire — to blush, to go red.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for reserved, then the Italian for chatterbox.

You'll want to know: arrossire.
arrossire — "to blush, to go red".
Quando parlo in pubblico arrossisco subito. When I speak in public I go red straight away.
An -isc- verb: arrossisco, arrossisci. It takes essere in the past: sono arrossito.

The word, taken apart.
arrossire is built from a- and rosso "red", which is inherited from Latin russus "red" (Lewis & Short).
