Sanskrit, chapter 467: Good and Bad Manners.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शिष्टः (śiṣṭaḥ) — well-bred; cultured.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for childish, then the Sanskrit for understanding.

You'll want to know: शिष्टः.
शिष्टः — śiṣṭaḥ — "well-bred; cultured".
शिष्टः बालः अतिथिं नमति। śiṣṭaḥ bālaḥ atithiṃ namati. The well-bred boy bows to the guest.

The word, taken apart.
शिष्टः (śiṣṭaḥ) is built on the root √śās, "to instruct, to train": one who has been taught; MW gives "disciplined, cultured, educated, wise" (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
उपहासः (upahāsaḥ) — mockery; derision.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for understanding, then the Sanskrit for well-bred.

You'll want to know: उपहासः.
उपहासः — upahāsaḥ — "mockery; derision".
अतिथेः उपहासः न उचितः। atitheḥ upahāsaḥ na ucitaḥ. Mocking a guest is not proper.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
उपहासः (upahāsaḥ) is built from upa, "towards", and the root √has, "to laugh", the root of हसति and उपहसति; MW gives "laughter, derision, mockery, jeer" (MW).

प्रियंवदा मीरा अतिथिना सह मधुरं वदति। priyaṃvadā mīrā atithinā saha madhuraṃ vadati. Affable Mira chats sweetly with the guest.

The word, taken apart.
प्रियंवदः (priyaṃvadaḥ) is built from प्रियः, "dear, pleasant", and vada, "speaking", from the root √vad of वदति: one who says pleasant things; MW gives "speaking kindly, agreeable, affable" (MW).
