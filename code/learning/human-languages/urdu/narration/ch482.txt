Urdu, chapter 482: Washing and Grooming.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نہلانا (nahlānā) — to bathe (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to press gently, then the Urdu for to warm a sore place.

You'll want to know: نہلانا.
نہلانا — nahlānā — "to bathe (someone)".
ماں نے بچے کو نہلایا۔ māṅ ne bachche ko nahlāyā. The mother bathed the baby.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کتے کو نہلایا, us ne kutte ko nahlāyā.

The word, taken apart.
نہلانا (nahlānā) is the causative of نہانا, "to bathe", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit snāti, "bathes" (CDIAL 13786).

Lesson 2 of 5.
مونڈنا (mūṅḍnā) — to shave the head.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Urdu for to warm a sore place, then the Urdu for to bathe (someone).

You'll want to know: مونڈنا.
مونڈنا — mūṅḍnā — "to shave the head".
نائی نے بچے کا سر مونڈا۔ nāī ne bachche kā sar mūṅḍā. The barber shaved the child's head.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے داڑھی مونڈی, us ne dāṛhī mūṅḍī.

The word, taken apart.
مونڈنا (mūṅḍnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin mū̃ḍnā under Sanskrit muṇḍayati, "shaves" (CDIAL 10194).

Lesson 4 of 5.
کٹوانا (kaṭvānā) — to have (something) cut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to shave the head, then the Urdu for a shave.

You'll want to know: کٹوانا.
کٹوانا — kaṭvānā — "to have (something) cut".
میں نے نائی سے بال کٹوائے۔ maiṅ ne nāī se bāl kaṭvāe. I had my hair cut at the barber's.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے ناخن کٹوائے, us ne nākhun kaṭvāe.

The word, taken apart.
کٹوانا (kaṭvānā) is built on کاٹنا, "to cut", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit kártati, "cuts" (CDIAL 2854): the form for having someone else do the cutting.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تراشنا (tarāshnā) — to trim; to pare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a shave, then the Urdu for to have (something) cut.

You'll want to know: تراشنا.
تراشنا — tarāshnā — "to trim; to pare".
دادا نے اپنی داڑھی تراشی۔ dādā ne apnī dāṛhī tarāshī. My grandfather trimmed his beard.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے مونچھ تراشی, us ne mūṅchh tarāshī.

The word, taken apart.
تراشنا (tarāshnā) is usually traced to Persian tarāsh, "cutting", from tarāshīdan, "to shave, to carve", made into an Urdu verb.
