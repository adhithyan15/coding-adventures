Arabic, chapter 487: Floods and shelter.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
غمر (ghamara) — to submerge; to flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for refreshing, then the Arabic for to be exposed.

You'll want to know: غمر.
غمر — ghamara — "to submerge; to flood".
غمر الماء الشارع بعد المطر. ghamara al-māʾ ash-shāriʿ baʿda al-maṭar. Water flooded the street after the rain.
Present: يغمر, yaghmur.

The word, taken apart.
غمر (ghamara) is built on the native Arabic root gh-m-r, "abundant water; to cover" (Lane). It is the plain verb.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 2 of 7.
جرف (jarafa) — to sweep away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be exposed, then the Arabic for to submerge.

You'll want to know: جرف.
جرف — jarafa — "to sweep away".
جرف الماء السيارات في الوادي. jarafa al-māʾ as-sayyārāt fī al-wādī. The water swept the cars away in the valley.
Present: يجرف, yajruf.
The noun jurf is a bank undercut by water, a cliff.

The word, taken apart.
جرف (jarafa) is built on the native Arabic root j-r-f, "to sweep away" (Lane). EtymDB lists Syriac relatives.

The word, taken apart.
ملجأ (maljaʾ) is built on the native Arabic root l-j-ʾ, "to take refuge" (Lane). The pattern mafʿal makes it the place of refuge.
