# (ഷ ദ സ ഭ ഥ ഗ ോ ം ൃ) — the thin nine

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-124, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-120] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **സ**. Then read **ം** and say why it is not a letter at all.

## Script: six letters, and three marks
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-121, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-123, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-124, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-129, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-130, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-132] -->

| piece | sound | where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **ഷ** | *ṣa* | almost only in words Malayalam took from Sanskrit |
| **ദ** | *da* | the voiced dental |
| **സ** | *sa* | the plain sibilant |
| **ഭ** | *bha* | voiced, with breath behind it |
| **ഥ** | *tha* | voiceless, with breath behind it |
| **ഗ** | *ga* | the voiced velar |

Read the right-hand column and a pattern surfaces that the chapters could not
show one at a time: **ഷ**, **ഭ** and **ഥ** are Sanskrit-borrowing letters. A
Malayalam word built from Dravidian roots rarely needs them, which is exactly
why they arrived late and were never asked for again.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-120, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-128] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Three marks, and each attaches differently.

- [YOU READ: **ോ** — a hanger, replacing the built-in vowel with a long *ō*]
- [YOU READ: **ം** — a mark that rides *after* the syllable rather than reshaping it]
- [YOU READ: **ൃ** — a hanger for a vowel that behaves like a consonant, inherited with the Sanskrit words that carry it]
