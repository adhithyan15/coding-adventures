Telugu, chapter 372: How a Story Works.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
కథకుడు (kathakuḍu) — a storyteller; a narrator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a boon, then the Telugu for a curse.

You'll want to know: కథకుడు.
కథకుడు — kathakuḍu — "a storyteller; a narrator".
కథకుడు పిల్లలకు ఒక వింత కథ చెప్పాడు. kathakuḍu pillalaku oka vinta katha ceppāḍu. The storyteller told the children a strange story.
కథ, a story, with -కుడు: one who tells it.

The word, taken apart.
కథకుడు (kathakuḍu) is borrowed from Sanskrit kathaka, "a narrator, one who recites a story" (Monier-Williams), built on kath, "to tell".

Lesson 3 of 5.
పాత్ర (pātra) — a character (in a story); a role.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a storyteller, then the Telugu for a plot.

You'll want to know: పాత్ర.
పాత్ర — pātra — "a character (in a story); a role".
ఆ నాటకంలో నాది చిన్న పాత్ర. ā nāṭakaṁlō nādi cinna pātra. I have a small part in that play.
Plural: పాత్రలు, pātralu.
Also a vessel or a pot: the Sanskrit word meant a vessel first.

The word, taken apart.
పాత్ర (pātra) is borrowed from Sanskrit pātra, "a drinking vessel, a dish" (CDIAL 8055); in Telugu it is also a part in a play.

కల్పన — kalpana — "imagination; fiction".
ఆ కథ పూర్తిగా కల్పన. ā katha pūrtigā kalpana. That story is pure imagination.

The word, taken apart.
కల్పన (kalpana) is borrowed from Sanskrit kalpanā, "making, preparing" (Monier-Williams); in Telugu it is what the mind makes up.

Lesson 5 of 5.
కట్టుకథ (kaṭṭukatha) — a made-up story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a character, then the Telugu for imagination.

You'll want to know: కట్టుకథ.
కట్టుకథ — kaṭṭukatha — "a made-up story".
అతను చెప్పింది అంతా కట్టుకథ. atanu ceppindi antā kaṭṭukatha. All he said was a made-up story.
కట్టు, to tie or put together, and కథ: a story stitched together.

The word, taken apart.
కట్టుకథ (kaṭṭukatha) is a Telugu compound: native kaṭṭu, "to tie, build" (DEDR 1147), and katha, "a story", from Sanskrit.
