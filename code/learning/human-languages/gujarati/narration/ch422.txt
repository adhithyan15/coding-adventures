Gujarati, chapter 422: Hawkers and Stalls.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
લારી (lārī) — a handcart (a street seller's).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to growl, then the Gujarati for to chase away.

You'll want to know: લારી.
લારી — lārī — "a handcart (a street seller's)".
શાકની લારી રોજ અમારી શેરીમાં આવે છે. śāknī lārī roj amārī śerīmā̃ āve che. The vegetable cart comes into our street every day.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
લારી (lārī) No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ફેરિયો (pheriyo) — a hawker; a street seller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to chase away, then the Gujarati for a handcart.

You'll want to know: ફેરિયો.
ફેરિયો — pheriyo — "a hawker; a street seller".
ફેરિયો ઘરે ઘરે જઈને રમકડાં વેચે છે. pheriyo ghare ghare jaīne ramakḍā̃ vece che. The hawker goes from house to house selling toys.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ફેરિયો (pheriyo) is built on ફેરો, a round, which goes back to phēra, "a turn", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 9078): the seller who goes on rounds.

Lesson 4 of 5.
કંદોઈ (kandoī) — a sweet-maker; a sweet seller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a hawker, then the Gujarati for a paan stall.

You'll want to know: કંદોઈ.
કંદોઈ — kandoī — "a sweet-maker; a sweet seller".
દિવાળીમાં કંદોઈની દુકાને ભીડ છે. divāḷīmā̃ kandoīnī dukāne bhīḍ che. At Diwali there is a crowd at the sweet-maker's shop.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કંદોઈ (kandoī) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit kāndavika, "a baker", built on kandu, an iron pan; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "a confectioner" (CDIAL 3034).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ખપવું (khapvũ) — to sell well; to be used up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a paan stall, then the Gujarati for a sweet-maker.

You'll want to know: ખપવું.
ખપવું — khapvũ — "to sell well; to be used up".
ગરમીમાં લીંબુ ઘણાં ખપે છે. garmīmā̃ lī̃bu ghaṇā̃ khape che. In the hot weather lemons sell well.
Past (it …): તે માલ ખપી ગયો, te māl khapī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
ખપવું (khapvũ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit kṣapyatē, "is wasted, is destroyed"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to be consumed" (CDIAL 3655): goods that are used up have sold.
