German, chapter 431: Nicht zufrieden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Beschwerde — the complaint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to bite, then the German for to crawl.

You'll want to know: die Beschwerde.
die Beschwerde — "the complaint".
Der Gast schreibt eine Beschwerde an das Hotel. The guest writes a complaint to the hotel.
Plural: die Beschwerden. Beschwerden also means aches, symptoms: Magenbeschwerden.

The word, taken apart.
Beschwerde is built on beschweren 'to weigh down, to burden', from schwer 'heavy', inherited from Old High German swār (EtymDB): a complaint is a weight put on someone.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
beschweren — to complain (mostly reflexive: sich beschweren).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to crawl, then the German for the complaint.

You'll want to know: beschweren.
beschweren — "to complain (mostly reflexive: sich beschweren)".
Sie beschwert sich über den Lärm im Flur. She complains about the noise in the corridor.
Reflexive, with über: ich beschwere mich über das Essen. Without sich it means to weigh down.

The word, taken apart.
beschweren is built from be- and schwer 'heavy', inherited from Old High German swār (EtymDB); the reflexive sense 'to complain' grew from 'to feel weighed down'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
reklamieren — to take back (faulty goods), to make a complaint about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the complaint, then the German for to complain.

You'll want to know: reklamieren.
reklamieren — "to take back (faulty goods), to make a complaint about".
Er reklamiert die neue Kamera, weil sie nicht funktioniert. He takes the new camera back because it does not work.
Past participle without ge-: ich habe reklamiert. Die Reklamation — the complaint about goods.

The word, taken apart.
reklamieren was borrowed from French réclamer (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin reclāmāre 'to cry out against, to protest', from clāmāre 'to shout' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
meckern — to grumble, to moan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to complain, then the German for to take back.

You'll want to know: meckern.
meckern — "to grumble, to moan".
Mein Onkel meckert oft über das Wetter. My uncle often moans about the weather.
Everyday speech. First the sound a goat makes.

The word, taken apart.
meckern first described the bleating of a goat and is usually explained as a sound word. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
unzufrieden — dissatisfied, unhappy (with something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to take back, then the German for to grumble.

You'll want to know: unzufrieden.
unzufrieden — "dissatisfied, unhappy (with something)".
Viele Gäste sind mit dem Frühstück unzufrieden. Many guests are unhappy with the breakfast.
With mit: unzufrieden mit dem Service.

The word, taken apart.
unzufrieden is built from un- 'not' and zufrieden 'content' (EtymDB), which comes from zu Frieden 'at peace'.
