# करूँगा — the third tense, and the one with no helper word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-KARNA-01, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say what you do and what you did. Say the stem of *to do*,
and hold it — the next tense attaches to it rather than standing beside it.

## You'll want to know: करूँगा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**करूँगा** (*karūṅgā*) — "I will do," said by a man.
**करूँगी** (*karūṅgī*) — the same, said by a woman.

The stem is **कर-**. Everything after it is the tense.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01, HI-CONCEPT-C04-MILENGE-01] -->

Three pieces, and each one has a job:

| piece | what it carries |
|---|---|
| कर- | the action |
| -ऊँ- | who is acting — *I* |
| -गा / -गी | the future itself, agreeing with the speaker |

English needs a second word for this: *I **will** do*. Hindi has none. The
tense is welded onto the verb, the way the person is welded into *hūṁ* and
*hai*.

You have met the **-गा** family once before, in the farewell *milenge*, where the
plural **-एंगे** was named as the future ending. That was one cell of a grid.
This chapter is the grid.

## Grammar Lens: the piece that comes from the old subjunctive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01, HI-CONCEPT-C05-KARNA-01] -->

**-गा** is not a random syllable. It comes from Sanskrit **गत** (*gata*),
"gone" — the participle of *to go*. A future in Hindi is built out of a word
meaning **gone**, which is the same instinct behind English *I am going to do
it*.

| speaker | form |
|---|---|
| a man | करूँगा |
| a woman | करूँगी |

Only the last letter moves, and it moves the way every **-आ / -ई** pair in this
language moves.
