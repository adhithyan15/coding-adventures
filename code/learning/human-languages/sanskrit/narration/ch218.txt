Lesson 1 of 5.
सम्मार्जनी (sammārjanī) — a broom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for equal, then the Sanskrit for uneven.

You'll want to know: सम्मार्जनी.
सम्मार्जनी — sammārjanī — "a broom".

The word, taken apart.
sammārjanī is built on sam-, "together, thoroughly", and the root √mṛj, "to wipe, rub clean". MW lists sammārjana, "sweeping or cleansing thoroughly", and also "a wisp or broom". MW compares √mṛj with Latin mulgēre and English milk.

Lesson 2 of 5.
कुठारः (kuṭhāraḥ) — an axe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for uneven, then the Sanskrit for a broom.

You'll want to know: कुठारः.
कुठारः — kuṭhāraḥ — "an axe".

The word, taken apart.
kuṭhāra is probably borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 3244, following Mayrhofer, connects it with kuṭṭ-, "to break, cut" (CDIAL 3236). It lives on as Hindi kulhāṛā and kulhāṛī, "axe".

Lesson 3 of 5.
खनित्रम् (khanitram) — a spade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a broom, then the Sanskrit for an axe.

You'll want to know: खनित्रम्.
खनित्रम् — khanitram — "a spade".

The word, taken apart.
khanitra is built on the root √khan, "to dig", with the instrument suffix -tra (MW), and is already in the Rigveda (CDIAL 3814). It is the tool of khanati, "digs", which you have already met.

Lesson 4 of 5.
हलम् (halam) — a plough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an axe, then the Sanskrit for a spade.

You'll want to know: हलम्.
हलम् — halam — "a plough".

The word, taken apart.
hala is the plough, and MW gives a root √hal, "to plough", while noting the root may have been made from the noun. Its deeper origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi hal, "plough" (CDIAL 14000).

Lesson 5 of 5.
दात्रम् (dātram) — a sickle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a spade, then the Sanskrit for a plough.

You'll want to know: दात्रम्.
दात्रम् — dātram — "a sickle".

The word, taken apart.
dātra is built on the root √do, "to cut", with the instrument suffix -tra (CDIAL 6260): "the cutter", a sickle. It is a different word from dātra, "gift", from √dā, "to give". Hindi dā, "sickle, chopper", comes from it.
