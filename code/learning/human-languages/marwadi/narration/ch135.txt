You'll want to know: काकड़ी.
काकड़ी — kākṛī — "a cucumber".

The word, taken apart.
काकड़ी (kākṛī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from karkaṭī, "Cucumis utilissimus". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 2817); the match is through Hindi kakṛī.

Lesson 4 of 5.
बैंगण (baiṅgaṇ) — an aubergine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a cucumber, then the Marwadi for okra.

You'll want to know: बैंगण.
बैंगण — baiṅgaṇ — "an aubergine".

The word, taken apart.
बैंगण (baiṅgaṇ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vātiṅgaṇa, "the egg-plant". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 11503); the match is through Hindi baigan. CDIAL thinks it shares an ultimate source with other names for the plant, possibly Dravidian; Persian bādingān was taken from Indo-Aryan.
