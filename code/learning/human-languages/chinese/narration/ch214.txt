Mandarin Chinese, chapter 214: Asking for Help.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wèntí — question; problem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for admission ticket, then the Mandarin for have a safe journey.

You'll want to know: wèntí.
wèntí — "question; problem".
wǒ yǒu yí ge wèntí — I've got a question.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wèntí is built from 问 'to ask' and tí 'a heading', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjunH and dej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'the forehead' and 'the title of a book'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying no and asking.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: wèntí]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wèntí, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ yǒu yí ge wèntí — I've got a question.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for admission ticket, then the Mandarin for have a safe journey, then say wèntí again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does wèntí mean? ("Question; problem".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
明白 (míngbai) — to grasp a meaning; to understand; clear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for problem, then the Mandarin for to tell someone.

You'll want to know: 明白.
明白 — míngbai — "to grasp a meaning; to understand; clear".
我明白你很关心我 wǒ míngbai nǐ hěn guānxīn wǒ — I know how much you care about me.

The word, taken apart.
明白 (míngbai) is built from 明 'bright' and 白 'white', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjaeng and baek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): bright and clear. In the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zěnme bàn — what should I do?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to grasp a meaning, then the Mandarin for a way to deal with it.

You'll want to know: zěnme bàn.
zěnme bàn — "what should I do?".
wǒ de hùzhào diū le zěnme bàn — I've lost my passport. What should I do?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zěnme bàn is built from zěnme 'how' and bàn 'to handle'. The last is inherited from Middle Chinese beanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of zěn, so the history of zěnme is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for saying no and asking.
