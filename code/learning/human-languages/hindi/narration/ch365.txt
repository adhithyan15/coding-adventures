Hindi, chapter 365: Theory and Practice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सिद्धांत (siddhānt) — a principle, a theory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an essay, then the Hindi for to pick out.

You'll want to know: सिद्धांत.
सिद्धांत — siddhānt — "a principle, a theory".
यह सिद्धांत किताब में अच्छा लगता है। yah siddhānt kitāb meṁ acchā lagtā hai. This theory looks good in a book.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सिद्धांत (siddhānt) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit siddhānta, "an established conclusion" (Monier-Williams).

तुम्हारा तर्क ज़्यादा देर नहीं टिकेगा। tumhārā tark zyādā der nahīṃ ṭikegā. Your argument won't hold up for long.
Past (it …): वह तर्क नहीं टिका, vah tark nahīṃ ṭikā.

The word, taken apart.
टिकना (ṭiknā) is inherited from ṭakk, "stop", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5420).

Lesson 5 of 5.
मनवाना (manvānā) — to get (someone) to accept.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an exception, then the Hindi for to hold up.

You'll want to know: मनवाना.
मनवाना — manvānā — "to get (someone) to accept".
अरुण ने अपनी बात मीरा से मनवाई। Aruṇ ne apnī bāt Mīrā se manvāī. Arun got Mira to accept his point.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने अपनी बात मनवाई, usne apnī bāt manvāī.

The word, taken apart.
मनवाना (manvānā) is the causative of मानना, "to accept", which is inherited from Sanskrit manyate, "thinks" (CDIAL 9857): to make someone accept.
