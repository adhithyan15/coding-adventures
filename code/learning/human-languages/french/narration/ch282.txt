French, chapter 282: Les gros titres.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le gros titre — the headline.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the dispatch, then the French for the news agency.

You'll want to know: le gros titre.
le gros titre — "the headline".
Le gros titre du jour parle du prix du pain. Today's headline is about the price of bread.
Faire les gros titres — to make the headlines. À la une — on the front page.

The word, taken apart.
titre is inherited from Latin titulus "inscription, label" (EtymDB), and gros from Late Latin grossus "thick" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la manchette — the banner headline; a shirt cuff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the news agency, then the French for the headline.

You'll want to know: la manchette.
la manchette — "the banner headline; a shirt cuff".
La manchette annonçait la victoire de l'équipe. The banner headline announced the team's victory.
On a shirt, les manchettes are the cuffs.

The word, taken apart.
manchette is a diminutive of manche "sleeve" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin manica "a long sleeve", built on manus "hand" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'éditorial — the editorial, the leading article.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the banner headline, then the French for the section.

You'll want to know: l'éditorial.
l'éditorial — "the editorial, the leading article".
Le directeur du journal écrit l'éditorial. The editor of the paper writes the editorial.
Plural les éditoriaux. Its writer is l'éditorialiste.

The word, taken apart.
éditorial is usually traced to English editorial (EtymDB lists no parent), built on editor, which comes from Latin ēditor "one who brings out", from ēdere "to give out" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
les faits divers — short news items, minor news stories.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the section, then the French for the editorial.

You'll want to know: les faits divers.
les faits divers — "short news items, minor news stories".
Il aime lire les faits divers. He likes reading the short news items.
Small stories: thefts, odd events, accidents. Singular: un fait divers.

The word, taken apart.
fait is inherited from Latin factum "a deed" (Lewis & Short), and divers is a learned borrowing from Latin dīversus "turned different ways" (EtymDB).
