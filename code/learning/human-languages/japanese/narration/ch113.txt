You'll want to know: はんこ.
はんこ — hanko — "a personal seal".

The word, taken apart.
はんこ (hanko) is usually explained as Sino-Japanese hankō, "printing; sealing", which JMdict lists, worn down. JMdict marks the spelling with "judge" and "child" as ateji. The account is not settled.

You'll want to know: だんご.
だんご — dango — "a dumpling".

The word, taken apart.
だんご (dango) is Sino-Japanese dan, "a round mass" (Middle Chinese dwan), borrowed from Chinese, with -go, a noun ending written with the character for child (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
だし (dashi) — soup stock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a personal seal, then the Japanese for a dumpling.

You'll want to know: だし.
だし — dashi — "soup stock".

The word, taken apart.
だし (dashi) is native: the noun made from dasu, "to put out, to draw out", by its stem: what is drawn out of the fish and kelp (JMdict). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
かつお (katsuo) — a bonito.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a dumpling, then the Japanese for soup stock.

You'll want to know: かつお.
かつお — katsuo — "a bonito".

The word, taken apart.
かつお (katsuo) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
わかめ (wakame) — wakame seaweed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for soup stock, then the Japanese for a bonito.

You'll want to know: わかめ.
わかめ — wakame — "wakame seaweed".

The word, taken apart.
わかめ (wakame) is native, usually explained as waka, "young", and me, an old word for edible seaweed. JMdict writes it with characters for "young" and "cloth", read for their meaning. It is inherited rather than borrowed.
