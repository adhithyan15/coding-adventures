Japanese, chapter 186: Savings, Fees and Tax.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
あまる (amaru) — to be in surplus; to be left over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to cost, then the Japanese for to be enough.

You'll want to know: あまる.
あまる — amaru — "to be in surplus; to be left over".
おかねが すこし あまります。 — okane ga sukoshi amarimasu — "A little money is left over."
Polite あまります, amarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
あまる (amaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be enough, then the Japanese for to be in surplus.

You'll want to know: ちょきん.
ちょきん — chokin — "savings; money put aside".
ぎんこうに ちょきんが あります。 — ginkō ni chokin ga arimasu — "I have savings in the bank."

The word, taken apart.
ちょきん (chokin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are cho, "store up" (Middle Chinese trjoX) and kin, "gold, money" (kim) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
