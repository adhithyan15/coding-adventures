Mandarin Chinese, chapter 183: In and Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
出去 (chūqù) — to go outside; to go out of here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for home cooking, then the Mandarin for a boxed meal.

You'll want to know: 出去.
出去 — chūqù — "to go outside; to go out of here".
外边很冷你还要出去吗 wàibian hěn lěng nǐ hái yào chūqù ma — It's cold out: are you still going out?

The word, taken apart.
出去 (chūqù) is built from 出 'to come out' and 去 'to go', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and khjoH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): out and away from here.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
xiàbān — to finish work; to come off a shift.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go out of here, then the Mandarin for to come inside.

You'll want to know: xiàbān.
xiàbān — "to finish work; to come off a shift".
nǐ jǐ diǎn xiàbān — What time do you finish work?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàbān is built from 下 'to go down' and bān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as dividing tokens of jade; the sense 'a shift at work' is not given there.
