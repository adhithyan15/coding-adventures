regalar — to give (as a present).

You'll want to know: regalar.
regalar — "to give (as a present)".
Le regalo un libro a mi madre. I am giving my mother a book.
The verb behind el regalo.

The word, taken apart.
regalar is probably from French régaler, "to treat to a feast"; where the French word itself came from is disputed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
envolver — to wrap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for success, then the Spanish for to give.

You'll want to know: envolver.
envolver — "to wrap".
¿Me lo envuelve para regalo? Could you gift-wrap it for me?
The o breaks into ue under stress: envuelvo.

The word, taken apart.
envolver from Latin involvere, "to roll in, to wrap up" (EtymDB 2.1) — inherited: in- became en-, and the stressed o opens into ue in envuelvo.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: el juguete.
el juguete — "the toy".
El niño tiene muchos juguetes. The child has a lot of toys.

The word, taken apart.
juguete is built on juego, "a game", with the small-ending -ete. Juego is Latin iocus, "a joke, a jest".

felicitar — "to congratulate, to wish a happy birthday".
Te llamo para felicitarte. I am calling to wish you a happy birthday.
¡Felicidades! — congratulations, happy birthday.

The word, taken apart.
felicitar from Late Latin felicitare, "to make happy" (EtymDB 2.1), from felix, "happy" — the felix inside feliz.

Lesson 5 of 5.
abrazar — to hug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the toy, then the Spanish for to congratulate.

You'll want to know: abrazar.
abrazar — "to hug".
Abraza a su abuela. He hugs his grandmother.
Un abrazo — a hug, and a common way to sign off a message.

The word, taken apart.
abrazar is a-, brazo, "arm", and -ar (EtymDB 2.1): to take into your arms. Brazo is Latin bracchium, "an arm", taken from Greek.
