Japanese, chapter 310: Waiting for an Answer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ずっと (zutto) — all along; the whole time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a person, then the Japanese for to confide.

You'll want to know: ずっと.
ずっと — zutto — "all along; the whole time".
ずっと へんじを まっています。 — zutto henji o matte imasu — "I have been waiting for an answer all this time."

The word, taken apart.
ずっと (zutto) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
じつは (jitsu wa) — actually; to tell the truth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to confide, then the Japanese for all along.

You'll want to know: じつは.
じつは — jitsu wa — "actually; to tell the truth".
じつは、きのう びょういんに いきました。 — jitsu wa, kinō byōin ni ikimashita — "To tell the truth, I went to the hospital yesterday."

The word, taken apart.
じつは (jitsu wa) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: jitsu, "truth", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese zyit), and wa, "the topic particle", is native Japanese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
とうとう (tōtō) — in the end; at long last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for all along, then the Japanese for actually.

You'll want to know: とうとう.
とうとう — tōtō — "in the end; at long last".
とうとう へんじは きませんでした。 — tōtō henji wa kimasen deshita — "In the end no answer came."

The word, taken apart.
とうとう (tōtō) is usually written in kana (JMdict). JMdict also gives a spelling it marks as rare: two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese, tō, "arrival" (Middle Chinese tawH) and tō, "head" (duw) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). Whether the word is Sino-Japanese, built from them, or a native adverb later given that spelling is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
かなう (kanau) — to come true.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for actually, then the Japanese for in the end.

You'll want to know: かなう.
かなう — kanau — "to come true".
いつか ゆめが かなうと おもいます。 — itsuka yume ga kanau to omoimasu — "I think my dream will come true one day."
Polite かないます, kanaimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ゆめが かないました。, yume ga kanaimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かなう (kanau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: まもなく.
まもなく — mamonaku — "shortly; in a moment".

The word, taken apart.
まもなく (mamonaku) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ma, "an interval", mo, "even", and naku, "without" (from nai) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
