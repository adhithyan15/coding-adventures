Spanish, chapter 694: Animales del campo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la gaviota — the seagull.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the vegetation, then the Spanish for the oak tree.

You'll want to know: la gaviota.
la gaviota — "the seagull".
Las gaviotas vuelan sobre el puerto. The seagulls fly over the harbour.
Common in ports and on beaches.

The word, taken apart.
gaviota is built on Latin gāvia "a sea bird, perhaps the seamew" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

el ave — "the large bird, the bird (formal); poultry".
En el lago hay muchas aves. There are a lot of birds on the lake.
Feminine, but el ave because of the stressed a; plural las aves.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el insecto — the insect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the seagull, then the Spanish for the large bird.

You'll want to know: el insecto.
el insecto — "the insect".
Por la noche hay muchos insectos. There are a lot of insects at night.
Una picadura de insecto — an insect bite.

The word, taken apart.
insecto is a learned borrowing from Latin insectum "cut into" (EtymDB), from in and secāre "to cut": an insect body looks cut into parts.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la mariposa — the butterfly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the large bird, then the Spanish for the insect.

You'll want to know: la mariposa.
la mariposa — "the butterfly".
En primavera hay muchas mariposas. There are a lot of butterflies in spring.
A moth is la polilla.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of mariposa is not settled; it is often explained as Mari, posa "Mary, settle down", from a children’s song.
