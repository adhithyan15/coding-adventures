# también — a word with your very first word inside it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PERO, ES-LEX-BIEN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Pero* sets two things against each other. Now the opposite move:
adding one thing **to** another.

[PAUSE 1s] And say *bien*, from your first chapter. You will need it in a
moment, in a place you would not expect.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `accent-mark` — the accent on **también** puts the stress on the last
  syllable: *tam-BYEN*.
- `b-soft` — the *b* here is gentle, the lips barely closing.

## The word, taken apart: también
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-TAMBIEN, ES-LEX-TAN, ES-ETYMON-TAMBIEN-TAN-BIEN]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BIEN] -->

**también** = **tan** + **bien**.

[PAUSE 2s] ***Tan*** means *so*, *as* — it is the word Spanish uses to say how
much of something there is. And ***bien*** you have had since chapter one.

[PAUSE 2s] So *también* is, literally, **"so well"**. Somebody says a thing;
you say *and that goes so well for me too*. Say it enough times and it stops
being a comment about how well anything is going, and becomes the plain word
for *also*.

> *Estudio español. Trabajo **también**.*

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TAMBIEN] -->

The most useful shape it takes is the shortest one:

> — *Estoy cansado.*
> — ***Yo también.***

[PAUSE 2s] Two words, and you have agreed with somebody. That is worth more in a
real conversation than most of the grammar in this book, and it costs nothing to
remember, because you already knew half of it.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice the accent, too. It is there because *también* is stressed on
its last syllable, which is where the *bien* sits — the borrowed half kept the
stress it had when it was a word of its own.
