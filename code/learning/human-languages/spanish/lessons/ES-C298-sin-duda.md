## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C298-ANSWER-04]; assesses=[] -->

**sin duda** — "no doubt," "certainly."

*Duda* comes from Latin **dubitare**, to waver, and *dubitare* is built on
**duo**, two. To doubt is to have two answers in front of you and no way to pick.
That is a much better picture of the state than the English word gives you.

The same *duo* is all over English. **Dubious**, obviously. **Double**, less
obviously. And **doubt** itself, which came through French as *doute* with no
*b* in it at all — the *b* was inserted centuries later by scholars who wanted
the Latin visible. English still writes it and still does not say it, which is
exactly what Spanish does with the *h* of *horno*.

So *sin duda* is "without the two": there is only one answer available, and this
is it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Four answers, and the dial is near its top. *Sin duda* is stronger than *en
efecto*, because *en efecto* agrees with what was said and *sin duda* says there
was never anything else to agree with.
