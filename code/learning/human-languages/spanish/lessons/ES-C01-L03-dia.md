# día — "day," and how Spanish makes plurals

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You have *hola* and *bien*. Now your first noun — *día*, "day" —
and riding along with it, the single rule that turns any Spanish word into
its plural.

## You'll want to know first

- [ES-C01-L02 (bien)](./ES-C01-L02-bien.md) — you'll pair *día* with *bueno*
  from that lesson very soon.

## Sounds you'll need

- `accent-i` — the written accent sits on the **í**, pulling the stress
  there: *día* is *DEE-a*, two beats. → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)
- `d-soft` — the **d** between vowels is soft, toward the *th* in *this*.

## The word, taken apart

**día** — "day." Root: Latin **dies** ("day"). The family fans across
English, mostly through French:

- **di**ary, **di**urnal ("of the day").
- **jour**nal, **jour**ney — through French *jour*, "a day" (a *journey* was
  once "a day's travel").
- quoti**di**an ("every-day"), meri**di**an ("mid-day").
- even *dismal* — from Latin *dies mali*, "evil days."

## Grammar Lens: making things plural

Here is the whole plural rule for now, in one line: **if a word ends in a
vowel, add *-s*.**

- *día* → **días** ("days").
- (If it ends in a consonant, you add *-es* instead — that's for later.)

That's it. You just learned how to pluralize most Spanish nouns, on your
third word.

> One flag for next lesson: *día* is **masculine** — *el día* — even though
> it ends in *-a*, which usually marks a feminine word. It's a famous
> exception (inherited from Latin, where *dies* was masculine). The full
> gender rules come later; you only need to know *día* is masculine so that
> the *next* lesson makes sense.

## Guided Practice

- [YOU SAY: "día" — *DEE-a*]
- [YOU SAY: "días" — now plural]
- [YOU SAY: "un día" (one day), "dos días" (two days)]

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] How do you make a vowel-ending Spanish noun plural? (Add *-s*.) So
*día* becomes…? (*días*.) And is *día* masculine or feminine? (Masculine —
the exception.)
