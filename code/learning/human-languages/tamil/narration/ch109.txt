Tamil, chapter 109: Letters From Your First Words.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
இ (i) — the letter that opens இல்லை.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: இ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read உ and ஓ aloud, and write each once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say இல்லை (illai), "no". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: இ.
இ — i.
It is the letter at the front of இல்லை (illai), "no", one of the first words in this book. You have been writing its sign, ◌ி, since நன்றி. This is the independent shape of the same vowel: the one a word uses when it begins with the sound.

Writing: இ.
1. start here: curl around the inner loop.
2. without lifting, sweep down the inner right curve.
3. without lifting, carry left and turn through the lower loop.
4. without lifting, climb the lower diagonal.
5. without lifting, carry right and turn around the lower loop, then lift.
6. put the pen down again and climb the outer left side.
7. without lifting, arch over the top and down the right — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frame 4, இ (Univ. of Texas at Austin), p. 192.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at இல்லை and point at இ inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: இ three times, saying i as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ஓ, then இ (i), from memory]
[your turn — read: இல்லை aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which character is this — இ? (i.) Which word did it come from? (இல்லை, illai, "no".)


Lesson 2 of 3.
◌ை (ai) — the sign that ends இல்லை.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ◌ை and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read ஓ and இ aloud, and write each once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say இல்லை (illai), "no". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ◌ை.
◌ை — ai.
It is a vowel sign, not a letter. It attaches to a consonant and replaces the built-in a with ai. Look at the end of இல்லை: the sign sits on the left of ல, although you say it after. Tamil writes this sign first and sounds it second.

Writing: ◌ை.
Put your pen on ◌ை (ai) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start the character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Script you'll notice: ஸ.
ஸ — sa.
It is inside நமஸ்காரம் (namaskāram), the borrowed bow-word you met beside வணக்கம். ஸ (sa) is a Grantha letter: Tamil added it to write words that came from Sanskrit, so you will see it mostly in borrowed words.

Writing: ஸ.
Put your pen on ஸ (sa) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
