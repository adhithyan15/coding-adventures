French, chapter 287: Le cambriolage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cambrioler — to burgle, to break into.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the drop, then the French for the statistic.

You'll want to know: cambrioler.
cambrioler — "to burgle, to break into".
Quelqu'un a cambriolé la maison pendant les vacances. Someone burgled the house during the holidays.
Said of a place, not a person: cambrioler un appartement.

The word, taken apart.
cambrioler is built on the old slang word cambriole "a small room", usually traced to Provençal cambro "room", which goes back to Latin camera "a vault" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the verb without a parent.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'effraction — the forced entry, breaking in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the burglar, then the French for the burglary.

You'll want to know: l'effraction.
l'effraction — "the forced entry, breaking in".
La police n'a trouvé aucune trace d'effraction. The police found no sign of a forced entry.
Entrer par effraction — to break in.

The word, taken apart.
effraction is a learned word built on Latin effractus, the past participle of effringere "to break open", from ex and frangere "to break" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le butin — the loot, the haul.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the burglary, then the French for the forced entry.

You'll want to know: le butin.
le butin — "the loot, the haul".
Le butin était caché dans une valise. The loot was hidden in a suitcase.
Also the haul of a happy day: le butin des soldes.

The word, taken apart.
butin comes from Old French butin "booty" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Middle Low German būte "exchange, a share of booty".
