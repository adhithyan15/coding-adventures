Japanese, chapter 520: Worries and Surprises.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きがかり (kigakari) — a worry; something on one's mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to betray, then the Japanese for safe (as a choice).

You'll want to know: きがかり.
きがかり — kigakari — "a worry; something on one's mind".
もし あめが ふったら、ははは こどもの ことが きがかりでしょう。 — moshi ame ga futtara, haha wa kodomo no koto ga kigakari deshō — "If it rains, my mother will be worried about the children."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
きがかり (kigakari) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese ki, "spirit" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), borrowed from Chinese, and native gakari, "hanging", the voiced form of kakari, from kakaru, "to hang" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
こころぼそい (kokorobosoi) — helpless; lonely and uneasy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for safe (as a choice), then the Japanese for a worry.

You'll want to know: こころぼそい.
こころぼそい — kokorobosoi — "helpless; lonely and uneasy".
もし ひとりで いったら、いもうとは こころぼそいでしょう。 — moshi hitori de ittara, imōto wa kokorobosoi deshō — "If my younger sister went on her own, she would feel lonely and uneasy."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
こころぼそい (kokorobosoi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from kokoro, "heart" and bosoi, the voiced form of hosoi, "thin" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
おもいがけない (omoigakenai) — unexpected.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a worry, then the Japanese for helpless.

You'll want to know: おもいがけない.
おもいがけない — omoigakenai — "unexpected".
もし おもいがけない ことが あったら、しらせて ください。 — moshi omoigakenai koto ga attara, shirasete kudasai — "If something unexpected happens, let me know."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
おもいがけない (omoigakenai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
よそうがい (yosōgai) — beyond expectation; unforeseen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for helpless, then the Japanese for unexpected.

You'll want to know: よそうがい.
よそうがい — yosōgai — "beyond expectation; unforeseen".
もし よそうがいの あめが ふったら、しあいは ちゅうしでしょう。 — moshi yosōgai no ame ga futtara, shiai wa chūshi deshō — "If unexpected rain falls, the match will probably be stopped."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
よそうがい (yosōgai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yo, "beforehand" (Middle Chinese yoH), sō, "concept" (sjangX) and gai, "outside" (ngwajH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
