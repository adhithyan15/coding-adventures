## You'll want to know: xiāoxi
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C157-XIAOXI-01]; assesses=[] -->

*xiāoxi* — "news; a message".

> *wǒ yǒu yí ge hǎo xiāoxi* — I've got some good news.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*xiāoxi* is built from xiāo and xi, the xi of *xiūxi*. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *sjew* and *sik* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to melt away; to wane' and 'to stop; to breed'. Neither gloss gives 'news', so how the pair came to mean it is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C50-SLEEP50-03] -->

*xiāoxi* is any news, and the message that brings it: a text from a friend is a *xiāoxi*.

The xi at the end is light, as in *xiūxi*.
