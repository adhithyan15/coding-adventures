## You'll want to know: jiàoxǐng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C158-WAKE158-01]; assesses=[] -->

*jiàoxǐng* — "to wake someone up".

> *māma měitiān qī diǎn jiàoxǐng wǒ* — Mum wakes me at seven every day.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jiàoxǐng* is built from 叫 'to call' and *xǐng* 'to wake', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kewH* and *sengX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as sobering up after wine: to call someone awake.
