# এটা (eṭā) — this one

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C21-DEM-ORDER-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-TOMARNAAMKI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **this shirt**, with both ends. Then say the question from
chapter two that asks somebody their name.

## You'll want to know: এটা
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C21-ETA-01]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C20-CLASSIFIER-01, BN-LEX-C10-JOL-01] -->

> **এটা কী?** — *eṭā ki?* — **what is this?**

> **এটা জল** — *eṭā jôl* — **this is water.**

Take the noun out of **এই জামাটা** and keep the two ends. What is left —
**এ** and **টা** — is **এটা**, and it means *this thing*, standing on its own
as the subject of a sentence.

Chapter two taught **কী** as the question word inside *what is your name*, and
then gave you nothing else to ask it about. **এটা** is the missing half. Between
them they make the first question a learner of any language actually uses, and
it has been out of reach for thirty-one chapters.

The far one works the same way: **ওটা** — *oṭā* — **that one**.

## Grammar Lens: no verb in the middle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C21-ETA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01, BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01] -->

Notice what is **not** in **এটা জল**. There is no *is*. Bengali joins a subject
to what it is by putting the two side by side and stopping, exactly as chapter
twenty-seven showed when it taught you to deny such a sentence with **নয়**:

- **এটা জল** — this is water
- **এটা জল নয়** — this is not water

So the sentence and its denial both arrive at once, because the denier was
already taught and had nothing to deny.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C21-ETA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C20-CLASSIFIER-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C16-NOY-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What two pieces is **এটা** made of? (**এ-**, the near head, and the
classifier.) Is there a word for **is** in **এটা জল**? (**No** — the two halves
sit side by side.) And which word denies it? (**নয়**.)
