Arabic, chapter 360: Agreeing and objecting.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
وافق (wāfaqa) — to approve; to agree to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to suggest, then the Arabic for convincing.

You'll want to know: وافق.
وافق — wāfaqa — "to approve; to agree to".
وافق المدير على الخطة الجديدة. wāfaqa al-mudīr ʿalā al-khiṭṭa al-jadīda. The manager approved the new plan.
Present: يوافق, yuwāfiq.
It takes على: وافق (wāfaqa) على …. اتفق is two sides agreeing with each other.

The word, taken apart.
وافق (wāfaqa) is built on the native Arabic root w-f-q, "to fit, to suit" (Lane). It is Form III, to be in accord with: to agree to a thing.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons and opinions.

إجماع (ijmāʿ) — consensus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to approve, then the Arabic for to object.

You'll want to know: إجماع.
إجماع — ijmāʿ — "consensus".
وصل الفريق إلى إجماع بعد نقاش طويل. waṣala al-farīq ilā ijmāʿ baʿda niqāsh ṭawīl. The team reached a consensus after a long discussion.
بالإجماع, bi-l-ijmāʿ, unanimously.

The word, taken apart.
إجماع (ijmāʿ) is built on the native Arabic root j-m-ʿ, "to gather, to bring together" (Lane). Ijmāʿ is the Form IV verbal noun, a bringing together: everyone gathered on one view.

Lesson 4 of 5.
جادل (jādala) — to argue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to object, then the Arabic for consensus.

You'll want to know: جادل.
جادل — jādala — "to argue".
جادل الولد أمه حول وقت النوم. jādala al-walad ummahu ḥawla waqt an-nawm. The boy argued with his mother about bedtime.
Present: يجادل, yujādil.
You argue with someone directly, with no preposition: جادله, argued with him.

The word, taken apart.
جادل (jādala) is built on the native Arabic root j-d-l, "to twist firmly" (Lane). It is Form III, to twist against someone: to argue with him.

The word, taken apart.
جدل (jadal) is built on the native Arabic root j-d-l, "to twist firmly" (Lane). Jadal is a verbal noun of the same root: arguing, and so a controversy.
