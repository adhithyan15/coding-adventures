# comisteis · tuvisteis — the last gap closes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-PRETERITE-AR-COMPLETE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Hablasteis*. Now the other two families, and then the hard stems.

## Grammar Lens: -isteis, for everything else
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-2PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-PLURAL] -->

| | |
|---|---|
| *comer* | ***comisteis*** |
| *vivir* | ***vivisteis*** |

[PAUSE 2s] One ending for both, exactly as everywhere else in the preterite.
The *-er* and *-ir* families stopped being two families the first time you met
this tense.

## Grammar Lens: and the strong stems take it too
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-2PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-PLURAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The stems that gave you trouble — *tuv-*, *hic-*, *estuv-*, *dij-* —
take the same *-isteis*, with no surprises left in them:

*tuvisteis · hicisteis · estuvisteis · dijisteis*

[PAUSE 2s] And *ser*/*ir*, which share one past: ***fuisteis***.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRETERITE-COMPLETE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SER-IR-PRETERITE-PLURAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] That was the last hole. **The preterite is finished** — every family,
every person, regular and strong alike, with nothing outstanding.

[PAUSE 2s] It has taken a long stretch of this book to build, and it is the
largest single structure in it. The review that named this debt out loud can now
be read without a caveat.
