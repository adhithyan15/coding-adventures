Lesson 1 of 5.
هواء (hawāʾ) — air.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a twin, then the Arabic for a bridegroom.

You'll want to know: هواء.
هواء — hawāʾ — "air".

The word, taken apart.
هواء (hawāʾ) is built on the native Arabic root h-w-y, "to fall; air" (Lane). Hawāʾ is the air.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ورد (ward) — roses.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for air, then the Arabic for a wave.

You'll want to know: ورد.
ورد — ward — "roses".

The word, taken apart.
ورد (ward) is listed by Lane under w-r-d, with a source calling it arabicized. It is usually given an Iranian origin, probably by way of Aramaic wardā, "rose"; EtymDB sets it beside wardā.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نخلة (nakhla) — a palm tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a wave, then the Arabic for roses.

You'll want to know: نخلة.
نخلة — nakhla — "a palm tree".

The word, taken apart.
نخلة (nakhla) is one palm tree of nakhl, "palm trees", built on the native Arabic root n-kh-l. Lane's short article on this root gives nakhala, "to sift", and nakhlī, "a cultivator of palm-trees".
