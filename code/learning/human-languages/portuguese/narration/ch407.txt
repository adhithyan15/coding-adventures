Portuguese, chapter 407: Debaixo do capô.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o capô — the bonnet (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the tow truck, then the Portuguese for the repair garage.

You'll want to know: o capô.
o capô — "the bonnet (of a car)".
O mecânico abriu o capô para ver o motor. The mechanic opened the bonnet to check the engine.
In Brazil also o capô; in the US, the hood.

The word, taken apart.
capô was borrowed from French capot 'bonnet, cover' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
a bagageira — the boot (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the repair garage, then the Portuguese for the bonnet.

You'll want to know: a bagageira.
a bagageira — "the boot (of a car)".
As malas grandes não cabem na bagageira. The big suitcases do not fit in the boot.
In Brazil: o porta-malas. In Portugal people also say a mala do carro.

The word, taken apart.
bagageira is built on bagagem 'luggage' with -eira, a place for things; bagagem came from French bagage (EtymDB lists the French word).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o pisca — the indicator, the turn signal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bonnet, then the Portuguese for the boot.

You'll want to know: o pisca.
o pisca — "the indicator, the turn signal".
Ele nunca liga o pisca antes de virar. He never puts the indicator on before turning.
Short for o pisca-pisca. Piscar o olho — to wink.

The word, taken apart.
pisca is built on piscar 'to blink'. The origin of piscar is not settled; EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o parafuso — the screw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the boot, then the Portuguese for the indicator.

You'll want to know: o parafuso.
o parafuso — "the screw".
Falta um parafuso na porta do carro. There is a screw missing from the car door.
Ter um parafuso a menos — to have a screw loose.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of parafuso is not settled; it is sometimes linked with fuso 'spindle', from Latin fūsus (Lewis & Short), but EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
desaparafusar — to unscrew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the indicator, then the Portuguese for the screw.

You'll want to know: desaparafusar.
desaparafusar — "to unscrew".
O mecânico desaparafusou a roda com cuidado. The mechanic carefully unscrewed the wheel.
The opposite is aparafusar, to screw in.

The word, taken apart.
desaparafusar is built from des- 'un-' and aparafusar 'to screw in', on parafuso 'screw', whose own origin is not settled (EtymDB lists aparafusar without a source).
