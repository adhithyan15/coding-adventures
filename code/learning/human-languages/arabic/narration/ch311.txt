Arabic, chapter 311: A birth.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ولادة (wilāda) — childbirth; a birth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to call out to, then the Arabic for a rescuer.

You'll want to know: ولادة.
ولادة — wilāda — "childbirth; a birth".
كانت الولادة في الليل. kānat al-wilāda fī al-layl. The birth was at night.
Birth certificate: شهادة ولادة (wilāda); many countries say شهادة ميلاد.

The word, taken apart.
ولادة (wilāda) is built on the native Arabic root w-l-d (Lane), "to give birth", the root of the taught walad, a child, and mīlād, a birthday.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مولود (mawlūd) — a newborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a rescuer, then the Arabic for childbirth.

You'll want to know: مولود.
مولود — mawlūd — "a newborn".
نام المولود بعد أن شرب الحليب. nāma al-mawlūd baʿda an shariba al-ḥalīb. The newborn fell asleep after drinking his milk.
Plural: مواليد, mawālīd.
The plural مواليد also names everyone born in a given year.

The word, taken apart.
مولود (mawlūd) is built on the native Arabic root w-l-d (Lane), "to give birth". Mawlūd is its passive participle, "one who is born".

Lesson 3 of 5.
قابلة (qābila) — a midwife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for childbirth, then the Arabic for a newborn.

You'll want to know: قابلة.
قابلة — qābila — "a midwife".
ساعدت القابلة الأم في الولادة. sāʿadat al-qābila al-umm fī al-wilāda. The midwife helped the mother with the birth.
Not the taught verb قابل, to meet: قابلة (qābila) is a noun here.

The word, taken apart.
قابلة (qābila) is built on the native Arabic root q-b-l (Lane), "to face; to receive". Qābila is the active participle, "she who receives": the midwife is usually explained as the woman who receives the child at its birth.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أنجب (anjaba) — to have a child, to bear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a newborn, then the Arabic for a midwife.

You'll want to know: أنجب.
أنجب — anjaba — "to have a child, to bear".
أنجبت جارتنا بنتا جميلة. anjabat jāratunā bintan jamīla. Our neighbour had a beautiful baby girl.
Present: ينجب, yunjib.
Of either parent: أنجب (anjaba) الرجل ثلاثة أولاد.

The word, taken apart.
أنجب (anjaba) is built on the native Arabic root n-j-b (Lane), the root of najīb, noble, excellent. Form IV anjaba was first "to beget a noble child", then to have a child.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تسمية (tasmiya) — a naming; a name given.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a midwife, then the Arabic for to have a child.

You'll want to know: تسمية.
تسمية — tasmiya — "a naming; a name given".
اختار الجد تسمية (tasmiya) المولود. ikhtāra al-jadd tasmiyat al-mawlūd. The grandfather chose the baby’s name.
The verb is سمى, to name.

The word, taken apart.
تسمية (tasmiya) is built on the native Arabic root s-m-w (Lane), "to be high", the root of the taught ism, a name. Tasmiya is the verbal noun of Form II sammā, "to give a name".
