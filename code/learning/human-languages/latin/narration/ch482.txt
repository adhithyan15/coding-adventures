Latin, chapter 482: River, Torrent, Confluence, To flow, To swim across.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
amnis, amnis — a river, a broad stream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rough, then the Latin for to slide down.

You'll want to know: amnis, amnis.
amnis, amnis — "a river, a broad stream".
Amnis magnus et altus per campōs it. A big, deep river runs through the plains.
Third declension, masculine: amnis, amnis m. A big, deep river, a word more of poetry and history than flūmen.

The word, taken apart.
amnis is inherited from Old Latin abnis, from PIE h₂ep- 'water' (EtymDB). Lewis & Short also derive it from the Sanskrit word for water.

Lesson 2 of 5.
torrēns, torrentis — a torrent, a rushing stream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to slide down, then the Latin for a river.

You'll want to know: torrēns, torrentis.
torrēns, torrentis — "a torrent, a rushing stream".
Post pluviam torrēns ē monte currit. After the rain a torrent rushes down from the mountain.
Third declension, masculine: torrēns, torrentis m. The present participle of torreō 'to burn', used as a noun: a stream that is fierce. English torrent comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
torrēns is the present participle of torreō 'to parch, to burn' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), used as a noun for a raging, boiling stream. English torrent comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
cōnfluēns, cōnfluentis — a confluence, the place where two rivers meet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a river, then the Latin for a torrent.

You'll want to know: cōnfluēns, cōnfluentis.
cōnfluēns, cōnfluentis — "a confluence, the place where two rivers meet".
Ad cōnfluentem duōrum flūminum oppidum est. At the confluence of two rivers there is a town.
Third declension, masculine: cōnfluēns, cōnfluentis m., also plural cōnfluentēs. Many towns stand at such a place. English confluence comes from the same verb.

The word, taken apart.
cōnfluēns is the present participle of cōnfluō 'to flow together', from con- and fluō 'to flow' (Lewis & Short). English confluence comes from the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fluō, fluere — to flow, to run (of water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a torrent, then the Latin for a confluence.

You'll want to know: fluō, fluere.
fluō, fluere — "to flow, to run (of water)".
Rīvus per prātum lentē fluit. A stream flows slowly through the meadow.
fluō, fluere, flūxī, flūxum. English fluid, fluent and flux come from it.

The word, taken apart.
fluō is inherited from PIE bʰlew- 'to swell, to flow' (EtymDB). English fluid, fluent, flux and influence come from it (Lewis & Short).

Mīlitēs flūmen trānsnatant, quod pōns nūllus est. The soldiers swim across the river, because there is no bridge.
trānsnatō, trānsnatāre, trānsnatāvī, also trānatō, with an accusative.

The word, taken apart.
