German, chapter 517: Im Theater.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Schauspieler — actor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to perform, then the German for appearance.

You'll want to know: der Schauspieler.
der Schauspieler — "actor".
Der Schauspieler hat die Rolle sehr gut gespielt. The actor played the role very well.
Plural: die Schauspieler. A woman is die Schauspielerin.

The word, taken apart.
Schauspieler is built on Schauspiel "play", from schauen "to look", inherited from Old High German scouwōn, and Spiel "game, performance" (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Plural: die Regisseure. Said the French way: re-shi-SÖR.

The word, taken apart.
Regisseur was borrowed from French régisseur "manager" (EtymDB), built on régir "to manage", from Latin regere "to rule".

Lesson 3 of 5.
das Publikum — audience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for actor, then the German for director.

You'll want to know: das Publikum.
das Publikum — "audience".
Das Publikum lachte über jeden Witz. The audience laughed at every joke.
No plural. Singular in German: Das Publikum war begeistert.

The word, taken apart.
Publikum comes from Medieval Latin publicum "the public", from Latin pūblicus "of the people" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
klatschen — to applaud; to gossip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for director, then the German for audience.

You'll want to know: klatschen.
klatschen — "to applaud; to gossip".
Am Ende klatschten alle begeistert. At the end everyone applauded enthusiastically.
Beifall klatschen — to applaud. Informal: über die Nachbarn klatschen — to gossip about the neighbours.

The word, taken apart.
klatschen is usually explained as an imitative word for a slapping sound; EtymDB lists it, glossed "to smack, to slap", without a source.
