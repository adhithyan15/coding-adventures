Latin, chapter 375: Things That Happen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
contingō, contingere — to touch; to happen (to), to fall to one's lot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to hinder, then the Latin for to throw into confusion.

You'll want to know: contingō, contingere.
contingō, contingere — "to touch; to happen (to), to fall to one's lot".
Mihi contigit ut eum vidērem. I had the good luck to see him.
contingō, contingere, contigī, contactum. Built on tangō, to touch. Contingit + dative, often of good luck. English contact and contingent.

The word, taken apart.
contingō is built from con- and tangō "to touch" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the vowel weakened inside the compound: what touches you is what happens to you. English contact and contingent come from it.

Ex parvīs initiīs magnum bellum exstitit. From small beginnings a great war arose.
exsistō, exsistere, exstitī. Ex + sistō, to set, to stand: to stand out, to appear. English exist.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mōtus, mōtūs — a movement; a stirring (of the mind); a disturbance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for by chance, then the Latin for unexpected.

You'll want to know: mōtus, mōtūs.
mōtus, mōtūs — "a movement; a stirring (of the mind); a disturbance".
Magnus mōtus in urbe fuit. There was a great commotion in the city.
mōtus, mōtūs m., from moveō. Mōtus animī, a stirring of the mind; mōtus terrae, an earthquake. English motion and motor belong to the family.

The word, taken apart.
mōtus is the noun built on moveō "to move" (Lewis & Short), from its participle mōtus. English motion and motor belong to the same family.
