# മഴ പെയ്താൽ (maḻa peytāl) — if it rains

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C75-AAL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C94-THREE-DAYS-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C347-ENKIL-VERB-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if he comes", then "tomorrow I will go". (*vannāl*; *nāḷe ñān pōkuṁ*.) Then "if you
are coming". (*nī varunnuṇṭeṅkil*.)

## Grammar Lens: -ആൽ, then what will happen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C347-REAL-CONDITION-01]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C75-AAL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C94-THREE-DAYS-01, ML-LEX-C183-KRIYA183-04, ML-LEX-C139-TIME-01, ML-LEX-C127-CARE-05, ML-LEX-C171-ACT171-02, ML-LEX-C63-FEEL-02] -->

A real condition is something that may well happen. Put **-ആൽ** on the past form, as in **വന്നാൽ**,
and say what follows in the future, or with **-ആം**, "I will":

> **മഴ പെയ്താൽ ഞാൻ വരില്ല.**
>
> *maḻa peytāl ñān varilla.* — "If it rains, I will not come."

> **നാളെ സമയം കിട്ടിയാൽ ഞാൻ വിളിക്കാം.**
>
> *nāḷe samayaṁ kiṭṭiyāl ñān viḷikkāṁ.* — "If I get time tomorrow, I will call."

It also states what is true whenever the first thing happens:

> **നേരത്തെ ഉറങ്ങിയാൽ രാവിലെ ക്ഷീണം തോന്നില്ല.**
>
> *nēratte uṟaṅṅiyāl rāvile kṣīṇaṁ tōnnilla.* — "If you sleep early, you do not feel tired in the morning."

Roughly, **-ആൽ** waits for an event, and **-എങ്കിൽ** checks a fact: *vannāl*, "if he comes";
*varunnuṇṭeṅkil*, "if it is so that he is coming".

## Grammar Lens: if it does not happen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C347-NEGATIVE-CONDITION-01]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C347-ILLENKIL-01, ML-LEX-C172-ACT172-04] -->

For "if … not", put **-ഇല്ലെങ്കിൽ** on the past form:

> **നീ വന്നില്ലെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ വിളിക്കും.**
>
> *nī vannilleṅkil ñān viḷikkuṁ.* — "If you do not come, I will call."
