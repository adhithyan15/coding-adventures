## You'll want to know: turba, turbae
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C460-TURBA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**turba, turbae** — "a crowd, a throng; an uproar".

> In viā turba magna est, et raeda lentē it.
>
> *There is a big crowd in the street, and the carriage goes slowly.*

First declension, feminine: *turba, turbae f.* First disorder and noise, then the crowd that makes it. English *disturb* and *turbulent* belong to the same family.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*turba* is usually linked with Greek *tyrbē* 'turmoil, disorder' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); whether it was borrowed or is a cousin is not settled. English *disturb* and *turbulent* come from words built on it.
