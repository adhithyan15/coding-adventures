Arabic, chapter 219: Hose, Wood, Glass, Copper, Silk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خرطوم (kharṭūm) — a hose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a piece, then the Arabic for weight.

You'll want to know: خرطوم.
خرطوم — kharṭūm — "a hose".

The word, taken apart.
خرطوم (kharṭūm) is built on the native Arabic root kh-r-ṭ-m, "the fore part of the nose; a trunk" (Lane). Lane's kharṭūm is the fore part of the nose, and an elephant's trunk; the hose is a modern sense.

You'll want to know: نحاس.
نحاس — nuḥās — "copper".

The word, taken apart.
نحاس (nuḥās), "copper", is an old noun that Lane lists under the native Arabic root n-ḥ-s, calling it a chaste Arabic word. Hebrew neḥoshet, "copper, bronze", is its cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for glass, then the Arabic for copper.

You'll want to know: حرير.
حرير — ḥarīr — "silk".

The word, taken apart.
حرير (ḥarīr), "silk", is an old native noun, filed under the root ḥ-r-r (Lane), the root of ḥarr, "heat", and ḥurr, "free"; Lane does not tie silk to either sense.
