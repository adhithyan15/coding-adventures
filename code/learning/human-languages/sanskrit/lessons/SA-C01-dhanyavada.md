# धन्यवादः (dhanyavādaḥ) — "thank you"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The full, un-worn Sanskrit shape of a "thank you" you already met to
the east.

## The letters in this word

New: **ध** dha (breathy *d*), **व** va, **द** da, and the conjunct **न्य** (**न**
+ **य** ya). With the visarga again, read **ध·न्य·वा·दः**, *dhanyavādaḥ*.

## The word, taken apart

**धन्यवादः** = *dhanya* ("worthy, deserving," from *dhana* "wealth") + *vāda* ("a
speaking, a statement," from the root √vad "to speak"). Literally "a saying of
'you are worthy.'" This is the **source** of the thanks you met to the east —
Hindi *dhanyavād*, Marathi and Punjabi likewise, Bengali *dhônyobad* — here in
its full Sanskrit shape, visarga and all.

## Why it's said this way

Classical Sanskrit thanks are more often shown in deed and honorific than said
with a set formula; *dhanyavāda* is the plain, dignified "thank you," and its
survival across every modern northern language shows how durable a good word can
be. Note you already knew all its pieces — only the ending (*-aḥ*) is new.
