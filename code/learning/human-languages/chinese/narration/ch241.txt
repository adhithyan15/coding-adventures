Mandarin Chinese, chapter 241: In Hospital.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhùyuàn — to be in hospital.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to throw up, then the Mandarin for to have an upset stomach.

You'll want to know: zhùyuàn.
zhùyuàn — "to be in hospital".
shàng ge yuè wǒ bàba zhùyuàn le — Last month my dad went into hospital.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhùyuàn is built from zhù 'to stay' and yuàn 'a walled courtyard', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjuH and hjwenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stop' and the second 'a wall round a courtyard'.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: zhùyuàn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhùyuàn, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: shàng ge yuè wǒ bàba zhùyuàn le — Last month my dad went into hospital.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to throw up, then the Mandarin for to have an upset stomach, then say zhùyuàn again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does zhùyuàn mean? ("To be in hospital".) Say it once more.

病房 — bìngfáng — "hospital ward".
医生现在在病房里 yīshēng xiànzài zài bìngfáng lǐ — The doctor's in the ward now.

The word, taken apart.
病房 (bìngfáng) is built from 病 'illness' and 房 'room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaengH and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a chamber'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
huīfù — to recover; to get back to normal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a surgical operation, then the Mandarin for to leave hospital.

You'll want to know: huīfù.
huīfù — "to recover; to get back to normal".
tā de shēntǐ huīfù de hěn kuài — He's recovering very quickly.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huīfù is built from huī 'vast' and fù 'to return', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khwoj and bjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'great' and the second 'to return'. The h of huī is not the regular outcome of that form.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.
