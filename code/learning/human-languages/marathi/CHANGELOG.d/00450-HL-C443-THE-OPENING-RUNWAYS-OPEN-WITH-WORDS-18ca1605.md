## HL-C443: the opening runways open with words

Chapters 2 and 5-8 used to teach letters before any word the reader could say.
Each of those chapters now opens with a few short words. Every letter in the
chapter is a piece of one of those words, so writing the letter means taking
apart a word the reader already knows.

| chapter | words, read before the letters | letters they anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **दुःख** (*duḥkh*, sorrow), **आभाळ** (*ābhāḷ*, the sky), **संत** (*sant*, a saint), **बेत** (*bet*, a plan) | ः आ भ ं त े, and ब ळ in chapter 3 |
| 5 | **अमृत** (*amṛt*, nectar), **किरण** (*kiraṇ*, a ray of light), **तूप** (*tūp*, ghee) | अ ृ ि ू, and ण प in chapter 7 |
| 6 | **घागर** (*ghāgar*, a water pot), **चव** (*cav*, taste), **छत्री** (*chatrī*, an umbrella), **जहाज** (*jahāj*, a ship), **झोका** (*jhokā*, a swing) | ग घ च छ ज झ (ख and ु come from दुःख) |
| 7 | **टाळी** (*ṭāḷī*, a clap), **ठाम** (*ṭhām*, firm), **डबा** (*ḍabā*, a lunch box) | ट ठ ड |
| 8 | **उलट** (*ulaṭ*, the other way round), **शेष** (*śeṣ*, what is left over), **ऊब** (*ūb*, warmth), **एकदा** (*ekdā*, once) | ल उ श ष ऊ ए |

Each word is taught romanized. Its other letters are exposure only and are not
charged to script closure. Each word is revisited by the two letter lessons it
anchors, or by the one it anchors and the lesson after it.

The chapters stay within the twelve-atom chapter budget: 2 goes to 11, 5 to 9,
6 to 12, 7 to 8 and 8 to 10. To make room, the sequence numbers of chapters
2-3 and 6-8 move up. Reading order within each chapter is otherwise unchanged.

Six cold letters remain:

- **Chapter 1's five.** That chapter is already at twelve atoms. Its letters
  build **हो** in the lesson itself.
- **ँ.** No everyday Marathi word writes it. The track shows it in Hindi
  comparisons only.
