# cūr? — quia, quod, nam: explaining

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C52-ASK-02, LA-LEX-C52-ASK-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask "why?". (*Cūr?*) Answer with "because". (*Quia.*)

[PAUSE 1s] You also own **explicō**, "to explain", and **causa**, "a
reason".

## Grammar Lens: the answer to cūr
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C284-CAUSA-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C113-WHY113-01, LA-LEX-C113-WHY113-05, LA-LEX-C197-ACT197-04, LA-LEX-C68-AEGER-01] -->

> **Cūr herī nōn vēnistī?** — Why didn't you come yesterday?
> **Nōn vēnī, quia aeger eram.** — I didn't come, because I was ill.

[PAUSE 2s] Both pasts: the not-coming is an event, *nōn vēnī*; the illness
is the scene behind it, *aeger eram*. After *quia* the verb is an ordinary
one, in whatever tense the facts need.

[PAUSE 2s] **nam**, "for", hangs the reason on afterwards, as a second
sentence: *Nōn vēnī. Nam aeger eram.* And *explicō* names what you are
doing: **Causam explicō**, I am explaining the reason.

## You'll want to know: quod, because
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C284-QUOD-CAUSA-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-03] -->

The four lines you wrote with joiners asked you for *quia*, because
**quod** has two jobs. Now you meet its second one as a reader:

> **Gaudeō quod vēnistī.** — I am glad that you came, glad because you came.

[PAUSE 2s] After a verb of feeling, *gaudeō*, *doleō*, *quod* gives the
cause. Where a noun stands before it and *quod* points back to it, it is
usually "which"; where no noun waits for it, it is usually "because" or
"that". Writing, *quia* is
still the safe choice; reading, expect both.

## The word, taken apart: explicō
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C197-ACT197-04] -->

**explicō** is *ex*, "out", with *plicō*, "to fold" (EtymDB 2.1), as its own
lesson said: to explain is to unfold. A Roman book was a *volūmen*, a roll,
and Cicero writes *volūmen explicāre* for opening one (Lewis & Short). An
explanation opens out what was rolled up inside the question.
