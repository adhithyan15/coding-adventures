# пять — five, and a cousin English kept whole

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-CHETYRE-01, RU-ETYMON-CHETYRE-KWETWORES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **четыре** and what three branches did with its first sound.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-PYAT-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **пять** — *pyat* — **five**

One syllable, ending in the soft sign that this book has been putting on the
ends of words since its first chapters. It softens the **т** and is not a sound
of its own.

## The word, taken apart — pentagon
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-PYAT-PENKWE]; assesses=[RU-LEX-PYAT-01] -->

**пять** continues Indo-European \**pénkʷe*, "five." Greek **pénte**, Latin
**quīnque** and English **five** are the same word.

The Greek form is the one an English speaker already owns: **pentagon**,
**pentathlon**, **Pentecost** — all of them carry *five* in a shape much closer
to **пять** than English **five** is. The number was in the reader's vocabulary
before the lesson was.
