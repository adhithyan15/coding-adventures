Spanish, chapter 240: Review --- Two Endings and One Little A.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
(review) — two endings and one little a — the smallest new machinery in this book, and one of the most useful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two endings, three families. (-ando for -ar; -iendo for everything else.)

What you've built.
Three chants.
[pause 2 seconds]
First. Hablando, comiendo, viviendo. — one form each, no agreement, no persons. The smallest thing this book has asked of you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Second. Estoy comiendo. — estar carries the person, the gerund never moves. And it means now: for tomorrow you still say voy a comer.
[pause 2 seconds]
Third. Veo a María, but veo la casa. — the little a in front of a person, and nothing in front of a house.

Grammar Lens: both of these are Latin's absence.
[pause 2 seconds]
The gerund is what is left of a Latin form after its case endings fell off — which is why it has exactly one shape and agrees with nothing.
[pause 2 seconds]
The personal a is what grew back because case endings fell off. Latin marked the receiving end with an ending; Spanish marks it with a preposition, but only where it matters, which is people.
[pause 2 seconds]
One rule is a leftover and the other is a repair. Same cause.
