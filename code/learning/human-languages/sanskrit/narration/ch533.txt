Sanskrit, chapter 533: Something in the Way.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
अन्तरायः (antarāyaḥ) — an impediment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a girdle, then the Sanskrit for presents.

You'll want to know: अन्तरायः.
अन्तरायः — antarāyaḥ — "an impediment".
यदि मार्गे अन्तरायः अस्ति, तर्हि वयम् अन्यं मार्गं गच्छामः। yadi mārge antarāyaḥ asti, tarhi vayam anyaṃ mārgaṃ gacchāmaḥ. If there is something in the way, we take another road.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अन्तरायः (antarāyaḥ) is built on antar, "between", and aya, "a going", from the root √i, "to go": what comes between; MW gives antarāya as intervention, an obstacle (MW).

Lesson 3 of 6.
स्तम्भयति (stambhayati) — brings to a standstill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an impediment, then the Sanskrit for an obstruction.

You'll want to know: स्तम्भयति.
स्तम्भयति — stambhayati — "brings to a standstill".
यदि वर्षा यानानि स्तम्भयति, तर्हि वयं श्वः आगच्छामः। yadi varṣā yānāni stambhayati, tarhi vayaṃ śvaḥ āgacchāmaḥ. If the rain brings the traffic to a standstill, we will come tomorrow.
Past (it …): वर्षा यानानि स्तम्भितवती, varṣā yānāni stambhitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
स्तम्भयति (stambhayati) comes from the causative of the root √stambh, "to prop, to make firm", the root of stambha, "a pillar"; MW gives stambhayati as to fix, to make stiff, and to stop, to check (MW).

Lesson 4 of 6.
अपसारयति (apasārayati) — moves out of the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an obstruction, then the Sanskrit for brings to a standstill.

You'll want to know: अपसारयति.
अपसारयति — apasārayati — "moves out of the way".
यदि मार्गे शिला अस्ति, तर्हि सः ताम् अपसारयति। yadi mārge śilā asti, tarhi saḥ tām apasārayati. If there is a stone on the road, he moves it out of the way.
Past (he …): सः शिलाम् अपसारितवान्, saḥ śilām apasāritavān.

The word, taken apart.
अपसारयति (apasārayati) is built on apa, "away", and the causative of the root √sṛ, "to run, to flow"; MW gives apasārayati as to make or let go away, to remove (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
कष्टम् (kaṣṭam) — trouble; hardship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for brings to a standstill, then the Sanskrit for moves out of the way.

You'll want to know: कष्टम्.
कष्टम् — kaṣṭam — "trouble; hardship".
यदि कष्टम् आगच्छति, तर्हि धैर्यं धारयतु। yadi kaṣṭam āgacchati, tarhi dhairyaṃ dhārayatu. If trouble comes, hold on to your courage.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
कष्टम् (kaṣṭam) is the word MW gives as bad, ill, wrong, and as a noun evil, trouble; MW suggests, with a "perhaps", the past participle of the root √kaṣ, "to rub, to scratch", so its origin is uncertain (MW).
