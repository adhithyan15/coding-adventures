Marathi, chapter 496: In-Laws.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
दीर (dīr) — a husband's brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a great-grandfather, then the Marathi for a father's sister.

You'll want to know: दीर.
दीर — dīr — "a husband's brother".
लग्नानंतर तिचा दीर तिच्याशी नीट वागतो. lagnānantar ticā dīr ticyāśī nīṭ vāgto. After the wedding her husband's brother treats her well.
Masculine.
A woman's husband's brother, usually the younger one.

The word, taken apart.
दीर (dīr) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit devṛ, "husband's brother"; CDIAL lists Marathi dīr (CDIAL 6546).

Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
वहिनी (vahinī) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Marathi vahinī, "elder brother's wife", under Sanskrit vadhū, "bride" (CDIAL 11250).

Lesson 5 of 6.
व्याही (vyāhī) — a child's father-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a wife's brother, then the Marathi for a brother's wife.

You'll want to know: व्याही.
व्याही — vyāhī — "a child's father-in-law".
लग्नात दोन्ही व्याही आनंदाने भेटले. lagnāt donhī vyāhī ānandāne bheṭle. At the wedding the two fathers-in-law met happily.
Masculine.
विहीण, a child's mother-in-law: the parents of a married couple are each other's व्याही (vyāhī).

The word, taken apart.
व्याही (vyāhī) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Marathi vyāhī, "son's or daughter's father-in-law", under Sanskrit vivāhin (CDIAL 11925), a word of marriage.
