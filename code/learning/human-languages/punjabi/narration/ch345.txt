Punjabi, chapter 345: Making Up Our Minds.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਸ਼ਰਤ (sharat) — a condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for stubbornness, then the Punjabi for an objection.

You'll want to know: ਸ਼ਰਤ.
ਸ਼ਰਤ — sharat — "a condition".
ਮੇਰੀ ਇੱਕ ਸ਼ਰਤ ਹੈ ਅਤੇ ਉਹ ਔਖੀ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ। merī ikk sharat hai ate oh aukhī nahīṁ hai. I have one condition, and it is not hard.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਸ਼ਰਤ (sharat) is a loan, usually traced through Persian to Arabic sharṭ, "condition".

What you've built.
One more word for asking and answering.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: sharat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: sharat, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: merī ikk sharat hai ate oh aukhī nahīṁ hai. — I have one condition, and it is not hard.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Punjabi for stubbornness, then the Punjabi for an objection, then say sharat again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ਸ਼ਰਤ (sharat) mean? ("A condition".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਮੁਕਾਉਣਾ (mukāuṇā) — to settle a matter; to bring to an end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a decision, then the Punjabi for an opinion.

You'll want to know: ਮੁਕਾਉਣਾ.
ਮੁਕਾਉਣਾ — mukāuṇā — "to settle a matter; to bring to an end".
ਕੀ ਅਸੀਂ ਅੱਜ ਗੱਲ ਮੁਕਾਵਾਂਗੇ? kī asīṁ ajj gall mukāvāṅge? Shall we settle the matter today?
Past (I …): ਮੈਂ ਮਾਮਲਾ ਮੁਕਾਇਆ, maiṁ māmlā mukāiā.

The word, taken apart.
ਮੁਕਾਉਣਾ (mukāuṇā) is the causative partner of mukkṇā, "to come to an end", which goes back to a reconstructed mukna, "loosed"; CDIAL lists Punjabi mukkṇā, "to come to an end, run short", under it (CDIAL 10157).
