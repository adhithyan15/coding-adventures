# Sanskrit C1 mock 1 — answer key and task notes

Use this key with [`rubric.md`](rubric.md) and the confidential
[`mock-1-examiner-script.md`](mock-1-examiner-script.md). Reading, Listening,
Writing, and Speaking are independent 100-point papers; each must reach
60/100. Play every listening part at 160–185 words per minute **once after one
unscored orienting preview**.

## Reading

10 B; 11 C; 12 A; 13 B; 14 C; 15 A; 16 B; 17 C; 18 A.

19 C; 20 A; 21 B; 22 C; 23 A; 24 B; 25 C; 26 A; 27 B.

28 A; 29 C; 30 B; 31 A; 32 C; 33 B; 34 A; 35 C; 36 B.

The four source-level key distributions are deliberately offset. Across the
paper A, B, and C each occur twelve times. Credit the keyed meaning, including
the distinction between stated evidence, qualification, implication, and
rhetorical function; do not accept an attractive option contradicted by the
source.

## Listening

1 A; 2 B; 3 C; 4 A; 5 B; 6 C; 7 A.

8 B; 9 C; 10 A; 11 B; 12 C; 13 A; 14 B.

15 C; 16 A; 17 B; 18 C; 19 A; 20 B; 21 C.

22 A; 23 B; 24 C; 25 A; 26 B; 27 C; 28 A.

Across the paper A occurs ten times, B nine times, and C nine times. Items 1–7
track proposal, safety, weak demand evidence, partial alignment, driver-rest
constraints, unresolved scheduling, and the conditional agreement. Items
8–14 track the lecture’s social-time thesis and its historical qualifications.
Items 15–21 track conservation, living access, consent, challenge, and the
director’s qualified answers. Items 22–28 track the narrator’s movement from
decorative object to a contextual and permission-bearing relational map.

## Writing

Task 1 requires an independently produced **180–220 word source synthesis**
addressed to the municipal environmental committee. A successful response
accurately combines both sources: the reported water-saving and early tree
signals; sensor failures, salinity, and the need for longer evidence; workers’
diagnostic observations and labour conditions; and a practicable joined
decision process. It must distinguish evidence from inference, reconcile or
explain disagreement, and propose measurable outcomes for water, health,
device failure, human intervention, training, and governance. Source copying
does not count as independent synthesis.

Task 2 requires an independently developed **300–380 word extended response**
to the public knowledge-network board. It must take a qualified position on
open cultural data, develop at least two relevant domains, answer a serious
counterargument, and propose workable rules for tiered access, renewed
consent, privacy, commercial reuse and benefit sharing, correction history,
urgent suspension, removal requests, and independent review. Reward a reasoned
boundary rather than an unsupported all-open or all-closed claim.

For both tasks apply the shared rubric’s Devanagari, external sandhi,
word-final visarga or anusvara, continuous śirorekhā, word-boundary,
development, synthesis, and register criteria. Documented alternatives used
consistently receive full credit. Do not reward copied prompt language as
independent production.

## Speaking

Part 1 must last **270–300 seconds** and make a source-grounded conditional
first-year recommendation for the stepwell. It should separate established
facts, uncertainty, and advice; integrate conservation, local governance,
equal access, revenue, and unresolved safety; answer a plausible objection;
and give measurable six-month stop, change, and expand conditions.

Part 2 must last **300–360 seconds**. Score the candidate’s own contribution
to comparison, challenge, repair or revision, and a qualified joint
recommendation for the station hall. The group must confront the exit,
electricity, noise, staffing, heritage, access, and success-measure constraints
rather than merely list the four uses. Do not treat interlocutor-supplied
language as candidate evidence.

Ask all six Part 3 prompts in order. Each examiner stimulus lasts **20–75
seconds**; this timing applies to the examiner’s expanded prompt, not to an
individual candidate answer. The candidate’s six responses together must last
**330–420 seconds** and must defend, delimit, revise, or reconstruct positions
with evidence. Keyword paper notes are allowed; a full prepared script is not.
Score development and critical control, interaction, organisation, Sanskrit
vocabulary and grammar, and pronunciation and fluency in the candidate’s
declared documented tradition; documented alternatives used consistently receive full credit.

## Recording and provenance checks

Before operational use, verify the one-play delivery **once after one unscored orienting preview** and the natural 160–185 words-per-minute pace. The
production log must replace placeholders A and B with two distinct actual
documented tradition names and retain speaker provenance and reviewer confirmation. Confirm that a qualified reviewer judged the educated regional
pronunciation natural rather than caricatured. Record and retain the complete
speaking paper for independent scoring, including timing and any technical
incident.
