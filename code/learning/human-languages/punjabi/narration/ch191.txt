Lesson 2 of 7.
One word for a limited amount — little, less, or not much.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve “water,” “time,” and “place” before describing their amount.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਘੱਟ (ghatt) — ghaṭṭ — little, less, or not much.
Use the bounded meaning “not much” here. No comparison word is needed. The next lesson retrieves this one form before it enters a complete sentence.

Across the family.
Turner groups Punjabi ghaṭṭ, “diminished,” with Prakrit ghaṭṭaï, “is ruined,” under reconstructed ghaṭṭati, “decreases, is wanting.” The old idea and the modern quantity meaning stay close. Source: Turner, CDIAL 4415.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: ghatt; YOU SAY: ਘੱਟ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: ਘੱਟ (ghatt)]
[once you have stopped driving — write: copy ਘੱਟ (ghatt) once]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Produce the word for “not much” without looking.

Lesson 4 of 7.
Build the water frame — there is not much water.
