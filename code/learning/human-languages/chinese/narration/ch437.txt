Mandarin Chinese, chapter 437: Mountains and Plains.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
山区 (shānqū) — mountain area.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a regional dialect, then the Mandarin for a regional accent.

You'll want to know: 山区.
山区 — shānqū — "mountain area".
wǒ nǎinai zhù zài shānqū — My grandmother lives up in the mountains.

The word, taken apart.
山区 (shānqū) is built from 山 'a mountain' and 区 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srean and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary gives the second as the name of a marsh, and the sense 'an area' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
píngyuán — plain; flat open land.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a regional accent, then the Mandarin for a mountain area.

You'll want to know: píngyuán.
píngyuán — "plain; flat open land".
běifāng de píngyuán hěn dà — The plains in the north are huge.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píngyuán is built from píng 'level' and yuán 'broad flat land', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaeng and ngjwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'right' and 'at peace' and the second, after an older dictionary, as broad flat land; the sense 'level' for the first is not given there.

The word, taken apart.
shāmò is built from shā 'sand' and mò 'a waste', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srae and mak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as stones scattered by water and gives the pair itself under the second.

You'll want to know: yímín.
yímín — "to emigrate; to settle abroad".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yímín is built from yí 'to move' and mín 'the people', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ye and mjin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to shift' and the second 'the common people'.
