## You'll want to know: 午觉
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C169-EARLY169-02]; assesses=[] -->

**午觉** — *wǔjiào* — "afternoon nap".

> **午觉以后我们喝茶** *wǔjiào yǐhòu wǒmen hē chá* — After the nap we'll have tea.

The new character, in pieces: 觉 — nine strokes: three short strokes on top, the cover piece of 学 under them, and 见 at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**午觉** (*wǔjiào*) is built from 午 'noon' and 觉 'sleep', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *nguX* and *kaewH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second with the word for sleeping. The same character read *jué*, as in 觉得, is its other reading, glossed 'to wake' and 'to know'.
