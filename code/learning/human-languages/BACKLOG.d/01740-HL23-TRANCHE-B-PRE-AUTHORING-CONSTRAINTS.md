## Tranche B: constraints established before authoring, including one content-safety hazard

`HL23` tranche B is thirty of the remaining sixty-one exam-derived lexemes, planned as chapters
400-405: travel, directions, shopping, food, media, images. The duplicate/confusability screen and
the etymology pass were both run **before** any prose existed, against the corpus as it stands
after the qualities rung. This entry records what they returned, so the authoring does not
rediscover it.

**Screen: 24 clean, 6 flagged, 0 duplicates.** The six are `HL23` §12.3's remaining flags —
`tren`/`tres`, `playa`/`plaza`, `recto`/`resto`, `gorro`/`gordo`, `costar`/`contar`,
`pollo`/`polvo` — every one to be **disambiguated, not dropped**, each teaching its own minimal
pair. No candidate is already owned, so there is no repeat of the `feo` duplicate.

### The content-safety hazard, and it is the most important line in this entry

**`pollo`/`polvo` must be taught as `pollo` vs `polvo` ONLY.** In peninsular Spanish `polla` is
vulgar slang for *penis*, and `echar un polvo` means *to have sex*. Corominas derives the obscene
sense from `polla` "girl" and names it directly. The natural way to drill the pair — inflecting
through `polla` and reaching for a verb — produces both.

Do not inflect into `polla`. Do not use `echar` near `polvo`. Do not build a gender-contrast drill
on this pair. **Leave a comment in the lesson source explaining the omission**, or a later editor
will complete the paradigm as a kindness.

The general form, now in `code/specs/HL24-the-lesson-authoring-gates.md` §6: **screen what the
drill produces, not what the headword means.** No gate catches this and none can — the hazard is
compositional, and every token in it is an ordinary word. `gallina` and `pollitos` are the safe
substitutes if the chapter needs other forms of the bird.

**A second hazard, milder, in the same chapter.** `verdura` carries DLE sense 4 *"f. obscenidad"*
and `verde` sense 12 *"Dicho de un cuento, de una comedia, de un chiste: Indecentes, eróticos."*
Unlike `polla` this is not accidentally producible by inflection, so the requirement is narrower:
**do not gloss `verde` as "just the colour."**

A register scan for `malson.` / `vulg.` / `obscen.` / `despect.` across the other twenty-eight
words returned **zero** — `recto` is clean, since its anatomical sense is clinical and unmarked
and *todo recto* is ordinary. **Two are flagged unverified rather than clean** and must not be
read as passes: `costo` (a suspected hashish sense the DLE does not list, but the absence could
not be confirmed) and the phrase *"hacer un tren"*. `HL24` §6.5 also carries a standing note that
`concha` is DLE-marked `malson.` across seven Latin American countries — a live hazard for any
beach or shopping chapter in any track.

### Claims killed before any prose was written

Three of these contradict briefs the coordinating agent had asserted, which is the argument for
running the pass first rather than fact-checking prose afterwards:

- **`aparcar` is NOT borrowed from English *park*.** It is `a-` + `parque` + `-ar`, built inside
  Spanish. Only the vehicular *sense* is plausibly an anglicism.
- **The Germanic → French → English → Spanish loop is wrong as morphology** and must not be taught
  as the word's route.
- **`servāre` and `servus` are not confidently unrelated**, so say nothing linking `reservar` to
  `servir` in either direction.

Two calibrations, both of which are about not over-swinging:

- **`wifi`: "Wireless Fidelity" is a backronym, but the honest verdict is DISPUTED, not false.**
  A lesson that flatly calls it a myth is as wrong as one that repeats it as fact. **Put no
  duration in the prose** — "about a year" traces to a single account through a subagent and was
  never independently verified. Say: the name came first as a Hi-Fi sound-alike, and "Wireless
  Fidelity" was attached afterward as marketing by the Alliance and later dropped.
- **`gorro`/`gorra` has no known etymology.** Corominas gives *de origen incierto* and hedges the
  `gorre` guess with *quizá* / *acaso*, so give **no origin story at all**. But **`gorra` is
  primary and `gorro` is derived from it — that much is real and worth keeping.** Teach the shape
  contrast instead (a `gorra` has a peak, a `gorro` is round and brimless, and they are **not** a
  gender pair of one object) plus *de gorra*, *pasar la gorra*, *gorrón*, *estar hasta el gorro*.

And one dispute settled in the dictionary's favour, which is worth recording because this document
has more often gone the other way: **RAE's French detour for `paella` is right.** Catalan has a
native `padella` beside the borrowed `paella`, and the lost intervocalic *-d-* is the evidence,
because Catalan does not drop it natively.

### Corominas is now directly readable

The DCECH full text is on archive.org. That removes the constraint that has forced hedging
throughout this programme — the DLE could always be checked directly while Corominas could only be
reached by proxy, and `HL23` §12 and §13 both record cases where a Wiktionary citation of Corominas
turned out to misreport him. **`HL24` §7 records the three volume identifiers that were actually
read, which entries were read verbatim in each, and — more importantly — the five words that may
NOT carry a Corominas citation** because the reading behind them was a proxy, a different headword,
or nothing at all. `talla` and `tren` are subagent-sourced; `camisia` was seen only in the
`CERVEZA` entry's list of *otros celtismos*; `helar`/`helado` has no reading at all; and `paella`
lives under the `PAILA` headword. Attaching his name to a fact reached another way is precisely
the error this programme keeps catching in other people's sources.
