German, chapter 527: Wäsche waschen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Trockner — tumble dryer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to vacuum, then the German for cloth.

You'll want to know: der Trockner.
der Trockner — "tumble dryer".
Die Handtücher kommen in den Trockner. The towels go in the dryer.
Plural: die Trockner. Built on trocknen — to dry.

The word, taken apart.
Trockner is built on trocknen "to dry", from trocken "dry", which is inherited from Middle High German trucken (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

die Wäscheleine — "clothes line".
Im Sommer hängen wir die Wäsche auf die Wäscheleine. In summer we hang the washing on the line.
Plural: die Wäscheleinen.

The word, taken apart.
Wäscheleine is built on Wäsche "washing" and Leine "line, rope", inherited from Old High German līna, from Proto-Germanic līnōn (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
bügeln — to iron.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for clothes line, then the German for clothes peg.

You'll want to know: bügeln.
bügeln — "to iron".
Am Sonntagabend bügelt sie ihre Blusen für die Woche. On Sunday evening she irons her blouses for the week.
Regular: hat gebügelt. In Switzerland also glätten.

The word, taken apart.
bügeln is built on Bügel "a curved handle, a hanger" (EtymDB); the link is usually explained by the bent handle of early irons.

Lesson 5 of 5.
falten — to fold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for clothes peg, then the German for to iron.

You'll want to know: falten.
falten — "to fold".
Nach dem Bügeln faltet er die Hemden. After ironing he folds the shirts.
Regular: hat gefaltet. Die Hände falten — to fold your hands.

The word, taken apart.
falten is inherited from Old High German faltan, from Proto-Germanic falþaną "to fold" (EtymDB); English fold is a cousin.
