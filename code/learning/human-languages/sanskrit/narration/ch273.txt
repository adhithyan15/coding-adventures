Lesson 1 of 3.
One reflexive relation, held as a whole — I myself do not know my own self.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Recall the known आत्मा, “the self” or “the soul.” Today that known word helps you hear who an object points back to inside one sentence.

You'll want to know: one complete attested sentence.
अहमेव स्वात्मानं न वेद्मि। ahameva svātmānaṃ na vedmi. “I myself do not know my own self.”
This complete attested sentence appears in Central Sanskrit University's Ādhunika Saṃskṛta Sāhitya Pustakālaya. The lesson preserves the source sentence rather than silently composing a new Sanskrit line from the English meaning.

Grammar Lens: notice one relation.
In this one whole line, the speaker is “I.” The form स्वात्मानम् is the object and points back to the first-person subject: the speaker does not know the speaker's own self.
Keep the complete sentence together. This lesson does not teach every reflexive form, the middle voice, or a productive method for building new case forms or sandhi combinations.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read the sentence once slowly. Then say it as one admission: “I myself do not know my own self.”

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say the complete line once more. Keep the object-to-speaker relation inside this attested sentence.
