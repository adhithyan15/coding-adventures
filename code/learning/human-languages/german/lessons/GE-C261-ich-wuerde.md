# ich würde — would

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ICH-HAETTE-GERN-01, GE-LEX-DAS-WAERE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I'd like a coffee" and "that would be lovely". (*Ich
hätte gern einen Kaffee. Das wäre schön.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Haben* and *sein* have "would" forms of their own. Most verbs
borrow one.

## Grammar Lens: würde, and the infinitive last
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-WUERDE-INFINITIV-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERBKLAMMER-01, GE-GRAMMAR-GERN-11, GE-LEX-C66-VERBEN3-02, GE-GRAMMAR-DASS-VERB-ENDE-01] -->

For "would" with an ordinary verb, German takes **würde** and puts the
verb's infinitive at the end, the same bracket a modal builds:

> *Ich würde gern nach Italien* **reisen**. — I would like to travel to
> Italy.
> *Was würdest du* **machen**? — What would you do?

The forms: *ich würde*, *du würdest*, *er würde*, *wir würden*, *ihr
würdet*, *Sie würden*.

After *dass* or *weil*, *würde* goes to the very end, after the infinitive:

> *Ich glaube, dass er gern* **kommen würde**.

In everyday German the split is roughly this: *haben*, *sein* and the
modals keep their one-word forms, *hätte*, *wäre*, *sollte*; most other
verbs take *würde*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-KONJUNKTIV-UMLAUT-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WERDEN-01, GE-LEX-DU-SOLLTEST-01] -->

**würde** is **wurde**, the Präteritum of *werden*, "to become", with an
umlaut. That is the same move as *hatte* to *hätte* and *war* to *wäre*:
an umlaut on the past marks the Konjunktiv II. Not every verb takes one:
*sollte*, *wollte* and the regular *-te* verbs look exactly like their
past, and the sentence tells you which is meant.

*Werden* ← Old High German *werdan* ← Proto-Germanic *\*werþaną*, "to
turn" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB does not list *würde*; the umlaut history is the
usual account.
