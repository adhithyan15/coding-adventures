French, chapter 440: Les quartiers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le faubourg — the old suburb, the outer district.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the belfry, then the French for the town walls.

You'll want to know: le faubourg.
le faubourg — "the old suburb, the outer district".
Ils habitent dans un faubourg tranquille. They live in a quiet old suburb.
First the part of a town outside its walls.

The word, taken apart.
faubourg comes from Old French fors bourg 'settlement outside the town' (EtymDB), from fors 'outside', from Latin forīs, and bourg 'town'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'arrondissement — the district (of a large city).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the town walls, then the French for the old suburb.

You'll want to know: l'arrondissement.
l'arrondissement — "the district (of a large city)".
Elle travaille dans le cinquième arrondissement. She works in the fifth district.
A masculine noun. Some large cities are divided into numbered arrondissements.

The word, taken apart.
arrondissement is built on arrondir 'to round off', from rond 'round', which goes back to Latin rotundus, from rota 'wheel' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la préfecture — the prefecture (regional state office).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the old suburb, then the French for the district.

You'll want to know: la préfecture.
la préfecture — "the prefecture (regional state office)".
Pour son titre de séjour, il va à la préfecture. For his residence permit he goes to the prefecture.
Where the State's office for a département is.

The word, taken apart.
préfecture is a learned borrowing from Latin praefectūra 'office of a prefect', from praeficere 'to put in charge', built on facere 'to do, to make' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le commissariat — the police station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the district, then the French for the prefecture.

You'll want to know: le commissariat.
le commissariat — "the police station".
Après le vol de son sac, elle est allée au commissariat. After her bag was stolen, she went to the police station.
In full: le commissariat de police.

The word, taken apart.
commissariat is built on commissaire 'commissioner', from Medieval Latin commissārius, from committere 'to entrust', built on mittere 'to send' (Lewis & Short).

La caserne des pompiers — the fire station.
