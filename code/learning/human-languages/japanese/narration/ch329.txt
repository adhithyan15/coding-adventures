5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じょうしき (jōshiki) — common sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to refrain from, then the Japanese for to cut in line.

You'll want to know: じょうしき.
じょうしき — jōshiki — "common sense".
かれは じょうしきが ないと おもいます。 — kare wa jōshiki ga nai to omoimasu — "I think he has no common sense."

The word, taken apart.
じょうしき (jōshiki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jō, "usual" (Middle Chinese dzyang) and shiki, "know" (syik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
あたりまえ (atarimae) — only natural; to be expected.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to cut in line, then the Japanese for common sense.

You'll want to know: あたりまえ.
あたりまえ — atarimae — "only natural; to be expected".
あたりまえの ことを しました。 — atarimae no koto o shimashita — "I did what anyone would have done."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
あたりまえ (atarimae) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from atari, "hitting (ataru)" and mae, "front", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
