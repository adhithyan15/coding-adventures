Bengali, chapter 443: Temper and Grumbling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ক্ষোভ (khobh) — resentment; bitterness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for free of worry, then the Bengali for to fidget.

You'll want to know: ক্ষোভ.
ক্ষোভ — khobh — "resentment; bitterness".
নতুন নিয়ম নিয়ে কর্মীরা ক্ষোভ জানাল। nôtun niyôm niye kôrmīrā khobh jānālô. The workers voiced their resentment over the new rule.

The word, taken apart.
ক্ষোভ (khobh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kṣobha, "shaking, agitation, emotion" (Monier-Williams); in Bengali it is the stirred-up bitterness of someone who feels wronged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
খেপানো (khepāno) — to provoke; to tease until someone gets cross.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for resentment, then the Bengali for irritable.

You'll want to know: খেপানো.
খেপানো — khepāno — "to provoke; to tease until someone gets cross".
পাড়ার ছেলেরা কুকুরটাকে খেপাল। pāṛār chhelerā kukurṭāke khepālô. The neighbourhood boys provoked the dog.
Past (he/she …): তিনি ভাইকে খেপালেন, tini bhāike khepālen.
Its plain partner is খেপে যাওয়া, to fly into a rage.

The word, taken apart.
খেপানো (khepāno) is the causative of khepā, "to go mad", which is probably Sanskrit kṣipta, "thrown" (CDIAL 3685), a word Monier-Williams also gives for a scattered, distracted mind: to drive someone wild.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ফুঁসে ওঠা (phũse oṭhā) — to seethe; to bristle with anger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for irritable, then the Bengali for to provoke.

You'll want to know: ফুঁসে ওঠা.
ফুঁসে ওঠা — phũse oṭhā — "to seethe; to bristle with anger".
খবরটা শুনে গ্রামের লোকজন ফুঁসে উঠল। khôbôrṭā shune grāmer lokjôn phũse uṭhlô. When they heard the news, the villagers seethed.
Past (he/she …): তিনি রাগে ফুঁসে উঠলেন, tini rāge phũse uṭhlen.

The word, taken apart.
ফুঁসে ওঠা (phũse oṭhā) is phũse, the -এ form of an imitative verb for the angry hiss of a snake, with oṭhā, "to rise" (CDIAL 1793).
