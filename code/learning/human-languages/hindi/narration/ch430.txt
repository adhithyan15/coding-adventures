Hindi, chapter 430: Capital and Bankruptcy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दिवाला (divālā) — bankruptcy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a currency, then the Hindi for to cash.

You'll want to know: दिवाला.
दिवाला — divālā — "bankruptcy".
उस दुकानदार का दिवाला निकल गया। us dukāndār kā divālā nikal gayā. That shopkeeper went bankrupt.
Masculine.
दिवाला (divālā) निकलना, to go bankrupt.

The word, taken apart.
दिवाला (divālā) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit dīpāvali, "a row of lamps" (CDIAL 6358), beside दिवाली; how a row of lamps came to mean bankruptcy is not explained there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
निधि (nidhi) — a fund; a treasure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to cash, then the Hindi for bankruptcy.

You'll want to know: निधि.
निधि — nidhi — "a fund; a treasure".
गाँव के लोगों ने स्कूल के लिए एक निधि बनाई। gā̃v ke logoṁ ne skūl ke liye ek nidhi banāī. The villagers set up a fund for the school.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
निधि (nidhi) is a tatsama, Sanskrit nidhi, "a place for storing; a store, a treasure" (Monier-Williams); banks and charities use it for a fund.

Lesson 3 of 5.
पूँजी (pū̃jī) — capital (money); savings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for bankruptcy, then the Hindi for a fund.

You'll want to know: पूँजी.
पूँजी — pū̃jī — "capital (money); savings".
दुकान खोलने के लिए पूँजी चाहिए। dukān kholne ke liye pū̃jī cāhiye. You need capital to open a shop.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
पूँजी (pū̃jī) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit puñja, "a heap, a quantity" (CDIAL 8251), which lists Hindi pū̃jī, "heap, store": the store of money a business is built on.

Lesson 4 of 5.
चुकना (cuknā) — to be paid off; to run out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a fund, then the Hindi for capital (money).

You'll want to know: चुकना.
चुकना — cuknā — "to be paid off; to run out".
हमारा क़र्ज़ अब चुक गया है। hamārā qarz ab cuk gayā hai. Our loan is paid off now.
Past (it …): वह क़र्ज़ चुक गया, vah qarz cuk gayā.

The word, taken apart.
चुकना (cuknā) is the intransitive partner of चुकाना, from cukk, "fall short of, stop", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 4848), which lists Hindi cuknā, "to be finished".

Lesson 5 of 5.
उगाहना (ugāhnā) — to collect (dues); to raise (donations).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for capital (money), then the Hindi for to be paid off.

You'll want to know: उगाहना.
उगाहना — ugāhnā — "to collect (dues); to raise (donations)".
हमने मेले के लिए मोहल्ले से पैसे उगाहे। hamne mele ke liye mohalle se paise ugāhe. We collected money from the neighbourhood for the fair.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने किराया उगाहा, usne kirāyā ugāhā.

The word, taken apart.
उगाहना (ugāhnā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit udgrāhayati, "causes to take up, causes to pay" (CDIAL 1967), which lists Hindi ugāhnā, "to accumulate".
