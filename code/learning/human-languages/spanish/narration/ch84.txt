Spanish, chapter 84: La --- The Pronoun Carries What Left.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
la — "it," for a feminine noun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I have it," about the book. (Lo tengo.) Now guess the feminine version before you read on.

Grammar Lens: the form you probably guessed.
la — it, standing in for something feminine.
Hago la comida. — I make the meal. La hago. — I make it.
If you guessed la, you were not lucky. You were doing what the shape of Spanish invites you to do: el and la mark the two genders everywhere else, so the object pronouns mark them the same way.
The pronoun agrees with the noun it replaces — not with you, and not with the verb. El libro is masculine, so lo. La comida and la casa are feminine, so la.
That last point is worth pausing on. La here is not agreeing with anything present in the sentence. The noun is gone; the pronoun is carrying its gender on its behalf. This is the first time in Spanish that a word has had to remember something that is no longer being said.

The word, taken apart.
Last chapter had a small mystery: why lo and not el, when both come from the same Latin word? Here that mystery quietly disappears.
The feminine object form in Latin was illam. Worn down as an article it gave la. Worn down as a pronoun it gave — la. Both routes arrived at the same place, because the vowel that got lost on the masculine side (elo becomes el) was never at risk on the feminine side.
So the masculine pair split into two shapes and the feminine pair did not. Not an exception to memorise: a single sound change that happened to bite one word and not the other.
