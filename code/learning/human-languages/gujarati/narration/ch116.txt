Lesson 1 of 5.
તરબૂચ (tarbūc) — a watermelon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lemon, then the Gujarati for a coconut.

You'll want to know: તરબૂચ.
તરબૂચ (tarbūc) — "a watermelon".

The word, taken apart.
તરબૂચ (tarbūc) is a loan, usually traced to Persian tarbuz, "watermelon". Gujarati ends it in c.

પપૈયું (papaiyũ) — "a papaya".

The word, taken apart.
પપૈયું (papaiyũ) is usually linked with papaya, a Caribbean word that Spanish and Portuguese sailors carried round the world. CDIAL has no entry for it, so the road it took into Gujarati is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
જામફળ (jāmphaḷ) — a guava.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a watermelon, then the Gujarati for a papaya.

You'll want to know: જામફળ.
જામફળ (jāmphaḷ) — "a guava".

The word, taken apart.
જામફળ (jāmphaḷ) appears to be jām, which CDIAL lists as the fruit of the rose-apple tree under Sanskrit jambu (CDIAL 5131), with phaḷ, "fruit" (CDIAL 9051). CDIAL lists a different Gujarati word for the guava, jamrukh (CDIAL 5135).

મગફળી (magphaḷī) — "peanuts".

The word, taken apart.
મગફળી (magphaḷī) is mag, "mung bean", from Sanskrit mudga (CDIAL 10198), and phaḷī, "pod", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit phala, "fruit" (CDIAL 9051).
