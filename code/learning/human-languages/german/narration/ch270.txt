German, chapter 270: Abwarten.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zögern — to hesitate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for for years, then the German for for hours.

You'll want to know: zögern.
zögern — "to hesitate".
Sie zögerte einen Moment und sagte dann Ja. She hesitated for a moment and then said yes.
Regular: zögerte, hat gezögert. Ohne zu zögern — without hesitating.

The word, taken apart.
zögern is usually traced to Middle High German zogen "to pull, to drag", a relative of ziehen "to pull": to drag things out. EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
abwarten — to wait and see; to wait for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for for hours, then the German for to hesitate.

You'll want to know: abwarten.
abwarten — "to wait and see; to wait for".
Wir warteten das Ende des Gewitters ab. We waited for the storm to end.
Separable: wir warten ab. The saying Abwarten und Tee trinken means 'be patient'.

The word, taken apart.
abwarten is built on warten "to wait", from Old High German wartēn "to watch, to look after" (EtymDB), with ab: to watch something to its end.

Lesson 3 of 5.
verzögern — to delay; mostly reflexive: sich verzögern, to be delayed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hesitate, then the German for to wait and see.

You'll want to know: verzögern.
verzögern — "to delay; mostly reflexive: sich verzögern, to be delayed".
Der Flug verzögerte sich um eine Stunde. The flight was delayed by an hour.
Often reflexive: sich verzögern — to be delayed. Die Verzögerung — the delay.

The word, taken apart.
verzögern is built on zögern "to hesitate", usually traced to Middle High German zogen "to drag", with the prefix ver-; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 4 of 5.
verlaufen — to go, to proceed; reflexive: to get lost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to wait and see, then the German for to delay.

You'll want to know: verlaufen.
verlaufen — "to go, to proceed; reflexive: to get lost".
Die Reise verlief ohne Probleme. The journey went without problems.
Perfekt with sein: Alles ist gut verlaufen. Reflexive, it means to get lost: Ich habe mich verlaufen.

The word, taken apart.
verlaufen is inherited from Middle High German verloufen, from Old High German farhloufan "to pass, to overtake" (EtymDB), built on laufen "to run".

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Ablauf — the sequence of events, the process.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to delay, then the German for to go.

You'll want to know: der Ablauf.
der Ablauf — "the sequence of events, the process".
Der Ablauf des Tages war genau geplant. The running order of the day was planned exactly.
Plural: die Abläufe. Also the end of a period: nach Ablauf der Frist.

The word, taken apart.
Ablauf is built on Lauf "a run", from laufen, Old High German hloufan (EtymDB), with ab: the way things run off.
