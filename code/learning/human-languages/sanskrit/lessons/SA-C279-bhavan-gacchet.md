# भवान् गच्छेत् (bhavān gacchet) — you should go

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C278-EXPLANATION-01, SA-LEX-BHAVAN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I will not come, because I am ill". (*ahaṃ na āgamiṣyāmi, yataḥ ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi*.) A friend answers with advice.

## You'll want to know: -एत्, "should"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C279-SHOULD-01]; assesses=[SA-LEX-BHAVAN, SA-LEX-C26-ROLE-05, SA-LEX-GACCHATI-GO, SA-LEX-C27-REPLY-04] -->

> **भवान् गच्छेत्।**
>
> *bhavān gacchet.* — "You should go."

**गच्छेत्** (*gacchet*) is **गच्छति** with *-et* in place of *-ati*: "should go". It speaks to **भवान्** (*bhavān*), "you", said with respect, and about anyone else:

> **भवान् वैद्यं गच्छेत्।**
>
> *bhavān vaidyaṃ gacchet.* — "You should go to the doctor."

> **सः सत्यं वदेत्।**
>
> *saḥ satyaṃ vadet.* — "He should tell the truth."

To a woman, **भवती** (*bhavatī*):

> **भवती गृहे तिष्ठेत्।**
>
> *bhavatī gṛhe tiṣṭhet.* — "You should stay at home."

**भवेत्** (*bhavet*), from **भवति**, is "should be", and also "may be":

> **सः गृहे भवेत्।**
>
> *saḥ gṛhe bhavet.* — "He may be at home."

Pāṇini (3.3.161) gives this form for bidding and asking.

## You'll want to know: त्वं पठेः, and मया गन्तव्यम्
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C279-SHOULD-02]; assesses=[SA-LEX-TVAM, SA-LEX-C229-SCH229-03, SA-LEX-PATHATI-READ, SA-LEX-C240-TIME240-03] -->

To a friend, with **त्वम्**, the ending is *-eḥ*:

> **त्वं प्रतिदिनं पठेः।**
>
> *tvaṃ pratidinaṃ paṭheḥ.* — "You should read every day."

For yourself, "I must" can be said another way, word for word "by me, to be gone":

> **मया गन्तव्यम्।**
>
> *mayā gantavyam.* — "I must go."

**गन्तव्यम्** (*gantavyam*) is shaped like **कर्तव्यम्** (*kartavyam*), "a duty", and **मया** (*mayā*) is "by me". Meet it when you hear it; you do not need to build it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **भवान् गच्छेत्** | you should go |
| **त्वं पठेः** | you (a friend) should read |
| **सः वदेत्** | he should say |
| **मया गन्तव्यम्** | I must go |

These verbs end in *-ati*, like **गच्छति**; a verb such as **करोति** has an optative of its own, met one at a time.
