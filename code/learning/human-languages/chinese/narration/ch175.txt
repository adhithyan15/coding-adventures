Lesson 1 of 7.
jiājù — furniture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a ladder, then the Mandarin for a light bulb.

You'll want to know: jiājù.
jiājù — "furniture".
wǒmen yào mǎi xīn jiājù — We're going to buy new furniture.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiājù is built from 家 'home' and jù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and gjuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'complete' and 'to provide': what a home is provided with.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 7.
面前 (miànqián) — in front of someone; in someone's presence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for furniture, then the Mandarin for a sofa.

You'll want to know: 面前.
面前 — miànqián — "in front of someone; in someone's presence".
我面前有一杯茶 wǒ miànqián yǒu yì bēi chá — There's a cup of tea in front of me.
The new character, in pieces: 面 (miàn) — nine strokes: a horizontal on top, a short stroke falling to the left, then a frame (a vertical, a horizontal that turns down), two verticals inside it, two short bars between them, and the bottom bar last. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
面前 (miànqián) is built from 面 (miàn) 'face' and 前 'before', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjienH and dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to face' and 'the front': before one's face.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 4 of 7.
面 (miàn) — a bar, a short stroke, then a frame.

Script — a bar, a short stroke, then a frame.
面 (miàn).
One piece, nine strokes, built from the outside in.
On top, two strokes. A horizontal across the top. A short stroke falling to the left under its middle.
The frame, two strokes. A vertical on the left. A horizontal along the top that turns down the right side.
The inside, four strokes. Two verticals. Then two short bars between them, one above the other.
Last, the bottom bar that closes the frame.
2 + 2 + 4 + 1 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 面 (miàn), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 面 (miàn)? (Nine.) What closes the frame? (The bottom bar, last of all.) What stands inside it? (Two verticals, with two short bars between them.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 面 (miàn), pinned stroke paths and medians.

The word, taken apart.
guìzi is built from guì 'a chest' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, gwijH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). In the word the second loses its tone.

shūjià is built from 书 'book' and jià 'frame'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese syo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is listed there as kaeH, glossed as the frame of a house: a frame for books.
