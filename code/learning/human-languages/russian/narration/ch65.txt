Russian, chapter 65: Big, Small, Old, Long, Short.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
большой (bolshóy) — big.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for bad, then the Russian for new.

You'll want to know: большой.
большой (bolshóy) — "big".

The word, taken apart.
большой (bolshóy) is built on the comparative больший, "greater", Proto-Slavic bolĭjĭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Sanskrit bálīyān, "stronger", and bálam, "strength", and Greek beltíōn, "better".

Lesson 2 of 5.
маленький (málenkiy) — small.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for new, then the Russian for big.

You'll want to know: маленький.
маленький (málenkiy) — "small".

The word, taken apart.
маленький (málenkiy) is малый, "small", with the fond -енький. малый from Proto-Slavic malŭ, related, Vasmer says, to Gothic smals, "small", and Greek mêlon, "sheep, small cattle": English small is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
старый (stáryy) — old.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for big, then the Russian for small.

You'll want to know: старый.
старый (stáryy) — "old". The opposite of новый.

The word, taken apart.
старый (stáryy) from Proto-Slavic starŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian stóras, "thick, stout", and Old Norse stórr, "big, strong": old was first "grown, strong".

Lesson 4 of 5.
длинный (dlínnyy) — long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for small, then the Russian for old.

You'll want to know: длинный.
длинный (dlínnyy) — "long".

The word, taken apart.
длинный (dlínnyy) is длина, "length", with the adjective ending -ный, on the root of долгий, "long (in time)". Vasmer relates долгий to Lithuanian ìlgas, Sanskrit dīrghá- and Greek dolichós, all "long", from PIE dḷh₁gʰós.

Lesson 5 of 5.
короткий (korótkiy) — short.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for old, then the Russian for long.

You'll want to know: короткий.
короткий (korótkiy) — "short".

The word, taken apart.
короткий (korótkiy) from Proto-Slavic kortŭkŭ — inherited; Church Slavonic has краткий, "brief". Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian kartùs, "bitter", and Sanskrit kaṭu-, "sharp", and perhaps to Latin curtus, "cut short".
