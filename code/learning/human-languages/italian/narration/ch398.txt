Italian, chapter 398: Guadagni e perdite.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guadagnare — to earn; to gain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the quote, then the Italian for good value.

You'll want to know: guadagnare.
guadagnare — "to earn; to gain".
Se lavori il sabato, guadagni di più. If you work on Saturdays, you earn more.
Guadagnarsi da vivere — to earn a living. Guadagnare tempo — to gain time.

The word, taken apart.
guadagnare comes from a Germanic verb: EtymDB traces it through Vulgar Latin wadanio "to pursue, to win; to graze" to Proto-Germanic waiþanjaną (EtymDB). Italian turned the Germanic w- into gu-, as in guerra.

Lesson 3 of 5.
il profitto — the profit; the benefit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to earn, then the Italian for the earnings.

You'll want to know: il profitto.
il profitto — "the profit; the benefit".
L'azienda ha fatto un profitto di un milione. The company made a profit of a million.
Trarre profitto da — to benefit from. At school, il profitto is progress.

The word, taken apart.
profitto came from Old French profit (EtymDB), from Latin prōfectus "progress, gain, profit" (Lewis & Short), from prōficere "to make progress, to be useful", built on facere "to make".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la perdita — the loss; the leak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the earnings, then the Italian for the profit.

You'll want to know: la perdita.
la perdita — "the loss; the leak".
Se vendi adesso, è una perdita. If you sell now, it is a loss.
Stress on the first syllable: pèrdita. A water leak is also una perdita.

The word, taken apart.
perdita is built on perdere "to lose", inherited from Latin perdere "to destroy, to lose" (Lewis & Short), built from per- and dare "to give": to give away for good.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fallire — to go bankrupt; to fail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the profit, then the Italian for the loss.

You'll want to know: fallire.
fallire — "to go bankrupt; to fail".
Se nessuno compra, il negozio fallisce. If no one buys, the shop will go bust.
Conjugated with -isc-: fallisce. Fallire un esame — to fail an exam.

The word, taken apart.
fallire is inherited from Vulgar Latin fallīre, a form of Latin fallere "to deceive, to escape notice, to fail" (EtymDB).
