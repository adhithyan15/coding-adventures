Latin, chapter 416: Justice and Obedience.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iūstitia, iūstitiae — justice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a whip, then the Latin for custody.

You'll want to know: iūstitia, iūstitiae.
iūstitia, iūstitiae — "justice".
Iūstitia fundāmentum cīvitātis est. Justice is the foundation of the state.
iūstitia, iūstitiae f., built on iūstus, fair and right. English justice.

The word, taken apart.
iūstitia is built on iūstus "righteous, fair" (EtymDB), which comes from iūs "right, law" (Lewis & Short). English justice comes from it through French.

Lēgibus obtemperāre dēbēmus. We must obey the laws.
obtemperō, obtemperāre, obtemperāvī, obtemperātum + dative, like pāreō.

Lesson 4 of 5.
coerceō, coercēre — to restrain, to keep in check.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fairness, then the Latin for to obey.

You'll want to know: coerceō, coercēre.
coerceō, coercēre — "to restrain, to keep in check".
Cōnsul sēditiōsōs coercuit. The consul restrained the rebels.
coerceō, coercēre, coercuī, coercitum, co- + arceō, to shut in. English coerce.

The word, taken apart.
coerceō is built from co- "together" and arceō "to shut in, to keep off" (Lewis & Short): to hem in on all sides. English coerce comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vindicō, vindicāre — to claim; to avenge, to punish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to obey, then the Latin for to restrain.

You'll want to know: vindicō, vindicāre.
vindicō, vindicāre — "to claim; to avenge, to punish".
Iniūriam vindicāvit. He avenged the wrong.
vindicō, vindicāre, vindicāvī, vindicātum. In law, to claim a thing as one's own; in lībertātem vindicāre, to set free. English vindicate.

The word, taken apart.
vindicō is built on vindex, vindicis "a claimant, a protector, an avenger" (EtymDB); Lewis & Short explain it as vim dīcō, to assert force. English vindicate comes from it.
