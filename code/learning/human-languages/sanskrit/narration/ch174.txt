Sanskrit, chapter 174: Steals, Eats up, Strikes, Keeps, Hurts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चोरयति (corayati) — steals.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for serves, then the Sanskrit for enjoys.

You'll want to know: चोरयति.
चोरयति — corayati — "steals".

The word, taken apart.
corayati "steals" is built on cora "a thief" (CDIAL 4933), with the verb-making ending -aya-. It lives on as Hindi cornā "to steal" (CDIAL 4933), and cora as Hindi cor "thief" (CDIAL 4931).

भक्षयति — bhakṣayati — "eats up, devours".

The word, taken apart.
bhakṣayati comes from the root √bhakṣ "eat, devour" (CDIAL 9342), which MW thinks probably a secondary form of √bhaj "share, partake". It lives on as Punjabi bhakkhṇā "to devour" (CDIAL 9342).

ताडयति — tāḍayati — "strikes, beats".

The word, taken apart.
tāḍayati comes from the root √taḍ "beat, strike" (CDIAL 5752), found from the Nirukta on. It lives on as Punjabi tāṛnā "to slap" and Bengali tāṛāna "to drive away" (CDIAL 5752).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पालयति (pālayati) — keeps, protects.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for eats up, then the Sanskrit for strikes.

You'll want to know: पालयति.
पालयति — pālayati — "keeps, protects".

The word, taken apart.
pālayati "guards, protects, keeps" is, according to CDIAL 8129 after EWA, an l-form of pārayati "brings across", which people later linked with √pā "protect". It lives on as Hindi pālnā "to rear, to keep" (CDIAL 8129).

Lesson 5 of 5.
पीडयति (pīḍayati) — hurts, troubles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for strikes, then the Sanskrit for keeps.

You'll want to know: पीडयति.
पीडयति — pīḍayati — "hurts, troubles".

The word, taken apart.
pīḍayati first meant "presses, squeezes", and then "hurts, troubles" (CDIAL 8226). It is inherited from Indo-European, cousin of Greek piezō "press" (EtymDB), and MW explains it as pi-√sad "sit upon"; pīḍā "pain" is its noun.
