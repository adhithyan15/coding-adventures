Hindi, chapter 54: Animals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गाय (gāy) — a cow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new run of words: what did the last two words of the chapter before mean?

You'll want to know: गाय.
गाय (gāy) — "a cow".
From Sanskrit गो (go), 'cow' — and this one goes all the way back. The reconstructed ancestor is gʷṓws, which gives Latin bōs, Greek boûs, and English cow.
So गाय (gāy) and cow are the same word, separated by five thousand years and most of a continent. It is one of the cleanest cousins in this whole book: no borrowing, no detour, just two ends of one inheritance.

What you've built.
The first of five animals, and a word English owns too.

Lesson 2 of 5.
घोड़ा (ghoṛā) — a horse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
What did the word before this one mean?

You'll want to know: घोड़ा.
घोड़ा (ghoṛā) — "a horse".
From Sanskrit घोटक (ghoṭaka), 'horse' — a word whose own origin is uncertain and may not be Indo-European at all.
What makes it worth stopping on is what did not happen. Sanskrit had अश्व (aśva), the inherited horse-word, a straight cousin of Latin equus and Greek híppos. It lost. Everyday Hindi rides घोड़ा (ghoṛā), and the ancient word survives only in names and formal compounds.

What you've built.
The second of five, and an inherited word that was pushed out.

Lesson 3 of 5.
मछली (machhlī) — a fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the 2 words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: मछली.
मछली (machhlī) — "a fish".
From Sanskrit मत्स्य (matsya), 'fish', through a Prakrit form in which the -tsy- cluster fused into the -chh- you now say.
The -ī on the end is the same feminine ending you have met on कुर्सी and बिल्ली, and मछली (machhlī) is feminine like them. The ending is doing grammatical work here, not naming anything.

What you've built.
The third of five, and another cluster that gave way.

Lesson 4 of 5.
चिड़िया (chiṛiyā) — a small bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the 3 words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: चिड़िया.
चिड़िया (chiṛiyā) — "a small bird".
The origin of चिड़िया (chiṛiyā) is not settled. It is usually grouped with a family of words for small birds that look as though they were built from the noise a bird makes, but that is a description of the family, not a documented derivation.
It is worth saying plainly that 'sounds like a bird' is a guess and not evidence. The honest position is that this everyday word has no agreed ancestor, and that is the fifth such word you have met.

What you've built.
The fourth of five, and a guess that this book will not make.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बकरी (bakrī) — a goat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the 4 words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: बकरी.
बकरी (bakrī) — "a goat".
From Sanskrit बर्कर (barkara), 'a kid, a young goat'. The masculine बकरा and feminine बकरी (bakrī) split the animal by sex, as Hindi does with बेटा and बेटी.
Notice what the pair does that English does not: goat covers both, and English has to add a word to say which. Hindi has already said it in the ending.

What you've built.
The fifth of five. That is the yard.
