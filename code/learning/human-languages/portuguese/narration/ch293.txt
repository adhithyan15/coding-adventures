Portuguese, chapter 293: A polícia na rua.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o agente — the officer, the agent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fugitive, then the Portuguese for the chase.

You'll want to know: o agente.
o agente — "the officer, the agent".
Um agente da polícia ajudou-nos. A police officer helped us.
The polite way to address a police officer: Sim, senhor agente.

The word, taken apart.
agente is a learned borrowing from Latin agēns "doing, acting" (EtymDB), from agere "to do, to drive".

Lesson 3 of 5.
patrulhar — to patrol.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the officer, then the Portuguese for the patrol.

You'll want to know: patrulhar.
patrulhar — "to patrol".
Os polícias patrulham o bairro de bicicleta. The police patrol the neighbourhood by bike.
Takes a direct object: patrulhar a costa.

The word, taken apart.
patrulhar was borrowed from French patrouiller, first "to paddle about in mud", then "to patrol", built on patte "a paw" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
vigiar — to keep watch over, to guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the patrol, then the Portuguese for to patrol.

You'll want to know: vigiar.
vigiar — "to keep watch over, to guard".
O meu pai vigia as crianças na praia. My father keeps an eye on the children at the beach.
Also of the police: vigiar uma casa.

The word, taken apart.
vigiar comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB) and goes back to Latin vigilāre "to stay awake, to keep watch" (Lewis & Short).
