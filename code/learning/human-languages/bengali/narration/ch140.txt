Bengali, chapter 140: Sharp, Blunt, Fresh, Stale, Raw.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ধারালো (dhārālo) — sharp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for straight, then the Bengali for crooked.

You'll want to know: ধারালো.
ধারালো — dhārālo — "sharp".

The word, taken apart.
ধারালো (dhārālo) is dhār, "edge", from Sanskrit dhārā, "sharp edge, blade" (CDIAL 6793), with the ending -ālo.

Lesson 4 of 5.
বাসি (bāsi) — stale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for blunt, then the Bengali for fresh.

You'll want to know: বাসি.
বাসি — bāsi — "stale".

The word, taken apart.
বাসি (bāsi) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vāsita, "infused, scented" (CDIAL 11604). CDIAL lists Bengali bāsi, "stale".

The word, taken apart.
কাঁচা (kā̃chā) (kāṁchā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kacca, "raw, unripe", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 2613).
