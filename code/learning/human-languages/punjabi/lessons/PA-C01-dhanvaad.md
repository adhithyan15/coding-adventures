# ਧੰਨਵਾਦ (dhannavād) — "thank you," the Sanskrit way

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Punjabi has *two* ways to say thank you. Here is the first — the one
inherited from Sanskrit.

## The letters in this word

New: **ਧ** dha (a breathy *d*), **ਵ** va, **ਦ** da, and the nasal dot **ੰ**
(*ṭippi*) sitting on **ਧੰ** dhaṁ. Read **ਧੰ·ਨ·ਵਾ·ਦ** → *dhannavād*.

## The word, taken apart

**ਧੰਨਵਾਦ** (*dhannavād*) = Sanskrit *dhanya* ("worthy, deserving") + *vāda* ("a
saying, a speaking") — "a saying of "you are worthy."" It is the same Sanskrit
"thank you" behind Hindi *dhanyavād* and Marathi *dhanyavād*: one word, shared
across the Indo-Aryan north.

## Why it's said this way

This is the formal, Sanskritic thanks — what you would write, or say in a
respectful setting. Hold it against the next word, *shukrīā*, which arrived from
an entirely different direction.
