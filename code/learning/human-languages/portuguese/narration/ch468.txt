Portuguese, chapter 468: Inveja e egoísmo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a inveja — envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to be proud, then the Portuguese for to boast.

You'll want to know: a inveja.
a inveja — "envy".
Tenho inveja das tuas férias na praia. I envy you your holiday at the beach.
Ter inveja de — to envy. O ciúme is jealousy in love.

The word, taken apart.
inveja is inherited from Latin invidia "envy, ill will" (Lewis & Short), built on invidēre "to look askance at, to begrudge".

Lesson 2 of 5.
invejoso — envious.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to boast, then the Portuguese for envy.

You'll want to know: invejoso.
invejoso — "envious".
O vizinho invejoso não gosta do nosso carro novo. The envious neighbour does not like our new car.
Built on a inveja with -oso; a woman is invejosa.

The word, taken apart.
invejoso is built on inveja "envy", inherited from Latin invidia (Lewis & Short); compare Latin invidiōsus "envious".

Lesson 4 of 5.
egoísta — selfish; selfish person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for envious, then the Portuguese for to envy.

You'll want to know: egoísta.
egoísta — "selfish; selfish person".
Ele é egoísta e nunca partilha nada. He is selfish and never shares anything.
The same form for a man and a woman: um egoísta, uma egoísta.

The word, taken apart.
egoísta was borrowed from French égoïste (EtymDB), a modern word built on Latin ego "I".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o egoísmo — selfishness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to envy, then the Portuguese for selfish.

You'll want to know: o egoísmo.
o egoísmo — "selfishness".
O egoísmo estraga muitas amizades. Selfishness spoils many friendships.
The person is um egoísta.

The word, taken apart.
egoísmo is usually traced to French égoïsme, built like égoïste on Latin ego "I"; EtymDB records that French source for egoísta (EtymDB).
