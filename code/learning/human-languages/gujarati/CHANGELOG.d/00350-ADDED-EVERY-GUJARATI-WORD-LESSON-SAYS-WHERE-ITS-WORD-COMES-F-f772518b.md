## Added — every Gujarati word lesson says where its word comes from

Every one of the track's 630 word lessons now has an `etymology_hook`, and every one says in its body where its word comes from. Before this change 529 had no hook and 543 had no visible origin (human-language-data `ETYMOLOGY_GAP_PINS.gujarati`, now 0 / 0).

- **New "taken apart" blocks (545).** Each sits after the section that introduces the word, opens with `<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->`, and agrees with the lesson's new hook. Phrase lessons get "The phrase, taken apart". The heard-first lessons of C20–C30 and the script-ramp words of C03–C07 got short blocks too.
- **Sources.** Inherited words and Sanskrit loans cite Turner's *Comparative Dictionary of the Indo-Aryan Languages* (CDIAL) beside the fact, read from the moli-mandala data files: 913 citations of 406 entries in 418 lessons, each checked by script for the entry number, the Sanskrit or reconstructed form and the Gujarati form, and read against the entry's Gujarati rows and sub-entries. CDIAL's own hedges are carried: "probably", queried links, Dravidian, Munda and Austroasiatic sources, the variant a Gujarati form is listed under (*\*ḍholla*, *\*ekkalla*, *\*maricca*, *\*kappāla*), and forms CDIAL marks as taken from Hindi (*paiso*, *halkũ*, *banvũ*, *bacāvvũ*). Reconstructed forms are starred. Arabic loans name their root, checked against Lane's *Arabic-English Lexicon* (38 lessons). Persian loans are written "usually traced to Persian …", with the Turkic or Mongolic word behind *bahādur*, *camcī* and *nokrī* named. English and Portuguese loans name the donor. DEDR is cited only for *cappal* and *campal* (DEDR 1963, "Cf. H. cappal").
- **Rough mix.** About 355 inherited words (tadbhavas and words CDIAL lists from reconstructed forms), 54 tatsamas and semi-tatsamas, 55 Persian and 40 Arabic loans, 33 English and 7 Portuguese loans, and 45 whose origin is not settled or only partly known. Those say so, after a search of CDIAL by form and by meaning; where CDIAL has a near lead it is given as a lead (*tapelī* and *pātelũ*, *raṇ* and Sindhi *riñu*, *kobī* and Hindi *kobhī*, *ahīṁ* and Sanskrit *iha*).
- **Lessons that already had a block or a hook.** The 24 "taken apart" blocks of C03–C37 that named no origin (*tame kem chho*, *pāchhā maḷīshũ*, *nathī*, *samajto nathī*, *ane*, *paṇ*, *ke*, *vichārũ chhũ ke*, *jāṇũ chhũ ke*, *kemke*, *kem … kemke*, *māṭe*, *tethī*, *jo*, *jo … to*, *jyāre*, *te*, *ame / āpaṇe*, *je*, *je … te*, *koṇ*, *kyāṁ*, *kyāre*, *keṭlā*) gained one origin sentence each, and their hooks were rewritten where they were not etymology. Hook-only lessons whose hook described pacing rather than origin (the heard-first words, the numbers 1–5, the ordinals) gained a block and a real hook. The chapter 1–5 lessons whose body already gave an origin gained a matching hook, and some gained one sentence (*āvjo*, *hā / nā*, *chhe*, *shũ*, *maḷīshũ*, *bolvũ*, *vāndho nahĩ*).
- **Corrections to older text (8).** *Namaste* is taken over whole from Sanskrit, not inherited through sound change. Sanskrit *ābhāra* means "a load, a burden"; "obligation" is the later sense. *Kāl* is *kalya*, "dawn; tomorrow", built on *kāla*, not *kāla* "time" itself. *Pāchhā* is listed under *\*paśca*, not *paścāt* itself. *Rahevũ* goes back to a reconstructed *\*rahati*: Sanskrit has no verb *rah* "to remain". An unsourced Bengali cousin of *sārũ* was removed. *Ke* is no longer said to be the question stem outright, since CDIAL has no entry for it and Hindi *ki* is usually traced to Persian. *Bolvũ* is "inherited", with CDIAL's doubt about its deeper source, rather than "native".
- **Font.** Long vowels with a nasal are written with ṁ in the new text (*dāṁt*, *pāṁchmũ*), because the book font has no long vowel with a tilde.
- **Durations.** Four lessons are raised to their computed length, all under 300 s: GU-C02-tamarun-naam-shun-chhe 180 → 190, GU-C38-biju 240 → 268, GU-C38-panchmu 240 → 281, GU-C39-ordinal-reach 240 → 279. The three ordinal lessons were already over their limit before this change (257, 268, 268 s). Every other lesson stays within its declared time.
- **Roots.** Each lesson's `roots:` gained the slugs for its sources, reusing the slugs of the Hindi and Marathi lessons for shared etyma. A lead that CDIAL does not confirm gets no slug, so the cousins panel joins only words that share a source. `check:root-slug-splits` reports nothing new.
