German, chapter 294: Naturkatastrophen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Katastrophe — the disaster, the catastrophe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to put out, then the German for the forest fire.

You'll want to know: die Katastrophe.
die Katastrophe — "the disaster, the catastrophe".
Das Wetter war eine Katastrophe. The weather was a disaster.
Plural: die Katastrophen. Colloquially, anything that went badly.

The word, taken apart.
Katastrophe comes from Latin catastropha, from Greek katastrophē "an overturning" (EtymDB), built on kata "down" and strephein "to turn".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: das Erdbeben.
das Erdbeben — "the earthquake".
Das Erdbeben dauerte nur zehn Sekunden. The earthquake lasted only ten seconds.
Plural: die Erdbeben. Erde 'earth' plus beben 'to shake'.

The word, taken apart.
Erdbeben joins Erde "earth" and beben "to tremble", from Old High German bibēn, Proto-Germanic bibjaną "to quake" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Überschwemmung — the flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the disaster, then the German for the earthquake.

You'll want to know: die Überschwemmung.
die Überschwemmung — "the flood".
Nach dem Regen gab es eine Überschwemmung im Tal. After the rain there was a flood in the valley.
Plural: die Überschwemmungen.

The word, taken apart.
Überschwemmung is built on überschwemmen "to flood", from schwemmen "to wash along", the causative of schwimmen "to swim"; EtymDB lists no parent.

Hochwasser joins hoch "high", Old High German hōh (EtymDB), and Wasser "water", both inherited Germanic words.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: einstürzen.
einstürzen — "to collapse, to fall in".
Die alte Brücke stürzte ein. The old bridge collapsed.
Separable, Perfekt with sein: Das Haus ist eingestürzt.

The word, taken apart.
einstürzen joins ein "in" and stürzen "to plunge", from Old High German sturzen (EtymDB).
