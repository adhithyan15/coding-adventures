Latin, chapter 540: Toys and play.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pūpa, pūpae — a doll; a little girl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for play, then the Latin for to wrestle.

You'll want to know: pūpa, pūpae.
pūpa, pūpae — "a doll; a little girl".
Fīliola cum pūpā suā dormit. The little daughter sleeps with her doll.
Feminine, first declension. Before her wedding a Roman girl gave her dolls to the household gods.

The word, taken apart.
pūpa is the feminine of pūpus "boy, child" (Lewis & Short). English puppet comes from it, and pupil from the diminutive pūpillus.

trochus, trochī — "a hoop".
Puer trochum per viam agit. The boy drives his hoop along the street.
Masculine, second declension. A metal hoop with little rings, driven with a stick.

The word, taken apart.
trochus was borrowed from Greek trochos "wheel, hoop" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
turbō, turbinis — a spinning top; a whirlwind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a doll, then the Latin for a hoop.

You'll want to know: turbō, turbinis.
turbō, turbinis — "a spinning top; a whirlwind".
Turbō in pavīmentō celeriter vertitur. The top spins fast on the floor.
Masculine, third declension. Anything that spins: a top, a whirlwind. English turbine.

The word, taken apart.
turbō belongs with turba "commotion" and the verb turbō "to disturb" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): a thing in whirling motion. English turbine comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
harpastum, harpastī — a hand-ball game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: harpastum, harpastī.
harpastum, harpastī — "a hand-ball game".
Iuvenēs in campō harpastō lūdunt. The young men play hand-ball on the field.
Neuter, second declension. A rough team game with a small ball, from Greek.

The word, taken apart.
harpastum was borrowed from Greek harpaston (Lewis & Short), a ball one snatches from another, from harpazo "to seize".

Lesson 5 of 5.
natātiō, natātiōnis — swimming; a swimming place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: natātiō, natātiōnis.
natātiō, natātiōnis — "swimming; a swimming place".
Natātiō in marī mihi placet. I like swimming in the sea.
Feminine, third declension, from natō — to swim.

The word, taken apart.
natātiō is the action noun of natō "to swim" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English natatorium, a swimming pool, comes from the same verb.
