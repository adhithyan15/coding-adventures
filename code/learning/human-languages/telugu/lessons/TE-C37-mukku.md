# ముక్కు (mukku) — "nose," an inherited word with a second word's fingerprints on it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C37-CEVI-01, TE-LEX-C37-KANNU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Eye and ear both descend cleanly from their Proto-Dravidian
roots. This word descends too — but not quite cleanly.

## You'll want to know: ముక్కు
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C37-MUKKU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ముక్కు** — *mukku* — **nose**

> **నా ముక్కు.** — *nā mukku.* — "My nose."

## The word, taken apart - one root, contaminated by another
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-ETYMON-C37-MUKKU-02]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C37-CEVI-01, TE-ETYMON-C37-CEVI-02] -->

**ముక్కు** descends from Proto-Dravidian **\*mūnk(k)u**, and it is a real
cognate of Kannada *mūgu*, Malayalam *mūkkŭ*, and Tamil *mūkku*. But
comparative Dravidian scholarship flags Telugu's own reflex as
**irregular**: the consonant in the middle of the word is probably not a
clean, regular descendant of *\*mūnk(k)u* alone. It looks contaminated by a
separate Dravidian root, **\*mok-**, "face" — as if two related words leaned
on each other and left a mark. *Cevi* showed a word that drifted very
little; *mukku* shows one that drifted in a specific, traceable way, nudged
by its neighbour.
