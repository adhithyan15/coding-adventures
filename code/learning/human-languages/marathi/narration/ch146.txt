Marathi, chapter 146: Thumb, Fingernail, Back, Waist, Chest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अंगठा (aṅgṭhā) — a thumb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for an elbow, then the Marathi for a wrist.

You'll want to know: अंगठा.
अंगठा — aṅgṭhā — "a thumb".

The word, taken apart.
अंगठा (aṅgṭhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from aṅguṣṭha, "thumb" (CDIAL 137).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Marathi for a wrist, then the Marathi for a thumb.

You'll want to know: नख.
नख — nakh — "a fingernail".

The word, taken apart.
नख (nakh) is Sanskrit nakha, "nail of finger or toe, claw", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 6914).

Lesson 5 of 5.
छाती (chātī) — the chest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for the back, then the Marathi for the waist.

You'll want to know: छाती.
छाती — chātī — "the chest".

The word, taken apart.
छाती (chātī) matches Hindi chātī, which CDIAL traces to a reconstructed chātti, "chest, breast" (CDIAL 5014). CDIAL lists no Marathi form under it.
