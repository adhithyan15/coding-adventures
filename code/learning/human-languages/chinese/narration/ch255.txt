Mandarin Chinese, chapter 255: A Part-Time Job.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
打工 (dǎgōng) — to work part-time; to have a part-time job.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a wedding, then the Mandarin for daily life.

You'll want to know: 打工.
打工 — dǎgōng — "to work part-time; to have a part-time job".
他晚上打工 tā wǎnshang dǎgōng — He works a part-time job in the evenings.
The new character, in pieces: 工 (gōng) — three strokes: a short bar, a vertical down from its middle, and a long bar across the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
打工 (dǎgōng) is built from 打 'to strike' and 工 (gōng) 'work', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and kuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'skill'. Middle Chinese taengX would regularly give dǎng; dǎ is irregular. How the pair came to mean casual work is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what I do.

Script — two bars and a post.
工 (gōng).
One piece, three strokes, top to bottom.
A short bar across the top.
A vertical down from the middle of the bar. It starts at the bar and does not cross it.
A long bar across the foot, longer than the top one, closing the vertical.

Lesson 3 of 6.
gōngzī — pay; wages.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for daily life, then the Mandarin for to work part-time.

You'll want to know: gōngzī.
gōngzī — "pay; wages".
zhège gōngzuò de gōngzī hěn gāo — This job pays well.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gōngzī is built from 工 (gōng) 'work' and zī 'goods', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng and tsij (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'goods' and 'to help'.

You'll want to know: 同事.
同事 — tóngshì — "colleague".
他是我的同事我们一起工作 tā shì wǒ de tóngshì wǒmen yìqǐ gōngzuò — He's a colleague of mine; we work together.

The word, taken apart.
同事 (tóngshì) is built from 同 'the same' and 事 'affairs', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwng and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'together': people who share the work.

Lesson 5 of 6.
jiābān — to work overtime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for wages, then the Mandarin for a colleague.

You'll want to know: jiābān.
jiābān — "to work overtime".
jīntiān wǒ yào jiābān — I have to work late today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiābān is built from jiā 'to add' and bān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to increase' and quotes an older dictionary for the second, 'to divide jade'. The sense 'a shift at work' is not given there, so how it came is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
lǎobǎn — boss; owner (of a business).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: lǎobǎn.
lǎobǎn — "boss; owner (of a business)".
wǒ de lǎobǎn rén hěn hǎo — My boss is a nice person.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: lǎo is said with a rising tone before bǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
lǎobǎn is built from lǎo 'old' and bǎn 'a board', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lawX and paenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean the boss is not settled.
