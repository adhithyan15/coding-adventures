# comía — hear both vowels

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-COMER, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-ONGOING-HABITUAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Comí** presents eating as completed. **Comía** leaves it open or
habitual: “I was eating” or “I used to eat.” One accent separates the two forms
in writing and in sound.

## Grammar Lens: the singular -er row
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-COMER, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-ONGOING-HABITUAL] -->

| person | open or habitual past |
|---|---|
| yo | com**ía** |
| tú | com**ías** |
| él / ella / usted | com**ía** |

Say **comía · comías · comía**. As with **hablaba**, the first and third forms
match. Plural persons wait.

## Grammar Lens: co-mí-a
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SOUND-WRITTEN-ACCENT, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

The accent in **-ía** keeps **i** and **a** in separate syllables:
**co-mí-a**, three beats. It appears in every form learned here. Compare final
stress in **comí** with the middle stress and extra syllable in **comía**.

## The ending, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR, ES-SOUND-WRITTEN-ACCENT] -->

The **-er** imperfect continues a Romance reshaping of Latin **-ēbam** material.
Among the changes, an intervocalic **b** was lost; later Spanish spelling marks
the stressed **í** that keeps **i-a** apart. This is a layered history, not a
single instruction to delete one letter.
