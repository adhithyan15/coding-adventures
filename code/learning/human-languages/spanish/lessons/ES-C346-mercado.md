## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-13]; assesses=[] -->

**el mercado** — "the market."

Latin **merx**, **mercis** meant *goods, wares* — the things themselves.
**Mercātus** was the act of trading them, and then the place where the trading
happened.

English took the same root by two roads. Through Latin came **commerce**,
**mercantile** and **mercenary**, a soldier who fights for goods rather than
for a flag. Through Old French came **merchant** and **market**.

The strange one is **mercy**. Latin **mercēs** was *the pay owed* for work or
wares. In church Latin it drifted: a *mercy* became a payment made where none
was owed at all, a wage handed to somebody who had not earned it.

So a market square and an act of mercy come out of the same Latin heap of
goods.
