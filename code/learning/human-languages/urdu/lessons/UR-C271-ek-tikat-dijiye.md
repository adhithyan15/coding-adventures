# ایک ٹکٹ دیجیے (ek ṭikaṭ dījiye) — at the ticket window

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-C270-DIRECTION-02, UR-LEX-C270-KITNA-01, UR-LEX-C102-LOC102-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "turn left", politely. (*bāeṅ muṛiye*.) Then "a ticket". (*ṭikaṭ*.) At the window you ask for one.

## You'll want to know: a ticket, please
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C271-TICKET-01]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C102-LOC102-03, UR-LEX-EK, UR-LEX-LAHORE, UR-LEX-C48-VERBS48-04] -->

> **ایک ٹکٹ دیجیے۔**
>
> *ek ṭikaṭ dījiye.* — "A ticket, please."

**دیجیے** (*dījiye*) is **دینا** asked politely. The place goes in front with **کا** (*kā*):

> **لاہور کا ایک ٹکٹ دیجیے۔**
>
> *Lāhaur kā ek ṭikaṭ dījiye.* — "A ticket to Lahore, please."

> **ٹکٹ کتنے کا ہے؟**
>
> *ṭikaṭ kitne kā hai?* — "How much is the ticket?"

**کتنے کا** (*kitne kā*), "of how much", asks a price.

## You'll want to know: when does it leave?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C271-TICKET-02]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C95-TIME95-05, UR-LEX-C80-TOWN80-02, UR-LEX-C93-TIME93-05, UR-LEX-C92-TIME92-03, UR-LEX-CHAR, UR-GRAMMAR-C265-FUTURE-01] -->

> **اگلی گاڑی کب ہے؟**
>
> *aglī gāṛī kab hai?* — "When is the next train?"

**گاڑی** (*gāṛī*) is feminine, so **اگلی** (*aglī*), and so the future too:

> **گاڑی کتنے بجے جائے گی؟**
>
> *gāṛī kitne baje jāe gī?* — "What time will the train leave?"

> **وہ چار بجے جائے گی۔**
>
> *voh chār baje jāe gī.* — "It will leave at four."
