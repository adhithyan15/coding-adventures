Portuguese, chapter 288: Um assalto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o assalto — the hold-up, the robbery with force.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pickpocket, then the Portuguese for the disappearance.

You'll want to know: o assalto.
o assalto — "the hold-up, the robbery with force".
O assalto ao banco durou dez minutos. The bank hold-up lasted ten minutes.
Assalto à mão armada — armed robbery.

The word, taken apart.
assalto is usually traced to Vulgar Latin adsaltāre "to jump at", from Latin saltāre "to jump, to leap" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o assaltante — the robber, the mugger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hold-up, then the Portuguese for to rob.

You'll want to know: o assaltante.
o assaltante — "the robber, the mugger".
O assaltante levou o dinheiro todo. The robber took all the money.
The same form for a woman: a assaltante.

The word, taken apart.
assaltante is built on assaltar "to rob" with the ending -ante; the verb is usually traced to Latin saltāre "to jump" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
arrombar — to break open, to force (a door).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to rob, then the Portuguese for the robber.

You'll want to know: arrombar.
arrombar — "to break open, to force (a door)".
Os assaltantes arrombaram a porta da garagem. The robbers forced the garage door.
Arrombar uma fechadura — to force a lock.

The word, taken apart.
arrombar is usually traced to a- and rombo "a hole, a breach"; the deeper origin is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o arrombamento — the break-in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the robber, then the Portuguese for to break open.

You'll want to know: o arrombamento.
o arrombamento — "the break-in".
Houve um arrombamento no prédio esta noite. There was a break-in in the building last night.
Built on arrombar.

The word, taken apart.
arrombamento is built on arrombar "to break open" with the noun ending -mento; the origin of the verb is not settled (EtymDB has no entry).
