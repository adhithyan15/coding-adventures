# शिक्षक स्कूल में हैं — the building, with people in it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C47-ROLE-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A place you can name is not yet a place you can put somebody in. Say
where the teacher is, out loud, before reading on.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-02, HI-LEX-C47-ROLE-01, HI-LEX-C47-ROLE-02] -->

Every word here is one you have met:

> शिक्षक स्कूल में हैं।
> *chātra* भी स्कूल में हैं।
> विद्यालय बड़ा है।

Three short lines, and the third one changes register on purpose: the sign over
the door calls the building **विद्यालय**, even though the two sentences before
it called the same building **स्कूल**.

## Grammar Lens: why the reading paper cares
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-02] -->

This matters beyond the vocabulary. An exam reading paper is built out of signs,
forms and notices — exactly the places where the **formal** word appears — while
the listening paper is built out of people talking, where the **casual** word
does.

A candidate who knows only **स्कूल** can follow the conversation and stall on
the sign. A candidate who knows only **विद्यालय** does the reverse.
