Arabic, chapter 291: If It Rains.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
إذا (idhā) — if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I have been working here for three years", then "I arrived a week ago". (Aʿmalu hunā mundhu thalāth sanawāt, waṣaltu qabla usbūʿ.)
[pause 1 second]
You have known idhā, "if", since chapter two hundred and seven. What it takes after it will surprise you.

Grammar Lens: idhā and the past.
After إذا — idhā — Arabic puts a past verb, and means the future:
إذا وصلت مبكرا، أشرب قهوة. — idhā waṣaltu mubakkiran, ashrabu qahwa — If I arrive early, I'll have a coffee.
Waṣaltu looks like "I arrived", but after idhā it is "if I arrive". The condition is something that may well happen: you are not sure, but you expect it. The second half, the result, can be a plain present.
With kāna: idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, adhhabu maʿaka, if I have time, I'll go with you.

Grammar Lens: in, the other "if".
إن — in — is "if" as well, usually described as a little less sure than idhā. It takes the past in the same way: in waṣalta mubakkiran, if you arrive early. You hear it inside a phrase you may know already: in shāʾa allāh, "if God wills".
In, if, with no doubled letter, is a different word from anna, that, and an, to.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If I arrive early, I'll have a coffee" — idhā waṣaltu mubakkiran, ashrabu qahwa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I have time, I'll go with you" — idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, adhhabu maʿaka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "if you arrive early", with in — in waṣalta mubakkiran]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a plan of your own with idhā and a past verb, then a present]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
After idhā, which tense? (The past, meaning the future.) "If I arrive"? (Idhā waṣaltu.) The other "if"? (In, as in in shāʾa allāh.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
إذا أمطرت (idhā amṭarat) — if it rains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if I arrive early, I'll have a coffee". (Idhā waṣaltu mubakkiran, ashrabu qahwa.) Then "if you arrive early", with the other "if". (In waṣalta mubakkiran.)
[pause 1 second]
The result in that sentence was a plain present. Many results are not, and then Arabic marks the seam.

Grammar Lens: fa- before the result.
إذا أمطرت، فسنشاهد فيلما في المنزل. — idhā amṭarat, fa-sa-nushāhidu fīlman fī al-manzil — If it rains, we'll watch a film at home.
Amṭarat is "it rained", and after idhā, "it rains". The result opens with sa-, and so it takes فـ in front: fa-sa-nushāhidu. It is the fa- of "so": the condition comes true, so this follows.

Grammar Lens: four results that take fa-.
A result needs fa- when it opens with sa- or sawfa, with lan, with an order, or with a noun. Four sentences, one of each:
idhā amṭarat, fa-sa-nushāhidu fīlman, idhā amṭarat, fa-lan nakhruja, we won't go out, idhā waṣalta, fa-ttaṣil bī, call me, idhā taʾakhkhara al-qiṭār, fa-l-ḥāfila afḍal, the bus is better.
A plain present, like ashrabu qahwa, needs none. When you are unsure, the fa- before sa- is the one you will use most.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, we won't go out" — idhā amṭarat, fa-lan nakhruja]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you arrive, call me" — idhā waṣalta, fa-ttaṣil bī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If the train is late, the bus is better" — idhā taʾakhkhara al-qiṭār, fa-l-ḥāfila afḍal]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: your own plan for tomorrow: idhā … fa-sa- …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains"? (Idhā amṭarat.) What goes in front of a result with sa-? (Fa-.) Name the four openings that call for it. (Sa- or sawfa, lan, an order, a noun.) Does a plain present need it? (No.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — if it rains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the train is better than the bus" and "the biggest city". (Al-qiṭār afḍal min al-ḥāfila, akbar madīna.) Then "for two years" and "a week ago". (Mundhu sanatayn, qabla usbūʿ.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and a condition with both its halves. The condition: idhā or in with a past verb, meaning something that may well happen. The result: a plain present as it is, or fa- in front when it opens with sa-, lan, an order or a noun.
[pause 2 seconds]
A plan with its condition in one breath:
إذا كان الطقس جميلا، فسنذهب إلى البحر. — idhā kāna aṭ-ṭaqs jamīlan, fa-sa-nadhhabu ilā al-baḥr — If the weather is nice, we'll go to the sea.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: idhā kāna aṭ-ṭaqs jamīlan, fa-sa-nadhhabu ilā al-baḥr]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you get lost, phone me" — idhā ḍalalta, fa-ttaṣil bī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If the pharmacist phones, I'll give him the number" — idhā ittaṣala aṣ-ṣaydalī, fa-sa-uʿṭīhi ar-raqm]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If I learn a lot, I'll become an engineer" — idhā taʿallamtu kathīran, fa-sa-uṣbiḥu muhandisan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, I won't travel" — idhā amṭarat, fa-lan usāfira]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: two plans of your own for the weekend: one with idhā and a plain present, one with fa-sa-]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains, we won't go out"? (Idhā amṭarat, fa-lan nakhruja.) Which tense follows idhā? (The past.) Which "if" sits in in shāʾa allāh? (In.)
