# prestar — the move that devolver undoes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02, ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *devolver*. Say *el cargador*. You can already say you gave the
charger back. Say how it reached you in the first place.

## You'll want to know: prestar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02] -->

**prestar** — to lend.

*Te presto el cargador* — I lend you the charger. And then, later, *te lo
devuelvo*.

## Grammar Lens: which way is it moving
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-02] -->

English has one word doing two opposite jobs — people say *borrow* and *lend*
and then muddle them. Spanish keeps the giver's side and marks the receiver's
with a preposition:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| te presto el libro | I lend you the book |
| pedir prestado un libro | to borrow a book |

So **prestar** is always the **lender** speaking. To be the borrower you say
*pedir prestado* — literally *to ask for it lent*, which uses the *pedir* under
**el pedido** from four chapters ago.

The three verbs now sit in a cycle you can say end to end: **prestar** hands it
over, the other person has it, **devolver** brings it back.
