Spanish, chapter 549: Casados.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
casado — married.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the toast, then the Spanish for the honeymoon.

You'll want to know: casado.
casado — "married".
Mi hermano está casado con una francesa. My brother is married to a Frenchwoman.
Estar casado con — to be married to.

The word, taken apart.
casado is built on the participle of casar 'to marry', which comes from casa 'house', inherited from Latin casa 'a hut' (Lewis & Short). To marry was to set up house.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: soltero.
soltero — "single, unmarried".
Mi tío nunca se casó; sigue soltero. My uncle never married; he is still single.
Un soltero is a single man; la despedida de soltero is a stag party.

The word, taken apart.
soltero is inherited from Latin sōlitārius 'alone, solitary' (EtymDB), built on sōlus 'alone'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
casar — to marry (someone to someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for married, then the Spanish for single.

You'll want to know: casar.
casar — "to marry (someone to someone)".
Los casó el alcalde. The mayor married them.
Without se it is the person who performs the wedding. Casarse is to get married.

The word, taken apart.
casar is built on casa 'house', which is inherited from Latin casa 'a hut, a cottage' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
brindar — to toast, to drink to; to offer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to marry, then the Spanish for the husband.

You'll want to know: brindar.
brindar — "to toast, to drink to; to offer".
Brindamos por el nuevo año. We drank a toast to the new year.
Brindar por — to drink to. Brindar ayuda — to offer help.

The word, taken apart.
brindar is built on brindis 'a toast' (EtymDB), which comes from the German words bring dir's 'I bring it to you'.
