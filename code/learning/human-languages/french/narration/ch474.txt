French, chapter 474: Rire aux éclats.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la gaieté — cheerfulness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for modest, then the French for to praise.

You'll want to know: la gaieté.
la gaieté — "cheerfulness".
Sa gaieté met tout le monde de bonne humeur. Her cheerfulness puts everyone in a good mood.
Also spelt la gaîté. Built on gai "cheerful".

The word, taken apart.
la gaieté is built on gai "cheerful" (EtymDB), which came into French from Old Occitan gai; it is usually traced further back to a Germanic word.

rigoler — "to laugh, to have fun (informal)".
On a bien rigolé pendant la soirée. We had a good laugh at the party.
Informal. Tu rigoles! — you are joking!

The word, taken apart.
rigoler goes back to Old French rigoler "to amuse oneself". The origin is not settled; a link with rire is often suggested.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le fou rire — fit of the giggles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for cheerfulness, then the French for to laugh.

You'll want to know: le fou rire.
le fou rire — "fit of the giggles".
Pendant le cours, nous avons eu un fou rire. During the lesson we got the giggles.
Avoir le fou rire — to be unable to stop laughing.

The word, taken apart.
le fou rire is "mad laughter": fou is inherited from Latin follis "bellows, a bag of wind", and rire from Latin rīdēre "to laugh" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
marrant — funny (informal).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: marrant.
marrant — "funny (informal)".
Ton frère est vraiment marrant. Your brother is really funny.
Informal, like drôle. Se marrer — to laugh.

The word, taken apart.
marrant is the present participle of se marrer "to laugh", a word of familiar French. The origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
chatouiller — to tickle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for fit of the giggles, then the French for funny.

You'll want to know: chatouiller.
chatouiller — "to tickle".
Le grand-père chatouille le bébé, qui rit. The grandfather tickles the baby, who laughs.
Être chatouilleux — to be ticklish.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of chatouiller is not settled. It is probably an expressive word; Germanic words for tickling, like Old English citelian, look similar (EtymDB).
