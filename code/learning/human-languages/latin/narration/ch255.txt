Latin, chapter 255: To hold, To burst, To warm, To fill, To measure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
teneō, tenēre — to hold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tent, then the Latin for a flag, a banner.

You'll want to know: teneō, tenēre.
teneō, tenēre — "to hold".

The word, taken apart.
teneō is inherited from Proto-Italic tenēō, "to hold" (EtymDB 2.1), usually traced to Proto-Indo-European ten-, "to stretch" (Lewis & Short compare Greek teinō, "to stretch"). English tenant comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
impleō, implēre — to fill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to burst, then the Latin for to warm.

You'll want to know: impleō, implēre.
impleō, implēre — "to fill".

The word, taken apart.
impleō is in, "in", with pleō, "to fill" (EtymDB 2.1), a verb found only in compounds. pleō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pleh1-, "to fill"; English full is a cousin.
