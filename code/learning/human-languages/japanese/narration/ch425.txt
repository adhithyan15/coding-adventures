7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
つうやく (tsūyaku) — interpreting; an interpreter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to search all over, then the Japanese for fortunately.

You'll want to know: つうやく.
つうやく — tsūyaku — "interpreting; an interpreter".
がいこくで あにが つうやくを しました。 — gaikoku de ani ga tsūyaku o shimashita — "Abroad, my brother did the interpreting."

The word, taken apart.
つうやく (tsūyaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tsū, "pass through" (Middle Chinese thuwng) and yaku, "translate" (yek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 7.
といあわせる (toiawaseru) — to make an enquiry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for interpreting, then the Japanese for translation.

You'll want to know: といあわせる.
といあわせる — toiawaseru — "to make an enquiry".
でんわで えきに といあわせました。 — denwa de eki ni toiawasemashita — "I phoned the station to ask."
Polite といあわせます, toiawasemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ちちが りょかんに といあわせました。, chichi ga ryokan ni toiawasemashita.

The word, taken apart.
といあわせる (toiawaseru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from toi, "asking" (from tou) and awaseru, "to bring together"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 7.
つうじる (tsūjiru) — to be understood; to get through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for translation, then the Japanese for to make an enquiry.

You'll want to know: つうじる.
つうじる — tsūjiru — "to be understood; to get through".
日本語が つうじて、あんしんしました。 — nihongo ga tsūjite, anshin shimashita — "I was relieved that my Japanese was understood."
Polite つうじます, tsūjimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あにの 日本語が つうじました。, ani no nihongo ga tsūjimashita.

The word, taken apart.
つうじる (tsūjiru) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the Sino-Japanese tsū, "pass through" (Middle Chinese thuwng), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese, made into a verb with the native ending -jiru, a form of suru, "to do", inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.
