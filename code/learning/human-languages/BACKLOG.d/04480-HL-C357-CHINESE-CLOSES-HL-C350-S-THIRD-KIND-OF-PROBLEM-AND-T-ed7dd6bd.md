## HL-C357 — Chinese closes HL-C350's third kind of problem, and the campaign's five tracks now disagree usefully about what a numeral tranche IS

**The last of HL-C350's zero-coverage tracks, and the only one whose problem was
HALF A SERIES rather than an absence.** Chinese taught 一 二 三 四 五 in chapter
3 — each with its own character and its own writing lesson — and then stopped,
for **seventeen chapters**. Chapters 20–21 add 18 lessons and close
`ZH-A1-NUM-01`, `ZH-A1-NUM-02` and `ZH-A1-AB-02`; coverage moves 70/191 (37%) to
73/191 (38%).

**THE NOTE FOUND THE LESSON BEFORE THE TRANCHE DID.** `ZH-A1-NUM-01` recorded
that 十 "appears in four lesson bodies, as the lower component of 早, where the
text says in as many words that on its own it is the character for ten and then
does not teach it." `ZH-W20-shi` is that sentence cashed, thirteen chapters
late — and it is the third time in this campaign that **an inventory note
written as an instruction got executed**, after Urdu's digits note and Russian's
"DERIVED, NOT DROPPED". A note written as a lament does not.

**FIVE TRACKS, AND THE TEENS ARE FIVE DIFFERENT JOBS.** This is the finding that
only exists because the campaign shipped together:

| track | eleven to nineteen | what the tranche did |
|---|---|---|
| chinese | **十一 = ten-one**, no exceptions | taught the rule; the reader BUILDS all nine |
| persian | *yâz-dah*, unit + *dah*, three bends | taught three, reader builds six |
| marwadi | *ughaṇīs* = **one less than twenty** | deferred; teaches TWENTY first so they can land later |
| urdu | nine irregular words | deferred, and says so to the reader |
| russian | *odinnadtsat*, unit + "on ten" | deferred behind the case system |

**A numeral point is not one kind of work.** Marwadi's teens are subtractive so
the round number has to come first; Chinese's are so regular that ten words
reach ninety-nine and the ORDER is the entire grammar; Urdu's are nine words and
a tranche that pretended otherwise would have shipped a rule that does not
exist. Every one of those five probes is shaped differently as a result, and
each one asserts what it does NOT claim.

**COST, MEASURED ACROSS THE CAMPAIGN.** Marwadi: one new letter for twelve
numbers. Urdu, Russian: none. Persian: none for the words, two recognition-only
letters for two headwords. Chinese: six characters, and its five single digits
cost **twelve strokes between them** — the three largest are the three quickest
characters in the book to write. **In four of five tracks the script was not the
blocker, and only a census of the corpus's own characters said so.** A coverage
column would have said the opposite in every case.

**THE SCRIPT INVENTORY AND THE VENDORED FONT GREW WITH THE CONTENT.** 六 七 八 九
十 百 join `data/scripts/chinese.json` with stroke orders read off the same
pinned Hanzi Writer Data snapshot the existing entries cite, and
`NotoSansSC-Subset.ttf` was regenerated by its own script from that file. The
upstream font's SHA-256 matched the checked-in pin exactly, so the subset is
reproducible rather than hand-edited — and the ordering matters: the font is
built FROM the inventory, so adding a character to a lesson without adding it to
`chinese.json` produces a book full of tofu that every other gate passes.

**九十九 IS THE DRILL THE TRACK HAD NO MATERIAL FOR.** Third-tone sandhi was
taught in chapter 1 and the corpus could not build two third tones in a row out
of what it had. Ninety-nine is two of them around a rise. **A numeral tranche
pays back into phonology as well as into age, price and date.**

**REINFORCEMENT.** 227 → 238, none of it this tranche's, and the R4 window count
FELL (64 → 62). Every other metric unchanged: script-closure 4, never-taught
glyphs 0, forward references 0, atom and chapter spikes 0, payoff surprises 0,
duration violations 0.

**WHAT HL-C350 STILL HOLDS.** `french` and `german` reach twenty and are measured
against a point demanding a hundred. That is a CEILING problem, not an absence,
and the repair is different in kind: neither track needs a numeral tranche, both
need the decades. Ordinals remain the corpus's weakest column, but the campaign
moved it: Persian, Urdu and Russian now teach one, so the count is five tracks
of twenty rather than two.
