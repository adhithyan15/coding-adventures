## You'll want to know: vacō, vacāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C538-OTIU-02]; assesses=[] -->

**vacō, vacāre** — "to be free, have time (for)".

> Hodiē vacō; tēcum ambulāre possum.
>
> *Today I am free; I can go for a walk with you.*

*vacō, vacāre, vacāvī, vacātum*. To be empty, then to be free for something: *vacāre litterīs* — to have time for literature. English *vacant*, *vacation*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**vacō** is inherited, through Proto-Italic, from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "empty" (EtymDB). English *vacant* and *vacation* come from it.
