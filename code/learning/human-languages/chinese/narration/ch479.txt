Lesson 1 of 6.
shāngbā — scar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sprain, then the Mandarin for to dress a wound.

You'll want to know: shāngbā.
shāngbā — "scar".
zhège shāngbā yǐjīng yǒu shí nián le — I've had this scar for ten years now.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shāngbā is built from 伤 'to hurt' and bā 'a scar'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese syang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to harm'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
血 (xiě) — blood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to dress a wound, then the Mandarin for a scar.

You'll want to know: 血.
血 — xiě — "blood".
你看这是血 nǐ kàn zhè shì xiě — Look, that's blood.
The new character, in pieces: 血 (xiě) — six strokes: a short stroke falling to the left at the top, then a dish of five, as at the foot of 温 wēn. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
Alone, and in everyday speech, 血 is said xiě; in words such as liúxuè it is said xuè.

The word, taken apart.
血 (xiě) is inherited from Middle Chinese xwet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), explained after an older book as what flows out of the flesh. The regular sound change gives xuè; the everyday xiě is a second, spoken reading, and how it arose is not settled.

Script — one short stroke over a dish.
血 (xiě).
Two pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top, one stroke. A short stroke falling to the left, above the middle.
The dish, five strokes, as at the foot of 温. A short vertical on the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two short verticals inside, then the long bar across the bottom.
1 + 5 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 血 (xiě), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 血 (xiě) have? (Two: one stroke and five.) Where have you written the dish before? (At the foot of 温.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
zhǐxuè — to stop the bleeding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a scar, then the Mandarin for blood.

You'll want to know: zhǐxuè.
zhǐxuè — "to stop the bleeding".
yīshēng yòng le shí fēnzhōng cái zhǐxuè — It took the doctor ten minutes to stop the bleeding.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǐxuè is built from zhǐ 'to stop' and 血 (xiě) 'blood', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyiX and xwet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stop'.
