# मैं ट्रेन से जाऊँगा — reading a correction in a paper

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01, HI-LEX-C69-GOING-05] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say *I will do*. Then say the verb for going. The future ending you
learned on one verb attaches to any stem, and this is the sentence that wants
it.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-05, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-04, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01] -->

| speaker | Hindi |
|---|---|
| A | आप बस से जाएँगे? |
| B | मैं बस से नहीं, ट्रेन से जाऊँगा। |

B does two things at once: rejects A's guess and supplies the replacement, in
one sentence with one verb at the end of it.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-05, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-04] -->

Find **नहीं** first. Everything before it is what is being denied; everything
after it up to the verb is what replaces it.

Then check the count: **two से** means a swap. An item that asks *how will B
travel* is answered by the phrase **after** the negative, and the commonest
wrong answer is the phrase before it — which is exactly why the paper prints
the rejected option first.

## Grammar Lens: the chapter in one sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-02, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-01, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-05] -->

**से** marks a start and **तक** marks a stop, and between them they carry both
a week and a working day:

**मैं सोमवार से शुक्रवार तक ट्रेन से जाऊँगा।**

Two **से** in that sentence, doing **different** jobs — the first opens a span,
the second names a means. Neither is a mistake, and telling them apart is a
matter of looking for a **तक** further along.
