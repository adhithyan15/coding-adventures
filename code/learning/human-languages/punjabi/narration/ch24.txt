Punjabi, chapter 24: Old Action Families at R4.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
Know and think return at R4 — retrieve know and think at R4 without adding language.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
No new language. Hear jāṇnā, “to know,” and sochṇā, “to think.” Say each meaning before reading on.

Guided Practice.
Read ਜਾਣਨਾ, ਸੋਚਣਾ once, then give the meanings aloud. This is recognition and speech retrieval. It awards no independent Gurmukhi writing evidence.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Say the meanings aloud in order: jāṇā / jāṇnā / sochṇā. Which pair differs by the extra n?
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Match the old root stories: which verb reaches English know, and which began with burn or glow and then grieve?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Without looking, say “to know; to think” in Punjabi.


Lesson 2 of 4.
Understand and read return at R4 — retrieve understand, read, falling tone, and subjoined ha at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 6 seconds]
Say samajhṇā and paṛhnā with the already-taught falling pitch. Give their meanings aloud.

Guided Practice.
Script recognition only.
Recognizing ੜ੍ਹ here is reading evidence only. Do not copy it, and do not count this lesson as independent writing.
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Hear samajhṇā / paṛhnā. Say understand / read aloud, keeping the falling pitch in each Punjabi word.
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Which old image belongs to each verb: wake up; recite or read aloud?
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
In ਪੜ੍ਹਨਾ, identify the small ha tucked under the preceding letter and then read the whole known word aloud.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say “understand; read” once more from meaning.


Lesson 3 of 4.
Write, take, and ask return at R4 — retrieve write, take, and ask with their old root stories at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 6 seconds]
Hear likhṇā, laiṇā, puchhṇā. Give the three meanings aloud before looking at the answer.

Guided Practice.
Three remembered histories.
Read ਲਿਖਣਾ, ਲੈਣਾ, ਪੁੱਛਣਾ once. The visible words support recognition; this lesson does not ask for or award independent Gurmukhi writing.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Say the meanings aloud in order: likhṇā / laiṇā / puchhṇā.
[question — say your answer, then pause 40 seconds]
Match each old cue: scratch; labhate with a consonant lost on the way; the prach ask-family.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
From meaning alone, say “write; take; ask.”


Lesson 4 of 4.
Help and like return at R4 — retrieve help and the dative liking frame at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 6 seconds]
Say madad karnā, “to help.” Then say the already-known line mainū̃ panjābī pasand hai, “I like Punjabi.”

Guided Practice.
Liking keeps the liker in the dative.
Read ਮਦਦ ਕਰਨਾ, ਮੈਨੂੰ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ once. Romanization records speech retrieval only and earns no Gurmukhi writing credit.
[question — say your answer, then pause 40 seconds]
Take apart madad karnā aloud: what does the whole phrase mean, where did madad come from, and what productive pattern does karnā make?
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
In mainū̃ panjābī pasand hai, identify the liker form and say why pasand does not behave like an ordinary conjugated verb. Then recall its old look-good image.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say “to help” and “I like Punjabi” once more without looking.
