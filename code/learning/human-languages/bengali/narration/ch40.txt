Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.

Reading.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
ভাত চা জল দুধ ভাই বোন.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and look at the top of each word rather than at the letters.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Bengali hangs its letters from a line instead of standing them on one. That line runs unbroken across a whole word and stops at its edge.
[pause 2 seconds]
So the line is a word boundary you can see before you have read anything. A Bengali word looks like one object first and a row of signs second, which is the opposite of how you learned to write it.
[pause 2 seconds]
ভাই and বোন are the shortest pair here and the easiest to tell apart at a glance, for exactly that reason.

Lesson 2 of 4.

Warm-up.

Reading.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
নমস্কার। ধন্যবাদ। হ্যাঁ। না। দয়া করে। স্বাগতম।.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the six lines down once, without stopping]
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the six lines again, and notice the mark at the end of each line]
It is not a full stop but a দাঁড়ি, a single upright stroke.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
নমস্কার is a bow written down: namas plus kara, the making of a bow. The commonest word in the language is a gesture that stopped being made.
[pause 2 seconds]
দয়া করে is two words and means having done kindness. Bengali asks for things by describing what the other person has already generously done, which is a habit worth noticing on the page where it sits complete.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and four of them are a single word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which line is the polite request?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What ends a Bengali sentence? (A দাঁড়ি — one upright stroke.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What is নমস্কার, literally? (The making of a bow.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
(reading) — the first passage.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now thirteen of them together.
[pause 1 second]
Thirty-six words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
নমস্কার। আমার নাম রাম। আমি ভালো। এটা আমার ভাই। আমার পরিবার ভালো। আমি ভাত আর দুধ খাই। চা বা জল। আমি পড়ি। আমার বন্ধু এখানে। জামা লাল, কিন্তু কাপড় নীল। প্রথম আর দ্বিতীয়। হ্যাঁ, ভালো। ধন্যবাদ।.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage once without stopping; do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive]
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage again and find the three joining words]

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
আর adds. বা offers a choice. কিন্তু turns the sentence.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two of those are inherited and the third is not: কিন্তু came through Persian, which is why it does not sound like its neighbours. You can hear the borrowing without being told about it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice also that the verb is last in every line that has one. আমি ভাত আর দুধ খাই — the eating comes after everything eaten, which is where Bengali always puts it.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
What colour is the shirt?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Where does a Bengali verb go? (Last.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of the three joiners is borrowed? (কিন্তু, through Persian.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Your first complete A1 writing paper — a complete 20-minute A1 form and named-reader message under assessment conditions.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Warm-up.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
This is an attempt, not a model. You will write the answers, and this page will not show you any copyable Bengali answer.
Put away romanization, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers. Set one timer for 20 minutes. Keep it running through both tasks. Write in Bengali script. Ordinary legible handwriting is enough; decorative calligraphy is not scored.

Writing — timed assessment production.
Task 1 — practical form: 7 minutes, 40 points.
Complete all six fields in Bengali script for a new neighbourhood activity group. Short answers are enough.
Your name.
Where you live or stay.
A language you speak.
One everyday action or kind of work you do.
One drink or food you like.
One person in your family.
At seven minutes, move on even if a field is unfinished. Do not restart the timer and do not polish the form with time meant for the message.
Task 2 — message: 13 minutes, 60 points.
Reader: Mira. Purpose: help Mira recognise you and arrange your first meeting tomorrow.
Write 30–40 words in Bengali script. Greet Mira, introduce yourself, say where you live and which language you speak, mention one everyday action or kind of work and one drink or food you like, ask where Mira is, and close politely. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose and connect the Bengali yourself.
When the 20-minute timer rings, stop, even if a line is unfinished. Record the number of completed form fields and the message word count. An honest timed attempt is more useful than a perfect untimed one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Only after you have stopped, review the same attempt. Do not rewrite it yet. Mark one strength and one next step under each assessment criterion:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — six fields are attempted; the named reader, meeting purpose, and requested message points are present.
Comprehensibility and organisation — Mira can follow the message and see how its ideas connect.
Bengali vocabulary, grammar, and register — familiar words and sentence patterns carry the intended meaning in a suitable everyday tone.
Bengali orthographic control — headline strokes, hasanta, vowel-sign placement, word spacing, and punctuation remain readable.
[pause 2 seconds]
Keep the paper. On another day, repeat the same two-task shape with new personal details. The goal is not to memorise this prompt; it is to make a complete Bengali attempt calmly inside the real limit.
