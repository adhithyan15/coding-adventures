Mandarin Chinese, chapter 105: In the Bathroom.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
máojīn — towel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for paper, then the Mandarin for dictionary.

You'll want to know: máojīn.
máojīn — "towel".
wǒ de máojīn hěn gānjìng — My towel is clean.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
máojīn is built from máo and jīn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, maw and kin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as hair and fur and explains the second as a cloth worn on the head. How the pair came to mean a towel is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 5 of 5.
jìngzi — mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for toothpaste, then the Mandarin for soap.

You'll want to know: jìngzi.
jìngzi — "mirror".
xǐshǒujiān lǐ yǒu jìngzi ma — Is there a mirror in the toilets?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìngzi is built from jìng 'mirror' and the noun ending zi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaengH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary's entry tells of a mirror sent as a gift to a king of old.
