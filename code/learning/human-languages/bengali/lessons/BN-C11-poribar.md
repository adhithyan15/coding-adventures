# পরিবার (pôribār) — "family," what surrounds you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C11-BONDHU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Last lesson named one person you are bound to. This one names the
whole group.

## You'll want to know: পরিবার
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C11-PORIBAR-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **পরিবার** — *pôribār* — **family**

**আমার পরিবার** (*āmār pôribār*) — my family.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**পরিবার** is **প** (*pô*) + **রি** (*ri*) + **বা** (*bā*) + **র** (*r*,
silent at the end, a bare consonant with nothing written after it). Four
syllables in the spelling, three said aloud: *pô-ri-bār*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**পরিবার** is Sanskrit **পরিবার** (*parivāra*), a second word Bengali kept
whole from Sanskrit rather than letting it wear down — like বন্ধু, another
tatsama. It is built from **pari-**, "around, about" (PIE **\*peri-**, the
same prefix English itself borrowed for **peri-meter** and **peri-phery**),
plus **√বৃ** (*√vṛ*, "to cover, to surround," PIE **\*h₂wer-**). That root's
secure cousins are Latin *operiō*, "to cover," and Lithuanian *suverti*, "to
shut" — no English word is claimed here, since none is secure. Put the pieces
together and a পরিবার is, at the root, **what stands around you and covers
you**.
