# अस्मान् (asmān) — us, several people, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C261-AVAM-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C261-DUAL-OBJECTS-03, SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C259-PLURAL-POSSESSORS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] “Us two” is **आवाम्** (*āvām*). “Our” for several possessors is
**अस्माकम्** (*asmākam*). Keep the **asm-** opening and make several people
the object.

## You'll want to know: अस्मान्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C262-ASMAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**अस्मान्** (*asmān*) means “us” when several people including the speaker are
the object.

> **सः अस्मान् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ asmān paśyati.*
>
> **He sees us.**

Whitney §491 lists **अस्मान्** as the independent accusative plural. Do not
confuse it with **अस्माकम्**, “our / of us”: **asmān** is a person reached by
the action; **asmākam** answers “whose?”.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **अस्मान्** comes from the first-person pronoun
paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit
Grammar* §491 records it as the independent accusative plural. This lesson
makes no deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
