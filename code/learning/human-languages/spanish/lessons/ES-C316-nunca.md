## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C316-ANSW-12]; assesses=[] -->

**nunca** — "never."

Latin **numquam** is two words fused together: *ne* "not" and *umquam* "ever."
Not-ever.

English **never** is also two words fused together: Old English *ne* "not" and
*æfre* "ever." Not-ever.

These two are not related. Latin and Germanic built them separately, out of
their own parts, in the same order, to the same shape. When two languages reach
the same construction by the same route without either copying the other, it
usually means the route was the obvious one — and "not ever" is about as obvious
as an idea gets.

*Nunca* does not change. No *-o*, no *-a*, nothing to agree with. It sits in
front of a verb and negates it, and that is the whole of its job.
