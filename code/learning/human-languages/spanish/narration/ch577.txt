Spanish, chapter 577: Defender una idea.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
argumentar — to argue, to give reasons.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the conviction, then the Spanish for the stance.

You'll want to know: argumentar.
argumentar — "to argue, to give reasons".
Tienes que argumentar tu respuesta. You have to give reasons for your answer.
To argue a point with reasons, not to quarrel: a quarrel is discutir or pelearse.

The word, taken apart.
argumentar is built on argumento, a learned borrowing from Latin argumentum 'proof', from arguere 'to make clear, to prove' (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: la objeción.

Poner una objeción — to raise an objection.

objeción is a learned borrowing from Latin obiectio 'a throwing against, an objection', built on obicere, from ob and iacere 'to throw' (EtymDB).

convencido is built on convencer, inherited from Latin convincere (EtymDB) 'to overcome, to prove wrong', con + vincere 'to conquer' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
razonable — reasonable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the objection, then the Spanish for convinced.

You'll want to know: razonable.
razonable — "reasonable".
Es un precio razonable. It is a reasonable price.
The same for both genders: una idea razonable.

The word, taken apart.
razonable is inherited from Late Latin rationabilis, built on ratio 'reckoning, reason', the word that also gave razón (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: lógico.
lógico — "logical; natural, to be expected".
Es lógico que estés cansado. It is natural that you are tired.
Es lógico que + subjunctive: it makes sense that...

The word, taken apart.
lógico is a learned borrowing from Latin logicus, taken from Greek logikos 'of reasoning', built on logos 'word, reason' (EtymDB).
