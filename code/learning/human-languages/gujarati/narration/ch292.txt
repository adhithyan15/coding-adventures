Lesson 1 of 6.
ઠગ (ṭhag) — a swindler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a prisoner, then the Gujarati for to set free.

You'll want to know: ઠગ.
ઠગ — ṭhag — "a swindler".
એક ઠગે ગામના લોકોને નકલી નોટો આપી. ek ṭhage gāmnā lokone naklī noṭo āpī. A swindler gave the villagers fake banknotes.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
ઠગ (ṭhag) comes from ṭhagg, "to cheat", a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati noun (CDIAL 5489). English thug was taken from this family of words.

A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
કૌભાંડ (kaubhā̃ḍ) No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled. It is a large fraud, usually one with money and people in high places in it.

લાંચ — lā̃c — "a bribe".
એક સિપાઈએ ઠગ પાસેથી લાંચ લીધી. ek sipāīe ṭhag pāsethī lā̃c līdhī. A constable took a bribe from the swindler.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
લાંચ (lā̃c) goes back to Sanskrit lañcā, "a present, a bribe", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati noun (CDIAL 10914). It is feminine: લાંચ લીધી, took a bribe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a scam, then the Gujarati for a bribe.

You'll want to know: જાસૂસ.
જાસૂસ — jāsūs — "a detective; a spy".
જાસૂસે કહ્યું કે જો ઠગ હજી શહેરમાં છે તો પોલીસ તેને પકડશે. jāsūse kahyũ ke jo ṭhag hajī śahermā̃ che to polīs tene pakaḍśe. The detective said that if the swindler was still in the city, the police would catch him.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
જાસૂસ (jāsūs) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic jāsūs, "a spy". In stories it is the detective who works out who did it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ઝડપવું (jhaḍapvũ) — to nab.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a bribe, then the Gujarati for a detective.

You'll want to know: ઝડપવું.
ઝડપવું — jhaḍapvũ — "to nab".
છેવટે પોલીસે ઠગને સ્ટેશન પર ઝડપ્યો. chevṭe polīse ṭhagne sṭeśan par jhaḍapyo. In the end the police nabbed the swindler at the station.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ઠગને ઝડપ્યો, teṇe ṭhagne jhaḍapyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઝડપવું (jhaḍapvũ) comes from jhaṭ, a form CDIAL reconstructs for a quick, sudden movement; CDIAL lists Gujarati jhaṛapvũ, "to snatch" (CDIAL 5327). In news reports it is what the police do to a wanted man: they grab him fast.
