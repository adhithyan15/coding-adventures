Gujarati, chapter 69: Things That Shine, Fly and Swim.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
તારો (tāro) — a star.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a monkey, then the Gujarati for a snake.

You'll want to know: તારો.
તારો (tāro) — "a star". Out at રાત.

The word, taken apart.
તારો (tāro) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it under tāraka, "belonging to the stars", a form beside Sanskrit tārā, "fixed star" (CDIAL 5798).

What you've built.
One more word for things that shine, fly and swim.

The word, taken apart.
મોર (mor) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it under mora, a form beside Sanskrit mayūra, "peacock" (CDIAL 9865). It reports that some scholars take the Sanskrit word from Dravidian, others from Austroasiatic, and H. W. Bailey from Iranian.
