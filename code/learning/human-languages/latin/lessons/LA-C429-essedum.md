## You'll want to know: essedum, essedī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C429-CARRUS-03]; assesses=[] -->

**essedum, essedī** — "a two-wheeled chariot; a light travelling carriage".

> Dīves essedō celerī ad vīllam vehitur.
>
> *The rich man rides to his country house in a fast two-wheeled carriage.*

Second declension, neuter: *essedum, essedī n.* First a war chariot of northern peoples, later a smart carriage in the city.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*essedum* names the war chariot of the Gauls and Britons (Lewis & Short, under *essedārius*), later a travelling carriage. It is usually thought to be a Gaulish word, but neither Lewis & Short nor EtymDB states an origin, so the line is hedged.
