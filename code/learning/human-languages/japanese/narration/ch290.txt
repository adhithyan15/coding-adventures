Japanese, chapter 290: A New Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
うつる (utsuru) — to move (house); to move over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to invite formally, then the Japanese for a married couple.

You'll want to know: うつる.
うつる — utsuru — "to move (house); to move over".
かぞくで いなかに うつりました。 — kazoku de inaka ni utsurimashita — "Our family moved to the country."
Polite うつります, utsurimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが となりの まちに うつりました。, ani ga tonari no machi ni utsurimashita.

The word, taken apart.
うつる (utsuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
しんこん (shinkon) — newly married.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a married couple, then the Japanese for to move (house).

You'll want to know: しんこん.
しんこん — shinkon — "newly married".
ふたりは しんこんです。 — futari wa shinkon desu — "The two are newly married."

The word, taken apart.
しんこん (shinkon) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shin, "new" (Middle Chinese sin) and kon, "marriage" (xwon) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
