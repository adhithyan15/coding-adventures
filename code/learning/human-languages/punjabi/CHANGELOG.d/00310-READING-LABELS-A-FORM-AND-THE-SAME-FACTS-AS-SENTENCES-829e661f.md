## Reading — labels, a form, and the same facts as sentences

Chapter 45 is Punjabi's reading rung, and it is shaped around the one thing this
track has taught more thoroughly than any other: the form.

Three lessons, ramping 6 -> 13 -> 43 tokens. Six labels read cold with no blank
after them; a form somebody else filled in; then the same six facts as
sentences.

- **No new word in any of the three.** Every Gurmukhi token was checked to occur
  in a lesson with a lower sequence number. Four drafted words -- ਮੇਰੀ, ਵਿੱਚ,
  ਰਹਿੰਦਾ, ਚੰਗੀ -- and later ਕਰਦਾ, ਬੋਲਦੇ, ਆਵੇਗਾ, ਵੀ, ਉਹਦਾ and ਆਵੇ were cut for
  failing that check. Punjabi has no free word for "in" and no verb "to live";
  it has a residence FIELD. The passage says only what the track has taught.
- The first draft used ਨਾਮ for the name label. Punjabi uses **ਨਾਂ**, and its
  practice names are ਅਮਨ and ਮਨਨ, not the ਮੀਰਾ the Indic tracks share. Both were
  caught by the check rather than by reading.
- The middle lesson is the track's own strength turned around. Filling a form,
  the label is the part you ignore; reading one, the label is the whole message.
  The paragraph then says the same six facts with ਹੈ said out loud, which is
  exactly what the colon was standing in for.
- The rung moves pre-A1 reading 0/3 -> 3/3. A1 stays 0/3: 43 words against a
  70-word part, which is the honest reading of a track whose content vocabulary
  is still mostly form values.
- Reinforcement moves in both directions and both are recorded: R4 misses fall
  by five, because the reading lessons put labels, joiners and pronouns taught
  long ago back in front of the reader at a long interval. R2 and R3 rise by
  four, because the three new reading skills arrive in the last chapter with
  nothing after them yet. That residue is chapter 46's work.
- The session map was extended to 270 rows, and its heading corrected: it still
  said "Chapters 1-36" while the track had reached 44.
