# नमस्कार (namaskār) — the more formal bow

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The dressier sibling of *namaste* — same root, one new piece, and
two new letters.

## You'll want to know first

- [नमस्ते](./HI-C01-namaste.md) — you already know **न म स**, the *halant*,
  and how consonants carry "a."

## The letters in this word

Two new consonants, and one new vowel sign:

- **क** = "ka" · **र** = "ra."
- **ा** is the *mātrā* for long **"ā"**: **क + ा → का** = "kā."

So the tail of the word, **कार**, reads **kā-r** (the final **र** here has its
inherent *a* softened) — *kār*. And **नमस्** you can already read (*namas*,
with the *halant* on स). Left to right: **न·म·स्·का·र** →

> **नमस्कार** = **namaskār**

## The word, taken apart

**नमस्कार** = **नमः** (*namaḥ*, "a bow," from root **नम्** *nam*, as in
*namaste*) + **कार** (*kāra*, "the making or doing of" — from the Sanskrit root
**कृ** *kṛ*, "to do, make"). So *namaskār* is literally **"the making of a
bow," "the act of homage."** Where *namaste* says "bow to *you*," *namaskār*
names the bow itself — a touch more formal and ceremonial.

That piece **कार** (*-kāra*, "the making/doing of") is a Sanskrit building
block you'll see again — e.g. in *ahaṃkāra* ("the making of 'I'" → ego). Learn
it once as "the act of ___."

## Why it's said this way

*namaskār* is a notch more **formal** than *namaste* — common in respectful,
official, or ceremonial settings, and as a courteous greeting to elders or in
writing. Both are "hello"; *namaskār* wears a tie.
