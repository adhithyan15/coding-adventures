Arabic, chapter 544: Instruments.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
عود (ʿūd) — an oud, a lute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a composer, then the Arabic for a choir.

You'll want to know: عود.
عود — ʿūd — "an oud, a lute".
يعزف جدي على العود. yaʿzifu jaddī ʿalā al-ʿūd. My grandfather plays the oud.
Plural: أعواد, aʿwād.
It also means a stick of wood.

The word, taken apart.
عود (ʿūd) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-w-d, "to return; wood" (Lane). The ʿūd is the instrument of wood; English lute goes back to al-ʿūd (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
كمان (kamān) — a violin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a choir, then the Arabic for an oud.

You'll want to know: كمان.
كمان — kamān — "a violin".
تتعلم أختي الكمان في المدرسة. tataʿallamu ukhtī al-kamān fī al-madrasa. My sister is learning the violin at school.

The word, taken apart.
كمان (kamān) was borrowed into Arabic and is usually traced to Persian kamān, "a bow": the violin is the instrument played with a bow.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a violin, then the Arabic for a drum.

You'll want to know: ناي.
ناي — nāy — "a reed flute".
سمعنا صوت الناي في الليل. samiʿnā ṣawt an-nāy fī al-layl. We heard the sound of the reed flute in the night.

The word, taken apart.
ناي (nāy) was borrowed from Persian nāy, "a pipe, a flute", whose Middle Persian ancestor also meant a reed (EtymDB): the flute is a hollow reed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
فرقة (firqa) — a band, a troupe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a drum, then the Arabic for a reed flute.

You'll want to know: فرقة.
فرقة — firqa — "a band, a troupe".
غنت فرقة موسيقية في الحفلة. ghannat firqa mūsīqiyya fī al-ḥafla. A band sang at the party.
Plural: فرق, firaq.
It names any group: فرقة (firqa) مسرحية, a theatre troupe.

The word, taken apart.
فرقة (firqa) is built on the native Arabic root f-r-q, "to separate, to divide" (Lane). Farq, a difference, which you know, is from the same root: a firqa is a part set apart.
