Gujarati, chapter 440: Streams and Canals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઝરણું (jharṇũ) — a mountain stream; a spring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a pebble, then the Gujarati for to echo.

You'll want to know: ઝરણું.
ઝરણું — jharṇũ — "a mountain stream; a spring".
પર્વત પરથી ઠંડું ઝરણું વહે છે. parvat parthī ṭhanḍũ jharṇũ vahe che. A cold stream flows down the mountain.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઝરણું (jharṇũ) is built on ઝરવું, to ooze, to trickle, which CDIAL lists under jharati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5346): water that trickles out of the rock.

Lesson 2 of 5.
પ્રવાહ (pravāh) — a current; a flow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to echo, then the Gujarati for a mountain stream.

You'll want to know: પ્રવાહ.
પ્રવાહ — pravāh — "a current; a flow".
ચોમાસામાં નદીનો પ્રવાહ ઘણો ઝડપી છે. comāsāmā̃ nadīno pravāh ghaṇo jhaḍpī che. In the monsoon the river's current is very fast.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પ્રવાહ (pravāh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit pravāha, "a stream, a current" (Monier-Williams), built on vah, to carry: what carries things along.

Lesson 3 of 5.
સંગમ (sangam) — a confluence (of rivers).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a mountain stream, then the Gujarati for a current.

You'll want to know: સંગમ.
સંગમ — sangam — "a confluence (of rivers)".
બે નદીઓના સંગમ પર એક મંદિર છે. be nadīonā sangam par ek mandir che. There is a temple where the two rivers meet.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંગમ (sangam) is a tatsama, Sanskrit saṁgama, "a coming together, a meeting" (Monier-Williams); a place where rivers meet is often a holy place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
નહેર (naher) — a canal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a current, then the Gujarati for a confluence.

You'll want to know: નહેર.
નહેર — naher — "a canal".
નહેરનું પાણી ખેતરો સુધી પહોંચે છે. nahernũ pāṇī khetro sudhī pahõce che. The canal water reaches the fields.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
નહેર (naher) is usually traced to Arabic nahr, "a river, a stream", which came through Persian: a river dug by hand.

Lesson 5 of 5.
વહાવવું (vahāvvũ) — to let flow (water); to channel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a confluence, then the Gujarati for a canal.

You'll want to know: વહાવવું.
વહાવવું — vahāvvũ — "to let flow (water); to channel".
અમે નહેરનું પાણી ખેતરમાં વહાવ્યું. ame nahernũ pāṇī khetarmā̃ vahāvyũ. We let the canal water flow into the field.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે પાણી વહાવ્યું, teṇe pāṇī vahāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
વહાવવું (vahāvvũ) is the causative partner of વહેવું, "to flow", a tadbhava of Sanskrit vahati, "carries" (CDIAL 11453): to make water flow.
