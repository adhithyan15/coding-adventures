Lesson 1 of 6.
el convenio — the agreement; the collective agreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the training, then the Spanish for the profile.

You'll want to know: el convenio.
el convenio — "the agreement; the collective agreement".
El convenio sube los sueldos un dos por ciento. The collective agreement raises wages by two per cent.
El convenio sets pay and hours for everyone in one kind of work.

The word, taken apart.
convenio comes from convenir, inherited from Latin convenire 'to come together, to agree' (EtymDB), built on venire 'to come'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
el autónomo — the self-employed worker; autonomous.

You'll want to know: el autónomo.
el autónomo — "the self-employed worker; autonomous".
Mi hermana trabaja como autónoma. My sister is self-employed.
In Spain ser autónomo is to be self-employed; much of Latin America says trabajador independiente.

The word, taken apart.
autónomo is a learned borrowing from Greek autonomos 'having its own laws', built from autos 'self' and nomos 'custom, law' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 6.
el teletrabajo — remote work, working from home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the self-employed worker, then the Spanish for the civil servant.

You'll want to know: el teletrabajo.
el teletrabajo — "remote work, working from home".
Hago teletrabajo dos días a la semana. I work from home two days a week.
The verb is teletrabajar.

The word, taken apart.
teletrabajo is built on Greek tele 'far' and trabajo. Trabajo is usually traced to Vulgar Latin tripālium, a device for torture (EtymDB), so the word for work first meant suffering.
