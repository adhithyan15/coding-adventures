Russian, chapter 35: Son, Daughter, Grandmother, Husband, Wife.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
сын (syn) — a son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for sadness, then the Russian for tiredness.

You'll want to know: сын.
сын (syn) — "a son".

The word, taken apart.
сын (syn) from Proto-Slavic synŭ — inherited from PIE suHnús, "son". Vasmer lists Lithuanian sūnùs, Sanskrit sūnú- and Gothic sunus, so English son is a cousin, and he links the word with a Sanskrit verb "to give birth".

Lesson 3 of 5.
бабушка (bábushka) — a grandmother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a son, then the Russian for a daughter.

You'll want to know: бабушка.
бабушка (bábushka) — "a grandmother".

The word, taken apart.
бабушка (bábushka) is баба, "old woman, granny", with -ушка, a fond diminutive. баба is a nursery word, says Vasmer, and Lithuanian bóba, "old woman", is the same kind of word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
муж (muzh) — a husband.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a daughter, then the Russian for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: муж.
муж (muzh) — "a husband".

The word, taken apart.
муж (muzh) from Proto-Slavic mǫžĭ, "a man" — inherited. Vasmer calls it undoubtedly related to Sanskrit mánu-, "man", and Gothic manna, so English man is a cousin. "Husband" grew out of "man".
