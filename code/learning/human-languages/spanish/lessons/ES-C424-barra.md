# la barra — a choice, a street, and a speed limit

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-07] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el guion*, the mark that joins. This one separates — and what
it separates are usually alternatives.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C424-08]; assesses=[] -->

**la barra** — "the slash." A *barra* is a bar or a rod, named for its shape.

Three jobs, all of which turn up on an A1 reading paper:

| use | example | reading |
|---|---|---|
| alternatives | y/o | and, or both |
| abbreviation | c/ Mayor | *calle* Mayor |
| per | km/h | kilometres per hour |

The middle one is the useful surprise: **c/** on a Spanish address is *calle*,
the street. An envelope, a form or a shop sign will print it that way, and it is
unreadable if nobody has told you.

## Grammar Lens: the mark that means "per"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-08, ES-ORTH-C424-07] -->

In a measurement, *la barra* is read aloud as **por**:

> 50 km/h — *cincuenta kilómetros por hora*

It is not silent and it is not spelled out. A listening paper that reads a speed
limit aloud gives you *por* where the page shows a slash.

No spaces around it in any of these uses.
