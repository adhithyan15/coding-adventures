French, chapter 159: Comfortable, Uncomfortable, Polite, Rude, Unpleasant.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
confortable — comfortable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for on the contrary, then the French for although.

You'll want to know: confortable.
confortable — "comfortable".

The word, taken apart.
confortable from English comfortable — borrowed in the 1700s. English had built it on Old French conforter, "to strengthen", from Latin fortis, "strong" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
poli / polie — polite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for comfortable, then the French for uncomfortable.

You'll want to know: poli / polie.
poli / polie — "polite".

The word, taken apart.
poli is the past participle of the verb for "to polish", from Latin polire, "to smooth" (EtymDB 2.1 gives politus, "polished"): good manners are polish.
