French, chapter 493: La première année.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la grossesse — pregnancy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for siblings, then the French for large family.

You'll want to know: la grossesse.
la grossesse — "pregnancy".
Pendant sa grossesse, elle a continué à travailler. During her pregnancy she kept on working.
Être enceinte — to be pregnant.

The word, taken apart.
la grossesse is built on gros "big" with -esse; gros is inherited from Late Latin grossus "thick".

Lesson 2 of 5.
accoucher — to give birth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for large family, then the French for pregnancy.

You'll want to know: accoucher.
accoucher — "to give birth".
Ma sœur a accouché d'un garçon ce matin. My sister gave birth to a boy this morning.
Accoucher de — to give birth to. L'accouchement — the birth.

The word, taken apart.
accoucher comes from Old French acouchier "to lay down, to take to one's bed" (EtymDB), built on coucher, from Latin collocāre "to place", itself built on locus "place" (Lewis & Short).

le nourrisson — infant, small baby.

You'll want to know: le nourrisson.
le nourrisson — "infant, small baby".
Le nourrisson dort presque toute la journée. The infant sleeps almost all day.
Formal: a baby of a few months.

The word, taken apart.
le nourrisson comes from Late Latin nūtrītiō "feeding, nursing", built on nūtrīre "to feed" (Lewis & Short), the source of nourrir.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bercer — to rock (a baby).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for infant, then the French for baby's bottle.

You'll want to know: bercer.
bercer — "to rock (a baby)".
Elle berce le bébé pour l'endormir. She rocks the baby to get it to sleep.
Spelling: nous berçons. Une berceuse — a lullaby.

The word, taken apart.
bercer comes from Old French bercier "to rock" (EtymDB), built on bers "cradle", which is usually traced to a Gaulish word.
