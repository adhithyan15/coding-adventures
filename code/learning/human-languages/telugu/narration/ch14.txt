Telugu, chapter 14: Seasons.
2 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 2.
వసంత ఋతువు, వేసవి, వానాకాలం, శీతాకాలం — the pattern completes itself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Telugu closes out the Dravidian season words the same way Kannada did: native words for summer/rain/cold, and a spring-word borrowed twice over from Sanskrit.

You'll want to know: The words.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
Telugu: వసంత ఋతువు (vasanta ṛtuvu). meaning: "spring". source: both vasanta and ఋతువు ṛtuvu ("season") are Sanskrit, like Kannada's pair.
Telugu: వేసవి (vēsavi). meaning: "summer". source: native Dravidian — cousin of Kannada's bēsige.
Telugu: వానాకాలం (vānākālam). meaning: "rainy season". source: వాన vāna ("rain," native) + kālam ("time," itself Sanskrit-derived but long assimilated).
Telugu: శీతాకాలం (śītākālam). meaning: "winter/cold season". source: śīta ("cold") leans Sanskrit-flavored here, unlike Tamil/Kannada's fully native cold-words.

Why it's said this way: Be honest about the pattern, one more time.
Telugu's vēsavi and Kannada's bēsige are close enough in shape to be genuine cousins — the same native Dravidian summer-word wearing two languages' sound changes. And once again, it's వానాకాలం (vānākālam, the rains) that actually organizes Andhra/Telangana's agricultural year, not a clean four-way Western split — the same honest point its neighbours make.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What's the relationship between Telugu's vēsavi and Kannada's bēsige? (Cousins — the same native Dravidian summer-word.) Which Dravidian languages borrow BOTH halves of their spring-word from Sanskrit (the season-name AND the word for "season" itself)? (Kannada and Telugu — vasanta ṛtu(vu) — unlike Tamil/Malayalam's single vasantham loan plus the long-assimilated kaalam.)

Script you'll notice: గ.
గ — ga.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not g, it is ga.
You already say these, and every one of them has గ (ga) somewhere inside it:
బాగా bāgā — well, fine — and the reply "నేను బాగున్నాను.
నేను తెలుగు మాట్లాడతాను — I speak Telugu.
నాకు తెలుగు వచ్చు nāku telugu vaccu — 'I know Telugu'.
ఒకటి రెండు మూడు నాలుగు ఐదు okaṭi reṇḍu mūḍu nālugu aidu — one to five.

Writing: గ — follow the numbered strip.
Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write గ (ga) yourself — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
