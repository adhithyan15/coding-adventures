Russian, chapter 481: From Shoulder Blade to Heel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
лопатка (lopátka) — a shoulder blade; a small spade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to comfort, then the Russian for consolation.

You'll want to know: лопатка.
лопатка (lopátka) — "a shoulder blade; a small spade".
У меня болит спина под левой лопаткой. U menyá bolít spiná pod lévoy lopátkoy. My back hurts under my left shoulder blade.
Feminine. Also a small spade or a trowel: a diminutive of лопата — a spade.

The word, taken apart.
лопатка (lopátka) is a diminutive of лопата 'spade, shovel', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic lopata (EtymDB); the flat bone was named after the flat blade.

Врач сказал, что у меня здоровый позвоночник. Vrach skazál, chto u menyá zdoróvyy pozvonóchnik. The doctor said that my spine is healthy.

позвоночник (pozvonóchnik) is built on позвонок 'vertebra' with -ник; EtymDB has no entry, and the origin of позвонок is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: бедро.
бедро (bedró) — "a thigh; the hip".
Он упал и ударился бедром. On upál i udárilsya bedróm. He fell and hit his hip.
Neuter; plural бёдра.

The word, taken apart.
бедро (bedró) is inherited from Proto-Slavic bedro, which EtymDB traces back to PIE bʰedʰros.

Новые туфли натёрли мне пятку. Nóvyye túfli natyórli mne pyátku. My new shoes have rubbed my heel.
Feminine. A diminutive in form of the old word пята, now the everyday word.

The word, taken apart.
пятка (pyátka) is a diminutive in form of пята 'heel', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic pęta 'heel' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ладонь (ladón) — a palm (of the hand).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a thigh, then the Russian for a heel.

You'll want to know: ладонь.
ладонь (ladón) — "a palm (of the hand)".
Ребёнок держал бабочку на ладони. Rebyónok derzhál bábochku na ladóni. The child held a butterfly on his palm.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB lists ладонь (ladón) without a source; it is usually traced to older долонь, from Proto-Slavic dolnь. Church Slavonic длань is the same word with -ла-: EtymDB traces длань through Church Slavonic to Proto-Slavic dolnь 'palm'.
