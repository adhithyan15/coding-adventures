Arabic, chapter 132: Coast, Waterfall, Cave, Valley, Hill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ساحل (sāḥil) — a coast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a branch, then the Arabic for an island.

You'll want to know: ساحل.
ساحل — sāḥil — "a coast".

The word, taken apart.
ساحل (sāḥil) is built on the native Arabic root s-ḥ-l, "to scrape, to pare" (Lane). Lane gives sāḥil as the shore; Swahili comes from its plural sawāḥil, "coasts".

Lesson 3 of 5.
كهف (kahf) — a cave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a coast, then the Arabic for a waterfall.

You'll want to know: كهف.
كهف — kahf — "a cave".

The word, taken apart.
كهف (kahf), "a cave", is an old native noun built on the letters k-h-f. Lane has no article on this root, but his sources use kahf to explain ghār, a cavern in a mountain.

تل (tall), "a hill, a mound", is an old native noun, filed under the root t-l-l (Lane). English tell, an archaeologist's mound, comes from it; Hebrew tel is a cousin.
