German, chapter 362: Freiwillig helfen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Ehrenamt — voluntary work, an unpaid post.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to integrate, then the German for violence.

You'll want to know: das Ehrenamt.
das Ehrenamt — "voluntary work, an unpaid post".
Neben dem Beruf hat sie ein Ehrenamt im Sportverein. Besides her job she has an unpaid post at the sports club.
Plural: die Ehrenämter. Literally an office of honour.

The word, taken apart.
Ehrenamt joins Ehre "honour", Old High German ēra (EtymDB), to Amt "office", from Middle High German ambahte (EtymDB), a word that goes back to Proto-Germanic ambahtaz "follower, servant".

Lesson 4 of 5.
spenden — to donate, to give.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for unpaid, then the German for voluntary.

You'll want to know: spenden.
spenden — "to donate, to give".
Jedes Jahr spenden wir Geld für Kinder in Not. Every year we donate money for children in need.
Blut spenden — to give blood. Die Spende — the donation.

The word, taken apart.
spenden goes back to Middle High German spenden and Old High German spentōn "to spend, to use" (EtymDB). It is usually traced to Medieval Latin spendere, from expendere "to weigh out, to pay out" (Lewis & Short); English spend is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
engagieren — to get involved (mostly reflexive, sich engagieren); to hire (an artist).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for voluntary, then the German for to donate.

You'll want to know: engagieren.
engagieren — "to get involved (mostly reflexive, sich engagieren); to hire (an artist)".
Sie engagiert sich seit Jahren für den Tierschutz. She has been involved in animal protection for years.
Mostly reflexive: sich für etwas engagieren. Said the French way: an-ga-ZHEE-ren. Einen Musiker engagieren — to hire a musician.

The word, taken apart.
engagieren was borrowed from French engager "to pledge, to bind" (EtymDB), built on gage "a pledge", which is usually traced to a Frankish word; EtymDB lists Old High German wetti "a pledge, a security" as a cousin.
