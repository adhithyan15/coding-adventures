# سلام (salām) — "peace," and the most famous root in the language

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] If you learn one Arabic root, learn this one. From three letters —
س ل م — come *peace*, *Islam*, *Muslim*, the greeting *salaam*, and even the
Hebrew *shalom*. It is the root under the whole culture's word for hello.

## You'll want to know first

- [the root system](./AR-C02-root-system.md)

## Sounds you'll need

- `rtl` — read right to left: س ل م → *s-l-m*, *sa-LĀM*.
- The three letters here are all "light" — no throaty or emphatic sounds.

## The word, taken apart

**سلام** (*salām*) — "peace." Root: **س–ل–م** (**s–l–m**), the idea of being
**whole, safe, sound, at peace**. This root blooms into an enormous family:

- **salām** (سلام) — peace
- **salima** — to be safe, sound
- **islām** (إسلام) — literally "submission [to God]," from the same *s-l-m*:
  entering a state of peace/safety through submission
- **muslim** (مسلم) — "one who submits" (the *mu---i-* pattern = "one who
  does ___")
- **sallama** — to greet, to hand over safely (to *give* someone peace)

And it reaches beyond Arabic: Hebrew **shalom** is the *same* Semitic root
(s-l-m), also "peace." When an Arabic speaker and a Hebrew speaker greet each
other, they are — etymologically — saying the identical word.

> English mostly meets this root as a borrowing: **salaam** (a deep greeting),
> and the given name **Salīm/Selim** ("safe, sound"). It's not a Latin cousin
> — it's a window into the Semitic family, parallel to how Latin roots opened
> the Romance languages.
