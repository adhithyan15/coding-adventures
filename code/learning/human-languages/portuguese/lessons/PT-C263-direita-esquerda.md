# à direita, à esquerda — following the answer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-DESCULPE-01, PT-GRAMMAR-COMO-SE-VAI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Stop a stranger and ask the way to the station. (*Desculpe, como
se vai para a estação?*)

[PAUSE 1s] Asking was the easy half. Now the answer comes back, fast.

## You'll want to know: à direita, à esquerda
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-DIREITA-ESQUERDA-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C89-LUGARES89-01, PT-LEX-PRIMEIRO-02] -->

**à direita** — "on the right, to the right". **à esquerda** — "on the left,
to the left". With **em frente**, "straight ahead", which you already have,
that is every direction a street can send you.

> *A estação é à direita.* — The station is on the right.
> *A primeira à esquerda.* — The first on the left.

That second one is a whole answer in four words: *a primeira* agrees with *a
rua*, the street it leaves unsaid. The accent on **à** marks two words in one,
*a*, "to", and *a*, "the", run together.

## Grammar Lens: siga em frente, vire à direita
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-VIRE-A-DIREITA-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C142-VERBOS142-03, PT-LEX-C87-LUGARES87-04, PT-LEX-C87-LUGARES87-03, PT-GRAMMAR-DEPOIS-ORDEM-01] -->

A stranger answering a *desculpe* answers politely, and the directions are
instructions:

> **Siga** em frente e **depois vire** à direita no semáforo.
> — Go straight on, and then turn right at the traffic lights.

**vire** is **virar**, "to turn", made a polite instruction. **siga** is the
same for **seguir**, "to go on", the verb inside your *a seguir*. You need to
**understand** these two, not build them yet.

A long answer comes in the order you will walk it, *depois* marking each piece.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**direita** has no parent in EtymDB 2.1. It is usually traced to Latin
*dīrēctus*, "straight, set in a straight line", the participle of *dīrigere*
(Lewis & Short, under *dirigo*).

**esquerda** is the surprise. It comes from Basque *ezker*, "left" (EtymDB
2.1), a language spoken in Iberia before Latin arrived, and so does Spanish *izquierda*. Latin's own word for the left hand,
*sinistra*, lives on in English **sinister**.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-DIREITA-ESQUERDA-01, PT-GRAMMAR-VIRE-A-DIREITA-02, PT-LEX-DESCULPE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which word is "left", and where does it come from? (***Esquerda***
— from **Basque**.) What is *vire*, and from which verb? (A polite "turn", from
***virar***.) What does *a primeira à direita* leave unsaid? (**The street** —
*a primeira* agrees with it.)
