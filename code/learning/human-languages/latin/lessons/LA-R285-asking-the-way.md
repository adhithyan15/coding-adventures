# Review — at the crossroads

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C285-VIA-01, LA-LEX-C285-ITER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a road", then "a journey". (*Via. Iter.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C285-QUA-VIA-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C285-DIRECTIO-01, LA-LEX-C285-ITER-FACIO-02] -->

Three lessons, and you can find your way through a Roman town. The
question: *quaesō, dīc mihi: quā viā ad …?* The answer: *rēctā viā ī*, *ad
dextram*, *ad sinistram*, and *dextrā* or *sinistrā* for where a place
already stands. And the whole of it: *iter faciō*, I am on a journey.

> *— Quaesō, dīc mihi: quā viā ad thermās?*
> *— Prīmum rēctā viā ī. Deinde ad sinistram. Thermae sunt dextrā, iūxtā
> pontem.*

[PAUSE 2s] The rungs of a story, *prīmum* and *deinde*, are the rungs of a
set of directions too.
