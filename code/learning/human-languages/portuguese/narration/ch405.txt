Portuguese, chapter 405: Um pneu furado.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o pneu — the tyre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to fill up, then the Portuguese for to fill to the top.

You'll want to know: o pneu.
o pneu — "the tyre".
O pneu da frente está vazio. The front tyre is flat.
Um pneu furado — a flat tyre, a puncture.

The word, taken apart.
pneu is short for pneumático, a learned borrowing from Latin pneumaticus, from Greek pneumatikós 'of wind or air' (EtymDB): a tyre is full of air.

Lesson 4 of 5.
a jante — the wheel rim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to puncture, then the Portuguese for the puncture.

You'll want to know: a jante.
a jante — "the wheel rim".
A jante ficou riscada quando bati no passeio. The rim got scratched when I hit the kerb.
In Brazil: o aro or a roda.

The word, taken apart.
jante was borrowed from French jante 'wheel rim', which EtymDB traces to Vulgar Latin gambita; the earlier history of that word is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o triângulo — the warning triangle; the triangle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the puncture, then the Portuguese for the wheel rim.

You'll want to know: o triângulo.
o triângulo — "the warning triangle; the triangle".
Ele pôs o triângulo atrás do carro parado. He put the warning triangle behind the stopped car.
In Portugal a car must carry o triângulo and a reflective vest.

The word, taken apart.
triângulo is a learned borrowing from Latin triangulus 'three-cornered', from trēs 'three' and angulus 'corner' (EtymDB).
