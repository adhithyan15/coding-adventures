Russian, chapter 62: Hare, Snake, Hen, Duck, Goat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
заяц (záyats) — a hare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bear, then the Russian for a fox.

You'll want to know: заяц.
заяц (záyats) — "a hare".

The word, taken apart.
заяц (záyats) from Proto-Slavic zajęcĭ — inherited. With a first sense "leaper", it has been compared with Sanskrit háyas, "horse", and jíhīte, "leaps up", and Lithuanian žaĩsti, "to frolic", but Vasmer cites doubts.

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: змея.
змея (zmeyá) — "a snake".

The word, taken apart.
змея (zmeyá) — a taboo name, "the earthly one, the one that crawls on the ground", from the root of земля, "earth" (Vasmer). Albanian makes "caterpillar" from its word for "earth" in the same way.

Lesson 3 of 5.
курица (kúritsa) — a hen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a hare, then the Russian for a snake.

You'll want to know: курица.
курица (kúritsa) — "a hen". It lays the яйцо.

The word, taken apart.
курица (kúritsa) is built on кура, "a hen", and the old кур, "a cock". Vasmer relates кур to Latvian kaurêt, "to roar", and Sanskrit kāuti, "cries out": the cock as the crier.

Lesson 4 of 5.
утка (útka) — a duck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a snake, then the Russian for a hen.

You'll want to know: утка.
утка (útka) — "a duck".

The word, taken apart.
утка (útka) is old утица, from Proto-Slavic ǫty, "duck" — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian ántis, Latin anas, Old High German anut and Greek nêssa, all "duck". The утка (útka) that means a false news story copies French canard, Vasmer notes.

You'll want to know: коза.

The word, taken apart.
коза (kozá) from Proto-Slavic koza — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Albanian kedh, "kid", and Old English hæcen, "kid", and to Gothic hakuls, "a cloak", since goatskin made cloaks.
