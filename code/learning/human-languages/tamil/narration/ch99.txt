Tamil, chapter 99: Gifts, Welcomes, Apologies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — please drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please come in, then the Tamil for please eat.

You'll want to know: குடியுங்கள்.
குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — "please drink".
தண்ணீர் குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — "please have some water".
Notice தண்ணீர்: it is the thing being drunk, and it carries no -ஐ. A thing can do without the ending that a person must have.

The word, taken apart.
குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) is native Dravidian. DEDR 1654 lists Malayalam kuṭi and Kannada kuḍi. It is kuḍi, "drink", with the polite ending.

What you've built.
Food, and now drink.

Lesson 2 of 5.
அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) — a gift given with affection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please eat, then the Tamil for please drink.

You'll want to know: அன்பளிப்பு.
அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) — "a present", literally "a giving of love".
அன்பு is "love"; அளிப்பு is "a giving". பரிசு can be a prize; அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) is always from the heart.

The word, taken apart.
அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) is native: aṉpu, "affection" (DEDR 330), and aḷippu, "giving", from aḷi, "to show favour, to bestow" (DEDR 301). A gift given out of love.

What you've built.
A second word for gift, with love in it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
நல்வரவு (nalvaravu) — welcome (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please drink, then the Tamil for a present.

You'll want to know: நல்வரவு.
நல்வரவு (nalvaravu) — "welcome", the formal word, literally "good arrival".
You will see it on banners and doorways; வாருங்கள் is what you say aloud.

The word, taken apart.
நல்வரவு (nalvaravu) is native: nal, "good" (DEDR 3610), and varavu, "coming" (DEDR 5270): "a good coming".

What you've built.
The written welcome.

Lesson 4 of 5.
மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள் (maṉṉittuviṭuṅkaḷ) — please forgive me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a present, then the Tamil for a formal welcome.

You'll want to know: மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள்.
மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள் (maṉṉittuviṭuṅkaḷ) — "please forgive me".
It is fuller and more earnest than மன்னிக்கவும்: for a real mistake, not a bumped elbow.

The word, taken apart.
மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள் (maṉṉittuviṭuṅkaḷ) is maṉṉi, "to pardon", with viṭu, "to let go" (DEDR 5393), and the polite ending. The origin of maṉṉi is uncertain: DEDR has no entry for it.

What you've built.
A fuller apology.

Lesson 5 of 5.
கவலைப்படாதீர்கள் (kavalaippaṭātīrkaḷ) — don't worry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a formal welcome, then the Tamil for please forgive me.

You'll want to know: கவலைப்படாதீர்கள்.
கவலைப்படாதீர்கள் (kavalaippaṭātīrkaḷ) — "please don't worry".
It is கவலை, "worry", with படு, "to suffer", and the respectful don't.

The word, taken apart.
கவலைப்படாதீர்கள் (kavalaippaṭātīrkaḷ) is native: kavalai, "worry" (DEDR 1328), and paṭu, "to experience, to undergo" (DEDR 3853), with the polite negative: "do not undergo worry".

What you've built.
Drink, a present, a welcome, forgiveness, and don't worry.
