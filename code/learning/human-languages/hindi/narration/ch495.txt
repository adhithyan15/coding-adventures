Hindi, chapter 495: Eating Well, Keeping Fit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कसरत (kasrat) — exercise; a workout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to warm (a sore part), then the Hindi for to cover someone (with a blanket).

You'll want to know: कसरत.
कसरत — kasrat — "exercise; a workout".
अरुण रोज़ सुबह आधा घंटा कसरत करता है। Aruṇ roz subah ādhā ghaṇṭā kasrat kartā hai. Arun exercises for half an hour every morning.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
कसरत (kasrat) is usually traced to Arabic kathra, "abundance, a great number", which reached Hindi through Persian kasrat. The sense "physical exercise" is the one Hindi and Urdu use; how it grew from "abundance" is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
योग (yog) — yoga.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to cover someone (with a blanket), then the Hindi for exercise.

You'll want to know: योग.
योग — yog — "yoga".
दादी रोज़ योग करती हैं, इसलिए वह चुस्त हैं। dādī roz yog kartī haiṁ, isliye vah cust haiṁ. Grandmother does yoga every day, which is why she is so spry.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
योग (yog) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit yoga, first "the act of yoking, joining" (Monier-Williams); English took yoga from the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पचना (pacnā) — to be digested.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for avoiding certain foods, then the Hindi for nourishing.

You'll want to know: पचना.
पचना — pacnā — "to be digested".
भारी खाना जल्दी नहीं पचता। bhārī khānā jaldī nahīṃ pactā. Heavy food is slow to digest.
Past (it …): वह खाना पच गया, vah khānā pac gayā.

The word, taken apart.
पचना (pacnā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi pācnā, "to cook", and from it the intransitive pacnā, "to be digested", under Sanskrit pacyátē, "is cooked, is digested" (CDIAL 7654). pacānā, "to digest", is its transitive partner.
