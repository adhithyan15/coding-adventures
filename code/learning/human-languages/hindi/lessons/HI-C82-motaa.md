# मोटा — thick, and the word that refuses to be only about people

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **पतला** and its feminine. The word in this lesson is its
opposite, and it ends the same way.

## You'll want to know: मोटा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-03]; assesses=[] -->

**मोटा** (*moṭā*) — "thick, stout, heavy."

Set it against **पतला** and the pair covers the whole range at once:

| thing | पतला | मोटा |
|---|---|---|
| a person | slim | stout |
| a rope | thin | thick |
| a book | slim | fat |
| a voice | reedy | deep |

That last row is the one to keep. A **मोटी आवाज़** is a *deep* voice, not a fat
one — the metaphor runs from the thickness of the string to the lowness of the
note, exactly as it does on any instrument.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**मोटा** is from Sanskrit **मूर्त** by way of Prakrit, carrying the sense of
something **solid, with body to it** — a shape that has taken form rather than
staying an outline.

Hindi extends it to mean **rough or approximate**, which follows from the same
picture: a *मोटा* estimate is one drawn with a thick line rather than a thin
one, so it has no fine detail. English does this with **broadly**, and the
metaphor is identical.

## Grammar Lens: all three of them, and the rule holds
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-03, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| adjective | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| लंबा | लंबा | लंबी |
| पतला | पतला | पतली |
| मोटा | मोटा | मोटी |

Three words, one ending, one behaviour. Against the four from the last chapter —
none of which ended in **-आ** and none of which moved — that is the cleanest
demonstration this book can give you that the ending is the rule.
