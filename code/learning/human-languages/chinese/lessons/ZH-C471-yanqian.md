## You'll want to know: 眼前
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C471-EYES471-02]; assesses=[] -->

**眼前** — *yǎnqián* — "before one's eyes; right now".

> **那个事故发生在我眼前** *nàge shìgù fāshēng zài wǒ yǎnqián* — That accident happened right in front of my eyes.

The new character, in pieces: 眼 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left an eye, five strokes, like 日 *rì* with one bar more; on the right six strokes, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two bars, a vertical that turns up at the foot, a short stroke falling to the left and a long one falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**眼前** (*yǎnqián*) is built from 眼 'eye' and 前 'in front', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ngeanX* and *dzen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the eye' and the second 'before'.
