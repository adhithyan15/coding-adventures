Arabic, chapter 488: Happy, bored, angry and sad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سعادة (saʿāda) — happiness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sentence, then the Arabic for a barber.

You'll want to know: سعادة.
سعادة — saʿāda — "happiness".
شعرت بسعادة كبيرة في العيد. shaʿartu bi-saʿāda kabīra fī al-ʿīd. I felt great happiness at the feast.

The word, taken apart.
سعادة (saʿāda) is built on the native Arabic root s-a-d, "to be lucky, to be happy" (Lane). Saʿāda is the state of being saʿīd, happy, a word you already know from the same root.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ملل (malal) — boredom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a barber, then the Arabic for happiness.

You'll want to know: ملل.
ملل — malal — "boredom".
شعر الطالب بالملل في المحاضرة الطويلة. shaʿara aṭ-ṭālib bi-l-malal fī al-muḥāḍara aṭ-ṭawīla. The student felt bored in the long lecture.
Arabic says شعر بالملل, to feel boredom, where English says to be bored.

The word, taken apart.
ملل (malal) is built on the native Arabic root m-l-l, "to be weary of a thing" (Lane). Malal is the noun of that weariness.

Lesson 3 of 5.
غضب (ghaḍiba) — to get angry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for happiness, then the Arabic for boredom.

You'll want to know: غضب.
غضب — ghaḍiba — "to get angry".
غضب أبي لأن أخي كسر النافذة. ghaḍiba abī li-anna akhī kasara an-nāfidha. My father got angry because my brother broke the window.
Present: يغضب, yaghḍab.
It takes من for the person: غضب (ghaḍiba) من أخيه.

The word, taken apart.
غضب (ghaḍiba) is built on the native Arabic root gh-ḍ-b, "anger, wrath" (Lane). The adjective ghāḍib, angry, and the noun ghaḍab, anger, come from the same root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تنهد (tanahhada) — to sigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to get angry, then the Arabic for to grieve.

You'll want to know: تنهد.
تنهد — tanahhada — "to sigh".
تنهدت أمي وجلست على الكرسي. tanahhadat ummī wa-jalasat ʿalā al-kursī. My mother sighed and sat down on the chair.
Present: يتنهد, yatanahhad.

The word, taken apart.
تنهد (tanahhada) is built on the native Arabic root n-h-d, "to swell, to rise" (Lane). Lane gives Form V tanahhada as to sigh: to let the chest rise with a long breath.
