Lesson 1 of 5.
داوطلب (dâvtalab) — a volunteer; a candidate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for consumption, then the Persian for to consume.

You'll want to know: داوطلب.
داوطلب (dâvtalab) — "a volunteer; a candidate".
صد داوطلب بعد از زلزله کمک کردند. sad dâvtalab ba'd az zelzele komak kardand. A hundred volunteers helped after the earthquake.
Also an exam candidate. Plural dâvtalabân.

The word, taken apart.
داوطلب (dâvtalab) joins dâv "a turn, a stake", a Persian word whose origin is not settled, to talab "seeking", from Arabic ṭalab: someone who seeks a turn.

Lesson 2 of 5.
خیریه (kheyriye) — a charity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to consume, then the Persian for a volunteer.

You'll want to know: خیریه.
خیریه (kheyriye) — "a charity".
او هر ماه به یک خیریه پول میدهد. u har mâh be yek kheyriye pul midahad. She gives money to a charity every month.
From kheyr "good". As an adjective: mo'assese-ye kheyriye, a charitable foundation.

The word, taken apart.
خیریه (kheyriye) is built on kheyr "good", borrowed from Arabic khayr "good, well" (EtymDB), with the ending -iye: a body that does good.

همه مردم صلح میخواهند. hame-ye mardom solh mikhâhand. All people want peace.
The opposite is jang, war. Solh kardan — to make peace.

The word, taken apart.
صلح (solh) was borrowed from Arabic ṣulḥ "peace, reconciliation" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: خشونت.
خشونت (khoshunat) — "violence".
خشونت هیچ مشکلی را حل نمیکند. khoshunat hich moshkeli râ hal nemikonad. Violence does not solve any problem.
Khoshunat-âmiz — violent.

The word, taken apart.
خشونت (khoshunat) was borrowed from Arabic khushūna "roughness, harshness" (EtymDB); in Persian it means violence.

Lesson 5 of 5.
اعتیاد (e'tiyâd) — addiction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for peace, then the Persian for violence.

You'll want to know: اعتیاد.
اعتیاد (e'tiyâd) — "addiction".
اعتیاد به موبایل بین نوجوانها زیاد است. e'tiyâd be mobâyl beyn-e nojavânhâ ziyâd ast. Addiction to mobile phones is common among teenagers.
E'tiyâd be — addiction to. Mo'tâd — addicted.

The word, taken apart.
اعتیاد (e'tiyâd) comes from Arabic i'tiyād "habit, familiarity" (EtymDB); in Persian a habit you cannot leave became an addiction.
