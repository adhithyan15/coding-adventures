Telugu, chapter 98: Nothing, Not Yet, That Will Do.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఏమీ లేదు (ēmī lēdu) — nothing, it's nothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for worry, then the Telugu for alone.

You'll want to know: ఏమీ లేదు.
ఏమీ లేదు (ēmī lēdu) — "nothing", "it's nothing".
The answer to ఏమైంది?, "what happened?", when you would rather not say. It ends on the లేదు you have had since your first chapter.

What you've built.
An answer that closes a question kindly.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ఇంకా లేదు (iṅkā lēdu) — not yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for alone, then the Telugu for nothing.

You'll want to know: ఇంకా లేదు.
ఇంకా లేదు (iṅkā lēdu) — "not yet".
Two words you own, ఇంకా, "still", and లేదు, "there isn't". Have you eaten? — ఇంకా లేదు (iṅkā lēdu).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
సరిపోతుంది (saripōtundi) — that will do, it is enough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for nothing, then the Telugu for not yet.

You'll want to know: సరిపోతుంది.
సరిపోతుంది (saripōtundi) — "that will do", "it is enough", and of clothes, "it fits".
It starts on సరి, "right, even", like సరే and సరిగ్గా.

What you've built.
A third word on the same right-even root.

Lesson 4 of 5.
నిజమే (nijamē) — that's true.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for not yet, then the Telugu for that will do.

You'll want to know: నిజమే.
నిజమే (nijamē) — "that's true", "quite right".
It is నిజం with -ఏ, the ending that says exactly this. Where నిజంగా? asks, నిజమే (nijamē) agrees.

What you've built.
Agreement, from the word for truth.

Lesson 5 of 5.
అదే (adē) — that's the one, the same.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for that will do, then the Telugu for that's true.

You'll want to know: అదే.
అదే (adē) — "that's the one", "the same one".
It is అది, "that", with the same -ఏ. When someone finally names what you meant, అదే (adē)!

What you've built.
Nothing, not yet, that will do, true, that's the one: five ways to close a question.
