Mandarin Chinese, chapter 167: The Day Before Yesterday.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
前天 (qiántiān) — the day before yesterday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tea leaves, then the Mandarin for to make tea.

You'll want to know: 前天.
前天 — qiántiān — "the day before yesterday".
前天是星期一 qiántiān shì xīngqīyī — The day before yesterday was Monday.

The word, taken apart.
前天 (qiántiān) is built from 前 'before' and 天 'day', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzen and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fāshēng is built from two parts inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): pjot, glossed 'to rise' and 'to start', the word in chūfā, and srjaeng, 生 'to be born': to start up and come into being.

Lesson 3 of 6.
没事 (méishì) — it's nothing; to be all right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the day before yesterday, then the Mandarin for to happen.

You'll want to know: 没事.
没事 — méishì — "it's nothing; to be all right".
没事我很好 méishì wǒ hěn hǎo — It's nothing. I'm fine.
The new character, in pieces: 事 (shì) — eight strokes: a horizontal, a small 口, a horizontal that turns down, a long horizontal, a shorter one, and last a vertical with a hook down through them all. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
没事 (méishì) is built from 没 'not to have' and 事 (shì) 'matter', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mwot and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to sink' and the second 'to employ' and 'to set up'; 'a matter' is the everyday sense, not the gloss. Mwot would regularly give mò, not méi.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Lesson 4 of 6.
事 (shì) — a bar, 口, three bars and a long hook.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a bar, 口, three bars and a long hook and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say méishì — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
口 有.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kǒu, yǒu]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a bar, 口, three bars and a long hook.
事 (shì).
One shape, eight strokes, threaded on a long hook.
A horizontal across the top.
Then a small 口, three strokes, as you write it.
Then three bars: a horizontal that turns down at its right end, a long horizontal across, and a shorter one under it.
Last, a vertical with a hook, from the top bar down through everything: the longest stroke.
1 + 3 + 3 + 1 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 事 (shì), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 事 (shì)? (Eight.) Which piece is second? (A small 口.) Which stroke comes last? (The long vertical with the hook.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 事 (shì), pinned stroke paths and medians.
