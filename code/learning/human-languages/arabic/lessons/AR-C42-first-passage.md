# (reading) — the first whole encounter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SKILL-READ-LINES, AR-C01-MARHABA-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, then six lines. Now read one encounter from its first
peace to its last.

[PAUSE 2s] Every form has appeared before. The only new thing is holding all of
them together without a romanized line underneath.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C41-YA-01, AR-LEX-C38-AW-01, AR-CONCEPT-C33-HALIB-01] -->

> السلام عليكم.
> وعليكم السلام.
> ما اسمك؟
> اسمي سامي.
> مرحبًا يا سامي.
> شاي أو حليب؟
> حليب؟
> لا.
> شاي؟
> نعم.
> حسنًا.
> شكرًا.
> مع السلامة.

[PAUSE 4s] Read the whole encounter once. Do not stop to translate a word; let
each turn answer the turn before it.

[PAUSE 4s] Read it again. This time notice the hinge: **شاي أو حليب؟** One
choice becomes two tiny questions, then a clear answer.

## The passage, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING, AR-FUNC-C38-HASANAN-01, AR-CONCEPT-SHUKRAN-21] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A connected passage does not have to be one speaker's paragraph.
Here the connection is the work each line does: greeting invites reply, a name
invites welcome, and **شاي أو حليب؟** opens a choice.

[PAUSE 2s] **لا** closes the first option. **نعم** accepts the second.
**حسنًا** closes the choice, and **شكرًا** acknowledges it. The passage is
connected by meaning before it is connected by grammar.

[PAUSE 2s] The first and last lines share **س-ل-م**. Arabic opens the encounter
with peace and closes it with safety, so the oldest root in the book gives the
whole reading a frame.
