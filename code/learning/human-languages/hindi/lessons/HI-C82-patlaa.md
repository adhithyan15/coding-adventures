# पतला — thin, and a root that fell over

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **लंबा** and its feminine form. The next word has the same
ending, so you already know what it will do.

## You'll want to know: पतला
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02]; assesses=[] -->

**पतला** (*patlā*) — "thin."

It works for a person, for a sheet of paper, and for a soup that has too much
water in it. English needs *thin*, *slim*, *slender* and *watery* for that
range; Hindi uses one word and lets the noun decide which English word you would
have reached for.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**पतला** goes back to Sanskrit **पत्र** and the root **पत्** (*pat*), "to fall."
A thin thing is one that has **fallen flat** — flattened out until there is
hardly anything left of it.

The same root is sitting in two Hindi words you may already recognise:

| word | the falling in it |
|---|---|
| **पत्ता** (*pattā*) | a leaf — the thing that falls |
| *patjhaṛ* | autumn — literally the leaf-fall |

Autumn in Hindi is named exactly the way American English names it: **fall**.
Two languages that never discussed it reached for the same picture.

## Grammar Lens: the second of the three
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| adjective | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| लंबा | लंबा | लंबी |
| पतला | पतला | पतली |

Same ending, same behaviour, no exception to learn. This is what a rule that
actually works looks like: you were told the shape, and every new word that has
the shape does the thing.
