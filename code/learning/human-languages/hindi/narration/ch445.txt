Hindi, chapter 445: Boats, Nets and Shells.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
किश्ती (kiśtī) — a small boat, a skiff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a coast, then the Hindi for a dip.

You'll want to know: किश्ती.
किश्ती — kiśtī — "a small boat, a skiff".
झील में एक छोटी किश्ती तैर रही थी। jhīl meṁ ek choṭī kiśtī tair rahī thī. A little boat was floating on the lake.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
किश्ती (kiśtī) is usually traced to Persian kištī, "a boat, a ship"; songs and poems love it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
जाल (jāl) — a net.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a small boat, then the Hindi for a boatman.

You'll want to know: जाल.
जाल — jāl — "a net".
मल्लाह ने पानी में जाल फेंका। mallāh ne pānī meṁ jāl phẽkā. The boatman cast his net into the water.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
जाल (jāl) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit jāla, "a net" (CDIAL 5213), which lists Hindi jāl, a net for birds or fish; it is a different word from jala, water.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ज्वार (jvār) — the high tide, a flood tide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a boatman, then the Hindi for a net.

You'll want to know: ज्वार.
ज्वार — jvār — "the high tide, a flood tide".
शाम को ज्वार आता है और पानी ऊपर चढ़ता है। śām ko jvār ātā hai aur pānī ūpar caṛhtā hai. In the evening the tide comes in and the water rises.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ज्वार (jvār) goes back to javacāra, "a flood tide", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5167), which lists Hindi jvār. A different word with the same spelling names the millet sorghum.
