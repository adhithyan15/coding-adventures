# حينما (ḥīnamā) — when

## You'll want to know: حينما
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C40-HINAMA-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C28-JAA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C37-PRESENT-I-01] -->

> **حينما جاء، كتب** — *ḥīnamā jāʾa, kataba* — **when he came, he wrote.**

> **أكتب حينما أسمع** — *aktub ḥīnamā asmaʿ* — **I write when I hear.**

The clause it opens can come first or second, and Arabic marks the seam with a
comma when it comes first — which is the same rule English follows and one of the
few places the two languages punctuate alike.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C40-HINAMA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C39-MA-01] -->

**حين** is an ordinary noun meaning **a time, a moment**, and **ما** is the same
general particle you have now met three times: as *what* in chapter two, as the
past-tense negator in chapter thirty-nine, and inside *limādhā* in this chapter.
Stuck onto **حين** it makes *at-the-moment-that*.

Arabic writes another word here, *ʿindamā*, built the same way on a preposition
meaning *at*. It is the commoner of the two in modern prose and it needs a letter
this track's script ladder has not reached, so it is named in romanization and
left for a script lesson. **حينما** costs no new letters at all.
