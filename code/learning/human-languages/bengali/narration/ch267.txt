Bengali, chapter 267: First, Then, Suddenly.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
বাড়ি গিয়ে খেলাম (bāṛi giye khelām) — one action, then the next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was reading when Mira came". (jôkhon āmi bôi pôṛchhilām, tôkhon mīrā elô.) Then the word for "please", দয়া করে: "having done a kindness". Its করে is the form of this lesson.

You'll want to know: the -e form.
আমি স্টেশনে গিয়ে টিকিট কিনলাম। āmi sṭeshône giye ṭikiṭ kinlām. — "I went to the station and bought a ticket."
গিয়ে is যাওয়া in the -e form: "having gone". It finishes one action so the next can start, and the last verb carries the tense for both. Most verbs add -এ to the stem: করা becomes করে (kore), দেখা becomes দেখে (dekhe), বসা becomes বসে (bose). An আ in the stem becomes এ: আসা becomes এসে (ese), নামা becomes নেমে (neme), হাঁটা becomes হেঁটে (hẽṭe). An এ said e, or an ও, rises: কেনা becomes কিনে (kine). The verbs whose stem ends in a vowel take -য়ে: খাওয়া becomes খেয়ে (kheye), নেওয়া becomes নিয়ে (niye), দেওয়া becomes দিয়ে (diye).
The -e form can also say how: হেঁটে গেলাম (hẽṭe gelām), "I went on foot". And it pairs with a second verb that adds a shade rather than a new action: বসে পড়লাম (bose pôṛlām), "I sat down", with পড়া in its other sense, "to fall"; হারিয়ে গেল, "it got lost", which you know.

You'll want to know: one doer.
Both actions belong to the same person: আমি বাড়ি গিয়ে খেলাম (āmi bāṛi giye khelām), "I went home and ate". When someone else does the second thing, Bengali says two sentences: আমি বাড়ি গেলাম। মীরা খেল।.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I got off the bus and walked home" — āmi bās theke neme hẽṭe bāṛi gelām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She ate and went out" — she kheye bāire gelô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We sat and drank tea" — āmrā bose chā khelām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say that you went to the station and bought a ticket, in one sentence.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
যাওয়া in the -e form? (গিয়ে.) খাওয়া? (খেয়ে.) Which verb carries the tense? (The last one.) Can the two actions have two doers? (No: then it is two sentences.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
প্রথমে … তারপর … শেষে (prôthôme … tārpôr … sheshe) — the order of a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I went home and ate". (āmi bāṛi giye khelām.) Then say প্রথমে, "at first", and শেষ, "the end".

You'll want to know: first, then.
প্রথমে আমরা চা খেলাম। তারপর বাজারে গেলাম। prôthôme āmrā chā khelām. tārpôr bājāre gelām. — "First we had tea. After that we went to the market."
প্রথমে, which you know, opens a story. তারপর (tārpôr), "after that, then", is new: তার, "of that", and পর, "after", the word inside পরে. তারপর starts each new step; পরে, "later", says the step came some time after.

You'll want to know: in the end.
শেষে (sheshe) is শেষ with -এ: "at the end, finally". It closes the story.
শেষে আমরা বাড়ি ফিরলাম। sheshe āmrā bāṛi phirlām. — "In the end we came back home."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "First I went to the station by bus" — prôthôme āmi bāse sṭeshône gelām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After that I bought a ticket" — tārpôr ṭikiṭ kinlām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In the end we came back home" — sheshe āmrā bāṛi phirlām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Tell three steps: first you had tea, after that you went to the market, in the end you came home.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What opens a story? (প্রথমে.) What starts each next step? (তারপর.) What closes it? (শেষে.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "first", "after that" and "in the end". (prôthôme, tārpôr, sheshe.)

You'll want to know: হঠাৎ.
হঠাৎ — hôṭhāt — "suddenly".
আমি হাঁটছিলাম, হঠাৎ ঝড় এল। āmi hā̃ṭchhilām, hôṭhāt jhôṛ elô. — "I was walking, and suddenly a storm came."
A story goes প্রথমে, তারপর; হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) is where it turns. It comes before the event that breaks into the scene.

The word, taken apart.
হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) is Sanskrit haṭhāt, "by force, forcibly", the ablative of haṭha, "force" (Monier-Williams). Monier-Williams also gives haṭhāt as "necessarily, inevitably". The Bengali sense, "suddenly", is not among the senses it lists, and the source does not say how the word came to it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: hôṭhāt]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was walking, and suddenly a storm came" — āmi hā̃ṭchhilām, hôṭhāt jhôṛ elô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We were eating; suddenly Mira came" — āmrā khāchchhilām, hôṭhāt mīrā elô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) mean? ("Suddenly".) What comes after it, a scene or an event? (An event, in the plain past.)


Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt), "suddenly". Then read ত (tô) and the hasanta ্, the mark that takes a letter's vowel away.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ৎ.
ৎ — t, the khanda ta, "a piece of ta". It is a ত with no vowel after it. Where a word ends in that bare t, as হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) does, Bengali often writes ৎ instead of ত with a hasanta.
In the print of this book, ৎ (t) has no headline. It is a small closed loop at the top, with a long slanting tail joined to the left side of the loop; the tail's lower end, at the bottom right, is a short upright foot.

Writing: ৎ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ৎ (t) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the small loop, the long tail, the short foot.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ৎ (t) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

[pause 2 seconds]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
গত শনিবার আমি দিদিমার গ্রামে গেলাম। প্রথমে বাসে স্টেশনে গেলাম। তারপর ট্রেনে চড়লাম। ট্রেনে আমি একটা বই পড়ছিলাম। হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) ঝড় এল। ঠিক তখন ট্রেন একটা নদী পার হচ্ছিল। জানালার বাইরে আকাশ কালো ছিল, আর গাছগুলো কাঁপছিল। বিকেলে ট্রেন গ্রামের স্টেশনে থামল। তখন ঝড় ছিল না। আমি ট্রেন থেকে নেমে হেঁটে বাড়ি গেলাম। বাড়ি গিয়ে দেখলাম, দিদিমা রান্না করছিলেন। দাদু বারান্দায় বসে চা খাচ্ছিলেন। সন্ধ্যায় আমরা ভাত আর মাছ খেলাম। তারপর দিদিমা আমাকে একটা পুরনো গল্প বললেন। রাতে আবার ঝড় এল, কিন্তু আমরা ভিতরে ছিলাম। শেষে আমি তাড়াতাড়ি শুলাম। কাল সকালে আমি আবার শহরে ফিরব। দিনটা আমার ভালো লাগল।.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
New forms: দিদিমার, "Grandmother's", with the -র of আমার; শুলাম (shulām), শোয়া in the past, "I lay down to sleep"; দিনটা আমার ভালো লাগল, "the day felt good to me".
[pause 2 seconds]
The scenes take -ছিল-; the events take the plain past, joined with -e forms and kept in order by প্রথমে, তারপর and শেষে. হঠাৎ (hôṭhāt) turns the story; ফিরব is the plan.
