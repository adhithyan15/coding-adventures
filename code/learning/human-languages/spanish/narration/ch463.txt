Spanish, chapter 463: El viaje guiado.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
organizar — to put it together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la agenda and encargar. Somebody has to put the whole thing together, and that is the verb next.

You'll want to know: organizar.
organizar — to organise, to arrange, to put on.
Organizar una excursión — to arrange a trip. Lo organiza el ayuntamiento — the town hall is putting it on. Organízate — get yourself sorted.
La organización is both the act and the body that does it, the same double sense as la instalación.

Grammar Lens: the z that becomes c.
You met this with rechacé. Here it is as a rule you can apply everywhere:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
verb: organizar. I did it: organicé.
verb: empezar. I did it: empecé.
verb: rechazar. I did it: rechacé.
Spanish almost never writes ze — outside a short list of oddities like zeta and zigzag — and in a verb ending it never does. Wherever an ending would put an e after the z, the z becomes a c, and the sound does not change at all, because z and c-before-e are the same sound already.
It is a spelling rule and nothing more, which is why every -zar verb in the language obeys it.

The word, taken apart.
Greek órganon, a tool or an instrument — from érgon, work.
So an organ is a working part, and to organise is to arrange the working parts so they function together. The body's órganos are named the same way: each one is a tool doing a job.
Érgon is worth keeping, because it is in English energy (work within), surgery — through kheirourgía, hand-work — and ergonomic. And the musical organ is the same Greek noun: an instrument, a tool for making sound.
Spanish took the word from Greek by way of Latin, which is why it kept its shape rather than wearing down.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el guía / la guía — the person, and the book.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la guardia and seguir. The first taught you a rule about articles, and the word coming next obeys it.

You'll want to know: el guía / la guía.
el guía — the guide, the person. la guía — the guidebook, and also a woman doing the job.
El guía habla inglés — the guide speaks English. Una guía de Madrid — a guidebook. La guía telefónica — the phone book.
Guiar is the verb: nos guió por el casco viejo — she led us round the old town.

Grammar Lens: the pattern you already met.
The duty-chemist lesson gave you this: the feminine names the thing, the masculine names a person. Here is the set again, with the new one in place:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
feminine: la guardia — the watch. masculine: el guardia — the officer.
feminine: la guía — the guidebook. masculine: el guía — the guide.
feminine: la policía — the force. masculine: el policía — the officer.
La guía complicates it, because a female guide is la guía as well — so the feminine covers both the object and a woman doing the job, and only context separates them. La policía does the same: it is the force, and it is also a policewoman. Llamé a la guía is almost certainly a person; compré la guía is certainly a book.

The word, taken apart.
A Germanic word — and nobody can tell you which one. The usual candidate is a reconstructed witan, to watch over, to show the way, which gave Occitan guidar and from there guiar. The asterisk is the warning you met on tripaliare: inferred, never written down. The Academy prefers a different Gothic form again. All the accounts agree on Germanic, and on watching.
So not one who knows, tempting as English wit makes it — that is the neighbouring branch, knowing and watching being two limbs off one root for seeing. The Germanic w- does surface as gu-, as in guardar and guerra.
Set it against seguir, which is Latin sequi, to follow. A guía knows the way and a follower sigue. Two words for the two ends of the same walk, one Germanic and one Latin — which is the ordinary condition of Spanish vocabulary rather than an oddity.

Lesson 3 of 5.
importar — to matter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say importante and interesar. The first has a verb inside it, and that verb behaves like the second.

You'll want to know: importar.
importar — to matter, to be of concern.
No importa — it doesn't matter. ¿Te importa si abro la ventana? — do you mind if I open the window? Me importa mucho — it matters a lot to me.
It also means to import goods, which is the literal sense, but the everyday one is mattering.

Grammar Lens: it works like gustar and interesar.
The thing that matters is the subject, and the person is an indirect object — the same shape as interesar:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
Spanish: me interesa el arte. word for word: art interests me.
Spanish: me importa el resultado. word for word: the result matters to me.
Spanish: no me importan los horarios. word for word: the timetables don't matter to me.
The verb agrees with the thing, not the person: importan for plural timetables. English puts the person first and Spanish puts the thing, which is why I don't mind comes out as no me importa — literally "it doesn't matter to me".
That makes ¿te importa…? the standard polite request: you are asking whether a thing would matter to them, not asking permission.

The word, taken apart.
Latin importare: in-, in, plus portare, to carry. To carry something in.
Importing goods is carrying them in, and that is the whole of what Rome meant by it. Mattering is the later half, invented inside the Romance languages long after Latin — you met that split under importante, and this is the verb it was cut from. A thing that imports brings weight in with it.
Portare is a wide family: transportar, exportar, English portable and the porter who carries your bags. Keep it apart from its look-alike porta, a gate — the word inside la puerta, el puerto and el pasaporte, which carries you through a gate rather than carrying anything itself. You were warned about this pair at la puerta, where English's two porters turned out to be two different words.

Lesson 4 of 5.
(organizar, el guía, importar) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say all three: to organise, the guide, to matter.

Grammar Lens: the verbs where the thing is the subject.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
Spanish: me gusta el libro. what is the subject: the book.
Spanish: me interesa el arte. what is the subject: art.
Spanish: me importa el resultado. what is the subject: the result.
Spanish: me duele la cabeza. what is the subject: the head.
Four verbs, one shape, and you now have the four this book has given you — the family is larger, and encantar, faltar and parecer all run on the same machine. The person is never the subject; the thing is, and the verb agrees with it. Me importan los horarios takes the plural because the timetables are doing the mattering.
The useful consequence: ¿te importa…? is how Spanish asks a small favour, and it is not asking permission. It asks whether the thing would weigh on them.

Grammar Lens: two spellings the ending forces.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
verb: organizar. I did it: organicé.
verb: rechazar. I did it: rechacé.
Verb endings never put an e after a z, so every -zar verb swaps to c before an e. The sound is unchanged; only the letter moves.
And la guía carries its accent for the reason la vía did — to keep the i from fusing with the a. Two syllables, not one.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the difference between the two groups]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Why it's said this way.
La excursión del sábado la organiza el centro cívico puts the object first and then repeats it as la. That doubling is not a mistake and not emphasis for its own sake: Spanish allows the object to lead the sentence, and when it does, a pronoun must hold its place beside the verb. English would say "the Saturday trip is organised by the civic centre" and reach for a passive; Spanish reorders instead.
Si a alguien le importa madrugar is importar in its natural habitat. The subject is madrugar — getting up early is what does the mattering — and a alguien le marks the person it matters to. Word for word: "if getting up early matters to anyone".
Note le even though alguien is already named. Spanish doubles the indirect object almost always, and leaving the le out would sound incomplete.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines to a group: say who is organising the outing, say a guide is coming, give the time, and ask whether an early start matters to anyone.
Then check yourself: did importar agree with the thing rather than the person, and did you keep the le?
