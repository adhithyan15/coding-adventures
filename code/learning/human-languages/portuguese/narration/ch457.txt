Portuguese, chapter 457: As árvores.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o carvalho — the oak (tree).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bush, then the Portuguese for to wade across.

You'll want to know: o carvalho.
o carvalho — "the oak (tree)".
No parque há um carvalho com duzentos anos. In the park there is a two-hundred-year-old oak.
Madeira de carvalho — oak wood.

The word, taken apart.
carvalho probably comes from a pre-Roman language of the Iberian Peninsula, but the origin is not settled; EtymDB lists it without a source.

You'll want to know: o sobreiro.
o sobreiro — "the cork oak".
O sobreiro cresce bem no sul do país. The cork oak grows well in the south of the country.

The word, taken apart.
sobreiro is built on old sobro (EtymDB) with -eiro; sobro is inherited from Latin sūber 'cork oak' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pine, then the Portuguese for the cork oak.

You'll want to know: a cortiça.
a cortiça — "cork".
As rolhas das garrafas são de cortiça. Bottle corks are made of cork.
Bark cut from the cork oak every nine years.

The word, taken apart.
cortiça is inherited from Latin corticea 'made of bark' (EtymDB), from cortex 'bark'.
