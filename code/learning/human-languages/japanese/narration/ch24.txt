Lesson 1 of 5.
いたみ (itami) — pain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fun, then the Japanese for scary.

You'll want to know: いたみ.
いたみ — itami — "pain".

The word, taken apart.
いたみ (itami) is native: the noun made from the verb itamu, "to hurt", by its stem, beside the adjective itai, "painful" (JMdict). It is inherited rather than borrowed; its kanji is a kun'yomi attached later.

The word, taken apart.
おじいさん (ojiisan) (ojīsan) is native jii, "old man; grandfather", with the polite prefix o- and the polite ending -san. JMdict writes it with characters for "grandfather", read for their meaning. It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
むすこ (musuko) — a son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a grandfather, then the Japanese for an uncle.

You'll want to know: むすこ.
むすこ — musuko — "a son".

The word, taken apart.
むすこ (musuko) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. It is usually explained as musu, "to be born, to grow", with ko, "child"; JMdict's kanji, "breath; son" and "child", are read as a whole for their meaning.

Lesson 5 of 5.
むすめ (musume) — a daughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an uncle, then the Japanese for a son.

You'll want to know: むすめ.
むすめ — musume — "a daughter".

The word, taken apart.
むすめ (musume) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
