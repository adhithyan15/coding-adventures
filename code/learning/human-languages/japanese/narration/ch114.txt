Lesson 1 of 5.
はくさい (hakusai) — Chinese cabbage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bonito, then the Japanese for wakame seaweed.

You'll want to know: はくさい.
はくさい — hakusai — "Chinese cabbage".

The word, taken apart.
はくさい (hakusai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are haku, "white" (Middle Chinese baek) and sai, "vegetables" (tshojH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 2 of 5.
にんにく (ninniku) — garlic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for wakame seaweed, then the Japanese for Chinese cabbage.

You'll want to know: にんにく.
にんにく — ninniku — "garlic".

The word, taken apart.
にんにく (ninniku) is written with characters for "big" and "garlic", read as a whole (JMdict). One account derives the word from a Buddhist term for enduring hardship; that is not settled. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.

Lesson 4 of 5.
はちみつ (hachimitsu) — honey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for garlic, then the Japanese for sweet bean paste.

You'll want to know: はちみつ.
はちみつ — hachimitsu — "honey".

The word, taken apart.
はちみつ (hachimitsu) mixes the two layers: native hachi, "bee", read as a kun'yomi, and mitsu, "honey", the on'yomi of its second character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mjit; JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
あみ (ami) — a net.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for sweet bean paste, then the Japanese for honey.

You'll want to know: あみ.
あみ — ami — "a net".

The word, taken apart.
あみ (ami) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
