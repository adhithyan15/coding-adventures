# ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, മുൻപ്, ശേഷം — two kinds of ordering

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C68-ORDINAL-08, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *at first*. Say *then*. You have had both for a long while.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C68-ORDINAL-08, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01, ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01] -->

You now have four words about order, and they split into **two pairs that do
different jobs**.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **ആദ്യം**, **പിന്നെ** | *ādyaṁ*, *pinne* | stand **alone** and order the telling |
| **മുൻപ്**, **ശേഷം** | *munpŭ*, *śēṣaṁ* | lean on **a word** and order events |

**ആദ്യം** and **പിന്നെ** need nothing in front of them. They say *first this,
then that* about the order you are **saying** things in.

**മുൻപ്** and **ശേഷം** cannot stand alone. Each one needs a thing to be before or
after, and that thing arrives wearing the dative.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01, ML-LEX-C64-SMALL-01] -->

**Until this chapter you could order your own sentences and not the world.**
*First … then …* arranges what you say. *Before two o'clock* and *after the
meal* arrange what happens, against something you name.

That is the whole of what the two new words add — and neither of them needed a
new piece of grammar to do it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01, ML-LEX-C68-ORDINAL-08] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which two of the four stand alone? (**ആദ്യം** and **പിന്നെ**.) Which
two need something to lean on? (**മുൻപ്** and **ശേഷം**.) What does the thing they
lean on wear? (**The dative**.)
