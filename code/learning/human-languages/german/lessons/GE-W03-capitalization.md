# Großschreibung — why German capitalises every noun

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-TAG-02, GE-GRAMMAR-CAPITAL-SPLITS-SENSE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You met this rule with *der Tag*, and you used it to tell *Morgen*
from *morgen*. Now here it is in full — and it turns out to be doing more work
than it looks.

## You'll want to know: Großschreibung
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-GROSSSCHREIBUNG-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02, GE-LEX-ESZETT-01] -->

> **Großschreibung** — "capital-writing." Every noun takes a capital, anywhere
> in the sentence.

Not just names or sentence starts — *every* noun:

> **Der Hund** frisst **das Essen** in **der Küche**.
> — "The dog eats the food in the kitchen."

Three nouns, three capitals, two of them mid-sentence. Also capitalised: the
polite *Sie*, and any word *used as* a noun (*das Gute*, "the good").

(The word itself shows off two things you now know: the **ß**, and the initial
**st** said *sht* — *GROHSS-shry-boong*.)

## Grammar Lens: the capital is a part-of-speech signal
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-CAPITAL-IS-PART-OF-SPEECH-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-GROSSSCHREIBUNG-01, GE-GRAMMAR-CAPITAL-SPLITS-SENSE-02] -->

The capital carries **meaning**. It separates words spelt alike that do
different jobs:

| Lower-case | Capital, so a noun |
|---|---|
| *essen* — to eat | *das Essen* — the food |
| *jung* — young | *der Junge* — the boy |
| *laut* — loud | *der Laut* — the sound |

This is the same machinery that split *morgen* from *Morgen*. With *der/die/das*
in front as well, German flags its nouns **twice** — which is why long German
sentences are easier to parse, not harder.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-GROSSSCHREIBUNG-01] -->

English used to do this too: read a page of 1700s English and the Nouns are
Capitalised. English dropped the habit in the eighteenth century; German kept it
and wrote it into the rules. It is a living fossil of older European printing.

## Writing — applying the rule
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-GROSSSCHREIBUNG-01, GE-GRAMMAR-CAPITAL-IS-PART-OF-SPEECH-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Capitalise the first letter of every noun, wherever it sits. Two tests if you
are unsure: is there a *der/die/das* in front, or does it end in *-ung*, *-heit*
or *-schaft*? Those are noun factories — *Zeitung*, *Freiheit*, *Freundschaft*.

Copy **Der Hund frisst das Essen** once, then write *Guten Morgen* and *bis
morgen* one under the other and check every capital.
