Hindi, chapter 498: Bringing Up a Child.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
परवरिश (parvariś) — nurture; upbringing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for newborn, then the Hindi for to be born.

You'll want to know: परवरिश.
परवरिश — parvariś — "nurture; upbringing".
दादी ने अरुण की परवरिश की। dādī ne Aruṇ kī parvariś kī. Grandmother brought Arun up.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
परवरिश (parvariś) is usually traced to Persian parvarish, "nurture, fostering".

Lesson 3 of 6.
पिलाना (pilānā) — to give someone a drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for nurture, then the Hindi for a lullaby.

You'll want to know: पिलाना.
पिलाना — pilānā — "to give someone a drink".
माँ ने बच्चे को दूध पिलाया। māṁ ne bacce ko dūdh pilāyā. The mother gave the baby some milk.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को पानी पिलाया, usne bacce ko pānī pilāyā.

The word, taken apart.
पिलाना (pilānā) is the causative partner of pīnā, "to drink", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit píbati, "drinks" (CDIAL 8209): to make someone drink.

खिलाना — khilānā — "to feed".
दादी ने अपने हाथ से बच्चों को खाना खिलाया। dādī ne apne hāth se baccoṁ ko khānā khilāyā. Grandmother fed the children with her own hands.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को खाना खिलाया, usne bacce ko khānā khilāyā.

The word, taken apart.
खिलाना (khilānā) is the causative partner of khānā, "to eat", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit khādati, "eats" (CDIAL 3865): to make someone eat. Another khilānā, "to make bloom", belongs to khilnā.

Lesson 5 of 6.
पलना (palnā) — to be brought up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to give someone a drink, then the Hindi for to feed.

You'll want to know: पलना.
पलना — palnā — "to be brought up".
अरुण अपने नाना के घर पला। Aruṇ apne nānā ke ghar palā. Arun was brought up at his grandfather's house.
Past (he …): वह गाँव में पला, vah gā̃v meṁ palā.

The word, taken apart.
पलना (palnā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi palnā, "to be reared", beside pālnā, "to rear", under Sanskrit pāláyati, "protects" (CDIAL 8129). It is the intransitive partner of pālnā.
