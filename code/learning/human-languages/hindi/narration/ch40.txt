Hindi, chapter 40: Pointing, and Asking.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
यह (yah) — this one — the thing near me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you POINT at them.

You'll want to know: यह.
यह — yah — this one — the thing near me.
Say it, and picture the thing it lands on as you say it. That is the whole word: it does not mean anything on its own, it means whatever the speaker is pointing at.
Its partner, the far one, comes in the next lesson. The two of them differ by one sound at the front — y- for near — and that is not a coincidence. It is the whole system, and the last lesson of this chapter says so.

The word, taken apart.
यह (yah) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from eṣa, "this" (CDIAL 2530).

Lesson 5 of 7.
कौन (kaun) — who? — asking about a person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you ASK about them.

You'll want to know: कौन.
कौन — kaun — who? — asking about a person.
Say it with an asking voice. It points at no one: it asks your listener to name the person.
Its partner, the question word for place, comes in the next lesson, and it opens with the same k-. That is the pattern: the near words you know open with y- (यह yah, यहाँ yahā̃), the far ones with v- (वह vah, वहाँ vahā̃), and the asking words with k-. The last lesson of this chapter lays it out.

The word, taken apart.
कौन (kaun) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kaḥ punar, literally "who, then", which CDIAL glosses "who" (CDIAL 2575).

Lesson 6 of 7.
कहाँ (kahā̃) — where? — asking about a place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you ASK about them.

You'll want to know: कहाँ.
कहाँ — kahā̃ — where? — asking about a place.
Say it with an asking voice. It points at no place: it asks your listener to point one out.
Its partner is कौन kaun, which you met in the last lesson: both open with k-, the asking sound. Its other partners, यहाँ yahā̃ and वहाँ vahā̃, differ from it only at the front. That is the whole system, and the last lesson of this chapter says so.

The word, taken apart.
कहाँ (kahā̃) (kahāṁ) is built inside Hindi: the question stem k- of क्या and कौन (kaun) with the place ending -hāṁ of यहाँ (yahā̃), "here", and वहाँ (vahā̃), "there". CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 7 of 7.
y- / v- / k- — six words, one machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the six words from this chapter out loud, in order. Listen to how they begin.

Grammar lens: one slot changes.
You did not learn six words. You learned three beginnings and reused them.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
meaning: near. word: यहाँ yahā̃.
meaning: far. word: वहाँ vahā̃.
meaning: question. word: कहाँ kahā̃.
Hindi runs the same machine on different consonants: यहाँ (yahā̃), वहाँ (vahā̃), कहाँ (kahā̃).
Change the front, and the meaning walks from near to far to a question. Nothing else in the word moves.
The same three beginnings run the thing-words too: यह yah and वह vah start exactly like the first two rows. This chapter did not teach you the matching question word for things — that one arrives later, and when it does you will already know what it has to start with.
This is worth more than the six words are. When you meet a new word in this family later, you will not have to be taught all three of it — you will be taught one and work out the others.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the near word, then the far word, then the question word]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: them again for place, not for things]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — notice: the part that did NOT change]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which beginning means near? (y-) Far? (v-) And which one turns the word into a question? (k-).
