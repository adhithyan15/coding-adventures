Spanish, chapter 728: Listos y sabios.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sabio — wise; the wise person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for polite, then the Spanish for rude.

You'll want to know: sabio.
sabio — "wise; the wise person".
Mi abuelo era un hombre sabio. My grandfather was a wise man.
Un sabio is a sage or a scholar.

The word, taken apart.
sabio comes from Latin sapidus (EtymDB), from sapere "to taste, to be wise", the source of saber.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la sabiduría — wisdom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for rude, then the Spanish for wise.

You'll want to know: la sabiduría.
la sabiduría — "wisdom".
La sabiduría viene con los años. Wisdom comes with the years.

The word, taken apart.
sabiduría belongs with sabio "wise" (EtymDB), and its form is usually traced to old sabidor "one who knows", built on saber, from Latin sapere "to taste, to be wise".

hábil is a learned borrowing from Latin habilis "apt, skilful" (EtymDB), from habēre "to hold"; English able is its inherited French form.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el ingenio — wit, ingenuity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for clever, then the Spanish for skilful.

You'll want to know: el ingenio.
el ingenio — "wit, ingenuity".
Tiene mucho ingenio para resolver problemas. She is very ingenious at solving problems.
Un ingenio can also be a clever device; in Latin America un ingenio is a sugar mill.

The word, taken apart.
ingenio was borrowed from Latin ingenium "inborn nature, talent" (EtymDB), built on in and the root of gignere "to beget": the gifts you are born with.
