Mandarin Chinese, chapter 528: By Chance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pèngqiǎo — as luck would have it; coincidentally.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a favourable deal, then the Mandarin for a checked pattern.

You'll want to know: pèngqiǎo.
pèngqiǎo — "as luck would have it; coincidentally".
yàoshi pèngqiǎo lùguò jiù lái wǒ jiā zuòzuò — If you happen to be passing, come round to my place.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pèngqiǎo is built from pèng 'to bump into' and qiǎo. No dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese khaewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'fine' and 'skilful'; the sense 'by coincidence' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
不得不 (bùdébù) — to be obliged to; cannot help but.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a checked pattern, then the Mandarin for as luck would have it.

You'll want to know: 不得不.
不得不 — bùdébù — "to be obliged to; cannot help but".
如果明天下雨我们就不得不改期 rúguǒ míngtiān xiàyǔ wǒmen jiù bùdébù gǎiqī — If it rains tomorrow, we'll have to change the date.
Tone: the first 不 keeps its falling tone before dé; the second is said bú before a falling tone (bùdébú qù) and bù before the others.

The word, taken apart.
不得不 (bùdébù) is built from 不 'not', 得 'to get' and 不 again, all inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut, tok and pjut (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): may not not do it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
chōuqiān — to draw lots.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for as luck would have it, then the Mandarin for to be obliged to.

You'll want to know: chōuqiān.
chōuqiān — "to draw lots".
yàoshi dàjiā dōu xiǎng qù wǒmen jiù chōuqiān — If everyone wants to go, let's draw lots.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōuqiān is built from chōu 'to pull out' and qiān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, trhjuw and tshjem (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to draw out' and the second, after an older dictionary, 'a test' and 'pointed'; the slip that is drawn is not named there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎdǔ — to make a bet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be obliged to, then the Mandarin for to draw lots.

You'll want to know: dǎdǔ.
dǎdǔ — "to make a bet".
wǒ gēn nǐ dǎdǔ tā míngtiān bú huì lái — I bet you he won't come tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before dǔ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dǎdǔ is built from 打 and dǔ 'to gamble', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and tuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second as gambling for play. The vowel of dǎ is not the regular outcome of taengX.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ǒurán — accidental; happening by chance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to draw lots, then the Mandarin for to make a bet.

You'll want to know: ǒurán.
ǒurán — "accidental; happening by chance".
rúguǒ zhǐ fāshēng yí cì kěnéng shì ǒurán — If it only happens once, it may be chance.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ǒurán is built from ǒu and 然 'so', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nguwH and nyen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses nguwH 'unexpected', and lists a rising-tone reading, nguwX, glossed 'a pair'; the rising tone of ǒu matches that other reading.
