# Review — what I think, and what follows

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C283-PUTO-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I think it is raining". (*Putō pluere.*) Then "I believe
you are tired". (*Crēdō tē fessum esse.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C283-MEA-SENTENTIA-01, LA-LEX-C283-MIHI-VIDETUR-02, LA-LEX-C283-ERGO-01, LA-ETYMON-C283-PUTO-03] -->

Three lessons, and an opinion has three shapes. **putō** with the
accusative and infinitive, which turns the sentence into what you think.
**meā sententiā**, which leaves it alone. And **mihi vidētur**, "it seems to
me", with the thing that seems as its subject. Then **ergō** to draw the
conclusion, beside *itaque*, which leans to stories, and *igitur*, which
leans to arguments.

> *Meā sententiā, Mārcus fessus est; ergō dormit.*
> — In my opinion Marcus is tired; so he is sleeping.

[PAUSE 2s] The opinion leaves *Mārcus* nominative. Say it with *putō*
instead, and he turns accusative: *Putō Mārcum fessum esse.*

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C283-MEA-SENTENTIA-01, LA-LEX-C283-MIHI-VIDETUR-02, LA-LEX-C282-DUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C282-ORDO-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "In my opinion"? (***Meā sententiā.***) "It seems to me"? (***Mihi
vidētur.***) What did *putō* first mean? (**To prune.**) After *putō*, which
case is the person in? (The **accusative**.) And from the last chapter: what
does *dum* mean? (**While.**) Which rung comes first? (***Prīmum.***)
