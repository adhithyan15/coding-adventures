## Grammar Lens: 会 before a verb, for what is going to happen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-GRAMMAR-HUI-FUTURE-01]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C90-HUI-01, ZH-LEX-C66-WEATHER66-03, ZH-LEX-C45-TODAY45-05, ZH-LEX-C147-HOUTIAN-01, ZH-TONE-SANDHI-BU] -->

> **会 before a verb can say what will probably happen, or what someone will do.**

> **明天会下雨** *míngtiān huì xiàyǔ* — It'll rain tomorrow.
> **后天不会下雨** *hòutiān bú huì xiàyǔ* — It won't rain the day after tomorrow.

不 before the fall of 会 is *bú*, as in *bú qù*.

You met 会 as *can*, for something learned: 我会开车. It is the same character in a different use. The rain has learned nothing, so 明天会下雨 is a forecast, not a skill; a time word in front usually tells you which one you hear.

You have said the future without 会 for a long time: **明天我们去** *míngtiān wǒmen qù* needs no extra word. 会 adds a forecast, what you expect to happen:

> **明天我会去** *míngtiān wǒ huì qù* — I'll go tomorrow.
