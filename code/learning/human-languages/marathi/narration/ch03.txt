Marathi, chapter 3: Six More Signs Make Thank You Readable.
7 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
द — dental da.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Keep the tongue-at-the-teeth position ready and add voice next.

Script.
द (da).
Keep the tongue at the teeth and add voice: da. Descend the short stem; lift for the outer body, inward curl, and down-right tail; finish the headline.

Writing — delayed copy and retrieval.
Look at द (da) for five seconds, cover it, and write it once. Then retrieve the preceding three names: e-mark, anusvāra, dental ta. Repair only one miss.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari da stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Write dental da after a delay, then retrieve the previous three sign names.


Lesson 2 of 7.
ध — dha, voiced and breathy.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Keep the dental voiced position ready; this sign adds breath.

Script.
ध (dha).
Say da, then add a puff: dha. Curl around the upper spiral and sweep through its shoulder; lift for the lower bowl; descend the right stem; finish the headline.

Writing — delayed copy and a wider return.
Hide ध (dha) after five seconds and write it once. Then, without models, name the first three signs of this runway: visarga, independent ā, bha.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari dha stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Write dha after a delay, then retrieve visarga, independent aa, and bha by name.


Lesson 3 of 7.
ब — ba without the puff.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Warm-up.

Script.
ब (ba).
This is ba, the quiet partner of bha. Circle the oval body; descend the right stem; draw the inner diagonal down-right; finish the headline.

Writing — heard cue.

Script.
य (ya).

Lesson 5 of 7.
ळ (ḷa) — Marathi's curled-back lla.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Curl the tongue tip back once without adding a vowel.

Script.
ळ (ḷa).
Curl the tongue tip back for ḷa. Draw the left loop through the centre and around the right loop in one run; add the short stem above the right loop; finish the headline.

Writing — heard cue and wider retrieval.
Cover the model.
[your turn — hear: curled-back ḷa.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
Write it once. Then retrieve the e-mark, anusvāra, and dental ta by name; those three have now crossed a wider gap.

Lesson 6 of 7.
व (va) — between English v and w.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Relax the lips for a light sound between English v and w.

Script.
व (va).
Marathi va can sound lighter than English v, toward w. Travel left around the top and circle the loop counterclockwise; descend the right stem; finish the headline.

Writing — heard cue.
Cover the model.
[your turn — hear: va.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
Write one consonant from sound. Uncover व (va) and compare loop, right stem, headline.

Lesson 7 of 7.
धन्यवाद (dhanyavād) — now read what you already mean.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
Nothing in this word is an unknown shape now. Read ध, न्, य, वा, द (da), join the middle consonants, and say धन्यवाद — dhanyavād, "thank you."

Guided Practice.
hear dhanyavād and point to धन्यवाद.
say the word from the English cue "thank you".
read धन्यवाद (dhanyavād) without romanization.
cover every model and write धन्यवाद (dhanyavād) from the heard cue.
Then retrieve the last three signs separately: ya, curled-back ḷa, va.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Stop after one independent attempt.
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Write the Marathi word you hear: dhanyavad. Then name the last three signs in this runway.
