Lesson 1 of 5.
वाद्यम् (vādyam) — a musical instrument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a book, then the Sanskrit for a picture.

You'll want to know: वाद्यम्.
वाद्यम् — vādyam — "a musical instrument".

The word, taken apart.
vādya comes from the root √vad "speak, sound": first "to be said", then "to be played", and so "a musical instrument" (CDIAL 11511). It lives on as Hindi bājā "musical instrument" (CDIAL 11511).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वीणा (vīṇā) — a veena.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a picture, then the Sanskrit for a musical instrument.

You'll want to know: वीणा.
वीणा — vīṇā — "a veena".

The word, taken apart.
MW calls vīṇā "the Indian lute" of doubtful derivation; EtymDB traces it to an Indo-Iranian word that Middle Persian also kept, so its source is not settled. It lives on as Hindi bīn "lute" (CDIAL 12048).

पट्टः (paṭṭaḥ) — a cloth, a strip.

You'll want to know: पट्टः.
पट्टः — paṭṭaḥ — "a cloth, a strip".

The word, taken apart.
paṭṭa "cloth, woven silk, a strip or bandage" is probably from a non-Aryan source, says CDIAL 7700, which rejects a link with pattra "leaf". It lives on as Hindi paṭṭī "strip of cloth" (CDIAL 7700).

Lesson 4 of 5.
रत्नम् (ratnam) — a jewel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a veena, then the Sanskrit for a cloth.

You'll want to know: रत्नम्.
रत्नम् — ratnam — "a jewel".

The word, taken apart.
ratna first meant "a gift, wealth" in the Rigveda and later "a jewel" (MW). MW derives it from the root √rā "give", while CDIAL 10600 refers it to √raṇ "rejoice", so its root is not settled. Pali kept it as ratana "jewel" (CDIAL 10600).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सुवर्णम् (suvarṇam) — gold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cloth, then the Sanskrit for a jewel.

You'll want to know: सुवर्णम्.
सुवर्णम् — suvarṇam — "gold".

The word, taken apart.
suvarṇa is built from su "good" and varṇa "colour" (CDIAL 13519): "of a good, bright colour", and so "gold". It wore down into Hindi, Punjabi and Bengali sonā "gold" (CDIAL 13519).
