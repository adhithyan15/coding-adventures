Mandarin Chinese, chapter 199: Sizes.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
chǐmǎ — clothing size; shoe size.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for engaged (of a phone line), then the Mandarin for phone signal.

You'll want to know: chǐmǎ.
chǐmǎ — "clothing size; shoe size".
nǐ chuān duōdà de chǐmǎ — What size do you wear?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: chǐ is said with a rising tone before mǎ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
chǐmǎ is built from chǐ, a measure of length, and mǎ 'a number sign', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhek and maeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the first as a measure of length and glosses the second as a stone like jade; how the pair came to mean the size of clothes is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chǐmǎ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for engaged (of a phone line), then the Mandarin for phone signal, then say chǐmǎ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 6.
báo — thin (of cloth or paper).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for phone signal, then the Mandarin for clothing size.

You'll want to know: báo.
báo — "thin (of cloth or paper)".
nǐ de yīfu tài báo le — Your clothes are too thin.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
báo is inherited from Middle Chinese bak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed by the pair 'thick and thin'. Mandarin has two outcomes of that form: báo in everyday speech, and bó in bookish words.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for clothing size, then the Mandarin for thin (of cloth or paper).

You'll want to know: féi.
féi — "loose; baggy (of clothes)".
zhè tiáo kùzi yǒudiǎnr féi — These trousers are a bit baggy.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
féi is inherited from Middle Chinese bj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'fat' and 'fleshy'. Its use for loose clothes is not given there, so how it came is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
tiāo — to pick out; to choose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for baggy, then the Mandarin for tight.

You'll want to know: tiāo.
tiāo — "to pick out; to choose".
qǐng bāng wǒ tiāo yí ge chǐmǎ — Could you help me pick out a size, please?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiāo is inherited from Middle Chinese thew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to stir up'. The sense 'to pick out' is not given there, so how it came is not settled.
