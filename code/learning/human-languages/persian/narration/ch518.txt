Persian, chapter 518: Playing an Instrument.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سه تار (setâr) — a setar (a three-stringed lute).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a santur, then the Persian for a tombak.

You'll want to know: سه تار.
سه تار (setâr) — "a setar (a three-stringed lute)".
او شبها برای خودش سه تار میزند. u shabhâ barâ-ye khodash setâr mizanad. At night she plays the setar for herself.
se 'three' + târ 'string'. The Indian sitar is usually traced to it.

The word, taken apart.
setâr is built on two native Persian words, se, 'three', and târ, 'string'. Hindi and Urdu sitār are usually traced to it.

پسر کوچک طبل میزند و همه را بیدار میکند. pesar-e kuchek tabl mizanad va hame râ bidâr mikonad. The little boy bangs a drum and wakes everyone up.

The word, taken apart.
tabl comes from Arabic ṭabl, 'drum' (EtymDB); Hindi tabla goes back to the same word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نواختن (navâkhtan) — to play (an instrument).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a daf, then the Persian for a drum.

You'll want to know: نواختن.
نواختن (navâkhtan) — "to play (an instrument)".
او پیانو را خیلی زیبا مینوازد. u piyâno râ kheyli zibâ minavâzad. She plays the piano beautifully.
Formal; present stem navâz-. In speech people say zadan: piyâno mizanad.

The word, taken apart.
navâkhtan is a native Persian verb, with the present stem navâz- seen in navâzesh. EtymDB lists it without a source, so its older history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
کوک کردن (kuk kardan) — to tune (an instrument); to wind (a clock).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a drum, then the Persian for to play.

You'll want to know: کوک کردن.
کوک کردن (kuk kardan) — "to tune (an instrument); to wind (a clock)".
او قبل از کنسرت تارش را کوک کرد. u qabl az konsert târash râ kuk kard. He tuned his tar before the concert.
kuk 'tuning' + kardan. kuk budan — to be in tune, and in speech 'to be in good form'.

The word, taken apart.
kuk means 'tuning' and 'winding', but no source used here gives its history, and its origin is not settled. kardan is inherited Persian.
