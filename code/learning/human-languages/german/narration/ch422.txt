German, chapter 422: Auf See.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Kreuzfahrt — the cruise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the life jacket, then the German for seasick.

You'll want to know: die Kreuzfahrt.
die Kreuzfahrt — "the cruise".
Meine Großeltern machen im Sommer eine Kreuzfahrt. My grandparents are going on a cruise in the summer.
Plural: die Kreuzfahrten. Das Kreuzfahrtschiff — the cruise ship.

The word, taken apart.
Kreuzfahrt is built from kreuzen 'to cruise, to sail back and forth', from Kreuz 'cross', which goes back to Latin crux (Lewis & Short), and Fahrt 'voyage', from fahren (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
anlegen — to dock, to moor; to put on; to invest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for seasick, then the German for the cruise.

You'll want to know: anlegen.
anlegen — "to dock, to moor; to put on; to invest".
Das Schiff legt am Morgen im Hafen an. The ship docks in the harbour in the morning.
Separable: das Schiff legt an, es hat angelegt. Geld anlegen — to invest money.

The word, taken apart.
anlegen is built from an 'on, at' and legen 'to lay', inherited from Old High German leggen (EtymDB): a boat laid alongside the quay.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Kapitän — the captain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to cast off, then the German for to rock.

You'll want to know: der Kapitän.
der Kapitän — "the captain".
Der Kapitän begrüßt die Gäste an Bord. The captain welcomes the passengers on board.
Plural: die Kapitäne. Also the captain of a team.

The word, taken apart.
Kapitän came into German through Middle High German kapitān from Medieval Latin capitāneus 'chief' (EtymDB), built on Latin caput 'head' (Lewis & Short).
