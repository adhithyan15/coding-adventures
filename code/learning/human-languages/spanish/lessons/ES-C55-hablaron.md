# hablaron

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-3PL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give the three past forms you have. (*Hablé, hablaste, habló*.) Now
the present for *they*. (*Hablan*.)

[PAUSE 1s] You have had a past tense for one person only. Today it grows.

## Grammar Lens: they, in the past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-3PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-3PL, ES-ORTHOGRAPHY-AR-PRETERITE-FINAL-STRESS] -->

> ***hablaron*** — they spoke

| | present | past |
|---|---|---|
| *ellos / ellas / ustedes* | **hablan** | **hablaron** |

The present ending is *-an*. The past ending is **-aron**, and the stress lands
on that **-a-**: *ha-BLA-ron*. No written accent this time — the word already
ends in *-n*, and a word ending in *-n* is stressed on the second-to-last
syllable by default, which is exactly where the stress is.

[PAUSE 2s] So *habló* needed its accent and *hablaron* does not. The rule you
learned for reading accents is doing the work.
