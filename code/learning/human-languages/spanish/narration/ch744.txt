Spanish, chapter 744: Flechazos y piropos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el flechazo — love at first sight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for well-being, then the Spanish for self-esteem.

You'll want to know: el flechazo.
el flechazo — "love at first sight".
Fue un flechazo: nos casamos en un año. It was love at first sight: we married within a year.
Literally an arrow shot, Cupid's arrow.

The word, taken apart.
flechazo is built on flecha "arrow" with -azo, the ending for a blow. Flecha is usually traced to French flèche.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el piropo — the compliment (flirtatious).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for self-esteem, then the Spanish for love at first sight.

You'll want to know: el piropo.
el piropo — "the compliment (flirtatious)".
Mi marido siempre me echa piropos. My husband is always paying me compliments.
Echar un piropo — to pay someone a flirtatious compliment. From a stranger in the street, a piropo is now widely seen as harassment.

The word, taken apart.
piropo comes from Latin pyrōpus "gold-bronze", from Greek pyrōpós "fire-coloured" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); the name of a red gem became the word for a sparkling compliment.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for love at first sight, then the Spanish for the compliment.

You'll want to know: el noviazgo.
el noviazgo — "the courtship, the engagement".
Tuvieron un noviazgo de cinco años. Their courtship lasted five years.
The time when two people are novios.

The word, taken apart.
noviazgo is built on novio with the ending -azgo; novio comes from a spoken Latin novius "newly married", from novus "new" (EtymDB, under novio).

You'll want to know: el pretendiente.
el pretendiente — "the suitor, the admirer".
Tiene muchos pretendientes. She has a lot of admirers.
Also a candidate for a job or a throne.

The word, taken apart.
pretendiente is built on pretender "to aim at, to court", a learned borrowing from Latin praetendere "to hold out in front" (EtymDB), from tendere "to stretch".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la complicidad — closeness, understanding; complicity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the courtship, then the Spanish for the suitor.

You'll want to know: la complicidad.
la complicidad — "closeness, understanding; complicity".
Entre ellos hay mucha complicidad. There is a great understanding between them.
Between friends or partners it is a warm word; in law it is complicity.

The word, taken apart.
complicidad is built on cómplice, usually described as a learned borrowing from Late Latin complex, complicem "closely joined", from plicāre "to fold".
