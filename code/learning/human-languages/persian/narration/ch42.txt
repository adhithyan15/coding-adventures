Lesson 1 of 5.
شکر (shekar) — sugar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a tomato, then the Persian for a carrot.

You'll want to know: شکر.
شکر — shekar — "sugar".

The word, taken apart.
shekar is usually traced to Sanskrit śarkarā, "grit, sugar", through Middle Persian šakar. Arabic sukkar took the word from Persian, and English sugar comes from that Arabic word by way of Latin and French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
پرتقال (porteghâl) — an orange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for butter, then the Persian for grapes.

You'll want to know: پرتقال.
پرتقال — porteghâl — "an orange".

The word, taken apart.
porteghâl is the country name Portugal. The sweet orange is usually said to have reached the region through Portuguese traders, and Persian, like Arabic, Greek and Turkish, named the fruit after them.
