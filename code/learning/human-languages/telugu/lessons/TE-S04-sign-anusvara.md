# ◌ం — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-140] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ◌ి — what does it do? And one from further back: ౧?

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time — and you have been saying it for pages
without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ం
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-04]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ం** — *a nasal, ridden on the syllable before it*.

It is a **nasal**, written as a mark rather than as a letter. It rides on the syllable before it and turns the end of it nasal.

You already say these, and every one of them has ◌ం somewhere inside it:

- **సంతోషం** — joy / pleased to meet you
- **నమస్కారం** *namaskāram* — hello / greetings (namaskāram — "a making of a bow")

## Writing: ◌ం — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ◌ం yourself —
slowly, and larger than it is printed.

> The strip shows **where to start the character and which way to travel**, in
> one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school,
> so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the
> only one.
