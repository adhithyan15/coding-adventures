Lesson 1 of 5.
cubitum, cubitī — an elbow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the chin, then the Latin for the back.

You'll want to know: cubitum, cubitī.
cubitum, cubitī — "an elbow".

The word, taken apart.
cubitum is built on cubō, "to lie down, to recline" (EtymDB 2.1): the elbow is what one leans on at table. English cubit, the length from elbow to fingertip, comes from it.

You'll want to know: pollex, pollicis.
pollex, pollicis — "a thumb".

The word, taken apart.
pollex was linked by the Romans with polleō, "to be strong" (Lewis & Short): the strongest finger. Modern scholars are less sure.

Lesson 3 of 5.
cutis, cutis — the skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an elbow, then the Latin for a thumb.

You'll want to know: cutis, cutis.
cutis, cutis — "the skin".

The word, taken apart.
cutis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kuHt-, "skin, hide" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek kytos, "a hollow, a skin", is compared (Lewis & Short), and English hide may be a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
os, ossis — a bone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a thumb, then the Latin for the skin.

You'll want to know: os, ossis.
os, ossis — "a bone".

The word, taken apart.
os, ossis, is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h3esth1, "bone" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek osteon, "bone", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). It is a different word from ōs, ōris, "mouth".
