Mandarin Chinese, chapter 264: Sorry, I Can't.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jùjué — to refuse; to turn down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sick leave, then the Mandarin for annual leave.

You'll want to know: jùjué.
jùjué — "to refuse; to turn down".
tā jùjuéle wǒ — He turned me down.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jùjué is built from jù 'to resist' and jué 'to cut off', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjoX and dzjwet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to resist' and the second 'to break off'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying no and asking.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 3 of 5.
bàoqiàn — sorry; regretful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to turn down, then the Mandarin for a shame.

You'll want to know: bàoqiàn.
bàoqiàn — "sorry; regretful".
hěn bàoqiàn wǒ míngtiān méiyǒu shíjiān — I'm very sorry, I don't have time tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàoqiàn is built from bào 'to hold' and qiàn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, bawX and kheamH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hold'. For the second it gives a sunken mouth, and in other readings 'not eating your fill'; the sense of being sorry is not given there, so how it came is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
qíshí — actually; as a matter of fact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: qíshí.
qíshí — "actually; as a matter of fact".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qíshí is built from qí 'its' and shí 'real', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gi and zyit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which calls the first a particle and glosses the second 'full' and 'true'.
