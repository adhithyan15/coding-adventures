6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tēmō, tēmōnis — a cart pole, the shaft of a wagon or plough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a covered carriage, then the Latin for a travelling coach.

You'll want to know: tēmō, tēmōnis.
tēmō, tēmōnis — "a cart pole, the shaft of a wagon or plough".
Agricola bovēs ad tēmōnem plaustrī iungit. The farmer harnesses the oxen to the pole of the wagon.
Third declension, masculine: tēmō, tēmōnis m. The long beam that runs forward from the cart to the yoke.

The word, taken apart.
tēmō is inherited. Lewis & Short connect it with the root tek- of texō 'to weave, to build', and EtymDB lists texō as a cousin; the exact path is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
modiolus, modiolī — a wheel hub, the nave of a wheel; a small measure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a travelling coach, then the Latin for a cart pole.

You'll want to know: modiolus, modiolī.
modiolus, modiolī — "a wheel hub, the nave of a wheel; a small measure".
In mediā rotā modiolus est. In the middle of the wheel is the hub.
Second declension, masculine: modiolus, modiolī m. A little measure: the hub looks like a small barrel.

The word, taken apart.
modiolus is a diminutive of modius 'a peck measure' (Lewis & Short), which is built on modus 'measure' (EtymDB). The round hub of a wheel looked like a small measuring tub.

Lesson 3 of 6.
sufflāmen, sufflāminis — a drag shoe, a brake (for a wheel).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cart pole, then the Latin for a wheel hub.

You'll want to know: sufflāmen, sufflāminis.
sufflāmen, sufflāminis — "a drag shoe, a brake (for a wheel)".
In clīvō raedārius sufflāmen sub rotā pōnit. On the slope the coachman puts the drag shoe under the wheel.
Third declension, neuter: sufflāmen, sufflāminis n. A block or chain that stops a wheel turning on a steep road. Also anything that holds a thing back.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of sufflāmen is not settled. Lewis & Short file it with sufflō 'to blow up' without further comment, while EtymDB lists Greek phalanx 'log, beam' as a cousin, which points to a word for a wooden block.

Lesson 4 of 6.
subiungō, subiungere — to yoke, to harness; to put under.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a wheel hub, then the Latin for a drag shoe.

You'll want to know: subiungō, subiungere.
subiungō, subiungere — "to yoke, to harness; to put under".
Agricola mūlōs plaustrō subiungit. The farmer harnesses the mules to the wagon.
subiungō, subiungere, subiūnxī, subiūnctum, often with a dative: equōs currū subiungere. English subjunctive comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
subiungō is built from sub- 'under' and iungō 'to join, to yoke' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English subjunctive comes from its participle subiūnctus.

disiungō, disiungere — "to unyoke; to separate".
Vesperī agricola bovēs disiungit et domum redit. In the evening the farmer unyokes the oxen and goes home.
disiungō, disiungere, disiūnxī, disiūnctum. English disjoint and disjunction come from it.

The word, taken apart.
disiungō is built from dis- 'apart' and iungō 'to join, to yoke' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English disjoint comes from it through French.
