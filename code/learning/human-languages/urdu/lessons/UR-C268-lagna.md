# لگنا (lagnā) — to seem, to be felt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-01, UR-LEX-C41-HEALTH41-05, UR-GRAMMAR-C267-KAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "hunger" and "thirst". (*bhūk*, *pyās*.) Urdu usually says "I am hungry" another way: hunger has struck you.

## You'll want to know: لگنا
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-C268-LAGNA-01]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-01, UR-LEX-C228-CHIZ228-04, UR-LEX-C70-ADJ70-03, UR-GRAMMAR-C264-PAST-01] -->

**لگنا** — *lagnā* — "to seem; to be felt".

> **مجھے بھوک لگی ہے۔**
>
> *mujhe bhūk lagī hai.* — "I'm hungry."

The one who feels it takes **مجھے** (*mujhe*), and the verb follows the thing felt: **بھوک** (*bhūk*) is feminine, so **لگی** (*lagī*).

> **مجھے یہ فلم اچھی لگی۔**
>
> *mujhe yih film achchhī lagī.* — "I liked this film."

Word for word, "this film seemed good to me". **فلم** is feminine: **اچھی لگی**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**لگنا** (*lagnā*) is inherited. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit *lagyati*, "adheres to" (CDIAL 10895): a hunger or a liking attaches itself to the one who feels it.
