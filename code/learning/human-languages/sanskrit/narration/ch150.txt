Lesson 1 of 5.
भेषजम् (bheṣajam) — a remedy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a clerk, then the Sanskrit for a leader.

You'll want to know: भेषजम्.
भेषजम् — bheṣajam — "a remedy".

The word, taken apart.
bheṣaja "healing; a remedy" is formed from bhiṣaj "a healer, a physician" (MW), already in the Rigveda. bhiṣaj is inherited from Indo-Iranian, with a cousin in Avestan (EtymDB).

शोथः — śothaḥ — "a swelling".

The word, taken apart.
śotha "a swelling, a tumour, dropsy" comes from the root √śū, also √śvi, "swell" (MW). It is a word of the medical writer Suśruta.

Lesson 4 of 5.
वमनम् (vamanam) — vomiting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wound, then the Sanskrit for a swelling.

You'll want to know: वमनम्.
वमनम् — vamanam — "vomiting".

The word, taken apart.
vamana comes from the root √vam "vomit" (CDIAL 11295). MW sets √vam beside Greek emeō and Latin vomere, the source of English vomit: one inherited Indo-European root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
उदरपीडा (udarapīḍā) — a stomach ache.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a swelling, then the Sanskrit for vomiting.

You'll want to know: उदरपीडा.
उदरपीडा — udarapīḍā — "a stomach ache".

The word, taken apart.
udarapīḍā is built from udara "belly, stomach", already in the Rigveda, and pīḍā "pain", from the root √pīḍ "press, hurt" (MW): "belly-pain", a stomach ache.
