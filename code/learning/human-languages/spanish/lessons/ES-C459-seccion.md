# la sección — a piece cut off

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-04, ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **cortar** and **el almacén**. A big store has been cut into

## You'll want to know: la sección
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01] -->

**la sección** — the section, the department.

*La sección de deportes* — the sports department. *¿En qué sección está?* —
which part of the shop is it in? *La sección de lácteos* — the dairy aisle.

Beyond shops: a section of a form, of a newspaper, of a report. Anywhere a
whole has been divided and the parts labelled.

Plural **secciones**, and the accent comes off, as it does for every *-ción*.

## Grammar Lens: sección, parte, departamento
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-01] -->

| what it names | the word |
|---|---|
| any piece of a whole | **la parte** |
| a piece somebody divided off on purpose | **la sección** |
| a unit of an organisation | **el departamento** |

*Una parte del libro* could be any stretch of it. *Una sección del libro* is
one somebody marked out and probably numbered. And a *departamento* has staff.

The middle one is the useful new distinction: *sección* always implies a
dividing hand, which is exactly what its root says.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-01, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-04] -->

Latin **secare**, **to cut** — the participle *sectus* gave *sectio*, a
cutting.

So a *sección* is a **cut piece**, and the family is full of cutting: **el
insecto** is an animal cut into segments, which is what you can see on an ant.

A word to keep *out* of the family, because it looks as though it belongs:
**la sierra** is a saw, and also a mountain range, since a ridge of peaks looks
like a saw's teeth. That picture is right, but the word is Latin *serra* and
has nothing to do with *secare*. Two words about cutting, unrelated.

English has **section**, **sector**, **dissect**, **segment** and **insect**
off the same root — and *insect* is the one worth remembering, because nobody
expects a bug to be named after a cut.

*Cortar*, which you already have, comes from a different Latin word —
*curtus*, shortened — so the two are not relatives, and Spanish kept both:
the everyday cutting and the bookish dividing.
