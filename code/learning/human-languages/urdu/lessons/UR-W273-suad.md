# ص (suād) — the letter that opens صبح

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C272-DDAL-01, UR-LEX-C273-SUBAH-01, UR-SCRIPT-C37-ZUAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ڈ and ض once each, and name them. (*ḍāl*, *zuād*.)

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **صبح** (*subah*), "the morning". This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ص
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-SCRIPT-C273-SUAD-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ص** — *suād*, the sound *s*, the same as **س**.

It opens **صبح** (*subah*). You already write **ض** (*zuād*): **ص** is the same body **without its dot**, an oval loop with a short tooth. At the start of a word, as here, the loop joins the next letter; standing alone, it ends in a bowl below the line. Urdu keeps it for words from Arabic.

## Writing: ص
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C273-SUAD-01] -->

Put your pen on ص and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C272-DDAL-01, UR-SCRIPT-C273-SUAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at **صبح** and point at ص inside it]
- [YOU TRACE: ص three times, saying *s* as you finish each one]
- [YOU WRITE: **صبح** once, joined, with no dot on its first letter]
- [YOU READ: **صبح** aloud once more]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C273-SUAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter is this — ص? (**suād**, *s*.) What tells it from ض? (No dot.) Which word did it come from? (**صبح**, *subah*, "the morning".)
