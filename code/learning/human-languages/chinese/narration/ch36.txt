Lesson 1 of 7.
来 (lái) — to come.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sir, then the Mandarin for to live somewhere.

You'll want to know: 来.
来 — lái — "to come".
妈妈来了 māma lái le — Mum's here.
The new character, in pieces: 来 (lái) — one piece built from the top down: a short bar, a dot and a short stroke under it, a long bar, a vertical through it, and two strokes spreading at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
来 (lái) is inherited from Middle Chinese loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to arrive'.

What you've built.
One more word about people.

Lesson 2 of 7.
来 (lái) — one piece, top to bottom.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — dà, shí]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — one piece, top to bottom.
来 (lái).
One piece, three layers, top to bottom.
The top layer, three strokes. A short horizontal. Under it, a dot on the left and a short left-falling stroke on the right, leaning in toward each other.
The middle, two strokes. A long horizontal, then the vertical through the middle of everything, from the very top, through both bars, to the foot.
The foot, two strokes. A left-falling stroke and a right-falling stroke, spreading out from the vertical like the legs of 大.
3 + 2 + 2 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 来 (lái), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many layers in 来 (lái)? (Three.) Which comes first, the dot or the short left-falling stroke? (The dot.) Which character you know ends with the same two spreading strokes? (大.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 来 (lái), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 7.
Měiguó — America; the United States.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to live somewhere, then the Mandarin for to come.

You'll want to know: Měiguó.
Měiguó — "America; the United States".
Měiguó hěn dà — America is huge.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
Měiguó is built from měi 'beautiful' and 国 'country', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mijX and kwok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Měi is usually explained as standing for a syllable of the name America.
