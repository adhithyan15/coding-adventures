# Morphology Spotlight: *clamare* — "to shout, call out"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Back in Unit 1, *me llamo* ("my name is") came from Latin
*clamare*, "to shout." That single word is actually the seed of a whole
family — this unit pulls on that thread properly.

## New Material

**The root**: Latin *clamare*, "to shout, call out." Related to *clamor*
("shouting, uproar") — itself borrowed straight into English unchanged.

**Spanish words built from it**:
- **llamar** — to call (already known, from Unit 1)
- **llamada** — a phone call, a calling-out (the noun form)
- **exclamar** — to exclaim (*ex-* "out" + *clamare* "to shout" — literally
  "to shout out")

**English cognates from the same root**:
- **claim** — originally "to call out, cry out" (as in, loudly assert
  something is yours)
- **exclaim** — same *ex-* + *clamare* compound Spanish has, borrowed
  independently into English
- **acclaim** — *ad-* ("toward") + *clamare* — "to shout praise toward"
- **clamor** — the noun, borrowed directly
- **proclamation** — *pro-* ("forth") + *clamare* — "to call forth publicly"

Notice the pattern: Spanish and English didn't borrow from each other here
— both borrowed independently from the same Latin source, centuries apart,
through different paths (Spanish directly from spoken Latin as it evolved;
English largely through Old French and later direct Latin borrowings in
the Renaissance). That's why they're cousins, not copies — different
spelling, different sound, provably the same root.
