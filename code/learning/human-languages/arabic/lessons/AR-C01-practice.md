# Practice — the Arabic greetings

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] No new words — just the greetings and their replies, and the two
big ideas that made them: **roots** and **al-**.

## What you've built

- **مرحبا** *marḥaban* — casual "welcome / hi" (root r-ḥ-b, *spaciousness*).
- **السلام عليكم** *as-salāmu ʿalaykum* — "peace be upon you" (root s-l-m);
  reply **وعليكم السلام** *wa-ʿalaykum as-salām*.
- **صباح الخير** *ṣabāḥ al-khayr* — "good morning"; reply **صباح النور**
  *ṣabāḥ an-nūr*.
- **مساء الخير** *masāʾ al-khayr* — "good evening"; reply **مساء النور**
  *masāʾ an-nūr*.
- **شكرا** *shukran* — "thanks"; reply **عفوا** *ʿafwan*.

The two engines underneath: (1) the **three-consonant root** (s-l-m → peace/
Islam/Muslim; sh-k-r → thanks/thankful), and (2) **al-** ("the"), which
assimilates before sun letters (*as-salām*, *an-nūr*) but not moon letters
(*al-khayr*) — the same *al-* smuggled into English *algebra* and Spanish
*azúcar*.

## Guided Practice

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: greet + reply, morning: "ṣabāḥ al-khayr" / "ṣabāḥ an-nūr"]
- [YOU SAY: the peace greeting + reply, both halves]

- [YOU SAY: "shukran" and its reply "ʿafwan"]

[PAUSE 3s] Name the two structural ideas this chapter runs on. (The
three-consonant **root**, and the attached article **al-** with sun/moon-letter
assimilation.) Next chapter: introducing yourself — *ismī…* ("my name is…") —
and the pronouns *anta/anti* ("you," and how Arabic marks it by gender).
