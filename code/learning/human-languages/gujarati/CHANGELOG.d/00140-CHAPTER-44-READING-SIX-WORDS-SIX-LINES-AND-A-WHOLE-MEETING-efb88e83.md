## Chapter 44 — reading: six words, six lines, and a whole meeting

Gujarati's reading rung, and the first connected Gujarati the track has asked
anyone to read rather than to write or to say.

Three lessons, ramping 6 -> 16 -> 35 tokens: six signboard words, six lines the
track had only ever said aloud, then those lines in the order two people would
actually use them.

- **No new word in any of the three.** Every Gujarati token was checked to occur
  in a lesson with a lower sequence number.
- The passage is a MEETING rather than a paragraph, and that was forced by what
  the track has taught. A first draft used locatives (શહેરમાં, ઘરમાં), conjugated
  verbs (પીઉં, ખાઉં, વાંચે, પડે) and oblique time words (સવારે, બપોરે) -- sixteen
  unseen forms in one 61-token draft. Gujarati has taught nouns, the copula and
  the joining words, but not case endings, not verb conjugation, and not one
  adjective: મોટું, નાનું, નવું, જૂનું and સુંદર are all still untaught, as are
  અહીં and ત્યાં. What the track CAN read is noun-plus-copula and the greeting
  exchange it has practised since chapter 2.
- So the third lesson is the book's own opening lines read back in conversation
  order, with nothing naming the speakers. Two people, and only the alternation
  says so. The repair line -- માફ કરો, હું સમજતો નથી -- sits eighth on purpose:
  somebody fails to understand and the conversation carries on, which is why the
  book taught the repair before it taught much else.
- Measured against Gujarati's own task shapes, pre-A1 reading moves 0/3 -> 3/3.
  Gujarati declares no A1 shape yet, so there is nothing above this to reach.
- One reinforcement window closes on balance (360 -> 359 misses): the rung
  retrieves signboard words and the opening greetings at a long interval, and
  the three reading skills it introduces sit where only their R1 is judgeable.
