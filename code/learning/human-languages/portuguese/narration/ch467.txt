Portuguese, chapter 467: Orgulho e vaidade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o orgulho — pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a jungle, then the Portuguese for a slipper.

You'll want to know: o orgulho.
o orgulho — "pride".
O meu pai fala do meu trabalho com muito orgulho. My father talks about my work with great pride.
Ter orgulho em alguém (or de alguém) — to be proud of someone.

The word, taken apart.
orgulho came into Old Portuguese from Catalan orgull "pride" (EtymDB), which was borrowed from a Frankish word urgol of the same meaning.

Lesson 4 of 5.
orgulhar — to be proud (orgulhar-se de); to make proud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for vanity, then the Portuguese for vain.

You'll want to know: orgulhar.
orgulhar — "to be proud (orgulhar-se de); to make proud".
A avó orgulha-se muito dos netos. Grandma is very proud of her grandchildren.
Mostly reflexive, with the pronoun after the verb: orgulho-me de ti — I am proud of you. In Brazil: me orgulho.

The word, taken apart.
orgulhar is built on orgulho "pride", which came into Portuguese from Catalan orgull (EtymDB), a borrowing from Frankish.

Lesson 5 of 5.
gabar — to boast (gabar-se); to praise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for vain, then the Portuguese for to be proud.

You'll want to know: gabar.
gabar — "to boast (gabar-se); to praise".
O meu colega gaba-se sempre do carro novo. My colleague is forever boasting about his new car.
Mostly reflexive, with the pronoun after the verb: gabar-se de — to boast about. Without -se, gabar alguém is to praise someone, a less common use.

The word, taken apart.
gabar comes from Occitan gabar "to boast" (EtymDB), which EtymDB traces to Old Norse gabb, a word usually glossed "mockery".
