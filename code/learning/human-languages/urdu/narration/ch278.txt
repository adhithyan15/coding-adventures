5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
داروغہ (dāroghā) — a police inspector.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for on the run, then the Urdu for a criminal.

You'll want to know: داروغہ.
داروغہ — dāroghā — "a police inspector".
داروغہ تھانے میں نہیں تھا۔ dāroghā thāne meṅ nahīṅ thā. The inspector was not at the police station.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
is usually traced to Persian dārogha, "an overseer", a word Persian is thought to have taken from Mongolian. In a town the داروغہ (dāroghā) runs the تھانہ.

Lesson 2 of 5.
رپورٹ (riporṭ) — a report (to the police).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a criminal, then the Urdu for a police inspector.

You'll want to know: رپورٹ.
رپورٹ — riporṭ — "a report (to the police)".
پولیس نے رپورٹ لکھی۔ polīs ne riporṭ likhī. The police wrote up a report.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رپورٹ (riporṭ) is borrowed from English report. At the police station it is the written account of a crime, and it is feminine: رپورٹ لکھی.

Lesson 3 of 5.
درج کرنا (darj karnā) — to register (in a record).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a police inspector, then the Urdu for a report (to the police).

You'll want to know: درج کرنا.
درج کرنا — darj karnā — "to register (in a record)".
داروغے نے رپورٹ درج کی۔ dāroghe ne riporṭ darj kī. The inspector registered the report.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے رپورٹ درج کی, us ne riporṭ darj kī.

The word, taken apart.
درج کرنا (darj karnā) is built from darj, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic darj, "a roll, a written record", and کرنا: to put a thing on the record. The thing written agrees: رپورٹ (riporṭ) درج کی.

Lesson 4 of 5.
حوالات (havālāt) — a lock-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a report (to the police), then the Urdu for to register.

You'll want to know: حوالات.
حوالات — havālāt — "a lock-up".
چور رات بھر حوالات میں رہا۔ chor rāt bhar havālāt meṅ rahā. The thief spent the whole night in the lock-up.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
حوالات (havālāt) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḥawāla, "a handing over, custody", in a plural form: the room at the police station where a person handed over is held for a night or two.
