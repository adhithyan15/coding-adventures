## You'll want to know: 西医
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C231-DOCTOR231-02]; assesses=[] -->

**西医** — *xīyī* — "western medicine; a doctor of it".

> **我身体不好头也疼想看西医** *wǒ shēntǐ bù hǎo tóu yě téng xiǎng kàn xīyī* — I'm not well and my head hurts too; I'd like to see a doctor of western medicine.

The new character, in pieces: 西 — six strokes: a bar on top, a box of two strokes, two strokes inside it that start above it, and a bar along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**西医** (*xīyī*) is built from 西 'west' and 医 'medicine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *sej* and *'i* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as a bird settling on its nest when the sun is in the west.
