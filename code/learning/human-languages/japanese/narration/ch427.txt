Lesson 1 of 5.
きんちょうする (kinchō suru) — to get nervous; to be tense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for refreshed, then the Japanese for to endure.

You'll want to know: きんちょうする.
きんちょうする — kinchō suru — "to get nervous; to be tense".
しあいの まえに きんちょうしました。 — shiai no mae ni kinchō shimashita — "I got nervous before the match."
Polite きんちょうします, kinchōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せんしゅが きんちょうしました。, senshu ga kinchō shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
きんちょうする (kinchō suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango) kinchō with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kin, "tense" (Middle Chinese kjinX) and chō, "stretch" (trjang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ためらう (tamerau) — to hesitate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be restless, then the Japanese for to get irritated.

You'll want to know: ためらう.
ためらう — tamerau — "to hesitate".
かれは へんじを ためらいました。 — kare wa henji o tameraimashita — "He hesitated to reply."
Polite ためらいます, tameraimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ともだちが すこし ためらいました。, tomodachi ga sukoshi tameraimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ためらう (tamerau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana; its kanji spelling borrows two characters that each mean "hesitate", chosen for the meaning and not the sound (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
