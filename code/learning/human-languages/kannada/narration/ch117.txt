You'll want to know: ಸುತ್ತಿಗೆ.

The word, taken apart.
ಸುತ್ತಿಗೆ (suttige) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil cutti and Telugu sutte (DEDR 2668).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಕತ್ತರಿ (kattari) — scissors.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a nail (to hammer), then the Kannada for a hammer.

You'll want to know: ಕತ್ತರಿ.
ಕತ್ತರಿ — kattari — "scissors".

The word, taken apart.
ಕತ್ತರಿ (kattari) comes from Sanskrit kartarī, "scissors, knife", reshaped to Kannada sounds: a tadbhava (CDIAL 2858). The rt became tt, as in Prakrit kattarī.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a hammer, then the Kannada for scissors.

You'll want to know: ಬಾಚಣಿಗೆ.
ಬಾಚಣಿಗೆ — bācaṇige — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
ಬಾಚಣಿಗೆ (bācaṇige) is native Dravidian, from bācu, "to comb", and DEDR 5357 lists bācaṇige, "a comb", itself: the old v- has become b-; the entry also has Tamil vār. DEDR 5357 compares Sanskrit vārakīra.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಸಾಬೂನು (sābūnu) — soap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for scissors, then the Kannada for a comb.

You'll want to know: ಸಾಬೂನು.
ಸಾಬೂನು — sābūnu — "soap".

The word, taken apart.
ಸಾಬೂನು (sābūnu) is a loan from Arabic ṣābūn, "soap", which came through Persian and Hindustani. Kannada adds -u at the end.
