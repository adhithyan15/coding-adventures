Arabic, chapter 508: Skin trouble.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
طفح جلدي (ṭafḥ jildī) — a rash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to vomit, then the Arabic for diarrhoea.

You'll want to know: طفح جلدي.
طفح جلدي — ṭafḥ jildī — "a rash".
ظهر طفح جلدي على يد الطفل. ẓahara ṭafḥ jildī ʿalā yad aṭ-ṭifl. A rash appeared on the child's hand.
Often shortened to طفح.

The word, taken apart.
طفح جلدي (ṭafḥ jildī) is built on two native Arabic roots: ṭafḥ from ṭ-f-ḥ, "to fill to the brim, to overflow" (Lane), and jildī, "of the skin", from jild, skin, which you know. A rash is the skin brimming over.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

الحكة تزعجني في الليل. al-ḥikka tuzʿijunī fī al-layl. The itching bothers me at night.

The word, taken apart.
حكة (ḥikka) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-k, "to rub, to scratch" (Lane). Ḥikka is the itch that makes you scratch.

حك (ḥakka) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-k, "to rub, to scratch" (Lane). Its last two root letters are the same, so they write as one doubled letter.

Lesson 4 of 5.
التهاب (iltihāb) — an inflammation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an itch, then the Arabic for to scratch.

You'll want to know: التهاب.
التهاب — iltihāb — "an inflammation".
عند أختي التهاب في الحلق. ʿinda ukhtī iltihāb fī al-ḥalq. My sister has a sore throat.
التهاب (iltihāb) الحلق is a sore throat.

The word, taken apart.
التهاب (iltihāb) is built on the native Arabic root l-h-b, "a flame, to blaze" (Lane). Iltihāb is the verbal noun of Form VIII, a flaring up: the body burning in one spot.
