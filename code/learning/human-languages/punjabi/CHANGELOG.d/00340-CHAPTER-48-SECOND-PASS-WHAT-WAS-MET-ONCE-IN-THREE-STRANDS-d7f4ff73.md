### Forty atoms, seventeen segments, nowhere to put them

The criterion asks for two revisits per atom; eleven of Punjabi's forty had
none, so the true cost was **51 retrievals**, not 40.

What decided the shape was not that number but where the atoms live. They sit on
**seventeen different path segments**, from rank 110 to rank 470, sequences 230
to 2160. There is no chapter anywhere in the book that comes after enough of
them to be worth placing a review in. So the tranche is a **new chapter 48 at
the end**, in three strands:

- **the form-field ladder** — three lessons
- **the Gurmukhi pieces** — two lessons
- **the courtesy and parting words** — one lesson

Three path segments rather than one, because a lesson's `spine_node` must equal
its segment's and the three strands serve three different nodes.

### The form ladder is the part worth reading

Every A1 field in this book was taught three times, and the three are not
repetition — they are three amounts of help:

| stage | what is on the page | what it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **supported** | a visible bank of values to copy | you can place a value in a field |
| **delayed** | the bank is shown, then covered | you can hold it while you write |
| **no model** | a label, a blank, nothing else | you can produce it |

Only the third is writing evidence. Three fields multiplied by three rungs is
nine separate moves, and the third lesson runs them **across** rather than down
— all three fields at the supported rung, then all three delayed, then all three
cold. Read that way the ladder stops being about forms at all: it is about what
is in the room when you produce something.

Residence is the only field that also teaches **repair** — change the one
dimension that is wrong and leave the rest standing — which is what a real form
demands and what a learner who can only write from scratch will get wrong.

### The Gurmukhi strand

Fifteen pieces met once. Sorted by what kind of thing each is rather than by when
it appeared: four standing shapes (**ਈ ਇ ਉ**, and **ਆ**, which is a letter
wearing a fused mark), a consonant (**ਠ**), and the marks that cannot stand at
all — **ੋ** above, **ੈ** above, **ੁ** below. Where a mark sits is how you tell
it from another at a glance, and **ਆ** is the counter-example that makes the rule
visible.

### The courtesy strand

Punjabi's two commonest courtesies are not words for *please* and *sorry*. They
are things done: **ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ ਕਰਕੇ** is *having done a kindness*, **ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰੋ** is
*make pardoned*, and **ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ** carries the same dative ending the book uses
elsewhere — the welcome is grammatically *addressed* to the arrivals. Three
partings in three registers: *rabb rākhā* between people who know each other,
*shubh rāt* formal and Sanskritic, and the everyday sign-off neither.
