Script: the whole of Sanskrit punctuation.
। — the daṇḍa, "the stick".
One vertical stroke, drawn from the headline down. It marks the end of a sentence, and it is very nearly the entire punctuation system of classical Sanskrit.
सः भारतात् आगच्छति। यः गच्छति सः पश्यति।.
Doubled — ॥ — it closes a verse, and the pair of strokes is what you see at the end of every line of a printed śloka.
What is worth noticing is everything the list does not contain. Classical Sanskrit has no comma, no colon, no brackets, no quotation marks, no question mark and no exclamation mark. Where English reaches for punctuation, Sanskrit reaches for a word: you met इति two chapters ago closing a quotation that no quotation mark was ever going to close, and किम् at the front of a sentence does the work of a question mark.
That is not a gap in the writing system. It is a different division of labour — the grammar carries what the marks carry in English, and one stick is enough for the rest.

Writing: ।.
1. draw a single vertical stroke top-to-bottom, the height of a letter, immediately after the last letter of the sentence.
Pen lifts: 0. The stroke is one movement and there is nothing to add to it.
The daṇḍa is a mark of punctuation rather than a letter: it carries no sound, takes no vowel sign, and never joins to what stands beside it.
