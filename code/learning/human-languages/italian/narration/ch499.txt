Italian, chapter 499: Litigi e tradimenti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il litigio — argument, quarrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to look after, then the Italian for to obey.

You'll want to know: il litigio.
il litigio — "argument, quarrel".
Abbiamo avuto un litigio per i soldi. We had an argument about money.
The noun for litigare.

The word, taken apart.
litigio is a learned borrowing from Latin lītigium "a quarrel" (EtymDB), from lītigāre "to dispute", built on līs, lītis "a lawsuit".

You'll want to know: riconciliarsi.
riconciliarsi — "to make up, to be reconciled".
Dopo il litigio ci siamo riconciliati. After the argument we made up.
In speech fare pace is more common.

The word, taken apart.
riconciliarsi comes from Latin reconciliāre "to bring together again" (Lewis & Short), built on conciliāre "to unite".

Lesson 3 of 5.
tradire — to betray; to cheat on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for argument, then the Italian for to make up.

You'll want to know: tradire.
tradire — "to betray; to cheat on".
Si sentiva tradita dalla sua migliore amica. She felt betrayed by her best friend.
Tradire il partner — to be unfaithful. An -isc- verb: tradisco.

The word, taken apart.
tradire is inherited from Latin trādere "to hand over, to betray" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la fedeltà — faithfulness, loyalty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to make up, then the Italian for to betray.

You'll want to know: la fedeltà.
la fedeltà — "faithfulness, loyalty".
Per me la fedeltà è importante in una coppia. For me faithfulness is important in a couple.
The adjective is fedele; the wedding ring is la fede.

The word, taken apart.
fedeltà is inherited from Latin fidēlitātem "faithfulness" (EtymDB), from fidēs "trust".

Lesson 5 of 5.
confidarsi — to confide (in someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to betray, then the Italian for faithfulness.

You'll want to know: confidarsi.
confidarsi — "to confide (in someone)".
Mi confido solo con mia sorella. I only confide in my sister.
Confidarsi con qualcuno. A secret you tell is una confidenza.

The word, taken apart.
confidarsi is usually traced to Vulgar Latin cōnfīdāre, from Latin cōnfīdere "to trust fully" (Lewis & Short), built on fīdere "to trust".
