Spanish, chapter 34: Hablamos --- the First Form That Is Not About One Person.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
hablamos — "we speak".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I speak", then "you speak" to a friend. (Hablo. Hablas.)

Grammar Lens: the first form that is not about one person.
Every verb form you have met so far — in every tense — has been about one person. That changes here.
Drop the -ar, add -amos:
hablamos — we speak.
One word again, no pronoun, and now it covers you and somebody else.
This is the form you will reach for constantly and have not had: we speak, we work, we study. Everything you could say about yourself, you can now say about a group you belong to.

The word, taken apart.
The -mos is remarkable for how little happened to it.
Latin marked we with -mus: amāmus, "we love". Spanish says -mos. One vowel changed in two thousand years, and nothing else — no consonant lost, no syllable worn away, no analogy pulling it out of shape.
Compare what became of the third person, which lost its -t entirely (hablat becomes habla). Endings do not decay at the same rate, and this one barely decayed at all.
And Portuguese and Italian kept it too — falamos, parliamo. If you ever meet another Romance language, -mos or -mo will be waiting for you, doing the same job.
