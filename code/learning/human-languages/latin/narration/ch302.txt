Latin, chapter 302: Speculātōrēs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
speculātor, speculātōris — a spy, a lookout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to grow stronger, then the Latin for to assert.

You'll want to know: speculātor, speculātōris.
speculātor, speculātōris — "a spy, a lookout".
Speculātor noctū in castra hostium vēnit. A spy came into the enemy camp by night.
Masculine, 3rd declension. A soldier sent ahead to watch. English speculator now means someone who gambles on prices.

The word, taken apart.
speculātor is built on speculor, "to keep watch", from specula, "a lookout", which belongs with speciō, "to look" (Lewis & Short). English speculate is from the same verb.

Explōrātōrēs nūntiāvērunt hostēs prope flūmen esse. The scouts reported that the enemy were near the river.
Masculine, 3rd declension. In Caesar the explōrātōrēs went ahead of the army.

The word, taken apart.
explōrātor is built on explōrō, "to search out" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor for the one who does it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
explōrō, explōrāre — to search out, to explore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a spy, then the Latin for a scout.

You'll want to know: explōrō, explōrāre.
explōrō, explōrāre — "to search out, to explore".
Mīlitēs silvam explōrāvērunt, sed nēminem invēnērunt. The soldiers searched the forest, but found nobody.
explōrō, explōrāre, explōrāvī, explōrātum. English explore.

The word, taken apart.
explōrō is usually traced to ex and plōrō, "to cry out", perhaps first the shout of hunters driving game out of cover; the details are not settled. English explore comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
prōdō, prōdere — to betray; to hand down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a scout, then the Latin for to search out.

You'll want to know: prōdō, prōdere.
prōdō, prōdere — "to betray; to hand down".
Servus dominum hostibus prōdidit. The slave betrayed his master to the enemy.
prōdō, prōdere, prōdidī, prōditum: to give forth. Also 'to hand down': memoriae prōdere, to record for later ages.

The word, taken apart.
prōdō is built on dō, "to give", with prō, "forth" (Lewis & Short): to give forth, to give up, and so to betray.

patefaciō, patefacere — "to open up; to disclose".
Ūna epistula tōtum cōnsilium patefēcit. A single letter disclosed the whole plan.
patefaciō, patefacere, patefēcī, patefactum. Cicero uses it of plots brought to light.

The word, taken apart.
patefaciō joins pateō, "to be open", and faciō, "to make" (EtymDB): to make open.
