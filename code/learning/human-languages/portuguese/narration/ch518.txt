Portuguese, chapter 518: Cantar em coro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o coro — choir; chorus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for accordion, then the Portuguese for to tune.

You'll want to know: o coro.
o coro — "choir; chorus".
Canto num coro há dez anos. I have sung in a choir for ten years.
Em coro — all together.

The word, taken apart.
coro is inherited through Old Portuguese (EtymDB) from Latin chorus, from Greek khorós "a dance, a band of dancers and singers".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: a melodia.
a melodia — "melody, tune".
Esta melodia não me sai da cabeça. I cannot get this tune out of my head.
The adjective is melodioso.

The word, taken apart.
melodia comes from Latin melōdia (EtymDB), from Greek melōidía "singing", built on mélos "song".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o refrão — chorus, refrain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for choir, then the Portuguese for melody.

You'll want to know: o refrão.
o refrão — "chorus, refrain".
Toda a gente canta o refrão. Everybody sings the chorus.
The part of a song that comes back.

The word, taken apart.
refrão came through Spanish refrán from Old French refrain (EtymDB), built on a verb from Latin frangere "to break": the song breaks off and starts again.

Lesson 5 of 5.
assobiar — to whistle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for chorus, then the Portuguese for to hum.

You'll want to know: assobiar.
assobiar — "to whistle".
O carteiro assobia a mesma canção todas as manhãs. The postman whistles the same song every morning.
A whistle with the mouth is um assobio.

The word, taken apart.
assobiar is usually traced to Latin sībilāre "to hiss, to whistle" (Lewis & Short).
