# శ · ఫ — two letters, one reason

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-159] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Without looking back: name the two days of the week that open on the
same letter, and say which letter it is.

## Script: both are here to keep a difference visible
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-159, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160] -->

> శ · ఫ

These two arrived chapters apart and for the same reason. Each is the shape
Telugu spends so that a difference you can hear is a difference you can see.

| letter | what it splits off |
|---|---|
| శ *śa* | a third s, beside స and its curled neighbour |
| ఫ *pha* | ప with the breath let out after it |

A script that skipped either one would still be readable — the reader would
work the sound out from the word. Telugu declines to make the reader do that.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-159, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU LOOK: at శుక్రవారం and శనివారం, and put a finger on the shared letter]
- [YOU SAY: ప, then ఫ, with a hand in front of your mouth — feel which one moves it]
- [YOU TRACE: each of the two once, saying its sound]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-159, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter opens both Friday and Saturday? (**శ**.) What does ఫ do
to ప? (**Lets the breath out after it.**) And what do the two letters have in
common? (**Each exists so a heard difference is also a written one.**)
