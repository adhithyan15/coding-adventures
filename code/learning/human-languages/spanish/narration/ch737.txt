Spanish, chapter 737: Del codo a la cadera.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el codo — the elbow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the beard, then the Spanish for the ear.

You'll want to know: el codo.
el codo — "the elbow".
Me di un golpe en el codo. I banged my elbow.
Codo con codo — side by side.

The word, taken apart.
codo is inherited from Latin cubitus "elbow" (EtymDB), through Old Spanish cobdo.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la muñeca — the wrist; the doll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the ear, then the Spanish for the elbow.

You'll want to know: la muñeca.
la muñeca — "the wrist; the doll".
Me duele la muñeca de tanto escribir. My wrist hurts from so much writing.
Two senses: the wrist and a doll.

The word, taken apart.
For muñeca the origin is not settled. Old Spanish munneca meant a doll (EtymDB); the wrist sense may come from the knob of bone, but no source is sure.

You'll want to know: la cadera.
la cadera — "the hip".
Mi abuela se rompió la cadera. My grandmother broke her hip.

The word, taken apart.
cadera is inherited from Latin cathedra "a seat, a chair", through spoken cathegra; the Latin came from Greek kathédra (EtymDB). The hips are what you sit on.

You'll want to know: la costilla.
la costilla — "the rib".
Me duele una costilla al respirar. One of my ribs hurts when I breathe.
Las costillas de cerdo — pork ribs.

The word, taken apart.
costilla comes from Late Latin costula "little rib" (EtymDB), a small form of costa "rib, side", the source of costa "coast".
