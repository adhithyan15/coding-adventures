French, chapter 311: Un grand jour.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fiancer — to get engaged (as se fiancer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the start of the school year, then the French for of age.

You'll want to know: fiancer.
fiancer — "to get engaged (as se fiancer)".
Ils se sont fiancés l'été dernier. They got engaged last summer.
Mostly reflexive: se fiancer avec quelqu'un. The person is le fiancé, la fiancée.

The word, taken apart.
fiancer is built on Old French fiance "trust, a promise", from fier "to trust", which goes back to Latin fīdus "faithful" (Lewis & Short).

la demande en mariage — "the marriage proposal".
Il a fait sa demande en mariage au restaurant. He proposed at the restaurant.
Demander quelqu'un en mariage — to propose to someone.

The word, taken apart.
mariage is built on marier, inherited from Latin marītāre "to wed" (EtymDB); demande is built on demander, from Latin dēmandāre "to entrust" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
marier — to get married (as se marier); to marry (a couple), to marry off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to get engaged, then the French for the marriage proposal.

You'll want to know: marier.
marier — "to get married (as se marier); to marry (a couple), to marry off".
Ils se sont mariés à la mairie. They got married at the town hall.
Reflexive for the couple: ils se marient en juin. Without se, the mayor marries them: le maire les a mariés.

The word, taken apart.
marier is inherited from Latin marītāre "to wed" (EtymDB), built on marītus "husband".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la pièce montée — the tiered wedding cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the marriage proposal, then the French for to get married.

You'll want to know: la pièce montée.
la pièce montée — "the tiered wedding cake".
À la fin du repas, on a servi la pièce montée. At the end of the meal, the wedding cake was served.
A tower of small cream puffs, a classic of French weddings.

The word, taken apart.
pièce is inherited from Late Latin pettia "a piece", of Gaulish origin (EtymDB); montée is built on monter "to go up", from Latin mōns "mountain" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to get married, then the French for the tiered wedding cake.

You'll want to know: la dragée.
la dragée — "the sugared almond".
Les invités ont reçu des dragées. The guests were given sugared almonds.
Given at weddings and christenings in France and Belgium.

The word, taken apart.
dragée comes from Old French dragie (EtymDB), usually traced to Latin tragēmata "sweets, dessert", a borrowing from Greek.
