Hindi, chapter 509: Trouble at the Factory.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हड़ताल (haṛtāl) — a strike (stopping work).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for admission (to a school or course), then the Hindi for a salary, pay (formal).

You'll want to know: हड़ताल.
हड़ताल — haṛtāl — "a strike (stopping work)".
कारख़ाने के मज़दूर तीन दिन से हड़ताल पर हैं। Kārkhāne ke mazdūr tīn din se haṛtāl par haiṁ. The factory workers have been on strike for three days.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
हड़ताल (haṛtāl) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit haṭṭa, "a market, a shop", with the Hindi sense "closing shops in mourning or protest" (CDIAL 13944). Today it is any stopping of work.

Lesson 3 of 5.
चंदा (candā) — a contribution; a collection (of money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a strike (stopping work), then the Hindi for a resignation.

You'll want to know: चंदा.
चंदा — candā — "a contribution; a collection (of money)".
मज़दूरों ने एक बीमार साथी के लिए चंदा दिया। Mazdūroṁ ne ek bīmār sāthī ke liye candā diyā. The workers chipped in money for a sick companion.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
चंदा (candā) is usually traced to Persian chanda, "a contribution", built on chand, "how many, how much": what each person puts in. It is not the candā, "moon", that CDIAL derives from Sanskrit candra (CDIAL 4661).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a resignation, then the Hindi for a contribution.

You'll want to know: मनमानी.
मनमानी — manmānī — "high-handedness; doing as one pleases".
मालिक की मनमानी से सब मज़दूर परेशान हैं। Mālik kī manmānī se sab mazdūr pareśān haiṁ. All the workers are fed up with the owner's high-handedness.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
मनमानी (manmānī) joins man, "mind", from Sanskrit manas (CDIAL 9822), and a form of mānnā, "to respect, obey", from Sanskrit manyate, "thinks fit" (CDIAL 9857): doing only what one's own mind will have.

Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
नारा (nārā) is usually traced to Arabic naʿra, "a shout, a cry", through Persian; Hindi uses it for the words a crowd shouts together.
