Japanese, chapter 233: Swimming and Running.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
あそぶ (asobu) — to play; to have fun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to refuse, then the Japanese for to inform.

You'll want to know: あそぶ.
あそぶ — asobu — "to play; to have fun".
きのう こうえんで ともだちと あそびました。 — kinō kōen de tomodachi to asobimashita — "Yesterday I played with friends in the park."
Polite あそびます, asobimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
あそぶ (asobu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
すいえい (suiei) — swimming; swimming as a sport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to play, then the Japanese for to swim.

You'll want to know: すいえい.
すいえい — suiei — "swimming; swimming as a sport".
なつやすみに すいえいを ならいました。 — natsuyasumi ni suiei o naraimashita — "I took swimming lessons in the summer holidays."

The word, taken apart.
すいえい (suiei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "water" (Middle Chinese sywijX) and ei, "swim" (hjwaengH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
