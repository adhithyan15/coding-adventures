# du solltest — you should

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERBKLAMMER-01, GE-LEX-KOENNEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I can take the umbrella". (*Ich kann den Regenschirm
mitnehmen.*) The modal second, the infinitive at the end.

## You'll want to know: du solltest
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-DU-SOLLTEST-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C148-VERB148-05, GE-LEX-C116-DINGE116-05] -->

**du solltest** — "you should": *doo ZOLL-test*.

> *Du solltest den Regenschirm mitnehmen.* — You should take the umbrella.

It is a modal, so it builds the same bracket as *kann*: *solltest* second,
the infinitive last. Its forms: *ich sollte*, *du solltest*, *er sollte*,
*wir sollten*, *Sie sollten*.

*Solltest* looks like a past, and it is spelled exactly like the
Präteritum of *sollen*. Here it is the **Konjunktiv II**, the mood of "it
would be good if". That is what makes it advice. The plain present, *du
sollst*, is stronger: it passes on an order or a duty, "you are to".

## Grammar Lens: man sollte, and two verbs at the end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-MAN-SOLLTE-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-DASS-VERB-ENDE-01, GE-LEX-C193-THING193-01] -->

For advice to everybody, German uses **man**, "one, people":

> *Man sollte den Fahrplan lesen.* — One should read the timetable.

*Man* takes the *er* form of the verb: *man sollte*, like *er sollte*.

After *dass* or *weil*, both verbs go to the end, and the modal comes
last of all:

> *Ich finde, dass du den Regenschirm mitnehmen* **solltest**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-ENTSCHULDIGUNG-03, GE-LEX-KONNTE-01] -->

**sollen** ← Old High German *sculan* ← Proto-Germanic *\*skulaną*, "to
owe" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB sets English *shall* and *should* beside it:
*should* is the past of *shall*, as *solltest* is of *sollen*. You met the
same root in *Entschuldigung*, inside *Schuld*, a debt or a fault: what you
should do is what you owe.
