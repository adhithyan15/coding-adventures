## You'll want to know: 我们
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C31-PEOPLE31-04]; assesses=[] -->

**我们** — *wǒmen* — "we; us".

> *wǒmen hái xíng* — We're doing all right.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**我们** (*wǒmen*) is built from 我 'I' and the plural ending men. 我 is inherited from Middle Chinese *ngaX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary glosses as what you call yourself; no dictionary we use records an older form of the ending, so its history is not settled.
