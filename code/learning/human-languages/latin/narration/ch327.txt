Latin, chapter 327: Spōnsālia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
spōnsa, spōnsae — a bride-to-be, a fiancée.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for orphaned, then the Latin for a ward.

You'll want to know: spōnsa, spōnsae.
spōnsa, spōnsae — "a bride-to-be, a fiancée".
Spōnsa eius ex oppidō vīcīnō erat. His bride-to-be was from a neighbouring town.
Feminine, a participle: a girl promised. Her man is the spōnsus. English spouse.

The word, taken apart.
spōnsa is the feminine participle of spondeō, "to promise solemnly" (EtymDB): she who has been promised. English spouse comes from it through French.

Ad spōnsālia multī amīcī vēnērunt. Many friends came to the engagement party.
Neuter, plural only. A ring was often given at the spōnsālia.

The word, taken apart.
spōnsālia is built on spōnsālis, "of a betrothal", from spōnsa (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
dēspondeō, dēspondēre — to promise in marriage, to betroth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bride-to-be, then the Latin for a betrothal.

You'll want to know: dēspondeō, dēspondēre.
dēspondeō, dēspondēre — "to promise in marriage, to betroth".
Pater fīliam suam amīcō dēspondit. The father promised his daughter in marriage to a friend.
dēspondeō, dēspondēre, dēspondī, dēspōnsum. Animum dēspondēre: to lose heart, which gives English despond.

The word, taken apart.
dēspondeō is built on spondeō, "to promise solemnly", with dē- (EtymDB): to promise away. English despond comes from its use with animum, "to lose heart".

Lesson 4 of 5.
nūpta, nūptae — a bride, a married woman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a betrothal, then the Latin for to promise in marriage.

You'll want to know: nūpta, nūptae.
nūpta, nūptae — "a bride, a married woman".
Nūpta ad domum marītī ducta est. The bride was led to her husband's house.
Feminine, the participle of nūbō (taught), said of the woman who marries. Nova nūpta: the new bride.

The word, taken apart.
nūpta is the feminine participle of nūbō, "to marry" (EtymDB), which is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to wed". English nuptial comes from the same family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mātrimōnium, mātrimōniī — marriage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to promise in marriage, then the Latin for a bride.

You'll want to know: mātrimōnium, mātrimōniī.
mātrimōnium, mātrimōniī — "marriage".
Puellam in mātrimōnium dūxit. He married the girl.
Neuter. In mātrimōnium dūcere: (of a man) to marry. English matrimony.

The word, taken apart.
mātrimōnium is built on māter, "mother", with the ending -mōnium (EtymDB): the state of a mother, of a wife. English matrimony comes from it.
