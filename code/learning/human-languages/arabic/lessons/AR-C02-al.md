# ال (al-) — "the," the one Arabic word hiding in English *algebra*

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] A tiny word — two letters, ال — that you have said hundreds of
times in English without knowing it. Every "al-" word you own in English is a
smuggled piece of Arabic.

## You'll want to know first

- [سلام (salām)](./AR-C02-salam.md) — you'll attach *al-* to it next lesson.

## Sounds you'll need

- `rtl` — ال = *alif* + *lām* = **al-**, glued to the *front* of a noun (not a
  separate word). → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart

**ال** (**al-**) — "the." Unlike English "the," it's **attached** to its noun:
*al-kitāb* = "the book," written الكتاب as one unit.

**The English cousin web is enormous — and backwards.** For ~800 years, Arabic
was the language of science and trade around the Mediterranean, and Spanish
and English absorbed thousands of Arabic words *with the al- still stuck on*:

- **al**gebra ← *al-jabr* ("the restoring")
- **al**cohol ← *al-kuḥl*
- **al**gorithm ← *al-Khwārizmī* (a mathematician's name)
- **al**chemy ← *al-kīmiyāʾ*
- **al**manac, **al**cove, **al**kali…

So when you learn Arabic *al-*, you're meeting an old friend: it's the *al-* in
front of half the science vocabulary in English.

## Grammar Lens: sun letters (why *al-salām* becomes *as-salām*)

One rule you'll hear immediately. When *al-* is followed by certain
consonants — the **"sun letters"** (including س *s*, ش *sh*, ت *t*, ر *r*, ن
*n*, د *d*) — the *l* of *al-* **assimilates** into that letter, doubling it:

- *al- + salām* → **as-salām** (not "al-salām") — "the peace"
- *al- + shams* ("sun") → **ash-shams**

Other consonants (the "moon letters," like خ *kh*, ق *q*, ك *k*) keep the *l*:
*al-khayr* stays *al-khayr*. You can even *hear* this rule inside Spanish: the
Arabic *as-sukkar* ("the sugar," sun-letter *s* assimilated) became Spanish
**azúcar** — the doubled-*s* is right there.
