French, chapter 368: À une condition.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
à condition que — provided that, on condition that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for plausible, then the French for the supposition.

You'll want to know: à condition que.
à condition que — "provided that, on condition that".
Tu peux sortir, à condition que tu sois à la maison avant minuit. You can go out, on condition that you are home before midnight.
Takes the subjunctive: que tu sois. With the same subject, à condition de + infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
condition comes through Old French from Latin condiciō "an agreement, terms, a condition" (EtymDB), from condīcere "to talk over, to agree on", built on dīcere "to say" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
pourvu que — as long as, provided that; let us hope that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the supposition, then the French for provided that.

You'll want to know: pourvu que.
pourvu que — "as long as, provided that; let us hope that".
Nous irons à la plage, pourvu qu'il fasse beau. We will go to the beach, as long as the weather is nice.
Takes the subjunctive: qu'il fasse. At the start of a sentence it is a wish: Pourvu qu'il vienne! — let us hope he comes.

The word, taken apart.
pourvu is the past participle of pourvoir "to provide", which comes from Latin prōvidēre "to foresee, to provide for" (Lewis & Short), built on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 3 of 5.
à moins que — unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for provided that, then the French for as long as.

You'll want to know: à moins que.
à moins que — "unless".
Je viendrai à pied, à moins qu'il ne pleuve. I will come on foot, unless it rains.
Takes the subjunctive, often with a ne that is not a negative: à moins qu'il ne pleuve. With the same subject, à moins de + infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
moins is inherited through Old French meins from Latin minus "less" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): at less than this.

Lesson 4 of 5.
au cas où — in case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for as long as, then the French for unless.

You'll want to know: au cas où.
au cas où — "in case".
Je prends un parapluie, au cas où il pleuvrait. I am taking an umbrella in case it rains.
Takes the conditional, not the subjunctive: au cas où il pleuvrait.

The word, taken apart.
cas comes through Old French cas from Latin cāsus "a falling, a chance event" (EtymDB), from cadere "to fall" (Lewis & Short): what may befall.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sinon — otherwise, or else.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for unless, then the French for in case.

You'll want to know: sinon.
sinon — "otherwise, or else".
Dépêche-toi, sinon nous allons manquer le train. Hurry up, or else we will miss the train.
It can also mean if not, apart from: personne, sinon lui.

The word, taken apart.
sinon is built from si "if" and non "not", which are inherited from Latin sī "if" and nōn "not" (Lewis & Short): if not.
