# weiß, taken apart

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-WEISS-WHITE-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WEISS-01, GE-SOUND-WEISS-01] -->

*Weiß* comes from Germanic \**hwītaz*, and so does English **white**. Not a
cousin, not a borrowing — the **same word**, and neither language moved its
meaning by a hair.

What separates them is spelling and nothing else:

| | German | English |
|---|---|---|
| the vowel | *ei* | *i* |
| the sharp *s* | **ß** | *t* plus a silent *e* |

That is the whole distance. *Weiß* and *white* look unrelated on the page and
are identical underneath, which is a useful warning about judging these pairs by
eye: **schwarz**/*swarthy* looked close and had drifted in meaning, while
*weiß*/*white* looks distant and has not drifted at all.
