Italian, chapter 397: Garanzie e fatture.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la garanzia — the guarantee, the warranty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the payment, then the Italian for the deposit.

You'll want to know: la garanzia.
la garanzia — "the guarantee, the warranty".
Se si rompe, è ancora in garanzia. If it breaks, it is still under warranty.
In garanzia — under warranty.

The word, taken apart.
garanzia is built on garante "guarantor", which came from French garant, a word of Germanic, probably Frankish, origin; EtymDB sets it beside Old High German werento "guarantor" (EtymDB), but the exact form behind it is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la fattura — the invoice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the guarantee, then the Italian for to guarantee.

You'll want to know: la fattura.
la fattura — "the invoice".
Se mi fa la fattura, posso scaricare la spesa. If you give me an invoice, I can deduct the cost.
Fare la fattura — to issue an invoice. It can also mean a spell or a curse.

The word, taken apart.
fattura is inherited from Latin factūra "a making, a piece of work" (Lewis & Short), from facere "to make": the record of the work done.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il preventivo — the quote, the estimate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to guarantee, then the Italian for the invoice.

You'll want to know: il preventivo.
il preventivo — "the quote, the estimate".
Prima di cominciare, l'idraulico ci fa un preventivo. Before starting, the plumber gives us a quote.
Chiedere un preventivo — to ask for a quote.

The word, taken apart.
preventivo is a learned formation on Latin praevent-, from praevenīre "to come before, to anticipate" (Lewis & Short), built on venīre "to come": the cost reckoned beforehand.

Lesson 5 of 5.
conveniente — good value, cheap; worthwhile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the invoice, then the Italian for the quote.

You'll want to know: conveniente.
conveniente — "good value, cheap; worthwhile".
Il treno è più conveniente della macchina. The train is better value than the car.
A false friend: it does not mean convenient (that is comodo).

The word, taken apart.
conveniente comes from Latin conveniēns "agreeing, suitable" (EtymDB), present participle of convenīre "to come together, to suit", built on venīre "to come".
