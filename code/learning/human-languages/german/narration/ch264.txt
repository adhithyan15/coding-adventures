German, chapter 264: Damals und neulich.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
damals — back then, at that time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for as long as, then the German for since then.

You'll want to know: damals.
damals — "back then, at that time".
Damals hatten wir kein Handy. Back then we had no mobile phone.
For a time that is over. In a story it goes with the Präteritum: Damals wohnte ich auf dem Land.

The word, taken apart.
damals is usually traced to Middle High German dō māles "at that time", from dō "then" and Mal "time, occasion"; EtymDB lists no parent.

You'll want to know: einst.
einst — "once, long ago; one day".
Hier stand einst eine Burg. Long ago a castle stood here.
Literary. It can also look ahead: einst wirst du es verstehen.

The word, taken apart.
einst is inherited from Middle High German einest, from Old High German einēst "once", built on ein "one" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
neulich — the other day, recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for back then, then the German for once.

You'll want to know: neulich.
neulich — "the other day, recently".
Neulich habe ich meine alte Freundin getroffen. The other day I met my old friend.
Conversational; for one event a short time ago, usually with the Perfekt.

The word, taken apart.
neulich is built on neu "new", an inherited Germanic word, with the ending -lich: newly, lately. EtymDB lists no parent for the adverb itself.

Lesson 4 of 5.
kürzlich — recently, lately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for once, then the German for the other day.

You'll want to know: kürzlich.
kürzlich — "recently, lately".
Die Bäckerei hat kürzlich zugemacht. The bakery closed recently.
A little more formal than neulich; common in written reports.

The word, taken apart.
kürzlich is built on kurz "short", which reached Old High German kurz from Latin curtus "shortened" (EtymDB): a short time ago.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vorgestern — the day before yesterday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the other day, then the German for recently.

You'll want to know: vorgestern.
vorgestern — "the day before yesterday".
Vorgestern war ich krank, aber heute geht es mir gut. The day before yesterday I was ill, but today I am well.
The partner of übermorgen, the day after tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
vorgestern joins vor "before" and gestern "yesterday", an inherited Germanic word and a cousin of the yester- in English yesterday; EtymDB lists no parent for the compound.
