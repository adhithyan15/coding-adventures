Latin, chapter 70: Son, Daughter, Grandparents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fīlius, fīliī — a son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for happy, then the Latin for sad.

You'll want to know: fīlius, fīliī.
fīlius, fīliī — "a son". English filial.

The word, taken apart.
fīlius is inherited. Old Latin had fīlios, "son", and the word is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dheh1ylios, "a suckling" (EtymDB 2.1), from a root for nursing.

Lesson 3 of 5.
avus, avī — a grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a son, then the Latin for a daughter.

You'll want to know: avus, avī.
avus, avī — "a grandfather".

The word, taken apart.
avus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2ewh2os, "grandfather, elder male relative" (EtymDB 2.1). Its family includes avunculus, "mother's brother", the source of English uncle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
familia, familiae — a family, a household.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a grandfather, then the Latin for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: familia, familiae.
familia, familiae — "a family, a household".

The word, taken apart.
familia is built on famulus, "a household slave, a servant" (Lewis & Short): a familia was first everyone under one master's roof, the slaves included. English family comes from it.
