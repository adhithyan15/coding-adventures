Russian, chapter 156: Rich, Poor, Clean, Dirty, Empty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
богатый (bogátyy) — rich.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for lazy, then the Russian for polite.

You'll want to know: богатый.
богатый (bogátyy) — "rich".

The word, taken apart.
богатый (bogátyy) comes from Proto-Slavic bogŭ: Vasmer gives both readings, "god", so "favoured by the gods" like Latin fortūnātus, and "a share, wealth", as in убогий, "poor", "without a share".

Lesson 2 of 5.
бедный (bédnyy) — poor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for polite, then the Russian for rich.

You'll want to know: бедный.
бедный (bédnyy) — "poor".

The word, taken apart.
бедный (bédnyy) is беда, "misfortune", with -ный: "unfortunate", then "poor". Vasmer compares Lithuanian bėdà, "trouble", and Latvian będa, "sorrow".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for rich, then the Russian for poor.

You'll want to know: чистый.
чистый (chístyy) — "clean".

The word, taken apart.
чистый (chístyy) from Proto-Slavic čistŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Old Prussian skijstan, "clean", and Lithuanian skýstas, "liquid", and perhaps to цедить, "to strain".

Lesson 4 of 5.
грязный (gryáznyy) — dirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for poor, then the Russian for clean.

You'll want to know: грязный.
грязный (gryáznyy) — "dirty".

The word, taken apart.
грязный (gryáznyy) is грязь, "mud", with -ный. Vasmer puts грязь with грязнуть, "to get stuck, to sink in": mud is what you sink into.
