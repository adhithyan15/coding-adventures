Portuguese, chapter 353: Os obstáculos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o obstáculo — the obstacle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the excuse, then the Portuguese for to justify.

You'll want to know: o obstáculo.
o obstáculo — "the obstacle".
O maior obstáculo é a língua. The biggest obstacle is the language.
Superar um obstáculo — to overcome an obstacle.

The word, taken apart.
obstáculo is a learned borrowing from Latin obstāculum "a hindrance, an obstacle" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from obstāre "to stand in the way", built on stāre "to stand".

Lesson 2 of 5.
o desafio — the challenge; the match (in sport).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to justify, then the Portuguese for the obstacle.

You'll want to know: o desafio.
o desafio — "the challenge; the match (in sport)".
Aprender uma língua nova é um desafio. Learning a new language is a challenge.
Aceitar um desafio — to take up a challenge. In Portugal also a football match.

The word, taken apart.
desafio is built on desafiar "to challenge", usually traced to des- and fiar "to trust", which EtymDB derives from Vulgar Latin fīdāre, a form of Latin fīdere "to trust" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): to challenge is to break the bond of trust.

Lesson 3 of 5.
impedir — to prevent, to stop (from).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the obstacle, then the Portuguese for the challenge.

You'll want to know: impedir.
impedir — "to prevent, to stop (from)".
A chuva impediu-nos de sair. The rain stopped us from going out.
Impedir alguém de fazer algo — to prevent someone from doing something.

The word, taken apart.
impedir is a learned borrowing from Latin impedīre "to entangle, to hinder" (EtymDB), built from in and pēs, pedis "foot" (Lewis & Short): to catch someone by the feet.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dificultar — to make difficult, to hinder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the challenge, then the Portuguese for to prevent.

You'll want to know: dificultar.
dificultar — "to make difficult, to hinder".
O trânsito dificultou a viagem. The traffic made the journey difficult.
Built on difícil: to make hard.

The word, taken apart.
dificultar comes from Latin difficultāre (EtymDB), built on difficultās "difficulty, trouble" (Lewis & Short), from difficilis "hard", on facilis "easy" with dis-.

The word, taken apart.
favorecer is built on favor "favour", from Latin favor "favour, goodwill, inclination" (Lewis & Short), with the verb ending -ecer.
