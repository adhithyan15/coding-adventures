French, chapter 517: Marbre et argile.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la sculpture — sculpture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for easel, then the French for to sketch.

You'll want to know: la sculpture.
la sculpture — "sculpture".
Le musée présente des sculptures modernes. The museum is showing modern sculptures.
The p is silent: scul-ture.

The word, taken apart.
la sculpture is a learned borrowing from Latin sculptūra (EtymDB), from sculpere "to carve in stone" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'argile — clay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sculpture, then the French for to sculpt.

You'll want to know: l'argile.
l'argile — "clay".
Les enfants font des petits pots en argile. The children are making little pots out of clay.
Feminine. La terre glaise is a close word.

The word, taken apart.
l'argile comes from Latin argilla (EtymDB), a borrowing from Greek árgillos "white clay", related to argós "white".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to sculpt, then the French for clay.

You'll want to know: le marbre.
le marbre — "marble".
Dans le hall, il y a un escalier en marbre. In the hall there is a marble staircase.
Rester de marbre — to remain unmoved.

The word, taken apart.
le marbre is inherited from Latin marmor "marble" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from Greek mármaros.
