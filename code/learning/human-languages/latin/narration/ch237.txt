7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
fīnis, fīnis — the end; a boundary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for love, then the Latin for friendship.

You'll want to know: fīnis, fīnis.
fīnis, fīnis — "the end; a boundary".

The word, taken apart.
fīnis has no sure origin; EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed Proto-Indo-European dhnh2-, "end", but no account is generally accepted. English final and finish come from it.

mora, morae — "a delay".

The word, taken apart.
mora is often linked with Proto-Indo-European (s)mer-, "to be mindful, to linger in thought" (EtymDB 2.1), the root of memor, "mindful" (Lewis & Short), but the link is uncertain.

Lesson 4 of 7.
cotīdiē — every day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a delay, then the Latin for sometimes.

You'll want to know: cotīdiē.
cotīdiē — "every day".

The word, taken apart.
cotīdiē is fused from quot, "as many as", and diē, "on the day": on every day there is. English quotidian comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 7.
prīdiē — the day before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sometimes, then the Latin for every day.

You'll want to know: prīdiē.
prīdiē — "the day before".

The word, taken apart.
prīdiē is usually explained as an old prī, "before", the root of prior, "earlier", with diē, "on the day" (Lewis & Short): on the day before.
