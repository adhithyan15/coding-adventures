You'll want to know: そこ.
そこ — soko — "there", the place near the person you are talking to.
You know ここ, koko, here, the place near you. Change the first sign and the place moves across to the listener: soko, there, by them. The second sign is the こ you already write. The first one is new, and the next lesson writes it.

Lesson 2 of 9.
そ (so) — one stroke, two zigzags.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

[pause 20 seconds]
Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say there, near the listener — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a library — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a water flask — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say noisy — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
そ — so.
One stroke, and the pen never lifts:
a short bar at the top, left to right.
turn sharply and run down and to the left until you reach the long middle bar.
turn back and run right along the whole of that bar.
double back a little way, then drop into an open curve that rounds the lower left and finishes along the base to the right.
The pen zigzags twice before it reaches the curve. Some typefaces print the top apart from the rest; written by hand here, it is one unbroken stroke.
With こ after it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: そこ, soko, there.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Trace そ (so) saying "bar, down, across, curve".
Write そこ from memory and say soko.
Write ここ and そこ (soko) side by side, and say which one is the place by the listener.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.

Lesson 5 of 9.
れ (re) — one first stroke, a third ending.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 25 seconds]
Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say this, a thing near you — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: write わ and ね, and say each one]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a chain — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say pure white — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
れ — re.
Two strokes, one lift:
the long vertical, from top to bottom.
a short bar from the left across the vertical; without lifting, turn sharply and cut down and to the left through the vertical, climb back up that line and rise to the right over an arch, come down its right side, round the foot and flick up to the right.
The first stroke and the zigzag are the ones your hand already makes in わ and ね. Only the ending of the second stroke is new:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
sign: わ. how the second stroke ends: a broad belly that curves back to the left.
sign: ね. how the second stroke ends: a small loop at the lower right.
sign: れ (re). how the second stroke ends: a drop and a flick out to the right.
With こ in front of it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: これ, kore, this.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Trace れ (re) saying "down; bar, zigzag, arch, flick".
Write わ, ね and れ in a row, and look only at the three endings.
Write これ from memory and say kore.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart; stroke count and order per KanjiVG.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
わ, ね and れ (re) begin the same way. How does れ (re) end?

それ — sore — "that", a thing near the person you are talking to.

[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]

You'll want to know: くるま.
くるま — kuruma — "a car".
Three beats: ku–ru–ma. The first and last signs are く and ま, and you write both. The middle one is new. It looks like ろ with a small loop tied at the bottom, and the next lesson writes it.

Script — the shape.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
る — ru.
One stroke, and the pen never lifts:
a short bar at the top, left to right.
turn sharply and run the long diagonal down to the lower left.
swing back up to the right and round the big bowl.
at the bottom, curl up into a small loop and finish where it meets the base.
Up to the bowl, this is ろ. Only the ending differs:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: ろ, ro. it ends with: a short tail curving left.
you write: る, ru. it ends with: a small closed loop.
With く and ま around it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: くるま, kuruma, a car.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

Write ろ and る (ru) side by side; check that only the second one closes a loop.
Write くるま from memory and say kuruma.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.
