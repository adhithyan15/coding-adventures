## You'll want to know: 亲人
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C511-KIN511-01]; assesses=[] -->

**亲人** — *qīnrén* — "one's close family; one's nearest and dearest".

> **在国外的时候我最想我的亲人** *zài guówài de shíhou wǒ zuì xiǎng wǒ de qīnrén* — When I was abroad, I missed my family most of all.

The new character, in pieces: 亲 — nine strokes in two pieces: on top the top of 意 *yì*, five strokes; under it four strokes, a bar, a vertical that hooks at the foot, a stroke falling left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**亲人** (*qīnrén*) is built from 亲 'close' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshin* and *nyin* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to love' and 'near'.
