# el mango — the handle

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MANO-01, ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la mano** and **el paraguas**. This word is a relative of the
first and a part of the second.

## You'll want to know: el mango
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-01] -->

**el mango** — the handle.

> Un paraguas azul, con el **mango** de madera.

The handle of a knife, a pan, a door, an umbrella: whatever part of a thing is
built for the hand.

Which is what the word says. It is a cousin of **la mano**, and once you hear
that, you will not need to store it separately.

## Grammar Lens: two mangos, and only one of them grows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-02, ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-01] -->

There is a second **mango** in Spanish, and it is a completely different word
that happens to be spelled the same. This is the situation
[la cola](./ES-C451-cola.md) prepared you for.

| the word | where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **el mango** — handle | Latin *manus*, the hand |
| **el mango** — the fruit | Portuguese *manga*, from Malay |

The Academy traces the fruit through Portuguese **manga** to Malay
*mangga*; behind that stands a Dravidian word, Tamil *māṅkāy*. It travelled
by ship and the handle did not. Nothing connects them but the spelling.

Context settles it every time, so this is not a hazard — it is a reminder that
*same letters* and *same word* are different claims.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-02, ES-LEX-MANO-01] -->

Latin **manus**, the hand — the root [la mano](./ES-C24-mano.md) already gave
you, along with *manual*, *manufacture* and *maintain*.

A **mango** is the hand-part. Spanish built it on a late Latin form of that
root and let the meaning settle on the graspable end of a thing.

English does not have this word, so it has nothing to lend you here. What it
does have is *manual*, *manuscript*, *manacle* — all of them hands. Put
*mango* beside them and it stops looking like a fruit.
