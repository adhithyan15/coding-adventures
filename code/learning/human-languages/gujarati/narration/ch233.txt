Gujarati, chapter 233: Animals that work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પ્રાણી (prāṇī) — animal, living creature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to take care, then the Gujarati for to get well.

You'll want to know: પ્રાણી.
પ્રાણી — prāṇī — "animal, living creature".
મેં જંગલમાં ઘણાં પ્રાણી જોયાં. — mẽ jangalmā̃ ghaṇā̃ prāṇī joyā̃ — "I saw many animals in the forest."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
પ્રાણી (prāṇī) is Sanskrit prāṇin, "living, a living creature", built on prāṇa, "breath of life", and taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 8932).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઘોડો (ghoḍo) — horse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to get well, then the Gujarati for animal.

You'll want to know: ઘોડો.
ઘોડો — ghoḍo — "horse".
ઘોડો સાંજે ઘરે આવ્યો. — ghoḍo sā̃je ghare āvyo — "The horse came home in the evening."

The word, taken apart.
ઘોડો (ghoḍo) is inherited from Sanskrit ghoṭa, "horse", a word CDIAL takes to be of non-Aryan, probably Dravidian, origin (CDIAL 4516).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ઊંટ (ū̃ṭ) — camel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for animal, then the Gujarati for horse.

You'll want to know: ઊંટ.
ઊંટ — ū̃ṭ — "camel".
ઊંટ રણમાં ચાલ્યો. — ū̃ṭ raṇmā̃ cālyo — "The camel walked in the desert."

The word, taken apart.
ઊંટ (ū̃ṭ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit uṣṭra, which meant a buffalo in the oldest texts and a camel later (CDIAL 2387).

Lesson 4 of 5.
બળદ (baḷad) — ox, bullock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for horse, then the Gujarati for camel.

You'll want to know: બળદ.
બળદ — baḷad — "ox, bullock".
બળદ ખેતરમાં ગયો. — baḷad khetarmā̃ gayo — "The ox went to the field."

The word, taken apart.
બળદ (baḷad) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from balivarda, "ox, bull" (CDIAL 9176).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ખેડવું (kheḍvũ) — to plough, to till.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for camel, then the Gujarati for ox.

You'll want to know: ખેડવું.
ખેડવું — kheḍvũ — "to plough, to till".
ગઈકાલે તેણે ખેતર ખેડ્યું. — gaīkāle teṇe khetar kheḍyũ — "Yesterday he ploughed the field."

The word, taken apart.
ખેડવું (kheḍvũ) comes from khēḍ-, "open up, plough", a reconstructed form that CDIAL places under khiḍ, "be open, expand"; it glosses the Gujarati verb "to plough, drive" (CDIAL 3882).
