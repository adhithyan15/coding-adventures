French, chapter 268: Tout d'abord.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tout d'abord — first of all, to begin with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a team, then the French for a birthday.

You'll want to know: tout d'abord.
tout d'abord — "first of all, to begin with".
Tout d'abord, nous avons pris un café. First of all, we had a coffee.
A little stronger than d'abord alone; it opens a list or a story.

The word, taken apart.
tout d'abord joins tout "all", inherited from Latin tōtus "whole" (Lewis & Short), and d'abord "at first", which is built on abord "approach", from aborder "to come alongside", itself built on bord "edge, side of a ship".

Lesson 2 of 5.
en premier lieu — in the first place, firstly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a birthday, then the French for first of all.

You'll want to know: en premier lieu.
en premier lieu — "in the first place, firstly".
En premier lieu, il a parlé de sa famille. In the first place, he talked about his family.
Formal; it orders the points of a speech or a report, often followed by en second lieu.

The word, taken apart.
lieu is inherited from Latin locus "place", and premier comes from Latin prīmārius "of the first rank", built on prīmus "first" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: par la suite.
par la suite — "afterwards, later on".
Par la suite, ils sont devenus amis. Afterwards, they became friends.
It looks back from a later point in the story: what came next.

The word, taken apart.
suite is inherited from Vulgar Latin sequita "a following" (EtymDB), built on Latin sequī "to follow".

Lesson 4 of 5.
en dernier lieu — lastly, last of all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for in the first place, then the French for afterwards.

You'll want to know: en dernier lieu.
en dernier lieu — "lastly, last of all".
En dernier lieu, elle a fermé la porte à clé. Last of all, she locked the door.
The partner of en premier lieu; careful, written French.

The word, taken apart.
dernier comes from Old French derrenier, usually traced to Latin dē retrō "from behind" (Lewis & Short); lieu is inherited from Latin locus "place".

Lesson 5 of 5.
pour finir — to finish, to end with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for afterwards, then the French for lastly.

You'll want to know: pour finir.
pour finir — "to finish, to end with".
Pour finir, nous avons chanté tous ensemble. To finish, we all sang together.
Common at the end of a story or a meal: pour finir, un petit café.

The word, taken apart.
finir is inherited from Latin fīnīre "to bound, to bring to an end" (EtymDB), built on fīnis "boundary, end" (Lewis & Short).
