# Mandarin Session Map — Opening Chapter Review Ledger

This is the fixed review ledger for Chapter 1's seven-lesson spoken greeting
slice. It is **not** the authoritative inventory or schedule for the whole
Mandarin track; [`curriculum.d/`](./curriculum.d/) and
[`chapters.d/`](./chapters.d/) carry that growing machine-readable order.
Every lesson here is an independent, prerequisite-safe step of three or four
minutes. A study session may combine the new step with the reviews shown here,
but it never turns the combined session into one indivisible lesson.

Reviews use the session-count intervals in
[`HL00`](../../../specs/HL00-human-language-curriculum-framework.md): a lesson
introduced in session *N* returns at *N+1, N+3, N+7,* and *N+15*.

## New-lesson sessions

| Session | New lesson | Reviews due | Cumulative practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | [`ZH-C01-tones`](./lessons/ZH-C01-tones.md): **mā má mǎ mà** | — | say one syllable on four pitches and hear four words |
| **S2** | [`ZH-C01-ni`](./lessons/ZH-C01-ni.md): **你** *nǐ* | the tones *(N+1)* | one character, named component by component |
| **S3** | [`ZH-C01-hao`](./lessons/ZH-C01-hao.md): **好** *hǎo* | *nǐ* *(N+1)* | two characters, both decomposed, both third tone |
| **S4** | [`ZH-C01-nihao`](./lessons/ZH-C01-nihao.md): **你好** *nǐ hǎo* | the tones *(N+3)*; *hǎo* *(N+1)* | greet and answer with one word |
| **S5** | [`ZH-C01-tone-sandhi`](./lessons/ZH-C01-tone-sandhi.md): **ní hǎo** | *nǐ* *(N+3)*; *nǐ hǎo* *(N+1)* | say the greeting as it is spoken, not as it is printed |
| **S6** | [`ZH-C01-hao-fond`](./lessons/ZH-C01-hao-fond.md): **好** *hào* | *hǎo* *(N+3)*; the sandhi rule *(N+1)* | recognise one character carrying two words |
| **S7** | [`ZH-C01-practice`](./lessons/ZH-C01-practice.md): the two-line meeting | *nǐ hǎo* *(N+3)*; *hào* *(N+1)* | run both voices from characters alone |

The sequence introduces one new fact at a time: what tone does, a character and
its components, a second character and what replaces etymology, the two of them
as one word, the rule that changes how that word is said, the proof that tone is
lexical, then the exchange. The final practice uses only those six lessons.

## Modality — what a commuter can actually do here

| Lesson | Modality | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `ZH-C01-tones` | 🚗 voice | pitch only; nothing to look at |
| `ZH-C01-ni` | 👁 sight | teaches the shape of 你 and its components |
| `ZH-C01-hao` | 👁 sight | teaches the shape of 好 and its components |
| `ZH-C01-nihao` | 🚗 voice | assembles two known characters into one spoken word |
| `ZH-C01-tone-sandhi` | 🚗 voice | a pitch rule; nothing on the page changes |
| `ZH-C01-hao-fond` | 🚗 voice | one known character, contrasted by tone alone |
| `ZH-C01-practice` | 🚗 voice | two spoken lines |

Five of these seven lessons are learnable by ear, but Chapter 1's HL08
**drivable prefix is 1**, because its two sight-dependent character lessons come
second and third. That describes this opening slice, not a permanent Mandarin
policy. Later vocabulary chapters deliberately hand over an unfamiliar meaning
by ear before revealing a dense new character, then add staged handwriting and
active retrieval. The opening greeting still keeps its component-first order
because its job is to teach what a character is before the learner meets a wider
spoken vocabulary.
