French, chapter 332: Assaisonner.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la passoire — the colander, the sieve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to turn golden, then the French for to brown on top.

You'll want to know: la passoire.
la passoire — "the colander, the sieve".
Il met les pâtes dans la passoire. He puts the pasta in the colander.
Informal: une mémoire comme une passoire — a memory like a sieve.

The word, taken apart.
passoire is built on passer "to pass", which is inherited from Vulgar Latin passāre, built on Latin passus "a step" (Lewis & Short).

assaisonner — "to season".
Elle assaisonne la salade avec de l'huile et du vinaigre. She dresses the salad with oil and vinegar.
L'assaisonnement — the seasoning, the dressing.

The word, taken apart.
assaisonner is built on saison "season, the right time", usually traced to Latin satiō "a sowing" (Lewis & Short): to bring food to the right point.

You'll want to know: déguster.
déguster — "to taste, to savour".
Nous avons dégusté les fromages de la région. We tasted the cheeses of the region.
To eat or drink slowly, with pleasure. Informal: il a dégusté — he really suffered.

The word, taken apart.
déguster is a learned borrowing from Latin dēgustāre "to taste" (EtymDB), built on gustus "taste".
