Arabic, chapter 332: Leaving home.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
هاجر (hājara) — to emigrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to flourish, then the Arabic for an emperor.

You'll want to know: هاجر.
هاجر — hājara — "to emigrate".
هاجر عمي إلى بلد بعيد قبل عشرين سنة. hājara ʿammī ilā balad baʿīd qabla ʿishrīn sana. My uncle emigrated to a distant country twenty years ago.
Present: يهاجر, yuhājir.
An emigrant is مهاجر.

The word, taken apart.
هاجر (hājara) is built on the native Arabic root h-j-r (Lane), "to leave, to cut oneself off from". It is Form III, "to leave one's land for another".

Lesson 3 of 6.
رحل (raḥala) — to move out; to depart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to emigrate, then the Arabic for to settle down.

You'll want to know: رحل.
رحل — raḥala — "to move out; to depart".
رحل الجيران عن الحي الشهر الماضي. raḥala al-jīrān ʿan al-ḥayy ash-shahr al-māḍī. The neighbours moved out of the area last month.
Present: يرحل, yarḥal.
Of a person who died: رحل (raḥala) عنا, he has left us.

The word, taken apart.
رحل (raḥala) is built on the native Arabic root r-ḥ-l (Lane), where raḥl is a camel saddle and raḥala, "to set off"; the taught riḥla, a journey, shares it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
نزح (nazaḥa) — to be displaced, to flee one’s home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to settle down, then the Arabic for to move out.

You'll want to know: نزح.
نزح — nazaḥa — "to be displaced, to flee one’s home".
نزح كثير من الناس بعد الفيضان. nazaḥa kathīr min an-nās baʿda al-fayaḍān. Many people left their homes after the flood.
Present: ينزح, yanzaḥ.
A displaced person is نازح.

The word, taken apart.
نزح (nazaḥa) is built on the native Arabic root n-z-ḥ (Lane), "to become far away", and "to draw all the water from a well". People who must go far from home are nāziḥūn.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ودع (waddaʿa) — to bid farewell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to move out, then the Arabic for to be displaced.

You'll want to know: ودع.
ودع — waddaʿa — "to bid farewell".
ودعت الأم ابنها في المطار. waddaʿat al-umm ibnahā fī al-maṭār. The mother said goodbye to her son at the airport.
Present: يودع, yuwaddiʿ.
A farewell is وداع; مع السلامة is what is said.

The word, taken apart.
ودع (waddaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root w-d-ʿ (Lane), "to leave, to let be". It is Form II, "to take leave of"; wadāʿ is a farewell.
