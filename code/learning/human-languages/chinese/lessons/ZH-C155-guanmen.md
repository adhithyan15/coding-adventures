## You'll want to know: guānmén
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C155-GUANMEN-01]; assesses=[] -->

*guānmén* — "to close (for the day); to shut the door".

> *wǎnshang bā diǎn guānmén* — It closes at eight in the evening.

Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*guānmén* is built from *guān* 'to close', the word you know, and 门 'a door'. *guān* is inherited from Middle Chinese *kwaen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains, through an older book, as a wooden bar held across a door.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C155-KAIMEN-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-CONG-DAO-01] -->

开门 and *guānmén* bracket a shop's day: *zǎoshang jiǔ diǎn kāimén, wǎnshang bā diǎn guānmén*.

A sign often gives only the span, with 到 between: 九点到八点.
