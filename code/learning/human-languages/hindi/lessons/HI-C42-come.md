# आना / आता / आती / आते — to come and its present forms

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A short run of everyday verbs, one at a time.

## You'll want to know: आना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-COME]; assesses=[] -->

**आना** (*ānā*) — "to come".

Every Hindi infinitive ends in **-ना** (*-nā*), so this is 'to come' in the form a dictionary lists.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**आना** (*ānā*) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. CDIAL lists it under *āpayati*, "causes to reach", from the verb whose old form *āpa* meant "came towards"; Prakrit already had *āvaï*, "comes" (CDIAL 1200).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-COME] -->

Take off **-ना** and add the endings you already know: **आता** (*ātā*,
masculine), **आती** (*ātī*, feminine), **आते** (*āte*, respectful or plural).
The action-chaining lesson will put **आता हूँ** after a chained action; now the form will already
be yours.
