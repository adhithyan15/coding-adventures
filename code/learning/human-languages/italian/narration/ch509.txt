Italian, chapter 509: L'arbitro fischia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'arbitro — referee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for abs, then the Italian for to sweat.

You'll want to know: l'arbitro.
l'arbitro — "referee".
L'arbitro ha sbagliato tutto oggi. The referee got everything wrong today.
A woman is l'arbitra.

The word, taken apart.
arbitro is a learned borrowing from Latin arbiter "a witness, a judge" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
fischiare — to whistle; to boo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to sweat, then the Italian for referee.

You'll want to know: fischiare.
fischiare — "to whistle; to boo".
Il pubblico ha fischiato la squadra. The crowd booed the team.
At a match, fischiare is to boo: the opposite of applaudire.

The word, taken apart.
fischiare is inherited from Late Latin fistulāre "to play the pipe" (EtymDB), from fistula "a pipe" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il fallo — foul.

Before the new one: say the Italian for referee, then the Italian for to whistle.

You'll want to know: il fallo.
il fallo — "foul".
Questo è fallo, arbitro! That is a foul, referee!
Fare fallo — to commit a foul.

The word, taken apart.
fallo "fault, foul" comes from the old verb fallare "to err", usually traced to Latin fallere "to deceive, to fail" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
il rigore — penalty (kick); strictness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to whistle, then the Italian for foul.

You'll want to know: il rigore.
il rigore — "penalty (kick); strictness".
L'arbitro ha dato il rigore all'ultimo minuto. The referee gave the penalty in the last minute.
Short for calcio di rigore. Also strictness: con rigore.

The word, taken apart.
rigore is a learned borrowing from Latin rigor "stiffness, hardness" (Lewis & Short); the penalty is the strict punishment of a foul.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ammonire — to book, to caution; to warn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for foul, then the Italian for penalty.

You'll want to know: ammonire.
ammonire — "to book, to caution; to warn".
L'arbitro lo ha ammonito dopo il fallo. The referee booked him after the foul.
In football, a yellow card: è stato ammonito.

The word, taken apart.
ammonire is a learned borrowing from Latin admonēre "to remind, to warn" (EtymDB), from monēre "to warn".
