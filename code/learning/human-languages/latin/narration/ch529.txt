Latin, chapter 529: Step-families.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
noverca, novercae — a stepmother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cousin, then the Latin for twin.

You'll want to know: noverca, novercae.
noverca, novercae — "a stepmother".
Noverca fīliōs marītī amat. The stepmother loves her husband's sons.
Feminine, first declension. In Roman stories the stepmother is often cruel, but not always.

The word, taken apart.
noverca is usually traced to novus "new" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): the new wife of the house.

Masculine, second declension.

vitricus is a native Latin noun, and its origin is not settled; neither Lewis & Short nor EtymDB gives a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prīvignus, prīvignī — a stepson.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a stepmother, then the Latin for a stepfather.

You'll want to know: prīvignus, prīvignī.
prīvignus, prīvignī — "a stepson".
Prīvignus novercae epistulam scrībit. The stepson writes a letter to his stepmother.
Masculine; prīvigna — a stepdaughter.

The word, taken apart.
prīvignus is built, Lewis & Short explains, on prīvus "separate" and the root of gignō "to beget": born of another line.

Lesson 5 of 5.
frāternus, frāterna, frāternum — brotherly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a stepson, then the Latin for a little daughter.

You'll want to know: frāternus, frāterna, frāternum.
frāternus, frāterna, frāternum — "brotherly".
Amor frāternus semper manet. Brotherly love always endures.
From frāter. English fraternal.

The word, taken apart.
frāternus is built on frāter "brother" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "brother"; English brother is a cousin. English fraternal comes from it.
