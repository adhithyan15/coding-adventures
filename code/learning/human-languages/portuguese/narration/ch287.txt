Portuguese, chapter 287: Roubos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o roubo — the robbery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the victim, then the Portuguese for the homicide.

You'll want to know: o roubo.
o roubo — "the robbery".
Houve um roubo na loja ao lado. There was a robbery in the shop next door.
Taking something by force or threat.

The word, taken apart.
roubo is built on roubar "to rob", which EtymDB traces through Medieval Latin to Frankish raubōn "to plunder".

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o furto — the theft (without violence).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the homicide, then the Portuguese for the robbery.

You'll want to know: o furto.
o furto — "the theft (without violence)".
O furto da bicicleta aconteceu de noite. The bike was stolen at night.
Roubo involves force or threat; furto is taking something quietly.

The word, taken apart.
furto is inherited from Latin fūrtum "theft" (EtymDB), from fūr "a thief".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o carteirista — the pickpocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the theft, then the Portuguese for to steal.

You'll want to know: o carteirista.
o carteirista — "the pickpocket".
Um carteirista tirou-me o telemóvel no elétrico. A pickpocket took my mobile on the tram.
Built on carteira ‘wallet’. In Brazil: o batedor de carteira.

The word, taken apart.
carteirista is built on carteira "wallet", from carta, which comes from Latin charta "paper" (EtymDB), with -ista for the person.

desaparecimento is built on desaparecer "to disappear", from des- and aparecer, which goes back to Latin appārēre "to come into sight" (Lewis & Short).
