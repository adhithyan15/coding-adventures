You'll want to know: 没有.
没有 — méiyǒu — "not have; there is no".
我没有手机 wǒ méiyǒu shǒujī — I don't have a phone.
这儿没有人 zhèr méiyǒu rén — There's nobody here.
The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
没有 (méiyǒu) is built from 没 'not' and 有 'to have'. 有 is inherited from Middle Chinese hjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 没 is there as mwot, glossed 'to sink'; the Guangyun does not give 'not', and the regular sound change from mwot gives mò, not méi, so how 没 came to mean 'not' and to be said méi is not settled.

What you've built.
有 has its own negative, and it is 没 (méi), not 不: 没有 (méiyǒu). Both jobs of 有 go with it, 'to have' and 'there is': 我没有手机, 这儿没有人.

Script — water, a small roof, and 又.
没 (méi).
Three pieces, left before right, top before bottom.
The water piece 氵, three strokes, as 汉 opens: two dots, then a stroke rising to the right.
A small roof, two strokes: a short left-falling stroke, then a horizontal that turns down and runs out to the right.
又, two strokes, under the roof, as 对 opens: across and falling to the left, then the long stroke falling to the right.
3 + 2 + 2 = seven.

Lesson 3 of 8.
不 — 没 (bù — méi): two words for not.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two words for 'not', and this lesson sorts them.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 没有, aloud — méiyǒu]
[your turn — recall: say 不 — bù, not]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 好不好 — hǎo bu hǎo, is that all right?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 不 for now and for habit, 没 for what did not happen.
不 says no about now, about habit, and about what you will or won't do. 没 (méi) says something did not happen.
我不去 wǒ bú qù — I'm not going; I don't go. 我没去 wǒ méi qù — I didn't go.
Same verb, same person; the negative alone moves the sentence from a decision to a report. 不 before the fall of 去 is bú, as in bú shì.
And 有 takes 没 (méi) every time, which is how the question works:
你有没有手机 nǐ yǒu méiyǒu shǒujī — Do you have a phone?
It is the same yes-or-no shape as 好不好: the verb, its negative, the verb again. The answer is 有 or 没有 (méiyǒu).

bīngxiāng — "fridge; refrigerator".
bīngxiāng lǐ yǒu niúnǎi — There's milk in the fridge.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bīngxiāng is built from bīng 'ice' and xiāng 'a box', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ping and sjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'frozen water' and 'a box; a chest'. The pair names a machine the Guangyun never saw: the compound is modern.

What you've built.
The place first, then 有, then the thing: bīngxiāng lǐ yǒu niúnǎi. The 里 after bīngxiāng is the one you write, and it means inside.

The exchange.
A: zhè shì nǐ de fángjiān — This is your room. B: 谢谢 xièxie — Thanks. B: fángjiān lǐ yǒu dēng ma — Is there a light in the room? A: yǒu, zài mén pángbiān — Yes, by the door. B: nǐ yǒu méiyǒu yàoshi — Have you got a key? A: 没有 méiyǒu — No, I haven't. A: bīngxiāng lǐ yǒu niúnǎi — There's milk in the fridge. B: 我不喝 wǒ bù hē — I don't drink it.
Two kinds of no in one room: 没有 (méiyǒu), there isn't any, and 我不喝, I don't drink it. The first reports, the second refuses.
