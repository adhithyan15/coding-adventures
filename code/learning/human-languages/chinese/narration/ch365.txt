Mandarin Chinese, chapter 365: Yes, but.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiànyì — to suggest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give official approval, then the Mandarin for to remind someone.

You'll want to know: jiànyì.
jiànyì — "to suggest".
yīnwèi nǐ bìng le suǒyǐ wǒ jiànyì nǐ qù kàn yīshēng — You're ill, so I suggest you go and see a doctor.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiànyì is built from jiàn and yì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjonH and ngjeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to set up' and the second 'to deliberate'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
可是 (kěshì) — but; yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to remind someone, then the Mandarin for to suggest.

You'll want to know: 可是.
可是 — kěshì — "but; yet".
你说得很有道理可是我不想去 nǐ shuō de hěn yǒu dàoli kěshì wǒ bù xiǎng qù — You've got a point, but I don't want to go.
The new character, in pieces: 可 (kě) — five strokes: a long bar on top, 口 kǒu under it on the left, three strokes, and a long vertical down the right that hooks at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
可是 (kěshì) is built from kě and 是, both inherited from Middle Chinese, khaX and dzyeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to permit' and the second 'right, as against wrong'. How the pair came to mean but is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

Script — a bar, a mouth and a hook.
可 (kě).
One piece, five strokes. Count it in three parts.
A long horizontal across the top.
口, three strokes, small, under the bar on the left: a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, and the bottom horizontal.
A long vertical down the right side, from the bar to the foot, hooking up to the left.
1 + 3 + 1 = five.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
可见 (kějiàn) — which goes to show; so it is clear that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to suggest, then the Mandarin for but (in everyday speech).

You'll want to know: 可见.
可见 — kějiàn — "which goes to show; so it is clear that".
他每天都准时来可见他很喜欢这儿 tā měitiān dōu zhǔnshí lái kějiàn tā hěn xǐhuan zhèr — He turns up on time every day, which shows he really likes it here.

The word, taken apart.
可见 (kějiàn) is built from 可 (kě) 'can' and 见 'to see', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khaX and kenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to permit' and the second 'to see'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
不如 (bùrú) — to do better to; might as well; to be not as good as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for but (in everyday speech), then the Mandarin for which goes to show.

You'll want to know: 不如.
不如 — bùrú — "to do better to; might as well; to be not as good as".
我们不如走路去 wǒmen bùrú zǒulù qù — We might as well walk.
The tone of bù stays falling here, because the next syllable is not a falling tone.

The word, taken apart.
不如 (bùrú) is built from 不 'not' and 如 'like', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut and nyo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'like' and 'as'.
