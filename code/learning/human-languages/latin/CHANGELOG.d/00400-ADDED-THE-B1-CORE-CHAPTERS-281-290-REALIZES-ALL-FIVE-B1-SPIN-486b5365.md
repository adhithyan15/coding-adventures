## Added — the B1 core, chapters 281-290, realizes all five B1 spine nodes

Ten chapters and 38 lessons, sequences 15400-15770, ahead of the B1
vocabulary. Each chapter ends on a short review; chapter 282 carries an
87-word reading and chapter 290 ends on a guided letter.

- **Narrating (281-282).** The perfect's six endings and the -āvī verbs
  (LA-C281-vidi-vidit); the imperfect of sum (LA-C281-eram) and of every
  other verb with -ba- (LA-C281-ambulabam); which past does which job
  (LA-C281-pluebat-venit, ASPECT-ONGOING-PAST); prīmum, deinde, tum,
  postrēmō with Caesar's vēnī, vīdī, vīcī (LA-C282-primum-deinde,
  CONNECTIVE-THEN); dum with the present (LA-C282-dum, NARRATIVE-SEQUENCE);
  a rainy day in both pasts (LA-C282-lectio), every form of which occurs in
  an earlier lesson.
- **Reasons (283-284).** putō and the accusative and infinitive
  (OPINION-I-THINK); meā sententiā and mihi vidētur; ergō beside itaque and
  igitur (CONNECTIVE-SO); dēbēs and dēbēbās (MODAL-SHOULD); cūr answered
  with quia, nam and the causal quod (EXPLANATION-GIVE).
- **Travel (285-286).** via and quā viā ad …? (DIRECTION-ASK); rēctā viā,
  ad dextram, ad sinistram, with Terence's directions scene; iter and iter
  faciō; nāvis; naulum, the Roman fare (TRANSPORT-TICKET); āmittō and quid
  accidit? (PROBLEM-REPORT).
- **Experience (287-288).** laetus eram and the quod of feeling
  (ADJECTIVE-FEELING); spērō and cupiō with an infinitive
  (AMBITION-EXPRESS); -ior, quam, maior, minor, melior (COMPARISON-BASIC);
  the accusative of duration and abhinc (TIME-DURATION).
- **Conditions (289-290).** sī and nisi (CONNECTIVE-IF); the real
  condition (CONDITIONAL-REAL); pendet ex and in tuā potestāte est
  (CONDITION-DEPENDS); velim, possim, mālim (MODAL-WOULD); sī … essem,
  the imperfect subjunctive built on the infinitive; and LA-W290-epistula,
  six lines to a friend opening S. D. and sī valēs, bene est.
- New headwords: dum, iter, nāvis, naulum, āmittō, abhinc, pendet ex, and
  meā sententiā, mihi vidētur. Items earlier lessons already use (putō,
  ergō, sī, spērō) and the English homograph via are taught in grammar
  lessons, which register no forward-reference matcher.
- Etymologies are checked against Lewis & Short and EtymDB 2.1, and hedged
  where both are silent (the -b- of -bam, eram from *esam, the -i- of
  velim, the origins of dum and ergō).
- The five B1 spine ledgers no longer omit any concept.
