Malayalam, chapter 19: Asking Someone's Age.
2 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 2.
നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എത്ര വയസ്സുണ്ട്? — "to you, how much age exists?"

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Same Sanskrit word as Kannada and Telugu — but Malayalam builds a genuinely different sentence around it.

You'll want to know.
വയസ്സ് (vayass) = "age" — the same Sanskrit वयस् (vayas, "age, vigor," PIE cousin of Latin vīs) behind Kannada's vayassu and Telugu's vayasu.

Grammar Lens: dative subject + "exists".
നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എത്ര വയസ്സുണ്ട്? — "How old are you?" (formal/plural) — literally "to you, how much age exists?" (ningaḷkku ethra vayassuṇṭŭ? — from ningaḷkku, "to you" [dative] + ethra vayassu, "how much age" + uṇṭŭ, "exists.")
എനിക്ക് ഇരുപത് വയസ്സുണ്ട്. — "I am twenty years old." — literally "to me twenty age exists." (enikku irupatu vayassuṇṭŭ.)
This is a real grammatical departure from Kannada and Telugu's plain "your age how-much?" Malayalam puts the person in the dative case ("to you," not "your") and adds ഉണ്ട് (uṇṭŭ, "exists, there is") — the same existential verb Malayalam uses for ordinary possession and existence sentences generally. So the literal shape is "to me, twenty years' worth of age exists" — a dative-subject-plus-existential-verb construction, genuinely different from its two Dravidian cousins even though the age-word itself is identical.

[pause 3 seconds]

(No — it uses a dative subject, "to you," plus the existential verb ഉണ്ട്, "exists.") What does enikku irupatu vayassuṇṭŭ literally mean? ("To me twenty age exists.")

Script you'll notice: യ.
യ — ya.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not y, it is ya.
You already say these, and every one of them has യ (ya) somewhere inside it:
പോയി വരാം pōyi varāṁ — goodbye (lit. "I'll go and come back").
ജോലി ചെയ്യുക jōli ceyyuka — to work (lit. "work-do").
എനിക്ക് മലയാളം അറിയാം enikku malayāḷam aṟiyām — 'I know Malayalam'.
ദയവായി dayavāyi — please (dayavāyi.
