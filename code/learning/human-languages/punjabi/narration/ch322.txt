Punjabi, chapter 322: A Cut and a Bruise.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ਸੱਟ (saṭṭ) — an injury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for breath, then the Punjabi for to swallow.

You'll want to know: ਸੱਟ.
ਸੱਟ — saṭṭ — "an injury".
ਮੇਰੀ ਲੱਤ ਉੱਤੇ ਇੱਕ ਵੱਡੀ ਸੱਟ ਹੈ। merī latt utte ikk vaḍḍī saṭṭ hai. I have a big injury on my leg.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਸੱਟ (saṭṭ) is inherited from Sanskrit sṛṣṭi, "emission, creation"; CDIAL lists Punjabi saṭṭ, "a blow, a wound", under it (CDIAL 13579). How the sense moved is not explained there.

ਚਾਕੂ ਨਾਲ ਮੇਰੀ ਉਂਗਲ ਉੱਤੇ ਜ਼ਖ਼ਮ ਹੋਇਆ। chākū nāl merī uṅgal utte zakham hoiā. I got a cut on my finger from the knife.

Lesson 3 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for an injury, then the Punjabi for a wound.

You'll want to know: ਸੁੱਜਣਾ.
ਸੁੱਜਣਾ — sujjṇā — "to swell up".
ਸੱਟ ਤੋਂ ਬਾਅਦ ਮੇਰਾ ਗਿੱਟਾ ਸੁੱਜਿਆ। saṭṭ ton bāad merā giṭṭā sujjiā. After the injury my ankle swelled up.
Past (it …): ਉਹ ਗੋਡਾ ਬਹੁਤ ਸੁੱਜਿਆ, oh goḍā bahut sujjiā.

The word, taken apart.
ਸੁੱਜਣਾ (sujjṇā) is inherited from Sanskrit śūyate, "is swollen"; CDIAL lists Punjabi sujjṇā, "to swell", under it (CDIAL 12568).

ਮੈਂ ਜ਼ਖ਼ਮ ਉੱਤੇ ਸਾਫ਼ ਪੱਟੀ ਬੰਨ੍ਹੀ। maiṁ zakham utte sāf paṭṭī bannhī. I tied a clean bandage over the wound.

You'll want to know: ਮਲ੍ਹਮ.
ਮਲ੍ਹਮ — malham — "an ointment".
ਰਾਤ ਨੂੰ ਮੈਂ ਸੱਟ ਉੱਤੇ ਮਲ੍ਹਮ ਮਲੀ। rāt nū̃ maiṁ saṭṭ utte malham malī. At night I rubbed ointment on the injury.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਮਲ੍ਹਮ (malham) is a loan, usually traced through Persian to Arabic marham, "an ointment".
