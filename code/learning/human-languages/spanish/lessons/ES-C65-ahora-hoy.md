# ahora — now

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MANANA, ES-LEX-HORA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Mañana* — tomorrow. *La hora* — the hour.

[PAUSE 1s] One more time word, and it is hiding something you already know.

## The word, taken apart: ahora
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-AHORA, ES-ETYMON-AHORA-HAC-HORA]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HORA-01] -->

***Ahora*** — *now*. Look at what is sitting in the middle of it: ***hora***,
the word for *hour* you learned long ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Latin said ***hac hora*** — *at this hour*. Two words, worn together
into one, and the *h* in the middle went silent along the way. *Now* is
literally **this hour**. Spanish is full of these — phrases that stopped being
phrases.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PROGRESSIVE-ESTAR-GERUND, ES-LEX-COMER] -->

[PAUSE 2s] And now you can put a time on the tense you learned for the moment:

> ***Ahora*** *estoy comiendo.* — Right now, I'm eating.

[PAUSE 2s] Notice which tense it takes. *Ahora* goes with the progressive,
because both mean *at this instant* — the word and the tense are saying the
same thing about the clock, which is why they sit together so comfortably.
