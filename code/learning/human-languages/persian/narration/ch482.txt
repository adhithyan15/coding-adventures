Persian, chapter 482: Hurt Feelings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دلخور (delkhor) — upset (with someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for calm, then the Persian for to get angry.

You'll want to know: دلخور.
دلخور (delkhor) — "upset (with someone)".
خواهرم از حرف من دلخور است. khâharam az harf-e man delkhor ast. My sister is upset at what I said.
The cause takes az: az kasi delkhor budan. Built on del 'heart' and khor, the stem of khordan.

The word, taken apart.
delkhor is built on two native Persian pieces, usually explained as del, 'heart', and khor, the present stem of khordan, 'to eat': a heart that is eaten at. EtymDB traces del to Middle Persian (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
رنجیدن (ranjidan) — to take offence; to be hurt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to get angry, then the Persian for upset.

You'll want to know: رنجیدن.
رنجیدن (ranjidan) — "to take offence; to be hurt".
او از شوخی دوستش رنجید. u az shukhi-ye dustash ranjid. He was hurt by his friend's joke.
Present stem ranj-: miranjad 'takes offence'. The noun ranj means 'suffering, toil'.

The word, taken apart.
ranjidan is built on the native Persian noun ranj, 'suffering, toil', with the verb ending -idan. EtymDB has no entry for it, and the older history of ranj is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
آزرده (âzorde) — hurt (in one's feelings), offended.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for upset, then the Persian for to take offence.

You'll want to know: آزرده.
آزرده (âzorde) — "hurt (in one's feelings), offended".
او از رفتار سرد همکارش آزرده شد. u az raftâr-e sard-e hamkârash âzorde shod. She was hurt by her colleague's cold manner.
The past participle of âzordan 'to hurt, to offend' (present stem âzâr-). âzâr also means 'harm'.

The word, taken apart.
âzorde is built on the native Persian verb âzordan, 'to hurt, to offend': it is that verb's past participle. EtymDB has no entry for it, and its older form is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
کینه (kine) — a grudge; spite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to take offence, then the Persian for hurt.

You'll want to know: کینه.
کینه (kine) — "a grudge; spite".
او از کسی کینه به دل نمیگیرد. u az kasi kine be del nemigirad. He doesn't hold a grudge against anyone.
kine dâshtan or kine be del gereftan — to bear a grudge.

The word, taken apart.
kine is a native Persian word, usually traced to Middle Persian kēn, 'hatred, revenge'. EtymDB has no entry for it, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 5 of 5.
شرمنده (sharmande) — ashamed; very sorry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for hurt, then the Persian for a grudge.

You'll want to know: شرمنده.
شرمنده (sharmande) — "ashamed; very sorry".
او از رفتار بدش شرمنده بود. u az raftâr-e badash sharmande bud. He was ashamed of his bad behaviour.
Also a polite formula: sharmande-am 'I'm so sorry (to trouble you)'. Built on sharm 'shame'.

The word, taken apart.
sharmande is built on the native Persian noun sharm, 'shame', with the ending -ande. EtymDB lists Hindi sharmindā as a borrowing of it, but gives no source for the Persian word itself (EtymDB).
