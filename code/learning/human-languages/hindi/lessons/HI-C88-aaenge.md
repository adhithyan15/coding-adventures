# आएँगे — the ending you have been saying since the first goodbye

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-03, HI-CONCEPT-C04-MILENGE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the farewell that means *see you again*. The last two syllables
of it are about to become a rule.

## You'll want to know: आएँगे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-04]; assesses=[] -->

**आएँगे** (*āeṅge*) — "we will come," "they will come," and "you will come" to
somebody you address as **आप**.

**हम आएँगे।** — We will come. **आप कब आएँगे?** — When will you come?

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-04, HI-CONCEPT-C04-MILENGE-01] -->

You met this ending in your fourth chapter, inside **मिलेंगे** — *we will meet*.
It was named there as the plural future, and it was true, but it was one word.
Now it is a slot you can fill:

| stem | plural future |
|---|---|
| मिल- | मिलेंगे |
| आ- | आएँगे |
| कर- | करेंगे |

The farewell was never a lump. It was this ending, on a verb meaning *to meet*.

## Grammar Lens: आप takes the plural, again
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-04, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01] -->

| subject | future |
|---|---|
| हम | आएँगे |
| वे | आएँगे |
| आप | आएँगे |

**आप** takes the plural ending even for one person, for the reason it has taken
a plural everywhere else: the polite level in this language is built out of
plural forms. That is now the fifth system in which one decision about how you
address somebody settles the whole row.

For a group of women, the ending nasalises differently and becomes **आएँगी** —
worth recognising, because a practice paper asks *आप कब आएँगी?* of a woman.
