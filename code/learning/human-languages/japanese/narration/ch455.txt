Lesson 1 of 6.
きょうりょくする (kyōryoku suru) — to cooperate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to do over, then the Japanese for to fail.

You'll want to know: きょうりょくする.
きょうりょくする — kyōryoku suru — "to cooperate".
みんなで きょうりょくして、しごとを おえました。 — minna de kyōryoku shite, shigoto o oemashita — "We all cooperated and finished the work."
Polite きょうりょくします, kyōryokushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): どうりょうが きょうりょくしました。, dōryō ga kyōryoku shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
きょうりょくする (kyōryoku suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango) kyōryoku with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kyō, "cooperation" (Middle Chinese hep) and ryoku, "power" (lik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

ひきつぐ (hikitsugu) — to take over (duties); to hand over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a command, then the Japanese for being in charge.

You'll want to know: ひきつぐ.
ひきつぐ — hikitsugu — "to take over (duties); to hand over".
かれは ちちの しごとを ひきつぎました。 — kare wa chichi no shigoto o hikitsugimashita — "He took over his father's work."
Polite ひきつぎます, hikitsugimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): むすこが みせを ひきつぎました。, musuko ga mise o hikitsugimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ひきつぐ (hikitsugu) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: hiki, "pulling (hiku)" and tsugu, "to succeed to" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
