Persian, chapter 467: Gardens.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
باغ (bâgh) — a garden; an orchard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a pasture, then the Persian for to graze.

You'll want to know: باغ.
باغ (bâgh) — "a garden; an orchard".
خانه پدربزرگم یک باغ بزرگ دارد. Khâne-ye pedarbozorgam yek bâgh-e bozorg dârad. My grandfather's house has a big garden.
The Persian garden, with water and trees in a walled square, is famous.

The word, taken apart.
bâgh is inherited: EtymDB traces it to Middle Persian bāg 'garden, orchard'.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to graze, then the Persian for a garden.

You'll want to know: باغچه.
باغچه (bâghche) — "a flower bed, a small garden (in a courtyard)".
مادرم در باغچه حیاط گل کاشت. Mâdaram dar bâghche-ye hayât gol kâsht. My mother planted flowers in the courtyard bed.
bâgh + -che 'little'.

The word, taken apart.
bâghche is a native word, which EtymDB lists with the sense 'small garden'; it is built from bâgh 'garden' and -che 'little'.

باغبان (bâghbân) درختهای باغ را آب داد. Bâghbân derakht-hâ-ye bâgh râ âb dâd. The gardener watered the trees in the garden.
bâgh + -bân 'keeper', as in negahbân.

The word, taken apart.
bâghbân is a native compound of bâgh 'garden', from Middle Persian bāg (EtymDB), and -bân 'keeper', as in negahbân 'guard'.

بچه ها روی چمن فوتبال بازی کردند. Bachche-hâ ru-ye chaman futbâl bâzi kardand. The children played football on the grass.
Also an old word for a meadow in poetry.

chaman is a native word, which EtymDB lists with the senses 'lawn, meadow'. Its older form is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
هرس کردن (haras kardan) — to prune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a gardener, then the Persian for grass.

You'll want to know: هرس کردن.
هرس کردن (haras kardan) — "to prune".
باغبان (bâghbân) در زمستان درختها را هرس میکند. Bâghbân dar zemestân derakht-hâ râ haras mikonad. The gardener prunes the trees in winter.
haras 'pruning' + kardan.

The word, taken apart.
haras kardan is built from haras 'pruning', whose origin is not settled, and kardan 'to do', inherited from Middle Persian kardan (EtymDB).
