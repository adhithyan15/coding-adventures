Spanish, chapter 691: La vida en el campo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el agricultor — the farmer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the swimsuit, then the Spanish for to lie down.

You'll want to know: el agricultor.
el agricultor — "the farmer".
El agricultor trabaja desde muy temprano. The farmer works from very early.
A woman is la agricultora. La agricultura is farming.

The word, taken apart.
agricultor is a learned borrowing from Latin agricultor "a farmer" (EtymDB), made from ager "field" and cultor "one who tills".

You'll want to know: el campesino.
el campesino — "the country person, the peasant, the farm worker".
Los campesinos venden fruta en el mercado. The country people sell fruit at the market.
As an adjective it means country: la vida campesina.

The word, taken apart.
campesino is built on campo "field, countryside", which is inherited from Latin campus "a field, a plain".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la finca — the country estate, the farm; the property.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the farmer, then the Spanish for the country person.

You'll want to know: la finca.
la finca — "the country estate, the farm; the property".
Mis tíos tienen una finca con caballos. My aunt and uncle have a farm with horses.
In Spain una finca is also a building or plot in town.

The word, taken apart.
finca is built on old fincar "to stay put, to settle" (EtymDB), which comes from a Vulgar Latin form of fīgere "to fix".

Lesson 5 of 5.
cultivar — to grow (crops), to farm; to cultivate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: cultivar.
cultivar — "to grow (crops), to farm; to cultivate".
Aquí cultivan arroz. They grow rice here.
El cultivo is the crop or the growing of it.

The word, taken apart.
cultivar is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin cultīvāre (EtymDB), built on colere "to till, to tend".
