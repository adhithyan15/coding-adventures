# थ — the hole in the dental row

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-TA-01, MR-SCRIPT-DHA-01, MR-QUESTION-KUTHE, MR-GRAMMAR-WH-ORDER] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter three gave you **त**, **द**, **ध** and **न**. Four of a row of
five. This is the fifth.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-SCRIPT-THA-01]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-TA-01, MR-SCRIPT-TTHA-01] -->

> थ

**थ** is *tha*: **त** with the breath let out, exactly the step you already know
from **क**/**ख** and from **ट**/**ठ**. The sound is a dental *t* with a puff after
it — never the English *th* of *this* or *thin*.

The shape is worth a second look, because it is the one letter in the row that
does not resemble its plain partner at all. **त** has a hook; **थ** is a loop with
a stem through it. Do not expect to derive it — learn it as its own picture.

Curled **ठ** and dental **थ** are the pair readers confuse: **ठ** is a bare circle,
**थ** carries a vertical stem down the right.

## Writing — guided copy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-THA-01, MR-SCRIPT-TTHA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Copy **थ** twice with the model visible. Then write **ठ** beside it and check
that only one of the two has a stem.

needs the other one, and arrives in the next lesson. End the line with **?** or
**!** as before, and never both.
