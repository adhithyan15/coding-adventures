Mandarin Chinese, chapter 122: Opening Hours.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
yíngyè — to be open for business.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to revise, then the Mandarin for wrong.

You'll want to know: yíngyè.
yíngyè — "to be open for business".
shāngdiàn jǐ diǎn yíngyè — What time does the shop open?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yíngyè is built from yíng and yè, both inherited from Middle Chinese, yweng and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to build, to plan' and 'affairs'.

What you've built.
One more word for signs and notices.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: yíngyè]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yíngyè, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: shāngdiàn jǐ diǎn yíngyè — What time does the shop open?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to revise, then the Mandarin for wrong, then say yíngyè again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does yíngyè mean? ("To be open for business".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 7.
shíkèbiǎo — timetable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to begin, then the Mandarin for to come to an end.

You'll want to know: shíkèbiǎo.
shíkèbiǎo — "timetable".
wǒ yǒu yì zhāng huǒchē shíkèbiǎo — I've got a train timetable.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shíkèbiǎo is built from 时 'time', kè 'a notch, a quarter of an hour' and biǎo 'table', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyi, khok and pjewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'to carve' and the third 'to make clear'.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 7.
dǎzhé — to give a discount; to be on sale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to come to an end, then the Mandarin for timetable.

You'll want to know: dǎzhé.
dǎzhé — "to give a discount; to be on sale".
zhège xīngqī dǎzhé — Things are on sale this week.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎzhé is built from 打 'to strike, to do' and zhé, both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and tsyet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'to bend and break'. How the pair came to mean a discount is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for signs and notices.
