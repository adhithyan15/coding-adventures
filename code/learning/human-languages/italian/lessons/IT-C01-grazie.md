# grazie — "thank you," from *grace*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "Thanks" — and it literally offers someone *grace*.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-SOUND-GRAZIE-02]; assesses=[] -->

- **grazie** = *GRAH-tsyeh*: **z** in Italian is *ts*; the final *-e* is sounded
  (so *-zie* is two beats).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-GRAZIE-03]; assesses=[] -->

**grazie** ("thanks") ← Latin **gratiae**, plural of **gratia** ("favour,
thanks, grace"). It's the *same* *gratia* behind English **grace**,
**grat**itude, **grat**eful, **grat**is ("free"), con**grat**ulate, and
in**grat**iate. So *grazie* literally wishes someone **grace**.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-PRAGMATIC-GRAZIE-04]; assesses=[] -->

Think of **grazie** as offering someone "graces" for what they did. In the next
chapter, you will learn the short reply people use after hearing it.
