Lesson 1 of 6.
l'arbitre — referee, umpire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sweat, then the French for to warm up.

You'll want to know: l'arbitre.
l'arbitre — "referee, umpire".
L'arbitre a donné un carton rouge. The referee gave a red card.
The same form for a man and a woman: un arbitre, une arbitre.

The word, taken apart.
l'arbitre is a learned borrowing from Latin arbiter "a witness, a judge" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
le sifflet — whistle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to warm up, then the French for referee.

You'll want to know: le sifflet.
le sifflet — "whistle".
Au coup de sifflet, les joueurs sont sortis. At the whistle, the players went off.
Le coup de sifflet final — the final whistle.

The word, taken apart.
le sifflet is built on siffler "to whistle", which goes back to Late Latin sīfilāre, a form of Latin sībilāre "to hiss, to whistle" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
le terrain — pitch, ground; plot of land.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for whistle, then the French for to whistle.

You'll want to know: le terrain.
le terrain — "pitch, ground; plot of land".
Les joueurs entrent sur le terrain. The players come onto the pitch.
Un terrain de foot — a football pitch. Also a plot: acheter un terrain.

The word, taken apart.
le terrain is inherited from Latin terrēnum "land, ground" (EtymDB), from terra "earth" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
marquer — to score; to mark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to whistle, then the French for pitch.

You'll want to know: marquer.
marquer — "to score; to mark".
Elle a marqué deux buts dans le match. She scored two goals in the match.
Marquer un but — to score a goal. Also to note down: marquer une adresse.

The word, taken apart.
marquer is built on marque "a mark, a sign", a word of Germanic origin related to English mark.
