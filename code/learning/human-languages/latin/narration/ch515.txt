Latin, chapter 515: The face.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vultus, vultūs — face, expression.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for indignation, then the Latin for to displease.

You'll want to know: vultus, vultūs.
vultus, vultūs — "face, expression".
Vultus mātris laetus est. Mother's face is happy.
Masculine, fourth declension. The face as it shows feeling and mood.

The word, taken apart.
vultus is a native Latin noun; EtymDB links it with a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to see", while Lewis & Short calls it doubtful, so the origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
faciēs, faciēī — appearance, shape; face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to displease, then the Latin for face.

You'll want to know: faciēs, faciēī.
faciēs, faciēī — "appearance, shape; face".
Puer faciem in aquā lavat. The boy washes his face in the water.
Feminine, fifth declension. The shape and look of a face, or of anything; vultus is the face as it shows feeling. English face.

The word, taken apart.
faciēs is usually traced to the root of faciō "to make" (EtymDB): the make of a thing, its shape; Lewis & Short links it with fax "torch" instead. English face and surface come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
crīnis, crīnis — hair, a lock of hair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for face, then the Latin for appearance.

You'll want to know: crīnis, crīnis.
crīnis, crīnis — "hair, a lock of hair".
Crīnēs puellae longī et flāvī sunt. The girl's hair is long and golden.
Masculine, third declension; usually plural. A word of poetry and fine prose; capillus is the everyday word.

The word, taken apart.
crīnis is a native Latin noun; Lewis & Short and EtymDB compare crista "crest", but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
supercilium, superciliī — eyebrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for appearance, then the Latin for hair.

You'll want to know: supercilium, superciliī.
supercilium, superciliī — "eyebrow".
Senex supercilia alba habet. The old man has white eyebrows.
Neuter, second declension. Also a frown, and so pride: supercilium — haughtiness. English supercilious.

The word, taken apart.
supercilium is built on super "above" and cilium "eyelid" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English supercilious comes from it, from a raised eyebrow.

Lesson 5 of 5.
palpebra, palpebrae — eyelid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for hair, then the Latin for eyebrow.

You'll want to know: palpebra, palpebrae.
palpebra, palpebrae — "eyelid".
Puer fessus palpebrās claudit. The tired boy closes his eyelids.
Feminine, first declension; usually plural. English palpebral, in medicine.

The word, taken apart.
palpebra is built on palpō "to stroke, touch softly" (EtymDB), with an ending for a tool: the soft lid that strokes the eye.
