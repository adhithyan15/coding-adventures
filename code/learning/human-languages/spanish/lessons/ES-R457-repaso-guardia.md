## Grammar Lens: the article doing the work
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-03] -->

| feminine — the thing | masculine — the person |
|---|---|
| la guardia — the watch | el guardia — the officer |
| la guía — the guidebook | el guía — the guide |
| la policía — the force | el policía — the officer |

One pattern, and it is worth having in advance: the **feminine** names the
abstract thing or the body, the **masculine** names a person doing it. A
Spanish speaker hears the article and knows which you meant before the noun
arrives.

So *la farmacia de guardia* has nothing to do with a man on a door.

## Grammar Lens: two of the three were relatives
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-01, ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02] -->

| new word | what it was built on |
|---|---|
| **el medicamento** | *el médico* — healer and the thing that heals |
| **encargar** | *el cargo*, and *el carrito* under that |
| **la guardia** | *guardar*, from Germanic *wardōn* |

All three, in fact. *Encargar* is the clearest: a cart, then a load, then a
charge on a bill, then a task loaded onto somebody. Four lessons apart and one
picture throughout.
