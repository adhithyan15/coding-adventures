Japanese, chapter 478: Growing Up Together.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ねだる (nedaru) — to pester for; to beg for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bother, then the Japanese for upbringing.

You'll want to know: ねだる.
ねだる — nedaru — "to pester for; to beg for".
すえっこが おかしを ねだっています。 — suekko ga okashi o nedatte imasu — "The youngest child is pestering for sweets."
Polite ねだります, nedarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): むすこが おかしを ねだりました。, musuko ga okashi o nedarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ねだる (nedaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana; its kanji spelling borrows characters meaning "strong" and "ask", chosen for the meaning and not the sound (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: まねる.
まねる — maneru — "to imitate; to mimic".
こどもは ははを まねています。 — kodomo wa haha o manete imasu — "The child is imitating her mother."
Polite まねます, manemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): おとうとが あにを まねました。, otōto ga ani o manemashita.

The word, taken apart.
まねる (maneru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. It shares its first part and its kanji spelling with the noun mane, "imitating"; that spelling pairs "true" and "resemble" for the meaning, not the sound (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
