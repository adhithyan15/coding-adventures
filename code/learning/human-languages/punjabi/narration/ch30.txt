Punjabi, chapter 30: Build One Fictional Phone Field.
15 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 15.
ਫ (pha) — one new base shape.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one letter until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Write the familiar ਪ once. The new base begins similarly but is not the same letter.

Script — one letter.
Trace ਫ slowly twice. This is pha. Stop before adding any dot or vowel mark.

Guided Practice.
Cover the model, write ਫ (pha) once, then compare only its shape.

Wrap-up recall.
One new base is enough.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Write the new Gurmukhi base letter pha.


Lesson 2 of 15.
ਫ਼ (fa) — place one dot below.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one mark until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Write ਫ (pha) once and leave room directly below it.

Script — one mark.
Add the small dot below: ਫ + ਼ = ਫ਼. This below-letter dot is pairin bindi; here it changes pha to the borrowed sound fa. It is not the above-letter bindi already used in ਹਾਂ.

Guided Practice.
Copy ਫ਼ (fa) once. Point to the dot below before you stop.

Wrap-up recall.
Only the base and its below-letter dot are scored here.
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Add the below-letter dot to ਫ (pha) and write the result.

Lesson 8 of 15.
Read one place at a time — recognise and produce each introduced phone digit separately.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
Shuffle four cards marked ੦ (zero), ੧, ੨, ੫. Name each card before writing.

You'll want to know — locate, do not guess.
In A, circle the second digit. In B, circle the fifth digit. Read the complete values only after locating those positions.

Guided Practice.
Hear: zero, one, two, two, five, one. Write one digit after each word, then compare with A.

Wrap-up recall.
No unintroduced numeral is scored.
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]

Lesson 9 of 15.
Choose A or B before writing — select one known fictional phone value before writing digits.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
Read the closed bank once: A ੦੧੨੨੫੧ (zero-one-two-two-five-one); B ੦੨੫੧੨੫ (zero-two-five-one-two-five).

You'll want to know — two fixed selectors.
For this fictional practice only, ਕ means A and ਖ means B. Point to the matching value before your pen moves. The selector is not part of the phone field.

Guided Practice.
For ਖ, select B. For ਕ, select A. Selection and digit writing are separate checks.

Wrap-up recall.
The selectors carry no special meaning outside this exercise.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
The fictional selector is ਖ. Select A or B, then give its known value.

Lesson 11 of 15.
Three digits, one space, three digits — group a six-digit fictional phone value as three plus three.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one grouping boundary until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Touch A in six places: ੦ (zero), ੧, ੨, ੨, ੫, ੧.

Script — one grouping boundary.
Insert one clear space after the third digit: ੦੧੨ ੨੫੧ (three digits, space, three digits) (heard digit string). The space helps the eye track the practice value; it does not remove or reorder a digit. B groups as ੦੨੫ ੧੨੫.

Guided Practice.
Copy grouped B once. Count three digits, make one space, then count three more.

Wrap-up recall.
This book keeps one grouping inside the bounded practice bank; it is not a claim about every real-world phone format.
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Rewrite fictional value A with its three-plus-three grouping.

Lesson 13 of 15.
Hear six digits and write them — transcribe one heard fictional phone value into grouped Gurmukhi digits.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
Without a number model, write the four introduced digits when you hear: zero, one, two, five.

Writing — digit by digit.
Listen twice: zero — one — two | two — five — one. The pause marks the one group space. Write Gurmukhi digits; do not write English numerals or words.

Guided Practice.
Read your six digits back left to right. Check the space only after checking all six positions.

Wrap-up recall.
Romanization and Latin digits do not earn the Gurmukhi writing score.
[question — say your answer, then pause 36 seconds]
Write in Gurmukhi digits: zero, one, two; pause; two, five, one.
