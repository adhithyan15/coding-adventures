## You'll want to know: 房东
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C203-RENT203-02]; assesses=[] -->

**房东** — *fángdōng* — "landlord; landlady".

> **我的房东人很好** *wǒ de fángdōng rén hěn hǎo* — My landlord is a nice person.

The new character, in pieces: 东 — five strokes: a bar, a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, a vertical that hooks to the left, then a short stroke falling to the left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**房东** (*fángdōng*) is built from 房 'house, room' and 东 'east', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *bjang* and *tuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean the owner who lets a room is not settled.
