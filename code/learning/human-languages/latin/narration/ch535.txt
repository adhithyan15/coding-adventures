Latin, chapter 535: Lovers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
amplector, amplectī — to embrace, hug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a kiss on the lips, then the Latin for an embrace.

You'll want to know: amplector, amplectī.
amplector, amplectī — "to embrace, hug".
Pater fīlium redeuntem amplectitur. The father embraces his son as he comes home.
Deponent: amplector, amplectī, amplexus sum: ambi- 'around' + plectō 'to twine'. Also to welcome an idea.

The word, taken apart.
amplector is built on ambi- "around" and plector, from plectō "to twine, plait" (EtymDB): to wind the arms around.

Lesson 2 of 5.
complector, complectī — to clasp, embrace; to include.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for an embrace, then the Latin for to embrace.

You'll want to know: complector, complectī.
complector, complectī — "to clasp, embrace; to include".
Amīcī post longum tempus sē complectuntur. The friends embrace after a long time.
Deponent: complector, complectī, complexus sum. Also to take in, to include. English complex comes from its participle.

The word, taken apart.
complector is built on con- and plectō "to twine" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English complex comes from its participle complexus.

Lesson 3 of 5.
amāns, amantis — a lover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to embrace, then the Latin for to clasp.

You'll want to know: amāns, amantis.
amāns, amantis — "a lover".
Amāns sub fenestrā puellae cantat. The lover sings under the girl's window.
The present participle of amō, used as a noun. Amantēs — lovers.

The word, taken apart.
amāns is the present participle of amō "to love", used as a noun (Lewis & Short). English amorous belongs to the same family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
amātor, amātōris — an admirer, lover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to clasp, then the Latin for a lover.

You'll want to know: amātor, amātōris.
amātor, amātōris — "an admirer, lover".
Amātor puellae flōrēs mittit. Her admirer sends the girl flowers.
Masculine, third declension. Also a lover of things: amātor librōrum. English amateur comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
amātor is built on amō "to love" with -tor (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English amateur, one who does a thing for love, comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
procus, procī — a suitor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lover, then the Latin for an admirer.

You'll want to know: procus, procī.
procus, procī — "a suitor".
Multī procī fīliam rēgis petunt. Many suitors seek the king's daughter.
Masculine, second declension; usually plural. A word of epic and comedy.

The word, taken apart.
procus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to ask" (EtymDB), the same root as precor "to pray": a suitor is one who asks.
