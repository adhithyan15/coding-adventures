Italian, chapter 450: Feste di paese.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la sagra — the village food festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to collapse, then the Italian for to clear.

You'll want to know: la sagra.
la sagra — "the village food festival".
Domenica c'è la sagra del pesce. On Sunday there is the fish festival.
Each one celebrates a local food: chestnuts, mushrooms, wine.

The word, taken apart.
sagra is inherited from Latin sacrum 'sacred rite' (EtymDB), from sacer 'holy': first the feast of a church.

Lesson 2 of 5.
il corteo — the parade, the procession, the march.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to clear, then the Italian for the village food festival.

You'll want to know: il corteo.
il corteo — "the parade, the procession, the march".
Il corteo passa per la via principale. The parade goes along the main street.
A political march too: un corteo di protesta.

The word, taken apart.
corteo is built on corte 'court', which is inherited from Latin cohors, cohortis 'yard, retinue' (Lewis & Short): first the people following a lord.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la processione — the procession (religious).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the village food festival, then the Italian for the parade.

You'll want to know: la processione.
la processione — "the procession (religious)".
La processione esce dalla chiesa alle sei. The procession leaves the church at six.
It often follows a statue of the patron saint.

The word, taken apart.
processione is a learned borrowing from Latin prōcessiō 'a going forward' (EtymDB), from prōcēdere 'to go forward', built on cēdere 'to go'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il patrono — the patron saint; the patron.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the parade, then the Italian for the procession.

You'll want to know: il patrono.
il patrono — "the patron saint; the patron".
Il giorno del patrono i negozi sono chiusi. On the patron saint's day the shops are closed.
La festa patronale — the patron saint's feast.

The word, taken apart.
patrono is a learned borrowing from Latin patrōnus 'protector, patron' (EtymDB), from pater 'father'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la bancarella — the stall (at a market).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the procession, then the Italian for the patron saint.

You'll want to know: la bancarella.
la bancarella — "the stall (at a market)".
Alle bancarelle vendono dolci e giocattoli. At the stalls they sell sweets and toys.
Built on banco: a little bench to sell from.

The word, taken apart.
bancarella is built on banco 'bench, counter', which was borrowed from a Germanic word for a bench (EtymDB).
