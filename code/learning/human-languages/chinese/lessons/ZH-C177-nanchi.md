## You'll want to know: nánchī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C177-FULL177-05]; assesses=[] -->

*nánchī* — "tasting bad; horrible to eat".

> *zhège cài bù xīnxiān hěn nánchī* — This dish isn't fresh, and it tastes horrible.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*nánchī* is built from *nán* 'hard' and 吃 'to eat': hard to eat. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese *nan* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); for the second the dictionary has *khek*, glossed 'to eat', under another way of writing it, though *khek* would regularly give qì.
