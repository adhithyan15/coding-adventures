Spanish, chapter 625: Legal o ilegal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vigente — in force, current.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the certificate, then the Spanish for official.

You'll want to know: vigente.
vigente — "in force, current".
Esta ley ya no está vigente. This law is no longer in force.
For laws, rules and prices.

The word, taken apart.
vigente is a learned borrowing from Latin vigens 'thriving, strong' (EtymDB), from vigere 'to be lively'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ilegal — illegal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for official, then the Spanish for in force.

You'll want to know: ilegal.
ilegal — "illegal".
Aparcar aquí es ilegal. Parking here is illegal.
The opposite of legal.

The word, taken apart.
ilegal is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin illegalis, built from in- 'not' and legalis, from lex 'law' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
denunciar — to report; to denounce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for illegal, then the Spanish for to break.

You'll want to know: denunciar.
denunciar — "to report; to denounce".
Denuncié el robo a la policía. I reported the theft to the police.
Denunciar to the police is to make a formal report.

The word, taken apart.
denunciar is a learned borrowing from Latin denuntiare 'to announce, to give notice', built on nuntius 'messenger' (EtymDB).
