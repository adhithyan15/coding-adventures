# legō — reading is picking things up

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-COGITO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Cōgitō** drives ideas together. The next verb gathers
something more concrete — and it is the single richest word in this book.

## You'll want to know: legō — "I read"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-LEGO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**Legō** means "**I read**." Its older and wider sense is "I gather, I pick
out." The dictionary form is **legere**. Every *g* is hard and every vowel
here is short: *LEH-goh*, *LEH-geh-reh*.

- **legō** — I read
- **legis** — you read, to one person
- **legit** — he reads, she reads
- **legimus** — we read
- **legitis** — you read, to several people
- **legunt** — they read

Reading is the younger meaning, and you can feel how it arrived: you pick
the letters up, one after another. A Roman normally did this aloud.

## The word, taken apart: the gathering that became reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-LEGO-02]; assesses=[] -->

The Proto-Indo-European root is ***\*leg-***, "to gather, to collect."

Straight from *legere* English has **legible**, **legend** ("things to be
read"), **lectern**, **lecture**, and — through Old French *leçon*, from
*lectiōnem*, "a reading" — **lesson**. A Roman **legion** was a *legiō*, a
levy of men picked out for service.

Prefixed, the verb becomes a machine. *Con-* gives **collect** (gather
together); *sē-* gives **select** (gather apart); *ē-* gives **elect** (pick
out). **Neglect** is *nec legere*, "not to pick up." **Elegant** is
*ēlegāns* — originally an insult, "too choosy."

Greek inherited the same root as *légō*, "I gather, I speak," and its noun
*lógos*, "word, reason." From those come **logic**, **catalogue**,
**dialogue**, **analogy**, **anthology** (a flower-gathering), and the
*-logy* ending on every science you can name.

One caution. Latin *lēx*, *lēgis* ("law") — behind **legal**,
**legislate**, **loyal**, and **privilege** — is often derived from
*legere*, "what is read out." That link is **debated**; treat it as a maybe.

And a false friend: English **read** is Germanic, from a word meaning "to
advise," still visible in **riddle**. It has nothing to do with *legō*.
