Tamil, chapter 485: Home Remedies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
கஷாயம் (kaṣāyam) — a herbal decoction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to fall out, then the Tamil for to turn grey.

You'll want to know: கஷாயம்.
கஷாயம் — kaṣāyam — "a herbal decoction".
சளிக்குப் பாட்டி கஷாயம் செய்து கொடுத்தாள். caḷikkup pāṭṭi kaṣāyam ceytu koṭuttāḷ. Grandmother made a decoction for the cold.
Written with the grantha ஷ, as Tamil writes it today.

The word, taken apart.
கஷாயம் (kaṣāyam) is borrowed from Sanskrit kaṣāya, "astringent; an astringent juice" (CDIAL 2974).

Lesson 2 of 5.
சுக்கு (cukku) — dry ginger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to turn grey, then the Tamil for a herbal decoction.

You'll want to know: சுக்கு.
சுக்கு — cukku — "dry ginger".
சுக்கு போட்ட காபி தொண்டைக்கு நல்லது. cukku pōṭṭa kāpi toṇṭaikku nallatu. Coffee with dry ginger is good for the throat.
Fresh ginger is இஞ்சி; dried, it is சுக்கு (cukku).

The word, taken apart.
The origin of சுக்கு (cukku) is not settled; it is sometimes compared with Sanskrit śuṇṭhī, "dry ginger" (CDIAL 12515), but the link is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
கஞ்சி (kañci) — rice gruel; starch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a herbal decoction, then the Tamil for dry ginger.

You'll want to know: கஞ்சி.
கஞ்சி — kañci — "rice gruel; starch".
காய்ச்சலின்போது அம்மா எனக்குக் கஞ்சி கொடுத்தாள். kāyccaliṉpōtu ammā eṉakkuk kañci koṭuttāḷ. When I had a fever, mother gave me rice gruel.
Also starch for clothes: கஞ்சி (kañci) போட்ட சட்டை, a starched shirt.

The word, taken apart.
கஞ்சி (kañci) is native Dravidian (DEDR 1107), the water poured off rice and the gruel made from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
களிம்பு (kaḷimpu) — an ointment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for rice gruel, then the Tamil for a medicated oil.

You'll want to know: களிம்பு.
களிம்பு — kaḷimpu — "an ointment".
மருத்துவர் புண்ணுக்கு ஒரு களிம்பு கொடுத்தார். maruttuvar puṇṇukku oru kaḷimpu koṭuttār. The doctor gave an ointment for the sore.
Its older sense is verdigris, the green rust on copper.

The word, taken apart.
களிம்பு (kaḷimpu) is native Dravidian (DEDR 1586), where it means verdigris and rust; the sense of ointment is the modern one.
