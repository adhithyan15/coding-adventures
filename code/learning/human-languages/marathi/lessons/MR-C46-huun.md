## You'll want to know: -हून
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-POSTP-HUUN]; assesses=[MR-A1-FORM-LABEL-SHAHAR, MR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE, MR-LEX-YENE] -->

> **-हून** — *-hūn* — **from**

> **मी पुण्याहून येतो.**
> — *mī puṇyāhūn yeto.* — **I come from Pune.**

That sentence is the second thing said in almost every spoken A1 interview, and
until this lesson the book could write **शहर: पुणे** on a form and not say it out
loud. A form field is a label; **-हून** is an answer.

The stem is the one you already bend: **पुणे → पुण्या-**, then **-हून**, no
space. And because **इथे** and **तिथे** are place words too, they take it and
give you two more:

> **इथून** — *ithūn* — **from here**
>
> **तिथून** — *tithūn* — **from there**

Notice what happened to the shape: **इथे** ends in **-ए** and the ending
replaced it, exactly as it does on **पुणे**. The rule you learned in chapter
forty-one is doing all the work.
