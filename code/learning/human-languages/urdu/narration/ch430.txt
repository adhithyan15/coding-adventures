Urdu, chapter 430: Heat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پارہ (pārā) — the temperature (in everyday talk); mercury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to shrink, then the Urdu for to go stiff.

You'll want to know: پارہ.
پارہ — pārā — "the temperature (in everyday talk); mercury".
گرمیوں میں پارہ چالیس سے اوپر جاتا ہے۔ garmiyoṅ meṅ pārā chālīs se ūpar jātā hai. In summer the temperature goes above forty.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
پارہ (pārā) comes from Sanskrit pārada, "quicksilver" (Monier-Williams); the mercury in a thermometer gave Urdu its everyday word for the temperature.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تپنا (tapnā) — to be burning hot; to scorch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the temperature (in everyday talk), then the Urdu for a degree (of temperature).

You'll want to know: تپنا.
تپنا — tapnā — "to be burning hot; to scorch".
دوپہر کو سڑک دھوپ میں تپتی ہے۔ dopahar ko saṛak dhūp meṅ taptī hai. At midday the road bakes in the sun.
Past (it …): وہ زمین دن بھر تپی, voh zamīn din bhar tapī.

The word, taken apart.
تپنا (tapnā) is inherited from Sanskrit tápyati, "is hot": CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin tāpnā, "to warm oneself", under it (CDIAL 5684).

Lesson 4 of 5.
حبس (habs) — airless heat; sultriness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a degree (of temperature), then the Urdu for to be burning hot.

You'll want to know: حبس.
حبس — habs — "airless heat; sultriness".
بارش سے پہلے بڑا حبس تھا۔ bārish se pehle baṛā habs thā. Before the rain it was very close and airless.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
حبس (habs) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḥabs, "shutting in, confinement"; the sense "airless heat" probably grew in Urdu.

Lesson 5 of 5.
جھلنا (jhalnā) — to fan (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to be burning hot, then the Urdu for airless heat.

You'll want to know: جھلنا.
جھلنا — jhalnā — "to fan (someone)".
گرمی میں ماں نے بچے کو پنکھا جھلا۔ garmī meṅ māṅ ne bachche ko pankhā jhalā. In the heat the mother fanned the child.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے پنکھا جھلا, us ne pankhā jhalā.

The word, taken apart.
جھلنا (jhalnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin jhalnā, "to move a fan to and fro", under jhal, "a sudden movement", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5351).
