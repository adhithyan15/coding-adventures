Latin, chapter 360: Plain, Clear and Absurd.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
manifestus, manifesta, manifestum — evident, plain; caught in the act.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for likely, then the Latin for probable.

You'll want to know: manifestus, manifesta, manifestum.
manifestus, manifesta, manifestum — "evident, plain; caught in the act".
Rēs manifesta est: fūr pecūniam habet. The matter is plain: the thief has the money.
manifestus, manifesta, manifestum. A thief manifestus is caught red-handed. English manifest.

The word, taken apart.
manifestus is built from manus "hand" and an old verb fendō "to strike", the one in dēfendō and offendō (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a thing you could strike with your hand, so plain to see. English manifest comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

perspicuus, perspicua, perspicuum — "clear, transparent; evident".
Haec ratiō perspicua est omnibus. This reasoning is clear to everyone.
perspicuus, perspicua, perspicuum. Water or glass you see through, and an argument you see through to the end. English perspicuous.

The word, taken apart.
perspicuus is built on perspiciō "to see through" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from per "through" and the old verb speciō "to look". English perspicuous comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
absurdus, absurda, absurdum — absurd, foolish; out of tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for evident, then the Latin for clear.

You'll want to know: absurdus, absurda, absurdum.
absurdus, absurda, absurdum — "absurd, foolish; out of tune".
Absurdum est hoc dīcere. It is absurd to say this.
absurdus, absurda, absurdum. First a voice out of tune, then anything that jars with sense. English absurd.

The word, taken apart.
absurdus is built from ab "away from" and surdus "deaf, dull-sounding" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a note that sounds wrong. English absurd comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
stultitia, stultitiae — foolishness, folly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for clear, then the Latin for absurd.

You'll want to know: stultitia, stultitiae.
stultitia, stultitiae — "foolishness, folly".
Magna stultitia est hostī crēdere. It is great folly to trust an enemy.
stultitia, stultitiae f., the noun of stultus, foolish. Its opposite is sapientia.

The word, taken apart.
stultitia is the noun built on stultus "foolish" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -itia that makes a quality.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cōnstantia, cōnstantiae — steadiness, firmness, consistency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for absurd, then the Latin for foolishness.

You'll want to know: cōnstantia, cōnstantiae.
cōnstantia, cōnstantiae — "steadiness, firmness, consistency".
Cōnstantiam eius omnēs laudant. Everyone praises his steadiness.
cōnstantia, cōnstantiae f. A person of cōnstantia holds the same view today as yesterday. English constancy.

The word, taken apart.
cōnstantia is the noun made from the present participle of a verb built on con- "together" and stō "to stand" (Lewis & Short): standing firm. English constancy and constant come from the same family.
