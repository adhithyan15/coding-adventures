Lesson 1 of 8.
进 (jìn) — to come in; to enter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for airport, then the Mandarin for ticket.

You'll want to know: 进.
进 — jìn — "to come in; to enter".
他进门了 tā jìn mén le — He's come in at the door.
The new character, in pieces: 进 (jìn) — a four-stroke piece read jǐng, a well, on the road piece of 这. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
进 (jìn) is inherited from Middle Chinese tsinH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'forward' and 'to advance'.

What you've built.
One more word for meeting people.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kāi, zhè]

Script — jǐng on the road piece.
进 (jìn).
Two pieces: jǐng first, the road last, though the road runs up the left and under.
Jǐng, four strokes: two horizontals, then a left-falling stroke and a vertical down through them, the same four as 开.
The road piece, three strokes, as in 这: a dot, then a stroke that turns and turns again, then a long sweep along the bottom to the right.
4 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — jiā, zì, kǒu]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — under the roof, a foot and 口.
客 (kè).
Three pieces, top to bottom.
The roof, three strokes, as on 家 and 字: a dot, a short stroke down the left, and a horizontal that turns down and hooks.
The walking foot, three strokes: a short left-falling stroke, a horizontal that turns into a long left-falling stroke, and a long stroke falling to the right.
口, three strokes, at the bottom.
3 + 3 + 3 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 客 (kè)? (Three.) Which characters you know have the same roof? (家 and 字.) What sits at the bottom? (口.)

Lesson 7 of 8.
jièshào — to introduce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: jièshào.
jièshào — "to introduce".
qǐng nǐ jièshào nǐ de jiārén — Tell us about your family, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jièshào is built from jiè and shào, both inherited from Middle Chinese, keajH and dzyewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as 'to help' and the second as 'to carry on'; how the pair came to mean introducing somebody is not settled.
