You'll want to know: 该.
该 — gāi — "it's time to; should (by now)".
我该回家了 wǒ gāi huí jiā le — It's time I went home.
该你了 gāi nǐ le — Your turn.
The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
该 (gāi) is inherited from Middle Chinese koj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'complete' and 'all'. 'Should' and 'it's time to' are not given there, so how the word came by them is not settled.

What you've built.
With 了 at the end, 该 (gāi) says the moment has come: 我该回家了, 该你了.
It sits before the verb, after the person, where 要 and 想 sit.

Lesson 3 of 8.
应该 (yīnggāi) — should; ought to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: the character you wrote last, in the sentence it belongs to.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 我该回家了, aloud — wǒ gāi huí jiā le]
[your turn — recall: say 医生 — yīshēng, a doctor — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 应该.
应该 — yīnggāi — "should; ought to".
你应该去看医生 nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng — You should see a doctor.
The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. The second is the 该 (gāi) you wrote last.

The word, taken apart.
应该 (yīnggāi) is built from 应 and 该 (gāi), both inherited from Middle Chinese. The Guangyun lists 应 under two readings: 'ing, glossed with a word that can mean 'ought to', and 'ingH, glossed 'things answering each other'; yīng is the first. 该 (gāi) is koj, glossed 'complete' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
该 (gāi) says the moment has come; 应该 (yīnggāi) gives advice, or says what is right.
Both sit before the verb: 你应该去看医生 nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng. 看医生 is to see a doctor.

Grammar Lens: 应该 and 不应该, advice and what is right.
应该 (yīnggāi) before a verb gives advice, or says what is right; 不应该 says what is not.
你应该去看医生 nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng — You should see a doctor. 你不应该这么晚回家 nǐ bù yīnggāi zhème wǎn huí jiā — You shouldn't come home so late.
不 goes in front of 应该 (yīnggāi), and stays bù before the level yīng.
To ask for advice: wǒ yīnggāi zěnme bàn?, what should I do?
该 (gāi) says the time has come; 应该 (yīnggāi) says what is right, whatever the time.

Lesson 6 of 8.
最好 (zuìhǎo) — had better.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: advice, and the character it starts with.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 你不应该这么晚回家, aloud — nǐ bù yīnggāi zhème wǎn huí jiā]
[once you have stopped driving — read: 最后, aloud — zuìhòu]

You'll want to know: 最好.
最好 — zuìhǎo — "had better; it would be best to".
你最好早点儿回家 nǐ zuìhǎo zǎo diǎnr huí jiā — You'd better go home early.
nǐ zuìhǎo bié qù — You'd better not go.
Every character in it is one you write.

The word, taken apart.
最好 (zuìhǎo) is built from 最 'most' and 好 'good', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tswajH and xawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'utmost' and 'good'. 'Best' is the plain sum of the two; 'had better' is the word's own use.

What you've built.
应该 (yīnggāi) says what is right; 最好 (zuìhǎo) says what is wise, the best of the choices.
It goes where 应该 (yīnggāi) goes, before the verb: 你最好早点儿回家 nǐ zuìhǎo zǎo diǎnr huí jiā.

Lesson 7 of 8.
děi — to have to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, and a character you know in another job.
[your turn — recall: say 最好 — zuìhǎo, had better — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: 你说得很好, aloud — nǐ shuō de hěn hǎo]

You'll want to know: děi.
děi — "to have to; must".
我得回家了 wǒ děi huí jiā le — I have to go home now.
明天我得去上课 míngtiān wǒ děi qù shàngkè — I have to go to class tomorrow.
It is written 得, the character of 说得好, but it is a different word with its own sound, děi.

The word, taken apart.
děi is written with 得, which is inherited from Middle Chinese tok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to get'. děi is not the regular modern sound of tok, and 'to have to' is not given there; how the character came to write this word is not settled.

What you've built.
应该 (yīnggāi) is advice, 最好 (zuìhǎo) is what is wise, and děi leaves no choice: 我得回家了.
So 得 in a sentence has two sounds: de after a verb, as in 说得好, and děi before one, as here.

你病了 应该去看医生 (nǐ bìng le, yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng) — two speakers weigh what a sick friend should, had better and has to do.

[your turn — recall: say hǎoxiàng, as if — R3]

The exchange.
A: wǒ hǎoxiàng bìng le — I think I'm ill. B: 你应该去看医生 nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng — You should see a doctor. A: 我得去上课 wǒ děi qù shàngkè — I have to go to class. B: 你病了 不应该去上课 nǐ bìng le, bù yīnggāi qù shàngkè — You're ill. You shouldn't go to class. A: 那我该做什么 nà wǒ gāi zuò shénme — Then what should I do? B: 你最好在家 nǐ zuìhǎo zài jiā — You'd better stay at home. A: 好 我该回家了 hǎo, wǒ gāi huí jiā le — All right. Time I went home.
Four words of advice and duty, in rising order of force: 最好 (zuìhǎo) is wise, 应该 (yīnggāi) is right, 该 (gāi) says the time has come, děi allows no choice.

你最好在家 nǐ zuìhǎo zài jiā — what is wise.
我该回家了 wǒ gāi huí jiā le — the time has come.
我得去上课 wǒ děi qù shàngkè — no choice.
