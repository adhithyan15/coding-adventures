## You'll want to know: la experiencia
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-03]; assesses=[] -->

**la experiencia** — experience.

**experiencia previa** is the set phrase every job advert uses: *se valora
experiencia previa*, *imprescindible experiencia previa*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-03, ES-LEX-C435-PAPEL-02] -->

You did not need this lesson to read the word, and that is worth noticing
rather than passing over.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| la experiencia | experience |
| la diferencia | difference |
| la paciencia | patience |

**-encia** and English **-ence** are the same ending, inherited by both from
Latin. Hundreds of words pair this way, and the pairing almost never misleads.

So the real content of this lesson is a **licence**: when a long Spanish noun
ends in **-ción**, **-encia**, **-idad** or **-mente**, try the English twin
first. You will be right far more often than wrong, and the cost of being wrong
is a moment.

That is not true of short words, where the false friends live — *embarazada*
does not mean embarrassed, and *éxito* is not exit. The long formal endings are
the safe ones.
