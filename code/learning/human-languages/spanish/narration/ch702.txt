Spanish, chapter 702: Mojarse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mojarse — to get wet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the drought, then the Spanish for to sweat.

You'll want to know: mojarse.
mojarse — "to get wet".
Me mojé porque no llevaba paraguas. I got wet because I had no umbrella.
Without se, mojar is to wet something.

The word, taken apart.
mojarse is built on mojar "to wet", which is usually traced to Vulgar Latin molliāre "to soften", from mollis "soft": wet things go soft.

Lesson 2 of 5.
empapado — soaked, drenched.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to sweat, then the Spanish for to get wet.

You'll want to know: empapado.
empapado — "soaked, drenched".
Llegamos al hotel empapados. We got to the hotel soaked.
Empapado hasta los huesos — soaked to the skin.

The word, taken apart.
empapado is the past form of empapar "to soak", which is built on papa "mush, pap", from Latin pappa "food", a word from baby talk (EtymDB).

barro comes from Vulgar Latin barrum "mud, clay" (EtymDB); the deeper origin is not settled, and it may be pre-Roman.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el impermeable — the raincoat; waterproof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the puddle, then the Spanish for the mud.

You'll want to know: el impermeable.
el impermeable — "the raincoat; waterproof".
Lleva el impermeable, va a llover. Take your raincoat, it is going to rain.
As an adjective: una chaqueta impermeable — a waterproof jacket.

The word, taken apart.
impermeable is a learned borrowing from Late Latin impermeābilis "that cannot be passed through" (EtymDB), built on meāre "to pass".
