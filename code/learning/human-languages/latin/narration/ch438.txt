Lesson 1 of 6.
cōnscendō, cōnscendere — to go on board, to embark; to climb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rudder, then the Latin for to row.

You'll want to know: cōnscendō, cōnscendere.
cōnscendō, cōnscendere — "to go on board, to embark; to climb".
Prīmā lūce mercātōrēs nāvem cōnscendunt. At first light the merchants go on board the ship.
cōnscendō, cōnscendere, cōnscendī, cōnscēnsum. Nāvem cōnscendere, to embark; in nāvem is also found.

The word, taken apart.
cōnscendō is built from con- and scandō 'to climb' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to climb up into a ship.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to row, then the Latin for to go on board.

You'll want to know: vectūra, vectūrae.
vectūra, vectūrae — "transport, carriage (of goods or people); the fare".
Mercātor prō vectūrā mercium nummōs dat. The merchant pays coins for the transport of his goods.
First declension, feminine: vectūra, vectūrae f., from vehō 'to carry'. Also the price paid for it.

The word, taken apart.
vectūra is built on vehō 'to carry' (Lewis & Short), from its participle vectus, with the ending -ūra.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to go on board, then the Latin for transport.

You'll want to know: nauclērus, nauclērī.
nauclērus, nauclērī — "a ship's master, a shipowner".
Nauclērus nautās vocat et nāvem parat. The ship's master calls the sailors and gets the ship ready.
Second declension, masculine: nauclērus, nauclērī m. The man who owns or commands a merchant ship.

The word, taken apart.
nauclērus was borrowed from Greek nauklēros 'shipowner' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from naus 'ship' and klēros 'lot, portion'.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for transport, then the Latin for a ship's master.

You'll want to know: vēlificor, vēlificārī.
vēlificor, vēlificārī — "to set sail, to sail".
Ventus secundus est, et nautae laetī vēlificantur. The wind is favourable, and the sailors set sail happily.
Deponent: vēlificor, vēlificārī, vēlificātus sum. Also an active form vēlificō.

The word, taken apart.
vēlificor is built on vēlum 'sail' and faciō 'to make' (Lewis & Short): to make sail. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 5 of 6.
portitor, portitōris — a ferryman, a boatman; a customs officer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a ship's master, then the Latin for to set sail.

You'll want to know: portitor, portitōris.
portitor, portitōris — "a ferryman, a boatman; a customs officer".
Portitor viātōrēs ad alteram rīpam vehit. The ferryman carries the travellers to the other bank.
Third declension, masculine: portitor, portitōris m. Two words in one spelling: a ferryman, and the officer who takes harbour dues.

The word, taken apart.
Lewis & Short list two words portitor: the customs officer, built on portus 'harbour', and the carrier or ferryman, from the root of portō 'to carry'. EtymDB does not list it.
