Russian, chapter 36: Children, a Child, Aunt, Grandchildren.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
дети (déti) — children.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a husband, then the Russian for a wife.

You'll want to know: дети.
дети (déti) — "children".

The word, taken apart.
дети (déti) is the plural of дитя, "a child", from Proto-Slavic dětę, děti — inherited. Vasmer traces it to the PIE root dʰeh₁(i)-, "to suckle", the root of доить, "to milk": a child is one who is nursed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ребёнок (rebyónok) — a child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a wife, then the Russian for children.

You'll want to know: ребёнок.
ребёнок (rebyónok) — "a child". One child; дети (déti) is the plural.

The word, taken apart.
ребёнок (rebyónok) grew from Old Russian робя, "child", Proto-Slavic orbę, with a diminutive ending; роб- became реб- by an old vowel assimilation (Vasmer). The same root gave раб, "slave", and Vasmer lists Latin orbus, "bereaved", and Greek orphanós, "orphan", as cousins.

Lesson 3 of 5.
тётя (tyótya) — an aunt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for children, then the Russian for a child.

You'll want to know: тётя.
тётя (tyótya) — "an aunt".

The word, taken apart.
тётя (tyótya) is a nursery word, says Vasmer, like тятя, an old word for "dad". Lithuanian tetà, "aunt", and Greek tétta, "daddy", are formed the same way.

Lesson 4 of 5.
внук (vnuk) — a grandson.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a child, then the Russian for an aunt.

You'll want to know: внук.
внук (vnuk) — "a grandson".

The word, taken apart.
внук (vnuk) from Proto-Slavic vŭnukŭ — inherited. Vasmer thinks it may belong with an old nursery word for an elder, seen in Old High German ano, "ancestor", and Latin anus, "old woman"; he finds other proposals less likely.

Lesson 5 of 5.
внучка (vnúchka) — a granddaughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an aunt, then the Russian for a grandson.

You'll want to know: внучка.
внучка (vnúchka) — "a granddaughter".

The word, taken apart.
внучка (vnúchka) is внук (vnuk), "grandson", with -ка, which makes the female word; к turns to ч before it, as in рука, "hand", and ручка, "a little hand; a pen".
