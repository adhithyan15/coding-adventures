Lesson 1 of 6.
chōukòng — to find time (for something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shop online, then the Mandarin for a budget.

You'll want to know: chōukòng.
chōukòng — "to find time (for something)".
yàoshi nǐ néng chōukòng lái kàn wǒ wǒ huì hěn gāoxìng — If you can find time to come and see me, I'll be really pleased.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōukòng is built from chōu 'to pull out' and kòng 'a gap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trhjuw and khuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to draw out' and the second 'a gap'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
约会 (yuēhuì) — a romantic date; an appointment (to meet).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a budget, then the Mandarin for to find time (for something).

You'll want to know: 约会.
约会 — yuēhuì — "a romantic date; an appointment (to meet)".
今天晚上我有一个约会 jīntiān wǎnshang wǒ yǒu yí ge yuēhuì — I've got a date this evening.
The new character, in pieces: 约 (yuē) — six strokes in two pieces: the silk piece on the left of 给, three strokes; on the right a short fall, a bar that turns down into a hook, and a dot inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
约会 (yuēhuì) is built from 约 (yuē) 'to bind' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jak and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tie up' and the second 'to come together': a meeting agreed on beforehand.

Lesson 3 of 6.
约 (yuē) — the silk piece of 给, then a hook around a dot.

Script — the silk piece of 给, then a hook around a dot.
约 (yuē).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the silk piece, three strokes, as on the left of 给: two strokes that each fall to the left and turn to the right, one under the other, then a short stroke rising to the right.
The right piece, three strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. A bar that runs right and turns down into a long hook. Then a dot inside.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 约 (yuē), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 约 (yuē)? (Six.) Where have you made the left piece before? (On the left of 给.) What comes last? (The dot inside the hook.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 约 (yuē), pinned stroke paths and medians.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: shīyuē.
shīyuē — "to miss an appointment".
rúguǒ nǐ shīyuē tā huì hěn shēngqì — If you don't turn up, he'll be angry.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shīyuē is built from shī 'to lose' and 约 (yuē) 'an agreement', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syit and 'jak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to let slip'.

时区 (shíqū) is built from 时 'time' and 区 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyi and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'an hour of the day'. The word is modern.
