# müde — "tired"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Ich bin* is a sentence waiting for one more word. Here is the word
that finishes it most often.

## You'll want to know: müde
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-MUEDE-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01] -->

> **müde** — "tired."

> **Ich bin müde.** — "I am tired."

Two syllables, stress on the first: *mü-duh*. It is an adjective, and after
*sein* an adjective takes **no ending at all** — the same bare form serves
every subject. *Ich bin müde*, *du bist müde*, *wir sind müde*.

That is worth noticing, because German adjectives are famous for their endings.
They only take them in front of a noun. Standing after *sein*, they stand still.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-FRONTING-01, GE-LEX-MUEDE-01] -->

- `umlaut-ue` — **ü** is the vowel you make by rounding your lips for *oo* and
  then saying *ee* without moving them. The dots are the fronting mark you
  already know: the mouth shifts forward, the lips stay round.

The final **-e** is a soft *uh*, never silent. *mü-duh*, two beats, the weight
on the first.
