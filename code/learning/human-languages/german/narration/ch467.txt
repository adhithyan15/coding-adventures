German, chapter 467: Scham und Verlegenheit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Scham — shame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to envy, then the German for to not begrudge.

You'll want to know: die Scham.
die Scham — "shame".
Vor Scham wurde sie ganz rot. She went bright red with shame.
No plural. Vor Scham — with shame.

The word, taken apart.
Scham is inherited from Old High German scama, from Proto-Germanic skamō "shame" (EtymDB); English shame is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
schämen — to be ashamed (sich schämen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to not begrudge, then the German for shame.

You'll want to know: schämen.
schämen — "to be ashamed (sich schämen)".
Ich schäme mich für meinen Fehler. I'm ashamed of my mistake.
Reflexive: sich schämen für + accusative, or sich schämen wegen.

The word, taken apart.
schämen comes from Middle High German schamen (EtymDB), a verb built on the noun that is today Scham, from Proto-Germanic skamō "shame".

Lesson 3 of 5.
verlegen — embarrassed, sheepish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for shame, then the German for to be ashamed.

You'll want to know: verlegen.
verlegen — "embarrassed, sheepish".
Der Junge lächelte verlegen und sagte nichts. The boy smiled sheepishly and said nothing.
There is also a verb verlegen — to misplace, or to move to another date: Ich habe meine Brille verlegt.

The word, taken apart.
The adjective verlegen is usually traced to Middle High German verlegen, the past participle of verligen "to lie too long, to grow sluggish", built on liegen "to lie"; EtymDB records only the verb verlegen "to misplace".

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Verlegenheit — embarrassment; awkward situation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to be ashamed, then the German for embarrassed.

You'll want to know: die Verlegenheit.
die Verlegenheit — "embarrassment; awkward situation".
In seiner Verlegenheit sah er auf den Boden. In his embarrassment, he looked at the floor.
Jemanden in Verlegenheit bringen — to embarrass someone. Plural, for awkward situations: die Verlegenheiten.

The word, taken apart.
Verlegenheit is built on the adjective verlegen "embarrassed" with -heit; verlegen is usually traced to a Middle High German participle meaning "having lain too long".

Lesson 5 of 5.
erröten — to blush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for embarrassed, then the German for embarrassment.

You'll want to know: erröten.
erröten — "to blush".
Bei dem Kompliment errötete sie. She blushed at the compliment.
Perfekt with sein: Sie ist errötet. A little literary; in speech, rot werden.

The word, taken apart.
erröten is built on er- and röten "to make red", from rot "red", which is inherited from Old High German rōt, from Proto-Germanic raudaz (EtymDB).
