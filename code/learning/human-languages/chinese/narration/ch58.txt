Mandarin Chinese, chapter 58: Things You Carry.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
手机 (shǒujī) — mobile phone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for chair, then the Mandarin for here.

You'll want to know: 手机.
手机 — shǒujī — "mobile phone".
这是我的手机 zhè shì wǒ de shǒujī — This is my phone.
The new character, in pieces: 机 (jī) — the tree piece on the left, the one at the top left of 想, and on the right a two-stroke piece read jǐ, as in the word for how many. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
手机 (shǒujī) is built from 手 'hand' and 机 (jī) 'machine'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese kj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains as the catch that sets off a crossbow. From that catch to any machine is the usual explanation, but the step is not given there.

What you've built.

Script — the tree piece, then jǐ.
机 (jī).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The tree piece, four strokes, narrow on the left, as at the top left of 想: a horizontal, a vertical down through it, a left-falling stroke, and a dot.
The right piece, two strokes, read jǐ, as in the word for how many: a left-falling stroke down the left side, then one stroke that runs across the top, turns down, bends right along the bottom and hooks up.
4 + 2 = six.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 机 (jī), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 机 (jī)? (Two — the tree piece, then jǐ.) Which character you know has the same tree piece? (想.) How does the last stroke end? (It runs along the bottom and hooks up.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
bāo — bag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for here, then the Mandarin for mobile phone.

You'll want to know: bāo.
bāo — "bag".
wǒ xiǎng mǎi yí ge bāo — I'd like to buy a bag.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāo is inherited from Middle Chinese paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to wrap'. The sense 'bag' is not given there; a thing that wraps is the likely step, but it is not settled.

The word, taken apart.
yīfu is built from yī and fu, both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'j+j and bjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the garment worn on the upper body, and lists 'clothes' among the senses of the second.
