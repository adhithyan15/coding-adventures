# longior quam — bigger than, faster than

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C93-MAGNUS-03, LA-LEX-C94-NOVUS-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "long", then "fast". (*Longus. Celer.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience is often a comparison: the road was longer than
you thought, the ship faster than the cart.

## Grammar Lens: -ior and quam
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C288-COMPARATIVUS-01, LA-LEX-C288-QUAM-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C286-NAVIS-01, LA-LEX-C244-RES244-01, LA-LEX-C285-ITER-01] -->

Take the stem of the adjective and add **-ior**, "more": *long-us*,
**longior**, longer. **quam** is "than":

> **Nāvis celerior est quam raeda.** — A ship is faster than a carriage.

[PAUSE 2s] For a neuter word the ending is **-ius**:

> **Mare altius est quam flūmen.** — The sea is deeper than the river.
> **Iter longius erat quam putāvī.** — The journey was longer than I thought.

[PAUSE 1s] *Mare* and *iter* are neuter, so *altius*, *longius*. *Nāvis* is feminine, and *-ior*
serves a man or a woman alike: *celerior*.

[PAUSE 1s] *Facilis* gives *facilior*, easier. An adjective with a vowel
before *-us*, like *idōneus*, takes **magis**, "more", in front instead:
*magis idōneus quam*, more suitable than.

## You'll want to know: maior, minor, melior
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C288-MAIOR-MELIOR-03]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C93-MAGNUS-01, LA-LEX-C93-MAGNUS-02] -->

Three common adjectives have a comparative of their own, learned whole:

> *magnus* — **maior**, bigger. *parvus* — **minor**, smaller.
> *bonus*, good — **melior**, better.

> **Rōma maior est quam oppidum meum.** — Rome is bigger than my town.

## The word, taken apart: maior and melior
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C93-MAGNUS-05] -->

The **-ior** ending is inherited: EtymDB 2.1 builds **maior** from the root
of *magnus*, Proto-Indo-European *\*megh2-*, "great", with a comparative
ending *\*-yos*. **melior** is traced to a Proto-Indo-European *\*melyos*,
"better", with no link to *bonus* at all, which is why it has to be learned
whole. English took *major*, *minor* and, through French, *ameliorate*.
