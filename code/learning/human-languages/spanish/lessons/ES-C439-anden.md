# el andén — where you wait for the train

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TREN, ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-15, ES-LEX-C330-WAY-12] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el tren**, **la estación** and **la parada**. You are inside the
station and the train has not come. Name the strip of concrete you are standing on.

## You'll want to know: el andén
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TREN] -->

**el andén** — the platform.

*El tren sale del andén 4* — the train leaves from platform 4. Announcements put
the number after the word, never before, and they say it fast: *andén cuatro*.

The written accent tells you where to put the weight: **an-DÉN**, on the last
syllable. Without it the word would be read *AN-den*, and the stress is the part
a listener uses to recognise it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-01] -->

It does not come apart, and this time that is not a trick. Nobody knows where
**andén** comes from.

The tempting guess is **andar**, to walk, because a platform is a place you walk
along. Dictionaries do not accept it: the endings do not line up, and the word
turns up first in senses that have nothing to do with walking. Other proposals
have been made and none has carried the field.

So the honest entry is *origin uncertain*, and that is worth meeting on purpose.
Any book that produces an etymology for every word is inventing some of them.
Spanish has a few hundred words like this, and the right response is to learn the
word and let the history stay open.
