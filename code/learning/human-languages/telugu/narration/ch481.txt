Telugu, chapter 481: Heat and Drought.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
వడగాలి (vaḍagāli) — a heatwave; a hot wind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to get bogged down, then the Telugu for to get stuck.

You'll want to know: వడగాలి.
వడగాలి — vaḍagāli — "a heatwave; a hot wind".
ఎండాకాలంలో వడగాలి వీస్తుంది. eṇḍākālaṁlō vaḍagāli vīstundi. In the hot season a scorching wind blows.
వడ, heat, + గాలి, wind. వడదెబ్బ, sunstroke, is the blow of the same heat.

The word, taken apart.
వడగాలి (vaḍagāli) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5225 lists Telugu vaḍa, "heat", and vaḍagāli, "the hot or land wind".

Lesson 2 of 5.
ఎండమావి (eṇḍamāvi) — a mirage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to get stuck, then the Telugu for a heatwave.

You'll want to know: ఎండమావి.
ఎండమావి — eṇḍamāvi — "a mirage".
ఎడారిలో దూరంగా ఎండమావి కనిపించింది. eḍārilō dūraṅgā eṇḍamāvi kanipiñcindi. A mirage appeared far off in the desert.
ఎండ, sunshine, + మావి. Something hoped for that is never reached is also an ఎండమావి (eṇḍamāvi).

The word, taken apart.
ఎండమావి (eṇḍamāvi) is a Telugu compound: ఎండ, "sunshine, the heat of the sun", native Dravidian (DEDR 869), and -మావి, whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఎండిపోవు (eṇḍipōvu) — to dry up; to wither.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a mirage, then the Telugu for a dust storm.

You'll want to know: ఎండిపోవు.
ఎండిపోవు — eṇḍipōvu — "to dry up; to wither".
కరువులో చెరువులన్నీ ఎండిపోయాయి. karuvulō ceruvulannī eṇḍipōyāyi. In the drought all the ponds dried up.
Past (it …): ఎండిపోయింది, eṇḍipōyindi.
ఎండు, to dry in the sun, + పోవు. Said of ponds, plants and throats. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: ఎండిపోయింది, it dried up.

The word, taken apart.
ఎండిపోవు (eṇḍipōvu) is native Dravidian: ఎండు, "to be heated by the sun, to dry up" (DEDR 869), with పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 5 of 5.
బీటలు వారు (bīṭalu vāru) — to crack open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a dust storm, then the Telugu for to dry up.

You'll want to know: బీటలు వారు.
బీటలు వారు — bīṭalu vāru — "to crack open".
ఎండలకు పొలాలు బీటలు వారాయి. eṇḍalaku polālu bīṭalu vārāyi. In the heat the fields cracked open.
Past (it …): బీటలు వారింది, bīṭalu vārindi.
బీటలు, cracks, + వారు. Said of dry earth and of old walls. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: బీటలు వారింది, it cracked open.

The word, taken apart.
బీటలు వారు (bīṭalu vāru) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5473 lists Telugu bīṭa, "a crack, a cleft", and the phrase bīṭaluvāru, "to crack", with Tamil veṭi, "to crack, to split".
