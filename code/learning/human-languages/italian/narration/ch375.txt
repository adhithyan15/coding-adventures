Italian, chapter 375: Cambio di programma.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
annullare — to cancel, to call off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the alternative, then the Italian for to adapt.

You'll want to know: annullare.
annullare — "to cancel, to call off".
Se piove, annulliamo la gita. If it rains, we will call off the trip.
Il volo è stato annullato — the flight has been cancelled.

The word, taken apart.
annullare comes from Late Latin annullāre "to bring to nothing" (EtymDB), built from ad and nūllus "none".

Lesson 2 of 5.
rinviare — to postpone, to put off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to adapt, then the Italian for to cancel.

You'll want to know: rinviare.
rinviare — "to postpone, to put off".
La riunione è stata rinviata a giovedì. The meeting has been put off until Thursday.
It also means to send back: rinviare un pacco.

The word, taken apart.
rinviare is built from ri- "again, back" and inviare "to send", usually traced to Late Latin inviāre "to put on the way", built on via "road" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
provvisorio — provisional, temporary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to cancel, then the Italian for to postpone.

You'll want to know: provvisorio.
provvisorio — "provisional, temporary".
Questo è solo un orario provvisorio. This is only a provisional timetable.
In via provvisoria — for the time being.

The word, taken apart.
provvisorio probably came from French provisoire, from Medieval Latin prōvīsōrius, built on prōvidēre "to foresee, to provide for" (Lewis & Short), on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
definitivo — final, definitive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to postpone, then the Italian for provisional.

You'll want to know: definitivo.
definitivo — "final, definitive".
Questa è la data definitiva: non cambia più. This is the final date: it will not change again.
In definitiva — all in all.

The word, taken apart.
definitivo is a learned borrowing from Latin dēfīnītīvus "that defines", from dēfīnīre "to set bounds" (Lewis & Short), built on fīnis "border, end".

Lesson 5 of 5.
stabilire — to fix, to set, to establish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for provisional, then the Italian for final.

You'll want to know: stabilire.
stabilire — "to fix, to set, to establish".
Dobbiamo ancora stabilire il giorno della partenza. We still have to fix the day of departure.
Conjugated with -isc-: stabilisco. Stabilirsi — to settle somewhere.

The word, taken apart.
stabilire is a learned borrowing from Latin stabilīre "to make firm" (Lewis & Short), from stabilis "steady", built on stāre "to stand".
