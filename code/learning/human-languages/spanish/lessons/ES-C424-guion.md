# el guion — the short one, and why it is not la raya

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la raya*. This lesson gives you a mark that looks like a small
version of it and does something completely different.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C424-07]; assesses=[] -->

**el guion** — "the hyphen."

The two marks are easy to confuse on a page and easy to keep apart once you
know what each is for:

| mark | length | job |
|---|---|---|
| la raya | long | opens a turn of speech |
| el guion | short | **joins** two words into one |

Its everyday A1 use is in names:

- **Ana-María** — a compound forename
- **Sánchez-Pérez** — a compound surname, often a married name or a double
  family name

A form asking for *apellidos* may come back with a hyphen in it, and the hyphen
is telling you the two halves are one name rather than two.

## Grammar Lens: the word changed its own spelling
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-07, ES-ORTH-C424-06] -->

This one is a small piece of history you will see both ways in print:

| spelling | status |
|---|---|
| guión | the older form, with a written accent |
| **guion** | the current recommendation |

The Academy reclassified *guion* as a one-syllable word in 2010, and a
one-syllable word carries no written accent. Older books print *guión* and are
not wrong for their date. Both will appear in front of you.
