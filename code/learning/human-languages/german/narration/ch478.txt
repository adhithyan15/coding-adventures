German, chapter 478: Reue und Verzeihung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Reue — remorse, regret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for reckless, then the German for to dare.

You'll want to know: die Reue.
die Reue — "remorse, regret".
Der Täter zeigte keine Reue. The culprit showed no remorse.
No plural.

The word, taken apart.
Reue is inherited from Old High German riuwa, from Proto-Germanic hrewwō "pain, sadness, regret" (EtymDB); English rue is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bereuen — to regret.

You'll want to know: bereuen.
bereuen — "to regret".
Sie bereut, dass sie so wenig gelernt hat. She regrets studying so little.
Inseparable: hat bereut.

The word, taken apart.
bereuen is built on be- and the old verb reuen "to cause sorrow", from the same source as Reue, Proto-Germanic hrewwō "pain, regret" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
verzeihen — to forgive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for remorse, then the German for to regret.

You'll want to know: verzeihen.
verzeihen — "to forgive".
Sie hat ihm den Fehler sofort verziehen. She forgave him the mistake at once.
Irregular: verzieh, hat verziehen. Verzeihung! — Excuse me!

The word, taken apart.
verzeihen comes from Middle High German verzīhen (EtymDB), built on zīhen "to accuse", from Proto-Germanic tīhaną (EtymDB); it is usually explained as first meaning "to give up a claim".

Lesson 4 of 5.
beleidigen — to insult, to offend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to regret, then the German for to forgive.

You'll want to know: beleidigen.
beleidigen — "to insult, to offend".
Mit seinem Witz hat er die Gastgeberin beleidigt. He offended the hostess with his joke.
Beleidigt sein — to be offended, to sulk. The noun: die Beleidigung.

The word, taken apart.
beleidigen is built on be- and Leid "harm, sorrow", which is inherited from Old High German leid, from Proto-Germanic laiþą (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
kränken — to hurt (someone's feelings).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to forgive, then the German for to insult.

You'll want to know: kränken.
kränken — "to hurt (someone's feelings)".
Seine harten Worte haben sie tief gekränkt. His harsh words hurt her deeply.
Of feelings only; a hurt body is verletzt.

The word, taken apart.
kränken is built on krank "sick", which is inherited from Old High German krank, from Proto-Germanic krangaz (EtymDB): to make someone feel ill.
