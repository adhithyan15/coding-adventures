Portuguese, chapter 391: As contas da casa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o rendimento — the income; the yield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for guilty, then the Portuguese for innocent.

You'll want to know: o rendimento.
o rendimento — "the income; the yield".
O rendimento da família subiu este ano. The family's income went up this year.
Rendimento mensal — monthly income.

The word, taken apart.
rendimento is built on render "to yield, to give back", usually traced to Vulgar Latin rendere, a form of Latin reddere "to give back" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: o orçamento.
o orçamento — "the budget; the quote, the estimate".
O nosso orçamento para as férias é pequeno. Our budget for the holidays is small.
Also a builder's quote: pedir um orçamento.

The word, taken apart.
orçamento is built on orçar "to estimate" (EtymDB), which EtymDB traces to Italian orzare, from Vulgar Latin ortiāre "to raise", on orīrī "to rise".

Lesson 3 of 5.
a despesa — the expense, the spending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the income, then the Portuguese for the budget.

You'll want to know: a despesa.
a despesa — "the expense, the spending".
As despesas da casa são altas. The household expenses are high.
Dividir as despesas — to share the costs.

The word, taken apart.
despesa goes back to Old Portuguese despesa (EtymDB), usually traced to Late Latin dispēnsa "things paid out", on dispendere "to weigh out", built on pendere "to weigh" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a prestação — the instalment; the performance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the budget, then the Portuguese for the expense.

You'll want to know: a prestação.
a prestação — "the instalment; the performance".
Pago o carro em prestações mensais. I pay for the car in monthly instalments.
Às prestações — in instalments. Also a performance at work or in sport.

The word, taken apart.
prestação is a learned borrowing from Latin praestātiō "a payment, a guarantee" (Lewis & Short), on praestāre "to stand before, to provide", built on stāre "to stand".

Lesson 5 of 5.
a poupança — the saving; savings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the expense, then the Portuguese for the instalment.

You'll want to know: a poupança.
a poupança — "the saving; savings".
Usei as minhas poupanças para comprar um carro. I used my savings to buy a car.
Conta poupança — savings account.

The word, taken apart.
poupança is built on poupar "to save", which is inherited from Latin palpāre "to touch softly, to stroke" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): to handle money gently.
