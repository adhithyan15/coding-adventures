Spanish, chapter 223: Aquel --- The Far One, and an Old Friend.
1 lesson. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
aquel — the far one, and an old friend in disguise.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Mine, here: este libro. Yours, there: ese libro.
[pause 1 second]
Now the one across the room, belonging to neither of us.

The word, taken apart: aquel.
aquel libro — that book over there.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
aquel libro, that book, over there.
aquella casa, that house, over there.
aquellos libros, those books, over there.
aquellas casas, those houses, over there.
[pause 2 seconds]
Look at the middle of that word. -el-. You have met it before, in your first chapter.
[pause 2 seconds]
Latin ille went two ways at once. Said quickly and without stress, leaning on the noun after it, it wore down to el — the article. Said with stress, and propped up in front with accu- (roughly look, there), it stayed a pointing word: accu + ille becomes aquel.
So el and aquel are the same Latin word, and the difference between them is two thousand years of one being emphasised and the other not.

Why it's said this way.
That leaves Spanish with three, where English has two:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
este, by me.
ese, by you.
aquel, by neither of us.
[pause 2 seconds]
English collapses the last two into that and then patches the difference back on when it matters — that one over there. Spanish keeps the three apart in the word itself, so it never needs the patch.
[pause 2 seconds]
And the middle one is the one to watch. If you find yourself reaching for aquel because the thing is a few steps away, stop and ask the real question: is it near the person you are talking to? If it is, it is ese, however far it is from you.

[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which word from chapter one is hiding inside aquel?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three words, three places. Which one is by the person you are talking to? (Ese.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And what did el and aquel both start out as? (Latin ille — one worn down, one propped up.)
