Hindi, chapter 306: Famine and Sickness.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अकाल (akāl) — a famine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a deluge, then the Hindi for a whirlpool.

You'll want to know: अकाल.
अकाल — akāl — "a famine".
अकाल की वजह यह थी कि उस साल बारिश नहीं हुई। akāl kī vajah yah thī ki us sāl bāriś nahīṃ huī. The reason for the famine was that the rains failed that year.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
अकाल (akāl) is Sanskrit akāla, "a wrong or bad time" (Monier-Williams). In Hindi it means a famine; the dictionary gives the older sense, and the step to a time of hunger is the language's own. A famine पड़ता है: it falls.

Lesson 2 of 5.
भुखमरी (bhukhmarī) — starvation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a whirlpool, then the Hindi for a famine.

You'll want to know: भुखमरी.
भुखमरी — bhukhmarī — "starvation".
अकाल में बहुत लोग भुखमरी से मरे। akāl meṁ bahut log bhukhmarī se mare. Many people died of starvation in the famine.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
भुखमरी (bhukhmarī) joins भूख, hunger, from Sanskrit bubhukṣā (CDIAL 9286), with मरना, to die: dying of hunger. The vowel of भूख shortens inside the word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
महामारी (mahāmārī) — an epidemic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for starvation, then the Hindi for to rot.

You'll want to know: महामारी.
महामारी — mahāmārī — "an epidemic".
उस साल शहर में महामारी फैली। us sāl śahar meṁ mahāmārī phailī. That year an epidemic spread through the city.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
महामारी (mahāmārī) is Sanskrit mahāmārī (Monier-Williams), mahā, great, and mārī, pestilence, a word of the family of mṛ, to die. It is a disease that spreads to a whole people.

Lesson 5 of 5.
हैज़ा (haizā) — cholera.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to rot, then the Hindi for an epidemic.

You'll want to know: हैज़ा.
हैज़ा — haizā — "cholera".
बाढ़ के बाद गाँव में हैज़ा फैल गया। bāṛh ke bād gā̃v meṁ haizā phail gayā. After the flood cholera broke out in the village.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
हैज़ा (haizā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic hayḍa, "cholera". Bad water after a flood was its usual cause.
