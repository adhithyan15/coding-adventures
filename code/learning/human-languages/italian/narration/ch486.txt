Italian, chapter 486: Starnuti e prurito.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
starnutire — to sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for gauze, then the Italian for to bandage.

You'll want to know: starnutire.
starnutire — "to sneeze".
In primavera starnutisco tutto il giorno. In spring I sneeze all day.
An -isc- verb. Lo starnuto — a sneeze; people say salute!

The word, taken apart.
starnutire is inherited from Latin sternūtāre "to sneeze often" (Lewis & Short), from sternuere "to sneeze".

You'll want to know: grattarsi.
grattarsi — "to scratch (oneself)".
Non grattarti, peggiora! Do not scratch, it makes it worse!
Without si: grattare — to scratch, to grate.

The word, taken apart.
grattarsi is built on grattare "to scratch", usually traced to a Germanic verb krattōn "to scratch".

You'll want to know: l'allergia.
l'allergia — "allergy".
Ho un'allergia ai gatti. I have an allergy to cats.
Essere allergico a — to be allergic to.

The word, taken apart.
allergia came from German Allergie (EtymDB, under English allergy), a modern coinage from Greek állos "other" and érgon "work, action".

Lesson 5 of 6.
la puntura — sting, bite (of an insect); injection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to scratch, then the Italian for allergy.

You'll want to know: la puntura.
la puntura — "sting, bite (of an insect); injection".
Ho una puntura di zanzara sul braccio. I have a mosquito bite on my arm.
In speech it can also be an injection: fare una puntura.

The word, taken apart.
puntura is inherited from Latin punctūra "a pricking" (EtymDB), from pungere "to prick" (Lewis & Short).
