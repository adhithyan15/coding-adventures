Portuguese, chapter 530: Senhorios e inquilinos.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
o senhorio — landlord.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for clothes peg, then the Portuguese for to iron.

You'll want to know: o senhorio.
o senhorio — "landlord".
O senhorio aumentou a renda. The landlord put up the rent.
A senhoria — landlady.

The word, taken apart.
senhorio is inherited from Old Portuguese sennorio "lordship" (EtymDB), built on senhor, from Latin senior "older".

Lesson 4 of 6.
a despensa — pantry, larder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for tenant, then the Portuguese for to furnish.

You'll want to know: a despensa.
a despensa — "pantry, larder".
Guardamos o arroz e as latas na despensa. We keep the rice and the tins in the pantry.
Not to be mixed up with a dispensa, an exemption.

The word, taken apart.
despensa is usually traced to Medieval Latin dispēnsa "a store of provisions", from dispendere "to weigh out", built on pendere "to weigh" (Lewis & Short).
