# لو (law) — if it were so

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C292-DEPENDS-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C292-HASABA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "it depends on the weather", then "according to the
timetable". (*Yaʿtamidu ʿalā aṭ-ṭaqs*, *ḥasaba al-jadwal*.)

[PAUSE 1s] *Idhā* is for what may well happen. For what is not so, and you
only imagine, Arabic has a third "if".

## Grammar Lens: law, and la- on the result
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C292-LAW-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C283-MADI283-01, AR-LEX-C88-TIME88-02, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-04, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01] -->

> **لو كان عندي وقت لسافرت.** — *law kāna ʿindī waqt la-sāfartu* — If I had
> time, I would travel.

**لو** — *law* — takes the past, and so does the result. The result usually
carries **لـ** — *la-* — written onto it: *la-sāfartu*, I would have
travelled, I would travel. The meaning is "but I don't have time". That is
the difference from *idhā*: *idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, sa-usāfiru* leaves it
open; *law* says it is not so.

You have said *law* since chapter six: *law samaḥt*, "if you would permit",
the polite "please" that does not take the yes for granted.

## Grammar Lens: if I were you, and if only
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C292-LAYTA-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C104-LOC104-04, AR-LEX-C54-HEALTH54-04, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-01, AR-LEX-C84-ADJ84-02] -->

> **لو كنت مكانك لذهبت إلى الطبيب.** — *law kuntu makānaka la-dhahabtu ilā
> aṭ-ṭabīb* — If I were you, I'd go to the doctor.

Word for word, "if I were in your place". **ليت** — *layta*, "if only" —
wishes without a result: *laytanī kuntu ghaniyyan*, if only I were rich;
*laytanī kuntu hunāka*, if only I had been there.
