Latin, chapter 361: Proof and Example.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
argūmentum, argūmentī — an argument, a proof; a plot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for foolishness, then the Latin for steadiness.

You'll want to know: argūmentum, argūmentī.
argūmentum, argūmentī — "an argument, a proof; a plot".
Hoc argūmentum mihi nōn persuādet. This argument does not persuade me.
argūmentum, argūmentī n. It is also the plot of a play. Built on arguō, to make clear. English argument.

The word, taken apart.
argūmentum is built on arguō "to make clear, to prove" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -mentum that names a means. English argument comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
exemplum, exemplī — an example; a warning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for steadiness, then the Latin for an argument.

You'll want to know: exemplum, exemplī.
exemplum, exemplī — "an example; a warning".
Pater exemplum bonum fīliō dedit. The father set his son a good example.
exemplum, exemplī n. Exemplī grātiā, for the sake of an example, gives English e.g.; exemplī causā means the same. Exemplum facere is to make an example of someone.

The word, taken apart.
exemplum comes from eximō "to take out" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from ex and emō, whose old sense was "to take": a piece taken out to show the rest. English example and sample come from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
documentum, documentī — a lesson, a proof; a warning example.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an argument, then the Latin for an example.

You'll want to know: documentum, documentī.
documentum, documentī — "a lesson, a proof; a warning example".
Eius poena documentum omnibus fuit. His punishment was a lesson to everyone.
documentum, documentī n. Built on the verb for teaching: something that teaches. In Latin it is a lesson or a proof, not a paper. English document.

The word, taken apart.
documentum is built on doceō "to teach" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -mentum: a thing that teaches. English document comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dēmōnstrō, dēmōnstrāre — to show, to point out; to prove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an example, then the Latin for a lesson.

You'll want to know: dēmōnstrō, dēmōnstrāre.
dēmōnstrō, dēmōnstrāre — "to show, to point out; to prove".
Testis dēmōnstrāvit reum tum domī fuisse. The witness showed that the accused was at home then.
dēmōnstrō, dēmōnstrāre, dēmōnstrāvī, dēmōnstrātum. With the accusative and infinitive it proves a claim. English demonstrate.

The word, taken apart.
dēmōnstrō is built from dē and mōnstrō "to show" (Lewis & Short), which belongs with moneō "to warn, to remind". English demonstrate comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cōnfirmō, cōnfirmāre — to strengthen; to confirm, to assert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lesson, then the Latin for to show.

You'll want to know: cōnfirmō, cōnfirmāre.
cōnfirmō, cōnfirmāre — "to strengthen; to confirm, to assert".
Litterae tuae opīniōnem meam cōnfirmant. Your letter confirms my opinion.
cōnfirmō, cōnfirmāre, cōnfirmāvī, cōnfirmātum. Built on firmus, firm: to make firm, then to state firmly. Animum cōnfirmāre, to give someone courage. English confirm.

The word, taken apart.
cōnfirmō is built from con- and a verb made on firmus "firm, strong" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to make thoroughly firm. English confirm comes from it.
