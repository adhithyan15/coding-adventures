French, chapter 345: Pour ou contre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
approuver — to approve (of).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the subtle difference, then the French for to qualify.

You'll want to know: approuver.
approuver — "to approve (of)".
Mes parents n'approuvent pas mon choix. My parents do not approve of my choice.
Approuver une décision — to approve of a decision.

The word, taken apart.
approuver comes from Latin approbāre "to approve, to assent to" (EtymDB), built from ad and probāre "to test, to find good" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: l'adversaire.
l'adversaire — "the opponent".
Son adversaire a gagné le débat. His opponent won the debate.
Masculine or feminine: un or une adversaire.

The word, taken apart.
adversaire comes from Latin adversārius "an opponent" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from adversus "turned toward, facing", built on vertere "to turn".

Lesson 5 of 5.
argumenter — to argue a case, to give reasons.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the supporter, then the French for the opponent.

You'll want to know: argumenter.
argumenter — "to argue a case, to give reasons".
Elle argumente avec calme. She argues her case calmly.
Not quarrelling: argumenter is to back a view with reasons; to quarrel is se disputer.

The word, taken apart.
argumenter is a learned borrowing from Latin argūmentārī "to bring forward proof" (Lewis & Short), from argūmentum "proof", built on arguere "to make clear".
