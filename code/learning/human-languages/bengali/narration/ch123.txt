Bengali, chapter 123: Cat, Elbow, Chest, Waist, Ankle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
বেড়াল (beṛāl) — a cat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a gecko, then the Bengali for a turtle.

You'll want to know: বেড়াল.
বেড়াল — beṛāl — "a cat".

The word, taken apart.
বেড়াল (beṛāl) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from biḍāla, "cat" (CDIAL 9237). CDIAL writes the Bengali forms birāl and bilāl.

Lesson 3 of 5.
বুক (buk) — the chest.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a cat, then the Bengali for an elbow.

You'll want to know: বুক.
বুক — buk — "the chest".

The word, taken apart.
বুক (buk) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bukka, "heart" (CDIAL 12064). CDIAL treats bukka as a non-Aryan word that crossed with Sanskrit vṛkka, "kidneys".

গোড়ালি — goṛāli — "an ankle".

The word, taken apart.
গোড়ালি (goṛāli) is built on goṛ, "foot, leg", which CDIAL lists under gōḍḍa, "foot, leg, knee", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 4272); CDIAL does not list গোড়ালি (goṛāli) itself.
