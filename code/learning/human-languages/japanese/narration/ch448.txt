Japanese, chapter 448: Stiff and Numb.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こる (koru) — to get stiff (of muscles).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for worn out, then the Japanese for lack of sleep.

You'll want to know: こる.
こる — koru — "to get stiff (of muscles)".
しごとで かたが こりました。 — shigoto de kata ga korimashita — "My shoulders got stiff from work."
Polite こります, korimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちの かたが こりました。, chichi no kata ga korimashita.

The word, taken apart.
こる (koru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to twist, then the Japanese for to go numb.

You'll want to know: まげる.
まげる — mageru — "to bend".
ひざを ゆっくり まげて ください。 — hiza o yukkuri magete kudasai — "Please bend your knees slowly."
Polite まげます, magemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): かんじゃが ゆびを まげました。, kanja ga yubi o magemashita.

The word, taken apart.
まげる (mageru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the transitive partner of magaru, "to turn; to bend", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
