Lesson 1 of 6.
やけど (yakedo) — a burn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go numb, then the Japanese for to bend.

You'll want to know: やけど.
やけど — yakedo — "a burn".
なべで てに やけどを しました。 — nabe de te ni yakedo o shimashita — "I burned my hand on the pot."

The word, taken apart.
やけど (yakedo) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana; its usual kanji spelling writes the meaning, "fire" and "wound", not the sound (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to bend, then the Japanese for a burn.

You'll want to know: ねんざ.
ねんざ — nenza — "a sprain".
しあいで あしくびを ねんざしました。 — shiai de ashikubi o nenza shimashita — "I sprained my ankle in the match."

The word, taken apart.
ねんざ (nenza) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nen, "twist" (Middle Chinese nep) and za, "sprain" (tswaH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
