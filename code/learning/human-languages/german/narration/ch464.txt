German, chapter 464: Die Hitzewelle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Hitzewelle — the heatwave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the thaw, then the German for to shiver.

You'll want to know: die Hitzewelle.
die Hitzewelle — "the heatwave".
Während der Hitzewelle sind die Schwimmbäder voll. During the heatwave the swimming pools are full.
Plural: die Hitzewellen.

The word, taken apart.
Hitzewelle is built from Hitze 'heat', an inherited word related to heiß 'hot', and Welle 'wave', also an inherited Germanic word (EtymDB lists Welle).

Dürre is built on dürr 'dry, withered', an inherited Germanic adjective related to Durst 'thirst'. EtymDB does not list the noun.

Lesson 4 of 5.
schwül — muggy, sultry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the drought, then the German for the humidity.

You'll want to know: schwül.
schwül — "muggy, sultry".
Vor dem Gewitter ist es oft schwül. Before a thunderstorm it is often muggy.
Die Schwüle — the muggy heat.

The word, taken apart.
schwül came into the standard language from Low German; it is usually linked to schwelen 'to smoulder'. EtymDB lists it with the sense 'sultry' but gives no source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
austrocknen — to dry out, to dry up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the humidity, then the German for muggy.

You'll want to know: austrocknen.
austrocknen — "to dry out, to dry up".
Im August trocknet der kleine Bach fast ganz aus. In August the little stream almost dries up completely.
Separable; Perfekt with sein when it means drying up: der Bach ist ausgetrocknet.

The word, taken apart.
austrocknen is built from aus 'out' and trocknen 'to dry', from trocken 'dry', inherited from Middle High German trucken (EtymDB).
