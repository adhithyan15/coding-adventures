Russian, chapter 46: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
завтрак (závtrak) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cucumber, then the Russian for a carrot.

You'll want to know: завтрак.
завтрак (závtrak) — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
завтрак (závtrak) — built on the root of завтра, "tomorrow", which is за, "after", with утро, "morning". Old Russian said zautrokŭ, "the morning meal" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
обед (obéd) — lunch, the midday meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a carrot, then the Russian for breakfast.

You'll want to know: обед.
обед (obéd) — "lunch, the midday meal".

The word, taken apart.
обед (obéd) from Proto-Slavic obědŭ — об-, "around", with the root of еда, "food", and есть, "to eat" (Vasmer). That root is PIE h₁ed-, "to eat", so English eat is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ужин (úzhin) — dinner, supper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for breakfast, then the Russian for lunch.

You'll want to know: ужин.
ужин (úzhin) — "dinner, supper".

The word, taken apart.
ужин (úzhin) — built on Proto-Slavic jugŭ, "south, noon", so it first meant "the midday meal" (Vasmer); Old Russian ужина was an afternoon meal. Latin merīdiēs is both "noon" and "south" in the same way.

You'll want to know: каша.

The word, taken apart.
каша (kásha) from Proto-Slavic kaša — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian kóšti, "to strain, to filter"; Trubachev, in a note, explains it otherwise.

Lesson 5 of 5.
пирог (piróg) — a pie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for dinner, then the Russian for porridge.

You'll want to know: пирог.
пирог (piróg) — "a pie".

The word, taken apart.
пирог (piróg) — Vasmer builds it on пир, "a feast", with the suffix -ог; others start from Old Russian пыро, "spelt", reshaped to match пир. Either way it is a Slavic word.
