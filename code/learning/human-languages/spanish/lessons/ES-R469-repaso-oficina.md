# (imprimir, la impresora, conectar, la llamada) — from cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Four words, no prompts. What the machine does, what the machine is,
what you do to the machine first, and what you make when it will not work.

## You'll want to know: the four, cold
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-04] -->

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| to print | **imprimir** |
| the printer | **la impresora** |
| to connect | **conectar** |
| the call | **la llamada** |

## Grammar Lens: two of these you could have guessed
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-04] -->

Two of the four were reachable before you were told, and for the same reason:
you already held the verb and the ending.

| built from | gives you |
|---|---|
| *imprimir* + **-dora** | la impresora |
| *llamar* + **-ada** | la llamada |

*La lavadora* taught the first ending and *el pedido* the second, and *el
contador* already warned you that the ending settles nothing about the
article. What this chapter added is a case where the article is not only
unpredictable but **meaningful**: *la impresora* is the machine, *el impresor*
is the person, and the one letter is the whole difference.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-03] -->

This chapter put three different routes side by side, and the words look alike
on the page whichever route they took.

| word | how it got here |
|---|---|
| imprimir | inherited — Latin *imprimere*, all the way down |
| conectar | borrowed from English, its parts Latin |
| la llamada | inherited — Latin *clamare*, through *llamar* |

*Conectar* is the odd one. It is the only word here whose Latin arrived by way
of English, which is why it looks as native as the other three and is not.
