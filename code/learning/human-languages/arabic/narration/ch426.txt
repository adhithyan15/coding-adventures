Arabic, chapter 426: Through customs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جمارك (jamārik) — customs (at a border).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to tighten, then the Arabic for arrival.

You'll want to know: جمارك.
جمارك — jamārik — "customs (at a border)".
وقفنا في الجمارك مدة طويلة. waqafnā fī al-jamārik mudda ṭawīla. We stood in customs for a long time.
The singular جمرك (jumruk) names one customs post; the plural names the service.

The word, taken apart.
جمارك (jamārik) is usually traced to Turkish gümrük, which goes back to Greek kommerkion, a duty on trade.

Lesson 2 of 5.
فتش (fattasha) — to search; to inspect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for arrival, then the Arabic for customs.

You'll want to know: فتش.
فتش — fattasha — "to search; to inspect".
فتش الموظف حقيبتي في الجمارك. fattasha al-muwaẓẓaf ḥaqībatī fī al-jamārik. The officer searched my bag at customs.
Present: يفتش, yufattish.

The word, taken apart.
فتش (fattasha) is built on the native Arabic root f-t-sh, "to search" (Lane). It is Form II, "to search thoroughly".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ختم (khatama) — to stamp; to seal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for customs, then the Arabic for to search.

You'll want to know: ختم.
ختم — khatama — "to stamp; to seal".
ختم الموظف جوازي عند الوصول. khatama al-muwaẓẓaf jawāzī ʿinda al-wuṣūl. The officer stamped my passport on arrival.
Present: يختم, yakhtim.
The noun khatm is a stamp; khatama also means to finish.

The word, taken apart.
ختم (khatama) is built on the native Arabic root kh-t-m, "to seal" (Lane). It is the root of khātam, "a ring", once a seal ring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to search, then the Arabic for to stamp.

You'll want to know: أفرغ.
أفرغ — afragha — "to empty out; to unpack".
أفرغ المسافر حقيبته أمام الموظف. afragha al-musāfir ḥaqībatahu amāma al-muwaẓẓaf. The traveller emptied out his bag in front of the officer.
Present: يفرغ, yufrigh.

The word, taken apart.
أفرغ (afragha) is built on the native Arabic root f-r-gh, "to be empty, to be free" (Lane). It is Form IV, "to make empty", beside fārigh, "empty".
