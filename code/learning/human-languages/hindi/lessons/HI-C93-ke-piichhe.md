# के पीछे — behind

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C51-WELCOME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *in front of the station*. Then say the word for a table, which
you have had since the chapter on furniture.

## You'll want to know: के पीछे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-05]; assesses=[] -->

**कुर्सी मेज़ के पीछे है।** — The chair is behind the table.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-05, HI-LEX-C93-REL-04] -->

Three place words, and they do not all do the same kind of job:

| slot-three word | what it tells you |
|---|---|
| पास | **how far** — close, no direction |
| सामने | **which side** — facing |
| पीछे | **which side** — away |

**पास** answers a different question from the other two, and mixing them is the
commonest way to get a direction item wrong. A shop can be **near** a station
and still be **behind** it; it cannot be in front of it and behind it at once.

## Grammar Lens: the one glyph worth pausing on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-05, HI-LEX-C51-WELCOME-04] -->

**पीछे** carries **छ**, the breathy partner of **च** that the previous chapter
taught. This is its first outing in an ordinary word rather than a number, and
it is worth saying slowly: the breath belongs to the **छ**, not to the vowel
either side of it.

Get that wrong and you say a different word. The pair **च / छ** does the same
work here that **त / थ** does elsewhere, and both pairs punish the same
inattention.
