Arabic, chapter 478: Herds and pastures.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
قطيع (qaṭīʿ) — a herd; a flock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for pure, then the Arabic for a strait.

You'll want to know: قطيع.
قطيع — qaṭīʿ — "a herd; a flock".
رأينا قطيعا من الغزلان. raʾaynā qaṭīʿan min al-ghizlān. We saw a herd of gazelles.

The word, taken apart.
قطيع (qaṭīʿ) is built on the native Arabic root q-ṭ-ʿ, "to cut" (Lane). A herd is a portion cut off from the rest.

Lesson 2 of 6.
مرعى (marʿā) — a pasture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a strait, then the Arabic for a herd.

You'll want to know: مرعى.
مرعى — marʿā — "a pasture".
ذهب القطيع إلى المرعى. dhahaba al-qaṭīʿ ilā al-marʿā. The herd went to the pasture.

The word, taken apart.
مرعى (marʿā) is built on the native Arabic root r-ʿ-y, "to pasture, to tend" (Lane). The pattern mafʿal makes it the place of grazing.

كان الببغاء في قفص صغير. kāna al-babbaghāʾ fī qafaṣ ṣaghīr. The parrot was in a small cage.
Plural: أقفاص, aqfāṣ.

The word, taken apart.
قفص (qafaṣ) is linked by EtymDB to Aramaic qupsā, a word usually glossed "a box, a basket"; the details of the borrowing are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
رعى (raʿā) — to graze; to tend (animals).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a cage, then the Arabic for fur.

You'll want to know: رعى.
رعى — raʿā — "to graze; to tend (animals)".
ترعى الأبقار في الحقل. tarʿā al-abqār fī al-ḥaql. The cows graze in the field.
Present: يرعى, yarʿā.
A shepherd is rāʿī, the one who tends.

The word, taken apart.
رعى (raʿā) is built on the native Arabic root r-ʿ-y, "to pasture, to tend" (Lane). It is the plain verb.
