Bengali, chapter 74: Expensive, Cheap, Low, Full, Empty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
দামি (dāmi) — expensive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for quickly, then the Bengali for easy.

You'll want to know: দামি.
দামি — dāmi — "expensive".

The word, taken apart.
দামি (dāmi) is dām, "price", with the ending -i. CDIAL lists Bengali dām under Sanskrit dramma, "a coin", which it traces to Greek drakhmē through Middle Persian (CDIAL 6622).

The word, taken apart.
The origin of সস্তা (sôstā) is not settled. CDIAL has no entry for it, and a search of its word lists by form and by the gloss "cheap" turns up nothing of this shape; the same word in Hindi, sastā, is untraced there too.

Lesson 3 of 5.
নিচু (nichu) — low.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for expensive, then the Bengali for cheap.

You'll want to know: নিচু.
নিচু — nichu — "low".

The word, taken apart.
নিচু (nichu) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nīca, "low" (CDIAL 7540). CDIAL writes the Bengali form nica.

You'll want to know: ভর্তি.
ভর্তি — bhôrti — "full".

The word, taken apart.
ভর্তি (bhôrti) is built on the stem bhar-, "fill": CDIAL lists Bengali bharā, "to load", under Sanskrit bharati, "bears, fills" (CDIAL 9397), but has no entry for ভর্তি (bhôrti) itself.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for low, then the Bengali for full.

You'll want to know: খালি.
খালি — khāli — "empty".

The word, taken apart.
খালি (khāli) is a loan from Arabic khālī, "empty", which reached Bengali through Persian. Its Arabic root is kh-l-w (Lane).
