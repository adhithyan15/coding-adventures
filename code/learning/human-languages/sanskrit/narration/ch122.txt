Lesson 1 of 5.
रजतम् (rajatam) — silver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a jewel, then the Sanskrit for gold.

You'll want to know: रजतम्.
रजतम् — rajatam — "silver".

The word, taken apart.
rajata first meant "whitish, silvery" in the Rigveda and then "silver" (MW). It is inherited from Indo-European, the same word as Latin argentum "silver" (EtymDB), whose symbol Ag is still on the periodic table. Pali kept it as rajata (CDIAL 10576).

Lesson 2 of 5.
काचः (kācaḥ) — glass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for gold, then the Sanskrit for silver.

You'll want to know: काचः.
काचः — kācaḥ — "glass".

The word, taken apart.
kāca "glass" goes back to the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, where it means glass beads; MW cites only a grammarians' root √kac for it, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi and Gujarati kāc "glass" (CDIAL 3007).

Lesson 3 of 5.
शकटम् (śakaṭam) — a cart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for silver, then the Sanskrit for glass.

You'll want to know: शकटम्.
शकटम् — śakaṭam — "a cart".

The word, taken apart.
śakaṭa "a cart, a waggon" appears from the Śrauta Sūtras on, and MW calls it of doubtful derivation; CDIAL 12236 suggests the root √śak with a query mark, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi chakṛā "bullock-cart" (CDIAL 12236).

भाण्डम् (bhāṇḍam) — a pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for glass, then the Sanskrit for a cart.

You'll want to know: भाण्डम्.
भाण्डम् — bhāṇḍam — "a pot".

The word, taken apart.
bhāṇḍa "a pot, any vessel; goods, wares" has an uncertain origin: MW queries a root √bhaṇḍ, and DEDR (appendix 50) notes Burrow's Indo-European proposal. It lives on as Punjabi bhāṁḍā "cooking pot" (CDIAL 9440).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cart, then the Sanskrit for a pot.

You'll want to know: खट्वा.
खट्वा — khaṭvā — "a cot".

The word, taken apart.
khaṭvā "a bedstead, a cot" appears from the Kauśika Sūtra on, and neither MW nor CDIAL gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi khāṭ "bedstead" and khaṭolā "cot" (CDIAL 3781).
