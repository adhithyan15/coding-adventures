Lesson 1 of 5.
сосиска (sosíska) — a frankfurter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for trainers, then the Russian for slippers.

You'll want to know: сосиска.
сосиска (sosíska) — "a frankfurter".

The word, taken apart.
сосиска (sosíska) comes from French saucisse, "a sausage", from Medieval Latin salsīcia, from salsus, "salted" (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
борщ (borshch) — borscht.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a frankfurter, then the Russian for a prawn.

You'll want to know: борщ.
борщ (borshch) — "borscht".

The word, taken apart.
борщ (borshch) first named a plant, hogweed, борщевик; the soup once cooked from it passed its name to the beet soup (Vasmer). Because the plant has sharp leaves, the name is derived from a root "bristly, prickly".

Lesson 4 of 5.
щи (shchi) — cabbage soup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a prawn, then the Russian for borscht.

You'll want to know: щи.
щи (shchi) — "cabbage soup".

The word, taken apart.
щи (shchi) from Old Russian шти, "a soup". Its origin is not settled: Jakobson, in a note to Vasmer, linked it with щавель, "sorrel", a soup green.

Lesson 5 of 5.
блин (blin) — a pancake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for borscht, then the Russian for cabbage soup.

You'll want to know: блин.
блин (blin) — "a pancake".

The word, taken apart.
блин (blin) from Old Russian mlinŭ, the older form: it is linked with молоть, "to grind", and мелю, "I grind" (Vasmer). A pancake is made of milled flour.
