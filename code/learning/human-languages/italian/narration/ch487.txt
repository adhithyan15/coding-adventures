Italian, chapter 487: Stomaco e testa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
masticare — to chew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for allergy, then the Italian for sting.

You'll want to know: masticare.
masticare — "to chew".
Mastica bene prima di mandare giù. Chew well before you swallow.
La gomma da masticare — chewing gum.

The word, taken apart.
masticare is inherited from Late Latin masticāre "to chew" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), usually linked to Greek mastikhân "to gnash the teeth".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: digerire.
digerire — "to digest".
Non ho digerito bene la cena. I did not digest dinner well.
Also figurative: non lo digerisco — I cannot stand him. An -isc- verb.

The word, taken apart.
digerire is a learned borrowing from Latin dīgerere "to divide, to dissolve" (EtymDB), from dis- and gerere "to carry" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il malessere — feeling unwell; unease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to chew, then the Italian for to digest.

You'll want to know: il malessere.
il malessere — "feeling unwell; unease".
Dopo pranzo ho avuto un leggero malessere. After lunch I felt a little unwell.
A vague feeling that something is wrong, in the body or the mind.

The word, taken apart.
malessere is built from male "badly", from Latin male, and essere "to be", from Latin esse (Lewis & Short): a being-unwell.

Lesson 4 of 5.

vomitare — "to vomit, to be sick".
Ho mangiato troppo e ho vomitato. I ate too much and was sick.
More politely: rimettere.

The word, taken apart.
vomitare is a learned borrowing from Latin vomitāre "to vomit often" (EtymDB), from vomere "to vomit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le vertigini — dizziness, vertigo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for feeling unwell, then the Italian for to vomit.

You'll want to know: le vertigini.
le vertigini — "dizziness, vertigo".
Quando guardo giù dal balcone ho le vertigini. When I look down from the balcony I feel dizzy.
Avere le vertigini — to feel dizzy; to be afraid of heights is soffrire di vertigini.

The word, taken apart.
vertigini is the plural of vertigine, a learned borrowing from Latin vertīgō "a turning round, dizziness" (Lewis & Short), from vertere "to turn".
