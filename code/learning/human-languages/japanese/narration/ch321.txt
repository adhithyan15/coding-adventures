Japanese, chapter 321: How Sure Are You?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぜったい (zettai) — absolutely; definitely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to comprehend, then the Japanese for especially.

You'll want to know: ぜったい.
ぜったい — zettai — "absolutely; definitely".
あぶないから、ぜったい いかないで ください。 — abunai kara, zettai ikanaide kudasai — "It's dangerous, so do not go there under any circumstances."

The word, taken apart.
ぜったい (zettai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are zetsu, "cut off" (Middle Chinese dzjwet) and tai, "opposite" (twojH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as zet-tai.

Lesson 2 of 5.
かならず (kanarazu) — without fail; certainly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for especially, then the Japanese for absolutely.

You'll want to know: かならず.
かならず — kanarazu — "without fail; certainly".
やくそくを したから、かならず いきます。 — yakusoku o shita kara, kanarazu ikimasu — "I promised, so I will go without fail."

The word, taken apart.
かならず (kanarazu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
