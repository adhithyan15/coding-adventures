Telugu, chapter 326: A Witness to the War.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
సాక్షి (sākṣi) — a witness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a watchman, then the Telugu for a milkman.

You'll want to know: సాక్షి.
సాక్షి — sākṣi — "a witness".
సాక్షి పోలీసుతో మాట్లాడాడు. sākṣi pōlīsutō māṭlāḍāḍu. The witness spoke with the police.
Plural: సాక్షులు, sākṣulu.
A ప్రత్యక్ష సాక్షి (sākṣi) is an eyewitness, one who saw it happen. A whole case can turn on one: it depends on the witness, సాక్షి (sākṣi) మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది.

The word, taken apart.
సాక్షి (sākṣi) is borrowed from Sanskrit sākṣin, "a witness" (CDIAL 13321). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word apart as sa- "with" and akṣi "eye": the witness is the one who was there with his own eyes.

Lesson 2 of 5.
యుద్ధం (yuddhaṁ) — a war; a battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a milkman, then the Telugu for a witness.

You'll want to know: యుద్ధం.
యుద్ధం — yuddhaṁ — "a war; a battle".
ఆ యుద్ధం చాలా రోజులు జరిగింది. ā yuddhaṁ cālā rōjulu jarigindi. That war went on for many days.
With చేయు it makes a verb: యుద్ధం (yuddhaṁ) చేయు, to fight a war.

The word, taken apart.
యుద్ధం (yuddhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit yuddha, "fought", and as a noun "a battle", the past participle of the root yudh, "to fight" (CDIAL 10499). Like many Sanskrit nouns in -a, it ends in -ం in Telugu.

Lesson 3 of 5.
సంఘటన (saṅghaṭana) — an incident; an event.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a witness, then the Telugu for a war.

You'll want to know: సంఘటన.
సంఘటన — saṅghaṭana — "an incident; an event".
నిన్న రాత్రి ఒక వింత సంఘటన జరిగింది. ninna rātri oka vinta saṅghaṭana jarigindi. A strange incident happened last night.
It goes with జరుగు, to happen: సంఘటన (saṅghaṭana) జరిగింది, the incident happened.

The word, taken apart.
సంఘటన (saṅghaṭana) is borrowed from Sanskrit saṁghaṭana, "union, a coming together" (Monier-Williams), built from sam- "together" and ghaṭ "to join". In Telugu the things that come together make an event, an incident.

Lesson 4 of 5.
దొంగిలించు (doṅgiliñcu) — to steal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a war, then the Telugu for an incident.

You'll want to know: దొంగిలించు.
దొంగిలించు — doṅgiliñcu — "to steal".
దొంగ నా సంచి దొంగిలించాడు. doṅga nā sañci doṅgiliñcāḍu. The thief stole my bag.
Past (I …): దొంగిలించాను, doṅgiliñcānu.
It is the verb of దొంగ, a thief, which you already know.

The word, taken apart.
దొంగిలించు (doṅgiliñcu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2982 lists Telugu doṅgilincu "to steal" beside doṅga "a thief", with Tamil toṅkaṉ "a robber".

Lesson 5 of 5.
పారిపోవు (pāripōvu) — to run away; to escape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for an incident, then the Telugu for to steal.

You'll want to know: పారిపోవు.
పారిపోవు — pāripōvu — "to run away; to escape".
పోలీసును చూసి దొంగ పారిపోయాడు. pōlīsunu cūsi doṅga pāripōyāḍu. Seeing the police, the thief ran away.
Past (I …): పారిపోయాను, pāripōyānu.
పారి is from పారు, to run; పోవు is to go. Many Telugu verbs end in -పోవు this way.

The word, taken apart.
పారిపోవు (pāripōvu) is a native Dravidian compound: pāru "to run, run away" (DEDR 3963) and pōvu "to go" (DEDR 4572).
