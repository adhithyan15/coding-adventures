Arabic, chapter 371: Aims and means.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
وسيلة (wasīla) — a means.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to exaggerate, then the Arabic for to generalize.

You'll want to know: وسيلة.
وسيلة — wasīla — "a means".
الحافلة وسيلة رخيصة للسفر. al-ḥāfila wasīla rakhīṣa li-s-safar. The bus is a cheap means of travel.

The word, taken apart.
وسيلة (wasīla) is built on the native Arabic root w-s-l, "to seek to draw near" (Lane). A wasīla is what brings you near to a goal: a means.

Lesson 2 of 5.
سعى (saʿā) — to strive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to generalize, then the Arabic for a means.

You'll want to know: سعى.
سعى — saʿā — "to strive".
سعى الشاب إلى عمل أفضل. saʿā ash-shābb ilā ʿamal afḍal. The young man strove for a better job.
Present: يسعى, yasʿā.
It takes إلى: سعى (saʿā) إلى ….

The word, taken apart.
سعى (saʿā) is built on the native Arabic root s-ʿ-y, usually traced to the sense "to run, to strive". Saʿā is a Form I verb with a weak last letter, so its present is yasʿā.

The word, taken apart.
حافز (ḥāfiz) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-f-z, "to urge on from behind" (Lane). Ḥāfiz is the active participle: what urges you on, an incentive.
