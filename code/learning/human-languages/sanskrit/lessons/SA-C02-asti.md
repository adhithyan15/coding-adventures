# अस्ति (asti) — "is," a cousin of *is* and *est*

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NAMA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The little verb "is" — and here you meet it in a form whose root is
shared by half the "to be" words on earth.

## Sounds you'll need

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-ASTI]; assesses=[] -->

*as · ti*. The *s* closes the first beat; *ti* follows cleanly after it.

## The word, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-AS-TO-BE]; assesses=[] -->

*asti* ("is") is the third-person singular of the root *as-* ("to be"), from Proto-Indo-European **\*h₁es-**. The same
root gives the family's copula elsewhere: English **is**, German *ist*, Latin *est*,
Greek *esti*. Hindi *hai* is often linked to *asti*, but CDIAL puts Old Hindi
*āhaï* "is" under a different verb, *ākṣeti* "dwells" (CDIAL 1031). Unlike Tamil and Bengali, which drop "is" in
the present, Sanskrit *keeps* an explicit copula, built on the very root
Europe kept too.
