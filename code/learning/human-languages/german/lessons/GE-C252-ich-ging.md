# ich ging, ich kam — the past a story is told in

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-TE-01, GE-LEX-SAGTE-01, GE-LEX-MACHTE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I said" in one word. (*Ich sagte.*) Now "I made". (*Ich
machte.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That *-te* is the Präteritum, the past a German book or a fairy
tale is told in. But the verbs a story leans on hardest, going, coming,
seeing, do not take *-te* at all.

## Grammar Lens: the vowel changes, and nothing is added
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-STARK-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-GEHEN-02, GE-LEX-KOMMEN-01, GE-LEX-SEHEN-01, GE-LEX-FAHREN-01, GE-LEX-NEHMEN-02, GE-LEX-STEHEN-04] -->

Six verbs you already own, and their one-word past:

> gehen — **ich ging**, I went
> kommen — **ich kam**, I came
> sehen — **ich sah**, I saw
> fahren — **ich fuhr**, I travelled, I drove
> nehmen — **ich nahm**, I took
> stehen — **ich stand**, I stood

No *-te* anywhere. The stem's vowel changes instead, and for *ich* and for
*er* or *sie* nothing is added: *ich ging*, *er ging*, just as *ich sagte*,
*er sagte*. The plural adds **-en**: *wir gingen*, *sie sahen*. And *du*
adds **-st**: *du gingst*.

These are the **strong** verbs. Which vowel each one takes is learned verb
by verb, like English *went*, *came* and *saw*.

A separable verb splits in the Präteritum as it does in the present:
*ankommen*, **ich kam in Berlin an**.

## The word, taken apart: why the vowel changes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-ABLAUT-02]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-DENTALES-PRAETERITUM-01] -->

Changing the vowel to make a past is older than German. The Germanic
languages inherited it, and English kept it too: *sing, sang*; *come,
came*. German calls it **Ablaut**, "off-sound".

*Kommen* goes back to Old High German *kweman* and *sehen* to *sehan*,
which EtymDB 2.1 traces to the Proto-Germanic parents of English *come* and
*see*. EtymDB does not list the past forms, so this is the usual account,
given as one: Old High German already said *quam*, "came", and *sah*,
"saw". *Ging* is usually traced to a second old verb of going, *gangan*.

The *-te* of *sagte* is the younger Germanic invention from chapter
twenty-five, and new verbs still take it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-STARK-01, GE-ETYMON-ABLAUT-02, GE-LEX-FAHREN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "I travelled"? (***Ich fuhr.***) "He took"? (***Er nahm***, the
same as *ich nahm*.) "We came"? (***Wir kamen.***) What changes in a strong
verb's past, if not the ending? (**The vowel.**) And what is the old name
for that change? (***Ablaut.***)
