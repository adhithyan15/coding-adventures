Japanese, chapter 300: The Fox That Changed Shape.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きつね (kitsune) — a fox.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to protect, then the Japanese for a feudal lord.

You'll want to know: きつね.
きつね — kitsune — "a fox".
やまに きつねが すんでいました。 — yama ni kitsune ga sunde imashita — "A fox lived in the mountains."

The word, taken apart.
きつね (kitsune) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ばける (bakeru) — to take the form of; to disguise oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a fox, then the Japanese for a raccoon dog.

You'll want to know: ばける.
ばける — bakeru — "to take the form of; to disguise oneself".
むかしばなしで、たぬきが おじいさんに ばけました。 — mukashibanashi de, tanuki ga ojiisan ni bakemashita — "In an old tale, a raccoon dog turned itself into an old man."
Polite ばけます, bakemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): きつねが おんなに ばけました。, kitsune ga onna ni bakemashita.
