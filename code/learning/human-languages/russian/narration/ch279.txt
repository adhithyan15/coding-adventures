Russian, chapter 279: News and headlines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
известие (izvéstiye) — a piece of news, a report.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to recall, then the Russian for to memorize.

You'll want to know: известие.
известие (izvéstiye) — "a piece of news, a report".
Мы получили известие о его приезде. My poluchíli izvéstiye o yevó priyézde. We received news of his arrival.
More formal than новость. Neuter; plural известия.

The word, taken apart.
известие (izvéstiye) is built on весть "news, tidings", from the old Slavic root of ведать "to know", which goes back to PIE weyd- "to see, to know" (EtymDB lists известие (izvéstiye) "news" with no parent).

Lesson 2 of 5.
выпуск (výpusk) — a bulletin, an issue, an edition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to memorize, then the Russian for a piece of news.

You'll want to know: выпуск.
выпуск (výpusk) — "a bulletin, an issue, an edition".
В вечернем выпуске новостей говорили о погоде. V vechérnem výpuske novostéy govoríli o pogóde. In the evening news bulletin they talked about the weather.
Выпуск новостей — a news bulletin; also an issue of a magazine.

The word, taken apart.
выпуск (výpusk) is built on выпускать "to let out, to issue", from пускать "to let go", inherited from Proto-Slavic pustiti (EtymDB): what is let out to the public.

Lesson 4 of 5.
статья (statyá) — an article.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bulletin, then the Russian for a headline.

You'll want to know: статья.
статья (statyá) — "an article".
Он написал статью о нашей школе. On napisál statyú o náshey shkóle. He wrote an article about our school.
Genitive статьи. Also an article of a law: статья (statyá) закона.

The word, taken apart.
статья (statyá) is usually traced to the root of стать "to stand, to become", inherited from Proto-Slavic stati (EtymDB), with an old sense "a part in its place"; EtymDB lists no parent for статья (statyá) itself.

Lesson 5 of 5.
сенсация (sensátsiya) — a sensation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a headline, then the Russian for an article.

You'll want to know: сенсация.
сенсация (sensátsiya) — "a sensation".
Эта новость стала настоящей сенсацией. Éta nóvost stála nastoyáshchey sensátsiyey. This news became a real sensation.
Feminine. The adjective is сенсационный.

The word, taken apart.
сенсация (sensátsiya) was borrowed from French sensation, built on Latin sensus "feeling", from sentīre "to feel" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the Russian word with no parent.
