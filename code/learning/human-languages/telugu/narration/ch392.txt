Telugu, chapter 392: Where Things Come From.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
మూలం (mūlaṁ) — a source; an origin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to stress, then the Telugu for to bring up.

You'll want to know: మూలం.
మూలం — mūlaṁ — "a source; an origin".
ఆ వార్తకు మూలం ఏమిటి? ā vārtaku mūlaṁ ēmiṭi? What is the source of that news?
First a root: మూల కారణం is the root cause.

The word, taken apart.
మూలం (mūlaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit mūla, "a root" (CDIAL 10250); in Telugu it is the root of anything, its source or origin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ఉద్భవించు (udbhaviñcu) — to originate; to spring from.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to bring up, then the Telugu for a source.

You'll want to know: ఉద్భవించు.
ఉద్భవించు — udbhaviñcu — "to originate; to spring from".
ఆ ఆచారం చాలా పాత కాలంలో ఉద్భవించింది. ā ācāraṁ cālā pāta kālaṁlō udbhaviñcindi. That custom arose in very old times.
Past (it …): ఉద్భవించింది, udbhaviñcindi.
Formal: for customs, ideas, languages and rivers, so its past is shown as ఉద్భవించింది, it arose.

The word, taken apart.
ఉద్భవించు (udbhaviñcu) is built on Sanskrit udbhava, "origin, birth" (CDIAL 2039), from the root bhū, "to become", with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.

Lesson 3 of 5.
దారితీయు (dāritīyu) — to lead to; to result in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a source, then the Telugu for to originate.

You'll want to know: దారితీయు.
దారితీయు — dāritīyu — "to lead to; to result in".
చిన్న అభిప్రాయభేదం పెద్ద గొడవకు దారితీసింది. cinna abhiprāyabhēdaṁ pedda goḍavaku dāritīsindi. A small difference of opinion led to a big quarrel.
Past (it …): దారితీసింది, dāritīsindi.
What it leads to takes -కి: గొడవకు దారితీయు (dāritīyu). Word for word, to take the road. Events lead to things, so its past is shown as దారితీసింది, it led to.

The word, taken apart.
దారితీయు (dāritīyu) is native Dravidian, a compound of dāri, "a way, a road" (DEDR 3170), and tīyu, "to take, to draw" (DEDR 3407).

Lesson 4 of 5.
కలుగు (kalugu) — to arise; to come about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to originate, then the Telugu for to lead to.

You'll want to know: కలుగు.
కలుగు — kalugu — "to arise; to come about".
ఆ వార్త విని నాకు ఆనందం కలిగింది. ā vārta vini nāku ānandaṁ kaligindi. Hearing that news, I felt joy.
Past (it …): కలిగింది, kaligindi.
The feeling is the subject and the person takes -కి: నాకు సందేహం కలిగింది, a doubt arose in me. So its past is shown in the third person, కలిగింది; no one says కలిగాను.

The word, taken apart.
కలుగు (kalugu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1300 lists Telugu kalugu, "to happen, to occur, to be produced, to exist", with Tamil kala, "to appear, to come into being".

Lesson 5 of 5.
తలెత్తు (talettu) — to crop up; to surface.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to lead to, then the Telugu for to arise.

You'll want to know: తలెత్తు.
తలెత్తు — talettu — "to crop up; to surface".
కొత్త ఇంట్లో చాలా సమస్యలు తలెత్తాయి. kotta iṇṭlō cālā samasyalu talettāyi. A lot of problems cropped up in the new house.
Past (it …): తలెత్తింది, talettindi.
తల, the head, + ఎత్తు, to raise: a problem raises its head. In this sense things crop up, not people, so its past is shown as తలెత్తింది, it cropped up.

The word, taken apart.
తలెత్తు (talettu) is native Dravidian, a compound of tala, "the head" (DEDR 3103), and ettu, "to lift, to raise" (DEDR 796).
