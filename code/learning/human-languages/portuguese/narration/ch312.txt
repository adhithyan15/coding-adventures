Portuguese, chapter 312: Noivos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o noivado — the engagement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for old age, then the Portuguese for the generation.

You'll want to know: o noivado.
o noivado — "the engagement".
O noivado durou dois anos. The engagement lasted two years.
Built on noivo, ‘fiancé, groom’.

The word, taken apart.
noivado is built on noivo (EtymDB), which goes back to Vulgar Latin novius "newly wed", from Latin novus "new".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a aliança — the wedding ring; the alliance.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the generation, then the Portuguese for the engagement.

You'll want to know: a aliança.
a aliança — "the wedding ring; the alliance".
Ele usa a aliança na mão esquerda. He wears his wedding ring on his left hand.
Also a political alliance.

The word, taken apart.
aliança came from French alliance "a union, a marriage" (EtymDB), built on allier "to bind", from Latin alligāre "to tie to" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o casamento — the wedding; marriage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the engagement, then the Portuguese for the wedding ring.

You'll want to know: o casamento.
o casamento — "the wedding; marriage".
O casamento foi numa quinta perto do rio. The wedding was at a country estate near the river.
Built on casar.

The word, taken apart.
casamento comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB), built on casar "to marry", from casa "a house", which is inherited from Latin casa "a hut, a cottage": to set up house.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pedir em casamento — to propose (marriage).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the wedding ring, then the Portuguese for the wedding.

You'll want to know: pedir em casamento.
pedir em casamento — "to propose (marriage)".
Ele pediu-a em casamento na praia. He proposed to her on the beach.
Pedir alguém em casamento; the pronoun goes on pedir: pediu-a.

The word, taken apart.
pedir is inherited from Latin petere "to seek, to ask for" (Lewis & Short); casamento is built on casar, from casa "a house".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o matrimónio — matrimony, marriage (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the wedding, then the Portuguese for to propose.

You'll want to know: o matrimónio.
o matrimónio — "matrimony, marriage (formal)".
Celebraram o matrimónio numa pequena igreja. They celebrated their marriage in a small church.
In Brazil: matrimônio.

The word, taken apart.
matrimónio is a learned borrowing from Latin mātrimōnium "marriage" (EtymDB), built on māter "mother".
