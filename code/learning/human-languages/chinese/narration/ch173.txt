6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yāoqǐng — to invite; an invitation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for smelling good, then the Mandarin for bland.

You'll want to know: yāoqǐng.
yāoqǐng — "to invite; an invitation".
tā yāoqǐng wǒmen qù tā jiā — He's invited us to his place.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yāoqǐng is built from yāo and 请 'to ask', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jiew and tshjengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to stop someone on the way'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
问候 (wènhòu) — to send regards to; to greet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bland, then the Mandarin for to invite.

You'll want to know: 问候.
问候 — wènhòu — "to send regards to; to greet".
nǐ bāng wǒ wènhòu nǐ bàba māma — Give my regards to your mum and dad.
The new character, in pieces: 候 (hòu) — ten strokes: the person piece, a thin vertical beside it, then on the right a small hooked top, a horizontal, and a base of five strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, two horizontals, and two long strokes falling to either side. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
问候 (wènhòu) is built from 问 'to ask' and 候 (hòu) 'to wait upon', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjunH and huwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to ask after someone. The second is a different word from 后 'after', huwX.

What you've built.

Script — the person piece, a thin stroke, then the right side.
候 (hòu).
Three pieces, left to right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The person piece, 亻, two strokes, as in 你.
A thin vertical, one short stroke standing beside it.
The right side, seven strokes. On top a short horizontal that turns and falls to the left, like a small hook. A horizontal under it. Then the base, five strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, a short horizontal, a longer horizontal, a long stroke falling to the left through them, and a long stroke falling to the right.
2 + 1 + 7 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 候 (hòu), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 候 (hòu)? (Ten.) What comes after the person piece? (One thin vertical.) How many strokes in the base? (Five.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
晚会 (wǎnhuì) — evening party; evening show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to invite, then the Mandarin for to send regards to.

You'll want to know: 晚会.
晚会 — wǎnhuì — "evening party; evening show".
晚上我有事不能去晚会 wǎnshang wǒ yǒushì bù néng qù wǎnhuì — I've got something on tonight, so I can't come to the party.

The word, taken apart.
晚会 (wǎnhuì) is built from 晚 'evening' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjonX and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to come together'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
jùhuì — get-together; party.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to send regards to, then the Mandarin for an evening party.

You'll want to know: jùhuì.
jùhuì — "get-together; party".
zhōumò wǒmen yǒu yí ge jùhuì nǐ lái ma — We're having a get-together at the weekend: are you coming?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jùhuì is built from jù 'to gather' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjuX and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'many' and 'to gather', and quotes an older book that glosses it with the second.

Lesson 6 of 6.
请客 (qǐngkè) — to pay for everyone; to treat (the others).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an evening party, then the Mandarin for a get-together.

You'll want to know: 请客.
请客 — qǐngkè — "to pay for everyone; to treat (the others)".
今天我请客每个人一杯茶 jīntiān wǒ qǐngkè měi ge rén yì bēi chá — It's on me today: a cup of tea each.

The word, taken apart.
请客 (qǐngkè) is built from 请 'to ask, to invite' and 客 'guest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjengX and khaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to have the others as your guests, and so to pay.
