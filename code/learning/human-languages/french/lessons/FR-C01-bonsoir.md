# bonsoir — good evening

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Same assembly as *bonjour*, one word swapped. If you have that one,
this is nearly free.

  [bonjour](./FR-C01-bonjour.md)

## The word, taken apart — by assembling it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-BONSOIR-09]; assesses=[FR-LEX-BON-03, FR-LEX-SOIR-08, FR-GRAM-ADJ-AGREEMENT-04] -->

**bonsoir** = **bon** ("good") + **soir** ("evening") = "good evening." *bon*
stays masculine because *soir* is masculine — same as *bonjour*, no new
agreement to learn. Said *bõ- swahr*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*bonsoir* is the evening swap for *bonjour*: as the day turns to evening
(roughly late afternoon on), French speakers switch from *bonjour* to
*bonsoir*. Both are the polite, all-purpose greeting for their time of day.
And like Spanish *buenas noches*, *bonsoir* works both **arriving** and
**leaving** in the evening — "good evening" on the way in, "goodnight-ish" on
the way out. (For actually sending someone to bed, French has a separate
phrase — the next two lessons.)
