Urdu, chapter 288: The Lamp That Fell.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
چراغ (chirāgh) — an oil lamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to flare up, then the Urdu for to be on fire.

You'll want to know: چراغ.
چراغ — chirāgh — "an oil lamp".
رات کو چراغ گر گیا۔ rāt ko chirāgh gir gayā. The lamp fell over in the night.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
چراغ (chirāgh) is usually traced to Persian chirāgh, "a lamp". It is the small clay lamp with oil and a wick.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an oil lamp, then the Urdu for a flame.

You'll want to know: دہکنا.
دہکنا — dahaknā — "to glow red-hot".
انگارے دہک رہے تھے۔ angāre dahak rahe the. The embers were glowing red.
Past (it …): وہ انگارا دہکا, voh angārā dahkā.

The word, taken apart.
دہکنا (dahaknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin dahaknā, "to blaze up", under Sanskrit dáhati, "burns" (CDIAL 6245). It is fire without flame, glowing.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تپش (tapish) — scorching heat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to glow red-hot, then the Urdu for brightness.

You'll want to know: تپش.
تپش — tapish — "scorching heat".
دوپہر کی تپش سے سب تھک گئے۔ dopahar kī tapish se sab thak gae. Everyone was worn out by the scorching midday heat.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
تپش (tapish) is usually traced to Persian tapish, "heat, a burning". It is the heat you feel on the skin, of fire or of the sun.
