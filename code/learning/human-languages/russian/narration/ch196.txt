Lesson 1 of 5.
крыса (krýsa) — a rat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a rose, then the Russian for an oak.

You'll want to know: крыса.
крыса (krýsa) — "a rat".

The word, taken apart.
крыса (krýsa) — Vasmer finds none of the existing explanations reliable: a reshaping of the root of грызть, "to gnaw", links with крот, "mole", or кровь, "blood", and others.

Lesson 2 of 5.
паук (paúk) — a spider.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an oak, then the Russian for a rat.

You'll want to know: паук.
паук (paúk) — "a spider".

The word, taken apart.
паук (paúk) is па- with ǫkŭ, "a hook", for the spider's bent legs (Vasmer). Vasmer relates the root to Greek ónkos, "a hook", and Latin uncus, "hooked".

Lesson 3 of 5.
комар (komár) — a mosquito.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a rat, then the Russian for a spider.

You'll want to know: комар.
комар (komár) — "a mosquito".

The word, taken apart.
комар (komár) from Proto-Slavic komarŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian kamãnė, "a bumblebee", and Old Prussian camus, "bumblebee", and probably Middle High German hummen, "to hum".

Lesson 4 of 5.
муха (múkha) — a fly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a spider, then the Russian for a mosquito.

You'll want to know: муха.
муха (múkha) — "a fly".

The word, taken apart.
муха (múkha) from Proto-Slavic muxa — inherited, with мошка, "a midge" (Vasmer). Latin musca, "fly", and Greek muîa, "fly", go back to the same PIE word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
жук (zhuk) — a beetle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a mosquito, then the Russian for a fly.

You'll want to know: жук.
жук (zhuk) — "a beetle".

The word, taken apart.
жук (zhuk) is built on a sound-imitating stem, the one in жужжать, "to buzz" (Vasmer): the beetle as the buzzer.
