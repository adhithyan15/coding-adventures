Gujarati, chapter 492: Two Homes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પિયર (piyar) — a married woman's parents' home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the father-in-law of one's child, then the Gujarati for the mother-in-law of one's child.

You'll want to know: પિયર.
પિયર — piyar — "a married woman's parents' home".
દિવાળીમાં મીરા પિયર જશે. divāḷīmā̃ Mīrā piyar jaśe. At Diwali Mira will go to her parents' home.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
પિયર (piyar) goes back to a reconstructed pitṛghara-, "father's house"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 8180).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the mother-in-law of one's child, then the Gujarati for a married woman's parents' home.

You'll want to know: સાસરું.
સાસરું — sāsrũ — "the in-laws' home".
લગ્ન પછી દીકરી સાસરે ગઈ. lagna pachī dīkrī sāsre gaī. After the wedding the daughter went to her in-laws' home.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
સાસરું (sāsrũ) is inherited from Sanskrit śvāśura, "belonging to the father-in-law"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "father-in-law's house" (CDIAL 12767).

Lesson 4 of 5.
પૌત્ર (pautra) — a grandson (a son's son).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the in-laws' home, then the Gujarati for a sister's child.

You'll want to know: પૌત્ર.
પૌત્ર — pautra — "a grandson (a son's son)".
દાદાને પૌત્ર સાથે રમવું ગમે છે. dādāne pautra sāthe ramvũ game che. Grandfather likes playing with his grandson.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પૌત્ર (pautra) is a tatsama, Sanskrit pautra, "a son's son, a grandson", as Monier-Williams records it.
