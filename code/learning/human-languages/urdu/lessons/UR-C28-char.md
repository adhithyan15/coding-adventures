## The word, taken apart — quattuor, four, chār
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-ETYMON-CHAR-FOUR-PIE]; assesses=[UR-LEX-CHAR] -->

**چار** continues Sanskrit *catvāraḥ*, from Indo-European
\**kʷetwóres*, "four."

The opening \**kʷ* is where this gets interesting, because three branches did
three different things with it. Latin kept it whole: **quattuor**. English lost
the lip-rounding and softened the *k* to *f*: **four**. Indo-Aryan pushed it
forward to a **ch**, which is what you are saying.

One sound, three descendants, and the numbers still line up.
