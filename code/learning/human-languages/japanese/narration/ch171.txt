Japanese, chapter 171: First of All, Next and Last.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
さいしょ (saisho) — the beginning; at first.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to celebrate, then the Japanese for to spend time.

You'll want to know: さいしょ.
さいしょ — saisho — "the beginning; at first".
あしたは さいしょに えきに いきます。 — ashita wa saisho ni eki ni ikimasu — "Tomorrow I will go to the station first."

The word, taken apart.
さいしょ (saisho) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sai, "most, utmost" (Middle Chinese tswajH) and sho, "first time, beginning" (tsrhjo) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 7.
まず (mazu) — first of all; to begin with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to spend time, then the Japanese for the beginning.

You'll want to know: まず.
まず — mazu — "first of all; to begin with".
あしたは まず かおを あらいます。 — ashita wa mazu kao o araimasu — "Tomorrow I will wash my face first of all."

The word, taken apart.
まず (mazu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji is a later kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
