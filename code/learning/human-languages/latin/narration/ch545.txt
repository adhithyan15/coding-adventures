Latin, chapter 545: Sound and harmony.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sonus, sonī — a sound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lyre-player, then the Latin for to play the cithara.

You'll want to know: sonus, sonī.
sonus, sonī — "a sound".
Sonus tubae per castra it. The sound of the trumpet goes through the camp.
Masculine, second declension. English sound comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
sonus belongs with sonō "to sound", inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to sound" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English sound comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to play the cithara, then the Latin for a sound.

You'll want to know: sonō, sonāre.
sonō, sonāre — "to sound, ring".
Tintinnābulum sonat; hospitēs adsunt. The bell rings; the guests are here.
sonō, sonāre, sonuī, sonitum. Italian sonata, a piece to be played, comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
sonō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to sound" (EtymDB). Italian sonata comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
resonō, resonāre — to resound, echo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sound, then the Latin for to sound.

You'll want to know: resonō, resonāre.
resonō, resonāre — "to resound, echo".
Tōta vallis cantū pāstōrum resonat. The whole valley echoes with the shepherds' song.
resonō, resonāre, resonāvī: re- 'back' + sonō. English resound, resonant.

The word, taken apart.
resonō is built on re- "back" and sonō "to sound" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English resound and resonant come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
symphōnia, symphōniae — a band of musicians; harmony.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sound, then the Latin for to resound.

You'll want to know: symphōnia, symphōniae.
symphōnia, symphōniae — "a band of musicians; harmony".
In convīviō symphōnia canit. At the dinner party a band plays.
Feminine, first declension, from Greek: sounds together, then the slaves who made music at dinner. English symphony.

The word, taken apart.
symphōnia was borrowed from Greek symphonia, built on syn "with" and phone "sound" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English symphony comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
concentus, concentūs — harmony, singing together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to resound, then the Latin for a band of musicians.

You'll want to know: concentus, concentūs.
concentus, concentūs — "harmony, singing together".
Concentus avium in silvā dulcis est. The chorus of the birds in the wood is sweet.
Masculine, fourth declension, from concinō. Also agreement between people.

The word, taken apart.
concentus is the action noun of concinō "to sing together" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
