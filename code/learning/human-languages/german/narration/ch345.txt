German, chapter 345: Zusammenhänge.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
der Zusammenhang — the connection, the context.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to affect, then the German for to concern.

You'll want to know: der Zusammenhang.
der Zusammenhang — "the connection, the context".
Es gibt einen Zusammenhang zwischen Schlaf und Gesundheit. There is a connection between sleep and health.
Plural: die Zusammenhänge. In diesem Zusammenhang — in this context.

The word, taken apart.
Zusammenhang is built from zusammen "together" and the family of hängen "to hang", from Old High German hengen and Proto-Germanic hangijaną (EtymDB): things that hang together.

Lesson 3 of 6.
der Beweis — the proof, the evidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the connection, then the German for to be connected.

You'll want to know: der Beweis.
der Beweis — "the proof, the evidence".
Die Polizei hat keinen Beweis gegen den Mann. The police have no proof against the man.
Plural: die Beweise. Der Beweis für + accusative. The verb is beweisen — to prove.

The word, taken apart.
Beweis is the noun of beweisen "to prove", built with be- on weisen "to show, to direct": a proof shows the thing. EtymDB has no history for the noun, so this is the plain make-up.

Lesson 4 of 6.
belegen — to back up (with evidence); to cover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to be connected, then the German for the proof.

You'll want to know: belegen.
belegen — "to back up (with evidence); to cover".
Die Zahlen belegen, dass mehr Menschen Fahrrad fahren. The figures back up the fact that more people are cycling.
Inseparable: es belegt, es hat belegt. Also to cover: ein belegtes Brötchen — a filled roll; der Platz ist belegt — the seat is taken.

The word, taken apart.
belegen is inherited from Old High German bileggen (EtymDB), built with be- on legen "to lay": you lay proof down. EtymDB lists English belay as a cousin.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the German for the proof, then the German for to back up.

You'll want to know: nachweisen.
nachweisen — "to prove, to demonstrate".
Der Mann konnte nachweisen, dass er zu Hause war. The man was able to prove that he was at home.
Separable and strong: er weist nach, er hat nachgewiesen.

The word, taken apart.
nachweisen is built from nach and weisen "to show, to direct" (EtymDB): you show the way back to the facts.
