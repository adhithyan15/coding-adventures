Japanese, chapter 405: A Famous Garden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ていえん (teien) — a landscaped garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for wonderful, then the Japanese for to look down on.

You'll want to know: ていえん.
ていえん — teien — "a landscaped garden".
てらの ていえんは しずかでした。 — tera no teien wa shizuka deshita — "The temple garden was quiet."

The word, taken apart.
ていえん (teien) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tei, "garden" (Middle Chinese deng) and en, "park" (hjwon) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
みごと (migoto) — splendid; magnificent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a landscaped garden, then the Japanese for famous.

You'll want to know: みごと.
みごと — migoto — "splendid; magnificent".
さくらが みごとに さいています。 — sakura ga migoto ni saite imasu — "The cherry trees are in splendid bloom."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
みごと (migoto) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mi, "seeing" (from miru) and goto, "thing"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
