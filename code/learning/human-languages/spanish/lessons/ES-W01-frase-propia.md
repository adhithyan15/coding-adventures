# Your own line — the first writing with no model

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTHO-HOLA-01, ES-LEX-HOLA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have written **hola** four ways: traced, copied, copied from
memory, and written from a sound alone.

[PAUSE 1s] Every one of those had something to reach for. This one does not.

## Writing — controlled composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-WRITING-CONTROLLED-COMPOSITION]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BUENOS-DIAS, ES-LEX-BUENAS-TARDES, ES-LEX-BUENAS-NOCHES, ES-LEX-ME-LLAMO] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

Here is your whole supply. Nothing outside it may go on the page.

> *hola* · *buenos días* · *buenas tardes* · *buenas noches* · *me llamo*

[PAUSE 2s] Now the situation. **It is four in the afternoon, and you are meeting
someone for the first time.**

Write two things, in this order: the greeting the hour asks for, and your name.

[PAUSE 3s] Two decisions live in that. Which of four greetings the clock allows
— and what has to come first so a stranger knows who is talking. Nobody is
supplying either one. That is what makes this different from every writing step
before it: the words are old, and the choosing is new.

Write it now, then read on.

Your line should be **Buenas tardes. Me llamo** and then your name. If you
reached for *buenos días*, check the hour again. If the name came first, read it
back aloud and hear how the greeting wants to open the door.
