Latin, chapter 90: Horse, Ox, Sheep, Goat, Pig.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
equus, equī — a horse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plain, then the Latin for a shore.

You'll want to know: equus, equī.
equus, equī — "a horse". English equestrian.

The word, taken apart.
equus is inherited. Old Latin wrote equos, and it is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h1ekwos, "horse" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek hippos, "horse", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
bōs, bovis — an ox, a cow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a shore, then the Latin for a horse.

You'll want to know: bōs, bovis.
bōs, bovis — "an ox, a cow". English bovine.

The word, taken apart.
bōs is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European gwows, "cow" (EtymDB 2.1), but its b suggests it reached Latin from a neighbouring Italic language such as Oscan or Umbrian: Latin's own sound changes turned that gw into v. English cow is a cousin.
