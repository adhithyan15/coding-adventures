Lesson 1 of 5.
जीरकम् (jīrakam) — cumin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a small lamp, then the Sanskrit for a door panel, a shutter.

You'll want to know: जीरकम्.
जीरकम् — jīrakam — "cumin".

The word, taken apart.
jīraka "cumin seed" is a word of the medical writer Suśruta, and neither MW nor CDIAL 5234 gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi and Bengali jīrā and Gujarati jīrũ "cumin" (CDIAL 5234).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a door panel, a shutter, then the Sanskrit for cumin.

You'll want to know: सर्षपः.
सर्षपः — sarṣapaḥ — "mustard".

The word, taken apart.
sarṣapa "mustard, mustard seed" goes back to the Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa (CDIAL 13281). Its origin is uncertain: DEDR 921, citing Burrow and Mayrhofer, discusses a possible link with Dravidian words for mustard.

हिंगु (hiṅgu) — asafoetida.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for cumin, then the Sanskrit for mustard.

You'll want to know: हिंगु.
हिंगु — hiṅgu — "asafoetida".

The word, taken apart.
hiṅgu "asafoetida" appears from the Mahābhārata on. EtymDB lists it as borrowed from Iranian, but neither MW nor CDIAL 14079 gives a derivation, so its origin is not settled. It lives on as Punjabi hiṅg and Hindi hīṅg (CDIAL 14079).

Lesson 4 of 5.
तिलः (tilaḥ) — sesame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for mustard, then the Sanskrit for asafoetida.

You'll want to know: तिलः.
तिलः — tilaḥ — "sesame".

The word, taken apart.
tila "sesame" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and is probably borrowed from Munda: CDIAL 5827 follows Kuiper and thinks a Dravidian source less likely. It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi til (CDIAL 5827).

Lesson 5 of 5.
चणकः (caṇakaḥ) — a chickpea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for asafoetida, then the Sanskrit for sesame.

You'll want to know: चणकः.
चणकः — caṇakaḥ — "a chickpea".

The word, taken apart.
caṇaka is caṇa "the chickpea" with the suffix -ka (CDIAL 4579). Neither MW nor CDIAL gives a derivation for caṇa, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi canā, caṇā (CDIAL 4579).
