# Practice — greeting the time of day

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-ZAOSHANGHAO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **zǎoshang hǎo**. Low, light, low.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-DIALOGUE-MORNING]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-ZAOSHANGHAO, ZH-LEX-ZAIJIAN, ZH-TONE-NEUTRAL] -->

> **A:** 早上好
>
> *zǎoshang hǎo* — good morning.
>
> **B:** 早上好
>
> *zǎoshang hǎo* — good morning.
>
> **A:** 再见
>
> *zàijiàn* — goodbye.
>
> **B:** 再见
>
> *zàijiàn* — goodbye.

Short, and for the first time you had a **choice** to make. 你好 would also have
been correct. 早上好 is the one that fits the hour, and choosing between them is
a thing a speaker does, not a thing a phrasebook does for you.

## What you've built — three tones, three behaviours
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-TONE-NEUTRAL, ZH-DIALOGUE-MORNING] -->

Line the greetings up and every tone behaviour you have been taught is visible at
once:

- **你好** — two third tones, side by side. The first **rises**: *ní hǎo*.
- **早上好** — two third tones, held apart by a light syllable. **Neither moves**.
- **再见** — two falling tones. **Neither moves.**

That middle line is the one that repays study. It is the same rule as the first
line, and it does not fire, because the condition it needs is not met. **A rule
you can only apply is half a rule; a rule you can also decline to apply is a
whole one.**

And none of it shows on the page. Every character in all three is written exactly
as it would be alone.
