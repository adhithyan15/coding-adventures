French, chapter 444: Les commerces de bouche.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la charcuterie — the pork butcher, the delicatessen; cold meats.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to salt, then the French for to clear of snow.

You'll want to know: la charcuterie.
la charcuterie — "the pork butcher, the delicatessen; cold meats".
À la charcuterie, nous achetons du jambon et du pâté. At the pork butcher's we buy ham and pâté.
De la charcuterie also means the cold meats themselves.

The word, taken apart.
charcuterie is built on charcutier, from chair cuite 'cooked meat'; chair goes back to Latin carō 'flesh' and cuite to coquere 'to cook' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le traiteur — the caterer; the deli (with ready meals).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pork butcher, then the French for the fishmonger’s.

You'll want to know: le traiteur.
le traiteur — "the caterer; the deli (with ready meals)".
Pour la fête, nous avons commandé des plats chez le traiteur. For the party we ordered dishes from the caterer.
A shop that sells ready-made dishes and caters for parties.

The word, taken apart.
traiteur is built on traiter 'to deal with, to serve food to', which is inherited from Latin tractāre 'to handle', from trahere 'to draw' (Lewis & Short).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: le primeur.
le primeur — "the greengrocer; early produce".
Chez le primeur, les fraises sont déjà arrivées. At the greengrocer's the strawberries are already in.
Les primeurs are the first fruit and vegetables of the year.

The word, taken apart.
primeur is built on the old adjective prime 'first', from Latin prīmus (Lewis & Short): the first produce of the year. EtymDB does not list it.
