Arabic, chapter 471: Bays and oceans.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خليج (khalīj) — a gulf; a bay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a riverbank, then the Arabic for sweet.

You'll want to know: خليج.
خليج — khalīj — "a gulf; a bay".
سبحنا في خليج صغير. sabaḥnā fī khalīj ṣaghīr. We swam in a small bay.

The word, taken apart.
خليج (khalīj) is built on the native Arabic root kh-l-j, "to draw away" (Lane). A khalīj was a channel drawn off a river, and then an arm of the sea.

Lesson 2 of 5.
محيط (muḥīṭ) — an ocean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for sweet, then the Arabic for a gulf.

You'll want to know: محيط.
محيط — muḥīṭ — "an ocean".
عبرت السفينة المحيط في شهر. ʿabarat as-safīna al-muḥīṭ fī shahr. The ship crossed the ocean in a month.

The word, taken apart.
محيط (muḥīṭ) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-w-ṭ, "to surround" (Lane). It is the Form IV participle: the sea that surrounds the land.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: تيار.
تيار — tayyār — "a current (of water or air)".
كان التيار في النهر قويا. kāna at-tayyār fī an-nahr qawiyyan. The current in the river was strong.
An electric current is tayyār kahrabāʾī.

The word, taken apart.
تيار (tayyār) is listed by Lane under t-y-r as the waves of a sea or a great river; the origin is not settled.

عمق (ʿumq) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-m-q, "to be deep" (Lane). It is the noun beside ʿamīq, "deep".
