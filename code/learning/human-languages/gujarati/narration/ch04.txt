Lesson 1 of 5.
મળીશું (maḷīshũ) — "(we) will meet".

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called The letters in this word until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A future-tense verb — the other half of "see you again."

The letters in this word.
મ (ma) + ળી (ḷī, with the retroflex ળ ḷ) + શું (shũ) becomes મળીશું (maḷīshũ).

The word, taken apart.
મળીશું (maḷīshũ, "we will meet") is the future of મળવું (maḷvũ, "to meet"), from Sanskrit મિલ્ (mil-, "to join, unite") — the same root as Hindi milnā and Punjabi milṇā. Note the retroflex ળ (ḷ): Gujarati keeps this extra letter — like Marathi and the Dravidian south, and unlike Hindi. The ending -શું (-shũ) is the "we will" future.

Grammar Lens: the future is an ending.
Gujarati marks the future with an ending on the stem: maḷ- ("meet") + -īshũ ("we will") becomes maḷīshũ. No helper-word like English "will."

Lesson 2 of 5.
કાલે મળીશું (kāle maḷīshũ) — "see you tomorrow".

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called The letters in this word until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A goodbye for tomorrow — built on a word that means both tomorrow and yesterday.

The letters in this word.
કા (kā) + લે (le) becomes કાલે (kāle, "on [the] kāl"). The base is કાલ (kāl).

The word, taken apart.
કાલ (kāl) means both "tomorrow" and "yesterday" — the day on either side of today — from Sanskrit કાલ (kāla, "time"). (It is the very same kāla behind Hindi's kal, Bengali's kāl, and Punjabi's akāl "timeless.") The verb's tense tells which: kāle maḷīshũ (future becomes "tomorrow we'll meet"), kāle maḷyā (past becomes "we met yesterday"). So કાલે મળીશું = "tomorrow we'll meet" = "see you tomorrow."

Lesson 3 of 5.
પાછા (pāchhā) — "again, back".

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called The letters in this word until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One small word turns a goodbye into "till next time." You already have the other half — āvjo ("come again") — from Chapter 1.

The letters in this word.
પા (pā) + છા (chhā) becomes પાછા (pāchhā).

The word, taken apart.
પાછા (pāchhā, "back, again, returning") is from Sanskrit પશ્ચાત્ (paścāt, "behind, afterward") — the same "behind/after" root behind English post- words, distantly. It carries the sense of return: pāchhā āvvũ ("to come back"), pāchhā jāvũ ("to go back"). Add it to "we'll meet" and you get the warm goodbye of the next lesson.

Lesson 4 of 5.
પાછા મળીશું (pāchhā maḷīshũ) — "we'll meet again".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The warm, explicit goodbye — the companion of Chapter 1's āvjo.

The phrase, taken apart.
પાછા મળીશું = પાછા (pāchhā, "again") + મળીશું (maḷīshũ, "we will meet") — "we'll meet again." Where Chapter 1's આવજો (āvjo) is the quick, warm "come again," pāchhā maḷīshũ spells out the promise: we will meet once more. It is the same "promise of return" the whole subcontinent prefers (Hindi phir milenge, Bengali āshi, Tamil pōy varugiṟēṉ).
