Lesson 1 of 5.
りんご (ringo) — an apple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for vegetables, then the Japanese for fruit.

You'll want to know: りんご.
りんご — ringo — "an apple".

The word, taken apart.
りんご (ringo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are rin, "forest" (Middle Chinese lim) and go, "a small red apple" (gim) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.

You'll want to know: みかん.
みかん — mikan — "a mandarin orange".

The word, taken apart.
みかん (mikan) is Sino-Japanese: two characters borrowed from Chinese, mitsu, "honey" (Middle Chinese mjit), and kan, "tangerine, citrus" (Middle Chinese kam), with mitsu shortened to mi (JMdict; Unihan).

Lesson 3 of 5.
いちご (ichigo) — a strawberry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an apple, then the Japanese for a mandarin orange.

You'll want to know: いちご.
いちご — ichigo — "a strawberry".

The word, taken apart.
いちご (ichigo) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
