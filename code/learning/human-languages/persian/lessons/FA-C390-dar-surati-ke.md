## You'll want to know: در صورتی که
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-C390-SHART-02]; assesses=[] -->

**در صورتی که** (*dar surati ke*) — "in the event that, if".

> در صورتی که باران بیاید، در خانه میمانیم.
>
> *dar surati ke bârân biyâyad, dar khâne mimânim.*
>
> If it rains, we will stay at home.

It takes the subjunctive, like *agar*, and sounds more formal. With the indicative it can also mean "whereas".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**در صورتی که** is literally "in a form that": *surat* comes from Arabic *ṣūra* "form, image" (EtymDB), here a state of affairs, and *dar* "in" and *ke* "that" are Persian.
