6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tānwèi — market stall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take the long way round, then the Mandarin for the pavement beside the road.

You'll want to know: tānwèi.
tānwèi — "market stall".
zhège tānwèi mài shuǐguǒ — This stall sells fruit.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tānwèi is built from tān 'to spread out' and wèi 'a place', both inherited from Middle Chinese, than and hwijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to open out' and the second as a place in a row.

Lesson 2 of 6.
市场 (shìchǎng) — market; marketplace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the pavement beside the road, then the Mandarin for a market stall.

You'll want to know: 市场.
市场 — shìchǎng — "market; marketplace".
去市场怎么走 qù shìchǎng zěnme zǒu — How do I get to the market?
The new character, in pieces: 市 (shì) — five strokes: a dot, a long bar, then a short vertical, a stroke that turns down and hooks, and a long vertical through the middle. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
市场 (shìchǎng) is built from 市 (shì) 'a market' and 场 'an open ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyiX and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, as where people go to buy and sell, and the second as a threshing floor.

Lesson 3 of 6.
市 (shì) — a dot and a bar over three strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a dot and a bar over three strokes.
市 (shì).
One piece, five strokes, top to bottom.
A dot on top, then a long bar, as at the top of 方.
Under the bar, three strokes like a small hanging cloth. A short vertical on the left. A stroke that runs right and turns down, hooking to the left at the foot. Then a long vertical through the middle, from the bar down past the hook.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 市 (shì), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 市 (shì)? (Five.) What comes first? (A dot.) What is the last stroke? (The long vertical through the middle.)

市区 — shìqū — "the built-up part of a city; downtown".
wǒmen zhù zài shìqū de yì jiā jiǔdiàn — We're staying at a hotel in town.

The word, taken apart.
市区 (shìqū) is built from 市 (shì) 'a market town' and 区 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyiX and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
