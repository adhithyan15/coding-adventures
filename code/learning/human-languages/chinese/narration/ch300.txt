Lesson 1 of 6.
dìzhèn — earthquake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pass out, then the Mandarin for to fight to save a patient.

You'll want to know: dìzhèn.
dìzhèn — "earthquake".
zhèr yǐqián yǒu guo dìzhèn — There was once an earthquake here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dìzhèn is built from 地 'the ground' and zhèn 'to shake', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dijH and tsyinH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'thunder' and 'to move': the ground shaking.

Lesson 2 of 6.
死 (sǐ) — to die.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to fight to save a patient, then the Mandarin for an earthquake.

You'll want to know: 死.
死 — sǐ — "to die".
我的花都死了 wǒ de huā dōu sǐ le — All my flowers have died.
The new character, in pieces: 死 (sǐ) — six strokes: a long bar on top; under it on the left the evening piece of 外, three strokes; on the right a short sweep and a vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up, as on the right of 比. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
死 (sǐ) is inherited from Middle Chinese sijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older dictionary for it: 'to be used up', what a person parts from.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a long bar over two small pieces.
死 (sǐ).
Three pieces: a bar on top, then two side by side under it.
The bar, one stroke, long, across the top.
Under it on the left, three strokes. The evening piece on the left of 外: a short sweep, a bar that turns into a long sweep to the lower left, and a dot.
On the right, two strokes. The right half of 比: a short sweep down to the left, then a vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up.
1 + 3 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 死 (sǐ), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 死 (sǐ)? (Six.) What comes first? (The long bar on top.) Where have you made the right piece? (On the right of 比.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
shēngmìng — the life of a living thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an earthquake, then the Mandarin for to die.

You'll want to know: shēngmìng.
shēngmìng — "the life of a living thing".
měi ge rén de shēngmìng dōu hěn zhòngyào — Every person's life matters.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shēngmìng is built from 生 'to live' and mìng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and mjaengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to grow' and the second 'to order'; its sense 'a life' is not given there.

You'll want to know: bàozhà.
bàozhà — "to explode; to blow up".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàozhà is built from bào 'to burst' and zhà 'to blow apart'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese paewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'fire cracking'; the Guangyun has no entry for the second, so its history is not settled.
