Latin, chapter 522: Pale, red and thin.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
palleō, pallēre — to be pale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for weak, then the Latin for weakness.

You'll want to know: palleō, pallēre.
palleō, pallēre — "to be pale".
Ancilla timōre pallet. The maid is pale with fear.
palleō, pallēre, palluī. English pallor comes from its noun.

The word, taken apart.
palleō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "grey, pale" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English pallor comes from the noun built on it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pallidus, pallida, pallidum — pale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for weakness, then the Latin for to be pale.

You'll want to know: pallidus, pallida, pallidum.
pallidus, pallida, pallidum — "pale".
Post morbum puella pallida est. After her illness the girl is pale.
From palleō. English pallid, and pale through French.

The word, taken apart.
pallidus is built on palleō "to be pale" with -idus (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English pallid comes from it, and pale came the same way through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
rubeō, rubēre — to be red.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be pale, then the Latin for pale.

You'll want to know: rubeō, rubēre.
rubeō, rubēre — "to be red".
Genae puerī rubent. The boy's cheeks are red.
rubeō, rubēre, rubuī, from ruber — red. Ērubēscō, already known, is to turn red.

The word, taken apart.
rubeō belongs with ruber "red", inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "red" (EtymDB). English ruby and rubicund come from the same family, and English red is a cousin.
