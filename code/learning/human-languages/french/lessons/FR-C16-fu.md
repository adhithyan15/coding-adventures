# je fus — the second stem

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The past of this verb does not begin with an **s**. That is not a
sound change. It is a different verb.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-ETRE-FU-08]; assesses=[] -->

The *passé simple* of *être* — the literary past you learned to recognise — is
**je fus**, **il fut**.

There is no way to get *fus* from *suis*, and nobody has ever tried. It comes
from Latin **fuī**, and *fuī* comes from Proto-Indo-European **\*bʰuH-**, "to
grow, to become."

That root went somewhere you will recognise. **\*bʰuH-** is also the source of
English **be**. So English's *be* and French's *fus* are the same ancient word,
and neither of them is related to French *suis* — which is the cousin of English
*is* and *am*.

English has exactly the same patchwork, and nobody notices because it is their
own: **am** and **is** from one root, **be** from another, **was** and **were**
from a third. French is doing what English does. It is only visible here because
it is somebody else's language.

related to *suis*? (**No** — different root entirely.) Which Latin word is it
from? (***fuī***.) Which English word comes from the same root? (**be**.) Does
English do the same thing? (**Yes** — *am*/*is*, *be*, and *was* come from
