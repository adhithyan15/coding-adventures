Lesson 1 of 5.
पक्षः (pakṣaḥ) — a wing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a creature, then the Sanskrit for a winged creature, a bird.

You'll want to know: पक्षः.
पक्षः — pakṣaḥ — "a wing".

The word, taken apart.
pakṣa is a Rigvedic word for "wing, feather, side" (CDIAL 7627). From the side of a body came the side of an argument and a party, and also each half of the lunar month. It lives on as Hindi pākh, "wing of a bird".

Lesson 2 of 5.
पुच्छः (pucchaḥ) — a tail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a winged creature, a bird, then the Sanskrit for a wing.

You'll want to know: पुच्छः.
पुच्छः — pucchaḥ — "a tail".

The word, taken apart.
puccha is the tail or hinder part, a word of the Atharvaveda (CDIAL 8249). MW gives no derivation, and CDIAL only compares piccha, "tail feather", so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Gujarati pũch, "tail".

Lesson 3 of 5.
शृंगम् (śṛṅgam) — a horn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wing, then the Sanskrit for a tail.

You'll want to know: शृंगम्.
शृंगम् — śṛṅgam — "a horn".

The word, taken apart.
śṛṅga is the horn of an animal, and also a mountain peak (MW). MW suggests it may be connected with śiras, "head", and compares Latin cornū and English horn.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: चञ्चुः.
चञ्चुः — cañcuḥ — "a beak".

The word, taken apart.
cañcu is probably borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 4569 follows Mayrhofer in taking it from a Dravidian source. It lives on as Bengali cãuc, "beak".

Lesson 5 of 5.
गृध्रः (gṛdhraḥ) — a vulture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a horn, then the Sanskrit for a beak.

You'll want to know: गृध्रः.
गृध्रः — gṛdhraḥ — "a vulture".

The word, taken apart.
gṛdhra is built on the root √gṛdh, "to be greedy" (MW): the vulture is the greedy bird. Both senses are in the Rigveda (CDIAL 4233), and it lives on as Hindi gīdh, "vulture".
