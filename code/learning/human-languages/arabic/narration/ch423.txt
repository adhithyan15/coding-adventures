Arabic, chapter 423: At the gate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بوابة (bawwāba) — a gate; a gateway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sentence, then the Arabic for a barber.

You'll want to know: بوابة.
بوابة — bawwāba — "a gate; a gateway".
انتظر المسافرون عند البوابة. intaẓara al-musāfirūn ʿinda al-bawwāba. The travellers waited at the gate.

The word, taken apart.
بوابة (bawwāba) is built on the native Arabic root b-w-b, "a door" (Lane). It shares its root with bāb, "a door"; Lane gives bawwāb, "a door-keeper", on the same root, and modern Arabic uses bawwāba for a gate or a gateway.

You'll want to know: مقعد.
مقعد — maqʿad — "a seat".
جلست في مقعد بجانب النافذة. jalastu fī maqʿad bi-jānib an-nāfidha. I sat in a seat beside the window.
Plural: مقاعد, maqāʿid.
The pattern mafʿal names a place: maqʿad is the place where one sits.

The word, taken apart.
مقعد (maqʿad) is built on the native Arabic root q-ʿ-d, "to sit down" (Lane).

Lesson 3 of 5.
حجز (ḥajaza) — to reserve; to book.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a gate, then the Arabic for a seat.

You'll want to know: حجز.
حجز — ḥajaza — "to reserve; to book".
حجزت مقعدا في القطار. ḥajaztu maqʿadan fī al-qiṭār. I booked a seat on the train.
Present: يحجز, yaḥjiz.
Lane gives the present both as yaḥjizu and yaḥjuzu. The noun ḥajz is a booking.

The word, taken apart.
حجز (ḥajaza) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-j-z, "to hold back, to withhold" (Lane). A booking holds a place back for someone. It is the plain verb.

بدأ الصعود بعد التأخير. badaʾa aṣ-ṣuʿūd baʿda at-taʾkhīr. Boarding began after the delay.

صعود (ṣuʿūd) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-ʿ-d, "to go up, to ascend" (Lane). It is the verbal noun of ṣaʿida, "to go up".

Lesson 5 of 5.
مدرج (madraj) — a runway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to reserve, then the Arabic for boarding.

You'll want to know: مدرج.
مدرج — madraj — "a runway".
كانت الرحلة تنتظر على المدرج. kānat ar-riḥla tantaẓiru ʿalā al-madraj. The flight was waiting on the runway.
Plural: مدارج, madārij.

The word, taken apart.
مدرج (madraj) is built on the native Arabic root d-r-j, "to go step by step" (Lane). Lane gives madraj as a way or route one goes along; modern Arabic gave the word the runway.
