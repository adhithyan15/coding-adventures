Italian, chapter 475: Che spavento!.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lo spavento — fright, scare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to get irritated, then the Italian for to huff.

You'll want to know: lo spavento.
lo spavento — "fright, scare".
Che spavento! Credevo di aver perso le chiavi. What a fright! I thought I had lost the keys.
Prendersi uno spavento — to get a fright.

The word, taken apart.
spavento comes from spaventare "to frighten", usually traced to Vulgar Latin expaventāre, built on Latin pavēre "to be afraid" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
spaventarsi — to get frightened.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to huff, then the Italian for fright.

You'll want to know: spaventarsi.
spaventarsi — "to get frightened".
Il cane si spaventa quando sente i tuoni. The dog gets frightened when it hears thunder.
Without si, spaventare is to frighten someone: mi hai spaventato!

The word, taken apart.
spaventarsi is the reflexive of spaventare "to frighten", usually traced to Vulgar Latin expaventāre, from Latin pavēre "to be afraid" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il panico — panic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for fright, then the Italian for to get frightened.

You'll want to know: il panico.
il panico — "panic".
Non farti prendere dal panico, troviamo una soluzione. Do not panic, we will find a solution.
Farsi prendere dal panico — to panic.

The word, taken apart.
panico comes through Latin pānicus from Greek panikós (EtymDB), an adjective made from the name of a woodland god whose voice was said to fill travellers with sudden fear.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il sollievo — relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to get frightened, then the Italian for panic.

You'll want to know: il sollievo.
il sollievo — "relief".
Che sollievo, sei arrivato! What a relief, you have arrived!
Tirare un sospiro di sollievo — to breathe a sigh of relief.

The word, taken apart.
sollievo is built on sollevare "to lift up", which is inherited from Latin sublevāre "to lift from below" (Lewis & Short), from levāre "to raise".

Lesson 5 of 5.
tranquillizzarsi — to calm down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for panic, then the Italian for relief.

You'll want to know: tranquillizzarsi.
tranquillizzarsi — "to calm down".
Respira piano e tranquillizzati. Breathe slowly and calm down.
Built on tranquillo. To calm someone else: tranquillizzare qualcuno.

The word, taken apart.
tranquillizzarsi is built on tranquillo "calm", a learned borrowing from Latin tranquillus "calm, still" (Lewis & Short), with the verb ending -izzare.
