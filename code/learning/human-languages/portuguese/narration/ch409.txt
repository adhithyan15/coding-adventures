Portuguese, chapter 409: Acidentes e filas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o engarrafamento — the traffic jam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the speed camera, then the Portuguese for to inspect.

You'll want to know: o engarrafamento.
o engarrafamento — "the traffic jam".
Ficámos uma hora no engarrafamento. We were stuck in the traffic jam for an hour.
Literally a 'bottling'. In Brazil also o congestionamento.

The word, taken apart.
engarrafamento is built on engarrafar 'to bottle, to put in a bottle' with -mento (EtymDB): traffic squeezed as if through a bottleneck.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: abrandar.
abrandar — "to slow down".
Os carros abrandam perto da escola. The cars slow down near the school.
Also of rain or wind: a chuva abrandou.

The word, taken apart.
abrandar is built from a-, brando 'soft, gentle' and -ar (EtymDB); brando is inherited from Latin blandus 'gentle, pleasant'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: a colisão.
a colisão — "the collision".
Houve uma colisão entre dois carros na ponte. There was a collision between two cars on the bridge.
A formal word, common in the news.

The word, taken apart.
colisão is a learned borrowing from Latin collīsiō (EtymDB), from collīdere 'to strike together' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
embater — to crash into, to hit.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: embater.
embater — "to crash into, to hit".
A mota embateu num carro parado. The motorbike crashed into a parked car.
Embater em — to crash into.

The word, taken apart.
embater is built from em- and bater 'to hit', which is inherited from Late Latin battere, Latin battuere 'to beat' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o desvio — the diversion, the detour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: o desvio.
o desvio — "the diversion, the detour".
Por causa das obras, há um desvio pela aldeia. Because of the roadworks, there is a diversion through the village.
The verb is desviar, to divert or to turn aside.

The word, taken apart.
desvio is built on desviar 'to turn aside', which is inherited from Latin dēviāre, on via 'way' (EtymDB).
