French, chapter 277: Étape par étape.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'étape — the step, the leg; a stop on a journey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the era, then the French for the length.

You'll want to know: l'étape.
l'étape — "the step, the leg; a stop on a journey".
La première étape du voyage était la plus longue. The first leg of the journey was the longest.
Also a stop on a trip: faire étape — to stop over.

The word, taken apart.
étape comes from Old French estaple "a market, a place to unload goods", which was borrowed from Middle Dutch stapel "a pile, a depot" (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: la phase.
la phase — "the phase".
Le projet entre dans sa dernière phase. The project is entering its last phase.
Also the phases of the moon: les phases de la lune.

The word, taken apart.
phase is a learned borrowing from Greek phásis "appearance", said first of the moon (EtymDB lists the French word without a parent).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: la chronologie.
la chronologie — "the chronology, the order of events".
Le policier a refait la chronologie de la soirée. The police officer went back over the order of events that evening.
Par ordre chronologique — in order of time.

The word, taken apart.
chronologie is a learned word built from Greek khrónos "time" and -logía "account, study" (EtymDB lists the French word without a parent).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'instant — the instant, the moment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the phase, then the French for the chronology.

You'll want to know: l'instant.
l'instant — "the instant, the moment".
Pendant un instant, personne n'a parlé. For a moment, nobody spoke.
Pour l'instant — for now; à l'instant — a moment ago.

The word, taken apart.
instant is a learned borrowing from Latin īnstāns "present, pressing" (EtymDB), from īnstāre "to stand near, to press on" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
à l'issue de — at the end of, following.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the chronology, then the French for the instant.

You'll want to know: à l'issue de.
à l'issue de — "at the end of, following".
À l'issue du match, les joueurs ont salué le public. At the end of the match, the players waved to the crowd.
Formal and frequent in news reports. L'issue alone is the way out, or the outcome.

The word, taken apart.
issue is the old past participle of Old French issir "to go out", which is inherited from Latin exīre "to go out" (Lewis & Short).
