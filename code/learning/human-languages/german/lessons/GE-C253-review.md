# Review — the journey, retold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DANN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-DANN-VERB-ZWEI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "then I read the message". (*Dann las ich die Nachricht.*)
And "in the end it was evening". (*Schließlich war es Abend.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SCHLIESSLICH-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ERZAEHL-GERUEST-02, GE-SKILL-READ-NARRATIVE-01] -->

Two chapters, and a German story is yours to tell. The scene in the
Präteritum: *Es war Samstag, und es regnete.* The steps, each with its verb
straight after it: *zuerst*, *dann*, *danach*, *schließlich*. One moment
marked with *als*. And a last line about how it felt.

[PAUSE 2s] Now tell the passage back, without reading it. It does not have
to be word for word. It has to be in order, and every step word has to
pull its verb in behind it.

- [YOU SAY: the scene: *Es war Samstag, und es regnete.*]
- [YOU SAY: the waiting: *Ich wartete, aber der Zug hatte Verspätung.*]

- [YOU SAY: *Als der Zug endlich kam, war ich sehr müde.*]
- [YOU SAY: *Danach kam ich in Berlin an.* Then one more line of your own about the grandmother]
- [YOU SAY: the end: *Schließlich war es Abend.*]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DANN-01, GE-LEX-SCHLIESSLICH-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ERZAEHL-GERUEST-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The four step words, in order? (***Zuerst, dann, danach,
schließlich.***) After each one, what comes next? (**The verb.**) Which one
closes the story? (***Schließlich.***)
