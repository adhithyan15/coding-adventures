# giorno — "day," and the road to English *journey*

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Your first noun — masculine, and a cousin of *journal* and *journey*.

## Sounds you'll need

- **gi** = the *j* of *jam*; **giorno** = *JOR-no*.

## The word, taken apart

**il giorno** (masc.) ← Latin **diurnum** ("daytime"), from **dies** ("day").
That detour through *diurnum* is why *giorno* looks nothing like *dies*:

- via *diurnum*: **jour**nal, ad**journ**, so**journ**, **journ**ey (a *day's*
  travel);
- via *dies*: **di**urnal, **di**ary.

Italian *giorno* and French *jour* are near-twins here; Spanish, taking *dies*
directly, got the very different *día*.

## Grammar Lens: masculine, plural by vowel-change

*giorno* is **masculine** (*il giorno*). Plural **i giorni** — Italian marks the
plural by changing the final vowel (*-o* → *-i*), not by adding *-s*.
