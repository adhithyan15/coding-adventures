# āpko / use — the paradigm closes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *hamẽ* and *tumhẽ*. Two corners of the set are filled. Say the
respectful one and the third-person one, and notice you cannot yet.

## You'll want to know: āpko and use
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04]; assesses=[] -->

**आपको** (*āpko*) — "to you," respectfully.
*use* — "to him, to her."

These two are built differently from the last pair. Instead of the nasal ending
they take a postposition, *ko*, meaning *to*:

> **आप** + **को** → **आपको**

*Use* is the worn-down version of the same thing: *us* + *ko*, squeezed
together by centuries of use, exactly as *maiṅ* + *ko* became *mujhe*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**आपको / उसे** (*āpko / use*) is *āp*, "you", with *ko*, "to", and *us*, "him, her", with the ending *-e*. *āp* comes from Sanskrit *ātman*, "self" (CDIAL 1135); CDIAL lists *us* under *asau*, "that" (CDIAL 972).

## Grammar Lens: the whole set, at last
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-02] -->

| subject | oblique |
|---|---|
| मैं | *mujhe* |
| हम | हमें |
| तुम | तुम्हें |
| आप | **आपको** |
| वह | *use* |

Five forms, and only the first and the last look irregular — because they are
the two that wore down. The middle three still show their parts.

**वे** has one too — *unhẽ* — built the same way, which the review lesson adds.

Note that the level you learned to choose in the possessive and imperative
chapters is here too: *tumhẽ* to a friend, *āpko* to a stranger. The same
decision, for the fourth time.
