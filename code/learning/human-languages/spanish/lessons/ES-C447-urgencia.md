# la urgencia — something pressing on you

## You'll want to know: la urgencia
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-21] -->

**la urgencia** — urgency, an emergency.

**Las urgencias**, in the plural, is **the emergency department**. The sign over
the door of a Spanish hospital says *URGENCIAS*, and *ir a urgencias* is to go
to A&E.

The adjective is **urgente**: *es urgente* — it is urgent. On an envelope or an
email subject line, *URGENTE* does the same work it does in English.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-03, ES-LEX-C333-QUIET-29] -->

Latin **urgēre**, **to press, to push hard**. An urgency is something **pressing
on you**, and English says the same thing when it calls a matter *pressing*.

And you already have a second Latin word for pressing. **La prisa** is from
*premere*, also to press — the root of English *press* itself.

| word | what presses |
|---|---|
| **la prisa** | *premere* — you are pressed for time |
| **la urgencia** | *urgēre* — the thing presses on you |

The difference is which way the pressure runs. *Tengo prisa* puts it on you:
*I* am in a hurry. *Es urgente* puts it on the matter: the thing itself is
pushing. You can have *prisa* about something that is not *urgente* at all, and
a genuinely *urgente* matter can find you with no *prisa* whatever.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-03] -->

The ending is one you have met. **-encia** turns a verb into the state of doing
it, the way *experiencia* did in the chapter on paperwork.

| verb | the state |
|---|---|
| urgir | la urgencia |
| existir | la existencia |
| preferir | la preferencia |

So an unfamiliar noun in **-encia** will nearly
always be hiding a verb, and the noun names *the condition of that verb
happening*. That is two endings now —
**-ción** for the act and **-encia** for the state — and between them they cover
a large share of the abstract nouns on any Spanish form.
