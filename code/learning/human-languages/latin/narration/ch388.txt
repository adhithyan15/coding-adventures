Latin, chapter 388: Towns and Associations.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
mūniceps, mūnicipis — a citizen of a free town; a fellow townsman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for stormy, then the Latin for to stir up.

You'll want to know: mūniceps, mūnicipis.
mūniceps, mūnicipis — "a citizen of a free town; a fellow townsman".
Ille mūniceps meus est. He comes from my home town.
mūniceps, mūnicipis m. and f.: one who took on the duties of a self-governing Italian town.

The word, taken apart.
mūniceps is built from mūnus "a duty, a service" and capiō "to take" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): one who takes on the town's duties.

decuriō, decuriōnis m. In a town the decuriōnēs formed the council; in the army a decuriō led ten horsemen. Built on decem, ten.

sodālis, sodālis m. and f. Members of a club, a guild or a priestly brotherhood were sodālēs.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of sodālis is not settled: Lewis & Short compare it with a root meaning "one's own way, custom", but give no sure account.
