French, chapter 350: Les répercussions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'impact — the impact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to influence, then the French for to generate.

You'll want to know: l'impact.
l'impact — "the impact".
Ce projet a un impact sur tout le quartier. This project has an impact on the whole neighbourhood.
Avoir un impact sur — to have an effect on. The final ct is said. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
impact comes from Latin impāctus, the participle of impingere "to dash against, to strike" (Lewis & Short), built on pangere "to fix, to drive in".

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la répercussion — the repercussion, the knock-on effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to generate, then the French for the impact.

You'll want to know: la répercussion.
la répercussion — "the repercussion, the knock-on effect".
Cette décision aura des répercussions sur les prix. This decision will have repercussions on prices.
Mostly in the plural: des répercussions.

The word, taken apart.
répercussion is a learned borrowing from Latin repercussiō "a rebounding" (Lewis & Short), from repercutere "to strike back", built on quatere "to shake, to strike".

Lesson 3 of 5.
déclencher — to set off, to trigger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the impact, then the French for the repercussion.

You'll want to know: déclencher.
déclencher — "to set off, to trigger".
Une petite remarque a déclenché la colère de mon père. A small remark set off my father's anger.
Of alarms too: l'alarme s'est déclenchée.

The word, taken apart.
déclencher is built from dé- and clenche "latch", which comes from a Frankish klinka "latch" (EtymDB): you lift the latch and things start moving.

Lesson 4 of 5.
susciter — to arouse, to give rise to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the repercussion, then the French for to set off.

You'll want to know: susciter.
susciter — "to arouse, to give rise to".
Sa décision a suscité beaucoup de questions. Her decision raised a lot of questions.
Formal: susciter la curiosité — to arouse curiosity.

The word, taken apart.
susciter is a learned borrowing from Latin suscitāre "to lift up, to rouse" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on citāre "to set in motion".

You'll want to know: le motif.
le motif — "the motive, the reason; the pattern".
Quel est le motif de votre visite? What is the reason for your visit?
Also a pattern on cloth: un motif à fleurs.

The word, taken apart.
motif comes from Late Latin mōtīvus "serving to move" (EtymDB), from movēre "to move" (Lewis & Short): what moves you to act.
