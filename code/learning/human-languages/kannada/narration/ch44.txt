Kannada, chapter 44: Verbs of Seeing and Saying.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ನೋಡು (nōḍu) — look.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four verbs for taking things in and putting them out, one at a time.

You'll want to know: ನೋಡು.
ನೋಡು (nōḍu) — "look".
Also 'to see' and, in the right sentence, 'to look after'. One stem covering watching, seeing and caring for is common across the Dravidian languages.

The word, taken apart.
ನೋಡು (nōḍu) is native Dravidian. DEDR 3794 lists it with Malayalam nōṭṭam, "viewing", and Tamil nōṭṭam, "examination".

Lesson 2 of 4.
ಕೇಳು (kēḷu) — listen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nōḍu mean?

You'll want to know: ಕೇಳು.
ಕೇಳು (kēḷu) — "listen".
And 'to ask'. Kannada uses the same stem for taking in words and for requesting them, which is less strange than it sounds — both are aimed at what someone else knows.

The word, taken apart.
ಕೇಳು (kēḷu) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil kēḷ and Malayalam kēḷkka (DEDR 2017).

Lesson 3 of 4.
ಮಾತಾಡು (mātāḍu) — speak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kēḷu mean?

You'll want to know: ಮಾತಾಡು.
ಮಾತಾಡು (mātāḍu) — "speak".
Built from māta, 'word' — a native Dravidian word, not a Sanskrit one — plus āḍu, 'to play'. To speak is to play with words.
DEDR 4834 lists Kannada mātu, māta, "word, saying", cognate with Telugu māṭa; āḍu is in DEDR 347.

Lesson 4 of 4.
ಬರೆ (bare) — write.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mātāḍu mean?

You'll want to know: ಬರೆ.
ಬರೆ (bare) — "write".
An old Dravidian stem, unrelated to the Sanskrit words for writing. It is the same root behind baraha, 'writing'.

The word, taken apart.
ಬರೆ (bare) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil vari and Malayalam vara (DEDR 5263). DEDR 5263 compares, with a query, Prakrit rajju, "a writer".
