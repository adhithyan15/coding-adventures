Spanish, chapter 238: Estoy Hablando --- The Payoff.
1 lesson. All 1 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 1.
estoy hablando — the payoff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Estar, all the way through: estoy, estás, está, estamos, están.
[pause 1 second]
And one gerund. Put them side by side.

Grammar Lens: standing in the middle of doing.
Estoy hablando. — I am speaking, right now.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
Estoy comiendo., I'm eating.
Está trabajando., he's working.
Estamos estudiando., we're studying.
[pause 2 seconds]
Only estar changes. The gerund sits there, the same shape for everybody, which is why the whole construction costs you one verb you already knew and one ending.

Why it's said this way.
It had to be estar rather than ser, and you already know why. Estar is the verb for how something temporarily stands. An action caught mid-stride is as temporary as things get.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now the difference from English, and it is worth having straight. English uses I'm + -ing for the future all the time: I'm eating with her tomorrow. Spanish will not.
Estoy comiendo — I am eating, at this moment, food in hand.
[pause 2 seconds]
For tomorrow, Spanish reaches for the form you learned at chapter 49: voy a comer. So the two languages look identical here and are not: the Spanish progressive means now, and only now.
