# പോലീസ് (pōlīsŭ) — the police

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C112-BANK-01, ML-CONCEPT-C34-SAHAAYIKKUKA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *a bank*. Say *to help*. You have had the verb for helping since
long before this chapter; you have had the bank for about a minute.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C112-POLICE-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C112-BANK-01] -->

**പോലീസ്** (*pōlīsŭ*) — **the police**.

One word covers the force and its officers alike, so you do not need a second
word to point at a person.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C112-POLICE-01, ML-LEX-C112-BANK-01] -->

This is the same story as the bank, one word later. English *police* came in
whole, and the letters trace the English sounds.

Two borrowings in two lessons is not a coincidence. Both name something that
came to Kerala as an institution with paperwork and a building, and a thing that
arrives already named tends to keep the name it came with.

So the pattern now has two members you can point at, and you should expect more.
When a word looks long and sounds oddly English as you sound it out, trust that
ear before you reach for a dictionary.
