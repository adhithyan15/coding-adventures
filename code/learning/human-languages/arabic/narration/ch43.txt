Lesson 1 of 1.
My own line — the first writing with no model.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Everything you have written so far had something to reach for.
[pause 1 second]
This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed: only what this book has taught you.

Writing — controlled composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write one line about yourself, right to left.
[pause 2 seconds]
And here is the extra decision Arabic asks for that most scripts do not. A letter does not have one shape. It has up to four, and which one you write depends on where in the word it stands — alone, at the start, in the middle, at the end.
[pause 2 seconds]
Copying hid that from you: the model already showed the right shape. Choosing your own words means choosing the shapes too, and a letter you have only ever copied in the middle of a word is a letter you may not yet be able to start one with.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the line. Then read it back and check the joins before you check anything else — a letter that should have connected and did not is the error a reader notices first.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many shapes can one Arabic letter have? (Up to four.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And what decides which one you write? (Where it stands in the word.)
