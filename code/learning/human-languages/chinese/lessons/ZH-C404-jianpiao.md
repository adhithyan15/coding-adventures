## You'll want to know: 检票
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C404-TICKET404-02]; assesses=[] -->

**检票** — *jiǎnpiào* — "to check tickets".

> **现在检票了** *xiànzài jiǎnpiào le* — They're checking tickets now.

The new character, in pieces: 检 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the tree piece, four strokes, as on the left of 机; on the right a long stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right, like a roof, then a short bar, two dots and a short stroke falling to the left, and a long bar across the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**检票** (*jiǎnpiào*) is built from 检 'to check' and 票 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese *kjemX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the seal on a document and 'to check'; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.
