Bengali, chapter 494: Looking for Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
বেকার (bekār) — unemployed; out of work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an office messenger, then the Bengali for to type.

You'll want to know: বেকার.
বেকার — bekār — "unemployed; out of work".
আমার ভাই এক বছর ধরে বেকার। Āmār bhāi ek bôchhôr dhôre bekār. My brother has been out of work for a year.

The word, taken apart.
বেকার (bekār) is usually traced to Persian bekār, "without work": be-, "without", and kār, "work".

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

You'll want to know: শিক্ষানবিশ.
শিক্ষানবিশ — shikkhānôbish — "an apprentice; a trainee".
আমি একটা কারখানায় শিক্ষানবিশ হলাম। Āmi ekṭā kārkhānāy shikkhānôbish hôlām. I became an apprentice at a factory.

The word, taken apart.
শিক্ষানবিশ (shikkhānôbish) joins Sanskrit śikṣā, "learning" (Monier-Williams), to an ending probably from Persian navīs, "one who writes": a learner at a desk.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: যোগ দেওয়া.
যোগ দেওয়া — jog deoyā — "to join a job; to join (a group)".
আমি সোমবার নতুন অফিসে যোগ দিলাম। Āmi sombār nôtun ôphise jog dilām. I joined the new office on Monday.
Past (he/she …): তিনি নতুন কোম্পানিতে যোগ দিলেন, tini nôtun kompānite jog dilen.

The word, taken apart.
যোগ দেওয়া (jog deoyā) joins যোগ, Sanskrit yoga, "joining" (Monier-Williams), to দেওয়া, "to give": to give oneself to a place of work.
