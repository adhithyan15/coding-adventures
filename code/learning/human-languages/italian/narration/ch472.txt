Italian, chapter 472: Pregi e difetti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
testardo — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for chatterbox, then the Italian for to blush.

You'll want to know: testardo.
testardo — "stubborn".
Mio nonno è testardo: non cambia mai idea. My grandfather is stubborn: he never changes his mind.
From testa "head": someone with a hard head. Avere la testa dura means the same.

The word, taken apart.
testardo is built on testa "head", which is inherited from Latin testa "an earthen pot, a shell" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); its later sense "skull" became the everyday word for head.

generoso — "generous".
È una persona generosa e aiuta sempre gli amici. She is a generous person and always helps her friends.
Of food: una porzione generosa — a big helping.

The word, taken apart.
generoso is a learned borrowing from Latin generōsus "of noble birth, noble-minded" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on genus "birth, family".

Lesson 4 of 5.
avaro — mean, stingy; miser.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for lazy, then the Italian for generous.

You'll want to know: avaro.
avaro — "mean, stingy; miser".
Non è avaro, ma non spende per cose inutili. He is not stingy, but he does not spend on useless things.
Also a noun: un avaro — a miser.

The word, taken apart.
avaro is inherited from Latin avārus "greedy" (EtymDB), from avēre "to long for, to crave" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
vantarsi — to boast, to show off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for generous, then the Italian for mean.

You'll want to know: vantarsi.
vantarsi — "to boast, to show off".
Il mio collega si vanta sempre della sua macchina nuova. My colleague is always boasting about his new car.
Vantarsi di qualcosa: the thing you boast about takes di.

The word, taken apart.
vantarsi is usually traced to Late Latin vānitāre "to speak emptily", built on Latin vānus "empty, vain" (Lewis & Short).
