French, chapter 67: Find, Fish, Chicken, Fruit, Apple.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
trouver — to find.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to win; to earn, then the French for to hide.

You'll want to know: trouver.
trouver — "to find".

The word, taken apart.
trouver from Old French trover, probably from Vulgar Latin tropare, "to compose a poem", from Latin tropus, "a figure of speech", from Greek tropos, "a turn" — inherited (EtymDB 2.1). This is the usual account; it is not certain.

The word, taken apart.
poulet — built from the French word for "hen", with -et, "little". The hen word from Latin pulla, the feminine of pullus, "a young animal" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: la pomme.
la pomme — "an apple".

The word, taken apart.
pomme from Latin poma, "fruits", the plural of pomum — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1) In Latin pomum was any fruit; French narrowed it to the apple.
