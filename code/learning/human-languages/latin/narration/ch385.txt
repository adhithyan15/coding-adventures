Latin, chapter 385: Ruling and Governing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
regō, regere — to rule, to guide, to direct.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for canvassing, then the Latin for to pick out.

You'll want to know: regō, regere.
regō, regere — "to rule, to guide, to direct".
Rēx populum iūstē regēbat. The king ruled the people justly.
regō, regere, rēxī, rēctum. Rēctus, straight, is its participle; rēx is the man who rules. English regent and correct.

The word, taken apart.
regō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to make straight, to direct" (EtymDB), the same root as in rēx and rēctus (Lewis & Short). English regent and correct come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
gubernō, gubernāre — to steer; to govern.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to pick out, then the Latin for to rule.

You'll want to know: gubernō, gubernāre.
gubernō, gubernāre — "to steer; to govern".
Nauta nāvem gubernat. The sailor steers the ship.
gubernō, gubernāre, gubernāvī, gubernātum. Gubernātor, a helmsman, is already known. English govern comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
gubernō was borrowed from Greek kybernaō "to steer a ship" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English govern comes from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
administrō, administrāre — to manage, to run; to administer.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: administrō, administrāre.
administrō, administrāre — "to manage, to run; to administer".
Prōvinciam bene administrāvit. He governed the province well.
administrō, administrāre, administrāvī, administrātum, built on minister, a servant, a helper. English administer.

The word, taken apart.
administrō is built from ad and ministrō "to serve" (EtymDB), which comes from minister "a servant, a helper" (Lewis & Short). English administer comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to steer, then the Latin for to manage.

You'll want to know: imperium, imperiī.
imperium, imperiī — "command, supreme power; an empire".
Cōnsulēs summum imperium habent. The consuls hold supreme power.
imperium, imperiī n., built on imperō, to command: the power to command an army and govern, then the land ruled. English empire and imperial.

The word, taken apart.
imperium is built on imperō "to command" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English empire comes from it through French, and imperial from its adjective.

Lesson 5 of 5.
prīnceps, prīncipis — the first, a leader; the emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to manage, then the Latin for command.

You'll want to know: prīnceps, prīncipis.
prīnceps, prīncipis — "the first, a leader; the emperor".
Prīnceps cīvitātis erat. He was the leading man of the state.
prīnceps, prīncipis m.: prīmus + capiō, the one who takes first place. As an adjective it means first. English prince and principal.

The word, taken apart.
prīnceps is built from prīmus "first" and capiō "to take" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): the one who takes first place. English prince and principal come from it.
