Arabic, chapter 456: Finding the right size.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حجم (ḥajm) — volume; size; bulk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to display, then the Arabic for a market stall.

You'll want to know: حجم.
حجم — ḥajm — "volume; size; bulk".
حجم الحقيبة صغير جدا. ḥajm al-ḥaqība ṣaghīr jiddan. The size of the bag is very small.
maqās is a clothing size; ḥajm is how big a thing is.

The word, taken apart.
حجم (ḥajm) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-j-m, "the bulk of a thing" (Lane).

You'll want to know: عينة.

أعطاني التاجر عينة صغيرة من العسل. aʿṭānī at-tājir ʿayyina ṣaghīra min al-ʿasal. The merchant gave me a small sample of the honey.

The word, taken apart.
عينة (ʿayyina) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-y-n, "the eye; a thing itself" (Lane). Modern Arabic uses it for a sample or a specimen.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ناسب (nāsaba) — to fit; to suit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a variety, then the Arabic for a sample.

You'll want to know: ناسب.
ناسب — nāsaba — "to fit; to suit".
لم يناسبني القميص. lam yunāsibnī al-qamīṣ. The shirt did not suit me.
Present: يناسب, yunāsib.

The word, taken apart.
ناسب (nāsaba) is built on the native Arabic root n-s-b, "kinship, relation" (Lane). It is Form III, "to be in keeping with"; munāsib, "suitable", is its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

ضاق الحذاء على قدمي. ḍāqa al-ḥidhāʾ ʿalā qadamī. The shoe was too tight on my foot.

The word, taken apart.
ضاق (ḍāqa) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-y-q, "to be narrow" (Lane). It is the plain verb beside ḍayyiq, "narrow".
