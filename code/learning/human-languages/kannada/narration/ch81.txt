Kannada, chapter 81: The Ones That Never Came Back.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(ಾ ಿ ೆ ೊ ೋ ೃ ಂ ಃ) (neladalli) — eight that cannot stand alone.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: six signs and two marks until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two of these you met in the book's first handful of pages. Name the one that lengthens a vowel, and the one that makes it i.

Script: six signs and two marks.
Six vowel signs, cold. Each attaches to a consonant and replaces the a built into it:
[a table of 6 rows, read aloud]
sign: ಾ. sound: ā.
sign: ಿ. sound: i.
sign: ೆ. sound: e.
sign: ೊ. sound: o.
sign: ೋ. sound: ō.
sign: ೃ. sound: ṛ.
The last is the odd one. ೃ writes a vowel that behaves like a consonant in the mouth, inherited from Sanskrit and rare enough that a reader can go pages without meeting it — which is exactly why it is here.

[pause 1 second]
Two marks now, and they are not vowel signs at all.
[your turn — read: ಂ — say what it adds to the syllable it follows]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: ಃ — say the same for this one]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: ೊ and ೋ back to back, and say what separates them]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
ಂ and ಃ ride after a syllable rather than reshaping its vowel. That is the division worth keeping: a vowel sign changes the sound already there, a mark adds one behind it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of the eight writes a vowel that acts like a consonant? (ೃ.) Which two follow a syllable instead of reshaping it? (ಂ and ಃ.) And which one only makes the vowel longer? (ಾ.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(ಇ ಈ ಉ ಊ ಓ) (nintiruva) — five that need nothing to lean on.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: the same vowel, two shapes until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two full-size letters, not signs. Name the one that says i and the one that says u.

Script: the same vowel, two shapes.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
standing letter: ಇ. sound: i. when you use it: the word opens on the vowel.
standing letter: ಈ. sound: ī. when you use it: the same, held long.
standing letter: ಉ. sound: u. when you use it: the word opens on the vowel.
standing letter: ಊ. sound: ū. when you use it: the same, held long.
standing letter: ಓ. sound: ō. when you use it: the word opens on the vowel.
Every one of these has a partner among the signs. The difference is not the sound — it is whether a consonant is there to carry it. A vowel with nothing in front of it must be written full-size; the moment a consonant arrives, the same vowel shrinks to a sign hanging off it.
Two pairs make this visible: ಇ against ಿ, and ಓ against ೋ. Same vowel, twice, and the shape tells you where in the word you are.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: ಈ and ಊ — say which is the long one in each pair]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: ಾ — and say why this one has no standing partner among the five]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What decides whether a vowel is written full-size or as a sign? (Whether a consonant is there to carry it.) Name the standing letter that pairs with ೋ. (ಓ.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(ಕ ಗ ಚ ಝ ಞ ಥ ಪ ಯ ಸ ಹ) (hattu) — ten, gathered at last.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: ten, sorted by where they are made until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two that differ by voice alone. Read ಕ, then ಗ, and say what your throat does on the second that it did not do on the first.

Script: ten, sorted by where they are made.
These ten arrived one per chapter, scattered from the book's opening to well past its middle, and no page ever put them side by side. Sorted by the mouth rather than by the order you met them:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
where: throat. letters: ಕ ka, ಗ ga.
where: palate. letters: ಚ ca, ಝ jha, ಞ ña.
where: teeth. letters: ಥ tha.
where: lips. letters: ಪ pa.
Read down the middle row. ಚ, ಝ and ಞ are made in the same place, and they differ only in voice, breath and nasality — which is the organising idea the whole Kannada consonant grid is built on.
ಞ is the one to look at twice. It is the nasal of the palatal row and is rare on the page; a reader who has not seen it since the chapter that taught it will not place it at all.
