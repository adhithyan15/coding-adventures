Japanese, chapter 353: Checking Things Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
てんけんする (tenken suru) — to inspect; to check over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to slip out of place, then the Japanese for a tool.

You'll want to know: てんけんする.
てんけんする — tenken suru — "to inspect; to check over".
ながく つかったら、くるまを てんけんした ほうが いいです。 — nagaku tsukattara, kuruma o tenken shita hō ga ii desu — "Once a car has been used for a long time, it should be checked over."
Polite てんけんします, tenkenshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ちちが くるまを てんけんしました。, chichi ga kuruma o tenken shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
てんけんする (tenken suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, tenken, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: ten, "spot" (Middle Chinese temX) and ken, "examination" (kjemX) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
