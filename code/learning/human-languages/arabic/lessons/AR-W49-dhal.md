# ذ (dhāl) — the letter that opens ذهب

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C49-TA-EMPHATIC-01, AR-SCRIPT-C49-ZA-EMPHATIC-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ط and ظ once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ذهب** (*dhahaba*), "he went".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ذ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SCRIPT-C49-DHAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ذ** — *dhāl*, the *th* of English "this".

It opens **ذهب** (*dhahaba*), "he went", the first verb in this book. It is
**د** (*dāl*) with **one dot above**, and like **د** it never joins to the letter
after it.

## Writing: ذ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-C49-DHAL-01] -->

- **1.** start here: begin at the upper tip and descend diagonally down and right through the curved shoulder
- **2.** turn left along the baseline without lifting
- **3.** lift once, then place the dot above — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 1.**

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: د ذ ر, independent Dhaal body followed by its upper dot (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-21).
