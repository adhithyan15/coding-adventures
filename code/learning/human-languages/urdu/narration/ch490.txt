Urdu, chapter 490: Uncles, Steps and Orphans.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
تایا (tāyā) — a father's elder brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a nephew (a sister's son), then the Urdu for a grandson (a daughter's son).

You'll want to know: تایا.
تایا — tāyā — "a father's elder brother".
میرے تایا ابو سے بڑے ہیں۔ mere tāyā abbū se baṛe haiṅ. My uncle is older than my father.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
تایا (tāyā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin tāyā under Sanskrit tātyá, "fatherly" (CDIAL 5759).

Lesson 2 of 5.
خالو (khālū) — a mother's sister's husband.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a grandson (a daughter's son), then the Urdu for a father's elder brother.

You'll want to know: خالو.
خالو — khālū — "a mother's sister's husband".
خالہ اور خالو کل آئیں گے۔ khālā aur khālū kal āeṅ ge. My aunt and uncle will come tomorrow.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
خالو (khālū) is built on خالہ, "a mother's sister", usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic khāla: the aunt's husband.

Lesson 5 of 5.
گود لینا (god lenā) — to adopt (a child).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for related by remarriage, then the Urdu for an orphan.

You'll want to know: گود لینا.
گود لینا — god lenā — "to adopt (a child)".
انہوں نے ایک یتیم لڑکی کو گود لیا۔ unhoṅ ne ek yatīm laṛkī ko god liyā. They adopted an orphan girl.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بچے کو گود لیا, us ne bachche ko god liyā.

The word, taken apart.
گود لینا (god lenā) is built from گود, "a lap", inherited from gōddī, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 4284), and لینا, "to take": to take a child into one's lap.
