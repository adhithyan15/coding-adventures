# aconsejar — to advise

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C321-COURT-10, ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-24] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el consejo** and **la biblioteca**. You have the noun. Today
you get the verb that goes with it.

## You'll want to know: aconsejar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C321-COURT-10] -->

**aconsejar** — to advise.

> ¿Qué **aconseja** a otras bibliotecas?

*Aconsejar a alguien* — to advise somebody. *Aconsejar hacer algo* — to advise
doing something. *Te aconsejo esperar* — I advise you to wait.

## Grammar Lens: the same machine, a different part fed into it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01, ES-LEX-C321-COURT-10] -->

[Acercarse](./ES-C443-acercarse.md) gave you this pattern outright — **a-**
plus a word plus **-ar**, *to make it that* — with *acercar* from *cerca*,
*alejar* from *lejos*, *aclarar* from *claro*.

Look at what those three have in common. Every one is built on a **preposition
or an adjective**. This word is not:

| built on | example |
|---|---|
| an adjective or preposition | aclarar, acercar |
| a **noun** | **aconsejar**, acompañar |

Same machine, different raw material. *Acercar* makes something near;
*aconsejar* does not make anything *consejo* — it **gives** one. So with a noun
the sense shifts from *make it that* to *supply that*, which is worth knowing
before you try to build your own.

Note the person goes in with **a**: *aconsejar **a** otras bibliotecas*. The
advice itself follows with no preposition at all.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01] -->

*El consejo* already traced it: Latin **consilium**, a deliberation — from
**con-**, together, and a root about **calling** or taking counsel.

So advising is, underneath, a **sitting together**: the word does not mean
telling somebody what to do. English keeps the same sense in **counsel** and
**council**, which are the same Latin word split into two spellings, one for
the advice and one for the people giving it.

That is a distinction English had to invent twice. Spanish does it with one
noun and one verb.
