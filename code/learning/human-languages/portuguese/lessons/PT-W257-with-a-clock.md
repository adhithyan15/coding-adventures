# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-MORAR-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-ME-CHAMO-04, PT-GRAMMAR-MORAR-04, PT-LEX-FALO-PORTUGUES-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What CAPLE publishes.** This track's A1 target is **ACESSO**, from CAPLE at
the University of Lisbon. It certifies **A1** in **four components**, over about
**one hour and thirty-five minutes**, and a pass is any overall result from
**55% to 100%**.

**What this page does not quote.** This book has not checked how many minutes
ACESSO gives its writing component, or how many words it asks for, so it gives
you no such number.

**What this book's own A1 paper sets.** Twenty minutes, in two parts: a
practical form, then a 30–40 word message to a named reader. That shape is the
project's own, read out of `task-shapes/a1.json`. It is not CAPLE's.

[PAUSE 2s] Today, the first part only, against a short clock.
Set a three-minute timer, then fill six fields, in Portuguese:

1. your name;
2. the city where you live;
3. one language you speak;
4. the city where you work;
5. one day of the week you work;
6. a number from one to ten, as a word.

[PAUSE 2s] When the three-minute timer rings, **stop**, even mid-word.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-FALO-PORTUGUES-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Spend at most one minute checking. The marks come first: the accent
on a day or a number, and the hyphen before **feira**.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson, because it changes
how you study rather than how you write today.

[PAUSE 2s] CAPLE passes ACESSO on **one overall figure, 55%**, across the four
components. A strong reader can carry a weak writer.

[PAUSE 2s] This book's contract is stricter: **60% on every paper, each on its
own**, and no total makes up for a paper that falls short. Study for the
stricter rule. If writing is your weak paper, reading practice does not repair
it.
