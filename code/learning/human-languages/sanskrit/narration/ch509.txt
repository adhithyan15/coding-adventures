6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
जर्जरः (jarjaraḥ) — rickety; worn to pieces.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for mends, then the Sanskrit for lime plaster.

You'll want to know: जर्जरः.
जर्जरः — jarjaraḥ — "rickety; worn to pieces".
मम आसन्दी जर्जरा अस्ति। mama āsandī jarjarā asti. My chair is rickety.

The word, taken apart.
जर्जरः (jarjaraḥ) is the adjective MW records as infirm, decrepit, decayed, broken in pieces; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 6.
रन्ध्रम् (randhram) — an opening; a hole.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for rickety, then the Sanskrit for slack.

You'll want to know: रन्ध्रम्.
रन्ध्रम् — randhram — "an opening; a hole".
मम स्यूते एकं रन्ध्रम् अस्ति। mama syūte ekaṃ randhram asti. There is a hole in my bag.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
रन्ध्रम् (randhram) probably comes from the root √rad, "to split", as MW suggests; MW gives randhra as a slit, an opening, a hole (MW).

Lesson 4 of 6.
स्यन्दते (syandate) — trickles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for slack, then the Sanskrit for an opening.

You'll want to know: स्यन्दते.
स्यन्दते — syandate — "trickles".
रन्ध्रात् जलं स्यन्दते। randhrāt jalaṃ syandate. Water trickles from the hole.
Past (it …): जलं स्यन्नवत्, jalaṃ syannavat.

The word, taken apart.
स्यन्दते (syandate) comes from the root √syand, "to move on, to flow, to trickle"; its past participle is syanna (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
जीर्णोद्धारः (jīrṇoddhāraḥ) — a repair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an opening, then the Sanskrit for trickles.

You'll want to know: जीर्णोद्धारः.
जीर्णोद्धारः — jīrṇoddhāraḥ — "a repair".
एतस्य गृहस्य जीर्णोद्धारः आवश्यकः अस्ति। etasya gṛhasya jīrṇoddhāraḥ āvaśyakaḥ asti. This house needs repairs.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
जीर्णोद्धारः (jīrṇoddhāraḥ) is built from jīrṇa, "worn out", and uddhāra, "a raising up"; MW gives jīrṇoddhāra as the repairing of what has fallen into ruin in a building (MW).
