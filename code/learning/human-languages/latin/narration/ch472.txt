Latin, chapter 472: Distaff, Spindle, Web on the loom, To spin, Wool-working.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
colus, colī — a distaff (for spinning).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sharpen, then the Latin for a stonecutter.

You'll want to know: colus, colī.
colus, colī — "a distaff (for spinning)".
Avia colum tenet et lānam trahit. Grandmother holds the distaff and draws out the wool.
Feminine, with second and fourth declension forms: colus, colī or colūs f. The stick that holds the raw wool.

The word, taken apart.
colus is inherited from PIE kʷélus, built on kʷel- 'to turn' (EtymDB), the turning stick of the spinner. Lewis & Short suggest a different root, for being bent or round, so the details are not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fūsus, fūsī — a spindle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a stonecutter, then the Latin for a distaff.

You'll want to know: fūsus, fūsī.
fūsus, fūsī — "a spindle".
Fūsus in manū ancillae celeriter vertitur. The spindle turns fast in the maid's hand.
Second declension, masculine: fūsus, fūsī m. The weighted stick on which the spun thread is wound.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of fūsus 'spindle' is not settled: Lewis & Short give the sense but no source, and EtymDB lists it without a parent. It is a different word from fūsus 'poured', from fundō.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tēla, tēlae — a web on the loom, the warp; a loom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a distaff, then the Latin for a spindle.

You'll want to know: tēla, tēlae.
tēla, tēlae — "a web on the loom, the warp; a loom".
Fēminae in ātriō ad tēlam labōrant. The women work at the loom in the hall.
First declension, feminine: tēla, tēlae f. Also a spider's web. French toile 'cloth' comes from it, and so English toilet, first a little cloth.

The word, taken apart.
tēla is built on texō 'to weave' (EtymDB), and Lewis & Short take it as contracted from an older texia, as āla stands beside axilla. French toile 'cloth' comes from it, and English toilet from the French diminutive toilette.

Lesson 4 of 5.
neō, nēre — to spin (thread).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a spindle, then the Latin for a web on the loom.

You'll want to know: neō, nēre.
neō, nēre — "to spin (thread)".
Ancillae vesperī lānam nent. In the evening the maids spin wool.
neō, nēre, nēvī, nētum, second conjugation. Short forms: net 'she spins', nent 'they spin'.

The word, taken apart.
neō is inherited from Proto-Italic nēō, from PIE (s)neh₁- 'to spin' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short compare Greek neō. English needle is usually counted a distant Germanic cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lānificium, lānificiī — wool-working, spinning and weaving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a web on the loom, then the Latin for to spin.

You'll want to know: lānificium, lānificiī.
lānificium, lānificiī — "wool-working, spinning and weaving".
Lānificium in vīllā labor fēminārum est. Wool-working in the house is women's work.
Second declension, neuter: lānificium, lānificiī n., from lāna 'wool' and faciō 'to make'. A Roman tomb often praised a woman for it.

The word, taken apart.
lānificium is built on lānificus 'wool-working', from lāna 'wool' and faciō 'to make' (Lewis & Short).
