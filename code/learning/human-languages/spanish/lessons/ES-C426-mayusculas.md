# las mayúsculas — and the four things Spanish refuses to capitalise

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write today's day and month in English. Now write them in Spanish,
and read on to find out what you almost certainly got wrong.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C426-01]; assesses=[] -->

**la mayúscula** — "the capital letter." **La minúscula** is the small one.
Latin *maiusculus*, "somewhat larger," and *minusculus*, "somewhat smaller" —
the two names describe nothing but size.

Spanish capitalises where you expect: the first word of a sentence, and proper
names — people, cities, countries, rivers.

## Grammar Lens: the four that English capitalises and Spanish does not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-01] -->

This is the highest-value fact in the chapter, because an English-trained hand
gets all four wrong and the marker sees every one:

| | English | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| days | Monday | **lunes** |
| months | January | **enero** |
| languages | Spanish | **español** |
| nationalities | I am Spanish | soy **español** |

Add religions and titles: *católico*, and *el rey*, *el presidente*, *doctor* —
all lower-case in running text.

The idea underneath is consistent. Spanish capitalises a **name**, not a
**category**. *España* is a name; *español* is a description of a kind of
person, and a description is an ordinary adjective.

## Grammar Lens: the title of a book or a film
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-01, ES-ORTH-C425-01] -->

| | |
|---|---|
| English | *One Hundred Years of Solitude* |
| Spanish | *Cien años de soledad* |

Only the first word takes a capital. English capitalises most words in a title;
Spanish treats a title as an ordinary sentence with a capital at the front.
