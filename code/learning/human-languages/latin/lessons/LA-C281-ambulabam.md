# ambulābam — I was walking

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was tired", the open past. (*Fessus eram.*) Now "I
walked", the closed one. (*Ambulāvī.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Eram* belongs to one verb. Every other verb makes its open past
with two letters slipped in before the ending.

## Grammar Lens: -ba- before the ending
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-IMPERFECTUM-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-AMBULO-01, LA-LEX-HABEO-01] -->

Take the present stem, add **-ba-**, then the endings *eram* taught you:

> **ambulābam** — I was walking. **ambulābās** — you were walking.
> **ambulābat** — he was walking. **ambulābāmus** — we were walking.
> **ambulābātis** — you all were walking. **ambulābant** — they were walking.

[PAUSE 2s] The vowel before *-ba-* comes from the infinitive. An **-āre**
verb keeps its *ā*, and an **-ēre** verb its *ē*: *habēre*, **habēbam**, I
had, I used to have; *sedēre*, **sedēbat**, he was sitting.

[PAUSE 1s] The other verbs put **-ēbam** on the stem: *legere*, **legēbam**,
I was reading; *volō* gives **volēbat**, he wanted. And the verbs with **-iō** keep their *i*: *venīre*,
**veniēbam**; *dormīre*, **dormiēbat**, he was sleeping.

[PAUSE 1s] What it says is the same for all of them: an action under way,
or a habit. *Ambulābam* is "I was walking", and also "I used to walk".

## The word, taken apart: the -b- of -bam
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-C281-BAM-02]; assesses=[] -->

Histories of Latin often trace the **-b-** of *-bam* to the root behind
*fuī*, Proto-Indo-European *\*bʰuH-*, "to become". That is a
reconstruction, and neither EtymDB nor Lewis & Short takes it up, so it is offered as the usual
account, not as a fact.

What is certain is where the ending went. Spanish kept it: *cantābat*, "he
was singing", is Spanish *cantaba*. French wore the *b* away, so the same
form there is *chantait*.
