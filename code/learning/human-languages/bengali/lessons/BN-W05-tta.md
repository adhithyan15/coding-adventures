## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-TTA-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-TA-01, BN-SCRIPT-DA-01, BN-SCRIPT-DDA-01] -->

> ট

You have been carrying three corners of a square without being shown the square.

|  | voice off | voice on |
|---|---|---|
| **tongue on the teeth** | **ত** *tô* | **দ** *dô* |
| **tongue curled back** | **ট** *ṭô* | **ড** *ḍô* |

Two switches, four letters. **ট** is the one the book never filled in: the
tongue curled back to the ridge, exactly where you put it for **ড**, and the
voice switched off, exactly as you do for **ত**. If you can make the other
three you can already make this one — you have never been asked to.

An English *t* lands between the two rows, which is the same trap **দ** and
**ড** set. To an English ear **ত** and **ট** are one letter. They are not, and
they spell different words.

With the mātrā:

> **ট** + **া** → **টা**

Hold on to that syllable. It is the shape of the next four lessons, and it is
the reason this letter could not wait any longer.
