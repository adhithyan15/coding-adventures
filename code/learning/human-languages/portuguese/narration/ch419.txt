Portuguese, chapter 419: No navio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o cruzeiro — the cruise; the stone cross (by a road).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the canoe, then the Portuguese for the boatman.

You'll want to know: o cruzeiro.
o cruzeiro — "the cruise; the stone cross (by a road)".
Os meus pais fizeram um cruzeiro de uma semana. My parents went on a week-long cruise.
In villages, um cruzeiro is a tall stone cross at a crossroads.

The word, taken apart.
cruzeiro is built on cruz 'cross', inherited from Latin crux, crucis (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists Spanish crucero beside it. A cruise crosses the sea back and forth.

o camarote — "the cabin (on a ship); the box (in a theatre)".
O nosso camarote tem uma janela redonda. Our cabin has a round window.
At a stadium or a concert, o camarote is the VIP box.

The word, taken apart.
camarote is built on câmara 'chamber' with the small-size ending -ote (EtymDB); câmara goes back to Latin camera, from Greek kamára 'vaulted room'.

You'll want to know: o marinheiro.
o marinheiro — "the sailor".
Um marinheiro ajudou-nos com as malas. A sailor helped us with our suitcases.
Marinheiro de primeira viagem — a beginner.

The word, taken apart.
marinheiro is built on marinha 'seashore, navy', from Latin marīnus 'of the sea', on mare 'sea' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB lists French marinier and Italian marinaio as cousins.
