The word, taken apart.
asper has no sure origin; Lewis & Short call it doubtful, and EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed root h2esp-, "to cut".

Lesson 2 of 5.
profundus, profunda, profundum — deep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for because of, then the Latin for rough.

You'll want to know: profundus, profunda, profundum.
profundus, profunda, profundum — "deep".

The word, taken apart.
profundus is prō, "forward, down", with fundus, "bottom" (EtymDB 2.1): reaching far down. English profound comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
necessārius, necessāria, necessārium — necessary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for deep, then the Latin for unknown.

You'll want to know: necessārius, necessāria, necessārium.
necessārius, necessāria, necessārium — "necessary".

The word, taken apart.
necessārius is built on necesse, "unavoidable" (Lewis & Short), which is usually explained as ne, "not", with the root of cēdō, "to give way": not to be avoided.
