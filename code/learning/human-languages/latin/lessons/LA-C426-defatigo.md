# dēfatīgō, dēfatīgāre — to wear out, to tire out

## You'll want to know: dēfatīgō, dēfatīgāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C426-LUTULENTUS-05]; assesses=[] -->

**dēfatīgō, dēfatīgāre** — "to wear out, to tire out".

> Via longa equōs dēfatīgat.
>
> *The long road wears the horses out.*

*dēfatīgō, dēfatīgāre, dēfatīgāvī, dēfatīgātum*. Often passive: *dēfatīgātus* 'worn out'. The simple verb *fatīgō* gives English *fatigue*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dēfatīgō* is built from *dē-* 'down, completely' and *fatīgō* 'to tire, to weary' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *fatigue* comes, through French, from *fatīgō*.
