6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
cōnstō, cōnstāre — to cost; to stand firm; to be agreed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a written bond, then the Latin for a moneylender.

You'll want to know: cōnstō, cōnstāre.
cōnstō, cōnstāre — "to cost; to stand firm; to be agreed".
Haec vīlla magnō cōnstitit. This country house cost a great deal.
cōnstō, cōnstāre, cōnstitī. The price is in the ablative: magnō, at a high price, parvō, cheaply. Cōnstat, it is agreed. English cost comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
cōnstō is built from con- and stō "to stand" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to stand firm, then to stand at a price. English cost comes from it through French.

auctiō, auctiōnis f., built on augeō, to increase: the price goes up and up. English auction.

The word, taken apart.
auctiō is the noun built on augeō "to increase" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a sale where the price keeps rising. English auction comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
liceor, licērī — to bid (at an auction).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to cost, then the Latin for an auction.

You'll want to know: liceor, licērī.
liceor, licērī — "to bid (at an auction)".
Hunc equum centum dēnāriīs licitus est. He bid a hundred denarii for this horse.
Deponent: liceor, licērī, licitus sum. The thing bid for goes in the accusative and the price in the ablative. It belongs with licet: to name what you are allowed to pay.

The word, taken apart.
liceor belongs with liceō "to be for sale, to be valued" and licet "it is allowed" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to put a value on a thing.

Lesson 4 of 6.
emptor, emptōris — a buyer.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for an auction, then the Latin for to bid.

You'll want to know: emptor, emptōris.
emptor, emptōris — "a buyer".
Emptor pretium nimis magnum esse dīxit. The buyer said the price was too high.
emptor, emptōris m., built on emō, to buy. Caveat emptor: let the buyer beware.

The word, taken apart.
emptor is built on emō "to buy" (Lewis & Short), from its supine emptum, with the ending -tor of the one who does it.
