Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 166: Up at Daybreak.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जागणो (jāgṇo) — to wake up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for brave, then the Marwadi for crazy.

You'll want to know: जागणो.
जागणो — jāgṇo — "to wake up".
हूं रोज तड़कै जागूं हूं। hū̃ roj taṛkai jāgū̃ hū̃. I wake up at daybreak every day.
Past (I …): हूं आज तड़कै जाग्यो, hū̃ āj taṛkai jāgyo.

The word, taken apart.
जागणो (jāgṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit jāgrati, "they are awake"; CDIAL lists Marwari jāgṇo, "to wake", under it (CDIAL 5175). Its partner जगावणो, to wake someone, is already yours.

Lesson 2 of 5.
खंगाळणो (khaṅgāḷṇo) — to rinse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for crazy, then the Marwadi for to wake up.

You'll want to know: खंगाळणो.
खंगाळणो — khaṅgāḷṇo — "to rinse".
हूं दिनूगै मूंडो खंगाळूं हूं। hū̃ dinūgai mū̃ḍo khaṅgāḷū̃ hū̃. I rinse my mouth early in the morning.
Past (I …): म्हैं लोटो खंगाळ्यो, mhaĩ loṭo khaṅgāḷyo.

The word, taken apart.
खंगाळणो (khaṅgāḷṇo) comes from a reconstructed khakkhālayati, "rinses", which CDIAL thinks imitates the sound of rinsing; it lists Hindi khãgālnā and a Gujarati verb for rinsing the mouth in the morning (CDIAL 3762). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.

Lesson 3 of 5.
नींद (nī̃d) — sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to wake up, then the Marwadi for to rinse.

You'll want to know: नींद.
नींद — nī̃d — "sleep".
आज म्हारी नींद पूरी कोनी। āj mhārī nī̃d pūrī konī. I haven't had my full sleep today.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नींद (nī̃d) is inherited from Sanskrit nidrā, "sleep"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari nīda (CDIAL 7200). It is feminine: म्हारी नींद.

Lesson 4 of 5.
पोढणो (poḍhṇo) — to lie down (to sleep).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to rinse, then the Marwadi for a night's sleep.

You'll want to know: पोढणो.
पोढणो — poḍhṇo — "to lie down (to sleep)".
दादी रोज आथण जल्दी पोढै है। dādī roj āthaṇ jaldī poḍhai hai. Grandmother lies down early every evening.
Past (he …): वो खाट माथै पोढ्यो, vo khāṭ māthai poḍhyo.

The word, taken apart.
पोढणो (poḍhṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit pravardhate, "grows up"; CDIAL explains the later sense "lie down, sleep" from an older "stretched out", and lists Old Marwari poḍhaï, "lies down" (CDIAL 8789).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सांझ (sā̃jh) — dusk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a night's sleep, then the Marwadi for to lie down.

You'll want to know: सांझ.
सांझ — sā̃jh — "dusk".
सांझ घणी सांत है। sā̃jh ghaṇī sānt hai. Dusk is very calm.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सांझ (sā̃jh) is inherited from Sanskrit sandhyā, "twilight", the joining of day and night; CDIAL lists Hindi sā̃jh (CDIAL 12918). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry.
