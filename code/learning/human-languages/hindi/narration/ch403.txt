Hindi, chapter 403: Stands and Footpaths.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
स्टैंड (sṭaiṇḍ) — a stand (for buses, taxis or rickshaws).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to doze, then the Hindi for to rest a while.

You'll want to know: स्टैंड.
स्टैंड — sṭaiṇḍ — "a stand (for buses, taxis or rickshaws)".
बस स्टैंड यहाँ से पास है। bas sṭaiṇḍ yahā̃ se pās hai. The bus stand is close to here.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
स्टैंड (sṭaiṇḍ) is borrowed from English stand, the place where buses, taxis or rickshaws wait for passengers: बस स्टैंड, रिक्शा स्टैंड.

Lesson 2 of 5.
राहगीर (rāhgīr) — a passer-by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to rest a while, then the Hindi for a stand.

You'll want to know: राहगीर.
राहगीर — rāhgīr — "a passer-by".
एक राहगीर ने हमें रास्ता बताया। ek rāhgīr ne hamẽ rāstā batāyā. A passer-by showed us the way.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
राहगीर (rāhgīr) is usually traced to Persian rāhgīr, "one who takes the road", from rāh, "road": anyone walking past whom you might ask the way.

Lesson 4 of 5.
टहलना (ṭahalnā) — to take a stroll; to walk up and down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a passer-by, then the Hindi for a pavement.

You'll want to know: टहलना.
टहलना — ṭahalnā — "to take a stroll; to walk up and down".
हम शाम को पार्क में टहलते हैं। ham śām ko pārk meṁ ṭahalte haiṁ. We stroll in the park in the evening.
Past (he …): वह नदी के किनारे टहला, vah nadī ke kināre ṭahlā.

The word, taken apart.
टहलना (ṭahalnā) goes back to ṭahall, "walk up and down", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5453), which lists Hindi ṭahalnā, "to take a walk". घूमना wanders; टहलना strolls for the pleasure of it.

The word, taken apart.
हाँफना (hā̃phnā) goes back to hamph, "pant", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 13973), which lists Hindi hā̃phnā; the word itself sounds like the breathing it names.
