Latin, chapter 166: To approve, To mark, To discuss, To promise, To allow.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
probō, probāre — to approve, to prove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to smile, then the Latin for to shout.

You'll want to know: probō, probāre.
probō, probāre — "to approve, to prove".

The word, taken apart.
probō is built on probus, "good, honest" (Lewis & Short): to find something good, to test and approve. probus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pro-bhwo-, "being in front, outstanding" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
disputō, disputāre — to discuss, to argue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to approve, then the Latin for to mark.

You'll want to know: disputō, disputāre.
disputō, disputāre — "to discuss, to argue".

The word, taken apart.
disputō is dis-, "apart", with putō, "to reckon" (EtymDB 2.1). Lewis & Short note that disputō was first a word of accounts: to go over a reckoning item by item.

Lesson 5 of 5.
permittō, permittere — to allow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to discuss, then the Latin for to promise.

You'll want to know: permittō, permittere.
permittō, permittere — "to allow".

The word, taken apart.
permittō is per, "through", with mittō, "to send, to let go" (EtymDB 2.1). Lewis & Short give permittō first as "to let go through", hence to allow.
