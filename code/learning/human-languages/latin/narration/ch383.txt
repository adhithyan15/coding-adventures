Latin, chapter 383: The Magistrates.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
magistrātus, magistrātūs — a public office; a magistrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for well known, then the Latin for of the people.

You'll want to know: magistrātus, magistrātūs.
magistrātus, magistrātūs — "a public office; a magistrate".
Magistrātūs quotannīs ā populō creantur. Magistrates are elected by the people every year.
magistrātus, magistrātūs m., built on magister: both the office and the man who holds it. Roman offices lasted a year.

The word, taken apart.
magistrātus is built on magister "a master" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): the office of one set over others. English magistrate comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
praetor, praetōris — a praetor, a judge and magistrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of the people, then the Latin for a public office.

You'll want to know: praetor, praetōris.
praetor, praetōris — "a praetor, a judge and magistrate".
Praetor iūs dīcit in forō. The praetor gives judgement in the Forum.
praetor, praetōris m. Next in rank below the consuls, he ran the law courts.

The word, taken apart.
praetor is usually taken as prae "before" and the root of eō "to go" (Lewis & Short): one who goes in front, an old word for a leader.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: aedīlis, aedīlis.
aedīlis, aedīlis — "an aedile, an officer for streets, markets and games".
Aedīlis macellum et viās cūrat. The aedile looks after the meat market and the streets.
aedīlis, aedīlis m., built on the word for a temple or a building: he first cared for the temples. English aedile.

The word, taken apart.
aedīlis is built on aedēs "a temple, a building" (EtymDB, which lists old Latin aidīlis; Lewis & Short). English aedile comes from it.

Quaestor pecūniam pūblicam servat. The quaestor keeps the public money.
quaestor, quaestōris m., built on quaerō, to seek, to inquire. The lowest regular office: the treasury and the accounts.

The word, taken apart.
quaestor is built on quaerō "to seek, to inquire" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor of the one who does it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cēnsor, cēnsōris — a censor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an aedile, then the Latin for a quaestor.

You'll want to know: cēnsor, cēnsōris.
cēnsor, cēnsōris — "a censor".
Cēnsor cīvēs et bona eōrum numerat. The censor counts the citizens and their property.
cēnsor, cēnsōris m., built on cēnseō, to assess. Every five years two censors drew up the list of citizens and could strike a senator off the roll. English censor.

The word, taken apart.
cēnsor is built on cēnseō "to assess, to value" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): the officer who assessed the citizens. English censor comes from it.
