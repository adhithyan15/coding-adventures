French, chapter 483: Mal au cœur.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la nausée — nausea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for itch, then the French for to scratch.

You'll want to know: la nausée.
la nausée — "nausea".
En bateau, j'ai souvent la nausée. On a boat I often feel sick.
Avoir la nausée or, more informally, avoir mal au cœur.

The word, taken apart.
la nausée is a learned borrowing from Latin nausea "seasickness" (EtymDB), from Greek nausía, built on naûs "ship".

You'll want to know: vomir.

Le petit a vomi dans la voiture. The little one was sick in the car.
A regular -ir verb: nous vomissons.

The word, taken apart.
vomir is inherited from Vulgar Latin vomīre, a form of Latin vomere "to vomit" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la migraine — migraine, bad headache.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for nausea, then the French for to vomit.

You'll want to know: la migraine.
la migraine — "migraine, bad headache".
Quand j'ai la migraine, je reste dans le noir. When I have a migraine, I stay in the dark.
In speech, also any bad headache.

The word, taken apart.
la migraine comes from Late Latin hēmicrānia (EtymDB), from Greek hēmikranía, built on hēmi- "half" and kraníon "skull": a pain on one side of the head.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le vertige — dizziness; fear of heights.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to vomit, then the French for migraine.

You'll want to know: le vertige.
le vertige — "dizziness; fear of heights".
En haut de la tour, j'ai eu le vertige. At the top of the tower, I felt dizzy.
Avoir le vertige — to feel dizzy, or to be afraid of heights.

The word, taken apart.
le vertige is a learned borrowing from Latin vertīgō "a turning round, dizziness", from vertere "to turn" (Lewis & Short).

digérer is a learned borrowing from Latin dīgerere "to divide, to arrange, to digest", built on gerere "to carry" (Lewis & Short).
