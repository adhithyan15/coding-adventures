Japanese, chapter 198: Getting On and Off the Train.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
のる (noru) — to ride; to get on (a train or bus).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for open for business, then the Japanese for not yet open.

You'll want to know: のる.
のる — noru — "to ride; to get on (a train or bus)".
まいあさ でんしゃに のります。 — maiasa densha ni norimasu — "I take the train every morning."
Polite のります, norimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
のる (noru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
じてんしゃ (jitensha) — a bicycle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to ride, then the Japanese for to get off.

You'll want to know: じてんしゃ.
じてんしゃ — jitensha — "a bicycle".
じてんしゃで えきまで いきます。 — jitensha de eki made ikimasu — "I go to the station by bicycle."

The word, taken apart.
じてんしゃ (jitensha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "self" (Middle Chinese dzijH), ten, "to turn" (trjwenX) and sha, "cart" (tsyhae) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
