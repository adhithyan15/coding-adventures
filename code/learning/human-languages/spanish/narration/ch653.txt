Spanish, chapter 653: La puerta de embarque.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
embarcar — to board; to embark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the label, then the Spanish for the backpack.

You'll want to know: embarcar.
embarcar — "to board; to embark".
Los pasajeros ya pueden embarcar. Passengers can now board.
Used for planes as well as ships.

The word, taken apart.
embarcar is built on barca "boat", which is inherited from Late Latin barca (EtymDB). Getting on a plane is still getting on a boat.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bandeja — the tray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the boarding, then the Spanish for the detector.

You'll want to know: la bandeja.
la bandeja — "the tray".
Pon el móvil en la bandeja. Put your phone in the tray.
At security your things go in una bandeja; on a plane, la bandeja is the seat tray.

The word, taken apart.
bandeja was borrowed from Portuguese bandeja (EtymDB), which is usually linked to banda "side"; the details are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la escala — the stopover; the scale; the ladder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the detector, then the Spanish for the tray.

You'll want to know: la escala.
la escala — "the stopover; the scale; the ladder".
El vuelo hace una escala de dos horas. The flight has a two-hour stopover.
Sin escalas — non-stop.

The word, taken apart.
escala comes from Latin scāla "a flight of steps, a ladder" (EtymDB), from scandere "to climb". The stopover sense came from sea travel, where an escala was a port of call.
