## Assessment: the pre-A1 task inventory, and a real ladder named but not borrowed

Kannada's pre-A1 four-skill task inventory is checked in, with the assessment
specification it cites. `reading-reach` now measures the track — the chapter-76
passage's 31 words against a 1-word ceiling, **3/3 parts in reach**, with a floor
committed.

**The Kannada Sahitya Parishat's four exams are consequential.** Kannada
Pravesha, Kava, Jaana and Ratna: a state government employee who clears them is
exempted from their own department's Kannada test. That is not a hobbyist
certificate — it decides whether a qualification requirement keeps hanging over
someone.

It is also, for that same reason, **not this book's ladder**. It is aimed at
state employees and schoolchildren *inside* Karnataka — people already living in
the language who need to certify literacy in it — rather than at someone
starting from no Kannada somewhere else, and it publishes no per-skill structure
or pass rule. The spec names the audience mismatch rather than implying the exam
is not serious.

**What the writing paper scores** is the **ಒತ್ತಕ್ಷರ**, the subscript consonant.
Kannada hangs a reduced second consonant *below* the base letter rather than
beside it, so a line occupies three tiers and a reader's eye has to travel down
as well as along — the corpus builds 1,488 of them. Beside it, the **rounded
letterform**: Kannada rounds nearly every letter because the script was cut into
palm leaf for a thousand years and a straight stroke splits a leaf along its
grain, and rounded shapes are easier to tell apart at a glance. A hand that
straightens them has made the page harder to read, so it is scored rather than
tolerated as style.

**The writing ramp, measured.** 50 script lessons and **not one writing-stage
directive**, while the pre-A1 paper requires delayed recall and dictation.
