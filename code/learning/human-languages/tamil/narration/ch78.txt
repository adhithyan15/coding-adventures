Tamil, chapter 78: Why, and Because.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ஏன் (ēṉ) — why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read சொல். Then say: I think this is a good house.

You'll want to know: ஏன்.
ஏன் (ēṉ) — "why".
Chapter forty gave you the shape of every Tamil question word: they begin on an e-. என்ன, எங்கே, எப்படி, எவ்வளவு. ஏன் (ēṉ) belongs to the same family, and it is the one that asks for a reason.
It holds a long ஏ where the others hold a short எ. The family is real; the length is part of this particular member.
On its own it is a whole question:
ஏன் (ēṉ)? — "Why?"
Or in front of a sentence:
ஏன் (ēṉ) தேநீர் வேண்டும்? — "Why do you want tea?"
This is the first question in the book that cannot be answered by naming a thing. Its answer is a sentence, and the next lesson is the word that lets you give one.

What you've built.
The sixth member of a question family whose pattern you have known since chapter forty.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) — because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Give an opinion about a house. Then ask why.

You'll want to know: ஏனென்றால்.
ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) — "because".
It is long, and it is long for a reason you can already read. Take it apart:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
piece: ஏன், ēṉ, why — last lesson.
piece: என்றால், eṉṟāl, if it is said.
"If you ask why —". That is the whole word. Tamil answers a why by repeating the question and then going on.
என்றால் is the same verb you met one chapter ago in என்று. There it closed a quotation; here it closes a question and turns it into a hinge.
தேநீர் வேண்டும், ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) எனக்குத் தாகம்.
tēnīr vēṇḍum, ēṉeṉṟāl enakkut tākam.
"I want tea, because I am thirsty."
The second half is a whole sentence — the dative one from chapter six, to me, thirst. ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) joins it on without changing either side.
Everyday speech shortens it to ஏன்னா, the same wearing-down that made ஆனால் into ஆனா.

What you've built.
You can ask for a reason and give one.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ஏன் (ēṉ) — the shortest question in the book.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ஏன் — two shapes, and one of them is a whole letter and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: ask why, then answer with because.

Script you'll notice: ஏன் — two shapes, and one of them is a whole letter.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
piece: ஏ, ē, the free-standing long e, because the word opens on it.
piece: ன், ṉ, ன with a புள்ளி.
ஏ, ன் becomes ஏன் (ēṉ).
Two shapes. Nothing else in this book is shorter and still a question.
Now set it beside the question word you read in chapter twenty-nine:
எப்படி opens on எ, ஏன் (ēṉ) opens on ஏ.
Same family, different length, and Tamil gives each length its own letter rather than a mark on one shape. That is why both had to be learnt separately, and why a reader who has both can now tell an eppaḍi from an ēṉ before hearing either.
The closing ன் is the alveolar n you first wrote in chapter seven, the same one that ends நான்.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the two letters that hold the two lengths]
[your turn — recall: ask ēṉ?, then answer with ēṉeṉṟāl, then read ஏன்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say eṉṟu, nel and mādam — three distances back — then ask ஏன் (ēṉ) about one of them]

Lesson 4 of 5.
அதனால் (adaṉāl) — because of that, so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read ஏன் (ēṉ). Then say because.

You'll want to know: அதனால்.
அதனால் (adaṉāl) — "because of that", "so".
ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) puts the reason after the result. அதனால் (adaṉāl) puts it before, and points back at it.
எனக்குத் தாகம். அதனால் (adaṉāl) தேநீர் வேண்டும்.
enakkut tākam. adaṉāl tēnīr vēṇḍum.
"I am thirsty. So I want tea."
Same two facts as the last lesson, in the other order. Both are ordinary Tamil, and which one you reach for depends on which half you want to lead with.
The word is அது — "that one", from chapter forty — carrying an ending that means by. By that. It can only point backwards, which is why it never opens a conversation.

What you've built.
The reason can now go on either side of the result.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — flip: say the same two facts with ēṉeṉṟāl instead]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say ēṉeṉṟāl, then read ஏன், then say adaṉāl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: read சொல், say pul and say āṇḍu — three distances back — then ask ஏன் (ēṉ) about one of them]

Lesson 5 of 5.
-க்காக (-kkāga) — what a thing is for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read ஏன் (ēṉ). Then say: I am thirsty, so I want tea.

Grammar lens: a reason that is not a sentence.
ஏனென்றால் (ēṉeṉṟāl) and அதனால் (adaṉāl) both join a whole sentence. Sometimes the reason is not a sentence at all — it is a person, or a thing.
-க்காக (-kkāga) is the ending for that. It grows on the dative -உக்கு you have had since chapter six.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: உங்கள். with the ending: உங்களுக்காக. it means: for you.
word: நண்பன். with the ending: நண்பனுக்காக. it means: for a friend.
உங்களுக்காக தேநீர். — uṅgaḷukkāga tēnīr. — "Tea for you."
Hear the -உக்கு still inside it. Chapter six taught that ending as to or for; -க்காக (-kkāga) sharpens it into for the sake of, which is purpose rather than direction.
This is the third answer to ஏன் (ēṉ) in one chapter: a following sentence, a preceding sentence, and now a single word.

What you've built.
A whole chapter about reasons: asking for one, giving one after, giving one before, and naming who a thing is for.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: uṅgaḷukkāga]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: uṅgaḷukkāga tēnīr]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: ask ēṉ? and answer it three ways — with ēṉeṉṟāl, with adaṉāl, and with uṅgaḷukkāga]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say payir, -um … -um and … eṉṟu niṉaikkiṟēṉ — three distances back — then ask ஏன் about one of them]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which older ending is inside -க்காக (-kkāga)? (-உக்கு, the dative from chapter six.)
