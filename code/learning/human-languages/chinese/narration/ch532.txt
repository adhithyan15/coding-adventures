Mandarin Chinese, chapter 532: Hoping for More.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
有望 (yǒuwàng) — with good hopes of it; likely to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a misfortune, then the Mandarin for to lose one's grip.

You'll want to know: 有望.
有望 — yǒuwàng — "with good hopes of it; likely to happen".
如果天气好飞机就有望正点起飞 rúguǒ tiānqì hǎo fēijī jiù yǒuwàng zhèngdiǎn qǐfēi — If the weather is good, the plane should take off on time.
The new character, in pieces: 望 (wàng) — eleven strokes in three pieces: on the top left a dot, a bar and a short vertical that turns right, three strokes; on the top right 月, four strokes; and 王 at the foot, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
有望 (yǒuwàng) is built from 有 'to have' and wàng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwX and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to look out for' and, after an older dictionary, as watching for someone far away to come back.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Script — a small piece and 月, then 王 under them.
望 (wàng).
Three pieces: two side by side on top, then one across the foot. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top left piece, three strokes: a dot, a bar under it, then a short vertical that turns right along the bottom.
The top right piece, 月, four strokes, as you wrote it: a stroke down that falls to the left, a bar that turns down and hooks, then two short bars inside.
The bottom piece, 王, four strokes, as you wrote it: a bar, a second bar, a vertical down through them, then the long bar at the foot.
3 + 4 + 4 = eleven.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 3 of 6.
bābude — to be dying to; to be eager for (colloquial).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lose one's grip, then the Mandarin for with good hopes of it.

You'll want to know: bābude.
bābude — "to be dying to; to be eager for (colloquial)".
wǒ bābude mǎshàng fàngjià — I'm dying for the holidays to start right away.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 不 and 得 are both said light here, bābude, so 不 does not change its tone.

The word, taken apart.
bābude is built from bā, 不 'not' and 得, all inherited from Middle Chinese, pae, pjut and tok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the name of a region in the west and of a creature; the sense 'to long for' is not given there, and how the three came to mean 'to be dying to' is not settled.

You'll want to know: 期望.
期望 — qīwàng — "to expect much of someone; expectations".
yàoshi nǐ qīwàng tài gāo jiù huì shīwàng — If you expect too much, you'll be disappointed.

The word, taken apart.
期望 (qīwàng) is built from 期 'a set time' and 望 (wàng) 'to look out for', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gi and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a time agreed on'.
