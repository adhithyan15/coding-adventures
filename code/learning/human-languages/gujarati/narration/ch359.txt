Gujarati, chapter 359: Honour and Shame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઇજ્જત (ijjat) — one's good name; honour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for cooperation, then the Gujarati for to keep up.

You'll want to know: ઇજ્જત.
ઇજ્જત — ijjat — "one's good name; honour".
ગામમાં તેના કુટુંબની ઘણી ઇજ્જત છે. gāmmā̃ tenā kuṭumbnī ghaṇī ijjat che. His family is highly respected in the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઇજ્જત (ijjat) is usually traced to Arabic ʿizzat, "might, honour", through Persian.

અમને અમારા ગામનું ગૌરવ છે. amne amārā gāmnũ gaurav che. We are proud of our village.

Lesson 3 of 5.
શરમ (śaram) — shame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for one's good name, then the Gujarati for dignity.

You'll want to know: શરમ.
શરમ — śaram — "shame".
ખોટું બોલ્યા પછી તેને શરમ આવી. khoṭũ bolyā pachī tene śaram āvī. After telling a lie he felt ashamed.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
શરમ (śaram) is usually traced to Persian sharm, "shame, modesty". શરમાવું (śarmāvũ), "to feel shy", is built on it.

You'll want to know: નમવું.
નમવું — namvũ — "to bow".
બાળકો ગુરુ આગળ નમે છે. bāḷko guru āgaḷ name che. The children bow before the teacher.
Past (he …): તે મંદિરમાં નમ્યો, te mandirmā̃ namyo.

The word, taken apart.
નમવું (namvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit námati, "bends, bows"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb (CDIAL 6956).
