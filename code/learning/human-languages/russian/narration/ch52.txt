Russian, chapter 52: Book, Pen, Paper, Notebook, Pencil.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
книга (kníga) — a book.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a knife, then the Russian for a fork.

You'll want to know: книга.
книга (kníga) — "a book".

The word, taken apart.
книга (kníga) from Proto-Slavic kŭniga. Vasmer, following Räsänen, takes it as a loan through early Turkic, Danube Bulgar küiniv, ultimately from Chinese k'üen, "a scroll"; Hungarian könyv, "book", has the same source. He finds the older Assyrian account less likely.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ручка (rúchka) — a pen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a fork, then the Russian for a book.

You'll want to know: ручка.
ручка (rúchka) — "a pen". Literally "a little hand".

The word, taken apart.
ручка (rúchka) is рука, "hand", with -ка, "little"; к turns to ч before it. The word means "a little hand", then "a handle", then "a pen", the thing you hold. Vasmer relates рука to Lithuanian rankà, "hand", and a Lithuanian verb for gathering.

Lesson 3 of 5.
бумага (bumága) — paper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a book, then the Russian for a pen.

You'll want to know: бумага.
бумага (bumága) — "paper".

The word, taken apart.
бумага (bumága) — Vasmer thinks it is most probably from Italian: Korsch derived it from bombagio, "cotton", and most early paper in Russia was Italian. Greek bómbyx, "silk", cannot explain the Russian form, he says.

The word, taken apart.
тетрадь (tetrád) from Byzantine Greek tetrádion, from tetrás, "a group of four", a sheet folded into four leaves (Vasmer).

The word, taken apart.
карандаш (karandásh) from Turkic karadaš, "black stone", as in Turkish karataş, "black slate" (Vasmer): kara, "black", and taş, "stone".
