Spanish, chapter 708: Celos y envidia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
los celos — jealousy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for anger, then the Spanish for to annoy.

You'll want to know: los celos.
los celos — "jealousy".
Los celos destruyen una pareja. Jealousy destroys a couple.
Plural in Spanish: tener celos — to be jealous.

The word, taken apart.
celos is the plural of celo "zeal", which comes from Latin zēlus, borrowed from Greek zēlos "zeal, eager rivalry, jealousy" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
celoso — jealous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to annoy, then the Spanish for jealousy.

You'll want to know: celoso.
celoso — "jealous".
Mi novio es muy celoso. My boyfriend is very jealous.
Ser celoso is your character; estar celoso is how you feel now.

The word, taken apart.
celoso is inherited from Late Latin zēlōsus, built on zēlus "zeal" (EtymDB). English jealous, through French, and zealous both go back to the same Latin family.

You'll want to know: la envidia.
la envidia — "envy".
Me da envidia tu viaje. I envy you your trip.
Envidia sana — healthy envy, the friendly kind.

The word, taken apart.
envidia was borrowed from Latin invidia "envy", built on invidēre "to eye with ill will", from in and vidēre "to see" (EtymDB).
