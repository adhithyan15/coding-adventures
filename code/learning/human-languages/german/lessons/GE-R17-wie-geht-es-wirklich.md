# Wie geht es dir? — and the whole ladder of answers

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-DU-SIE-01, GE-PRAGMATICS-WELLBEING-REGISTER-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Ask a friend how they are. Then ask a stranger. One word moves, and
the capital letter moves with it — say which.

## Guided Practice: four rungs, top to bottom
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WIE-GEHT-ES-DIR-01, GE-SOUND-GUT-01, GE-LEX-SO-LALA-01, GE-PRAGMATICS-ES-GEHT-SHRUG-02] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: the question, informally — *Wie geht es dir?*]
- [YOU SAY: the good answer — *gut*]
- [YOU SAY: the middle, sing-song — *so lala*]
- [YOU SAY: the shrug — *es geht*]
- [YOU SAY: the question, formally — *Wie geht es Ihnen?*]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-PRAGMATICS-ES-GEHT-SHRUG-02, GE-PRAGMATICS-WELLBEING-REGISTER-02] -->

**es geht** says *it goes* and nothing more — and that is the point. German does
not wave *how are you* past as a greeting, so an answer with no adjective is heard
as an answer: somewhere between *I'm managing* and *don't ask*, and it may well
draw a follow-up.

**dir → Ihnen** makes the same fact from the other side: one word that changes not
the question but who you are allowed to be to the person.

## Guided Practice: and the first thing that can be wrong
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-COMPOUND-KOPFSCHMERZEN-04, GE-SOUND-KOPF-03] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: the head — *Kopf*, and hold that final **pf** as one sound]
- [YOU SAY: what you have when it hurts — *Kopfschmerzen*]

A compound built the ordinary German way: the part of you, then the pains, run
together into a single word. Say the seam out loud once, then stop hearing it.

## Guided Practice — head and hand, from memory
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KOPF-02, GE-ETYMON-KOPF-05, GE-LEX-HAUPT-02, GE-ETYMON-HAUPT-03, GE-SOUND-GRIMMS-LAW-04, GE-CULTURE-HEAD-CONTAINERS-05, GE-LEX-HAND-02, GE-SOUND-HAND-03, GE-ETYMON-HAND-04, GE-ETYMON-HAND-MANUS-05] -->

- What did *der Kopf* first mean? (A **cup** — Late Latin *cuppa*, English
  *cup*.) The older head-word? (**Haupt**, as in *Hauptstadt* — beside *caput*
  and *head*, its *k → h* is **Grimm's law**.)
- Which French word took the same road? (***tête***, once a pot.)
- Say "the hand", then "the hands". (***die Hand***, *d* said as *t*; ***die
  Hände***, *d* back.)
- Is *Hand* kin to French *main*? (**No** — Germanic *\*handuz*, English
  *hand*; *manus* came in only as loans, like *Manuskript*.)

## Wrap-up Recall
