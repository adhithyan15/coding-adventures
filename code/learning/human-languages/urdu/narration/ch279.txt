Urdu, chapter 279: What the Witness Saw.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پہچاننا (pahchānnā) — to recognize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a lock-up, then the Urdu for a statement.

You'll want to know: پہچاننا.
پہچاننا — pahchānnā — "to recognize".
اگر میں قریب ہوتا تو اسے پہچانتا۔ agar maiṅ qarīb hotā to use pahchāntā. If I had been closer, I would have recognized him.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے چور کو پہچانا, us ne chor ko pahchānā.

The word, taken apart.
پہچاننا (pahchānnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin pahcānnā under Sanskrit pratyabhijānāti, "recognizes" (CDIAL 8637). A person recognized takes کو, and the verb stays masculine: چور کو پہچانا.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to recognize, then the Urdu for a person's description.

You'll want to know: شبہ.
شبہ — shubah — "a suspicion".
مجھے پڑوسی پر شبہ ہے۔ mujhe paṛosī par shubah hai. I suspect the neighbour.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
شبہ (shubah) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic shubha, "a doubt, an uncertainty". The person suspected takes پر: پڑوسی پر شبہ.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a person's description, then the Urdu for a suspicion.

منظر — manzar — "a scene; a sight".
میں وہ منظر نہیں بھولوں گا۔ maiṅ voh manzar nahīṅ bhūlūṅ gā. I will not forget that scene.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
منظر (manzar) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic manẓar, "a place looked at, a sight". It is what lay before the eyes at one moment.

Lesson 5 of 5.
نظر آنا (nazar ānā) — to be seen; to come into view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a suspicion, then the Urdu for a scene.

You'll want to know: نظر آنا.
نظر آنا — nazar ānā — "to be seen; to come into view".
مجھے کھڑکی میں ایک آدمی نظر آیا۔ mujhe khiṛkī meṅ ek ādmī nazar āyā. I saw a man at the window.
Past (he …): وہ نظر آیا, voh nazar āyā.

The word, taken apart.
نظر آنا (nazar ānā) is built from nazar, a look, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic naẓar, "a looking", and آنا: a thing comes into your look. The one who sees takes مجھے, and the verb follows the thing seen: آدمی نظر آیا.
