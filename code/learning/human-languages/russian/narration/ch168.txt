Lesson 1 of 5.
свадьба (svádba) — a wedding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for petrol, then the Russian for a petrol station.

You'll want to know: свадьба.
свадьба (svádba) — "a wedding".

The word, taken apart.
свадьба (svádba) — Old Russian сватьба; it comes from сват, "a matchmaker, an in-law" (Vasmer), and first meant "match-making". Vasmer connects сват with свой, "one's own".

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a wedding, then the Russian for a bridegroom.

You'll want to know: невеста.
невеста (nevésta) — "a bride, a fiancée".

The word, taken apart.
невеста (nevésta) — Vasmer still finds the old explanation best: "the unknown one", не, "not", with the root of ведать, "to know", for the new bride is a stranger in her husband's house. Trubachev's note cites other views.

Lesson 4 of 5.
родственник (ródstvennik) — a relative.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bridegroom, then the Russian for a bride.

You'll want to know: родственник.
родственник (ródstvennik) — "a relative".

The word, taken apart.
родственник (ródstvennik) is built on родство, "kinship", from род, "family, kin", with -ник. Vasmer relates род to Latvian rads, "a relative", and Sanskrit várdhati, "grows".

The word, taken apart.
пенсионер (pensionér) comes from French pensionnaire, built on pension, from Latin pēnsiō, "a payment", from pendere, "to weigh, to pay".
