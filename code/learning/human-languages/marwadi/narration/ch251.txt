Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 251: Neck, Chest and Shoulders.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गरदन (gardan) — the neck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for the waving of a lamp in worship, then the Marwadi for an uproar.

You'll want to know: गरदन.
गरदन — gardan — "the neck".
म्हारी गरदन लांबी है। mhārī gardan lā̃bī hai. My neck is long.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गरदन (gardan) is usually traced to Persian gardan, "neck".

Lesson 2 of 5.
कांधो (kā̃dho) — a shoulder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for an uproar, then the Marwadi for the neck.

You'll want to know: कांधो.
कांधो — kā̃dho — "a shoulder".
म्हारो कांधो भारी लागै है। mhāro kā̃dho bhārī lāgai hai. My shoulder feels heavy.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कांधो (kā̃dho) is inherited from Sanskrit skandha, "shoulder". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi kā̃dhā (CDIAL 13627).

म्हारी छाती मांय दरद है। mhārī chhātī mā̃y darad hai. I have a pain in my chest.

छाती (chhātī) is inherited from a reconstructed chātti, "chest, breast"; CDIAL lists Marwari chātī under it (CDIAL 5014).
