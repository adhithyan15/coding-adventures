# दाम (dām) — a price

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C72-COST-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the unit a price is counted in, and then say ten of them.

## You'll want to know: दाम
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C72-COST-03]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01] -->

**दाम** (*dām*) — "a price".

This one has travelled further than any word in the chapter. The Greek coin was
the *drachmē*; traders carried the name east; Sanskrit wrote it द्रम्म
(*dramma*); Hindi wore it down to दाम. A Greek weight of silver ended up as the
ordinary Hindi word for what a thing costs.

Now put it with a word you have had since the second chapter. **क्या** is
"what", and

> **दाम क्या है?** — *dām kyā hai?* — "what is the price?"

is a whole question, built from one new word and two you already own. This is
the sentence a market runs on, and you can now say it.

You have also seen दाम before, standing in a row of look-alikes beside **नाम**
and **काम** when you were learning to pick a name out of a line. Now it means
something.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Three: पैसा, रुपया, दाम — and a question you can ask with the third.
