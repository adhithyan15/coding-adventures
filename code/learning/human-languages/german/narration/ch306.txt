German, chapter 306: Herzlichen Glückwunsch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gratulieren — to congratulate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the ceremony, then the German for to open officially.

You'll want to know: gratulieren.
gratulieren — "to congratulate".
Alle gratulierten ihr zum Geburtstag. Everyone congratulated her on her birthday.
With the dative and zu: Ich gratuliere dir zum Abitur.

The word, taken apart.
gratulieren is a learned borrowing from Latin grātulārī "to wish joy", built on grātus "pleasing, thankful" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Glückwunsch — the good wishes, congratulations.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to open officially, then the German for to congratulate.

You'll want to know: der Glückwunsch.
der Glückwunsch — "the good wishes, congratulations".
Er schickte ihr einen Glückwunsch zum Geburtstag. He sent her birthday wishes.
Plural: die Glückwünsche. Herzlichen Glückwunsch! — congratulations!

The word, taken apart.
Glückwunsch joins Glück "luck", Middle High German gelücke (EtymDB), and the inherited Wunsch "wish".

Lesson 3 of 5.
überreichen — to present, to hand over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to congratulate, then the German for the good wishes.

You'll want to know: überreichen.
überreichen — "to present, to hand over".
Der Chef überreichte ihm ein Geschenk. The boss presented him with a gift.
Not separable: sie hat überreicht. Formal.

The word, taken apart.
überreichen joins über "over" and reichen "to hand, to reach", from Old High German reihhen (EtymDB), a cousin of English reach.

Lesson 4 of 5.
beschenken — to give presents to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the good wishes, then the German for to present.

You'll want to know: beschenken.
beschenken — "to give presents to".
Die Großeltern beschenkten die Kinder reich. The grandparents gave the children lots of presents.
The person is the object: jemanden beschenken.

The word, taken apart.
beschenken is built on schenken "to give as a present", from Old High German scenken "to pour in, to give drink to" (EtymDB): first a drink poured for a guest, then any gift.

Lesson 5 of 5.
auspacken — to unwrap, to unpack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to present, then the German for to give presents to.

You'll want to know: auspacken.
auspacken — "to unwrap, to unpack".
Das Kind packte die Geschenke sofort aus. The child unwrapped the presents at once.
Separable. The opposite of einpacken, already known.

The word, taken apart.
auspacken joins aus "out" and packen "to pack", which was borrowed from Middle Low German packen (EtymDB).
