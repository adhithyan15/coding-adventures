Lesson 1 of 5.
پیچ کس (pechkas) — a screwdriver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for friendship, then the Urdu for shame.

You'll want to know: پیچ کس.
پیچ کس — pechkas — "a screwdriver".

The word, taken apart.
پیچ کس (pechkas) is "screw-turner". It joins Persian pēch, "a twist, a screw", and kash, "pulling, drawing".

Lesson 2 of 5.
اوزار (auzār) — a tool.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for shame, then the Urdu for a screwdriver.

You'll want to know: اوزار.
اوزار — auzār — "a tool".

The word, taken apart.
اوزار (auzār) is a loan, usually traced through Persian to Arabic awzār, the plural of wizr, "a burden", which Lane also gives as "weapons, the gear of war" (root w-z-r). Persian and Urdu use it for tools.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نل (nal) — a tap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a tool, then the Urdu for a pipe.

You'll want to know: نل.
نل — nal — "a tap".

The word, taken apart.
نل (nal) is "a tube". CDIAL lists Hindi nalā, "hollow reed, windpipe", under naḍaka, "hollow of a bone", a form beside Sanskrit naḍa, "a kind of reed", and notes possible Dravidian contact (CDIAL 6936).
