# One, two, or several — me/us or you

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C262-OBJECT-PERSON-NUMBER-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the decision aloud: person first, then number. Do not recite a
table; answer each scene separately.

## Guided Practice — choose before you speak

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE, SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03, SA-PRON-C261-AVAM-01, SA-PRON-C261-YUVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C261-DUAL-OBJECTS-03, SA-PRON-C262-ASMAN-01, SA-PRON-C262-YUSMAN-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C262-OBJECT-PERSON-NUMBER-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s each]
- [YOU SAY: the speaker alone is seen — *saḥ mām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: one familiar addressee is seen — *saḥ tvām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: the speaker and one companion are seen — *saḥ āvām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: two familiar addressees are seen — *saḥ yuvām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: several people including the speaker are seen — *saḥ asmān paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: several familiar addressees are seen — *saḥ yuṣmān paśyati*]

## Grammar Lens: the boundary of this chapter

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C262-OBJECT-PERSON-NUMBER-03] -->

These six are the independent full forms taught here. Classical Sanskrit also
has short alternatives in particular positions, and it has third-person and
respectful object forms. Those belong to later lessons. This set is **not the
whole Classical Sanskrit pronoun system**; it is the complete first- and
second-person full-form choice across one person, two people, and several
people.

Source for the full and short forms: William Dwight Whitney, *Sanskrit
Grammar* (1889), §491.

## Wrap-up Recall

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03, SA-PRON-C261-AVAM-01, SA-PRON-C261-YUVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C261-DUAL-OBJECTS-03, SA-PRON-C262-ASMAN-01, SA-PRON-C262-YUSMAN-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C262-OBJECT-PERSON-NUMBER-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Without a table, answer six prompts: me, you one, us two, you two,
us several, you several. (*Mām; tvām; āvām; yuvām; asmān; yuṣmān.*)
