Kannada, chapter 316: Bigger, Best and How Long.
3 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ಮನೆಗಿಂತ (maneginta) — bigger than, best of all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "big", "small" and "more". (doḍḍa, cikka, heccu.) Then "I would love to go to Mysuru one day." (nanage ondu dina maisūrige hōgabēku anta āse ide.)

You'll want to know: -ಗಿಂತ, than.
ಈ ಮನೆ ಆ ಮನೆಗಿಂತ ದೊಡ್ಡದು. ī mane ā maneginta doḍḍadu. — "This house is bigger than that house."
The thing you measure against takes -ಗಿಂತ, "than". It is the "to" ending with -ಇಂತ on it: ಮನೆಗೆ becomes ಮನೆಗಿಂತ (maneginta); ನನಗೆ becomes ನನಗಿಂತ (nanaginta, "than me"). Where "to" is -ಕ್ಕೆ, "than" is -ಕ್ಕಿಂತ: ಮರಕ್ಕೆ becomes ಮರಕ್ಕಿಂತ (marakkinta, "than the tree").
The adjective needs no "-er". ದೊಡ್ಡ stays ದೊಡ್ಡ; at the end of the sentence it takes -ದು: ದೊಡ್ಡದು (doḍḍadu, "(it is) big"), ಚಿಕ್ಕದು (cikkadu, "(it is) small"), like the ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು of "it is good".
ಬಸ್ಸಿಗಿಂತ ರೈಲು ಬೇಗ ಹೋಗುತ್ತದೆ. bassiginta railu bēga hōguttade. — "The train goes faster than the bus."
ಕಾಫಿಗಿಂತ ಚಹಾ ನನಗೆ ಹೆಚ್ಚು ಇಷ್ಟ. kāphiginta cahā nanage heccu iṣṭa. — "I like tea more than coffee."
With a liking, ಹೆಚ್ಚು, "more", says how much more.

You'll want to know: ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ, most of all.
ಇದು ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ ಚಿಕ್ಕ ಕೋಣೆ. idu ellakkinta cikka kōṇe. — "This is the smallest room."
ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ is ಎಲ್ಲ, "all", the ಎಲ್ಲಾ you know, with -ಕ್ಕಿಂತ: "than all", so "most". For people it is ಎಲ್ಲರಿಗಿಂತ (ellariginta, "than everyone"):
ಅವಳು ಎಲ್ಲರಿಗಿಂತ ಬೇಗ ಬಂದಳು. avaḷu ellariginta bēga bandaḷu. — "She came earliest of all."

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Than the house"? (ಮನೆಗಿಂತ (maneginta).) "Than me"? (ನನಗಿಂತ.) "It is bigger"? (ದೊಡ್ಡದು: no "-er".) "Most of all"? (ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ; for people, ಎಲ್ಲರಿಗಿಂತ.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
ಎರಡು ತಿಂಗಳಿಂದ (eraḍu tiṅgaḷinda) — how long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "time" (samaya) and "until" (varege). Then "This house is bigger than that house." (ī mane ā maneginta doḍḍadu.)

You'll want to know: -ಇಂದ, for … now.
ನಾನು ಎರಡು ತಿಂಗಳಿಂದ ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲಿಯುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ. nānu eraḍu tiṅgaḷinda kannaḍa kaliyuttiddēne. — "I have been learning Kannada for two months."
ತಿಂಗಳು (tiṅgaḷu, "a month") with -ಇಂದ, "from": "from two months (back to now)". The -ಇಂದ of ಇಲ್ಲಿಂದ, "from here", now measures time. The verb stays in the present, because the learning is still going on: ಕಲಿಯುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ is ಕಲಿಯುತ್ತ + ಇದ್ದೇನೆ, "I am, learning", the present twin of ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ. English says "have been"; Kannada says "am".
ನೀವು ಎಷ್ಟು ಸಮಯದಿಂದ ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀರಿ? nīvu eṣṭu samayadinda illi iddīri? — "How long have you been here?"
ಇದ್ದೀರಿ (iddīri, "you are"), said with respect. The answer: ಮೂರು ವರ್ಷದಿಂದ (mūru varṣadinda, "for three years").
A stretch that is over takes no ending at all: ನಾನು ಒಂದು ವಾರ ಮೈಸೂರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೆ (nānu ondu vāra maisūrinalli idde, "I was in Mysuru for a week"). ವಾರ (vāra, "a week").

You'll want to know: from … until.
ನಾನು ಬೆಳಗ್ಗೆ ಒಂಬತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಯಿಂದ ಸಂಜೆ ಐದು ಗಂಟೆಯವರೆಗೆ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ. nānu beḷagge ombattu gaṇṭeyinda sañje aidu gaṇṭeyavarege kelasa māḍuttēne. — "I work from nine in the morning until five in the evening."
-ಇಂದ marks where the stretch starts, ವರೆಗೆ where it ends: ಗಂಟೆಯಿಂದ … ಗಂಟೆಯವರೆಗೆ. The same pair works for places: ಇಲ್ಲಿಂದ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣದವರೆಗೆ (illinda nildāṇadavarege, "from here to the station").

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"For two months now"? (ಎರಡು ತಿಂಗಳಿಂದ (eraḍu tiṅgaḷinda).) Which tense goes with it? (The present, for what is still going on.) "How long?" (ಎಷ್ಟು ಸಮಯದಿಂದ?) "From … until"? (-ಇಂದ … ವರೆಗೆ.)
