# बस से — the same three letters, twice over

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C48-REPLY-02, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You already read **बस** as a reply meaning *enough, that's all*. Read
it again in **बस से** and notice it cannot possibly mean that here.

## You'll want to know: बस से
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02]; assesses=[] -->

**बस** — the vehicle. **बस से** — by bus.

**मैं बस से स्कूल जाता हूँ।** — I go to school by bus.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02, HI-LEX-C48-REPLY-02] -->

These are **two different words** that happen to be spelled alike:

| word | where it comes from | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| बस | Persian *bas* | enough, that's all |
| बस | English *bus* | the vehicle |

The first has been in the language for centuries. The second arrived with the
vehicle, and English had shortened it from *omnibus* — Latin for **"for
everybody"** — which is a fair description of a bus and a strange thing to find
inside three Devanagari letters.

Nothing marks them apart on the page. **The sentence does the work**: a reply on
its own is the old word, and a noun with a postposition behind it is the
vehicle.

## Grammar Lens: reading the one from the other
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C81-SCHOOL-01] -->

| what you see | which word |
|---|---|
| बस। | enough |
| बस से | by bus |

The moment **से** follows it, it is a thing you can travel in. That is the sort
of decision a reading paper tests without ever saying so, and you now make it
from the shape of the sentence rather than from the word.
