Mandarin Chinese, chapter 70: More to Eat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ròu — meat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dinner, then the Mandarin for tasty.

You'll want to know: ròu.
ròu — "meat".
wǒ bù chī ròu — I don't eat meat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ròu is inherited from Middle Chinese nyuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed with the pair 'bone and flesh'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 5.
bāozi — steamed bun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tasty, then the Mandarin for meat.

You'll want to know: bāozi.
bāozi — "steamed bun".
wǒ chī le sān ge bāozi — I ate three buns.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāozi is built from bāo 'to wrap', the same bāo as the word for bag, and the noun ending zi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, paew and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a filling wrapped in dough.

miàntiáo — "noodles".

The word, taken apart.
miàntiáo is built from miàn 'flour' and tiáo 'a twig, a strip', both inherited from Middle Chinese, menH and dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): strips of flour.

Lesson 4 of 5.
jiǎozi — dumpling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for steamed bun, then the Mandarin for noodles.

You'll want to know: jiǎozi.
jiǎozi — "dumpling".
xīngqītiān wǒmen chī jiǎozi — We have dumplings on Sunday.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎozi is built from jiǎo and the noun ending zi, which is inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The first has no reading in any dictionary we use, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tāng — soup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for noodles, then the Mandarin for dumpling.

You'll want to know: tāng.
tāng — "soup".
nǐ hē tāng ma — Would you like some soup?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tāng is inherited from Middle Chinese thang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'hot water'. Soup is not given there; from hot water to soup is the likely step, but it is not settled.
