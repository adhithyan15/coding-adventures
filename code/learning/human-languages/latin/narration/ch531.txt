Latin, chapter 531: Jobs in a great house.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ātriēnsis, ātriēnsis — a steward, house-manager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of a slave, then the Latin for a footman.

You'll want to know: ātriēnsis, ātriēnsis.
ātriēnsis, ātriēnsis — "a steward, house-manager".
Ātriēnsis servīs labōrem dat. The steward gives the slaves their work.
Masculine, third declension, from ātrium: the slave in charge of the hall and the house.

The word, taken apart.
ātriēnsis is built on ātrium "hall" with -ēnsis (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Iānitor ad iānuam sedet et hospitēs salūtat. The doorkeeper sits by the door and greets the guests.
Masculine, third declension, from iānua — door. English janitor.

The word, taken apart.
iānitor is built on iānua "door" with -tor (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English janitor comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vīlica, vīlicae — a farm housekeeper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a porter, then the Latin for a litter-bearer.

You'll want to know: vīlica, vīlicae.
vīlica, vīlicae — "a farm housekeeper".
Vīlica cēnam familiae rūsticae parat. The farm housekeeper prepares dinner for the farm slaves.
Feminine, first declension: the wife of the vīlicus, the farm manager. She ran the farm kitchen and stores.

The word, taken apart.
vīlica is the feminine of vīlicus "farm manager", built on vīlla "country house, farm" (Lewis & Short).
