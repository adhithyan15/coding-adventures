Persian, chapter 485: Worry and Stress.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اضطراب (ezterâb) — anxiety.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for depressed, then the Persian for sorry.

You'll want to know: اضطراب.
اضطراب (ezterâb) — "anxiety".
قبل از امتحان اضطراب دارم. qabl az emtehân ezterâb dâram. I feel anxious before an exam.
ezterâb dâshtan — to be anxious. Formal; in speech esteres is more usual.

The word, taken apart.
ezterâb is a loan, usually traced to Arabic iḍṭirāb, 'agitation, unrest'. EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
استرس (esteres) — stress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for sorry, then the Persian for anxiety.

You'll want to know: استرس.
استرس (esteres) — "stress".
کار زیاد به او استرس میدهد. kâr-e ziyâd be u esteres midahad. A lot of work stresses him out.
esteres dâshtan — to be stressed; esteres dâdan — to cause stress. Persian breaks up the st- cluster with vowels.

The word, taken apart.
esteres is a modern loan, usually traced to English stress, with vowels added to fit Persian syllables. EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
دلشوره (delshure) — a nagging worry; butterflies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for anxiety, then the Persian for stress.

You'll want to know: دلشوره.
دلشوره (delshure) — "a nagging worry; butterflies".
شب قبل از سفر دلشوره داشتم. shab-e qabl az safar delshure dâshtam. The night before the trip I had butterflies.
del 'heart' + shure 'stirring'. delshure dâshtan — to feel uneasy.

The word, taken apart.
delshure is built on del, 'heart', which EtymDB traces to Middle Persian (EtymDB), and shure, usually linked with shuridan, 'to be stirred up': a churning heart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
کلافه (kalâfe) — exasperated, fed up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for stress, then the Persian for a nagging worry.

You'll want to know: کلافه.
کلافه (kalâfe) — "exasperated, fed up".
او از گرمای تابستان کلافه شده است. u az garmâ-ye tâbestân kalâfe shode ast. She's worn out by the summer heat.
Colloquial and very common: kalâfe shodam — I'm fed up.

The word, taken apart.
kalâfe also names a skein of yarn, and a tangled skein is the picture behind the feeling. No source used here gives its history, and its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
آه کشیدن (âh keshidan) — to sigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a nagging worry, then the Persian for exasperated.

You'll want to know: آه کشیدن.
آه کشیدن (âh keshidan) — "to sigh".
او به پنجره نگاه کرد و آه کشید. u be panjere negâh kard va âh keshid. She looked at the window and sighed.
âh 'a sigh' + keshidan 'to pull, to draw'; present stem kesh-.

The word, taken apart.
âh is usually explained as imitative, built on the sound of a sigh itself, and keshidan, 'to pull, to draw', is inherited Persian: to draw a sigh.
