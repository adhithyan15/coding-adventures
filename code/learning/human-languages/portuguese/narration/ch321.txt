Portuguese, chapter 321: No Carnaval.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o carnaval — the carnival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pilgrim, then the Portuguese for the pilgrimage.

You'll want to know: o carnaval.
o carnaval — "the carnival".
No carnaval, as crianças vão mascaradas para a escola. At carnival the children go to school in fancy dress.
The days before Lent; often written with a capital, o Carnaval.

The word, taken apart.
carnaval came from Italian carnevale (EtymDB), from Medieval Latin carnelevale "the putting away of meat", built on carō "flesh" and levāre "to lift".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: o desfile.
o desfile — "the parade".
O desfile passou pela rua principal. The parade went down the main street.
Um desfile de moda — a fashion show.

The word, taken apart.
desfile is built on a verb that came from French défiler "to march in file", from fil "a thread", which goes back to Latin fīlum "a thread" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o disfarce — the disguise; a fancy-dress outfit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the parade, then the Portuguese for the mask.

You'll want to know: o disfarce.
o disfarce — "the disguise; a fancy-dress outfit".
Fiz o meu disfarce com papel e cola. I made my fancy-dress outfit out of paper and glue.
For carnival, people also say a fantasia in Brazil.

The word, taken apart.
disfarce is built on disfarçar "to disguise"; the origin of the verb is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
disfarçar — to disguise; to hide (a feeling).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the mask, then the Portuguese for the disguise.

You'll want to know: disfarçar.
disfarçar — "to disguise; to hide (a feeling)".
Ele disfarçou o medo com um sorriso. He hid his fear behind a smile.
Disfarçar-se de — to dress up as: disfarçou-se de pirata.

The word, taken apart.
disfarçar is usually linked to des- and an old word for a face or a farce, but the origin is not settled; EtymDB has no entry.
