# la instalación — the act, and the thing

## You'll want to know: la instalación
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01] -->

**la instalación** — the installation, the fitting: and also the thing
fitted.

*La instalación del aire acondicionado* — putting the air conditioning in.
*La instalación eléctrica es vieja* — the wiring is old. And in the plural,
the premises: *las instalaciones deportivas* — the sports facilities.

**Instalar** is the verb, for a boiler and for a program alike.

## Grammar Lens: -ción names the act and then the result
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-02, ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-01] -->

This double sense is not a quirk of one word. It is what **-ción** does:

| word | the act | the result |
|---|---|---|
| la instalación | fitting it | the wiring |
| la construcción | building it | the building |
| la decoración | decorating | the decor |

The noun names the doing, and then drifts onto whatever the doing left behind.
When you meet a *-ción* word, expect both readings and let the sentence pick:
*durante la instalación* is the act, *revisar la instalación* is the thing.

Every one is feminine, and every one takes an accent in the singular that it
loses in the plural — *instalación*, *instalaciones* — for the reason
*almacén* did.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-02, ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-14] -->

Old French **installer**: **in-** plus **stalle**, a stall — a seat in a
choir, from a Germanic word for a standing-place.

To *install* somebody was to put them **in their stall**: to seat a canon in
his place in the cathedral. The word began as ceremony, moved to furniture,
and now means putting a boiler on a wall or software on a machine.

The Germanic *stall* is still around in English for a horse's, in *stall* the
market stand, and in **stand** itself. A boiler and a cathedral canon are
installed with the same word because both have been put where they belong.
