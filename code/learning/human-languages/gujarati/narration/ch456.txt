Gujarati, chapter 456: Hurt Feelings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
દુભાવું (dubhāvũ) — to be hurt (in one's feelings).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to make someone cry, then the Gujarati for weeping.

You'll want to know: દુભાવું.
દુભાવું — dubhāvũ — "to be hurt (in one's feelings)".
મારા જવાબથી મીરા દુભાઈ ગઈ. mārā javābthī Mīrā dubhāī gaī. Mira was hurt by my answer.
Past (she …): તે દુભાઈ ગઈ, te dubhāī gaī.

The word, taken apart.
દુભાવું (dubhāvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit dabhnōti, "hurts, deceives"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verbs of this family, "to tease, vex" (CDIAL 6175).

Lesson 4 of 5.
અવગણના (avgaṇnā) — disregard; being ignored.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for unwelcome, then the Gujarati for a taunt.

You'll want to know: અવગણના.
અવગણના — avgaṇnā — "disregard; being ignored".
માતા અને પિતાની અવગણના ન કરવી જોઈએ. mātā ane pitānī avgaṇnā na karvī joīe. One should not neglect one's mother and father.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
અવગણના (avgaṇnā) is a tatsama, close to Sanskrit avagaṇana, "disregard, contempt", as Monier-Williams records it, with the feminine -ā.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઠેસ (ṭhes) — a knock; a hurt to one's pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a taunt, then the Gujarati for disregard.

You'll want to know: ઠેસ.
ઠેસ — ṭhes — "a knock; a hurt to one's pride".
તેના જવાબથી મને ઘણી ઠેસ લાગી. tenā javābthī mane ghaṇī ṭhes lāgī. His answer was a hard knock for me.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઠેસ (ṭhes) goes back to a reconstructed ṭhēcc-, "push"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "knock" (CDIAL 5511), and Gujarati also uses it for a knock to the feelings.
