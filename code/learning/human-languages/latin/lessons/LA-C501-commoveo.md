## You'll want to know: commoveō, commovēre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C501-AFFE-01]; assesses=[] -->

**commoveō, commovēre** — "to move deeply, disturb".

> Lacrimae fīliae patrem valdē commovent.
>
> *His daughter's tears move the father deeply.*

*commoveō, commovēre, commōvī, commōtum*. Of the body, to shake; of the mind, to stir or upset. English *commotion*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**commoveō** is built on *con-* and *moveō* "to move" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English *commotion* comes from the related noun *commōtiō*.
