## Fixed — every Bengali word lesson says where its word comes from

HL24 §9 asks every word lesson for an `etymology_hook` and an origin the learner can see. The Bengali track had 1,136 word lessons without a hook and 1,144 without a visible origin; both counts are now 0, and the three "taken apart" blocks that named no source now do.

- **What changed in the lessons.** 1,168 lessons were edited. 1,144 gained a `## The word, taken apart` block (`## The phrase, taken apart` for phrases) or an origin sentence in an existing section; 23 whose text already named an origin gained only a hook. Every hook agrees with its block and is at most 120 characters.
- **Sources.** Inherited words and Sanskrit loans follow Turner's CDIAL: 1,502 citations across 701 lessons and 573 entries, each checked by script that the entry exists, that its headword matches, and that the Bengali form stands among its rows in the right sense. Where CDIAL has the etymon but no Bengali row, the block names the Old or Middle Bengali, Assamese or Oriya form it gives instead. CDIAL's own hedges are carried (probably, perhaps, a query, a Dravidian, Munda or Austroasiatic source, a word taken from Hindi). Tatsamas and tadbhavas are told apart by sound change. Arabic loans (42 lessons) name their root in Lane's Lexicon and say they came through Persian; Persian loans are "usually traced to"; English, Portuguese and Chinese loans name the donor word.
- **Rough tally by origin.** About 343 inherited words, 218 Sanskrit loans, 268 compounds and phrases taken apart into their parts, 143 English loans, 92 Persian, 39 Arabic, 19 Portuguese or other.
- **Not settled.** 46 origins are said to be not settled, and in 32 more one part is (an ending such as *-ṭā*, *-gulo*, *-der* or *-mon*, or a first element such as *ābār*). Each was searched in CDIAL by form and by gloss across all its languages and its addenda first, and the closest forms found are named with their entry numbers where they are close enough to help.
- **Older passages corrected.** আমি is no longer said to come from *asmi* "I am" and to share a root with English *am*: CDIAL lists it under *asmad*, the stem of "we" (CDIAL 986). কাল "tomorrow" comes from *kalya* "dawn" (CDIAL 3104), not *kāla* "time". তুমি is traced to the plural *yuṣmad-* (CDIAL 10511). ধন্যবাদ's *dhanya* means "fortunate", not "worthy". থাকা's link to *sthā* is now "probably" (CDIAL 13737). আসা names CDIAL's *āsadati* and Chatterji's *āviśati* instead of calling the stem unknown. Two grammar-lesson hooks that asserted how নয় and নেই were formed now say the details are not settled.
- **Durations.** Nine `max_seconds` values were raised to exactly their computed length, all under 300 seconds: BN-C02-tomar-naam-ki 180 → 189, BN-C03-tumi-kemon-achho 240 → 255, BN-C16-nei 240 → 247, BN-C16-noy 235 → 248, BN-C19-abar-bolben 240 → 245, BN-C21-eta 250 → 255, BN-C25-ke-suffix 250 → 265, BN-C26-kothay 240 → 295, BN-C27-choturtho 240 → 257. Every other edited lesson stays within its declared length.
- **Roots.** New `roots` slugs reuse an existing slug for the same etymon wherever one exists; `npm run check:root-slug-splits` still reports 77 known entries and none new.
