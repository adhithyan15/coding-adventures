Japanese, chapter 231: Calls and Messages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
もしもし (moshimoshi) — hello on the phone; excuse me (calling out).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a junior, then the Japanese for to do one's best.

You'll want to know: もしもし.
もしもし — moshimoshi — "hello on the phone; excuse me (calling out)".
でんわで もしもしと いいます。 — denwa de moshimoshi to iimasu — "On the phone, people say moshimoshi."

The word, taken apart.
もしもし (moshimoshi) is usually called a native Japanese word (wago), not borrowed from Chinese, and JMdict gives it in kana alone; it is a doubled form, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ばんごう (bangō) — a number; a phone number.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to do one's best, then the Japanese for hello on the phone.

You'll want to know: ばんごう.
ばんごう — bangō — "a number; a phone number".
ちちは でんわの ばんごうを よく まちがえます。 — chichi wa denwa no bangō o yoku machigaemasu — "My father often gets phone numbers wrong."

The word, taken apart.
ばんごう (bangō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ban, "turn, number" (Middle Chinese phjon) and gō, "number, name" (hawH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
つたえる (tsutaeru) — to pass on; to convey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for contact, then the Japanese for a response.

You'll want to know: つたえる.
つたえる — tsutaeru — "to pass on; to convey".
じょうしに かいぎの じかんを つたえます。 — jōshi ni kaigi no jikan o tsutaemasu — "I pass on the meeting time to my boss."
Polite つたえます, tsutaemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
つたえる (tsutaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
