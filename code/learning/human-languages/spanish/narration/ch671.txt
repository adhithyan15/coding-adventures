Spanish, chapter 671: Cerca y lejos.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
cercano — nearby, close.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to cross, then the Spanish for to turn off.

You'll want to know: cercano.
cercano — "nearby, close".
Busco una farmacia cercana. I am looking for a nearby chemist.
Un amigo cercano — a close friend.

The word, taken apart.
cercano is built on cerca "near" (EtymDB), which comes from Latin circā "around".

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
lejano — distant, far-off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to turn off, then the Spanish for nearby.

You'll want to know: lejano.
lejano — "distant, far-off".
Viven en un pueblo lejano. They live in a far-off village.
The adverb is lejos.

The word, taken apart.
lejano is built on lejos "far" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin laxius "more loosely, more widely", from laxus "loose".

atajo is built on atajar "to cut short", from tajar "to cut" (EtymDB), which comes from Late Latin taliāre "to cut".

adelante — "forward, ahead".
Siga adelante hasta el cruce. Keep going to the junction.
¡Adelante! also means come in! or go ahead!

The word, taken apart.
adelante comes from delante "in front" and Old Spanish denante (EtymDB), a fusion of Latin de, in and ante "before".

Lesson 5 of 6.
el rodeo — the detour, the long way round; the rodeo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the short cut, then the Spanish for forward.

You'll want to know: el rodeo.
el rodeo — "the detour, the long way round; the rodeo".
Si vamos por el puente, damos un rodeo. If we go over the bridge, we go the long way round.
Sin rodeos — without beating about the bush.

The word, taken apart.
rodeo is built on rodear "to go round", from rueda "wheel", which is inherited from Latin rota.
