Italian, chapter 411: Sotto il cofano.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il cofano — the bonnet (of a car); the chest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to tow, then the Italian for the garage.

You'll want to know: il cofano.
il cofano — "the bonnet (of a car); the chest".
Il meccanico apre il cofano. The mechanic opens the bonnet.
In American English, the hood. Its old sense is a chest or coffer.

The word, taken apart.
cofano is inherited from Latin cophinus 'basket' (EtymDB), from Greek kóphinos; it first meant a chest or coffer.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: il bagagliaio.
il bagagliaio — "the boot (of a car)".
Metti le valigie nel bagagliaio. Put the suitcases in the boot.
In American English, the trunk. Il bagaglio is luggage.

The word, taken apart.
bagagliaio is built on bagaglio 'luggage', which came from French bagage, built on Old French bague 'bundle'; where that word comes from is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la frizione — the clutch; the rub, the rubdown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bonnet, then the Italian for the boot.

You'll want to know: la frizione.
la frizione — "the clutch; the rub, the rubdown".
Premi la frizione e cambia marcia. Press the clutch and change gear.
Frizione also means friction, and a rubdown with lotion.

The word, taken apart.
frizione is a learned borrowing from Latin frictiō 'rubbing' (EtymDB), from fricāre 'to rub': the clutch plates rub together.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il fanale — the light (of a car), the lamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the boot, then the Italian for the clutch.

You'll want to know: il fanale.
il fanale — "the light (of a car), the lamp".
Un fanale della moto è rotto. One of the motorbike lights is broken.
Il fanale posteriore — the rear light.

The word, taken apart.
fanale is usually traced to Greek phanós 'lantern, torch', borrowed through Byzantine Greek, the Greek of the eastern empire.

Lesson 5 of 5.
svitare — to unscrew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the clutch, then the Italian for the light.

You'll want to know: svitare.
svitare — "to unscrew".
Svita la ruota con la chiave. Unscrew the wheel with the wrench.
The opposite is avvitare, to screw in.

The word, taken apart.
svitare is built from s- and vite 'screw', which is inherited from Latin vītis 'vine' (EtymDB): the thread of a screw winds like a vine.
