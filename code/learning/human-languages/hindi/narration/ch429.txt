Hindi, chapter 429: Slips, Cards and Signatures.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
पर्ची (parcī) — a slip (of paper), a chit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a budget, then the Hindi for to collect (money owed).

You'll want to know: पर्ची.
पर्ची — parcī — "a slip (of paper), a chit".
बैंक में पहले एक पर्ची भरिए। baiṅk meṁ pahle ek parcī bhariye. At the bank, fill in a slip first.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
पर्ची (parcī) is usually traced to Persian parča, "a piece, a scrap", with the Hindi ending -ī for a small one: a slip for a bank, a doctor or a lottery.

Lesson 3 of 6.
दस्तख़त (dastkhat) — a signature (everyday).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a slip, then the Hindi for a card.

You'll want to know: दस्तख़त.
दस्तख़त — dastkhat — "a signature (everyday)".
यहाँ अपने दस्तख़त कीजिए। yahā̃ apne dastkhat kījiye. Please sign here.
Masculine.
Usually plural in agreement: मैंने दस्तख़त (dastkhat) किए, I signed.

The word, taken apart.
दस्तख़त (dastkhat) is usually traced to Persian dastkhaṭṭ, from dast, "hand", and khaṭṭ, "writing": your hand's own mark. हस्ताक्षर is the formal word.

Lesson 4 of 6.
मुद्रा (mudrā) — a currency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a card, then the Hindi for a signature (everyday).

You'll want to know: मुद्रा.
मुद्रा — mudrā — "a currency".
हर देश की अपनी मुद्रा होती है। har deś kī apnī mudrā hotī hai. Every country has its own currency.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
मुद्रा (mudrā) is a tatsama, Sanskrit mudrā, "a seal, a stamp" (Monier-Williams); a stamped coin led to money in general, and modern Hindi uses it for a currency.

Lesson 5 of 6.
भुनाना (bhunānā) — to cash; to change (a note).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a signature (everyday), then the Hindi for a currency.

You'll want to know: भुनाना.
भुनाना — bhunānā — "to cash; to change (a note)".
मैंने बैंक में चेक भुनाया। maiṁne baiṅk meṁ cek bhunāyā. I cashed the cheque at the bank.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने सौ का नोट भुनाया, usne sau kā noṭ bhunāyā.

The word, taken apart.
भुनाना (bhunānā) is listed by CDIAL under Sanskrit bhajyate, "is broken" (CDIAL 9361), with Hindi bhunnā, "to be changed (of money)", and its causative bhunānā: to break a big note into smaller money. CDIAL calls the u of these forms unexplained.
