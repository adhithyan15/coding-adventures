Malayalam, chapter 164: Aviyal, Pappadam, Hot, Cold, Long.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
അവിയൽ (aviyal) — aviyal, a mixed-vegetable dish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for beans, then the Malayalam for dal.
[pause 1 second]
Ask "where from?" (Eviṭe ninnŭ?)

You'll want to know: അവിയൽ.
അവിയൽ — aviyal — "aviyal, a mixed-vegetable dish".

The word, taken apart.
അവിയൽ (aviyal) is native Dravidian, from the verb "to boil, to cook in steam"; DEDR 268 lists the root with Tamil avi.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ചൂടുള്ള (cūṭuḷḷa) — hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for aviyal, then the Malayalam for a pappadam.
[pause 1 second]
Say ninety and a hundred. (Toṇṇūṟŭ, nūṟŭ.)

You'll want to know: ചൂടുള്ള.
ചൂടുള്ള — cūṭuḷḷa — "hot".

The word, taken apart.
ചൂടുള്ള (cūṭuḷḷa) is native Dravidian, "having heat": cūṭŭ, "heat" (DEDR 2654), with the ending -uḷḷa, "that has", from the verb "to be" (DEDR 697, with Tamil uḷ). DEDR 2654 compares Sanskrit cūḍ.

You'll want to know: നീളമുള്ള.

നീളമുള്ള (nīḷamuḷḷa) is native Dravidian, "having length": the noun for "length, distance" (DEDR 3692, with Tamil nīḷ, "to grow long"), with -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697).
