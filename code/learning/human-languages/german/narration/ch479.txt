German, chapter 479: Schnupfen und Gänsehaut.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
erkälten — to catch a cold (sich erkälten).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to insult, then the German for to hurt.

You'll want to know: erkälten.
erkälten — "to catch a cold (sich erkälten)".
Im Regen habe ich mich erkältet. I caught a cold in the rain.
Reflexive: sich erkälten. Erkältet sein — to have a cold.

The word, taken apart.
erkälten comes from Middle High German erkelten (EtymDB), built on kalt "cold", which is inherited from Old High German kalt, from Proto-Germanic kaldaz.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Schnupfen — head cold, runny nose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hurt, then the German for to catch a cold.

You'll want to know: der Schnupfen.
der Schnupfen — "head cold, runny nose".
Ich habe Schnupfen und brauche viele Taschentücher. I have a runny nose and need lots of tissues.
Usually without an article: Ich habe Schnupfen. No plural in everyday use.

The word, taken apart.
Schnupfen is built on the verb schnupfen "to sniff" (EtymDB), probably an imitative word for the sound of a blocked nose.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Hustensaft — cough syrup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to catch a cold, then the German for head cold.

You'll want to know: der Hustensaft.
der Hustensaft — "cough syrup".
Der Hustensaft schmeckt nach Kirschen. The cough syrup tastes of cherries.
Plural: die Hustensäfte. Built on der Husten — cough, and der Saft — juice.

The word, taken apart.
Hustensaft is built on Husten "cough", from Old High German huosto, and Saft "juice", from Middle High German saf (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Schweiß — sweat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for head cold, then the German for cough syrup.

You'll want to know: der Schweiß.
der Schweiß — "sweat".
Nach dem Joggen war mein T-Shirt nass vor Schweiß. After jogging my T-shirt was wet with sweat.
No plural. The verb is schwitzen — to sweat.

The word, taken apart.
Schweiß is inherited from Middle High German sweiz and Old High German sweiz, from Proto-Germanic swait- "sweat" (EtymDB); English sweat is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Gänsehaut — goose bumps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for cough syrup, then the German for sweat.

You'll want to know: die Gänsehaut.
die Gänsehaut — "goose bumps".
Bei dieser Musik bekomme ich Gänsehaut. This music gives me goose bumps.
No plural. As in English, built on the goose: die Gans.

The word, taken apart.
Gänsehaut is built on Gans "goose", inherited from Old High German gans, from Proto-Germanic gans (EtymDB), and Haut "skin": skin like a plucked goose.
