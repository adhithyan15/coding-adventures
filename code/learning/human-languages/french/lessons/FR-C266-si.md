# si — if

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C64-VERBES16-05, FR-GRAM-DEPUIS-PRESENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I don't know". (*Je ne sais pas.*) Then "I have been living
here for three years". (*J'habite ici depuis trois ans.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Most sentences so far have said what is, or what was. This
chapter says what follows **if** something else is true, and it begins with
two letters.

## Grammar Lens: si opens a condition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-SI-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-FERMER-10, FR-LEX-C73-MAISON25-02, FR-GRAM-IMPERATIF-01] -->

**si** — "if".

> **Si** tu as faim, mange. — If you're hungry, eat.
> **Si** tu as froid, ferme la fenêtre. — If you're cold, shut the window.

Whatever follows *si* is the **condition**; the rest of the sentence is what
follows from it. The condition can come first, with a comma, or second,
without one: *Ferme la fenêtre **si** tu as froid.* Notice that French
**has** hunger and cold, *tu as faim*, *tu as froid*, where English **is**
hungry and cold.

## Grammar Lens: s'il, and si for "whether"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PLEASE-02, FR-LEX-C96-TEMPS48-04] -->

Before **il** and **ils**, *si* drops its vowel: **s'il**, **s'ils**. Before
*elle* it keeps it: *si elle*. You have said *s'il* since the chapter on
please, because *s'il vous plaît* is "if it pleases you".

After a verb of knowing or asking, *si* also means "whether", and your *je
ne sais pas* comes in useful:

> ***Je ne sais pas si** c'est loin.* — I don't know whether it's far.

## The word, taken apart: one spelling, two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-SI-SIC-02]; assesses=[FR-PRAGMATICS-SI-05] -->

You have met **si** before, as the "yes" that contradicts a negative: *Tu ne
viens pas ? — Si !* That one goes back to Latin *sīc*, "so, thus". The *si*
of this lesson is inherited from Latin *sī*, "if", a conditional particle
(Lewis & Short, under *si*). EtymDB 2.1 lists both Latin words behind the
French spelling. Two words, worn into one shape.

The ear tells them apart by their place. The "yes" *si* stands alone, as
an answer. The "if" *si* opens a whole clause.
