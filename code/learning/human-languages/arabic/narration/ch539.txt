Arabic, chapter 539: Games for all ages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نرد (nard) — dice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to knit, then the Arabic for to sew.

You'll want to know: نرد.
نرد — nard — "dice".
رمى أخي النرد وفاز. ramā akhī an-nard wa-fāza. My brother threw the dice and won.

The word, taken apart.
نرد (nard) was borrowed from Persian: it is a Persian word, arabicized, for the game of backgammon and its pieces (Lane). Today it names the dice.

Lesson 2 of 5.
أرجوحة (urjūḥa) — a swing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to sew, then the Arabic for dice.

You'll want to know: أرجوحة.
أرجوحة — urjūḥa — "a swing".
يلعب الأطفال على الأرجوحة في الحديقة. yalʿabu al-aṭfāl ʿalā al-urjūḥa fī al-ḥadīqa. The children play on the swing in the park.
Plural: أراجيح, arājīḥ.

The word, taken apart.
أرجوحة (urjūḥa) is built on the native Arabic root r-j-ḥ, "to outweigh, to go down (of a scale)" (Lane). A swing goes up and down like the pans of a scale.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تسلى (tasallā) — to have fun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for dice, then the Arabic for a swing.

You'll want to know: تسلى.
تسلى — tasallā — "to have fun".
تسلينا بلعب الورق في المساء. tasallaynā bi-laʿib al-waraq fī al-masāʾ. We had fun playing cards in the evening.
Present: يتسلى, yatasallā.
لعب الورق is playing cards.

The word, taken apart.
تسلى (tasallā) is built on the native Arabic root s-l-w, "to forget, to be consoled" (Lane). Form V tasallā is to take one's mind off things.

Lesson 4 of 5.
بستنة (bastana) — gardening.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a swing, then the Arabic for to have fun.

You'll want to know: بستنة.
بستنة — bastana — "gardening".
يقضي جدي وقته في البستنة. yaqḍī jaddī waqtahu fī al-bastana. My grandfather spends his time gardening.

The word, taken apart.
بستنة (bastana) is a modern Arabic noun built from bustān, "an orchard", which you know: Arabic made a four-letter root b-s-t-n from it. Bustān is usually traced to Persian.
