Arabic, chapter 400: Whatever and wherever.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
لولا (lawlā) — if not for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for as long as, then the Arabic for otherwise.

You'll want to know: لولا.
لولا — lawlā — "if not for".
لولا المطر لذهبنا إلى البحر. lawlā al-maṭar la-dhahabnā ilā al-baḥr. If not for the rain, we would have gone to the sea.
The result takes لـ, as after لو: لولا (lawlā) … لـ ….

The word, taken apart.
لولا (lawlā) is built from the native Arabic law, "if", and lā, "not": if it were not for. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مهما (mahmā) — whatever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for otherwise, then the Arabic for if not for.

You'll want to know: مهما.
مهما — mahmā — "whatever".
مهما حدث، سأبقى صديقك. mahmā ḥadatha, sa-abqā ṣadīqak. Whatever happens, I will stay your friend.
Like other condition words, it is often followed by the past: مهما (mahmā) حدث.

The word, taken apart.
مهما (mahmā) is a native Arabic particle of condition, whatever. Grammarians usually trace it to mā, "what", said twice; its origin is not settled.

كلما زرت جدتي، طبخت لي. kullamā zurtu jaddatī, ṭabakhat lī. Whenever I visit my grandmother, she cooks for me.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سواء (sawāʾ) — whether.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for whatever, then the Arabic for whenever.

You'll want to know: سواء.
سواء — sawāʾ — "whether".
سنخرج سواء أمطرت أم لا. sa-nakhruju sawāʾ amṭarat am lā. We will go out whether it rains or not.
It pairs with أم: سواء (sawāʾ) … أم ….

The word, taken apart.
سواء (sawāʾ) is built on the native Arabic root s-w-y, "to be equal, level" (Lane). Sawāʾ is equality: it is all the same, whether one thing or the other.
