# ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ) — a remedy; medication

## You'll want to know: ఔషధం
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C321-AUSADHAM-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C50-LEAVE-03, TE-LEX-C84-UNWELL-01, TE-LEX-C48-ROLE-03, TE-LEX-C97-MOOD-01, TE-LEX-C42-ADJ-03, TE-LEX-C92-DOCTOR-02] -->

**ఔషధం** — *auṣadhaṁ* — "a remedy; medication".

> **ప్రయాణంలో నాకు జ్వరం వచ్చింది. అక్కడ ఒక వైద్యుడు నాకు ఔషధం ఇచ్చాడు.**
>
> *prayāṇaṁlō nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi. akkaḍa oka vaidyuḍu nāku auṣadhaṁ iccāḍu.* — "On the trip I got a fever. A doctor there gave me a remedy."

> **నవ్వు మంచి ఔషధం.**
>
> *navvu mañci auṣadhaṁ.* — "Laughter is a good remedy."

Everyday Telugu says **మందు**. **ఔషధం** is the careful, learned word: you will read it on the signs of
chemists' shops (**ఔషధాలు**, "medicines"), on labels, and in sentences about what heals in a wider sense,
like the one about laughter.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ఔషధం** (*auṣadhaṁ*) is borrowed from Sanskrit *auṣadha*, which CDIAL describes as "made of herbs" and as "a
herb" (CDIAL 2571), with Telugu's noun ending **-ం**. A remedy was first a plant.
