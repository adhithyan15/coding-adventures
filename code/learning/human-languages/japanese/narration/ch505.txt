Japanese, chapter 505: Sumo and Archery.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りきし (rikishi) — a sumo wrestler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an artist, then the Japanese for a picture book.

You'll want to know: りきし.
りきし — rikishi — "a sumo wrestler".
りきしは おおきいです。 — rikishi wa ōkii desu — "Sumo wrestlers are big."

The word, taken apart.
りきし (rikishi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are riki, "power" (Middle Chinese lik) and shi, "gentleman" (dzriX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: どひょう.
どひょう — dohyō — "a sumo ring".
りきしが どひょうに あがります。 — rikishi ga dohyō ni agarimasu — "The wrestlers step up into the ring."

The word, taken apart.
どひょう (dohyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are do, "soil" (Middle Chinese thuX) and hyō, "bag", a sense the Middle Chinese rhyme books do not give for this character, so no older reading is cited for it (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ゆみ (yumi) — a bow (for arrows); archery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sumo ring, then the Japanese for a sumo referee.

You'll want to know: ゆみ.
ゆみ — yumi — "a bow (for arrows); archery".
おとうとは ゆみを ならっています。 — otōto wa yumi o naratteimasu — "My younger brother is learning archery."

The word, taken apart.
ゆみ (yumi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: よける.
よける — yokeru — "to dodge; to keep out of the way of".
いしを よけて あるきます。 — ishi o yokete arukimasu — "I walk around the stones."
Polite よけます, yokemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ねこが くるまを よけました。, neko ga kuruma o yokemashita.
