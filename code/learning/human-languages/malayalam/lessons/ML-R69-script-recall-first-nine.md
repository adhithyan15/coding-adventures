# (ക ര ന മ ത ഴ ് ി ാ) — nine, and three kinds of thing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-01, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-05] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two consonants you have been reading since the book's opening pages.
Read **ക**, then **ന**, and say each one's name.

## Script: six letters, one killer, two hangers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-01, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-109, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-05, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-114, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-118] -->

These arrived one per chapter, and no page has put them side by side. Sorted by
what kind of thing each one is rather than by when you met it:

| kind | pieces |
|---|---|
| **letters** — each carries a built-in *a* | **ക** *ka* · **ര** *ra* · **ന** *na* · **മ** *ma* · **ത** *ta* · **ഴ** *ḻa* |
| **the killer** | **്** — takes the built-in *a* away |
| **hangers** | **ി** *i* · **ാ** *ā* — replace the built-in *a* with another vowel |

**ഴ** is the one to look at twice. It is the sound Malayalam is named for and
that almost no other language has; a reader who has not seen it since the
chapter that taught it will not place it at all.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-02, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-03, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

[PAUSE 1s] The three marks, cold, and what each does to the letter it touches.

- [YOU READ: **്** — say what it removes]
- [YOU READ: **ി** — say what it replaces]
- [YOU READ: **ാ** — say the same, and say how it differs from the one above]

A letter on its own already has a vowel in it. That is the fact all three of
these depend on: the killer takes it away, and the hangers swap it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-02, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-118, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What vowel is inside a bare Malayalam letter? (**A short *a*.**)
Which of the nine takes it away? (**്**, the virama.) And which one is the sound
the language is named for? (**ഴ**.)
