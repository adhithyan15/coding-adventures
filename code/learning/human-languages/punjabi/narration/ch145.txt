Punjabi, chapter 145: Brave, Calm, Hungry, Thirsty, Fresh.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਬਹਾਦਰ (bahādar) — brave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for clever, then the Punjabi for lazy.

You'll want to know: ਬਹਾਦਰ.
ਬਹਾਦਰ — bahādar — "brave".

The word, taken apart.
ਬਹਾਦਰ (bahādar) is a loan, usually traced to Persian bahādur, "brave, a hero". Persian itself took the word from a Turkic or Mongol word for "hero".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਭੁੱਖਾ (bhukkhā) — hungry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for brave, then the Punjabi for calm.

You'll want to know: ਭੁੱਖਾ.
ਭੁੱਖਾ — bhukkhā — "hungry".

The word, taken apart.
ਭੁੱਖਾ (bhukkhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bubhukṣaka, "hungry", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9284). It is built on Sanskrit bubhukṣā, "hunger".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਪਿਆਸਾ (piāsā) — thirsty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for calm, then the Punjabi for hungry.

You'll want to know: ਪਿਆਸਾ.
ਪਿਆਸਾ — piāsā — "thirsty".

The word, taken apart.
ਪਿਆਸਾ (piāsā) came into Punjabi from Hindi: CDIAL lists Hindi piyāsā, "thirsty", under pipāsaka, a form it sets beside Sanskrit pipāsita, "thirsty", and marks Punjabi peāsā as taken from it (CDIAL 8200).
