# నా (nā) — "my"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "My" — native Dravidian, no European cousin.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C02-NAA-01]; assesses=[] -->

*(Skim if you read Telugu.)* **న** (na) + **ా** (long *ā*) → **నా** (*nā*).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-ETYMON-C02-NAA-02]; assesses=[] -->

**నా** (*nā*) means "my." It belongs to Telugu's native Dravidian first-person
family, not to the Indo-European family behind English *I/my*. You will meet
the related word for "I" in its own lesson; today you only need *nā*.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

న (*na*) + ా (long ā) → నా (*nā*).
