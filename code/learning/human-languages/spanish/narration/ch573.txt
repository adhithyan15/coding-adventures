Spanish, chapter 573: Lo consiguió.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
alcanzar — to reach, to catch up with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to develop, then the Spanish for to overcome.

You'll want to know: alcanzar.
alcanzar — "to reach, to catch up with".
Después de tres horas alcanzamos al grupo. After three hours we caught up with the group.
It takes c before e: alcancé. Alcanzar un objetivo — to achieve a goal.

The word, taken apart.
alcanzar is usually traced to spoken Latin incalciāre 'to follow at the heels' (EtymDB), built on calx 'heel'. To catch up is to be on someone's heels.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fracasar — to fail.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for to overcome, then the Spanish for to reach.

You'll want to know: fracasar.
fracasar — "to fail".
El proyecto fracasó por falta de dinero. The project failed for lack of money.
Used for plans and projects; to fail an exam is suspender.

The word, taken apart.
fracasar was borrowed from Italian fracassare 'to smash, to shatter' (EtymDB). A plan that fails is smashed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
sufrir — to suffer; to undergo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to fail, then the Spanish for the failure.

You'll want to know: sufrir.
sufrir — "to suffer; to undergo".
Sufrió un accidente de tráfico. He was in a road accident.
Sufrir un accidente — to have an accident. Sufrir de — to suffer from.

The word, taken apart.
sufrir comes from Latin sufferre 'to bear up under' (Lewis & Short), built on sub 'under' and ferre 'to carry', usually through a spoken Latin form sufferīre.

Lesson 5 of 5.
resultar — to turn out, to prove to be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the failure, then the Spanish for to suffer.

You'll want to know: resultar.
resultar — "to turn out, to prove to be".
El examen resultó muy fácil. The exam turned out to be very easy.
Resultar herido — to be injured: Dos personas resultaron heridas.

The word, taken apart.
resultar is a learned borrowing from Latin resultāre 'to spring back, to rebound' (EtymDB), built on resilīre 'to leap back', from salīre 'to leap' (Lewis & Short). A result is what springs back from an action.
