Arabic, chapter 164: To shout, To pray, To joke, To believe, To remember.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
صرخ (ṣarakha) — to shout, to scream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to shake hands with, then the Arabic for to hug.

You'll want to know: صرخ.
صرخ — ṣarakha — "to shout, to scream".

The word, taken apart.
صرخ (ṣarakha) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-r-kh, "to cry out" (Lane). Ṣarakha is the plain verb, "he cried out".

Lesson 2 of 5.
صلى (ṣallā) — to pray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to hug, then the Arabic for to shout.

You'll want to know: صلى.
صلى — ṣallā — "to pray".

The word, taken apart.
صلى (ṣallā) is listed by Lane under ṣ-l-w. Many trace the Arabic words for prayer to Aramaic ṣlōthā, "prayer", and ṣallā is usually taken as built from that borrowed noun.

اعتقد (iʿtaqada) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-q-d, "to tie a knot" (Lane). Iʿtaqada is Form VIII, "to bind one's heart to a thing", to believe.
