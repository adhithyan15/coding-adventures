Hindi, chapter 110: Once More, Please.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दोबारा (dobārā) — again, a second time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for alone, then the Hindi for cold.

You'll want to know: दोबारा.
दोबारा (dobārā) — "again, a second time".
दोबारा (dobārā) बोलिए — "please say it again". It is दो, "two", and बार, "a time".

The word, taken apart.
दोबारा (dobārā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian dobāra, "twice, a second time". It is built from do, "two", and bār, "a time".

What you've built.
The repair a beginner needs most.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dobārā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: dobārā, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say dobārā bolie]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for alone, then the Hindi for cold, then say dobārā again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does दोबारा (dobārā) mean? ("Again, a second time".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
ज़ोर से (zor se) — loudly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for cold, then the Hindi for again.

You'll want to know: ज़ोर से.
ज़ोर से (zor se) — "loudly", literally "with force".
थोड़ा ज़ोर से (zor se) बोलिए — "please speak a little louder", with the softening थोड़ा.

The word, taken apart.
ज़ोर से (zor se) is zor, "force", with se, "with". zor is usually traced to Persian zōr, "strength"; se is inherited (CDIAL 13310).

What you've built.
Again, and louder.

Lesson 3 of 5.
एक मिनट (ek minaṭ) — one minute, a moment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for again, then the Hindi for loudly.

You'll want to know: एक मिनट.
एक मिनट (ek minaṭ) — "one minute", said the way English says "a moment".
English minute, borrowed, after the एक you count with.

The word, taken apart.
एक मिनट (ek minaṭ) is ek, "one", from Sanskrit eka (CDIAL 2462), and minaṭ, English minute.

What you've built.
A way to buy time while you find the word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
रुकिए (rukie) — please wait, please stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for loudly, then the Hindi for one minute.

You'll want to know: रुकिए.
रुकिए (rukie) — "please wait", "please stop".
रुक (ruk, the stem of the verb "to stop") + -ie, the respectful ending of बोलिए becomes रुकिए (rukie). रुकिए (rukie), एक मिनट (ek minaṭ) — "wait, one minute".

The word, taken apart.
रुकिए (rukie) is the verb ruknā, "to stop", with the respectful ending -ie. CDIAL lists ruknā under a reconstructed rokk, "stop", whose link with Sanskrit rudh-, "to obstruct", it calls not clear (CDIAL 10827).

What you've built.
Wait, one minute, again, louder.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मतलब (matlab) — meaning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for one minute, then the Hindi for please wait.

You'll want to know: मतलब.
मतलब (matlab) — "meaning".
धीरे का मतलब (matlab) क्या है? — "what does dhīre mean?". Put any word in front of का मतलब (matlab) क्या है? and it becomes the question that turns any speaker into a teacher.

The word, taken apart.
मतलब (matlab) is a loan from Arabic maṭlab, "something sought, an aim", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ṭ-l-b (Lane). Hindi uses it for what a word aims at: its meaning.

What you've built.
Again, louder, one minute, wait, what does it mean.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: matlab]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: matlab, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask dhīre kā matlab kyā hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for one minute, then the Hindi for please wait, then say matlab again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does मतलब (matlab) mean? ("Meaning".) Say it once more.
