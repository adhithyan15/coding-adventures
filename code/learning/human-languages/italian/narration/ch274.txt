Italian, chapter 274: Nel frattempo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nel frattempo — in the meantime, meanwhile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for subsequently, then the Italian for after which.

You'll want to know: nel frattempo.
nel frattempo — "in the meantime, meanwhile".
Lui è andato a prendere il pane; nel frattempo io ho fatto il caffè. He went to get the bread; in the meantime I made the coffee.
Close to intanto, a little more formal.

The word, taken apart.
frattempo joins fra "between" and tempo "time", which is inherited from Latin tempus "time" (Lewis & Short): the time in between.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: contemporaneamente.
contemporaneamente — "at the same time, simultaneously".
Due telefoni hanno suonato contemporaneamente. Two phones rang at the same time.
Built on contemporaneo, ‘of the same time’.

The word, taken apart.
contemporaneamente is built on contemporaneo, a learned borrowing from Latin contemporāneus "of the same time", from con- "together" and tempus "time" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
non appena — as soon as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for in the meantime, then the Italian for at the same time.

You'll want to know: non appena.
non appena — "as soon as".
Non appena sono arrivato, ha cominciato a piovere. As soon as I arrived, it started to rain.
It means the same as appena here; the non adds nothing negative.

The word, taken apart.
appena is inherited from Latin ad paene, built on paene "almost" (EtymDB); non comes from Latin nōn "not".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a un certo punto — at a certain point, at some point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for at the same time, then the Italian for as soon as.

You'll want to know: a un certo punto.
a un certo punto — "at a certain point, at some point".
A un certo punto la luce si è spenta. At a certain point the light went out.
A favourite phrase of storytellers to bring in a turn in the story.

The word, taken apart.
punto is inherited from Latin punctum "a point, a prick", from pungere "to prick" (EtymDB); certo comes from Latin certus "fixed, sure".

Lesson 5 of 5.
d'un tratto — all of a sudden.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: d'un tratto.
d'un tratto — "all of a sudden".
D'un tratto tutti si sono alzati. All of a sudden everybody stood up.
Also tutto d'un tratto; storytellers love it.

The word, taken apart.
tratto is inherited from Latin tractus "a pulling, a stretch", from trahere "to draw, to drag" (EtymDB): in one pull, in one go.
