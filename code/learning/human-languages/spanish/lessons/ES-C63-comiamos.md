# comíamos · éramos · íbamos — every one with an accent

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-IMPERFECT-PLURAL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The *-ar* imperfect is whole. Now *comía*, which has had only its
singular until now.

## Grammar Lens: the -ía plural
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-IMPERFECT-PLURAL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-IR-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say them: *nosotros* ***comíamos***. *Vosotros* ***comíais***.
*Ellos* ***comían***.

[PAUSE 2s] And here the accent is on **every** form, not just one. The *-ía*
puts an *i* and an *a* next to each other, and Spanish would ordinarily run two
such vowels together into a single beat. The accent keeps them apart, so *comía*
stays three syllables rather than collapsing into two.

[PAUSE 2s] So the two imperfect sets are mirror images: in *-aba* only one form
needs a mark, and in *-ía* they all do.

## Grammar Lens: and the three irregulars
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-IRREGULAR-PLURAL, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-COMPLETE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-VER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

Spanish has exactly **three** irregular imperfects, and you have had their
singulars for a long time. Their plurals:

| | |
|---|---|
| *ser* | ***éramos*** · *erais* · *eran* |
| *ir* | ***íbamos*** · *ibais* · *iban* |
| *ver* | ***veíamos*** · *veíais* · *veían* |

[PAUSE 2s] That is the whole irregular list for this tense — three verbs, and
now every form of all three. **The imperfect is complete.**
