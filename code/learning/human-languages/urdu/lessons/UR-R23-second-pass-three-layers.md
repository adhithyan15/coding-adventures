# Where a word came from, and what to say when you lose it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-ARAM-SE] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Someone is speaking faster than you can follow. Two words, from
memory, that ask them to slow down.

## You'll want to know: an Urdu word arrives by one of several roads
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-REGISTER-ENGLISH-LAYER, UR-REGISTER-DOUBLE-LOAN] -->

| word | the road it took |
|---|---|
| **کوٹ** *koṭ* | straight from English, one generation old |
| **ہوا** *hawā* | Arabic to Persian, Persian to Urdu — a loan of a loan |
| **گرمی** *garmī* | Persian, on a root it shares with Greek *thermós* |

Three different histories, and the spelling announces none of them. **کوٹ** is
the sharpest case: an identical **کوٹ** meaning "fort" came in from Sanskrit
*koṭṭa* centuries earlier, so the two are homographs rather than cousins and the
page gives you no warning at all.

**ہوا** is the shape worth naming, because it is not the usual one. A word that
splits from a shared root into two roads has two descendants; **ہوا** was handed
along a single chain, Arabic to Persian to Urdu, arriving once.

## You'll want to know: the phrase for when the road runs out
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-ARAM-SE, UR-SCRIPT-GAF-LETTER, UR-SCRIPT-READ-MEETING-01] -->

> **آرام سے** — *ārām se* — slowly, gently, take your time.
> **آرام سے کہیں** — *ārām se kaheṅ* — please say it slowly.

**سے** is the postposition *with*, so the phrase is literally **with ease**.
Every letter in it is already yours, and it is worth more in a first
conversation than any of the three words above.
