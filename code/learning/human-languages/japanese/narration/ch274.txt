Japanese, chapter 274: When the Earth Shook.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じしん (jishin) — an earthquake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to sue, then the Japanese for evidence.

You'll want to know: じしん.
じしん — jishin — "an earthquake".
きのう じしんが ありました。 — kinō jishin ga arimashita — "There was an earthquake yesterday."

The word, taken apart.
じしん (jishin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "ground" (Middle Chinese dijH) and shin, "quake" (tsyinH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
くずれる (kuzureru) — to crumble; to give way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for evidence, then the Japanese for an earthquake.

You'll want to know: くずれる.
くずれる — kuzureru — "to crumble; to give way".
じしんで ふるい たてものが くずれました。 — jishin de furui tatemono ga kuzuremashita — "Old buildings came down in the earthquake."
Polite くずれます, kuzuremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): やまが くずれました。, yama ga kuzuremashita.

The word, taken apart.
くずれる (kuzureru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
