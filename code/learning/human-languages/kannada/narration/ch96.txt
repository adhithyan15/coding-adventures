Kannada, chapter 96: Greetings People Actually Say.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ (ūṭa āytā) — have you eaten?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for maybe, then the Kannada for certainly.

You'll want to know: ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ.
ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (ūṭa āytā?) — "have you eaten?", literally "has the meal happened?". In the south this is a greeting as much as a question.

What you've built.
The warmest everyday hello.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ūṭa āytā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ūṭa āytā, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask ūṭa āytā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Kannada for maybe, then the Kannada for certainly, then say ūṭa āytā again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ (ūṭa āytā) mean? ("Have you eaten?".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಹಲೋ (halō) — hello (on the phone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for what's new, then the Kannada for long time.

You'll want to know: ಹಲೋ.
ಹಲೋ (halō) — "hello", borrowed from English and used above all when answering the phone. Face to face, ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ is still the greeting.

What you've built.
The phone's greeting.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಕ್ಷೇಮವೇ (kṣēmavē) — are you well?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for long time, then the Kannada for hello.

You'll want to know: ಕ್ಷೇಮವೇ.
ಕ್ಷೇಮವೇ? (kṣēmavē?) — "are you well?", a formal, warm question. ಕ್ಷೇಮ (kṣēma) is "welfare"; ‑ಏ makes it a question.

What you've built.
The formal cousin of hēgiddīrā.
