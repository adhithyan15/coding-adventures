Lesson 1 of 5.
el ferry — the ferry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the photocopy, then the Spanish for the consulate.

You'll want to know: el ferry.
el ferry — "the ferry".
Tomamos el ferry a la isla. We took the ferry to the island.
Also el transbordador. Plural los ferris.

The word, taken apart.
ferry was borrowed from English ferry (EtymDB), from Old English ferian "to carry, to convey".

Lesson 2 of 5.
el crucero — the cruise; the cruise ship.

You'll want to know: el crucero.
el crucero — "the cruise; the cruise ship".
Hicimos un crucero por las islas. We went on a cruise round the islands.
It also names the ship itself: un crucero enorme.

The word, taken apart.
crucero is built on cruz "cross", inherited from Latin crux; a crucero crosses the sea back and forth.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: navegar.
navegar — "to sail; to browse (the internet)".
Navegamos dos días hasta la costa. We sailed for two days to the coast.
Navegar por internet — to browse the web.

The word, taken apart.
navegar comes from Latin nāvigāre "to sail" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), built from nāvis "ship" and agere "to drive".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el muelle — the quay, the dock; the spring (metal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the cruise, then the Spanish for to sail.

You'll want to know: el muelle.
el muelle — "the quay, the dock; the spring (metal)".
El barco espera en el muelle. The boat is waiting at the quay.
A second muelle is a metal spring.

The word, taken apart.
muelle for a quay comes from Catalan moll, from Latin mōlēs "a mass; a dam, a pier" (Lewis & Short). The muelle that means a spring is a different word, from Latin mollis "soft".

Lesson 5 of 5.
desembarcar — to disembark, to go ashore; to unload.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to sail, then the Spanish for the quay.

You'll want to know: desembarcar.
desembarcar — "to disembark, to go ashore; to unload".
Desembarcamos por la tarde. We went ashore in the afternoon.
The opposite of embarcar.

The word, taken apart.
desembarcar is built on embarcar with des- "un-"; both come from barca "boat", Late Latin barca (EtymDB).
