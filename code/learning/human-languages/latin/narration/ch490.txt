Latin, chapter 490: Sadness and grief.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
trīstitia, trīstitiae — sadness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to leap for joy, then the Latin for pleasantness.

You'll want to know: trīstitia, trīstitiae.
trīstitia, trīstitiae — "sadness".
Post mortem patris magna trīstitia domum tenet. After the father's death great sadness fills the house.
Feminine, first declension, from trīstis — sad.

The word, taken apart.
trīstitia is built on trīstis "sad, gloomy" with -itia (Lewis & Short); EtymDB traces trīstis to a Proto-Indo-European form meaning "in a bad mood".

Lesson 3 of 5.
maereō, maerēre — to grieve, mourn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sadness, then the Latin for sorrow.

You'll want to know: maereō, maerēre.
maereō, maerēre — "to grieve, mourn".
Senex maeret, quod amīcus eius periit. The old man grieves because his friend has died.
maereō, maerēre, maeruī; no supine in common use. Sadness that shows, as a mourner's does.

The word, taken apart.
maereō is a native Latin verb; Lewis & Short compares Greek misos "hatred" and Latin miser "wretched", but EtymDB gives no parent, so the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
maeror, maerōris — grief, mourning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sorrow, then the Latin for to grieve.

You'll want to know: maeror, maerōris.
maeror, maerōris — "grief, mourning".
Maeror viduae magnus est. The widow's grief is great.
Masculine, third declension. Grief that shows, in tears and mourning clothes.

The word, taken apart.
maeror is built on the verb maereō "to grieve" with the noun ending -or, as dolor is built on doleō (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
lūctuōsus, lūctuōsa, lūctuōsum — sorrowful, mournful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to grieve, then the Latin for grief.

You'll want to know: lūctuōsus, lūctuōsa, lūctuōsum.
lūctuōsus, lūctuōsa, lūctuōsum — "sorrowful, mournful".
Ille diēs lūctuōsus omnibus cīvibus fuit. That day was a sorrowful one for all the citizens.
Built on lūctus — mourning. It can mean causing grief or feeling it.

The word, taken apart.
lūctuōsus is built on lūctus "mourning", the noun of lūgeō "to mourn", with -ōsus "full of" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB derives it from lūctus).
