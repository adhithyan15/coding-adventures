5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਅਮਰੂਦ (amrūd) — a guava.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a watermelon, then the Punjabi for a papaya.

You'll want to know: ਅਮਰੂਦ.
ਅਮਰੂਦ — amrūd — "a guava".

The word, taken apart.
ਅਮਰੂਦ (amrūd) is a loan, usually traced to Persian amrūd, "pear". Punjabi uses it for the guava.

You'll want to know: ਮੂੰਗਫਲੀ.

The word, taken apart.
ਮੂੰਗਫਲੀ (mū̃gphalī) (mūṁgphalī) is built in Punjabi from two old words: mūṁg, "mung bean", from Sanskrit mudga (CDIAL 10198), and phalī, "pod", from phala, "fruit" (CDIAL 9051). CDIAL marks Punjabi mūṁg, with a query, as perhaps taken from Hindi.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਲੱਸੀ (lassī) — lassi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for peanuts, then the Punjabi for cooked greens.

You'll want to know: ਲੱਸੀ.
ਲੱਸੀ — lassī — "lassi".

The word, taken apart.
ਲੱਸੀ (lassī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lassī, "buttermilk", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 10998).

The word, taken apart.
ਖੀਰ (khīr) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kṣīrī, "a dish prepared with milk" (CDIAL 3705). It is built on Sanskrit kṣīra, "milk".
