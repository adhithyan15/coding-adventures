Portuguese, chapter 444: Coisas da rua.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o poste — the post, the pole.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to report, then the Portuguese for to apply for.

You'll want to know: o poste.
o poste — "the post, the pole".
O carro bateu num poste de luz. The car hit a lamp post.
O poste de luz — the lamp post.

The word, taken apart.
poste goes back to Latin postis 'doorpost' (Lewis & Short), probably through French poste.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o ecoponto — the recycling bins, the recycling point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to apply for, then the Portuguese for the post.

You'll want to know: o ecoponto.
o ecoponto — "the recycling bins, the recycling point".
Levamos as garrafas ao ecoponto. We take the bottles to the recycling bins.
Green for glass, blue for paper, yellow for plastic and metal.

The word, taken apart.
ecoponto is a modern formation from eco-, cut from ecologia, and ponto 'point', inherited from Latin pūnctum (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o pilarete — the bollard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the recycling bins, then the Portuguese for the gutter.

You'll want to know: o pilarete.
o pilarete — "the bollard".
Há pilaretes para os carros não subirem ao passeio. There are bollards to stop cars driving onto the pavement.
A small form of o pilar, the pillar.

The word, taken apart.
pilarete is a small form of pilar 'pillar', which goes back to Latin pīla 'pillar' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
obstruir — to obstruct, to block.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the gutter, then the Portuguese for the bollard.

You'll want to know: obstruir.
obstruir — "to obstruct, to block".
Um camião obstruiu a rua durante a manhã. A truck blocked the street all morning.
Obstruir a passagem — to block the way.

The word, taken apart.
obstruir is a learned borrowing from Latin obstruere 'to build up against, to block' (EtymDB), on struere 'to build'.
