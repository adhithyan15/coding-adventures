Spanish, chapter 415: A Package Abroad.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
el paquete — a bundle packed for travel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall el impreso, the form you fill in. At a post office, the form may travel with el paquete, the package or parcel.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish borrowed paquete through French paquet, a small bundle. English packet travelled from the same word, and pack is a close family clue. Picture several things packed together into one parcel.

Grammar Lens: what happens to the package?
The noun is masculine: el paquete, un paquete. Use enviar for the action: enviar el paquete, to send the package.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el paquete]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un paquete]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: enviar el paquete]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Package? (El paquete.) To send the package? (Enviar el paquete.)
Source: DLE: paquete.


Lesson 2 of 4.
el extranjero — the world outside one's country.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall el paquete. If it crosses a national border, you can send it al extranjero, abroad.

The word, taken apart.
The family reaches back to Latin extraneus, “from outside.” It travelled through Romance in several directions; English strange and stranger are distant relatives. Spanish extranjero can name a foreign person, but el extranjero in the frame al extranjero means abroad.

Grammar Lens: a + el.
Spanish contracts a + el to al: enviar el paquete al extranjero. The place frame, not a person's nationality, is the useful meaning here.
