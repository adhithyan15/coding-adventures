Bengali, chapter 41: Letters You Have Read All Along.
7 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ং (anusvar) — the ng in বাংলা.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ং and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write থ and ঞ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say বাংলা (bānglā), "Bengali". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ং.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
ং — anusvar, the sound ng.
It is inside বাংলা (bānglā), the name of the language itself. The small mark right after বা closes that syllable with ng.

Writing: ং.
Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ং (anusvar) yourself — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
The strip shows where to start this letter and which way to travel, in one attested order, and names its source beneath it. Teachers vary, so treat it as a sound way in, not the only one.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at বাংলা and point at ং (anusvar) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ং (anusvar) three times, saying ng as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ঞ, then ং (anusvar), from memory]
[once you have stopped driving — read: বাংলা aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ং? (anusvar, ng.) Which word did it come from? (বাংলা, bānglā, "Bengali".)

Lesson 6 of 7.
(five letters) (pā̃cṭi akṣar) — From the letter to the word — a first pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 3 seconds]
Say বাংলা (bānglā), then write the letter it gave you.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
letter: ং. name: anusvar. the word it came from: বাংলা bānglā.
letter: ঃ. name: bisarga. the word it came from: দুঃখিত dukkhito.
letter: ঝ. name: jha. the word it came from: বোঝা bojhā.
letter: ফ. name: pha. the word it came from: মাফ maf.
letter: শ. name: sha. the word it came from: শাড়ি shari.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter in the table from memory, then check it]
[your turn — say: the word each one came from]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: each word in the table aloud, and point at its letter]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word did each letter come from? (ং (anusvar) from বাংলা; ঃ (bisarga) from দুঃখিত; ঝ (jha) from বোঝা; ফ (pha) from মাফ; শ (sha) from শাড়ি.)
