# बच्चे — more than one, and an ending you have seen four times

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C39-BACCHA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say **बच्चा** for one child. Try to say *children*, and
notice the track has taught you a great many words and never how to have two of
anything.

## You'll want to know: बच्चे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**बच्चे** (*bacce*) — children.

**एक बच्चा** — one child. **दो बच्चे** — two children.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

The ending moved from **-आ** to **-ए**, and you have watched that happen four
times already:

| where | singular | plural or masculine-plural |
|---|---|---|
| adjectives | काला | काले |
| the habitual | बोलता | बोलते |
| the past copula | था | थे |
| **a noun** | **बच्चा** | **बच्चे** |

**Nouns bend the same way adjectives do**, which is not a coincidence: to Hindi
they are the same kind of word wearing the same endings. Learning noun plurals
is not a new system — it is the one system arriving at a new place.

This applies to **masculine nouns ending in -आ** and to those only. A noun
shaped otherwise does something else, and the next lessons take those in turn.

## Grammar Lens: what changes with it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-CONCEPT-C39-BACCHA-01] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| बच्चा लंबा है। | The child is tall. |
| बच्चे लंबे हैं। | The children are tall. |

Three words moved and the reason is one. The noun went plural, so the adjective
followed it and the copula followed the adjective. **Agreement travels
outward** from the noun, and once the noun is right the rest is automatic.
