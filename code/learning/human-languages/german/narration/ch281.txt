German, chapter 281: Der Einbruch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
einbrechen — to break in, to burgle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the victim, then the German for the crime scene.

You'll want to know: einbrechen.
einbrechen — "to break in, to burgle".
In der Nacht brachen Diebe in das Geschäft ein. At night thieves broke into the shop.
Separable, Perfekt with sein: Jemand ist eingebrochen. Also of ice: Das Eis brach ein.

The word, taken apart.
einbrechen joins ein "in" and brechen "to break", from Old High German brehhan, Proto-Germanic brekaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
klauen — to pinch, to nick (colloquial).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the burglary, then the German for the burglar.

You'll want to know: klauen.
klauen — "to pinch, to nick (colloquial)".
Jemand hat mein Fahrrad geklaut. Someone has nicked my bike.
Colloquial for stehlen. Regular: klaute, hat geklaut.

The word, taken apart.
klauen is usually linked to Klaue "claw", from an older verb meaning "to grab with the claws"; EtymDB lists no parent, so the link is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Beute — the loot; the prey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the burglar, then the German for to pinch.

You'll want to know: die Beute.
die Beute — "the loot; the prey".
Die Diebe teilten die Beute. The thieves shared out the loot.
No plural in this sense. For animals, the prey.

The word, taken apart.
Beute comes from Middle High German biute, Old High German biuta (EtymDB), usually traced to a Low German word for "exchange, sharing out".
