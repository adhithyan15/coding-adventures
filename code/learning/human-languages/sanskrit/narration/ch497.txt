Sanskrit, chapter 497: Wedding Rites.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कन्यादानम् (kanyādānam) — giving away the bride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pavilion, then the Sanskrit for the seven steps.

You'll want to know: कन्यादानम्.
कन्यादानम् — kanyādānam — "giving away the bride".
पिता कन्यादानं करोति। pitā kanyādānaṃ karoti. The father gives away the bride.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
कन्यादानम् (kanyādānam) is built from kanyā, "a girl, a daughter", and dāna, "a gift", from the root √dā of ददाति; MW gives "giving a girl in marriage" (MW).

पाणिग्रहणम् अग्नेः समीपे भवति। pāṇigrahaṇam agneḥ samīpe bhavati. The taking of hands happens beside the fire.

पाणिग्रहणम् (pāṇigrahaṇam) is built from pāṇi, "the hand", and grahaṇa, "taking", from the root √grah of गृह्णाति; MW gives it as pāṇi-graha, "taking the bride by the hand, marriage" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
जुहोति (juhoti) — offers into the fire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for taking the bride's hand, then the Sanskrit for a fire altar.

You'll want to know: जुहोति.
जुहोति — juhoti — "offers into the fire".
वधूः अग्नौ लाजान् जुहोति। vadhūḥ agnau lājān juhoti. The bride offers puffed rice into the fire.
Past (he …): सः अग्नौ लाजान् हुतवान्, saḥ agnau lājān hutavān.

The word, taken apart.
जुहोति (juhoti) comes from the root √hu, "to offer into the fire", a third-class verb that doubles the root (ju-ho-ti); MW's ppp huta is "offered in fire" (MW).

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: सौभाग्यम्.
सौभाग्यम् — saubhāgyam — "good fortune".
सौभाग्यं भवतु इति वृद्धाः वदन्ति। saubhāgyaṃ bhavatu iti vṛddhāḥ vadanti. "May good fortune be yours," say the elders.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
सौभाग्यम् (saubhāgyam) is built on su-bhaga, "lucky", with the lengthening of its first vowel; MW gives "good luck, good fortune, happiness (esp. conjugal felicity)" (MW).
