Lesson 1 of 5.
মশলা (môshlā) — spice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a parcel, then the Bengali for a magazine.

You'll want to know: মশলা.
মশলা — môshlā — "spice".

The word, taken apart.
মশলা (môshlā) is a loan from Arabic maṣāliḥ, "things that are needful", which reached Bengali through Persian. Its Arabic root is ṣ-l-ḥ (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 5.
জিরে (jire) — cumin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a magazine, then the Bengali for spice.

You'll want to know: জিরে.
জিরে — jire — "cumin".

The word, taken apart.
জিরে (jire) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from jīraka, "cummin-seed" (CDIAL 5234). CDIAL lists Bengali jīr, "cummin-seed".
