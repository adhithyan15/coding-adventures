## Grammar Lens: four shapes off one past form
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C90-PAST-PARTICIPLE-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C111-ITTU-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C111-PERFECT-01, ML-CONCEPT-C32-VARUKA-02] -->

| | |
|---|---|
| **വന്നു** | he came |
| **വന്ന** | that came — a vowel sign taken **off** |
| **വന്നിട്ട്** | having come — **-ഇട്ട്** put **on** |
| **വന്നിട്ടുണ്ട്** | he has come — that, plus **ഉണ്ട്** |

**Every row after the first is the row above it with one change.** Nothing in
this chapter asked you to learn a word; it asked you to notice what a form you
already had could be turned into.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-UNDU-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C111-PERFECT-01] -->

**You could say that something happened, and not that it has happened.** The
difference matters more than it looks: answering *is he here?* with the plain
past reports yesterday, and leaves the question standing.

The word that closed the gap was one you had held for a long time. **ഉണ്ട്** was
given to you as *there is* and *somebody has*, and it is the second job that
builds this. English does the same thing with *have*, which is a coincidence
worth having noticed, because very little else about the two verbs matches.
