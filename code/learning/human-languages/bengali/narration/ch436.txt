Bengali, chapter 436: Buds and Sprouts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অঙ্কুর (ôngkur) — a sprout; a shoot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for wood, then the Bengali for to trim.

You'll want to know: অঙ্কুর.
অঙ্কুর — ôngkur — "a sprout; a shoot".
বৃষ্টির পরে বীজ থেকে অঙ্কুর বেরোল। brishṭir pôre bīj theke ôngkur berolô. After the rain shoots came up from the seeds.

The word, taken apart.
অঙ্কুর (ôngkur) is Sanskrit aṅkurá, "a sprout, a shoot" (CDIAL 109), taken over whole; CDIAL also lists the inherited Bengali ã̄kur.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ফোটা (phoṭā) — to bloom; to open (of a flower); to boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a sprout, then the Bengali for a mango blossom.

You'll want to know: ফোটা.
ফোটা — phoṭā — "to bloom; to open (of a flower); to boil".
সকালে বাগানে গোলাপ ফুটল। sôkāle bāgāne golāp phuṭlô. In the morning the roses bloomed in the garden.
Past (he/she …): সেই ফুল ফুটল, sei phul phuṭlô.
The stem is ফুট- in most forms: ফুটল, ফুটবে. Also of water coming to the boil: জল ফুটল.

The word, taken apart.
ফোটা (phoṭā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali phuṭā, "to burst", under the reconstructed sphuṭyati, "is burst open" (CDIAL 13845): a bud bursts open, and so does boiling water.

Lesson 4 of 5.
গজানো (gôjāno) — to sprout; to grow (of leaves or hair).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a mango blossom, then the Bengali for to bloom.

You'll want to know: গজানো.
গজানো — gôjāno — "to sprout; to grow (of leaves or hair)".
বর্ষায় উঠোনে ঘাস গজাল। bôrshāy uṭhone ghās gôjālô. In the monsoon grass sprang up in the courtyard.
Past (he/she …): সেই ঘাস গজাল, sei ghās gôjālô.

The word, taken apart.
গজানো (gôjāno) is the everyday verb for leaves, grass or hair coming up; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
পোঁতা (põtā) — to plant (in the ground); to bury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to bloom, then the Bengali for to sprout.

You'll want to know: পোঁতা.
পোঁতা — põtā — "to plant (in the ground); to bury".
অরুণ উঠোনে একটা আমগাছ পুঁতল। ôrun uṭhone ekṭā āmgāchh pũtlô. Arun planted a mango tree in the courtyard.
Past (he/she …): তিনি গাছ পুঁতলেন, tini gāchh pũtlen.
The stem is পুঁত- in most forms: পুঁতি, পুঁতল; পোঁত- in সে পোঁতে.

The word, taken apart.
পোঁতা (põtā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali põtā, "to bury", under the reconstructed pragupta, "hidden" (CDIAL 8474): to plant is to hide in the ground.
