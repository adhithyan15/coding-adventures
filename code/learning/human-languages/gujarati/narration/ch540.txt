Lesson 1 of 6.
બળજબરી (baḷjabrī) — coercion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to take the plunge, then the Gujarati for resolve.

You'll want to know: બળજબરી.
બળજબરી — baḷjabrī — "coercion".
જો તમે બળજબરી કરશો તો તે કામ નહીં કરે. jo tame baḷjabrī karśo to te kām nahī̃ kare. If you use force, he won't do the work.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
બળજબરી (baḷjabrī) is built from baḷ, "strength", from Sanskrit bala (Monier-Williams), and jabrī, usually traced to Arabic jabr, "compulsion", through Persian.

Lesson 2 of 6.
બંધન (bandhan) — a tie (that binds).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for resolve, then the Gujarati for coercion.

You'll want to know: બંધન.
બંધન — bandhan — "a tie (that binds)".
જો નોકરીનું બંધન ન હોત તો હું આખું વરસ પ્રવાસ કરત. jo nokrīnũ bandhan na hot to hũ ākhũ varas pravās karat. If my job didn't tie me down, I would travel all year.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
બંધન (bandhan) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit bandhana, "binding, a bond" (Monier-Williams), from the root of બાંધવું.

Lesson 3 of 6.
બંધાવું (bandhāvũ) — to be bound (by a promise).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for coercion, then the Gujarati for a tie (that binds).

You'll want to know: બંધાવું.
બંધાવું — bandhāvũ — "to be bound (by a promise)".
જો હું વચન ન આપત તો હું ન બંધાત. jo hũ vacan na āpat to hũ na bandhāt. If I had not given my word, I would not have been bound.
Past (he …): તે વચનથી બંધાયો, te vacanthī bandhāyo.

The word, taken apart.
બંધાવું (bandhāvũ) is the intransitive partner of બાંધવું, "to tie", which CDIAL files under Sanskrit bandhati, "binds" (CDIAL 9139): to be tied, by a rope or by a promise.

The word, taken apart.
જકડવું (jakaḍvũ) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit yatá, "held, restrained", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to tighten, bind" (CDIAL 10400).

Lesson 5 of 6.
સોગંદ (sogand) — an oath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be bound, then the Gujarati for to hold fast.

You'll want to know: સોગંદ.
સોગંદ — sogand — "an oath".
જો તમે સોગંદ ખાશો તો જ હું માનીશ. jo tame sogand khāśo to ja hũ mānīś. Only if you swear will I believe you.
A masculine noun, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
સોગંદ (sogand) is usually traced to Persian saugand, "an oath". Gujarati "eats" an oath: સોગંદ ખાવા.
