Mandarin Chinese, chapter 206: Upstairs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lóu — a building of several floors; a floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for almost, then the Mandarin for distance.

You'll want to know: lóu.
lóu — "a building of several floors; a floor".
wǒ zhù de lóu hěn xīn — The building I live in is new.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lóu is inherited from Middle Chinese luw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a house of more than one storey'.

What you've built.
One more word for asking where things are.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: lóu, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ zhù de lóu hěn xīn — The building I live in is new.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for almost, then the Mandarin for distance, then say lóu again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does lóu mean? ("A building of several floors; a floor".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
shànglóu — to go upstairs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a building of several floors, then the Mandarin for storey.

You'll want to know: shànglóu.
shànglóu — "to go upstairs".
wǒmen zuò diàntī shànglóu — We're taking the lift upstairs.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shànglóu is built from 上 'to go up' and lóu 'a building of several floors', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangX and luw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to climb'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
bàngōngshì — office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for storey, then the Mandarin for to go upstairs.

You'll want to know: bàngōngshì.
bàngōngshì — "office".
lǎoshī zài bàngōngshì — The teacher is in the office.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàngōngshì is built from bàn 'to handle', 公 'public' and shì 'a room', all inherited from Middle Chinese, beanH, kuwng and syit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to provide': a room for public business.
