Lesson 1 of 6.
मस्तिष्कः (mastiṣkaḥ) — the brain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the stomach, then the Sanskrit for breathes.

You'll want to know: मस्तिष्कः.
मस्तिष्कः — mastiṣkaḥ — "the brain".
मस्तिष्केण नरः चिन्तयति। mastiṣkeṇa naraḥ cintayati. A person thinks with the brain.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
मस्तिष्कः (mastiṣkaḥ) MW gives "the brain" from the Rigveda on; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 6.
कपालम् (kapālam) — the skull.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for breathes, then the Sanskrit for the brain.

You'll want to know: कपालम्.
कपालम् — kapālam — "the skull".
कठिनं कपालं मस्तिष्कं रक्षति। kaṭhinaṃ kapālaṃ mastiṣkaṃ rakṣati. The hard skull protects the brain.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
कपालम् (kapālam) is traced to the root √kamp in the Uṇādi list that MW follows; its first senses are "a cup, a dish", and "the skull, the cranium" comes after them (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
मुण्डयति (muṇḍayati) — shaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a beard, then the Sanskrit for an eyelash.

You'll want to know: मुण्डयति.
मुण्डयति — muṇḍayati — "shaves".
नापितः मम पितुः श्मश्रु मुण्डयति। nāpitaḥ mama pituḥ śmaśru muṇḍayati. The barber shaves my father's beard.
Past (he …): सः शिरः मुण्डितवान्, saḥ śiraḥ muṇḍitavān.

The word, taken apart.
मुण्डयति (muṇḍayati) is traced by MW to a root √muṇḍ, "to shave", which MW thinks probably made up to explain words such as muṇḍa, "shaven"; its history is not settled (MW).
