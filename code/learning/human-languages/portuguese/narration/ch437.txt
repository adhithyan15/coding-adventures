Portuguese, chapter 437: Ruas e becos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a avenida — the avenue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the road junction, then the Portuguese for to carry on.

You'll want to know: a avenida.
a avenida — "the avenue".
A avenida principal tem muitas lojas. The main avenue has a lot of shops.
Shortened to Av. in addresses.

The word, taken apart.
avenida was borrowed from Spanish avenida, which came from French avenue 'way of approach' (EtymDB), on Latin advenīre 'to arrive'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a travessa — the side street; the serving dish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to carry on, then the Portuguese for the avenue.

You'll want to know: a travessa.
a travessa — "the side street; the serving dish".
A padaria fica numa travessa da rua principal. The bakery is in a side street off the main road.
For serving food, uma travessa is a big dish.

The word, taken apart.
travessa goes back to Latin trānsversa 'lying across', from trānsvertere 'to turn across' (Lewis & Short): a street that crosses another.

Lesson 4 of 5.
a viela — the narrow lane, the alleyway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the side street, then the Portuguese for the alley.

You'll want to know: a viela.
a viela — "the narrow lane, the alleyway".
No bairro antigo, as vielas são estreitas e escuras. In the old quarter, the alleyways are narrow and dark.
Typical of the old quarters of hill towns.

The word, taken apart.
viela is usually explained as a small form of via 'way' with -ela, from Latin via (Lewis & Short); EtymDB has no Portuguese entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
a bifurcação — the fork (in the road), the split.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the alley, then the Portuguese for the narrow lane.

You'll want to know: a bifurcação.
a bifurcação — "the fork (in the road), the split".
Na bifurcação, a estrada da direita vai para a praia. At the fork, the right-hand road goes to the beach.
The verb is bifurcar-se.

The word, taken apart.
bifurcação is a learned formation on Latin bifurcus 'two-pronged', from bi- 'two' and furca 'fork' (Lewis & Short).
