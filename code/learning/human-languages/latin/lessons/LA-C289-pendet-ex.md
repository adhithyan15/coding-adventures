# pendet ex — it depends

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C159-ACT159-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C289-SI-REAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You own **pendeō**, "to hang". Say "if it rains, we stay in the
farmhouse". (*Sī pluit, in vīllā manēmus.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Asked whether you will walk tomorrow, the honest answer is often
neither yes nor no.

## You'll want to know: pendet ex
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C289-PENDET-EX-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C242-QUAL242-03, LA-LEX-TEMPESTAS-PLUIT-01, LA-LEX-C221-RES221-04, LA-LEX-C112-WANT112-02] -->

**pendet ex** — "it depends on", word for word "it hangs from". **ex** is
"out of, from", and the thing it hangs from goes in the ablative:

> **Tōtum ex tempestāte pendet.** — It all depends on the weather.

[PAUSE 2s] Cicero says it of hope: **spēs pendet ex fortūnā**, hope hangs
on fortune (Lewis & Short quote it under *pendeo*). English "depend" is the
same picture, *dē-pendēre*, to hang down from.

## You'll want to know: in tuā potestāte est
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C289-IN-POTESTATE-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C109-CAN109-05, LA-LEX-C289-SI-01] -->

When it depends on the person you are talking to:

> **In tuā potestāte est.** — It is up to you; it is in your power.

[PAUSE 2s] You own **potestās**, "power, the ability to act"; *in tuā
potestāte* is "in your power". Cicero, writing about fate, asks what is *in
nostrā potestāte*, in our power.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**pendeō**, as its own lesson said, is the intransitive partner of *pendō*,
"to weigh, to hang up", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European
*\*(s)pend-*, "to stretch, to pull" (EtymDB 2.1). **ex** is inherited from
Proto-Indo-European *\*h1eghs*, "out" (EtymDB 2.1), and English *exit*
begins with it. A thing that *pendet ex* is held up by something else, and
falls if that gives way.
