Telugu, chapter 534: Quarrels.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
గొడవపడు (goḍavapaḍu) — to have a row; to quarrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for hospitality, then the Telugu for to chat.

You'll want to know: గొడవపడు.
గొడవపడు — goḍavapaḍu — "to have a row; to quarrel".
చిన్న విషయానికి అన్నదమ్ములు గొడవపడ్డారు. cinna viṣayāniki annadammulu goḍavapaḍḍāru. The brothers had a row over a small thing.
Past (I …): గొడవపడ్డాను, goḍavapaḍḍānu.
-తో గొడవపడు (goḍavapaḍu), to quarrel with.

The word, taken apart.
గొడవపడు (goḍavapaḍu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2048 lists Telugu goḍava, "bother, worry, fuss", with Kannada goḍave; the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853) makes the verb.

Lesson 2 of 5.
తగాదా (tagādā) — a dispute; a row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to chat, then the Telugu for to have a row.

You'll want to know: తగాదా.
తగాదా — tagādā — "a dispute; a row".
పొలం గురించి రెండు కుటుంబాల మధ్య తగాదా వచ్చింది. polaṁ guriñci reṇḍu kuṭumbāla madhya tagādā vaccindi. A dispute arose between two families over a field.
Often about land or money.

The word, taken apart.
తగాదా (tagādā) is usually traced to Arabic taqāḍā, "a demand, a claim", which came through Hindustani.

బొమ్మ కొనలేదని పాప అలిగింది. bomma konalēdani pāpa aligindi. The little girl sulked because nobody bought her the doll.

The word, taken apart.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మనస్పర్థ (manaspartha) — a rift; a misunderstanding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to sulk, then the Telugu for a compromise.

You'll want to know: మనస్పర్థ.
మనస్పర్థ — manaspartha — "a rift; a misunderstanding".
చిన్న మనస్పర్థ వల్ల స్నేహితులు మాట్లాడుకోవడం మానేశారు. cinna manaspartha valla snēhitulu māṭlāḍukōvaḍaṁ mānēśāru. Because of a small rift the friends stopped talking.
Plural మనస్పర్థలు; మనసు, mind, + స్పర్థ, rivalry.

The word, taken apart.
మనస్పర్థ (manaspartha) is a learned compound built from Sanskrit manas, "mind", and spardhā, "rivalry, emulation" (Monier-Williams).
