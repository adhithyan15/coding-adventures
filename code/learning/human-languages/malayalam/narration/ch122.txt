Malayalam, chapter 122: Quick Replies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ശരിക്കും (śarikkuṁ) — really.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a station, then the Malayalam for a map.

You'll want to know: ശരിക്കും.
ശരിക്കും (śarikkuṁ) — "really, truly", built on ശരി (śari), "right, okay". ശരിക്കും? — "really?".

The word, taken apart.
ശരിക്കും (śarikkuṁ) is śari, "right", with two endings: "rightly, truly". The origin of śari itself is uncertain; it is usually traced to Sanskrit sadṛśa, "alike" (CDIAL 13120).

What you've built.
Okay, turned into really.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ഒന്നുമില്ല (onnumilla) — nothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a map, then the Malayalam for really.

You'll want to know: ഒന്നുമില്ല.
ഒന്നുമില്ല (onnumilla) — "nothing", literally "not even one": ഒന്ന്, "one", ‑ഉം, "even", ഇല്ല, "not".

The word, taken apart.
ഒന്നുമില്ല (onnumilla) is native Dravidian: onnŭ, "one" (DEDR 990), -um, "even", and illa, "not" (DEDR 2559): "not even one".

What you've built.
One, even, not.

Lesson 3 of 5.
അയ്യോ (ayyō) — oh no!

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for really, then the Malayalam for nothing.

You'll want to know: അയ്യോ.
അയ്യോ (ayyō) — "oh no!", "oh dear!", heard everywhere in the south when something goes wrong.

The word, taken apart.
അയ്യോ (ayyō) is an exclamation shared across the south of India; Tamil says aiyō. It is a cry rather than a word built from a root, so no dictionary entry lies behind it.

What you've built.
The sound of a small disaster.

Lesson 4 of 5.
അതുതന്നെ (atutanne) — that very one, the same.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for nothing, then the Malayalam for oh no.

You'll want to know: അതുതന്നെ.
അതുതന്നെ (atutanne) — "that very one, exactly that": അത് (atŭ), "that", with തന്നെ, "itself".

The word, taken apart.
അതുതന്നെ (atutanne) is native Dravidian: atŭ, "that" (DEDR 1), with tanne, "itself", built on tān, "self" (DEDR 3196, with Tamil tāṉ).

What you've built.
That, made emphatic.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ഓഹോ (ōhō) — oh, I see.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for oh no, then the Malayalam for the same.

You'll want to know: ഓഹോ.
ഓഹോ (ōhō) — "oh, I see!", the sound of understanding arriving.

The word, taken apart.
ഓഹോ (ōhō) is an exclamation, the sound of understanding arriving. It is made of sound rather than built from a root, so no dictionary entry lies behind it.

What you've built.
The happy opposite of ayyō.
