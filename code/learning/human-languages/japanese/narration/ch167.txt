Japanese, chapter 167: Starting, Finishing and Calling Off.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はじまる (hajimaru) — to begin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for this evening, then the Japanese for every night.

You'll want to know: はじまる.
はじまる — hajimaru — "to begin".
あした まつりが はじまります。 — ashita matsuri ga hajimarimasu — "The festival begins tomorrow."
Polite はじまります, hajimarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
はじまる (hajimaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Polite はじめます, hajimemasu; an ichidan verb. Its intransitive partner is はじまる, hajimaru.

The word, taken apart.
はじめる (hajimeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as hajimaru, "to begin"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ちゅうし (chūshi) — calling off; suspension.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to finish, then the Japanese for to become different.

You'll want to know: ちゅうし.
ちゅうし — chūshi — "calling off; suspension".
あしたの まつりは ちゅうしでしょう。 — ashita no matsuri wa chūshi deshō — "Tomorrow's festival will probably be called off."

The word, taken apart.
ちゅうし (chūshi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are chū, "middle" (Middle Chinese trjuwng) and shi, "stop" (tsyiX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
