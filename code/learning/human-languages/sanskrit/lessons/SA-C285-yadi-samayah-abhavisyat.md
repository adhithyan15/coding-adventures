# यदि समयः अभविष्यत् (yadi samayaḥ abhaviṣyat) — what would have been

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C284-CONDITIONAL-REAL-01, SA-GRAMMAR-PAST-AUGMENT-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C275-FUTURE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if there is time, I will come". (*yadi samayaḥ bhaviṣyati, tarhi aham āgamiṣyāmi*.) Then say "he went", the old way. (*saḥ agacchat*.) The next form puts those two together.

## You'll want to know: the conditional
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C285-WOULD-01]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C139-FUT139-04, SA-LEX-C86-WEEKEND-04, SA-GRAMMAR-PAST-AUGMENT-01] -->

> **यदि समयः अभविष्यत्,**
>
> *yadi samayaḥ abhaviṣyat,*
>
> **तर्हि अहम् अगमिष्यम्।**
>
> *tarhi aham agamiṣyam.* — "If there had been time, I would have gone."

**अभविष्यत्** (*abhaviṣyat*), "would have been", is the future stem **भविष्य-** with the **अ-** and the *-t* of **अगच्छत्** around it. For "I" the ending is *-am*: **अगमिष्यम्** (*agamiṣyam*), "I would have gone". It says that the thing did not happen (Pāṇini 3.3.139; the **अ-**, 6.4.71).

| the future | the conditional |
|---|---|
| **भविष्यति**, will be | **अभविष्यत्**, would have been |
| **गमिष्यति**, will go | **अगमिष्यत्**, would have gone |
| **करिष्यति**, will do | **अकरिष्यत्**, would have done |
| **गमिष्यामि**, I will go | **अगमिष्यम्**, I would have gone |

After the prefix **आ-**, the **अ-** melts into its long *ā*: **आगमिष्यम्** (*āgamiṣyam*), "I would have come".

> **यदि समयः अभविष्यत्,**
>
> *yadi samayaḥ abhaviṣyat,*
>
> **तर्हि अहम् आगमिष्यम्।**
>
> *tarhi aham āgamiṣyam.* — "If there had been time, I would have come."

## You'll want to know: both halves, and "if he were to"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C285-WOULD-02]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C279-SHOULD-01, SA-LEX-C74-MARKET-02, SA-LEX-C170-WRK170-01] -->

Both halves take it, for a past that did not come about:

> **यदि धनम् अभविष्यत्,**
>
> *yadi dhanam abhaviṣyat,*
>
> **तर्हि सः कार्यम् अकरिष्यत्।**
>
> *tarhi saḥ kāryam akariṣyat.* — "If there had been money, he would have done the work."

You may also hear **यदि** with the *-et* of advice, "if he were to …"; meet it, and say the conditional yourself:

> **यदि सः आगच्छेत्,**
>
> *yadi saḥ āgacchet,*
>
> **तर्हि मीरा प्रसन्ना भवेत्।**
>
> *tarhi mīrā prasannā bhavet.* — "If he were to come, Mira would be pleased."
