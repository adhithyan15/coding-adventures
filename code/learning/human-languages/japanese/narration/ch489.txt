Japanese, chapter 489: Fields and Harvests.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
のうぎょう (nōgyō) — farming; agriculture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for iron, then the Japanese for construction work.

You'll want to know: のうぎょう.
のうぎょう — nōgyō — "farming; agriculture".
そふは のうぎょうを しています。 — sofu wa nōgyō o shiteimasu — "My grandfather is a farmer."

The word, taken apart.
のうぎょう (nōgyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nō, "agriculture" (Middle Chinese nowng) and gyō, "business" (ngjaep) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
たがやす (tagayasu) — to plough; to till.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for farming, then the Japanese for a harvest.

You'll want to know: たがやす.
たがやす — tagayasu — "to plough; to till".
のうかは はるに たんぼを たがやします。 — nōka wa haru ni tanbo o tagayashimasu — "Farmers plough the rice fields in spring."
Polite たがやします, tagayashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): のうかが たんぼを たがやしました。, nōka ga tanbo o tagayashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
たがやす (tagayasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

[pause 2 seconds]

The word, taken apart.
こむぎ (komugi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from ko, "small" and mugi, "wheat, barley" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
