Arabic, chapter 443: Bed and view.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بواب (bawwāb) — a doorman; a caretaker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to set alight, then the Arabic for pine trees.

You'll want to know: بواب.
بواب — bawwāb — "a doorman; a caretaker".
حمل البواب أمتعتنا إلى المصعد. ḥamala al-bawwāb amtiʿatanā ilā al-miṣʿad. The doorman carried our luggage to the lift.

The word, taken apart.
بواب (bawwāb) is built on the native Arabic root b-w-b, "a door" (Lane). The trade pattern faʿʿāl makes him the man of the door.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ملاءة (mulāʾa) — a bed sheet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for pine trees, then the Arabic for a doorman.

You'll want to know: ملاءة.
ملاءة — mulāʾa — "a bed sheet".
كانت الملاءة نظيفة وناعمة. kānat al-mulāʾa naẓīfa wa-nāʿima. The sheet was clean and soft.

The word, taken apart.
ملاءة (mulāʾa) is built on the native Arabic root m-l-ʾ, "to fill" (Lane). Lane gives mulāʾa as a cloth wrapped about the body, often two pieces sewn together; modern Arabic uses it for a bed sheet.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أطل (aṭalla) — to overlook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a bed sheet, then the Arabic for cleanliness.

You'll want to know: أطل.
أطل — aṭalla — "to overlook".
تطل غرفتنا على البحر. tuṭillu ghurfatunā ʿalā al-baḥr. Our room overlooks the sea.
Present: يطل, yuṭill.
It takes ʿalā: aṭalla ʿalā, "to look out over".

The word, taken apart.
أطل (aṭalla) is usually traced to the native Arabic root ṭ-l-l, "to look down from above". It is Form IV of a doubled root.
