Latin, chapter 421: Trade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
commercium, commerciī — trade, commerce; dealings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a buyer, then the Latin for a seller.

You'll want to know: commercium, commerciī.
commercium, commerciī — "trade, commerce; dealings".
Inter duās urbēs magnum commercium erat. There was a lot of trade between the two cities.
commercium, commerciī n., com- + merx, goods. Any dealings with people, not only buying and selling. English commerce.

The word, taken apart.
commercium is built from com- "together" and merx, mercis "goods" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English commerce comes from it.

In prōvinciā negōtiābātur. He was doing business in the province.
Deponent: negōtior, negōtiārī, negōtiātus sum, built on negōtium, business. English negotiate.

The word, taken apart.
negōtior is built on negōtium "business" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which is nec "not" and ōtium "leisure". English negotiate comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
nūndinae, nūndinārum — market day; a market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for trade, then the Latin for to do business.

You'll want to know: nūndinae, nūndinārum.
nūndinae, nūndinārum — "market day; a market".
Rūsticī nūndinīs in oppidum veniunt. Country people come into town on market days.
nūndinae, nūndinārum f. pl., the ninth day, counted inclusively: every eighth day was market day.

The word, taken apart.
nūndinae is built on nūndinus "of the ninth day" (EtymDB), from novem "nine" and the root of diēs "day" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
portōrium, portōriī — harbour dues, customs duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to do business, then the Latin for market day.

You'll want to know: portōrium, portōriī.
portōrium, portōriī — "harbour dues, customs duty".
Mercātōrēs portōrium solvunt. The merchants pay customs duty.
portōrium, portōriī n., a toll on goods carried through a harbour, a gate or across a frontier.

The word, taken apart.
portōrium comes from the root of portō "to carry" (Lewis & Short), and EtymDB links it with porta "a gate": a toll on what is carried through.
