Sanskrit, chapter 318: The Army Marches.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सेना (senā) — an army.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a peace treaty, then the Sanskrit for a vassal.

You'll want to know: सेना.
सेना — senā — "an army".
सेना नगरात् निर्गच्छति। senā nagarāt nirgacchati. The army marches out of the city.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सेना (senā) comes from the root √si, "to bind", by MW's account; its oldest sense is a missile or spear, and its usual sense an army, an armed force (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
प्रयाति (prayāti) — sets out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a warrior, then the Sanskrit for a war.

You'll want to know: प्रयाति.
प्रयाति — prayāti — "sets out".
प्रातः सेना युद्धाय प्रयाति। prātaḥ senā yuddhāya prayāti. In the morning the army sets out for battle.
Past (it …): सेना प्रातः प्रयातवती, senā prātaḥ prayātavatī.

The word, taken apart.
प्रयाति (prayāti) is built from pra, "forth", and the root √yā, "to go"; MW gives pra-yā as to go forth, set out, advance (MW).
