# म्हणणे (mhaṇṇe) — to say these words

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-SANGNE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last word arrived by ear alone, with one letter still owed to
your hand. This one owes you nothing: every piece of it is already yours to
write.

## You'll want to know: म्हणणे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-MHANNE]; assesses=[MR-LEX-SANGNE] -->

> **म्हणणे** — *mhaṇṇe* — **to say**

> **मी "नमस्कार" म्हणतो / म्हणते.** — *mī "namaskār" mhaṇto / mhaṇte.* —
> **I say "namaskār."**

Two verbs, two jobs. *Sāṅgṇe* tells a person something. **म्हणणे** utters the
words themselves — what came out of the mouth, quoted.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-NAMASKAR-READ-01, MR-SCRIPT-RA-01] -->

**म** bound to **ह** by the halant you have written since **नमस्कार**, then
**ण** twice, then **े**. **म्ह-ण-णे**. Say it with the breath and the *m*
together, not one after the other.

The same verb gives **म्हणून** *mhaṇūn*, "therefore" — literally *having said
that*, which is how a good many Marathi sentences turn a corner.

## The word, taken apart — a b that became an m
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-MHANNE-BHAN]; assesses=[MR-ETYMON-SANGNE-KENS] -->

**म्हणणे** comes through Prakrit **bhaṇai** from Sanskrit **भणति** *bhaṇati*,
"speaks." The opening **भ** *bh-* did something Marathi is fond of: it shifted
to the **म्ह** *mh-* cluster, breath and nasal at once. Hindi kept a plainer
descendant in *bhannā*. Set beside *sāṅgṇe*, which came from a root for
proclaiming, Marathi ended up with one verb for reciting outward and one for
plain uttering.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-MHANNE, MR-ETYMON-MHANNE-BHAN, MR-LEX-SANGNE, MR-ETYMON-SANGNE-KENS, MR-SCRIPT-NAMASKAR-READ-01, MR-SCRIPT-RA-01] -->

- [YOU SAY: **mhaṇṇe** — to say]

- [YOU CONTRAST: *sāṅgā* and **म्हणतो** — telling a person, uttering the words]
- [YOU WRITE: **म्हणणे**, and notice that every piece was already yours]
- [YOU READ ALOUD: **नमस्कार**, whose halant and whose **र** you are reusing here]
