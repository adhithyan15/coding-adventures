Lesson 1 of 6.
xīnniáng — bride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a happy occasion, then the Mandarin for the bridegroom.

You'll want to know: xīnniáng.
xīnniáng — "bride".
xīnniáng jīntiān tèbié piàoliang — The bride is especially beautiful today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xīnniáng is built from 新 'new' and niáng 'a young woman', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sin and nrjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a title for a young woman.

Lesson 2 of 6.
经过 (jīngguò) — the sequence of events; what happened.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the bridegroom, then the Mandarin for the bride.

You'll want to know: 经过.
经过 — jīngguò — "the sequence of events; what happened".
他说了那天的经过 tā shuō le nà tiān de jīngguò — He told us what happened that day.
The new character, in pieces: 经 (jīng) — eight strokes in three pieces: the silk piece of 给 on the left, three strokes; on the right a fork of two strokes, a bar that turns into a sweep to the lower left and a short stroke falling to the right, over 工, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
经过 (jīngguò) is built from 经 (jīng) 'to pass through' and 过 'to go past', both inherited from Middle Chinese, keng and kwaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the warp of a cloth' and 'a path' and the second 'to cross' and 'to go beyond'.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
经 (jīng) — the silk piece, then a fork over 工.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — gěi, gōng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the silk piece, then a fork over 工.
经 (jīng).
Three pieces: the silk piece on the left; on the right a fork over 工. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The silk piece, three strokes. As on the left of 给: two strokes that each drop to the left and turn to the right, then a stroke rising to the right.
The fork, two strokes. A bar that turns into a sweep down to the lower left, then a short stroke falling to the right.
工, three strokes: a short bar, a vertical, and a long bar across the foot.
3 + 2 + 3 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 经 (jīng), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 经 (jīng)? (Three.) Where have you made the left piece? (On the left of 给.) What sits at the foot of the right side? (工.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 经 (jīng), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 4 of 6.
新人 (xīnrén) — the bride and groom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the bride, then the Mandarin for the whole story of what happened.

You'll want to know: 新人.
新人 — xīnrén — "the bride and groom".
今天的新人是我的同学 jīntiān de xīnrén shì wǒ de tóngxué — The couple getting married today were my classmates.

The word, taken apart.
新人 (xīnrén) is built from 新 'new' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sin and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the new people, the couple who marry that day.
