## You'll want to know: 下午茶
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C166-TEA166-02]; assesses=[] -->

**下午茶** — *xiàwǔchá* — "afternoon tea (a light meal)".

> **我们三点吃下午茶** *wǒmen sān diǎn chī xiàwǔchá* — We have afternoon tea at three.

The new character, in pieces: 茶 — nine strokes in three pieces: the grass piece of 菜 on top, the two strokes of 人 under it, and at the foot a horizontal, a vertical with a hook, and two short strokes on either side of it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**下午茶** (*xiàwǔchá*) is built from 下午 'afternoon' and 茶 'tea'. All three parts are inherited from Middle Chinese, *haeX*, *nguX* and *drae* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary marks the tea character as a popular form. Tea with something to eat, as a light meal of its own.
