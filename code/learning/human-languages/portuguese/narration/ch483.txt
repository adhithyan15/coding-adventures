Portuguese, chapter 483: Espirros e comichão.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
espirrar — to sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for heel, then the Portuguese for navel.

You'll want to know: espirrar.
espirrar — "to sneeze".
Na primavera, o meu pai espirra o dia todo. In spring, my father sneezes all day.
Past: espirrei, espirrou.

The word, taken apart.
espirrar is inherited from Latin exspīrāre "to breathe out" (EtymDB), built on spīrāre "to breathe".

Lesson 3 of 5.
alérgico — allergic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sneeze, then the Portuguese for sneeze.

You'll want to know: alérgico.
alérgico — "allergic".
Sou alérgico a gatos. I am allergic to cats.
Ser alérgico a — to be allergic to. The illness is a alergia.

The word, taken apart.
alérgico is built on alergia, which is usually traced to German Allergie, a medical coinage from Greek állos "other" and érgon "work".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a comichão — itch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for sneeze, then the Portuguese for allergic.

You'll want to know: a comichão.
a comichão — "itch".
A picada do mosquito deu-me comichão. The mosquito bite made me itch.
In Brazil: a coceira. Ter comichão — to itch.

The word, taken apart.
comichão is usually traced to Latin comēstiō "a consuming", from comedere "to eat up" (Lewis & Short): an itch feels as if the skin were being eaten.
