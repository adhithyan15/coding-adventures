## You'll want to know: chuǎnghuò
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C539-BLAME539-05]; assesses=[] -->

*chuǎnghuò* — "to get into trouble; to cause a mess".

> *yàoshi nǐ zài chuǎnghuò wǒ jiù gàosu nǐ māma* — If you get into trouble again, I'll tell your mum.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*chuǎnghuò* is built from *chuǎng* 'to rush' and *huò* 'disaster'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese *hwaX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'harm'. The dictionary lists the first as *trhimH*, a horse dashing out of a gate, but chuǎng is not the regular outcome of that form, so its history is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for saying what would happen.
