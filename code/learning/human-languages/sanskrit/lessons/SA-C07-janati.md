# जानाति (jānāti) — "knows," and English *know* is the same word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-DHATU-GANA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two ways a root has become a stem so far: nothing added, or a vowel
added. Here is the third, and it is the boldest.

## You'll want to know: जानाति
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-JANATI-KNOW]; assesses=[] -->

| Sanskrit | sound | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| जानाति | *jānāti* | he, she, or it **knows** |
| ज्ञान | *jñāna* | **knowledge** |

The root is **ज्ञा** (*jñā*). Its class, the ninth, does not add a vowel at the
end — it plants a whole syllable **-ना-** (*-nā-*) between root and ending:
*jā* + *nā* + *ti* → *jānāti*. The marker sits inside the word.

## Sounds you'll need: the letters in जानाति
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**जा** is *jā*; **ना** *nā* and **ति** *ti* close it. The root's own spelling
**ज्ञ** is a famous stacked letter, *j* over *ñ*, said *gya* across much of India
today and *jña* in the classical description. Pick one and stay with it.

## The word, taken apart — the most travelled root in the book
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-GNO-DESCENDANTS]; assesses=[] -->

**ज्ञा** is PIE \**gno-*. English **know** is that root undisguised, and so are
**can** and **cunning**, which began as "know how."

Latin took it as *noscere*: **notice**, **notion**, **cognition**, **ignore**
("not-know"), **noble** ("well-known"). Greek took it as *gnōsis*: **diagnosis**,
**prognosis**, **agnostic**.

One root, a whole shelf of English vocabulary, and Sanskrit **ज्ञान** *jñāna*
sitting at the other end of it.

## What you've built: six verbs, three ways to build a stem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-ASTI-BHAVATI-BE, SA-LEX-GACCHATI-GO, SA-LEX-AGACCHATI-COME, SA-LEX-KHADATI-EAT, SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE, SA-LEX-JANATI-KNOW, SA-GRAMMAR-DHATU-GANA, SA-GRAMMAR-UPASARGA-PREFIX] -->

*Asti*, *bhavati*, *gacchati*, *āgacchati*, *khādati*, *paśyati*, *jānāti*. Every
one is a root plus a class plus an ending, and the class was the only thing that
varied: nothing inserted (*as-ti*), a vowel appended (*bhava-ti*, *khāda-ti*), or
a syllable planted inside (*jā-nā-ti*). Prefix one and you aim it: *ā-gacchati*.
