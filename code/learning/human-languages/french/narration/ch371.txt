French, chapter 371: Changement de programme.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
annuler — to cancel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to adapt, then the French for supple.

You'll want to know: annuler.
annuler — "to cancel".
Le concert est annulé à cause de la pluie. The concert is cancelled because of the rain.
Annuler une réservation — to cancel a booking.

The word, taken apart.
annuler comes from Late Latin annūllāre "to bring to nothing" (EtymDB), built on nūllus "none" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
reporter — to postpone, to put off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for supple, then the French for to cancel.

You'll want to know: reporter.
reporter — "to postpone, to put off".
Nous avons reporté la réunion à jeudi. We postponed the meeting to Thursday.
Reporter à plus tard — to put off till later. The noun un reporter is a news reporter.

The word, taken apart.
reporter is built from re- "back" and porter "to carry", after Latin reportāre "to carry back" (Lewis & Short), on portāre "to carry": you carry the date further on.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: provisoire.
provisoire — "temporary, provisional".
C'est une solution provisoire, en attendant mieux. It is a temporary solution, until something better comes.
One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
provisoire comes from Medieval Latin prōvīsōrius, built on Latin prōvīsus, the participle of prōvidēre "to foresee, to provide for" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: définitif.
définitif — "final, definitive".
Sa décision est définitive. Her decision is final.
Feminine définitive. En définitive — in the end.

The word, taken apart.
définitif is a learned borrowing from Latin dēfīnītīvus (EtymDB), from dēfīnīre "to set limits, to define", built on fīnis "end, boundary" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
programmer — to schedule, to plan; to program.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for temporary, then the French for final.

You'll want to know: programmer.
programmer — "to schedule, to plan; to program".
Nous avons programmé la fête pour samedi. We scheduled the party for Saturday.
Also to program a machine.

The word, taken apart.
programmer is built on programme, from Late Latin programma "a proclamation, an edict" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from Greek prógramma "a written public notice", built from pró "before" and gráphein "to write".
