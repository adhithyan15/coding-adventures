Bengali, chapter 439: Happiness and Hardship.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
সুখ (sukh) — happiness; comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to feel, then the Bengali for to become aware of.

You'll want to know: সুখ.
সুখ — sukh — "happiness; comfort".
আমার দাদু গ্রামে সুখে আছেন। āmār dādu grāme sukhe āchhen. My grandfather lives happily in the village.
সুখে থাকা, to live happily.

The word, taken apart.
সুখ (sukh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sukha, "pleasant, easy" (CDIAL 13451), and so comfort and happiness; সুখী (taught), contented, is the adjective made from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
কষ্ট (kôshṭô) — suffering; hardship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to become aware of, then the Bengali for happiness.

You'll want to know: কষ্ট.
কষ্ট — kôshṭô — "suffering; hardship".
গরমে বাসে যেতে আমার কষ্ট হয়। gôrôme bāse jete āmār kôshṭô hôy. Travelling by bus in the heat is hard for me.
কষ্ট (kôshṭô) হওয়া, to be hard for someone: আমার কষ্ট (kôshṭô) হয়. কষ্ট (kôshṭô) পাওয়া, to suffer, to be hurt.

The word, taken apart.
কষ্ট (kôshṭô) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kaṣṭa, "bad, ill, painful" (Monier-Williams); Bengali uses it as a noun, the hardship itself.

তৃপ্তি — tripti — "satisfaction; contentment".
দিদিমার রান্না খেয়ে আমরা তৃপ্তি পেলাম। didimār rānnā kheye āmrā tripti pelām. We ate my grandmother's cooking with real satisfaction.
তৃপ্তি (tripti) পাওয়া, to find satisfaction; তৃপ্তি (tripti) করে খাওয়া, to eat with relish.

The word, taken apart.
তৃপ্তি (tripti) is a tatsama, Sanskrit tṛpti, "satisfaction, contentment" (Monier-Williams): the feeling of having had enough.

উপভোগ করা — upôbhog kôrā — "to enjoy".
আমরা ছুটিটা পুরো উপভোগ করলাম। āmrā chhuṭiṭā puro upôbhog kôrlām. We enjoyed the holiday to the full.
Past (he/she …): তিনি গানটা উপভোগ করলেন, tini gānṭā upôbhog kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
উপভোগ করা (upôbhog kôrā) is Sanskrit upabhoga, "enjoyment, using" (Monier-Williams), with kôrā, "to do" (CDIAL 2814).

Lesson 5 of 5.
মেতে ওঠা (mete oṭhā) — to get carried away; to be swept up in excitement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for satisfaction, then the Bengali for to enjoy.

You'll want to know: মেতে ওঠা.
মেতে ওঠা — mete oṭhā — "to get carried away; to be swept up in excitement".
খেলা দেখে ছেলেরা আনন্দে মেতে উঠল। khælā dekhe chhelerā ānônde mete uṭhlô. Watching the game, the boys got carried away with joy.
Past (he/she …): তিনি গল্পে মেতে উঠলেন, tini gôlpe mete uṭhlen.

The word, taken apart.
মেতে ওঠা (mete oṭhā) is mete, the -এ form of mātā, "to be wild with excitement", which belongs with the Bengali māt, "delight", that CDIAL lists under a reconstructed form (CDIAL 9753), with oṭhā, "to rise" (CDIAL 1793).
