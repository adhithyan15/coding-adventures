Sanskrit, chapter 298: After the Fire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
क्षतिः (kṣatiḥ) — damage; harm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for puts out a fire, then the Sanskrit for dies down.

You'll want to know: क्षतिः.
क्षतिः — kṣatiḥ — "damage; harm".
अग्निना ग्रामस्य क्षतिः भवति। agninā grāmasya kṣatiḥ bhavati. The fire does damage to the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
क्षतिः (kṣatiḥ) MW gives kṣati as injury, loss and damage, beside kṣata, "wounded", but gives no root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
नाशयति (nāśayati) — destroys.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dies down, then the Sanskrit for damage.

You'll want to know: नाशयति.
नाशयति — nāśayati — "destroys".
अग्निः गृहाणि नाशयति। agniḥ gṛhāṇi nāśayati. The fire destroys the houses.
Past (it …): अग्निः गृहाणि नाशितवान्, agniḥ gṛhāṇi nāśitavān.

The word, taken apart.
नाशयति (nāśayati) comes from the root √naś, "to be lost, perish", which also gives naśyati, in its causative form: to make perish, destroy (MW).

गृहस्य छदिः भग्नम् अस्ति। gṛhasya chadiḥ bhagnam asti. The roof of the house is broken.

Lesson 4 of 5.
पौरः (pauraḥ) — a citizen; a townsman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for destroys, then the Sanskrit for broken.

You'll want to know: पौरः.
पौरः — pauraḥ — "a citizen; a townsman".
आहताः पौराः औषधं पिबन्ति। āhatāḥ paurāḥ auṣadhaṃ pibanti. The injured townspeople take medicine.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पौरः (pauraḥ) is built on pura, "a town", with the lengthened first vowel that makes "belonging to"; MW gives paura as a townsman, a citizen (MW).
