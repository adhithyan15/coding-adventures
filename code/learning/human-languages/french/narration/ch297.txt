French, chapter 297: Accident de la route.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le carambolage — the pile-up, the multiple crash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the ransom, then the French for to escape.

You'll want to know: le carambolage.
le carambolage — "the pile-up, the multiple crash".
Il y a eu un carambolage sur l'autoroute ce matin. There was a pile-up on the motorway this morning.
A billiards word first: caramboler is to hit two balls in one shot.

The word, taken apart.
carambolage is built on caramboler "to hit two balls at once", from carambole, the red ball in billiards, which was borrowed from Spanish carambola (EtymDB lists the noun without a parent).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le chauffard — the reckless driver, the road hog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to escape, then the French for the pile-up.

You'll want to know: le chauffard.
le chauffard — "the reckless driver, the road hog".
Un chauffard a grillé le feu rouge. A reckless driver went through the red light.
The suffix -ard adds a scornful sense. Griller un feu — to run a red light.

The word, taken apart.
chauffard is built on chauffeur "driver" with the scornful suffix -ard; chauffer "to heat" is inherited from Latin calefacere "to make warm" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'excès de vitesse — speeding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pile-up, then the French for the reckless driver.

You'll want to know: l'excès de vitesse.
l'excès de vitesse — "speeding".
Il a payé une amende pour excès de vitesse. He paid a fine for speeding.
Un excès de vitesse — a speeding offence.

The word, taken apart.
excès is a learned borrowing from Latin excessus "a going out, a going beyond", from excēdere (Lewis & Short); vitesse is built on vite "fast", whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le constat — the accident report (for insurance); a finding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reckless driver, then the French for speeding.

You'll want to know: le constat.
le constat — "the accident report (for insurance); a finding".
Les deux conducteurs ont rempli un constat. The two drivers filled in an accident report.
Le constat amiable — the joint report that drivers fill in together.

The word, taken apart.
constat was borrowed from Latin cōnstat "it is established, it is agreed" (EtymDB), from cōnstāre "to stand firm".

Lesson 5 of 5.
le dérapage — the skid; a slip-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for speeding, then the French for the accident report.

You'll want to know: le dérapage.
le dérapage — "the skid; a slip-up".
Sur la route mouillée, le camion a fait un dérapage. On the wet road, the lorry went into a skid.
Also in politics: un dérapage verbal — an unfortunate remark.

The word, taken apart.
dérapage is built on the verb meaning "to skid", which is usually traced to Provençal and to a Germanic root meaning "to snatch"; EtymDB lists no parent.
