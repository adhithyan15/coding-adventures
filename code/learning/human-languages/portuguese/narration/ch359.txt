Portuguese, chapter 359: No escritório.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o gerente — the manager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the strike, then the Portuguese for to lay claim to.

You'll want to know: o gerente.
o gerente — "the manager".
O gerente da loja resolveu o problema. The shop manager sorted out the problem.
Same form for a woman: a gerente.

The word, taken apart.
gerente is a learned borrowing from Latin gerēns (EtymDB), the present participle of gerere "to carry, to carry on, to manage" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: o departamento.
o departamento — "the department".
Ela trabalha no departamento de vendas. She works in the sales department.
O departamento de recursos humanos — the human resources department.

The word, taken apart.
departamento was borrowed from French département (EtymDB), built on Old French departir "to divide", which goes back to Latin partīre "to divide", on pars "part" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o colaborador — the staff member, the colleague; the collaborator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the department, then the Portuguese for the task.

You'll want to know: o colaborador.
o colaborador — "the staff member, the colleague; the collaborator".
A empresa tem cem colaboradores. The company has a hundred staff.
Companies in Portugal often call their staff colaboradores. Feminine a colaboradora.

The word, taken apart.
colaborador is built on colaborar "to work together", a learned borrowing from Late Latin collabōrāre, from con- and labōrāre "to work" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
delegar — to delegate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the task, then the Portuguese for the staff member.

You'll want to know: delegar.
delegar — "to delegate".
A chefe delegou o trabalho em dois colegas. The boss delegated the work to two colleagues.
Delegar em alguém — to delegate to someone.

The word, taken apart.
delegar is a learned borrowing from Latin dēlēgāre "to send, to assign, to delegate" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from dē and lēgāre "to send, to depute".
