# مسئول (mas'ul) — the person in charge

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-AZ-DAST-DADAN-01, FA-GRAMMAR-PROBLEM-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I missed the train", then "what should I do?". (*Qatâr râ az
dast dâdam*, *che kâr konam?*)

[PAUSE 1s] The person who can answer that question has a title of their own.

## You'll want to know: مسئول
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-C295-MASUL-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-BEBAKHSHID-01, FA-LEX-C70-TOWN70-02, FA-GRAMMAR-EZAFE-OWNER, FA-GRAMMAR-CHI-AST-FUSION] -->

**مسئول** — *mas'ul* — "the person in charge"; as a description, "responsible".

> **ببخشید، مسئول ایستگاه کجاست؟** — *bebakhshid, mas'ul-e istgâh kojâst?* —
> Excuse me, where is the person in charge of the station?

The ezafe hangs the place on it: *mas'ul-e istgâh*, *mas'ul-e hotel*. As a
description it follows its noun: **او مسئول است**, *u mas'ul ast*, "she is
responsible", "she's in charge".

Say it *mas-'ul*, with the catch after *mas*, the same catch as in *ta'rif*.
It is written with a letter of its own, and the next lesson puts it in your
hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C291-EYN-01] -->

*mas'ul* is borrowed from Arabic *masʾūl* (EtymDB 2.1). The Arabic word is
usually read as the passive participle of *saʾala*, "to ask", which EtymDB
lists on its own: *masʾūl* is "asked", and so the one who has to answer
when a thing goes wrong. EtymDB itself does not link the two, so that step is
given as the usual account.

The catch here is not **ع**. It is the other catch Arabic spells, the
glottal stop called *hamze*, and in Persian the two sound the same.
