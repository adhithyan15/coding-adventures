Latin, chapter 269: Gloomy, Drunk, Sober, Greedy, Thrifty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
maestus, maesta, maestum — gloomy, mournful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for ready, then the Latin for facing.

You'll want to know: maestus, maesta, maestum.
maestus, maesta, maestum — "gloomy, mournful".

The word, taken apart.
maestus belongs with maereō, "to grieve, to mourn" (Lewis & Short), whose origin is uncertain.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ēbrius, ēbria, ēbrium — drunk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for facing, then the Latin for gloomy.

You'll want to know: ēbrius, ēbria, ēbrium.
ēbrius, ēbria, ēbrium — "drunk".

The word, taken apart.
ēbrius may be inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root h1egwh-, "to drink" (EtymDB 2.1); Lewis & Short still called it doubtful. Its opposite is sōbrius, "sober".

Lesson 3 of 5.
sōbrius, sōbria, sōbrium — sober.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for gloomy, then the Latin for drunk.

You'll want to know: sōbrius, sōbria, sōbrium.
sōbrius, sōbria, sōbrium — "sober".

The word, taken apart.
sōbrius is usually explained as sē-, "without", with ēbrius, "drunk" (EtymDB 2.1): not drunk. English sober comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
avārus is built on aveō, "to long for, to crave" (Lewis & Short): craving. English avarice comes from its family.
