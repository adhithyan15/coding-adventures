# بلیت رفت و برگشت (bilit-e raft-o bargasht) — a return ticket

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a ticket", "a train" and "to come back". (*Bilit*, *qatâr*,
*bargashtan*.)

[PAUSE 1s] You found the station. At the window, one phrase does nearly all
the work.

## You'll want to know: بلیت رفت و برگشت
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-RAFT-O-BARGASHT-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-MIKHAHAM-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-EZAFE-OWNER, FA-LEX-C65-TIME65-04] -->

**بلیت رفت و برگشت** — *bilit-e raft-o bargasht* — "a return ticket".

> **یک بلیت رفت و برگشت به تهران میخواهم.** — *yek bilit-e raft-o bargasht be
> Tehrân mikhâham* — I'd like a return ticket to Tehran.

*Mikhâham* here is a request, "I'd like", and it is how people ask at the
window. Add the day after the place: *barâ-ye fardâ*, "for tomorrow".

If you are not coming back by train, the ticket is for the going alone:
*bilit-e raft*. The clerk may check with *faqat raft?*, "one way only?".

Say *raft-o bargasht* as one run. The *va*, "and", shrinks to *-o* and leans
on the word before it, as *-e* does.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-RAFTAN, FA-GRAMMAR-SIMPLE-PAST-01] -->

*bilit* is the French loan from chapter one hundred and thirteen, *billet*
(EtymDB). The other two words are verbs you already own, with their endings
taken off. *Raft* is the past stem of *raftan*: "going". *Bargasht* is the past
stem of *bargashtan*: "coming back", from the prefix *bar-*, which here gives the sense "back", and *gashtan*, "to
turn". Persian makes nouns this way: a past stem, standing alone, names the
action.
