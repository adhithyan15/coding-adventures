# आवाम् (āvām) — us two, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03, SA-GRAMMAR-NUMERAL-DUAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **माम्**, “me”. Then recall the Sanskrit number reserved for
exactly two: the dual. Now make the object be the speaker and one other person.

## You'll want to know: आवाम्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C261-AVAM-01]; assesses=[] -->

**आवाम्** (*āvām*) means “us two” as the object.

> **सः आवाम् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ āvām paśyati.*
>
> **He sees us two.**

Whitney §491 lists **आवाम्** as the independent dual form used for both
subject and object. Here its position in the known frame makes the object job
clear. Hold the first vowel long: *ā-vām*.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **आवाम्** comes from the first-person pronoun paradigm;
it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit Grammar*
§491 records it as the independent dual used for both subject and object. This
lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
