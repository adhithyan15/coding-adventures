German, chapter 473: Kichern und grinsen.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
kichern — to giggle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for homesickness, then the German for to miss.

You'll want to know: kichern.
kichern — "to giggle".
Die Mädchen kicherten in der letzten Reihe. The girls were giggling in the back row.
A softer, higher laugh than lachen.

The word, taken apart.
kichern is usually explained as an imitative word, copying the sound of a high, held-back laugh; EtymDB lists it without a source, so its origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
grinsen — to grin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to miss, then the German for to giggle.

You'll want to know: grinsen.
grinsen — "to grin".
Er grinste, als er seine gute Note sah. He grinned when he saw his good mark.
Often a little cheeky: frech grinsen.

The word, taken apart.
grinsen is usually traced to Middle High German grinsen, beside grīnen "to bare the teeth"; its older origin is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 4 of 6.
albern — silly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to grin, then the German for to smile quietly.

You'll want to know: albern.
albern — "silly".
Die Kinder waren müde und deshalb albern. The children were tired and therefore silly.
In speech also herumalbern — to fool around.

The word, taken apart.
albern is inherited from Middle High German alwære and Old High German alawāri "friendly, good-natured" (EtymDB); the meaning sank from kind to simple to silly.
