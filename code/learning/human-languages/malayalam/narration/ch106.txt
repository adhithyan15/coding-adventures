Malayalam, chapter 106: Before and After.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
മുൻപ് (munpŭ) — before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say at two o'clock. Then say the word you have for then — the one that puts one turn after another.

You'll want to know.
മുൻപ് (munpŭ) — before.
It goes after. English puts before in front of what it is before; Malayalam puts it behind:
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ് — raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ — "before two o'clock."
Read the two phrases right to left and they come out as English: before — two o'clock. Keep രണ്ട് മണി together as one piece.

Why it's said this way.
Look at what it is standing on. മണിക്ക് — the hour with the dative on it, exactly as you built it. മുൻപ് (munpŭ) does not attach to the word and does not change it. It is a separate word that leans on an ending you already make.
The word itself is built on mun, the old Dravidian word for the front. What is in front of you in space became what is in front of you in time.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 2 of 4.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — after.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say before two o'clock. Then say the word for the meal.

You'll want to know.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — after.
It stands where മുൻപ് (munpŭ) stands, on the same kind of ending:
ഊണിന് ശേഷം — ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ — "after the meal."

Grammar Lens: the same case, wearing its other shape.
ഊണിന് is the dative again — and it is the other of the two shapes you were given. You were told the choice is made by the sound the word finishes on, and here is the part of that you can use: a word ending in a consonant takes -ിന്. ഊണ് ends in ണ്, so -ിന് it is. The hour ended in a vowel and took -ക്ക്.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ്, raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ, before two o'clock.
ഊണിന് ശേഷം, ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ, after the meal.
Same case, two shapes, and both of these words lean on it. You are not learning a new piece of machinery — you are hanging two more words on a piece you have had since your first case ending.

Why it's said this way.
ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) is Sanskrit, and its plain meaning is the remainder — what is left over. After the meal is, in the word's own terms, what is left of the day once the meal is done.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
After the meal? (ഊണിന് ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ).) Which two shapes of the dative did this chapter use? (-ക്ക് on the hour, -ിന് on the meal.) What does ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) plainly mean? (The remainder — what is left over.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, മുൻപ് (munpŭ), ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) — ordering a run, and ordering against a point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say at first. Say then. You have had both for a long while.

You'll want to know.
You now have four words about order, and they split into two pairs that do different jobs — though not the job you might expect.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ആദ്യം, പിന്നെ, ādyaṁ, pinne, order a run of things.
മുൻപ്, ശേഷം, munpŭ, śēṣaṁ, order one thing against a named point.
Both pairs order real events. You were shown ആദ്യം ഊണ്, പിന്നെ ചായ — lunch first, then tea — and that is the world being ordered, not a story about it.
What the first pair cannot do is name what it is measuring against. It lays things out in a run: this, then that. മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) do something the other pair has no way to do — they take a named time or event and place something relative to it.

What you've built.
Until this chapter you could put events in a run and not anchor them. First lunch, then tea says which came first and says nothing about when either happened. Before two o'clock and after the meal fix an event against a point you have named.
That is the whole of what the two new words add — and neither of them needed a new piece of grammar to do it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which two lay a run out in order? (ആദ്യം and പിന്നെ.) Which two measure against a named point? (മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ).) In this chapter, what did that named thing wear? (The dative.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
(before, after) (munpuṁ śēṣavuṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say before and after.

Grammar Lens: both of them lean, and neither of them attaches.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ്, raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ, before two o'clock.
ഊണിന് ശേഷം, ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ, after the meal.
Neither word joined onto the word in front of it. They stay separate words — but they are not passengers: it is ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) that puts ഊണ് into the dative in the first place. The hour was already going to wear that ending; the meal wears it because the postposition asks for it.
Both stand behind what they are about, which is the opposite of where English puts them.

What you've built.
The ending underneath both of them is your first case ending, and it has been busy. It was glossed to and for. It carries the person who knows, wants or has something — എനിക്ക്. You put it on an hour to mean at. And now these two postpositions lean on it as well. No new machinery in this chapter at all.
And you can now order in two ways: ആദ്യം and പിന്നെ lay a run out, while മുൻപ് (munpŭ) and ശേഷം (śēṣaṁ) measure against a point you name.
