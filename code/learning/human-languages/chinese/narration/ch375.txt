Lesson 1 of 6.
qióng — poor (having little money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an ethnic group, then the Mandarin for to develop and grow.

You'll want to know: qióng.
qióng — "poor (having little money)".
yǐqián zhèr de rén dōu hěn qióng — People here used to be poor.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qióng is inherited from Middle Chinese gjuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to come to the very end'. How running out came to mean being poor is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
社会 (shèhuì) — society.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to develop and grow, then the Mandarin for poor (having little money).

You'll want to know: 社会.
社会 — shèhuì — "society".
wǒmen de shèhuì xūyào rèxīn de rén — Our society needs warm-hearted people.
The new character, in pieces: 社 (shè) — seven strokes in two pieces: on the left the sign piece, four strokes; on the right a short bar, a vertical and a long bar, the earth piece at the foot of 在. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
社会 (shèhuì) is built from 社 (shè) 'the altar of the soil' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyaeX and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the first with the god of grain and glosses the second 'to come together'. People meeting at the village altar is usually given as the start; the sense 'society' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

Script — the sign piece, then the earth piece.
社 (shè).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the sign piece, four strokes. A short dot at the top. A bar that runs right, turns and falls to the lower left, all one stroke. A vertical down from under it. Then a short dot to the right of the vertical.
The right piece, the earth piece, three strokes, as at the foot of 在: a short bar, a vertical down through it, then a long bar across the foot.
4 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 社 (shè), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 社 (shè)? (Two.) How many strokes in the left piece? (Four.) Where have you made the right piece before? (At the foot of 在.)

Lesson 4 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: juān.
juān — "to donate".
tā měi nián dōu juān yìxiē qián — He gives some money away every year.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
juān is inherited from Middle Chinese ywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to throw away'. Giving up money for others is the later sense, and it is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 6.
zhìyuànzhě — volunteer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for society, then the Mandarin for to donate (money).

You'll want to know: zhìyuànzhě.
zhìyuànzhě — "volunteer".
wǒ xiǎng dāng zhìyuànzhě yīnwèi wǒ xiǎng bāng biérén — I want to be a volunteer because I want to help other people.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhìyuànzhě is built from zhì 'what the heart longs for', yuàn 'to wish' and zhě 'one who', all inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyiH, ngjwonH and tsyaeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the last as a grammar word. A volunteer is one who serves of their own will.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.
