## You'll want to know: 愿意
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C535-WILLING535-01]; assesses=[] -->

**愿意** — *yuànyì* — "to be willing (to)".

> **要是你愿意我们明天就去** *yàoshi nǐ yuànyì wǒmen míngtiān jiù qù* — If you're willing, we'll go tomorrow.

The new character, in pieces: 愿 — fourteen strokes in four pieces: a bar and a long stroke falling to the left, like a cliff; under it 白, five strokes, and 小, three strokes; and 心 at the foot, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**愿意** (*yuànyì*) is built from 愿 and 意 'what one means', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ngjwonH* and *'iH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to desire' and 'to think of'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for saying what would happen.
