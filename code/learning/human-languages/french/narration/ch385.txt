French, chapter 385: La paperasse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la bureaucratie — the bureaucracy, the red tape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to renew, then the French for the renewal.

You'll want to know: la bureaucratie.
la bureaucratie — "the bureaucracy, the red tape".
Il y a trop de bureaucratie dans ce pays. There is too much red tape in this country.
The t is said like s: bureaucrassie.

The word, taken apart.
bureaucratie is a French formation from bureau "office" and -cratie "rule" (EtymDB), from Greek krátos "power": rule by offices.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la démarche — the step (to get something done); the gait.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: la démarche.
la démarche — "the step (to get something done); the gait".
J'ai fait toutes les démarches pour obtenir mon visa. I took all the steps to get my visa.
Faire des démarches — to take the necessary steps. Also the way someone walks.

The word, taken apart.
démarche is formed on an old verb démarcher (EtymDB), built from dé- and marcher "to walk"; marcher is usually traced to a Frankish word for treading.

You'll want to know: la procédure.
la procédure — "the procedure".
La procédure est longue, mais simple. The procedure is long, but simple.
In law, a court case: une procédure judiciaire.

The word, taken apart.
procédure is built on procéder "to proceed", from Latin prōcēdere "to go forward" (Lewis & Short), built on cēdere "to go".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le certificat — the certificate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the step, then the French for the procedure.

You'll want to know: le certificat.
le certificat — "the certificate".
Le médecin m'a donné un certificat pour l'école. The doctor gave me a certificate for school.
Un certificat médical — a doctor's note.

The word, taken apart.
certificat comes from Medieval Latin certificātum (EtymDB), built on Latin certus "sure" and facere "to make" (Lewis & Short): a paper that makes a fact sure.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'attestation — the official statement, the attestation.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: l'attestation.
l'attestation — "the official statement, the attestation".
La banque demande une attestation de domicile. The bank is asking for proof of address.
A signed paper that confirms a fact. Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
attestation is a learned borrowing from Late Latin attestātiō (EtymDB), from attestārī "to bear witness to" (Lewis & Short), built on testis "witness".
