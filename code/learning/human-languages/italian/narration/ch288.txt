Italian, chapter 288: La notizia si diffonde.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'annuncio — the announcement; the advert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the TV channel, then the Italian for the broadcaster.

You'll want to know: l'annuncio.
l'annuncio — "the announcement; the advert".
L'annuncio è arrivato alle otto di sera. The announcement came at eight in the evening.
In a paper, gli annunci are the small ads.

The word, taken apart.
annuncio is built on annunciare "to announce", from Latin annūntiāre, built on nūntius "messenger" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
la statistica — the statistic; statistics.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the broadcaster, then the Italian for the announcement.

You'll want to know: la statistica.
la statistica — "the statistic; statistics".
Secondo le statistiche, il turismo è cresciuto. According to the statistics, tourism has grown.
Secondo le statistiche — according to the figures.

The word, taken apart.
statistica is usually traced to Modern Latin statisticus "of the state", built on Latin status "condition, state" (Lewis & Short): first, figures about a state.

rivelare — "to reveal".
Il giornale ha rivelato il nome del vincitore. The paper revealed the winner's name.
Rivelarsi — to turn out: si è rivelato un errore.

The word, taken apart.
rivelare is a learned borrowing from Latin revēlāre "to unveil" (Lewis & Short), built on vēlum "veil".

Lesson 4 of 6.
diffondere — to spread (news).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the statistic, then the Italian for to reveal.

You'll want to know: diffondere.
diffondere — "to spread (news)".
La notizia si è diffusa in pochi minuti. The news spread in a few minutes.
Past participle diffuso: una notizia molto diffusa.

The word, taken apart.
diffondere is a learned borrowing from Latin diffundere "to pour out, to spread" (Lewis & Short), from dis- and fundere "to pour".

Lesson 5 of 6.
approfondire — to go into in depth, to look more deeply into.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to reveal, then the Italian for to spread.

You'll want to know: approfondire.
approfondire — "to go into in depth, to look more deeply into".
Domani approfondiamo questo argomento. Tomorrow we will go into this topic in depth.
Built on profondo, ‘deep’: to make deeper.

The word, taken apart.
approfondire is built on profondo "deep", from Latin profundus (Lewis & Short), with ad- and the verb ending -ire.
