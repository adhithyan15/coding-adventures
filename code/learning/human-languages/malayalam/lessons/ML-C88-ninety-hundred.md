# തൊണ്ണൂറ് നൂറ് (toṇṇūṟŭ, nūṟŭ) — ninety, and a hundred

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C88-TENS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the tens from the lesson before, and say the piece they all
share.

## You'll want to know: നൂറ്, and then തൊണ്ണൂറ്
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C88-HUNDRED-01, ML-CONCEPT-C88-NINETY-01]; assesses=[] -->

**നൂറ്** — *nūṟŭ* — a hundred. A single word, owing nothing to *ten*.

Now the one that breaks the run:

**തൊണ്ണൂറ്** — *toṇṇūṟŭ* — ninety.

**There is no -പത്- in it.** Every other ten was *digit plus ten*, and this one
is not built that way at all.

Look at how it ends. **The word for a hundred, a line above, is sitting inside
it** — *toṇ-* **ṇūṟŭ**. Ninety is named from **above**, as a hundred short of
something, where the other tens are named from below as so many tens.

| | built from |
|---|---|
| *eṇpathŭ* 80 | eight **tens** |
| **തൊണ്ണൂറ്** 90 | a **hundred**, reduced |
| **നൂറ്** 100 | itself |

That is why the lesson gives you a hundred **first**. Learn *toṇṇūṟŭ* after
*nūṟŭ* and it is one irregular word with a visible reason; learn it before, and
it is noise.

**A run of regular forms tends to hold one member built on a different idea**,
and finding out which idea usually costs less than memorising the exception.
