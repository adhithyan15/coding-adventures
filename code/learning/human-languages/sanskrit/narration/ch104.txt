Lesson 1 of 5.
शाटिका (śāṭikā) — a sari.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a bangle, then the Sanskrit for a shawl.

You'll want to know: शाटिका.
शाटिका — śāṭikā — "a sari".

The word, taken apart.
śāṭikā is śāṭī "a strip of cloth, a garment" (MW) with the suffix -kā; MW takes śāṭa from a root √śaṭ with a query, so the deeper source is uncertain. śāṭī wore down into Hindi sāṛī (CDIAL 12381), the source of English sari (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
पादत्राणम् (pādatrāṇam) — a shoe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a shawl, then the Sanskrit for a sari.

You'll want to know: पादत्राणम्.
पादत्राणम् — pādatrāṇam — "a shoe".

The word, taken apart.
pādatrāṇa is built from pāda "foot" and trāṇa "protection" (MW): "foot-guard". The pair is old, already in Suśruta (CDIAL 8060), and pāda is the inherited word for "foot" that MW sets beside Latin pes and English foot.

Lesson 3 of 5.
उष्णीषम् (uṣṇīṣam) — a turban.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a sari, then the Sanskrit for a shoe.

You'll want to know: उष्णीषम्.
उष्णीषम् — uṣṇīṣam — "a turban".

The word, taken apart.
uṣṇīṣa "anything wound round the head, a turban" is old, already in the Atharvaveda. The grammarians quoted by MW read it as uṣṇa "heat" and īṣ "ward off", "what keeps off the heat", but that is a learned guess, and its origin is uncertain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कञ्चुकः (kañcukaḥ) — a shirt, a blouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a shoe, then the Sanskrit for a turban.

You'll want to know: कञ्चुकः.
कञ्चुकः — kañcukaḥ — "a shirt, a blouse".

The word, taken apart.
kañcuka "a close-fitting jacket, a bodice; a snake's slough" is possibly borrowed from Munda: CDIAL 2626 gives that view after Kuiper and EWA, while MW suggests a root √kañc with a query. The origin is uncertain; Pali kept it as kañcuka (CDIAL 2626).

Lesson 5 of 5.
दूरभाषः (dūrabhāṣaḥ) — a telephone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a turban, then the Sanskrit for a shirt.

You'll want to know: दूरभाषः.
दूरभाषः — dūrabhāṣaḥ — "a telephone".

The word, taken apart.
dūrabhāṣa is built from dūra "far" and bhāṣa "speech", from the root √bhāṣ "speak" (MW): "far-speech". It is a modern coinage, made the way English telephone was made from Greek tele "far" + phōnē "voice".
