# (reading) — six old words, with the training wheels gone

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-C01-SALAM-01, AR-CONCEPT-SHUKRAN-21] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have heard, said and written every word below. Nothing here is
new.

[PAUSE 2s] What is new is the empty space beneath each word. There is no
romanized line to catch you. Let the Arabic carry its own sound.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C33-SHAY-01, AR-CONCEPT-C05-NAAM-01, AR-CONCEPT-C05-LA-01] -->

> سلام
> شكرًا
> شاي
> نعم
> لا
> حسنًا

[PAUSE 4s] Read down once, right to left, without translating.

[PAUSE 3s] Read again. The first and third words stretch an **ا** into a long
sound, while **شكرًا** and **حسنًا** share the little **ـًا** ending.

## The script, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-FUNC-C38-HASANAN-01, AR-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Arabic letters change coat when they join, but they do not change
identity. The **ح** in **حسنًا** is the same hook body you learned in **خير**;
the word only asks you to recognize it at speed.

[PAUSE 2s] And **لا** is still the lām-alif pair from chapter five. Reading is
not a new script lesson. It is trusting the shapes you already paid for.
