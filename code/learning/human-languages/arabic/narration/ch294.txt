Arabic, chapter 294: Suddenly, it happened.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
فجأة (fajʾatan) — suddenly, all of a sudden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to announce, then the Arabic for to declare.

You'll want to know: فجأة.
فجأة — fajʾatan — "suddenly, all of a sudden".
انقطعت الكهرباء فجأة. inqaṭaʿat al-kahrabāʾ fajʾatan. Suddenly the electricity went off.
An adverb with -an, like غالبا. It often opens a sentence: فجأة (fajʾatan) سمعنا صوتا.

The word, taken apart.
فجأة (fajʾatan) is built on the native Arabic root f-j-ʾ (Lane), "to come upon someone unawares". The noun fajʾa, "a sudden coming", takes the adverb ending -an.

Lesson 2 of 5.
وقع (waqaʿa) — to take place; to fall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to declare, then the Arabic for suddenly.

You'll want to know: وقع.
وقع — waqaʿa — "to take place; to fall".
وقع الحادث قرب المدرسة. waqaʿa al-ḥādith qurba al-madrasa. The accident happened near the school.
Present: يقع, yaqaʿ.
Of events: وقع (waqaʿa) حادث, an accident happened. Of places, يقع means it lies or is located.

The word, taken apart.
وقع (waqaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root w-q-ʿ (Lane), "to fall, to drop down". A thing that falls upon people is a thing that happens, and wāqiʿ is "what has fallen out": reality.

Lesson 3 of 5.
انفجر (infajara) — to explode, to burst.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for suddenly, then the Arabic for to take place.

You'll want to know: انفجر.
انفجر — infajara — "to explode, to burst".
انفجر البالون فوق رأس الطفل. infajara al-bālūn fawqa raʾs aṭ-ṭifl. The balloon burst above the child’s head.
Present: ينفجر, yanfajir.

The word, taken apart.
انفجر (infajara) is built on the native Arabic root f-j-r (Lane), "to cleave, to make water gush out", the root of fajr, "dawn". It is Form VII, "to burst open".

Lesson 4 of 5.
في البداية (fī al-bidāya) — at first, in the beginning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to take place, then the Arabic for to explode.

You'll want to know: في البداية.
في البداية — fī al-bidāya — "at first, in the beginning".
في البداية كان الطريق سهلا. fī al-bidāya kāna aṭ-ṭarīq sahlan. At first the road was easy.
It opens a story; ثم and بعد ذلك carry it on.

The word, taken apart.
في البداية (fī al-bidāya) is the preposition fī, "in", with bidāya, "a beginning", which is built on the native Arabic root b-d-ʾ, "to begin" (Lane), the root of badaʾa.

Lesson 5 of 5.
في تلك اللحظة (fī tilka al-laḥẓa) — at that moment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to explode, then the Arabic for at first.

You'll want to know: في تلك اللحظة.
في تلك اللحظة — fī tilka al-laḥẓa — "at that moment".
في تلك اللحظة دخل الأستاذ الصف. fī tilka al-laḥẓa dakhala al-ustādh aṣ-ṣaff. At that moment the teacher came into the classroom.
A story moves on with it: something happens, and in that moment something else begins.

The word, taken apart.
في تلك اللحظة (fī tilka al-laḥẓa) is "in that moment": tilka is "that" for a feminine noun, and laḥẓa is built on the native Arabic root l-ḥ-ẓ, "to glance" (Lane). A moment is as long as a glance.
