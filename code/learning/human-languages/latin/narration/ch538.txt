Latin, chapter 538: Free time.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ōtiōsus, ōtiōsa, ōtiōsum — at leisure, idle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for faithless, then the Latin for to abandon.

You'll want to know: ōtiōsus, ōtiōsa, ōtiōsum.
ōtiōsus, ōtiōsa, ōtiōsum — "at leisure, idle".
Diē fēstō cīvēs ōtiōsī in forō ambulant. On the holiday the citizens stroll at leisure in the forum.
From ōtium: free of business, with no blame meant. English otiose.

The word, taken apart.
ōtiōsus is built on ōtium "leisure" with -ōsus (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English otiose comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
vacō, vacāre — to be free, have time (for).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to abandon, then the Latin for at leisure.

You'll want to know: vacō, vacāre.
vacō, vacāre — "to be free, have time (for)".
Hodiē vacō; tēcum ambulāre possum. Today I am free; I can go for a walk with you.
vacō, vacāre, vacāvī, vacātum. To be empty, then to be free for something: vacāre litterīs — to have time for literature. English vacant, vacation.

The word, taken apart.
vacō is inherited, through Proto-Italic, from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "empty" (EtymDB). English vacant and vacation come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.

fēriātus, fēriāta, fēriātum — "on holiday, keeping holiday".
Puerī fēriātī tōtum diem lūdunt. The boys on holiday play all day.
The participle of fērior — to keep holiday, from fēriae, already known.

The word, taken apart.
fēriātus is the participle of fērior "to keep holiday", built on fēriae "holidays" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
relaxō, relaxāre — to relax, ease; to loosen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be free, then the Latin for on holiday.

You'll want to know: relaxō, relaxāre.
relaxō, relaxāre — "to relax, ease; to loosen".
Post labōrem animum mūsicā relaxō. After work I relax my mind with music.
relaxō, relaxāre, relaxāvī, relaxātum: re- + laxō 'to loosen'. English relax.

The word, taken apart.
relaxō is built on re- and laxō "to loosen", from laxus "slack" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English relax and release come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
remissiō, remissiōnis — relaxation; a letting go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for on holiday, then the Latin for to relax.

You'll want to know: remissiō, remissiōnis.
remissiō, remissiōnis — "relaxation; a letting go".
Post studia remissiō animī necessāria est. After study, relaxation of the mind is necessary.
Feminine, third declension, from remittō — to send back, slacken. English remission.

The word, taken apart.
remissiō is the action noun of remittō "to send back, slacken", built on re- and mittō (Lewis & Short). English remission comes from it.
