## You'll want to know: முதல்
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C82-ORDINAL-01]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C62-JOIN-05] -->

**முதல்** (*mutal*) — "**first**".

You already know this word. Look at **முதலில்** (*mutalil*), "first of all",
which this book already taught as a way of putting one thing before another:

> **முதல்** + **இல்** → **முதலில்**

**இல்** is the ending that means "in" or "at". Take it off and what is left is
**முதல்**, first — and it was standing there in your mouth the whole time,
carrying the meaning, without ever being named.

**முதல் நாள்** (*mutal nāḷ*), the first day. **முதல் பாதை** (*mutal pātai*), the
first road.

## Across the family: the word four languages built on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-ETYMON-C82-ORDINAL-02]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C82-ORDINAL-01] -->

**முதல்** is one of the oldest words in this family, and its sisters did four
different things with it:

| language | what it did with *mutal* | its word for "first" |
|---|---|---|
| Tamil | kept the meaning outright | **முதல்** *mutal* |
| Kannada | built an ordinal on it | **ಮೊದಲನೆಯ** *modalaneya* |
| Telugu | built an ordinal on it | **మొదటి** *modaṭi* |
| Malayalam | kept the word, changed the sense to "from" | uses **ഒന്നാം** *onnāṁ* instead |

None of the four builds "first" on its own word for **one**, and three of them
build it on this word. Tamil is where it still simply *means* first.
