German, chapter 339: Etwas hervorheben.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Äußerung — the remark, the statement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for obviously, then the German for to suspect.

You'll want to know: die Äußerung.
die Äußerung — "the remark, the statement".
Seine Äußerung über die Kollegen war nicht nett. His remark about his colleagues was not nice.
Plural: die Äußerungen. The noun of äußern — to express.

The word, taken apart.
Äußerung is the noun of äußern "to express", built on außer, from Middle High German ūzer and Old High German ūzar "out of" (EtymDB): you put a thought outside.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hinweisen — to indicate, to call attention to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to suspect, then the German for the remark.

You'll want to know: hinweisen.
hinweisen — "to indicate, to call attention to".
Der Arzt hat darauf hingewiesen, dass ich mehr Wasser trinken soll. The doctor noted that I should drink more water.
Separable and strong: er weist darauf hin, er hat darauf hingewiesen. Auf etwas hinweisen — to call attention to something.

The word, taken apart.
hinweisen is built from hin "towards there" and weisen "to show, to direct" (EtymDB): you show someone where to look.

Lesson 4 of 5.
hervorheben — to bring out, to single out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to indicate, then the German for the hint.

You'll want to know: hervorheben.
hervorheben — "to bring out, to single out".
Im Bericht hebt die Lehrerin hervor, dass meine Tochter sehr fleißig ist. In the report the teacher singles out how hard-working my daughter is.
Separable and strong: sie hebt hervor, sie hat hervorgehoben. Literally to lift something out to the front.

The word, taken apart.
hervorheben is built from hervor "forth, out" and heben "to lift", inherited from Old High German heffen (EtymDB): you lift a point out so that everyone notices it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
darlegen — to set out, to explain clearly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the hint, then the German for to bring out.

You'll want to know: darlegen.
darlegen — "to set out, to explain clearly".
Sie legt ihre Gründe ruhig und klar dar. She sets out her reasons calmly and clearly.
Separable: sie legt dar, sie hat dargelegt. Formal, often in writing.

The word, taken apart.
darlegen is built from dar "there, forth" and legen "to lay", inherited from Old High German leggen and Proto-Germanic lagjaną (EtymDB): you lay your reasons out in front of people.
