Lesson 1 of 5.
おもちゃ (omocha) — a toy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a school outing, then the Japanese for a pleasure trip.

You'll want to know: おもちゃ.
おもちゃ — omocha — "a toy".
こどもは おもちゃで あそびます。 — kodomo wa omocha de asobimasu — "Children play with toys."

The word, taken apart.
おもちゃ (omocha) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and usually written in kana; its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
なわとび (nawatobi) — skipping rope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a pleasure trip, then the Japanese for a toy.

You'll want to know: なわとび.
なわとび — nawatobi — "skipping rope".
こうえんで なわとびを しました。 — kōen de nawatobi o shimashita — "I skipped rope in the park."

The word, taken apart.
なわとび (nawatobi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from nawa, "rope" and tobi, "jumping" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
すべりだい (suberidai) — a slide (in a playground).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a running race (between children), then the Japanese for a riddle.

You'll want to know: すべりだい.
すべりだい — suberidai — "a slide (in a playground)".
こうえんの すべりだいは たかいです。 — kōen no suberidai wa takai desu — "The slide in the park is high."

The word, taken apart.
すべりだい (suberidai) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: native suberi, "sliding", from suberu, "to slide", and Sino-Japanese dai, "a stand", borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
