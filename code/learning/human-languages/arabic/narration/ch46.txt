Arabic, chapter 46: Letters From Your First Greetings.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ش (shīn) — the letter that opens شكرا.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ش and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ع and ه once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say شكرا (shukran), "thank you". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ش.
ش — shīn, the sound sh.
It opens شكرا (shukran), "thank you", one of the first words in this book. You already write س (sīn): three small teeth and a bowl. ش (shīn) is the same body with three dots above. One skeleton, and the dots decide the sound.

Writing: ش.
1. start here: shape the three close teeth from right to left.
2. flow directly into the final bowl without lifting.
3. lift, then place the lower-left dot.
4. lift again, then place the lower-right dot.
5. lift a third time, then place the centered upper dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: س ش (shīn) ص (ṣād) ض, independent Shiin at 00:00.7–00:03.0 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).

Lesson 2 of 3.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ه and ش (shīn) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say صباح الخير (ṣabāḥ al-khayr), "good morning". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ص.
ص — ṣād, a heavy s.
It opens صباح الخير (ṣabāḥ al-khayr), "good morning". Say ṣabāḥ and feel the tongue press low and the back of the mouth widen: that heaviness is what the dot under the romanization (ṣ) marks. The letter is a closed oval with a bowl trailing after it.

Writing: ص.
1. start here: close the oval clockwise from its lower-left junction.
2. turn left and rise into the short shoulder without lifting.
3. lift, restart at the baseline junction, and sweep through the trailing bowl — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: س ش (shīn) ص (ṣād) ض, independent Saad at 00:01.1–00:03.3 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).

Lesson 3 of 3.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: د and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ش (shīn) and ص (ṣād) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say الحمد لله (al-ḥamdu lillāh), "praise be to God". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: د.
د — dāl, the sound d.
It closes الحمد in الحمد لله (al-ḥamdu lillāh), the everyday answer to "how are you?". د (dāl) is a non-connector, like ا and ر: it joins to the letter before it but never to the one after, so a small gap always follows it.

Writing: د.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend diagonally down and right through the curved shoulder.
2. turn left along the baseline without lifting — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: د (dāl) ذ ر, independent Daal at 00:07.0–00:07.6 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023).
