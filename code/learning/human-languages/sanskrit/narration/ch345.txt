Sanskrit, chapter 345: Evidence.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रत्ययः (pratyayaḥ) — conviction; firm belief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for consents, then the Sanskrit for is it not so.

You'll want to know: प्रत्ययः.
प्रत्ययः — pratyayaḥ — "conviction; firm belief".
मम प्रत्ययः अस्ति यत् सः सत्यं वदति। mama pratyayaḥ asti yat saḥ satyaṃ vadati. I am convinced that he is telling the truth.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रत्ययः (pratyayaḥ) is built on prati, "towards", and the root √i, "to go"; MW gives praty-aya "belief, firm conviction, trust" and "proof, ascertainment" (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
तथ्यम् (tathyam) — a fact; the truth of a matter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for conviction, then the Sanskrit for testimony.

You'll want to know: तथ्यम्.
तथ्यम् — tathyam — "a fact; the truth of a matter".
एतत् तथ्यम् अस्ति, न मिथ्या। etat tathyam asti, na mithyā. This is a fact, not a falsehood.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
तथ्यम् (tathyam) MW gives tathya "being really so, true" and the neuter "truth"; "a fact" is the sense modern Sanskrit gives it, and MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
साधयति (sādhayati) — proves; brings about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for testimony, then the Sanskrit for a fact.

You'll want to know: साधयति.
साधयति — sādhayati — "proves; brings about".
अरुणः प्रमाणेन मतं साधयति। aruṇaḥ pramāṇena mataṃ sādhayati. Arun proves his view with evidence.
Past (he …): सः तत् साधितवान्, saḥ tat sādhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
साधयति (sādhayati) comes from the root √sidh, "to succeed", in its causative form; MW gives the root the sense "to be proved or established" and sādhita "brought about, accomplished" (MW).
