Sanskrit, chapter 42: Five More Replies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कदाचित् (kadācit) — sometimes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did chatram mean?

You'll want to know: कदाचित्.
कदाचित् (kadācit) — "sometimes".
कदाचित् (kadācit) answers "when?" with "now and then".
Both pieces are yours already: कदा, the when you ask questions with, and a small cit fastened on the end. कदाचित् (kadācit) is when gone vague — at some time or other, and no promise which.
It is also the polite way to leave a door open: not yes, not no, perhaps sometimes.

What you've built.
A reply that answers when without saying when.

Lesson 2 of 5.
सहसा (sahasā) — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did kadācit mean?

You'll want to know: सहसा.
सहसा (sahasā) — "suddenly".
सहसा (sahasā) is "all at once, without warning".
Underneath it is sahas, "force, a sudden strength", put into the shape that means by means of: by force, and so at a rush. कृपया, the please you already say, is made the very same way out of kṛpā.
It carries a shade of judgement with it — a thing done सहसा (sahasā) is a thing done without thinking first.

What you've built.
One thing happening at a rush, and a mild opinion about it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रायः (prāyaḥ) — mostly, for the most part.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did sahasā mean?

You'll want to know: प्रायः.
प्रायः (prāyaḥ) — "mostly, for the most part".
प्रायः (prāyaḥ) is "for the most part, in the main, usually".
Say it when something holds most of the time and you would rather not promise more than that. It is pra-, "forward", with the going-piece you met in विहगः: what runs out in front, what happens in the main.
Beside सर्वम्, which claims everything, प्रायः (prāyaḥ) claims almost everything and keeps its hedge.

What you've built.
A reply that is honest about not being certain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
किञ्चित् (kiñcit) — a little, somewhat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did prāyaḥ mean?

You'll want to know: किञ्चित्.
किञ्चित् (kiñcit) — "a little, somewhat".
किञ्चित् (kiñcit) is "a bit, somewhat, a small amount".
It is किम्, the what you have been asking with since your first questions, wearing the same little cit that softened कदाचित् (kadācit). What gone vague is something — and something said of a quantity is a little.
अल्पम् describes a small thing; किञ्चित् (kiñcit) measures a small amount of it.

What you've built.
A small helping of an answer.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बहु (bahu) — much, many.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the last word of the chapter: what did prāyaḥ mean, and what did kiñcit mean?

You'll want to know: बहु.
बहु (bahu) — "much, many".
बहु (bahu) is "much" of one thing and "many" of several — the far end of the answer किञ्चित् (kiñcit) starts.
Greek pakhus, "thick, stout", is its cousin, and thickness is the sense underneath: plenty as bulk rather than as a count.
Hindi bahut, "very, a lot", is बहु with an ending on it, and it is one of the commonest words anyone says today.

What you've built.
Five replies about how much and how often: sometimes, suddenly, mostly, a little, much.
