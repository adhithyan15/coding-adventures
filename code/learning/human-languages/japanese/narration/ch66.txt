Lesson 1 of 5.
かご (kago) — a basket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a potato, then the Japanese for burdock root.

You'll want to know: かご.
かご — kago — "a basket".

The word, taken apart.
かご (kago) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たいこ (taiko) — a drum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a raft, then the Japanese for a flute.

You'll want to know: たいこ.
たいこ — taiko — "a drum".

The word, taken apart.
たいこ (taiko) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tai, "big, great" (Middle Chinese thajH) and ko, "drum" (kuX) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
