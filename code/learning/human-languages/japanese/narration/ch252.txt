Japanese, chapter 252: One Thing, Then the Next.
7 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
おきて / いって (okite / itte) — one action, then the next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say, to a friend, "I got up" and "I went". (okita, itta.) Then say "I washed my face", politely. (kao o araimashita.)

You'll want to know: た becomes て.
おきて、かおを あらいました。 okite, kao o araimashita. — "I got up and washed my face."
おきて (okite) is おきた (okita) with え in place of あ: た becomes て. A sentence can hold several actions this way, in the order they happened, and the last verb says when: ました puts all of them in the past.
あさ おきて、かおを あらいました。 asa okite, kao o araimashita. — "In the morning I got up and washed my face."
The other verbs that drop る do the same, and so do する and くる: たべた gives たべて (tabete), した gives して (shite), きた gives きて (kite).
ばんごはんを たべて、ねました。 bangohan o tabete, nemashita. — "I ate dinner and went to bed."
You know this form already from a request: いって ください (itte kudasai), "please say it". That いって comes from 言う, "to say"; いく makes the same sound, as the next section shows.

You'll want to know: って, んで, いて, して.
The past by the last kana gives the -て form by the last kana, with the same swap: だ becomes で.
いった becomes いって (itte), かった becomes かって (katte), あらった becomes あらって (aratte).
のんだ becomes のんで (nonde), あそんだ becomes あそんで (asonde).
かいた becomes かいて (kaite), およいだ becomes およいで (oyoide), はなした becomes はなして (hanashite).
かおを あらって、でかけました。 kao o aratte, dekakemashita. — "I washed my face and went out."
えきに いって、きっぷを かいました。 eki ni itte, kippu o kaimashita. — "I went to the station and bought a ticket."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I drank tea and went out" — ocha o nonde, dekakemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I drank coffee and went out" — kōhī o nonde, dekakemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I drank water and went out" — mizu o nonde, dekakemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, politely, that you got up and washed your face.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
The -て form of おきる? (おきて.) Of する? (して.) Of いく? (いって.) Of のむ? (のんで.) Which verb says when it happened? (The last one.)


Lesson 2 of 7.
たべてから (tabete kara) — after doing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I ate breakfast and went out". (asagohan o tabete, dekakemashita.) Now make the order matter: first breakfast, and only then out.

You'll want to know: -てから.
あさごはんを たべてから、でかけました。 asagohan o tabete kara, dekakemashita. — "After eating breakfast, I went out."
から (kara) after the -て form makes "after": the first action was over before the second began. -て alone lines actions up; -てから says one waited for the other.
てがみを かいてから、ねました。 tegami o kaite kara, nemashita. — "After writing a letter, I went to bed."

You'll want to know: plans and habits.
With the non-past, -てから gives the order of a plan or a habit:
はを みがいてから、ねます。 ha o migaite kara, nemasu. — "I brush my teeth before I go to bed."
しゅくだいを してから、あそびます。 shukudai o shite kara, asobimasu. — "I will play after doing my homework."
Two more words for "and then", which start a new sentence, come next.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "After eating dinner, I went to bed" — bangohan o tabete kara, nemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After eating lunch, I went to bed" — hirugohan o tabete kara, nemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After eating breakfast, I went to bed" — asagohan o tabete kara, nemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that you will play after doing your homework.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"After eating"? (たべてから (tabete kara).) "After writing"? (かいてから.) What does から add to -て? (The first action is over before the next begins.)


Lesson 3 of 7.
それから (sorekara) — and then; after that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "after eating breakfast, I went out". (asagohan o tabete kara, dekakemashita.) Then say "that", a thing near the listener. (sore.)

You'll want to know: それから.
それから — sorekara — "and then", "after that". It opens the next sentence, and 、 follows it.
あさごはんを たべました。 asagohan o tabemashita. — "I ate breakfast."
それから、でかけました。 sorekara, dekakemashita. — "After that, I went out."

The word, taken apart.
それから (sorekara) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: それ (sore), "that", and から (kara), "from", so "from that point on". JMdict lists it as an expression, "and then; after that" (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: sorekara]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After that, I went out" — sorekara, dekakemashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After that, I went home" — sorekara, kaerimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'and then, after that'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"And then", at the start of a sentence? (それから, sorekara.) Which mark follows it? (、.)


Lesson 4 of 7.
そして (soshite) — and; joining two sentences.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "after that, I went out". (sorekara, dekakemashita.)

You'll want to know: そして.
そして — soshite — "and", at the start of a sentence. それから (sorekara) says what came next in time; そして adds the next thing.
うみに いきました。 umi ni ikimashita. — "I went to the sea."
そして、およぎました。 soshite, oyogimashita. — "And I swam."

The word, taken apart.
そして (soshite) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana, and among its kanji spellings gives one whose first kanji KANJIDIC2 glosses "so", followed by して (shite), the -て form of する: "doing so", "and so" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: soshite]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "And I swam" — soshite, oyogimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "And I played" — soshite, asobimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'and', at the start of a sentence.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"And", opening a sentence? (そして, soshite.) "And then", in time? (それから, sorekara.)


Lesson 5 of 7.
月日 (tsukihi) — the passing of time.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: You'll want to know: 月日.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "the moon". (tsuki.) Then say "fast". (hayai.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
You'll want to know: 月日.
月日 — tsukihi — "the passing of time": months and days, gone by.
月日は はやいです。 tsukihi wa hayai desu. — "Time flies."
Three beats: tsu–ki–hi. The second sign is 日, the kanji you write, read ひ (hi) inside this word. The first is new: 月, read つき (tsuki) here, "the moon", the word you know. The next lesson writes it.

The word, taken apart.
月日 (tsukihi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: つき (tsuki), "the moon, a month", and ひ (hi), "a day", each written with its kanji and read the native way, as a kun'yomi. JMdict glosses the whole as "time; years; days", and KANJIDIC2 gives つき for the first kanji and ひ for the second (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tsukihi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Time flies" — tsukihi wa hayai desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the moon, tsuki, then tsukihi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'the passing of time'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"The passing of time"? (月日, tsukihi.) Which sign do you already write? (日.) How is the new one read here? (つき, tsuki.)


Lesson 6 of 7.
月 (tsuki) — the moon, four strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say tsukihi, the passing of time. Its second sign is 日, which you write. Today the first.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the shape.
月 — tsuki.
Its parts:
a tall frame, open at the bottom.
two short bars inside.
Four strokes, three lifts:
draw the left side from the top down, sweeping a little to the left at the foot.
lift, draw the top from left to right, then without lifting turn down the right side and hook left at its foot.
lift, draw the upper inside bar from left to right.
lift, draw the lower inside bar from left to right.
Like 日 it is taller than it is wide and has bars inside, but it is open at the bottom, and its first stroke sweeps away to the left. Here it is read つき (tsuki), as in 月日 (tsukihi). Kanji have more than one reading; this lesson asks only for the one in the word you know.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
Find 月 (tsuki) in 月日 and say tsukihi.
Copy 月 three times, saying tsuki at each one.
Find it once more, in 月日は はやいです (tsukihi wa hayai desu), and say the line.

Wrap-up Recall.
Which way does the first stroke sweep at its foot? (A little to the left.)

[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
