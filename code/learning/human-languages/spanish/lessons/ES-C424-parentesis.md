# los paréntesis — a putting-in-beside

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *las comillas*. The next mark also sets something apart from the
sentence — but what it sets apart is an aside rather than a quotation.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C424-06]; assesses=[] -->

**los paréntesis** — "the parentheses," the curved pair.

The word is Greek and it describes the job precisely: **para-** beside,
**en-** in, **thesis** a putting. A *parénthesis* is *a putting-in-beside* — a
remark placed next to the sentence rather than inside its grammar.

> Mi hermana (la mayor) vive en Madrid.

Remove what is between them and the sentence still stands: *Mi hermana vive en
Madrid.* That is the test for whether the mark is the right one.

## Grammar Lens: spacing, and where the point goes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-06] -->

| | correct | wrong |
|---|---|---|
| spacing | `casa (grande) blanca` | `casa ( grande ) blanca` |
| with a point | `... en Madrid.` | `... en Madrid).` inside |

No space on the inside of either curve. And when a parenthesis closes at the end
of a sentence, the point goes **outside** it — the aside finishes first, then
the sentence does.
