Italian, chapter 531: Il bucato.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il bucato — laundry, washing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to post, then the Italian for follower.

You'll want to know: il bucato.
il bucato — "laundry, washing".
Il sabato faccio il bucato. On Saturdays I do the washing.
Fare il bucato — to do the washing.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of bucato is not settled; it is usually traced to a Germanic verb for soaking clothes in lye.

Lesson 2 of 5.
appendere — to hang (up).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for follower, then the Italian for laundry.

You'll want to know: appendere.
appendere — "to hang (up)".
Appendo il cappotto all'ingresso. I hang my coat up in the hall.
The past participle is appeso: il quadro è appeso al muro.

The word, taken apart.
appendere is inherited from Latin appendere "to hang up, to weigh out" (EtymDB), from pendere "to hang".

Lesson 3 of 5.
lo stendino — clothes horse, drying rack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for laundry, then the Italian for to hang.

You'll want to know: lo stendino.
lo stendino — "clothes horse, drying rack".
Metto lo stendino sul balcone. I put the clothes horse out on the balcony.
Built on stendere "to spread out, to hang out".

The word, taken apart.
stendino is built on stendere "to spread out", inherited from Latin extendere "to stretch out" (Lewis & Short), from tendere.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la molletta — clothes peg; hair clip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to hang, then the Italian for clothes horse.

You'll want to know: la molletta.
la molletta — "clothes peg; hair clip".
Mi servono altre mollette per le lenzuola. I need more pegs for the sheets.
Also a hair clip.

The word, taken apart.
molletta is a little molla "spring" (EtymDB), from mollare "to let go", built on Latin mollis "soft" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il ferro da stiro — iron (for clothes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for clothes horse, then the Italian for clothes peg.

You'll want to know: il ferro da stiro.
il ferro da stiro — "iron (for clothes)".
Attenta, il ferro da stiro è ancora caldo. Careful, the iron is still hot.
Stirare — to iron.

The word, taken apart.
ferro da stiro is built from ferro "iron", inherited from Latin ferrum (EtymDB), and stiro, from stirare "to stretch, to iron".
