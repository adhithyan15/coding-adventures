German, chapter 441: Häuser und Läden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Fassade — the façade, the front (of a building).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the cobbles, then the German for the fountain.

You'll want to know: die Fassade.
die Fassade — "the façade, the front (of a building)".
Die Fassade vom Rathaus ist frisch gestrichen. The front of the town hall has been freshly painted.
Plural: die Fassaden. Figurative: Das ist nur Fassade — it is all for show.

The word, taken apart.
Fassade was borrowed from French façade (EtymDB), which came from Italian facciata, built on faccia 'face', from Latin faciēs (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Schaufenster — the shop window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the fountain, then the German for the façade.

You'll want to know: das Schaufenster.
das Schaufenster — "the shop window".
Im Schaufenster stehen schöne Schuhe. There are nice shoes in the shop window.
Same form in the plural. Einen Schaufensterbummel machen — to go window-shopping.

The word, taken apart.
Schaufenster is built from schauen 'to look', inherited from Old High German scouwōn (EtymDB), and Fenster 'window', an early borrowing from Latin fenestra (Lewis & Short).

The word, taken apart.
Dachrinne is built from Dach 'roof' and Rinne 'channel', both inherited: Rinne goes back to Old High German rinna, from the verb for running water (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

die Markthalle — "the covered market, the market hall".
In der Markthalle kauft sie Käse und Obst. She buys cheese and fruit in the covered market.
Plural: die Markthallen.

The word, taken apart.
Markthalle is built from Markt 'market', which goes back through Old High German marchāt to Latin mercātus (EtymDB), and Halle 'hall', an inherited Germanic word.
