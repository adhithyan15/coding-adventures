German, chapter 511: Im Wasser.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Freibad — open-air pool.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for cheering, then the German for to boo.

You'll want to know: das Freibad.
das Freibad — "open-air pool".
Im Sommer gehen wir jeden Tag ins Freibad. In summer we go to the open-air pool every day.
Plural: die Freibäder. An indoor pool is das Hallenbad.

The word, taken apart.
Freibad is built on frei "free, open" and Bad "bath", inherited from Old High German bad, from Proto-Germanic baþą (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
kraulen — to swim front crawl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to boo, then the German for open-air pool.

You'll want to know: kraulen.
kraulen — "to swim front crawl".
Mein Sohn kann schon kraulen. My son can already swim front crawl.
A different verb kraulen means to scratch gently: den Hund hinter den Ohren kraulen.

The word, taken apart.
The swimming verb kraulen was borrowed from English crawl; the other kraulen, "to scratch gently", comes from krauen (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for open-air pool, then the German for to swim front crawl.

You'll want to know: tauchen.
tauchen — "to dive (underwater)".
Im Urlaub sind wir zwischen bunten Fischen getaucht. On holiday we dived among colourful fish.
Perfekt mostly with sein: wir sind getaucht. A diver is der Taucher.

The word, taken apart.
tauchen is inherited from Middle High German tūchen, touchen, from Proto-Germanic dūkaną (EtymDB).

paddeln — "to paddle".
Wir paddeln mit dem Boot über den See. We paddle across the lake in the boat.
Perfekt with sein for a trip: Wir sind über den See gepaddelt.

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Kanu — canoe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to dive, then the German for to paddle.

You'll want to know: das Kanu.
das Kanu — "canoe".
Mit dem Kanu sind wir den Fluss hinuntergefahren. We went down the river by canoe.
Plural: die Kanus.

The word, taken apart.
Kanu came into German through English canoe and Spanish canoa, usually traced to a Caribbean word for a dugout boat; EtymDB has no entry for the German word.
