## You'll want to know: the bus has gone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C321-MISSED-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C105-LOC105-02, TE-LEX-C280-KRIYA280-02] -->

> **అయ్యో! బస్సు వెళ్ళిపోయింది.**
>
> *ayyō! bassu veḷḷipōyindi.* — "Oh no! The bus has gone."

**అయ్యో** (*ayyō*, "oh no"). **వెళ్ళిపోయింది** is **వెళ్ళి**, "having gone", with **పోయింది**, "went
away": gone, and not coming back. Telugu says what the bus did; English says what you missed.

> **నేను బస్సు అందుకోలేకపోయాను.**
>
> *nēnu bassu andukōlēkapōyānu.* — "I could not catch the bus."

**అందుకోలేకపోయాను** (*andukōlēkapōyānu*, "I could not catch") is **అందుకో**, "to catch", with **-లేకపోయాను**, "I could not".

## You'll want to know: it is lost
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C321-LOST-REPORT-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C112-THINGS112-01, TE-LEX-C130-THINGS130-03, TE-LEX-C72-MARKET-02, TE-LEX-C237-PANI237-01, TE-LEX-C277-PANI277-01, TE-LEX-C105-LOC105-01, TE-LEX-C75-WHEN-02] -->

> **నా సంచి పోయింది.**
>
> *nā sañci pōyindi.* — "My bag is lost."

**పోవు**, "to go", says "to be lost" too: the bag "went". Then say what was in it and when it went:

> **సంచిలో నా ఫోను, నా డబ్బు ఉన్నాయి.**
>
> *sañcilō nā phōnu, nā ḍabbu unnāyi.* — "My phone and my money were in the bag."

> **నిన్న సాయంత్రం స్టేషనులో పోయింది. నేను పోలీసులకు ఫిర్యాదు ఇవ్వాలి.**
>
> *ninna sāyantraṁ sṭēṣanulō pōyindi. nēnu pōlīsulaku phiryādu ivvāli.* — "It went yesterday evening, at the station. I must make a complaint to the police."

**ఉన్నాయి** (*unnāyi*, "they are, they were") is the plural of **ఉంది**. A report answers three questions: what,
where, when.
