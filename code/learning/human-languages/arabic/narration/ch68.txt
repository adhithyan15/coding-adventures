Lesson 1 of 5.
برتقال (burtuqāl) — oranges.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for honey, then the Arabic for grapes.

You'll want to know: برتقال.
برتقال — burtuqāl — "oranges".

The word, taken apart.
برتقال (burtuqāl) is named after Portugal, whose traders brought the sweet orange from the east; the same name runs through Turkish portakal and Greek portokali. It has no Arabic root.

The word, taken apart.
ليمون (laymūn) is a loan from Persian līmūn, līmū, "lemon" (EtymDB), a word usually traced further east to an Indic source. It has no Arabic root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بيض (bayḍ) — eggs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for oranges, then the Arabic for lemons.

You'll want to know: بيض.
بيض — bayḍ — "eggs".

The word, taken apart.
بيض (bayḍ) is built on the native Arabic root b-y-ḍ, "to be white, to whiten" (Lane). Bayḍ, "eggs", is named on the root of whiteness, which also gives abyaḍ, "white".

Lesson 5 of 5.
فطور (fuṭūr) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for eggs, then the Arabic for onions.

You'll want to know: فطور.
فطور — fuṭūr — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
فطور (fuṭūr) is built on the native Arabic root f-ṭ-r, "to cleave; to break a fast" (Lane). Lane gives fuṭūr as the food or drink with which a fast is broken.
