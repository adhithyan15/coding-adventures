Japanese, chapter 250: What I Did Yesterday.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
みた (mita) — the plain past, what friends say.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new form: say "I saw a film yesterday", politely. (kinō eiga o mimashita.) The polite past is what you say to a teacher or in a shop. To a friend, the past is shorter.

You'll want to know: the plain past.
A friend asks what you did yesterday, and you answer as friends do:
えいがを みた。 eiga o mita. — "I saw a film."
きのう えいがを みた。 kinō eiga o mita. — "I saw a film yesterday."
みた (mita) is みました (mimashita) without the politeness: the plain past, the form friends and family use with each other.
For みる (miru), "to see", drop the last る and add た. たべる (taberu), "to eat", goes the same way: たべた (tabeta), "ate".
ばんごはんを たべた。 bangohan o tabeta. — "I ate dinner."
うちで ばんごはんを たべた。 uchi de bangohan o tabeta. — "I ate dinner at home."
The polite form shows the way too: take ます off ねます (nemasu) and add た: ねた (neta), "went to bed". From おきます (okimasu) comes おきた (okita), "got up".
きのう はやく ねた。 kinō hayaku neta. — "I went to bed early yesterday."

You'll want to know: した and きた.
Two more verbs: した (shita), "did". It is the past of する (suru), "to do", the verb inside します (shimasu). And くる (kuru), "to come", the verb inside きます (kimasu), has きた (kita), "came".
しゅくだいを した。 shukudai o shita. — "I did my homework."
ともだちが きた。 tomodachi ga kita. — "A friend came."
きのう ともだちが きた。 kinō tomodachi ga kita. — "A friend came yesterday."
The plain past has a second job, and later lessons give it to you: it goes inside a polite sentence, in front of the word that makes the sentence polite. So みた (mita) is worth owning even if you speak politely to everyone.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I ate dinner" — bangohan o tabeta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I ate breakfast" — asagohan o tabeta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I ate lunch" — hirugohan o tabeta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, as you would to a friend, that you saw a film yesterday.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I saw", to a friend? (みた, mita.) "I got up"? (おきた, okita.) "Did"? (した, shita.) "Came"? (きた, kita.) And "I saw", to a teacher? (みました, mimashita.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
かった / のんだ / かいた (katta / nonda / kaita) — the past by the last kana.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I ate" and "I did", to a friend. (tabeta, shita.) Then say "I bought shoes", politely. (kutsu o kaimashita.) Most verbs do not drop a る: their -た depends on their last kana.

You'll want to know: った and んだ.
かう (kau), "to buy", ends in う. Its plain past is かった (katta): a small っ, a short catch, then た.
くつを かった。 kutsu o katta. — "I bought shoes."
つ and る go the same way. まつ (matsu), "to wait", has まった (matta). かえります (kaerimasu) is the polite form of かえる (kaeru), "to go home", and its past is かえった (kaetta). (A different かえる, "to change", drops its る like みる.)
えきで まった。 eki de matta. — "I waited at the station."
はやく うちに かえった。 hayaku uchi ni kaetta. — "I went home early."
む and ぶ, and the rare verbs in ぬ, end in んだ. のむ (nomu), "to drink", has のんだ (nonda); あそぶ (asobu), "to play", has あそんだ (asonda).
おちゃを のんだ。 ocha o nonda. — "I drank tea."
こうえんで あそんだ。 kōen de asonda. — "I played in the park."

You'll want to know: いた, いだ, した, and いった.
A verb in く ends in いた: かく (kaku), "to write", has かいた (kaita); きく (kiku), "to listen", has きいた (kiita). One in ぐ ends in いだ: およぐ (oyogu), "to swim", has およいだ (oyoida).
てがみを かいた。 tegami o kaita. — "I wrote a letter."
うみで およいだ。 umi de oyoida. — "I swam in the sea."
A verb in す ends in した: はなす (hanasu), "to speak", has はなした (hanashita).
ともだちと はなした。 tomodachi to hanashita. — "I talked with a friend."
And one verb in く goes its own way: いく (iku), "to go", has いった (itta), like かった.
きのう やまに いった。 kinō yama ni itta. — "I went to the mountains yesterday."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I went to the sea" — umi ni itta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I went to the river" — kawa ni itta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I went to the park" — kōen ni itta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, as you would to a friend, that you went to the sea yesterday.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Bought"? (かった, katta.) "Drank"? (のんだ, nonda.) "Wrote"? (かいた, kaita.) "Swam"? (およいだ, oyoida.) "Talked"? (はなした, hanashita.) "Went"? (いった, itta.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — what I did yesterday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I saw" and "I bought", to a friend. (mita, katta.)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: I saw a film yesterday. — to a friend]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I got up early and ate breakfast.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: A friend came, and we played in the park.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I drank tea and talked with a friend.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I went to the sea and swam.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
A friend asks what you did yesterday. Say that you wrote a letter.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two sentences for each of those, or one? (Two: おきた。あさごはんを たべた。 The form that joins them comes later.) "Waited"? (まった, matta.) "Listened"? (きいた, kiita.) "Played"? (あそんだ, asonda.) "Went home"? (かえった, kaetta.)
