Lesson 1 of 7.
少年 (shàonián) — teenage boy; teenager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for all one's life, then the Mandarin for to look back fondly on.

You'll want to know: 少年.
少年 — shàonián — "teenage boy; teenager".
他十五岁的时候是一个快乐的少年 tā shíwǔ suì de shíhou shì yí ge kuàilè de shàonián — At fifteen he was a happy teenager.

The word, taken apart.
少年 (shàonián) is built from 少 'young' and 年 'a year', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syewH and nen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, in this falling tone, 'young'; in its rising tone, syewX, it is 'few'.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 时期.
时期 — shíqī — "period; phase".
那个时期当地的人都没有手机 nàge shíqī dāngdì de rén dōu méiyǒu shǒujī — In that period nobody there had a mobile phone.

The word, taken apart.
时期 (shíqī) is built from 时 'a time' and 期 'a set time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyi and gi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to meet' and 'a limit'. Middle Chinese gi would regularly give a rising tone, qí; the level tone of qī is irregular.

Lesson 3 of 7.
年代 (niándài) — decade; era.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a teenage boy, then the Mandarin for a period in history.

You'll want to know: 年代.
年代 — niándài — "decade; era".
他八十年代去过中国 tā bāshí niándài qù guo Zhōngguó — He went to China in the eighties.
The new character, in pieces: 代 (dài) — five strokes: the person piece 亻 on the left; on the right a bar rising to the right, a long stroke that slants down to the right and flicks up, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
年代 (niándài) is built from 年 'a year' and dài 'an age', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nen and dojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to take turns' and gives this very word as its example.

Script — the person piece, then a slanting hook.
代 (dài).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes, as in 你 and 信.
The right piece, three strokes. A short bar that rises a little to the right. Then a long stroke from the top, crossing the bar and slanting down to the right, flicking up at the foot. Last, a dot at the top right.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 代 (dài), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 代 (dài)? (Five.) Which stroke flicks up at the foot? (The long slanting stroke, the fourth.) What comes last? (A dot at the top right.)
