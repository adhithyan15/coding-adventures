Arabic, chapter 509: Germs and jabs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
عدوى (ʿadwā) — an infection, contagion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an inflammation, then the Arabic for to swell.

You'll want to know: عدوى.
عدوى — ʿadwā — "an infection, contagion".
انتقلت العدوى من طفل إلى طفل. intaqalat al-ʿadwā min ṭifl ilā ṭifl. The infection passed from child to child.

The word, taken apart.
عدوى (ʿadwā) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-d-w, "to run, to pass beyond" (Lane). ʿAdwā is a thing that runs from one person to another.

Lesson 2 of 5.
فيروس (fayrūs) — a virus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to swell, then the Arabic for an infection.

You'll want to know: فيروس.
فيروس — fayrūs — "a virus".
الزكام سببه فيروس. az-zukām sababuhu fayrūs. A cold is caused by a virus.

The word, taken apart.
فيروس (fayrūs) is a modern loan, usually traced to Latin virus, "poison, slime", which reached Arabic through European languages.

Lesson 3 of 5.
جرثومة (jurthūma) — a germ.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an infection, then the Arabic for a virus.

You'll want to know: جرثومة.
جرثومة — jurthūma — "a germ".
يقتل الصابون الجراثيم. yaqtulu aṣ-ṣābūn al-jarāthīm. Soap kills germs.
Plural: جراثيم, jarāthīm.
It is mostly met in the plural جراثيم.

The word, taken apart.
جرثومة (jurthūma) is built on a native four-letter root, j-r-th-m, where it named the root or origin of a thing; modern Arabic gave it the sense of a germ. The origin of the root itself is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
تطعيم (taṭʿīm) — a vaccination.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a virus, then the Arabic for a germ.

You'll want to know: تطعيم.
تطعيم — taṭʿīm — "a vaccination".
أخذ الأطفال التطعيم في المدرسة. akhadha al-aṭfāl at-taṭʿīm fī al-madrasa. The children had their vaccination at school.

The word, taken apart.
تطعيم (taṭʿīm) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-ʿ-m, "to eat, to taste" (Lane), the root of ṭaʿām, food. Lane gives Form II ṭaʿʿama as "to graft" a branch; modern Arabic uses the same word for putting a vaccine into the body.
