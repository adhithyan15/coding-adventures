# consultar — look it up

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-06, ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **revisar** and **el documento**. Both are about paper, and
what you do to it differs.

## You'll want to know: consultar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO] -->

**consultar** — to look up, to check, to consult.

*Consulte el horario* — check the timetable. *Lo consulto y te digo* — I'll
look into it and get back to you. *Consultar con el jefe* — to check with the
boss.

Two nouns come with it: **la consulta**, which is both the query and a
doctor's surgery, and **el consultorio**, the consulting room.

## Grammar Lens: consultar, revisar, mirar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-04, ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-06] -->

Three verbs that all come out as "check" in English, now separated by what
you are after:

| what you want | the verb |
|---|---|
| a fact you do not have | **consultar** |
| faults in something you do have | **revisar** |
| to aim your eyes at it | **mirar** |

*Consulto el horario* — I do not know the time and the timetable does. *Reviso
el horario* — I am checking the timetable for mistakes, which is a different
errand entirely. *Miro el horario* — my eyes are on it, and the sentence has
said nothing about why.

Note the preposition: you *consultar* a document directly, but *consultar
**con*** a person. The **con** marks that the other party can answer back.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-04, ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03] -->

Latin **consultare**, the repeated form of **consulere** — to take counsel,
to deliberate.

The Roman **consul** is the same root: the officer who takes counsel for the
state. And **el consejo** — advice, and a council — came down the spoken road
from *consilium*, a close relation.

So *consultar* and *el consejo* are family, and the pair shows the split this
chapter keeps meeting: *consejo* is worn smooth by centuries of speech,
*consultar* was lifted from Latin by clerks with the **l** and the **t** still
in place.
