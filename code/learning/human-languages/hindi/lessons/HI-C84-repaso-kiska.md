# pā̃ch mālik, ek niyam — five owners, and one bend that covers them all

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name as many possessives as you can before reading the table. Aim
for five.

## Grammar Lens: the whole grid
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-02, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-04, HI-CONCEPT-C02-MERAA-01] -->

Every one of these ends in **-आ**, so every one bends the same way:

| owner | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| my | merā | merī |
| your (respectful) | āpkā | āpkī |
| your (familiar) | tumhārā | tumhārī |
| his, her, its | uskā | uskī |
| our | hamārā | hamārī |
| the subject's own | apnā | apnī |

Six forms in the first column and not one of them is irregular. The agreement
is with the **thing owned** throughout, which is why nothing in the middle
column tells you anything about the owner.

## Grammar Lens: the bend, and the two jobs of -ए
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-05, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-06, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

Put a postposition after any of them and the **-आ** becomes **-ए**:

| direct | before a postposition |
|---|---|
| merā | **mere** pās |
| uskā | **uske** liye |
| hamārā | **hamāre** ghar meṅ |
| apnā | **apne** dost ke *sāth* |

The form **-ए** therefore has two jobs, and the sentence tells you which:

- *mere dost* — no postposition follows, so this is the **plural**: my friends.
- *mere pās* — a postposition follows, so this is the **oblique**: near me.

Nothing in the spelling distinguishes them. What comes next does.
