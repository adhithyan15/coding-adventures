Urdu, chapter 55: Potato, Tomato, Carrot, Sugar, Salt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
آلو (ālū) — a potato.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an apple, then the Urdu for a banana.

You'll want to know: آلو.
آلو — ālū — "a potato".

The word, taken apart.
آلو (ālū) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ālu, "an edible root" (CDIAL 1388). When the potato came to India, it took the name of an older root vegetable.

Lesson 4 of 5.
چینی (chīnī) — sugar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a tomato, then the Urdu for a carrot.

You'll want to know: چینی.
چینی — chīnī — "sugar".

The word, taken apart.
چینی (chīnī) means "Chinese": sugar was named after China. CDIAL lists Hindi cīnā, "Chinese", under Sanskrit cīna, "Chinese" (CDIAL 4841); chīnī is the same name with the ending -ī.
