# der Donner — "thunder"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SOUND-LAW-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Run the sound law backwards on this one before you read it. German
has no *th*; what would English *thunder* look like in German?

## You'll want to know: der Donner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-DONNER-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SOUND-LAW-01, GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **der Donner** — "thunder."

*Th-* becomes *d-*, exactly as it did in *drei*. Take the *th* off *thunder*,
put a *d* there, and you have most of the German word already.

This is the second time that rule has paid, and it will keep paying: German
*d* answers English *th* right across the vocabulary.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DONNER-01] -->

- `vowel-o` — short and open, the *o* of English *on*.
- `r-uvular-german` — the *-er* at the end is barely a consonant; it colours the
  vowel and stops. *DON-ah*, not *DON-err*.
