Bengali, chapter 358: Alike and Different.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মিল (mil) — a likeness; a match.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a conclusion of a text, then the Bengali for a notion.

You'll want to know: মিল.
মিল — mil — "a likeness; a match".
দুই ভাইয়ের চেহারায় অনেক মিল আছে। dui bhāiyer chehārāy ônek mil āchhe. The two brothers look very alike.

The word, taken apart.
মিল (mil) is of the same family as মেলানো, from Sanskrit milana, meeting (CDIAL 10134 lists Bengali milan): the place where two things meet and agree.

আমি একজন সাধারণ মানুষ। āmi ekjôn sādhārôn mānush. I'm an ordinary person.

Lesson 5 of 5.
গুলিয়ে ফেলা (guliye phelā) — to mix up; to confuse (two things).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a link, then the Bengali for ordinary.

You'll want to know: গুলিয়ে ফেলা.
গুলিয়ে ফেলা — guliye phelā — "to mix up; to confuse (two things)".
আমি প্রায়ই এই দুই শব্দ গুলিয়ে ফেলি। āmi prāyi ei dui shôbdo guliye pheli. I often mix up these two words.
Past (he/she …): তিনি নামগুলো গুলিয়ে ফেললেন, tini nāmgulo guliye phellen.

The word, taken apart.
গুলিয়ে ফেলা (guliye phelā) is the -এ form of গোলা, to dissolve or stir into water, with ফেলা 'to throw' (CDIAL 8651 lists Bengali phelā), which marks a thing done and finished: two things stirred together until you cannot tell them apart. The source of গোলা is not settled; CDIAL lists Bengali gholā 'to stir, mix' (4526) and galā 'to melt' (4074), and neither is certain.
