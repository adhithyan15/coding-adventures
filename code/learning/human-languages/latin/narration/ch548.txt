Latin, chapter 548: Beauty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pulchritūdō, pulchritūdinis — beauty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a small cube, then the Latin for to engrave.

You'll want to know: pulchritūdō, pulchritūdinis.
pulchritūdō, pulchritūdinis — "beauty".
Pulchritūdō hortī hospitēs dēlectat. The beauty of the garden delights the guests.
Feminine, third declension, from pulcher. English pulchritude.

The word, taken apart.
pulchritūdō is built on pulcher "beautiful" with -tūdō (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English pulchritude comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
speciōsus, speciōsa, speciōsum — good-looking, handsome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to engrave, then the Latin for beauty.

You'll want to know: speciōsus, speciōsa, speciōsum.
speciōsus, speciōsa, speciōsum — "good-looking, handsome".
Iuvenis speciōsus est, sed superbus. The young man is good-looking, but proud.
From speciēs — appearance. Later also showy but false: English specious.

The word, taken apart.
speciōsus is built on speciēs "appearance, look", from speciō "to behold" (Lewis & Short). English specious comes from it, with the later sense "fair on the surface".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ēlegāns, ēlegantis — elegant, refined, tasteful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for beauty, then the Latin for good-looking.

You'll want to know: ēlegāns, ēlegantis.
ēlegāns, ēlegantis — "elegant, refined, tasteful".
Domus eius parva, sed ēlegāns est. His house is small but elegant.
One ending. First choosy, then of good taste. English elegant.

The word, taken apart.
ēlegāns is probably an old form of ēligēns, the participle of ēligō "to pick out" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from legō "to choose". English elegant comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēfōrmis, dēfōrme — ugly, misshapen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for elegant, then the Latin for an ornament.

You'll want to know: dēfōrmis, dēfōrme.
dēfōrmis, dēfōrme — "ugly, misshapen".
Statua dēfōrmis est, sed antīqua. The statue is ugly, but old.
Two endings. dē- 'away from' + fōrma 'shape, beauty'.

The word, taken apart.
dēfōrmis is built on dē- "away from" and fōrma "shape, beauty" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English deformed comes from the related verb dēfōrmō.
