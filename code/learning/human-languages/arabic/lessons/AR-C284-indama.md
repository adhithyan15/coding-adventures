# عندما (ʿindamā) — when, in a story

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C284-QISSA284-01, AR-LEX-C40-HINAMA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "she told a story". (*Ḥakat qiṣṣa*.) Then say *ḥīnamā jāʾa,
kataba*, "when he came, he wrote".

[PAUSE 1s] *Ḥīnamā* has a twin that you will hear far more often in a story.

## Grammar Lens: ʿindamā and the past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C284-INDAMA-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C105-LOC105-05, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-03, AR-LEX-C100-LOC100-04, AR-LEX-C255-ACT255-03, AR-LEX-C55-FAMILY55-05] -->

**عندما** — *ʿindamā* — "when". It is *ʿinda*, "at", with *mā* after it, as
*ḥīnamā* is *ḥīna*, "time", with *mā*. Put a past verb after it, and the
clause names the moment the next event hangs on:

> **عندما وصلنا إلى المطار، اتصلت بزوجتي.** — *ʿindamā waṣalnā ilā
> al-maṭār, ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī* — When we arrived at the airport, I phoned
> my wife.

The *ʿindamā* clause can follow as well: *ittaṣaltu bi-zawjatī ʿindamā
waṣalnā*. Either way, the arrival comes first and the call second.

## Grammar Lens: one event after another
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C284-BADA-AN-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C119-THINGS119-04, AR-LEX-C64-VERBS64-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01] -->

*Baʿda* means "after" before a noun: *baʿda al-ʿashāʾ*, after dinner. Before
a whole clause it takes *an*, "that", and the past:

> **بعد أن أكلنا، خرجنا.** — *baʿda an akalnā, kharajnā* — After we had
> eaten, we went out.

So a story can be ordered two ways: by joiners, *awwalan … thumma*, or by
clauses, *ʿindamā* and *baʿda an*. Good stories mix them.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which two pieces make *ʿindamā*? (***ʿInda*** and ***mā***.) Its
twin? (***Ḥīnamā.***) What does *baʿda* take before a clause? (***An***,
then the past.) In *ʿindamā waṣalnā, ittaṣaltu*, which came first? (**The
arrival.**)
