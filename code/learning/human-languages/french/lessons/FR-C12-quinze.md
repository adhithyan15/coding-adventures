# quinze — fifteen

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Back to one syllable — and this is the teen whose Latin half you are
most likely to already know from English.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-QUINZE-06]; assesses=[] -->

- `nasal-in` — the **in** is the nasal vowel you have in **pain** and **vin**.
- The **qu** is a plain **k** again.
- Together: **quinze** = *KANZ*, one syllable, the vowel through the nose.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**quinze** is Latin **quīndecim** — *quīnque* ("five") plus *decem*.

The French digit **cinq** has travelled a long way from *quīnque*, so *quinze*
does not obviously look like *cinq*. But the Latin **quīn-** is loud in English,
and that is the way in:

- **quin**tet — a group of five
- **quin**tuplet — one of five
- **quin**tessence — the *fifth* essence, the one the ancients added to earth,
  water, air and fire

So when *cinq* and *quinze* refuse to look related, go through English: *quintet*
is the bridge between them.
