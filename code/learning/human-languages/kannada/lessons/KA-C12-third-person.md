# ಅವನು, ಅವಳು, ಅದು, ಅವರು — people and things outside the conversation

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C12-KUTUMBA-01, KA-ETYMON-C12-KUTUMBA-02, KA-LEX-C03-NAANU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **ನಾನು** for “I”, then name father, mother, older brother and
older sister. Now point away from “I” and “you”: this lesson gives those people
their pronouns.

## Grammar Lens: four third-person forms
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C12-THIRD-PERSON-03]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C12-KUTUMBA-01, KA-ETYMON-C12-KUTUMBA-02] -->

- **ಅವನು** (*avanu*) — he
- **ಅವಳು** (*avaḷu*) — she
- **ಅದು** (*adu*) — it, that non-human thing
- **ಅವರು** (*avaru*) — they; also respectful “he” or “she”

Use **ಅವರು** for one respected adult as well as for several people. The
sentence around it tells you whether it means respectful singular or plural.

## Grammar Lens: the first split is human or non-human
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C12-HUMAN-NONHUMAN-04]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C12-THIRD-PERSON-03] -->

Kannada does not make every noun carry masculine or feminine marking. In the
third person, the useful first question is **human or non-human**:
**ಅವನು / ಅವಳು / ಅವರು** point to people; **ಅದು** points to a non-human thing.
That split will matter again when verbs and plurals change shape.
