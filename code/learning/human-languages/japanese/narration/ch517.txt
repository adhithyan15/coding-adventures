Japanese, chapter 517: Making Up One's Mind.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
けつだんする (ketsudan suru) — to make a firm decision.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the will to do something, then the Japanese for to tackle (a challenge).

You'll want to know: けつだんする.
けつだんする — ketsudan suru — "to make a firm decision".
もし はやく けつだんしたら、きっぷが やすいでしょう。 — moshi hayaku ketsudanshitara, kippu ga yasui deshō — "If you decide early, the tickets will probably be cheaper."
Polite けつだんします, ketsudanshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): しゃちょうが けつだんしました。, shachō ga ketsudanshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
けつだんする (ketsudan suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun ketsudan with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ketsu, "decide" (Middle Chinese kwet) and dan, "severance" (dwanX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
おもいきる (omoikiru) — to give up all thought of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to make a firm decision, then the Japanese for to resolve (to do).

You'll want to know: おもいきる.
おもいきる — omoikiru — "to give up all thought of".
もし ねだんが あがったら、くるまは おもいきります。 — moshi nedan ga agattara, kuruma wa omoikirimasu — "If the price goes up, I will give up on the car."
Polite おもいきります, omoikirimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが くるまを おもいきりました。, chichi ga kuruma o omoikirimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おもいきる (omoikiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from omou, "to think", and kiru, "to cut"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

もし じかんが あったら、よく かんがえて はんだんします。 — moshi jikan ga attara, yoku kangaete handan shimasu — "If I have time, I will think it over and decide."
Polite はんだんします, handanshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): いしゃが はんだんしました。, isha ga handanshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
はんだんする (handan suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun handan with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are han, "judgement" (Middle Chinese phanH) and dan, "judgement", whose Middle Chinese reading for this sense does not match the voiced Japanese one, so none is cited (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
