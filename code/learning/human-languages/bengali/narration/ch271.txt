Bengali, chapter 271: Tickets, and When Things Go Wrong.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
নৌকা (noukā) — a boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: ask "How do I get to the station?" (sṭeshône kībhābe jābô?)

You'll want to know: নৌকা.
নৌকা — noukā — "a boat".
আমরা নৌকায় নদী পার হলাম। āmrā noukāy nôdī pār hôlām. — "We crossed the river by boat."
নৌকায়, with -য়, is "in a boat, by boat", as বাসে is "by bus". A নৌকা (noukā) is small, rowed or sailed on a river; the big ship is জাহাজ, which you know.

The word, taken apart.
নৌকা (noukā) is a tatsama: Sanskrit naukā, which Monier-Williams gives as "a small boat or ship". Beside it Monier-Williams lists nau, "a ship, boat".

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We crossed the river by boat" — āmrā noukāy nôdī pār hôlām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "A boat is small; a ship is big" — noukā chhoṭo; jāhāj bôṛo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does নৌকা (noukā) mean? ("A boat".) "By boat"? (নৌকায়.) The big ship? (জাহাজ.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ৌ (-ou) — the sign around the ন of নৌকা (noukā).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ৌ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say নৌকা (noukā), "a boat". Then write নো (no): ে in front of the ন, া after it.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ৌ.
ৌ — -ou, the vowel of নৌকা (noukā). Like ো, it is written on both sides of its consonant: one part in front, one after.
Set the print of নৌ beside নো. In front of the ন, both have ে. After it, নো has an upright stroke like া. নৌ has an upright stroke too, but its top stands above the headline in a hook, bent over to the left, its tip at the upper left.

Writing: ৌ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside নৌ and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large: the ে in front, the ন, then the stroke after it with its hook above the headline. Check the copy against নো: only the hook differs.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ৌ (-ou) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — write: নৌকা (noukā), then নৌকায়]

Lesson 3 of 5.
একটা টিকিট দেবেন? (ekṭā ṭikiṭ deben?) — at the ticket window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "We crossed the river by boat". (āmrā noukāy nôdī pār hôlām.) Then টিকিট, "a ticket".

You'll want to know: asking for a ticket.
একটা টিকিট দেবেন? ekṭā ṭikiṭ deben? — "Could I have a ticket, please?"
Bengali often asks politely with the respectful future, "will you give?", where English says "could I have". দেবেন is দেওয়া in that form. To say where to, put the place in the -র form: গ্রামের একটা টিকিট, "a ticket to the village". To ask for a seat: জানালার পাশে একটা আসন পাব? (jānālār pāshe ekṭā āsôn pābô?), "can I get a seat by the window?".

You'll want to know: the fare and the time.
ভাড়া কত? ট্রেন কখন ছাড়বে? bhāṛā kôtô? ṭren kôkhôn chhāṛbe? — "What's the fare? When does the train leave?"
কত, "how much", asks the fare. কখন, "when", the third of chapter thirty's যখন, তখন, কখন, asks the time. ছাড়বে, "will leave", is ছাড়া, "to leave, to let go", in the future.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Could I have a ticket to the village, please?" — grāmer ekṭā ṭikiṭ deben?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What's the fare?" — bhāṛā kôtô?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When does the train leave?" — ṭren kôkhôn chhāṛbe?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Ask for a ticket, then ask the fare and when the train leaves.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
The polite "could I have"? (দেবেন?) "How much is the fare?" (ভাড়া কত?) "When will it leave?" (কখন ছাড়বে?)


Lesson 4 of 5.
আমার ব্যাগ হারিয়ে গেছে (āmār byāg hāriye gechhe) — when something goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask "When does the train leave?" (ṭren kôkhôn chhāṛbe?) Then say হারিয়ে যাওয়া, "to get lost", which you know.

You'll want to know: something is lost.
আমার মানিব্যাগ হারিয়ে গেছে। āmār mānibyāg hāriye gechhe. — "My wallet has gone missing."
গেছে is a form of যাওয়া that says something happened and still matters now: the wallet went, and it is still gone. This book teaches it only in these set phrases. Whose it is goes in the -র form: আমার ব্যাগ, মীরার পাসপোর্ট. Then what you will do: পুলিশকে জানাব (pulishke jānābô), "I'll tell the police".

You'll want to know: the train has left.
ছাড়া, "to leave", which you know, makes the same phrase: ছেড়ে গেছে, "has left".
আমার ট্রেন ছেড়ে গেছে। পরের ট্রেন কখন? āmār ṭren chheṛe gechhe. pôrer ṭren kôkhôn? — "My train has left. When is the next train?"

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "My passport has gone missing" — āmār pāsporṭ hāriye gechhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'll tell the police" — pulishke jānābô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My train has left. When is the next train?" — āmār ṭren chheṛe gechhe. pôrer ṭren kôkhôn?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that your bag has gone missing, then that your train has left.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"My bag has gone missing"? (আমার ব্যাগ হারিয়ে গেছে (āmār byāg hāriye gechhe).) "The train has left"? (ট্রেন ছেড়ে গেছে.) What does গেছে add? (It happened, and it still matters now.)
