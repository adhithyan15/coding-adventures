Latin, chapter 481: Enclosed valley, Plain, Rocky, Rough, To slide down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
convallis, convallis — an enclosed valley, a deep valley.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for steep, then the Latin for to climb.

You'll want to know: convallis, convallis.
convallis, convallis — "an enclosed valley, a deep valley".
Inter montēs convallis viridis est. Between the mountains there is a green valley.
Third declension, feminine: convallis, convallis f. A valley shut in on all sides.

The word, taken apart.
convallis is built from con- 'together, all round' and vallis 'valley' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
plānitiēs, plānitiēī — a plain, level ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to climb, then the Latin for an enclosed valley.

You'll want to know: plānitiēs, plānitiēī.
plānitiēs, plānitiēī — "a plain, level ground".
Post montēs plānitiēs magna est, et via rēcta est. Beyond the mountains there is a great plain, and the road is straight.
Fifth declension, feminine: plānitiēs, plānitiēī f., from plānus 'flat'. English plain comes from plānus.

The word, taken apart.
plānitiēs is built on plānus 'flat, level' with the suffix -itiēs (Lewis & Short). English plain and plane come from plānus.

saxōsus, saxōsa, saxōsum — "rocky, stony".
Ager saxōsus est; agricola eum arāre nōn potest. The field is stony; the farmer cannot plough it.
An adjective of three endings, from saxum 'rock' with -ōsus 'full of'.

The word, taken apart.
saxōsus is built on saxum 'rock, stone' with the suffix -ōsus 'full of' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
cōnfragōsus, cōnfragōsa, cōnfragōsum — rough, broken (of ground).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plain, then the Latin for rocky.

You'll want to know: cōnfragōsus, cōnfragōsa, cōnfragōsum.
cōnfragōsus, cōnfragōsa, cōnfragōsum — "rough, broken (of ground)".
Locus cōnfragōsus est, et equī cadunt. The ground is rough, and the horses stumble.
An adjective of three endings: ground full of breaks and holes. Also of a hard question: knotty.

The word, taken apart.
cōnfragōsus is built from con- and fragōsus 'broken, rough' (EtymDB), which comes from frangō 'to break' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēlābor, dēlābī — to slide down, to slip down, to fall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rocky, then the Latin for rough.

You'll want to know: dēlābor, dēlābī.
dēlābor, dēlābī — "to slide down, to slip down, to fall".
Nix dē monte dēlābitur. Snow slides down from the mountain.
Deponent: dēlābor, dēlābī, dēlāpsus sum, with dē or ex.

The word, taken apart.
dēlābor is built from dē- 'down' and lābor 'to slip, to glide' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English lapse comes from lābor.
