# estropear / estropeado — it no longer works

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C447-JUICIO-04, ES-LEX-ROMPER] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **torpe** and **romper**. Being the first often leads to the
second.

## You'll want to know: estropear
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ROMPER] -->

**estropear** — to ruin, to wreck, to put out of action.

*He estropeado el móvil* — I've wrecked my phone. *La lluvia estropeó la
fiesta* — the rain ruined the party. With **-se** it happens by itself: *se ha
estropeado la lavadora* — the washing machine has broken down.

And the form you meet most is the adjective:

**estropeado** — out of order, damaged, spoiled.

> Ascensor **estropeado**. Usen las escaleras.

That is a real sign, on a real lift, and it is the reason this word earns its
place before the polite ones.

## Grammar Lens: estropeado is not roto
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-04, ES-LEX-ROMPER] -->

Both translate as "broken" and they are not interchangeable:

| what you mean | the word |
|---|---|
| it is in pieces | **roto** |
| it will not work | **estropeado** |

A washing machine full of water that does nothing is **estropeada** and not
*rota* — nothing came apart. A plate in two halves is **rota** and calling it
*estropeada* would sound like an understatement.

Food takes *estropeado* too: *la fruta se ha estropeado* — the fruit has gone
off. Again the test holds. The fruit is not in pieces; it no longer does its
job.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-04, ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-03] -->

Spanish took it from Italian **stroppiare**, to cripple or maim, which goes
back to Latin **turpis** — shameful, ugly.

So **estropear** and **torpe** are cousins, and the pair is worth holding side
by side. The same Latin word for *shameful* came out of Italian as "wreck a
thing" and out of Spanish as "clumsy" — an apology you make over a spilled
glass. One branch kept the weight and one branch lost nearly all of it.

The **e-** at the front is Spanish's own doing. Spanish will not open a word
with *st-*, so it fits a vowel in front: *stroppiare* became *estropear*, the
way *stare* became *estar* and *studiare* became *estudiar*.
