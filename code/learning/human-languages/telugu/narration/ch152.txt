Telugu, chapter 152: A Word for Buzzing — Then Its First Letter.
3 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ) — a buzz, a hum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Imagine a bee circling close enough that its sound fills the air. You already know క, the dot-like ం, and the long ా sign. One rare first letter will turn those familiar pieces into a name for that sound.

You'll want to know: ఝంకారం.
ఝంకారం — jhaṅkāraṁ — "a buzz" or "a hum".
Say it in three pieces: jhaṅ, kā, raṁ. The first sound is an aspirated jh, with a small breath after the voice. Read the word as ఝం-కా-రం.
This is a learned, literary word derived from Sanskrit. Older dictionaries also give it a wider sense of a loud resonant noise. Keep today's image narrow: the steady buzzing or humming sound, especially the sound of a bee.

What you've built.
You can hear and say the whole word before the next lesson asks your hand to isolate its uncommon first letter.

Lesson 2 of 3.
ఝ — the breathed first letter in ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ఝ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ) once. What sound can it name? (A buzz or hum.)
[pause 1 second]
Now slow the first piece: jhaṅ. The base letter carrying that breathed jh is today's whole task.

Script you'll notice: ఝ.
ఝ — jha.
In ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ), the dot-like ం closes the first syllable: ఝం. Remove that sign and the base letter is ఝ.
This aspirated letter is uncommon in everyday Telugu vocabulary. That is why the book gave you ఝంకారం (jhaṅkāraṁ) before asking you to practise the shape.

Writing: ఝ — five movements, five runs.
Follow the filmstrip slowly. Movements 1–3 travel clockwise through the three round sections. Movement 4 curls upward through the flourish above them. Movement 5 draws the separate stem downward beneath the middle-right side.
The numbered route is one attested school-style order; Telugu handwriting can vary. Keep the five pen-down runs distinct and let all three bowls stay round.
