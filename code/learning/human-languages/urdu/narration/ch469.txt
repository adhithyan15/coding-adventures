Urdu, chapter 469: Hiccups and Colds.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ڈکار (ḍakār) — a burp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to scratch an itch, then the Urdu for a rash.

You'll want to know: ڈکار.
ڈکار — ḍakār — "a burp".
کھانے کے بعد اسے ڈکار آئی۔ khāne ke bād use ḍakār āī. He burped after the meal.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ڈکار (ḍakār) is inherited from ḍakkāra, "belch", a form CDIAL reconstructs and treats as an imitation of the sound (CDIAL 5521).

Lesson 3 of 5.
کھنکارنا (khaṅkārnā) — to clear one's throat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a burp, then the Urdu for a hiccup.

You'll want to know: کھنکارنا.
کھنکارنا — khaṅkārnā — "to clear one's throat".
بولنے سے پہلے دادا نے کھنکارا۔ bolne se pehle dādā ne khaṅkārā. My grandfather cleared his throat before speaking.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کھنکارا, us ne khaṅkārā.

The word, taken apart.
کھنکارنا (khaṅkārnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin khãkārnā under khaṅkh, "cough, hawk", a form it reconstructs and treats as an imitation of the sound (CDIAL 3763).

Lesson 4 of 5.
بلغم (balgham) — phlegm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a hiccup, then the Urdu for to clear one's throat.

You'll want to know: بلغم.
بلغم — balgham — "phlegm".
کھانسی کے ساتھ بلغم آتا ہے۔ khāṅsī ke sāth balgham ātā hai. Phlegm comes up with the cough.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
بلغم (balgham) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic balgham, "phlegm", which Arabic probably took from Greek phlegma.

Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
نزلہ (nazlā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic nazla, "a flowing down", and so a running cold.
