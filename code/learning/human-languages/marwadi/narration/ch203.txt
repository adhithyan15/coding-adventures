Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 203: How Far Is It?.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
आंतरो (ā̃tro) — a distance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for crosswise, then the Marwadi for slanting.

You'll want to know: आंतरो.
आंतरो — ā̃tro — "a distance".
अठै सूं टेसण तांई घणो आंतरो है। aṭhai sū̃ ṭesaṇ tā̃ī ghaṇo ā̃tro hai. It's a long way from here to the station.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आंतरो (ā̃tro) is inherited from Sanskrit antara, "interior; an interval". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Gujarati ā̃tro, "distance, interval" (CDIAL 357).

Lesson 2 of 6.
डग (ḍag) — a step; a pace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for slanting, then the Marwadi for a distance.

You'll want to know: डग.
डग — ḍag — "a step; a pace".
मंदिर दस डग आगै है। mandir das ḍag āgai hai. The temple is ten paces ahead.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
डग (ḍag) is inherited from a reconstructed ḍag, "step, pace"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari ḍaga, "step, stride" (CDIAL 5523).

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a pace, then the Marwadi for a direction.

You'll want to know: भटकणो.
भटकणो — bhaṭakṇo — "to lose one's way; to wander".
हूं सहर मांय भटकूं हूं। hū̃ sahar mā̃y bhaṭkū̃ hū̃. I keep getting lost in the city.
Past (I …): हूं भटक्यो, hū̃ bhaṭakyo.

The word, taken apart.
भटकणो (bhaṭakṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit bhraṣṭa, "fallen". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi bhaṭaknā, "to wander" (CDIAL 9655).

Lesson 5 of 6.
दीसणो (dīsṇo) — to be visible; to appear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a direction, then the Marwadi for to lose one's way.

You'll want to know: दीसणो.
दीसणो — dīsṇo — "to be visible; to appear".
अठै सूं मंदिर दीसै है। aṭhai sū̃ mandir dīsai hai. The temple can be seen from here.
Past (it …): वो मंदिर दीस्यो, vo mandir dīsyo.

The word, taken apart.
दीसणो (dīsṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit dṛśyate, "is seen"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari verb dīsaï, "is seen" (CDIAL 6516).
