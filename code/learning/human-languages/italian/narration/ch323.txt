Italian, chapter 323: C'era una volta.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
c'era una volta — once upon a time (there was).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the lantern, then the Italian for to dance.

You'll want to know: c'era una volta.
c'era una volta — "once upon a time (there was)".
C'era una volta una ragazza che viveva nel bosco. Once upon a time there was a girl who lived in the woods.
The opening of almost every fairy tale; c'era is the imperfetto of c'è.

The word, taken apart.
volta "a time, a turn" is built on voltare, usually traced to Vulgar Latin volvitāre, from Latin volvere "to roll, to turn" (Lewis & Short).

Il nonno ci leggeva un racconto ogni sera. Grandfather used to read us a story every evening.
Built on raccontare, ‘to tell’.

racconto is built on raccontare "to tell", from ri-, a- and contare "to count, to tell", from Latin computāre (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la favola — the fable, the tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for once upon a time, then the Italian for the story.

You'll want to know: la favola.
la favola — "the fable, the tale".
Nella favola la volpe parla con il lupo. In the fable the fox talks to the wolf.
Una vita da favola — a fairy-tale life.

The word, taken apart.
favola is inherited from Latin fābula "a story, a tale" (EtymDB), from fārī "to speak".

Il libro narra la storia di un ragazzo. The book tells the story of a boy.
A literary raccontare.

narrare is a learned borrowing from Latin nārrāre "to tell, to relate" (Lewis & Short), built on gnārus "knowing".
