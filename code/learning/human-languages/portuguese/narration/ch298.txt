Portuguese, chapter 298: Depois do veredicto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o veredicto — the verdict.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sentence, then the Portuguese for to acquit.

You'll want to know: o veredicto.
o veredicto — "the verdict".
O veredicto saiu ao fim da tarde. The verdict came out late in the afternoon.
The decision of a court on the facts.

The word, taken apart.
veredicto goes back to Latin vērē dictum "truly said", from vērus "true" and dīcere "to say" (Lewis & Short), probably through English verdict.

You'll want to know: recorrer.
recorrer — "to appeal (in court); to turn to (for help)".
O advogado vai recorrer da decisão. The lawyer is going to appeal against the decision.
Recorrer a alguém — to turn to someone for help.

The word, taken apart.
recorrer comes from Latin recurrere "to run back, to have recourse" (EtymDB), built on currere "to run".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o recluso — the inmate, the prisoner (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to appeal, then the Portuguese for the appeal.

You'll want to know: o recluso.
o recluso — "the inmate, the prisoner (formal)".
O recluso trabalha na biblioteca da cadeia. The inmate works in the prison library.
Formal and official; in speech, o preso.

The word, taken apart.
recluso is a learned borrowing from Late Latin reclūsus "shut up, enclosed", from reclūdere, built on claudere "to shut" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
libertar — to set free, to release.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: libertar.
libertar — "to set free, to release".
Libertaram o recluso ao fim de dez anos. They released the inmate after ten years.
Libertar um refém — to free a hostage.

The word, taken apart.
libertar was borrowed from Medieval Latin lībertāre (EtymDB), built on lībertās "freedom", from līber "free".
