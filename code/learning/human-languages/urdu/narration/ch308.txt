Urdu, chapter 308: Kings and Emperors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شہنشاہ (shahanshāh) — an emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a slap, then the Urdu for to twist.

You'll want to know: شہنشاہ.
شہنشاہ — shahanshāh — "an emperor".
شہنشاہ ایک بڑے محل میں رہتا تھا۔ shahanshāh ek baṛe mahal meṅ rahtā thā. The emperor lived in a great palace.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
شہنشاہ (shahanshāh) is usually traced to Persian shāhanshāh, "king of kings", built from shāh, a king, the word inside بادشاہ.

تخت — takht — "a throne".

Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
تخت (takht) is usually traced to Persian takht, "a throne, a raised wooden seat". تخت پر بیٹھنا is to come to the throne.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

دربار — darbār — "a royal court".
دربار میں سب خاموش تھے۔ darbār meṅ sab khāmosh the. Everyone at court was silent.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
دربار (darbār) is usually traced to Persian darbār, "a royal court": the hall where the king sat and heard his people.

Lesson 5 of 5.
وارث (vāris) — an heir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a crown, then the Urdu for a royal court.

You'll want to know: وارث.
وارث — vāris — "an heir".
بادشاہ کا کوئی وارث نہیں تھا۔ bādshāh kā koī vāris nahīṅ thā. The king had no heir.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
وارث (vāris) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic wārith, "one who inherits". Urdu says its last letter as s.
