Portuguese, chapter 290: Armas e ameaças.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a arma — the weapon, the gun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the con man, then the Portuguese for the accomplice.

You'll want to know: a arma.
a arma — "the weapon, the gun".
A polícia encontrou a arma no rio. The police found the weapon in the river.
Uma arma de fogo — a firearm.

The word, taken apart.
arma is inherited from Latin arma "tools, arms" (EtymDB), a plural that Portuguese came to treat as a singular.

a pistola — "the pistol, the handgun".
O assaltante tinha uma pistola. The robber had a pistol.
Also a spray gun: uma pistola de pintura.

The word, taken apart.
pistola was borrowed through German Pistole (EtymDB), from Czech píšťala "a pipe, a whistle": the long thin barrel looked like a pipe.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ameaçar — to threaten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the weapon, then the Portuguese for the pistol.

You'll want to know: ameaçar.
ameaçar — "to threaten".
O homem ameaçou o empregado com uma faca. The man threatened the employee with a knife.
Ameaçar de morte — to threaten to kill.

The word, taken apart.
ameaçar comes down from Old Portuguese amẽaçar (EtymDB), built on Vulgar Latin minācia "a threat", from Latin mināx "threatening".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o assassino — the murderer, the killer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to threaten, then the Portuguese for the threat.

You'll want to know: o assassino.
o assassino — "the murderer, the killer".
Nos filmes, o assassino acaba por voltar ao mesmo lugar. In films, the killer ends up going back to the same place.
A woman is a assassina.

The word, taken apart.
assassino came through Italian assassino, which is usually traced to Arabic ḥaššāšīn, the name of a medieval sect; EtymDB has the Portuguese word with no source.
