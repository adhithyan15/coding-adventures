# sī pluit — a condition that may well be true

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C289-SI-01, LA-GRAMMAR-WEATHER-VERBS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if it rains, I don't go walking". (*Sī pluit, nōn ambulō.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That is a **real** condition: it may well rain, and if it does,
the second half follows. Latin keeps both halves plain.

## Grammar Lens: ordinary verbs on both sides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C289-SI-REAL-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C108-VESTIS-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-LEX-C107-URBS-03] -->

> **Sī pluit, in vīllā manēmus.** — If it rains, we stay in the farmhouse.

[PAUSE 2s] Each half has the tense the facts need. A real condition about
the past takes the perfect, the way any past event does:

> **Sī herī in forō fuistī, amīcum vīdistī.** — If you were in the forum
> yesterday, you saw my friend.

[PAUSE 2s] The speaker does not know whether you were there. If you were,
the seeing follows. Nothing in the verbs is pretending.

## Why it's said this way: advice with sī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C198-ACT198-05, LA-GRAMMAR-C284-DEBERE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The second half can be a command, or the *dēbēs* of advice:

> **Sī fessus es, quiēsce.** — If you are tired, rest.
> **Sī aeger es, quiēscere dēbēs.** — If you are ill, you should rest.

[PAUSE 1s] *Quiēsce* is the command of *quiēscō*: the stem with *-e*, like
*ignōsce* in *ignōsce mihi*.
