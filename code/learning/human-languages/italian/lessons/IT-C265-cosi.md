# quindi, così — the arrow that points forward

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PERCHE-02, IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give an opinion with a reason: "If you ask me, it's going to
rain, because there's no sun." (*Secondo me piove, perché non c'è il sole.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Perché* runs from the claim back to its reason. This lesson runs
the other way.

## Grammar Lens: the two arrows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C155-PERCHE155-01, IT-LEX-C178-VERBI178-01] -->

The same two facts, in opposite orders:

> Ho preso l'autobus **perché** pioveva. — I took the bus **because** it was
> raining.
> Pioveva, **quindi** ho preso l'autobus. — It was raining, **so** I took the
> bus.

**Perché** points **back**, from what happened to its cause. **Quindi** points
**forward**, from the cause to what followed. Which one you reach for depends
only on which fact you want to say first, and that is a choice about emphasis,
not about grammar.

Notice the tenses keep their jobs. *Pioveva* is the scene, the reason; *ho
preso* is the event, the consequence.

## Grammar Lens: così, the spoken so
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-COSI-QUINDI-02]; assesses=[IT-ETYMON-COSI-COSI-03] -->

**così** can stand in the same place: *Pioveva, **così** ho preso l'autobus.*
It is lighter and more conversational than *quindi*, which suits a letter or a
report as well as speech. Both are right; *così* is what you will hear most at
a table, *quindi* what you will read most.

You have been saying this word since chapter two. **Così così**, "so-so", is
*così* twice: "this way, that way".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**così** comes from Latin *eccum sīc*, "look, thus" (EtymDB 2.1 lists it as a
compound of the two). The *sīc*, "thus, in this way", is the one English
borrowed whole as *sic*, the mark an editor writes after a quoted mistake:
"thus it stood". **Quindi** has no row in EtymDB 2.1. The usual account takes it back to
*eccum inde*, "look, from there", and Latin *inde* did mean "from that place"
(Lewis & Short): from there, and so, therefore.

*sīc* mean? (**Thus**, in this way.)
