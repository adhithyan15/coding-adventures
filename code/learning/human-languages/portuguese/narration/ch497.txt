Portuguese, chapter 497: Brigas e reconciliações.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
brigar — to quarrel, to fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to stroke, then the Portuguese for to spoil.

You'll want to know: brigar.
brigar — "to quarrel, to fight".
Os irmãos brigam por tudo. The brothers fight over everything.
Very common in Brazil; in Portugal zangar-se and discutir are frequent too.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB traces brigar to Gothic brikan "to break"; other accounts suggest other sources, so the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
separar — to separate; to split up (separar-se).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to spoil, then the Portuguese for to quarrel.

You'll want to know: separar.
separar — "to separate; to split up (separar-se)".
Os pais dele separaram-se no ano passado. His parents split up last year.
Separar o lixo — to sort the rubbish.

The word, taken apart.
separar is a learned borrowing from Latin sēparāre "to separate" (EtymDB), built on sē- "apart" and parāre "to prepare".

You'll want to know: trair.
trair — "to betray; to cheat on".
Ele traiu a confiança dos amigos. He betrayed his friends' trust.
Present: traio, trais, trai.

The word, taken apart.
trair is inherited, through Old Portuguese traer, from Latin trādere "to hand over, to betray" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Para ela, a mentira foi uma traição. For her, the lie was a betrayal.
Built like trair on Latin trādere.

The word, taken apart.
traição is inherited, through Old Portuguese traiçon, from Latin trāditiōnem "a handing over" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
reconciliar — to reconcile; to make up (reconciliar-se).

Warm-up.

Depois de anos sem se falarem, os dois irmãos reconciliaram-se. After years of not speaking, the two brothers made up.

The word, taken apart.
