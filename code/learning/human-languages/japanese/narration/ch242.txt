Japanese, chapter 242: Partners and Best Friends.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かれ (kare) — he; a boyfriend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to sign up, then the Japanese for to do.

You'll want to know: かれ.
かれ — kare — "he; a boyfriend".
かれは がくせいですか。 — kare wa gakusei desu ka — "Is he a student?"

The word, taken apart.
かれ (kare) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
かのじょ (kanojo) — she; a girlfriend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to do, then the Japanese for he.

You'll want to know: かのじょ.
かのじょ — kanojo — "she; a girlfriend".
かのじょは いま きょうしつに いません。 — kanojo wa ima kyōshitsu ni imasen — "She is not in the classroom now."

The word, taken apart.
かのじょ (kanojo) is a mixed word: native kano, "that", joined to Sino-Japanese jo, "woman" (Middle Chinese nrjoX), borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
こいびと (koibito) — a sweetheart; a lover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for he, then the Japanese for she.

You'll want to know: こいびと.
こいびと — koibito — "a sweetheart; a lover".
あにには こいびとが いません。 — ani ni wa koibito ga imasen — "My elder brother does not have a sweetheart."

The word, taken apart.
こいびと (koibito) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from koi, "love", and hito, "person", with h voiced to b (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
しんゆう (shin'yū) — a close friend; a best friend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for she, then the Japanese for a sweetheart.

You'll want to know: しんゆう.
しんゆう — shin'yū — "a close friend; a best friend".
かれは あにの しんゆうですか。 — kare wa ani no shin'yū desu ka — "Is he my elder brother's best friend?"

The word, taken apart.
しんゆう (shin'yū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shin, "intimate" (Middle Chinese tshin) and yū, "friend" (hjuwX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
つきあう (tsukiau) — to go out with; to keep company with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sweetheart, then the Japanese for a close friend.

You'll want to know: つきあう.
つきあう — tsukiau — "to go out with; to keep company with".
あねは かれと つきあいますか。 — ane wa kare to tsukiaimasu ka — "Will my elder sister go out with him?"
Polite つきあいます, tsukiaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
つきあう (tsukiau) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from tsuki, from tsuku, "to stick to", and au, "to do together" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
