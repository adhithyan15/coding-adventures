## You'll want to know: 面前
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C175-FURN175-03]; assesses=[] -->

**面前** — *miànqián* — "in front of someone; in someone's presence".

> **我面前有一杯茶** *wǒ miànqián yǒu yì bēi chá* — There's a cup of tea in front of me.

The new character, in pieces: 面 — nine strokes: a horizontal on top, a short stroke falling to the left, then a frame (a vertical, a horizontal that turns down), two verticals inside it, two short bars between them, and the bottom bar last. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**面前** (*miànqián*) is built from 面 'face' and 前 'before', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *mjienH* and *dzen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to face' and 'the front': before one's face.
