# estás / está — the two forms, and what they are for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ESTAR, ES-ETYMON-STARE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Estar* is "to stand." Now put it to work. Two forms, one job:
saying how somebody is **right now**.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [estar](./ES-C04-estar.md) — the verb and its Latin "to stand" root.
- [tú *or* usted](./ES-C03-tu-usted-register.md) — why *usted* takes he/she

## Grammar Lens: state and location use estar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-STATE-LOCATION-ESTAR]; assesses=[] -->

Use **estar** for a **current state** or a **location** — the two things
that can change without changing what something *is*:

- a state: *estoy bien* — "I'm well" (today; maybe not tomorrow).
- a location: *está aquí* — "it's here" (for now).

"How are you?" asks about a current state, so it takes *estar*. Spanish has
a second be-verb for identity, and it arrives in its own later lesson;
keeping it out of today's drill lets one contrast settle at a time.

## Grammar Lens: the two forms you need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-ESTA-ESTAS]; assesses=[] -->

| you're addressing | form | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **tú** (informal) | **estás** | "you are" |
| **usted** (formal) | **está** | "you are" |

Notice that **está** is also "he is / she is." That is not a coincidence and
not something extra to learn — it is the *usted*-takes-he/she-forms rule you
already met, showing up for the first time in a real verb.
