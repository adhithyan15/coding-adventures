Lesson 1 of 6.
ふくし (fukushi) — welfare; social services.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to give care, then the Japanese for bringing up children.

You'll want to know: ふくし.
ふくし — fukushi — "welfare; social services".
こうれいしゃが ふえたので、ふくしが ひつようです。 — kōreisha ga fueta node, fukushi ga hitsuyō desu — "The elderly have grown in number, so welfare is needed."

The word, taken apart.
ふくし (fukushi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are fuku, "blessing" and shi, "welfare" (Middle Chinese trhiX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 6.
きふする (kifu suru) — to donate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for bringing up children, then the Japanese for welfare.

You'll want to know: きふする.
きふする — kifu suru — "to donate".
たいふうで みんなが こまったので、おかねを きふしました。 — taifū de minna ga komatta node, okane o kifu shimashita — "The typhoon left everyone in trouble, so I gave money."
Polite きふします, kifushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ちちが おかねを きふしました。, chichi ga okane o kifu shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
きふする (kifu suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, kifu, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: ki, "draw near" (Middle Chinese kjeH) and fu, "attach" (pjuH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
あつかう (atsukau) — to handle; to treat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fund-raising, then the Japanese for looking after.

You'll want to know: あつかう.
あつかう — atsukau — "to handle; to treat".
この おさらは たかいから、こどもは あつかわないで ください。 — kono osara wa takai kara, kodomo wa atsukawanaide kudasai — "This plate is expensive, so children, please do not handle it."
Polite あつかいます, atsukaimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): てんいんが にもつを あつかいました。, ten'in ga nimotsu o atsukaimashita.

The word, taken apart.
あつかう (atsukau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
