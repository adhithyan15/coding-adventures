# el almacén — where the boxes wait

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06, ES-LEX-C366-REF-31] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la tienda** and **la caja**. The shop sells them; somewhere
else keeps them.

## You'll want to know: el almacén
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06] -->

**el almacén** — the warehouse, and in a shop the stockroom out the back.

*Está en el almacén* — it's in the stockroom. *No queda en almacén* — there's
none left in stock. *Los almacenes* — the plural, and note the accent
disappears: *almacén* needs it, *almacenes* does not.

**Almacenar** is the verb, to store. A computer stores the same way: *datos
almacenados*.

## Grammar Lens: the accent that comes and goes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01, ES-LEX-C366-REF-31] -->

The stress never moves. The mark does:

| singular | plural |
|---|---|
| el almac**é**n | los almac**e**nes |
| el exam**e**n → | los ex**á**menes |

*Almacén* ends in **n** with the stress on the last syllable, which is not the
default, so it needs a mark. Add *-es* and the stress is now on the
second-to-last syllable, which **is** the default — so the mark comes off.

*Examen* runs the opposite way for the same reason, and having the two side by
side is the clearest way to see that the accent is a correction to a rule, not
a property of the word.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01] -->

Arabic **al-makhzan**, the storehouse — from *khazana*, to store up.

The **al-** is the Arabic definite article, and Spanish swallowed it whole.
That happened to hundreds of words during the centuries of Arabic rule, and
once you know the sign you can spot them: *almohada*, *alcalde*, *alfombra*,
*algodón*, *azúcar*, *aceite*. Every *al-* at the front of a Spanish word is
worth a second look.

So *el almacén* is, strictly, "the the-storehouse". Spanish was not troubled
by this and neither is English, which took *magazine* from the same Arabic
word by way of French — a store of articles, and then a store of ammunition.
