## You'll want to know: 书店
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C190-TOWN190-01]; assesses=[] -->

**书店** — *shūdiàn* — "bookshop".

> **这儿的书店很大** *zhèr de shūdiàn hěn dà* — The bookshops here are big.

The new character, in pieces: 店 — eight strokes: a roof of a dot, a bar and a long stroke falling to the left, and under it a vertical with a short stroke to its right, over 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**书店** (*shūdiàn*) is built from 书 'book' and 店 'shop', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *syo* and *temH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the second, after an older book, as a place where goods are set out for sale.
