Latin, chapter 504: Honesty and pretence.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
probitās, probitātis — honesty, uprightness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for charming, then the Latin for playful.

You'll want to know: probitās, probitātis.
probitās, probitātis — "honesty, uprightness".
Omnēs probitātem eius laudant. Everyone praises his honesty.
Feminine, third declension, from probus — upright. English probity.

The word, taken apart.
probitās is built on probus "good, upright" (Lewis & Short), which EtymDB traces to a Proto-Indo-European compound meaning "being in front, excellent". English probity comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 6.
sincērus, sincēra, sincērum — genuine, sincere.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for playful, then the Latin for honesty.

You'll want to know: sincērus, sincēra, sincērum.
sincērus, sincēra, sincērum — "genuine, sincere".
Amīcitia nostra sincēra est. Our friendship is genuine.
Of things, pure and unmixed; of people, honest. English sincere.

The word, taken apart.
sincērus is a native Latin adjective whose origin is not settled; EtymDB and Lewis & Short both link its first part with sim-, as in simplex. English sincere comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
mendāx, mendācis — lying; a liar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for honesty, then the Latin for genuine.

You'll want to know: mendāx, mendācis.
mendāx, mendācis — "lying; a liar".
Nēmō mendācī crēdit. No one believes a liar.
One ending: mendāx for all genders. As a noun, a liar. Mendācium, already known, is the lie.

The word, taken apart.
mendāx belongs with mentior "to lie" and mendācium "a lie" (Lewis & Short). English mendacious comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
simulō, simulāre — to pretend, imitate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for genuine, then the Latin for lying.

You'll want to know: simulō, simulāre.
simulō, simulāre — "to pretend, imitate".
Servus morbum simulat, quod labōrāre nōn vult. The slave pretends to be ill, because he does not want to work.
simulō, simulāre, simulāvī, simulātum: to pretend that something is so. English simulate.

The word, taken apart.
simulō is built on similis "like" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): to make a thing look like another. English simulate comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dissimulō, dissimulāre — to hide, disguise (a feeling).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for lying, then the Latin for to pretend.

You'll want to know: dissimulō, dissimulāre.
dissimulō, dissimulāre — "to hide, disguise (a feeling)".
Puella dolōrem suum dissimulat et rīdet. The girl hides her pain and smiles.
dissimulō, dissimulāre, dissimulāvī, dissimulātum: to pretend that something is not so. English dissimulate.

The word, taken apart.
dissimulō is built on dissimilis "unlike" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English dissimulate comes from it, and dissemble is a reshaped form.
