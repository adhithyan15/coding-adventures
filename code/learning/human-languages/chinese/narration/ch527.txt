Mandarin Chinese, chapter 527: Ordering Online.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
xiàdān — to place an order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for in large amounts, then the Mandarin for a small amount of.

You'll want to know: xiàdān.
xiàdān — "to place an order".
wǒ xiàdān yǐjīng sān tiān le dōngxi hái méi dào — I placed the order three days ago, and it still hasn't arrived.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàdān is built from 下 'to put down' and dān 'a slip of paper', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX and tan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go down' and the second 'single'; the sense 'an order slip' is modern.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bāozhuāng — to wrap goods for sale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: bāozhuāng.
bāozhuāng — "to wrap goods for sale".
zhè jiā shāngdiàn de dōngxi bāozhuāng de zuì hǎokàn — This shop packages its things the most beautifully.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāozhuāng is built from 包 'to wrap' and zhuāng 'to pack', both inherited from Middle Chinese, paew and tsrjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wrap up' and the second 'to bind up, to dress'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: huìyuán.
huìyuán — "a member of a club or a shop's scheme".
wǒ dāng le sān nián huìyuán — I've been a member for three years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huìyuán is built from 会 'to meet' and yuán, both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwajH and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to come together' and the second as the number of things; the sense 'a member' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
格子 (gézi) — a checked pattern; squares.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a member of a club, then the Mandarin for a favourable deal.

You'll want to know: 格子.
格子 — gézi — "a checked pattern; squares".
wǒ zuì xǐhuan zhè tiáo gézi qúnzi — This checked skirt is my favourite.

The word, taken apart.
格子 (gézi) is built from 格 'a pattern' and 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaek and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is said light, as at the end of many names of things.
