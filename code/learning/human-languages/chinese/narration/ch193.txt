Mandarin Chinese, chapter 193: Prices Up and Down.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tèjià — special offer; reduced price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to queue, then the Mandarin for to be open to the public.

You'll want to know: tèjià.
tèjià — "special offer; reduced price".
jīntiān shuǐguǒ tèjià — Fruit is on special offer today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tèjià is built from tè 'special' and jià 'price', the jià of jiàqián, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dok and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a bull' and 'alone'.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: tèjià]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tèjià, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jīntiān shuǐguǒ tèjià — Fruit is on special offer today.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to queue, then the Mandarin for to be open to the public, then say tèjià again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does tèjià mean? ("Special offer; reduced price".) Say it once more.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: zhǎngjià.
zhǎngjià — "to get dearer; to go up in price".
niúnǎi zhǎngjià le — Milk has gone up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǎngjià is built from zhǎng 'to rise' and jià 'price'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as water in flood, with a level and a departing tone only, so the rising tone of zhǎng is not the regular outcome, and its history is not settled.

zhìliàng — "quality".
zhège hěn piányi zhìliàng yě hěn hǎo — It's cheap, and the quality is good too.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhìliàng is built from zhì 'substance' and liàng 'a measure', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyit and ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'plain' and 'true', and the second as measuring grain.
