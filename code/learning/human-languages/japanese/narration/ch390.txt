Japanese, chapter 390: On the Expressway.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こうそくどうろ (kōsokudōro) — an expressway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a footbridge (over a road), then the Japanese for to get through.

You'll want to know: こうそくどうろ.
こうそくどうろ — kōsokudōro — "an expressway".
こうそくどうろで まちに いきました。 — kōsokudōro de machi ni ikimashita — "We went into town on the expressway."

The word, taken apart.
こうそくどうろ (kōsokudōro) is Sino-Japanese (kango): four Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "high" (Middle Chinese kaw), soku, "fast" (suwk), dō, "street" (dawX) and ro, "road" (luH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
おいこす (oikosu) — to overtake; to pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for speed, then the Japanese for a licence.

You'll want to know: おいこす.
おいこす — oikosu — "to overtake; to pass".
こうそくどうろで くるまを おいこしました。 — kōsokudōro de kuruma o oikoshimashita — "I overtook a car on the expressway."
Polite おいこします, oikoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): くるまが じてんしゃを おいこしました。, kuruma ga jitensha o oikoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おいこす (oikosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from oi, "chasing" (from ou) and kosu, "to cross over"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
