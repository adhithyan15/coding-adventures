6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
decollare — to take off (of a plane).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the search, then the Italian for the pass.

You'll want to know: decollare.
decollare — "to take off (of a plane)".
L'aereo decolla tra cinque minuti. The plane takes off in five minutes.
Il decollo is take-off; figuratively, il progetto è decollato — the project took off.

The word, taken apart.
decollare was borrowed from French décoller 'to come unstuck', built on colle 'glue', from Greek kólla: the wheels come unstuck from the ground.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to take off, then the Italian for to land.

You'll want to know: la turbolenza.
la turbolenza — "the turbulence".
Durante il volo c'è stata un po' di turbolenza. During the flight there was a bit of turbulence.
Turbolento also describes a restless person.

The word, taken apart.
turbolenza is a learned borrowing from Latin turbulentia, from turbulentus 'restless', built on turba 'commotion, crowd' (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: allacciare.
allacciare — "to fasten, to buckle".
Allacciate le cinture, per favore. Fasten your seatbelts, please.
Slacciare — to unfasten; allacciare le scarpe — to tie your shoes.

The word, taken apart.
allacciare is built from a- and laccio 'lace, noose', which is inherited from Latin laqueus 'noose, snare' (Lewis & Short).
