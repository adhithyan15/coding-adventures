Latin, chapter 221: Rule, Difference, Outcome, Fortune, Accident.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rēgula, rēgulae — a rule, a ruler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a young person, then the Latin for a widow.

You'll want to know: rēgula, rēgulae.
rēgula, rēgulae — "a rule, a ruler".

The word, taken apart.
rēgula is built on regō, "to keep straight, to rule" (Lewis & Short), with -ula, an ending for a tool: a straight stick, a ruler. English rule comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: discrīmen, discrīminis.
discrīmen, discrīminis — "a difference; a crisis".

The word, taken apart.
discrīmen is built on crī-, the root of discernō, "to separate, to distinguish" (Lewis & Short), also seen in crīmen, "a charge", with -men, an ending for a result: a dividing line, then a turning point.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fortūna, fortūnae — fortune, luck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a difference; a crisis, then the Latin for an outcome.

You'll want to know: fortūna, fortūnae.
fortūna, fortūnae — "fortune, luck".

The word, taken apart.
fortūna is built on fors, "chance, luck" (Lewis & Short), with -tūna, an ending also in Neptūnus, "Neptune". fors is usually traced to the root of ferō, "to bring": what chance brings.
