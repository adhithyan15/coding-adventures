Bengali, chapter 482: More Relatives.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
জেঠা (jeṭhā) — a father's elder brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an elder brother's wife, then the Bengali for a paternal grandmother.

You'll want to know: জেঠা.
জেঠা — jeṭhā — "a father's elder brother".
জেঠা বাবার চেয়ে পাঁচ বছরের বড়। jeṭhā bābār cheye pā̃ch bôchhôrer bôṛo. My father's elder brother is five years older than my father.
কাকা (taught) is the father's younger brother; জেঠা (jeṭhā) the elder.

The word, taken apart.
জেঠা (jeṭhā) is listed by CDIAL for Bengali under Sanskrit jyēṣṭhatāta, "eldest father" (CDIAL 5289).

ভাইঝির জন্মদিনে আমি একটা পুতুল কিনলাম। bhāijhir jônmôdine āmi ekṭā putul kinlām. I bought a doll for my niece's birthday.

The word, taken apart.
ভাইঝি (bhāijhi) puts ভাই (taught), brother, with jhi, "daughter", which CDIAL lists for Bengali under Sanskrit duhitṛ, "daughter" (CDIAL 6481).

Lesson 5 of 5.
বেয়াই (beyāi) — a child's father-in-law; a co-father-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a sister's son, then the Bengali for a brother's daughter.

You'll want to know: বেয়াই.
বেয়াই — beyāi — "a child's father-in-law; a co-father-in-law".
বিয়ের পরে দুই বেয়াই বন্ধু হলেন। biyer pôre dui beyāi bôndhu hôlen. After the wedding the two fathers-in-law became friends.

The word, taken apart.
বেয়াই (beyāi) is listed by CDIAL for Bengali, as a son's or daughter's father-in-law, under Sanskrit vivāhin (CDIAL 11925), from the same root as বিয়ে (taught), a wedding.
