## You'll want to know: áoyè
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C169-EARLY169-04]; assesses=[] -->

*áoyè* — "to stay up late; to stay up all night".

> *wǒ zuótiān áoyè le jīntiān hěn lèi* — I stayed up late last night, and I'm tired today.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*áoyè* is built from *áo* 'to simmer' and *yè* 'night', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ngaw* and *yaeH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to simmer'. How the pair came to mean staying up is not settled.
