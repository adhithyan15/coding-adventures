## You'll want to know: jiāxīn
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C491-BOSS491-01]; assesses=[] -->

*jiāxīn* — "to get a pay rise; to raise someone's pay".

> *wǒ gōngzuò le liǎng nián hái méiyǒu jiāxīn* — I've worked for two years and still haven't had a pay rise.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jiāxīn* is built from *jiā* 'to add' and *xīn* 'firewood', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kae* and *sin* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to increase' and the second 'firewood'. Firewood standing for pay is the usual explanation, but the dictionary does not give that sense.
