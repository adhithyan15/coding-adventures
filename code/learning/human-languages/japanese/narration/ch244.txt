Japanese, chapter 244: Meeting and Parting.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しりあい (shiriai) — an acquaintance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for siblings, then the Japanese for to bring up.

You'll want to know: しりあい.
しりあい — shiriai — "an acquaintance".
かれは ちちの しりあいですか。 — kare wa chichi no shiriai desu ka — "Is he an acquaintance of my father's?"

The word, taken apart.
しりあい (shiriai) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from shiri, from shiru, "to know", and ai, "mutual", from au (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

わかれる (wakareru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
けっこん (kekkon) — marriage; a wedding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to part, then the Japanese for to welcome.

You'll want to know: けっこん.
けっこん — kekkon — "marriage; a wedding".
あには らいねん けっこんしますか。 — ani wa rainen kekkon shimasu ka — "Is my elder brother getting married next year?"

The word, taken apart.
けっこん (kekkon) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ketsu, "tie" (Middle Chinese ket) and kon, "marriage" (xwon) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
