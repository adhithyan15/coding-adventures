Lesson 1 of 5.
مزیدار (mazedār) — tasty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for quiet, then the Urdu for splendid.

You'll want to know: مزیدار.
مزیدار — mazedār — "tasty".

The word, taken apart.
مزیدار (mazedār) is "holding taste": maza, "taste, relish", usually traced to Persian maza, with the Persian -dār, "holder".

Lesson 3 of 5.
انوکھا (anokhā) — unusual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for tasty, then the Urdu for useless.

You'll want to know: انوکھا.
انوکھا — anokhā — "unusual".

The word, taken apart.
انوکھا (anokhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from anapekṣa, "careless" (CDIAL 289). It is built on apekṣā, "consideration, expectation": what is strange is what nobody expected.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نقلی (naqlī) — fake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for useless, then the Urdu for unusual.

You'll want to know: نقلی.
نقلی — naqlī — "fake".

The word, taken apart.
نقلی (naqlī) is naql, "a copy", with the ending -ī, "of". naql is Arabic, "a moving across; a copying", and reached Urdu through Persian (Lane, root n-q-l).
