Japanese, chapter 224: Students and Classrooms.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
がくせい (gakusei) — a student; a university student.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for open for business, then the Japanese for not yet open.

You'll want to know: がくせい.
がくせい — gakusei — "a student; a university student".
がくせいは まいにち がっこうに いきます。 — gakusei wa mainichi gakkō ni ikimasu — "The students go to school every day."

The word, taken apart.
がくせい (gakusei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gaku, "learning" (Middle Chinese haewk) and sei, "life, birth" (srjaeng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おしえる (oshieru) — to teach; to tell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a classroom, then the Japanese for a class.

You'll want to know: おしえる.
おしえる — oshieru — "to teach; to tell".
せんせいは にほんごを おしえます。 — sensei wa nihongo o oshiemasu — "The teacher teaches Japanese."
Polite おしえます, oshiemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
おしえる (oshieru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
