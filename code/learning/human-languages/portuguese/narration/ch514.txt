Portuguese, chapter 514: Ringues e espadas.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
o boxe — boxing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to compete, then the Portuguese for stopwatch.

You'll want to know: o boxe.
o boxe — "boxing".
Ele treina boxe no ginásio. He trains in boxing at the gym.
A boxer is um pugilista.

The word, taken apart.
boxe is usually traced, through French boxe, to English box "to hit with the fist".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 3 of 6.
a espada — sword.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for boxing, then the Portuguese for (boxing) ring.

You'll want to know: a espada.
a espada — "sword".
No museu há uma espada muito antiga. In the museum there is a very old sword.
Espadas is also a suit in a pack of cards: spades.

The word, taken apart.
espada is inherited, through Old Portuguese espada (EtymDB), from Latin spatha "a broad two-edged sword" (Lewis & Short), which was taken from Greek spáthē "a broad blade".

Lesson 5 of 6.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for sword, then the Portuguese for judo.

You'll want to know: a esgrima.
a esgrima — "fencing".
Na esgrima, os atletas usam uma máscara. In fencing, the athletes wear a mask.
The sport with swords.

The word, taken apart.
esgrima comes from Old Italian scrima (EtymDB), which is usually traced to a Germanic verb meaning "to protect".
