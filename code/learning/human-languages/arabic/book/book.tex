\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This is the Arabic volume of the same project as the Spanish, French, and
German ones --- and it changes the etymology game entirely. The Romance and
Germanic books trace words back to Latin or shared Germanic roots. Arabic
doesn't need tracing: its roots are on the \emph{surface}. Nearly every Arabic
word is built from a \textbf{three-consonant root} carrying a core meaning,
poured into fixed patterns --- so \emph{peace}, \emph{Islam}, \emph{Muslim},
and the greeting \emph{salaam} are visibly one family (s-l-m), the way this
whole series wishes Latin were.

The reader already reads Arabic but is rusty, so the script is reintroduced
\emph{inside real words}, right-to-left, never as an up-front alphabet drill.
And because the reader knows Spanish, Arabic's long shadow over Spanish is a
recurring thread: the article \emph{al-} smuggled into \emph{algebra} and
\emph{alcohol}; the sun-letter assimilation you can still hear in
\emph{az\'{u}car} (\emph{as-sukkar}).

Same three commitments as the other volumes: one word per lesson, gone deep;
the widest honest web of connections; the cultural or grammatical reason, not
just the rule. Released under CC~BY-SA~4.0.
