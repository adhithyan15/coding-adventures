Sanskrit, chapter 505: Clay and Fire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मृद् (mṛd) — clay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lives by, then the Sanskrit for skilled.

You'll want to know: मृद्.
मृद् — mṛd — "clay".
कुम्भकारः नद्याः तीरात् मृदम् आनयति। kumbhakāraḥ nadyāḥ tīrāt mṛdam ānayati. The potter brings clay from the riverbank.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
मृद् (mṛd) is the word MW records for earth, soil and clay, set beside the root √mṛd, "to press, to crush"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for skilled, then the Sanskrit for clay.

You'll want to know: मृन्मयः.
मृन्मयः — mṛnmayaḥ — "earthen".
कुम्भकारः मृन्मयानि पात्राणि रचयति। kumbhakāraḥ mṛnmayāni pātrāṇi racayati. The potter makes earthen pots.

The word, taken apart.
मृन्मयः (mṛnmayaḥ) is built on mṛd, "clay", with -maya, "made of"; MW gives mṛnmaya, so spelt (MW calls mṛṇmaya a wrong reading), as made of earth or clay, earthen (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
मृद्नाति (mṛdnāti) — kneads.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for clay, then the Sanskrit for earthen.

You'll want to know: मृद्नाति.
मृद्नाति — mṛdnāti — "kneads".
कुम्भकारः मृदं मृद्नाति। kumbhakāraḥ mṛdaṃ mṛdnāti. The potter kneads the clay.
Past (he …): सः मृदं मृदितवान्, saḥ mṛdaṃ mṛditavān.

The word, taken apart.
मृद्नाति (mṛdnāti) comes from the root √mṛd, "to press, to squeeze, to crush", whose present is mṛdnāti; its past participle is mṛdita (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
आपाकः (āpākaḥ) — a kiln.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for earthen, then the Sanskrit for kneads.

You'll want to know: आपाकः.
आपाकः — āpākaḥ — "a kiln".
कुम्भकारः पात्राणि आपाके स्थापयति। kumbhakāraḥ pātrāṇi āpāke sthāpayati. The potter puts the pots in the kiln.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आपाकः (āpākaḥ) is built on ā and the root √pac, "to cook, to bake"; MW gives āpāka as a baking-oven, a potter's kiln (MW).
