German, chapter 400: Freie Sicht.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Scheinwerfer — the headlight; the spotlight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the brake, then the German for to change gear.

You'll want to know: der Scheinwerfer.
der Scheinwerfer — "the headlight; the spotlight".
Am Abend schaltet sie die Scheinwerfer ein. In the evening she switches the headlights on.
Same form in the plural: die Scheinwerfer. On a stage, the spotlight is also der Scheinwerfer.

The word, taken apart.
Scheinwerfer is built from Schein 'light, glow' and Werfer 'thrower', from werfen 'to throw': a thrower of light. Both are inherited: Schein goes back to Proto-Germanic skīnaną 'to shine' and werfen to werpaną 'to throw' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Windschutzscheibe — the windscreen (US: windshield).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to change gear, then the German for the headlight.

You'll want to know: die Windschutzscheibe.
die Windschutzscheibe — "the windscreen (US: windshield)".
Ein kleiner Stein hat die Windschutzscheibe getroffen. A small stone hit the windscreen.
Plural: die Windschutzscheiben. Three parts: Wind + Schutz + Scheibe.

The word, taken apart.
Windschutzscheibe is built from Wind, Schutz 'protection' and Scheibe 'pane, disc', all inherited Germanic words: Wind goes back to Proto-Germanic windaz and Scheibe to Old High German scība (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Scheibenwischer — the windscreen wiper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the headlight, then the German for the windscreen.

You'll want to know: der Scheibenwischer.
der Scheibenwischer — "the windscreen wiper".
Bei starkem Regen laufen die Scheibenwischer sehr schnell. In heavy rain the wipers go very fast.
Same form in the plural. Often shortened to der Wischer.

The word, taken apart.
Scheibenwischer is a compound of Scheibe 'pane' and Wischer 'wiper', from wischen 'to wipe', which is inherited through Middle High German wischen from Old High German wisken (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Rückspiegel — the rear-view mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the windscreen, then the German for the windscreen wiper.

You'll want to know: der Rückspiegel.
der Rückspiegel — "the rear-view mirror".
Vor dem Abbiegen schaut die Fahrerin in den Rückspiegel. Before turning off, the driver looks in the rear-view mirror.
Same form in the plural. The mirrors on the sides are die Außenspiegel.

The word, taken apart.
Rückspiegel is built from rück- 'back' and Spiegel 'mirror'. Spiegel is an early borrowing from Latin speculum 'mirror', built on specere 'to look' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists Middle High German spiegel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
blinken — to indicate (in a car); to flash, to twinkle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the windscreen wiper, then the German for the rear-view mirror.

You'll want to know: blinken.
blinken — "to indicate (in a car); to flash, to twinkle".
Er blinkt rechts und fährt in die kleine Straße. He indicates right and turns into the little street.
Er blinkt, er hat geblinkt. Stars also blinken: they twinkle.

The word, taken apart.
blinken is inherited: EtymDB traces it through Middle Low German blinken to Proto-Germanic blinkaną 'to shine, to gleam'. English blink is a cousin.
