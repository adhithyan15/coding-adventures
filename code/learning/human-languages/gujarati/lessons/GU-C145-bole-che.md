# તે ગુજરાતી બોલે છે — the present, for someone else

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C143-ACT143-05, GU-CONCEPT-C05-HUNGUJARATIBOLUNCHHUN-01, GU-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-STEM-PERSON-COPULA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say the Gujarati for *to change*, the last verb of
the A1 words. Then the first sentence you ever built about yourself.

- [YOU RECALL: *badalvũ*, then *hũ gujarātī bolũ chũ*]

That sentence has three working pieces: the stem **બોલ-** (*speak*), the ending
**-ું** that says *I*, and **છું**, the copula that agrees with *I*. Change the
person and two of the three pieces move.

## Grammar Lens: one ending for he, she and તું
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-E-01]; assesses=[GU-PRON-TE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C02-TUTAME-01] -->

> **તે ગુજરાતી બોલે છે.** — *te gujarātī bole che.* — He speaks Gujarati. She speaks Gujarati.

The stem stays **બોલ-**. The person ending becomes **-ે**, and the copula
becomes **છે**, the commonest word in this book. The familiar *you* takes the
very same pair:

> **તું ગુજરાતી બોલે છે.** — *tũ gujarātī bole che.* — You speak Gujarati.

| who | verb | copula |
|---|---|---|
| હું | બોલું | છું |
| તું, તે | બોલે | છે |

**તું** and **તે** share one verb form, so only the pronoun in front tells them
apart. **જવું** bends its stem a little and keeps the idea: **તે જાય છે**, *she goes*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-E-01, GU-PRON-TE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which two pieces change when *I speak* becomes *she speaks*? (The
ending **-ું** becomes **-ે**, and **છું** becomes **છે**.) Which pronoun shares
her verb form? (**તું**, the familiar *you*.)
