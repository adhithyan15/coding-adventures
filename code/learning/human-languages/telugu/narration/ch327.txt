Telugu, chapter 327: Once Upon a Time.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఒకప్పుడు (okappuḍu) — at one time; once.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to steal, then the Telugu for to run away.

You'll want to know: ఒకప్పుడు.
ఒకప్పుడు — okappuḍu — "at one time; once".
ఒకప్పుడు ఇక్కడ ఒక పెద్ద అడవి ఉండేది. okappuḍu ikkaḍa oka pedda aḍavi uṇḍēdi. At one time there was a big forest here.
Stories often open with ఒకప్పుడు (okappuḍu), the way English opens with once upon a time: ఒక, one, and అప్పుడు, then.

The word, taken apart.
ఒకప్పుడు (okappuḍu) is a native Telugu compound: oka, "one" (DEDR 990), and appuḍu, "at that time" (DEDR 4559).

Lesson 2 of 5.
రాజు (rāju) — a king.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to run away, then the Telugu for at one time.

You'll want to know: రాజు.
రాజు — rāju — "a king".
ఆ రాజు చాలా మంచివాడు. ā rāju cālā mañcivāḍu. That king is a very good man.
Plural: రాజులు, rājulu.
The capital, రాజధాని, is the seat of the king: the same Sanskrit word sits inside it.

The word, taken apart.
రాజు (rāju) is borrowed from Sanskrit rājan, "a chieftain, a king" (CDIAL 10679). Telugu gives it the native ending -ఉ.

Lesson 4 of 5.
రాజ్యం (rājyaṁ) — a kingdom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a king, then the Telugu for a queen.

You'll want to know: రాజ్యం.
రాజ్యం — rājyaṁ — "a kingdom".
ఆ రాజ్యంలో ప్రజలు సంతోషంగా ఉండేవారు. ā rājyaṁlō prajalu santōṣaṅgā uṇḍēvāru. In that kingdom the people lived happily.
Plural: రాజ్యాలు, rājyālu.
A Sanskrit -a noun ending in -ం: its plural is రాజ్యాలు, the way పుస్తకం gives పుస్తకాలు. In the old stories a kingdom depends on its king: రాజ్యం (rājyaṁ) రాజు (rāju) మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది.

The word, taken apart.
రాజ్యం (rājyaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit rājya, "kingship", and later "a kingdom" (CDIAL 10694), built on rājan, "a king".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఏలు (ēlu) — to reign over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a queen, then the Telugu for a kingdom.

You'll want to know: ఏలు.
ఏలు — ēlu — "to reign over".
ఆ రాజు చాలా సంవత్సరాలు రాజ్యం ఏలాడు. ā rāju cālā saṁvatsarālu rājyaṁ ēlāḍu. That king ruled the kingdom for many years.
Past (I …): ఏలాను, ēlānu.
A native verb of the old stories: రాజ్యం (rājyaṁ) ఏలు (ēlu), to rule a kingdom.

The word, taken apart.
ఏలు (ēlu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5157 lists Telugu ēlu, "to rule, govern", with Tamil āḷ, "to rule, reign over".
