# Mock 1 — answer key and administration notes

**Form id:** `pre-a1-mock-1` · **Paper:** [`mock-1-paper.md`](mock-1-paper.md) ·
**Rubric:** [`rubric.md`](rubric.md)

Objective items are right or wrong. Rated items are scored on the rubric's four
dimensions, 0–5 each, scaled to the item's points. Accepted regional variants
are listed per item; the list is **not exhaustive**, and a trained Spanish
reviewer adjudicates any consistent documented form it does not name.

## Paper 1 — Reading (25)

### Part 1 — Match the word (6 × 2.5 = 15)

| # | word | answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | el pan | **C** (bread) |
| 2 | la llave | **D** (key) |
| 3 | la mano | **A** (hand) |
| 4 | el reloj | **F** (clock) |
| 5 | el agua | **E** (water) |
| 6 | la mesa | **B** (table) |

> **Why *el agua*.** Item 5 is the one deliberate trap on the paper, and it is a
> trap for the *rater*, not the candidate. *Agua* is feminine and takes *el* in
> the singular because it begins with a stressed **a**. A candidate cannot get
> this wrong — it is a matching item — but a rater who "corrects" *el agua* to
> *la agua* anywhere else on this form is wrong, and this note is here so that
> does not happen.

### Part 2 — Choose the reply (4 × 2.5 = 10)

| # | answer | why the distractors fail |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **(a) De nada** | *¿Y tú?* hands a question back that was not asked; *hasta mañana* closes a conversation that has not ended |
| 8 | **(b) Me llamo Ana** | *Mucho gusto* answers an introduction, not a question; *hasta pronto* closes a conversation instead of answering it |
| 9 | **(a) Bien, ¿y tú?** | both distractors are repair strategies, correct Spanish for a different situation |
| 10 | **(b) Adiós** | *sí* answers nothing; *¿quién?* asks about a person |

## Paper 2 — Listening (25)

### Recording script

Read at an unhurried natural pace. Each item **twice**, eight seconds apart.

**Part 1 (items 11–16)** — one word each, 1–2 seconds:

> 11. gracias  ·  12. señora  ·  13. el café  ·  14. por favor  ·  15. mañana  ·  16. el padre

**Part 2 (items 17–20)** — one short exchange each, 3–8 seconds:

> **17.** — Hola, ¿cómo se llama usted? — Me llamo Elena.
> **18.** — Hola, ¿cómo estás? — Bien, gracias.
> **19.** — Un café, por favor. — Sí, señor.
> **20.** — Adiós. — Hasta mañana.

### Part 1 answers (6 × 2.5 = 15)

11 **(c)** · 12 **(b)** · 13 **(a)** · 14 **(b)** · 15 **(a)** · 16 **(b)**

> **Items 12 and 15 are minimal pairs and are the paper's real listening
> measure.** *Señor* and *señora* differ by one final vowel; *mañana*, *mano*
> and *más* share an opening syllable. The other four items are recognition,
> which a candidate can pass on gist. Expect these two to carry most of the
> spread between candidates.

### Part 2 answers (4 × 2.5 = 10)

| # | accept | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | **Elena** | see the note on names below |
| 18 | **bien** | accept *bien, gracias* |
| 19 | **un café** | accept **café** alone |
| 20 | **mañana** | accept *hasta mañana*. **Do not accept *manana*** — see the rubric: ñ is a separate letter |

Recognisable spelling earns the mark; a missing written accent does not cost it.

> **On the name in item 17.** The rubric's spelling criterion says *recognisable
> spelling of a **taught** word*, and a proper name is not a taught word. That is
> the point of the item rather than a hole in it: catching an unfamiliar name out
> of running speech and getting it onto paper is the single most common thing a
> beginner has to do with a language. The mark is for a spelling a Spanish reader
> would read back as the same name, so *Elena* earns it and so does nothing else
> here — Spanish orthography is regular enough that this name has one spelling.

## Paper 3 — Writing (25)

### Part 1 — Look, cover, write (2 × 2.5 = 5)

**Invigilator:** show each word alone for **five seconds**, then cover it
completely. No second showing.

| # | show | accept |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | **gracias** | *gracias* |
| 22 | **mañana** | *mañana*; accept without the accent, **not** with *n* for *ñ* |

### Part 2 — Dictation (3 items, 10 points)

**Dictate, each twice:**

> 23. **mil gracias**  ·  24. **por favor**  ·  25. **me llamo**

| # | accept | common misses |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | mil gracias | both words are needed; *gracias* alone is a different, weaker formula and does not earn the mark |
| 24 | por favor | — |
| 25 | me llamo | *me yamo* is **not** accepted: yeísmo makes the two spellings sound alike, but the criterion is a recognisable spelling of the **taught** word, and *llamo* is what the course taught |

> Item 25 is the one place this paper asks the candidate to know something the
> sound does not tell them. Most Spanish speakers pronounce *ll* and *y*
> identically, so the spelling has to come from having read and written the
> word, which is exactly what a dictation is for.

### Part 3 — Write the answer (2 × 5 = 10, rated)

**26.** Reply to *Hola, me llamo Ana. ¿Y tú?*

Full credit for a reply that greets **and** names: *Hola, me llamo …* Accept
*Me llamo …* alone, and *Soy …*. Accept any name. A bare *hola* completes half
the task and takes dimension 1 to 2 or 3.

**27.** Ask politely for a coffee.

Full credit for *Un café, por favor.* Accept *Café, por favor*, *Quiero un café,
por favor*, and *Un café, por favor, señor/señora*. **The courtesy formula is
required**: a bare *un café* is intelligible and complete as a request, so
dimensions 2 and 3 can still score, but dimension 1 caps at 3 because the prompt
asked for *politely*.

## Paper 4 — Speaking (25, all rated)

**Interlocutor:** speak at a natural unhurried pace. Offer **one** slow
repetition per prompt if asked, and record that you did. Do not prompt with the
answer.

### Part 1 — Greet and say who you are (2 × 5 = 10)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | *Hola.* | the greeting returned: *hola*. Accept *hola* plus a courtesy formula |
| 29 | *¿Cómo se llama usted?* | *Me llamo …*; accept *Soy …* |

Note item 29 is asked with **usted**. A candidate who answers in the *tú*
register has still done the task — the answer form does not change — and loses
nothing. This item tests naming, not register; register arrives at A1.

### Part 2 — Familiar questions (2 × 4 = 8)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | *¿Cómo estás?* | *Bien*, *Bien, gracias*, *Regular*, *Más o menos* — a negative answer is as correct as a positive one |
| 31 | *Hablo español. ¿Y usted?* | *Sí*, *Hablo español*, *Sí, hablo español*, *Poco*, *No mucho* |

### Part 3 — Ask for something (2 items, 7 points: 4 + 3)

| # | prompt | full credit |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | picture of water | *Agua, por favor* · *Un vaso de agua, por favor* |
| 33 | interlocutor speaks too fast | *Más despacio, por favor* · *No entiendo. Más despacio, por favor* |

> **Item 33 is the most important item on the paper.** Everything else asks the
> candidate to produce Spanish they have rehearsed. This one asks them to repair
> a conversation that is going wrong — the one skill that decides whether a
> pre-A1 speaker can hold any real exchange at all. A candidate who says nothing
> and waits scores 0 on dimension 1; a candidate who says *no entiendo* and
> stops has done half the task and should score around 3.
