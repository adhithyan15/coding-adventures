Hindi, chapter 441: Dawn and Dusk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सवेरा (saverā) — early morning, daybreak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to clear (of clouds), then the Hindi for to blow away.

You'll want to know: सवेरा.
सवेरा — saverā — "early morning, daybreak".
हम सवेरे पाँच बजे निकले। ham savere pā̃c baje nikle. We set off at five in the early morning.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सवेरा (saverā) goes back to savēla, "in time, early", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 13291), which lists Hindi saverā, "early morning".

Lesson 2 of 5.
सूर्योदय (sūryoday) — sunrise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to blow away, then the Hindi for daybreak.

You'll want to know: सूर्योदय.
सूर्योदय — sūryoday — "sunrise".
पहाड़ से सूर्योदय बहुत सुंदर दिखता है। pahāṛ se sūryoday bahut sundar dikhtā hai. The sunrise looks beautiful from the mountain.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सूर्योदय (sūryoday) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sūryodaya (Monier-Williams), from sūrya, "the sun", and udaya, "going up"; the two vowels a + u have merged into o.

Lesson 4 of 5.
क्षितिज (kṣitij) — the horizon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for sunrise, then the Hindi for sunset.

You'll want to know: क्षितिज.
क्षितिज — kṣitij — "the horizon".
सूरज क्षितिज के पीछे छिप गया। sūraj kṣitij ke pīche chip gayā. The sun went down behind the horizon.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
क्षितिज (kṣitij) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kṣitija, "born of the earth", which the astronomers used for the horizon (Monier-Williams): where the sky seems to rise out of the earth.

Lesson 5 of 5.
गोधूलि (godhūli) — dusk (when the cattle come home).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for sunset, then the Hindi for the horizon.

You'll want to know: गोधूलि.
गोधूलि — godhūli — "dusk (when the cattle come home)".
गोधूलि में गायें गाँव लौट रही थीं। godhūli meṁ gāyẽ gā̃v lauṭ rahī thīṁ. At dusk the cows were coming back to the village.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
गोधूलि (godhūli) is a tatsama, Sanskrit godhūli (Monier-Williams), literally the dust of go, a word for both the cow and the earth. Monier-Williams reads it as earth-dust, the haze that seems to rise at that hour; the usual telling is the dust raised by the cattle coming home. It is still an auspicious time for weddings.
