\section*{The three facts}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Consonants carry a built-in ``a.''} \pa{ਕ} ka, \pa{ਨ} na,
    \pa{ਮ} ma --- written left to right under a connecting top line.
  \item \textbf{A vowel sign changes it.} \pa{ਾ} \=a (\pa{ਕਾ} k\=a), \pa{ਿ} i,
    \pa{ੀ} \=\i, \pa{ੁ} u, \pa{ੂ} \=u, \pa{ੇ} e, \pa{ੋ} o. A \emph{bindi} \pa{ਂ}
    or \emph{\d{t}ippi} \pa{ੰ} nasalises the vowel.
  \item \textbf{Word-initial vowels ride a carrier.} Three bearer letters ---
    \pa{ੳ} (u-series), \pa{ਅ} (a-series), \pa{ੲ} (i-series) --- hold a vowel that
    starts a word: \pa{ਅ} $+$ nothing $=$ \emph{a}, \pa{ਆ} $=$ \=a.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Worth flagging}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The \emph{pair bindi} (a dot beneath).} It marks Perso-Arabic
    sounds Punjabi borrowed: \pa{ਸ਼} \emph{sh}, \pa{ਖ਼} \emph{x/kh}, \pa{ਜ਼}
    \emph{z}, \pa{ਫ਼} \emph{f}, \pa{ਗ਼} \emph{\d{g}}. The script literally
    \emph{annotates} its loanwords.
  \item \textbf{Tone.} Punjabi is unusual among Indian languages in being
    \emph{tonal} --- certain letters (the old voiced-aspirates \pa{ਘ ਝ ਢ ਧ ਭ})
    now trigger a low or high tone on the syllable. You will pick this up by ear;
    the lessons flag it only where it changes a word.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Gurmukhi and the Punjabi story}

\textbf{Gurmukhi} means ``from the mouth of the Guru'': the second Sikh Guru,
Angad, shaped it in the 16th century to write the community's teachings. Punjabi
is \textbf{Indo-Aryan} (like Hindi, Marathi, Bengali --- all from Sanskrit),
spoken across Punjab in India and Pakistan; in Pakistan it is more often written
in a Perso-Arabic script (\emph{Shahmukhi}), a mirror of Punjabi's own two
inheritances. The lessons trace both --- the Sanskrit and the Persian.
