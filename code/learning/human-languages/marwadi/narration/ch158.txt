Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 158: Yes or No?.
6 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
आप चाय पीवो हो? (āp chāy pīvo ho?) — a yes-or-no question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say you drink tea, to आप: आप चाय पीवो हो।.

Grammar Lens: the voice does it.
Say the same sentence again and let your voice rise at the end. That is the whole question:
आप चाय पीवो हो? āp chāy pīvo ho? — Do you drink tea?
वो बीमार है? vo bīmār hai? — Is he ill?
Nothing moves and nothing is added. In print, only the end mark changes:? for ।.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: आप दूध पीवो हो? — āp dūdh pīvo ho? — Do you drink milk?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: राम अठै है? — rām aṭhai hai? — Is Ram here?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: आप मांस खावो हो? — āp mā̃s khāvo ho? — Do you eat meat?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What turns वो बीमार है। into a question? (The voice, rising at the end.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Ask politely: do you drink tea?


Lesson 2 of 6.
हां सा, कोनी सा — answering yes or no.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask do you drink tea? Then say the polite yes and the plain no: हां सा, कोनी.

Grammar Lens: the answer, then the sentence.
Answer with हां सा or कोनी सा, then say the sentence back:
हां सा, हूं पीऊं हूं। hā̃ sā, hū̃ pīū̃ hū̃. — Yes, I do.
कोनी सा, हूं कोनी पीऊं। konī sā, hū̃ konī pīū̃. — No, I don't.
सा keeps the answer as polite as the question.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: राम अठै है? — rām aṭhai hai? — Is Ram here?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: कोनी सा, राम अठै कोनी। — konī sā, rām aṭhai konī. — No, Ram isn't here.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: हां सा, हूं दूध पीऊं हूं। — hā̃ sā, hū̃ dūdh pīū̃ hū̃. — Yes, I drink milk.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
The polite yes? (हां सा.) The polite no? (कोनी सा.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Answer 'Do you drink tea?' politely with no.


Lesson 3 of 6.
कद (kad) — when.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say where, why and who. (कठै, क्यूं, कुण.)

You'll want to know: कद.
कद — kad — "when".
आप कद आवो हो? āp kad āvo ho? — When do you come?

The word, taken apart.
कद (kad) asks when. Monier-Williams gives Sanskrit kadā, "when?", but CDIAL has no entry for कद, so how the Marwari word came down is not settled. कदी, "sometimes, ever", opens the same way.

What you've built.
कद sits where the answer will go, as कठै does: वो कद जावै है? (vo kad jāvai hai?), when does he go?

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kad]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: आप कद आवो हो? — āp kad āvo ho? — When do you come?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say where, then kad again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does कद (kad) mean? ("When".) Say it once more.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Marwadi for 'when'.


Lesson 4 of 6.
ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo) — to recognise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say to know and to see. (जाणणो, देखणो.) Then I understand a little.

You'll want to know: ओळखणो.
ओळखणो — oḷakhṇo — "to recognise".
हूं यो गीत ओळखूं हूं। hū̃ yo gīt oḷakhū̃ hū̃. — I recognise this song.
आप यो गीत ओळखो हो? āp yo gīt oḷakho ho? — Do you recognise this song?
It opens on a letter you have not written yet: the vowel o, standing alone. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo) goes back to Sanskrit upalakṣayati, which CDIAL glosses "beholds". In that entry CDIAL gives Gujarati oḷakhvũ, "to know, distinguish", and no Marwari form (CDIAL 2226).

What you've built.
Its stem ओळख ends in a consonant: ओळखूं हूं, ओळखै है, ओळखो हो.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: oḷakhṇo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: आप यो गीत ओळखो हो? — āp yo gīt oḷakho ho? — Do you recognise this song?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: हां सा, हूं ओळखूं हूं। — hā̃ sā, hū̃ oḷakhū̃ hū̃. — Yes, I recognise it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo) mean? ("To recognise".) Say it once more.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Marwadi for 'to recognise'.


Lesson 5 of 6.
ओ (o) — the letter that opens ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ओ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write अ once, and name it. Then say छोरो (chhoro): its o is the sign ो.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo), "to recognise". It opens on the vowel o standing alone, and this lesson puts that letter in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ओ.
ओ — o.
It opens ओळखणो (oḷakhṇo). It is built on अ: the joined bowls, the shoulder and the inner stem, then a trailing stem, as आ has, and above it a separate arc, the same small arc the sign ो carries.

Writing: ओ.
1. curve right around the upper bowl and continue down and around the lower bowl without lifting.
2. lift and sweep the middle shoulder right.
3. without lifting, climb up the inner stem to the headline.
4. descend the inner stem.
5. lift and descend the trailing stem.
6. lift at the trailing stem's headline junction and sweep the upper arc upward and left.
7. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 4.
The source shows six movements; native writers draw ओ (o) as five pen-down strokes (60% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 49 of 82). This ductus follows the six movements in order and direction, lifting four times: the middle shoulder runs on, climbing the inner stem and descending it, and every other movement starts with a lift.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari ओ (o) stroke order.svg’, panels 1–6, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
