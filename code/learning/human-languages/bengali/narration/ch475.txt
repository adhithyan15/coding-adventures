Bengali, chapter 475: Small Injuries.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ক্ষত (khôtô) — a wound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for cancer, then the Bengali for jaundice.

You'll want to know: ক্ষত.
ক্ষত — khôtô — "a wound".
নার্স ক্ষতটায় মলম লাগালেন। nārs khôtôṭāy môlôm lāgālen. The nurse put ointment on the wound.

The word, taken apart.
ক্ষত (khôtô) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kṣata, "wounded", and as a noun "a wound" (CDIAL 3645).

Lesson 5 of 5.
হোঁচট খাওয়া (hõchôṭ khāoyā) — to stumble; to trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to sprain, then the Bengali for to swell.

You'll want to know: হোঁচট খাওয়া.
হোঁচট খাওয়া — hõchôṭ khāoyā — "to stumble; to trip".
রাতে রাস্তায় পাথরে হোঁচট খেয়ে অরুণ পড়ে গেল। rāte rāstāy pāthôre hõchôṭ kheye ôrun pôṛe gelô. At night Arun tripped on a stone in the road and fell.
Past (he/she …): তিনি সিঁড়িতে হোঁচট খেলেন, tini sĩṛite hõchôṭ khelen.

The word, taken apart.
হোঁচট খাওয়া (hõchôṭ khāoyā) is hõchôṭ, "a stumble", whose origin is not settled, with khāoyā, "to eat" (CDIAL 3865): Bengali "eats" a stumble as it eats a scolding.
