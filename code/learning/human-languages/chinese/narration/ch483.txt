Mandarin Chinese, chapter 483: Getting Better.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hǎozhuǎn — to take a turn for the better; to start to improve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to disinfect, then the Mandarin for to care for the sick.

You'll want to know: hǎozhuǎn.
hǎozhuǎn — "to take a turn for the better; to start to improve".
tā zhìliáo le yí ge yuè bìngqíng hǎozhuǎn le — He had treatment for a month, and his illness has taken a turn for the better.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: hǎo is said with a rising tone before zhuǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
hǎozhuǎn is built from 好 'good' and zhuǎn 'to turn', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawX and trjwenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to move' and 'to turn round'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.
