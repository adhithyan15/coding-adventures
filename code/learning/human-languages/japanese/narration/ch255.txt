Japanese, chapter 255: Advice, and So.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
いった ほうが いいです (itta hō ga ii desu) — you had better.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say, plainly, "I rested" and "I won't drink". (yasunda, nomanai.) Then say "medicine". (kusuri.)

You'll want to know: -た ほうが いいです.
やすんだ ほうが いいです。 yasunda hō ga ii desu. — "You had better rest."
ほう (hō) is "side, way": "the side of having rested is good". The plain past goes before it even when the advice is about now or later.
くすりを のんだ ほうが いいです。 kusuri o nonda hō ga ii desu. — "You had better take some medicine."
びょういんに いった ほうが いいです。 byōin ni itta hō ga ii desu. — "You had better go to the hospital."

You'll want to know: -ない ほうが いいです.
Advice against takes the plain "not":
おさけを のまない ほうが いいです。 osake o nomanai hō ga ii desu. — "You had better not drink alcohol."
あぶないから、いかない ほうが いいです。 abunai kara, ikanai hō ga ii desu. — "It is dangerous, so you had better not go."
This is advice. Saying that something must be done needs another form, which comes later.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "You had better not drink alcohol" — osake o nomanai hō ga ii desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You had better not drink coffee" — kōhī o nomanai hō ga ii desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You had better not drink cold water" — tsumetai mizu o nomanai hō ga ii desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that someone had better go to the hospital.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"You had better rest"? (やすんだ ほうが いいです.) "You had better not go"? (いかない ほうが いいです.) Which form goes before ほうが for advice for? (The plain past.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
だから (dakara) — so; that is why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "it is cold, so I won't go". (samui kara, ikimasen.) Then "you had better not go". (ikanai hō ga ii desu.)

You'll want to know: だから.
だから — dakara — "so", "that is why". It opens a new sentence with the result, after a sentence that gave the reason:
あめが ふっています。 ame ga futte imasu. — "It is raining."
だから、いきません。 dakara, ikimasen. — "So I won't go."
It is plain, the word for a friend; the next lesson gives its polite partner.

The word, taken apart.
だから (dakara) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: だ (da), the plain form of です, and から (kara), "because". JMdict lists it as a conjunction, "so; therefore", written in kana (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dakara]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "So I won't go" — dakara, ikimasen]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "So I will rest" — dakara, yasumimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'so, that is why', to a friend.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"So", opening a sentence, to a friend? (だから, dakara.) Which comes first, the reason or だから? (The reason, in the sentence before.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
ですから / それで (desu kara / sore de) — so, and so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "so I won't go", to a friend. (dakara, ikimasen.) Then say "a holiday". (yasumi.)

You'll want to know: ですから.
To a teacher, in a shop or at work, だから (dakara) becomes ですから (desu kara), with です in place of だ:
あしたは やすみです。 ashita wa yasumi desu. — "Tomorrow is a holiday."
ですから、みせは しまっています。 desu kara, mise wa shimatte imasu. — "So the shop is closed."

You'll want to know: それで.
それで (sore de) is "and so": it tells what came of something that happened. It looks back at an event, where だから (dakara) and ですから also give advice and decisions.
でんしゃが とまりました。 densha ga tomarimashita. — "The train stopped."
それで、おくれました。 sore de, okuremashita. — "And so I was late."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "And so I was late" — sore de, okuremashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "And so I went home" — sore de, kaerimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "And so I walked" — sore de, arukimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that the train stopped, and so you were late.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"So", politely? (ですから.) "So", to a friend? (だから (dakara).) "And so", for what came of an event? (それで.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — advice, and so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you had better rest". (yasunda hō ga ii desu.) Then "so I won't go", to a friend. (dakara, ikimasen.)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: You had better take some medicine.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: You had better not drink cold water.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: It is raining. So I won't go. — to a friend]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: The train stopped. And so I was late.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I think you had better go to the hospital.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I asked my teacher's opinion.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, politely, that it is raining, so the person had better not go.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Advice for? (-た ほうが いいです.) Advice against? (-ない ほうが いいです.) "So", to a friend and politely? (だから (dakara), ですから.) "And so", for what came of it? (それで.)
