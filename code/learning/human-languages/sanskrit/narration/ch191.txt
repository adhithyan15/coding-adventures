Lesson 1 of 5.
शरावः (śarāvaḥ) — an earthen dish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an amla, gooseberry, then the Sanskrit for a rose apple.

You'll want to know: शरावः.
शरावः — śarāvaḥ — "an earthen dish".

The word, taken apart.
śarāva is a shallow dish or platter of earthenware, and also its flat lid (MW). Neither MW nor CDIAL 12334 gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi sarā, "earthen cover".

Lesson 2 of 5.
कुम्भः (kumbhaḥ) — a pitcher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a rose apple, then the Sanskrit for an earthen dish.

You'll want to know: कुम्भः.
कुम्भः — kumbhaḥ — "a pitcher".

The word, taken apart.
kumbha is a Rigvedic word for a water jar (CDIAL 3308). MW compares Greek kymbē, "cup", and CDIAL compares Avestan xumba-, "pot", so it is an old word shared with Iranian. Kumbha is also the sign Aquarius, the water-carrier.

Lesson 3 of 5.
उलूखलम् (ulūkhalam) — a mortar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an earthen dish, then the Sanskrit for a pitcher.

You'll want to know: उलूखलम्.
उलूखलम् — ulūkhalam — "a mortar".

The word, taken apart.
ulūkhala is a wooden mortar, already in the Rigveda. CDIAL 2360 takes it as borrowed from Dravidian, following Mayrhofer, and notes it shares the ending -ala with musala, "pestle". Punjabi ukkhal, "small wooden mortar", comes from a variant of it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
मुसलम् (musalam) — a pestle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pitcher, then the Sanskrit for a mortar.

You'll want to know: मुसलम्.
मुसलम् — musalam — "a pestle".

The word, taken apart.
musala is probably not an Indo-Aryan word: CDIAL 10223 says its sounds point to a non-Aryan origin, with Bloch suggesting Dravidian. It lives on as Hindi mūsal, "small pestle".

Lesson 5 of 5.
चालनी (cālanī) — a sieve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a mortar, then the Sanskrit for a pestle.

You'll want to know: चालनी.
चालनी — cālanī — "a sieve".

The word, taken apart.
cālanī is built on the root √cal, "to move, shake" (MW): a sieve is what you shake to sift. CDIAL 4771 lists it, and it lives on as Hindi cālnī, "sieve".
