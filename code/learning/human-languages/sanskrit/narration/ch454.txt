Sanskrit, chapter 454: Envy and Dislike.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ईर्ष्यति (īrṣyati) — envies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a dear friend, then the Sanskrit for sends word.

You'll want to know: ईर्ष्यति.
ईर्ष्यति — īrṣyati — "envies".
सः धनिकाय मित्राय ईर्ष्यति। saḥ dhanikāya mitrāya īrṣyati. He envies his rich friend.
Past (he …): सः मित्राय ईर्ष्यितवान्, saḥ mitrāya īrṣyitavān.

The word, taken apart.
ईर्ष्यति (īrṣyati) comes from the root √īrṣy, "to envy, to feel impatient at another's prosperity", which takes the dative; MW gives no further derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
जुगुप्सते (jugupsate) — detests.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sends word, then the Sanskrit for envies.

You'll want to know: जुगुप्सते.
जुगुप्सते — jugupsate — "detests".
मीरा अनुचितं कार्यं जुगुप्सते। mīrā anucitaṃ kāryaṃ jugupsate. Mira detests wrong deeds.
Past (she …): सा अनुचितं कार्यं जुगुप्सितवती, sā anucitaṃ kāryaṃ jugupsitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
जुगुप्सते (jugupsate) is built on the desiderative of the root √gup, "to guard": wanting to keep oneself from a thing; MW's ppp jugupsita is "disliked, detested" (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for envies, then the Sanskrit for detests.

You'll want to know: द्वेषः.
द्वेषः — dveṣaḥ — "hatred".
द्वेषः मित्रम् अपि शत्रुं करोति। dveṣaḥ mitram api śatruṃ karoti. Hatred turns even a friend into an enemy.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
द्वेषः (dveṣaḥ) comes from the root √dviṣ, "to hate, to dislike"; MW gives "hatred, dislike, repugnance, enmity" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
वैरम् (vairam) — enmity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for detests, then the Sanskrit for hatred.

You'll want to know: वैरम्.
वैरम् — vairam — "enmity".
सिंहस्य वृकस्य च वैरम् अस्ति। siṃhasya vṛkasya ca vairam asti. There is enmity between the lion and the wolf.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
वैरम् (vairam) is built on vīra, "a hero, a fighting man", by lengthening its first vowel; MW gives "enmity, hostility, a feud" (MW).
