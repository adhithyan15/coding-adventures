Japanese, chapter 379: Finding a Seat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
すわる (suwaru) — to sit down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to cram in, then the Japanese for to stand up.

You'll want to know: すわる.
すわる — suwaru — "to sit down".
でんしゃで ははの となりに すわりました。 — densha de haha no tonari ni suwarimashita — "On the train I sat next to my mother."
Polite すわります, suwarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おじいさんが ざせきに すわりました。, ojiisan ga zaseki ni suwarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
すわる (suwaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

しゃりょう — sharyō — "a railway carriage".
まえの しゃりょうは すいていました。 — mae no sharyō wa suite imashita — "The front carriage was empty."

The word, taken apart.
しゃりょう (sharyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sha, "car" (Middle Chinese tsyhae) and ryō, "counter for carriages" (ljangH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
