Kannada, chapter 12: Family.
3 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ಅಪ್ಪ, ಅಮ್ಮ, and four sibling words — the same system as Tamil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Kannada asks the same question Tamil does about any sibling: older, or younger? — and the words for answering it are almost identical.

The word, taken apart: Parents — native, and matching Tamil.
ಅಪ್ಪ (appa) = "father" — matches Tamil appā closely.
ಅಮ್ಮ (amma) = "mother" — matches Tamil ammā closely.

The word, taken apart: Four sibling words, age before gender.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
Kannada: ಅಣ್ಣ (aṇṇa). meaning: older brother. compare Tamil: aṇṇaṉ — near match.
Kannada: ತಮ್ಮ (tamma). meaning: younger brother. compare Tamil: tambi — near match.
Kannada: ಅಕ್ಕ (akka). meaning: older sister. compare Tamil: akkā — near match.
Kannada: ತಂಗಿ (tangi). meaning: younger sister. compare Tamil: tangai — near match.
Just like Tamil, Kannada has no single word that simply means "brother" or "sister" — you name the sibling's relative age every time, not just their gender. This age-first system runs across the whole Dravidian family, not just one language.

[your turn — say: "appa, amma" — father, mother]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "aṇṇa, tamma" — older/younger brother]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "akka, tangi" — older/younger sister]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many Kannada sibling words are there, and what do they split on? (Four — ಅಣ್ಣ/ತಮ್ಮ, ಅಕ್ಕ/ತಂಗಿ — split by relative age, not just gender.) How closely do Kannada's words match Tamil's? (Very closely — nearly the same forms across the whole set.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
ರ — one character, met inside words you already say.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ರ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: ದ — what does it do?
[pause 2 seconds]
One character this time. Just one — and you have been saying it for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

Script you'll notice: ರ.
ರ — ra.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not r, it is ra.
You already say these, and every one of them has ರ (ra) somewhere inside it:
ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ namaskāra — hello / greetings (namaskāra — "a making of a bow").
ಸರಿ sari — okay / alright / correct (sari).
ಹೆಸರು hesaru — name.
ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? nīvu hēgiddīrā — how are you? (respectful).

Writing: ರ — copy what you see.
Put your pen on ರ (ra) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start the character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at these words, and find ರ (ra) in the ones that have it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ಸರಿ, ಧನ್ಯವಾದ.
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ರ three times, saying ra as you finish each one]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
