Hindi, chapter 517: Bricks and Mortar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ईंट (ī̃ṭ) — a brick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a hole, then the Hindi for to trickle down.

You'll want to know: ईंट.
ईंट — ī̃ṭ — "a brick".
मज़दूर ईंटें सिर पर उठाकर ले जा रहे हैं। Mazdūr ī̃ṭeṁ sir par uṭhākar le jā rahe haiṁ. The labourers are carrying bricks on their heads.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ईंट (ī̃ṭ) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit iṣṭakā, "a brick" (CDIAL 1600).

दीवारों पर नया चूना लगा है, इसलिए कमरा साफ़ दिखता है। Dīvāroṁ par nayā cūnā lagā hai, isliye kamrā sāf dikhtā hai. The walls have fresh whitewash, so the room looks clean.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
चूना (cūnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit cūrṇa, "powder", which already meant lime in Sanskrit (CDIAL 4889).

लीपना — līpnā — "to smear (a floor or wall with mud)".
गाँव में औरतें मिट्टी से आँगन लीपती हैं। Gā̃v meṁ aurteṁ miṭṭī se āṁgan līptī haiṁ. In the village the women smear the courtyard with mud.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने चूल्हा लीपा, usne cūlhā līpā.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
चिनना (cinnā) — to lay (bricks); to build up (a wall).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for plaster (on a wall), then the Hindi for to smear (a floor or wall with mud).

You'll want to know: चिनना.
चिनना — cinnā — "to lay (bricks); to build up (a wall)".
मिस्त्री ईंटें चिनकर दीवार बना रहा है। Mistrī ī̃ṭeṁ cinkar dīvār banā rahā hai. The mason is laying bricks and building a wall.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दीवार चिनी, usne dīvār cinī.

The word, taken apart.
चिनना (cinnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit cinōti, "heaps up, collects" (CDIAL 4814). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to build up (a wall), gather".
