Tamil, chapter 74: And, and Also.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
-உம் … -உம் (-um … -um) — how Tamil says "and".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mādam mean, and what did āṇḍu mean?

Grammar lens: no word goes between them.
-உம் … -உம் (-um … -um) — "both … and".
English drops a word between two things. Tamil puts an ending on each of them and leaves the space between them empty.
பால் (pāl) is milk. தேநீர் (tēnīr) is tea. Milk and tea:
பாலும் தேநீரும் — pālum tēnīrum.
Both words are still there, in the same order, and nothing new sits between them. Each one has grown a tail instead.
The tail is always -உம் (-um), and it lands on every item in the list — the last one included. Two things, two tails.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you have: பால், தேநீர். you say: பாலும் (pālum) தேநீரும்.
you have: மேசை, கட்டில். you say: மேசையும் கட்டிலும்.
மேசை ends in a vowel, so a light ய் slides in before the ending: மேசையும்.

What you've built.
The first thing so far that joins two words you already had.

[your turn — say: pālum tēnīrum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mēsaiyum kaṭṭilum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — count: how many tails a list of two carries]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say mādam, then say āṇḍu, then join the two — mādamum āṇḍum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say nēṟṟu, kuṭai and tarai — three distances back — then join two of them with -உம் (-um)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does Tamil keep its "and"? (On the end of every item, as -உம் (-um) — never between them.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
-உம் (-um) on its own — "also", "too".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say milk and tea in Tamil. How many -உம் (-um) endings did that take?

Grammar lens: one tail, not two.
Put -உம் (-um) on two words and you have joined them. Put it on one word and it means something else: that one too.
நான் (nāṉ) is "I". நான் with the tail:
நானும் — nāṉum — "me too".
Somebody says they are going. You say நானும் and you are going with them. One word, one ending, a whole reply.
The same tail on the things around you:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: நீங்கள். with -உம் (-um): நீங்களும். it means: you too.
word: இது. with -உம் (-um): இதுவும். it means: this one too.
இது ends in a vowel, so வ் slides in the way ய் did after மேசை.
This is one of the most-used endings in the language, and you have now met both of its jobs.

What you've built.
One ending, two jobs: on two words it joins, on one word it adds.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nāṉum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nīṅgaḷum, then iduvum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: pālum tēnīrum against pālum alone — two tails join, one tail adds]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say āṇḍu, then say pālum tēnīrum, then say nāṉum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: read இன்று, say tuṭaippam and say kūrai — three distances back — then join two of them with -உம் (-um)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Someone says they are leaving and you are leaving too. One word. (நானும்.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
பாலும் (pālum) — an ending you can now see.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: பாலும் — where the tail begins and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: say pālum tēnīrum, then say nāṉum.

Script you'll notice: பாலும் — where the tail begins.
You have read பால் already. Adding the joining ending changes one thing on the page, and it is worth seeing exactly where.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
piece: பா, pā, ப with the long-ா sign.
piece: லு, lu, ல with the ு sign.
piece: ம், m, ம with a புள்ளி.
பா, லு, ம் becomes பாலும் (pālum).
Look at what happened to the middle. In பால் the ல carried a புள்ளி and had no vowel of its own. The ending brings a உ, the ல takes it, and the புள்ளி moves along to the ம் that arrived with it.
Nothing was inserted between two words. The seam is inside the word.
The same seam in தேநீரும்: ர picks up ு, and ம் closes the word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: பா — then லு — then ம்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: பாலும் (pālum) தேநீரும்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the letter that lost its புள்ளி, and say where the புள்ளி went]
[your turn — recall: say pālum tēnīrum, then say nāṉum, then read பாலும்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say muṉbu, sīppu and eḻu — three distances back — then join two of them with -உம் (-um)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read பாலும் (pālum). Which letter changed when the ending arrived? (ல — it took the உ the ending brought.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
-உம் … -உம் இல்லை (-um … -um illai) — neither one nor the other.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say nāṉum. Then read பாலும் (pālum) aloud.

Grammar lens: the list stays, the end changes.
English swaps the whole join to say the opposite: both … and becomes neither … nor. Tamil keeps the join it already has and puts இல்லை after it.
பாலும் தேநீரும் இல்லை — pālum tēnīrum illai — "neither milk nor tea".
இல்லை is the very first word of chapter one, the one that means no, there isn't. It has been waiting for a list to stand at the end of.
Say the shop has neither:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you say: பாலும் (pālum) தேநீரும். it means: milk and tea.
you say: பாலும் (pālum) தேநீரும் இல்லை. it means: neither milk nor tea.
One word longer, and the meaning has turned over. Nothing inside the list moved.

What you've built.
Four things in this chapter and only one ending: joining, adding, reading the seam, denying the lot.

Guided Practice.
