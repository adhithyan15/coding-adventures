Arabic, chapter 531: Growing up and growing old.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مراهق (murāhiq) — a teenager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to wean, then the Arabic for to bring up.

You'll want to know: مراهق.
مراهق — murāhiq — "a teenager".
ابن جارنا مراهق ويحب الموسيقى العالية. ibn jārinā murāhiq wa-yuḥibbu al-mūsīqā al-ʿāliya. Our neighbour's son is a teenager and loves loud music.

The word, taken apart.
مراهق (murāhiq) is built on the native Arabic root r-h-q, "to come near" (Lane). Murāhiq is the Form III participle: one coming near to manhood.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
كبر (kabira) — to get older; to grow up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to bring up, then the Arabic for a teenager.

You'll want to know: كبر.
كبر — kabira — "to get older; to grow up".
كبر الأطفال وسافروا إلى المدينة. kabira al-aṭfāl wa-sāfarū ilā al-madīna. The children grew up and went off to the city.
Present: يكبر, yakbar.
Kabira, yakbaru, is to grow older; kabura, yakburu, with the same letters, is to be big or great.

The word, taken apart.
كبر (kabira) is built on the native Arabic root k-b-r, "to be big, great" (Lane). Kabīr, big, which you know, is from the same root.

نضج التين في آخر الصيف. naḍija at-tīn fī ākhir aṣ-ṣayf. The figs ripened at the end of the summer.
Present: ينضج, yanḍaj.
Of a person it means to grow mature.

The word, taken apart.
نضج (naḍija) is built on the native Arabic root n-ḍ-j, "to be ripe, to be well cooked" (Lane). Nāḍij, ripe, which you know, is its participle.
