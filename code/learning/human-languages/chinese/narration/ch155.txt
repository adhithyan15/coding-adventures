You'll want to know: 从.
从 — cóng — "from".
从这儿到大学 cóng zhèr dào dàxué — from here to the university.
我从中国来 wǒ cóng Zhōngguó lái — I come from China.
The next lesson puts the character in your hand. It is the cóng of cónglái.

The word, taken apart.
从 (cóng) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to go to; to follow'. 'From' is a later sense, assumed here, not quoted. It is the cóng of cónglái.

What you've built.
从 (cóng) comes before the starting point, and the whole phrase goes before the verb: 我, 从中国, 来.
到 after it marks where things end: 从这儿到大学.

Script — 人 twice, the left one narrower.
从 (cóng).
One piece you own, written twice.
The left 人, two strokes, narrow: the left-falling stroke, then a short stroke falling to the right that stops early, almost a dot, so the second 人 has room.
The right 人, two strokes, full size: the left-falling stroke, then the long stroke falling to the right.
2 + 2 = four. One person following another.

Lesson 3 of 8.
从八点到十点 (cóng bā diǎn dào shí diǎn) — from and to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two characters you write, a start and an end.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 从这儿, aloud — cóng zhèr]
[your turn — recall: say jǐ diǎn, what time]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say zhǔnshí, on time]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 从 … 到 …, a start and an end.
从 (cóng) marks where something starts, 到 where it ends. A time or a place goes after each.
从八点到十点 cóng bā diǎn dào shí diǎn — from eight to ten o'clock 从这儿到大学 cóng zhèr dào dàxué — from here to the university.
On a sign, 到 often stands alone between two days or two times, with 从 (cóng) left out:
星期一到星期五 xīngqīyī dào xīngqīwǔ — Monday to Friday.
In a sentence the whole phrase goes before the verb:
我从八点到十点上课 wǒ cóng bā diǎn dào shí diǎn shàngkè — I have class from eight to ten.

You'll want to know: chūfā.
chūfā — "to set off; to depart".
huǒchē bā diǎn chūfā — The train leaves at eight.
wǒmen jǐ diǎn chūfā — What time do we set off?
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chūfā is built from 出 'to go out', the 出 you write, and fā 'to send off; to start', the fā of fāshāo. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and pjot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to go forward; to appear' and 'to start up'.

What you've built.
chūfā says when a journey starts; 到 says when it arrives: bā diǎn chūfā, shí diǎn dào.
On a timetable the start is often written 开, as a train or a bus 开 when it leaves.

You'll want to know: 开门.
开门 — kāimén — "to open (for business); to open the door".
星期天不开门 xīngqītiān bù kāimén — It's closed on Sundays.
早上九点开门 zǎoshang jiǔ diǎn kāimén — It opens at nine in the morning.

The word, taken apart.
开门 (kāimén) is built from 开 'to open' and 门 'a door'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, khoj and mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), khoj glossed 'to open; to undo'.

What you've built.
A shop that 开门 (kāimén) is open for business, not only its door. On a sign the hours come first: 早上九点到晚上八点开门.
不 stays bù before the level tone of kāi: 不开门.

You'll want to know: guānmén.
guānmén — "to close (for the day); to shut the door".
wǎnshang bā diǎn guānmén — It closes at eight in the evening.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guānmén is built from guān 'to close', the word you know, and 门 'a door'. guān is inherited from Middle Chinese kwaen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains, through an older book, as a wooden bar held across a door.

What you've built.
开门 and guānmén bracket a shop's day: zǎoshang jiǔ diǎn kāimén, wǎnshang bā diǎn guānmén.
A sign often gives only the span, with 到 between: 九点到八点.

B: 十点半到 shí diǎn bàn dào — At half past ten. A: 书 星期天开门吗 shū, xīngqītiān kāimén ma — The bookshop: is it open on Sunday? B: 星期天不开门 xīngqītiān bù kāimén — It's closed on Sundays. A: 星期六呢 xīngqīliù ne — And Saturday? B: 上午十点到下午五点开门 shàngwǔ shí diǎn dào xiàwǔ wǔ diǎn kāimén — Open from ten to five. A: xiàwǔ wǔ diǎn guānmén — So it closes at five.
