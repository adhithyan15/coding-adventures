# My own line — the first writing with no model

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-WA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Everything you have written so far had something to reach for.

[PAUSE 1s] This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed:
only what this book has taught you.

## Writing — controlled composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-WA-01, AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **one line** about yourself, right to left.

[PAUSE 2s] And here is the extra decision Arabic asks for that most scripts do
not. A letter does not have one shape. It has up to **four**, and which one you
write depends on **where in the word it stands** — alone, at the start, in the
middle, at the end.

[PAUSE 2s] Copying hid that from you: the model already showed the right shape.
Choosing your own words means choosing the shapes too, and a letter you have
only ever copied in the middle of a word is a letter you may not yet be able to
start one with.

[PAUSE 3s] Write the line. Then read it back and check the **joins** before you
check anything else — a letter that should have connected and did not is the
error a reader notices first.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] How many shapes can one Arabic letter have? (**Up to four.**)

[PAUSE 2s] And what decides which one you write? (**Where it stands in the
word.**)
