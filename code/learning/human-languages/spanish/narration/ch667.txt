Spanish, chapter 667: Un piso de vacaciones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el apartamento — the flat, the apartment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the caravan, then the Spanish for the torch.

You'll want to know: el apartamento.
el apartamento — "the flat, the apartment".
Alquilamos un apartamento cerca de la playa. We rented a flat near the beach.
Spain also says el piso; much of Latin America says el departamento.

The word, taken apart.
apartamento is usually traced to Italian appartamento, perhaps through French appartement; the Italian is built on appartare "to set apart", from Latin ad and pars "part" (EtymDB).

el propietario — "the proprietor, the owner; the landlord".
El propietario nos da las llaves. The owner gives us the keys.
A woman is la propietaria. In speech el dueño is common.

The word, taken apart.
propietario is a learned borrowing from Latin proprietārius "an owner, a proprietor" (Lewis & Short), built on proprius "own".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: el anfitrión.
el anfitrión — "the host".
El anfitrión nos espera en la puerta. The host is waiting for us at the door.
A woman is la anfitriona.

The word, taken apart.
anfitrión comes from the name Anfitrión, Greek Amphitryōn (EtymDB), a Theban hero of Greek legend; in an old comedy a great dinner is given at his house.

inquilino is a learned borrowing from Latin inquilīnus "a tenant, a lodger" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from incolere "to dwell in".

Lesson 5 of 5.
alquilar — to rent, to hire; to rent out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the host, then the Spanish for the tenant.

You'll want to know: alquilar.
alquilar — "to rent, to hire; to rent out".
Alquilamos un coche para tres días. We are hiring a car for three days.
Se alquila on a sign — for rent.

The word, taken apart.
alquilar is built on alquiler "rent" (EtymDB), which comes from Andalusian Arabic al-kirá "the hire, the rent", with the Arabic article fused on.
