Lesson 1 of 5.
il curriculum — the CV, the résumé.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to soften, then the Italian for to make up for.

You'll want to know: il curriculum.
il curriculum — "the CV, the résumé".
Ho mandato il mio curriculum a dieci aziende. I sent my CV to ten companies.
Short for curriculum vitae; the plural is the same: i curriculum.

The word, taken apart.
curriculum is taken whole from Latin curriculum "a running, a race, a racetrack" (Lewis & Short), built on currere "to run"; curriculum vitae is "the path of a life".

Lesson 3 of 5.
candidarsi — to apply, to put yourself forward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the CV, then the Italian for the application.

You'll want to know: candidarsi.
candidarsi — "to apply, to put yourself forward".
Mi sono candidata per un posto in banca. I applied for a job at a bank.
Reflexive, with essere: mi sono candidata. Also to stand in an election.

The word, taken apart.
candidarsi is built on candidato, a learned borrowing from Latin candidātus "dressed in white" (Lewis & Short): a Roman seeking office wore a whitened toga.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'assunzione — the hiring, the taking on (of staff).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to apply, then the Italian for the candidate.

You'll want to know: l'assunzione.
l'assunzione — "the hiring, the taking on (of staff)".
L'azienda ha fermato le assunzioni per un anno. The company stopped hiring for a year.
Built on assumere "to hire". Un'assunzione a tempo indeterminato — a permanent job.

The word, taken apart.
assunzione comes from Latin assūmptiō "a taking up, an adoption" (EtymDB), from assūmere "to take to oneself", built on sūmere "to take".
