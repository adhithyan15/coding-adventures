Lesson 1 of 5.
микроволновка (mikrovolnóvka) — a microwave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for terrible, then the Russian for excellent.

You'll want to know: микроволновка.
микроволновка (mikrovolnóvka) — "a microwave".

The word, taken apart.
микроволновка (mikrovolnóvka) is микроволновая печь, "microwave oven", folded into one word with -ка: Greek mikrós, "small", with волна, "a wave".

чайник (cháynik) — a kettle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for excellent, then the Russian for a microwave.

You'll want to know: чайник.
чайник (cháynik) — "a kettle".

The word, taken apart.
чайник (cháynik) is чай, "tea", with -ник, a suffix for a vessel. чай came from Chinese chá, through Persian and Turkic.

Lesson 3 of 5.
миска (míska) — a bowl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a microwave, then the Russian for a kettle.

You'll want to know: миска.
миска (míska) — "a bowl".

The word, taken apart.
миска (míska) is a diminutive of миса, "a bowl", an old loan from Vulgar Latin mēsa, Latin mēnsa, "a table", perhaps through Gothic mēs, "a table, a dish" (Vasmer).

Lesson 5 of 5.
кружка (krúzhka) — a mug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bowl, then the Russian for a saucer.

You'll want to know: кружка.
кружка (krúzhka) — "a mug".

The word, taken apart.
кружка (krúzhka) — from the 1400s; it perhaps came through Polish kruż, "a jug", or straight from Middle High German krûse, which is traced to Greek krōssós, "a water jar" (Vasmer).
