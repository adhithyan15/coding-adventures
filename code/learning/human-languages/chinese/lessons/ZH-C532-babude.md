## You'll want to know: bābude
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C532-HOPE532-02]; assesses=[] -->

*bābude* — "to be dying to; to be eager for (colloquial)".

> *wǒ bābude mǎshàng fàngjià* — I'm dying for the holidays to start right away.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Tone: 不 and 得 are both said light here, bābude, so 不 does not change its tone.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*bābude* is built from *bā*, 不 'not' and 得, all inherited from Middle Chinese, *pae*, *pjut* and *tok* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the name of a region in the west and of a creature; the sense 'to long for' is not given there, and how the three came to mean 'to be dying to' is not settled.
