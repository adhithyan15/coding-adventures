Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: అంట్లు.
అంట్లు — aṇṭlu — "dirty dishes".

The word, taken apart.
అంట్లు (aṇṭlu) is native Dravidian, from aṇṭu, "to stick; uncleanness, defilement by touch", which DEDR 96 lists with Kannada aṇṭu, "to stick; an impurity".

Lesson 4 of 5.
అగరుబత్తి (agarubatti) — an incense stick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a mixer-grinder, then the Telugu for dirty dishes.

You'll want to know: అగరుబత్తి.
అగరుబత్తి — agarubatti — "an incense stick".

The word, taken apart.
అగరుబత్తి (agarubatti) is Sanskrit agaru, "fragrant aloe-wood" (CDIAL 49), and batti, "stick, wick", from Sanskrit varti, "wick" (CDIAL 11359). CDIAL takes agaru from Dravidian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
కుంకుమ (kuṅkuma) — kumkum, vermilion powder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for dirty dishes, then the Telugu for an incense stick.

You'll want to know: కుంకుమ.
కుంకుమ — kuṅkuma — "kumkum, vermilion powder".

The word, taken apart.
కుంకుమ (kuṅkuma) is Sanskrit kuṅkuma, "saffron", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 3214). Telugu uses it for the red powder worn on the forehead.
