French, chapter 477: De la hanche au talon.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la cuisse — thigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for optimistic, then the French for pessimistic.

You'll want to know: la cuisse.
la cuisse — "thigh".
Après la course, j'ai mal aux cuisses. After the run my thighs hurt.
Also a leg of chicken: une cuisse de poulet.

The word, taken apart.
la cuisse is inherited, through Old French, from Latin coxa "hip" (Lewis & Short); the word slid down from the hip to the thigh.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the French for pessimistic, then the French for thigh.

You'll want to know: le mollet.
le mollet — "calf (of the leg)".
Le cycliste a des mollets très forts. The cyclist has very strong calves.
The soft back part of the lower leg.

The word, taken apart.
le mollet is built on mol, the old form of mou "soft", with the small ending -et (EtymDB); mou is inherited from Latin mollis "soft" (Lewis & Short).

Ces chaussures me font mal au talon. These shoes hurt my heel.
Also the heel of a shoe: des talons hauts — high heels.

The word, taken apart.
le talon is inherited from Vulgar Latin tālo (EtymDB), from Latin tālus "the ankle, the anklebone" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'orteil — toe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for calf, then the French for heel.

You'll want to know: l'orteil.
l'orteil — "toe".
Je me suis cogné l'orteil contre un pied de table. I stubbed my toe on the leg of a table.
Masculine. Le gros orteil — the big toe. Fingers are les doigts; toes can also be les doigts de pied.

The word, taken apart.
l'orteil comes from Old French arteil, from Latin articulus "a small joint, a limb" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); its first vowel was later changed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la hanche — hip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for heel, then the French for toe.

You'll want to know: la hanche.
la hanche — "hip".
Elle porte son bébé sur la hanche. She carries her baby on her hip.
The h is aspirated: la hanche, not l'hanche.

The word, taken apart.
la hanche comes from a Germanic word for the haunch. English haunch is the same word, taken from Old French (EtymDB).
