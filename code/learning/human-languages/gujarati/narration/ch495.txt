Gujarati, chapter 495: A Baby on the Way.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સગર્ભા (sagarbhā) — pregnant; a pregnant woman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for elderly, then the Gujarati for an old people's home.

You'll want to know: સગર્ભા.
સગર્ભા — sagarbhā — "pregnant; a pregnant woman".
મારી ભાભી સગર્ભા છે. mārī bhābhī sagarbhā che. My sister-in-law is pregnant.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સગર્ભા (sagarbhā) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sagarbhā, "a pregnant woman", as Monier-Williams records it: sa-, "with", and garbha, "womb, child".

Lesson 3 of 5.
સીમંત (sīmant) — a baby-shower ceremony.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for pregnant, then the Gujarati for childbirth.

You'll want to know: સીમંત.
સીમંત — sīmant — "a baby-shower ceremony".
સાતમા મહિને ભાભીનું સીમંત થયું. sātmā mahine bhābhīnũ sīmant thayũ. In her seventh month my sister-in-law had her baby-shower ceremony.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
સીમંત (sīmant) is a tatsama: Monier-Williams gives Sanskrit sīmanta as "the parting of the hair" and also as the hair-parting rite held for a woman expecting a child; Gujarati uses the word for that ceremony.

Lesson 4 of 5.
અવતરવું (avtarvũ) — to come into the world; to be born.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for childbirth, then the Gujarati for a baby-shower ceremony.

You'll want to know: અવતરવું.
અવતરવું — avtarvũ — "to come into the world; to be born".
ગઈકાલે રાતે અમારા ઘરે દીકરી અવતરી. gaīkāle rāte amārā ghare dīkrī avtarī. Last night a baby daughter was born in our home.
Past (it …): તે બાળક અવતર્યું, te bāḷak avtaryũ.

The word, taken apart.
અવતરવું (avtarvũ) goes back to Sanskrit ava-tṛ, "to come down, to descend" (Monier-Williams), the verb behind avatāra: a child who comes down into the world.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: જણવું.
જણવું — jaṇvũ — "to give birth to".
ગાયે નાનું વાછરડું જણ્યું. gāye nānũ vācharḍũ jaṇyũ. The cow gave birth to a little calf.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે દીકરો જણ્યો, teṇe dīkro jaṇyo.

The word, taken apart.
જણવું (jaṇvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit janayati, "causes to be born, begets"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to bear" (CDIAL 5102).
