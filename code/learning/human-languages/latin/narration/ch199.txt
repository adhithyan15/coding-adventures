Latin, chapter 199: To compare, To persuade, To blame, To share, To avoid.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
comparō, comparāre — to compare; to get ready.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to marry, then the Latin for to rest.

You'll want to know: comparō, comparāre.
comparō, comparāre — "to compare; to get ready".

The word, taken apart.
comparō is two verbs in one form. "To compare" is built on compar, "matched, equal" (Lewis & Short), from com-, "together", and pār, "equal"; "to get ready" is com- with parō, "to prepare".

persuādeō, persuādēre — "to persuade".

The word, taken apart.
persuādeō is per, "thoroughly", with suādeō, "to advise, to urge": to urge someone all the way. suādeō belongs with suāvis, "sweet".

reprehendō, reprehendere — "to blame, to criticise".

The word, taken apart.
reprehendō is re-, "back", with prehendō, "to seize" (EtymDB 2.1): to pull back, to hold someone back with blame.
