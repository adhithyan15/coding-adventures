## You'll want to know: el grupo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C358-REF-14] -->

**el grupo** — the group.

**cambiar de grupo** — to switch groups. **el grupo de los martes** — the
Tuesday group.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-02, ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-01] -->

This one does not come apart. It arrived whole, by way of Italian, and there is
no Spanish piece inside it to recognise.

That is the second thing this run has taught you about long-versus-short words:

| kind of word | what to do |
|---|---|
| long and formal | look for the ending |
| short and plain | learn it whole |

**el grupo** is short and plain, so there is nothing to unfold. Spending time
hunting for a join in a five-letter noun is the opposite of the habit — the
habit is to look **once**, cheaply, and move on when nothing is there.

Where it matters most for you is administrative: courses, trips, exams and
sports are all divided into *grupos*, and the DELE pass rule itself is written
in terms of two of them.
