Gujarati, chapter 48: Knee, Heart and a Fever.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઘૂંટણ (ghū̃ṭaṇ) — a knee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lip, then the Gujarati for the tongue.

You'll want to know: ઘૂંટણ.
ઘૂંટણ (ghū̃ṭaṇ) — "a knee". Halfway down the પગ.

The word, taken apart.
ઘૂંટણ (ghū̃ṭaṇ) (ghūṁṭaṇ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it under ghuṇṭa, a variant of the lexical Sanskrit ghuṭa, "ankle" (CDIAL 4479). It takes the word from Dravidian, after T. Burrow.

લોહી (lohī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lohita, "red; blood" (CDIAL 11165). It is a form of rohita, "red".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ખાંસી (khā̃sī) — a cough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for blood, then the Gujarati for a fever.

You'll want to know: ખાંસી.
ખાંસી (khā̃sī) — "a cough". Often comes with a તાવ (tāv).

The word, taken apart.
ખાંસી (khā̃sī) (khāṁsī) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit kāsa, "cough" (CDIAL 3138). CDIAL explains its kh- as imitative, the sound of the cough itself.
