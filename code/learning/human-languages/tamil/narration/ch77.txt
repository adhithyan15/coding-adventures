Tamil, chapter 77: Saying, and Thinking.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
சொல் (sol) — to say, to tell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read ஆனால். Then offer somebody milk or tea.

You'll want to know: சொல்.
சொல் (sol) — "to say, to tell".
You have been using this verb since chapter eight without being told its name. சொல்லுங்கள் — "please tell me" — is சொல் (sol) with the respectful ending you met in the same chapter.
Now it stands on its own, the way போ and வா and சாப்பிடு do.
சொல் (sol) — say it.
சொல்லுங்கள் — please say it.
சொல் (sol) also means a word. The same three letters name the thing and the act, and no Tamil speaker finds that odd: a word is what saying produces.
You will need this verb for the next lesson, because Tamil has a particular way of handing somebody else's words to it.

What you've built.
A verb you have been saying since chapter eight, now yours to use.

Lesson 2 of 4.
என்று (eṉṟu) — the end of a quotation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Offer somebody a choice of two drinks. Then say the verb to say.

Grammar lens: the quotation carries its own closing word.
English opens a quotation with that or with a comma. Tamil closes it, with என்று (eṉṟu).
"சரி" என்று (eṉṟu) சொல்லுங்கள்.
"sari" eṉṟu solluṅgaḷ. — 'Say "okay".'
The words being reported come first, whole and unchanged. என்று (eṉṟu) then marks where they end, and the verb of saying comes last, where every Tamil verb goes.
"பால் வேண்டும்" என்று (eṉṟu) சொல்லுங்கள்.
'Say "I want milk".'
என்று (eṉṟu) is itself made from an old verb meaning to say, so what you are doing is saying that something was said. Two sayings, one sentence — and that is why nothing needs to be added at the front.
Everyday speech shortens it to -ன்னு, stuck straight onto the quotation: sari-ṉṉu solluṅgaḷ. You will hear that far more often than the full word.

What you've built.
A way to put a whole sentence inside another one.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: eṉṟu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "sari" eṉṟu solluṅgaḷ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: "pāl vēṇḍum" eṉṟu solluṅgaḷ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: offer pāl alladu tēnīr?, then say sol, then say the whole quoted line]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say āṉāl, vēṇḍukōḷ and aḻu — three distances back — then quote one of them with என்று (eṉṟu)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does என்று (eṉṟu) stand — before the quoted words or after them? (After. It closes them, and the verb follows.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
சொல் (sol) — a sign on both sides of one letter.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: சொல் — two letters, and a sign that surrounds one and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: say the verb to say, then close a quotation with என்று (eṉṟu).

Script you'll notice: சொல் — two letters, and a sign that surrounds one.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
piece: சொ, so, ச with the ொ sign.
piece: ல், l, ல with a புள்ளி.
சொ, ல் becomes சொல் (sol).
The ொ sign is the one you first met inside சொல்லுங்கள் in chapter eight, and it is unusual: it puts one piece before its letter and another after it. The letter sits in the middle of its own vowel.
Compare it with ோ in போ, which does the same thing for the long ō. Tamil has a small family of signs that wrap around, and both of them are in words you already say.
The word ends the way ஆனால் did, on a ல் carrying a புள்ளி.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the two halves of the ொ sign, and the letter caught between them]
[your turn — recall: say sol, then say "sari" eṉṟu solluṅgaḷ, then read சொல்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say alladu, aṉumati and iṉṟu — three distances back — then quote one of them with என்று (eṉṟu)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 4.
… என்று நினைக்கிறேன் (… eṉṟu niṉaikkiṟēṉ) — I think that …

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read சொல் (sol). Then say: 'Say "okay".'

You'll want to know: what என்று hands to நினை.
நினை (ninai) has been in the book since chapter thirty-three: "to think". Until now it had nothing to think about, because a thought is a whole sentence and nothing could carry one.
என்று (eṉṟu) carries one.
இது நல்ல வீடு என்று (eṉṟu) நினைக்கிறேன்.
idu nalla vīḍu eṉṟu niṉaikkiṟēṉ.
"I think this is a good house."
The opinion comes first and stands whole — இது நல்ல வீடு, a sentence you could say on its own. என்று (eṉṟu) closes it. Then the verb, built the way பேசுகிறேன் was in chapter five: நினை, the middle piece for now, and -ஏன் for I.
Swap the front half for anything you can say:
இது சிறிய கடை என்று (eṉṟu) நினைக்கிறேன்.

What you've built.
An opinion, which is the first thing here that is yours rather than a fact.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: niṉaikkiṟēṉ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: idu nalla vīḍu eṉṟu niṉaikkiṟēṉ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — swap: put idu siṟiya kaḍai in the front half and say the whole thing]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say "sari" eṉṟu solluṅgaḷ, then read சொல், then give your opinion]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: read ஆனால், say tayakkam and say nēṟṟu — three distances back — then quote one of them with என்று (eṉṟu)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What stands between your opinion and the verb நினைக்கிறேன்? (என்று (eṉṟu).)
