Hindi, chapter 276: What Happened, and Who Did It.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
वह गया (vah gayā) — the past, by who did it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say turning over and a stretch. (karvaṭ, aṅgṛāī.) Then the host's question खाना खाया?, "have you eaten?" Its खाया is a past, and this chapter builds every past like it.

You'll want to know: the past, by who did it.
कल मैं बाज़ार गया। kal maiṁ bāzār gayā. — "Yesterday I went to the market."
With a past, कल is yesterday. गया is जाना in the past, with no है after it. Its ending follows the one who went, as अच्छा, अच्छी, अच्छे follow a noun:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
who went: a man: मैं, वह. the past: गया (gayā).
who went: a woman. the past: गई (gaī).
who went: men, or आप, तुम, हम. the past: गए (gae).
who went: women. the past: गईं (gaīṁ).

You'll want to know: how a stem takes the ending.
Most verbs put the ending on the stem: बैठना becomes वह बैठा, वह बैठी (vah baiṭhā, vah baiṭhī). A stem ending in a vowel adds -या for a man: आना becomes आया, आई (āyā, āī). Two change the stem: जाना becomes गया, and होना becomes हुआ, हुई (huā, huī): क्या हुआ?, "what happened?"
"Did not": मैं नहीं गया (maiṁ nahīṁ gayā). A bare stem with गया says the change is complete, as in देर हो गई and वह सो गया (vah so gayā), "he fell asleep".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "She came home in the evening" — vah śām ko ghar āī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We sat down. Then we slept." — ham baiṭhe. phir ham soe.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you went to the market yesterday, then that she came home in the evening.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"He came"? (आया.) "She went"? (गई.) "What happened?" (क्या हुआ?) "I didn't go"? (मैं नहीं गया.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
उसने रोटी खाई (usne roṭī khāī) — the past that follows the thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "she went". (vah gaī.) खाना खाया? leaves the eater out; this lesson puts the eater in.

Grammar Lens: the doer takes ने.
उसने खाना खाया। usne khānā khāyā. — "He (or she) ate the meal."
With a verb that has an object, such as eating, reading or saying, the past changes: the doer takes ने (ne). The pronouns fuse with it: मैंने, तुमने, आपने, उसने, हमने, उन्होंने (maiṁne, tumne, āpne, usne, hamne, unhoṁne). A name takes ने after it: मीरा ने. उसने is "he" or "she"; the verb no longer says which.

You'll want to know: the verb follows the thing.
मीरा ने रोटी खाई। Mīrā ne roṭī khāī. — "Mira ate a roti."
रोटी is feminine, so खाई, whoever ate it. With खाना, masculine, खाया; with books, मैंने किताबें पढ़ीं (maiṁne kitābeṁ paṛhīṁ). When the thing takes को, or there is none, the verb stays -आ: उसने मीरा को देखा (usne Mīrā ko dekhā), "he saw Mira".
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
sentence: वह गई — no thing. the verb follows: the one who went.
sentence: उसने रोटी खाई (usne roṭī khāī) — ने and a thing. the verb follows: the thing.
sentence: उसने मीरा को देखा — the thing has को. the verb follows: nothing: -आ.
Four verbs make this past their own way, for a masculine thing and a feminine one: किया / की, दिया / दी, लिया / ली, पिया / पी (kiyā / kī, diyā / dī, liyā / lī, piyā / pī), from करना, देना, लेना and पीना.
कहना (kahnā), "to say", is regular. Your क्या कहा? was its past: उसने कहा कि वह कल आएगा (usne kahā ki vah kal āegā), "he said he would come tomorrow". लाना, "to bring", never takes ने: वह फल लाया (vah phal lāyā).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Mira drank tea" — Mīrā ne cāy pī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She saw Arun" — usne Aruṇ ko dekhā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you ate a roti, then that Arun read a book.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Why खाई in उसने रोटी खाई (usne roṭī khāī)? (रोटी is feminine.) Now the other way: "he ate the meal"? (उसने खाना खाया.) Why देखा in उसने मीरा को देखा? (The thing has को.) "He said"? (उसने कहा.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — what happened, and who did it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "Yesterday I went to the market". (kal maiṁ bāzār gayā.) Then "she came" and "what happened?" (vah āī, kyā huā?)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: I came home in the evening, and then I slept.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Mira ate a roti. Arun ate the meal.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: What did you do yesterday? I went to the market.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: She saw Arun at the station.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: He said that he would come tomorrow.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which past follows the one who went? (A verb with no thing: वह गई.) Which follows the thing? (The past with ने: मीरा ने रोटी खाई.) Now the other way round: in उसने खाना खाया, which word tells you that खाना is masculine? (खाया.) And when the thing takes को? (The verb stays -आ: उसने मीरा को देखा.)
