Arabic, chapter 529: In-laws and break-ups.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
صهر (ṣihr) — a son-in-law; a brother-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for single, then the Arabic for to love passionately.

You'll want to know: صهر.
صهر — ṣihr — "a son-in-law; a brother-in-law".
ساعد صهري أبي في نقل الأثاث. sāʿada ṣihrī abī fī naql al-athāth. My son-in-law helped my father move the furniture.
Plural: أصهار, aṣhār.

The word, taken apart.
صهر (ṣihr) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-h-r, "kinship by marriage" (Lane). Lane gives ṣihr as a relation by marriage.

Lesson 2 of 5.
كنة (kanna) — a daughter-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to love passionately, then the Arabic for a son-in-law.

You'll want to know: كنة.
كنة — kanna — "a daughter-in-law".
تحب جدتي كنتها كثيرا. tuḥibbu jaddatī kannatahā kathīran. My grandmother loves her daughter-in-law very much.

The word, taken apart.
كنة (kanna) is an old native Arabic word for a son's or a brother's wife, usually traced to the root k-n-n, "to cover, to shelter"; how the sense grew is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
نسيب (nasīb) — an in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a son-in-law, then the Arabic for a daughter-in-law.

You'll want to know: نسيب.
نسيب — nasīb — "an in-law".
جاء نسيبي لزيارتنا في العيد. jāʾa nasībī li-ziyāratinā fī al-ʿīd. My in-law came to visit us at the feast.

The word, taken apart.
نسيب (nasīb) is built on the native Arabic root n-s-b, "lineage, relationship" (Lane). Nasab, lineage, is from the same root: a nasīb is kin by marriage.

Lesson 4 of 5.
عديل (ʿadīl) — a brother-in-law (by sisters).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a daughter-in-law, then the Arabic for an in-law.

You'll want to know: عديل.
عديل — ʿadīl — "a brother-in-law (by sisters)".
عديلي متزوج من أخت زوجتي. ʿadīlī mutazawwij min ukht zawjatī. My brother-in-law is married to my wife's sister.
Two men married to two sisters are عديلان.

The word, taken apart.
عديل (ʿadīl) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-d-l, "to be equal, to be fair" (Lane). Lane gives ʿadīl as an equal, a counterpart; the sense of two men with sisters for wives is usually explained from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
طلاق (ṭalāq) — a marriage break-up; a divorce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an in-law, then the Arabic for a brother-in-law.

You'll want to know: طلاق.
طلاق — ṭalāq — "a marriage break-up; a divorce".
بعد الطلاق سكن صديقي وحده. baʿda aṭ-ṭalāq sakana ṣadīqī waḥdahu. After the divorce my friend lived alone.

The word, taken apart.
طلاق (ṭalāq) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-l-q, "to be free, to be let go" (Lane). Ṭalāq is the letting go of a marriage.
