# A grammar-source citation must pin the page that states each claimed rule

When one sentence cites a source for two grammatical claims, verify and record
the exact printed page for each claim. Do not infer that a later example is the
source's explicit analysis, and do not carry a candidate page range from search
notes into learner-visible prose without opening the relevant page.

For Gill and Gleason's *A Reference Grammar of Punjabi*, the masculine
direct–oblique adjective paradigm is on pp. 71–72, while the explicit noun or
conditional-infinitive plus **ਵਾਲਾ** adjective-phrase discussion is on printed
p. 107. A shopkeeper example elsewhere does not replace that analysis.
