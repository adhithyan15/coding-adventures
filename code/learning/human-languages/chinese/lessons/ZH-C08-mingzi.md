# 名字 — the word for a name

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-TONE-NEUTRAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **zǎoshang**. Second syllable light and short.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-MINGZI]; assesses=[ZH-SCRIPT-MING] -->

**名字** *míngzi* — **name**.

Both halves are characters you have just written, and both mean something:

- **名** *míng* — **name**
- **字** *zì* — **character**, a written unit

So the word is **"name-character"**, and that is not a stretched hook. A Chinese
personal name *is* a small number of characters, usually two or three, each
chosen deliberately for what it means. The word for "name" says what a name is
made of.

## Sounds you'll need — the second syllable goes light again
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-TONE-NEUTRAL, ZH-LEX-MINGZI] -->

*míngzi*, not *míngzì*.

**字** is fourth tone standing alone, and you practised it that way — but in this
word it goes **neutral**, short and light, exactly as 上 did inside 早上. Second
syllables do this often, and you now know the signal: **no tone mark means no
tone.**

*míng* rises; *zi* rides on the end of it. [REPEAT x2]
