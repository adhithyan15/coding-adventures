## Grammar Lens: places, things and people take different endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-C420-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C354-WHERE-28, ES-LEX-PELICULA] -->

Five words, and the gender of each is decided by a different kind of evidence:

| word | article | what decides it |
|---|---|---|
| el teatro | el | the **-o** ending of a place-word |
| el cine | el | inherited with the shortened word |
| la película | la | the **-a** ending of a thing-word |
| el actor | el | the person is male |
| la actriz | la | the person is female |

That is the useful split. For a **place** or a **thing**, the ending tells you
the article and the world has no say in it. For a **person**, the world decides
and the ending follows.

So *la película* is feminine for a reason that has nothing to do with anyone
being female, while *la actriz* is feminine for exactly that reason.
