Japanese, chapter 153: Out, Back Home and Things Left Behind.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
がいしゅつ (gaishutsu) — going out; being out of the office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to forget, then the Japanese for to be late.

You'll want to know: がいしゅつ.
がいしゅつ — gaishutsu — "going out; being out of the office".
ちちは いま がいしゅつちゅうです。 — chichi wa ima gaishutsuchū desu — "My father is out right now."

The word, taken apart.
がいしゅつ (gaishutsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gai, "outside" (Middle Chinese ngwajH) and shutsu, "go out" (tsyhwit) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
わすれもの (wasuremono) — something left behind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for going out, then the Japanese for coming home.

You'll want to know: わすれもの.
わすれもの — wasuremono — "something left behind".
この かさは ちちの わすれものです。 — kono kasa wa chichi no wasuremono desu — "This umbrella is something my father left behind."

The word, taken apart.
わすれもの (wasuremono) is native Japanese, built from wasure, "forgetting", the stem of wasureru, and mono, "thing", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
なくす (nakusu) — to lose; to mislay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for coming home, then the Japanese for something left behind.

You'll want to know: なくす.
なくす — nakusu — "to lose; to mislay".
いもうとは よく かさを なくします。 — imōto wa yoku kasa o nakushimasu — "My younger sister often loses her umbrella."
Polite なくします, nakushimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
なくす (nakusu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji is a later kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
まにあう (maniau) — to be in time; to make it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for something left behind, then the Japanese for to lose.

You'll want to know: まにあう.
まにあう — maniau — "to be in time; to make it".
まいあさ でんしゃに まにあいます。 — maiasa densha ni maniaimasu — "Every morning I make it to the train in time."
Polite まにあいます, maniaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
まにあう (maniau) is native Japanese, built from ma, "interval, space", the particle ni, and au, "to fit": to fit inside the time. All three parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
