# The name exchange, cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISMII-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Eight lessons ago this book taught you to give your name and ask for
somebody else's, and has not asked since. Say the whole exchange from memory
before you read another line.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISM-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-02] -->

| word | letters, right to left | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **اسم** *ism* | *alif · sīn · mīm* | name — root **s–m–w**, cousin of Hebrew *shem* |
| **ما** *mā* | *mīm · alif* | what, standing before a noun |

Neither needed a new letter. **اسم** has no tie to English *name* or Latin
*nōmen* — the resemblance is an accident, and the real cousin is Hebrew.

## Grammar Lens: four suffixes and no verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-II-MY-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-II-MY-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-ISMUKA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISMII-02] -->

| glued on | means | gives |
|---|---|---|
| **ـي** *-ī* | my | **اسمي** *ismī* |
| **ـك** *-ka* | your, to a man | **اسمك** *ismuka* |
| **ـك** *-ki* | your, to a woman | **اسمك** *ismuki* |

Arabic has no separate word for *my*. It fastens **ي**, the *yāʾ*, onto the end
of the noun — the habit that also fastens **الـ** for *the*. The suffixes are the
grammar of the exchange, and the two written forms of *your name* are identical
on the page.

There is no word for **is** either. *Ismī Arun* is literally *my-name Arun* — the
zero copula, shared with the Dravidian languages.

## The two words: أنت, split by the listener
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-03] -->

**أنت** is *alif* with a hamza seat, then *nūn*, then *tāʾ* — one skeleton for
both genders. What splits them is a vowel mark that everyday text leaves off:
**أنتَ** *anta* to a man, **أنتِ** *anti* to a woman.

The European and Dravidian tracks split *you* by **register**. Arabic splits it
by the **listener's gender** instead — and the Semitic *-ta* / *-ti* ending is
the same *t* that marks *you* in Hebrew *atta* / *att*.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-TASHARRAFNA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-TASHARRAFNA-02] -->

**تشرفنا** *tasharrafnā* closes it — letters *tāʾ · shīn · rāʾ · fāʾ*, root
**sh–r–f**, honour: *sharaf* (honour), *sharīf* (noble, the title behind English
*sheriff*). The polite close is not *pleased to meet you* but **we have been
honoured**.

- [YOU SAY: ask a man for his name — *mā ismuka?*]
- [YOU SAY: ask a woman — *mā ismuki?*]
- [YOU SAY: answer with your own — *ismī …*]
- [YOU SAY: close the exchange — *tasharrafnā*]
- [YOU RECALL: the verb Arabic left out of every line above]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-03, AR-CONCEPT-C02-TASHARRAFNA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-ISMUKA-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Name the Hebrew cousin of **اسم**. (*Shem*.) Say which axis Arabic
uses to split **أنت**, where register would be the obvious choice. (**The listener's
gender**.) Give the literal sense of **تشرفنا**. (***We have been honoured***.)
And say where the gender of the question sits. (**In the suffix** — *-ka* to a
man, *-ki* to a woman.)
