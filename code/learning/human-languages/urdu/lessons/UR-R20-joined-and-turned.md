## Guided Practice: the distant band — every noun in the book, two at a time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-AUR, UR-LEX-YA, UR-GRAMMAR-NA-NA, UR-LEX-PANI, UR-LEX-CHAI, UR-LEX-DOODH, UR-LEX-ROTI, UR-LEX-LAL, UR-LEX-SAFED, UR-LEX-KALA, UR-LEX-NILA, UR-LEX-QAMIZ, UR-LEX-JUTA, UR-LEX-TOPI, UR-LEX-KOAT, UR-LEX-BHAI, UR-LEX-BAHAN, UR-LEX-DOST, UR-LEX-KHANDAN, UR-LEX-KAAN, UR-LEX-NAAK, UR-LEX-AANKH, UR-LEX-MUNH, UR-LEX-DIL, UR-LEX-GARMI, UR-LEX-SARDI, UR-LEX-AAM, UR-LEX-AASMAAN, UR-LEX-PURANA, UR-GRAMMAR-ADJECTIVE-AGREEMENT] -->

[PAUSE 4s] This is what the chapter actually bought. Chapters eight to eighteen
taught thirty-odd nouns that could only ever be named one at a time, and every
one of them is now combinable. Go back and take them two at a time, cold, from
memory — most of these were taught between twenty and sixty lessons ago and have
not been asked for since.

- [YOU SAY: two foods joined, then the same two as a choice — *pānī aur chāy* … *dūdh yā roṭī*]
- [YOU SAY: two colours, and mind the agreement — *kālā aur nīlā*, *lāl aur safed*]
- [YOU SAY: two garments, then two refused — *qamīz aur jūtā* … *na ṭopī, na koṭ*]
- [YOU SAY: two people, then the word that holds them all — *bhāī aur bahan* … *dost aur khāndān*]
- [YOU SAY: the face, four parts at once — *kān, nāk, āṅkh aur muṅh*]
- [YOU SAY: the one that is not on the face — *dil*]
- [YOU SAY: heat against cold, joined and then chosen — *garmī aur sardī* … *garmī yā sardī*]
- [YOU SAY: three from chapter eighteen — *ām, āsmān aur purānā*]

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which of this chapter's joiners is inherited and which two are
borrowed? (**اور** inherited; **یا** from Persian, **لیکن** from Arabic.) Which
one can disagree? (**لیکن**.) And what turns any of these sentences into a
question? (**کیا at the front**, and nothing else moved.)
