Lesson 1 of 5.
চুল কাটা (chul kāṭā) — to have a haircut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to fight, then the Bengali for to run away.

You'll want to know: চুল কাটা.
চুল কাটা — chul kāṭā — "to have a haircut".

The phrase, taken apart.
চুল কাটা (chul kāṭā) is chul, "hair", from Sanskrit cūḍa, "tuft of hair", a word CDIAL traces to Dravidian or Munda (CDIAL 4883), + kāṭā, "to cut", from Sanskrit kartati, "cuts" (CDIAL 2854).

Lesson 2 of 5.
সাজা (sājā) — to dress up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to run away, then the Bengali for to have a haircut.

You'll want to know: সাজা.
সাজা — sājā — "to dress up".

The word, taken apart.
সাজা (sājā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from sajjayati, "makes ready" (CDIAL 13091). CDIAL lists Bengali sājā, "to make ready, dress".

চড়া — chôṛā — "to ride, to climb".

The word, taken apart.
চড়া (chôṛā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from caḍhati, "rises", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4578). CDIAL lists Bengali caṛā, "to mount".
