French, chapter 454: Le relief.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la falaise — the cliff.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cardboard box, then the French for the registered letter.

You'll want to know: la falaise.
la falaise — "the cliff".
Du haut de la falaise, on voit toute la baie. From the top of the cliff the whole bay is in view.
Usually over the sea.

The word, taken apart.
falaise comes from Old French faleise, from Frankish falisa 'rock, cliff' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le plateau — the plateau, the high plain; the tray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the registered letter, then the French for the cliff.

You'll want to know: le plateau.
le plateau — "the plateau, the high plain; the tray".
Le village est sur un plateau, au-dessus de la vallée. The village is on a plateau above the valley.
Plural les plateaux. Un plateau is also a tray, and a TV set.

The word, taken apart.
plateau is built on plat 'flat', which goes back to Vulgar Latin plattus, from Greek platýs 'broad' (EtymDB).

sommet comes from Old French somet (EtymDB), a small som 'top', from Latin summum 'highest point', from summus 'highest'.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: le col.
le col — "the mountain pass; the collar".
La route du col est fermée en hiver. The road over the pass is closed in winter.
Le col de la chemise — the shirt collar.

The word, taken apart.
col is inherited through Old French col 'neck' from Latin collum 'neck' (EtymDB): a low point between two mountains, like a neck.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le versant — the mountainside, the slope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the summit, then the French for the mountain pass.

You'll want to know: le versant.
le versant — "the mountainside, the slope".
Les vignes poussent sur le versant sud. The vines grow on the south-facing side.
Le versant nord — the north face.

The word, taken apart.
versant is the present participle of verser 'to pour, to tip', used as a noun (EtymDB lists it); verser goes back to Latin versāre, from vertere 'to turn' (Lewis & Short).
