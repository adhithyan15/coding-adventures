Urdu, chapter 420: Income and Budgets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
آمدنی (āmdanī) — an income.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an asset, then the Urdu for to pack up.

You'll want to know: آمدنی.
آمدنی — āmdanī — "an income".
اس کی آمدنی کم ہے۔ us kī āmdanī kam hai. He doesn't earn much.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
آمدنی (āmdanī) is usually traced to Persian āmadanī, "what comes in", from the verb for "to come".

Lesson 3 of 5.
کفایت (kifāyat) — thrift; economy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an income, then the Urdu for a budget.

You'll want to know: کفایت.
کفایت — kifāyat — "thrift; economy".
ہمیں کفایت سے خرچ کرنا چاہیے۔ hameṅ kifāyat se kharch karnā chāhiye. We should spend carefully.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
کفایت (kifāyat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic kifāya, "sufficiency, enough"; کفایت سے is "thriftily".

Lesson 4 of 5.
بانٹنا (bāṅṭnā) — to share out; to divide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a budget, then the Urdu for thrift.

You'll want to know: بانٹنا.
بانٹنا — bāṅṭnā — "to share out; to divide".
دونوں بھائیوں نے منافع بانٹا۔ donoṅ bhāiyoṅ ne munāfā bāṅṭā. The two brothers shared the profit.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے مٹھائی بانٹی, us ne miṭhāī bāṅṭī.

The word, taken apart.
بانٹنا (bāṅṭnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin bā̃ṭnā, "shares", under Sanskrit váṇṭati, "shares" (CDIAL 11238).

Lesson 5 of 5.
بھنانا (bhunānā) — to cash (a cheque); to change (a note).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for thrift, then the Urdu for to share out.

You'll want to know: بھنانا.
بھنانا — bhunānā — "to cash (a cheque); to change (a note)".
سارا نے بینک میں چیک بھنایا۔ sārā ne baink meṅ chek bhunāyā. Sara cashed a cheque at the bank.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے نوٹ بھنایا, us ne noṭ bhunāyā.

The word, taken apart.
بھنانا (bhunānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi bhunnā, "to be changed (of money)", and bhunānā under Sanskrit bhajyate, "is broken" (CDIAL 9361): a big note is broken into small ones.
