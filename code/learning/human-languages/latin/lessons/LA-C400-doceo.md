## You'll want to know: doceō, docēre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C400-DOCEO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**doceō, docēre** — "to teach".

> Magister puerōs litterās docet.
>
> *The teacher teaches the boys their letters.*

*doceō, docēre, docuī*; its participle follows in a later lesson. Two accusatives, the person and the thing: *puerōs litterās docet*. English *doctor*, first a teacher, and *docile*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*doceō* is inherited from Proto-Italic *\*dokeō* (EtymDB), usually taken as a causative beside *decet* "it is fitting": to make someone take a thing in. English *doctor* and *doctrine* come from it.
