Marathi, chapter 6: The Throat Row and the Palate Row.
8 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
ख (kha) — ka with the breath let out.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Say ka with your hand in front of your mouth. Now say it again and push the air out after it. That second sound has its own letter.

Script.
ख (kha).
ख is kha: क said with a puff of breath released after it. Marathi keeps the two apart the way English keeps pin and bin apart — swap them and you have a different word.
The shape is not क with a mark added; it is its own letter. Draw the left loop, then the upright with its foot, then the headline across the top. You will need it for khāṇe, "to eat".

Writing — observe and trace.
Trace ख twice with the model visible. Say ka, then kha, as your pen finishes each one, and listen for the puff that only the second has.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
ka or kha — which one moves the air? (kha.)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari kha stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Trace aspirated kha, then say what separates it from ka.


Lesson 2 of 8.
ग (ga) — the same place, with the voice on.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Put two fingers on your throat and say ka, then ga. The place in your mouth does not move. Something else switches on.

Script.
ग (ga).
ग is ga: the same back-of-the-mouth closure as क, made with the voice running. That is the only difference, and your fingers felt it a moment ago.
The shape is a single curve that swings down and left from the headline and finishes with a small tail on the right. It is the first letter of gāv, "village", and it sits inside sāṅgṇe, "to tell" — a verb this book uses long before it shows you this letter.

Writing — guided copy.
Copy ग (ga) twice with the model visible. Then write ख (kha) once from memory beside it, and check the two are not the same shape.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of ka and ga switches the voice on? (ga.)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari ga stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Copy ga, then write aspirated kha from memory.


Lesson 3 of 8.
घ (gha) — voice and breath together.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
You have the puff, and you have the voice. Now do both in one sound.

Script.
घ (gha).
घ is gha: voiced like ग (ga), breathy like ख (kha). Four letters now sit at the same place in your mouth, and only voice and breath tell them apart:
क plain, ख (kha) breath, ग (ga) voice, घ (gha) voice and breath.
You already say this letter. Kāḷjī ghyā, "take care", ends in it, and so does gheṇe, "to take". Draw the left bowl, then the right stem, then the headline.

Writing — delayed copy.
Look at घ (gha) for five seconds, cover it, and write it once. Uncover and check it against ग: the bowl on the left is what you are looking for.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Of ka, kha, ga, gha — which one has both? (gha.)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari gha stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Write gha after a delay, then name the two things it has that ka does not.


Lesson 4 of 8.
च (ca) — one letter, two Marathi sounds.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Move forward in your mouth: from the back, where ka lives, to the roof, right behind your teeth.

Script.
च (ca).
च is ca, close to English ch in church. Marathi does something Hindi does not: in many words it says this letter as ts, the sound at the end of English cats. Cār, "four", is one of them.
One letter, two pronunciations, and no mark on the page to tell you which — that is a thing you learn word by word. The shape is a bowl opening to the right, hung under the headline with a short stem.

Writing — guided copy.
Copy च twice with the model visible. Say ca on the first and tsa on the second, so the hand learns that one shape carries both.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many sounds does च carry in Marathi? (Two.)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari ca stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Copy ca, then name its second Marathi pronunciation.


Lesson 5 of 8.
छ (cha) — the palate, with the breath let out.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
The same pair you learned at the back of the mouth happens again at the roof of it.

Script.
छ (cha).
छ (cha) is chha: च (ca) with the breath released, exactly the step that took you from क to ख (kha). Devanagari does this all the way down the alphabet — plain, breathy, voiced, voiced-and-breathy — which is why learning one row teaches you how to hear the next.
The shape is a closed loop with a tail curving away below. It carries vicāraṇe in some spellings and stands at the head of chān, "nice".

Writing — observe and trace.
Trace छ (cha) twice, then trace च (ca) once beside it. Point at the closed loop: that is what the breath costs you on the page.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of च (ca) and छ (cha) releases the breath? (छ (cha).)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari cha stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Trace aspirated chha, then say which earlier pair it copies.


Lesson 6 of 8.
ज (ja) — voice at the palate.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Fingers on the throat again. Say ca, then ja. Same roof, voice on.

Script.
ज (ja).
ज is ja, and it shadows च (ca) exactly: where a word says च (ca) as ts, it usually says ज as dz. The two travel together.
You need it for jāṇe, "to go", and it hides inside kāḷjī, "care", in the phrase you already use for goodbye. Draw the top stroke, the middle knot, and the descending stem under the headline.

Writing — delayed copy.
Look at ज (ja) for five seconds, cover it, and write it once. Then retrieve the names of the two letters before it in this chapter.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What does ज (ja) become where च (ca) becomes ts? (dz.)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari ja stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Write ja after a delay, then retrieve the two preceding sign names.


Lesson 7 of 8.
झ (jha) — the letter inside mājhe.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
One more of the four-way sets, and this one closes a word you have been saying since your first introduction.

Script.
झ (jha).
झ is jha: voiced like ज (ja), breathy like छ (cha). The palate now has its full four, in the same order the back of your mouth did.
This is the letter sitting in the middle of mājhe, "my" — माझे — a word this book taught you to say long before it taught you to read it. Every sign in that word is now yours.

Writing — guided copy.
Copy माझे twice with the model visible, naming each sign as you form it: ma, long ā, jha, e-mark. This is the first whole word this runway has made writable.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which sign in माझे did you learn today? (झ (jha).)
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari jha stroke animation.

Lesson 8 of 8.
Two rows, told apart by voice and breath — cold retrieval of the velar and palatal rows by voice and breath.

Warm-up.
Two questions decide every letter in this chapter: is the voice on, and is the breath let out? Answer those and you have the row.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Back of the mouth: plain, breathy, voiced, voiced-and-breathy. (ka, kha, ga, gha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Roof of the mouth, same four steps. (ca, cha, ja, jha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of the eight sits inside mājhe? (jha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which one does Marathi also say as ts? (ca.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Reaching back one chapter, with nothing on the page: which hook goes deeper, short u or long ū? (ū.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which curl opens the other way? (The vocalic r.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which nasal mark wears a crescent? (The candrabindu.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And which vowel is already inside every consonant? (Short a.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 60 seconds]
Say the four velar letters in order, then the four palatal ones, naming for each whether voice and breath are on. Then reach back a chapter for the two below-the-line hooks, the vocalic-r curl, the crescent nasal, and the vowel every consonant hides.
