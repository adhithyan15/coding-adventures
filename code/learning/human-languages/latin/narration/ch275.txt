Latin, chapter 275: Silk, Mud, Dust, Smoke, Ash.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sēricum, sēricī — silk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for loss, then the Latin for a debt.

You'll want to know: sēricum, sēricī.
sēricum, sēricī — "silk".

The word, taken apart.
sēricum is built on Sērēs, "the Seres", the people of the far east whom the Romans knew as makers of silk (Lewis & Short): the Seric stuff. English silk probably comes from the same name.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lutum, lutī — mud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a debt, then the Latin for silk.

You'll want to know: lutum, lutī.
lutum, lutī — "mud".

The word, taken apart.
lutum is usually linked with luō, "to wash" (Lewis & Short), or with a root for dirt seen in polluō, "to soil"; the details are uncertain. It is a different word from lūtum, "a plant used for yellow dye".

Lesson 3 of 5.
pulvis, pulveris — dust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for silk, then the Latin for mud.

You'll want to know: pulvis, pulveris.
pulvis, pulveris — "dust".

The word, taken apart.
pulvis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pel-, "flour, dust" (EtymDB 2.1); pollen, "fine flour", is from the same root. English powder comes from pulvis through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fūmus, fūmī — smoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for mud, then the Latin for dust.

You'll want to know: fūmus, fūmī.
fūmus, fūmī — "smoke".

The word, taken apart.
fūmus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dhuh2mos, "smoke" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek thymos, "spirit, passion", is a cousin (Lewis & Short), as is English dust.
