Russian, chapter 51: Cup, Plate, Spoon, Knife, Fork.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
чашка (cháshka) — a cup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the bathroom, then the Russian for a clock.

You'll want to know: чашка.
чашка (cháshka) — "a cup". For чай or кофе.

The word, taken apart.
чашка (cháshka) is чаша, "a bowl, a goblet", with -ка, "little". чаша from Proto-Slavic čaša, which Vasmer thinks is probably related to Old Prussian kiosi, "a goblet".

The word, taken apart.
тарелка (tarélka) — older тарель, recorded from 1509; Vasmer thinks it apparently came through Polish talerz and Czech talíř from Middle High German talier, from Italian tagliere, "a trencher", from tagliare, "to cut".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ложка (lózhka) — a spoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cup, then the Russian for a plate.

You'll want to know: ложка.
ложка (lózhka) — "a spoon".

The word, taken apart.
ложка (lózhka) from Old Russian lŭžĭka, beside lŭžica, from Proto-Slavic lŭžĭka — inherited. Vasmer thinks it is probably related to Albanian lugë, "spoon", and perhaps to a root "to break, to split", since Swedish sked, "spoon", goes with a verb "to split".

Lesson 4 of 5.
нож (nozh) — a knife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a plate, then the Russian for a spoon.

You'll want to know: нож.
нож (nozh) — "a knife".

The word, taken apart.
нож (nozh) from Proto-Slavic nožĭ — inherited. Vasmer will not separate it from вонзить, "to thrust in", and заноза, "a splinter", and reports the usual link with Greek nýssō, "I prick": the knife as the thing that pierces.

Lesson 5 of 5.
вилка (vílka) — a fork.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a spoon, then the Russian for a knife.

You'll want to know: вилка.
вилка (vílka) — "a fork".

The word, taken apart.
вилка (vílka) is вилы, "a pitchfork", with -ка, "little": a little pitchfork. вилы from Proto-Slavic vidla, first "something bent", from the root of вить, "to twist" (Vasmer).
