Malayalam, chapter 203: Tasty, Rotten, Loose, Tight, Empty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
രുചിയുള്ള (ruciyuḷḷa) — tasty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a drawback, then the Malayalam for fairness.

You'll want to know: രുചിയുള്ള.
രുചിയുള്ള — ruciyuḷḷa — "tasty".

The word, taken apart.
രുചിയുള്ള (ruciyuḷḷa) joins Sanskrit ruci, "light, beauty", later "relish, taste" (CDIAL 10762), to native -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ചീഞ്ഞ (cīñña) — rotten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for fairness, then the Malayalam for tasty.

You'll want to know: ചീഞ്ഞ.
ചീഞ്ഞ — cīñña — "rotten".

The word, taken apart.
ചീഞ്ഞ (cīñña) is native Dravidian, a past form of the verb "to rot, be spoiled": DEDR 1606 lists that verb with Tamil cī, "pus", and Kannada kī, "to become pus".

The word, taken apart.
കാലിയായ (kāliyāya) joins kāli, "empty", from Persian khālī by way of Hindustani, to native āya, "having become" (DEDR 333). DEDR 333 compares Sanskrit ām.
