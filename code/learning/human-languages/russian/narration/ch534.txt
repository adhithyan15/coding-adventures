Russian, chapter 534: Mornings at Home.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
будить (budít) — to wake (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to burn out, then the Russian for insulating tape.

You'll want to know: будить.
будить (budít) — "to wake (someone)".
Каждое утро мама будит меня в семь. Kázhdoye útro máma búdit menyá v sem. Every morning Mum wakes me at seven.
Imperfective; present бужу, будишь (д becomes ж). Perfective разбудить.

The word, taken apart.
будить (budít) is inherited from Proto-Slavic buditi 'to awaken', which EtymDB traces to PIE bʰewdʰ- 'to awaken'.

You'll want to know: высыпаться.
высыпаться (vysypátsya) — "to get enough sleep".
В выходные я наконец высыпаюсь. V vykhodnýye ya nakonéts vysypáyus. At the weekend I finally get enough sleep.
Imperfective; perfective выспаться (высплюсь), with the stress on вы-.

The word, taken apart.
высыпаться (vysypátsya) is built on вы- and the root of спать 'to sleep', with -ся; EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 4 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to get enough sleep, then the Russian for to do one's hair.

You'll want to know: краситься.
краситься (krásitsya) — "to put on make-up".
Она не красится, когда сидит дома. Oná ne krásitsya, kogdá sidít dóma. She doesn't wear make-up when she's at home.
Imperfective; perfective накраситься. Also to dye one's hair. The reflexive of красить — to paint.

The word, taken apart.
краситься (krásitsya) is the reflexive of красить 'to paint', built on краска 'paint', from the root of краса 'beauty'; EtymDB has no entry for the reflexive.

Lesson 5 of 7.
халат (khalát) — a dressing gown; a white coat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to do one's hair, then the Russian for to put on make-up.

You'll want to know: халат.
халат (khalát) — "a dressing gown; a white coat".
Утром папа пьёт кофе в халате. Útrom pápa pyot kófe v khaláte. In the morning Dad drinks his coffee in his dressing gown.
Masculine. Also a doctor's or a cook's white coat.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB lists халат (khalát), glossed 'dressing gown; robe', without a source; it is usually traced through a Turkic language to Arabic xilʿa 'a robe of honour'.
