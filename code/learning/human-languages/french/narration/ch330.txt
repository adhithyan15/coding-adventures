5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bouillir — to boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the whisk, then the French for to whisk.

You'll want to know: bouillir.
bouillir — "to boil".
L'eau bout depuis cinq minutes. The water has been boiling for five minutes.
Irregular: l'eau bout, elle a bouilli. Faire bouillir — to bring to the boil.

The word, taken apart.
bouillir is inherited through Old French boillir from Latin bullīre "to bubble, to boil" (EtymDB), built on bulla "a bubble".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to boil, then the French for boiling.

You'll want to know: la marmite.
la marmite — "the cooking pot, the stockpot".
La soupe cuit dans une grande marmite. The soup is cooking in a big pot.
A large pot with a lid and two handles.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of marmite is not settled; an Old French adjective marmite "hypocritical" is listed beside it (EtymDB), perhaps a pot that hides what it holds.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mijoter — to simmer; to cook up (a plan).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for boiling, then the French for the cooking pot.

You'll want to know: mijoter.
mijoter — "to simmer; to cook up (a plan)".
La soupe mijote doucement. The soup is simmering gently.
Informal: qu'est-ce que tu mijotes? — what are you up to?

The word, taken apart.
The origin of mijoter is not settled; it is usually linked to an old dialect word for a place where fruit ripens (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la cocotte — the casserole dish, the cast-iron pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cooking pot, then the French for to simmer.

You'll want to know: la cocotte.
la cocotte — "the casserole dish, the cast-iron pot".
Elle fait cuire le poulet dans une cocotte. She cooks the chicken in a casserole dish.
La cocotte-minute — the pressure cooker. In baby talk, une cocotte is a hen.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of cocotte is not settled: one sense is a child's word for a hen, the other an older word for a pot (EtymDB lists no parent).
