5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
یخچال (yakhchâl) — a fridge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a frying pan, then the Persian for a stove.

You'll want to know: یخچال.
یخچال — yakhchâl — "a fridge".

The word, taken apart.
yakhchâl is yakh, "ice", with châl, "pit": the ice-pit, the old domed ice-house, now the fridge. Both parts are native Persian; yakh is usually traced to Middle Persian yax.

Lesson 2 of 5.
کبریت (kebrit) — matches.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a stove, then the Persian for a fridge.

You'll want to know: کبریت.
کبریت — kebrit — "matches".

The word, taken apart.
kebrit is borrowed from Arabic kibrīt, "sulphur" (EtymDB; root k-b-r-t, Lane); the first matches were sticks tipped with sulphur. Lane notes that some doubt the Arabic word is native Arabic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for matches, then the Persian for a ball.

You'll want to know: بادبادک.
بادبادک — bâdbâdak — "a kite".

The word, taken apart.
bâdbâdak is built on bâd, "wind", doubled, with the small-thing ending -ak: a little wind-thing. bâd is native, usually traced to Middle Persian wād.
