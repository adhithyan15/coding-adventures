## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C376-REF-70]; assesses=[] -->

**el pimiento** — "pepper."

*Pimiento* comes from **pimienta**, black pepper, and *pimienta* is Latin
**pigmenta** — the plural of **pigmentum**.

A *pigmentum* started out as colouring matter, the stuff a painter grinds. In
later Latin it slid: from colouring matter to **a drug**, from a drug to **an
ingredient**, from an ingredient to **a condiment**. And the condiment that
mattered most in medieval Europe was pepper. So a word that began in the
paint pot ended up meaning the spice.

It is tempting to shorten that to "the pepper is named for its colour." Do
not. That version takes the *earliest* sense of the Latin word, skips the
three steps that actually carried it, and then points at a red pepper as
though the colour were the proof. The dictionary's own gloss lays the route
out, and the route runs through the apothecary, not the paint.

English keeps the older sense alive in **pigment**, and **paint** is a
relative — both are built on *pingere*, "to paint."

One more turn. The pepper on a Spanish table is American, and it was named by
transfer: Europeans reached for the word they already had for the hot thing
you grind. *Pimienta* stayed with the peppercorn; *pimiento* went to the
newcomer.
