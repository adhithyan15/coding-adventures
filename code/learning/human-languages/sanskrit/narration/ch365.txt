Sanskrit, chapter 365: Theory and Practice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सिद्धान्तः (siddhāntaḥ) — a principle; a theory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an essay, then the Sanskrit for investigates.

You'll want to know: सिद्धान्तः.
सिद्धान्तः — siddhāntaḥ — "a principle; a theory".
सिद्धान्तः सरलः, किन्तु प्रयोगः कठिनः। siddhāntaḥ saralaḥ, kintu prayogaḥ kaṭhinaḥ. The principle is simple, but putting it into practice is hard.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सिद्धान्तः (siddhāntaḥ) is built from siddha, "established", and anta, "an end"; MW gives siddhānta "an established end" and "a demonstrated conclusion" (MW).

You'll want to know: तत्त्वम्.
तत्त्वम् — tattvam — "reality; the truth of a thing".
पण्डितः धर्मस्य तत्त्वं जानाति। paṇḍitaḥ dharmasya tattvaṃ jānāti. The scholar knows the true nature of dharma.

The word, taken apart.
तत्त्वम् (tattvam) is built from tat, "that", with the abstract ending -tva; MW gives tat-tva "true or real state, truth, reality" (MW).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: उपलक्षयति.
उपलक्षयति — upalakṣayati — "notices; observes".
वैज्ञानिकः नक्षत्राणि उपलक्षयति। vaijñānikaḥ nakṣatrāṇi upalakṣayati. The scientist observes the stars.
Past (he …): सः दोषम् उपलक्षितवान्, saḥ doṣam upalakṣitavān.

The word, taken apart.
उपलक्षयति (upalakṣayati) is built on upa, "towards", and the verb lakṣaya, "to mark"; MW gives upa-√lakṣ "to observe, to perceive" and "to pay attention to" (MW).
