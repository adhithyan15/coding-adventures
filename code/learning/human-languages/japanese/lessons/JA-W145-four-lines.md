# Four lines, and the joint you do not write

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C25-FAMILY25-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson asked for writing **finished on time**. This one
asks for writing that **holds together**.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one **ともだち** who will read it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C53-ADJ53-05, JA-LEX-C54-TIME54-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can
be connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that
a line stops standing on its own.

[PAUSE 2s] In many languages the joints are little words between the
sentences. This book has not taught you those for Japanese, and you do not
need them yet. Your four lines are held together by two things you already
own.

[PAUSE 2s] **The first is a time word at the front of each line.** **あさ**,
then **ゆうがた**: the reader follows your day because each line says where
in it they are.

[PAUSE 2s] **The second is the joint you do not write.** Chapter 138 taught
you that Japanese leaves out who does it when that is clear. Say who once,
or not at all, and every line after it leans on the one before. Name
yourself in every line and you have written a **list**, not a message.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C53-ADJ53-05, JA-LEX-C54-TIME54-01, JA-LEX-C138-OKIMASU, JA-LEX-C138-HATARAKIMASU, JA-LEX-C138-KAERIMASU, JA-LEX-C138-NEMASU, JA-LEX-C25-FAMILY25-01, JA-LEX-KONNICHIWA] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the **ともだち** you picked. The first greets
them with **こんにちは**. The other three walk through your day in order,
each opening on a time word and closing on a polite verb: **おきます**,
**はたらきます**, **かえります** or **ねます**.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no
vocabulary; the whole exercise is the joining.

[PAUSE 2s] End every line with **。**, a small open circle, not a dot.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C138-OKIMASU, JA-LEX-C138-NEMASU] -->
<!-- hl-activity: {"id":"JA-W145-four-lines-nemasu","kind":"text","assesses":["JA-LEX-C138-NEMASU"],"prompt":"Write the polite verb that can close the last line of your day: going to bed.","answer":"ねます","accepted":["nemasu"],"feedback":{"correct":"Right: ねます, a polite verb in ます, then 。.","incorrect":"The verb for going to bed is ねます."},"response_seconds":15} -->

[PAUSE 2s] Read your third line alone. It names no one: who comes home, or goes
to bed? Only the lines before it say. It leans on its neighbours, and
that is what connected means.

[PAUSE 2s] Then count how many lines name who does it. If every line does,
you wrote a list, which is where you were one lesson ago.
