French, chapter 319: Le réveillon.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le réveillon — the festive late-night dinner (24 or 31 December).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the garland, then the French for to decorate.

You'll want to know: le réveillon.
le réveillon — "the festive late-night dinner (24 or 31 December)".
Toute la famille passe le réveillon chez ma tante. The whole family spends the festive dinner at my aunt's.
It falls on the evenings of 24 and 31 December.

The word, taken apart.
réveillon is built on réveiller "to wake up", from veiller "to stay awake", which is inherited from Latin vigilāre "to keep watch" (Lewis & Short).

le sapin — "the fir tree; the Christmas tree".
Les enfants décorent le sapin. The children decorate the Christmas tree.
Decorated in December, often with des guirlandes.

The word, taken apart.
sapin is inherited from Latin sappīnus "a fir tree" (EtymDB), probably of Gaulish origin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bûche — the log; the yule log cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la bûche.
la bûche — "the log; the yule log cake".
Pour le dessert, nous avons mangé une bûche au chocolat. For dessert, we ate a chocolate yule log.
A rolled cake shaped like a log, eaten in December.

The word, taken apart.
bûche comes from Old French busche, inherited from Vulgar Latin busca "firewood" (EtymDB), usually traced to a Germanic word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la galette — the king cake; a flat round cake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the fir tree, then the French for the log.

You'll want to know: la galette.
la galette — "the king cake; a flat round cake".
En janvier, on mange la galette des rois. In January, people eat the king cake.
La galette des rois hides a small figure; whoever finds it wears the paper crown.

The word, taken apart.
galette is built on galet "a flat pebble", a diminutive of an old word that is usually traced to Gaulish (EtymDB lists no parent).
