# फ औ । — a sound that moved, a vowel that stands, a stroke that stops

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-PHA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The aspirated partner of **प**. Say its name, then say what its sound
has done that the other aspirated letters have not.

## Script: three marks, three different kinds of fact
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-PHA-01, MR-SCRIPT-AU-INDEPENDENT-01, MR-SCRIPT-DANDA] -->

| | what it is |
|---|---|
| **फ** | the gap in the lip row — and the one letter whose **sound has drifted** |
| **औ** | the standing **au**, last of the vowels that can begin a word alone |
| **।** | the upright stroke that ends a sentence, now largely replaced by a full stop |

Three different kinds of fact sit in that column, which is why they are worth
holding together rather than as three isolated shapes.

**फ** is a fact about **speech**: the letter records an aspirated *p*, and the
sound modern speakers make has moved away from it. **औ** is a fact about
**position**: a vowel needs its own standing letter when nothing carries it.
**।** is a fact about **convention**: a mark that is still correct and is quietly
losing ground to an imported one.

Drift, position, convention. A script old enough to have a history carries all
three at once, and none of them is a rule about pronunciation.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-PHA-01, MR-SCRIPT-AU-INDEPENDENT-01, MR-SCRIPT-DANDA] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which of the three is a fact about a sound that moved? (**फ**.) When does a vowel need a standing letter? (**When no consonant is carrying it.**) What does **।** do, and what is replacing it? (**Ends a sentence**; the **full stop**.) And what do the three have in common? (**Nothing** — drift, position and convention are three different kinds of fact, which is the point.)
