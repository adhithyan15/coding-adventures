## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C307-KIN-09]; assesses=[] -->

**el primo** --- "the cousin."

You have met **primus** before, in *primero*. It means "first," and it is the
root under English *prime*, *primary*, *premier* and *primitive*.

So why is a cousin called "first"? Because Latin said **consobrinus primus** ---
"first cousin," exactly as English still does. Spanish dropped the long word and
kept the adjective. What is left is a relative whose entire name is a ranking:
first.

English did the reverse and kept the long word. *Cousin* is worn down from
*consobrinus*, which is *con-* plus *sobrinus*, "sister's child." Both languages
took half of the same Latin phrase --- and they took different halves.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

An old root paying off again: *primero* and *primo* are the same word.
