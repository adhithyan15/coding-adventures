Mandarin Chinese, chapter 337: Best Wishes.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gōngxǐ — to congratulate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give a present, then the Mandarin for to shake hands with someone.

You'll want to know: gōngxǐ.
gōngxǐ — "to congratulate".
gōngxǐ nǐ zhǎodào le xīn gōngzuò — Congratulations on finding a new job.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gōngxǐ is built from gōng and 喜 'joy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjowng and xiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'respectful' and the second 'joy'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
祝 (zhù) — to wish someone well.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shake hands with someone, then the Mandarin for to congratulate someone.

You'll want to know: 祝.
祝 — zhù — "to wish someone well".
祝你生日快乐 zhù nǐ shēngrì kuàilè — Happy birthday to you!
The new character, in pieces: 祝 (zhù) — nine strokes: on the left a spirit piece of four strokes, a dot, a stroke that runs right and turns down to the left, a vertical and a dot; on the right 口 over 儿. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
祝 (zhù) is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it, quoting an older dictionary, as the words of praise spoken by the master of a sacrifice. The dictionary also lists tsyuwk, 'one who calls on the spirits'; the falling tone of zhù matches the first.

Script — the spirit piece, then 口 over 儿.
祝 (zhù).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the spirit piece, four strokes. A dot. A bar that runs right and turns down to the left. A vertical under it. Then a short dot at its right.
The right piece, five strokes. First 口: a short vertical, a bar that turns down, the bar that closes it. Under it 儿: a stroke falling to the left, then a vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up.
4 + 5 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 祝 (zhù), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 祝 (zhù)? (Nine.) What is on the right? (口 over 儿.) How does the spirit piece end? (With a short dot.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
心意 (xīnyì) — kind thoughts; a token of regard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to congratulate someone, then the Mandarin for to wish someone well.

You'll want to know: 心意.
心意 — xīnyì — "kind thoughts; a token of regard".
我们送你一个红包这是我们的一点儿心意 wǒmen sòng nǐ yí ge hóngbāo zhè shì wǒmen de yìdiǎnr xīnyì — We're giving you a red envelope, a small token of our regard.

The word, taken apart.
心意 (xīnyì) is built from 心 'the heart' and 意 'intent', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sim and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'the will'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
hǎoyùn — good luck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to wish someone well, then the Mandarin for a token of regard.

You'll want to know: hǎoyùn.
hǎoyùn — "good luck".
zǒu yǐqián dàjiā dōu zhù wǒ hǎoyùn — Before I left, everyone wished me good luck.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǎoyùn is built from 好 'good' and yùn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawX and hjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to move' and 'to carry'. The sense 'luck' is not given there.
