Arabic, chapter 498: Making others feel, and blame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أسعد (asʿada) — to make happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for hesitant, then the Arabic for to hold back.

You'll want to know: أسعد.
أسعد — asʿada — "to make happy".
أسعدت الهدية جدتي كثيرا. asʿadat al-hadiyya jaddatī kathīran. The present made my grandmother very happy.
Present: يسعد, yusʿid.

The word, taken apart.
أسعد (asʿada) is built on the native Arabic root s-a-d, "to be lucky, to be happy" (Lane). It is Form IV: to make someone saʿīd.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

Lesson 5 of 5.
لام (lāma) — to blame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to make laugh, then the Arabic for to make angry.

You'll want to know: لام.
لام — lāma — "to blame".
لامني المدير على التأخير. lāmanī al-mudīr ʿalā at-taʾkhīr. The manager blamed me for the delay.
Present: يلوم, yalūm.
It takes على for the fault.

The word, taken apart.
لام (lāma) is built on the native Arabic root l-w-m, "to blame, to censure" (Lane). Its middle root letter w shows as alif in the past and as ū in the present.
