Italian, chapter 373: A una condizione.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
purché — provided that, as long as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for plausible, then the Italian for the precondition.

You'll want to know: purché.
purché — "provided that, as long as".
Puoi uscire, purché tu sia a casa prima di mezzanotte. You can go out, as long as you are home before midnight.
Always followed by the subjunctive: purché tu sia, purché lui venga.

The word, taken apart.
purché is built from pure "simply, only", from Latin pūrē "purely" (Lewis & Short), on pūrus "clean, pure", and che "that": "only that".

Lesson 2 of 5.
qualora — if, in case (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the precondition, then the Italian for provided that.

You'll want to know: qualora.
qualora — "if, in case (formal)".
Qualora non foste soddisfatti, vi restituiamo i soldi. Should you not be satisfied, we will give you your money back.
Formal and written, followed by the subjunctive. In speech say se.

The word, taken apart.
qualora is built from quale "which", from Latin quālis, and ora "hour", from Latin hōra (Lewis & Short): "at whatever hour", and so "if ever".

Lesson 3 of 5.
altrimenti — otherwise, or else.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for provided that, then the Italian for if.

You'll want to know: altrimenti.
altrimenti — "otherwise, or else".
Sbrigati, altrimenti perdiamo l'autobus. Hurry up, otherwise we will miss the bus.
Neutral in speech and writing.

The word, taken apart.
altrimenti is built from altro "other" and the adverb ending -mente (EtymDB); altro is inherited from Latin alter "the other", and -mente from mēns, mentis "mind": "in another way".

Lesson 4 of 5.
sennò — or else, otherwise (casual).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for if, then the Italian for otherwise.

You'll want to know: sennò.
sennò — "or else, otherwise (casual)".
Mettiti la giacca, sennò hai freddo. Put your jacket on, or else you will be cold.
Spoken and informal; also written se no.

The word, taken apart.
sennò is se no "if not" written as one word: se is inherited from Latin sī "if" and no from Latin nōn "not" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
eventuale — possible, any (that may arise).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for otherwise, then the Italian for or else.

You'll want to know: eventuale.
eventuale — "possible, any (that may arise)".
Per eventuali domande, scrivete alla segreteria. For any questions, write to the office.
It means possible, not final: eventualmente is if need be, not eventually.

The word, taken apart.
eventuale is usually traced to French éventuel, built on Latin ēventus "an outcome, an event" (Lewis & Short), from ēvenīre "to come out, to happen", on venīre "to come".
