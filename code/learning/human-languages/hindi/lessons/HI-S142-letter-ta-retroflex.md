# ट — one run, then the headline

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-139] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: **फ** — how many pen lifts did it take?

[PAUSE 2s] This one takes fewer.

## Script you'll notice: ट
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-142]; assesses=[] -->

**ट** — *ṭa*.

What it is made of:

- a descending central stem joined to a counterclockwise **open** round body
- the top shirorekhā

The word *open* is doing real work there. The round part comes back up toward
the stem and **stops short of meeting it**, leaving a gap at the upper right.
Keep that gap when you draw it.

## Script: the tongue goes back
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-142] -->

**ट** is a **retroflex**: the tongue curls back and taps the roof of the mouth,
further back than it goes for **त**. Two different letters for what an English
ear hears as one *t*, and Hindi uses both.

Words that were yours long before this page:

- **रोटी** — bread
- **घंटा** — an hour

## Writing: ट
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-142] -->

- **1.** start at the central stem's headline junction, descend, turn left through the upper shoulder, and sweep counterclockwise around the open round body to its up-right tip without lifting
- **2.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 1.** Everything below the headline is one unbroken run, and only
**ढ** is built the same way.

> Verified two-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline;
> everyday handwriting may narrow or simplify the curve differently.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein,
