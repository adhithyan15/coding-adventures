Arabic, chapter 339: Invasions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
غزا (ghazā) — to invade, to raid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a cemetery, then the Arabic for a coffin.

You'll want to know: غزا.
غزا — ghazā — "to invade, to raid".
غزا الجيش البلاد المجاورة. ghazā al-jaysh al-bilād al-mujāwira. The army invaded the neighbouring lands.
Present: يغزو, yaghzū.
Of space: غزو الفضاء, the conquest of space.

The word, taken apart.
غزا (ghazā) is built on the native Arabic root gh-z-w (Lane), "to aim at, to seek", and "to go out to fight"; ghazw is a raid.

هاجم (hājama) is built on the native Arabic root h-j-m (Lane), "to come upon someone suddenly". It is Form III, "to rush upon an enemy".

Lesson 4 of 5.
قاد (qāda) — to lead; to drive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to attack, then the Arabic for to occupy.

You'll want to know: قاد.
قاد — qāda — "to lead; to drive".
قاد أبي السيارة طوال الليل. qāda abī as-sayyāra ṭuwāla al-layl. My father drove the car all night.
Present: يقود, yaqūd.
Of an army: قاد (qāda) الجيش, he led the army.

The word, taken apart.
قاد (qāda) is built on the native Arabic root q-w-d (Lane), "to lead a horse or camel by its rope"; a car is led the same way, so qāda is also to drive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

انسحب اللاعب من المباراة. insaḥaba al-lāʿib min al-mubārāh. The player pulled out of the match.
Present: ينسحب, yansaḥib.

The word, taken apart.
انسحب (insaḥaba) is built on the native Arabic root s-ḥ-b (Lane), "to drag along", the root of the taught saḥaba. It is Form VII, "to be drawn back".
