Urdu, chapter 334: A Heated Argument.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بحث کرنا (bahas karnā) — to argue; to debate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a benefit, then the Urdu for to use.

You'll want to know: بحث کرنا.
بحث کرنا — bahas karnā — "to argue; to debate".
وہ دونوں ہر بات پر بحث کرتے ہیں۔ voh donoṅ har bāt par bahas karte haiṅ. Those two argue about everything.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے استاد سے بحث کی, us ne ustād se bahas kī.

The word, taken apart.
بحث کرنا (bahas karnā) is built from bahas, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic baḥth, "an inquiry, a discussion", and کرنا, "to do".

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to argue, then the Urdu for a reasoned argument.

You'll want to know: ٹوکنا.
ٹوکنا — ṭoknā — "to interrupt; to cut in".
میں بات کر رہا تھا اور اس نے مجھے ٹوکا۔ maiṅ bāt kar rahā thā aur us ne mujhe ṭokā. I was speaking and he cut me off.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے مجھے ٹوکا, us ne mujhe ṭokā.

The word, taken apart.
ٹوکنا (ṭoknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin ṭoknā, "to check, to question", under ṭōkk, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5476).

Lesson 4 of 5.
تکرار (takrār) — an altercation; a row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a reasoned argument, then the Urdu for to interrupt.

You'll want to know: تکرار.
تکرار — takrār — "an altercation; a row".
دکان پر گاہک اور دکاندار میں تکرار ہوئی۔ dukān par gāhak aur dukāndār meṅ takrār huī. A customer and the shopkeeper had a row in the shop.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
تکرار (takrār) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic takrār, "repetition"; the sense "a row", words thrown back and forth, is Urdu's own, as far as our sources show.
