# अ- … -त् — the past, reviewed

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-PAST-AUGMENT-01, SA-LEX-C17-TIME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **ह्यः**, then say "yesterday the boy went".

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-PAST-PATTERN-01, SA-LEX-C17-TIME-03, SA-LEX-SHVAH, SA-LEX-C17-TIME-04] -->

Three time words have been sitting in this book waiting for a verb.

- **ह्यः** — yesterday, from the time words
- **अद्य** — today, from the time words
- **श्वः** — tomorrow, learnt with your first farewell

Until this chapter only one of the three could be used: **अद्य**, with the
present. The other two named times the language could not talk about. **अद्य**
and the present, **ह्यः** and the past — two of the three now work, and the
third waits on a tense this book has not reached.

That is worth naming plainly rather than glossing over. A track can teach a word
and leave it unusable, and the only way to notice is to check what the learner
can actually build with it.
