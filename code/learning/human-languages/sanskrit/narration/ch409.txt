Sanskrit, chapter 409: Wheels and Tracks.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
अक्षः (akṣaḥ) — an axle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an elephant goad, then the Sanskrit for a stable.

You'll want to know: अक्षः.
अक्षः — akṣaḥ — "an axle".
शकटस्य अक्षः दृढः अस्ति। śakaṭasya akṣaḥ dṛḍhaḥ asti. The cart's axle is strong.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अक्षः (akṣaḥ) is the word MW gives for an axle; MW gives no derivation within Sanskrit, so its origin is uncertain; MW compares Latin axis and Greek axōn (MW).

Lesson 2 of 6.
नेमिः (nemiḥ) — a wheel's rim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a stable, then the Sanskrit for an axle.

You'll want to know: नेमिः.
नेमिः — nemiḥ — "a wheel's rim".
रथस्य नेमिः दृढा अस्ति। rathasya nemiḥ dṛḍhā asti. The chariot wheel's rim is strong.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नेमिः (nemiḥ) comes from the root √nam, "to bend", which also gives namati, "bows"; MW gives nemi as the felly or rim of a wheel (MW).

Lesson 4 of 6.
भ्रमयति (bhramayati) — sets spinning; turns round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wheel's rim, then the Sanskrit for a spoke.

You'll want to know: भ्रमयति.
भ्रमयति — bhramayati — "sets spinning; turns round".
बालः चक्रं भ्रमयति। bālaḥ cakraṃ bhramayati. The boy sets the wheel spinning.
Past (he …): सः चक्रं भ्रमितवान्, saḥ cakraṃ bhramitavān.

The word, taken apart.
भ्रमयति (bhramayati) is built on the root √bhram, "to wander, to turn", which gives bhramati, "wanders"; MW gives the causative participle bhramita as made to go round, whirled round (MW).

Lesson 5 of 6.
वर्त्म (vartma) — a wheel track; a rut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a spoke, then the Sanskrit for sets spinning.

You'll want to know: वर्त्म.
वर्त्म — vartma — "a wheel track; a rut".
पंके शकटस्य गभीरं वर्त्म अस्ति। paṅke śakaṭasya gabhīraṃ vartma asti. There is a deep cart track in the mud.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
वर्त्म (vartma) comes from the root √vṛt, "to turn, to roll", which also gives vartate, "is present"; MW gives vartman as the track or rut of a wheel, a path, a road (MW).
