Latin, chapter 413: The Verdict.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
condemnō, condemnāre — to condemn, to find guilty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an informer, then the Latin for a defence.

You'll want to know: condemnō, condemnāre.
condemnō, condemnāre — "to condemn, to find guilty".
Iūdicēs eum furtī condemnāvērunt. The judges found him guilty of theft.
condemnō, condemnāre, condemnāvī, condemnātum, with the charge in the genitive. English condemn.

The word, taken apart.
condemnō is built from con- and damnō "to condemn" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from damnum "a loss, a fine". English condemn comes from it.
