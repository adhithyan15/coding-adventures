Hindi, chapter 338: Fairies and Ghosts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
परी (parī) — a fairy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a jewel, then the Hindi for to bite (of a snake).

You'll want to know: परी.
परी — parī — "a fairy".
कहानी में एक परी आसमान से आई। kahānī meṁ ek parī āsmān se āī. In the story a fairy came down from the sky.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
is usually traced to Persian parī, "a fairy", a winged spirit of Persian tales. A very beautiful girl is also called a परी.

Lesson 3 of 5.
भूत (bhūt) — a ghost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a fairy, then the Hindi for magic.

You'll want to know: भूत.
भूत — bhūt — "a ghost".
लोग कहते थे कि उस पुराने महल में भूत रहता है। log kahte the ki us purāne mahal meṁ bhūt rahtā hai. People used to say that a ghost lived in that old palace.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
भूत (bhūt) is Sanskrit bhūta, "what has been", and so a being and a spirit (Monier-Williams). The same word means the past in grammar; in stories it is a ghost.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for magic, then the Hindi for a ghost.

You'll want to know: डायन.
डायन — ḍāyan — "a witch".
कहानी की डायन जंगल में अकेली रहती थी। kahānī kī ḍāyan jaṅgal meṁ akelī rahtī thī. The witch in the story lived alone in the forest.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
डायन (ḍāyan) goes back to Sanskrit ḍākinī, a female attendant of the goddess Kālī (CDIAL 5542), where CDIAL lists Hindi ḍāin and ḍākin: in village tales, a witch.
