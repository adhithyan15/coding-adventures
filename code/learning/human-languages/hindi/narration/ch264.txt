Hindi, chapter 264: A sitar, A tabla, A bell, A toy, A doll.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सितार (sitār) — a sitar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an anklet, then the Hindi for a flute.

You'll want to know: सितार.
सितार — sitār — "a sitar".

The word, taken apart.
सितार (sitār) is a loan, usually traced to Persian sitār, "three strings". It is built from sih, "three", and tār, "string".

The word, taken apart.
तबला (tablā) is a loan from Arabic ṭabl, "a drum", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ṭ-b-l (Lane).

घंटी — ghaṇṭī — "a bell".

The word, taken apart.
घंटी (ghaṇṭī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghaṇṭikā, "small bell" (CDIAL 4421). It is built on ghaṇṭā, "bell".

खिलौना — khilaunā — "a toy".

The word, taken apart.
खिलौना (khilaunā) is built on khel-, "play", with the ending -aunā: a thing to play with. CDIAL lists the verb under a reconstructed khel-, "play" (CDIAL 3918).
