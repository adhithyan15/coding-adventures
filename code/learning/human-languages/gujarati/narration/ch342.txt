Gujarati, chapter 342: A Dispute.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વિવાદ (vivād) — a dispute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a context, then the Gujarati for to raise a point.

You'll want to know: વિવાદ.
વિવાદ — vivād — "a dispute".
મિલકત માટે બે ભાઈઓ વચ્ચે વિવાદ થયો. milkat māṭe be bhāīo vacce vivād thayo. A dispute arose between two brothers over property.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વિવાદ (vivād) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit vivāda, "a dispute, a quarrel" (Monier-Williams).

You'll want to know: તકરાર.
તકરાર — takrār — "a squabble".
બાળકો રમકડા માટે તકરાર કરતાં હતાં. bāḷko ramakḍā māṭe takrār kartā̃ hatā̃. The children were squabbling over a toy.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
તકરાર (takrār) is usually traced to Arabic takrār, "repetition", through Persian; Gujarati uses it for a petty quarrel, a sense the donor word does not have.

મતભેદ — matbhed — "a disagreement".
આ યોજના પર અરુણ અને મીરા વચ્ચે મતભેદ છે. ā yojnā par Aruṇ ane Mīrā vacce matbhed che. Arun and Mira disagree about this plan.

The word, taken apart.
મતભેદ (matbhed) is built from મત (mat), "an opinion", and Sanskrit bheda, "a split, a difference" (Monier-Williams): a split of opinion.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ટોકવું (ṭokvũ) — to cut someone short.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a disagreement, then the Gujarati for a refutation.

You'll want to know: ટોકવું.
ટોકવું — ṭokvũ — "to cut someone short".
અરુણ દરેક વખતે મીરાને ટોકે છે. Aruṇ darek vakhte Mīrāne ṭoke che. Arun cuts Mira short every time.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે છોકરાને ટોક્યો, teṇe chokrāne ṭokyo.

The word, taken apart.
ટોકવું (ṭokvũ) is inherited from ṭōkk, "strike, bite", a form CDIAL reconstructs; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to scold" (CDIAL 5476), and today it is said of cutting a speaker short.
