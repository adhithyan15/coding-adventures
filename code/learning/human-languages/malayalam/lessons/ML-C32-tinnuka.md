# തിന്നുക (tinnuka) — "to eat," and the ending that names a verb's homeland

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-VARUKA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Malayalam took in a great deal of Sanskrit. Eating is one of the
places it never reached.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-TINNUKA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **തിന്നുക** — *tinnuka* — **to eat**

> **ഉണ്ണുക** — *uṇṇuka* — **to eat a meal, to dine**

Two inherited verbs, both alive. *Tinnuka* is eating in general. *Uṇṇuka* is
sitting down to a proper meal, and gives the noun **ഊണ്**
(*ūṇŭ*), "lunch." The tenses are regular: *tinnunnu*, *tinnu*, *tinnuṁ*.

## Sounds you'll need: the letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**തി** (*ti*) + **ന്നു** (a doubled *nn* with *u*) + **ക** (*ka*) →
**തിന്നുക**, with the long *n* you held in *vannu*. In **ഉണ്ണുക** that doubled
letter is retroflex **ണ്ണ** (*ṇṇ*): *uṇ-ṇu-ka*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**തിന്നുക** goes back to Dravidian *tiṉ-*, and the family kept it: Telugu
**తిను** (*tinu*), Kannada **ತಿನ್ನು** (*tinnu*).

Tamil kept *tiṉ* too, as **தின்**, but narrowed it to chewing and built a **new**
everyday word from two of its own:
**சாப்பிடு** (*sāppiḍu*) is *sāppu* ("food") plus *iḍu* ("to put"). Put food.
Tamil's ordinary eat-verb is home-made; Malayalam's is the family's original.

## Grammar Lens: -ഉക and -ഇക്കുക, two doors into the verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-TINNUKA-02]; assesses=[] -->

Every verb so far has ended in **-ഉക** (*-uka*): *tinnuka*, *pōkuka*, *varuka*,
*irikkuka*. Chapter 5's "to speak" did not — it was **സംസാരിക്കുക**
(*saṁsārikkuka*), with a longer **-ഇക്കുക** (*-ikkuka*). That difference is a
passport stamp:

| ending | what it marks |
|---|---|
| **-ഉക** *-uka* | an inherited Dravidian verb |
| **-ഇക്കുക** *-ikkuka* | a Sanskrit word made into a verb |

*Saṁsārikkuka* is built on Sanskrit *saṁsāra*; Chapter 9's **ക്ഷമിക്കുക**
(*kṣamikkuka*, "to forgive") on *kṣamā*. Malayalam needed one dependable way to
make a verb of any borrowed noun, and **-ഇക്കുക** is it: a naturalisation
ceremony you can hear.
