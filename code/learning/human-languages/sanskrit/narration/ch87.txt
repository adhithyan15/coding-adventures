Sanskrit, chapter 87: Eleven to a Thousand.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
एकादश (ekādaśa) — eleven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for time, then the Sanskrit for a moment.
[pause 1 second]
Say six to ten again — ṣaṭ, sapta, aṣṭa, nava, daśa — and hear their English partners: six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

You'll want to know: एकादश.
एकादश (ekādaśa) — "eleven": एक, "one", with दश, "ten". One and ten.

The word, taken apart.
Ekādaśa is built from eka, "one", and daśa, "ten", both inherited numerals (MW). It lives on as Hindi igārah, "eleven" (CDIAL 2485).

What you've built.
One plus ten.

Lesson 2 of 5.
द्वादश (dvādaśa) — twelve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a moment, then the Sanskrit for eleven.

You'll want to know: द्वादश.
द्वादश (dvādaśa) — "twelve": द्वा-, "two", with दश. The twelve months of a संवत्सरः.

The word, taken apart.
Dvādaśa is built from dvā, the old form of "two", and daśa, "ten" (MW). It lives on as Hindi bārah, "twelve" (CDIAL 6658).

What you've built.
Two plus ten.

Lesson 3 of 5.
विंशतिः (viṃśatiḥ) — twenty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for eleven, then the Sanskrit for twelve.

You'll want to know: विंशतिः.
विंशतिः (viṃśatiḥ) — "twenty", a new word, not built from दश.

The word, taken apart.
MW takes viṃśati as probably "two decades", and it is inherited: MW compares Latin vīgintī and Greek eikosi. It lives on as Hindi bīs, "twenty" (CDIAL 11616).

What you've built.
A fresh word for two tens.

Lesson 4 of 5.
शतम् (śatam) — a hundred.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for twelve, then the Sanskrit for twenty.

You'll want to know: शतम्.
शतम् (śatam) — "a hundred". The English word hundred is its distant cousin.

The word, taken apart.
Śata is inherited: MW compares Latin centum, Greek hekaton and the hund- of English hundred. It lives on as Hindi sau, "hundred" (CDIAL 12278).

What you've built.
Ten tens.

Lesson 5 of 5.
सहस्रम् (sahasram) — a thousand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for twenty, then the Sanskrit for a hundred.

You'll want to know: सहस्रम्.
सहस्रम् (sahasram) — "a thousand", ten शतम् (śatam).

The word, taken apart.
MW suggests sahasra is sa- "one" with hasra, the cousin of Greek khilioi, "thousand", and compares Persian hazār (CDIAL 13307). The Persian word is a sister of the Sanskrit one, not borrowed from it.

What you've built.
Ten hundreds.
