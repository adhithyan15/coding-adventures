Latin, chapter 330: Fūnus.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
fūnus, fūneris — a funeral; death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to die, then the Latin for to depart.

You'll want to know: fūnus, fūneris.
fūnus, fūneris — "a funeral; death".
Fūnus māne per forum dūcēbātur. In the morning the funeral was being led through the forum.
Neuter, 3rd declension. The funerals of great men passed through the forum with music. English funeral.

The word, taken apart.
fūnus is a Latin word whose origin is not settled; Lewis & Short link it, with doubt, with a word for smoke. English funeral comes from its family.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to depart, then the Latin for a funeral.

You'll want to know: sepeliō, sepelīre.
sepeliō, sepelīre — "to bury".
Mīlitēs sociōs suōs prope viam sepelīvērunt. The soldiers buried their comrades near the road.
sepeliō, sepelīre, sepelīvī, sepultum. Graves lay outside the city walls, along the roads.

The word, taken apart.
sepeliō is an inherited verb whose earlier origin is not settled (Lewis & Short, EtymDB). English sepulchre belongs to its family.

You'll want to know: sepultūra, sepultūrae.
sepultūra, sepultūrae — "burial".
Sepultūra in sepulcrō familiae facta est. The burial took place in the family tomb.
Feminine, from sepultum, the supine of sepeliō. English sepulture.

The word, taken apart.
sepultūra is built on sepultus, the participle of sepeliō, "to bury" (EtymDB), with the ending -ūra.

You'll want to know: rogus, rogī.
rogus, rogī — "a funeral pyre".
Fīlius rogum patris accendit. The son lit his father's pyre.
Masculine. The pyre was built of wood outside the walls; the ash was gathered into an urna (taught).

The word, taken apart.
rogus is inherited; EtymDB links it with a Proto-Indo-European root, but the link is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
exsequiae, exsequiārum — a funeral procession, funeral rites.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for burial, then the Latin for a funeral pyre.

You'll want to know: exsequiae, exsequiārum.
exsequiae, exsequiārum — "a funeral procession, funeral rites".
Multī cīvēs exsequiās cōnsulis secūtī sunt. Many citizens followed the consul's funeral procession.
Feminine, plural: those who follow the body out. English exequies.

The word, taken apart.
exsequiae is built on exsequor, "to follow out" (Lewis & Short), from ex and sequor, "to follow": the following of the dead.
