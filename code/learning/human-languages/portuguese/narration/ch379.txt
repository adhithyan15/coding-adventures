Portuguese, chapter 379: Passar ou chumbar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
chumbar — to fail (an exam); to fill (a tooth).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to learn by heart, then the Portuguese for the test.

You'll want to know: chumbar.
chumbar — "to fail (an exam); to fill (a tooth)".
Chumbei no exame de condução. I failed my driving test.
European Portuguese, informal; in Brazil reprovar or ser reprovado. Chumbar um aluno — to fail a student.

The word, taken apart.
chumbar first meant "to weight or seal with lead" and is built on chumbo "lead", inherited from Latin plumbum "lead" (Lewis & Short); how it came to mean "to fail" is not settled.

aprovar comes from Latin approbāre "to regard as good, to approve" (EtymDB), built from ad and probāre "to esteem as good, to prove" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a média — the average (mark).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to pass, then the Portuguese for the assessment.

You'll want to know: a média.
a média — "the average (mark)".
Ela acabou o curso com média de dezassete. She finished the degree with an average of seventeen.
In Portugal marks go from 0 to 20; a média is your average. Em média — on average.

The word, taken apart.
média comes from Latin media, the feminine of medius "middle" (Lewis & Short): the middle value.

Lesson 5 of 5.
a licenciatura — the bachelor's degree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the assessment, then the Portuguese for the average.

You'll want to know: a licenciatura.
a licenciatura — "the bachelor's degree".
Ela tem uma licenciatura em economia. She has a degree in economics.
In Portugal, a three-year first degree; licenciado — graduate.

The word, taken apart.
licenciatura is built on licenciar "to license, to grant a degree", on licença "permission", from Latin licentia "freedom, licence", on licēre "to be allowed" (Lewis & Short).
