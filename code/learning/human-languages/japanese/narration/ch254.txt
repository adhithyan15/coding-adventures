Japanese, chapter 254: What I Think, and Why.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
と おもいます (to omoimasu) — I think that …

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to think", and "I think", politely. (omou, omoimasu.) Then say, plainly, "it will rain" and "he won't come". (ame ga furu, konai.) Those plain forms are about to get their second job.

You'll want to know: a plain clause, then と.
いいと おもいます。 ii to omoimasu. — "I think it is good."
What you think goes first, in the plain form; と (to) closes it, and おもいます (omoimasu) keeps the whole sentence polite.
あした あめが ふると おもいます。 ashita ame ga furu to omoimasu. — "I think it will rain tomorrow."
The plain "not" and the plain past go before と the same way:
ともだちは こないと おもいます。 tomodachi wa konai to omoimasu. — "I don't think my friend will come."
ちちは うちに かえったと おもいます。 chichi wa uchi ni kaetta to omoimasu. — "I think my father has gone home."

You'll want to know: どう おもいますか.
To ask what someone thinks, どう (dō), "how", goes where the opinion would be:
どう おもいますか。 dō omoimasu ka. — "What do you think?"
この えいがを どう おもいますか。 kono eiga o dō omoimasu ka. — "What do you think of this film?"
おもしろいと おもいます。 omoshiroi to omoimasu. — "I think it is interesting."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I think it is interesting" — omoshiroi to omoimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think it is good" — ii to omoimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think it is dangerous" — abunai to omoimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that you think it will rain tomorrow.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I think he will come"? (くると おもいます.) "I don't think he will come"? (こないと おもいます.) "What do you think?" (どう おもいますか.) Which form goes before と? (The plain one.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
から / ので (kara / node) — because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I don't think my friend will come". (tomodachi wa konai to omoimasu.) Then say "cold" and "a reason". (samui, riyū.)

You'll want to know: から after a clause.
さむいから、いきません。 samui kara, ikimasen. — "It is cold, so I won't go."
から (kara) after a clause gives the reason, and the result follows it. The clause before it takes a plain form:
じかんが ないから、いそぎます。 jikan ga nai kara, isogimasu. — "I have no time, so I will hurry."
あめが ふったから、いきませんでした。 ame ga futta kara, ikimasen deshita. — "It rained, so I did not go."

You'll want to know: ので, and りゆうは …からです.
ので (node) gives a reason too, more softly; it is the one to use when asking for something or excusing yourself:
あたまが いたいので、やすみます。 atama ga itai node, yasumimasu. — "My head hurts, so I will rest."
When someone asks why, りゆうは (riyū wa), "the reason is", opens the answer, and からです closes it:
りゆうは じかんが ないからです。 riyū wa jikan ga nai kara desu. — "The reason is that I have no time."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "My head hurts, so I will rest" — atama ga itai node, yasumimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My stomach hurts, so I will rest" — onaka ga itai node, yasumimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My foot hurts, so I will rest" — ashi ga itai node, yasumimasu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that it is cold, so you won't go.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which comes first, the reason or the result? (The reason, with から or ので.) "The reason is that I have no time"? (りゆうは じかんが ないからです.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
言い出す (iidasu) — to suggest; to be the first to say.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: You'll want to know: 言い出す.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "to say" and "to take out". (iu, dasu.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
You'll want to know: 言い出す.
言い出す — iidasu — "to suggest", "to be the first to say". Polite 言い出します, iidashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが りょこうを 言い出しました, chichi ga ryokō o iidashimashita.
ちちが りょこうを 言い出しました。 chichi ga ryokō o iidashimashita. — "My father suggested a trip."
It is 言う with だす, "to take out", after it: to bring an idea out first. The first kanji is the 言 you write. The second is new, read だ (da) here; the next lesson writes it.

The word, taken apart.
言い出す (iidasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the stem of 言う (iu), "to say", and だす (dasu), "to take out", each written with its kanji and read the native way. JMdict gives "to begin to say" and "to suggest (doing)" among its senses, and KANJIDIC2 gives い.う and だ.す as the two kanji's native readings (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My mother suggested a trip" — haha ga ryokō o iidashimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'to suggest'.

Wrap-up Recall.

"To suggest"? (言い出す, iidasu.) Its polite past? (言い出しました.) How is the new kanji read here? (だ, da.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
出 (da) — out, five strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say iidasu, to suggest. Its first kanji is 言, which you write. Then call up 年: six strokes, the long stem last.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the shape.
出 — da.
Its parts:
two open boxes, one stacked on the other.
one long centre stem through both.
Five strokes, four lifts:
draw the long centre stem from the top down.
lift, draw the upper left side down, then without lifting turn right along the base of the upper part.
lift, draw the upper right side down.
lift, draw the lower left side down, then without lifting turn right along the bottom.
lift, draw the lower right side down.
Unlike 年, the long stem comes first here, and the four sides hang from it. Here it is read だ (da), as in 言い出す (iidasu); this lesson asks only for that reading.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
Find 出 (da) in 言い出す and say iidasu.
Copy 出 three times, saying da at each one.
Find it once more, in 言い出しました (iidashimashita), and say the word.

Wrap-up Recall.
How many strokes in all? (Five.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
いけん (iken) — an opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "What do you think?" (dō omoimasu ka.) Then "my father suggested a trip". (chichi ga ryokō o iidashimashita.)

You'll want to know: いけん.
いけん — iken — "an opinion", what someone thinks.
いけんは ありますか。 iken wa arimasu ka. — "Do you have an opinion?"
ちちの いけんを ききました。 chichi no iken o kikimashita. — "I asked my father's opinion."

The word, taken apart.
いけん (iken) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are i, "idea" (Middle Chinese 'iH) and ken, "see" (kenH), which KANJIDIC2 also glosses "opinion" (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict glosses the word "opinion; view; comment" (JMdict).
