## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-SKILL-READ-SENTENCES]; assesses=[LA-PRAGMATICS-QUID-AGIS-01, LA-LEX-VALEO-FAMILY-01, LA-LEX-QUID-TIBI-NOMEN-01] -->

> Salvē!
> Quid agis?
> Valeō.
> Grātiās tibi agō.
> Mihi nōmen est Mārcus.
> Valē!

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and this time watch the last letter of **agis**, **valeō**
and **agō**.

## Grammar Lens: the person rides on the verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-GRATIAS-03, LA-SKILL-READ-SENTENCES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Latin does not need to write **ego**, “I,” before **valeō** or
**agō**. The **-ō** already says who acts. In **agis**, the **-s** points to
“you.”

[PAUSE 2s] That makes a Latin line short without making it incomplete. A reader
looks to the verb's right edge for a person English usually puts at the left.
