## You'll want to know: ধীরে
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C19-DHIRE-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C05-BOLA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C13-FUTURE-POLITE-02] -->

> **ধীরে বলবেন** — *dhīre bôlben* — **please speak slowly.**

> **ধীরে ধীরে বলবেন** — *dhīre dhīre bôlben* — the same thing, said the way Bengali actually says it.

The doubling is not emphasis for a foreigner's benefit. Bengali turns an adverb
up by repeating it, and **ধীরে ধীরে** is the ordinary form of this word in
speech; the single **ধীরে** is what a dictionary lists.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-II-MATRA-01, BN-LEX-C19-DHIRE-01] -->

**ধীরে** is Sanskrit *dhīra*, which meant **steady, composed, deliberate** long
before it meant *slow*. The word is about the manner of a person, and only then
about the speed of a thing — which is why asking somebody to speak *dhīre* is a
gentler request than asking them to speak slowly in English.

Its second letter carries the long *i*-sign chapter twenty-four taught on
**নীল**. Four pieces, all of them already read.
