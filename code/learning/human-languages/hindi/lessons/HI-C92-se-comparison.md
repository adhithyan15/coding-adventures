# से बड़ा — bigger, with no word for *-er*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-01, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have **बड़ा** and **छोटा**. Try to say that one thing is *bigger
than* another, and notice English changed the adjective to do it.

## You'll want to know: से बड़ा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03]; assesses=[] -->

**आदमी बच्चे से बड़ा है।** — The man is bigger than the child.

The adjective **does not change**. The thing compared against takes **से**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01] -->

English has two machines for this — **-er** on short adjectives and *more* in
front of long ones — and both are extra grammar. Hindi has neither:

| English | Hindi |
|---|---|
| bigger **than** X | X **से** बड़ा |
| smaller **than** X | X **से** छोटा |

The postposition you learned for *from* and *by* is doing this too, and the
logic holds: the comparison **proceeds from** the thing measured against.
Measured **from** that book, this one is big.

So there is no comparative form to learn in this language. **There is one more
use of a word you already have.**

## Grammar Lens: word order, and the reason the note was wrong
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-01] -->

| slot | what goes in it |
|---|---|
| first | the thing being described |
| second | what it is measured against, plus **से** |
| third | the adjective, then the verb |

Note what **बच्चे से** is doing there: that is the oblique from the last
chapter, earning its keep the very next time a postposition turns up.

English puts *than X* **after** the adjective; Hindi puts it **before**. That
reversal is the only thing to drill, and it follows from the rule that has held
since the first sentence you read: **the verb goes last, and everything else
queues in front of it.**
