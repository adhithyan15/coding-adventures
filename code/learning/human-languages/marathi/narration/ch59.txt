Marathi, chapter 59: What It Costs.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ै (-ai) — the ai vowel sign.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three chapters ago you learned ौ by adding a stroke to ो. The same trick works one more time.

Script.
ै (-ai).
ै (-ai) is ai, and it is े with a second stroke — the identical relationship ौ has to ो. Two marks, one rule, and with this one the four vowel signs of the e and o family are complete.
Hear them apart: के is ke; कै is kai, wider in the mouth. If you have met the pair ो / ौ, you already know what your voice has to do.

Writing — guided copy.
Copy कै twice with the model visible. Then write के beside it, and को and कौ underneath, and check that the second stroke falls in the same place in both pairs.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What is the difference between े and ै (-ai)? (One stroke.) Which other pair works the same way? (ो and ौ.)
Source: Devanagari ai vowel sign.


Lesson 2 of 4.
पैसे (paise) — money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can count to twenty and you cannot name the one thing people count most often.

You'll want to know: पैसे.
पैसे — paise — money.
The word the sign in the last lesson was for. पैसे (paise) is Marathi's ordinary word for money, and it is plural only — there is no singular पैसा in this sense, the way English has no singular scissors. You will always hear it and always say it in this shape.
Underneath it is the paisa, the coin that is a hundredth of a rupee, which is why the plural is the natural form: money is counted in many small pieces.
Now the numbers earn their keep. किती पैसे (paise)? — how much money? — is the question the speaking paper's transactional part is built on, and you have owned किती since chapter thirty-seven with nothing to ask it about.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: paise — money]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī paise? — how much money?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mājhyākaḍe vīs paise āhet — I have twenty]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Ask how much money, then say you have twenty.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What shape is पैसे (paise) always in? (Plural.) Which sign did it need? (ै (-ai).)
Sources: Wiktionary: पैसे (paise).


Lesson 3 of 4.
रुपया (rupayā) — rupee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
पैसे (paise) is money in general. No price is ever quoted in it.

You'll want to know: रुपया.
रुपया — rupayā — rupee.
वीस रुपये — vīs rupaye — twenty rupees.
This is the word a price is named in, and it is masculine, so it takes a plural ending after a number: one रुपया (rupayā), twenty रुपये. Say the pair out loud until the change is automatic, because a number is almost never said without it.
The history is a good one. Sanskrit रूप्य (rūpya) meant "wrought silver" — silver that had been given a SHAPE, from रूप (rūpa), form. A rupee is, at root, shaped metal. The same coin name travelled out of India far enough that it is now the currency of half a dozen countries.
With a number in front of it you can finally answer the transactional part of the speaking paper: किती? — वीस रुपये.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: rupayā, then vīs rupaye — and hear the ending change]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī rupaye? — how many rupees?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say इ and name a word it opens]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Ask how much, then answer twenty rupees.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What did रूप्य mean in Sanskrit? (Wrought silver — shaped metal.) What happens to रुपया (rupayā) after a number? (It becomes रुपये.)
Sources: Wiktionary: रुपया (rupayā).


Lesson 4 of 4.
A price asked and answered — a price asked and answered — the first transaction this book can carry out.

Warm-up.
[pause 4 seconds]
Twenty numbers, a word for money and a word for the currency. That is a whole exchange, and it is the first one.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kitī rupaye? — and answer with any number to twenty]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mājhyākaḍe paise āhet — I have money]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ek … vīs once more, and stop where the book stops]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say ithe — here]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Ask a price and answer it, then say how much money you have.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word names the currency and which names money in general? (रुपया (rupayā); पैसे (paise).) How far can you count? (To twenty.) Then stop.
