Mandarin Chinese, chapter 197: Finding the Way.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
问路 (wènlù) — to ask the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take off, then the Mandarin for to reach a destination.

You'll want to know: 问路.
问路 — wènlù — "to ask the way".
他在问路 tā zài wènlù — He's asking the way.

The word, taken apart.
问路 (wènlù) is built from 问 'to ask' and 路 'road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjunH and luH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
找到 (zhǎodào) — to find.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to reach a destination, then the Mandarin for to ask the way.

You'll want to know: 找到.
找到 — zhǎodào — "to find".
我找到书店了 wǒ zhǎodào shūdiàn le — I've found the bookshop.
The new character, in pieces: 找 (zhǎo) — seven strokes: on the left the hand piece of 打; on the right a bar, a long slanting hook, a stroke falling to the left across it, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
找到 (zhǎodào) is built from 找 (zhǎo) 'to look for' and 到 'to arrive': looking that gets there. 到 is inherited from Middle Chinese tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of 找 (zhǎo), so its history is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
找 (zhǎo) — the hand piece, then a hook and a fall.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the hand piece, then a hook and a fall and Guided Practice.

打 钱.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — dǎ, qián]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the hand piece, then a hook and a fall.
找 (zhǎo).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the hand piece, three strokes, the same three as in 打: a short bar, a vertical that hooks to the left at its foot, then a stroke rising to the right across it.
The right piece, four strokes. A bar. A long slanting hook from the top down to the lower right, flicking up at its foot. A stroke falling to the left across it. Then a dot at the top right.
3 + 4 = seven. The right piece is the right piece of 钱 with one bar fewer.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 找 (zhǎo), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 找 (zhǎo)? (Seven.) Where has your hand made the left piece before? (In 打.) What comes last? (The dot at the top right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
mílù — to lose your way; to get lost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: mílù.
mílù — "to lose your way; to get lost".
wǒ mílù le — I'm lost.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mílù is built from mí 'confused' and 路 'road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mej and luH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be bewildered'.

The word, taken apart.
yìzhí is built from 一 'one' and zhí 'straight', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit and drik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'upright'.
