Latin, chapter 397: Oaths and Agreements.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iūsiūrandum, iūrisiūrandī — an oath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a solemn promise, then the Latin for to bind.

You'll want to know: iūsiūrandum, iūrisiūrandī.
iūsiūrandum, iūrisiūrandī — "an oath".
Iūsiūrandum dedit sē reditūrum esse. He gave his oath that he would return.
iūsiūrandum, iūrisiūrandī n.: both parts decline, iūre iūrandō, by oath. Built from iūs and iūrō, to swear.

The word, taken apart.
iūsiūrandum is built from iūs "right, law" and iūrandum, the gerundive of iūrō "to swear" (Lewis & Short): a right that must be sworn.

Cōnventiō inter eōs facta est. An agreement was made between them.
cōnventiō, cōnventiōnis f., built on conveniō, to come together: convenit, it is agreed. English convention.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
pacīscor, pacīscī — to strike a bargain, to come to terms.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an oath, then the Latin for an agreement.

You'll want to know: pacīscor, pacīscī.
pacīscor, pacīscī — "to strike a bargain, to come to terms".
Cum hostibus dē pāce pacīscēbantur. They were bargaining with the enemy about peace.
Deponent: pacīscor, pacīscī, pactus sum, kin to pāx, peace. Cum aliquō pacīscī, to strike a bargain with someone.

The word, taken apart.
pacīscor is built on an old verb pacō "to come to terms" with the ending -scō (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), and belongs with pāx "peace": to fix terms.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pactum, pactī — a pact, an agreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an agreement, then the Latin for to strike a bargain.

You'll want to know: pactum, pactī.
pactum, pactī — "a pact, an agreement".
Pactum servāre dēbēmus. We must keep the pact.
pactum, pactī n., the participle of pacīscor: a thing agreed. Quō pactō?, in what way? English pact.

The word, taken apart.
pactum is the participle of pacīscor "to make a bargain" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a thing agreed. English pact comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
stipulor, stipulārī — to bargain for, to demand a formal promise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to strike a bargain, then the Latin for a pact.

You'll want to know: stipulor, stipulārī.
stipulor, stipulārī — "to bargain for, to demand a formal promise".
Stipulātus est ut pecūnia redderētur. He stipulated that the money be returned.
Deponent: stipulor, stipulārī, stipulātus sum. In Roman law the stipulātiō was a contract made by question and answer. English stipulate.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of stipulor is not settled: Lewis & Short give no sure account, and EtymDB has no parent for it. English stipulate comes from it.
