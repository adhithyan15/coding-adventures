You'll want to know: газета.
газета (gazéta) — "a newspaper".

The word, taken apart.
газета (gazéta) comes from Italian gazzetta, the source also of French gazette; Vasmer traces it to Venetian gazeta, a small coin paid for the news sheet.

Lesson 3 of 5.
словарь (slovár) — a dictionary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a newspaper, then the Russian for a magazine.

You'll want to know: словарь.
словарь (slovár) — "a dictionary".

The word, taken apart.
словарь (slovár) is слово, "a word", with -арь, a suffix for a thing or keeper: a book of words. Слово from Proto-Slavic slovo — inherited; Vasmer relates it to Sanskrit śrávas, "fame".

Lesson 4 of 5.
линейка (linéyka) — a ruler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a magazine, then the Russian for a dictionary.

You'll want to know: линейка.
линейка (linéyka) — "a ruler".

The word, taken apart.
линейка (linéyka) comes from линея, an older form of линия, "a line" (Vasmer), with -йка; линия came through Polish linia or German Linie from Latin līnea, "a linen thread", from līnum, "flax".

папка (pápka) — "a folder".

The word, taken apart.
папка (pápka) comes from German Pappe, "pasteboard", named for the paste that once glued its layers, from Pappe, "mush" (Vasmer).
