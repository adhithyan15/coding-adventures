# en mi opinión — marking a claim as yours

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say what happened and how it felt. This chapter is for saying what you THINK, and defending it.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [durante](./ES-C269-durante.md) — the last word of the experience chapter.
- [creer](./ES-C41-creer.md) — you can already say *creo*, "I believe."

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- Five syllables, and the accent on *opinión* is doing the usual work:
  *en-mi-o-pi-NIÓN*. → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-EN-MI-OPINION]; assesses=[] -->

**en mi opinión** — "in my opinion."

*Opinión* is Latin **opinari**, "to suppose, to think" — and the *-ión* ending is
the same one you met in *habitación*, turning a verb into the noun for its
result. An opinion is *a supposing*, made into a thing you can hand someone.

English took the identical word from the identical place, which is why you can
read it before you can say it.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SEMANTIC-CLAIM-MARKED]; assesses=[] -->

There is a difference between saying something and **marking it as a claim**,
and this phrase does the marking. *Es difícil* asserts that a thing is hard. *En
mi opinión, es difícil* says the same and hands the listener a place to disagree.

That is what makes it the opening move of an argument rather than a statement of
fact — you are inviting the reply.
