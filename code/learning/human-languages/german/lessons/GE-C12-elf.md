# elf — "eleven"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ZEHN-01, GE-LEX-EINS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can count to ten. Both languages then do something odd for two
numbers, and they do the same odd thing.

## You'll want to know: elf
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ELF-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ZEHN-01] -->

> **elf** — "eleven."

Not *einszehn*. Not "oneteen" in English either. Eleven is the first of two
numbers that refuse the pattern, in German and in English alike.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ELF-01] -->

- `herz-short-e` — a short open *e*, the vowel of English *bed*.
- `f-final` — and a clean *f* to close.

*elf*, one syllable, said as it looks. This is the rare German word an English
reader can pronounce on sight.
