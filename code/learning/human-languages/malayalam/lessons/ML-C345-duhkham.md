# ദുഃഖം (duḥkhaṁ) — grief, sorrow

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C116-FEELINGS-01, ML-LEX-SUKHAM-01, ML-LEX-C250-VASTU250-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "sadness", then "well-being", then "news". (*saṅkaṭaṁ*,
*sukhaṁ*, *vārtta*.)

## You'll want to know: ദുഃഖം
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C345-DUHKHAM-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C116-FEELINGS-01, ML-LEX-C250-VASTU250-03, ML-GRAMMAR-C340-APPOL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01] -->

**ദുഃഖം** — *duḥkhaṁ* — "grief, sorrow".

> **ആ വാർത്ത കേട്ടപ്പോൾ എനിക്ക് വലിയ ദുഃഖമുണ്ടായി.**
>
> *ā vārtta kēṭṭappōḷ enikkŭ valiya duḥkhamuṇṭāyi.* — "When I heard that news, I felt deep sorrow."

**സങ്കടം** is the everyday sadness of a bad day. **ദുഃഖം** is heavier: grief, or sorrow you speak
of with care.

> **അവരുടെ ദുഃഖം എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലാകുന്നു.**
>
> *avaruṭe duḥkhaṁ enikkŭ manassilākunnu.* — "I understand their grief."

The mark after **ദു** is a breath, *ḥ*. Many speakers let it turn into a held *k*, close to
*dukkhaṁ*. The next lesson puts the mark in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ദുഃഖം** (*duḥkhaṁ*) ← Sanskrit *duḥkha*, "difficulty, pain", a tatsama with Malayalam's noun
ending (CDIAL 6375). CDIAL notes it was formed after *sukha*, the word you know as **സുഖം**: the two
are a pair, ease and its opposite.
