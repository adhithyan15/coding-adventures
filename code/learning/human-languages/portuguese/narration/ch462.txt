Portuguese, chapter 462: O calor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
abafado — muggy, stuffy, close.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the snail, then the Portuguese for to crawl.

You'll want to know: abafado.
abafado — "muggy, stuffy, close".
Hoje o tempo está abafado. The weather is muggy today.
A room with no air is also abafado.

The word, taken apart.
abafado is built on abafar 'to smother, to stifle'; the origin of abafar is not settled (EtymDB lists it without a source).

What you've built.

You'll want to know: a seca.
a seca — "the drought".
Este ano houve uma seca no sul. This year there was a drought in the south.
Informal in Portugal: Que seca! — How boring!

The word, taken apart.
seca is built on seco 'dry', which is inherited from Latin siccus (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: a insolação.
a insolação — "sunstroke".
Ele apanhou uma insolação na praia. He got sunstroke at the beach.
Apanhar uma insolação — to get sunstroke.

The word, taken apart.
insolação is a learned borrowing from Latin īnsōlātiō 'putting in the sun' (EtymDB), on sōl 'sun'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
transpirar — to sweat, to perspire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the light breeze, then the Portuguese for sunstroke.

You'll want to know: transpirar.
transpirar — "to sweat, to perspire".
Com este calor, transpiro muito. In this heat, I sweat a lot.
A transpiração — sweat.

The word, taken apart.
transpirar is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin trānspīrāre 'to breathe through' (EtymDB), from trāns and spīrāre 'to breathe'.
