# quiero — "I want," and a masterclass in prefixes

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] This is the lesson where you stop learning words and start
learning *how Latin builds them*. One root, one set of prefixes, and a whole
wall of English words falls into place — plus one of the most useful verbs in
Spanish.

## You'll want to know first

- [ES-C01-L06 (por favor)](./ES-C01-L06-por-favor.md) — you met the prefix
  *pro-* there; this lesson is about prefixes as a system.

## Sounds you'll need

- `qu-k` — **qu** is a hard **k** and the **u is silent**: *quiero* is
  *KYEH-ro*, not *KWEE-eh-ro*. (Silent-*u*-after-*q*, straight from Latin —
  same as English *quiche*, *quay*.)
- `diphthong-ie` — **ie** is a quick *YEH*.

## The word, taken apart

**quiero** — "I want." Infinitive **querer** ("to want"). Root: Latin
**quaerere** ("to seek, to ask, to search for"). Hold that meaning — *to
seek* — because it's the thread through everything below.

First, the plain cousins in English, all "seeking/asking" words:

- **quer**y — a question, a seeking.
- **quest** — a search. **quest**ion — a thing you ask.

Now the **prefix machine**. A prefix is a small piece bolted onto the front
of a root that steers its meaning. Take *quaerere* (in English it usually
wears the coats *-quire*, *-quer*, *-quisite*) and watch:

| Prefix | Means | + root | = English word | = literally |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **in-** | into, upon | in + quire | **inquire** | to seek *into* |
| **re-** | again, repeatedly | re + quire | **require** | to seek *insistently* → demand |
| **ac-** (ad-) | toward | ac + quire | **acquire** | to seek *toward* → get |
| **con-** | fully, together | con + quer | **conquer** | to seek *fully* → overcome |
| **ex-** | out | ex + quisite | **exquisite** | *sought out* → refined, choice |
| **per-** | through | per + quisite | **perquisite** | a thing sought *through* a job → a **perk** |

That's your example made real: put **in-** in front of **quiry** and you get
**inquiry** — "a seeking-into." Every word in that table is *quiero*'s
cousin. You already knew them all; now you know they're one family, and you
know the machine that built them.

> Careful — a **false friend**: *quarrel* and *querulous* look related but
> come from a *different* Latin root, *queri* ("to complain"). Not this
> family. (This is exactly why the book flags roots: resemblance isn't
> proof.)

## Why it's said this way

Here's the beautiful cultural twist. *Querer* means "to want" — but it's also
the ordinary Spanish word for **"to love"** a person. *Te quiero* = "I love
you." Spanish says love as *"I want/seek you."* From the root's point of view,
loving someone is *seeking* them — the same *quaerere* that gives us *quest*
and *inquiry*. Where English keeps "want" and "love" far apart, Spanish lets
one word of *seeking* cover both, and the etymology tells you why.

(A note for later: *querer* is "stem-changing" — *quer**er*** but *qu**ie**ro*,
with an *-ie-* that appears in the middle. You'll meet that pattern properly
soon; for now just say *quiero*.)
