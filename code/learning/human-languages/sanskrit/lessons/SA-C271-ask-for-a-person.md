# Ask for a familiar person

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C14-DEIXIS-03, SA-LEX-ASTI, SA-LEX-C83-SCHOOL-03, SA-LEX-AM-YES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall four known pieces: **अध्यापकः**, “teacher”; **अत्र**,
“here”; **अस्ति**, “is”; and **आम्**, “yes.”

## Grammar Lens: build the person request

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01]; assesses=[] -->

At a doorway or on a call, put the familiar person first and ask whether that
person is here. The question mark and rising question voice turn the known
statement into a bounded availability question. Then answer by echoing the
statement, as chapter 1 taught:

> **अध्यापकः अत्र अस्ति?**
>
> *adhyāpakaḥ atra asti?*
>
> “Is the teacher here?”
>
> **आम्, अध्यापकः अत्र अस्ति।**
>
> *ām, adhyāpakaḥ atra asti.*
>
> “Yes, the teacher is here.”

Nothing in this exchange is a new word. The new skill is the complete
doorway/telephone turn: asking whether a familiar person is present and giving
the positive response. It does not claim a general polar-question system.

## Guided Practice

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] You call the school and need to speak with the teacher. Ask, “Is the
teacher here?” Then give the positive answer, “Yes, the teacher is here.”

## What you've built

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C271-ASK-FOR-PERSON-01] -->

**अध्यापकः अत्र अस्ति? — आम्, अध्यापकः अत्र अस्ति।** (*adhyāpakaḥ atra asti?
— ām, adhyāpakaḥ atra asti.*) is one bounded way to ask for a familiar person
and respond. It does not establish a general service-counter or telephone
script.
