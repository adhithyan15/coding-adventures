Japanese, chapter 394: Up the Hill and Down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
さかみち (sakamichi) — a hill road; a sloping street.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an arrow (on a sign), then the Japanese for to look back.

You'll want to know: さかみち.
さかみち — sakamichi — "a hill road; a sloping street".
さかみちを ゆっくり のぼりました。 — sakamichi o yukkuri noborimashita — "I slowly climbed the hill road."

The word, taken apart.
さかみち (sakamichi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from saka, "slope" and michi, "road"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

What you've built.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for steep, then the Japanese for width.

You'll want to know: くだる.
くだる — kudaru — "to descend; to go down (a slope or river)".
さかみちを くだって、かわまで いきました。 — sakamichi o kudatte, kawa made ikimashita — "I went down the hill road as far as the river."
