Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 216: Setting Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
निकळणो (nikaḷṇo) — to set out; to come out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for brave, then the Marwadi for crazy.

You'll want to know: निकळणो.
निकळणो — nikaḷṇo — "to set out; to come out".
हूं काल तड़कै घर सूं निकळसूं। hū̃ kāl taṛkai ghar sū̃ nikaḷsū̃. Tomorrow I'll set out from home at daybreak.
Past (he …): वो तड़कै निकळ्यो, vo taṛkai nikaḷyo.

The word, taken apart.
निकळणो (nikaḷṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed niṣkalati, "comes out"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari verb nikalaï, with the same sense, under it (CDIAL 7478).

Lesson 2 of 5.
साथी (sāthī) — a companion; a fellow traveller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for crazy, then the Marwadi for to set out.

You'll want to know: साथी.
साथी — sāthī — "a companion; a fellow traveller".
राम म्हारो साथी है। rām mhāro sāthī hai. Ram is my travelling companion.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
साथी (sāthī) is inherited from a reconstructed sārthin, "having part in a company", built on Sanskrit sārtha, a band of travellers; CDIAL lists Marwari sāthī, "a comrade" (CDIAL 13366).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कोस (kos) — a kos (a distance of about two miles).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to set out, then the Marwadi for a companion.

You'll want to know: कोस.
कोस — kos — "a kos (a distance of about two miles)".
गांव अठै सूं दस कोस है। gā̃v aṭhai sū̃ das kos hai. The village is ten kos from here.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कोस (kos) is inherited from Sanskrit krośa, first "a shout", then the distance a shout carries (Monier-Williams); CDIAL lists Old Marwari kosa, "a distance of about two miles" (CDIAL 3611).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मुसाफर (musāphar) — a traveller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a companion, then the Marwadi for a kos.

You'll want to know: मुसाफर.
मुसाफर — musāphar — "a traveller".
मुसाफर दूर सूं आवै है। musāphar dūr sū̃ āvai hai. The traveller comes from far away.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
मुसाफर (musāphar) is usually traced to Persian musāfir, "a traveller", which Persian took from Arabic; Marwadi says it with ph and ends it in -ar.

Lesson 5 of 5.
डेरो (ḍero) — a camp; a stopping place for the night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a kos, then the Marwadi for a traveller.

You'll want to know: डेरो.
डेरो — ḍero — "a camp; a stopping place for the night".
आज रात म्हारो डेरो डूंगर कनै है। āj rāt mhāro ḍero ḍū̃gar kanai hai. Tonight my camp is near the hill.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
डेरो (ḍero) is inherited from a reconstructed ḍēra, "a resting-place"; CDIAL lists Marwari ḍero, "a tent, a shelter" (CDIAL 5564).
