# preparar — make ready beforehand

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-POSTRE, ES-LEX-TERMINAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall **el postre**, dessert. Before it is served, someone has to
make it ready: **preparar un postre**, “to prepare a dessert.” You can
**terminar** a task; first you may need to **preparar** its parts.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-PREPARAR]; assesses=[] -->

Latin **praeparāre** joined **prae**, “before,” to **parāre**, “make ready.”
Spanish **preparar** and English **prepare** preserve that family almost
unchanged. The older verb **parar** is visible inside, but do not translate the
modern compound as “pre-stop”: use the whole word, “prepare.”

## Grammar Lens: prepare a thing or prepare to act
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PREPARAR] -->

Use a noun directly: **preparar un postre**. Before another action, Spanish can
use **prepararse para**; this lesson keeps the simpler A1 object frame.
