# dijo

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-SINGULAR-PATTERN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The strong pattern, once more: a stem that changed shape, and then
endings that carry no stress. (*Tuve*, *tuvo*.)

[PAUSE 1s] One more stem is about to join them.

## Grammar Lens: the stem is dij-
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DECIR-PRETERITE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-SINGULAR-PATTERN] -->

*Decir* builds its past on **dij-**, and in the singular nothing is new at all:

| | |
|---|---|
| *tuve* · *tuvo* | the stem you know |
| ***dije*** · ***dijo*** | the same endings, on a new stem |

[PAUSE 2s] Unstressed at the end, exactly like the others. *Dijo*, not *dijó*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ETYMON-DIJO-DIXIT]; assesses=[] -->

Latin said ***dīxit***. That *x* stood for a [ks] — the sound at the end of
English *box*.

[PAUSE 2s] Old Spanish kept the letter and softened the sound: *dixo*, with the
*x* said roughly like English *sh*. Centuries later that sound hardened at the
back of the mouth into the one *j* spells today, and the spelling followed the
sound. **dīxit → dixo → dijo.**

So the *j* in *dijo* is not a decoration. It is a Latin *x*, wearing modern
clothes.

## Grammar Lens: where it breaks the pattern
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-J-STEM-PRETERITE-PLURAL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-PLURAL] -->

You learned the strong plural as stem plus *-ieron*: *tuvieron*, *hicieron*,
*estuvieron*. Apply that here and you would get *dijieron*.

**It is *dijeron*.** The *i* is gone.

[PAUSE 2s] The *i* in *-ieron* is not a full vowel — it is a glide, the *y* sound
of English *yes*, leaning on the vowel after it. And Spanish does not keep a
glide next to a sound already made in that part of the mouth. The *j* was there
first, so the glide goes.

This is not a fact about *decir*. **Any strong stem ending in *j* does it**, and
you will meet more of them; when you do, the ending will already be familiar.
