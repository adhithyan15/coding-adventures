# होशियार — holding your wits, which is a compliment and a warning at once

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say **आसान**. It ends in a consonant and never bends. The word in
this lesson ends in a consonant too — so predict what it will do before reading
on.

## You'll want to know: होशियार
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02]; assesses=[] -->

**होशियार** (*hośiyār*) — "clever, sharp, alert."

It ends in **र**, a consonant, so it does not bend. *Ek hośiyār laṛkā*, *ek
hośiyār laṛkī* — the adjective is the same shape for both.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Persian, and built from two pieces:

- **होश** (*hoś*) — consciousness, one's senses, one's wits
- **-यार** (*-yār*) — holding, keeping

So a *hośiyār* person is one who **keeps hold of their senses**. Not one who
knows a great deal — one who is *awake*.

That is why the word does double duty. Called out sharply, **होशियार!** means
**"look out!"** — the Indian Railways used it as the standard shouted warning.
The same word praises a bright child and stops a man walking into a moving cart,
because both are about being alert.

**होश** on its own is worth keeping. *Hoś meṅ ānā* is "to come to one's senses,"
and *hoś uṛnā* is literally "for one's wits to fly away" — to be stunned.

## Grammar Lens: the first one that does not bend
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| adjective | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| बुरा | बुरा | बुरी |
| होशियार | होशियार | होशियार |

The difference is not that one is about character and the other about worth. It
is the last letter, and nothing else. **बुरा** is inherited and ends in **-आ**;
**होशियार** is Persian and ends in a consonant.
