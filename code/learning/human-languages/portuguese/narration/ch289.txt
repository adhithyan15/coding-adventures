Portuguese, chapter 289: Burlas e fraudes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a fraude — the fraud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to break open, then the Portuguese for the break-in.

You'll want to know: a fraude.
a fraude — "the fraud".
A polícia descobriu uma fraude com cartões bancários. The police uncovered a fraud with bank cards.
Fraude fiscal — tax fraud.

The word, taken apart.
fraude is a learned borrowing from Latin fraus, fraudis "deceit, harm" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a burla — the scam, the swindle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the break-in, then the Portuguese for the fraud.

You'll want to know: a burla.
a burla — "the scam, the swindle".
Aquele e-mail era uma burla. That e-mail was a scam.
European Portuguese; in Brazil people say o golpe.

The word, taken apart.
burla came from Spanish burla "mockery, a trick" (EtymDB), which goes back to Late Latin burra "nonsense, trickery".

burlar — "to swindle, to con".
Um homem burlou várias pessoas idosas. A man swindled several elderly people.
In Brazil it means rather ‘to get round’ a rule: burlar a lei.

The word, taken apart.
burlar is built on burla "a trick, a scam" (EtymDB), from Spanish burla "mockery".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o burlão — the con man, the swindler.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: o burlão.
o burlão — "the con man, the swindler".
O burlão prometia dinheiro fácil. The con man promised easy money.
European Portuguese; a woman is a burlona.

The word, taken apart.
burlão is built on burla "a scam" with the ending -ão for a person; burla came from Spanish (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o cúmplice — the accomplice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to swindle, then the Portuguese for the con man.

You'll want to know: o cúmplice.
o cúmplice — "the accomplice".
O burlão tinha um cúmplice dentro do banco. The con man had an accomplice inside the bank.
The same form for a woman: a cúmplice.

The word, taken apart.
cúmplice is a learned borrowing from Late Latin complex "closely joined, a partner" (EtymDB), from complicāre "to fold together".
