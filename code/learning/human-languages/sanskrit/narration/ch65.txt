Sanskrit, chapter 65: The Last Letter You Read.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ङ (ṅa) — the nasal before ख in सङ्ख्या.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ङ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write इ and ऋ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say सङ्ख्या (saṅkhyā), "number". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ङ.
ङ — ṅa, the nasal made where k and kh are made.
It is inside सङ्ख्या (saṅkhyā), "number", from the chapter on numbers. Say saṅkhyā: the ṅ sits at the back of the mouth, ready for the kh after it. Sanskrit spells that nasal out as ङ (ṅa); Hindi writes the same word संख्या, with a dot above instead.

Writing: ङ.
Put your pen on ङ (ṅa) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at सङ्ख्या and point at ङ (ṅa) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ङ (ṅa) three times, saying ṅ as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ऋ, then ङ (ṅa), from memory]
[your turn — read: सङ्ख्या aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ङ? (ṅa, ṅ.) Which word did it come from? (सङ्ख्या, saṅkhyā, "number".)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(one letter) (ekam akṣaram) — From the letter to the word — a first pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say सङ्ख्या (saṅkhyā), then write the letter it gave you.

Guided Practice.
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: ङ. name: ṅa. the word it came from: सङ्ख्या saṅkhyā.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter in the table from memory, then check it]
[your turn — say: the word each one came from]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: each word in the table aloud, and point at its letter]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word did each letter come from? (ङ (ṅa) from सङ्ख्या.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(one letter) (ekam akṣaram) — From the word back to the letter — a second pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write ङ (ṅa) without looking, then say the word it came from.

Guided Practice.
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: ङ. name: ṅa. the word it came from: सङ्ख्या saṅkhyā.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: each word in the table, then write the letter it gave you without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter once more, slowly, larger than it is printed]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say each word in the table once more, and name the letter you took from it.
