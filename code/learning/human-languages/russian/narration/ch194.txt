Lesson 1 of 5.
пельмени (pelméni) — dumplings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a button, then the Russian for earrings.

You'll want to know: пельмени.
пельмени (pelméni) — "dumplings".

The word, taken apart.
пельмени (pelméni) comes from Komi and Udmurt peľńań, from peľ, "ear", with ńań, "bread", for their shape (Vasmer, after Kalima).

Lesson 3 of 5.
капуста (kapústa) — cabbage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for dumplings, then the Russian for a salad.

You'll want to know: капуста.
капуста (kapústa) — "cabbage".

The word, taken apart.
капуста (kapústa) — recorded from 1193; Vasmer thinks it probably blends Medieval Latin composita, Italian composta, "a mixture, pickled greens", with Old High German chapuz, "cabbage", from Latin caputium, "a cabbage head", from caput, "head".

Lesson 5 of 5.
горох (gorókh) — peas.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for cabbage, then the Russian for beetroot.

You'll want to know: горох.
горох (gorókh) — "peas".

The word, taken apart.
горох (gorókh) from Proto-Slavic gorxŭ — inherited. Vasmer thinks it probably related to Lithuanian garšvà, a weed, and Sanskrit gharṣati, "rubs". Bulgarian has грах, "peas", with -ра- for Russian -оро-.
