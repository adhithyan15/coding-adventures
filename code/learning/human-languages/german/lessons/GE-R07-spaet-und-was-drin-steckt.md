# spät — one short word with two spelling rules inside it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SPAET-01, GE-SOUND-SPAET-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say **spät**. Listen to how it starts — the *sp* is not the *sp* of
English *spade*. Say what your mouth does instead.

## Guided Practice: the end of a day, in four moves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SPAET-01, GE-LEX-SPAETER-02, GE-LEX-BIS-MORGEN-01, GE-PRAGMATICS-GUTE-NACHT-03] -->

[PAUSE 1s] The week is in your mouth now. These four are what you say when one
of its days is running out.

- [YOU SAY: it is late — *es ist spät*]
- [YOU SAY: later, not now — *später*]
- [YOU SAY: until tomorrow — *bis morgen*]
- [YOU SAY: the last thing, at the door — *Gute Nacht*]

**Gute Nacht** is not the night-time *Guten Abend*. It is spoken on parting, not
on arriving — you say it to someone who is leaving or going to bed, and saying
it on entering a room at ten at night is the kind of mistake that gets you a
puzzled look rather than a correction.

## Grammar Lens: two marks, two different jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-MARKS-GRAMMAR-03, GE-GRAMMAR-ESZETT-VOWEL-LENGTH-02] -->

Both marks are in words you have been saying for weeks, and they do opposite
kinds of work:

| mark | what it is | what it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **ä** in *spät* | two dots that were once an **e** | **grammar** — a changed vowel, marking a form |
| **ß** in *Fuß* | two s's written as one letter | **sound** — the vowel in front of it is **long** |

The umlaut is a *grammatical* signal: it marks that something has happened to
the word. The eszett is a *phonetic* one: it tells you how long to hold the
vowel before it. One points backwards at the word's history, the other forwards
at your mouth.

That is why **spät → später** changes nothing about the ä. The comparative is
built by adding **-er**, and the vowel was already what it was.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ESZETT-VOWEL-LENGTH-02, GE-GRAMMAR-UMLAUT-MARKS-GRAMMAR-03, GE-LEX-BIS-MORGEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which of the two marks tells you about a **sound**, and which about
**grammar**? (**ß** is sound — a long vowel before it; **umlaut** is grammar.)
What were the two dots, originally? (**An e.**) Say goodbye to someone you will
see the next day. (*Bis morgen.*)
