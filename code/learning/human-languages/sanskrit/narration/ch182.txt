Lesson 1 of 5.
विस्मयः (vismayaḥ) — wonder, surprise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the pulse, then the Sanskrit for fitness, health.

You'll want to know: विस्मयः.
विस्मयः — vismayaḥ — "wonder, surprise".

The word, taken apart.
vismaya is built on vi- and the root √smi, "to smile" (MW), so wonder starts as a smile of surprise (CDIAL 12019). The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares English smile and Latin mīrus, "wonderful".

You'll want to know: शोकः.
शोकः — śokaḥ — "grief".

The word, taken apart.
śoka is built on the root √śuc, "to burn, glow" (MW). In the Rigveda it means "heat" as well as "pain", and later "grief" (CDIAL 12619): sorrow as something that burns.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कृतज्ञता (kṛtajñatā) — gratitude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for grief, then the Sanskrit for contentment.

You'll want to know: कृतज्ञता.
कृतज्ञता — kṛtajñatā — "gratitude".

The word, taken apart.
kṛtajñatā is built from kṛta, "what was done", from the root √kṛ "to do", and jña, "knowing", with -tā, "-ness" (MW). Gratitude is knowing what was done for you.

Lesson 5 of 5.
स्मृतिः (smṛtiḥ) — memory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for contentment, then the Sanskrit for gratitude.

You'll want to know: स्मृतिः.
स्मृतिः — smṛtiḥ — "memory".

The word, taken apart.
smṛti is built on the root √smṛ, "to remember", with the noun suffix -ti (MW). MW compares Latin memor, "mindful", the source of English memory. The same word names the body of remembered tradition, as opposed to śruti, what was heard.
