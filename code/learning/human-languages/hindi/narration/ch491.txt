Hindi, chapter 491: In Hospital.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
दाख़िल (dākhil) — admitted; entered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to pick at, then the Hindi for a scar.

You'll want to know: दाख़िल.
दाख़िल — dākhil — "admitted; entered".
तेज़ बुख़ार की वजह से दादा अस्पताल में दाख़िल हैं। tez bukhār kī vajah se dādā aspatāl meṁ dākhil haiṁ. Grandfather is in hospital with a high fever.

The word, taken apart.
दाख़िल (dākhil) is usually traced to Arabic dākhil, "entering, inside", which reached Hindi through Persian. aspatāl meṁ dākhil honā is to be admitted to hospital.

Lesson 4 of 6.
बुलवाना (bulvānā) — to have someone sent for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a hospital ward, then the Hindi for a stretcher.

You'll want to know: बुलवाना.
बुलवाना — bulvānā — "to have someone sent for".
दादा की तबीयत बिगड़ी, तो मीरा ने डाक्टर को बुलवाया। dādā kī tabīyat bigṛī, to Mīrā ne ḍākṭar ko bulvāyā. When my grandfather took a turn for the worse, Mira sent for the doctor.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने डाक्टर को बुलवाया, usne ḍākṭar ko bulvāyā.

The word, taken apart.
बुलवाना (bulvānā) is the second causative of bolnā: bulānā is "to call", and bulvānā is "to get someone else to call" a person. CDIAL lists bulānā under bōll, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 9321).

Lesson 5 of 6.
जान (jān) — a life; the life in a body.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a stretcher, then the Hindi for to have someone sent for.

You'll want to know: जान.
जान — jān — "a life; the life in a body".
डाक्टरों ने बच्चे की जान बचाई। ḍākṭaroṁ ne bacce kī jān bacāī. The doctors saved the child's life.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
जान (jān) is usually traced to Persian jān, "life, soul". jān bacānā is to save a life; jīvan is life as a span of years, jān the life in a body.
