# Review — how it felt, and what comes next

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-ESPERO-INFINITIVO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I hope to travel". (*Espero viajar.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-SENTIMENTO-ESTAVA-01, PT-GRAMMAR-CONTENTE-COM-02, PT-LEX-ESPERO-INFINITIVO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-O-MEU-SONHO-02, PT-LEX-PERDI-01, PT-LEX-C127-QUALIDADES127-02, PT-LEX-C91-VIAGEM91-03] -->

An experience told well has two ends. The feeling looks back, with *estava*;
the hope looks forward, with *espero* or *o meu sonho é*.

> *A viagem era longa. Perdi o comboio, por isso estava zangado. Mas no fim
> estava contente com a viagem. Espero voltar.*

[PAUSE 2s] Follow the feelings. *Estava zangado* came from an event, *perdi*,
and *por isso* joined them; *estava contente* closed the story; *espero
voltar* opened the next one.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-SENTIMENTO-ESTAVA-01, PT-LEX-ESPERO-INFINITIVO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01, PT-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which verb and which past do the feelings take? (***Estar***, in the
**imperfeito**: *estava zangado*, *estava contente*.) Which one is an event?
(***Perdi.***) What goes between *espero* and *voltar*? (**Nothing.**) And
why is a feeling's past called the *imperfeito*? (Latin *imperfectus*, **not
carried through**: a feeling has no edge.)
