Sanskrit, chapter 500: In-Laws and Ancestors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
देवरः (devaraḥ) — a husband's brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a sister's son, then the Sanskrit for a daughter's son.

You'll want to know: देवरः.
देवरः — devaraḥ — "a husband's brother".
मम देवरः चिकित्सकः अस्ति। mama devaraḥ cikitsakaḥ asti. My husband's brother is a doctor.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
देवरः (devaraḥ) is built on devṛ, "a husband's brother (esp. his younger brother)"; MW compares Greek daēr and Latin levir (MW).

प्रपितामहः (prapitāmahaḥ) is built from pra, "before", and पितामहः, "a grandfather"; MW gives "a paternal great-grandfather" and, in the plural, "ancestors" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सन्ततिः (santatiḥ) — offspring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wife's brother, then the Sanskrit for a great-grandfather.

You'll want to know: सन्ततिः.
सन्ततिः — santatiḥ — "offspring".
माता पिता च सन्ततेः सुखं चिन्तयतः। mātā pitā ca santateḥ sukhaṃ cintayataḥ. Parents think of their children's happiness.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सन्ततिः (santatiḥ) is built from sam and the root √tan, "to stretch": a line stretched on; MW gives "uninterrupted succession, lineage, progeny, offspring" (MW).
