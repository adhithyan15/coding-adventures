You'll want to know: мясо.
мясо (myáso) — "meat".

The word, taken apart.
мясо (myáso) from Proto-Slavic męso — inherited; the old nasal ę became Russian я. Vasmer lists Old Prussian mensā, Sanskrit māṃsá- and Gothic mimz, all "meat".

Lesson 2 of 5.
рыба (rýba) — fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an answer, then the Russian for meat.

You'll want to know: рыба.
рыба (rýba) — "fish".

The word, taken apart.
рыба (rýba) from Proto-Slavic ryba — inherited, but its deeper origin is unclear. Vasmer thought it was probably a taboo stand-in for an older word, the cousin of Lithuanian žuvìs, "fish", which fishermen avoided because it sounded like звать, "to call". A comparison with Old High German rûppa, "caterpillar", is also reported.

You'll want to know: яйцо.
яйцо (yaytsó) — "an egg".

The word, taken apart.
яйцо (yaytsó) from Proto-Slavic aje, "egg", with a diminutive ending — inherited. Vasmer lists Old High German ei, Old Norse egg, Latin ōvum and Greek ōión: English egg is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
масло (máslo) — butter; oil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for fish, then the Russian for an egg.

You'll want to know: масло.
масло (máslo) — "butter; oil".

The word, taken apart.
масло (máslo) from Proto-Slavic maslo, from maz-slo, "what you smear", on the root of мазать, "to smear" (Vasmer). So butter and oil share one word: both are spread.
