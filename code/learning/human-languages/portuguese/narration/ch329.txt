Portuguese, chapter 329: Páginas da história.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o antepassado — the ancestor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the shipwreck, then the Portuguese for the treasure.

You'll want to know: o antepassado.
o antepassado — "the ancestor".
Um antepassado meu viveu nesta casa. An ancestor of mine lived in this house.
Usually plural: os nossos antepassados.

The word, taken apart.
antepassado joins ante "before", from Latin ante (Lewis & Short), and passado "past": one who lived before us.

You'll want to know: a batalha.
a batalha — "the battle".
A batalha durou um dia inteiro. The battle lasted a whole day.
Also any hard struggle: uma batalha contra a doença.

The word, taken apart.
batalha comes from Late Latin battālia "fighting, fencing" (EtymDB), built on battuere "to beat".

Lesson 4 of 5.
conquistar — to conquer; to win (over).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the battle, then the Portuguese for to invade.

You'll want to know: conquistar.
conquistar — "to conquer; to win (over)".
O rei conquistou a cidade. The king conquered the city.
Also to win someone over: conquistou o público.

The word, taken apart.
conquistar is usually traced to Vulgar Latin conquaesītāre, from Latin conquīrere "to seek out", built on quaerere "to seek" (Lewis & Short).
