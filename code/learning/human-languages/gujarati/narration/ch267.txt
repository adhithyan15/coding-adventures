Gujarati, chapter 267: Upset and disappointed.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
નારાજ (nārāj) — displeased, upset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to cry, then the Gujarati for to feel shy.

You'll want to know: નારાજ.
નારાજ — nārāj — "displeased, upset".
શું તમે મારાથી નારાજ છો? — śũ tame mārāthī nārāj cho? — "Are you upset with me?"

The word, taken apart.
નારાજ (nārāj) is a loan, usually traced to Persian nārāz, "displeased". CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
નિરાશ (nirāś) — disappointed, hopeless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to feel shy, then the Gujarati for displeased.

You'll want to know: નિરાશ.
નિરાશ — nirāś — "disappointed, hopeless".
હું પરિણામથી નિરાશ નથી. — hũ pariṇāmthī nirāś nathī — "I am not disappointed with the result."

The word, taken apart.
નિરાશ (nirāś) is Sanskrit nirāśa, "hopeless", built from nir-, "without", and āśā, "hope", and taken over as a tatsama; CDIAL lists Gujarati nirās (CDIAL 7278).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ચિડાવું (ciḍāvũ) — to get irritated, to get cross.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for disappointed, then the Gujarati for to feel restless.

You'll want to know: ચિડાવું.
ચિડાવું — ciḍāvũ — "to get irritated, to get cross".
મારા પિતા ક્યારેય નથી ચિડાતા. — mārā pitā kyārey nathī ciḍātā — "My father never gets irritated."

The word, taken apart.
ચિડાવું (ciḍāvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from ciḍ, "be angry", a reconstructed form; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to be provoked" (CDIAL 4794).
