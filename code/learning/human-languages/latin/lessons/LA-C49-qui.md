## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-03]; assesses=[] -->

**quī, quae, quod** — "**who, which, that**."

This is the single most common word in surviving Latin prose, and it does one job:
it hangs a second statement onto a noun in the first.

> **Amīcus quī venit.** — The friend **who** comes.
> **Liber quem legō.** — The book **which** I am reading.

Three forms, because it agrees with the noun it hangs on: **quī** for a masculine
one, **quae** for a feminine one, **quod** for a neuter one.

You already know its relatives. **Quid** ("what") from the wellbeing exchange and **quis**
("who") are the question words built on the same Proto-Indo-European stem
***\*kwi-***. And Grimm's Law — the *piscis* law, *p* to *f* — does *kw* to *hw*
as well, which is why English **who**, **what**, **which** and **why** are
inherited cousins of this word rather than borrowings from it. The spelling *wh-*
is the old *hw-* with its letters swapped.
