# da tre anni — how long, and still going

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PIU-DI-01, IT-LEX-C78-TEMPO-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the train is faster than the bus". (*Il treno è più veloce
dell'autobus.*) Then say "a year". (*Un anno.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a length. Italian measures it two ways, and the
choice depends on whether it is over.

## You'll want to know: da tre anni
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-DA-DURATA-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C60-VERBI5-02, IT-GRAMMAR-ABITARE-04, IT-LEX-C78-TEMPO-02] -->

> **Studio** l'italiano **da tre anni**. — I have been studying Italian for
> three years.
> **Abito** qui **da due mesi**. — I have lived here for two months.

Listen to the verbs. English says *have been studying*, a form that reaches back into the past. Italian
says **studio**, the plain **present**, because the studying is still going on
today. **da** with a length of time means "from that far back, up to now".

That is the one to practise until it is automatic, because an English speaker's
instinct sends them to the past, and the past here says something different.

## Grammar Lens: per, for a stretch that is over
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-PER-DURATA-02]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-PASSATO-PROSSIMO-02, IT-LEX-C187-TEMPO187-03, IT-LEX-C92-VIAGGIO92-05] -->

When the stretch has **ended**, the verb goes into the passato prossimo and the
length takes **per**:

> **Ho abitato** a Roma **per due anni**. — I lived in Rome for two years.
> *(and then I left)*

So one English word, *for*, splits in two. **da** + present: it is still going
on. **per** + passato prossimo: it is over. And **durante**, which you have, is
"during": it names the stretch something happened inside, *durante il
viaggio*, "during the journey", not its length.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**da** is two Latin prepositions run together: *dē*, "from, down from", and
*ab*, "from, away from" (EtymDB 2.1 lists it as a compound of the two). So
*da tre anni* is, quite literally, "from three years": counting from a point
three years back, up to the present moment.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-DA-DURATA-01, IT-GRAMMAR-PER-DURATA-02, IT-LEX-C60-VERBI5-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which tense goes with *da tre anni*, and why? (The **present** —
it is still going on.) Which word takes over when the stretch is finished?
(***Per***, with the passato prossimo.) And what is *da*, taken apart?
(***Dē*** and ***ab***, "from" twice.)
