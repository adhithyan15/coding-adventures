Italian, chapter 340: Il dibattito.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il dibattito — the debate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to maintain, then the Italian for the point of view.

You'll want to know: il dibattito.
il dibattito — "the debate".
Stasera c'è un dibattito in televisione sulla scuola. Tonight there is a debate on television about schools.
Un dibattito acceso — a heated debate.

The word, taken apart.
dibattito is built on dibattere "to debate", from dis- and battere "to beat", which is inherited from Latin battuere "to beat, to strike" (Lewis & Short). Ideas are knocked back and forth.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la tesi — the thesis, the position (argued for).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the point of view, then the Italian for the debate.

You'll want to know: la tesi.
la tesi — "the thesis, the position (argued for)".
La sua tesi è semplice: lavoriamo troppo. His thesis is simple: we work too much.
The same in the plural: le tesi. At university, la tesi is the dissertation.

The word, taken apart.
tesi is a learned borrowing from Latin thesis "a proposition" (Lewis & Short), which came from Greek thésis "a setting down", from the verb tithénai "to put".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'argomentazione — the line of argument, the reasoning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the debate, then the Italian for the thesis.

You'll want to know: l'argomentazione.
l'argomentazione — "the line of argument, the reasoning".
La tua argomentazione è chiara, ma non sono d'accordo. Your line of argument is clear, but I do not agree.
The chain of reasons you give; one single reason is un argomento.

The word, taken apart.
argomentazione is a learned borrowing from Latin argūmentātiō "the bringing of proof" (Lewis & Short), from argūmentum "proof, evidence", built on arguere "to make clear, to prove".

Lesson 4 of 5.
convincente — convincing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the thesis, then the Italian for the line of argument.

You'll want to know: convincente.
convincente — "convincing".
Non è una risposta molto convincente. It is not a very convincing answer.
Opposite: poco convincente. One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
convincente is the present participle of convincere, which comes from Latin convincere "to overcome in argument, to prove" (Lewis & Short), built on vincere "to conquer".

Lesson 5 of 5.
persuadere — to persuade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the line of argument, then the Italian for convincing.

You'll want to know: persuadere.
persuadere — "to persuade".
Ho cercato di persuadere mio padre a comprare una macchina nuova. I tried to persuade my father to buy a new car.
Persuadere qualcuno a fare qualcosa. Past participle: persuaso.

The word, taken apart.
persuadere is a learned borrowing from Latin persuādēre "to bring over by talking, to convince" (Lewis & Short), built from per- and suādēre "to advise, to urge".
