Portuguese, chapter 461: Bichos pequenos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o lagarto — the lizard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the falcon, then the Portuguese for to graze.

You'll want to know: o lagarto.
o lagarto — "the lizard".
Um lagarto apanha sol numa pedra. A lizard is sunbathing on a stone.
A small one is a lagartixa.

The word, taken apart.
lagarto is inherited from Vulgar Latin lacartus, from Latin lacertus 'lizard' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o sapo — the toad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the lizard, then the Portuguese for the viper.

You'll want to know: o sapo.
o sapo — "the toad".
À noite ouvimos os sapos no lago. At night we hear the toads in the lake.
Engolir um sapo — to swallow a toad: to put up with something unpleasant.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of sapo is not settled; it may be a pre-Roman word of the Iberian Peninsula, and EtymDB lists it without a source.

You'll want to know: o caracol.
o caracol — "the snail; the curl".
Depois da chuva, há caracóis no jardim. After the rain, there are snails in the garden.
In Portugal, caracóis are a summer snack. Escada de caracol — a spiral staircase.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rastejar — to crawl, to creep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the toad, then the Portuguese for the snail.

You'll want to know: rastejar.
rastejar — "to crawl, to creep".
As cobras rastejam pelo chão. Snakes crawl along the ground.
Built on o rasto, the trail.

The word, taken apart.
rastejar is built on rasto 'trail, track', which is usually traced to Latin rāstrum 'rake' (Lewis & Short), itself linked with rādere 'to scrape': the marks a rake leaves on the ground.
