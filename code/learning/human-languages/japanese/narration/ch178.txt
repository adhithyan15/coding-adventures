Japanese, chapter 178: Meat and Milk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぶたにく (butaniku) — pork.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to get burnt, then the Japanese for to go bad.

You'll want to know: ぶたにく.
ぶたにく — butaniku — "pork".
にくやで ぶたにくを かいます。 — nikuya de butaniku o kaimasu — "I buy pork at the butcher's."

The word, taken apart.
ぶたにく (butaniku) is built from native buta, "pig", and Sino-Japanese niku, "meat", a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese nyuwk) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ぎゅうにく (gyūniku) — beef.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go bad, then the Japanese for pork.

You'll want to know: ぎゅうにく.
ぎゅうにく — gyūniku — "beef".
ぎゅうにくは すこし たかいです。 — gyūniku wa sukoshi takai desu — "Beef is a little expensive."

The word, taken apart.
ぎゅうにく (gyūniku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gyū, "cow" (Middle Chinese ngjuw) and niku, "meat" (nyuwk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ひやす (hiyasu) — to chill; to cool.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for chicken meat, then the Japanese for milk.

You'll want to know: ひやす.
ひやす — hiyasu — "to chill; to cool".
なつは すいかを ひやします。 — natsu wa suika o hiyashimasu — "In summer we chill watermelon."
Polite ひやします, hiyashimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ひやす (hiyasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
