# ъ (tvyórdyy znak) — the hard sign inside объявление

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-C93-SHCHA-01, RU-SCRIPT-SOFT-SIGN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write щ and ь once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **объявление** (*obyavléniye*), "an announcement".
You met it a moment ago, at the station. This lesson takes one letter out of it
and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ъ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-SCRIPT-C266-TVYORDYY-ZNAK-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-OBYAVLENIYE-01] -->

**ъ** — the **hard sign**, *tvyórdyy znak*.

It is inside **объявление** (*obyavléniye*), right after **об-**. It looks like
**ь** with a short flag at the top left, and like **ь** it has no sound of its own.
It keeps the **б** apart from the *y* that **я** carries: *ob-yav-lé-ni-ye*, with a
small break after the *b*, and not *o-byav-*.

It turns up mostly where a prefix ending in a consonant meets **е**, **ё**, **ю**
or **я**: **подъезд** (*podyézd*), the entrance to a block of flats, is *под-* with
*-езд*; **съесть** (*syest*), "to eat up", is *с-* with *есть*, "to eat".

Both signs put a *y* before the next vowel. **ь** also softens the consonant, and
sits inside a word: **семья** (*semyá*), "a family". **ъ** asks for a hard one, and
sits after a prefix. Before the spelling reform of 1918 it also closed every word
ending in a hard consonant: **домъ** for **дом**.

## Writing: ъ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-C266-TVYORDYY-ZNAK-01] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ъ yourself —
slowly, and larger than it is printed.

> The strip shows **where to start this letter and which way to travel**, in one
> attested order, and names its source beneath it. Teachers vary, so treat it as
> a sound way in, not the only one.
