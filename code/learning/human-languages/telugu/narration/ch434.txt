Telugu, chapter 434: Systems and Situations.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
వ్యవస్థ (vyavastha) — a system; an institution.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for dominance, then the Telugu for to be deprived of.

You'll want to know: వ్యవస్థ.
వ్యవస్థ — vyavastha — "a system; an institution".
మన విద్యా వ్యవస్థలో మార్పులు రావాలి. mana vidyā vyavasthalō mārpulu rāvāli. Changes must come in our education system.
Plural: వ్యవస్థలు, vyavasthalu.
Plural వ్యవస్థలు.

The word, taken apart.
వ్యవస్థ (vyavastha) is borrowed from Sanskrit vyavasthā, "respective difference; abiding in one place, steadiness" (Monier-Williams); Telugu uses it for a settled order, a system.

Lesson 4 of 5.
సందర్భం (sandarbhaṁ) — an occasion; a context.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a policy, then the Telugu for a state of affairs.

You'll want to know: సందర్భం.
సందర్భం — sandarbhaṁ — "an occasion; a context".
ఆ మాట ఏ సందర్భంలో అన్నారు? ā māṭa ē sandarbhaṁlō annāru? In what context was that said?
Plural: సందర్భాలు, sandarbhālu.
సందర్భంగా, on the occasion of: పుట్టినరోజు సందర్భంగా, on the occasion of a birthday.

The word, taken apart.
సందర్భం (sandarbhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit saṁdarbha, "stringing or binding together; a literary composition" (Monier-Williams); Telugu uses it for the setting of a word or an event.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఏర్పడు (ērpaḍu) — to be formed; to take shape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a state of affairs, then the Telugu for an occasion.

You'll want to know: ఏర్పడు.
ఏర్పడు — ērpaḍu — "to be formed; to take shape".
ఆ సంఘం పది సంవత్సరాల కిందట ఏర్పడింది. ā saṅghaṁ padi saṁvatsarāla kindaṭa ērpaḍindi. That association was formed ten years ago.
Past (it …): ఏర్పడింది, ērpaḍindi.
Groups, habits and ideas take shape: అలవాటు ఏర్పడింది, a habit formed. Its past is shown in the third person, ఏర్పడింది; ఏర్పడ్డాను, I was formed, is not said.

The word, taken apart.
ఏర్పడు (ērpaḍu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 905 lists Telugu ērpaḍu beside ēlu, "to accept, to admit", with -పడు, the verb paḍu (DEDR 3853).
