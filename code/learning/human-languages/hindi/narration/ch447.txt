Hindi, chapter 447: Bushes and Creepers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कुंज (kuñj) — a bower, a grove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to bloom, then the Hindi for to wilt.

You'll want to know: कुंज.
कुंज — kuñj — "a bower, a grove".
बगीचे के कोने में एक छोटा कुंज है। bagīce ke kone meṁ ek choṭā kuñj hai. There is a little bower in the corner of the garden.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
कुंज (kuñj) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kuñja, "a place overrun with plants or creepers, a bower" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
बेल (bel) — a creeper, a vine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to wilt, then the Hindi for a bower.

You'll want to know: बेल.
बेल — bel — "a creeper, a vine".
दीवार पर हरी बेल चढ़ रही है। dīvār par harī bel caṛh rahī hai. A green creeper is climbing the wall.
Feminine.
A different बेल (bel), masculine, is the wood-apple tree.

The word, taken apart.
बेल (bel) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit velli, "a creeping plant" (CDIAL 12123), which lists Hindi bel, "creeper".

Lesson 3 of 5.
काई (kāī) — moss; green scum (on water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a bower, then the Hindi for a creeper.

You'll want to know: काई.
काई — kāī — "moss; green scum (on water)".
पत्थरों पर काई थी, इसलिए मैं गिर गया। pattharoṁ par kāī thī, isliye maiṁ gir gayā. There was moss on the stones, so I fell over.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
काई (kāī) goes back to kāvikā, "scum", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 3109), which lists Hindi kāī, "green scum on stagnant water".

Lesson 5 of 5.
लिपटना (lipaṭnā) — to cling; to wind round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for moss, then the Hindi for an insect.

You'll want to know: लिपटना.
लिपटना — lipaṭnā — "to cling; to wind round".
बेल पेड़ से लिपट गई। bel peṛ se lipaṭ gaī. The creeper wound itself round the tree.
Past (he …): वह बच्चा माँ से लिपट गया, vah baccā māṁ se lipaṭ gayā.

The word, taken apart.
लिपटना (lipaṭnā) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit lipyate, "is smeared" (CDIAL 11061), with Hindi lipaṭnā, "to stick": to stick close round something.
