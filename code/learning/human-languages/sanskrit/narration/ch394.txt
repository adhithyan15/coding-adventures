Sanskrit, chapter 394: Setting Out on Pilgrimage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तीर्थयात्रा (tīrthayātrā) — a pilgrimage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for radio, then the Sanskrit for the internet.

You'll want to know: तीर्थयात्रा.
तीर्थयात्रा — tīrthayātrā — "a pilgrimage".
अहं श्वः तीर्थयात्रां गमिष्यामि। ahaṃ śvaḥ tīrthayātrāṃ gamiṣyāmi. Tomorrow I will go on a pilgrimage.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तीर्थयात्रा (tīrthayātrā) is built from tīrtha, "a ford, a holy bathing place", and yātrā, "a journey"; MW gives the compound as a journey to holy places, a pilgrimage (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
प्रतिष्ठते (pratiṣṭhate) — starts on a journey; sets out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the internet, then the Sanskrit for a pilgrimage.

You'll want to know: प्रतिष्ठते.
प्रतिष्ठते — pratiṣṭhate — "starts on a journey; sets out".
यात्रिकः प्रातः गृहात् प्रतिष्ठते। yātrikaḥ prātaḥ gṛhāt pratiṣṭhate. The pilgrim sets out from home in the morning.
Past (he …): सः प्रातः प्रस्थितवान्, saḥ prātaḥ prasthitavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिष्ठते (pratiṣṭhate) is built from the prefix pra, "forth", and the root √sthā, "to stand", which also gives tiṣṭhati, "stands"; MW gives pra-sthā as to set out or depart, with the past participle prasthita, and Pāṇini (1.3.22) gives √sthā the middle endings after pra (MW).

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pilgrimage, then the Sanskrit for starts on a journey.

You'll want to know: पाथेयम्.
पाथेयम् — pātheyam — "food for the journey".
माता मह्यं पाथेयं ददाति। mātā mahyaṃ pātheyaṃ dadāti. Mother gives me food for the journey.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
पाथेयम् (pātheyam) is built on pathin, "a road", with the ending -eya, "belonging to"; MW gives pātheya as provisions for a journey (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
याति (yāti) — travels; goes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for starts on a journey, then the Sanskrit for food for the journey.

You'll want to know: याति.
याति — yāti — "travels; goes".
सः धूमयानेन नगरं याति। saḥ dhūmayānena nagaraṃ yāti. He travels to the city by train.
Past (he …): सः नगरं यातवान्, saḥ nagaraṃ yātavān.

The word, taken apart.
याति (yāti) comes from the root √yā, "to go, to travel", which also gives yātrā, "a journey", and yāna, "a vehicle"; MW gives the participle yāta, "gone"; it lives on as Hindi jānā, "to go" (MW; CDIAL 10452).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: कमण्डलुः.
कमण्डलुः — kamaṇḍaluḥ — "an ascetic's water-pot".
यात्रिकः कमण्डलौ जलं वहति। yātrikaḥ kamaṇḍalau jalaṃ vahati. The pilgrim carries water in his water-pot.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कमण्डलुः (kamaṇḍaluḥ) is the word MW gives for the water-pot that ascetics and pilgrims carry; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
