# cinco piezas sueltas — five things that finish five different jobs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-01, ES-ORTH-C428-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the five before reading the table. They have less in common
than usual, which is the point.

## Grammar Lens: what each one completes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-01, ES-ORTH-C428-02, ES-ORTH-C428-03, ES-ORTH-C428-04, ES-ORTH-C428-05, ES-ORTH-C424-07] -->

| word | finishes |
|---|---|
| la arroba | reading an address aloud |
| el guion bajo | the same |
| ¡Felicidades! | congratulating somebody |
| ¡Salud! | raising a glass, and answering a sneeze |
| el animal | naming a creature you cannot name |

The address needed only these two: *punto* and *guion* were already yours from
the punctuation chapter.

Each was the **last missing piece** of something the corpus could otherwise
nearly do. That is what makes them worth a chapter together despite having
nothing else in common: the address needed two more words, the social phrases
were absent although the adjectives behind them were taught, and a dozen
creatures had no category.

## Grammar Lens: two of them are nouns doing other work
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-03, ES-ORTH-C428-04, ES-ORTH-C428-05] -->

| word | as a noun | as an act |
|---|---|---|
| salud | health | cheers, bless you |
| felicidad | happiness | — |
| felicidades | — | congratulations |

**¡Salud!** drops its article and becomes a wish. **¡Felicidades!** takes a
plural and becomes an act. Neither is a new word in the ordinary sense; both are
an existing word put to a different use, which is why the adjectives behind them
were never enough on their own.
