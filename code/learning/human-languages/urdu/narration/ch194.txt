Urdu, chapter 194: Square, Crooked, Shallow, Stale, Spicy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
چوکور (chaukor) — square.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for greedy, then the Urdu for worried.

You'll want to know: چوکور.
چوکور — chaukor — "square".

The word, taken apart.
چوکور (chaukor) is "four-edged": chau-, "four", with kor, "edge", which CDIAL lists under a reconstructed korā, "edge" (CDIAL 3531).

The word, taken apart.
ٹیڑھا (ṭeṛhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from treḍḍha, "slanting, squinting", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6071). It is a form CDIAL gives beside a reconstructed treḍḍa.

Lesson 4 of 5.
باسی (bāsī) — stale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for crooked, then the Urdu for shallow.

You'll want to know: باسی.
باسی — bāsī — "stale".

The word, taken apart.
باسی (bāsī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vāsita, "infused, scented" (CDIAL 11604). The sense turned from "scented" to "smelling, stale".
