Lesson 1 of 6.
zhòngshù — to plant trees.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to cut down on, then the Mandarin for disposable (used once).

You'll want to know: zhòngshù.
zhòngshù — "to plant trees".
měi nián chūntiān wǒmen dōu qù shān lǐ zhòngshù — Every spring we go to the hills to plant trees.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhòngshù is built from zhòng 'to plant' and shù 'a tree', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyowngH and dzyuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'trees as a whole' and 'to set up'.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 成为.
成为 — chéngwéi — "to become".
他想成为一个医生 tā xiǎng chéngwéi yí ge yīshēng — He wants to become a doctor.
The new character, in pieces: 成 (chéng) — six strokes: a short bar, a long stroke falling to the left, a small turning hook, the long slanting hook of 我, a short fall and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
成为 (chéngwéi) is built from 成 (chéng) 'to complete' and 为 'to make', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and hjwe (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to finish' and the second 'to do'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
成 (chéng) — a bar, a fall, a small turn, then the slanting hook of 我.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. Script — a bar, a fall, a small turn, then the slanting hook of 我.
成 (chéng).
One shape, six strokes in a fixed order. The order is the thing to hold.
A bar, left to right, from the left edge to past the middle.
A long stroke falling to the left from the left end of the bar down to the foot.
A small turning hook under the bar: a short bar that turns down and hooks to the left.
The long slanting hook, as in 我: from the top, a long stroke down to the lower right that flicks up at the end. Then a short stroke falling to the left across it, and a dot at the upper right.
Six strokes in all.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 成 (chéng), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 成 (chéng)? (Six.) Which stroke flicks up at the end? (The long slanting hook, the fourth.) What comes last? (The dot at the upper right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
成本 (chéngběn) — production cost; cost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to plant trees, then the Mandarin for to become.

You'll want to know: 成本.
成本 — chéngběn — "production cost; cost".
请保安的成本很高 qǐng bǎo'ān de chéngběn hěn gāo — It costs a lot to hire security guards.

The word, taken apart.
成本 (chéngběn) is built from 成 (chéng) 'to complete' and 本 'the root', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and pwonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'the foot of a tree': what a finished thing rests on. The sense 'cost' is not given there.
