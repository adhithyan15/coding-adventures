Gujarati, chapter 272: Promises, hope and courage.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
હિંમત (himmat) — courage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for foolish, then the Gujarati for stubborn.

You'll want to know: હિંમત.
હિંમત — himmat — "courage".
મારામાં બોલવાની હિંમત નથી. — mārāmā̃ bolvānī himmat nathī — "I do not have the courage to speak."

The word, taken apart.
હિંમત (himmat) is a loan from Arabic himma, "resolve, aspiration", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is h-m-m, "to intend" (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 7.
વચન (vacan) — promise, word.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for stubborn, then the Gujarati for courage.

You'll want to know: વચન.
વચન — vacan — "promise, word".
શું તમે વચન આપો છો? — śũ tame vacan āpo cho? — "Do you promise?"
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
વચન (vacan) is Sanskrit vacaná, "speaking, statement", taken over as a tatsama. CDIAL also lists the inherited Gujarati veṇ, "word, promise", from the same word (CDIAL 11199).

Lesson 3 of 7.

Warm-up.

નોતરવું — notarvũ — "to invite".
શું તમે પડોશીને લગ્નમાં નોતર્યા? — śũ tame paḍośīne lagnamā̃ notaryā? — "Did you invite the neighbours to the wedding?"

The word, taken apart.
નોતરવું (notarvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nimantrayatē, "invites" (CDIAL 7235).

મારો ભાઈ મને નથી સતાવતો. — māro bhāī mane nathī satāvto — "My brother does not bother me."

સતાવવું (satāvvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit saṁtāpayati, "causes to be heated; torments"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to tease, vex" (CDIAL 12886).

Lesson 5 of 7.
આશા રાખવી (āśā rākhvī) — to hope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to invite, then the Gujarati for to pester.

You'll want to know: આશા રાખવી.
આશા રાખવી — āśā rākhvī — "to hope".
હું આશા રાખું છું કે તમે આવશો. — hũ āśā rākhũ chũ ke tame āvśo — "I hope that you will come."

The word, taken apart.
આશા રાખવી (āśā rākhvī), to keep a hope, is built from āśā, Sanskrit āśā́, "hope", taken over as a tatsama (EtymDB; CDIAL 7278 cites it), and the taught verb rākhvũ, "to keep".
