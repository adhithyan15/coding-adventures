# ◌ृ — written as one vowel, said as another

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-111] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: **क** — what sound does it carry?

[PAUSE 2s] Something is about to go underneath it.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ृ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-144]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ृ** — a small hook below the consonant.

It is a **vowel sign**, so it attaches to a consonant rather than standing on its
own, and it **replaces** the *a* built into that consonant.

Worked through: **क** + **◌ृ** = **कृ**.

## Script: the name and the sound have come apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-144] -->

This mark is called the **vocalic ṛ** — a vowel made out of an *r*, which is a
thing Sanskrit had and Hindi does not.

**So a Hindi speaker says it as *ri*.** You will see **कृ** written and hear
*kri*. The spelling records where the word came from; the mouth does something
simpler.

That is worth knowing early, because it explains a word that would otherwise
look unreadable to you on the page and sound perfectly ordinary out loud.

| written | said |
|---|---|
| ◌ृ | *ri* |

Nearly every word carrying this mark is one Hindi took from Sanskrit, which is
why the mark is uncommon and the words that carry it are not.

## Writing: ◌ृ — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-144] -->

Put your pen on **◌ृ** and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
