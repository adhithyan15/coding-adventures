Mandarin Chinese, chapter 44: Places in Town.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
车 (chē) — car; vehicle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go out, then the Mandarin for school.

You'll want to know: 车.
车 — chē — "car; vehicle".
你有车吗 nǐ yǒu chē ma — Have you got a car?
The new character, in pieces: 车 (chē) — four strokes: a bar, a stroke that falls and turns, a long bar, and a vertical through them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
车 (chē) is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains as a cart, first made, it says, by the Yellow Emperor.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 2 of 6.
车 (chē) — four strokes, the vertical last.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — four strokes, the vertical last.
车 (chē).
One piece, four strokes.
A short horizontal at the top.
A stroke that falls to the left and turns right, along under the first bar.
A long horizontal across the bottom.
Last, the vertical, from the top bar down through everything to the foot.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 车 (chē), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 车 (chē)? (Four.) Which stroke is last? (The vertical.) Which is the longest bar? (The third stroke, along the bottom.)

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàndiàn is built from 饭 'meal' and diàn 'shop', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjonH and temH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

The word, taken apart.
chūzūchē is built from chūzū 'to rent out' and 车 (chē) 'car'; its parts are inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhwit, tsu 'tax' and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
