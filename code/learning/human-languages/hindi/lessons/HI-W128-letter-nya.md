# ञ (ña) — the letter hidden inside ज्ञ

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-C128-I-SIGN-01, HI-SCRIPT-C128-I-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ◌ि and इ once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **संज्ञा** (*saṁjñā*), "noun".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ञ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-C128-NYA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ञ** — *ña*, a nasal made where *j* is made.

It is hidden inside **संज्ञा** (*saṁjñā*), "noun". The joined letter **ज्ञ** is **ज**
and **ञ** written as one, and Hindi says the pair *gy*. You have been reading **ञ**
inside that join without seeing it on its own.

## Writing: ञ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-C128-NYA-01] -->

- **1.** start here: sweep clockwise around the open-left bowl
- **2.** lift, then sweep the shoulder right and rise to the headline
- **3.** lift, then descend the short lower stem
- **4.** lift, then draw the shirorekha left-to-right — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 3.**

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ञ-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
