# Letters that hold hands, and two that will not

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-SIN-01, FA-SCRIPT-MIM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write س, then م. Four letters are now yours.

## Script — the running line
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-JOINING-01]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-ALEF-01] -->

Persian is not written as separate letters sitting side by side. Letters
**join**, running into one another along a single line, the way English cursive
does — except that in Persian it is not a style. It is the ordinary way to write,
and printed text does it too.

Because they join, most letters **change shape** depending on where they sit:
a letter at the start of a joined run, one in the middle, one at the end, and one
standing alone can all look different. They are the same letter.

That sounds like four times the work. It is not, and here is why: the changes are
almost always **the same trimming**. A letter's identifying part stays, and the
finishing flourish is cut short so the next letter can begin.

Look at sin, which you can already write:

> alone: **س** — in the middle of a run: **ـسـ**

The three teeth survive. The bowl is what gets trimmed away.

## Script — and the letters that refuse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-JOINING-01, FA-SCRIPT-ALEF-01] -->

Now the rule that saves you the most trouble.

**A few letters join to the letter before them, but never to the letter after.**
They connect on the right, and refuse on the left, so the run **breaks** and the
next letter starts fresh as though beginning a new word.

**alef is one of them.** So when you meet a gap in the middle of a Persian word,
it is very often not a gap at all — it is one of these non-joining letters doing
exactly what it always does.

That single fact turns a page of Persian from an unbroken puzzle into something
with visible seams.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-SIN-01, FA-SCRIPT-LAM-01, FA-SCRIPT-ALEF-01, FA-SCRIPT-MIM-01] -->

Four letters, and they spell a word you have been saying since the very first
lesson:

> سلام

Read it right to left: **س** *s*, **ل** *l*, **ا** *â*, **م** *m*. Say it:
**salâm**.

Two things to notice. The short vowel between s and l is **not written** —
Persian normally prints only the long vowels, and readers supply the rest. And
the break before the final م is alef refusing to join, exactly as promised.
