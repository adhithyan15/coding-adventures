Latin, chapter 468: To dye, Purple shellfish, Purple dye, Paint, Paintbrush.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tingō, tingere — to dye, to colour; to wet, to dip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tanner, then the Latin for a furrier.

You'll want to know: tingō, tingere.
tingō, tingere — "to dye, to colour; to wet, to dip".
Servus lānam in aquā calidā tingit. The slave dyes the wool in hot water.
tingō, tingere, tīnxī, tīnctum, also spelled tinguō. English tinge, tint and tincture come from it.

The word, taken apart.
tingō is inherited from PIE teng- 'to dip, to soak' (EtymDB), and Greek tengō 'to wet' is a cousin. English tinge, tint and tincture come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
mūrex, mūricis — the purple shellfish, murex; purple dye.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a furrier, then the Latin for to dye.

You'll want to know: mūrex, mūricis.
mūrex, mūricis — "the purple shellfish, murex; purple dye".
Ex mūrice color purpureus fit. A purple colour is made from the murex shell.
Third declension, masculine: mūrex, mūricis m. Thousands of these sea snails went into a little purple dye. English keeps murex.

The word, taken apart.
mūrex is usually traced to Greek myax 'sea mussel' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short give the sense but no source, so the path is hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ostrum, ostrī — purple dye; purple cloth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to dye, then the Latin for the purple shellfish.

You'll want to know: ostrum, ostrī.
ostrum, ostrī — "purple dye; purple cloth".
Dīves vestem ostrō tīnctam gerit. The rich man wears a garment dyed with purple.
Second declension, neuter: ostrum, ostrī n. A word of poetry for the dye and for cloth coloured with it.

The word, taken apart.
ostrum was borrowed from Greek ostreon 'oyster, shellfish' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), since the dye came from a shellfish.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pigmentum, pigmentī — a paint, a colour, a pigment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the purple shellfish, then the Latin for purple dye.

You'll want to know: pigmentum, pigmentī.
pigmentum, pigmentī — "a paint, a colour, a pigment".
Pictor pigmenta in tabulā miscet. The painter mixes his paints on a board.
Second declension, neuter: pigmentum, pigmentī n., from pingō 'to paint'. English pigment comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
pigmentum is built on pingō 'to paint' with the suffix -mentum (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English pigment comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pēnicillus, pēnicillī — a paintbrush, a fine brush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for purple dye, then the Latin for a paint.

You'll want to know: pēnicillus, pēnicillī.
pēnicillus, pēnicillī — "a paintbrush, a fine brush".
Pictor pēnicillō tenuī flōrēs pingit. The painter paints flowers with a fine brush.
Second declension, masculine: pēnicillus, pēnicillī m. (also pēnicillum n.). English pencil comes from it: the first pencils were fine brushes.

The word, taken apart.
pēnicillus is a diminutive of pēniculus 'brush' (Lewis & Short), which first meant 'a little tail', since brushes were made of ox-tails and horse-tails. English pencil comes from it through French.
