Mandarin Chinese, chapter 489: On the Staff.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tiàocáo — to move to another employer; to change jobs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to translate, then the Mandarin for to earn money.

You'll want to know: tiàocáo.
tiàocáo — "to move to another employer; to change jobs".
gōngzuò yālì tài dà suǒyǐ tā tiàocáo le — The pressure at work was too much, so he moved to another firm.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiàocáo is built from tiào 'to jump' and cáo 'a trough', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dew and dzaw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to leap' and the second 'a horse's trough'. A horse leaving one trough for the next is the picture usually given, but the dictionary does not give the sense.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 2 of 6.
职工 (zhígōng) — member of staff; employee (of a firm or factory).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to earn money, then the Mandarin for to move to another employer.

You'll want to know: 职工.
职工 — zhígōng — "member of staff; employee (of a firm or factory)".
zhè jiā gōngchǎng yǒu yìbǎi ge zhígōng — This factory has a hundred staff.
The new character, in pieces: 职 (zhí) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left an ear piece of six strokes, a bar, two verticals with two short bars between them and a stroke rising at the foot; on the right 只 zhǐ, five strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
职工 (zhígōng) is built from 职 (zhí) 'a post' and 工 'work', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyik and kuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be in charge of' and, after an older dictionary, 'an occupation'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
职 (zhí) — an ear piece, then zhǐ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — an ear piece, then zhǐ.
职 (zhí).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, six strokes. A bar on top. A short vertical on the left, then a long vertical on the right that runs down past everything else to the foot. Two short bars between them. Last, a stroke that rises to the right across the bottom.
The right piece, 只 zhǐ, five strokes, as you wrote it: 口 kǒu, then a stroke falling to the left and a dot falling to the right under it.
6 + 5 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 职 (zhí), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 职 (zhí) have? (Two.) What is the right piece read? (zhǐ.) Which stroke of the left piece runs longest? (The vertical on its right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
全职 (quánzhí) — full-time (of a job).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to move to another employer, then the Mandarin for a member of staff.

You'll want to know: 全职.
全职 — quánzhí — "full-time (of a job)".
quánzhí de gōngzuò bǐ dǎgōng lèi — A full-time job is more tiring than casual work.

The word, taken apart.
全职 (quánzhí) is built from 全 'whole' and 职 (zhí) 'a post', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjwen and tsyik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'complete'.
