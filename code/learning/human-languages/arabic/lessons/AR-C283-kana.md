# كان (kāna) — was

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01, AR-LEX-C92-TOWN92-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I wrote" and "she wrote". (*Katabtu*, *katabat*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Now say "I am at the office". (*Anā fī al-maktab*.) There is no
"am" in it: in the present, Arabic sets the two halves side by side. In the
past it cannot, and it needs a verb.

## You'll want to know: كان
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C283-MADI283-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C93-TOWN93-03, AR-LEX-C55-FAMILY55-01] -->

**كان** — *kāna* — "he was, it was": the past of "to be".

> **كنت في المكتب أمس.** — *kuntu fī al-maktab ams* — I was at the office
> yesterday.

> **كانت جدتي في القرية.** — *kānat jaddatī fī al-qarya* — My grandmother was
> in the village.

It takes the endings of the lesson before, with one change. Where the ending
starts with a consonant, the long *ā* shrinks to *u*: *kuntu*, I was;
*kunta*, *kunti*, you were; *kunnā*, we were. Where it does not, the *ā*
stays: *kāna*, *kānat*, *kānū*, they were.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**كان** (*kāna*) is built on the native Arabic root *k-w-n*, "to be, to come
into being" (Lane). Lane lists *makāna*, "a place of being", among the words of the same
root. The *w* of the root is the reason for the two
shapes: it surfaces as long *ā* in *kāna* and as short *u* in *kuntu*.
