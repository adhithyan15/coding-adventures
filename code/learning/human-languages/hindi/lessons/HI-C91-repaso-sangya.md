# saṁjñā kī tālikā — the noun, on one page

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the first question you ask a noun before making it plural, and
say why it comes first.

## Grammar Lens: the four plurals
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-04] -->

| noun | plural |
|---|---|
| बच्चा (m, -आ) | बच्चे |
| आदमी (m, other) | आदमी |
| किताब (f, consonant) | किताबें |
| भाषा (f, -आ) | भाषाएँ |

Gender first, ending second. One row swaps a vowel, two add a nasal, and one
does nothing at all.

## Grammar Lens: the ending that means two things
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-06, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05] -->

| what you see | how many | why |
|---|---|---|
| बच्चे लंबे हैं | more than one | nothing follows the noun |
| बच्चे को | one | a postposition follows |

**The postposition is the signal.** That is the single most useful thing in this
chapter, because **-ए** is everywhere and a reader who cannot tell the two apart
will miscount every sentence that has one.

## Grammar Lens: the space in front of the noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-07, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-05] -->

| before the noun | after the noun |
|---|---|
| nothing, एक, यह, वह | पर, से, को |

A Hindi noun phrase is a noun with an optional word in front and an optional
word behind. Everything this chapter taught fits in that one picture.
