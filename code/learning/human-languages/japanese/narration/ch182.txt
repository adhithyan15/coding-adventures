Japanese, chapter 182: Paying, Cash and Coins.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はらう (harau) — to pay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to sell, then the Japanese for to sell well.

You'll want to know: はらう.
はらう — harau — "to pay".
ここで おかねを はらいます。 — koko de okane o haraimasu — "Customers pay here."
Polite はらいます, haraimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
はらう (harau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おつり (otsuri) — change (money back).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to sell well, then the Japanese for to pay.

You'll want to know: おつり.
おつり — otsuri — "change (money back)".
おつりは じゅうえんです。 — otsuri wa jūen desu — "The change is ten yen."

The word, taken apart.
おつり (otsuri) is built from polite o- and native tsuri, written with the same kanji as tsuri, "fishing"; how the money sense arose is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
げんきん (genkin) — cash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pay, then the Japanese for change.

You'll want to know: げんきん.
げんきん — genkin — "cash".
げんきんで はらいます。 — genkin de haraimasu — "I pay in cash."

The word, taken apart.
げんきん (genkin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gen, "present, actual" (Middle Chinese henH) and kin, "gold, money" (kim) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

さつ — satsu — "banknote; paper money".
さいふに さつが ありません。 — saifu ni satsu ga arimasen — "There are no banknotes in my wallet."

The word, taken apart.
さつ (satsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): one Chinese character, "tag, paper money", read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese tsreat) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
こぜに (kozeni) — small coins; small change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for cash, then the Japanese for banknote.

You'll want to know: こぜに.
こぜに — kozeni — "small coins; small change".
こぜにで はらいます。 — kozeni de haraimasu — "I pay with small coins."

The word, taken apart.
こぜに (kozeni) is built from native ko-, "small", and zeni, "coin"; KANJIDIC2 lists zeni as a kun reading, but it looks like an early loan from Chinese (Middle Chinese dzjen), so its history is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
