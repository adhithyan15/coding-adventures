Japanese, chapter 219: Slips, Falls and Breaks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ころぶ (korobu) — to fall over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a wound, then the Japanese for life.

You'll want to know: ころぶ.
ころぶ — korobu — "to fall over".
こどもは ころびませんでしたか。 — kodomo wa korobimasen deshita ka — "Didn't the child fall over?"
Polite ころびます, korobimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ころぶ (korobu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
たおれる (taoreru) — to collapse; to fall down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to slip, then the Japanese for to snap.

You'll want to know: たおれる.
たおれる — taoreru — "to collapse; to fall down".
かぜで きが たおれました。 — kaze de ki ga taoremashita — "A tree fell down in the wind."
Polite たおれます, taoremasu; an ichidan verb. Its partner たおす, taosu, means "to knock down".

The word, taken apart.
たおれる (taoreru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it pairs with taosu, "to knock down" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
