## You'll want to know: 血
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C479-BLOOD479-02]; assesses=[] -->

**血** — *xiě* — "blood".

> **你看这是血** *nǐ kàn zhè shì xiě* — Look, that's blood.

The new character, in pieces: 血 — six strokes: a short stroke falling to the left at the top, then a dish of five, as at the foot of 温 *wēn*. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

Alone, and in everyday speech, 血 is said xiě; in words such as liúxuè it is said xuè.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**血** (*xiě*) is inherited from Middle Chinese *xwet* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), explained after an older book as what flows out of the flesh. The regular sound change gives *xuè*; the everyday *xiě* is a second, spoken reading, and how it arose is not settled.
