# The staged-above-A1 entries come down, and the written paper finally moves

HL23 §10.5 found that the verb famine and the noun famine are **multiplicative on
the same items**. §11 acts on it, and the arithmetic was proved before anything
was built:

| granted | mock 1 G1 | mock 2 G1 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| the ~100 missing nouns **alone** | 28,33 | 26,33 | **NO APTO** |
| the 17 staged entries **alone** | 13,33 | 7,17 | NO APTO |
| **both** | **33,33** | **33,33** | **APTO** |

Authoring every noun the exam asks for and stopping there lands **1,67 and 3,67
short**. The staging half is load-bearing, so it ships first and every authoring
tranche after it is measured on top.

## Three rungs, and one node that was simply mis-staged

- **`SPINE-NAME-EVERYDAY-THINGS`** (A1, LEXICON) — *I can name common everyday things.*
- **`SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-LIKE`** (A1, FUNCTION) — *I can say what I like and what I do not like.*
- **`SPINE-SAY-WHY`** (A1, FUNCTION) — *I can give one reason for something.*

`gustar` got its own rung rather than being absorbed by the everyday-action node
— liking is not an action — and `porque` got its own rather than dragging B1's
`SPINE-GIVE-REASONS` wording down. Eight verbs (ask, stand, write, wait, read,
bring, come, walk) moved onto `SPINE-NAME-EVERYDAY-ACTIONS` with **no `canDo`
change**, because each is a common everyday action.

**`SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-WANT` was mis-staged, and that is the best finding here.** Its
`canDo` — *"I can say what I want or need, and ask for it"* — is an A1 capability
that DELE A1 tests directly. It sat at A2 because it declared the A2
`SPINE-SAY-WHAT-I-DO` as a prerequisite, and *quiero un café* needs one memorised
form, not present-tense machinery. Restaged A1, prerequisite dropped, **`canDo`
untouched, because nothing was wrong with it.** HL23 has audited concepts and
never audited node stages; a stage audit is filed in `BACKLOG.d`.

## Two published price tables corrected

§8.2 and §9.1 priced a move as "realizing lessons that are not extension-resident".
That over-counts: a concept moves without its lesson moving when the whole
segment is retargeted. §9.1's `VERB-HAVE` row predicted two
`misplaced-shared-realization` repairs and an emptied `GE-PATH-018` — neither
happened.

The consequential correction: the four "9-track" concepts are the **same twenty
realizers in the same segments** (`ES-PATH-031` holds all four Spanish ones), so
moving them together costs one split per segment instead of one per concept.
`gustar` is §8.2's most expensive row and is nearly free when it travels with its
neighbours.

And one invariant learned the hard way: **a segment split is an extension split.**
Cutting a segment across an extension's lesson set breaks two `validateCurriculum`
rules at once and produced 173 errors before it was caught.
