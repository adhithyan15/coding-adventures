German, chapter 318: Krieg und Frieden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Krieg — the war.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hand down, then the German for the fate.

You'll want to know: der Krieg.
der Krieg — "the war".
Der Krieg dauerte sechs Jahre. The war lasted six years.
Plural: die Kriege.

The word, taken apart.
Krieg is inherited from Middle High German kriec, Old High German krieg, from Proto-Germanic krīgaz (EtymDB); it is usually said to have first meant "effort, struggle".

You'll want to know: der Frieden.
der Frieden — "the peace".

The word, taken apart.
Frieden goes back to Proto-Germanic friþuz "peace" (EtymDB), with the old English cousin frith "peace".

You'll want to know: besiegen.
besiegen — "to defeat, to beat".
Die kleine Armee besiegte die große. The small army defeated the big one.
Regular: besiegte, hat besiegt.

The word, taken apart.
besiegen is built on Sieg "victory", Old High German sigu, Proto-Germanic segaz (EtymDB), with be-.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Niederlage — the defeat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the battle, then the German for to defeat.

You'll want to know: die Niederlage.
die Niederlage — "the defeat".
Nach der Niederlage gingen die Soldaten nach Hause. After the defeat the soldiers went home.
Plural: die Niederlagen. Also in sport.

The word, taken apart.
Niederlage comes from Middle High German niderlāge (EtymDB), from nieder "down", Old High German nidar, and Lage "a lying, a laying", usually linked to legen "to lay": being laid low.
