# う — two strokes, and a vowel that stretches

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-TO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write と. Tick, then sweep, no join.

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-SCRIPT-U-01]; assesses=[] -->

> う

Two strokes, the simplest pair in this chapter:

1. a **short tick** at the top, roughly horizontal
2. a **wide curve** beneath it, falling from the upper left, bellying right, and
   tapering away to the lower left

Read **u**: close to the *oo* of *food* but shorter, and with the lips much less
rounded than an English speaker expects. Do not push them forward.

## What you've built — and what this sign does after と
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-U-01, JA-SCRIPT-TO-01] -->

Something happens when う follows と, and it is the reason the written word and
the spoken word look like they disagree.

The five-sign sequence you are building ends **と + う** — *to* then *u*, which
reads as five beats. But the final two signs are heard as a long **o**:

> written: a-ri-ga-to-u
>
> spoken: a-ri-ga-**too**

**う after an o-sound does not add a syllable. It holds the one before it for a
second beat.** The beat is still there — Japanese counts it, and dropping it
makes the word sound clipped and wrong — but it is the same vowel continuing,
not a new one.

Katakana will later receive a separate one-stroke length mark for the same job.
Here the only written cue is the **う** you have just learned.
