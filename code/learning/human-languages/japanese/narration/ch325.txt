Lesson 1 of 5.
したがう (shitagau) — to obey; to follow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to criticize, then the Japanese for an excuse.

You'll want to know: したがう.
したがう — shitagau — "to obey; to follow".
せんせいの いけんに したがった ほうが いいです。 — sensei no iken ni shitagatta hō ga ii desu — "You should follow the teacher's advice."
Polite したがいます, shitagaimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): がくせいは したがいました。, gakusei wa shitagaimashita.

The word, taken apart.
したがう (shitagau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
うけとめる (uketomeru) — to take to heart; to catch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to obey, then the Japanese for to go against.

You'll want to know: うけとめる.
うけとめる — uketomeru — "to take to heart; to catch".
いけんが ただしいと おもったので、ちちは うけとめました。 — iken ga tadashii to omotta node, chichi wa uketomemashita — "My father thought the point was fair, so he took it to heart."
Polite うけとめます, uketomemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ちちが うけとめました。, chichi ga uketomemashita.

The word, taken apart.
うけとめる (uketomeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from ukeru, "to receive" and tomeru, "to stop", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.
けっきょく (kekkyoku) — in the end; after all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go against, then the Japanese for to take to heart.

You'll want to know: けっきょく.
けっきょく — kekkyoku — "in the end; after all".
あめが ふったので、けっきょく いきませんでした。 — ame ga futta node, kekkyoku ikimasen deshita — "It rained, so in the end we did not go."

The word, taken apart.
けっきょく (kekkyoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ketsu, "tie" (Middle Chinese ket) and kyoku, "conclusion" (gjowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as kek-kyoku.
