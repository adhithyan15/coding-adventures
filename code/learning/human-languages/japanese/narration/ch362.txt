Japanese, chapter 362: What Will It Cost?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ひよう (hiyō) — a cost; an expense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a loss, then the Japanese for to go out of business.

You'll want to know: ひよう.
ひよう — hiyō — "a cost; an expense".
ひようは ばしょに よります。 — hiyō wa basho ni yorimasu — "The cost depends on the place."

The word, taken apart.
ひよう (hiyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hi, "expense" (Middle Chinese phj+jH) and yō, "utilize" (yowngH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
けいさんする (keisan suru) — to calculate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go out of business, then the Japanese for an expense.

You'll want to know: けいさんする.
けいさんする — keisan suru — "to calculate".
かいものが おわったら、けいさんして ください。 — kaimono ga owattara, keisan shite kudasai — "When the shopping is done, please add it up."
Polite けいさんします, keisanshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): てんいんが けいさんしました。, ten'in ga keisan shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
けいさんする (keisan suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, keisan, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: kei, "plan" (Middle Chinese kejH) and san, "calculate" (swanX) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
