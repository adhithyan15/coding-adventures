> **चौथा** — *chauthā* — **fourth**

The *au* sign you wrote in chapter fifty-six is the visible part of this word.
**चार** (*chār*) gives **चौ-** (*chau-*), and then an ending you have not seen
before takes over: **-था**.

## Grammar Lens: four words, three shapes, no rule
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-CHAUTHA, MR-LEX-PAHILA, MR-LEX-TISRA, MR-LEX-DUSRA, MR-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-SET] -->

The chapter's four words, laid out against their numbers:

| how many | which one in the line | what happened |
|---|---|---|
| **एक** | **पहिला** | nothing survives |
| **दोन** | **दुसरा** | **-सरा** |
| **तीन** | **तिसरा** | **-सरा** |
| **चार** | **चौथा** | **-था** |

Three different shapes across four words. Marathi did not build these; it
**inherited** them, each one worn down separately over centuries — Molesworth
records **चौथा** as coming straight from Sanskrit **चतुर्थ**.

So do not look for a rule here. **There is none, and this book will not invent
one**: these four are four words, and the honest instruction is to learn them as
four words. What matters is that the list ENDS. There are exactly four of them,
and you now have all four.
