## You'll want to know: غضب
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C488-MAZAJ488-03]; assesses=[] -->

**غضب** — *ghaḍiba* — "to get angry".

> غضب أبي لأن أخي كسر النافذة.
>
> *ghaḍiba abī li-anna akhī kasara an-nāfidha.*
>
> My father got angry because my brother broke the window.

Present: يغضب, *yaghḍab*.

It takes من for the person: غضب من أخيه.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**غضب** (*ghaḍiba*) is built on the native Arabic root *gh-ḍ-b*, "anger, wrath" (Lane). The adjective ghāḍib, angry, and the noun ghaḍab, anger, come from the same root.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for describing what you lived and felt.
