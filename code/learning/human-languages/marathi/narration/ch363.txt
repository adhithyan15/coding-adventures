Marathi, chapter 363: Power and Its Abuse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सत्ता (sattā) — power (political).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to protest, then the Marathi for to boycott.

You'll want to know: सत्ता.
सत्ता — sattā — "power (political)".
सत्ता हातात आली की माणूस बदलतो. sattā hātāt ālī kī māṇūs badalto. When power comes into his hands, a man changes.
Feminine.
सत्तेवर येणे, to come to power.

The word, taken apart.
सत्ता (sattā) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit sattā, "existence, being" (Monier-Williams). Marathi uses it for the power to rule.

You'll want to know: पारदर्शकता.
पारदर्शकता — pārdarśaktā — "transparency".
सरकारी कामात पारदर्शकता हवी. sarkārī kāmāt pārdarśaktā havī. Government work needs transparency.
Feminine.
पारदर्शक, transparent, + -ता.

The word, taken apart.
पारदर्शकता (pārdarśaktā) is a learned noun built from Sanskrit pāradarśaka, "showing the opposite shore" (Monier-Williams), with -tā, "-ness": what lets one see through.

Lesson 5 of 5.
चिथावणे (cithāvṇe) — to incite; to provoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for transparency, then the Marathi for self-interest.

You'll want to know: चिथावणे.
चिथावणे — cithāvṇe — "to incite; to provoke".
कोणीतरी गर्दीला चिथावलं. koṇītrī gardīlā cithāvlaṁ. Someone incited the crowd.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने चिथावलं, tyāne cithāvlaṁ.
The one incited takes -ला; चिथावणी, incitement.

The word, taken apart.
चिथावणे (cithāvṇe) is built on an older verb, Marathi cithṇẽ, "to take offence", which CDIAL lists under citth-, "pound, tear" (CDIAL 4802): to make someone take offence.
