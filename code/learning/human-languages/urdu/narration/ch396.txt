Urdu, chapter 396: On the Highway.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شاہراہ (shāhrāh) — a highway; a main road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a helmet, then the Urdu for to get dented.

You'll want to know: شاہراہ.
شاہراہ — shāhrāh — "a highway; a main road".
شاہراہ پر ٹرک تیز چلتے ہیں۔ shāhrāh par ṭrak tez chalte haiṅ. Lorries drive fast on the highway.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
شاہراہ (shāhrāh) is built from Persian shāh, "a king", and rāh, "a road" (راہ): it is usually traced to Persian shāhrāh, "the king's road".

Lesson 2 of 5.
محصول (mahsūl) — a toll; a duty (on goods).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to get dented, then the Urdu for a highway.

You'll want to know: محصول.
محصول — mahsūl — "a toll; a duty (on goods)".
شاہراہ پر ہر گاڑی محصول دیتی ہے۔ shāhrāh par har gāṛī mahsūl detī hai. Every vehicle pays a toll on the highway.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
محصول (mahsūl) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic maḥṣūl, "what is gathered, revenue"; in Urdu it is a toll, or a duty paid on goods.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ناکہ (nākā) — a checkpoint (on a road).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a highway, then the Urdu for a toll.

You'll want to know: ناکہ.
ناکہ — nākā — "a checkpoint (on a road)".
پولیس کا ناکہ پل کے پاس ہے۔ polīs kā nākā pul ke pās hai. The police checkpoint is near the bridge.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ناکہ (nākā) is inherited: CDIAL files it under nākka, "like a nose, projecting", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 7037); the sense "a checkpoint" is probably a later one, from a post that juts into the road.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ہانکنا (hāṅknā) — to drive (animals or a cart).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a checkpoint (on a road), then the Urdu for a convoy.

You'll want to know: ہانکنا.
ہانکنا — hāṅknā — "to drive (animals or a cart)".
ایک کسان شاہراہ کے کنارے بکریاں ہانک رہا تھا۔ ek kisān shāhrāh ke kināre bakriyāṅ hāṅk rahā thā. A farmer was driving goats along the edge of the highway.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے گھوڑا ہانکا, us ne ghoṛā hāṅkā.

The word, taken apart.
ہانکنا (hāṅknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin hā̃knā, "to cry out, to drive off", under Sanskrit hakkayati, "calls to" (CDIAL 13939): animals are driven along with shouts.
