## You'll want to know: dǎsǎn
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C518-SUN518-04]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎsǎn* — "to put up an umbrella".

> *wàipó chūmén de shíhou zǒngshì dǎsǎn* — Grandma always puts up an umbrella when she goes out.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before sǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎsǎn* is built from 打 and *sǎn* 'an umbrella', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *sanX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'a canopy'. The vowel of dǎ is not the regular outcome of *taengX*.
