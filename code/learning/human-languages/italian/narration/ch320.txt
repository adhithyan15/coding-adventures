Italian, chapter 320: Feste di paese.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
la fiera — the fair, the trade show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to dress up in costume, then the Italian for to parade.

You'll want to know: la fiera.
la fiera — "the fair, the trade show".
A ottobre c'è la fiera del libro. In October there is the book fair.
An old, unrelated fiera means a wild beast.

The word, taken apart.
fiera "fair" is inherited from Late Latin fēria "a holiday, a feast day" (Lewis & Short, under fēriae "holidays"). EtymDB lists fiera under Latin fera "wild beast", the source of a different fiera.

Lesson 2 of 6.

You'll want to know: il palio.
il palio — "the palio, the traditional horse race".
Il palio si corre in piazza due volte l'anno. The palio is run in the square twice a year.
Named after the prize, a painted banner.

The word, taken apart.
palio is inherited from Latin pallium "a cloak, a cloth" (Lewis & Short): the race is run for a precious cloth.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the fair, then the Italian for the palio.

You'll want to know: la giostra.
la giostra — "the merry-go-round, the funfair ride".
I bambini vogliono andare sulla giostra. The children want to go on the merry-go-round.
Long ago, la giostra was a joust between knights.

The word, taken apart.
giostra was borrowed from Old French joste "a joust" (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin iuxtāre "to come close", built on iuxtā "near".

Lesson 5 of 6.
il santo — the saint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the merry-go-round, then the Italian for the name day.

You'll want to know: il santo.
il santo — "the saint".
Ogni paese festeggia il suo santo. Every village celebrates its own saint.
Before a man's name it usually becomes San, or Sant' before a vowel; a woman saint is santa.

The word, taken apart.
santo is inherited from Latin sānctus "holy" (EtymDB).
