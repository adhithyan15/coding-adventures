Latin, chapter 377: Trades of the Town.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fullō, fullōnis — a fuller, a cleaner of cloth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a labourer, then the Latin for to work.

You'll want to know: fullō, fullōnis.
fullō, fullōnis — "a fuller, a cleaner of cloth".
Fullō vestēs sordidās lavat. The fuller washes dirty clothes.
fullō, fullōnis m. The fuller cleaned and finished woollen cloth by treading it in tubs; his shop was the fullōnica.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of fullō is not settled: Lewis & Short give none, and EtymDB has no parent for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
aurifex, aurificis — a goldsmith.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to work, then the Latin for a fuller.

You'll want to know: aurifex, aurificis.
aurifex, aurificis — "a goldsmith".
Aurifex ānulum aureum fēcit. The goldsmith made a gold ring.
aurifex, aurificis m., from aurum, gold, and faciō, to make.

The word, taken apart.
aurifex is built from aurum "gold" and faciō "to make" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a gold-maker.

Lesson 3 of 5.
lignārius, lignāriī — a carpenter, a worker in wood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fuller, then the Latin for a goldsmith.

You'll want to know: lignārius, lignāriī.
lignārius, lignāriī — "a carpenter, a worker in wood".
Lignārius mēnsās et sellās facit. The carpenter makes tables and chairs.
lignārius, lignāriī m., often faber lignārius: a smith (faber) who works in wood, lignum.

The word, taken apart.
lignārius is built on lignum "wood" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -ārius of a man busy with something.

Lesson 4 of 5.
caementārius, caementāriī — a mason, a builder in stone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a goldsmith, then the Latin for a carpenter.

You'll want to know: caementārius, caementāriī.
caementārius, caementāriī — "a mason, a builder in stone".
Caementāriī mūrum novum aedificant. The masons are building a new wall.
caementārius, caementāriī m., built on caementum, rough-cut stone set in mortar. English cement comes from caementum. A late word, in Jerome.

The word, taken apart.
caementārius is built on caementum "rough-cut stone" (Lewis & Short), which comes from caedō "to cut". English cement comes from caementum through French.
