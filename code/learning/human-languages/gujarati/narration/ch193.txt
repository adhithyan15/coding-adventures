Gujarati, chapter 193: Which way to go.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
દિશા (diśā) — direction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for bill, then the Gujarati for to waste.

You'll want to know: દિશા.
દિશા — diśā — "direction".
સ્ટેશન આ દિશામાં છે. — sṭeśan ā diśāmā̃ che — "The station is in this direction."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
દિશા (diśā) is Sanskrit diśā, "direction, region", a form of díś, taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 6339).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ડાબું (ḍābũ) — left, left-hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to waste, then the Gujarati for direction.

You'll want to know: ડાબું.
ડાબું — ḍābũ — "left, left-hand".
મંદિર ડાબે છે. — mandir ḍābe che — "The temple is on the left."
ડાબે, ḍābe, 'on the left'.

The word, taken apart.
ડાબું (ḍābũ) is listed by CDIAL, glossed "left", under ḍavva, "left-hand, crosswise", and its variant ḍābba, reconstructed forms. CDIAL calls the group non-Aryan and cites A. Master, who traced it to Dravidian (CDIAL 5539); DEDR sets it beside Dravidian words for left, such as Telugu ḍā (DEDR 449).

Lesson 3 of 5.
જમણું (jamṇũ) — right-hand, right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for direction, then the Gujarati for left.

You'll want to know: જમણું.
જમણું — jamṇũ — "right-hand, right".
બેંક જમણે છે. — bẽk jamṇe che — "The bank is on the right."

The word, taken apart.
જમણું (jamṇũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "right (not left)", under Sanskrit jamana, "eating": the right hand is the hand one eats with (CDIAL 5127).

Lesson 5 of 5.
વળવું (vaḷvũ) — to turn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for right-hand, then the Gujarati for straight.

You'll want to know: વળવું.
વળવું — vaḷvũ — "to turn".
બસ મંદિર પાસે ડાબે વળે છે. — bas mandir pāse ḍābe vaḷe che — "The bus turns left by the temple."

The word, taken apart.
વળવું (vaḷvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit válati, "turns, turns to", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to turn, turn round, bend" (CDIAL 11405).
