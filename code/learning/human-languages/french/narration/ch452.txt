French, chapter 452: Les bonnes affaires.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la remise — the discount; the shed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the overdraft, then the French for the statement.

You'll want to know: la remise.
la remise — "the discount; the shed".
Le vendeur m'a fait une remise de dix pour cent. The salesman gave me ten per cent off.
Une remise is also a garden shed.

The word, taken apart.
remise is the feminine past participle of remettre (EtymDB), from Latin remittere 'to send back', built on mittere 'to send'.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: le rabais.
le rabais — "the reduction, the price cut".
Ces chaussures sont vendues au rabais. These shoes are being sold at a reduced price.
Au rabais — cut-price, sometimes cheap and poor.

The word, taken apart.
rabais is built on rabaisser 'to bring down', from baisser 'to lower', which goes back to Vulgar Latin bassiāre, from Late Latin bassus 'low'. EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
brader — to sell off cheap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reduction, then the French for to haggle.

You'll want to know: brader.
brader — "to sell off cheap".
En janvier, les boutiques bradent leurs vêtements d'hiver. In January the shops sell off their winter clothes cheap.
Also figurative: brader son talent — to throw it away.

The word, taken apart.
brader is usually traced to Dutch braden 'to roast', from the meat roasted at old street markets; EtymDB lists it without a source, and the details are not settled.

Chaque septembre, la ville organise une grande braderie. Every September the town holds a big street sale.

braderie is built on brader 'to sell off cheap' (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Dutch braden 'to roast'.
