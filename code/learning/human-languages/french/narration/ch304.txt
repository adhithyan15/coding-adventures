French, chapter 304: Au bord de l'eau.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la noyade — drowning, a drowning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to collapse, then the French for the rubble.

You'll want to know: la noyade.
la noyade — "drowning, a drowning".
Il y a beaucoup de noyades en été. There are a lot of drownings in summer.

The word, taken apart.
noyade is built on noyer "to drown" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin necāre "to kill".

Lesson 2 of 5.
noyer — to drown (as se noyer); to flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the rubble, then the French for drowning.

You'll want to know: noyer.
noyer — "to drown (as se noyer); to flood".
Il s'est presque noyé dans le lac. He nearly drowned in the lake.
Reflexive for a person who drowns: il s'est noyé. Without se, to drown someone or to flood: noyer le moteur. The noun le noyer is a walnut tree.

The word, taken apart.
noyer is inherited from Latin necāre "to kill" (EtymDB), which in Late Latin narrowed to killing by water (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bouée — the buoy; the rubber ring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to drown, then the French for the rescuer.

You'll want to know: la bouée.
la bouée — "the buoy; the rubber ring".
On a lancé une bouée à l'homme dans l'eau. Someone threw a lifebuoy to the man in the water.
La bouée de sauvetage — the lifebuoy; for children, une bouée is a swim ring.

The word, taken apart.
bouée is usually traced to Middle Dutch boeie "buoy"; the origin is not fully settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Le maître-nageur surveille la piscine. The lifeguard watches over the pool.

The word, taken apart.
maître is inherited from Latin magister "master", and nageur is built on nager "to swim", which comes from Latin nāvigāre "to sail" (Lewis & Short).
