# मीरा जी — putting a name in front of somebody

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C07-HAAN-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the small word you learned that turns a bare **हाँ** into a
polite one.

## You'll want to know: मीरा जी
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-CONCEPT-C97-ADDRESS-01]; assesses=[] -->

**मीरा जी** (*Mīrā jī*) — how you address somebody called Mira.

A name, then **जी**. That is the whole of it.

## Grammar Lens: the word you already had, in a new position
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C97-ADDRESS-01, HI-CONCEPT-C07-HAAN-02] -->

| what you say | what it does |
|---|---|
| जी हाँ | makes a yes polite |
| मीरा जी | makes a name an address |

**जी** has been in your mouth since the early chapters, doing politeness work
after a yes. Behind a name it does the same work in a different place: it turns
somebody being *talked about* into somebody being *talked to*.

Hindi has no separate ending for this the way some languages do. **The position
is the grammar** — the name comes first, **जी** follows it, and nothing else in
the sentence changes.

It works for anybody you would use **आप** with, which is most people you have
not known since childhood. With a close friend or a child, the bare name on its
own is the ordinary thing.
