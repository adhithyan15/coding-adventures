Lesson 1 of 5.
piscātor, piscātōris — a fisherman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a barber, then the Latin for a shepherd.

You'll want to know: piscātor, piscātōris.
piscātor, piscātōris — "a fisherman".

The word, taken apart.
piscātor is built on piscor, "to fish", from piscis, "a fish" (EtymDB 2.1), with -tor, "one who does it". piscis is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pisk-, "fish" (EtymDB 2.1); English fish is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
servus, servī — a slave, a servant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a shepherd, then the Latin for a fisherman.

You'll want to know: servus, servī.
servus, servī — "a slave, a servant".

The word, taken apart.
servus has no sure origin. Romans tied it to servō, "to keep, to save" (Lewis & Short), and an Etruscan source has also been suggested; neither is proven.
