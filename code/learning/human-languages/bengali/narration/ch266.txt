Bengali, chapter 266: What Was Going On.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
আমি পড়ছিলাম (āmi pôṛchhilām) — going on now, going on then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was at home" and "I will go". (chhilām, jābô.) Both are whole facts. This lesson is about things in the middle of happening.

You'll want to know: going on now.
আমি এখন একটা বই পড়ছি। āmi ekhôn ekṭā bôi pôṛchhi. — "I'm reading a book now."
What is going on at this moment takes -ছ- after the stem: আমি পড়ছি (āmi pôṛchhi), তুমি পড়ছ (tumi pôṛchhô), সে পড়ছে (she pôṛchhe), আপনি পড়ছেন (āpni pôṛchhen). After a stem that ends in a vowel it is -চ্ছ-: যাচ্ছি (jāchchhi), "I'm going"; সে খাচ্ছে (she khāchchhe); কী হচ্ছে? (kī hôchchhe?), "what's happening?". An এ said e rises, as it does in the past: শেখা becomes শিখছি (shikhchhi).

You'll want to know: going on then.
Put -ছিল- where -ছ- was, and the same scene is in the past:
কাল সন্ধ্যায় আমি বই পড়ছিলাম। kāl sôndhyāy āmi bôi pôṛchhilām. — "Yesterday evening I was reading."
আমি পড়ছিলাম (āmi pôṛchhilām) — I was reading.
তুমি পড়ছিলে (tumi pôṛchhile) — you were reading.
সে পড়ছিল (she pôṛchhilô) — he or she was reading.
আপনি / তিনি পড়ছিলেন (pôṛchhilen) — with respect.
and যাচ্ছিলাম (jāchchhilām), "I was going". The plain past, পড়লাম, is an event; পড়ছিলাম is the scene around one.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "She is eating" — she khāchchhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We were going to the market" — āmrā bājāre jāchchhilām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What is happening?" — kī hôchchhe?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you are reading a book now, then that yesterday evening you were reading.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I'm going"? (যাচ্ছি.) "He was eating"? (খাচ্ছিল.) Which is the scene and which the event: পড়লাম or পড়ছিলাম? (পড়ছিলাম is the scene.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
যখন … তখন … (jôkhon … tôkhon …) — the scene and the event.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Warm-up.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was reading". (āmi pôṛchhilām.) You know যখন, "when", and its partner তখন, "then": যখন আমি আসি, তখন চা খাই. Read it aloud.

You'll want to know: the scene and the event.
যখন আমি বই পড়ছিলাম, তখন মীরা এল। jôkhon āmi bôi pôṛchhilām, tôkhon mīrā elô. — "I was reading when Mira came."
The যখন half is the scene, in the going-on past. The তখন half is the event that broke into it, in the plain past. English puts "when" on the event; Bengali puts যখন on the scene, and তখন opens the event.

You'll want to know: তখন on its own.
তখন also stands alone in a story: "then, at that time".
তখন রাত ছিল। বাইরে ঝড় হচ্ছিল। tôkhon rāt chhilô. bāire jhôṛ hôchchhilô. — "It was night then. A storm was going on outside."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I was reading when Mira came" — jôkhon āmi bôi pôṛchhilām, tôkhon mīrā elô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It was night then" — tôkhon rāt chhilô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We were going to the market when the storm came" — jôkhon āmrā bājāre jāchchhilām, tôkhon jhôṛ elô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you were eating when Mira phoned.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which half takes -ছিল-? (The যখন half, the scene.) What does তখন mean on its own? ("Then, at that time".)


Lesson 3 of 6.
ঠিক (ṭhik) — correct; exactly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "It was night then". (tôkhon rāt chhilô.)

You'll want to know: ঠিক.
ঠিক — ṭhik — "correct; exactly".
তুমি ঠিক (tumi ṭhik) is "you're right", and ঠিক আছে (ṭhik āchhe), "it's all right", is the everyday "fine, agreed". Before a time or a place, ঠিক is "exactly":
ঠিক তখন ট্রেন এল। ṭhik tôkhon ṭren elô. — "Right then the train came."

The word, taken apart.
ঠিক (ṭhik) goes back to a form ṭhīkka, "firm, right" (CDIAL 5503), which no text records (hence the star), and which CDIAL derives, probably, from Sanskrit sthita, "standing". CDIAL lists the Bengali ঠিক, "correct", under it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ṭhik]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You're right" — tumi ṭhik]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Right then the train came" — ṭhik tôkhon ṭren elô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ঠিক (ṭhik) mean? ("Correct", and before a time, "exactly".) "Fine, agreed"? (ঠিক (ṭhik) আছে.)


Lesson 4 of 6.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. [once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say ঠিক (ṭhik), "correct". Then read ট aloud (ṭô), the letter of টাকা.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ঠ.
ঠ — ṭhô: the ট sound with a breath after it, as খ is ক with a breath. It opens ঠিক (ṭhik).
In the print of this book, ঠ (ṭhô) has a headline crossed by one curved line. Above the headline, the line rises in a curve that bows out to the left, its tip at the top. Below the headline it makes a large closed loop, shaped like a drop hanging from the headline, that fills the lower part of the letter; at its left side the loop has a sharp corner. ঠ (ṭhô) has no stem.
Set it beside ট: ট has a raised hook above its headline too, but below it a stem, and a bowl that ends in a solid knob. ঠ (ṭhô) has no knob.

Writing: ঠ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ঠ (ṭhô) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the curve above the headline, the line crossing it, the closed loop and its corner.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ঠ (ṭhô) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ওঠা (oṭhā) — to rise; to get up.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Warm-up.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ঠ (ṭhô), the letter of the last lesson, and say ঠিক (ṭhik). The next word carries ঠ (ṭhô) too.

You'll want to know: ওঠা.
ওঠা — oṭhā — "to rise; to get up".
সে রোজ সকালে তাড়াতাড়ি ওঠে। she roj sôkāle tāṛātāṛi oṭhe. — "She gets up early every morning."
ওঠে is the present for he or she, and the moon and the sun "rise" with it too: রাতে চাঁদ ওঠে (rāte chā̃d oṭhe), "the moon rises at night". In most other forms the o rises to u, as in খোলা becomes খুললাম: the past uṭhlô, "got up", and the -e form uṭhe. At the start of a word that u needs a letter of its own, which comes three chapters on; until then, say those forms, and write ওঠে.

The word, taken apart.
ওঠা (oṭhā) goes back to a reconstructed ut-sthāti, "stands up" (CDIAL 1900): ut-, "up", on the root sthā, "to stand". CDIAL lists the Bengali verb under it, as oṭhā and uṭhā, "to get up, stand".

Wrap-up Recall.
