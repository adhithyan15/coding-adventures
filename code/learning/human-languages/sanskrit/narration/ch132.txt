Lesson 1 of 5.
कृशः (kṛśaḥ) — thin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for clear, then the Sanskrit for thick.

You'll want to know: कृशः.
कृशः — kṛśaḥ — "thin".

The word, taken apart.
kṛśa "lean, thin, emaciated" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB). Pali wore it down to kisa "lean" (CDIAL 3441).

vakra "crooked, curved, bent" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and CDIAL 11185 sets it under the root √vañc "go crookedly", noting that similar words may be non-Aryan. Pali kept it as vakka "crooked" (CDIAL 11185).

The word, taken apart.
ṛju "straight, upright, honest" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-European: Latin rēctus and English right are its cousins (EtymDB). Marathi kept it as ujū "straight, honest" (CDIAL 2448).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पूर्णः (pūrṇaḥ) — full.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for crooked, then the Sanskrit for straight.

You'll want to know: पूर्णः.
पूर्णः — pūrṇaḥ — "full".

The word, taken apart.
pūrṇa is the past participle of the root √pṝ "fill" (CDIAL 8339): "filled, full". It is inherited from Indo-European, and Latin plēnus (English plenty) and Persian pur "full" are cousins (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
रिक्तः (riktaḥ) — empty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for straight, then the Sanskrit for full.

You'll want to know: रिक्तः.
रिक्तः — riktaḥ — "empty".

The word, taken apart.
rikta is the past participle of the root √ric "leave, empty" (CDIAL 10729): "left, emptied". The root is inherited from Indo-European, kin to Latin linquere "leave" (EtymDB), and rikta lives on as Hindi rītā "empty" (CDIAL 10729).
