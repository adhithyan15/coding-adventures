German, chapter 470: Geduld und Gelassenheit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Geduld — patience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to breathe a sigh of relief, then the German for to sigh.

You'll want to know: die Geduld.
die Geduld — "patience".
Mit kleinen Kindern braucht man viel Geduld. With small children you need a lot of patience.
No plural. Die Geduld verlieren — to lose patience.

The word, taken apart.
Geduld is inherited from Middle High German gedult and Old High German gidult (EtymDB), usually traced to Proto-Germanic gaþuldiz; dulden "to endure" is related.

Lesson 2 of 5.
gelassen — calm, composed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to sigh, then the German for patience.

You'll want to know: gelassen.
gelassen — "calm, composed".
Trotz des Stresses blieb mein Chef ganz gelassen. Despite the stress, my boss stayed quite calm.
Also the past participle of lassen: Ich habe den Schirm im Bus gelassen.

The word, taken apart.
gelassen is the old past participle of lassen "to let, to let go", which is inherited from Old High German lāzan, from Proto-Germanic lētaną (EtymDB): someone who has let things go.

Lesson 3 of 5.
beruhigen — to calm (down).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for patience, then the German for calm.

You'll want to know: beruhigen.
beruhigen — "to calm (down)".
Die Mutter beruhigt das weinende Baby. The mother calms the crying baby.
Sich beruhigen — to calm down: Er hat sich schnell beruhigt.

The word, taken apart.
beruhigen is built on be- and ruhig "calm", from Ruhe "rest", which is inherited from Old High German ruowa, from Proto-Germanic rōwō (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Aufregung — excitement; agitation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for calm, then the German for to calm.

You'll want to know: die Aufregung.
die Aufregung — "excitement; agitation".
Vor lauter Aufregung konnte sie nicht schlafen. She was so excited she could not sleep.
Built on sich aufregen. Plural, rare: die Aufregungen.

The word, taken apart.
Aufregung is built on aufregen with -ung; regen "to stir" goes back to Middle High German regen "to excite, to move" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
zappeln — to fidget, to wriggle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to calm, then the German for excitement.

You'll want to know: zappeln.
zappeln — "to fidget, to wriggle".
Der kleine Junge zappelt auf seinem Stuhl. The little boy is fidgeting on his chair.
Jemanden zappeln lassen — to keep someone on tenterhooks.

The word, taken apart.
zappeln is usually traced to Middle High German zabelen "to wriggle"; EtymDB lists the German word without a source, and its older origin is not settled.
