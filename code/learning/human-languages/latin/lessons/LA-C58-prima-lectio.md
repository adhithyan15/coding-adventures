# (reading) — the first connected scene

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-SKILL-READ-SENTENCES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, then six lines. Now the lines become one meeting.

[PAUSE 1s] Forty-two words, and every one has already appeared in an earlier lesson.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C50-POINT-01, LA-LEX-C50-POINT-03, LA-LEX-C52-ASK-04, LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04, LA-ETYMON-FRATER-SOROR-02, LA-ETYMON-PATER-MATER-02, LA-ETYMON-AQUA-VINUM-01, LA-ETYMON-PANIS-01] -->

> Salvē! Mihi nōmen est Mārcus. Quid tibi nōmen est?
> Mihi nōmen est Iūlia. Quid agis?
> Valeō, grātiās tibi agō. Et tū?
> Valeō. Haec est soror mea. Illa est māter mea.
> Haec aqua est. Ille pānis est.
> Ante merīdiem est. Post merīdiem, valē!

[PAUSE 3s] Read the whole scene once without stopping. Let the endings tell you
who acts, and let the cases keep each name and relationship in place.

[PAUSE 3s] Read it again. This time follow the three pointers: **haec**,
**illa**, **ille**.

## Grammar Lens: Latin holds together without articles
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING, LA-LEX-C57-ORDER-01, LA-LEX-C57-ORDER-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] English would keep supplying “the” and written pronouns. Latin does
not. **Haec aqua est** needs no article; **valeō** needs no **ego**. Gender,
case and verb ending carry the links instead.

[PAUSE 2s] That is why the scene can look spare while saying everything it
needs. Reading Latin is not filling in missing English words. It is learning to
trust the information Latin chose to write.

[PAUSE 2s] The final pair does the same work with time. **Ante** and **post**
put the scene on opposite sides of **merīdiēs** without a longer explanation.
