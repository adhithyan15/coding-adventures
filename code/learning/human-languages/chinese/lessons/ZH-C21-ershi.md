# 二十 — twenty, and ninety-nine numbers from ten words

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-SHIYI, ZH-GRAMMAR-DECIMAL-TEENS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **shíyī**, and build seventeen.

## You'll want to know first — turn the pair round
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-ERSHI, ZH-GRAMMAR-DECIMAL-TENS]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-NUM-ER, ZH-LEX-NUM-SHI, ZH-GRAMMAR-DECIMAL-TEENS] -->

> **二十** — *èrshí* — **twenty**

**十一** is ten-one, eleven. **二十** is two-ten, twenty. The same two words in
the other order, and the order is the entire difference.

That gives every ten: **三十** thirty, **四十** forty, **九十** ninety. Put a
unit on the end of one and you have everything between: **二十一**, *èrshíyī*,
twenty-one; **九十九**, ninety-nine.

**Ten words reach ninety-nine.** English needs *eleven*, *twelve* and *thirteen*
before its own pattern begins, and *twenty*, *thirty* and *fifty* are three more
shapes again. Mandarin needs none of them.

## Sounds you'll need — two dips in a row
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-TONE-SANDHI-THIRD, ZH-LEX-NUM-JIU] -->

**九十九**, ninety-nine, is the drill this book has been waiting for. *jiǔ* is
third tone and so is the *jiǔ* at the end, and the rule you met in chapter 1
applies: a third tone before another third tone lifts and becomes a rise.

Say it: **jiú-shí-jiǔ**. The first nine rises, the last one dips.
