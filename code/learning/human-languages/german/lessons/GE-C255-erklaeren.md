# erklären, warum — explaining why

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C146-VERB146-01, GE-LEX-WARUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to explain". (*Erklären.*) Then ask "why is the train
late?" (*Warum hat der Zug Verspätung?*)

## Grammar Lens: a question inside a sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ERKLAEREN-WARUM-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C192-THING192-04, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-LETZT-01] -->

Put that question inside a request and its verb moves to the end:

> *Warum* **hat** *der Zug Verspätung?* — Why is the train late?
> *Können Sie mir erklären, warum der Zug Verspätung* **hat**? — Can you
> explain to me why the train is late?

Inside a sentence, **warum** opens a clause the way *weil* and *dass* do.
*Wie*, *wo* and *wann* behave the same way: *Ich erkläre dir, wie das*
**geht**. *Weißt du, wann der Zug* **kommt**?

*Erklären* takes the person in the dative: *mir*, *dir*, *Ihnen*, "to me,
to you".

## Why it's said this way: an explanation in three steps
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ERKLAERUNG-DREI-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WEIL-01, GE-LEX-DARUM-01, GE-GRAMMAR-WEIL-DARUM-02, GE-LEX-C59-GESUNDHEIT-01, GE-LEX-C144-VERB144-02] -->

A clear explanation in German moves the way it does in English: what,
why, and what follows.

> *Ich komme heute nicht.* — what.
> *Ich komme nicht, weil ich Fieber* **habe**. — why, with *weil*.
> *Darum* **rufe** *ich dich an.* — what follows, with *darum*.

Each step has its own word order: plain, verb last, verb second. Said in a
row, the three hold together as one explanation.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C146-VERB146-01] -->

**erklären** is *er-* + *klar*, "clear": to make clear. EtymDB 2.1 traces
*klar* through Middle High German *klār* to Latin *clārus*, "bright,
clear". The noun **die Erklärung**, the explanation, is built on the same
verb.

end.**) The three steps of an explanation? (**What, why with** ***weil***,

(***Klar***, clear.)
