Kannada, chapter 350: An Evening of Honours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸು (sanmānisu) — to honour; to felicitate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a charioteer, then the Kannada for to fall in battle.

You'll want to know: ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸು.
ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸು — sanmānisu — "to honour; to felicitate".
ಊರಿನವರು ಹಳೆಯ ಶಿಕ್ಷಕರನ್ನು ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸಿದರು. ūrinavaru haḷeya śikṣakarannu sanmānisidaru. The townspeople honoured their old teacher.
Past (I …): ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸಿದೆ, sanmāniside.
The person honoured takes -ಅನ್ನು, often with a shawl and a garland. Towns also honour the workers who keep their streets ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ, clean.

The word, taken apart.
ಸನ್ಮಾನಿಸು (sanmānisu) is built on Sanskrit sanmāna, "respect or esteem" (Monier-Williams), with the Kannada verb ending -isu.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಕೊಂಡಾಡು (koṇḍāḍu) — to extol; to praise highly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to fall in battle, then the Kannada for to honour.

You'll want to know: ಕೊಂಡಾಡು.
ಕೊಂಡಾಡು — koṇḍāḍu — "to extol; to praise highly".
ಜನರು ಆ ಶಿಕ್ಷಕರ ಸೇವೆಯನ್ನು ಕೊಂಡಾಡಿದರು. janaru ā śikṣakara sēveyannu koṇḍāḍidaru. People praised that teacher's service.
Past (I …): ಕೊಂಡಾಡಿದೆ, koṇḍāḍide.
Stronger and more public than plain praise: to sing someone's praises.

The word, taken apart.
ಕೊಂಡಾಡು (koṇḍāḍu) is usually explained as a native compound of ಕೊಂಡು, from koḷ, "to take, to accept" (DEDR 2151), and āḍu, "to act, to speak" (DEDR 347).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಬಿರುದು (birudu) — an honorific title.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to honour, then the Kannada for to extol.

You'll want to know: ಬಿರುದು.
ಬಿರುದು — birudu — "an honorific title".
ರಾಜ ಆ ಕವಿಗೆ ಒಂದು ಬಿರುದು ಕೊಟ್ಟನು. rāja ā kavige ondu birudu koṭṭanu. The king gave that poet an honorific title.
Plural: ಬಿರುದುಗಳು, birudugaḷu.
A title of honour given by a king or a society, used with the name.

The word, taken apart.
ಬಿರುದು (birudu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5414 lists Kannada biridu, biruda, birudu, "praise, a badge of honour", beside Tamil virutu, "a title, a banner, a badge of victory"; Sanskrit has a similar biruda.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to extol, then the Kannada for an honorific title.

You'll want to know: ಕೀರ್ತಿ.
ಕೀರ್ತಿ — kīrti — "fame; glory".
ಆ ಆಟಗಾರನ ಕೀರ್ತಿ ದೇಶದ ತುಂಬಾ ಹರಡಿತು. ā āṭagārana kīrti dēśada tumbā haraḍitu. That player's fame spread across the country.
ಕೀರ್ತಿ (kīrti) ತರು, to bring glory: ಅವಳು ಊರಿಗೆ ಕೀರ್ತಿ (kīrti) ತಂದಳು.

The word, taken apart.
ಕೀರ್ತಿ (kīrti) is borrowed from Sanskrit kīrti, "report", and later "fame" (CDIAL 3200).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸು (udghāṭisu) — to inaugurate; to open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for an honorific title, then the Kannada for fame.

You'll want to know: ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸು.
ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸು — udghāṭisu — "to inaugurate; to open".
ಹಿರಿಯ ಲೇಖಕರು ಹೊಸ ಗ್ರಂಥಾಲಯವನ್ನು ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸಿದರು. hiriya lēkhakaru hosa granthālayavannu udghāṭisidaru. A senior writer opened the new library.
Past (I …): ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸಿದೆ, udghāṭiside.
Buildings, events and meetings are opened: ಸಮಾರಂಭವನ್ನು ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸು (udghāṭisu). The noun is ಉದ್ಘಾಟನೆ.

The word, taken apart.
ಉದ್ಘಾಟಿಸು (udghāṭisu) is built on Sanskrit udghāṭana, "the act of opening, unlocking" (Monier-Williams), with the Kannada verb ending -isu.
