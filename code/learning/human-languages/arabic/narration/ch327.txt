Arabic, chapter 327: That night.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
في تلك الليلة (fī tilka al-layla) — that night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an arrow, then the Arabic for armour.

You'll want to know: في تلك الليلة.
في تلك الليلة — fī tilka al-layla — "that night".
في تلك الليلة لم ينم أحد في البيت. fī tilka al-layla lam yanam aḥad fī al-bayt. That night nobody in the house slept.

The word, taken apart.
في تلك الليلة (fī tilka al-layla) is "in that night": layla, one night, is built on the inherited Semitic root l-y-l (Lane), the root of the taught layl; EtymDB sets it beside Hebrew laylā and Aramaic lēlyā.

Lesson 2 of 5.
في الوقت نفسه (fī al-waqt nafsih) — at the same time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for armour, then the Arabic for that night.

You'll want to know: في الوقت نفسه.
في الوقت نفسه — fī al-waqt nafsih — "at the same time".
تطبخ أمي وتتكلم في الهاتف في الوقت نفسه. taṭbukhu ummī wa-tatakallamu fī al-hātif fī al-waqt nafsih. My mother cooks and talks on the phone at the same time.

The word, taken apart.
في الوقت نفسه (fī al-waqt nafsih) is "in the time itself": waqt is built on the native Arabic root w-q-t (Lane), and nafs, "self, soul", on n-f-s.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for that night, then the Arabic for at the same time.

You'll want to know: حالما.
حالما — ḥālamā — "as soon as".
حالما وصلت اتصلت بأمي. ḥālamā waṣaltu ittaṣaltu bi-ummī. As soon as I arrived I called my mother.
A past verb follows it in a story.

The word, taken apart.
حالما (ḥālamā) is built on ḥāl, "a state, the present moment", from the native Arabic root ḥ-w-l (Lane), with mā: "in the moment that".

Lesson 4 of 5.
إلى أن (ilā an) — until.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for at the same time, then the Arabic for as soon as.

You'll want to know: إلى أن.
إلى أن — ilā an — "until".
انتظرنا إلى أن توقف المطر. intaẓarnā ilā an tawaqqafa al-maṭar. We waited until the rain stopped.
A verb follows: إلى أن (ilā an) وصل. Before a noun, say حتى.

The word, taken apart.
إلى أن (ilā an) joins the preposition ilā, "to, as far as", and an, "that": "up to the time that". Lane treats both as old particles; their origin beyond Arabic is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for as soon as, then the Arabic for until.

You'll want to know: طوال.
طوال — ṭuwāla — "throughout, all through".
بقي المريض في السرير طوال الأسبوع. baqiya al-marīḍ fī as-sarīr ṭuwāla al-usbūʿ. The patient stayed in bed all week.

The word, taken apart.
طوال (ṭuwāla) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-w-l (Lane), "to be long", the root of the taught ṭawīl. Ṭuwāla is "for the length of".
