Spanish, chapter 640: Cómo nos movemos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
veranear — to spend the summer (on holiday).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to seem, then the Spanish for to keep.

You'll want to know: veranear.
veranear — "to spend the summer (on holiday)".
Veraneamos en la costa todos los años. We spend every summer on the coast.
Built on el verano. El veraneo is the summer holiday.

The word, taken apart.
veranear is built on verano "summer", which is inherited from Vulgar Latin vērānum (tempus), built on Latin vēr "spring" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
el automóvil — the car, the automobile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to keep, then the Spanish for to spend the summer.

You'll want to know: el automóvil.
el automóvil — "the car, the automobile".
Mi abuelo compró su primer automóvil en 1970. My grandfather bought his first car in 1970.
A formal or old-fashioned word; in speech, el coche in Spain, el carro or el auto in Latin America.

The word, taken apart.
automóvil came from French automobile, a word built from Greek autós "self" (EtymDB) and Latin mōbilis "movable": a carriage that moves by itself.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la circulación — the traffic (flow); circulation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to spend the summer, then the Spanish for the car.

You'll want to know: la circulación.
la circulación — "the traffic (flow); circulation".
A esta hora hay mucha circulación en el centro. At this time there is a lot of traffic in the centre.
On the radio: la circulación es lenta — traffic is slow. It is also the circulation of the blood.

The word, taken apart.
circulación is built on circular "to move round", a learned borrowing from Late Latin circulāre (EtymDB), from circulus "a small ring", a little circus.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the car, then the Spanish for the traffic.

You'll want to know: la furgoneta.
la furgoneta — "the van".
Llevamos las bicis en la furgoneta. We are taking the bikes in the van.
A small van; much of Latin America says la camioneta.

The word, taken apart.
furgoneta is a little furgón "goods wagon", which came from French fourgon (EtymDB). Where the French word comes from is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el conductor — the driver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the traffic, then the Spanish for the van.

You'll want to know: el conductor.
el conductor — "the driver".
El conductor del autobús es muy amable. The bus driver is very kind.
A woman is la conductora. In Spain the verb is conducir.

The word, taken apart.
conductor is a learned borrowing from Latin conductor, which meant "one who hires, a contractor" (Lewis & Short); it is built on condūcere "to bring together", from dūcere "to lead" (EtymDB).
