Lesson 1 of 5.
голова (golová) — the head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for first, then the Russian for second.

You'll want to know: голова.
голова (golová) — "the head".

The word, taken apart.
голова (golová) from Proto-Slavic golva, "head" — inherited, and a cousin of Lithuanian galvà, "head" (Vasmer). East Slavic says -оло- where Church Slavonic says -ла-, so the same word came back from church books as глава, "chapter; the head of something".

Lesson 2 of 5.
рука (ruká) — a hand, an arm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for second, then the Russian for the head.

You'll want to know: рука.
рука (ruká) — "a hand, an arm". One word for both.

The word, taken apart.
рука (ruká) from Proto-Slavic rǫka, "hand" — inherited; the old nasal ǫ became Russian у. Vasmer pairs it with Lithuanian rankà, "hand", and links both with Lithuanian riñkti, "to gather": the hand as the gatherer.

Lesson 3 of 5.
нога (nogá) — a leg, a foot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the head, then the Russian for a hand.

You'll want to know: нога.
нога (nogá) — "a leg, a foot". One word for both, like рука (ruká).

The word, taken apart.
нога (nogá) from Proto-Slavic noga — inherited. Vasmer gives "claw" as the first sense and lists Lithuanian nagà, "hoof", Latin unguis, "nail", and Old High German nagal, "nail": English nail is a cousin, from PIE h₃nogʷʰ-, "nail, claw".

Lesson 4 of 5.
живот (zhivót) — the belly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a hand, then the Russian for a leg.

You'll want to know: живот.
живот (zhivót) — "the belly".

The word, taken apart.
живот (zhivót) from Proto-Slavic životŭ, "life" — inherited, from the root of жить, "to live". Old Russian životŭ meant "life, property"; "belly" came later (Vasmer). Lithuanian gyvatà, "life", and Latin vīta, "life", are cousins (Vasmer).

You'll want to know: зуб.
зуб (zub) — "a tooth".

The word, taken apart.
зуб (zub) from Proto-Slavic zǫbŭ, "tooth" — inherited. Vasmer sets it beside Sanskrit jámbha-, "tooth", Greek gómphos, "peg", and Old High German kamb, "comb", so English comb is a cousin, from PIE ǵómbʰos, "tooth, peg".
