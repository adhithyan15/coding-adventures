# (reading) — six lines, two of them one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-SKILL-READ-LABELS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words alone. These are whole lines.

[PAUSE 1s] You have said every one of them.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-SKILL-READ-PHRASES]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C52-ASK-04] -->

> Salvē!
> Valē!
> Nōmen meum est Marcus.
> Ego sum discipulus.
> Iterum!
> Bonus est.

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and notice that two of the six are a single word, and that
both of them are things you say to somebody.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C53-SCHOOL-01, LA-SKILL-READ-PHRASES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Salvē** and **Valē** are commands. *Be well.* *Be strong.* A Roman
greeting and a Roman farewell are both orders, which is why neither needs
anything around it.

[PAUSE 2s] **Bonus est** has no subject on the page at all. Latin puts the
person in the verb, so *est* is already *he is* or *it is*, and the sentence is
complete with two words.

[PAUSE 2s] That is the second thing reading Latin asks of you: expect the
subject to be missing, and expect the verb to have it.
