Spanish, chapter 529: La explosión.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la explosión — the explosion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the earthquake, then the Spanish for the flood.

You'll want to know: la explosión.
la explosión — "the explosion".
Oímos una explosión muy fuerte. We heard a very loud explosion.

The word, taken apart.
explosión is a learned borrowing from Latin explōsiō 'a driving off the stage by clapping' (Lewis & Short), built on plaudere 'to clap'. The noise of the crowd became the noise of a blast.

Lesson 2 of 5.
explotar — to explode; to exploit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the flood, then the Spanish for the explosion.

You'll want to know: explotar.
explotar — "to explode; to exploit".
La bombona de gas explotó. The gas canister exploded.
It also means to exploit: explotar a los trabajadores.

The word, taken apart.
explotar was borrowed from French exploiter 'to make use of' (EtymDB). The sense 'to explode' is usually explained as a later Spanish growth, pulled along by explosión.

Lesson 3 of 5.
estallar — to burst, to break out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the explosion, then the Spanish for to explode.

You'll want to know: estallar.
estallar — "to burst, to break out".
Estalló una tormenta por la tarde. A storm broke out in the afternoon.
Also for wars and scandals: Estalló la guerra.

The word, taken apart.
estallar is usually traced to an old verb astellar 'to break into splinters', built on astilla 'a splinter'; the details are not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
los escombros — the rubble, the debris.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to explode, then the Spanish for to burst.

You'll want to know: los escombros.
los escombros — "the rubble, the debris".
Sacaron a dos personas de entre los escombros. They pulled two people out of the rubble.
Almost always in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
escombros is built on escombrar 'to clear out', which EtymDB traces to spoken Latin excomborāre and a Celtic word comboros, usually glossed 'a barrier, a heap'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
destruir — to destroy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to burst, then the Spanish for the rubble.

You'll want to know: destruir.
destruir — "to destroy".
El fuego destruyó tres casas. The fire destroyed three houses.
It takes a y: destruyo, destruyó.

The word, taken apart.
destruir comes from Latin dēstruere 'to tear down, to destroy' (EtymDB), the reverse of struere 'to pile up, to build'.
