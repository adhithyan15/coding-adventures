Lesson 1 of 5.
dēverticulum, dēverticulī — a roadside inn, a lodging; a side road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of the shore, then the Latin for to sail out.

You'll want to know: dēverticulum, dēverticulī.
dēverticulum, dēverticulī — "a roadside inn, a lodging; a side road".
Nox venit, itaque viātōrēs in dēverticulum intrant. Night is coming, so the travellers go into a roadside inn.
Second declension, neuter: dēverticulum, dēverticulī n. A place you turn off the road for: first a side road, then the inn at its end.

The word, taken apart.
dēverticulum is built on dēvertō 'to turn aside' with the suffix -culum 'place for' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a place you turn aside to. dēvertō comes from vertō 'to turn'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dēversor, dēversārī — to lodge, to stay as a guest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sail out, then the Latin for a roadside inn.

You'll want to know: dēversor, dēversārī.
dēversor, dēversārī — "to lodge, to stay as a guest".
Mercātor apud amīcum in oppidō dēversātur. The merchant lodges with a friend in the town.
Deponent: dēversor, dēversārī, dēversātus sum, often with apud 'at the house of'. The inn is a dēversōrium.

The word, taken apart.
dēversor is a frequentative built on dēvertō 'to turn aside' (Lewis & Short): to keep turning in, to stay as a guest. dēversōrium 'inn' comes from the same verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.

pernoctō, pernoctāre — "to spend the night, to stay all night".
Viātōrēs in stabulō pernoctant, quod caupōna plēna est. The travellers spend the night in the stable, because the inn is full.
pernoctō, pernoctāre, pernoctāvī, pernoctātum. From per 'through' and nox 'night'.

pernoctō is built from per 'through' and nox, noctis 'night' (Lewis & Short): to stay through the night.

Lesson 5 of 5.
hospitālis, hospitāle — hospitable; of a guest or host.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to spend the night, then the Latin for a stable-keeper.

You'll want to know: hospitālis, hospitāle.
hospitālis, hospitāle — "hospitable; of a guest or host".
Caupō hospitālis est et viātōribus bonam cēnam dat. The innkeeper is hospitable and gives the travellers a good dinner.
An adjective of two endings, from hospes 'host, guest'. English hospital and hospitable come from the same family.

The word, taken apart.
hospitālis is built on hospes, hospitis 'host, guest', from PIE gʰóspot- 'master of guests' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English hospital comes from it through French.
