# The table words, second pass — and how to ask for them

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C39-TII-01, TE-LEX-C39-PAALU-01, TE-LEX-C40-BHOJANAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Three words, from memory and without the page: tea, milk, and the
word for a meal.

## You'll want to know: three words, three different journeys
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C39-TII-01, TE-LEX-C39-PAALU-01, TE-LEX-C40-BHOJANAM-01, TE-ETYMON-C40-BHOJANAM-02] -->

The three sit together on a table and came from three different places:

| word | where it came from |
|---|---|
| **టీ** *ṭī* | borrowed, and by sea rather than by the overland road |
| **పాలు** *pālu* | the family's own — the whole Dravidian group shares it |
| **భోజనం** *bhōjanaṁ* | Sanskrit, and formal in tone because of it |

**భోజనం** is the one to weigh. It is not the everyday word for food; it is the
polite noun for **a meal**, the sit-down kind, and its tone is the reason it
belongs beside *please* rather than beside a shopping list.

## Grammar Lens: two ways to put "please" on a request
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C08-DAYACHESI-01, TE-PRAGMATICS-C08-DAYACHESI-03] -->

Asking for any of the three uses what **దయచేసి** and the **‑ండి** ending settled:

- The **‑ండి** ending on the verb is where ordinary Telugu keeps *please*. A
  request with it is already polite.
- **దయచేసి** is the extra warmth on top — formal, or emphatic, and not the
  everyday word English speakers expect it to be.

So **దయచేసి టీ** is a warm, slightly formal ask, and the same request made with
a ‑ండి verb and no **దయచేసి** is the ordinary polite one. Neither is rude.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C39-TII-01, TE-LEX-C39-PAALU-01, TE-LEX-C40-BHOJANAM-01, TE-ETYMON-C40-BHOJANAM-02, TE-LEX-C08-DAYACHESI-01, TE-PRAGMATICS-C08-DAYACHESI-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which of the three words is the family's own? (**పాలు**.) Which
arrived from Sanskrit, and what does that do to its tone? (**భోజనం** — it makes
it **formal**.) Where does everyday Telugu keep *please*? (**In the ‑ండి ending
on the verb.**) And what is **దయచేసి** for? (**Extra warmth, or formality, on
top of an ending that is already polite.**)
