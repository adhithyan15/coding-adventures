Malayalam, chapter 138: Animals of Kerala.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പക്ഷി (pakṣi) — a bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for an aeroplane, then the Malayalam for an auto.

You'll want to know: പക്ഷി.
പക്ഷി (pakṣi) — "a bird". A കാക്ക is one.

The word, taken apart.
പക്ഷി (pakṣi) from Sanskrit pakṣin, "winged; bird", taken over whole as a tatsama (CDIAL 7636). It is built on pakṣa, "wing".

What you've built.
The general word for anything with wings.

പാമ്പ് (pāmpŭ) — "a snake". പാമ്പ്! is the one word to shout.

The word, taken apart.
പാമ്പ് (pāmpŭ) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil pāmpu, Kannada pāvu and Telugu pāmu (DEDR 4085).

Lesson 4 of 5.
കടുവ (kaṭuva) — a tiger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a snake, then the Malayalam for a monkey.

You'll want to know: കടുവ.
കടുവ (kaṭuva) — "a tiger", in the forests of the hills.

The word, taken apart.
കടുവ (kaṭuva) is of uncertain origin. DEDR 1140 lists Malayalam kaṭuvan, "the male of cats, pigs", with Tamil kaṭuvaṉ, "tom-cat", and the tiger's name may be the same word, but that is not certain.

What you've built.
The big cat.
