Japanese, chapter 391: Crossing Safely.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おうだんほどう (ōdanhodō) — a pedestrian crossing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a licence, then the Japanese for to overtake.

You'll want to know: おうだんほどう.
おうだんほどう — ōdanhodō — "a pedestrian crossing".
おうだんほどうを わたって ください。 — ōdanhodō o watatte kudasai — "Please cross at the pedestrian crossing."

The word, taken apart.
おうだんほどう (ōdanhodō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): four Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ō, "sideways" (Middle Chinese hwaeng), dan, "severance" (dwanX), ho, "walk" (buH) and dō, "street" (dawX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
たちどまる (tachidomaru) — to stop walking; to stand still.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a pedestrian, then the Japanese for one-way traffic.

You'll want to know: たちどまる.
たちどまる — tachidomaru — "to stop walking; to stand still".
しんごうの まえで たちどまりました。 — shingō no mae de tachidomarimashita — "I stopped in front of the traffic light."
Polite たちどまります, tachidomarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ほこうしゃが たちどまりました。, hokōsha ga tachidomarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
たちどまる (tachidomaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tachi, "standing" (from tatsu) and domaru, "stopping" (from tomaru); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
