German, chapter 393: Dateien und Ordner.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
die Datei — the (computer) file.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the charger, then the German for the plug.

You'll want to know: die Datei.
die Datei — "the (computer) file".
Die Datei ist zu groß für eine E-Mail. The file is too big for an email.
Plural: die Dateien. Eine Datei öffnen — to open a file.

The word, taken apart.
Datei is usually explained as a 20th-century German blend of Daten "data", from Latin datum "something given" (EtymDB), and Kartei "card index". EtymDB has no entry for Datei itself.

Lesson 2 of 6.
speichern — to save (a file); to store.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the plug, then the German for the.

You'll want to know: speichern.
speichern — "to save (a file); to store".
Ich habe vergessen, den Text zu speichern. I forgot to save the text.
Eine Datei speichern — to save a file. Der Speicher — the memory; also a storehouse or an attic.

The word, taken apart.
speichern is made from Speicher "storehouse" (EtymDB), from Middle High German spīcher, Old High German spīhhāri and Late Latin spīcārium "a granary", from spīca "an ear of grain".

Lesson 3 of 6.
der Ordner — the folder; the ring binder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the, then the German for to save.

You'll want to know: der Ordner.
der Ordner — "the folder; the ring binder".
Alle Fotos sind in einem Ordner auf dem Computer. All the photos are in a folder on the computer.
Plural: die Ordner. In an office, der Ordner is a ring binder.

The word, taken apart.
Ordner is formed with -er on ordnen "to put in order", inherited from Old High German ordinōn, an early borrowing from Latin ōrdināre (EtymDB), from ōrdo "row, order".

Lesson 4 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: der Anhang.
der Anhang — "the attachment; the appendix".
Meinen Lebenslauf finden Sie im Anhang. You will find my CV attached.
Plural: die Anhänge. In a book, the appendix.

The word, taken apart.
Anhang goes with anhängen "to attach", built on hängen "to hang", Old High German hengen, Proto-Germanic hangijaną (EtymDB): what hangs on at the end.

Lesson 5 of 6.
anhängen — to attach; to add on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the folder, then the German for the attachment.

You'll want to know: anhängen.
anhängen — "to attach; to add on".
Ich habe die Fotos an die E-Mail angehängt. I attached the photos to the email.
Separable: ich hänge an, ich habe angehängt.

The word, taken apart.
anhängen is built from an "on" and hängen "to hang", Old High German hengen, Proto-Germanic hangijaną "to cause to hang" (EtymDB).
