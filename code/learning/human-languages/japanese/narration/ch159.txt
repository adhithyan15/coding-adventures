Japanese, chapter 159: Housework and Sorting the Rubbish.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かじ (kaji) — housework; chores.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to open up, then the Japanese for dark.

You'll want to know: かじ.
かじ — kaji — "housework; chores".
こどもも かじを てつだいます。 — kodomo mo kaji o tetsudaimasu — "The children help with the housework too."

The word, taken apart.
かじ (kaji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ka, "house, home" (Middle Chinese kae) and ji, "matter, affair" (dzriH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
すてる (suteru) — to throw away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dark, then the Japanese for housework.

You'll want to know: すてる.
すてる — suteru — "to throw away".
まいしゅう ごみを すてます。 — maishū gomi o sutemasu — "I throw out the rubbish every week."
Polite すてます, sutemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
すてる (suteru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
