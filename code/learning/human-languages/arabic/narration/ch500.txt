Arabic, chapter 500: Vain, selfish, cowardly and bold.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مغرور (maghrūr) — conceited, vain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for loyal, then the Arabic for faithful.

You'll want to know: مغرور.
مغرور — maghrūr — "conceited, vain".
لا يحب الفريق اللاعب المغرور. lā yuḥibbu al-farīq al-lāʿib al-maghrūr. The team does not like the conceited player.

The word, taken apart.
مغرور (maghrūr) is built on the native Arabic root gh-r-r, "to deceive, to beguile" (Lane). Maghrūr is the passive participle: one beguiled, here by his own worth.

Lesson 3 of 5.
أناني (anānī) — selfish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for conceited, then the Arabic for arrogant.

You'll want to know: أناني.
أناني — anānī — "selfish".
الطفل الأناني لا يشارك أخاه في ألعابه. aṭ-ṭifl al-anānī lā yushāriku akhāhu fī alʿābihi. The selfish child does not share his toys with his brother.

The word, taken apart.
أناني (anānī) is a modern Arabic word, usually traced to the pronoun anā, "I", with the adjective ending -ī: a person always saying I. The noun anāniyya is selfishness.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: جبان.
جبان — jabān — "cowardly; a coward".
هرب الرجل الجبان من الكلب. haraba ar-rajul al-jabān min al-kalb. The cowardly man ran away from the dog.
The noun جبن, cowardice, is written like jubn, cheese.

The word, taken apart.
جبان (jabān) is built on the native Arabic root j-b-n, "to be cowardly" (Lane). Jabān is the adjective of a lasting trait.
