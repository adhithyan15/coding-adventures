# pluēbat … vēnit — the scene and the event

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-WEATHER-VERBS-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-IMPERFECTUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "it rains", with no subject. (*Pluit.*) Now put it in the
open past, the way *legere* gives *legēbat*. (*Pluēbat*, it was raining.)

## Grammar Lens: which past for which job
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-SCAENA-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C175-TEMP175-04, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01] -->

> **Pluēbat. Subitō amīcus vēnit.** — It was raining. Suddenly a friend came.

[PAUSE 2s] The rain was already going on, and it went on afterwards: that is
the **imperfect**, *pluēbat*. The friend's arrival happened once, at one
moment, and was done: that is the **perfect**, *vēnit*.

[PAUSE 2s] So a story runs on two tracks. The imperfect paints the
**scene**: weather, places, what people were doing. The perfect moves the
**events** along.

> **In forō ambulābam; subitō amīcum vīdī.** — I was walking in the forum;
> suddenly I saw a friend.

[PAUSE 1s] A word like *subitō*, "suddenly", nearly guarantees a perfect
after it: suddenness is an event.

## Why it's said this way: habitābam or habitāvī?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-SCAENA-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-AVI-02, LA-LEX-C160-ACT160-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Sometimes both are right. *Rōmae habitāvī* counts it up from
outside: I lived in Rome, a closed stretch. *Rōmae habitābam* puts you
inside it: I was living in Rome then.

[PAUSE 2s] One small trap: *pluit* in the perfect is spelled *pluit* too, "it
rained", the way *accidit* serves two tenses. Only the story around it
decides.

## The word, taken apart: perfect and imperfect
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-C281-PERFECTUM-02]; assesses=[] -->

The names are Latin. **perfectum** comes from *perficiō*, "to carry through,
to finish" (Lewis & Short), which EtymDB 2.1 builds from *per-*, "through",
and *faciō*, "to make, to do". The perfect is the past that was carried
through. **imperfectum** puts *in-*, "not", in front: unfinished, still
going, which is what *pluēbat* is.
