## You'll want to know: dǎrǎo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C209-RULE209-04]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎrǎo* — "to disturb".

> *qǐng bú yào dǎrǎo wǒ* — Please don't disturb me.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before rǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎrǎo* is built from 打 'to strike' and *rǎo* 'to trouble', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *nyewX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to throw into disorder' and 'to vex'.
