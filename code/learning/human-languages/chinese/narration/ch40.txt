6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
买 (mǎi) — to buy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for milk, then the Mandarin for tea.

You'll want to know: 买.
买 — mǎi — "to buy".
你们买水吗 nǐmen mǎi shuǐ ma — Are you buying water?
The new character, in pieces: 买 (mǎi) — a lid with two dots under it, and below them a shape like 大 with its last stroke cut down to a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
买 (mǎi) is inherited from Middle Chinese meaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to trade at market'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
买 (mǎi) — a lid, two dots, and a cut-down 大.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a lid, two dots, and a cut-down 大.
买 (mǎi).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, three strokes. A horizontal that hooks down at its right end, like a lid. Then two dots under it, one above the other.
The bottom piece, three strokes. A long horizontal, a left-falling stroke through it, and a dot on the right. It is 大 with its last stroke cut down to a dot.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 买 (mǎi), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 买 (mǎi)? (Two — the lid with its dots, then the cut-down 大.) What is under the lid? (Two dots.) What is the last stroke? (A dot, where 大 has a long fall.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 买 (mǎi), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 6.
píngguǒ — apple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tea, then the Mandarin for to buy.

You'll want to know: píngguǒ.
píngguǒ — "apple".
wǒ mǎi le sān ge píngguǒ — I bought three apples.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píngguǒ is built from píng and guǒ 'fruit'. Guǒ is inherited from Middle Chinese kwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'fruit of a tree'; the first character is listed there as bjaeng, a name for several plants, duckweed among them, and how it came to name the apple is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
shuǐguǒ — fruit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to buy, then the Mandarin for apple.

You'll want to know: shuǐguǒ.
shuǐguǒ — "fruit".
wǒ xǐhuan chī shuǐguǒ — I like fruit.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: shuǐguǒ is two third tones in a row, so shuǐ rises and only guǒ keeps the dip. The spelling does not show the change; your voice makes it.

The word, taken apart.
shuǐguǒ is built from 水 'water' and guǒ 'fruit', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sywijX and kwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 6 of 6.
jīdàn — egg.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fruit, then the Mandarin for bread.

You'll want to know: jīdàn.
jīdàn — "egg".
tā xǐhuan chī jīdàn — He likes eggs.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīdàn is built from jī 'chicken' and dàn 'egg'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese kej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary calls the animal that knows the hour; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.
