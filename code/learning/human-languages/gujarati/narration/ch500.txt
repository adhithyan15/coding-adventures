Gujarati, chapter 500: What They Left Us.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
વારસો (vārso) — an inheritance; a heritage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a prayer meeting after a death, then the Gujarati for to offer with respect.

You'll want to know: વારસો.
વારસો — vārso — "an inheritance; a heritage".
દાદાએ અમને પુસ્તકોનો વારસો આપ્યો. dādāe amne pustakono vārso āpyo. Grandfather left us a heritage of books.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વારસો (vārso) is usually traced to Arabic wāriṯ, "heir", which reached Gujarati through Persian wāris.

Lesson 5 of 7.
ત્યજવું (tyajvũ) — to abandon; to give up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an orphan, then the Gujarati for adopted.

You'll want to know: ત્યજવું.
ત્યજવું — tyajvũ — "to abandon; to give up".
તેણે ગુસ્સો ત્યજી દીધો. teṇe gusso tyajī dīdho. He gave up his anger.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ઘર ત્યજ્યું, teṇe ghar tyajyũ.

The word, taken apart.
ત્યજવું (tyajvũ) goes back to Sanskrit tyajati, "leaves, abandons", which Monier-Williams records under tyaj.
