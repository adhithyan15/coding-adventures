Mandarin Chinese, chapter 361: A Good Guest, a Good Host.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dǎ zhāohu — to say hello to someone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the occasion you are in, then the Mandarin for to jump the queue.

You'll want to know: dǎ zhāohu.
dǎ zhāohu — "to say hello to someone".
wǒmen jiànmiàn de shíhou yīnggāi dǎ zhāohu — We should say hello when we meet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎ zhāohu is built from 打 'to strike' and zhāohu, all three inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX, tsyew and xu (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike', the second 'to beckon' and the third 'to call out'. The last syllable is said light.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jūgōng — to bow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to say hello to someone, then the Mandarin for to look after your guests.

You'll want to know: jūgōng.
jūgōng — "to bow".
yǎnchū yǐhòu yǎnyuán yìqǐ jūgōng — After the show, the actors bow together.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jūgōng is built from jū and gōng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjuwk and kjuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to examine closely' and 'to rear', and the second 'the body'. How the pair came to mean bowing is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
看不起 (kànbuqǐ) — to look down on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to bow, then the Mandarin for to propose a toast.

You'll want to know: 看不起.
看不起 — kànbuqǐ — "to look down on".
你不应该看不起他 nǐ bù yīnggāi kànbuqǐ tā — You shouldn't look down on him.
The tone of bù is lost here: between the two parts it is said light, bu.

The word, taken apart.
看不起 (kànbuqǐ) is built from 看, 不 'not' and 起 'to rise', all three inherited from Middle Chinese, khan, pjut and khiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to view' and the third 'to rise'. How the three came to mean looking down on someone is not settled.
