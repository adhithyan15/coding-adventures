French, chapter 429: Petits soucis dans la chambre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'ampoule — the light bulb; the blister.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to drip, then the French for to unblock.

You'll want to know: l'ampoule.
l'ampoule — "the light bulb; the blister".
L'ampoule de la lampe ne marche plus. The bulb in the lamp has gone.
A feminine noun. After a long walk, une ampoule is a blister.

The word, taken apart.
ampoule is inherited from Latin ampulla 'small flask', built on amphora (EtymDB): a bulb looks like a little glass flask.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la prise — the socket (for electricity), the plug; the hold.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la prise.
la prise — "the socket (for electricity), the plug; the hold".
Il n'y a pas de prise près du lit. There is no socket near the bed.
Une prise de courant — a socket; une prise also a grip or hold.

The word, taken apart.
prise is the feminine past participle of prendre 'to take', used as a noun (EtymDB); prendre is inherited from Latin prehendere 'to grasp'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la moquette — the fitted carpet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the socket, then the French for the minibar.

You'll want to know: la moquette.
la moquette — "the fitted carpet".
La moquette de la chambre est un peu sale. The carpet in the room is a bit dirty.
Un tapis is a rug; la moquette covers the whole floor. In Belgium also le tapis plain.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of moquette is not settled; it was first a kind of thick cloth. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the minibar, then the French for the fitted carpet.

You'll want to know: la tache.
la tache — "the stain, the spot".
Il y a une tache de café sur le drap. There is a coffee stain on the sheet.
No accent: une tache is a stain; une tâche is a task.

The word, taken apart.
tache comes from Old French tache (EtymDB), which is usually traced to a Germanic word meaning 'sign, mark'.
