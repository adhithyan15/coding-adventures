Lesson 1 of 5.
मनोरञ्जनम् (manorañjanam) — entertainment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for ripe, then the Sanskrit for sucks.

You'll want to know: मनोरञ्जनम्.
मनोरञ्जनम् — manorañjanam — "entertainment".
रविवासरे वयं मनोरञ्जनाय उद्यानं गच्छामः। ravivāsare vayaṃ manorañjanāya udyānaṃ gacchāmaḥ. On Sunday we go to the park for some fun.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
मनोरञ्जनम् (manorañjanam) is built from manas, "the mind", and rañjana, "colouring, delighting", from the root √rañj, "to be coloured, to be pleased"; MW records manorañjana only as the title of a book, so "entertainment" is the modern sense (MW).

बालस्य हास्यं मह्यं रोचते। bālasya hāsyaṃ mahyaṃ rocate. I love the child's laughter.

The word, taken apart.
हास्यम् (hāsyam) comes from the root √has, "to laugh", which also gives hasati; MW gives hāsya as laughter, mirth, and jest, fun, amusement (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
विनोदयति (vinodayati) — amuses.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for entertainment, then the Sanskrit for fun.

You'll want to know: विनोदयति.
विनोदयति — vinodayati — "amuses".
पितामहः गीतेन बालान् विनोदयति। pitāmahaḥ gītena bālān vinodayati. Grandfather amuses the children with a song.
Past (he …): सः बालान् विनोदितवान्, saḥ bālān vinoditavān.

The word, taken apart.
विनोदयति (vinodayati) is built on vi, "away", and the causative of the root √nud, "to push", which also gives nudati: to drive dullness away; MW gives vinud as to drive away, and to divert (MW).

रुचिः (ruciḥ) comes from the root √ruc, "to shine, to be pleasing", which also gives rocate; MW gives ruci as light, and as liking, taste, relish (MW).
