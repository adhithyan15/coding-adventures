Lesson 1 of 6.
સામ્રાજ્ય (sāmrājya) — an empire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for enmity, then the Gujarati for to challenge.

You'll want to know: સામ્રાજ્ય.
સામ્રાજ્ય — sāmrājya — "an empire".
આ સામ્રાજ્ય દરિયાથી પર્વત સુધી હતું. ā sāmrājya dariyāthī parvat sudhī hatũ. This empire stretched from the sea to the mountains.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
સામ્રાજ્ય (sāmrājya) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sāmrājya, "complete sovereignty, an empire" (Monier-Williams), built on samrāj, a supreme ruler.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an empire, then the Gujarati for an emperor.

You'll want to know: વંશ.
વંશ — vanś — "a dynasty; a lineage".
આ વંશનો છેલ્લો રાજા ખૂબ નબળો હતો. ā vanśno chello rājā khūb nabḷo hato. The last king of this dynasty was very weak.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વંશ (vanś) is a tatsama, Sanskrit vaṁśa, "a bamboo cane", and then "a family line", one joint after another (CDIAL 11175). It is masculine: આ વંશનો રાજા.

Lesson 4 of 6.
કબજો (kabjo) — control; possession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an emperor, then the Gujarati for a dynasty.

You'll want to know: કબજો.
કબજો — kabjo — "control; possession".
દુશ્મને કિલ્લા પર કબજો કર્યો. duśmane killā par kabjo karyo. The enemy took control of the fort.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કબજો (kabjo) is usually traced to Persian qabza, "a grasp, a hold", a word Persian took from Arabic. A place is taken with પર: કિલ્લા પર કબજો કર્યો.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ઉથલાવવું (uthlāvvũ) — to overthrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a dynasty, then the Gujarati for control.

You'll want to know: ઉથલાવવું.
ઉથલાવવું — uthlāvvũ — "to overthrow".
લોકોએ નબળા રાજાને ઉથલાવ્યો. lokoe nabḷā rājāne uthlāvyo. The people overthrew the weak king.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે રાજાને ઉથલાવ્યો, teṇe rājāne uthlāvyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઉથલાવવું (uthlāvvũ) is the causative of uthalvũ, to be upset, which CDIAL lists under utthalati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 1805): to turn something over, and so to bring down a ruler.
