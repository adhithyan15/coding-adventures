Japanese, chapter 174: Pots, Knives and Plates.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
なべ (nabe) — pot; saucepan, hot pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be left over, then the Japanese for to savour.

You'll want to know: なべ.
なべ — nabe — "pot; saucepan, hot pot".
ふゆは なべを よく たべます。 — fuyu wa nabe o yoku tabemasu — "In winter we often eat hot pot."

The word, taken apart.
なべ (nabe) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi; its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ほうちょう (hōchō) — kitchen knife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to savour, then the Japanese for pot.

You'll want to know: ほうちょう.
ほうちょう — hōchō — "kitchen knife".
ちちは ほうちょうを よく つかいます。 — chichi wa hōchō o yoku tsukaimasu — "My father often uses a kitchen knife."

The word, taken apart.
ほうちょう (hōchō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. Today they are hō, "wrap", and chō, a character that also counts tools (Middle Chinese teng); JMdict also lists an older spelling whose first character the Guangyun glosses "kitchen" (baew), so the history of the pair is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

さかなを おさらに だします。 — sakana o osara ni dashimasu — "I serve the fish on a plate."
