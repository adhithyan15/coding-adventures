French, chapter 367: Supposons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
supposer — to suppose, to assume.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the solidarity, then the French for the community.

You'll want to know: supposer.
supposer — "to suppose, to assume".
Je suppose que tu es fatigué après ce voyage. I suppose you are tired after that trip.
Supposer que + indicative. À supposer que takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
supposer comes through Middle French (EtymDB) from Latin suppōnere "to put under, to put in place of" (Lewis & Short), which took the sense "to assume" in Medieval Latin; it is built on pōnere "to put".

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'hypothèse — the hypothesis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the community, then the French for to suppose.

You'll want to know: l'hypothèse.
l'hypothèse — "the hypothesis".
Dans cette hypothèse, nous partirions plus tôt. On that hypothesis, we would leave earlier.
Dans l'hypothèse où takes the conditional. Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
hypothèse is a learned borrowing from Latin hypothesis (EtymDB), from Greek hupóthesis "a foundation, a supposition", built from hupó "under" and thésis "a placing".

Lesson 3 of 5.
présumer — to presume.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: présumer.
présumer — "to presume".
Je présume que le magasin est fermé le dimanche. I presume the shop is closed on Sundays.
Formal: je présume que oui — I presume so.

The word, taken apart.
présumer is a learned borrowing from Latin praesūmere "to take beforehand, to suppose" (Lewis & Short), built on sūmere "to take".

Ce n'est qu'une supposition: je n'en suis pas sûr. It is only an assumption: I am not sure of it.

supposition is a learned borrowing from Latin suppositiō "a putting under, a substitution" (Lewis & Short), from suppōnere, built on pōnere "to put".
