# sāṅgṇe — to tell, by ear first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-CHALNE, MR-SENSE-CHALEL-OKAY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have asked someone's name with a whole question. There is a
shorter way, and it turns on a verb you have not met.

## You'll want to know: sāṅgṇe
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-SANGNE]; assesses=[] -->

> *sāṅgṇe* — **to tell**

Asked of a person you are being polite to, it comes out *sāṅgā*:

> *tumchaṁ nāv sāṅgā.* — **Tell me your name.**

Two syllables, and it does the work your longer question was doing.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

This word is here **by ear only**, on purpose. Its middle consonant is *ga* —
a letter this book has shown you inside other words but has never taught your
hand to make, and asking you to read a shape nobody taught you would be the one
thing a gentle ramp must not do.

So carry *sāṅgṇe* the way a child carries a word off a shop sign: sayable,
understandable, useful today, and readable later. The letter is owed to you,
and it is written down as owed.

## The word, taken apart — counting, then telling
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-SANGNE-SAMKHYA]; assesses=[] -->

*Sāṅgṇe* is often traced to Sanskrit *śaṃsati*, "recites, proclaims", and
through it to the root behind Latin *cēnseō* and English **census**. CDIAL does
not file it there. It lists Marathi *sāṅgṇe*, "to declare, to bid", under
Sanskrit *saṃkhyāti*, "reckons" (CDIAL 12842), beside Prakrit verbs
for saying such as *saṃghai*. Bloch once linked those Prakrit verbs with a root
seen in Greek *omphē*, "voice"; CDIAL records that view without taking it.

So on CDIAL's account the verb for telling began as a verb for counting up.
English took the same turn with an unrelated word: to **tell** once meant to
count, which is why a bank still has tellers.
