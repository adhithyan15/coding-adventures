# ち — two strokes, four roman letters, one beat

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-NI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three strokes: vertical, short bar, long bar. Write the sign read *ni*.

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-SCRIPT-CHI-01]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-HIRAGANA-MORA] -->

> ち

Two strokes:

1. a **short horizontal** across the top
2. the **body** — down from the middle of that bar, curving left, then round into
   a full belly to the right and up

Read **chi**: the *ch* of English *cheese*, then the tight *i*.

**Count what just happened.** One sign. One beat. **Four roman letters** to
describe it. The romanization is a pronunciation aid written for people who do not
read the script yet — it is not a smaller version of the writing system, and the
number of letters in it tells you nothing about the number of beats in the word.

This is the moment the roman spelling starts costing you more than it pays, and it
is why the signs are worth the effort.
