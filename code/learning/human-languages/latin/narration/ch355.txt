Latin, chapter 355: Condīmenta.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
condiō, condīre — to season, to spice; to pickle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to fry, then the Latin for to roast.

You'll want to know: condiō, condīre.
condiō, condīre — "to season, to spice; to pickle".
Coquus carnem pipere et sale condīvit. The cook seasoned the meat with pepper and salt.
condiō, condīre, condīvī, condītum. First to preserve fruit in vinegar or salt, then to flavour.

The word, taken apart.
condiō is taken by Lewis & Short as a side form of condō, "to put away, to store": first to put fruit away in pickle, then to season.

Lesson 2 of 5.
condīmentum, condīmentī — a seasoning, a spice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to roast, then the Latin for to season.

You'll want to know: condīmentum, condīmentī.
condīmentum, condīmentī — "a seasoning, a spice".
Famēs optimum condīmentum est. Hunger is the best seasoning.
Neuter, from condiō. Cicero passes on the saying that hunger seasons food. English condiment.

The word, taken apart.
condīmentum is built on condiō, "to season" (EtymDB), with the ending -mentum. English condiment comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.

aspergō, aspergere, aspersī, aspersum: ad + spargō, 'to scatter'. English asperse.

The word, taken apart.
aspergō is built on spargō, "to scatter", with ad- (Lewis & Short); the vowel changes inside the compound.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a seasoning, then the Latin for to sprinkle.

You'll want to know: īnfundō, īnfundere.
īnfundō, īnfundere — "to pour in, to pour on".
Garum et oleum in patinam īnfūdit. He poured fish sauce and oil into the pan.
īnfundō, īnfundere, īnfūdī, īnfūsum: in + fundō, 'to pour'. English infuse.

The word, taken apart.
īnfundō is built on fundō, "to pour", with in, "into" (Lewis & Short). English infuse comes from its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cōlō, cōlāre — to strain, to filter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sprinkle, then the Latin for to pour in.

You'll want to know: cōlō, cōlāre.
cōlō, cōlāre — "to strain, to filter".
Vīnum per linteum cōlāvit. He strained the wine through a cloth.
cōlō, cōlāre, cōlāvī, cōlātum, from cōlum, 'a strainer'. Not the verb colō, colere, 'to farm'. English colander.

The word, taken apart.
cōlō is built on cōlum, "a strainer, a colander" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short). English colander comes from its family.
