## You'll want to know: 牙医
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C473-TEETH473-02]; assesses=[] -->

**牙医** — *yáyī* — "dentist".

> **我的牙医很和气** *wǒ de yáyī hěn héqi* — My dentist is very nice.

The new character, in pieces: 牙 — four strokes: a short bar on top; a short stroke down on the left that turns and runs right; a vertical that hooks to the left at the foot; and a long stroke falling to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**牙医** (*yáyī*) is built from 牙 'tooth' and 医 'doctor', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ngae* and *'i* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'teeth' and the second 'to heal'.
