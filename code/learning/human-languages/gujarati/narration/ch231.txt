Gujarati, chapter 231: Pills and ointment.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ગોળી (goḷī) — pill, tablet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to suffer, then the Gujarati for to faint.

You'll want to know: ગોળી.
ગોળી — goḷī — "pill, tablet".
હું રાત્રે ગોળી નથી લેતો. — hũ rātre goḷī nathī leto — "I don't take the pill at night."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગોળી (goḷī) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit gola, "ball", through golikā, "little ball", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "pill" (CDIAL 4321).

Lesson 2 of 5.
મલમ (malam) — ointment, balm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to faint, then the Gujarati for pill.

You'll want to know: મલમ.
મલમ — malam — "ointment, balm".
શું આ મલમ સારો છે? — śũ ā malam sāro che? — "Is this ointment good?"

The word, taken apart.
મલમ (malam) is a loan, usually traced to Arabic marham, "ointment", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is r-h-m (Lane).

Lesson 5 of 5.
મટવું (maṭvũ) — to be cured, to clear up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for nurse, then the Gujarati for treatment.

You'll want to know: મટવું.
મટવું — maṭvũ — "to be cured, to clear up".
શું તમારો તાવ મટ્યો? — śũ tamāro tāv maṭyo? — "Has your fever gone?"

The word, taken apart.
મટવું (maṭvũ) is probably a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to be rubbed out", under Sanskrit mṛṣṭa, "rubbed, washed", though it calls one change of sound unexplained (CDIAL 10299); an illness that is cured is rubbed out.
