Lesson 1 of 6.
dǎnzi — courage; nerve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a precious treasure, then the Mandarin for to hide away.

You'll want to know: dǎnzi.
dǎnzi — "courage; nerve".
xiǎoshíhou wǒ de dǎnzi hěn xiǎo — When I was little I was very timid.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎnzi is built from dǎn 'the gall' and 子, here a light noun ending, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tamX and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which pairs the first with the liver.

Lesson 2 of 6.
鬼 (guǐ) — ghost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to hide away, then the Mandarin for the nerve to face danger.

You'll want to know: 鬼.
鬼 — guǐ — "ghost".
那个房子里有鬼 nàge fángzi lǐ yǒu guǐ — There's a ghost in that house.
The new character, in pieces: 鬼 (guǐ) — nine strokes: a short stroke falling left over a box with a bar inside; from the box two long strokes, one falling to the left, one bending right along the foot and hooking up; and on the right a small turning stroke and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
鬼 (guǐ) is inherited from Middle Chinese kjw+jX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the word by one of like sound, 'to return home'.

Script — a box with long legs.
鬼 (guǐ).
Three pieces: the box, then the legs, then the small piece on the right.
The box, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left on top. Then a short vertical on the left, a bar that runs right and turns down, a bar across the middle, and the bar that closes the foot.
The legs, two strokes. A long stroke that starts at the top of the box, runs down through its middle and falls away to the left. Then a stroke that runs down, bends right along the foot and hooks up at the end.
The small piece, two strokes, inside the bend on the right: a short stroke that falls to the left and turns right, then a dot.
5 + 2 + 2 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 鬼 (guǐ)? (Nine.) How many pieces? (Three: the box, the legs, the small piece.) Which stroke hooks up? (The seventh, along the foot.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
nàoguǐ — to be haunted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the nerve to face danger, then the Mandarin for a ghost in a story.

You'll want to know: nàoguǐ.
nàoguǐ — "to be haunted".
dàjiā dōu shuō nàge fángzi nàoguǐ — Everyone says that house is haunted.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nàoguǐ is built from nào 'to make a din' and 鬼 (guǐ) 'a ghost', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nraewH and kjw+jX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'not quiet' and 'to disturb'.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a ghost in a story, then the Mandarin for to be haunted by a ghost.

You'll want to know: 见鬼.
见鬼 — jiànguǐ — "how absurd; curse it".
见鬼了我的手机不见了 jiànguǐ le wǒ de shǒujī bú jiàn le — What on earth? My phone's gone!

The word, taken apart.
见鬼 (jiànguǐ) is built from 见 'to see' and 鬼 (guǐ) 'a ghost', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kenH and kjw+jX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to look'. The exclamation is not given there.

Lesson 6 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be haunted by a ghost, then the Mandarin for how absurd.

You'll want to know: 人间.
人间 — rénjiān — "the human world".
传说七月的时候鬼会来人间 chuánshuō qīyuè de shíhou guǐ huì lái rénjiān — Legend says that in the seventh month ghosts come into the world of the living.

The word, taken apart.
人间 (rénjiān) is built from 人 'people' and 间 'between', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyin and kean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the second under an older form of the character and glosses it 'a gap'.
