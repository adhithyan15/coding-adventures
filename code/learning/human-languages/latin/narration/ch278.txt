Lesson 1 of 5.
volūmen, volūminis — a book-roll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an arrow, then the Latin for a trumpet.

You'll want to know: volūmen, volūminis.
volūmen, volūminis — "a book-roll".

The word, taken apart.
volūmen is built on volvō, "to roll" (Lewis & Short), with -men, an ending for a result: something rolled up. volvō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European welw-, "to roll" (EtymDB 2.1). English volume comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pāgina, pāginae — a page.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a trumpet, then the Latin for a book-roll.

You'll want to know: pāgina, pāginae.
pāgina, pāginae — "a page".

The word, taken apart.
pāgina is built on the root of pangō, "to fasten, to fix" (Lewis & Short): first a row of vines fastened together, then a column of writing. The root is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European peh2g-, "to fasten" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
littera, litterae — a letter of the alphabet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a book-roll, then the Latin for a page.

You'll want to know: littera, litterae.
littera, litterae — "a letter of the alphabet".

The word, taken apart.
littera has no sure origin; the old link with linō, "to smear", is doubtful, and a loan from Greek diphthera, "a hide for writing on", through Etruscan has also been suggested.

Lesson 4 of 5.
verbum, verbī — a word.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a page, then the Latin for a letter of the alphabet.

You'll want to know: verbum, verbī.
verbum, verbī — "a word".

The word, taken apart.
verbum is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European werdhom, "word" (EtymDB 2.1); English word is a cousin (Lewis & Short).
