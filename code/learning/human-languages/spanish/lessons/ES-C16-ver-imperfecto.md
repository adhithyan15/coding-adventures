# veía — keep the extra e you already met

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VER, ES-GRAMMAR-VER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You learned **ver** before its past: **veo, ves, ve**. Its imperfect
keeps **ve-** and adds the familiar **-ía** row. That gives **veía** — “I was
seeing” or “I used to see.”

## Grammar Lens: the singular ver row
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-VER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VER, ES-SOUND-WRITTEN-ACCENT] -->

| person | open or habitual past |
|---|---|
| yo | **veía** |
| tú | **veías** |
| él / ella / usted | **veía** |

Say **veía · veías · veía**. Keep **í-a** in separate syllables. Plural persons
wait.

## The extra e, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-VER-VIDERE, ES-GRAMMAR-VER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

Old Spanish **veer** displays the **ve-** shape clearly. The imperfect
**veía** preserves that vowel before the familiar **-ía** material. It is useful
to call this an exceptional learned row, but not to pretend that **ver** was
already known before this chapter or that the history is merely “regular plus
one letter.”
