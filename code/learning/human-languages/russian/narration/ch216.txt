Russian, chapter 216: Calm, Nervous, Happy, Sad, Strict.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
спокойный (spokóynyy) — calm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for unfortunately, then the Russian for fortunately.

You'll want to know: спокойный.
спокойный (spokóynyy) — "calm".

The word, taken apart.
спокойный (spokóynyy) is built on покой, "rest, peace", which Vasmer relates to Latin quiēs, "rest", and Gothic hveila, "a while": English while is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
нервный (nérvnyy) — nervous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for fortunately, then the Russian for calm.

You'll want to know: нервный.
нервный (nérvnyy) — "nervous".

The word, taken apart.
нервный (nérvnyy) is нерв, "a nerve", with -ный. нерв is a bookish loan through German Nerv or French nerf from Latin nervus, "a sinew, a nerve" (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
счастливый (schastlívyy) — happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for calm, then the Russian for nervous.

You'll want to know: счастливый.
счастливый (schastlívyy) — "happy".

The word, taken apart.
счастливый (schastlívyy) is built on счастье, "luck, happiness", usually explained as "a good share", from часть, "a share" (Vasmer). Vasmer relates часть to Lithuanian kąsti, "to bite": a share is a bite.

Lesson 5 of 5.
строгий (strógiy) — strict.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for happy, then the Russian for sad.

You'll want to know: строгий.
строгий (strógiy) — "strict".

The word, taken apart.
строгий (strógiy) — it is usually taken as a loan from Polish srogi, "severe", and the whole group is linked with стеречь, "to guard", and сторож, "a watchman" (Vasmer); Trubachev's note cites other views.
