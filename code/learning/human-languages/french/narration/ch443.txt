French, chapter 443: L'hiver en ville.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'égout — the sewer, the drain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the roof gutter, then the French for the covered market.

You'll want to know: l'égout.
l'égout — "the sewer, the drain".
Après l'orage, les égouts sont pleins. After the storm the sewers are full.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
égout is built on égoutter 'to let drip, to drain' (EtymDB), from goutte 'drop', which goes back to Latin gutta.

Lesson 3 of 5.
déblayer — to clear away (snow, rubble).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the sewer, then the French for the gutter.

You'll want to know: déblayer.
déblayer — "to clear away (snow, rubble)".
Les voisins ont déblayé la neige devant l'immeuble. The neighbours cleared the snow in front of the building.
Le déblaiement — the clearing.

The word, taken apart.
déblayer comes from Old French desbleer 'to clear a field of its corn', usually traced to blé 'corn'; EtymDB lists the verb without a source.

La ville sale les routes quand il gèle. The town puts salt on the roads when it freezes.
C'est salé! — that is pricey (informal).

The word, taken apart.
saler goes back to Vulgar Latin salāre, from Latin sal 'salt' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
déneiger — to clear of snow, to plough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to clear away, then the French for to salt.

You'll want to know: déneiger.
déneiger — "to clear of snow, to plough".
Le matin, les camions déneigent les grandes rues. In the morning the lorries clear the snow from the main streets.
Very common in Québec and in the mountains: le déneigement.

The word, taken apart.
déneiger is built from dé- and neige 'snow', which goes back to Latin nix, nivis (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.
