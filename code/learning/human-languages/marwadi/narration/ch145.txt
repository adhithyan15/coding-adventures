5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
गुलाबी (gulābī) — pink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for thin, then the Marwadi for short.

You'll want to know: गुलाबी.
गुलाबी — gulābī — "pink".

The word, taken apart.
गुलाबी (gulābī) is a loan, usually traced to Persian gulābī, "rose-coloured". It is built on Persian gulāb, "rose-water".

Lesson 2 of 5.
केसरियो (kesariyo) — saffron-coloured.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for short, then the Marwadi for pink.

You'll want to know: केसरियो.
केसरियो — kesariyo — "saffron-coloured".

The word, taken apart.
केसरियो (kesariyo) is built with the suffix -iyo on kesar, "saffron", which CDIAL lists as Gujarati and Hindi kesar under Sanskrit kesara, "hair, filament" (CDIAL 3474): saffron is the flower's filament.

बैंगणी — baiṅgṇī — "purple".

The word, taken apart.
बैंगणी (baiṅgṇī) is built with -ī on baiṅgaṇ, "aubergine", from Sanskrit vātiṅgaṇa, which CDIAL thinks may come from Dravidian (CDIAL 11503). CDIAL lists Hindi baĩjnī, "purple", under the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
नारंगी (nāraṅgī) — orange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for sky-blue, then the Marwadi for purple.

You'll want to know: नारंगी.
नारंगी — nāraṅgī — "orange".

The word, taken apart.
नारंगी (nāraṅgī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nāraṅga, "orange tree". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 7073); the match is through Hindi nāraṅgī and Gujarati nārãgī. The colour is named for the fruit.
