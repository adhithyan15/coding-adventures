Lesson 1 of 5.
сдача (sdácha) — change (money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for tears, then the Russian for a smile.

You'll want to know: сдача.
сдача (sdácha) — "change (money)".

The word, taken apart.
сдача (sdácha) comes from сдать, "to hand over", с- with дать, "to give": change is the money handed back.

Lesson 2 of 5.
скидка (skídka) — a discount.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a smile, then the Russian for change.

You'll want to know: скидка.
скидка (skídka) — "a discount".

The word, taken apart.
скидка (skídka) comes from скидывать, "to throw off", с-, "off", with кидать, "to throw": a discount is knocked off the price. Vasmer relates кидать to Sanskrit códati, "urges on".

Lesson 3 of 5.
покупка (pokúpka) — a purchase.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for change, then the Russian for a discount.

You'll want to know: покупка.
покупка (pokúpka) — "a purchase".

The word, taken apart.
покупка (pokúpka) comes from покупать, "to buy", with -ка: a thing bought. купить came from Gothic kaupōn, "to trade", from Latin caupō, "a trader" (Vasmer).

Lesson 5 of 5.
директор (diréktor) — a director, a head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a purchase, then the Russian for a colleague.

You'll want to know: директор.
директор (diréktor) — "a director, a head".

The word, taken apart.
директор (diréktor) — from 1720; it came through German Direktor or Polish dyrektor from Latin dīrēctor, "one who directs", from regere, "to guide" (Vasmer).
