Urdu, chapter 324: A Tale of Long Ago.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سنانا (sunānā) — to tell a story; to recite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to accept, then the Urdu for to keep up.

You'll want to know: سنانا.
سنانا — sunānā — "to tell a story; to recite".
امی نے ہمیں ایک گیت سنایا۔ ammī ne hameṅ ek gīt sunāyā. Mum sang us a song.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے نظم سنائی, us ne nazm sunāī.

The word, taken apart.
is the causing partner of سننا, to hear: CDIAL lists Hindi sunānā, "informs", under Sanskrit śṛṇoti, "hears" (CDIAL 12598). To سنانا is to make someone hear: a story, a song, a poem.

Lesson 2 of 5.
داستان (dāstān) — an epic tale; a long story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to keep up, then the Urdu for to tell a story.

You'll want to know: داستان.
داستان — dāstān — "an epic tale; a long story".
دادا نے ایک لمبی داستان سنائی۔ dādā ne ek lambī dāstān sunāī. Grandfather told a long tale.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
is usually traced to Persian dāstān, "a tale". Urdu's old داستان went on for nights, with kings, giants and fairies.

Lesson 3 of 5.
قصہ (qissā) — a tale; an anecdote.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to tell a story, then the Urdu for an epic tale.

You'll want to know: قصہ.
قصہ — qissā — "a tale; an anecdote".
یہ قصہ سب جانتے ہیں۔ yih qissā sab jānte haiṅ. Everyone knows this story.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic qiṣṣa, "a story". A قصہ (qissā) is short; a داستان (dāstān) is long.
