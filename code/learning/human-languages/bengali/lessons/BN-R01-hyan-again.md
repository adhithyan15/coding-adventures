# হ্যাঁ and নমস্কার, side by side

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-NOMOSHKAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 5s] Say the greeting, with the hands.

## Guided Practice — the distant band
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-NOMOSHKAR-01, BN-SCRIPT-NOMOSHKAR-READ-01, BN-SCRIPT-HYAN-READ-01] -->

- [YOU SAY: hello, and goodbye, with one word — *nômoshkar*]
- [YOU SAY: *yes* — *hyã*]
- [YOU SAY: how many pieces are in each (**seven**, and **five**)]

These two words are the bookends of the reading strand. The greeting was the
first thing you could decode and it took nine lessons of letters to earn. *Yes*
came three chapters later and cost three.

The difference is not that the second word is simpler. It is that by the time it
arrived you already owned four of its five pieces — which is the argument for
buying letters early and spending them often, made in two words.
