German, chapter 142: The Future, Next Week, the Day after Tomorrow, Next Year, To Plan.

Lesson 1 of 5.
die Zukunft — the future.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to repair, then the German for to start.

You'll want to know: die Zukunft.
die Zukunft — "the future". German builds the future with werden and an infinitive at the very end: Ich werde Deutsch lernen. — "I will learn German."

The word, taken apart.
Zukunft from Middle High German zuokunft from Old High German zuochumft: zu-, "to", + a noun built on kommen, "to come" (EtymDB 2.1). The future is what is coming.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nächste Woche — next week.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to start, then the German for the future.

You'll want to know: nächste Woche.
nächste Woche — "next week". Put the time first and the verb stays second: Nächste Woche werde ich Deutsch lernen. Werde comes before ich, and lernen waits at the end.

The word, taken apart.
nächste Woche is "nearest" + Woche, "week". Woche from Old High German wohha from Proto-Germanic wikōn, "turn, succession" (EtymDB 2.1), the same word as English week.

Lesson 3 of 5.
übermorgen — the day after tomorrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the future, then the German for next week.

You'll want to know: übermorgen.
übermorgen — "the day after tomorrow". When a time word already points ahead, spoken German keeps the present tense: Übermorgen fahre ich. — "I am going the day after tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
übermorgen is the word for "over, beyond" + morgen, "tomorrow": the day beyond tomorrow. EtymDB 2.1 lists it beside Dutch overmorgen.

Lesson 4 of 5.
nächstes Jahr — next year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for next week, then the German for the day after tomorrow.

You'll want to know: nächstes Jahr.
nächstes Jahr — "next year". Werden changes with the person: ich werde, du wirst, er wird. Nächstes Jahr wirst du Deutsch lernen.

The word, taken apart.
nächstes Jahr is "nearest" + Jahr, "year" from Old High German jār from Proto-Germanic jērą, English year. (EtymDB 2.1) nächst- is the superlative of the word for "near".

Lesson 5 of 5.
vorhaben — to plan, to intend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the day after tomorrow, then the German for next year.

You'll want to know: vorhaben.
vorhaben — "to plan, to intend". It splits like the other separable verbs: Was hast du vor? — "What are you planning?"

The word, taken apart.
vorhaben is the word for "before, in front" + haben, "to have": to have a plan in front of you. haben is the same verb as English have. (EtymDB 2.1).
