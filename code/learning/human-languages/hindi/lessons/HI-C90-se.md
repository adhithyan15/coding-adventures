# से — where you came from, and what you came by

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C36-GHAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have **पर** for being *at* a place. Try to say you came *from*
one, and notice you have the place and no way to leave it.

## You'll want to know: से
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**से** (*se*) — "from," and also "by."

**घर से** — from home. **हाथ से** — by hand.

Like **पर**, it trails its noun.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-CONCEPT-C13-HAATH-01] -->

English splits this into two words and Hindi does not:

| English | Hindi |
|---|---|
| from home | घर से |
| by hand | हाथ से |

Both are the same idea seen from two angles. **Where something starts** and
**what it travels through** are, to Hindi, one relation: the thing *proceeds
from* the noun. A journey proceeds from your house; an action proceeds from your
hand.

Once you see that, the second use stops being a second word to learn. A vehicle
you travel in takes **से** for exactly the same reason your hand does.

## Grammar Lens: two postpositions, one habit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01] -->

| postposition | what it marks |
|---|---|
| पर | at a point |
| से | from it, or by way of it |

Both trail the noun, both are one syllable, and neither has an English word
sitting in the same place. That is the whole class: **the small word comes
second**, and knowing the noun tells you where to put it.

A question you will be asked in a speaking interview turns on this word: *आप
कहाँ से हैं?* — where are you from?
