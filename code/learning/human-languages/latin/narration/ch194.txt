Lesson 1 of 5.
sententia, sententiae — an opinion; a sentence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bracelet, then the Latin for a necklace.

You'll want to know: sententia, sententiae.
sententia, sententiae — "an opinion; a sentence".

The word, taken apart.
sententia is shortened from sentientia, "a feeling" (Lewis & Short), built on sentiō, "to feel, to think": what one thinks.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ōrātiō, ōrātiōnis — a speech.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an opinion; a sentence, then the Latin for conversation.

You'll want to know: ōrātiō, ōrātiōnis.
ōrātiō, ōrātiōnis — "a speech".

The word, taken apart.
ōrātiō is built on ōrō, "to speak, to plead, to pray" (Lewis & Short), with -tiō, an ending for an act. ōrō is often linked with ōs, "mouth" (EtymDB 2.1), but the link is uncertain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
rūmor, rūmōris — a rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for conversation, then the Latin for a speech.

You'll want to know: rūmor, rūmōris.
rūmor, rūmōris — "a rumour".

The word, taken apart.
rūmor is often traced to a Proto-Indo-European root rewH-, "to roar, to shout", but the link is not certain. Lewis & Short put raucus, "hoarse", in the same family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
historia, historiae — history.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a speech, then the Latin for a rumour.

You'll want to know: historia, historiae.
historia, historiae — "history".

The word, taken apart.
historia was borrowed from Greek historia, "inquiry, a narrative" (Lewis & Short), from histōr, "one who knows" (EtymDB 2.1), whose root is usually given as weyd-, "to see, to know", the root of videō, "to see".
