Mandarin Chinese, chapter 338: Festival Visits.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
过节 (guòjié) — to celebrate a festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for good luck, then the Mandarin for to make a wish.

You'll want to know: 过节.
过节 — guòjié — "to celebrate a festival".
去年我们回老家过节 qùnián wǒmen huí lǎojiā guòjié — Last year we went back to our home town for the festival.

The word, taken apart.
过节 (guòjié) is built from 过 'to pass' and 节 'a festival', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaH and tset (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go beyond' and the second 'a joint of bamboo'. The sense 'a festival' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

You'll want to know: bàifǎng.
bàifǎng — "to pay a visit to".
guòjié de shíhou wǒmen qù bàifǎng le lǎoshī — Over the festival we went to visit our teacher.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàifǎng is built from bài 'to bow' and fǎng 'to call on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, peajH and phjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to consult'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tuányuán — to be reunited as a family.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to celebrate a festival, then the Mandarin for to pay a visit to someone.

You'll want to know: tuányuán.
tuányuán — "to be reunited as a family".
guònián de shíhou jiārén dōu huílai tuányuán — At the new year the whole family comes back together.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuányuán is built from tuán and yuán, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dwan and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first with this very word and the second 'the round body of the heavens'.

You'll want to know: 假日.
假日 — jiàrì — "holiday; day off".

The word, taken apart.
假日 (jiàrì) is built from 假 'leave' and 日 'a day', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaeH and nyit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first in this falling tone 'a holiday from work'.
