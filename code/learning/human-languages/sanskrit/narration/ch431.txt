Sanskrit, chapter 431: Dear and Cheap.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अर्घः (arghaḥ) — worth; value.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a customer, then the Sanskrit for haggles.

You'll want to know: अर्घः.
अर्घः — arghaḥ — "worth; value".
रत्नस्य अर्घः महान् अस्ति। ratnasya arghaḥ mahān asti. The jewel's worth is great.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अर्घः (arghaḥ) comes from the root √arh, "to deserve, to be worth"; MW gives argha as worth, value, price (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मिश्रयति (miśrayati) — mixes; adulterates.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for costly, then the Sanskrit for low-priced.

You'll want to know: मिश्रयति.
मिश्रयति — miśrayati — "mixes; adulterates".
कृपणः वणिक् दुग्धे जलं मिश्रयति। kṛpaṇaḥ vaṇik dugdhe jalaṃ miśrayati. The stingy merchant mixes water into the milk.
Past (he …): सः दुग्धे जलं मिश्रितवान्, saḥ dugdhe jalaṃ miśritavān.

The word, taken apart.
मिश्रयति (miśrayati) is built on miśra, "mixed", as MW notes; MW gives miśrayati as to mix or blend, with the participle miśrita; MW compares Latin miscere and English mix (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
निकषः (nikaṣaḥ) — a touchstone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for low-priced, then the Sanskrit for mixes.

You'll want to know: निकषः.
निकषः — nikaṣaḥ — "a touchstone".
स्वर्णकारः निकषे सुवर्णं घर्षति। svarṇakāraḥ nikaṣe suvarṇaṃ gharṣati. The goldsmith rubs the gold on the touchstone.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
निकषः (nikaṣaḥ) is built from ni and the root √kaṣ, "to rub"; MW gives nikaṣa as rubbing in and as the touchstone, the stone on which gold is rubbed to test it (MW).
