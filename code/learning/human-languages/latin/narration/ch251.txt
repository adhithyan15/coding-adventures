Lesson 1 of 5.
catulus, catulī — a puppy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a laurel, then the Latin for a violet.

You'll want to know: catulus, catulī.
catulus, catulī — "a puppy".

The word, taken apart.
catulus, "a young animal, a puppy", has no sure origin. Lewis & Short make it a diminutive of catus, "a cat", but catulus is far older than that word for cat, so the link is unlikely.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for a puppy, then the Latin for a tiger.

You'll want to know: clāva, clāvae.
clāva, clāvae — "a club".

The word, taken apart.
clāva is often traced to Proto-Indo-European kelh2-, "to beat, to break" (EtymDB 2.1), with percellō, "to strike down", in the same family (Lewis & Short); the link is not certain.

catēna, catēnae — "a chain".

The word, taken apart.
catēna has no sure origin; EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed Proto-Indo-European kat-, "to twist, to plait". English chain comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
scūtum, scūtī — a shield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a club, then the Latin for a chain.

You'll want to know: scūtum, scūtī.
scūtum, scūtī — "a shield".

The word, taken apart.
scūtum is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root skei-, "to cut, to split" (EtymDB 2.1): a shield of split boards. Old Irish sciath, "shield", is a cousin; Lewis & Short's link with Greek skytos, "leather", is less likely.
