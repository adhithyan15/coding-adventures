Malayalam, chapter 360: By Chance or on Purpose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
യാദൃച്ഛികമായി (yādṛcchikamāyi) — by chance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for now and then, then the Malayalam for right after.

You'll want to know: യാദൃച്ഛികമായി.
യാദൃച്ഛികമായി — yādṛcchikamāyi — "by chance".
ചന്തയിൽ വെച്ച് ഞാൻ യാദൃച്ഛികമായി എന്റെ പഴയ അധ്യാപകനെ കണ്ടു. cantayil veccŭ ñān yādṛcchikamāyi enṟe paḻaya adhyāpakane kaṇṭu. At the market I met my old teacher by chance.
The -ആയി ending makes an adverb from the adjective യാദൃച്ഛികം, chance.

The word, taken apart.
യാദൃച്ഛികമായി (yādṛcchikamāyi) is built from Sanskrit yādṛcchika, "spontaneous, accidental, unexpected" (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam adverb ending -ആയി.

Lesson 2 of 5.
മനപ്പൂർവം (manappūrvaṁ) — on purpose; deliberately.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for right after, then the Malayalam for by chance.

You'll want to know: മനപ്പൂർവം.
മനപ്പൂർവം — manappūrvaṁ — "on purpose; deliberately".
അവൻ മനപ്പൂർവം ഗ്ലാസ് പൊട്ടിച്ചില്ല. avan manappūrvaṁ glāsŭ poṭṭiccilla. He did not break the glass on purpose.
Literally with the mind first: done with intent. The careful written spelling is മനഃപൂർവം.

The word, taken apart.
മനപ്പൂർവം (manappūrvaṁ) is usually traced to Sanskrit manas, "mind", and pūrva, "preceded by": an act that the mind went before.

Lesson 3 of 5.
അബദ്ധം (abaddhaṁ) — a blunder; a silly mistake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for by chance, then the Malayalam for on purpose.

You'll want to know: അബദ്ധം.
അബദ്ധം — abaddhaṁ — "a blunder; a silly mistake".
ഞാൻ ഒരു വലിയ അബദ്ധം ചെയ്തു. ñān oru valiya abaddhaṁ ceytu. I made a big blunder.
By mistake is അബദ്ധത്തിൽ: without meaning to.

The word, taken apart.
അബദ്ധം (abaddhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit abaddha, "unbound; unmeaning, nonsensical" (Monier-Williams). A blunder is something that makes no sense once it is done.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ഭാഗ്യം (bhāgyaṁ) — luck; good fortune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for on purpose, then the Malayalam for a blunder.

You'll want to know: ഭാഗ്യം.
ഭാഗ്യം — bhāgyaṁ — "luck; good fortune".
ഭാഗ്യത്തിന് ഞങ്ങൾക്ക് ബസ് കിട്ടി. bhāgyattinŭ ñaṅṅaḷkkŭ basŭ kiṭṭi. Luckily we caught the bus.
ഭാഗ്യത്തിന് means luckily; ഭാഗ്യവശാൽ, fortunately, is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
ഭാഗ്യം (bhāgyaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit bhāgya, "fortune, one's lot" (CDIAL 9434), from bhaga, "a share": luck is the share that falls to you.

Lesson 5 of 5.
വിധിവശാൽ (vidhivaśāl) — as fate willed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a blunder, then the Malayalam for luck.

You'll want to know: വിധിവശാൽ.
വിധിവശാൽ — vidhivaśāl — "as fate willed".
വിധിവശാൽ അന്ന് അവൻ ആ ബസിൽ കയറിയില്ല. vidhivaśāl annŭ avan ā basil kayaṟiyilla. As fate would have it, he did not get on that bus that day.
Built like ഭാഗ്യവശാൽ, fortunately: by the power of fate.

The word, taken apart.
വിധിവശാൽ (vidhivaśāl) is borrowed whole from Sanskrit vidhivaśāt, "through the power of fate or destiny" (Monier-Williams), from vidhi, "rule, fate" (CDIAL 11755), and vaśa, "power".
