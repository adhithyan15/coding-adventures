Spanish, chapter 701: Calor y sequía.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
caluroso — hot (weather); warm (welcome).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the snowfall, then the Spanish for the hoarfrost.

You'll want to know: caluroso.
caluroso — "hot (weather); warm (welcome)".
Fue un verano muy caluroso. It was a very hot summer.
Un caluroso saludo — a warm greeting.

The word, taken apart.
caluroso is built on calor "heat", which is inherited from Latin calor (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
templado — mild, temperate; lukewarm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the hoarfrost, then the Spanish for hot.

You'll want to know: templado.
templado — "mild, temperate; lukewarm".
En primavera el tiempo es templado. In spring the weather is mild.
Agua templada — lukewarm water.

The word, taken apart.
templado is the past form of templar "to temper" (EtymDB), Old Spanish temprar, from Latin temperāre "to mix in due measure".

Lesson 3 of 5.
el bochorno — the muggy heat, close weather; embarrassment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for hot, then the Spanish for mild.

You'll want to know: el bochorno.
el bochorno — "the muggy heat, close weather; embarrassment".
¡Qué bochorno hace hoy! It is so muggy today!
It also means embarrassment: ¡qué bochorno!

The word, taken apart.
bochorno is inherited from Latin vulturnus "east wind" (EtymDB); the name of a hot wind became the name for hot, heavy weather.

Lesson 4 of 5.

la sequía — "the drought".
Este año hay sequía y no llueve. There is a drought this year and it does not rain.
Built on seco, dry.

The word, taken apart.
sequía is built on seco "dry" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin siccus "dry".
