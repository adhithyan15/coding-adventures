## You'll want to know: 有点儿
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C93-YOUDIANR-01]; assesses=[] -->

**有点儿** — *yǒudiǎnr* — "a bit (of something unwanted); rather".

> **有点儿大** *yǒudiǎnr dà* — It's a bit big.

> **太大了** *tài dà le* — It's too big.

Two dips in a row: spoken, the first rises, *yóudiǎnr*. It is written *yǒudiǎnr*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**有点儿** (*yǒudiǎnr*) is built from 有 'to have', 点 'a dot' and the r ending: having a dot of it. 有 and 点 are inherited from Middle Chinese, *hjuwX* and *temX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C91-YIDIANR-01, ZH-LEX-HEN-01] -->

有点儿 + an adjective is a mild complaint: *yǒudiǎnr lěng*, it's a bit cold. 很 is neutral; 太 … 了 is strong. And 一点儿 is a different job: it goes after a verb, 要一点儿水.
