Spanish, chapter 553: Cambios en el trabajo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el relevo — the replacement, the handover; the relay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the border, then the Spanish for to become independent.

You'll want to know: el relevo.
el relevo — "the replacement, the handover; the relay".
Su hija tomó el relevo en la tienda. Her daughter took over at the shop.
Tomar el relevo — to take over. In sport, los relevos is the relay.

The word, taken apart.
relevo is built on relevar 'to relieve, to replace' (EtymDB), which comes from Latin relevāre 'to lift up, to lighten', built on levāre 'to raise' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
el finiquito — the final settlement, the severance pay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the replacement, then the Spanish for the promotion.

You'll want to know: el finiquito.
el finiquito — "the final settlement, the severance pay".
Cobró el finiquito al dejar la empresa. He got his final settlement when he left the company.
The money owed to a worker when a job ends, and the paper that settles it.

The word, taken apart.
finiquito is built on finiquitar 'to settle an account', usually traced to the old phrase fin y quito 'end and quit'. Fin comes from Latin fīnis 'end'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
despedir — to dismiss, to fire; to see off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the promotion, then the Spanish for the final settlement.

You'll want to know: despedir.
despedir — "to dismiss, to fire; to see off".
Despidieron a veinte trabajadores. They fired twenty workers.
It also means to see someone off: Fuimos a despedirla al aeropuerto. Stem change: despido.

The word, taken apart.
despedir comes from Latin dē and expetere 'to ask for, to seek' (EtymDB), built on petere 'to seek'.
