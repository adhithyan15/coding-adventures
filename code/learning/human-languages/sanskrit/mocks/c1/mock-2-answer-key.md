## Writing

Task 1 is a 180–220-word source synthesis. It must accurately integrate the
night market's income, access, safety, transport, waste, disturbance, sleep,
and evidence-limit findings. It must preserve the distinction between measured
sound and household experience, propose at least three workable safeguards and
two genuinely different outcome measures, and avoid inventing data. A response
that merely copies either source is not independent production.

Task 2 is a 300–380-word developed essay for educated citizens. It must take a
clear position on ethical use of volunteer-generated data, compare at least two
use domains, and address sampling, training, verification, privacy, credit,
decision authority, a credible counterargument, proportionate evidential
thresholds, and return of value to contributing communities.

Apply the shared rubric's source fidelity, intellectual development,
organisation, audience and register, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar, external
sandhi, word-final visarga or anusvara, continuous shirorekha, Devanagari, and
word-boundary criteria. Romanization, a non-Devanagari response, dictionary,
translator, spell-checker, or copyable answer model is forbidden.

## Speaking

Task 1 is a 270–300-second presentation synthesising both expanded-library-
hours sources. It must distinguish demand evidence from recommendation,
address resource limits, paid trained staffing, safety, transport, access
equity, evidence limits, and a predeclared six-week review.

Task 2 is a 300–360-second collaborative synthesis. The candidate must compare
at least two credible community-garden layouts or operating models and reach
explicit decisions on soil testing, accessible circulation, water priority,
time and light limits, allocation, maintenance responsibility, review, and any
remaining disagreement. Score the candidate's own contribution; a partner
cannot compensate for missing task fulfilment.

Use all six expanded challenge-and-defence prompt cards in order. Each complete
examiner stimulus is delivered as live speech in 20–75 seconds; this is the
stimulus duration, not an estimate for reading a short written question and not
an individual candidate-response limit. Never show a prompt card to the
candidate. The six oral candidate responses total 330–420 seconds overall.
Require reasoned defence, qualification or reformulation, responsive oral
interaction, and conditions under which the candidate would revise the
proposal. Preparation is exactly 15 minutes with paper notes on the
examiner-provided source materials only; every scored Speaking response is oral
or live-speech, never writing. Record the complete 22-minute paper for
independent scoring.

Score pronunciation and fluency in the candidate's declared documented
Sanskrit pronunciation tradition. Do not penalise a documented alternative
merely because it differs from the recording voices. Classical non-Vedic
Sanskrit is in scope; Vedic accent and morphology are out of scope. This is a
project-defined assessment, not an external qualification.
