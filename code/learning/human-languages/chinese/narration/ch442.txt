Mandarin Chinese, chapter 442: The River.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
duì'àn — the far bank; the opposite bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to light a fire, then the Mandarin for made by nature.

You'll want to know: duì'àn.
duì'àn — "the far bank; the opposite bank".
wǒmen zuò chuán qù duì'àn — We're taking a boat across to the other side.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
duì'àn is built from 对 'facing' and àn 'a bank', both inherited from Middle Chinese, twojH and nganH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the high edge of the water.

Lesson 2 of 6.
河 (hé) — river.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for made by nature, then the Mandarin for the far bank.

You'll want to know: 河.
河 — hé — "river".
cūnzi qiánbian yǒu yì tiáo hé — There's a river in front of the village.
The new character, in pieces: 河 (hé) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left the water piece of 洗, three strokes; on the right 可 kě, five strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
河 (hé) is inherited from Middle Chinese ha (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where it is the name of one river, the one said to rise in the far west and run to the sea; the sense 'any river' is usually explained as a widening of that name.

Script — the water piece, then 可.
河 (hé).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the water piece, three strokes, as in 洗: two dots, then a stroke that rises to the right.
The right piece, 可 kě, five strokes, as you wrote it: a long bar; under it 口, three strokes, set to the left; then a long vertical down the right side that hooks to the left at the foot.
3 + 5 = eight.

Lesson 4 of 6.
上游 (shàngyóu) — upper reaches (of a river); upstream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the far bank, then the Mandarin for a river.

You'll want to know: 上游.
上游 — shàngyóu — "upper reaches (of a river); upstream".
我们坐船去河的上游 wǒmen zuò chuán qù hé de shàngyóu — We're taking a boat up the river.

The word, taken apart.
上游 (shàngyóu) is built from 上 'above' and 游 'to float', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangH and yuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); how the second came to name a stretch of a river is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
zhǎngshuǐ — to rise after rain (of a river).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a river, then the Mandarin for upstream.

You'll want to know: zhǎngshuǐ.
zhǎngshuǐ — "to rise after rain (of a river)".
zhè jǐ tiān xiàyǔ hé lǐ zhǎngshuǐ le — It has rained these last few days, and the river has risen.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: zhǎng is said with a rising tone before shuǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
zhǎngshuǐ is built from zhǎng 'to swell' and 水 'water', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjang and sywijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a great flood of water; today's falling-rising tone is irregular beside it.
