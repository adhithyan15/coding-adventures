Gujarati, chapter 407: Far from Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પરદેશ (pardeś) — abroad; a foreign country.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an official fee, then the Gujarati for to empty out.

You'll want to know: પરદેશ.
પરદેશ — pardeś — "abroad; a foreign country".
મારો ભાઈ ત્રણ વરસથી પરદેશમાં રહે છે. māro bhāī traṇ varasthī pardeśmā̃ rahe che. My brother has lived abroad for three years.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પરદેશ (pardeś) comes from Sanskrit paradeśa, "another or a foreign country" (Monier-Williams): any land that is not your own.

Lesson 2 of 5.
વતન (vatan) — a homeland; one's home town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to empty out, then the Gujarati for abroad.

You'll want to know: વતન.
વતન — vatan — "a homeland; one's home town".
દિવાળીમાં બધા વતન પાછા આવે છે. divāḷīmā̃ badhā vatan pāchā āve che. At Diwali everyone comes back to their home town.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
વતન (vatan) is usually traced to Arabic waṭan, "a homeland, a dwelling place", which came through Persian: the place you belong to, wherever you live now.

પગપાળા — pagpāḷā — "on foot".
અમે સ્ટેશન સુધી પગપાળા ગયા. ame sṭeśan sudhī pagpāḷā gayā. We went to the station on foot.

The word, taken apart.
પગપાળા (pagpāḷā) is built from પગ, a foot, and pāḷũ, "on foot", which CDIAL lists for Gujarati under pādala, "belonging to the feet", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 8066).

Lesson 5 of 5.
હિજરાવું (hijrāvũ) — to pine for home; to grieve inwardly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a dollar, then the Gujarati for on foot.

You'll want to know: હિજરાવું.
હિજરાવું — hijrāvũ — "to pine for home; to grieve inwardly".
પરદેશમાં દાદી વતન માટે હિજરાતી હતી. pardeśmā̃ dādī vatan māṭe hijrātī hatī. Abroad, grandmother used to pine for her home town.
Past (she …): તે ઘણી હિજરાઈ, te ghaṇī hijrāī.

The word, taken apart.
હિજરાવું (hijrāvũ) is usually traced to Persian hijrān, "separation", a word of Arabic origin: the ache of being far from people and places you love.
