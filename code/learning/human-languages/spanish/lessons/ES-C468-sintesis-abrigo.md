# (el aviso de la parada) — read it, then decide

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el grado** and **el abrigo**. The first decides the second,
which is the whole task here.

## Reading: the screen, and the message
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-02, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-04] -->

On the screen at the bus stop:

> **07:40 — LÍNEA 12 — 6 min**
>
> Ahora: **2 grados**. Máxima hoy: 7 grados.
>
> *(La máxima is the day's high.)*

And a message on your phone:

> Hace mucho frío. Ponte el **abrigo**, no la **chaqueta**. Y llévate la
> **bufanda**, que estuviste enfermo la semana pasada.

## Grammar Lens: what the message is doing that the screen cannot
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-01, ES-LEX-C468-ABRIGO-03] -->

The screen gives a number. The message gives a decision, and the two are not
the same kind of statement.

| the screen | the message |
|---|---|
| *2 grados* | *ponte el abrigo, no la chaqueta* |

Note **no la chaqueta**. With the chosen thing said first, Spanish needs only
*no* and the thing ruled out. Turn it round and you are back in the shape
*sino* gave you — *no la chaqueta, sino el abrigo*. Same correction, two
orders.

And *que estuviste enfermo* gives the reason, with the spoken **que** that
stands in for *porque*, which *la bufanda* introduced two lessons ago. A
screen rarely explains itself; a person usually does.
