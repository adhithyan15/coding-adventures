# ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ (ōduttidde) — what was going on, and what used to happen

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-PAST-PERSON-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-PAST-STEMS-01, KA-LEX-C46-ASK-05, KA-LEX-C33-OODU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I read a book". (*nānu ondu pustaka ōdide*.) Then "I was", on its own. (*idde*.)

[PAUSE 1s] The first is an event: it happened and it was over. A story also needs what was going on at the time.

## You'll want to know: -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ, 'I was doing'
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-ONGOING-PAST-01]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C24-RATRI-01, KA-LEX-C07-OMBATTU-01, KA-ETYMON-C18-GANTE-01, KA-LEX-C32-IRU-01] -->

> **ನಿನ್ನೆ ರಾತ್ರಿ ಒಂಬತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ನಾನು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ.**
>
> *ninne rātri ombattu gaṇṭege nānu pustaka ōduttidde.* — "At nine last night I was reading a book."

**ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ** is two pieces you know: **ಓದುತ್ತ**, the "reading" inside **ಓದುತ್ತೇನೆ**, and **ಇದ್ದೆ**, "I was": "I was, reading". The ending is the ending of **ಇದ್ದೆ**, so it changes the same way:

- **ನಾನು ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ** (*nānu ōduttidde*) — I was reading
- **ಅವನು ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದನು** (*avanu ōduttiddanu*) — he was reading
- **ಅವಳು ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದಳು** (*avaḷu ōduttiddaḷu*) — she was reading
- **ಅವರು ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು** (*avaru ōduttiddaru*) — they were reading

For "it" the ending is the **ಇತ್ತು** of "it was": **ಮಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತಿತ್ತು** (*maḷe baruttittu*), "it was raining". **ಮಳೆ** (*maḷe*, "rain") "comes" in Kannada.

## Grammar Lens: the same form for a habit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-HABITUAL-PAST-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-GRANDMOTHER, KA-LEX-C172-KALA172-02, KA-ETYMON-C27-SANJE-01, KA-LEX-ANCHOR-KATHE, KA-LEX-C92-WORK-01, KA-LEX-C32-HOOGU-01] -->

The same form says "used to", for something that happened many times over:

> **ನನ್ನ ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ ಸಂಜೆ ಒಂದು ಕಥೆ ಹೇಳುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು.**
>
> *nanna ajji pratidina sañje ondu kathe hēḷuttiddaru.* — "My grandmother used to tell a story every evening."

**ಹೇಳುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು** has the **-ಅರು** of "they", because a grandmother is spoken of with respect, the way **ಅವರು** can mean "he" or "she".

Which meaning you have comes from the time words. One point in time, **ರಾತ್ರಿ ಒಂಬತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ**, gives "was doing". A word of repeating, **ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ**, gives "used to": **ನಾನು ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ ಶಾಲೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ** (*nānu pratidina śālege hōguttidde*, "I used to go to school every day").
