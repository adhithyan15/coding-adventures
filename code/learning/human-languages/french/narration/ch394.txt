French, chapter 394: Gagner et perdre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le bénéfice — the profit; the benefit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for profitable, then the French for good value.

You'll want to know: le bénéfice.
le bénéfice — "the profit; the benefit".
L'entreprise a fait un bénéfice cette année. The company made a profit this year.
Au bénéfice de — for the benefit of.

The word, taken apart.
bénéfice is a learned borrowing from Latin beneficium "a kindness, a favour" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from bene "well" and facere "to do".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rembourser — to pay back, to refund.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the loss, then the French for the bankruptcy.

You'll want to know: rembourser.
rembourser — "to pay back, to refund".
Je te rembourse demain, promis. I will pay you back tomorrow, I promise.
Se faire rembourser — to get a refund.

The word, taken apart.
rembourser is built from re-, en- and bourse "purse": to put back into the purse. bourse comes from Late Latin bursa "a hide, a leather bag" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'emprunt — the loan (that you take out).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the bankruptcy, then the French for to pay back.

You'll want to know: l'emprunt.
l'emprunt — "the loan (that you take out)".
Ils ont fait un emprunt pour acheter la maison. They took out a loan to buy the house.
From emprunter, to borrow. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
emprunt is formed on emprunter "to borrow", usually traced to a Vulgar Latin imprūmūtāre, built on Latin mūtuum "a loan" (Lewis & Short).
