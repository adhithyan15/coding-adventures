## Grammar Lens: eighteen forms, and you built each one
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-INDICATIVE-COMPLETE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-COMPLETE, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-3PL, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-3PL] -->

| who | *-ar* | *-er* | *-ir* |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**o** | com**o** | viv**o** |
| tú | habl**as** | com**es** | viv**es** |
| él · ella · usted | habl**a** | com**e** | viv**e** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**emos** | viv**imos** |
| vosotros | habl**áis** | com**éis** | viv**ís** |
| ellos · ustedes | habl**an** | com**en** | viv**en** |

**This is the present tense of the Spanish language.** Not a summary of it, not
the regular part of it with exceptions pending — every regular verb in Spanish
sits in that grid, and there are thousands of them.

Two things are worth reading off it before you close the chapter:

- **The *yo* row is the same in all three families.** It always was. That is why
  the second and third families cost so little when they arrived.
- ***-er* and *-ir* differ in exactly two boxes** — *nosotros* and *vosotros*.
  Four of their six forms are identical.

So the whole grid is really one set of endings with a family vowel, plus two
exceptions. That is the shape you have actually learned.
