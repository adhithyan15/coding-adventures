Lesson 1 of 5.
architectus, architectī — an architect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for freedom, then the Latin for glory.

You'll want to know: architectus, architectī.
architectus, architectī — "an architect".

The word, taken apart.
architectus was borrowed from Greek arkhitektōn, "a master builder" (Lewis & Short), from arkhi-, "chief", and tektōn, "builder" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
textor, textōris — a weaver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for glory, then the Latin for an architect.

You'll want to know: textor, textōris.
textor, textōris — "a weaver".

The word, taken apart.
textor is built on texō, "to weave" (Lewis & Short), with -tor, "one who does it". texō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European teks-, "to weave, to fashion"; English text and textile come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
colōnus, colōnī — a tenant farmer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a potter, then the Latin for a farm manager.

You'll want to know: colōnus, colōnī.
colōnus, colōnī — "a tenant farmer".

The word, taken apart.
colōnus is built on colō, "to till, to tend" (Lewis & Short): one who tills the land. colō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kwel-, "to turn, to move about" (EtymDB 2.1). English colony comes from the same family.
