## You'll want to know: die Jahreszeit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-JAHRESZEIT-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-JAHR-01, GE-LEX-ZEIT-01] -->

> **die Jahreszeit** — "season," and literally "**year-time**."

*Jahres-* is *Jahr* with the linking *-es-* German puts between the halves of a
compound, and *-zeit* is *Zeit*. Nothing is hidden. The plural is
*die Jahreszeiten*, and it is the title of this chapter.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-NATIVE-VS-LOAN-01, GE-LEX-JAHRESZEIT-01] -->

English does not do this. English has ***season***, from French *saison*, from
Latin *satio* — "the sowing." A borrowed word, opaque unless somebody explains
it, which is what the previous sentence had to do.

German assembled its word out of two it already owned, and the result explains
itself to anyone who has the halves. You had the halves two lessons ago.

That is the difference between the two halves of this chapter and the last one.
The months arrived as labels. The year, the time and the seasons were already
here, and German named them out of stock.
