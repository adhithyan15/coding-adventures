Marathi, chapter 435: Shortages.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
टंचाई (ṭañcāī) — a shortage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to rush about, then the Marathi for to find a way.

You'll want to know: टंचाई.
टंचाई — ṭañcāī — "a shortage".
उन्हाळ्यात गावात पाण्याची टंचाई असते. unhāḷyāt gāvāt pāṇyācī ṭañcāī aste. In summer the village is short of water.
Feminine.
The thing in short supply takes -ची: पाण्याची टंचाई (ṭañcāī).

The word, taken apart.
टंचाई (ṭañcāī) is a Marathi word for a shortage of water, food or money; its origin is not settled.

You'll want to know: तुटवडा.
तुटवडा — tuṭvaḍā — "a scarcity".
बाजारात कांद्याचा तुटवडा आहे. bājārāt kāndyācā tuṭvaḍā āhe. There is a scarcity of onions in the market.
Masculine.
Built on तुट-, the stem of तुटणे.

The word, taken apart.
तुटवडा (tuṭvaḍā) is built on the stem of तुटणे, "to break, to fall short" (CDIAL 6065), with a noun ending.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a shortage, then the Marathi for a scarcity.

You'll want to know: कोरडा.
कोरडा — korḍā — "parched; dry".
या वर्षी विहीर कोरडी पडली. yā varṣī vihīr korḍī paḍlī. This year the well went dry.
कोरडा (korḍā) पडणे, to go dry; कोरडी, कोरडं for the other genders.

The word, taken apart.
कोरडा (korḍā) is a Marathi adjective for land, wells or weather without water; its origin is not settled.

उन्हाळ्यात नदी आटली. unhāḷyāt nadī āṭlī. In summer the river dried up.
Past (he …): तो आटला, to āṭlā.
For water in a pot too: दूध आटलं, the milk boiled down.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 5 of 6.
नासणे (nāsṇe) — to go bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for parched, then the Marathi for to dry up.

You'll want to know: नासणे.
नासणे — nāsṇe — "to go bad".
उन्हामुळे दूध नासलं. unhāmuḷe dūdh nāslaṁ. The milk went bad in the heat.
Past (he …): तो नासला, to nāslā.
Milk, fruit and cooked food नासतात.

The word, taken apart.
नासणे (nāsṇe) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi nāsṇẽ, "to be spoilt", under Sanskrit naśyati, "is lost, perishes" (CDIAL 7027).
