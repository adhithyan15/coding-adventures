Gujarati, chapter 185: Haggling over a deal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ભાવતાલ કરવો (bhāvtāl karvo) — to haggle, to bargain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to measure, then the Gujarati for to ask for.

You'll want to know: ભાવતાલ કરવો.
ભાવતાલ કરવો — bhāvtāl karvo — "to haggle, to bargain".
તે બજારમાં ભાવતાલ કરે છે. — te bajārmā̃ bhāvtāl kare che — "She haggles in the market."
Past તેણે ભાવતાલ કર્યો, teṇe bhāvtāl karyo.

The word, taken apart.
ભાવતાલ કરવો (bhāvtāl karvo) is built from bhāv, "rate" (CDIAL 9475), paired with tāl, and the light verb karvũ, "to do". The origin of tāl is not settled: CDIAL has no entry for the pair.

Lesson 2 of 5.
સોદો (sodo) — deal, bargain.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to ask for, then the Gujarati for to haggle.

You'll want to know: સોદો.
સોદો — sodo — "deal, bargain".
આ સોદો સારો છે. — ā sodo sāro che — "This is a good deal."

The word, taken apart.
સોદો (sodo) is a loan, usually traced to Persian saudā, "trade, bargain". CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ઓછું (ochũ) — less, too little.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to haggle, then the Gujarati for deal.

You'll want to know: ઓછું.
ઓછું — ochũ — "less, too little".
આ દુકાનમાં ભાવ ઓછો છે. — ā dukānmā̃ bhāv ocho che — "The rate is lower in this shop."

The word, taken apart.
ઓછું (ochũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from ōccha, "small, thin, mean", a reconstructed form, and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "deficient, inferior" (CDIAL 2540).

Lesson 5 of 5.
પારખવું (pārakhvũ) — to test, to judge quality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for less, then the Gujarati for pair.

You'll want to know: પારખવું.
પારખવું — pārakhvũ — "to test, to judge quality".
ગ્રાહક કેરી પારખે છે. — grāhak kerī pārakhe che — "The customer tests the mangoes."

The word, taken apart.
પારખવું (pārakhvũ) comes from Sanskrit párīkṣatē, "observes, examines". CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb as parakhvũ and pārakhvũ, and marks the long-vowel form as taken from Marathi (CDIAL 7904).
