Japanese, chapter 436: Cheerful or Quiet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ほがらか (hogaraka) — cheerful; sunny-natured.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to smile gently, then the Japanese for to fall silent.

You'll want to know: ほがらか.
ほがらか — hogaraka — "cheerful; sunny-natured".
あねは いつも ほがらかです。 — ane wa itsumo hogaraka desu — "My elder sister is cheerful all the time."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ほがらか (hogaraka) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
むくち (mukuchi) — taciturn; of few words.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for cheerful, then the Japanese for bright.

You'll want to know: むくち.
むくち — mukuchi — "taciturn; of few words".
ちちは あさは むくちです。 — chichi wa asa wa mukuchi desu — "My father says little in the mornings."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
むくち (mukuchi) is a mixed word: Sino-Japanese mu, "nothing" (Middle Chinese mju), borrowed from Chinese, joined to native kuchi, "mouth", the kun'yomi of its second kanji (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ひとみしり (hitomishiri) — shy with strangers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for bright, then the Japanese for taciturn.

You'll want to know: ひとみしり.
ひとみしり — hitomishiri — "shy with strangers".
むすめは すこし ひとみしりです。 — musume wa sukoshi hitomishiri desu — "My daughter is a little shy with strangers."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ひとみしり (hitomishiri) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: hito, "person", mi, "seeing (miru)", and shiri, "knowing (shiru)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
