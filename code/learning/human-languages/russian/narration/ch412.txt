Russian, chapter 412: Pedestrians and cyclists.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
пешеход (peshekhód) — a pedestrian.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to drop off, then the Russian for to fasten one's seat belt.

You'll want to know: пешеход.
пешеход (peshekhód) — "a pedestrian".
Водитель остановился, и пешеход перешёл улицу. Vodítel ostanovílsya, i peshekhód pereshyól úlitsu. The driver stopped, and the pedestrian crossed the street.
Masculine; built on пешком — on foot.

The word, taken apart.
пешеход (peshekhód) is built from пеш- 'on foot', as in пешком, and ход 'a going', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic xodŭ 'motion' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
велосипедист (velosipedíst) — a cyclist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to fasten one's seat belt, then the Russian for a pedestrian.

You'll want to know: велосипедист.
велосипедист (velosipedíst) — "a cyclist".
В парке много велосипедистов. V párke mnógo velosipedístov. There are a lot of cyclists in the park.
Feminine велосипедистка.

The word, taken apart.
велосипедист (velosipedíst) is built on велосипед 'a bicycle' with the ending -ист. Велосипед was borrowed from French vélocipède, from Latin vēlōx 'swift' and pēs 'foot' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
уступать (ustupát) — to give way; to give up (a seat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pedestrian, then the Russian for a cyclist.

You'll want to know: уступать.
уступать (ustupát) — "to give way; to give up (a seat)".
В автобусе молодёжь уступает место пожилым. V avtóbuse molodyózh ustupáyet mésto pozhilým. On the bus young people give up their seats to the elderly.
Imperfective; perfective уступить (уступлю, уступишь). Takes the dative of the person: уступить дорогу пешеходу.

The word, taken apart.
уступать (ustupát) is built from у- 'away' and ступать 'to step': to step aside for someone. EtymDB lists ступать without a deeper source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
пропускать (propuskát) — to let through; to miss (a bus, a lesson).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cyclist, then the Russian for to give way.

You'll want to know: пропускать.
пропускать (propuskát) — "to let through; to miss (a bus, a lesson)".
Водитель останавливается и пропускает велосипедиста. Vodítel ostanávlivayetsya i propuskáyet velosipedísta. The driver stops and lets the cyclist through.
Imperfective; the perfective is пропустить (пропущу, пропустишь). Пропустить автобус — to miss the bus.

The word, taken apart.
пропускать (propuskát) is built from про- 'through' and пускать 'to let go, to allow' (EtymDB lists пускать with that sense).

Lesson 5 of 5.
разметка (razmétka) — road markings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to give way, then the Russian for to let through.

You'll want to know: разметка.
разметка (razmétka) — "road markings".
Зимой разметки на дороге почти не видно. Zimóy razmétki na doróge pochtí ne vídno. In winter the road markings are hard to make out.
Feminine and singular for all the lines: дорожная разметка (razmétka).

The word, taken apart.
разметка (razmétka) is built on разметить 'to mark out', from раз- and метить 'to mark', the root also found in замечать 'to notice'. EtymDB does not list the word.
