## You'll want to know: dǎtōng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C320-PHONE320-03]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎtōng* — "to get through on the phone".

> *dì'èr tiān wǒ cái dǎtōng tā de diànhuà* — I didn't get through to him until the next day.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎtōng* is built from 打 'to strike' and *tōng* 'to reach', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *thuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hit' and the second 'to reach'. Middle Chinese *taengX* would not regularly give dǎ: the modern vowel is irregular.
