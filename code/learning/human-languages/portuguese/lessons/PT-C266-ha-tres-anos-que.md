# há três anos que — how long, and still going

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-MAIS-DO-QUE-01, PT-GRAMMAR-HA-TIME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the train is faster than the bus". (*O comboio é mais rápido
do que o autocarro.*) Then "three years ago". (*Há três anos.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a length, and Portuguese measures it with that
same *há*.

## You'll want to know: há três anos que
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-HA-QUE-DURACAO-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-04, PT-GRAMMAR-MORAR-04, PT-LEX-PRETERITO-PERFEITO-02] -->

> **Há três anos que estudo** português. — I have been studying Portuguese for
> three years.
> **Estudo** português **há três anos.** — The same, the other way round.

Listen to the verb. English says *have been studying*; Portuguese says
**estudo**, the plain **present**, because the studying is still going on.

Now set it beside the *há* you learned for "ago". With an event in the past,
the same three words mean something else:

> *Fui a Lisboa há três anos.* — I went to Lisbon three years ago.

So the verb decides: with the present, it is still going; with the perfeito,
it happened that long ago.

## Grammar Lens: desde, and durante
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-DESDE-DURANTE-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C182-TEMPO182-03, PT-LEX-TER-HAVER-02] -->

**desde**, "since", which you have, names the starting point instead of the
length, and it takes the present for the same reason:

> **Moro** aqui **desde** janeiro. — I have lived here since January.

When the stretch has **ended**, the verb goes into the perfeito and the length
can take **durante**, "for, during":

> **Morei** em Lisboa **durante** dois anos. — I lived in Lisbon for two years.
> *(and then I left)*

One English word, *for*, splits in two. *Há … que* with the present: still
going on. *Durante* with the perfeito: over. In Brazil you will also hear
*faz três anos que* for the first.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**há** is a form of **haver**, inherited from Latin *habēre*, "to have"
(EtymDB 2.1), so *há três anos que estudo* is, word for word, "it has three
years that I study". **desde** is a compound of *des*, itself Latin *dē* plus
*ex*, and *de* (EtymDB 2.1): "from, out of, from". **durante** is inherited from
Latin *dūrāns*, "lasting" (EtymDB 2.1), built on *dūrus*, "hard": what lasts
is what holds hard.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-HA-QUE-DURACAO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-DESDE-DURANTE-02, PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which tense goes with *há três anos que*, and why? (The **present**
— it is still going on.) What does *há três anos* mean with the perfeito?
(**Three years ago.**) And which word takes over when the stretch is finished?
(***Durante***, with the perfeito.)
