Lesson 2 of 12.
き (ki) — さ, with a second bar.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say a station — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say that, a thing near the listener — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: write さ: the bar, the diagonal with its hook, the curve]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a barber — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
き — ki.
Four strokes, three lifts:
the upper bar, left to right.
the lower bar, left to right.
the long diagonal from the top, down to the right through both bars; at its foot, turn sharply and hook back to the left.
a separate curve: start at the left, drop down, and finish along the base to the right.
The diagonal, the hook and the curve are the ones your hand already makes in さ. Only the top differs:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: さ, sa. bars at the top: one.
you write: き, ki. bars at the top: two.
With え in front of it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: えき, eki, a station.

Guided Practice.
Trace き (ki) saying "bar, bar, diagonal and hook, curve".
Write さ and き (ki) side by side, and count the bars.
Write えき from memory and say eki.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.

[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
さ has one bar at the top. How many does き (ki) have?

Lesson 5 of 12.
け (ke) — は, without the loop.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say a pond — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: write は: the left stroke, the bar, the loop]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say correct — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a doctor — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
け — ke.
Three strokes, two lifts:
the left vertical, top to bottom; at the foot, turn and flick up to the right.
the bar, left to right.
the long vertical from the top, through the bar: run straight down, then sweep away to the lower left.
The first two strokes are the ones your hand already makes in は. Only the last stroke differs:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: は, ha. the last stroke ends: in a small loop.
you write: け, ke. the last stroke ends: in a sweep to the lower left.
With い in front of it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: いけ, ike, a pond.

Guided Practice.
Trace け (ke) saying "down and flick, bar, down and sweep".
Write は and け (ke) side by side; check that only the first one ties a loop.
Write いけ from memory and say ike.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart; stroke count and order per KanjiVG.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
は and け begin the same way. How does the last stroke of け end?

Script — the shape.
ぬ — nu.
Two strokes, one lift:
a short diagonal, from the upper left down to the right.
start at the top and cut down to the left across the first stroke; loop round the lower left, rise and run over the arch, come down the right side, then tie a small loop at the bottom and flick out to the right.
Up to the bottom of the right side, this is め. Only the ending differs:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you write: め, me. it ends with: an open curve.
you write: ぬ, nu. it ends with: a small closed loop and a flick.
With い in front of it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: いぬ, inu, a dog.

Guided Practice.
Trace ぬ (nu) saying "short stroke; down, arch, loop".
Write め and ぬ (nu) side by side; check that only the second one closes a loop.
Write いぬ from memory and say inu.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.

Lesson 11 of 12.
へ (he) — one stroke, one peak.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Four recalls, then the sign.
[your turn — recall: say a room — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say this morning — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: write ほ, and say the row it belongs to]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say square — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the shape.
へ — he.
One stroke, and the pen never lifts:
start low on the left and rise to a low peak.
turn over the peak and run down to the right.
The peak sits left of the middle, so the way down is longer than the way up. Keep the peak low and the whole sign wide.
On its own after a place word, へ is also a particle, and then it is read e, the way は is read wa as the topic marker. That use comes later.
With や after it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: へや, heya, a room.

Guided Practice.
Trace へ (he) saying "up, over, down".
Write the whole row は ひ ふ へ ほ, and say it.
Write へや from memory and say heya.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate form.
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart; stroke count and order per KanjiVG.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
How many times does the pen lift while you write へ?
