Gujarati, chapter 419: Streets and Lanes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
શેરી (śerī) — a street; a lane (in a town).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a water tank, then the Gujarati for a tenant.

You'll want to know: શેરી.
શેરી — śerī — "a street; a lane (in a town)".
અમારી શેરીમાં બધાં ઘર જૂનાં છે. amārī śerīmā̃ badhā̃ ghar jūnā̃ che. All the houses in our street are old.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
શેરી (śerī) No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

ફળિયું — phaḷiyũ — "a neighbourhood lane (and its houses)".
ફળિયાનાં બાળકો સાથે રમે છે. phaḷiyānā̃ bāḷko sāthe rame che. The children of the lane play together.

The word, taken apart.
ફળિયું (phaḷiyũ) is a lane together with the houses that open onto it, and the families in them. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
મહોલ્લો (mahollo) — a quarter of a town; a locality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a street, then the Gujarati for a neighbourhood lane.

You'll want to know: મહોલ્લો.
મહોલ્લો — mahollo — "a quarter of a town; a locality".
આ મહોલ્લામાં એક મોટું મંદિર છે. ā mahollāmā̃ ek moṭũ mandir che. There is a big temple in this quarter.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મહોલ્લો (mahollo) is usually traced to Arabic maḥalla, "a place where people settle; a quarter of a town", which came through Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
રહેવાસી (rahevāsī) — a resident; an inhabitant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a neighbourhood lane, then the Gujarati for a quarter of a town.

You'll want to know: રહેવાસી.
રહેવાસી — rahevāsī — "a resident; an inhabitant".
ગામના રહેવાસીઓએ નવો કૂવો ખોદ્યો. gāmnā rahevāsīoe navo kūvo khodyo. The villagers dug a new well.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
રહેવાસી (rahevāsī) is built from રહેવું, to live, and -વાસી, "a dweller", an ending that goes back to Sanskrit vāsa, "a dwelling" (Monier-Williams): the people who live in a place.
