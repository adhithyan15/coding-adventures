### Added — Malayalam chapter 94, the three day words, one of which the learner already owned

- `ML-A1-TIME-05` closes. Malayalam A1 coverage 200/243 -> **201/243**, with 42
  points unmapped. **Only two words are new.**
- **innu** (today) and **innale** (yesterday) have **zero** occurrences across
  every Malayalam lesson file, so both are clean introductions.
- **naale is not, and the difference decided the chapter's shape.** It appears in
  FIVE lessons -- `ML-C04-naale-kaanaam`'s phrase headword, `ML-C23-naal` (which
  even glosses it as "tomorrow" in passing), `ML-C32-kaanuka`,
  `ML-S01-letter-ka` and `ML-S04-vowel-sign-aa` -- and is owned by **none** of
  them, because `ML-C04`'s headword is the multi-word *naale kaanaam* and
  `continuity.ts` keeps a multi-word headword WHOLE.
- Claiming *naale* as a headword here would have converted that invisible debt
  into **five forward references reaching back to sequence 240**. So
  `ML-C94-three-days` is a **grammar** lesson about the word leaving the
  farewell, not a vocabulary lesson about the word.
- That is the honest description as well as the safe one. The learner has had
  *naale* since the first week and could not use it anywhere except to end a
  conversation: a learner who had said that goodbye a hundred times still could
  not say *tomorrow I will go*.
- `ML-C94-innu` puts the time word in front and names what it changes -- every
  sentence in the book so far has floated free of any particular day, and the
  listener had to guess from the room.
- `ML-C94-innale` shows the shared opening of the two words and **deliberately
  does not take the tail apart**. The shared front is what keeps the pair apart
  from *tomorrow*, which begins somewhere else; no story is forced onto the rest.
- `ML-C94-three-days` sets the three day words against the three verb endings
  already taught -- *innale njaan poyi* / *innu njaan pokunnu* / *naale njaan
  pokum*. The middle of every line is identical. The lesson says plainly that the
  **verb** carries the tense and the day word only says **which day**; neither
  does the other's work.
- The recall names the transferable habit: **a phrase you learned whole may be
  holding a word you can use apart from it.** When one detaches, you gain a word
  without learning one.
- The tenses were built chapters ago and had nowhere to land. Every sentence the
  learner owns can now be dated.
