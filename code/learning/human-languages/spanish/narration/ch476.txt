Spanish, chapter 476: En clase.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el alumno — the pupil, the student.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the material, then the Spanish for the gardener.

You'll want to know: el alumno.
el alumno — "the pupil, the student".
Cada alumno tiene un libro. Each pupil has a book.
A girl or woman is la alumna.

The word, taken apart.
alumno from Latin alumnus, "a foster child, a pupil" (EtymDB 2.1), from alere, "to feed, to bring up" — a learned borrowing: worn down in speech, its -mn- would have changed, as damnum became daño, "harm".

Practicar un deporte — to play a sport regularly.

The word, taken apart.
practicar is built from práctica, "practice", from Late Latin practicus, "active", taken from Greek praktikos, "fit for doing", built on prassein, "to do".

Lesson 3 of 5.
comprender — to understand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pupil, then the Spanish for to practise.

You'll want to know: comprender.
comprender — "to understand".
No comprendo la pregunta. I do not understand the question.
A close twin of entender, slightly more formal.

The word, taken apart.
comprender from Latin comprehendere, "to grasp, to seize" (Lewis & Short): com-, "together", and prehendere, "to take hold of". To understand is to get a grip on a thing.

You'll want to know: el error.
el error — "the mistake, the error".
Hay un error en la factura. There is a mistake on the bill.
Por error — by mistake.

The word, taken apart.
error from Latin error, "a wandering, a going astray" (EtymDB 2.1), from errare, "to wander" — taken from Latin unchanged. A mistake is a step off the path.

Lesson 5 of 5.
aburrido — bored; boring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to understand, then the Spanish for the mistake.

You'll want to know: aburrido.
aburrido — "bored; boring".
La película es aburrida. The film is boring.
With estar it means bored (estoy aburrido); with ser it means boring (es aburrido).

The word, taken apart.
aburrido is the participle of aburrir, "to bore", from Latin abhorrere, "to shrink back from, to shudder at" (Lewis & Short) — inherited. With the ending -ecer, the same Latin verb also gave aborrecer, "to loathe".
