Portuguese, chapter 403: Vidros e espelhos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o vidro — the glass (material); the car window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the clutch, then the Portuguese for to put into gear.

You'll want to know: o vidro.
o vidro — "the glass (material); the car window".
Ela abre o vidro do carro porque está calor. She opens the car window because it is hot.
Uma garrafa de vidro — a glass bottle. A drinking glass is um copo.

The word, taken apart.
vidro is inherited from Latin vitrum 'glass' (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o retrovisor — the rear-view mirror, the wing mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to put into gear, then the Portuguese for the glass.

You'll want to know: o retrovisor.
o retrovisor — "the rear-view mirror, the wing mirror".
Antes de mudar de faixa, ele olha para o retrovisor. Before changing lanes, he checks the mirror.
O espelho retrovisor in full.

The word, taken apart.
retrovisor is a learned formation from retro- 'backwards' and visor 'viewer', built on Latin vidēre 'to see' (Lewis & Short): the mirror that sees behind.

Lesson 3 of 5.
a buzina — the horn (of a car).

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a buzina.
a buzina — "the horn (of a car)".
O motorista do táxi tocou a buzina duas vezes. The taxi driver sounded the horn twice.
Tocar a buzina — to sound the horn.

The word, taken apart.
buzina is inherited from Latin būcina 'curved trumpet, horn' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the Portuguese word without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
embaciar — to mist up, to steam up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the horn, then the Portuguese for to honk.

You'll want to know: embaciar.
embaciar — "to mist up, to steam up".
Com o frio, os vidros embaciam por dentro. In the cold, the windows mist up on the inside.
Os vidros estão embaciados — the windows are misted up.

The word, taken apart.
embaciar is built from em- and baço 'dull, without shine'. Baço is inherited from Latin badius 'reddish-brown' (EtymDB).
