## You'll want to know: nympha, nymphae
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C347-MONSTRUM-04]; assesses=[] -->

**nympha, nymphae** — "a nymph".

> Nymphae in silvīs et fontibus habitābant.
>
> *Nymphs lived in the woods and springs.*

Feminine, a Greek loan; also 'a bride, a young woman'. Spirits of springs, trees and mountains.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**nympha** was borrowed from Greek *nymphē*, "bride, nymph" (Lewis & Short). English *nymph* comes from it.
