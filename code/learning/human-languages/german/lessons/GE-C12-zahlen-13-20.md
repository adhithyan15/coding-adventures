# Practice — thirteen to twenty

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ELF-01, GE-LEX-ZWOELF-01, GE-LEX-DREIZEHN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] No new words. One rule, run eight times, and two small wearings-down
along the way.

## Grammar Lens: the rule and its two exceptions that are not exceptions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ZIFFER-PLUS-ZEHN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ZAHL-KLIPPUNG-01, GE-LEX-SECHZEHN-01, GE-LEX-SIEBZEHN-01] -->

| number | digit | joined |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | *drei* | *dreizehn* |
| 14 | *vier* | *vierzehn* |
| 15 | *fünf* | *fünfzehn* |
| 16 | *sech-* | *sechzehn* |
| 17 | *sieb-* | *siebzehn* |
| 18 | *acht* | *achtzehn* |
| 19 | *neun* | *neunzehn* |

Six digits go in whole and two are trimmed at the join. The rule never changes.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-ZWANZIG-ZWEI-ZEHN-01, GE-LEX-ZWANZIG-01, GE-LEX-ZEHN-01, GE-LEX-ELF-01] -->

Eleven to twenty is three ideas and you now hold all three: two **leftovers**,
seven **digit-plus-ten** compounds, and one **two-tens**.

Here is the contrast worth carrying. The Romance sisters all **break** partway
through the teens and switch to a different construction — Portuguese at
sixteen, French and Italian at seventeen. German does not break at all. Two
leftovers, then one clean rule to twenty, and English marches beside it the whole
distance because it is doing the same thing with the same worn-down words.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ZIFFER-PLUS-ZEHN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ZAHL-KLIPPUNG-01, GE-ETYMON-ZWANZIG-ZWEI-ZEHN-01, GE-LEX-ZWANZIG-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What is the rule from thirteen up? (**A digit, then** *zehn*.) Which
two digits are trimmed? (***Sechs*** **and** ***sieben***.) Does the rule ever
break? (**No**.) What is *zwanzig* underneath? (**Two tens**.) And where do the
Romance languages break? (**Partway through the teens** — sixteen or seventeen.)
