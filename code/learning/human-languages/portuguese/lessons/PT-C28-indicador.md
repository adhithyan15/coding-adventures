## You'll want to know: The written form
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-INDICADOR-04]; assesses=[] -->

> **1.º** — *primeiro*. **1.ª** — *primeira*. **2.º**, **3.ª**, **10.º**.
> **o 1.º andar** — the first floor. **a 2.ª vez** — the second time.

A lift panel, a doorplate and a date do not spell the word out. They write the
digit and put a **raised letter** after it, and that letter is the last letter of
the word you would have said: **o** for *primeiro*, **a** for *primeira*.

## What you've built: the mark agrees, because it is a letter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-NOTICE-C28-ORDINAIS-04]; assesses=[] -->

This is why the mark comes in two shapes rather than one. It is not a symbol for
"ordinal"; it is the tail of the word, left standing above the line. So it
changes exactly when the word changes:

| written | said | means |
|---|---|---|
| **o 3.º andar** | *o terceiro andar* | the third floor |
| **a 5.ª vez** | *a quinta vez* | the fifth time |

Read the panel in a Lisbon lift now — **1.º**, **2.º**, **3.º** — and you are
reading this chapter and the last one aloud. Ten ordinals, five of them already
in your week before you met them, and a written form that tells you which one to
say.
