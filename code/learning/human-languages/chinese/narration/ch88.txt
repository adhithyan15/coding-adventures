Lesson 1 of 8.
两 (liǎng) — two of something.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: 两.
两 — liǎng — "two (of something)".
两个人 liǎng ge rén — two people.
The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
两 (liǎng) is inherited from Middle Chinese ljangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where it names a weight and, quoting an older dictionary, 'twice'. 'Two of a thing' grew from those senses by a step the Guangyun does not show.

What you've built.
Counting aloud, two is 二: yī, èr, sān. In front of a measure word it is 两: 两个人 (liǎng ge rén), two people. èr ge rén is not said.

Script — a bar, a frame, two small 人.
两 (liǎng).
A bar on top, then a frame, then what the frame holds.
The top bar, one stroke, across.
The frame, two strokes: a short vertical on the left, then a horizontal that turns down the right side and hooks to the left.
Two small 人, four strokes, the left one first: each a left-falling stroke and a short stroke falling to the right, rising through the top of the frame.
1 + 2 + 4 = seven.

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 3 of 8.
yī becomes yí ge, yì tiān: the tone 一 takes — 一 changes its tone with the syllable after it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the measure words, the sound that changes in front of them.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 两个人, aloud — liǎng ge rén]
[your turn — recall: say 一百 — yìbǎi, a hundred]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 一起 — yìqǐ, together]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Sounds you'll need — 一 moves.
一 has three spoken tones, and the next syllable picks one.
Counting, and inside numbers and dates, it stays level: yī, èr, sān; 星期一 xīngqīyī.
Before a fourth tone it rises: 一个 yí ge. 个 is fourth tone, even when it is said light.
Before the other three tones it falls: 一天 yì tiān, 一年 yì nián, 一百 yìbǎi.
This book writes the tone you say, as it writes bú shì for 不是.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 一年 — yì nián — falling, before a second tone]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 一百 — yìbǎi — falling, before a third tone]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

You'll want to know: bēi.
bēi — "a cup of; a glass of", a measure word.
liǎng bēi chá — two cups of tea.
yì bēi shuǐ — a glass of water.
Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bēi is inherited from Middle Chinese pwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed through an older book as a cup. It is the bēi of bēizi; as a measure word it counts what a cup holds.

What you've built.
A measure word counts by the container: liǎng bēi chá, two cups' worth. 一 before its level tone falls: yì bēi.

The word, taken apart.
běn is inherited from Middle Chinese pwonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the root; the beginning'. Counting books with it is a later use the Guangyun does not record.

What you've built.
个 counts almost anything; běn is the word a speaker reaches for with books. 一 before its dip falls: yì běn.

zhī — a measure word for animals, and for one of a pair.

The word, taken apart.
zhī is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'one'; the Guangyun, quoting an older dictionary, describes the old character as a hand holding a single bird.

What you've built.
Cats, dogs, birds: animals take zhī. So does one of a pair, such as a hand: yì zhī shǒu.

Lesson 8 of 8.
两个人 (liǎng ge rén) — counting people, cups, books, tables and cats.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The chapter closes here, with two people looking for a table.
[your turn — recall: say liǎng zhī māo, two cats — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say shàngbian, on top — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say jǐ, how many — R4, recognition at distance]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange.
A: nǐmen jǐ ge rén — How many of you are there? B: 两个人 liǎng ge rén — Two. A: 你们喝什么 nǐmen hē shénme — What will you drink? B: liǎng bēi chá — Two cups of tea. A: hǎo, nà zhāng zhuōzi — Fine. That table, then. B: nàr yǒu liǎng zhī māo — There are two cats there. A: zhuōzi shàngbian yǒu yì běn shū — And there's a book on the table.
Every count has its measure word: 个 for people, bēi for tea, zhāng for the table, zhī for the cats, běn for the book. And every two is 两 (liǎng).

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
How many cups of tea does B ask for?

What you've built — a number, a measure word, a thing.
Every count has three parts, in this order: the number, the measure word, the thing.
两个人 liǎng ge rén — two people.
yì bēi shuǐ — a glass of water.
yì běn shū — a book.
liǎng zhāng piào — two tickets.
liǎng zhī māo — two cats.
Listen. Nǐ yǒu jǐ běn shū?
Speak. Count what is on your table, with the right measure word for each.
