Lesson 1 of 5.
उल्टी (ulṭī) — vomiting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a husband's sister, then the Hindi for a sister's husband.

You'll want to know: उल्टी.
उल्टी — ulṭī — "vomiting".

The word, taken apart.
उल्टी (ulṭī) is "an overturning", from ulaṭnā, "to be overturned", which CDIAL lists under a reconstructed ullaṭati, "turns over" (CDIAL 2368).

Lesson 2 of 5.
घाव (ghāv) — a wound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a sister's husband, then the Hindi for vomiting.

You'll want to know: घाव.
घाव — ghāv — "a wound".

The word, taken apart.
घाव (ghāv) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghāta, "killing; blow, bruise" (CDIAL 4460). CDIAL lists the Hindi forms as ghāu and ghāy.

Lesson 4 of 5.
टीका (ṭīkā) — an injection, a vaccination.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a wound, then the Hindi for a bandage.

You'll want to know: टीका.
टीका — ṭīkā — "an injection, a vaccination".

The word, taken apart.
टीका (ṭīkā) comes from a reconstructed ṭikka, "mark, spot", and CDIAL glosses the Hindi word "caste-mark" (CDIAL 5458). Hindi now also uses it for an injection.
