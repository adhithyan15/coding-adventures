# (reading) — six words, nothing said first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-TE-HAND] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have written every sign in every one of these. Not one word is
new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them before you read them.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[JA-LEX-MIMI, JA-LEX-KUCHI] -->

> て
> みみ
> くち
> はな
> かお
> め

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once. Do not sound out each sign — look at the
whole word and let it arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to build.
Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-HANA-NOSE, JA-LEX-KAO, JA-LEX-ME-EYE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two of these are one sign long. **て** and **め** are whole words,
and you have written both as signs on their own.

[PAUSE 2s] That is worth noticing, because it is the thing hiragana does that
an alphabet does not: a sign can be a letter and a word at the same time, and
nothing on the page tells you which it is. Only knowing the word does.

[PAUSE 2s] Six is enough for one sitting.
