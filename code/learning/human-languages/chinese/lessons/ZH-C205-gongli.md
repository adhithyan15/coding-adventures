## You'll want to know: 公里
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C205-FAR205-02]; assesses=[] -->

**公里** — *gōnglǐ* — "kilometre".

> **书店在东边离这儿两公里** *shūdiàn zài dōngbian lí zhèr liǎng gōnglǐ* — The bookshop is to the east, two kilometres from here.

The new character, in pieces: 公 — four strokes: 八 on top, then a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**公里** (*gōnglǐ*) is built from 公 'common, public' and 里 *lǐ*. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese *kuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'common' and 'without bias'; the dictionary glosses 里 as a neighbourhood of twenty-five households. Its use as a measure of distance, and the pair's use for the kilometre, are not given there, so they are not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for asking where things are.
