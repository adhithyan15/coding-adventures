## Sixteen more filmstrips lift the pen where native writers do

- The filmstrips for త (TE-S01), న (TE-S109), ప (TE-S113), య (TE-S110),
  ర (TE-S06), ల (TE-S114), వ (TE-S117), శ (TE-S159), ష (TE-S158),
  హ (TE-S125), ఠ (TE-S155), జ (TE-S127), చ (TE-S112), అ (TE-S162),
  ఎ (TE-S124) and ఒ (TE-S165) lifted the pen after numbered or ordered
  movements of their tracing source. They now lift only where native
  writers do: త, న, ర, ల, వ, శ, చ, అ, ఎ and ఒ in one unbroken stroke, ప,
  హ, ఠ and జ in two, and య in three. In HP Labs India's native-writer
  samples, 51% to 99% of writers use those counts; for య, three strokes is
  the most common count (48%). ష now takes three strokes, not four: its
  short tail leaves the body partway up, after the source has finished the
  body, so it stays apart (27% of writers use three; 69% use two).
- The connected letters now climb up the flourish's or chevron's left arm
  before drawing it, so those filmstrips show one more frame. Every
  movement and its direction is still shown, frame by frame.
- ఆ, ఇ, ఏ and ఝ keep their lifts for now: joining their parts would mean
  reversing a movement, skipping ink the source draws, or retracing.
