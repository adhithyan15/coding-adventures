Italian, chapter 392: In tribunale.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'imputato — the defendant, the accused.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the criminal complaint, then the Italian for the corruption.

You'll want to know: l'imputato.
l'imputato — "the defendant, the accused".
L'imputato dice di essere innocente. The defendant says he is innocent.
A woman is l'imputata.

The word, taken apart.
imputato is the past participle of imputare "to charge with", a learned borrowing from Latin imputāre "to reckon in, to charge to" (Lewis & Short), built on putāre "to reckon".

In sport, the defence too. Per legittima difesa — in self-defence.

difesa is inherited from Late Latin dēfēnsa "a defending" (EtymDB), from dēfendere "to ward off, to defend".

Sputare sentenze — to lay down the law, to pontificate.

sentenza is a learned borrowing from Latin sententia "an opinion, a judgement, a maxim" (Lewis & Short), from sentīre "to feel, to think".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la pena — the penalty, the punishment; the pity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the verdict, then the Italian for the conviction.

You'll want to know: la pena.
la pena — "the penalty, the punishment; the pity".
Per questo reato la pena è molto alta. For this crime the penalty is very high.
Vale la pena — it is worth it. Mi fa pena — I feel sorry for him.

The word, taken apart.
pena is inherited from Latin poena "penalty, punishment" (Lewis & Short), an early borrowing from Greek poinḗ "blood money, a fine".
