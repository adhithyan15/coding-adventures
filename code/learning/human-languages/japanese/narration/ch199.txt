Japanese, chapter 199: Departures, Arrivals and Expresses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しゅっぱつ (shuppatsu) — departure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a subway, then the Japanese for to get to.

You'll want to know: しゅっぱつ.
しゅっぱつ — shuppatsu — "departure".
しゅっぱつは ごごです。 — shuppatsu wa gogo desu — "Departure is in the afternoon."

The word, taken apart.
しゅっぱつ (shuppatsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shutsu, "to go out" (Middle Chinese tsyhwit) and hatsu, "to set off" (pjot) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as shup-patsu.

Lesson 4 of 5.
とまる (tomaru) — to come to a stop; to stop moving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for arrival, then the Japanese for a limited express.

You'll want to know: とまる.
とまる — tomaru — "to come to a stop; to stop moving".
でんしゃが えきに とまります。 — densha ga eki ni tomarimasu — "The train stops at the station."
Polite とまります, tomarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
とまる (tomaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
