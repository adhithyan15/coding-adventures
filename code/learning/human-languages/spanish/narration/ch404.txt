Spanish, chapter 404: Wi-Fi, Mobile Phones and Films.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
apagar — make the active thing quiet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the telephone. (El teléfono.)

Sounds you'll need.
a-pa-GAR: keep all three a sounds clear and stress the final syllable.

The word, taken apart.
apagar — “to turn off” a device or light, and “to put out” a fire.
It continues Latin pacāre, “to calm or pacify.” The meaning moved from making something quiet to stopping its light, fire or activity. In the cinema, apagar el teléfono means “to turn off the phone.”

[your turn — say: apagar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: apagar el teléfono]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: por favor, apagar el teléfono]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say to turn off. (Apagar.)
Source: DLE: apagar.


Lesson 2 of 6.
el wifi — the connection with a deliberately catchy name.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say internet. (Internet.)

Sounds you'll need.
In much of the Spanish-speaking world you will hear WEE-fee. El wifi is widely used; la wifi also occurs in parts of the Spanish-speaking world.

The word, taken apart.
el wifi — “the Wi-Fi” or “Wi-Fi.”
The name was coined to sound like Hi-Fi. The phrase Wireless Fidelity was attached afterward as marketing by the industry alliance and was later dropped. That history is why treating the phrase as the name's simple original expansion is misleading, while pretending the Alliance never used it also goes too far.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Sources: DLE: wifi and Phil Belanger's account of the naming.


Lesson 3 of 6.
el móvil — the phone named for moving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the telephone and Wi-Fi. (El teléfono, el wifi.)

Sounds you'll need.
MO-vil: the written accent keeps the stress on mó.

The word, taken apart.
el móvil — “the mobile phone,” especially in Spain. Across much of Latin America, el celular is more usual.
Latin mōbilis meant “movable.” Spanish teléfono móvil is literally a mobile telephone; el móvil leaves the noun unstated because the movable device is understood.

Lesson 4 of 6.
la película — from a thin skin to a film.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the cinema and to begin. (El cine, empezar.)

Sounds you'll need.
pe-LEE-ku-la: the written accent keeps the stress on lí.

The word, taken apart.
la película — “the film” or “the movie.”
Latin pellicula meant a little skin, a diminutive of pellis, “skin.” A thin photographic layer, and then the strip coated with it, carried the word into cinema. The material's name became the movie's name.
