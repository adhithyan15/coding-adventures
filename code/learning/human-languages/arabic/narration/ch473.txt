Arabic, chapter 473: Desert and jungle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
واحة (wāḥa) — an oasis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a slope, then the Arabic for an edge.

You'll want to know: واحة.
واحة — wāḥa — "an oasis".
نزلنا في واحة صغيرة في الصحراء. nazalnā fī wāḥa ṣaghīra fī aṣ-ṣaḥrāʾ. We stopped at a small oasis in the desert.

The word, taken apart.
واحة (wāḥa) was borrowed from Coptic, which goes back to ancient Egyptian (EtymDB); English oasis comes from the same word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
سراب (sarāb) — a mirage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an edge, then the Arabic for an oasis.

You'll want to know: سراب.
سراب — sarāb — "a mirage".
رأى المسافر سرابا في الصحراء. raʾā al-musāfir sarāban fī aṣ-ṣaḥrāʾ. The traveller saw a mirage in the desert.

The word, taken apart.
سراب (sarāb) is built on the native Arabic root s-r-b, "to flow; to go away" (Lane). A mirage shimmers like flowing water and then is gone.

أدغال — adghāl — "a jungle; thick undergrowth".
تعيش النمور في الأدغال. taʿīshu an-numūr fī al-adghāl. Tigers live in the jungle.

The word, taken apart.
أدغال (adghāl) is usually traced to the native Arabic root d-gh-l, "a thicket". It is a plural in form.

يعيش الضفدع في المستنقع. yaʿīshu aḍ-ḍifdaʿ fī al-mustanqaʿ. The frog lives in the swamp.

The word, taken apart.
مستنقع (mustanqaʿ) is built on the native Arabic root n-q-ʿ, "standing water" (Lane). It is the place-noun of Form X: where water stands.
