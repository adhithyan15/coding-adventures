# The same writing, with a clock

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C41-SCHOOL41-03, JA-LEX-C54-TIME54-05, JA-LEX-C112-THINGS112-04, JA-LEX-C94-LIKE94-03, JA-LEX-ANCHOR-KAZOKU, JA-LEX-C25-FAMILY25-01, JA-SCRIPT-SMALL-YU-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What the real exam publishes, and what it does not.** The JLPT, the
receptive test this track anchors to, has **no writing paper** at any level.
It tests vocabulary and grammar, reading and listening, and nothing a
candidate writes is scored. So there is no official A1 writing clock to
rehearse against, and this lesson does not pretend there is one.

The task comes instead from the JF Standard's A1 Can-dos, which follow the
CEFR: complete a small form, and write a short personal message. This
track's assessment contract sets that message at **30 to 40 signs**, for a
reader you name, such as a **ともだち**. Japanese has no spaces to count words
by, so length is counted in signs: every kana counts as one, small **っ** and
**ゅ** included. Under thirty and the message is not done; well past forty and
you spent minutes the task did not ask for.

[PAUSE 2s] Today you rehearse the form only, against **this book's own
clock**, not a published one. Set a **three-minute timer**, then fill five
fields: **なまえ**, **とし**, **しごと**, **しゅみ**, **かぞく**. Fill every one.
If a field needs a word this book has not given you, write a number or the
nearest word you own; never look one up.

[PAUSE 2s] Speed is where stroke order shows. A sign written in the order
you learned keeps its shape when you hurry; one written out of order is the
one that falls apart. When the three-minute timer rings, **stop**, even
mid-sign.

[PAUSE 2s] Spend at most one minute checking, and do not rewrite the timed
answer. Did you write **しゅみ** with a small **ゅ**? Under a clock, that is
the sign a hurried hand writes full size.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson. JLPT N5 passes
on **80 of 180** overall, and a candidate also needs **38 of 120** on
vocabulary, grammar and reading together and **19 of 60** on listening.
Below either, the candidate fails, whatever the total says.

[PAUSE 2s] Writing appears nowhere in those numbers. A JLPT pass says
nothing about what you just did, which is why this book scores writing as
**its own paper**: 60 of 100, never averaged against the others.
