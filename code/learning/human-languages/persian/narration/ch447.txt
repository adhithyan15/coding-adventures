Persian, chapter 447: Corners and house numbers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نبش (nabsh) — a (street) corner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a distance, then the Persian for to move away.

You'll want to know: نبش.
نبش (nabsh) — "a (street) corner".
نانوایی در نبش خیابان است. Nânvâyi dar nabsh-e khiyâbân ast. The bakery is on the corner of the street.
Used in addresses: nabsh-e kuche-ye dovvom — on the corner of the second lane.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of nabsh is not settled, and EtymDB does not list it. It is the corner where a lane meets a street, and it appears in many Iranian addresses.

Lesson 2 of 5.
پلاک (pelâk) — a house number; a number plate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to move away, then the Persian for a.

You'll want to know: پلاک.
پلاک (pelâk) — "a house number; a number plate".
خانه ما پلاک دوازده است. Khâne-ye mâ pelâk-e davâzdah ast. Our house is number twelve.
Pelâk-e mâshin — a car's number plate.

The word, taken apart.
pelâk was borrowed from French plaque 'a plate, a sheet of metal' (EtymDB): the small metal plate with a house number on it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
کدپستی (kodposti) — a postcode (US: a zip code).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a, then the Persian for a house number.

You'll want to know: کدپستی.
کدپستی (kodposti) — "a postcode (US: a zip code)".
کدپستی (kodposti) را روی پاکت نوشتم. Kodposti râ ru-ye pâkat neveshtam. I wrote the postcode on the envelope.
Iranian postcodes have ten digits.

The word, taken apart.
kodposti is built from two French loans: kod 'code', usually traced to French code, and posti 'postal', from post, usually traced to French poste.

Lesson 4 of 5.
اصلی (asli) — main, principal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a house number, then the Persian for a postcode.

You'll want to know: اصلی.
اصلی (asli) — "main, principal".
این خیابان اصلی شهر است. In khiyâbân-e asli-ye shahr ast. This is the main street of the town.
Khiyâbân-e asli — a main road.

The word, taken apart.
asli was borrowed from Arabic aṣlī (EtymDB), an adjective built on aṣl 'origin, root'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
فرعی (far'i) — side, minor (of a road); secondary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a postcode, then the Persian for main.

You'll want to know: فرعی.
فرعی (far'i) — "side, minor (of a road); secondary".
از خیابان اصلی وارد یک خیابان فرعی شدیم. Az khiyâbân-e asli vâred-e yek khiyâbân-e far'i shodim. From the main road we turned into a side street.
The opposite of asli.

The word, taken apart.
far'i was borrowed from Arabic and is usually traced to Arabic farʿī 'of a branch', from farʿ 'branch'. EtymDB does not list it.
