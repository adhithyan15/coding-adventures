# schw- — three letters, two sounds

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-BRUDER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One sound cluster before the next word, because the word is unreadable
without it.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-SOUND-SCHW-01]; assesses=[] -->

- `sch-sh` — **sch** is **one** sound, and it is the *sh* of English *ship*. Not
  *s-k-h*, not *sk*. Three letters, one consonant.
- `w-as-v` — and German *w* is a **v**, which you have from *Winter*.
- `schw-cluster` — put them together and *schw-* comes out **shv-**.

English can say *sh* and English can say *v*, but it never puts them side by
side at the start of a word. Say *shhh*, hold it, then let it fall straight into
*v* without a vowel between: *shv*.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-SCHW-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: the first sound alone — "sh"]
- [YOU SAY: the second alone — "v"]
- [YOU SAY: the two joined, no vowel — "shv"]
- [YOU SAY: it again, slower — "shv"]

[REPEAT x2] "shv."

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-SCHW-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] How many sounds is *sch*? (**One** — English *sh*.) How is German *w*
said? (**As a** *v*.) So what does *schw-* sound like? (***Shv-***.) Is there a
vowel between them? (**No**.) Next: the word that starts with it.
