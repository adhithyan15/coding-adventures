Japanese, chapter 415: Caught in a Shower.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ゆうだち (yūdachi) — a sudden evening shower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for shade, then the Japanese for dazzling.

You'll want to know: ゆうだち.
ゆうだち — yūdachi — "a sudden evening shower".
なつの ごごに ゆうだちが ふりました。 — natsu no gogo ni yūdachi ga furimashita — "A sudden shower fell on the summer afternoon."

The word, taken apart.
ゆうだち (yūdachi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from yū, "evening" and dachi, "rising" (from tatsu); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
びっしょり (bisshori) — soaking wet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sudden evening shower, then the Japanese for light rain.

You'll want to know: びっしょり.
びっしょり — bisshori — "soaking wet".
あめで ふくが びっしょり ぬれました。 — ame de fuku ga bisshori nuremashita — "My clothes got soaking wet in the rain."

The word, taken apart.
びっしょり (bisshori) is a native mimetic word that pictures being drenched right through: an imitative Japanese form, inherited rather than borrowed, whose older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ぬらす (nurasu) — to get something wet; to wet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for light rain, then the Japanese for soaking wet.

You'll want to know: ぬらす.
ぬらす — nurasu — "to get something wet; to wet".
あめで くつを ぬらしました。 — ame de kutsu o nurashimashita — "I got my shoes wet in the rain."
Polite ぬらします, nurashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもが ふくを ぬらしました。, kodomo ga fuku o nurashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぬらす (nurasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the partner of nureru, "to get wet" (JMdict).
