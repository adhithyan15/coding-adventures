# interesar — what lies between you and a thing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-INTERESANTE, ES-LEX-ESTUDIAR, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **interesante** and **estudiar**. Say **doler**. You can call a
class interesting. Now say that it interests *you*.

## You'll want to know: interesar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-INTERESANTE] -->

**interesar** — to interest, to be of interest to somebody.

*Me interesa la clase* — the class interests me, I am interested in it. *No me
interesa* — I am not interested, which is the ordinary way to decline an offer.
**interesarse por** is to take an interest in: *se interesó por mi salud*.

You already had **interesante**. This is the verb the adjective was made from,
so the only new thing is what it does in a sentence.

## Grammar Lens: the same machine again
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01] -->

*Me interesa el curso* has the same shape as *me duele la cabeza*.

| Spanish | the subject | the person |
|---|---|---|
| me duele **la cabeza** | la cabeza | me |
| me interesa **la clase** | la clase | me |

The verb agrees with the thing, not with you — *me interesan **las clases***,
plural. Once you have seen the pattern three times (*gustar*, *doler*,
*interesar*) it stops being a special case and becomes what this whole family of
verbs does.

English turns them the other way round and needs an adjective to do it: *I am
interested*. Spanish keeps the thing in charge.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02] -->

Latin **inter esse** — **inter**, between, plus **esse**, to be. To *interest*
somebody was for a thing **to be between** them and something else: to be
involved, to be at stake.

That older sense is still in **el interés** on a loan, which is what is at stake
in the deal, and in English **interested party** — not a party that is curious,
but one with something at stake.

So *interesante* does not mean "makes me curious" at root. It means **standing
between**, involved, concerned. The curiosity sense came later and took over.
