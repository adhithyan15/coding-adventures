## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-HEN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**很** is *hěn* — **third tone**, the dip, the same as 好.

Its pieces, in words: on the left, the person piece from 你 with one more
left-falling stroke above it; on the right, a piece read *gěn*. The next lesson
puts it in your hand.

## Grammar Lens: no 是, and a plain 很 instead
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-HEN-01, ZH-LEX-HAO] -->

> **我很好** *wǒ hěn hǎo* — I'm fine.

In Mandarin an adjective is the whole predicate: there is no 是 in "I am fine",
and a plain 很 stands in front of 好 instead. Here 很 has lost most of its
*very*, so 我很好 is "I'm fine", while a bare 我好 sounds like the start of a
contrast — I am fine, but somebody else is not.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**很** is an old character: the Guangyun records it as *honX* (in Baxter's
transcription), glossed "defiant". How that word came to mean "very" is not
settled.
