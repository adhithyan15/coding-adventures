# তুমি কেমন আছো? (tumi kēmon āchho?) — "how are you?"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-KEMON-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-TUMIAPNI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The everyday "how are you?" — and the first time Bengali brings back a
verb "to be."

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-KEMON-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-TUMIAPNI-01] -->

The new word is **আছো** (*āchho*, "are you"), from the verb **আছা** (*āchhā*, "to
be, to exist, to have") + the familiar-you ending **-ও** (*-o*). Note the **ছ**
(*chh*) — a breathy *ch*.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C02-TUMIAPNI-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-KEMON-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01] -->

**তুমি কেমন আছো?** = **তুমি** (*tumi*, familiar "you") + **কেমন** (*kēmon*, "how")
+ **আছো** (*āchho*, "are") — "you how are?", the verb **last**.

## Grammar Lens: now the copula returns
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C02-TUMIAPNI-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-KEMON-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01] -->

In Chapter 2, Bengali dropped "is": *āmār nām Arun* ("my name Arun"). That **zero
copula** works for *equations* (X is Y). But "how are you?" asks about a **state**
— how you *exist* — and for that Bengali uses a real verb, **আছা** (*āchhā*). So:
no verb for "my name is Arun," a real verb for "how are you." Respectful form:
**আপনি কেমন আছেন?** (*āpni kēmon āchhen?* — *āchhen* for *āpni*). Match the "you"
to the verb-ending.
