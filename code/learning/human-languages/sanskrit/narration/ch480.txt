Sanskrit, chapter 480: Bites and Wounds.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दशति (daśati) — bites.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for goes about, then the Sanskrit for a plague.

You'll want to know: दशति.
दशति — daśati — "bites".
सर्पः पथिकं दशति। sarpaḥ pathikaṃ daśati. The snake bites the traveller.
Past (it …): शुनकः बालं दष्टवान्, śunakaḥ bālaṃ daṣṭavān.

The word, taken apart.
दशति (daśati) comes from the root √daṃś, "to bite", which drops its nasal in the present daśati (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for poison, then the Sanskrit for an injury.

You'll want to know: अस्थिभंगः.
अस्थिभंगः — asthibhaṅgaḥ — "a broken bone".
वृद्धः सोपानात् अपतत्, तस्य अस्थिभंगः अभवत्। vṛddhaḥ sopānāt apatat, tasya asthibhaṅgaḥ abhavat. The old man fell down the stairs and broke a bone.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अस्थिभंगः (asthibhaṅgaḥ) is built from अस्थि, "a bone", and bhaṅga, "breaking", from the root √bhañj; MW gives "fracture of the bones" (MW).

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: स्रवति.
स्रवति — sravati — "oozes".
व्रणात् रक्तं स्रवति। vraṇāt raktaṃ sravati. Blood oozes from the wound.
Past (it …): रक्तं व्रणात् स्रुतवत्, raktaṃ vraṇāt srutavat.

The word, taken apart.
स्रवति (sravati) comes from the root √sru, "to flow, to stream"; MW's ppp sruta is "streaming, flowing" (MW).
