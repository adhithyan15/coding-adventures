# مساء (masāʾ) — "evening"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The evening mirror of *ṣabāḥ*, and built the very same way.

## You'll want to know first

- [صباح (ṣabāḥ)](./AR-C02-sabah.md) — its morning counterpart.

## Sounds you'll need

- `hamza` — the **ء** (hamza) at the end is a **glottal stop** — the little
  catch in the middle of English "uh-oh." *ma-SĀʾ*. → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart

**مساء** (*masāʾ*) — "evening." Root: **م–س–و/ي** (**m–s–w**), the idea of
**evening / the day drawing to a close**:

- **masāʾ** — evening
- **amsā** — to become / to reach evening (the exact mirror of *aṣbaḥa* "to
  reach morning" — Arabic has a "to become" verb for each end of the day)

So *ṣabāḥ* and *masāʾ* are a matched pair: dawn and dusk, each with its own
"to become" verb. Neatly, that mirrors nothing in the Romance evening words
(French *soir* and Spanish *tarde* both came from "late") — Arabic instead
names evening as its own event, the counterpart of morning.
