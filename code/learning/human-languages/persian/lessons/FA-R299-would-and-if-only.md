# Review — what would be, and what may be

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-UNREAL-CONDITION-01, FA-GRAMMAR-WOULD-MI-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if I had the money, I'd buy a car". (*Agar pul dâshtam, yek
mâshin mikharidam.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-AGAR-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-REAL-CONDITION-01, FA-GRAMMAR-UNREAL-CONDITION-01, FA-GRAMMAR-DUST-DASHTAM-01, FA-GRAMMAR-KASH-01, FA-GRAMMAR-PAST-CONTINUOUS-01] -->

Two chapters, and two kinds of "if". A condition that may come true takes
the subjunctive: *agar bârân biyâyad, dar khâne mimânim*. One that is not
true moves into the past, the plain past for *budan* and *dâshtan*, and its
result takes the *mi-* past: *agar havâ khub
bud, be pârk miraftim*, "if the weather were good, we'd go to the park".

[PAUSE 2s] The *mi-* past has now done three jobs. In chapter two hundred and
ninety it painted a scene, *mikhândam*, and told a habit, *har ruz
miraftam*. Here it says what would happen, *miraftim*.

[PAUSE 2s] *Dust dâshtam* holds a wish back, and *kâsh* sighs for one:

> *dust dâshtam be safar beravam, vali vaqt nadâram. kâsh vaqt dâshtam!*
