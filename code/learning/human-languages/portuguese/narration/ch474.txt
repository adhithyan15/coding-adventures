Portuguese, chapter 474: Rabugentos e birras.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
rabugento — grumpy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for unfriendly, then the Portuguese for to offend.

You'll want to know: rabugento.
rabugento — "grumpy".
De manhã, antes do café, sou rabugento. In the morning, before coffee, I am grumpy.
Informal. Ser rabugento is a trait; estar rabugento is a mood.

The word, taken apart.
rabugento is built on rabugem "mange in dogs" (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin rōbīgō "rust, blight" (Lewis & Short): a grumpy person is as touchy as a dog with mange.

Lesson 4 of 6.
a birra — tantrum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for grumbler, then the Portuguese for to sulk.

You'll want to know: a birra.
a birra — "tantrum".
O menino fez uma birra no supermercado. The little boy threw a tantrum in the supermarket.
Fazer uma birra — to throw a tantrum. In Brazil birra can also mean stubbornness.

The word, taken apart.
birra is traced in EtymDB to Spanish birria, which goes back to Latin verrēs "a boar"; the link is not certain.

choramingar — "to whine, to whimper".
O cão choramingava à porta. The dog was whimpering at the door.
Built on chorar "to cry": a small, whining cry.

The word, taken apart.
choramingar is built on chorar "to cry", which is inherited from Latin plōrāre "to wail" (Lewis & Short).
