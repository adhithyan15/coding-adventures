- **One numeral per lesson, because Marathi's are the Sanskrit ones worn down
  and each has to be learned rather than derived.** What makes that affordable
  is that every one of them still shows the wear: **अकरा** is *one-ten* with the
  **एक** at the front and **दश** worn to **-रा** at the back, and once a reader
  has seen that twice they are learning one construction rather than fifteen
  words. The etymology is load-bearing here, not decoration.
- **Twenty comes before nineteen, on purpose.** **एकोणीस** is **एक** + **ऊन** +
  **वीस** — *one less than twenty*, counting DOWN from the round number, which
  is how every nineteen in the language is built. The round number has to exist
  first, so the count breaks order for one lesson and says why on the page. It
  also removed a forward reference: teaching nineteen first would have put
  **वीस** in bold one lesson before it was taught.

  where its partner is, because the letter ledger asks that a sign be spent

  them idle for fifteen lessons.
