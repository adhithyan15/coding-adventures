# (reading) — six lines off a form

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-SKILL-READ-INSTRUCTIONS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you things somebody says. These are things
somebody writes down.

[PAUSE 1s] Nothing here is new.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-SKILL-READ-DETAILS]; assesses=[FR-SKILL-READ-INSTRUCTIONS] -->

> Nom : Marie
> Âge : vingt ans
> Lundi : le travail
> Mardi : un livre
> Une heure
> Dix minutes

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and notice the colon. French puts a space before that too,
and the space is the clearest signal on the page that a label has ended.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-SKILL-READ-DETAILS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A form is the first thing a beginner can read end to end. It puts
the label before the value every time, and it never asks for a verb.

[PAUSE 2s] That is why the last two lines are the hardest, not the first four.
**Une heure** and **Dix minutes** have no label in front of them, so nothing
tells you what they are for until you have read them.

[PAUSE 2s] Six lines, and four of them do half your work.
