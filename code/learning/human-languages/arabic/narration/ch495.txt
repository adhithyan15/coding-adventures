Arabic, chapter 495: Annoyed and fed up.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
انزعاج (inziʿāj) — annoyance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for frustrated, then the Arabic for to get bored.

You'll want to know: انزعاج.
انزعاج — inziʿāj — "annoyance".
تكلم الجار عن الموسيقى العالية بانزعاج. takallama al-jār ʿan al-mūsīqā al-ʿāliya bi-inziʿāj. The neighbour spoke about the loud music with annoyance.
The adjective مزعج, annoying, is from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
انزعاج (inziʿāj) is built on the native Arabic root z-ʿ-j, "to disquiet, to unsettle" (Lane). Inziʿāj is the verbal noun of Form VII: being unsettled.

What you've built.
One more word for describing what you lived and felt.

Lesson 3 of 6.
مستاء (mustāʾ) — displeased, upset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for annoyance, then the Arabic for annoyed.

You'll want to know: مستاء.
مستاء — mustāʾ — "displeased, upset".
الزبون مستاء من الخدمة. az-zabūn mustāʾ min al-khidma. The customer is unhappy with the service.

The word, taken apart.
مستاء (mustāʾ) is built on the native Arabic root s-w-ʾ, "to be evil, to be bad" (Lane). Mustāʾ is the participle of Form VIII, istāʾa: one who takes a thing badly.

Lesson 4 of 6.
تحمل (taḥammala) — to put up with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for annoyed, then the Arabic for displeased.

You'll want to know: تحمل.
تحمل — taḥammala — "to put up with".
لا يتحمل أبي الحر. lā yataḥammalu abī al-ḥarr. My father cannot stand the heat.
Present: يتحمل, yataḥammal.

The word, taken apart.
تحمل (taḥammala) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-m-l, "to carry" (Lane). Ḥamala, to carry, which you know, is from the same root; Form V is to carry a weight oneself.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ضجر (ḍajira) — to be fed up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for displeased, then the Arabic for to put up with.

You'll want to know: ضجر.
ضجر — ḍajira — "to be fed up".
ضجر المسافرون من التأخير الطويل. ḍajira al-musāfirūn min at-taʾkhīr aṭ-ṭawīl. The travellers were fed up with the long delay.
Present: يضجر, yaḍjar.
It takes من, as مل does.

The word, taken apart.
ضجر (ḍajira) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-j-r, "to be vexed, disquieted" (Lane). Lane gives yaḍjaru as its present.
