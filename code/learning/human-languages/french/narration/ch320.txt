French, chapter 320: À votre santé !.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
trinquer — to clink glasses, to drink to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the log, then the French for the king cake.

You'll want to know: trinquer.
trinquer — "to clink glasses, to drink to".
Nous avons trinqué aux mariés. We clinked glasses to the bride and groom.
Informal: c'est moi qui ai trinqué — I paid the price.

The word, taken apart.
trinquer was borrowed from German trinken "to drink" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le toast — the toast (with a drink); toasted bread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the king cake, then the French for to clink glasses.

You'll want to know: le toast.
le toast — "the toast (with a drink); toasted bread".
Le père de la mariée a porté un toast. The bride's father proposed a toast.
Porter un toast à quelqu'un — to drink to someone. Also a slice of toasted bread.

The word, taken apart.
toast is usually traced to English toast (EtymDB lists no parent), which comes from Old French toster "to grill", built on Latin tostus "roasted", from torrēre (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
souhaiter — to wish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to clink glasses, then the French for the toast.

You'll want to know: souhaiter.
souhaiter — "to wish".
Je te souhaite un joyeux anniversaire. I wish you a happy birthday.
Souhaiter que + subjunctive: je souhaite qu'il vienne.

The word, taken apart.
souhaiter comes from Old French sohaidier (EtymDB), built with sub- on a Germanic verb meaning "to promise, to command".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bougie — the candle; the spark plug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the toast, then the French for to wish.

You'll want to know: la bougie.
la bougie — "the candle; the spark plug".
Elle a soufflé les trente bougies de son gâteau. She blew out the thirty candles on her cake.
In an engine, une bougie is a spark plug.

The word, taken apart.
bougie comes from the name of a North African port town that sold candle wax to Europe in the Middle Ages (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le vœu — the wish; the vow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to wish, then the French for the candle.

You'll want to know: le vœu.
le vœu — "the wish; the vow".
Elle a fait un vœu avant de souffler les bougies. She made a wish before blowing out the candles.
Meilleurs vœux! — best wishes for the new year.

The word, taken apart.
vœu comes from Old French vou (EtymDB), inherited from Latin vōtum "a vow, a wish", from vovēre "to vow" (Lewis & Short).
