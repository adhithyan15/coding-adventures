Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 6: Tharo Naam: Ask One Name.
8 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
Hear thāro — turn from my to your.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Say mhāro nām rām hai once. Write न, then है, from memory. Point to yourself on mhāro.

You'll want to know.
thāro — your, before a masculine singular noun.
Listen twice: thā-ro. Let a small breath follow t. Point to another person when you hear it. Spelling waits for the next lesson.

Guided Practice.
Hear mhāro and point to yourself. Hear thāro and point gently away. Change the order twice.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say the pair once: mhāro — my; thāro — your.
Sources: SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015) and Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat language report.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
You hear thāro. Does it point to my or your?


Lesson 2 of 8.
थ (tha) — one breathed consonant.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Read हां, say yes, then hear thāro, point away, and say your.

Script.
थ (tha).
Read थ as tha: the tongue begins near the teeth and a small breath follows. It is one new shape. The remaining signs in thāro are already known.

Guided Practice.
Keep थ visible. Finger-trace it once, then trace one printed model once. Say tha after your hand stops.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model and write थ (tha) once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Read the one new sign थ (tha).


Lesson 3 of 8.
थारो (thāro) — your.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Say thāro and its meaning. Read म्हारो and give the opposite direction: my.

You'll want to know.
थ (tha) + ा + रो becomes थारो — thāro — your.
Only थ (tha) was new. The rest comes from राम and म्हारो.

Guided Practice.
Look for five seconds, cover थारो (thāro), wait five seconds, then write it. Uncover and compare थ (tha), ा, and रो separately.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read the two words once without romanization.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Write the pair for my and your in that order.


Lesson 4 of 8.
Hear kāĩ — what.

Warm-up.
[pause 25 seconds]
Retrieve हां सा once by ear, voice, eye, and writing; point to ै in है. Then read थारो, hear thāro nām, and give its meaning: your name.

You'll want to know.
kāĩ — what.
Listen for one long, lightly nasal middle and a final long ī. Say the whole word once rather than exaggerating the nasal sound. Its old interrogative root belongs to the same deep family as Sanskrit kim and English what.

Guided Practice.
Hear thāro nām kāĩ hai? Point to the question mark and say What is your name? No spelling yet.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say the four-beat question once from sound.
Sources: SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015), Turner, A Comparative Dictionary of the Indo-Aryan Languages, and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, September 1999.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
You hear kāĩ in a question. What does it ask for?


Lesson 5 of 8.
क (ka) — the first sign in kāĩ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Hear kāĩ and say what.

Script.
क (ka).
Read क as ka. Keep the sound short and unaspirated. Only this one shape is new today.

Guided Practice.
Keep क visible. Finger-trace it once, then trace one printed model once. Say ka after your hand stops.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model and write क (ka) once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Read the one new sign क (ka).


Lesson 6 of 8.
ई — long ī standing alone.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Write प, then क (ka), once each. Point to attached ी in पाणी and say its long vowel.

Script.
ई (ī).
Read ई as long ī. Use this independent form when the vowel stands on its own. Attached ी still belongs after a consonant.

Guided Practice.
Keep ई (ī) visible and copy it once. Then point: ई (ī) stands alone; ी attaches.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model, wait three seconds, and write ई (ī) once.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Write the long ī sign that can stand alone.


Lesson 7 of 8.
कांई (kāĩ) — what, with no new sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Write known ण and new थ (tha) once each. Say kāĩ and its meaning, then write क (ka) and independent ई (ī). Point to the known nasal dot in हां.

You'll want to know.
क (ka) + ा + ं + ई (ī) becomes कांई — kāĩ — what.
No shape is new. Read the whole word smoothly; the dot lightly nasalizes the long middle rather than creating an extra beat.

Guided Practice.
Look for five seconds, cover कांई (kāĩ), wait five seconds, and write it. Uncover and compare क (ka), ा, ं, and ई (ī) separately.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read थारो (thāro) नाम कांई (kāĩ) है? once without romanization.
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
After hiding the model, write kāĩ and give its meaning.


Lesson 8 of 8.
Practice — ask one name and give one name.

Warm-up.
[pause 25 seconds]
Say the four older meanings as you hear rām-rām sā, ābhār, hā(n) sā, and pāṇī. Write only पाणी from the last cue.

You'll want to know.
थारो (thāro) नाम कांई (kāĩ) है? म्हारो नाम राम है।.
The question asks What is your name? The answer says My name is Ram. In speech, replace rām with the name the speaker chooses. This book teaches one documented central-Marwari form; regional speakers may use another possessive or copula, and that variation is not an error.

Guided Practice.
Listen: hear one of the two lines and identify question or answer.
Speak: hear the question and answer with a chosen name, without notes.
Read: match the printed question to the printed answer.
Write: hear the question, wait ten seconds, and write it with no model.
Score each line separately. Speaking cannot cover a missing word in writing; reading cannot cover an unanswered spoken question.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one complete exchange. Accuracy comes before speed.
Sources: Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat language report, SIL International, Marwari–English Dictionary (2015), and Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Jāgtī Jot, September 1999.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
From sound alone, write the Marwadi question What is your name?, then answer aloud with a chosen name.
