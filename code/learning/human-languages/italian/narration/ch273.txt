Italian, chapter 273: Prima di tutto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dapprima — at first, initially.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for exactly, then the Italian for almost.

You'll want to know: dapprima.
dapprima — "at first, initially".
Dapprima non ho capito niente, poi ho riso. At first I did not understand anything, then I laughed.
A careful, written word; in speech people often say all'inizio.

The word, taken apart.
dapprima is built from da "from" and prima "first, before", which goes back to Latin prīmus "first" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
innanzitutto — first of all, above all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for almost, then the Italian for at first.

You'll want to know: innanzitutto.
innanzitutto — "first of all, above all".
Innanzitutto, grazie a tutti di essere qui. First of all, thank you all for being here.
Also written innanzi tutto; innanzi is an old word for ‘before, ahead’.

The word, taken apart.
innanzitutto joins innanzi "before, ahead", usually traced to Late Latin in ante, and tutto "all": before everything else. Latin ante meant "before" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
in seguito — later on, afterwards.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for at first, then the Italian for first of all.

You'll want to know: in seguito.
in seguito — "later on, afterwards".
In seguito ho trovato un lavoro migliore. Later on I found a better job.
In seguito a + a noun means ‘following, as a result of’: in seguito all'incidente.

The word, taken apart.
seguito is the past participle of seguire "to follow", which is inherited from Latin sequī "to follow" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
successivamente — subsequently, after that (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for first of all, then the Italian for later on.

You'll want to know: successivamente.
successivamente — "subsequently, after that (formal)".
Successivamente il treno è ripartito. Subsequently the train set off again.
Formal and common in reports and on the news; in everyday talk, poi.

The word, taken apart.
successivamente is built on successivo "following", a learned word usually traced to Late Latin successīvus, from succēdere "to come after" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
dopodiché — after which, and after that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for later on, then the Italian for subsequently.

You'll want to know: dopodiché.
dopodiché — "after which, and after that".
Abbiamo cenato, dopodiché siamo usciti. We had dinner, after which we went out.
Joins two events in a row. Also written dopo di che.

The word, taken apart.
dopodiché fuses dopo di che "after which". Dopo is usually traced to Late Latin dē post, built on Latin post "after" (Lewis & Short).
