Sanskrit, chapter 537: A Game of Chance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पाशकः (pāśakaḥ) — a die.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for by chance, then the Sanskrit for turns out well.

You'll want to know: पाशकः.
पाशकः — pāśakaḥ — "a die".
यदि पाशकः षट् दर्शयति, तर्हि त्वं जयसि। yadi pāśakaḥ ṣaṭ darśayati, tarhi tvaṃ jayasi. If the die shows six, you win.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पाशकः (pāśakaḥ) is built on pāśa, "a cord, a noose", with the ending -ka; MW gives pāśaka as a snare, and also as a die (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
हारयति (hārayati) — loses at play.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for turns out well, then the Sanskrit for a die.

You'll want to know: हारयति.
हारयति — hārayati — "loses at play".
यदि त्वं द्यूतं क्रीडसि, तर्हि त्वं धनं हारयसि। yadi tvaṃ dyūtaṃ krīḍasi, tarhi tvaṃ dhanaṃ hārayasi. If you gamble, you lose money.
Past (he …): सः सर्वं धनं हारितवान्, saḥ sarvaṃ dhanaṃ hāritavān.
Future: हारयिष्यति, hārayiṣyati.

The word, taken apart.
हारयति (hārayati) comes from the causative of the root √hṛ, "to take, to carry off": MW gives it as to have a thing taken from oneself, to lose it, above all at play (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
वञ्चकः (vañcakaḥ) — a cheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a die, then the Sanskrit for loses at play.

You'll want to know: वञ्चकः.
वञ्चकः — vañcakaḥ — "a cheat".
यदि सः वञ्चकः अस्ति, तर्हि अहं न क्रीडामि। yadi saḥ vañcakaḥ asti, tarhi ahaṃ na krīḍāmi. If he is a cheat, I won't play.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वञ्चकः (vañcakaḥ) comes from the causative of the root √vañc, "to go crookedly", which means "to deceive", with the agent ending -aka; MW gives vañcaka as deceiving, a deceiver, a cheat (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
लुब्धः (lubdhaḥ) — covetous; greedy for gain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for loses at play, then the Sanskrit for a cheat.

You'll want to know: लुब्धः.
लुब्धः — lubdhaḥ — "covetous; greedy for gain".
यदि त्वं लुब्धः असि, तर्हि त्वं सर्वं धनं हारयसि। yadi tvaṃ lubdhaḥ asi, tarhi tvaṃ sarvaṃ dhanaṃ hārayasi. If you are greedy, you lose all your money.

The word, taken apart.
लुब्धः (lubdhaḥ) comes from the root √lubh, "to go astray, to long for eagerly", as its past participle; MW gives lubdha as bewildered, and as greedy, covetous (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
द्यूतकारः (dyūtakāraḥ) — a gambler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cheat, then the Sanskrit for covetous.

You'll want to know: द्यूतकारः.
द्यूतकारः — dyūtakāraḥ — "a gambler".
यदि द्यूतकारः सदा जयति, तर्हि सः वञ्चकः भवेत्। yadi dyūtakāraḥ sadā jayati, tarhi saḥ vañcakaḥ bhavet. If a gambler wins every time, he may be a cheat.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
द्यूतकारः (dyūtakāraḥ) is built from dyūta, "gambling, dice", and kāra, "doing", from the root √kṛ; MW gives dyūtakāra as the same as dyūtakara, a gambler (MW).
