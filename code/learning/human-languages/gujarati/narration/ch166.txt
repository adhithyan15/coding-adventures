Gujarati, chapter 166: Chopping, peeling and grinding.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સમારવું (samārvũ) — to chop, to cut up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to mix, then the Gujarati for to boil.

You'll want to know: સમારવું.
સમારવું — samārvũ — "to chop, to cut up".
તે છરીથી શાક સમારે છે. — te charīthī śāk samāre che — "She chops the vegetables with a knife."

The word, taken apart.
સમારવું (samārvũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to repair, adorn; prepare (vegetables) for cooking", under Sanskrit saṁvārayati, "keeps off; puts in order" (CDIAL 13021).

Lesson 2 of 5.
છોલવું (cholvũ) — to peel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to boil, then the Gujarati for to chop.

You'll want to know: છોલવું.
છોલવું — cholvũ — "to peel".
હું બટાકા છોલું છું. — hũ baṭākā cholũ chũ — "I peel the potatoes."

The word, taken apart.
છોલવું (cholvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from chōll, "to peel, skin", a reconstructed form that Burrow, cited in CDIAL, traces to a Dravidian source; CDIAL also compares Sanskrit challi, "bark" (CDIAL 5073).

Lesson 4 of 5.
કોથમીર (kothmīr) — fresh coriander.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to peel, then the Gujarati for onion.

You'll want to know: કોથમીર.
કોથમીર — kothmīr — "fresh coriander".
કોથમીર લીલી અને તાજી છે. — kothmīr līlī ane tājī che — "The coriander is green and fresh."

The word, taken apart.
કોથમીર (kothmīr) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit kustumbarī, "coriander", which CDIAL counts as non-Aryan in origin, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati kothmīr (CDIAL 3380).

Lesson 5 of 5.
વાટવું (vāṭvũ) — to grind, to crush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for onion, then the Gujarati for fresh coriander.

You'll want to know: વાટવું.
વાટવું — vāṭvũ — "to grind, to crush".
તે આદુ અને મરચું વાટે છે. — te ādu ane marcũ vāṭe che — "She grinds ginger and chilli."

The word, taken apart.
વાટવું (vāṭvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit vartayati, "causes to turn, whirls", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to pound by rolling" (CDIAL 11356).
