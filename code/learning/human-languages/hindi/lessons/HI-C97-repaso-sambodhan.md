# संबोधन — two openings, one question to pick between them

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C97-PRIYA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the word a written message opens with, and say which side of the
name it sits on.

## Grammar Lens: the two side by side
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C97-ADDRESS-01, HI-LEX-C97-PRIYA-01] -->

| where | what you say or write |
|---|---|
| out loud | मीरा जी |
| on paper | प्रिय मीरा |

One question chooses between them: **are you speaking or writing?** Answer it
and the opening is decided, along with which side of the name the extra word
goes on.

## Grammar Lens: what the two have in common
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C97-ADDRESS-01, HI-LEX-C97-PRIYA-01] -->

Neither of them changes the sentence that follows. Hindi marks address by
putting a word beside the name rather than bending anything, so whatever you
were going to say stays exactly as it was and the opening sits in front of it.

That is worth holding on to, because a great deal of what this track has taught
you does the opposite. Endings agree, vowels shorten, verbs bend for who is
speaking. **Address is the corner of the grammar that asks nothing of the rest
of the sentence.**
