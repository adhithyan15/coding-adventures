Portuguese, chapter 422: As bagagens.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o porão — the hold (of a plane or ship); the cellar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the runway, then the Portuguese for the time zone.

You'll want to know: o porão.
o porão — "the hold (of a plane or ship); the cellar".
A mala grande vai no porão. The big suitcase goes in the hold.
In Brazil o porão is mostly a basement.

The word, taken apart.
porão comes from Old Portuguese prão (EtymDB); its earlier origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: a balança.
a balança — "the scales".
No aeroporto, a balança mostra vinte e três quilos. At the airport, the scales show twenty-three kilos.
A balança comercial — the balance of trade.

The word, taken apart.
balança is inherited from Old Portuguese balança (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin bilancia, built on Latin bilanx 'having two scale pans' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
empacotar — to pack up, to wrap into parcels.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hold, then the Portuguese for the scales.

You'll want to know: empacotar.
empacotar — "to pack up, to wrap into parcels".
Empacotamos os livros em caixas de papel. We pack up the books in cardboard boxes.
For a suitcase people usually say fazer a mala.

The word, taken apart.
empacotar is built from em- and pacote 'packet', a small form of paca 'bundle' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
extraviar — to lose, to mislay (luggage or post); to go astray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to pack up, then the Portuguese for the padlock.

You'll want to know: extraviar.
extraviar — "to lose, to mislay (luggage or post); to go astray".
A companhia aérea extraviou a minha mala. The airline lost my suitcase.
A bagagem extraviada — lost luggage.

The word, taken apart.
extraviar is built from extra- 'outside' and via 'way', from Latin via (Lewis & Short): to send off the right road.
