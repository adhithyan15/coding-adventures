Mandarin Chinese, chapter 56: Free Time.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wán — to play; to have fun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sing, then the Mandarin for coffee.

You'll want to know: wán.
wán — "to play; to have fun".
háizi xǐhuan wán — Children love to play.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wán is inherited from Middle Chinese ngwanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to toy with'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: wán]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wán, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: háizi xǐhuan wán — Children love to play.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to sing, then the Mandarin for coffee, then say wán again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does wán mean? ("To play; to have fun".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
dǎqiú — to play ball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for coffee, then the Mandarin for to play.

You'll want to know: dǎqiú.
dǎqiú — "to play ball".
xīngqītiān wǒmen dǎqiú — We play ball on Sunday.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎqiú is built from dǎ 'to strike' and qiú 'ball', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and gjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), though dǎ is not the sound taengX would regularly give. The dictionary glosses gjuw as 'fine jade', and under another way of writing it as a ball of fur that is struck.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yùndòng — sport; exercise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to play, then the Mandarin for to play ball.

You'll want to know: yùndòng.
yùndòng — "sport; exercise".
wǒ xǐhuan yùndòng — I like sport.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yùndòng is built from yùn 'to move round, to carry' and dòng 'to move', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjunH and duwngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The sense 'sport' is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to play ball, then the Mandarin for sport.

You'll want to know: pǎobù.
pǎobù — "to run; to jog".
wǒ zǎoshang pǎobù — I go for a run in the mornings.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pǎobù is built from pǎo 'to run' and bù 'step', both inherited from Middle Chinese, baew and buH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses baew as a foot pawing the ground.
