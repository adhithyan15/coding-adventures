5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
古怪 (gǔguài) — eccentric; peculiar (of a person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to come off worse, then the Mandarin for to get into trouble.

You'll want to know: 古怪.
古怪 — gǔguài — "eccentric; peculiar (of a person)".
原来他是一个古怪的人 yuánlái tā shì yí ge gǔguài de rén — So he's an odd sort of man.

The word, taken apart.
古怪 (gǔguài) is built from 古 'old' and 怪 'strange', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and kweajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'of old' and the second 'strange'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
liánlei — to drag someone into trouble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get into trouble, then the Mandarin for eccentric.

You'll want to know: liánlei.
liánlei — "to drag someone into trouble".
yàoshi wǒ chūshì le jiù huì liánlei dàjiā — If anything happens to me, everyone will be dragged into it.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liánlei is built from lián 'to join' and lei, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ljen and ljweH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to join, to continue' and the second as being caught up in another's offence. The second is said light here.

Lesson 4 of 5.
前因后果 (qiányīn hòuguǒ) — the whole chain of causes and effects.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to drag someone into trouble, then the Mandarin for bound to happen.

You'll want to know: 前因后果.
前因后果 — qiányīn hòuguǒ — "the whole chain of causes and effects".
yàoshi nǐ zhīdào qiányīn hòuguǒ jiù bú huì guài tā le — If you knew the whole story, you wouldn't blame him.

The word, taken apart.
前因后果 (qiányīn hòuguǒ) is built from 前 'before', 因 'a cause', 后 'after' and 果 'fruit', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dzen, 'jin, huwX and kwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the cause that came before and the fruit that came after.

Lesson 5 of 5.
怎么回事 (zěnme huí shì) — what is going on here; what's the matter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bound to happen, then the Mandarin for the whole chain of causes.

You'll want to know: 怎么回事.
怎么回事 — zěnme huí shì — "what is going on here; what's the matter".
这是怎么回事 zhè shì zěnme huí shì — What's going on here?

The word, taken apart.
怎么回事 (zěnme huí shì) is built from 怎么 'how', 回 'a time' and 事 'a matter'. 回 and 事 are inherited from Middle Chinese, hwoj and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of 怎, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
