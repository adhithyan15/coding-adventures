## HL-C217 — Telugu's remaining script-closure debt is all UPSTREAM of the ladder

Teaching the nine never-taught letters **హ ణ జ భ ఋ థ ృ ఞ ౌ**, each placed in the
chapter after the first word that carries it, took Telugu's closure violations
from 45 to 30; adding `romanization` to the seventeen headwords that had none
took it to 22. What is left will not yield to more of the same, and the reason
is worth writing down before the next author tries.

**Every one of the 22 survivors sits at or before chapter 19, and the script
ladder does not start until chapter 6.** A script lesson only teaches the
lessons that come *after* it in reading order, so nothing added at chapter 22 or
later can clear a violation at chapter 2. The remaining work is therefore not
"teach more letters" but "start the ladder earlier", which is a different and
larger change to the track's shape.

Three concrete sub-problems, in the order they are worth attacking:

- **The ten Telugu digits ౦ ౧ ౨ ౩ ౪ ౫ ౬ ౭ ౮ ౯ are shown and never taught.**
  They appear in exactly two lessons, `TE-C07-numbers-1-5` and
  `TE-C07-numbers-6-10`, both in chapter 7 — one chapter after the ladder
  begins. Ten script lessons squeezed between chapters 6 and 7 would close both
  violations and drop the track's never-taught glyph count from 13 to 3. This is
  the cheapest remaining win and it is self-contained.
- **Three month-letters ఖ ఘ ఢ are still never taught**, first shown in
  `TE-C16-nelalu`. (ఠ and ఫ picked up credit incidentally from the example words
  in the జ and ణ lessons; those three did not.) `TE-C16-nelalu` itself is now
  clean, because its twelve month names became exposure the moment they got a
  romanization — so this is a glyph-inventory gap, not a reader-facing one.
- **The two practice lessons are the biggest single violations and the least
  fixable.** `TE-C02-practice` asks for 19 untaught glyphs and
  `TE-C01-practice` for 16, because they recap whole words before any letter has
  been named. Neither has a headword to exempt. The honest options are to move
  the first script lessons ahead of chapter 1 — which contradicts the
  gloss-first rule those chapters are built on — or to accept that a recap of
  words the reader can already *say* is exposure, and to say so in the
  measurement rather than in the prose.

The third bullet is the one that needs a decision rather than a tranche. The
exposure rule currently turns on a lesson's `romanization` field, and a practice
lesson has no single headword to hang one on. Until that is settled, Telugu's
closure number has a floor of roughly 15 that no amount of authoring will move.
