Gujarati, chapter 283: If, then, or else.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
જો …, તો … (jo …, to …) — if, then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if", then "if … then", from A2. (જો, જો … તો ….) Then "you should rest". (tamāre ārām karvo joīe.)

Grammar Lens: two halves.
જો તમને તાવ છે, તો તમારે આરામ કરવો જોઈએ. jo tamne tāv che, to tamāre ārām karvo joīe. — "If you have a fever, then you should rest."
જો (jo) opens the condition, and તો (to), "then", opens what follows from it. Each half is a whole sentence with its own verb. In quick speech તો can drop, but written down it marks where the second half begins.

You'll want to know: if it is so now.
જો તમને ભૂખ છે, તો ખાઈ લો. jo tamne bhūkh che, to khāī lo. — "If you're hungry, then go ahead and eat."
When the condition is about now, it takes the present: જો તમને ભૂખ છે, જો તે ઘરે છે. The second half can be advice, a request with -ઓ, or a plain statement: જો ઠંડી છે, તો હું ઘરે રહીશ (jo ṭhanḍī che, to hũ ghare rahīś).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If you're hungry, go ahead and eat" — jo tamne bhūkh che, to khāī lo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it's cold, I'll stay at home" — jo ṭhanḍī che, to hũ ghare rahīś]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Tell someone, with respect: if you have a fever, then you should go to the doctor.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word opens the condition? (જો.) Which opens the second half? (તો.) "If you have a fever"? (જો તમને તાવ છે.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
જો વરસાદ પડશે, તો … (jo varsād paḍśe, to …) — if it happens.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if you have a fever, then you should rest". (jo tamne tāv che, to tamāre ārām karvo joīe.) Then "we will stay". (ame rahīśũ.)

Grammar Lens: the future after જો.
જો વરસાદ પડશે, તો અમે ઘરે રહીશું. jo varsād paḍśe, to ame ghare rahīśũ. — "If it rains, we'll stay at home."
English says "if it rains"; Gujarati says "if it will rain": a condition about later takes the future, પડશે, and so does the result, રહીશું. "If it doesn't rain" is the નહીં form: જો વરસાદ નહીં પડે, તો અમે જઈશું (jo varsād nahī̃ paḍe, to ame jaīśũ).

You'll want to know: the past after જો.
જો ટ્રેન છૂટી ગઈ, તો હું બસમાં આવીશ. jo ṭren chūṭī gaī, to hũ basmā̃ āvīś. — "If I miss the train, I'll come by bus."
When the condition would be over before the result starts, it can take the plain past: "if the train has gone, then …". જો મારું પાકીટ ખોવાઈ ગયું, તો હું પોલીસમાં ફરિયાદ કરીશ (jo mārũ pākīṭ khovāī gayũ, to hũ polīsmā̃ phariyād karīś).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If it doesn't rain, we'll go" — jo varsād nahī̃ paḍe, to ame jaīśũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If my wallet is lost, I'll make a complaint" — jo mārũ pākīṭ khovāī gayũ, to hũ phariyād karīś]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say: if it rains, we'll stay at home; and if I miss the train, I'll come by bus.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains"? (જો વરસાદ પડશે.) "If it doesn't rain"? (જો વરસાદ નહીં પડે.) "If I miss the train"? (જો ટ્રેન છૂટી ગઈ.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
નહીંતર (nahī̃tar) — otherwise; or else.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "if it rains, we'll stay at home". (jo varsād paḍśe, to ame ghare rahīśũ.) Then "quickly" and "an umbrella". (જલદી, છત્રી.)

You'll want to know: નહીંતર.
નહીંતર — nahī̃tar — "otherwise; or else". First what to do, then નહીંતર, then what will happen if it is not done:
જલદી ચાલો, નહીંતર ટ્રેન છૂટી જશે. jaldī cālo, nahī̃tar ṭren chūṭī jaśe. — "Walk quickly, or else we'll miss the train."
છત્રી લો, નહીંતર તમે પલળી જશો. chatrī lo, nahī̃tar tame palḷī jaśo. — "Take an umbrella, otherwise you'll get soaked."
It says in one word what જો … નહીં …, તો … says in a whole clause.

The word, taken apart.
નહીંતર (nahī̃tar) is built from નહીં, "not", and -tar: "if not, then". CDIAL lists Gujarati nahitar, "otherwise", under Sanskrit tarhi, "then" (CDIAL 5730).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nahī̃tar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Walk quickly, or else we'll miss the train" — jaldī cālo, nahī̃tar ṭren chūṭī jaśe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Take an umbrella, otherwise you'll get soaked" — chatrī lo, nahī̃tar tame palḷī jaśo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does નહીંતર (nahī̃tar) mean? ("Otherwise; or else".) Which two words are inside it? (નહીં and -tar, "then".)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — if, then, or else.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the city is bigger than the village". (śaher gām kartā̃ moṭũ che.) Then "I have lived here for two years". (hũ be varasthī ahī̃ rahũ chũ.) And "my wish is to live in the village". (mārī icchā gāmmā̃ rahevānī che.)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: If you have a fever, then you should rest.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If you're hungry, go ahead and eat.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If it rains, we'll stay at home. If it doesn't rain, we'll go.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If I miss the train, I'll come by bus. The bus is the cheapest.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: The shop is open from morning until evening. Go quickly, otherwise it will close.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains", about tomorrow: which tense? (The future: પડશે.) "If I miss the train"? (જો ટ્રેન છૂટી ગઈ.) "Or else"? (નહીંતર (nahī̃tar).)
