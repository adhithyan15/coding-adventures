Sanskrit, chapter 94: Outside, Above, Below, In front, Behind.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बहिः (bahiḥ) — outside.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for right; south, then the Sanskrit for inside.

You'll want to know: बहिः.
बहिः — bahiḥ — "outside".

The word, taken apart.
bahiḥ is bahis "out, outside", with the final s said as ḥ at the end of a phrase (MW). MW gives no source for it, and its origin is not settled. Pali and Prakrit kept it as bahi (CDIAL 9186).

उपरि — upari — "above".

The word, taken apart.
upari is built on upa "near, up to" (CDIAL 2218), and MW sets it beside Greek hyper, Latin super and English over: one inherited Indo-European word. Marathi var "on" comes from it (CDIAL 2218).

अधः (adhaḥ) — below.

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for outside, then the Sanskrit for above.

You'll want to know: अधः.
अधः — adhaḥ — "below".

The word, taken apart.
adhaḥ is adhas "below, down", with the final s said as ḥ at the end of a phrase (MW). MW compares Latin infra "below", the infra- of English infrared, so it is inherited from Indo-European. Pali kept it as adho (CDIAL 246).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अग्रे (agre) — in front.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for above, then the Sanskrit for below.

You'll want to know: अग्रे.
अग्रे — agre — "in front".

The word, taken apart.
agre is the locative, "at the front", of agra "front, tip, top" (MW). MW refers agra to the root √aṅg, following the grammarians. The word lives on as Hindi āge "ahead, before" (CDIAL 68).

Lesson 5 of 5.
पृष्ठतः (pṛṣṭhataḥ) — behind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for below, then the Sanskrit for in front.

You'll want to know: पृष्ठतः.
पृष्ठतः — pṛṣṭhataḥ — "behind".

The word, taken apart.
pṛṣṭhataḥ is built from pṛṣṭha "back" and the ending -tas "from, on the side of": "from the back" (MW). MW takes pṛṣṭha as probably pra-stha "standing out", the back being the part of an animal that stands out. pṛṣṭha lives on as Kumaoni pūṭh "back" (CDIAL 8371).
