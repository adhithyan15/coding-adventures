# નામ (nām) — "name," a word older than Europe

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The first word of the introduction — and a bridge between India and
Europe.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ન** (*na*) + **ા** (long *ā*) + **મ** (*ma*) → **નામ** (*nām*).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-NAAM-01] -->

**નામ** (*nām*, "name") ← Sanskrit **નામન્** (*nāman*), from Proto-Indo-European
**\*h₃nómn** (its final *n* syllabic) — the same root as English **name**, Latin *nōmen* (→ **noun**),
Greek *onoma*, and Hindi *nām*. Gujarati *nām* and English *name* are cousins from
before either language existed.
