शंखः (śaṅkhaḥ) — a conch.

You'll want to know: शंखः.
शंखः — śaṅkhaḥ — "a conch".

The word, taken apart.
śaṅkha "a conch-shell" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and is inherited from Indo-European: MW sets it beside Greek konchē and Latin concha "shell", and EtymDB agrees. It lives on as Hindi saṅkh "conch" (CDIAL 12263).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वेणुः (veṇuḥ) — a flute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a small pitcher, then the Sanskrit for a conch.

You'll want to know: वेणुः.
वेणुः — veṇuḥ — "a flute".

The word, taken apart.
veṇu first meant "a bamboo, a reed" in the Rigveda and later "a flute" (MW). MW thinks it probably comes from the root √ve "weave", but CDIAL 12096 finds its connections unclear, so its origin is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
मृदंगः (mṛdaṅgaḥ) — a mridanga drum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a conch, then the Sanskrit for a flute.

You'll want to know: मृदंगः.
मृदंगः — mṛdaṅgaḥ — "a mridanga drum".

The word, taken apart.
MW explains mṛdaṅga "a drum" as probably mṛdam + -ga, "going about while being beaten", from the root √mṛd "press, beat". CDIAL 10289 compares mardala, another drum, so the formation is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
नूपुरम् (nūpuram) — an anklet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a flute, then the Sanskrit for a mridanga drum.

You'll want to know: नूपुरम्.
नूपुरम् — nūpuram — "an anklet".

The word, taken apart.
nūpura "an anklet" has an uncertain origin: CDIAL 7577 reports both a Middle Indic form reshaped after keyūra "armlet" and Kuiper's non-Aryan account, comparing Santali lipur. It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi neur "anklet" (CDIAL 7577).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: कुण्डलम्.
कुण्डलम् — kuṇḍalam — "an earring".

The word, taken apart.
kuṇḍala "a ring, an earring" is taken to be borrowed from Dravidian, after Burrow (CDIAL 3268; EWA). It lives on as Punjabi kuṇḍal "coil, earring" (CDIAL 3268).
