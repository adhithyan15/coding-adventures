# अगर … तो … (agar … to …) — if, then

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C247-THG247-04, HI-GRAMMAR-C281-TO-SO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if". (**अगर**, *agar*.) Then open a reply with "so, then": **तो चलिए**. The two words meet in this lesson.

## You'll want to know: अगर … तो … with the future
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C286-AGAR-TO-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C247-THG247-04, HI-GRAMMAR-C281-TO-SO-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-FUTURE-PERSON-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-WILL-BE-01, HI-LEX-C184-THG184-05, HI-CONCEPT-C36-GHAR-01, HI-LEX-C280-LAGNA-01] -->

> **अगर बारिश होगी, तो हम घर पर रहेंगे।**
>
> *agar bāriś hogī, to ham ghar par raheṅge.* — "If it rains, we will stay at home."

**अगर** opens the condition and **तो** opens what follows from it. When both halves are still to come, Hindi puts both in the future: **होगी**, **रहेंगे**. English says "if it rains"; Hindi says "if it will rain".

- **अगर तुम आओगे, तो मुझे अच्छा लगेगा।** (*agar tum āoge, to mujhe acchā lagegā.*) — If you come, I'll be glad.

Keep both words: **अगर** at the start, **तो** at the turn.

## You'll want to know: if it is so now
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C286-IF-NOW-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C63-STATE-01, HI-LEX-C166-READ166-03, HI-LEX-C115-COMEIN-01, HI-LEX-C106-UNWELL-04, HI-LEX-C70-LEISURE-05, HI-LEX-C280-LAGNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C281-CHAHIYE-SHOULD-01] -->

> **अगर तुम्हें भूख लगी है, तो खाना खाओ।**
>
> *agar tumhẽ bhūkh lagī hai, to khānā khāo.* — "If you're hungry, eat."

When the condition is true now, it stays in the present, and the second half can ask, advise or tell:

- **अगर समय है, तो आइए।** (*agar samay hai, to āie.*) — If you have time, do come.
- **अगर आप बीमार हैं, तो आपको आराम करना चाहिए।** (*agar āp bīmār haiṁ, to āpko ārām karnā cāhiye.*) — If you're ill, you should rest.
