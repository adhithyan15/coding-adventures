Marathi, chapter 399: Drivers and Conductors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चालक (cālak) — a driver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a destination, then the Marathi for to set aside.

You'll want to know: चालक.
चालक — cālak — "a driver".
चालकाने बस हळूहळू थांबवली. cālakāne bas haḷūhaḷū thāmbavlī. The driver brought the bus slowly to a stop.
Masculine.
The agent of a past transitive verb takes -ने: चालकाने बस थांबवली.

The word, taken apart.
चालक (cālak) is a tatsama formation on cāl-, the causative stem of Sanskrit cal, "to move", with the agent ending -aka: the one who makes the vehicle move. The same stem gives cālana, "causing to move" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वाहक (vāhak) — a bus conductor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to set aside, then the Marathi for a driver.

You'll want to know: वाहक.
वाहक — vāhak — "a bus conductor".
वाहकाने माझं तिकीट तपासलं. vāhakāne mājhaṁ tikīṭ tapāslaṁ. The conductor checked my ticket.
Masculine.
On a bus the चालक (cālak) drives and the वाहक (vāhak) sells and checks tickets.

The word, taken apart.
वाहक (vāhak) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit vāhaka, "one who carries, a carrier" (Monier-Williams); on a bus it names the conductor.

Lesson 3 of 5.
चालवणे (cālavṇe) — to drive; to run.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a driver, then the Marathi for a bus conductor.

You'll want to know: चालवणे.
चालवणे — cālavṇe — "to drive; to run".
माझे काका बस चालवतात. mājhe kākā bas cālavtāt. My uncle drives a bus.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने चालवलं, tyāne cālavlaṁ.
Not the same as चालणे, to walk: चालवणे (cālavṇe) is to make something go, so गाडी चालवणे (cālavṇe) is to drive and दुकान चालवणे (cālavṇe) is to run a shop.

The word, taken apart.
चालवणे (cālavṇe) is built on चालणे, "to walk, to go", which CDIAL lists as Marathi ċālṇẽ under calyati, "goes" (CDIAL 4721), as its causative: to make a vehicle or a business go.

Lesson 4 of 5.
सहप्रवासी (sahapravāsī) — a fellow traveller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a bus conductor, then the Marathi for to drive.

You'll want to know: सहप्रवासी.
सहप्रवासी — sahapravāsī — "a fellow traveller".
माझ्या सहप्रवाशाने मला पाणी दिलं. mājhyā sahapravāśāne malā pāṇī dilaṁ. My fellow traveller gave me some water.
Masculine.
सह- means together with; before -ने and -ला it becomes सहप्रवाशा-.

The word, taken apart.
सहप्रवासी (sahapravāsī) is a compound of Sanskrit saha, "together with" (Monier-Williams), and प्रवासी, "a traveller", built on Sanskrit pravāsa, "a journey abroad".

Lesson 5 of 5.
वाटसरू (vāṭsarū) — a wayfarer; a passer-by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to drive, then the Marathi for a fellow traveller.

You'll want to know: वाटसरू.
वाटसरू — vāṭsarū — "a wayfarer; a passer-by".
एका वाटसरूने आम्हाला रस्ता सांगितला. ekā vāṭsarūne āmhālā rastā sāṅgitlā. A passer-by told us the way.
Masculine.
An old, warm word for someone on the road, often walking.

The word, taken apart.
वाटसरू (vāṭsarū) is built on वाट, "a path", inherited from Sanskrit vartman, "track of a wheel, path" (CDIAL 11366); the ending marks the person on it.
