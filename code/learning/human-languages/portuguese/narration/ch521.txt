Portuguese, chapter 521: Comédia, ópera e bailado.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a comédia — comedy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for premiere, then the Portuguese for to premiere.

You'll want to know: a comédia.
a comédia — "comedy".
Vimos uma comédia e rimos muito. We saw a comedy and laughed a lot.
Uma comédia romântica — a romantic comedy.

The word, taken apart.
comédia comes from Latin cōmoedia (EtymDB), from Greek kōmōidía "comedy", a word usually analysed as kômos "a revel" plus ōidḗ "song".

You'll want to know: o bailado.
o bailado — "ballet (a dance piece)".
Fomos ver um bailado no teatro. We went to see a ballet at the theatre.
Also o ballet. Built on bailar "to dance".

The word, taken apart.
bailado is built on bailar "to dance", which goes back to Late Latin ballāre "to dance" (Lewis & Short).

The word, taken apart.
camarim was borrowed from Italian camerino "a little room" (EtymDB), from Latin camera "a vaulted room".
