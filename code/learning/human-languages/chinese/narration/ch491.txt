Lesson 1 of 7.
jiāxīn — to get a pay rise; to raise someone's pay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for exhausting (of work), then the Mandarin for to fire (from a job).

You'll want to know: jiāxīn.
jiāxīn — "to get a pay rise; to raise someone's pay".
wǒ gōngzuò le liǎng nián hái méiyǒu jiāxīn — I've worked for two years and still haven't had a pay rise.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiāxīn is built from jiā 'to add' and xīn 'firewood', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and sin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to increase' and the second 'firewood'. Firewood standing for pay is the usual explanation, but the dictionary does not give that sense.

Lesson 2 of 7.
上司 (shàngsi) — one's superior at work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to fire (from a job), then the Mandarin for to get a pay rise.

You'll want to know: 上司.
上司 — shàngsi — "one's superior at work".
我的新上司比以前的好 wǒ de xīn shàngsi bǐ yǐqián de hǎo — My new boss is better than the last one.
The new character, in pieces: 司 (sī) — five strokes: one long stroke for the frame, a bar along the top that turns down the right side and hooks; a short bar under it; then 口 kǒu inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
The second syllable is light: shàngsi, with no tone of its own.

The word, taken apart.
上司 (shàngsi) is built from 上 'above' and 司 (sī) 'to be in charge', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangH and si (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a ruler' and the second 'to be in charge'.

Script — a hooked frame, a short bar, then kǒu.
司 (sī).
The frame, one stroke. A bar along the top that turns down the right side and hooks to the left at the foot.
A short bar inside, under the top.
Then 口 kǒu, three strokes, under the short bar: the left side, the top that turns down, and the bottom.
1 + 1 + 3 = five.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 司 (sī) have? (Five.) Which stroke comes first? (The frame: a top bar that turns down and hooks.) What sits inside? (A short bar and 口.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
mìshū — secretary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get a pay rise, then the Mandarin for one's superior at work.

You'll want to know: mìshū.
mìshū — "secretary".
wǒ zài gōngsī dāng le sān nián mìshū — I was a secretary at a company for three years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mìshū is built from mì 'secret' and 书 'writing', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pijH and syo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the first in an older form, glossed 'close' and 'secret'. The modern mì is irregular: pijH would regularly give bì.

Lesson 5 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for one's superior at work, then the Mandarin for a secretary.

You'll want to know: 职场.
职场 — zhíchǎng — "the workplace; the world of work".
wǒ juéde zhíchǎng bǐ xuéxiào lèi — I find working life more tiring than school.

The word, taken apart.
职场 (zhíchǎng) is built from 职 'a post' and 场 'a ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyik and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a threshing floor'; the modern third tone of chǎng is irregular for drjang.
