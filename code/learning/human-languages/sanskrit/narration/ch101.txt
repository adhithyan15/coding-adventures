Lesson 1 of 5.
कंकतिका (kaṅkatikā) — a comb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a needle, then the Sanskrit for thread.

You'll want to know: कंकतिका.
कंकतिका — kaṅkatikā — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
kaṅkatikā is kaṅkata "comb", already in the Atharvaveda (MW), with the small-thing suffix -ikā. CDIAL 2598 calls kaṅkata of doubtful Indo-European origin, and it lives on as Hindi kaṅghī "comb" (CDIAL 2598).

Lesson 2 of 5.
फेनकम् (phenakam) — soap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for thread, then the Sanskrit for a comb.

You'll want to know: फेनकम्.
फेनकम् — phenakam — "soap".

The word, taken apart.
phenaka is built on phena "foam" with the suffix -ka: "the foaming thing". MW sets phena beside English foam, an inherited Indo-European word; using phenaka for soap is modern, since MW gives it other senses.

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रोञ्छनम् (proñchanam) — a towel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a comb, then the Sanskrit for soap.

You'll want to know: प्रोञ्छनम्.
प्रोञ्छनम् — proñchanam — "a towel".

The word, taken apart.
proñchana "wiping" is formed from proñchati "wipes off", which MW splits as pra + √uñch. CDIAL 9011 finds that link doubtful and suggests the verb is a Sanskritized Prakrit word, perhaps from Dravidian; Hindi pochnā "to wipe" comes from it. Using proñchana for a towel is modern.

Lesson 4 of 5.
उपनेत्रम् (upanetram) — glasses, spectacles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for soap, then the Sanskrit for a towel.

You'll want to know: उपनेत्रम्.
उपनेत्रम् — upanetram — "glasses, spectacles".

The word, taken apart.
upanetra is built from upa "near, additional" and netra "eye"; MW explains netra as "the guiding organ", from the root √nī "lead". The pairing for spectacles is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
स्यूतः (syūtaḥ) — a bag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a towel, then the Sanskrit for glasses.

You'll want to know: स्यूतः.
स्यूतः — syūtaḥ — "a bag".

The word, taken apart.
syūta is the past participle of √siv "sew": "something sewn", and MW records it as "a sack, a coarse canvas bag". The root is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB) and is the same root as English sew.
