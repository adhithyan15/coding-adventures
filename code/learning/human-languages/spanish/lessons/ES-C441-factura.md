# la factura — a making, itemised

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PAGAR, ES-LEX-C311-ASK-07, ES-LEX-C311-ASK-08] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **pagar**, **la cuenta** and **el precio**. In a restaurant you
ask for *la cuenta*. For work done on a **contrato**, the paper has a different
name.

## You'll want to know: la factura
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C311-ASK-07] -->

**la factura** — the invoice, the bill.

*La factura de la luz* — the electricity bill. *¿Me hace una factura?* — could
you make me out an invoice? The verb is **facturar**, to invoice, and also to
check in luggage at an airport.

**la cuenta** is what you ask for at a table; **la factura** is the formal
document, with a number on it, that a business issues and a tax office may want
to see.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04] -->

Latin **facere**, to make or do, plus **-tūra**, an ending that names **the
thing an action produces**.

| verb | the thing it produces |
|---|---|
| facere, to make | **factura**, a making |
| escribir, to write | escritura, a writing |
| leer, to read | lectura, a reading |

A *factura* is literally **a making** — the itemised account of what was made or
done for you. The ending is still productive, so when you meet an unfamiliar
noun in **-ura**, look for the verb inside it before you look it up.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-03] -->

*Facere* is one of the two or three hardest-working roots in the language, and
you can see it under a lot of words that do not look related.

**hacer** is its direct Spanish descendant — *facere* lost its **f** to an **h**
the way *filius* became *hijo*. **satisfacer** is *satis* plus *facere*, to make
enough. **fácil** is what can be done.

So *la factura* and *hacer* are the same word arriving by different routes: one
worn smooth by a thousand years of speech, one lifted off the page by clerks who
needed a formal noun. That is the same doublet shape as **nadar** and **la
natación** — and, as there, the learned twin is the one on the official paper.

A **precio fijo** and a **factura** belong to the same world: both are things
written down so they stop moving.
