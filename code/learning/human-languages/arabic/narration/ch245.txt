Arabic, chapter 245: Necklace, Size, Telephone, Mobile phone, Screen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قلادة (qilāda) — a necklace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for pineapple, then the Arabic for almonds.

You'll want to know: قلادة.
قلادة — qilāda — "a necklace".

The word, taken apart.
قلادة (qilāda) is built on the native Arabic root q-l-d, "to hang on the neck" (Lane). Qilāda is a thing hung on the neck.

Lesson 3 of 5.
هاتف (hātif) — a telephone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a necklace, then the Arabic for a size.

You'll want to know: هاتف.
هاتف — hātif — "a telephone".

The word, taken apart.
هاتف (hātif) is built on the native Arabic root h-t-f, "to call out". Lane's lexicon has no article on this root. Hātif is the active participle, "one who calls out"; the telephone is a modern sense.

Lesson 4 of 5.
جوال (jawwāl) — a mobile phone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a size, then the Arabic for a telephone.

You'll want to know: جوال.
جوال — jawwāl — "a mobile phone".

The word, taken apart.
جوال (jawwāl) is built on the native Arabic root j-w-l, "to roam, to go about" (Lane). Jawwāl is the faʿʿāl adjective, "much roaming"; the mobile phone is a modern sense.
