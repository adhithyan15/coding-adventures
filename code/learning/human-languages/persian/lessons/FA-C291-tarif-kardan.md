# تعریف کردن (ta'rif kardan) — to tell what happened

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C78-WORLD78-05, FA-GRAMMAR-PAST-CONTINUOUS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a story", then "I was reading". (*Dâstân*, *mikhândam*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Chapter two hundred and ninety gave you the two pasts a story is
made of. This is the verb for telling one.

## You'll want to know: تعریف کردن
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-C291-TARIF-KARDAN-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C78-WORLD78-01, FA-GRAMMAR-RA-OBJECT-01, FA-STEM-KON] -->

**تعریف کردن** — *ta'rif kardan* — "to tell", of a story or of what happened.

> **او داستان سفر را تعریف کرد.** — *u dâstân-e safar râ ta'rif kard* — She
> told the story of the trip.

It runs on *kardan*, so the tenses come for free: *ta'rif mikonam*, I tell;
*ta'rif kardam*, I told; *ta'rif mikardam*, I was telling.

Put *az*, "from", in front of a thing and the verb turns to praise: **از هتل
تعریف کرد**, *az hotel ta'rif kard*, "he spoke well of the hotel".

Say it *ta'-rif*, with a small catch after *ta*, as if the word drew breath.
That catch is a letter of its own, and the next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-COMPOUND-VERB-KARDAN] -->

*ta'rif* is borrowed from Arabic *taʿrīf* (EtymDB 2.1). It is usually
explained as "making known", a noun on the root *ʿ-r-f* of Arabic *ʿarafa*,
"to know". *Kardan* is the Persian verb "to do" from chapter seven. So
*ta'rif kardan* is "to do a making-known": you tell a story, and now the
listener knows it. To praise a hotel is to make its good side known.
