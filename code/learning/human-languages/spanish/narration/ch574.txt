Spanish, chapter 574: Cómo me enteré.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
enterarse — to hear about, to find out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to suffer, then the Spanish for to turn out.

You'll want to know: enterarse.
enterarse — "to hear about, to find out".
Me enteré de la noticia por la radio. I heard the news on the radio.
Enterarse de algo — to find out about something. ¿Te has enterado? — have you heard?

The word, taken apart.
enterarse is built on entero 'whole', inherited from Latin integer 'untouched, whole' (Lewis & Short). To find out is to get the whole story.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

What does enterarse mean? ("To hear about, to find out".) Say it once more.

You'll want to know: recibir.
recibir — "to receive, to get".
Recibí una carta de mi tía. I got a letter from my aunt.
It also means to welcome: Nos recibieron muy bien.

The word, taken apart.
recibir is inherited from Latin recipere 'to take back, to receive' (Lewis & Short), built on re- and capere 'to take'.

You'll want to know: la notificación.
la notificación — "the notification, the alert".

Also an official notice: una notificación del juzgado.

The word, taken apart.
notificación is built on notificar, a learned borrowing from Latin nōtificāre 'to make known' (Lewis & Short), built on nōtus 'known' and facere 'to make'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el telediario — the television news.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to receive, then the Spanish for the notification.

You'll want to know: el telediario.
el telediario — "the television news".
Vi la noticia en el telediario de las nueve. I saw the story on the nine o'clock news.
The word in Spain; in Latin America people say el noticiero.

The word, taken apart.
telediario is built on tele-, from Greek tēle 'far', and diario 'daily', which comes from Latin diārium 'a daily allowance' (Lewis & Short), from diēs 'day'.
