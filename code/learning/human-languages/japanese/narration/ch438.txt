Japanese, chapter 438: Stubborn and Hasty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
がんこ (ganko) — stubborn; obstinate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for modest, then the Japanese for spiteful.

You'll want to know: がんこ.
がんこ — ganko — "stubborn; obstinate".
そふは すこし がんこです。 — sofu wa sukoshi ganko desu — "My grandfather is a little stubborn."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
がんこ (ganko) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gan, "stubborn" (Middle Chinese ngwaen) and ko, "harden" (kuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
いばる (ibaru) — to act big; to boss people around.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for short-tempered, then the Japanese for leisurely.

You'll want to know: いばる.
いばる — ibaru — "to act big; to boss people around".
あの せんぱいは いつも いばっています。 — ano senpai wa itsumo ibatte imasu — "That senior acts big all the time."
Polite いばります, ibarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): しゃちょうが いばりました。, shachō ga ibarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
いばる (ibaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict gives two kanji spellings that differ in their first character, so the spelling was attached to the word later (JMdict).
