Mandarin Chinese, chapter 100: Around Town.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yínháng — bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a shower, then the Mandarin for to comb one's hair.

You'll want to know: yínháng.
yínháng — "bank".
yínháng zài nǎr — Where's the bank?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yínháng is built from yín 'silver' and háng 'a row', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngin and hang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Háng is usually explained as a row of shops and then a trade; the step is not given there, so how the pair came to mean a bank is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking where things are.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: yínháng]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yínháng, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to have a shower, then the Mandarin for to comb one's hair, then say yínháng again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does yínháng mean? ("Bank".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.

chāoshì — "supermarket".
wǒ qù chāoshì mǎi shuǐguǒ — I'm off to the supermarket for some fruit.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chāoshì is built from chāo and shì 'market', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trhjew and dzyiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to leap'; 'super' is its sense in this modern word.
