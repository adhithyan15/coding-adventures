# vīdī, vīdit — the past of what happened

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C162-PAS162-01, LA-LEX-C162-PAS162-04, LA-LEX-C162-PAS162-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I recently read a book". (*Nūper librum lēgī.*) Now "before,
I lived in Rome". (*Anteā Rōmae habitāvī.*) And ask "what happened?" (*Quid
accidit?*)

[PAUSE 1s] All three are the **perfect**, Latin's past for a thing that
happened and was over. Here are its six persons.

## Grammar Lens: one stem, six endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-VIDEO-01, LA-LEX-VENIO-01, LA-LEX-SUM-01] -->

Every verb has a **perfect stem**: for *videō*, **vīd-**, with a long *ī*.
Onto it go six endings, the same for every verb:

> **vīdī** — I saw. **vīdistī** — you saw.
> **vīdit** — he saw, she saw. **vīdimus** — we saw.
> **vīdistis** — you all saw. **vīdērunt** — they saw.

Stories lean on two of them: **-it** for one person, **-ērunt** for
several.

[PAUSE 2s] The stem is learned with each verb. *veniō* has **vēn-**:
*vēnit*, *vēnērunt*. *sum* has **fu-**: *fuī*, I was; *fuit*, he was.

[PAUSE 1s] *Vīdī* is also "I have seen"; the sentence decides.

## You'll want to know: the -āvī verbs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-AVI-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-AMBULO-01, LA-LEX-C160-ACT160-05] -->

Most **-āre** verbs add **-āv-**, like the *habitāvī* you own:

> ambulāre — **ambulāvī**, I walked; **ambulāvit**, he walked
> clāmāre — **clāmāvit**, he shouted
> nārrāre — **nārrāvit**, she told; **nārrāvērunt**, they told

The stems learned one by one are the others: *vīdī*, *vēnī*, *fuī*, *lēgī*,
and *bibī*, from *bibō*, with nothing added at all.

## The word, taken apart: fuī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-ETYMON-SUM-02] -->

**fuī** looks nothing like *sum* because it is built on another root, the
one the chapter on *sum* named: Proto-Indo-European *\*bʰuH-*, "to become",
which EtymDB 2.1 lists among the parents of *sum* and which also gives
English *be*.

**vīdī** keeps the root of *videō* with a longer vowel (Lewis & Short give
*vīdi* as its perfect). That vowel is how the ear tells *videt*, "he sees",
from *vīdit*, "he saw".

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-AVI-02, LA-ETYMON-SUM-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "He saw"? (***Vīdit.***) "They came"? (***Vēnērunt.***) "I
walked"? (***Ambulāvī.***) Which ending says "they"? (***-ērunt.***) And
which root is behind *fuī*? (***\*bʰuH-***, "to become", the root of English
*be*.)
