Lesson 1 of 6.
la cathédrale — the cathedral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to wander, then the French for to pace up and down.

You'll want to know: la cathédrale.
la cathédrale — "the cathedral".
La cathédrale est au centre de la vieille ville. The cathedral is in the middle of the old town.
The bishop's church.

The word, taken apart.
cathédrale is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin (ecclēsia) cathedrālis 'church with a bishop's chair', from cathedra 'chair', from Greek kathédra (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: le clocher.
le clocher — "the bell tower, the steeple".
On voit le clocher du village de très loin. The village steeple is visible from far away.
L'esprit de clocher — small-town thinking.

The word, taken apart.
clocher is built on cloche 'bell', which goes back to Late Latin clocca, usually traced to a Celtic word.

Lesson 4 of 6.
le beffroi — the belfry (town bell tower).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the basilica, then the French for the bell tower.

You'll want to know: le beffroi.
le beffroi — "the belfry (town bell tower)".
Du haut du beffroi, la vue sur la ville est superbe. From the top of the belfry the view over the town is superb.
Many towns in the north of France and in Belgium have a beffroi.

The word, taken apart.
beffroi is usually traced to Middle High German bercfrit 'tower that protects'; EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
les remparts — the town walls, the ramparts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the bell tower, then the French for the belfry.

You'll want to know: les remparts.
les remparts — "the town walls, the ramparts".
Le soir, nous faisons le tour des remparts. In the evening we walk round the town walls.
Mostly plural.

The word, taken apart.
rempart is built on remparer 'to fortify again', from re- and emparer, which is usually traced to Provençal amparar 'to protect', from Latin ante and parāre (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.
