French, chapter 458: Au bord de la mer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la dune — the dune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the moss, then the French for the bark.

You'll want to know: la dune.
la dune — "the dune".
Derrière la plage, il y a de hautes dunes. Behind the beach there are high dunes.
Une dune de sable — a sand dune.

The word, taken apart.
dune was borrowed from Middle Dutch dūne (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le récif — the reef.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tide, then the French for the cove.

You'll want to know: le récif.
le récif — "the reef".
Les bateaux évitent le récif près du port. The boats steer clear of the reef near the harbour.
Un récif de corail — a coral reef.

The word, taken apart.
récif was borrowed from Spanish arrecife, which is usually traced to Arabic ar-raṣīf 'causeway, raised way'. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

le coquillage — "the seashell; the shellfish".
La petite fille ramasse des coquillages sur la plage. The little girl collects shells on the beach.
Les coquillages are also shellfish to eat.

The word, taken apart.
coquillage is built on coquille 'shell' (EtymDB lists it), which goes back to Vulgar Latin conchilia, from Latin concha 'shellfish, shell'.
