Hindi, chapter 21: Numbers Six to Ten.
2 lessons. All 2 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 2.
छह, सात, आठ, नौ, दस — six to ten, the same erosion twice more.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You already watched trīṇi wear down to tīn in two careful steps. Two more numbers here do exactly the same thing.

You'll want to know — The five.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
6. Hindi: छह. said: chhah.
7. Hindi: सात. said: sāt.
8. Hindi: आठ. said: āṭh.
9. Hindi: नौ. said: nau.
10. Hindi: दस. said: das.

You'll want to know — sapta becomes satta becomes sāt, aṣṭa becomes aṭṭha becomes āṭh — the same mechanism again.
Put seven and eight beside their Sanskrit ancestors:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
Sanskrit: saptá. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: satta. Hindi: sāt.
Sanskrit: aṣṭá. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: aṭṭha. Hindi: āṭh.
This is the identical two-step process you already watched turn trīṇi into tīn:
The consonant cluster (-pt-, -ṣṭ-) simplifies into a doubled consonant (-tt-, -ṭṭh-) — the same weight, packed into one place.
The doubled consonant simplifies again, and the vowel lengthens to pay for what was lost — satta becomes sāt, aṭṭha becomes āṭh.
Same trade, same reason, two more numbers. This is exactly what the anchor lesson meant by "not decay in any bad sense" — it's the standard Prakrit weight-conservation move, and by now you've seen it three times.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

(Cluster simplifies to a geminate, then the geminate simplifies again and the vowel lengthens — the same weight-conservation move, three numbers running.) Lesson 2 of 2.
छह, नौ, दस — three different paths.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Seven and eight repeated a familiar weight trade. Six, nine, and ten show that not every number took the same road.

You'll want to know — ṣaṣ becomes chhah — a sibilant moves forward.
छह (chhah) continues Sanskrit ṣaṣ, "six." The retroflex sibilant ṣ shifted forward into Hindi छ (ch). This is separate from cluster simplification, but it is still two thousand years of ordinary speech reshaping a Sanskrit word.

You'll want to know — nava becomes nau — a consonant dissolves.
नौ (nau) continues Sanskrit nava. The v between vowels weakened and disappeared; the vowels on either side contracted into the diphthong -ava- becomes -au-. Compare pāṁch: there a nasal moved into the vowel; here an intervocalic consonant dissolved between two vowels.

You'll want to know — daśa becomes das — the quiet path.
दस (das) continues Sanskrit daśa most plainly: the final vowel drops. There is no cluster to simplify and no middle consonant to lose.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ṣaṣ … chhah" — the sibilant shifts]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "nava … nau" — v dissolves into a diphthong]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "daśa … das" — only the final vowel drops]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What happened to v in nava becomes nau? (It dissolved between vowels, which contracted.) Which form changed least? (Das — only the final vowel dropped.) Count six to ten once more. (Chhah, sāt, āṭh, nau, das.)
