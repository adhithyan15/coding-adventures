# ਪਰਿਵਾਰ — what surrounds you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-DOST, PA-ETYMON-DOST-CHOOSE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **ਦੋਸਤ** (*dost*) named one person, chosen. This word names the group you
never chose at all.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-PARIVAR]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਪਰਿਵਾਰ** — *parivār* — **family**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ਪ** *p*, **ਰ** *r*, **ਿ** short *i*, **ਵ** *v* — the same **ਵ** you read in
**ਵੇਖਣਾ** — **ਾ** long *ā*, **ਰ** *r* again. *Pa-ri-vā-r*, four syllables, no
new letter in any of them.

## The word, taken apart — two Sanskrit pieces, still visible
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-PARIVAR-SURROUND]; assesses=[PA-LEX-DOST, PA-LEX-ROTI] -->

**ਪਰਿਵਾਰ** comes straight from Sanskrit **parivāra**, and unlike **dost** or
**roṭī**, this one splits cleanly into two working pieces: **pari-**,
"**around**," plus **vṛ-**, "**to cover, to surround**." A *parivāra* is, word
for word, what encloses you — the same **pari-** that opens *parikramā*, a
walk made *around* something sacred, and a **vṛ-** that also sits inside
*varṇa*, "covering" — the word Sanskrit used for both colour and social
class.

Unlike **dost**, this is Sanskrit through and through. Unlike **roṭī**, its
root is not a mystery — you can point at both halves and say what each one
meant on its own.
