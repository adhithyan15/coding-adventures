Japanese, chapter 483: Clay and China.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ねんど (nendo) — clay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for made of wood, then the Japanese for to stick into.

You'll want to know: ねんど.
ねんど — nendo — "clay".
こどもが ねんどで ねこを つくりました。 — kodomo ga nendo de neko o tsukurimashita — "The child made a cat out of clay."

The word, taken apart.
ねんど (nendo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nen, "sticky" (Middle Chinese nrjem) and do, "soil" (thuX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
せともの (setomono) — china; earthenware.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to stick into, then the Japanese for clay.

You'll want to know: せともの.
せともの — setomono — "china; earthenware".
せとものの ちゃわんを かいました。 — setomono no chawan o kaimashita — "I bought a china teacup."

The word, taken apart.
せともの (setomono) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: seto, written with the characters for "shallows" and "door", and mono, "thing" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

うつわ (utsuwa) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かためる (katameru) — to harden; to make firm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for china, then the Japanese for a bowl.

You'll want to know: かためる.
かためる — katameru — "to harden; to make firm".
ねんどを てで かためます。 — nendo o te de katamemasu — "I press the clay firm with my hands."
Polite かためます, katamemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): こどもが ゆきを かためました。, kodomo ga yuki o katamemashita.

The word, taken apart.
かためる (katameru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, the transitive partner of katamaru, "to harden"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
かたまる (katamaru) — to set hard; to harden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bowl, then the Japanese for to harden.

You'll want to know: かたまる.
かたまる — katamaru — "to set hard; to harden".
ねんどは いちにちで かたまります。 — nendo wa ichinichi de katamarimasu — "The clay sets in a day."
Polite かたまります, katamarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ねんどが かたまりました。, nendo ga katamarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かたまる (katamaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, the intransitive partner of katameru, "to make firm"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
