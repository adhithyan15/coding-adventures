Tamil, chapter 79: When.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
எப்போது (eppōdu) — when?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say so, therefore. Then say: tea for you.

You'll want to know: எப்போது.
எப்போது (eppōdu) — "when?"
You have had இப்போது, "now", since chapter forty-eight, and you have had the pattern behind it since chapter forty: i- for near, a- for far, e- for a question.
So this word was already half yours:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: இப்போது, ippōdu, now — this time.
word: எப்போது, eppōdu, when? — which time.
The shared piece is போது, a word meaning a time, an occasion. Change only the front and you have walked from a statement to a question, exactly as இங்கே walked to எங்கே.
எப்போது (eppōdu) வேண்டும்? — "When do you want it?"

What you've built.
The question word for time, and the small word போது that sits inside it.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say adaṉāl, then say uṅgaḷukkāga, then ask eppōdu?]

Lesson 2 of 4.
அப்போது (appōdu) — then, at that time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say for you. Then ask when?

You'll want to know: அப்போது.
அப்போது (appōdu) — "then, at that time".
The third seat was empty and now it is filled:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
இப்போது, ippōdu, now.
அப்போது, appōdu, then.
எப்போது, eppōdu, when?
Near, far, question — the same three fronts you first met on இங்கே, அங்கே, எங்கே.
Then in Tamil means a time somebody has already named. It looks backwards, the way அதனால் does, and for the same reason: both begin on the a- that means that one, over there.
நாளை. அப்போது (appōdu) சொல்லுங்கள். — "Tomorrow. Tell me then."

What you've built.
A complete set of three, from a pattern you were given nearly forty chapters ago.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: all three in order — ippōdu, appōdu, eppōdu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — notice: the front that means that one, in அப்போது (appōdu) and in அதனால்]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say uṅgaḷukkāga, then ask eppōdu?, then answer appōdu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say aṟuvaṭai, read பாலும் and say ēṉeṉṟāl — three distances back — then say when one of them happens]

Lesson 3 of 4.
எப்போது (eppōdu) — the doubled letter, read across a seam.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: எப்போது — one letter, twice, on either side of a புள்ளி and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: say now, then, when? in that order.

Script you'll notice: எப்போது — one letter, twice, on either side of a புள்ளி.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
piece: எ, e, the free-standing short e, because the word opens on it.
piece: ப், p, ப with a புள்ளி.
piece: போ, pō, ப with the wrapping ோ sign.
piece: து, du, த with the ு sign.
எ, ப், போ, து becomes எப்போது (eppōdu).
The middle is the shape you first read in சட்டை: the same letter written twice, the first one wearing a புள்ளி and leaning on the second. Here it is ப, and the doubling is exactly what makes the pp you hear.
Read the seam and the word divides itself: எ is the question front from chapter forty, and போது is the time-word. அப்போது (appōdu) is the same page with one letter changed at the start.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — read: எப்போது (eppōdu), then அப்போது (appōdu)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the two ப shapes, and say which one carries no vowel]
[your turn — recall: ask eppōdu?, then answer appōdu, then read எப்போது]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say -um … -um illai, say kuḍi and read ஏன் — three distances back — then say when one of them happens]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read எப்போது (eppōdu). Which one letter would you change to read then instead? (The first — எ becomes அ.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
-உம் போது (-um pōdu) — when one thing happens, so does another.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read எப்போது (eppōdu). Then say then, at that time.

Grammar lens: the time-word does the joining.
எப்போது (eppōdu) asks about a time. போது on its own can pin one, and when a verb stands in front of it the whole thing becomes when that happens.
The verb takes -உம் first:
வா becomes வரும் becomes வரும் போது — "at the coming time" — "when [you] come".
நீங்கள் வரும் போது, சொல்லுங்கள்.
nīṅgaḷ varum pōdu, solluṅgaḷ. — "When you come, tell me."
Two things happening at once, held together by a word that means time. No word for when is needed, because போது already is one.
One warning, because it would otherwise mislead you. The -உம் here is not the joining ending from chapter seventy-four. That one sat on nouns and meant and. This one sits on verbs and marks a happening. Same two letters, two different jobs — and the word in front of them tells you which.
Everyday speech runs it together and clips it, so you will hear varumbōdu as one word.

What you've built.
The last of this chapter's three: a time you ask about, a time you point at, and a time you build out of a verb.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: varum pōdu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nīṅgaḷ varum pōdu, solluṅgaḷ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — separate: say pālum and then varum — the same ending, two different jobs]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: answer appōdu, then read எப்போது (eppōdu), then say the whole varum pōdu line]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — return to: say -ā, naḍa and adaṉāl — three distances back — then say when one of them happens]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two words end in -உம்: பாலும் and வரும். What tells them apart? (What they sit on — a naming word joins, a doing word marks a happening.)
