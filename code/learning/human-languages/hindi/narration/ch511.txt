Hindi, chapter 511: Clay and Fire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चाक (cāk) — a potter's wheel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to knock in, then the Hindi for to bore.

You'll want to know: चाक.
चाक — cāk — "a potter's wheel".
कुम्हार चाक पर मिट्टी के दीये बनाता है। Kumhār cāk par miṭṭī ke dīye banātā hai. The potter makes clay lamps on his wheel.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
चाक (cāk) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit cakra, "a wheel" (CDIAL 4538). CDIAL gives the Hindi word as "any kind of wheel"; at the potter's it is his wheel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ढालना (ḍhālnā) — to pour into a mould; to cast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a potter's kiln, then the Hindi for a mould.

You'll want to know: ढालना.
ढालना — ḍhālnā — "to pour into a mould; to cast".
लोहार ने गरम लोहा साँचे में ढाला। Lohār ne garam lohā sā̃ce meṁ ḍhālā. The blacksmith poured hot iron into the mould.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने एक घंटी ढाली, usne ek ghaṇṭī ḍhālī.

The word, taken apart.
ढालना (ḍhālnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from ḍhalati, "bends over, falls", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5581). CDIAL lists Hindi ḍhālnā, "to pour", under its causative; casting is pouring into a mould.
