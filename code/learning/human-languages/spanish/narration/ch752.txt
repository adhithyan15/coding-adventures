Lesson 1 of 5.
el ajedrez — chess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the collection, then the Spanish for watercolour.

You'll want to know: el ajedrez.
el ajedrez — "chess".
Juego al ajedrez con mi abuelo. I play chess with my grandfather.
Jugar al ajedrez (in Latin America also jugar ajedrez).

The word, taken apart.
ajedrez was borrowed from Andalusian Arabic aš-šaṭranj (EtymDB), which came from Persian and, further back, from Sanskrit caturaṅga "the four-limbed army".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la baraja — the pack of cards.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for chess, then the Spanish for the jigsaw puzzle.

You'll want to know: la baraja.
la baraja — "the pack of cards".
Saca la baraja y jugamos. Get the cards out and let us play.
Barajar — to shuffle. La baraja española has 40 or 48 cards.

The word, taken apart.
baraja is built on barajar (EtymDB), which in Old Spanish meant to quarrel, then to mix up and shuffle. Beyond that the origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
apostar — to bet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pack of cards, then the Spanish for magic.

You'll want to know: apostar.
apostar — "to bet".
Te apuesto a que gana el Madrid. I bet you Madrid win.
Te apuesto a que... — I bet you that... Stem change: apuesto.

The word, taken apart.
apostar is usually explained as built on puesto "put, placed", the participle of poner, which comes from Latin pōnere "to put": to bet is to put money down.
