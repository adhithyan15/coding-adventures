Mandarin Chinese, chapter 48: Telling the Time.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
点 (diǎn) — o'clock; dot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for this year, then the Mandarin for date.

You'll want to know: 点.
点 — diǎn — "o'clock; dot".
我们明天八点去 wǒmen míngtiān bā diǎn qù — We're going at eight tomorrow.
The new character, in pieces: 点 (diǎn) — a small vertical and bar on top of 口, standing over four dots. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
点 (diǎn) is inherited from Middle Chinese temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a dot made with the brush'. Its use for the hours is not given there, so how it took that job is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for times and partings.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: diǎn, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒmen míngtiān bā diǎn qù — We're going at eight tomorrow.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for this year, then the Mandarin for date, then say diǎn again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 点 (diǎn) mean? ("O'clock; dot".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 6.
点 (diǎn) — a mark on 口, over four dots.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a mark on 口, over four dots and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.

The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say diǎn — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
口 明.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kǒu, míng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a mark on 口, over four dots.
点 (diǎn).
Three pieces, top to bottom.
Two strokes on top. A short vertical, then a short horizontal from its middle out to the right.
口, three strokes, wide, under them.
Four dots along the bottom, left to right; the first leans left, the other three lean right.
2 + 3 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 点 (diǎn), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 点 (diǎn)? (Three.) Which character you know is in the middle? (口.) How many dots at the bottom? (Four.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 点 (diǎn), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for minute, then the Mandarin for half.

You'll want to know: jǐ.
jǐ — "how many; a few".
xiànzài jǐ diǎn — What time is it?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
jǐ asks for a small number, as in jǐ diǎn; duōshao asks for any amount.

The word, taken apart.
jǐ is inherited from Middle Chinese kj+jX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'how many'.
