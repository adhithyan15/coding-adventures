# petit, petite — the ending that wakes the letter before it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-ADJ-AGREEMENT-04, FR-LEX-BON-03, FR-PHON-CONSONNE-FINALE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **bon** and **bonne**. You have had both since page one, and the
difference between them has never been named.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-FEMININ-E-01]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-ADJ-AGREEMENT-04, FR-PHON-CONSONNE-FINALE-01, FR-LEX-BON-03, FR-GRAM-ADJ-APRES-NOM-10] -->

> The feminine of an adjective adds **-e**. That **-e** is itself silent — and it
> makes the consonant in front of it audible.

> **petit** — *puh-TEE*. · **petite** — *puh-TEET*.

> **grand** — *grahn*. · **grande** — *grahnd*.

Nothing is added to the sound at the end. What changes is that the *t* and the
*d* stop being final, so the rule from two lessons ago no longer silences them.

This is worth holding on to, because it makes the feminine **easier to hear than
to see**. A French listener knows *petite* from *petit* instantly — one ends in a
consonant and one does not — while a reader has to notice a letter that is never
said.

The colours behave the same way, and you already own three of them: *vert* is
*vair* and *verte* is *vairt*; *blanc* and *blanche*; *noir* and *noire*, where
the *r* was already sounding and nothing changes at all.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-FEMININ-E-01, FR-PHON-CONSONNE-FINALE-01, FR-GRAM-ADJ-AGREEMENT-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Is the feminine *-e* pronounced? (**No.**) Then what does it do?
(**It stops the consonant before it being final**, so that consonant sounds.) Is
the feminine easier to hear or to see? (**To hear** — the difference is a whole
consonant.)
