French, chapter 527: Repasser et ranger.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le fer à repasser — iron (for clothes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for clothes peg, then the French for coat hanger.

You'll want to know: le fer à repasser.
le fer à repasser — "iron (for clothes)".
Le fer à repasser est encore chaud. The iron is still hot.
Often le fer alone. La planche à repasser — the ironing board.

The word, taken apart.
le fer à repasser is "an iron for pressing": fer is inherited from Latin ferrum "iron" (Lewis & Short), and repasser is built on passer.

Lesson 2 of 5.
froissé — creased, crumpled; offended.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for coat hanger, then the French for iron.

You'll want to know: froissé.
froissé — "creased, crumpled; offended".
Ta chemise est toute froissée. Your shirt is all creased.
Also hurt, offended: il est froissé par ta remarque.

The word, taken apart.
froissé is the participle of froisser "to crumple" (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin frustiāre "to break into bits", from Latin frustum "a crumb, a bit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
tremper — to soak; to dip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for iron, then the French for creased.

You'll want to know: tremper.
tremper — "to soak; to dip".
Je fais tremper le pantalon taché dans l'eau froide. I am soaking the stained trousers in cold water.
Faire tremper — to leave to soak. Être trempé — to be soaked.

The word, taken apart.
tremper is inherited from Latin temperāre "to mix in due proportion" (Lewis & Short), with its letters later shifted; the sense moved to mixing with water.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la penderie — wardrobe (for hanging clothes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for creased, then the French for to soak.

You'll want to know: la penderie.
la penderie — "wardrobe (for hanging clothes)".
Les manteaux sont dans la penderie de l'entrée. The coats are in the hall wardrobe.
A cupboard with a rail for hanging; l'armoire has shelves too.

The word, taken apart.
la penderie is built on pendre "to hang" with -erie; pendre is inherited from Latin pendēre "to hang" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
accrocher — to hang (up), to hook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to soak, then the French for wardrobe.

You'll want to know: accrocher.
accrocher — "to hang (up), to hook".
Il accroche sa veste derrière la porte. He hangs his jacket behind the door.
Accrocher un tableau — to hang a picture. S'accrocher — to hold on.

The word, taken apart.
accrocher is built on croc "a hook" (EtymDB), which comes from Frankish krōk, a Germanic word.
