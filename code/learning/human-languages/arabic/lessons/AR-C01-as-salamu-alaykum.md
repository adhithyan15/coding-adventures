# السلام عليكم (as-salāmu ʿalaykum) — "peace be upon you"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Now assemble the pieces — *al-* and *salām* — into the great
universal greeting of the Arabic-speaking world.

## The letters in this word

*(Skim if you read Arabic.)* Three new letters, all in *ʿalaykum*:

- **ع** = *ʿayn* — a voiced tightening deep in the throat, with **no English
  equivalent**; approximate it for now.
- **ي** = *yāʾ*, "y" · **ك** = *kāf*, "k."

The first half, **السلام**, is just *al-* + *salām* — already yours.

## The word, taken apart

**السلام عليكم** = **as-salāmu** (*al-* + *salām*, with the sun-letter *s*
assimilating the *l*) + **ʿalaykum** ("upon you [all]"). Literally **"the peace
[be] upon you."**

Notice something: there is **no word for "is."** Arabic sets the two phrases
side by side — "the-peace / upon-you" — and lets "is" be understood. (Where
Spanish would need *está*, Arabic needs nothing.)

## Why it's said this way

This is the greeting with real weight — any time of day, any register. Its reply
is **fixed**: **وعليكم السلام** (*wa-ʿalaykum as-salām*) — "*and* upon you the
peace." You give peace; it is given back. To greet, in Arabic, is literally to
bless.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Read **السلام عليكم**. What are its two pieces, and why is the *s*
doubled? (*al-*+*salām* → *as-salām* (sun letter) + *ʿalaykum*.) Where is the
word for "is"? (There isn't one — Arabic leaves it understood.)
