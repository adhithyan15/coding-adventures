Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 28: Too Dear: The Answer and the Counter.
14 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 14.
Hear ghaṇo — very.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the price payoff, the request payoff, and the six-word weather payoff, then say expensive and cheap.

You'll want to know.
ghaṇo — very.
Two beats. The first carries the breathed gh of ghar; the second carries the same retroflex ṇ as kitṇo and pāṇī.
The source prints it in front of a description, where English puts very: the food is ghaṇo tasty. Put it in front of mahaṅgo and you have said more than that a thing costs money: you have said it costs too much, which is the whole reason a bargain begins.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.

Lesson 3 of 14.
Hear ye ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai — this is very expensive.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Say the price question, write very and expensive, then say and write the sweet crumbled wheat dessert.

You'll want to know.
ye ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai — this is very expensive.
Four words, every one of them already yours. ye points, ghaṇo strengthens, mahaṅgo describes, hai closes. The order is fixed: the strengthener always comes before the word it strengthens.
Say it once flat, as a fact, and once slowly, the way you would actually say it across a counter when you mean to keep talking. The words do not change; the pace does, and the pace is what tells a seller a bargain has begun.

Lesson 6 of 14.
Hear thoṛu — a little.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Recall the three-word transport payoff, then say cart and bus and write both.

You'll want to know.
thoṛu — a little.
Two beats. The first is the breathed th of ṭhelā; the middle carries the flapped ṛ you already say inside kapṛā and ghoṛo.
Hold it against ghaṇo, which you learned four lessons ago. ghaṇo makes a word bigger; thoṛu makes an amount smaller. A seller hears the difference at once, and it is the difference between complaining about a price and offering to meet it.

Lesson 7 of 14.
थोड़ु (thoṛu) — a little, on the page.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Say a little, then write clothes and horse.

You'll want to know.
थो + ड़ु becomes थोड़ु — thoṛu — a little.
Three of this track's most easily confused marks meet in four signs. ड़ is ड with the nukta under it, exactly as in कपड़ा and घोड़ो; the short ु hangs beneath it. Write कपड़ा, घोड़ो, and थोड़ु (thoṛu) in a column and check that all three dots sit at the same height.

Guided Practice.
Look at थोड़ु (thoṛu), cover it, wait five seconds, and write it. Check the dot, then the ु below it.

Lesson 8 of 14.
Hear karo — make it.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the seven-word shopping payoff, then write a little and cheap.

You'll want to know.
karo — make it; do it.
Two beats, and the ending is one you have already heard. Say dikhāvo and karo one after the other: both close on the same -o, because both are the same kind of asking word, said to one person across a counter.
dikhāvo asks somebody to show you a thing. karo asks them to make something so — and what they are to make it, you say in front of the word.

Lesson 9 of 14.

क + रो becomes करो — karo — make it.
Three signs, and the middle one is र, the very first sign this book taught. Set करो (karo) beside ख and say both: क and ख differ only by breath, and only one of them belongs in this word.

Lesson 10 of 14.
Hear thoṛu sasto karo — make it a little cheaper.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Say this is very expensive, then say item and cheap and write both.

You'll want to know.
thoṛu sasto karo — make it a little cheaper.
Three words, all yours: thoṛu shrinks the amount, sasto is the sastā you learned in Chapter 19, and karo asks.
The source prints sasto here, with -o rather than the -ā the earlier lesson taught. Both forms are used; the ending shifts with what the word is describing. Learn the printed line as it stands, and treat sastā and sasto as the same word wearing two endings rather than two different words.
Say the whole exchange in order: ask the price, say it is too much, then this.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.

What does thoṛu sasto karo ask for?


Lesson 11 of 14.
थोड़ु सस्तो करो। (thoṛu sasto karo.) — the counter-offer on the page.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know.
थोड़ु (thoṛu) | सस्तो | करो (karo)।.
Three written words. सस्तो is the सस्ता of Chapter 19 with ो where ा stood; its vowel-killer between the two स does not move. The stop at the end is the । you have written since Chapter 9.

Lesson 13 of 14.
Payoff — ask, react, counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the price payoff and the seven-word shopping payoff, then write clothes.

Guided Practice.
Three turns, in this order and no other.
Hear the three lines shuffled and put them back in order.
Produce all three from meaning cues, as one continuous exchange.
Read the three printed lines and say who speaks which.
Write all three from dictation, without a model.
Pass each skill separately. You can now open a price, react to it, and make one counter-offer. Hearing a number in reply, agreeing a final price, and paying are still untaught.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9; Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.

Lesson 14 of 14.
Close the writing loop for ़, ् and ं — delayed closure for the three marks this chapter can least afford to drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]

Guided Practice.
