Portuguese, chapter 337: Dar argumentos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
argumentar — to argue (with reasons), to make a case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to debate, then the Portuguese for the argument.

You'll want to know: argumentar.
argumentar — "to argue (with reasons), to make a case".
Ela argumentou que o bairro precisa de mais árvores. She argued that the neighbourhood needs more trees.
Argumentar que… — to argue that… It is about giving reasons, not about quarrelling.

The word, taken apart.
argumentar is a learned borrowing from Latin argūmentārī "to adduce proof of a thing" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), formed on argūmentum "proof".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[your turn — recall: say the Portuguese for to debate, then the Portuguese for the argument, then say argumentar again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does argumentar mean? ("To argue (with reasons), to make a case".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
lógico — logical.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the argument, then the Portuguese for to argue.

You'll want to know: lógico.
lógico — "logical".
A tua resposta é lógica: se chove, ficamos em casa. Your answer is logical: if it rains, we stay at home.
É lógico! — of course! Feminine lógica; a lógica is also the noun logic.

The word, taken apart.
lógico is a learned borrowing from Latin logicus (EtymDB), from Greek logikós "of speech, of reasoning", built on lógos "word, reason".

Lesson 3 of 5.
convincente — convincing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to argue, then the Portuguese for logical.

You'll want to know: convincente.
convincente — "convincing".
Não foi uma resposta muito convincente. It was not a very convincing answer.
The opposite is pouco convincente. One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
convincente goes back to Latin convincēns, the present participle of convincere "to overcome, to prove" (Lewis & Short); it is a learned form beside the inherited verb convencer.

Lesson 4 of 5.
persuadir — to persuade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for logical, then the Portuguese for convincing.

You'll want to know: persuadir.
persuadir — "to persuade".
Tentei persuadir o meu pai a comprar um carro mais pequeno. I tried to persuade my father to buy a smaller car.
Persuadir alguém a fazer algo — to persuade someone to do something.

The word, taken apart.
persuadir is a learned borrowing from Latin persuādēre "to convince, to persuade" (EtymDB), built from per- and suādēre "to advise, to urge" (Lewis & Short).
