# raconter — to tell a story, which is to count it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-DABORD-01, FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can hold a scene still and put events in order with
*d'abord*. This lesson gives the act a name, then puts it all together.

## You'll want to know: raconter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-RACONTER-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C232-TRAV232-05, FR-LEX-FRERE-06, FR-GRAM-IMPERATIF-01] -->

**raconter** — "to tell", for a story or for what happened. It is a regular
*-er* verb: *je raconte*, *j'ai raconté*, *je racontais*.

> *Je raconte une histoire à mon frère.* — I tell my brother a story.
> *Raconte !* — Go on, tell me!

You have **l'histoire**, which is both "history" and "a story", so
**raconter une histoire** is the whole act in three words. The person who
hears it comes after **à**.

## Grammar Lens: the backbone of a told story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-RECIT-SQUELETTE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-MAIS-03, FR-LEX-C153-VERBES153-03, FR-LEX-C137-VERBES89-01, FR-LEX-CETAIT-01, FR-GRAM-RECIT-ORDRE-02] -->

Here is what a story tells, with both pasts to work with:

> **C'était** samedi. **Il faisait** beau. **D'abord, je suis allé** au
> marché. **Ensuite, j'ai rencontré** un ami, et nous **avons parlé**. **Mais
> il a commencé** à pleuvoir, et **enfin je suis rentré** à la maison.
>
> *It was Saturday. The weather was fine. First I went to the market. Then I
> met a friend, and we talked. But it started to rain, and in the end I went
> back home.*

*Il fait beau* is fine weather, with the *beau* you met inside *beaucoup*.
*C'était*, *il faisait*: the scene, held still. *Je suis allé*, *j'ai
rencontré*, *nous avons parlé*, *il a commencé*, *je suis rentré*: the
events, put in order by *d'abord*, *ensuite* and *enfin*. **Mais** marks the
turn, the point where the story stops going the way it was going. *Et* adds;
*mais* turns.

## The word, taken apart: counting it out
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**raconter** goes back to Old French *raconter* (EtymDB 2.1). It is built on
**conter**, "to tell", which EtymDB 2.1 traces to Latin *computāre*, "to
reckon up, to compute" (Lewis & Short, under *computo*), with *re-* and *a-*
in front. To tell a story was to count it out, one piece after another.
English kept both senses in one word: you **count** a sum, and you
**recount** what happened.
