## You'll want to know: 后天
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C147-HOUTIAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**后天** — *hòutiān* — "the day after tomorrow".

> **后天我们去** *hòutiān wǒmen qù* — We're going the day after tomorrow.

> **今天 明天 后天** *jīntiān, míngtiān, hòutiān* — today, tomorrow, the day after

The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. It is the *hòu* you already say for behind and after.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**后天** (*hòutiān*) is built from 后 'after' and 天 'day'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *huwX* and *then* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun lists *huwX* under the traditional form of the character and glosses it 'before and after'; it glosses *then* 'the heavens above', so 'day' is assumed here, not quoted.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C45-TODAY45-02, ZH-LEX-C45-TODAY45-03] -->

后天 takes its place in the row of days you already say: *zuótiān*, 今天, 明天, 后天.

Like the others it goes early in the sentence, before the verb: **后天我们去** *hòutiān wǒmen qù*.
