Latin, chapter 470: Adze, Drill, Plane, File, To hew.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ascia, asciae — an adze, a carpenter's axe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a blacksmith, then the Latin for to hammer.

You'll want to know: ascia, asciae.
ascia, asciae — "an adze, a carpenter's axe".
Faber asciam in manū tenet. The carpenter holds an adze in his hand.
First declension, feminine: ascia, asciae f. A tool with the blade across the handle, for shaping beams.

The word, taken apart.
ascia is inherited; Lewis & Short compare Greek axinē 'axe' as a cousin. EtymDB gives no parent, so the link is hedged.

terebra, terebrae — "a drill, a borer, an auger".
Faber terebrā in tabulā labōrat. The carpenter works on the board with a drill.
First declension, feminine: terebra, terebrae f., from terō 'to rub, to wear away'.

The word, taken apart.
terebra is built on terō 'to rub, to wear away' with the tool suffix -bra (EtymDB): a tool that bores by turning.

Lesson 3 of 6.
runcīna, runcīnae — a plane (a carpenter's tool).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an adze, then the Latin for a drill.

You'll want to know: runcīna, runcīnae.
runcīna, runcīnae — "a plane (a carpenter's tool)".
Faber tabulam runcīnā parat. The carpenter prepares the board with a plane.
First declension, feminine: runcīna, runcīnae f. An iron blade in a wooden block, for smoothing wood.

The word, taken apart.
runcīna was borrowed from Greek rhykanē 'plane', as an ancient grammarian already said (Lewis & Short); the form was changed to look like runcō 'to weed'.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a drill, then the Latin for a plane.

You'll want to know: līma, līmae.
līma, līmae — "a file (tool); polishing".
Faber ferrum līmā pūrgat. The smith cleans the iron with a file.
First declension, feminine: līma, līmae f. Also the polishing of a poem: līmae labor, the work of the file.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of līma is not settled: Lewis & Short say plainly that it is unknown, and EtymDB gives no sure parent.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dolō, dolāre — to hew, to chop into shape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plane, then the Latin for a file.

You'll want to know: dolō, dolāre.
dolō, dolāre — "to hew, to chop into shape".
Fabrī trabēs asciīs dolant. The carpenters hew the beams with adzes.
dolō, dolāre, dolāvī, dolātum. Rough shaping of wood with an axe or adze.

The word, taken apart.
dolō is inherited from Proto-Italic dolaō (EtymDB); Lewis & Short link it with a root meaning 'to split, to tear apart'.
