# recordar — to remember, and to remind

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C364-REF-30, ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-22] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el corazón** and **la cita**. The heart is inside the verb
that keeps the appointment.

## You'll want to know: recordar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C464-CITA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-22] -->

**recordar** — to remember, and also to remind.

*No recuerdo su nombre* — I don't remember his name. *Recuérdame la cita* —
remind me about the appointment. *Te recuerdo que mañana es fiesta* — I remind
you that tomorrow is a holiday.

One verb, both jobs. Which it is depends on whether somebody else is being
told: *recuerdo* alone is remembering, *te recuerdo* is reminding.

## Grammar Lens: recordar is not acordarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C464-CITA-01, ES-LEX-C463-GUIA-01] -->

Spanish has a second verb for remembering and the two are near-twins:

| Spanish | what it takes |
|---|---|
| *recordar algo* | a plain object |
| *acordarse **de** algo* | *de*, always |

*No recuerdo la dirección* and *no me acuerdo de la dirección* mean the same
thing. The difference is grammatical, not semantic: *recordar* takes the thing
directly, *acordarse* needs its **de**, and dropping it is one of the commonest
mistakes learners make.

Both are **o → ue** verbs: *recuerdo*, *me acuerdo*. The same break you have
been tracking since *la rueda*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C464-CITA-01, ES-LEX-C364-REF-30] -->

Latin **recordari**: **re-**, back, plus **cor**, **cordis** — **the heart**.

To remember is to **bring back through the heart**, because Latin located
memory and thought there rather than in the head. That is the same *cor* inside
**el corazón**, which you have had for many chapters.

The family is everywhere once seen: **el acuerdo**, an agreement — hearts
brought together — and **recordar**'s English cousin **record**, which is
literally a thing committed to memory. **Courage** is *cor* too, by way of
French, and so is **concordance**.

So a Spanish speaker asking you to remember something is asking you to run it
back past your heart.
