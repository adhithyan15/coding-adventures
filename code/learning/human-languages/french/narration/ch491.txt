French, chapter 491: Quand ça se termine.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le conjoint — spouse, partner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for eldest, then the French for younger.

You'll want to know: le conjoint.
le conjoint — "spouse, partner".
Les conjoints sont invités à la fête de l'entreprise. Spouses are invited to the company party.
A woman is la conjointe. Formal: married or living together. In Québec, le conjoint is very common in speech.

The word, taken apart.
le conjoint is the participle of conjoindre "to join together", from Latin coniungere, so coniūnctus "joined", built on iungere "to join" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
séparer — to separate; (se séparer) to split up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for younger, then the French for spouse.

You'll want to know: séparer.
séparer — "to separate; (se séparer) to split up".
Après dix ans de vie commune, ils se sont séparés. After ten years together, they split up.
Reflexive for couples: se séparer.

The word, taken apart.
séparer is a learned borrowing from Latin sēparāre "to part, to separate" (EtymDB), built from sē- "apart" and a verb parāre; which parāre is meant is not settled (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
rompre — to break up; to break.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for to separate, then the French for separation.

You'll want to know: rompre.
rompre — "to break up; to break".
Il a rompu avec sa copine au mois de mai. He broke up with his girlfriend in May.
Rompre avec quelqu'un — to break up with someone. Present il rompt.

The word, taken apart.
rompre is inherited from Latin rumpere "to break, to burst" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Après la rupture, elle est partie vivre à la campagne. After the break-up, she went to live in the country.

The word, taken apart.
la rupture is a learned borrowing from Late Latin ruptūra "a break", from rumpere "to break" (Lewis & Short).
