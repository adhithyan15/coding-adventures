Portuguese, chapter 365: Justo e injusto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a justiça — the justice; the courts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to show, then the Portuguese for to demand.

You'll want to know: a justiça.
a justiça — "the justice; the courts".
As pessoas querem justiça. People want justice.
Fazer justiça — to do justice. A justiça also means the courts as a system.

The word, taken apart.
justiça comes from Latin iūstitia "justice, equity" (Lewis & Short), through Old Portuguese justiça (EtymDB); it is built on iūstus "fair, righteous".

You'll want to know: injusto.
injusto — "unfair, unjust".

Feminine injusta. The opposite of justo.

The word, taken apart.
injusto is a learned borrowing from Latin iniūstus "unjust" (Lewis & Short), built from in- "not" and iūstus "fair, righteous".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o preconceito — the prejudice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for unfair, then the Portuguese for the injustice.

You'll want to know: o preconceito.
o preconceito — "the prejudice".
Ainda há muito preconceito contra os idosos. There is still a lot of prejudice against old people.
Ter preconceitos — to be prejudiced.

The word, taken apart.
preconceito is built from pre- "before" and conceito "idea", from Latin conceptus "a collecting, a conceiving", on concipere "to take in", built on capere "to take" (Lewis & Short): an idea formed before the facts.

Muitos filmes repetem o mesmo estereótipo. Many films repeat the same stereotype.
First a printing plate cast in one solid piece.

The word, taken apart.
estereótipo is built from Greek stereós "solid" and tipo "type" (EtymDB), probably through French stéréotype; it first named a printing plate cast in one solid piece.
