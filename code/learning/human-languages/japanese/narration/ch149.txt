Japanese, chapter 149: Putting Clothes On and Taking Them Off.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きる (kiru) — to wear; to put on (clothes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to grow a beard, then the Japanese for make-up.

You'll want to know: きる.
きる — kiru — "to wear; to put on (clothes)".
あさ ふくを きます。 — asa fuku o kimasu — "I put on my clothes in the morning."
Polite きます, kimasu; an ichidan verb. Shoes and trousers go on with はく, haku.

The word, taken apart.
きる (kiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ようふく (yōfuku) — Western clothes; clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to take off, then the Japanese for to wear on the head.

You'll want to know: ようふく.
ようふく — yōfuku — "Western clothes; clothes".
ちちは まいにち ようふくを きます。 — chichi wa mainichi yōfuku o kimasu — "My father wears Western clothes every day."

The word, taken apart.
ようふく (yōfuku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yō, "ocean, Western" (Middle Chinese yang) and fuku, "clothing" (bjuwk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
したぎ (shitagi) — underwear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to wear on the head, then the Japanese for Western clothes.

You'll want to know: したぎ.
したぎ — shitagi — "underwear".
まいにち したぎを あらいます。 — mainichi shitagi o araimasu — "I wash my underwear every day."

The word, taken apart.
したぎ (shitagi) is native Japanese, built from shita, "under", and ki, "wearing", from kiru, voiced to gi; both parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
