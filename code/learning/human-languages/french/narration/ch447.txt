6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
le pressing — the dry cleaner’s.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the locksmith, then the French for to sew back on.

You'll want to know: le pressing.
le pressing — "the dry cleaner’s".
Il dépose sa veste au pressing le lundi. He drops his jacket off at the dry cleaner's on Mondays.
Le nettoyage à sec — dry cleaning.

The word, taken apart.
pressing was borrowed from English pressing 'ironing', a sense English does not use for a shop. EtymDB does not list it.

What you've built.

la laverie — "the launderette".
Les étudiants lavent leur linge à la laverie. The students wash their clothes at the launderette.
Une laverie automatique; in Québec, une buanderie.

The word, taken apart.
laverie is built on laver 'to wash', which is inherited from Latin lavāre (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
détacher — to remove a stain from; to untie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the dry cleaner’s, then the French for the launderette.

You'll want to know: détacher.
détacher — "to remove a stain from; to untie".
Le pressing a bien détaché ma chemise blanche. The dry cleaner got the stain out of my white shirt.
Two senses: from tache 'stain', and from attacher — détacher sa ceinture, to undo one's belt.

The word, taken apart.
détacher in the sense 'to untie' comes from Old French destachier (EtymDB), the opposite of attacher; in the sense 'to remove a stain' it is built on tache 'stain'.

You'll want to know: teindre.
teindre — "to dye".
Elle a teint son vieux pull en bleu. She dyed her old jumper blue.
Irregular: je teins, j'ai teint. La teinturerie — another word for the dry cleaner's.

The word, taken apart.
teindre is inherited through Old French taindre from Latin tingere 'to dye, to wet' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
retoucher — to alter (a garment); to touch up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to remove a stain from, then the French for to dye.

You'll want to know: retoucher.
retoucher — "to alter (a garment); to touch up".
Le tailleur a retouché le bas du pantalon. The tailor altered the bottom of the trousers.
Une retouche — an alteration; also on photos.

The word, taken apart.
retoucher is built from re- 'again' and toucher 'to touch' (EtymDB lists the verb), which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin verb that imitates a knock.
