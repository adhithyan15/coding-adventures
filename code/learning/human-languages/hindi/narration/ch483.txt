Hindi, chapter 483: Strength and Breath.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ताक़त (tāqat) — strength; power.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for fair-skinned, then the Hindi for lean.

You'll want to know: ताक़त.
ताक़त — tāqat — "strength; power".
दूध पीने से बच्चों को ताक़त मिलती है। dūdh pīne se baccoṁ ko tāqat miltī hai. Drinking milk gives children strength.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ताक़त (tāqat) is usually traced to Arabic ṭāqa, "ability, strength", which reached Hindi through Persian.

अस्सी की उम्र में भी नानी बहुत चुस्त हैं। assī kī umr meṁ bhī nānī bahut cust haiṁ. Even at eighty, my grandmother is very spry.

The word, taken apart.
चुस्त (cust) is usually traced to Persian chust, "quick, active, tight". Clothes can be cust too: close-fitting.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.

दम — dam — "stamina; breath".
दादा में अभी भी बहुत दम है। dādā meṁ abhī bhī bahut dam hai. Grandfather still has plenty of stamina.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
दम (dam) is usually traced to Persian dam, "breath", and so the strength to keep going. dam lenā is to catch one's breath.

Lesson 4 of 6.
बल (bal) — force; might.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for spry, then the Hindi for stamina.

You'll want to know: बल.
बल — bal — "force; might".
अरुण ने पूरे बल से दरवाज़ा धकेला। Aruṇ ne pūre bal se darvāzā dhakelā. Arun pushed the door with all his strength.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
बल (bal) is inherited from Sanskrit bála, "strength, power": CDIAL lists Hindi bal, "strength, ability", under it (CDIAL 9161). dublā is built on its opposite, durbala.

Lesson 5 of 6.
थकाना (thakānā) — to tire someone out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for stamina, then the Hindi for force.

You'll want to know: थकाना.
थकाना — thakānā — "to tire someone out".
लंबे सफ़र ने बच्चों को थका दिया। lambe safar ne baccoṁ ko thakā diyā. The long journey tired the children out.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चों को थकाया, usne baccoṁ ko thakāyā.

The word, taken apart.
थकाना (thakānā) is the causative partner of thaknā, "to become weary", which CDIAL lists under sthakk, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 13737).
