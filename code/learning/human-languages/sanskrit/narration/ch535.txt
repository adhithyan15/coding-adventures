Sanskrit, chapter 535: Keeping One's Word.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रतिपालयति (pratipālayati) — keeps to; abides by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for turns down, then the Sanskrit for consent.

You'll want to know: प्रतिपालयति.
प्रतिपालयति — pratipālayati — "keeps to; abides by".
यदि सः नियमं प्रतिपालयति, तर्हि सर्वे तं विश्वसन्ति। yadi saḥ niyamaṃ pratipālayati, tarhi sarve taṃ viśvasanti. If he keeps the rule, everyone trusts him.
Past (he …): सः नियमं प्रतिपालितवान्, saḥ niyamaṃ pratipālitavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिपालयति (pratipālayati) is built on prati and the root √pāl, "to guard, to keep", which also gives pālayati; MW gives pratipālayati as to protect, to keep, to observe, and to wait for (MW).

You'll want to know: सत्यसन्धः.
सत्यसन्धः — satyasandhaḥ — "true to one's word".
यदि सः सत्यसन्धः अस्ति, तर्हि सः अवश्यम् आगच्छति। yadi saḥ satyasandhaḥ asti, tarhi saḥ avaśyam āgacchati. If he keeps his word, he will surely come.

The word, taken apart.
सत्यसन्धः (satyasandhaḥ) is built from satya, "true", and saṃdhā, "a compact, a promise"; MW gives satyasaṃdha as true to engagements, keeping one's promise (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
विश्वस्तः (viśvastaḥ) — trusted; trustworthy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for keeps to, then the Sanskrit for true to one's word.

You'll want to know: विश्वस्तः.
विश्वस्तः — viśvastaḥ — "trusted; trustworthy".
यदि मित्रं विश्वस्तम् अस्ति, तर्हि भयं न अस्ति। yadi mitraṃ viśvastam asti, tarhi bhayaṃ na asti. If a friend is trustworthy, there is nothing to fear.

The word, taken apart.
विश्वस्तः (viśvastaḥ) is built on vi and the root √śvas, "to breathe", as its past participle, the root of viśvasiti, "trusts"; MW gives viśvasta as full of confidence, and as trusted, faithful (MW).

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for true to one's word, then the Sanskrit for trusted.

You'll want to know: भंगः.
भंगः — bhaṅgaḥ — "a breaking".
यदि नियमस्य भंगः भवति, तर्हि अध्यापकः कुपितः भवति। yadi niyamasya bhaṅgaḥ bhavati, tarhi adhyāpakaḥ kupitaḥ bhavati. If a rule is broken, the teacher gets angry.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
भंगः (bhaṅgaḥ) comes from the root √bhañj, "to break"; MW gives bhaṅga as breaking, splitting, breaking down (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
वचनम् (vacanam) — one's word; a statement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for trusted, then the Sanskrit for a breaking.

You'll want to know: वचनम्.
वचनम् — vacanam — "one's word; a statement".

A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
वचनम् (vacanam) comes from the root √vac, "to speak", with the ending -ana; MW gives vacana as an utterance, a statement, a word (MW).
