### The finding is where the marks already were

**Every Kannada sentence in every reading passage in this book ends in a Latin
full stop**, and until this chapter no lesson had named one. The chapter opens
on exactly that: look at the end of any line in the chapter before it, and
something is sitting there that nothing has told you about.

Three points cost one short chapter because **Kannada borrows the whole Latin
set** — shape and job together. There is no second system to learn and nothing
in the chapter looks unfamiliar, which is the honest reason it is short.

### The one contrast worth the page

`KA-A1-L-13` derives from a Spanish demand that includes the **inverted opening
mark**. Kannada has no counterpart, so a reader meets the question mark at the
end or not at all — and **the words have to carry the question until then**.
That changes reading aloud: the rise in the voice comes from recognising a
question word, not from a mark not yet reached.

### Read rather than written

`KA-A1-L-14` is probed as a **recognition** point. The colon, the brackets, the
quotation marks and the dash are what a notice or a form puts in front of a
reader; at this level the demand is knowing what each signals rather than
deciding where to place one. The recall lesson sorts the whole set into the
three a reader writes — **.** **,** **?** — and the rest they read.

That lesson introduces nothing, and it is there for the reason the last two
chapters in this campaign also needed one: an atom introduced by a chapter's
last lesson has nothing left to revisit it.

### What this does not close

`KA-A1-L-09` — *the script is not closed: 27 characters are used but never
taught* — **stays open**. Nineteen characters remain after chapters 67 to 73,
and punctuation does not touch that debt. A test assertion that used to guard
"the script is still short" by counting this column has been rewritten to name
`KA-A1-L-09` directly, because the column now houses punctuation too and the
count moved without the character debt shrinking by one.

**Four of Kannada's unmapped points can never be covered** and are marked
untransferable: capital letters, written accentuation and superscript
abbreviation letters have no Kannada counterpart at all, and neither does
Spanish's mid-distance demonstrative. The real ceiling for this track is
**254/258**.

61 exam points remain open. Coverage means the teaching exists, not that a
reader scores.
