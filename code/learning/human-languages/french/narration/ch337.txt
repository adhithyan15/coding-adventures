French, chapter 337: Trouver un accord.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
admettre — to admit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to retort, then the French for to contradict.

You'll want to know: admettre.
admettre — "to admit".
Je dois admettre que ton idée était meilleure. I have to admit that your idea was better.
Conjugated like mettre; past participle admis. It also means to let in: être admis à l'université.

The word, taken apart.
admettre comes through Old French admettre from Latin admittere "to let in, to allow" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from ad "to" and mittere "to send": you let a fact in.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: le consensus.
le consensus — "the consensus, broad agreement".
Après deux heures, nous avons trouvé un consensus. After two hours, we reached a consensus.
The final s is said: consensuss.

The word, taken apart.
consensus was borrowed whole from Latin cōnsēnsus "agreement, accord" (EtymDB), from cōnsentīre "to agree", built on sentīre "to feel" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le compromis — the compromise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to admit, then the French for the consensus.

You'll want to know: le compromis.
le compromis — "the compromise".
Finalement, nous avons trouvé un compromis: un film, puis une pizza. In the end we found a compromise: a film, then a pizza.
Trouver un compromis — to reach a compromise. The same form in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
compromis comes from Latin compromissum "a mutual promise to abide by an arbiter's award" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), built on prōmittere "to promise".

Lesson 4 of 5.
céder — to give in, to give way; to hand over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the consensus, then the French for the compromise.

You'll want to know: céder.
céder — "to give in, to give way; to hand over".
Il a insisté, et j'ai fini par céder. He insisted, and I ended up giving in.
Céder à quelqu'un — to give in to someone. Je cède, with è before a silent ending.

The word, taken apart.
céder is a learned borrowing from Latin cēdere "to go, to withdraw, to yield" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
concéder — to concede, to grant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the compromise, then the French for to give in.

You'll want to know: concéder.
concéder — "to concede, to grant".
Je te concède ce point: tu avais raison. I grant you that point: you were right.
Je concède, like céder. Formal; in speech, je reconnais que is common.

The word, taken apart.
concéder comes from Latin concēdere "to withdraw, to grant" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), a strengthened form of cēdere "to go, to yield".
