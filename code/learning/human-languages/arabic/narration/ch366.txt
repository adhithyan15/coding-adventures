Arabic, chapter 366: Claims and doubts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ادعى (iddaʿā) — to claim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to refute, then the Arabic for to be convinced.

You'll want to know: ادعى.
ادعى — iddaʿā — "to claim".
ادعى الرجل أنه طبيب. iddaʿā ar-rajul annahu ṭabīb. The man claimed that he was a doctor.
Present: يدعي, yaddaʿī.
Followed by أن. The noun is ادعاء, iddiʿāʾ, a claim.

The word, taken apart.
ادعى (iddaʿā) is built on the native Arabic root d-ʿ-w, "to call, to summon" (Lane). It is Form VIII, to call a thing one's own: to claim.

Lesson 2 of 5.
زعم (zaʿama) — to allege.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be convinced, then the Arabic for to claim.

You'll want to know: زعم.
زعم — zaʿama — "to allege".
زعم التاجر أن الساعة من ذهب. zaʿama at-tājir anna as-sāʿa min dhahab. The merchant alleged that the watch was made of gold.
Present: يزعم, yazʿum.
زعم (zaʿama) suggests the speaker doubts the claim.

The word, taken apart.
زعم (zaʿama) is built on the native Arabic root z-ʿ-m, "to assert" (Lane). Zaʿama is a Form I verb; Lane notes that it often marks a claim that may be untrue.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: صدق.
صدق — ṣaddaqa — "to believe someone".
لم يصدق أحد قصته. lam yuṣaddiq aḥad qiṣṣatah. Nobody believed his story.
Present: يصدق, yuṣaddiq.
Form I صدق (ṣaddaqa), ṣadaqa, with the same letters, means to tell the truth.

The word, taken apart.
صدق (ṣaddaqa) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-d-q, "to be truthful" (Lane). It is Form II, to hold to be truthful: to believe someone.
