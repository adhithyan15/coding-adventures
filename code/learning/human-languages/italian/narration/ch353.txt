Italian, chapter 353: Come reagire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la reazione — the reaction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to unleash, then the Italian for to trigger.

You'll want to know: la reazione.
la reazione — "the reaction".
La sua reazione mi ha sorpreso. Her reaction surprised me.
Una reazione forte — a strong reaction.

The word, taken apart.
reazione is built from re- "back" and azione "action", a learned borrowing from Latin āctiō "a doing", from agere "to do, to drive" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il pretesto — the pretext.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the reaction, then the Italian for to react.

You'll want to know: il pretesto.
il pretesto — "the pretext".
Ha usato il mal di testa come pretesto per non uscire. He used the headache as a pretext for not going out.
Con il pretesto di — on the pretext of.

The word, taken apart.
pretesto is a learned borrowing from Latin praetextus "an outward show, a pretext" (Lewis & Short), from praetexere "to weave in front, to cover", built on texere "to weave".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la scusa — the excuse; the apology.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to react, then the Italian for the pretext.

You'll want to know: la scusa.
la scusa — "the excuse; the apology".
Non cercare scuse: hai sbagliato tu. Do not look for excuses: you got it wrong.
Chiedere scusa — to apologise. Scusa! — sorry! (to a friend); scusi! to a stranger.

The word, taken apart.
scusa is built on scusare "to excuse", inherited from Latin excūsāre "to free from a charge" (Lewis & Short), built from ex "out of" and causa "a case, a charge".

Lesson 5 of 5.
giustificare — to justify.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the pretext, then the Italian for the excuse.

You'll want to know: giustificare.
giustificare — "to justify".
Niente può giustificare un comportamento così. Nothing can justify behaviour like that.
Giustificarsi — to justify yourself. At school, giustificare un ritardo — to give a reason for being late.

The word, taken apart.
giustificare is a learned borrowing from Late Latin iūstificāre "to deal justly with, to justify" (Lewis & Short), built from iūstus "right, fair" and facere "to make".
