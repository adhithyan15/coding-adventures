# आपके पास क्या है? — asking what somebody has, without a verb for having

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *mere pās ek kitāb hai*. Now turn it into a question addressed
to somebody you would call *āp*, and notice that *āpkā* will have to bend.

## Grammar Lens: the question bends too
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-05, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-06, HI-CONCEPT-C02-MERAA-01] -->

| | sentence |
|---|---|
| question | आपके पास क्या है? |
| answer | मेरे पास एक किताब है। |

*Āpkā* becomes *āpke* for the same reason *merā* becomes *mere*: **पास** stands
after it. The question and the answer are the identical construction with the
owner swapped.

There is no verb for *have* anywhere in either line. **है** is the ordinary
copula, and it is agreeing with *kitāb*.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-05, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-04, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-02] -->

Read aloud. Every word has been taught:

> — आपके पास क्या है?
> — मेरे पास एक किताब है।
> — तुम्हारे पास क्या है?
> — मेरे पास एक कलम है।
> — हमारे घर में क्या है?
> — हमारे घर में सब है।

Six lines, three different owners, and the same bend in every one of them:
*āpke*, *tumhāre*, *hamāre* — every possessive ending in **-ए** because a
postposition stands after it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-05, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which word in *mere pās ek kitāb hai* means *have*? (None — the
construction is *near me there is*.) Why is it *āpke pās* rather than *āpkā
pās*? (*Pās* is a postposition, so the possessive goes oblique.)
