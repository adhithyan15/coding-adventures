Spanish, chapter 746: Engaños y coqueteos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
acariciar — to stroke, to caress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to betray, then the Spanish for betrayal.

You'll want to know: acariciar.
acariciar — "to stroke, to caress".
Le gusta acariciar al gato. She likes stroking the cat.
Una caricia — a caress.

The word, taken apart.
acariciar is built on caricia, which was borrowed from Italian carezza (EtymDB), from caro "dear", Latin cārus.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ligar — to chat up, to pick up; to tie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to stroke, then the Spanish for the deception.

You'll want to know: ligar.
ligar — "to chat up, to pick up; to tie".
Fue a la discoteca a ligar. He went to the club to pick someone up.
Colloquial, mainly in Spain: ligar con alguien. Also to tie, to bind.

The word, taken apart.
ligar is usually traced to Latin ligāre "to tie, to bind" (Lewis & Short); the slang sense of flirting grew from the idea of tying.

Lesson 4 of 5.
coquetear — to flirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the deception, then the Spanish for to chat up.

You'll want to know: coquetear.
coquetear — "to flirt".
Coquetea con todo el mundo. She flirts with everyone.
Coqueto — flirtatious; also vain about one's looks.

The word, taken apart.
coquetear is built on coqueta, borrowed from French coquette (EtymDB), from coq "cockerel": showing off like a strutting cock.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el amante — the lover; lover of something.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to chat up, then the Spanish for to flirt.

You'll want to know: el amante.
el amante — "the lover; lover of something".
Es un amante de la música. He is a music lover.
La amante for a woman. Amante de — keen on.

The word, taken apart.
amante comes from Latin amāns, amantem "loving" (EtymDB), the present participle of amāre.
