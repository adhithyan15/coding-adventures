Arabic, chapter 112: Longing, Ambition, Purpose, Project, Suggestion.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شوق (shawq) — longing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a wish, then the Arabic for a preference.

You'll want to know: شوق.
شوق — shawq — "longing".

The word, taken apart.
شوق (shawq) is built on the native Arabic root sh-w-q, "to long for" (Lane). Shawq is the verbal noun, "longing".

Lesson 3 of 5.
غاية (ghāya) — a purpose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for longing, then the Arabic for ambition.
[pause 1 second]
Name the four seasons. (Rabīʿ, ṣayf, kharīf, shitāʾ.) Which one also names two lunar months? (Rabīʿ.)

You'll want to know: غاية.
غاية — ghāya — "a purpose".

The word, taken apart.
غاية (ghāya) is built on the native Arabic root gh-y-y, "the utmost limit, a goal" (Lane). Lane's ghāya is the furthest point of a thing.

Lesson 4 of 5.
مشروع (mashrūʿ) — a project.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for ambition, then the Arabic for a purpose.

You'll want to know: مشروع.
مشروع — mashrūʿ — "a project".

The word, taken apart.
مشروع (mashrūʿ) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-ʿ, "to enter upon; to lay down a law" (Lane). Mashrūʿ is the passive participle, "laid down, lawful"; the project is a modern sense.
