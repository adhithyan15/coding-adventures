Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 257: Anger and Shame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
रीस (rīs) — anger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for joy, then the Marwadi for high spirits.

You'll want to know: रीस.
रीस — rīs — "anger".
रीस मत करो। rīs mat karo. Don't get angry.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
रीस (rīs) is inherited from Sanskrit riṣ, "injury". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Gujarati rīs and Hindi ris, both "anger", under it; how harm became anger is not spelled out there (CDIAL 10746).

Lesson 2 of 5.
रूसणो (rūsṇo) — to sulk; to take offence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for high spirits, then the Marwadi for anger.

You'll want to know: रूसणो.
रूसणो — rūsṇo — "to sulk; to take offence".
छोटी बहन रोज रूसै है। chhoṭī bahan roj rūsai hai. My little sister sulks every day.
Past (he …): वो बेटो रूस्यो, vo beṭo rūsyo.

The word, taken apart.
रूसणो (rūsṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit ruṣyati, "is angry"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari rūsaï, "to get angry", under it (CDIAL 10794).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ओळमो (oḷmo) — a reproach; a complaint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for modesty, then the Marwadi for a sigh.

You'll want to know: ओळमो.
ओळमो — oḷmo — "a reproach; a complaint".
यो ओळमो साचो है। yo oḷmo sācho hai. This complaint is fair.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ओळमो (oḷmo) is inherited from Sanskrit upālambha, "reproach"; CDIAL lists Marwari olamũ, "reproach", under it (CDIAL 2314). This book writes its l as ळ, as in काळो and ओळखणो.
