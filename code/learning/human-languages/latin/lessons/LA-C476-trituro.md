## You'll want to know: trītūrō, trītūrāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C476-TRITURO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**trītūrō, trītūrāre** — "to thresh".

> In āreā servī frūmentum trītūrant.
>
> *On the threshing floor the slaves thresh the grain.*

*trītūrō, trītūrāre, trītūrāvī, trītūrātum*, from *trītūra* 'threshing'. Oxen often trod the grain out. A late word; earlier writers say *frūmentum terere*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*trītūrō* is built on *trītūra* 'threshing' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from *terō* 'to rub'. English *triturate* 'to grind to powder' comes from it.
