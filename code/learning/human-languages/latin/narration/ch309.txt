Latin, chapter 309: Accūsātor et patrōnus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
accūsō, accūsāre — to accuse, to charge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a law, then the Latin for law.

You'll want to know: accūsō, accūsāre.
accūsō, accūsāre — "to accuse, to charge".
Mercātōrem fūrtī accūsāvērunt. They accused the merchant of theft.
accūsō, accūsāre, accūsāvī, accūsātum, with the genitive of the charge: fūrtī accūsāre.

The word, taken apart.
accūsō is built on causa, "a cause, a lawsuit", with ad- (EtymDB): to call someone to account. English accuse comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
accūsātor, accūsātōris — a prosecutor, an accuser.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for law, then the Latin for to accuse.

You'll want to know: accūsātor, accūsātōris.
accūsātor, accūsātōris — "a prosecutor, an accuser".
Accūsātor longam ōrātiōnem habuit. The prosecutor made a long speech.
Masculine, 3rd declension. Rome had no public prosecutor: any citizen could act as accūsātor, and young men won fame that way.

The word, taken apart.
accūsātor is built on accūsō, "to accuse" (EtymDB), with the ending -tor for the one who does it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
advocātus, advocātī — a supporter in court, a legal adviser.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to accuse, then the Latin for a prosecutor.

You'll want to know: advocātus, advocātī.
advocātus, advocātī — "a supporter in court, a legal adviser".
Reus multōs amīcōs advocātōs habēbat. The defendant had many friends supporting him in court.
Masculine. In Cicero an advocātus stood by a friend in court; later the word meant a pleader. English advocate.

The word, taken apart.
advocātus is the participle of advocō, "to call to oneself" (EtymDB), from ad and vocō, "to call": one who has been called in. English advocate comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
patrōnus, patrōnī — a patron; a defending counsel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a prosecutor, then the Latin for a supporter in court.

You'll want to know: patrōnus, patrōnī.
patrōnus, patrōnī — "a patron; a defending counsel".
Patrōnus prō reō dīxit et iūdicēs mōvit. The counsel spoke for the defendant and moved the judges.
Masculine. A patron protected his dependants and spoke for them in court. English patron.

The word, taken apart.
patrōnus is built on pater, "father" (EtymDB): one who stands to others as a father. English patron comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
testimōnium, testimōniī — evidence, testimony.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a supporter in court, then the Latin for a patron.

You'll want to know: testimōnium, testimōniī.
testimōnium, testimōniī — "evidence, testimony".
Testimōnium ancillae ūtile erat. The maid's evidence was useful.
Neuter, from testis, 'witness' (taught). Testimōnium dīcere: to give evidence.

The word, taken apart.
testimōnium is built on testis, "witness" (EtymDB), with the ending -mōnium. English testimony comes from it.
