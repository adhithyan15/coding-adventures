Spanish, chapter 723: Generosos y egoístas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
generoso — generous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for cowardly, then the Spanish for to dare.

You'll want to know: generoso.
generoso — "generous".
Mi tío es muy generoso con todos. My uncle is very generous to everyone.
Ser generoso con alguien.

The word, taken apart.
generoso comes from Latin generōsus "of noble birth" (EtymDB), built on genus "birth, family"; noble birth was expected to give freely.

egoísta — "selfish; the selfish person".

The word, taken apart.
egoísta is built on Latin ego "I" with the ending -ista; the word probably came in through French égoïste.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la amabilidad — kindness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for generous, then the Spanish for selfish.

You'll want to know: la amabilidad.
la amabilidad — "kindness".
Gracias por su amabilidad. Thank you for your kindness.
A polite formula in letters and shops. The adjective is amable.

The word, taken apart.
amabilidad is a learned borrowing from Latin amābilitās (EtymDB), built on amābilis "lovable", from amāre "to love".

Lesson 4 of 5.
tacaño — stingy, mean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for selfish, then the Spanish for kindness.

You'll want to know: tacaño.
tacaño — "stingy, mean".
No seas tacaño y paga la cena. Do not be stingy, pay for dinner.
The opposite of generoso. Feminine tacaña.

The word, taken apart.
tacaño was borrowed from Italian taccagno "stingy" (EtymDB); further back the origin is not certain.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: cruel.
cruel — "cruel".
Fue muy cruel con su hermano. He was very cruel to his brother.
Same form for men and women; plural crueles.

The word, taken apart.
cruel is usually traced to Latin crūdēlis "hard-hearted, cruel" (Lewis & Short), a word built on crūdus "raw, bloody".
