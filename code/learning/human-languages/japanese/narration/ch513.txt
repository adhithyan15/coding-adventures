Lesson 1 of 5.
きかくする (kikaku suru) — to plan (an event); to organize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to clash (of plans), then the Japanese for changing every day.

You'll want to know: きかくする.
きかくする — kikaku suru — "to plan (an event); to organize".
もし かれが りょこうを きかくしたら、たのしいでしょう。 — moshi kare ga ryokō o kikakushitara, tanoshii deshō — "If he plans the trip, it will surely be fun."
Polite きかくします, kikakushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): かいしゃが りょこうを きかくしました。, kaisha ga ryokō o kikakushimashita.

The word, taken apart.
きかくする (kikaku suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun kikaku with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "undertake" and kaku, "brush-stroke" (Middle Chinese hweak, the reading of this character for "to plan") (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
みなおす (minaosu) — to look over again; to review.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for changing every day, then the Japanese for to plan (an event).

You'll want to know: みなおす.
みなおす — minaosu — "to look over again; to review".
もし もんだいが あったら、よていを みなおします。 — moshi mondai ga attara, yotei o minaoshimasu — "If there is a problem, I will review the schedule."
Polite みなおします, minaoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): せんせいが しけんを みなおしました。, sensei ga shiken o minaoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
みなおす (minaosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from miru, "to see", and naosu, "to fix"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
だんどり (dandori) — arrangements; the order of the steps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to bring forward (a date), then the Japanese for to adjust.

You'll want to know: だんどり.
だんどり — dandori — "arrangements; the order of the steps".
もし だんどりが よかったら、しごとは はやく おわるでしょう。 — moshi dandori ga yokattara, shigoto wa hayaku owaru deshō — "If the arrangements are good, the work will probably finish early."

The word, taken apart.
だんどり (dandori) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese dan, "steps" (Middle Chinese dwanH), borrowed from Chinese, and native dori, "taking", the voiced form of tori, from toru, "to take" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
