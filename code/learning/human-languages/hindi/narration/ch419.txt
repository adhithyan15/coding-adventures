5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तिराहा (tirāhā) — a T-junction, a meeting of three roads.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for beside, then the Hindi for to deliver.

You'll want to know: तिराहा.
तिराहा — tirāhā — "a T-junction, a meeting of three roads".
तिराहे से बाएँ मुड़िए। tirāhe se bāẽ muṛiye. Turn left at the T-junction.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
तिराहा (tirāhā) is the three-way partner of चौराहा: ti-, "three", as in तिकोना, with rāh, "road", usually traced to Persian rāh.

Lesson 2 of 5.
चक्कर (cakkar) — a round; a detour; dizziness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to deliver, then the Hindi for a T-junction.

You'll want to know: चक्कर.
चक्कर — cakkar — "a round; a detour; dizziness".
ग़लत रास्ते की वजह से हमें लंबा चक्कर लगाना पड़ा। ġalat rāste kī vajah se hamẽ lambā cakkar lagānā paṛā. Because of the wrong road we had to go a long way round.
Masculine.
चक्कर (cakkar) आना, to feel dizzy: मुझे चक्कर (cakkar) आ रहा है.

The word, taken apart.
चक्कर (cakkar) is a semi-learned form of Sanskrit cakra, "a wheel, a circle" (CDIAL 4538); the fully inherited Hindi form in that entry is cāk, "a wheel, a millstone". A चक्कर is any going round: a detour, a round of the market, a spinning head.
