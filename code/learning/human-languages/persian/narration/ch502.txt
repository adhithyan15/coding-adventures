Persian, chapter 502: Babies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
گهواره (gahvâre) — a cradle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for twins, then the Persian for a child.

You'll want to know: گهواره.
گهواره (gahvâre) — "a cradle".
مادر بچه را در گهواره گذاشت. mâdar bachche râ dar gahvâre gozâsht. The mother put the baby in the cradle.
Plural gahvârehâ.

The word, taken apart.
gahvâre is inherited: EtymDB traces it to a Middle Persian word meaning 'cot, cradle' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
لالایی (lâlâyi) — a lullaby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a child, then the Persian for a cradle.

You'll want to know: لالایی.
لالایی (lâlâyi) — "a lullaby".
مادربزرگ هر شب برای نوه اش لالایی میخواند. mâdarbozorg har shab barâ-ye naveash lâlâyi mikhânad. Grandmother sings her grandchild a lullaby every night.
lâlâyi khândan — to sing a lullaby. In baby talk lâlâ kardan means 'to sleep'.

The word, taken apart.
lâlâyi is built on lâlâ, the soothing sound sung to a baby, with the ending -i. It is usually explained as imitative, and EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
پوشک (pushak) — a nappy, a diaper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a cradle, then the Persian for a lullaby.

You'll want to know: پوشک.
پوشک (pushak) — "a nappy, a diaper".
پدر پوشک بچه را عوض کرد. pedar pushak-e bachche râ 'avaz kard. The father changed the baby's nappy.
pushak 'avaz kardan — to change a nappy.

The word, taken apart.
pushak is usually linked with the native verb pushidan, 'to cover, to wear', with the ending -ak. EtymDB has no entry for it, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 5 of 5.
شیر دادن (shir dâdan) — to breastfeed; to feed (milk).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a nappy, then the Persian for a pram.

You'll want to know: شیر دادن.
شیر دادن (shir dâdan) — "to breastfeed; to feed (milk)".
مادر هر سه ساعت به بچه شیر میدهد. mâdar har se sâ'at be bachche shir midahad. The mother feeds the baby every three hours.
shir 'milk' + dâdan 'to give'; present stem deh-.

The word, taken apart.
shir dâdan is built on two inherited words: shir, 'milk', usually traced to Middle Persian šīr, and dâdan, 'to give'.
