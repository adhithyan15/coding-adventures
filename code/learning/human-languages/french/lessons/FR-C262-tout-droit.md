# tout droit — straight on, and then the turns

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C188-LIEU188-04, FR-LEX-C188-LIEU188-05, FR-LEX-EXCUSEZ-MOI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "on the right". (*À droite.*) Then "on the left". (*À
gauche.*)

[PAUSE 1s] You asked the way, and a stranger is answering. This lesson is
what you will hear.

## You'll want to know: tout droit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-TOUT-DROIT-01]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPERATIF-01, FR-LEX-ALLER-06] -->

**tout droit** — "straight on".

> *Allez **tout droit**.* — Go straight on.

Listen hard to the end of it. In **droit** the final *t* is silent: "drwa".
In **droite** it is said: "drwat". *Tout droit* is straight ahead; *à
droite* is a turn to the right. The two sound almost alike, and they send
you in different directions, so a careful listener waits for that *t*.

## Grammar Lens: directions, as commands
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-TOURNEZ-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C179-VERBES179-05, FR-LEX-C144-VERBES144-05, FR-LEX-C208-VERBES208-02, FR-LEX-C98-TEMPS50-05] -->

A stranger gives directions in the *vous* command: the *vous* form of the
present, without the *vous*.

> *Allez tout droit, puis **tournez** à gauche.* — Go straight on, then turn
> left.
> ***Continuez** jusqu'au musée.* — Keep going as far as the museum.
> ***Traversez** la rue.* — Cross the street.
> ***Prenez** la rue à droite.* — Take the street on the right.

Five verbs carry almost every set of directions: *aller*, *tourner*,
*continuer*, *traverser* and *prendre*. The order words come back too:
*d'abord*, *puis*, *ensuite*. And a kind stranger may end with a distance:
*C'est à deux minutes.* — It's two minutes away.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**tout** is inherited from Latin *tōtus*, "all, whole" (EtymDB 2.1), so
*tout droit* is "all straight", the whole way. **droit** has no parent in
EtymDB 2.1; it is usually traced to Latin *dīrēctus*, "set straight" (Lewis &
Short, under *dirigo*), which English kept as **direct**. **Droite**, the
right hand, is the same word in the feminine. That the right hand was named
as the "straight" or proper one is the usual account, offered here as that.
