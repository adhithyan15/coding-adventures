Japanese, chapter 9: One More Time, Please.
12 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 12.
すみません (sumimasen) — open the repair gently.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Say the farewell from the last chapter once. Now imagine that the other person has spoken and you missed it. Do not guess; open a repair.

You'll want to know.
すみません (sumimasen)。 — su-mi-ma-se-n — excuse me; I am sorry.
Five morae, each given room. This polite expression can apologise, attract attention, or soften the request that follows. Here it means: “Excuse me — I need help with what was just said.”
Its shape is useful, not a literal translation burden. It is related to sumu, “to be settled or finished,” in a polite negative form: the small social debt is not yet settled. Keep the practical job first.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — hear: sumimasen becomes point to repair, not farewell]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: su | mi | ma | se | n]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[your turn — choose: you missed a word becomes すみません (sumimasen)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
The Japanese spelling is exposure for now. The next two lessons isolate its two new signs before either becomes load-bearing.

Wrap-up Recall.
Say it once, then stop. The request itself comes later.

Lesson 2 of 12.
み (mi) — one looped bridge.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Say sumimasen once, then write known ま. The new sign shares the m opening but carries a different vowel.

Script — the shape.
み — mi.
Write it in two strokes. First make the bent left body, ending in a small turn. Then begin at the upper right, sweep down through the middle, and finish by lifting to the right. Say mi only when the second stroke ends.

Guided Practice.

Lesson 3 of 12.
め (me) — cross, loop, release.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Find み in すみません (sumimasen), then say the five-mora repair opener.

Script — the shape.
め — me.
Two strokes. The short first stroke leans down to the right. The longer second stroke crosses it, curves into an open loop, and releases at the lower right. Keep that final opening visible; do not close it into a circle.

Lesson 4 of 12.
よく (yoku) — how fully?

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say sumimasen. Point to み and め (me) inside it.

You'll want to know.
よく (yoku) — yo-ku — well; fully.
Two morae. Yoku is the adverbial partner of yoi, “good.” In the coming line, it says that understanding did not happen well or fully. It does not itself mean “understand.”
You already write よ. Treat く (ku) as visible support for now; its own tiny writing lesson follows.

Lesson 5 of 12.
く (ku) — one angled stroke.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 8 seconds]
Write known よ and say yoku.

Script — the shape.
く — ku.
One stroke. Begin high on the right, sweep down-left to the point, then turn and sweep down-right. Keep it narrow and open. The turn is part of the same stroke.

Lesson 6 of 12.
わかりません (wakarimasen) — I do not understand.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Read よく (yoku), then open a repair with sumimasen.

You'll want to know.
わかりません (wakarimasen)。 — wa-ka-ri-ma-se-n — I do not understand.
Six morae. The wakari- part carries “understand”; polite -masen makes the predicate negative. Japanese often leaves out “I” when the situation already makes the experiencer obvious.
Combine the two known pieces only in sound for now:
yoku wakarimasen — I do not understand well / fully.

Lesson 7 of 12.
ど (do) — voice a sign you already own.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — carry the voicing mark until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Write と. Add the two-stroke dakuten beside it. Say wakarimasen once as the reason a repetition may be needed.

Script — carry the voicing mark.
と to + ゛ becomes ど do.
The base shape does not change. Write と first, then add the two short dakuten strokes at the upper right. The mark voices the opening consonant: t becomes d while the vowel stays o.

Guided Practice.

Read と / ど as to / do without changing the vowel.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 8 of 12.
もう (mō) — one more time begins here.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say sumimasen, yoku wakarimasen. Write め (me) once.

You'll want to know.
もう — mō — again; one more.
Two morae: mo | o. The macron in mō hides the second beat, but the Japanese spelling shows it with final う. In other settings this adverb can mean “already” or “more.” A request for repetition selects “again.”

Lesson 9 of 12.
も (mo) — two bars, then the curve.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the shape.

Three strokes. Draw the central curve first, from the upper left down and around. Then add the upper short horizontal and the lower short horizontal. Both bars cross the central stroke.

Lesson 10 of 12.
いちど (ichido) — one time, all known signs.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Write い, ち, and と. Add the dakuten to と and read the result do. Write め (me) once as a review pulse.

You'll want to know.
いちど (ichido) — i-chi-do — one time; once.
The word is built transparently: ichi is “one,” and do counts one occurrence. All four visible marks are already yours: い + ち + と + ゛.
Now join it to the previous word:
もういちど — mō ichido — one more time.

Guided Practice.
Hear i | chi | do without looking.
Write いちど (ichido) from the three morae.
Read もういちど, keeping the two beats in もう (mō).

Wrap-up Recall.
Circle the dakuten. It changes the final mora, not the first two.

Lesson 11 of 12.
おねがいします (onegai shimasu) — please, as a polite request.

Warm-up.
[pause 15 seconds]
Read いちど (ichido). Say sumimasen, yoku wakarimasen. Write く.

You'll want to know.
おねがいします (onegai shimasu)。 — o-ne-ga-i shi-ma-su — please; I request it.
The pieces make the social direction visible: respectful o-, negai “request or wish,” and polite shimasu “do.” Learn the whole as the courteous request ending. Do not translate each piece while speaking.
The common kanji spelling can wait. This chapter earns the sound and social use first, without switching writing systems mid-ramp.

Lesson 12 of 12.
もういちど、おねがいします (mō ichido, onegai shimasu) — ask for one more pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 25 seconds]
Write み, め (me), く, ど, も (mo). Say sumimasen, yoku wakarimasen. Then retrieve mō, ichido, and onegaishimasu separately.

You'll want to know.
もういちど、おねがいします。 mō ichido, onegai shimasu — one more time, please.
Nothing new is hidden inside the line. もう (mō) supplies “again,” いちど (ichido) supplies “one time,” and おねがいします (onegai shimasu) makes the request polite. The first two words are now readable; the final expression remains supported exposure until its own longer script runway is complete.

Guided Practice.
Listen: distinguish sumimasen from mō ichido, onegaishimasu.
Speak: say the opener, then the request, with a small pause.
Read: independently read もういちど; use the printed support for the rest.
Write: from dictation, write only the independently earned block もういちど.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate repair. Chapter 10 will ask for slower speech instead.
Source: Japan Foundation Irodori Starter lesson 2, pp. 1–3.
