Portuguese, chapter 380: Se eu fosse ministro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o ministro — the minister.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the average, then the Portuguese for the bachelor's degree.

You'll want to know: o ministro.
o ministro — "the minister".
Se eu fosse ministro, construía mais escolas. If I were a minister, I would build more schools.
Feminine a ministra. O primeiro-ministro — the prime minister.

The word, taken apart.
ministro comes from Latin minister "servant, attendant" (EtymDB), built on minus "less" (Lewis & Short): first a servant of the state.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o partido — the political party.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bachelor's degree, then the Portuguese for the minister.

You'll want to know: o partido.
o partido — "the political party".
Ele vota sempre no mesmo partido. He always votes for the same party.
Also the participle of partir: o copo está partido — the glass is broken.

The word, taken apart.
partido is the participle of partir "to divide" used as a noun for a side or faction; EtymDB traces it through Old Portuguese partido to partir, which is inherited from Latin partīre "to share, to divide", on pars "part" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: o parlamento.
o parlamento — "the parliament".
O parlamento aprovou a lei ontem. Parliament passed the law yesterday.
Its members are os deputados.

The word, taken apart.
parlamento is usually traced to Old French parlement "discussion, meeting, assembly" (EtymDB), built on parler "to speak", which goes back to Late Latin parabolāre, on parabola "a comparison" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
eleger — to elect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the political party, then the Portuguese for the parliament.

You'll want to know: eleger.
eleger — "to elect".
Elegeram uma mulher para presidente da câmara. They elected a woman as mayor.
Two participles: elegido with ter, eleito with ser: foi eleita.

The word, taken apart.
eleger is a learned borrowing from Latin ēligere "to pick out, to choose", built from ex and legere "to gather, to choose" (Lewis & Short).
