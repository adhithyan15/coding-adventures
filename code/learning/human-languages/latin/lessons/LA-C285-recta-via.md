# rēctā viā, ad dextram — following the directions

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C285-QUA-VIA-02, LA-LEX-C114-WHY114-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask the way to the forum, politely. (*Quaesō, dīc mihi: quā viā
ad forum?*) And say "right", then "left". (*Dexter. Sinister.*)

## Grammar Lens: three directions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C285-DIRECTIO-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C146-QUAL146-05, LA-LEX-C114-WHY114-05, LA-LEX-EO-01] -->

> **Rēctā viā ī.** — Go straight on.
> **Deinde ad dextram.** — Then to the right.
> **Tum ad sinistram.** — Then to the left.

[PAUSE 2s] **ī** is the command of *eō*: "go!", a whole sentence in one
long vowel. *Rēctā viā* is "by the straight road", the ablative of *via*
with *rēctus* agreeing.

[PAUSE 2s] **ad dextram** and **ad sinistram** are "towards the right" and
"towards the left": *dextram* and *sinistram* are the feminine accusative,
because *manum*, "hand", is understood. Where something already is, Latin
says **dextrā**, "on the right", and **sinistrā**, "on the left".

> **Thermae sunt dextrā, iūxtā pontem.** — The baths are on the right,
> next to the bridge.

## Why it's said this way: directions in a comedy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C244-RES244-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C282-ORDO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Roman comedy has a scene built on exactly this. In Terence's
*Adelphoe*, a slave gets rid of an old man by sending him across town:
*ad sinistram*, then *ad dextram*, along *hāc rēctā plateā*, "this straight
street", to a place where the person he wants is not. Lewis & Short quote
the *ad dextram* from that play. A Roman audience laughed because they gave
and got directions this way every day.

## The word, taken apart: dextram
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C114-WHY114-04] -->

**dextram** is the adjective *dexter* with *manum* left out: Lewis & Short
give *ad dextram* as short for *ad dextram manum* or *partem*, "to the right
hand" or "side". The
*dexter* lesson traced the word itself to Proto-Indo-European *\*deks-*,
"right", and named Greek *dexios* as a cousin.
