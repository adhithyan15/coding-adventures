Latin, chapter 358: Agreeing and Disagreeing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
assentior, assentīrī — to agree (with), to assent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to suppose, then the Latin for an opinion.

You'll want to know: assentior, assentīrī.
assentior, assentīrī — "to agree (with), to assent".
Tibi assentior: nōn est tūtum. I agree with you: it is not safe.
Deponent: assentior, assentīrī, assēnsus sum, with the dative of the person: tibi assentior. English assent.

The word, taken apart.
assentior is built from ad "towards" and sentiō "to feel, to think" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to bring your feeling over to someone else's. English assent comes from it.

dissentiō, dissentīre, dissēnsī, dissēnsum. The person you disagree with goes in the ablative with ā / ab. English dissent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
cōnsentiō, cōnsentīre — to agree together, to be of one mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to agree, then the Latin for to disagree.

You'll want to know: cōnsentiō, cōnsentīre.
cōnsentiō, cōnsentīre — "to agree together, to be of one mind".
Omnēs cīvēs dē hāc rē cōnsentiunt. All the citizens agree about this matter.
cōnsentiō, cōnsentīre, cōnsēnsī, cōnsēnsum. Where assentior is one person agreeing with another, cōnsentiō is many minds agreeing together. English consent.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsentiō is built from con- "together" and sentiō "to feel, to think" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to feel together. English consent comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cōnsēnsus, cōnsēnsūs — agreement, unanimity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to disagree, then the Latin for to agree together.

You'll want to know: cōnsēnsus, cōnsēnsūs.
cōnsēnsus, cōnsēnsūs — "agreement, unanimity".
Omnium cōnsēnsū eum ducem fēcērunt. By common consent they made him leader.
cōnsēnsus, cōnsēnsūs m., fourth declension. Omnium cōnsēnsū, by everyone's agreement, is a stock phrase. English consensus is the same word.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsēnsus is the noun built on cōnsentiō "to agree together" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), and English took it over whole as consensus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
contrōversia, contrōversiae — a dispute, a controversy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to agree together, then the Latin for agreement.

You'll want to know: contrōversia, contrōversiae.
contrōversia, contrōversiae — "a dispute, a controversy".
Inter vīcīnōs contrōversia dē agrō erat. There was a dispute between the neighbours about a field.
contrōversia, contrōversiae f. A quarrel at law over a boundary or a will, and a set debate in the schools of speaking. English controversy.

The word, taken apart.
contrōversia is built on contrōversus "turned the other way" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from contrā "against" and the participle of vertō "to turn": two sides turned against each other. English controversy comes from it.
