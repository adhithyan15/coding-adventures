Sanskrit, chapter 316: Kings and Emperors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सम्राट् (samrāṭ) — an emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for is alarmed, then the Sanskrit for hurries.

You'll want to know: सम्राट्.
सम्राट् — samrāṭ — "an emperor".
सम्राट् प्रासादे वसति। samrāṭ prāsāde vasati. The emperor lives in the palace.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सम्राट् (samrāṭ) is built from sam, "fully", and the root √rāj, "to shine, rule"; MW gives samrāj as a universal ruler, a paramount sovereign (MW).

सम्राट् सिंहासने उपविशति। samrāṭ siṃhāsane upaviśati. The emperor sits on the throne.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a throne, then the Sanskrit for a lord.

You'll want to know: राज्यम्.
राज्यम् — rājyam — "a kingdom".
तस्य राज्यं विशालम् अस्ति। tasya rājyaṃ viśālam asti. His kingdom is vast.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
राज्यम् (rājyam) is built on rājan, "a king" (rājā), with the ending -ya; MW gives rājya as kingship, sovereignty, and a kingdom (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
शास्ति (śāsti) — rules; governs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lord, then the Sanskrit for a kingdom.

You'll want to know: शास्ति.
शास्ति — śāsti — "rules; governs".
सम्राट् धर्मेण राज्यं शास्ति। samrāṭ dharmeṇa rājyaṃ śāsti. The emperor rules the kingdom justly.
Past (he …): सः राज्यं शासितवान्, saḥ rājyaṃ śāsitavān.

The word, taken apart.
शास्ति (śāsti) comes from the root √śās, "to chastise, correct", which MW also gives as to restrain, rule, govern; MW gives śāsita as governed, ruled (MW).
