# How are you, cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-HALUKA-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Ask after somebody and answer them back, both parts, without reading
on. If the question came out as a memorised noise rather than three pieces you
can name, this page is for you.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C03-HAL-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-HAL-02] -->

| word | letters, right to left | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **كيف** *kayfa* | *kāf · yāʾ · fāʾ* | how — a question word, partner to **ما** |
| **حال** *ḥāl* | *ḥāʾ · alif · lām* | state, condition — root **ḥ–w–l**, to turn |

The **ح** is the deep throat *ḥ*, not an English *h*. And **حال** is the whole
point of the question: English asks after **you**, Arabic asks after your
**state** — the thing that turns and changes, which is exactly what its root
means.

## Grammar Lens: the same suffix, one lesson on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-HALUKA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-HALUKA-02] -->

**كيف حالك؟** bolts three pieces you already own together: *how* + *state* +
*your*. The suffix behaves identically to the one that gave you **اسمي**, and it
splits the same way.

| to a man | to a woman |
|---|---|
| **كيف حالك؟** *kayfa ḥāluka* | **كيف حالك؟** *kayfa ḥāluki* |

Literally: **how — your-state**. No *is*, no *are*. The same zero copula that ran
through the name exchange runs through this one.

## How to answer: two replies where English has one
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-BI-KHAYR-02, AR-CONCEPT-C03-AL-HAMDU-LILLAH-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-AL-HAMDU-LILLAH-02] -->

**بخير** *bi-khayr* is **بـ** (*in*) plus **خير** (*khayr*, good) — literally *in
goodness*. You had met *khayr* long before you were told: it sits inside the
morning greeting **صباح الخير**, *morning of the good*.

**الحمد لله** *al-ḥamdu lillāh* comes back more often than *bi-khayr* does. Every
letter in it is one you can write — *alif, lām, ḥāʾ, mīm, dāl, hāʾ* — and it says
**the praise is to God**, from the root **ḥ–m–d**, to praise.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C03-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C03-PRACTICE-02, AR-CONCEPT-C03-KAYFA-HALUKA-02] -->

- [YOU SAY: ask a man — *kayfa ḥāluka?*]
- [YOU SAY: ask a woman — *kayfa ḥāluki?*]
- [YOU SAY: the plain answer — *bi-khayr, shukran*]
- [YOU SAY: the commoner answer — *al-ḥamdu lillāh*]
- [YOU RECALL: the three attaching pieces inside these lines — *al-*, *bi-*, *li-*]
