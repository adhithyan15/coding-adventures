Mandarin Chinese, chapter 357: Owning Up.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
rèncuò — to admit you were wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an error, then the Mandarin for to put a mistake right.

You'll want to know: rèncuò.
rèncuò — "to admit you were wrong".
tā bù kěn rèncuò — He won't admit he was wrong.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rèncuò is built from rèn and 错 'wrong', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyinH and tshak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to recognize'.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: 气话.
气话 — qìhuà — "something said in anger".
他说的是气话 tā shuō de shì qìhuà — He only said it because he was angry.

The word, taken apart.
气话 (qìhuà) is built from 气 'breath' and 话 'talk', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khj+jH and hwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'breath' and the second 'speech'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
口气 (kǒuqì) — tone of voice; way of saying something.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to admit you were wrong, then the Mandarin for something said in anger.

You'll want to know: 口气.
口气 — kǒuqì — "tone of voice; way of saying something".
我觉得你说话的口气不太好 wǒ juéde nǐ shuōhuà de kǒuqì bú tài hǎo — I don't think your tone is very nice.

The word, taken apart.
口气 (kǒuqì) is built from 口 'the mouth' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khuwX and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, quoting an older dictionary, 'what people speak and eat with', and the second 'breath'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
pīpíng — to criticize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for something said in anger, then the Mandarin for a tone of voice.

You'll want to know: pīpíng.
pīpíng — "to criticize".
yīnwèi tā chídào le suǒyǐ lǎoshī pīpíng le tā — He was late, so the teacher told him off.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pīpíng is built from pī and píng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, phej and bjaeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and 'to push', and the second 'to weigh up'.
