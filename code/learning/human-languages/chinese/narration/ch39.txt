Mandarin Chinese, chapter 39: Drinking.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
喝 (hē) — to drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hungry, then the Mandarin for dish of food.

You'll want to know: 喝.
喝 — hē — "to drink".
你喝什么 nǐ hē shénme — What would you like to drink?
The new character, in pieces: 喝 (hē) — 口 on the left; on the right 日 on top of a wrap with a small shape inside it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
喝 (hē) is an old character: the Guangyun records it as xat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to shout at'. The sense 'to drink' is not given there, so how it came to mean drink is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for hungry, then the Mandarin for dish of food, then say hē again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 喝 (hē) mean? ("To drink".) Say it once more.

口 日 吃.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kǒu, rì, chī]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a mouth, a sun and a wrap.
喝 (hē).
Three pieces: 口 on the left, then 日, then the wrap under it. Do not count to twelve; count three.
口, three strokes, small, as in 吃.
日, four strokes, short and wide, at the top right.
The wrap, five strokes. A short left-falling stroke. A horizontal that turns down and hooks, making a roof. Inside it, a small left-falling stroke and a dot. Then a corner stroke, down and along, closing them in.
3 + 4 + 5 = twelve, and the first seven are two shapes you know.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 喝 (hē), all twelve, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 喝 (hē)? (Three — 口, 日, and the wrap.) How many strokes before the wrap starts? (Seven.) What goes inside the wrap? (A small left-falling stroke and a dot.)

Lesson 3 of 7.
水 (shuǐ) — water.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dish of food, then the Mandarin for to drink.

You'll want to know: 水.
水 — shuǐ — "water".
我喜欢喝水 wǒ xǐhuan hē shuǐ — I like drinking water.
The new character, in pieces: 水 (shuǐ) — a hooked vertical down the middle, one stroke on its left that runs across and falls away, and two strokes on its right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
水 (shuǐ) is inherited from Middle Chinese sywijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains as one of the five elements, the one of the north, and as 'level'.

Lesson 4 of 7.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xiǎo, hē]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the middle first, then the sides.
水 (shuǐ).
One piece: the middle, then left, then right.
The middle, one stroke. A long vertical that hooks to the left at the foot, as in 小.
The left side, one stroke. A short horizontal that turns and falls away to the lower left.
The right side, two strokes. A short left-falling stroke toward the middle, then a long right-falling stroke out to the lower right.
1 + 1 + 2 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 水 (shuǐ), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which stroke of 水 (shuǐ) comes first? (The hooked vertical down the middle.) Which character you know also starts in the middle? (小.) How many strokes on the right? (Two.)

Lesson 6 of 7.
niúnǎi — milk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for water, then the Mandarin for thirsty.

You'll want to know: niúnǎi.
niúnǎi — "milk".
háizi xǐhuan hē niúnǎi — Children like milk.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
niúnǎi is built from niú 'cattle' and nǎi 'milk'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese ngjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a large beast'. The second is probably the word the Guangyun records as nreaX, glossed 'milk', under another way of writing the character.
