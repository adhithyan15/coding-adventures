## You'll want to know: दूसरा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C75-ORDINAL-02]; assesses=[] -->

**दूसरा** (*dūsrā*) — "**second**".

**दूसरा दिन** (*dūsrā din*) — the second day. **दूसरी किताब** (*dūsrī kitāb*) —
the second book.

Like *pahlā*, there is no **दो** visible in it, though this one does descend from
the Sanskrit word for two.

## You'll want to know: the other meaning
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C75-ORDINAL-03]; assesses=[] -->

**दूसरा** (*dūsrā*) also means "**other**, **another**", and that is not a separate word to
learn — it is the same one.

- **दूसरा आदमी** (*dūsrā ādmī*) — the other man.
- **दूसरा घर** (*dūsrā ghar*) — the other house.

Hold both senses together. A chapter two ahead turns on them: once you can say
*ek* and *dūsrā* in one breath, you can say "**one … the other**", which is a
thing Hindi does with no extra machinery at all.
