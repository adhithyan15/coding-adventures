French, chapter 391: Mettre de côté.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'épargne — the savings, saving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the expense, then the French for the contribution.

You'll want to know: l'épargne.
l'épargne — "the savings, saving".
Ils ont mis leur épargne sur un compte à la banque. They put their savings in a bank account.
Feminine. Formal; in speech, les économies.

The word, taken apart.
épargne is formed from épargner, inherited through Old French espargnier from a Frankish sparanjan "to spare" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
investir — to invest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the savings, then the French for to save up.

You'll want to know: investir.
investir — "to invest".
Il a investi son argent dans un appartement. He invested his money in a flat.
A -ir verb like finir: nous investissons.

The word, taken apart.
investir is a learned borrowing from Latin investīre "to clothe, to cover" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on vestis "clothing"; the money sense came later, probably through Italian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le taux — the rate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to invest, then the French for the investment.

You'll want to know: le taux.
le taux — "the rate".
Le taux d'intérêt est de trois pour cent. The interest rate is three per cent.
The same form in the plural. Le taux de chômage — the unemployment rate.

The word, taken apart.
taux comes from Old French tauxer "to fix a price" (EtymDB), from Latin taxāre "to appraise, to value" (Lewis & Short).
