Spanish, chapter 537: Desde entonces.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
desde entonces — since then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for as soon as, then the Spanish for the moment that.

You'll want to know: desde entonces.
desde entonces — "since then".
Desde entonces no lo he visto. I haven't seen him since then.

The word, taken apart.
desde entonces is built on desde 'since' and entonces, which comes from Latin in tunc 'at that time' (EtymDB), with an -s added on the end.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hasta entonces — until then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the moment that, then the Spanish for since then.

You'll want to know: hasta entonces.
hasta entonces — "until then".
Hasta entonces, nadie sabía nada. Until then, nobody knew anything.
In a goodbye: ¡Hasta entonces! — see you then!

The word, taken apart.
hasta entonces is built on hasta, which is usually traced to Arabic ḥattā 'until', and entonces, from Latin in tunc 'at that time' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
al día siguiente — the next day, the following day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for since then, then the Spanish for until then.

You'll want to know: al día siguiente.
al día siguiente — "the next day, the following day".
Al día siguiente volvió a la tienda. The next day he went back to the shop.
In a story about the past use al día siguiente, not mañana.

The word, taken apart.
al día siguiente is built on siguiente 'following', from seguir, which comes from Latin sequī 'to follow' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
hace poco — a short time ago, recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for until then, then the Spanish for the next day.

You'll want to know: hace poco.
hace poco — "a short time ago, recently".
Hace poco me mudé a esta ciudad. I moved to this city a short time ago.
Hace + time = ago: hace dos años.

The word, taken apart.
hace poco is built on hace, from hacer, inherited from Latin facere 'to make', and poco, from Latin paucus 'few, little' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: anteayer.
anteayer — "the day before yesterday".
Anteayer hubo un accidente en mi calle. The day before yesterday there was an accident on my street.

The word, taken apart.
anteayer is built on ante 'before' and ayer 'yesterday', which comes from Latin herī 'yesterday' (Lewis & Short).
