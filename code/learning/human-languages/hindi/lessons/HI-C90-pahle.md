# पहले — putting one thing in front of another

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say that you get up and that you go to work. Try to say
**which of the two happens first**, and notice the track has never given you a
way.

## You'll want to know: पहले
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05]; assesses=[] -->

**पहले** (*pahle*) — first, beforehand, earlier.

**पहले मैं उठता हूँ।** — First I get up.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05, HI-CONCEPT-C28-SUBAH-01] -->

**पहला** is *first*, the ordinal, and **पहले** is the same word in the shape an
adverb takes — the **-ए** ending you have now met on adjectives, on the past
copula and on the continuous.

It does two jobs, and the difference is where it stands:

| position | what it means |
|---|---|
| in front of the sentence | first, to begin with |
| after a time word | before that time |

*सुबह से पहले* is *before morning* — the time word, **से**, then **पहले**.
That is the second use, and it is why this lesson comes after the one on **से**.

## Grammar Lens: sequencing a day
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| पहले मैं उठता हूँ। | First I get up. |
| मैं काम पर जाता हूँ। | I go to work. |

Two sentences and an order between them. That is the whole of what a
one-minute spoken description needs — and until this word existed, every
sentence in such a description floated free of the others.
