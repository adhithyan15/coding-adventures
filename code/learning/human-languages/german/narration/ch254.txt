German, chapter 254: What I Think — ich finde, dass; darum.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ich finde, dass — an opinion, with its verb at the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "because German is beautiful". (Weil Deutsch schön ist.) Where did ist go? (To the end.)

Grammar Lens: dass does what weil does.
dass means "that", the "that" of "I think that …". It opens a clause, and like weil it sends the verb to the end:
Ich finde, dass Deutsch schön ist. — I think German is beautiful. Ich glaube, dass der Zug Verspätung hat. — I believe the train is late.
German sets the dass clause off with a comma. German can drop dass, as English drops "that", but then the verb returns to second place: Ich glaube, der Zug hat Verspätung. Both are standard.
To disagree, put nicht on the opinion verb: Ich finde nicht, dass das richtig ist.

Why it's said this way: three verbs of opinion.
ich finde is a judgement of your own, often about something you have tried.
ich glaube is a belief you hold without being sure.
ich meine is "I think, I mean". The verb is meinen, and you know its noun already: die Meinung, the opinion. Meinen also means "to mean": Was meinst du?, what do you mean?
One trap in the spelling: dass with two s joins clauses; das with one is "the" or "that one". Before 1996 the joiner was written daß. Swiss spelling does without ß altogether, so Swiss writers used dass long before then.

The word, taken apart.
dass and das go back to one Old High German word, daz, from Proto-Germanic þat (EtymDB 2.1), which EtymDB sets beside English that. English kept one spelling for both jobs; German later split it in two, by the usual account in the sixteenth century.
meinen from Old High German meinen from Proto-Germanic mainijaną, "to mean, to think" (EtymDB 2.1): the same verb as English mean.

Lesson 2 of 4.
meiner Meinung nach — in my opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think that is right", with dass. (Ich finde, dass das richtig ist.)
[pause 1 second]
There is a shorter way in, and it does not move the verb to the end.

You'll want to know: meiner Meinung nach.
meiner Meinung nach — "in my opinion": MY-ner MY-noong nahkh.
Meiner Meinung nach ist das richtig. — In my opinion, that is right.
Two things are unusual in it. Nach comes after its noun, and here it means "according to". Nach meiner Meinung exists too, but nach last is the usual order. And meiner is mein with the dative ending -er, the case nach asks for. With "your": deiner Meinung nach.

Grammar Lens: one place, then the verb.
The whole phrase, three words long, takes the first place of the sentence, exactly as dann did. So the verb comes next, and the subject waits behind it:
Meiner Meinung nach ist das wichtig. Meiner Meinung nach hat der Zug Verspätung.
Compare the two ways in:
Ich finde, dass das wichtig ist. — the verb at the end. Meiner Meinung nach ist das wichtig. — the verb second.
Same opinion, two word orders. Meiner Meinung nach is at home in a discussion and in writing.

The word, taken apart.
Meinung is the verb meinen + -ung, from Old High German meinen (EtymDB 2.1).
nach is set by EtymDB 2.1 beside nah, "near", from Old High German nāh, and beside English nigh. From "near" it came to mean "after" and, behind a noun, "according to"; EtymDB does not record those steps, so they are given here as the usual account.

Lesson 3 of 4.
darum — that's why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the German for "therefore", from chapter two hundred and fifty. (Deshalb.) And the question "why?" (Warum?)

You'll want to know: darum.
darum — "that's why, so": da-RUM, with a short u; when you point hard at the reason, DA-rum.
Es regnet, darum nehme ich den Regenschirm. — It's raining, so I'm taking the umbrella. Der Zug hatte Verspätung, darum kam ich spät. — The train was late, that's why I came late.
Darum and deshalb do the same job, and either fits in both sentences. Darum has one more use: on its own, it is the curt answer to warum. "Warum?" "Darum!", because.

Grammar Lens: two directions, two word orders.
One fact, told two ways:
Ich nehme den Regenschirm, weil es regnet. Es regnet, darum nehme ich den Regenschirm.
weil points back to the cause, and its verb goes to the end. darum points forward to the result. It takes the first place of its clause, like dann, so its verb comes straight after it: darum nehme ich, not darum ich nehme.
The comma before darum is where one clause stops and the next begins. In a longer stretch, darum can open a fresh sentence: Es regnet. Darum nehme ich den Regenschirm.

The word, taken apart.
darum is da(r), "there, that", + um, "around, about": about that. You took warum apart the same way, "about what". EtymDB 2.1 lists darum without its parts, but traces um through Old High German umbi to Proto-Germanic umbi, beside Greek amphí and Latin ambi-, "around".

Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — what I think, and why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I believe that the train is late". (Ich glaube, dass der Zug Verspätung hat.)

What you've built.
Three lessons, and an argument has all its joints. An opinion: ich finde, ich glaube or ich meine, then dass and the verb at the end. Or meiner Meinung nach in the first place, with the verb right after it. A reason with weil, verb last. A consequence with darum, verb second.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two sentences, and both word orders in them:
Meiner Meinung nach kommen wir spät, weil der Zug Verspätung hat. Der Zug hat Verspätung, darum nehme ich ein Taxi.
Verb second after the opinion phrase, verb last after weil, and verb second again after darum.
