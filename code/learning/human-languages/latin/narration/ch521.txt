Latin, chapter 521: Being ill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aegrōtus, aegrōta, aegrōtum — sick, ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to yawn, then the Latin for to sneeze.

You'll want to know: aegrōtus, aegrōta, aegrōtum.
aegrōtus, aegrōta, aegrōtum — "sick, ill".
Fīlius aegrōtus in lectō iacet. The sick son lies in bed.
Built on aeger. As a noun, aegrōtus — a patient.

The word, taken apart.
aegrōtus is built on aeger "sick" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
aegrōtō, aegrōtāre — to be ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: aegrōtō, aegrōtāre.
aegrōtō, aegrōtāre — "to be ill".
Avus iam trēs diēs aegrōtat. Grandfather has been ill for three days now.
aegrōtō, aegrōtāre, aegrōtāvī, from aegrōtus. With iam, the present means has been.

Lesson 3 of 5.
languidus, languida, languidum — faint, weak, listless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sick, then the Latin for to be ill.

You'll want to know: languidus, languida, languidum.
languidus, languida, languidum — "faint, weak, listless".
Post febrem corpus languidum est. After the fever the body is weak.
From langueō — to be faint. English languid.

The word, taken apart.
languidus is built on langueō "to be faint, weary" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English languid comes from it, and languish from the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dēbilis, dēbile — weak, disabled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be ill, then the Latin for faint.

You'll want to know: dēbilis, dēbile.
dēbilis, dēbile — "weak, disabled".
Senex dēbilis lentē ambulat. The weak old man walks slowly.
Two endings. Of a limb that will not work, or of general weakness.

The word, taken apart.
dēbilis is built on dē- "away from" and habilis "handy, able" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).
