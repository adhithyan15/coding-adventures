Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 27: How Much Is This? No New Signs.
9 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 9.
Hear kitṇo — how much.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the three-counter request payoff. Say price and weather, then write both and point to the ौ inside one of them.

You'll want to know.
kitṇo — how much.
Three beats: ki-tṇo, said as two in ordinary speech. The ṇ is the retroflex n already heard inside pāṇī, made with the tongue curled back.
You have known bhāv, price, since Chapter 18. kitṇo is what you say when you want somebody to tell you one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.

Lesson 2 of 9.
कितणो (kitṇo) — the question word on the page.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Say how much, write ग, then write heat.

You'll want to know.
कि + त + णो becomes कितणो — kitṇo — how much.
Every unit is old. The ण is the same retroflex sign that sits in the middle of पाणी, and it is what makes this word Marwadi rather than the Hindi form a reader may expect. Write पाणी and कितणो (kitṇo) one under the other and check that the ण is drawn the same way in both.

Guided Practice.
Look at कितणो (kitṇo), cover it, wait five seconds, and write it. Check the ि sits before क and that the last sign carries ो.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
From kitṇo, write the word that asks how much.


Lesson 3 of 9.
Hear ye kitṇe kū hai? — how much is this?

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Say item and cold and write both, then write ड and है on their own.

You'll want to know.
ye kitṇe kū hai? — how much is this?
Four words, three of which you own: ye points, kitṇe is the asking word from the last lesson, and hai is the is you have said since Chapter 5. Between them sits kū, one short beat that does the work English does with for.
The asking word arrives as kitṇe, not kitṇo. Marwadi bends the ending of that word before kū, exactly as the source prints it; learn the whole line as one heard shape now and the bending will be taught on its own later.
Hear it twice. Say it once with your hand on the thing you are asking about.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What are you asking when you say ye kitṇe kū hai?


Lesson 4 of 9.
ये कितणे कू है? (ye kitṇe kū hai?) — the price question on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Say how much is this, write shop, then write ु on its own.

You'll want to know.
ये | कितणे | कू | है?
Four written words. ये and है you have written before; कितणे is last lesson's कितणो (kitṇo) with े where ो stood; कू is क carrying the long ू you first drew for मिलसू.

Guided Practice.
Read the line once. Cover it for ten seconds and write all four words. Open the model and repair only the word that changed, then rewrite that word alone.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 6 of 9.
Hear ye kitṇo huā? — how much altogether?

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Recall the five-word shopping payoff, then say money and goods and write both.

You'll want to know.
ye kitṇo huā? — how much is that altogether?
The same three-word shape, with huā where kū hai stood. The asking word keeps its own ending here: kitṇo, not kitṇe.
The two questions do different jobs. ye kitṇe kū hai? asks the price of one thing while you are still deciding. ye kitṇo huā? asks for the total once you have stopped choosing. Hear both, one after the other, and say which one comes last.

Lesson 7 of 9.
ये कितणो हुआ? (ye kitṇo huā?) — the total question on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Write आ on its own, then write formal thanks, then write the price question from the last lesson.

You'll want to know.
ये | कितणो (kitṇo) | हुआ?
Three written words, all made of old parts. हुआ is ह carrying the short ु of दुकान, followed by the standing आ you wrote for आभार. This is the first word in the track where a full independent vowel stands inside a word rather than starting one.

Guided Practice.
Read the line once. Cover it for ten seconds and write the three words. Check that हुआ keeps its ु below the first sign and its आ at full height.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Write the line that asks for the total.


Lesson 8 of 9.
Payoff — one price question, three counters.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the three-word shopping payoff, the three-word transport payoff, and the five-word food payoff.

Guided Practice.
Only the second word changes.
Identify which of the three counters you heard the question asked at.
Produce all three from meaning cues alone.
Read three printed lines and match each to its counter.
Write all three from dictation, then write the total question once.
Pass each skill separately. You can now ask to see a thing and ask what it costs. Understanding the answer, saying it is too dear, and settling on a price are still untaught.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 9 of 9.
Close the writing loop for ू, ण and आ — delayed closure for the three older signs this chapter leaned on hardest.

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Recall the three-counter price payoff, say water, then write ख.

Guided Practice.
Without a model, write four single signs from sound cues: ू, ण, आ, ई. This chapter added no sign of its own, so the four it borrowed most are the four it has to prove.
