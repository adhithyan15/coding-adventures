Lesson 1 of 6.
dòushā — sweet bean paste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a solar term of the year, then the Mandarin for to tend the family graves.

You'll want to know: dòushā.
dòushā — "sweet bean paste".
zhège miànbāo lǐ yǒu dòushā — There's bean paste in this bun.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dòushā is built from dòu 'beans' and shā 'sand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwH and srae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'beans' and the second 'grit scattered by water'.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 月饼.
月饼 — yuèbǐng — "mooncake".
我买了两个月饼 wǒ mǎi le liǎng ge yuèbǐng — I bought two mooncakes.
The new character, in pieces: 饼 (bǐng) — nine strokes: on the left the food piece of 饭, three strokes; on the right two short strokes, two bars, and two strokes down. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
月饼 (yuèbǐng) is built from 月 'the moon' and 饼 (bǐng) 'a cake', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwot and pjiengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which gives the second only its reading.

Script — the food piece, then two bars and two legs.
饼 (bǐng).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the food piece, three strokes, as in 饭: a stroke falling to the left, a short bar that turns down to the left, then a vertical that flicks up to the right.
The right piece, six strokes. Two short strokes on top, the left one a dot, the right one falling to the left. Two bars, the lower one longer. Then a stroke down the left that falls away to the left, and a vertical down the right.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

xiànr — "filling".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiànr No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled. The ending r is the 儿 of 一会儿.

Lesson 5 of 6.
pǐncháng — to taste; to sample.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a mooncake, then the Mandarin for the filling of a dumpling.

You'll want to know: pǐncháng.
pǐncháng — "to taste; to sample".
dàjiā dōu pǐncháng le wǒ zuò de yuèbǐng — Everyone tasted the mooncakes I made.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pǐncháng is built from pǐn and cháng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, phimX and dzyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a rank' and 'a kind', and the second 'to try' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'to taste with the mouth'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
生意 (shēngyi) — business; trade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the filling of a dumpling, then the Mandarin for to taste food with care.

You'll want to know: 生意.
生意 — shēngyi — "business; trade".
每年这个时候月饼的生意最好 měinián zhège shíhou yuèbǐng de shēngyi zuì hǎo — Every year at this time the mooncake trade is at its best.

The word, taken apart.
生意 (shēngyi) is built from 生 'life' and 意 'intent', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaeng and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to grow' and the second 'the will'. How the pair came to mean business is not settled.
