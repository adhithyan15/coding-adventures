# ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? — the exchange, with nothing left out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C01-HAUDU-02, KA-GRAMMAR-C01-SARI-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two ways to agree. Say the one that answers a question, then the one
that accepts what someone has said.

## Guided Practice: the whole exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C02-NIMMA-HESARU-ENU-01, KA-LEX-C02-NANNA-HESARU-01, KA-LEX-C02-SANTOSHA-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: ask a stranger their name — *nimma hesaru ēnu?*]
- [YOU SAY: answer with your own — *nanna hesaru … *]
- [YOU SAY: that meeting them is a pleasure — *santōṣa*]
- [YOU SAY: agree that you followed it — *haudu*]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C02-NIMMA-HESARU-ENU-02, KA-GRAMMAR-C02-NANNA-HESARU-02, KA-GRAMMAR-C02-NIINU-NIIVU-02] -->

Two things about this exchange that no translation of it carries.

**There is no verb in either sentence.** *Nimma hesaru ēnu?* is *your name
what?* and *nanna hesaru …* is *my name …* — Kannada puts nothing where English
insists on *is*. The question word alone finishes the question, and the naming
alone finishes the answer.

**And the word for *you* chose the register before you opened your mouth:**

| | familiar | respectful |
|---|---|---|
| you | **ನೀನು** *nīnu* | **ನೀವು** *nīvu* |
| your | *ninna* | **ನಿಮ್ಮ** *nimma* |

The question above uses *nimma*, and that is not an accident — a stranger gets
the respectful form, and the possessive follows the pronoun rather than being
chosen separately.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] How many verbs are in *nimma hesaru ēnu?* (**None.**) Which of the
two agreements accepts a statement rather than answering a question? (*Sari*.)
Which form of *you* would you use with someone you are meeting for the first time? (*Nīvu*.)
