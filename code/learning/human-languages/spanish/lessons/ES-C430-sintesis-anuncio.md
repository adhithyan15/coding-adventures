# Synthesis — a shop window and a sentence about yourself

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-02, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the three garments this chapter added, before they appear in a
price list.

## Reading: el escaparate
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-02, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-03, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-04, ES-LEX-C360-REF-16] -->

> **ROPA — REBAJAS**

| artículo | precio |
|---|---|
| vaqueros | 25 € |
| jerséis | 18 € |
| bolsos | 30 € |

Every line is readable, and one of them is teaching you something: the middle
row is a **plural**, and the accent on *jerséis* is exactly where the singular
did not need one.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-02, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-03, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-04] -->

Say what is cheapest, what is dearest, and what you would buy:

> *Los jerséis son los más baratos.*
> *Quiero unos vaqueros.*

[PAUSE 4s]

Note *unos vaqueros* rather than *un vaquero*. A pair of trousers is a plural
thing, so the article is plural too.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-01, ES-LEX-TRABAJO] -->

The other half of the chapter, in the two lines a form or an interview wants:

> — *¿Trabajas?*
> — *No, estoy en paro.*

**Estoy**, not *soy*. Being out of work is a situation, and Spanish keeps
situations away from *ser*. The same answer in the positive is *sí, tengo
trabajo* — the noun you have had since the professions chapter, finally with a
negative to stand against.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-01, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-03, ES-LEX-C430-VIDA-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which verb answers *¿Trabajas?* in the negative? (*Estoy* — *estoy en
paro*.) Which word in the price list gained an accent in the plural?
(*Jerséis*.) Why *unos vaqueros* rather than *un*? (The garment is plural.)
