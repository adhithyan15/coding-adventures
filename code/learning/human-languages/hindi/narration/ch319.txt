Lesson 1 of 6.
पीटना (pīṭnā) — to beat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to quake, then the Hindi for a shock (of grief or fright).

You'll want to know: पीटना.
पीटना — pīṭnā — "to beat".
दो आदमियों ने एक चोर को पीटा। do ādmiyoṁ ne ek cor ko pīṭā. Two men beat up a thief.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने ढोल पीटा, usne ḍhol pīṭā.

The word, taken apart.
goes back to Sanskrit piṭṭayati, to pound into a flat mass, beside which CDIAL reconstructs piṭṭyatē, "is struck" (CDIAL 8165). It is hitting again and again; you also पीटना (pīṭnā) a drum: ढोल पीटना (pīṭnā).

बाज़ार में दो दुकानदारों के बीच झड़प हुई। bāzār meṁ do dukāndāroṁ ke bīc jhaṛap huī. A scuffle broke out between two shopkeepers in the market.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
is set by CDIAL under jhaṭ, a form it reconstructs for quick, sudden movements (CDIAL 5327), where it gives Hindi jhaṛap, a fight: the same family as झटका, a jolt. A झड़प is short and sudden, between two people or two small groups.

घूँसा — ghū̃sā — "a punch".
उसने चोर को एक घूँसा मारा। usne cor ko ek ghū̃sā mārā. He punched the thief once.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
घूँसा (ghū̃sā) is inherited from ghūssa, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 4498). You घूँसा मारना, strike a punch: the punch is the object, so the verb is masculine.

ऐंठना — aiṇṭhnā — "to twist; to wring".
सिपाही ने चोर का हाथ ऐंठा। sipāhī ne cor kā hāth aiṇṭhā. The constable twisted the thief's arm.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने रस्सी ऐंठी, usne rassī aiṇṭhī.

The word, taken apart.
ऐंठना (aiṇṭhnā) is set by CDIAL under Sanskrit āveṣṭate, of the family of veṣṭ, to wind round (CDIAL 1448), where Hindi is aĩṭhnā. It begins with ऐ, the vowel of ऐसा. You ऐंठते हो a rope, an arm or a wet cloth.
