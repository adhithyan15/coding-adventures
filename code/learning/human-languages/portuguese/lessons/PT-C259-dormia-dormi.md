# dormia / dormi — the same sleep, told two ways

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-FAZIA-FRIO-01, PT-LEX-HAVIA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Paint a cold morning in two short sentences. (*Fazia frio. Não
havia sol.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Nothing has happened in it yet. This lesson is about what happens.

## Grammar Lens: the edge for -er and -ir verbs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-PERFEITO-I-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-PRETERITO-PERFEITO-02, PT-LEX-C41-VERBOS2-04, PT-LEX-C43-VERBOS4-03] -->

Chapter fifteen gave you the past with an edge for *-ar* verbs: *falei*,
*falou*. The *-er* and *-ir* verbs make it with an **-i**:

> **dormi** — I slept · **dormiu** — he or she slept
> **comi** — I ate · **comeu** — he or she ate

The other persons follow: *dormiste*, *dormimos*, *dormiram*. Listen to the
stress, which falls on that last *-i*: *dor-MI*. And set it beside the
imperfeito: **dormi** has an edge, **dormia** has none. One letter apart, and
they answer different questions.

## Grammar Lens: the scene and the events
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFEITO-01, PT-LEX-C232-VERBOS232-05, PT-GRAMMAR-PAIS-03] -->

Take one fact, that you slept, and let the tense decide what kind of fact it
is:

> **Dormia.** — I was sleeping. *(no edge: what was going on)*
> **Dormi.** — I slept. *(an edge: a thing that happened and ended)*

Neither is more correct. A told story needs both, in a division of labour:

> The **imperfeito** paints the **scene**: what was going on, what things
> were like.
> The **perfeito** drops the **events** into it: what happened.

*Fazia frio. Eu dormia. A minha mãe telefonou.* — It was cold. I was asleep.
My mother phoned.

English has no separate ending for this. It leans on *was ‑ing* and *used to*
and lets the context carry the rest. Portuguese settles it in the ending.

## The word, taken apart: why "imperfect"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-03]; assesses=[PT-LEX-FAZIA-FRIO-01] -->

**imperfeito** is inherited from Latin *imperfectus*, "unfinished" (EtymDB
2.1): *in-*, "not", plus *perfectus*, "carried all the way through", which is
itself *per-*, "through", plus *facere*, "to make" (Lewis & Short). That
*facere* is the one inside last lesson's *fazia*.

So the two names describe the two jobs. The **perfeito** is the past carried
all the way through, to its edge. The **imperfeito** is the
**not-carried-through** past: unfinished in time, with no edge on it.
