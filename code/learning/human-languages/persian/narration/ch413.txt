Persian, chapter 413: Prices and the economy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اقتصاد (eqtesâd) — the economy; economics.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the stock exchange, then the Persian for bankrupt.

You'll want to know: اقتصاد.
اقتصاد (eqtesâd) — "the economy; economics".
اقتصاد کشور در حال رشد است. eqtesâd-e keshvar dar hâl-e roshd ast. The country's economy is growing.
Eqtesâdi — economic.

The word, taken apart.
اقتصاد (eqtesâd) was borrowed from Arabic iqtiṣād "economy; economics" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
تورم (tavarrom) — inflation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for bankrupt, then the Persian for the economy.

You'll want to know: تورم.
تورم (tavarrom) — "inflation".
تورم امسال بیشتر از پارسال است. tavarrom emsâl bishtar az pârsâl ast. Inflation this year is higher than last year.
Literally swelling, and also a medical word.

The word, taken apart.
تورم (tavarrom) comes from Arabic tawarrum "swelling" (EtymDB): inflation is prices swelling.

Lesson 3 of 5.
گرانی (gerâni) — high prices, the cost of living.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the economy, then the Persian for inflation.

You'll want to know: گرانی.
گرانی (gerâni) — "high prices, the cost of living".
مردم از گرانی شکایت میکنند. mardom az gerâni shekâyat mikonand. People complain about high prices.
Gerân "expensive" + -i.

The word, taken apart.
گرانی (gerâni) is native Persian, built from gerân "heavy, expensive", which EtymDB traces to Middle Persian grān, with the noun suffix -i.

Lesson 4 of 5.
یارانه (yârâne) — a subsidy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for inflation, then the Persian for high prices.

You'll want to know: یارانه.
یارانه (yârâne) — "a subsidy".
دولت به خانواده ها یارانه میدهد. dowlat be khânevâde hâ yârâne midahad. The government gives families a subsidy.
A modern coinage built on yâr "helper".

The word, taken apart.
یارانه (yârâne) is a modern Persian coinage, usually explained as built on yâr "friend, helper" with the suffix -âne: money that helps.
