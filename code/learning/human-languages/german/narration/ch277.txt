German, chapter 277: Der Streik.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
der Streik — the strike (at work).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to increase, then the German for to sink.

You'll want to know: der Streik.
der Streik — "the strike (at work)".
Wegen des Streiks fuhren keine Züge. Because of the strike no trains ran.
Plural: die Streiks.

The word, taken apart.
Streik was borrowed from English strike (EtymDB) and given German spelling.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
protestieren — to protest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the strike, then the German for to go on strike.

You'll want to know: protestieren.
protestieren — "to protest".
Viele Bürger protestierten gegen die neue Straße. Many citizens protested against the new road.
With gegen: gegen etwas protestieren. Perfekt without ge-: hat protestiert.

The word, taken apart.
protestieren was borrowed from French protester, from Latin prōtestārī (EtymDB) "to declare in public", built on testis "a witness" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
demonstrieren — to demonstrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to go on strike, then the German for to protest.

You'll want to know: demonstrieren.
demonstrieren — "to demonstrate".
Am Samstag demonstrierten tausend Menschen in der Stadt. On Saturday a thousand people demonstrated in the town.
Short noun: die Demo. Also to show how something works.

The word, taken apart.
demonstrieren is a learned borrowing from Latin dēmonstrāre "to show" (EtymDB), built on monstrāre "to show" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
die Krise — the crisis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to protest, then the German for to demonstrate.

You'll want to know: die Krise.
die Krise — "the crisis".
Das Land war in einer tiefen Krise. The country was in a deep crisis.
Plural: die Krisen.

The word, taken apart.
Krise was borrowed through Latin crisis from Greek krisis "a decision, the turning point of an illness"; EtymDB lists no parent, so this follows the usual account.
