# ञ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one, the ones you have already met: य · ◌ृ · अ. Say what each of them does.

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. One only — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ञ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-206]; assesses=[] -->

**ञ** — *ña*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *ñ*, it is **ña**.

What it is made of:

- a clockwise open-left bowl
- a rightward shoulder rising to the headline
- a short descending lower stem
- the top shirorekhā

You already say these words, and ञ is one of the shapes inside them — the rest of their shapes are still ahead of you, so here the character stands on its own:

- *eka dva tri catur pañca* — one to five — Old Indo-Aryan ancestors preserved in Sanskrit, and cousins of the English ones
- *eka · dva · tri · catur · pañca* — the Sanskrit, Latin, and English number family and the sound laws between them

## Writing: ञ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-206] -->

- **1.** start at the open upper-left tip and curve clockwise around the bowl to the open lower-left tip
- **2.** lift at the bowl's right junction, sweep right through the shoulder, then curve upward along the right side to the headline
- **3.** lift at the lower right-side junction and descend the short stem below the bowl
- **4.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 3.** The pen comes up 3 times and no more.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ञ-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
