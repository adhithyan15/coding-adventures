Lesson 3 of 6.
杯 (bēi) — a tree piece, then 不.

Script — a tree piece, then 不.
杯 (bēi).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, four strokes: the tree piece, as on the left of 机. A horizontal. A vertical down through it. A stroke falling to the lower left from the crossing. Then a dot to the lower right, where 本 has a long stroke.
The right piece, 不, four strokes, as you write it: a horizontal, a stroke falling to the left, a vertical, and a dot.
4 + 4 = eight.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 杯 (bēi)? (Eight.) What is the right-hand piece? (不.) How does the tree piece end? (With a dot, not a long stroke.)

You'll want to know: sháozi.
sháozi — "spoon; ladle".
wǒ yào yí ge sháozi — Could I have a spoon?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sháozi is built from sháo 'ladle' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyak and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a drinking vessel. In the word the second loses its tone.

Lesson 5 of 6.
chāzi — fork.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a teacup, then the Mandarin for a spoon.

You'll want to know: chāzi.
chāzi — "fork".
nǐ yòng kuàizi háishi yòng chāzi — Chopsticks or a fork?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chāzi is built from chā and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsrhae and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'hands crossed', and under a second reading 'two branches': a forked thing. In the word the second loses its tone.
