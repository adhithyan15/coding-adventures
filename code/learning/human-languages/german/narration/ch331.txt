German, chapter 331: Jemanden überzeugen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
überzeugen — to convince.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the aspect, then the German for to advocate.

You'll want to know: überzeugen.
überzeugen — "to convince".
Der Verkäufer hat mich überzeugt: Ich nehme das blaue Fahrrad. The salesman convinced me: I am taking the blue bike.
Inseparable: er überzeugt, er hat überzeugt. Sich überzeugen — to see for yourself that something is so.

The word, taken apart.
überzeugen is built on zeugen "to testify", from Middle High German ziugen (EtymDB), with über- "over". It is usually said to have begun in the law courts, for proving something against a person with witnesses.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Überzeugung — the conviction, the firm belief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: die Überzeugung.
die Überzeugung — "the conviction, the firm belief".
Er hat das aus Überzeugung gemacht, nicht für Geld. He did it out of conviction, not for money.
Plural: die Überzeugungen. Ich bin der festen Überzeugung, dass … — I firmly believe that …

The word, taken apart.
Überzeugung is the noun of überzeugen "to convince", formed with -ung; the verb is built on zeugen "to testify", from Middle High German ziugen (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
überreden — to talk (someone) into, to persuade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for convincing, then the German for the conviction.

You'll want to know: überreden.
überreden — "to talk (someone) into, to persuade".
Meine Freundin hat mich überredet, mit ins Kino zu kommen. My friend talked me into coming along to the cinema.
Inseparable: sie überredet, sie hat überredet. Überreden works with pressure and many words; überzeugen works with reasons.

The word, taken apart.
überreden is built from über "over" and reden "to talk", inherited from Middle High German reden and Old High German redōn "to speak" (EtymDB): you talk someone over to your side.

Lesson 5 of 5.
argumentieren — to argue (a case), to give arguments.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the conviction, then the German for to talk.

You'll want to know: argumentieren.
argumentieren — "to argue (a case), to give arguments".
Sie argumentiert gut, aber ich bleibe bei meiner Meinung. She argues well, but I am sticking to my opinion.
It means to reason, not to quarrel; a quarrel is der Streit. Perfekt: hat argumentiert, with no ge-.

The word, taken apart.
argumentieren is a learned borrowing from Latin argūmentārī "to bring forward proof, to prove" (Lewis & Short), from argūmentum "proof", with the German verb ending -ieren.
