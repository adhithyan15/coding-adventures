Latin, chapter 84: Chest, Paper, Pen, Tablet, Mirror.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
arca, arcae — a chest, a box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cup, then the Latin for a knife.

You'll want to know: arca, arcae.
arca, arcae — "a chest, a box".

The word, taken apart.
arca is usually linked with arceō, "to shut in, to keep off" (Lewis & Short): a chest keeps things shut away. English ark comes from arca.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a knife, then the Latin for a chest.

You'll want to know: charta, chartae.
charta, chartae — "paper, a sheet". English chart and card.

The word, taken apart.
charta was borrowed from Greek khartēs, "a sheet of papyrus" (Lewis & Short), which may come from kharassō, "to scratch, to engrave" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
stilus, stilī — a stylus, a writing stick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a chest, then the Latin for paper.

You'll want to know: stilus, stilī.
stilus, stilī — "a stylus, a writing stick". English style.

The word, taken apart.
stilus is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root steyg-, "to prick, to pierce" (EtymDB 2.1); English stick is a cousin (Lewis & Short). The spelling with y came later, from a mistaken link with Greek stylos, "pillar".

Lesson 5 of 5.
speculum, speculī — a mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a stylus, then the Latin for a writing tablet.

You'll want to know: speculum, speculī.
speculum, speculī — "a mirror".

The word, taken apart.
speculum is built on speciō, "to look at" (Lewis & Short), with -ulum, an ending for a tool: the thing one looks into. speciō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European spek-, "to look" (EtymDB 2.1).
