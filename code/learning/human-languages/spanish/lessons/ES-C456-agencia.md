# la agencia — somebody who acts for you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C349-CLOCK-20, ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-14] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la agenda** and **la oficina**. One of those two is about to
turn out to be a relative.

## You'll want to know: la agencia
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-14] -->

**la agencia** — the agency.

*Una agencia de viajes* — a travel agency. *La agencia inmobiliaria* — the
estate agent's. *Lo he reservado por agencia* — I booked it through an agency.

**El agente** is the person. And the thing an agency charges you for its
trouble is **la comisión**, which appears on the bill beside the *coste*.

## Grammar Lens: -encia names the doing, again
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-05, ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-04] -->

You have a set of noun-making endings now, and this is another:

| ending | example |
|---|---|
| -anza | la mudanza |
| -miento | el seguimiento |
| -encia | la agencia |

**-encia** and its twin **-ancia** come off Latin present participles, which
is why they so often name an activity or a quality: *la paciencia*, *la
distancia*, *la experiencia*, *la importancia*.

Every **-encia** and **-ancia** noun is feminine. Set beside *-anza*, *-eza*
and *-ción*, that is five feminine suffix families against the masculine
*-miento*.

Keep the claim on the **suffixes**, though, not on abstract nouns in general —
*el coste* and *el cargo* from this same chapter are masculine and end in
neither. What the endings buy you is an article you no longer have to guess
at, one ending at a time.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-05, ES-LEX-C349-CLOCK-20] -->

Latin **agere**, **to do**, **to drive**, **to act**.

An *agencia* is a place that **acts on your behalf**, and an *agente* is the
one doing the acting. Now look at **la agenda**, which you already have: it is
*agenda*, "the things that must be done" — a Latin plural that Spanish took as
a singular noun.

So your diary and a travel agency are the same verb: one lists what must be
done, the other does it for you.

English is full of the family too — **agent**, **agenda**, **act**, **agile**,
**agitate** — and Spanish keeps *el acto*, *la acción*, *exigente* and *ágil*
off the same root.
