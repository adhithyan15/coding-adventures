## Added — A2 vocabulary: 600 words in chapters 153–272; Gujarati attains A2

- **120 new chapters (153–272) teach 600 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 234 of them are verbs, which clears the verb target the track was 48 short of. Each lesson gives:
  - the word in Gujarati script with its romanization (the recent scheme: *c* for ચ, *che*, a tilde for a nasalised long vowel, *n* before a stop after a short vowel);
  - a short example that uses only taught words and forms, its romanization word for word, and its translation;
  - a note on the verb's partner, a causative, gender, or a near-homograph;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons, and a review lesson closes every six chapters. Every chapter sits on an A2 spine node:
  - 153–182 (150 words): home and dress, housework, food and cooking, and time, frequency and plans.
  - 183–212 (150 words): shopping, money and the bank, the town, services and directions, and travel by road and rail.
  - 213–242 (150 words): weather and the seasons, health and the body, and animals, plants and farming.
  - 243–272 (150 words): school, study and work, leisure, music and sport, and family, friends and feelings.
- **Gujarati now teaches 1,241 headwords at or below A2**, past the level gate's target of 1,200. With the A2 core (chapters 145–152), Gujarati attains A2.
- **Script.** Every glyph is one the book has taught, including the core's ઓ, ડ and ઊ, so script closure and letter anchoring stay at zero. Blocked letters (ઇ ઐ ઔ ઋ ઙ ઞ ઝ ષ, ક્ષ, જ્ઞ) appear nowhere.
- **Sources.** Origins are checked against CDIAL (Turner) for inherited and tatsama words, Lane's Arabic–English Lexicon for Arabic roots, and DEDR where CDIAL points to Dravidian. EtymDB is used only as a cross-check. Words the sources do not settle say so. An independent review of each tranche fixed errors in 99 of the 600 words (16.5%), mostly CDIAL sub-entries and hedges dropped from the source, plus root slugs aligned with the corpus.
- **No forward references and no hidden duplicates.** Every candidate headword was screened against every earlier lesson's text. The forward-reference census is empty, and Gujarati's ceiling of 0 holds. Four verb pairs that differ only by vowel length or ḷ/l (વાળવું/વળવું, ઉતારવું/ઊતરવું, મરવું/મારવું, લાદવું/લડવું) have distinct concept ids.
