Bengali, chapter 285: Threats and Tricks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
শাসানো (shāsāno) — to threaten; to warn sternly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a prisoner, then the Bengali for bail.

You'll want to know: শাসানো.
শাসানো — shāsāno — "to threaten; to warn sternly".
ডাকাতরা দোকানদারকে শাসাল, কিন্তু তিনি ভয় পেলেন না। ḍākātrā dokāndārke shāsālô, kintu tini bhôy pelen nā. The robbers threatened the shopkeeper, but he wasn't afraid.
Past (he/she …): সেই ডাকাত দোকানদারকে শাসাল, sei ḍākāt dokāndārke shāsālô.

The word, taken apart.
শাসানো (shāsāno) comes down from Sanskrit śāsati, 'instructs, punishes, rules' (CDIAL 12419, which lists Bengali sāsā, 'to punish', and notes the -ānō verb, 'to threaten'). Its root śās- is also the root of শাস্তি, a punishment: to threaten is to hold the punishment over someone.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ঘুষ.
ঘুষ — ghush — "a bribe".
চোর দারোগাকে টাকা দিতে চাইল, কিন্তু তিনি ঘুষ নিলেন না। chor dārogāke ṭākā dite chāilô, kintu tini ghush nilen nā. The thief tried to give the inspector money, but he would not take a bribe.

The word, taken apart.
ঘুষ (ghush) is filed in CDIAL 4499 under a Middle Indo-Aryan ghūssā, 'a present', which lists Bengali ghus, 'a bribe': a present with a purpose.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ফাঁসানো (phā̃sāno) — to frame; to trap (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to threaten, then the Bengali for a bribe.

You'll want to know: ফাঁসানো.
ফাঁসানো — phā̃sāno — "to frame; to trap (someone)".
আসল চোর আমার ভাইকে ফাঁসাতে চাইল। āsôl chor āmār bhāike phā̃sāte chāilô. The real thief tried to frame my brother.
Past (he/she …): সেই চোর তাকে ফাঁসাল, sei chor tāke phā̃sālô.

The word, taken apart.
ফাঁসানো (phā̃sāno) comes down from Sanskrit spāśayati, 'causes to be bound' (CDIAL 13814, which lists Bengali phā̃sā, 'to be ensnared'). A ফাঁস is a noose: to frame someone is to slip a noose on them.

Lesson 4 of 5.
লুট করা (luṭ kôrā) — to loot; to plunder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a bribe, then the Bengali for to frame.

You'll want to know: লুট করা.
লুট করা — luṭ kôrā — "to loot; to plunder".
ডাকাতরা রাতে বাজার লুট করল। ḍākātrā rāte bājār luṭ kôrlô. The robbers looted the market at night.
Past (he/she …): সেই ডাকাত দোকান লুট করল, sei ḍākāt dokān luṭ kôrlô.

The word, taken apart.
লুট করা (luṭ kôrā) puts লুট, 'plunder' — CDIAL 11078, a Middle Indo-Aryan luṭṭati, which lists Bengali luṭā, 'plunders' — before করা. English loot was borrowed from this Indian word.

প্রতারণা (prôtārônā) — fraud; deception.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to frame, then the Bengali for to loot.

You'll want to know: প্রতারণা.
প্রতারণা — prôtārônā — "fraud; deception".
একটা লোক প্রতারণা করে গ্রামের মানুষের টাকা নিল। ekṭā lok prôtārônā kôre grāmer mānusher ṭākā nilô. A man tricked the villagers out of their money.

The word, taken apart.
প্রতারণা (prôtārônā) is Sanskrit pratāraṇa, from the causative of pra-√tṝ, which Monier-Williams gives both as 'ferrying across' and as 'deceiving': taking someone across, and taking them in.
