# Chapter 1 — Practice and Recall

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] No new words — just the five, and the roots that reach both east and
west.

## Rapid recall

[PAUSE 1s each]
- [YOU SAY: hello, "a bow to you" — *namaste*]
- [YOU SAY: hello, "the making of a bow" — *namaskāraḥ*]
- [YOU SAY: thank you — *dhanyavādaḥ*]
- [YOU SAY: welcome, "well come" — *svāgatam*]
- [YOU SAY: yes and no — *ām* / *na*]

## The taproot both ways

[PAUSE 2s] Sanskrit points east *and* west. Trace each:
- [YOU SAY: which Sanskrit word do Hindi/Marathi/Punjabi/Bengali's greetings come
  from? (*namas* / *namaste*)]
- [YOU SAY: which Greek prefix is *su-* "good" the twin of? (*eu-*)]
- [YOU SAY: which English verb is the root √gam cousin to? (*come*)]
- [YOU SAY: which Latin word (last track) is *na* cousin to? (*nōn*)]

## Roots you now carry

[PAUSE 2s]
- *namas* "a bow" → every Indo-Aryan hello; root √nam "to bend"
- *te* "to you" → English **thee**, Latin *tē*
- *su-* "good" → Greek **eu-** (euphoria, eulogy)
- √gam "come" → English **come**, Latin *venīre*
- *na* "not" → English **no/not/none**, Latin *nōn*, German *nein*

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] In 1786 Sir William Jones said Sanskrit, Latin, and Greek shared "a
stronger affinity than could have been produced by accident." Name two words from
this chapter that show it. (Any two: *te*↔*thee*/*tē*, *su-*↔*eu-*, √gam↔*come*,
*na*↔*nōn*.)
