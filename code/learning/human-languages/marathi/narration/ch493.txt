Marathi, chapter 493: Dizzy and Stumbling.

Lesson 1 of 5.
कुबडी (kubḍī) — a crutch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to get breathless, then the Marathi for to choke on food.

You'll want to know: कुबडी.
कुबडी — kubḍī — "a crutch".
पाय मोडल्यामुळे तो कुबडी घेऊन चालतो. pāy moḍlyāmuḷe to kubḍī gheūn cālto. Since he broke his leg he walks with a crutch.
Feminine. Plural: कुबड्या, kubḍyā.
Plural कुबड्या, a pair of crutches.

The word, taken apart.
कुबडी (kubḍī) is a Marathi noun whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
चक्कर येणे (cakkar yeṇe) — to feel dizzy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to choke on food, then the Marathi for a crutch.

You'll want to know: चक्कर येणे.
चक्कर येणे — cakkar yeṇe — "to feel dizzy".
उन्हात उभं राहिल्यामुळे तिला चक्कर आली. unhāt ubhaṁ rāhilyāmuḷe tilā cakkar ālī. She felt dizzy from standing in the sun.
Past (to him …): त्याला चक्कर आली, tyālā cakkar ālī.
Dative. चक्कर also means a round: चक्कर मारणे, to go for a spin.

The word, taken apart.
चक्कर येणे (cakkar yeṇe) is a Marathi phrase built on चक्कर, a loan from Hindustani cakkar, "a whirl", which goes back to Sanskrit cakra, "wheel" (CDIAL 4538); येणे is the light verb.

दगडाला पाय लागून तो अडखळला. dagḍālā pāy lāgūn to aḍkhaḷlā. His foot hit a stone and he stumbled.

Of feet and of words: बोलताना अडखळणे (aḍkhaḷṇe), to stumble over words.

थंडीत रस्त्यावरचा कुत्रा कुडकुडत होता. thaṇḍīt rastyāvarcā kutrā kuḍkuḍat hotā. The street dog was shivering in the cold.
Past (he …): तो कुडकुडला, to kuḍkuḍlā.
Of cold, where थरथरणे also covers fear.

कुडकुडणे (kuḍkuḍṇe) is a Marathi verb built on a doubled syllable; its origin is not settled.
