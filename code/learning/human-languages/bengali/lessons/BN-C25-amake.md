# আমাকে (āmāke) — me

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C25-OBJECT-KE-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-AMI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Put the object ending on **ভাই**. Then say **I** and **my**.

## You'll want to know: আমাকে
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C25-AMAKE-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-AMI-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C25-OBJECT-KE-01] -->

> **আমাকে** — *āmāke* — **me**, **to me.**

A pronoun is a living noun, so it takes the living noun's ending — the third
time this book has said that and the third time it has been true without an
exception:

| job | the word | ending |
|---|---|---|
| I | **আমি** | — |
| my | **আমার** | **-র** |
| me | **আমাকে** | **-কে** |

Same stem **আমা-**, three endings, no memorisation.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

The stem **আমা-** goes back to Sanskrit *asmad*, the stem of "we" (CDIAL 986); where the ending **-কে** comes from is not settled.

## Grammar Lens: the frame that had no filler
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C25-AMAKE-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C09-DATIVE-02, BN-LEX-C09-BHALOLAGA-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-BHALO-01, BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01] -->

Chapter nine taught something odd and important: Bengali does not say *I like
tea*. It says, near enough, **tea is good to me** — the liking is not something
you do, it is something that happens **to** you.

That frame needs a *to me*, and the book had none. It has one now:

> **আমাকে চা ভালো লাগে** — *āmāke chā bhālo lāge* — **I like tea** (literally,
> *tea feels good to me*).

A whole family of Bengali sentences works this way — liking, needing, feeling
cold, being hungry — and every one of them was out of reach for want of this
word.
