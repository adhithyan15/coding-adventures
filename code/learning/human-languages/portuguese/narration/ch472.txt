Portuguese, chapter 472: Espertos e ingénuos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
esperto — clever, smart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for liar, then the Portuguese for to pretend.

You'll want to know: esperto.
esperto — "clever, smart".
A minha filha é muito esperta e aprende tudo depressa. My daughter is very clever and learns everything quickly.
Quick-witted rather than learned; inteligente is broader.

The word, taken apart.
esperto goes back to Old Portuguese esperto (EtymDB); it is usually traced to Latin expertus "tried, experienced", but the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ingénuo — naive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for clever, then the Portuguese for cunning.

You'll want to know: ingénuo.
ingénuo — "naive".
Era tão ingénuo que acreditava em tudo. He was so naive that he believed everything.
In Brazil: ingênuo. Not English ingenious: it means naive.

The word, taken apart.
ingénuo is a learned borrowing from Latin ingenuus "native, freeborn", and so "frank, open" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
desconfiar — to suspect; to distrust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for cunning, then the Portuguese for naive.

You'll want to know: desconfiar.
desconfiar — "to suspect; to distrust".
Desconfio que o meu filho não fez os trabalhos de casa. I suspect my son has not done his homework.
Desconfiar que — to suspect that; desconfiar de alguém — to distrust someone.

The word, taken apart.
desconfiar is built from des- and confiar "to trust", which goes back to Latin cōnfīdere "to trust fully" (Lewis & Short), built on fīdere "to trust", a relative of fidēs "faith".
