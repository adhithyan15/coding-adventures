Persian, chapter 451: People in town.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
شلوغی (sholughi) — the crowds, crowdedness; the rush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a statue, then the Persian for a fountain.

You'll want to know: شلوغی.
شلوغی (sholughi) — "the crowds, crowdedness; the rush".
از شلوغی بازار خسته شدیم. Az sholughi-ye bâzâr khaste shodim. We got tired of the crowds in the bazaar.
Built on sholugh 'crowded, busy' + -i.

The word, taken apart.
sholughi is built from sholugh 'crowded, noisy' and the noun ending -i. The origin of sholugh is not settled, and EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 6.
نیمکت (nimkat) — a bench.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a fountain, then the Persian for the crowds.

You'll want to know: نیمکت.
نیمکت (nimkat) — "a bench".
پیرمرد روی نیمکت پارک نشسته بود. Pirmard ru-ye nimkat-e pârk neshaste bud. The old man was sitting on a bench in the park.
Also a school bench or desk.

The word, taken apart.
nimkat is a native word that EtymDB lists without older forms. It is usually analysed as nim 'half' and kat 'couch, bed': a half-couch.

Lesson 3 of 6.
ساکن (sâken) — a resident, an inhabitant; living (in a place).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the crowds, then the Persian for a bench.

You'll want to know: ساکن.
ساکن (sâken) — "a resident, an inhabitant; living (in a place)".
او ده سال ساکن این محله است. U dah sâl sâken-e in mahalle ast. He has lived in this neighbourhood for ten years.
Arabic plural sokkân 'inhabitants'. Also 'still, motionless'.

The word, taken apart.
sâken was borrowed from Arabic and is usually traced to Arabic sākin 'dwelling, at rest'. EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
رهگذر (rahgozar) — a passer-by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a bench, then the Persian for a resident.

You'll want to know: رهگذر.
رهگذر (rahgozar) — "a passer-by".
یک رهگذر راه ایستگاه را به ما نشان داد. Yek rahgozar râh-e istgâh râ be mâ neshân dâd. A passer-by showed us the way to the station.
rah (a short form of râh) + gozar 'passing'.

The word, taken apart.
rahgozar is a native compound of rah, a short form of râh 'road' (Middle Persian rāh, EtymDB), and gozar, the present stem of gozashtan 'to pass'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
همشهری (hamshahri) — someone from the same town, a fellow townsman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a resident, then the Persian for a passer-by.

You'll want to know: همشهری.
همشهری (hamshahri) — "someone from the same town, a fellow townsman".
در فرودگاه یک همشهری دیدم. Dar forudgâh yek hamshahri didam. At the airport I met someone from my home town.
ham- 'same' + shahr 'town' + -i. Also the name of a newspaper.

The word, taken apart.
hamshahri is a native compound of ham- 'same, together' and shahr 'town', from Middle Persian šahr (EtymDB), with the ending -i.
