Spanish, chapter 655: Un vuelo movido.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
el desfase horario — jet lag; the time difference.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the crew, then the Spanish for the flight attendant.

You'll want to know: el desfase horario.
el desfase horario — "jet lag; the time difference".
Con el desfase horario no duermo bien. With jet lag I do not sleep well.
Many people also say el jet lag.

The word, taken apart.
desfase is built on fase "phase", from Greek phásis "appearance" (EtymDB); horario is built on hora "hour", from Latin hōra.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
la turbulencia — the turbulence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the flight attendant, then the Spanish for jet lag.

You'll want to know: la turbulencia.
la turbulencia — "the turbulence".
Hay turbulencias, abróchense el cinturón. There is turbulence, fasten your seat belts.
Often plural on a plane: hay turbulencias.

The word, taken apart.
turbulencia is a learned borrowing from Latin turbulentia (EtymDB), from turbulentus "restless, stormy" (Lewis & Short), built on turba "uproar, crowd".

You'll want to know: el mareo.
el mareo — "travel sickness; dizziness".
Estas pastillas son para el mareo. These pills are for travel sickness.
Built on marear, which comes from mar: the first mareo was at sea.

The word, taken apart.
mareo is built on marear "to make seasick" (EtymDB), which comes from mar "sea", inherited from Latin mare.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: marearse.
marearse — "to feel sick (in a vehicle), to get dizzy".
Me mareo si leo en el coche. I feel sick if I read in the car.
Estar mareado — to feel sick or dizzy.

The word, taken apart.
marearse is built on mar "sea", inherited from Latin mare (EtymDB); it first meant to be seasick.
