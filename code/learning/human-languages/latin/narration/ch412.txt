Latin, chapter 412: The Inquiry.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
cognitiō, cognitiōnis — an inquiry, a hearing; knowledge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a promise to appear in court, then the Latin for a legal expert.

You'll want to know: cognitiō, cognitiōnis.
cognitiō, cognitiōnis — "an inquiry, a hearing; knowledge".
Cōnsul cognitiōnem dē caede habuit. The consul held an inquiry into the murder.
cognitiō, cognitiōnis f., built on cognōscō: getting to know, and a judge's hearing of a case. English cognition.

The word, taken apart.
cognitiō is the noun built on cognitus, the participle of cognōscō "to get to know" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English cognition comes from it.

You'll want to know: dēlātor, dēlātōris.
dēlātor, dēlātōris — "an informer, an accuser".
Dēlātōrēs multōs innocentēs accūsāvērunt. Informers accused many innocent people.
dēlātor, dēlātōris m., built on the verb for reporting a name to a magistrate. Under the emperors, informers could grow rich.

The word, taken apart.
dēlātor is built on dēlātum, the supine of dēferō "to carry down, to report" (Lewis & Short): one who reports a name. English delator comes from it (EtymDB).
