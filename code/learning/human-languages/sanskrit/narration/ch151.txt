Lesson 1 of 5.
उत्साहः (utsāhaḥ) — enthusiasm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for vomiting, then the Sanskrit for a stomach ache.

You'll want to know: उत्साहः.
उत्साहः — utsāhaḥ — "enthusiasm".

The word, taken apart.
utsāha comes from ud-√sah "be equal to, be able", the root √sah "bear, prevail" with ud "up" (MW; CDIAL 1882): first "power, firmness", then "energy, zeal".

ālasya is built from alasa "lazy" with the first vowel lengthened (CDIAL 1371): "laziness, sloth". It lives on as Hindi ālsī "lazy" (CDIAL 1371).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मत्सरः (matsaraḥ) — jealousy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for courage, then the Sanskrit for laziness.

You'll want to know: मत्सरः.
मत्सरः — matsaraḥ — "jealousy".

The word, taken apart.
matsara probably comes from the root √mad "be drunk, exult" (MW; CDIAL 9756): in the Rigveda it means "exhilarating", and later "envious, jealous". A second matsara, "mosquito", gave Hindi macchar; CDIAL 9757 thinks it possibly the same word, named for its hum.
