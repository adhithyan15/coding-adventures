Lesson 1 of 5.
আকার (ākār) — a shape, a size.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Bengali for anger, then the Bengali for peace.

You'll want to know: আকার.
আকার — ākār — "a shape, a size".

The word, taken apart.
আকার (ākār) is Sanskrit ākāra, "shape", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 1004).

Lesson 2 of 5.
টুকরো (ṭukro) — a piece.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for peace, then the Bengali for a shape.

You'll want to know: টুকরো.
টুকরো — ṭukro — "a piece".

The word, taken apart.
টুকরো (ṭukro) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ṭukka, "piece", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5466). CDIAL lists Bengali ṭukrā, "small piece", made with an extra -r-.

Lesson 5 of 5.
কল (kôl) — a tap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a queue, then the Bengali for a procession.

You'll want to know: কল.
কল — kôl — "a tap".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Bengali kal, "lock of a gun, machine", under Sanskrit kalā, "any practical art", a word it takes from Dravidian (CDIAL 2927). A tap is a kôl in the sense "a device".
