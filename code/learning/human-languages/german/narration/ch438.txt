German, chapter 438: Die alte Stadt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Altstadt — the old town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the information, then the German for to know one's way around.

You'll want to know: die Altstadt.
die Altstadt — "the old town".
Die Altstadt hat viele kleine Gassen. The old town has lots of little lanes.
Plural: die Altstädte, rare.

The word, taken apart.
Altstadt is built from alt 'old', inherited from Proto-Germanic aldaz (EtymDB), and Stadt 'town', from Old High German stat 'place' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Kathedrale — the cathedral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to know one's way around, then the German for the old town.

You'll want to know: die Kathedrale.
die Kathedrale — "the cathedral".
Die Kathedrale ist über siebenhundert Jahre alt. The cathedral is over seven hundred years old.
Plural: die Kathedralen. Many German towns say der Dom or das Münster.

The word, taken apart.
Kathedrale is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin (ecclēsia) cathedrālis 'church with a bishop's chair', from Latin cathedra 'chair', from Greek kathédra (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Glockenturm — the bell tower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the old town, then the German for the cathedral.

You'll want to know: der Glockenturm.
der Glockenturm — "the bell tower".
Vom Glockenturm sieht man die ganze Stadt. From the bell tower the whole town can be seen.
Plural: die Glockentürme.

The word, taken apart.
Glockenturm is built from Glocke 'bell', which goes back to Old High German glocca (EtymDB) and is usually traced to a Celtic word like Old Irish clocc, and Turm 'tower', from Latin turris.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Stadtmauer — the town wall, the city wall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the cathedral, then the German for the bell tower.

You'll want to know: die Stadtmauer.
die Stadtmauer — "the town wall, the city wall".
Um die Altstadt steht noch eine alte Stadtmauer. There is still an old wall round the old town.
Plural: die Stadtmauern. Die Mauer is a strong outside wall; die Wand is a wall inside a house.

The word, taken apart.
Stadtmauer is built from Stadt 'town', an inherited word, and Mauer 'wall', an early borrowing from Latin mūrus (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Stadttor — the town gate, the city gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bell tower, then the German for the town wall.

You'll want to know: das Stadttor.
das Stadttor — "the town gate, the city gate".
Durch das alte Stadttor fahren keine Autos. No cars drive through the old town gate.
Plural: die Stadttore.

The word, taken apart.
Stadttor is built from Stadt 'town' and Tor 'gate', both inherited: Tor goes back through Middle High German to Old High German tor (EtymDB).
