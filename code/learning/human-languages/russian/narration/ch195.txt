Russian, chapter 195: Nature, Branch, Root, Rose, Oak.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
природа (priróda) — nature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for beetroot, then the Russian for peas.

You'll want to know: природа.
природа (priróda) — "nature".

The word, taken apart.
природа (priróda) is при- with род, "birth, kin": what one is born with, then nature itself. It copies Greek phýsis and Latin nātūra, both "birth, nature".

Lesson 2 of 5.
ветка (vétka) — a branch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for peas, then the Russian for nature.

You'll want to know: ветка.
ветка (vétka) — "a branch".

The word, taken apart.
ветка (vétka) comes from ветвь, "a bough", which Vasmer puts with вить, "to twist", and relates to Lithuanian vytìs, "a willow switch", and Latin vītis, "a vine".

Lesson 5 of 5.
дуб (dub) — an oak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a root, then the Russian for a rose.

You'll want to know: дуб.
дуб (dub) — "an oak".

The word, taken apart.
дуб (dub) from Proto-Slavic dǫbŭ — inherited. Vasmer apparently starts from dombros, from the root of Greek démō, "I build", and Old Norse timbr: English timber would be a cousin; the account is not settled.
