Mandarin Chinese, chapter 513: Ambitions.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
chuàngyè — to start a business; to found an enterprise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to become famous, then the Mandarin for a top school.

You'll want to know: chuàngyè.
chuàngyè — "to start a business; to found an enterprise".
dàxué bìyè yǐhòu wǒ xiǎng zìjǐ chuàngyè — After university I want to start my own business.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chuàngyè is built from chuàng 'to found' and 业 'an undertaking', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsrhjangH and ngjaep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary gives the first as another spelling of a word it glosses, after an older dictionary, 'to found a craft and begin an undertaking'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

dádào — "to attain (a goal); to reach a level".
tā xué le sān nián dádào le zìjǐ de mùbiāo — He studied for three years and reached his goal.

The word, taken apart.
dádào is built from dá 'to get through' and 到 'to arrive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dat and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to reach through' and the second 'to arrive'.

fèndòu — "to struggle (towards a goal)".
tā fèndòu le shí nián cái chénggōng — He struggled for ten years before he succeeded.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fèndòu is built from fèn 'to spread the wings' and dòu 'to fight', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjunH and tuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a bird spreading its feathers and the second, after an older dictionary, as two men facing each other with weapons.

The word, taken apart.
亲手 (qīnshǒu) is built from 亲 'close' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshin and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
