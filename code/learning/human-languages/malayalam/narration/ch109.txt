Malayalam, chapter 109: Neither This Nor That.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa) — neither of them is here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Join two things with the ending you know: tea and coffee.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then say the word for there isn't any.

You'll want to know.
ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa). cāyayum kāppiyum illa — "There is neither tea nor coffee."
Nothing in that sentence is new. You built ചായയും കാപ്പിയും when you learned to join two things, and ഇല്ല has been yours since your first conversation. Put the one in front of the other and Malayalam has said neither.

Grammar Lens: English buys a special word for this and Malayalam does not.
English reaches for a pair of words it keeps only for this job — neither … nor — and they behave unlike anything else in the sentence.
Malayalam spends nothing:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ചായയും കാപ്പിയും, the joining you already do.
ഇല്ല, the negative you already have.
The list is built the ordinary way, with the ending on every item, and then the whole list is denied at once. What English treats as a special construction, Malayalam treats as a list that happens to end in a negative.
Both items are inside the denial, which is the thing to hear. You are not saying there is no tea, and coffee. The ending has tied the two together before ഇല്ല arrives, so the negative lands on the pair.

[your turn — say: the same pair, denied — ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which part of that sentence ties the two things together]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How does Malayalam say neither tea nor coffee? (ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa).) How many new words did that need? (None — the joining ending and the negative, both already yours.) Does the negative land on one item or the pair? (The pair.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
അധ്യാപകനും വൈദ്യനും അല്ല (adhyaapakanum vaidyanum alla) — neither of them is what you called it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say neither tea nor coffee.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then say which of your two negatives denies that a thing is there, and which denies that a thing is so.

You'll want to know.
അധ്യാപകനും വൈദ്യനും അല്ല (adhyaapakanum vaidyanum alla). adhyāpakanum vaidyanum alla — "[He] is neither a teacher nor a doctor."
Same joining ending on each item. Different word at the end.

Grammar Lens: the choice you learned does not go away.
You were told that Malayalam makes you pick a negative where English spends one word on both. That decision survives into this construction, and English's neither … nor hides it completely:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa), neither is there.
അധ്യാപകനും വൈദ്യനും അല്ല (adhyaapakanum vaidyanum alla), neither is what you called it.
An English speaker says neither … nor for both rows and never notices a question was asked. You have to answer it before you can finish the sentence — and the question is the same one as ever: are you denying that something is present, or denying that something is so?
The joining half does not change. Only the last word does, which is worth noticing: the work of coordinating stayed where it was, and all the difficulty lives in the negative.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the tea-and-coffee sentence, and say which negative it ends in]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which negative you would reach for to deny that two people are in the room]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which part of the sentence changes between the two kinds of neither? (Only the last word — the joining is identical.) Which negative denies that something is present? (ഇല്ല.) Which denies that something is what you called it? (അല്ല.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(neither … nor) (randum alla, randum illa) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say neither tea nor coffee, then neither a teacher nor a doctor.

Grammar Lens: one joining, two endings.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല (caayayum kaappiyum illa), neither is there.
അധ്യാപകനും വൈദ്യനും അല്ല (adhyaapakanum vaidyanum alla), neither is so.
The left half of those two sentences is built the same way. Every item takes the joining ending, and the pair is denied as a pair. What differs is the last word, and picking it is the same decision you have been making since you owned two negatives.

What you've built.
You could join things and you could deny things, and you had never been shown that doing both at once needs nothing further. English keeps a special pair of words for this and teaches them as their own lesson. Malayalam had already given you every piece; what was missing was the sentence that puts them together.
