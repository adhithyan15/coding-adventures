Lesson 1 of 3.
దయచేసి (dayacēsi) — please (having done compassion).

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script you'll notice: Sound & structure point until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Telugu's "please" carries a whole small sentence inside it: "having done (me) a compassion."

You'll want to know: The word.
దయచేసి (dayacēsi) = please, literally "having done kindness / compassion." Two pieces:
దయ (daya) = compassion, kindness, grace — from Sanskrit daya.
చేసి (cēsi) = "having done," from the verb చేయు (cēyu) "to do."
So dayacēsi is having done (a) kindness — you ask by crediting the listener with the grace you hope they'll show.
It is the twin of Tamil tayavu seytu ("do the kindness") — both use a "do" verb — and cousins with Kannada dayaviṭṭu ("having placed compassion"). Further off, Arabic min faḍlik ("from your grace") and Hindi kṛpayā (from kṛpā, "compassion") ask in the very same spirit.

The word, taken apart - The Dravidian family, side by side.
daya + a light verb, four ways:
Telugu దయచేసి dayacēsi — compassion + having done.
Tamil தயவுசெய்து tayavu seytu — kindness + do.
Kannada ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು dayaviṭṭu — compassion + having placed.
Malayalam ദയവായി dayavāyi — compassion + as / having become.

Script you'll notice: Sound & structure point.
The middle syllable చే is ca wearing the long‑ē sign ‑ే (the counterpart of the short‑e sign ‑ె), giving cē; then సి is sa + the i sign (‑ి). Telugu marks a vowel's length and quality by the little sign riding on the consonant — the consonant stays put, the vowel-sign changes the tune.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is the Telugu word for please? (దయచేసి dayacēsi.) What do its pieces mean? ("Compassion" daya + "having done" cēsi.) Which cousins ask the same way? (Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam with daya — plus Arabic faḍl, Hindi kṛpā.) Which sign sits on the middle syllable? (The long‑ē sign ‑ే, making చ (ca) into చే.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
‑ండి (-aṇḍi) — where Telugu actually keeps "please".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You now have దయచేసి (dayacēsi) for please. Now the honest part: most of the time, a Telugu speaker asking politely does not say it.

You'll want to know: The ending does the work.
Telugu politeness rides on the respectful command ending ‑ండి (-aṇḍi). It is not a separate word you add — it is a tail on the verb, and it is already "please."
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
respectful form: కూర్చోండి kūrcōṇḍi, please sit.
respectful form: చెప్పండి ceppaṇḍi, please tell me.
Each of these is a verb with ‑ండి (-aṇḍi) on the end, and each is already polite — no extra word required. There is a shorter form of every one of them, used with a friend or a child; you will meet those verbs properly later, so take the ‑ండి (-aṇḍi) forms as wholes for now. The short form is not rude, it is close, and using it with a stranger is the mistake. Leaving out దయచేసి (dayacēsi) is not.

Why it's said this way: Where దయచేసి actually goes.
The standalone దయచేసి (dayacēsi) is formal or emphatic. It is not the everyday "please" — it is the one you reach for to warm a request further, on top of an ending that is already polite:
కూర్చోండి — please sit. Complete, polite, ordinary.
దయచేసి (dayacēsi) కూర్చోండి — do please sit. Warmer, more insistent, more formal.
So the two stack. What they do not do is substitute: దయచేసి (dayacēsi) in front of the short form reads as oddly as "kindly sit down, mate."
English keeps politeness in a word you add; Telugu keeps it in the shape of the verb. A learner hunting for a word to translate please will reach for దయచేసి (dayacēsi) far too often and still sound abrupt — because the abruptness was never in the missing word. It was in the ending.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What carries "please" in ordinary Telugu speech? (The ending ‑ండి -aṇḍi, on the verb.) Is కూర్చోండి already polite without దయచేసి (dayacēsi)? (Yes — the ending is the politeness.) When do you add దయచేసి (dayacēsi)? (To make a request warmer or more formal, on top of ‑ండి.) What is wrong with putting దయచేసి (dayacēsi) in front of the short form? (The short ending undercuts the polite word.)

One character this time — and you have been saying it for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

Script you'll notice: చ.
చ — ca.
It is a consonant, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an a already in it. So it is not c, it is ca.
You already say these, and every one of them has చ (ca) somewhere inside it:
పని చేయు pani cēyu — to work (lit. "work-do").
నాకు తెలుగు వచ్చు nāku telugu vaccu — 'I know Telugu'.
దయచేసి dayacēsi — please (dayacēsi.

Writing: చ — copy what you see.
Put your pen on చ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start the character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
