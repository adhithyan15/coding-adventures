Mandarin Chinese, chapter 233: How People Look.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yàngzi — appearance; manner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to crash into, then the Mandarin for an ambulance.

You'll want to know: yàngzi.
yàngzi — "appearance; manner".
tā de yàngzi hěn xiàng tā bàba — He takes after his dad.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yàngzi is built from yàng 'shape' and the noun ending 子, inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). No dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
yǎnjìng — glasses; spectacles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an ambulance, then the Mandarin for appearance.

You'll want to know: yǎnjìng.
yǎnjìng — "glasses; spectacles".
wǒ de yǎnjìng ne — Where are my glasses?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǎnjìng is built from yǎn 'eye' and jìng 'mirror', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngeanX and kjaengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the eye'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
miáotiao — slim; slender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for glasses for the eyes, then the Mandarin for a beard.

You'll want to know: miáotiao.
miáotiao — "slim; slender".
tā hěn miáotiao — She's very slender.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
miáotiao is built from miáo 'a sprout' and tiáo 'a twig'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a small branch'; the dictionary glosses the first, mjew there, 'young crops in the field'. In the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pífū — skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a beard, then the Mandarin for slender.

You'll want to know: pífū.
pífū — "skin".
tā de pífū hěn bái — She has very fair skin.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pífū is built from pí 'hide' and fū 'skin'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese bje (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed with this very word, 'skin'; the dictionary glosses the second, pju there, the same way.
