Malayalam, chapter 368: In Court.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
കോടതി (kōṭati) — a court of law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to kidnap, then the Malayalam for to keep watch on.

You'll want to know: കോടതി.
കോടതി — kōṭati — "a court of law".
നാളെ അച്ഛന് കോടതിയിൽ പോകണം. nāḷe acchanŭ kōṭatiyil pōkaṇaṁ. Tomorrow father has to go to court.
In court is കോടതിയിൽ. A lawyer, വക്കീൽ, argues there.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of കോടതി (kōṭati) is not settled. It is the everyday Malayalam word for a court of law, where a വക്കീൽ, a lawyer, works.

Lesson 2 of 5.
കേസ് (kēsŭ) — a court case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to keep watch on, then the Malayalam for a court of law.

You'll want to know: കേസ്.
കേസ് — kēsŭ — "a court case".
ആ കേസ് പത്ത് വർഷം നീണ്ടു. ā kēsŭ pattŭ varṣaṁ nīṇṭu. That case dragged on for ten years.
A case is filed: കേസ് (kēsŭ) കൊടുക്കുക.

The word, taken apart.
കേസ് (kēsŭ) was borrowed from English case, in its legal sense. Like many English nouns, it ends in a short -ŭ in Malayalam.

Lesson 3 of 5.
വിചാരണ (vicāraṇa) — a trial.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a court of law, then the Malayalam for a court case.

You'll want to know: വിചാരണ.
വിചാരണ — vicāraṇa — "a trial".
കേസിന്റെ വിചാരണ അടുത്ത മാസം തുടങ്ങും. kēsinṟe vicāraṇa aṭutta māsaṁ tuṭaṅṅuṁ. The trial of the case will begin next month.
A court holds it: വിചാരണ (vicāraṇa) നടത്തുക.

The word, taken apart.
വിചാരണ (vicāraṇa) is borrowed from Sanskrit vicāraṇa, "consideration, reflexion, discussion" (Monier-Williams): a trial is the court's weighing of a case.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ന്യായാധിപൻ (nyāyādhipan) — a judge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a court case, then the Malayalam for a trial.

You'll want to know: ന്യായാധിപൻ.
ന്യായാധിപൻ — nyāyādhipan — "a judge".
ന്യായാധിപൻ സാക്ഷിയോട് ചോദ്യങ്ങൾ ചോദിച്ചു. nyāyādhipan sākṣiyōṭŭ cōdyaṅṅaḷ cōdiccu. The judge asked the witness questions.
The formal word: ന്യായം, justice, + അധിപൻ, a chief.

The word, taken apart.
ന്യായാധിപൻ (nyāyādhipan) is built from Sanskrit nyāya, "rule, method; a judicial sentence" (CDIAL 7614), which gave ന്യായം (nyāyaṁ), and adhipa, "a ruler, a commander" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
പ്രതി (prati) — the accused; a defendant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a trial, then the Malayalam for a judge.

You'll want to know: പ്രതി.
പ്രതി — prati — "the accused; a defendant".
പ്രതി കോടതിയിൽ ഒന്നും പറഞ്ഞില്ല. prati kōṭatiyil onnuṁ paṟaññilla. The accused said nothing in court.
Also a copy of a book or a paper: രണ്ട് പ്രതി (prati), two copies.

The word, taken apart.
പ്രതി (prati) is usually traced to Sanskrit prati, "towards, against, in opposition to" (CDIAL 8540): the accused is the one the case stands against.
