Latin, chapter 465: Peck, Pint, By weight, Quarter-as, Money-changer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
modius, modiī — a peck (a measure of grain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to weigh out, then the Latin for to weigh carefully.

You'll want to know: modius, modiī.
modius, modiī — "a peck (a measure of grain)".
Agricola trēs modiōs frūmentī vendit. The farmer sells three pecks of grain.
Second declension, masculine: modius, modiī m., genitive plural often modium. About nine litres, the standard grain measure.

The word, taken apart.
modius is built on modus 'measure' with the suffix -ius (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
pondō — by weight, in weight; a pound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a peck, then the Latin for a pint.

You'll want to know: pondō.
pondō — "by weight, in weight; a pound".
Pistor duo pondō farīnae emit. The baker buys two pounds of flour.
An old ablative of pondus 'weight', used as an adverb and as an undeclined noun: decem pondō, ten pounds. English pound comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
pondō is an old ablative of pondus 'weight' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from the root of pendō 'to weigh'. English pound comes from it, borrowed early into Germanic.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for a pint, then the Latin for by weight.

You'll want to know: quadrāns, quadrantis.
quadrāns, quadrantis — "a quarter-as (a small coin); a quarter".
Prō balneō tantum quadrantem solvimus. We pay only a quarter-as for the bath.
Third declension, masculine: quadrāns, quadrantis m. A quarter of an as, the price of a public bath. English quadrant comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
quadrāns is built on quattuor 'four' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a fourth part. English quadrant comes from it.

Second declension, masculine: nummulārius, nummulāriī m. He tested and changed coins at a small table.
