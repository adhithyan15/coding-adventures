Mandarin Chinese, chapter 99: Getting Washed.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
洗 (xǐ) — to wash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for every day, then the Mandarin for after.

You'll want to know: 洗.
洗 — xǐ — "to wash".
我去洗手 wǒ qù xǐ shǒu — I'm going to wash my hands.
The new character, in pieces: 洗 (xǐ) — the water piece of 汉 on the left; on the right a six-stroke piece read xiān, the first syllable of xiānsheng. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
洗 (xǐ) is inherited from Middle Chinese sejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to wash and bathe'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
洗 (xǐ) — the water piece, then xiān.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the water piece, then xiān and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — hàn, méi, ér]

Script — the water piece, then xiān.
洗 (xǐ).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The water piece, three strokes, as in 汉 and 没: a dot, a second dot under it, then a stroke that rises from the bottom left.
The right piece, xiān, six strokes: a short left-falling stroke, a horizontal, a vertical down through it, a long horizontal; then a left-falling stroke, and a vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up, as at the foot of 儿.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 洗 (xǐ), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 洗 (xǐ)? (Two — the water piece, then xiān.) Which characters you know have the same water piece? (汉 and 没.) What does the last stroke look like? (The right stroke of 儿.)

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for after, then the Mandarin for to wash.

You'll want to know: shuāyá.
shuāyá — "to brush one's teeth".
wǒ měitiān zǎoshang shuāyá — I brush my teeth every morning.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shuāyá is built from shuā 'to brush' and yá 'tooth', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srwaet and ngae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to brush and wipe' and 'teeth'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
xǐliǎn — to wash one's face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to wash, then the Mandarin for to brush one's teeth.

You'll want to know: xǐliǎn.
xǐliǎn — "to wash one's face".
nǐ xǐliǎn le ma — Have you washed your face?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones in a row: xǐ is said with a rising tone before liǎn, though both are written with the third tone.

The word, taken apart.
xǐliǎn is built from 洗 (xǐ) 'to wash' and liǎn 'face'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese sejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary has the second only as leamX, the name of a kind of stew, so how it came to mean face is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.

You'll want to know: xǐzǎo.
xǐzǎo — "to have a shower; to have a bath".
wǒ wǎnshang xǐzǎo — I have a shower in the evening.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones again: xǐ rises before zǎo, and is still written xǐ.

The word, taken apart.
xǐzǎo is built from 洗 (xǐ) and zǎo, both inherited from Middle Chinese, sejX and tsawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to wash and bathe' and the second with the first: to wash.
