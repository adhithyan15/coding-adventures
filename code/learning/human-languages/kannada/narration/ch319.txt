Kannada, chapter 319: A Thief and a War.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಯುದ್ಧ (yuddha) — a war; a battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for chalk, then the Kannada for a ruler.

You'll want to know: ಯುದ್ಧ.
ಯುದ್ಧ — yuddha — "a war; a battle".
ಯುದ್ಧದ ಫಲಿತಾಂಶ ಹವಾಮಾನದ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿತ್ತು. yuddhada phalitāṃśa havāmānada mēle avalambisittu. The outcome of the war depended on the weather.
With ಮಾಡು it makes a verb: ಯುದ್ಧ (yuddha) ಮಾಡು, to fight a war.

The word, taken apart.
ಯುದ್ಧ (yuddha) is borrowed from Sanskrit yuddha, "fought", and as a noun "a battle", the past participle of the root yudh, "to fight" (CDIAL 10499). Kannada keeps the Sanskrit form whole, as it does many learned nouns.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಸೈನಿಕ (sainika) — a soldier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a ruler, then the Kannada for a war.

You'll want to know: ಸೈನಿಕ.
ಸೈನಿಕ — sainika — "a soldier".
ಸೈನಿಕರು ಯುದ್ಧದಿಂದ ಮನೆಗೆ ಹಿಂತಿರುಗಿದರು. sainikaru yuddhadinda manege hintirugidaru. The soldiers came back home from the war.
Plural: ಸೈನಿಕರು, sainikaru.

The word, taken apart.
ಸೈನಿಕ (sainika) is borrowed from Sanskrit sainika, "belonging to an army", and as a noun "a soldier" (Monier-Williams). It is built on senā, "an army", with the ending -ika, "one who belongs to".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸು (ākramisu) — to invade; to attack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a war, then the Kannada for a soldier.

You'll want to know: ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸು.
ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸು — ākramisu — "to invade; to attack".
ಸೈನಿಕರು ಆ ದೇಶವನ್ನು ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸಿದರು. sainikaru ā dēśavannu ākramisidaru. The soldiers invaded that country.
Past (I …): ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸಿದೆ, ākramiside.
What is attacked takes -ಅನ್ನು: ದೇಶವನ್ನು ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸು (ākramisu), to invade a country.

The word, taken apart.
ಆಕ್ರಮಿಸು (ākramisu) is built from Sanskrit ākramaṇa, "stepping on, an advance" (CDIAL 1016), from ā- "towards" and kram "to step", with the Kannada ending -isu that turns a Sanskrit noun into a verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಕಳ್ಳ (kaḷḷa) — a thief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a soldier, then the Kannada for to invade.

You'll want to know: ಕಳ್ಳ.
ಕಳ್ಳ — kaḷḷa — "a thief".
ಕಳ್ಳ ನನ್ನ ಚೀಲವನ್ನು ಕದ್ದನು. kaḷḷa nanna cīlavannu kaddanu. The thief stole my bag.
Plural: ಕಳ್ಳರು, kaḷḷaru.
A woman thief is ಕಳ್ಳಿ. The verb beside it is ಕದಿ, to steal: ಕದ್ದನು, he stole.

The word, taken apart.
ಕಳ್ಳ (kaḷḷa) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1372 lists Kannada kaḷḷa "a thief" beside Tamil kaḷḷaṉ "a thief, a robber" and kaḷ "to steal".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಓಡಿಹೋಗು (ōḍihōgu) — to run away; to escape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to invade, then the Kannada for a thief.

You'll want to know: ಓಡಿಹೋಗು.
ಓಡಿಹೋಗು — ōḍihōgu — "to run away; to escape".
ಪೊಲೀಸರನ್ನು ನೋಡಿ ಕಳ್ಳ ಓಡಿಹೋದನು. polīsarannu nōḍi kaḷḷa ōḍihōdanu. Seeing the police, the thief ran away.
Past (I …): ಓಡಿಹೋದೆ, ōḍihōde.
ಓಡಿ is ಓಡು, to run, as "having run"; ಹೋಗು adds "away". Run and go make one verb.

The word, taken apart.
ಓಡಿಹೋಗು (ōḍihōgu) is a native Dravidian compound: ōḍu "to run" (DEDR 1041) and hōgu "to go" (DEDR 4572).
