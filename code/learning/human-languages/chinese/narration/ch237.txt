Mandarin Chinese, chapter 237: Plants.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zhíwù — plant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for luckily, then the Mandarin for nearly.

You'll want to know: zhíwù.
zhíwù — "plant".
wǒ xǐhuan zhíwù — I love plants.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhíwù is built from zhí 'to plant' and wù 'thing', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyik and mjut (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to plant' and 'to set up'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
花生 (huāshēng) — peanut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for nearly, then the Mandarin for a plant.

You'll want to know: 花生.
花生 — huāshēng — "peanut".
我现在不吃花生了 wǒ xiànzài bù chī huāshēng le — I don't eat peanuts any more.

The word, taken apart.
花生 (huāshēng) is built from 花 'flower' and 生 'to grow', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwae and srjaeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); how the pair came to name the peanut is not settled.

You'll want to know: yèzi.
yèzi — "leaf".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yèzi is built from yè 'leaf' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, yep and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'branches and leaves'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fāyá — to sprout; to bud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a peanut, then the Mandarin for a leaf.

You'll want to know: fāyá.
fāyá — "to sprout; to bud".
chūntiān lái le xiǎo cǎo fāyá le — Spring is here, and the grass is coming up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fāyá is built from fā 'to send out' and yá 'a shoot'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese pjot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to rise' and 'to open out'; the dictionary glosses the second, ngae there, 'a sprout'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 5 of 5.
xīguā — watermelon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a leaf, then the Mandarin for to sprout.

xīguā — "watermelon".
xiàtiān wǒmen chángcháng chī xīguā — We eat a lot of watermelon in summer.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xīguā is built from 西 'west' and guā 'melon', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sej and kwae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a gourd and lists many kinds.
