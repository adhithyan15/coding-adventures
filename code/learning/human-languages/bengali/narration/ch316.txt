Bengali, chapter 316: The Siege.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ঘেরা (gherā) — to surround; to besiege.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a gun, then the Bengali for a cannon.

You'll want to know: ঘেরা.
ঘেরা — gherā — "to surround; to besiege".
শত্রুর সৈন্যরা দুর্গটা ঘিরে ফেলল। shôtrur soinyôrā durgôṭā ghire phellô. The enemy soldiers surrounded the fort.
Past (he/she …): সেই সেনাবাহিনী শহরটা ঘিরল, sei senābāhinī shôhôrṭā ghirlô.
The stem vowel rises in the past: ঘিরল, surrounded.

The word, taken apart.
ঘেরা (gherā) is filed in CDIAL 4474 under a Middle Indo-Aryan ghir-, 'go round', and ghēr-, 'surround', which list Bengali gherā, 'to surround'.

পিছোনো — pichhono — "to fall back; to retreat".
খাবার শেষ হলে শত্রুরা পিছোল। khābār shesh hôle shôtrurā pichholô. When their food ran out, the enemy fell back.

পিছোনো (pichhono) is built on পিছে, behind — CDIAL 7990, a Middle Indo-Aryan paśca, 'the hinder part', which lists Bengali pichā, 'the rear' — as পিছনে is: to move to the back.
