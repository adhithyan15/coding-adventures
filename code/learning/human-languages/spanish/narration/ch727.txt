Spanish, chapter 727: Sensibles y educados.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
sensible — sensitive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for modest, then the Spanish for to show off.

You'll want to know: sensible.
sensible — "sensitive".
Es un chico muy sensible. He is a very sensitive boy.
A false friend: sensible is sensato.

The word, taken apart.
sensible comes from Latin sēnsibilis "having feeling" (EtymDB), from sentīre "to feel"; Spanish kept the feeling sense.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 4 of 6.
educado — polite, well-mannered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for sensitivity, then the Spanish for to offend.

You'll want to know: educado.
educado — "polite, well-mannered".
Tus hijos son muy educados. Your children are very polite.
A false friend: it means polite, not only educated. Maleducado is rude.

The word, taken apart.
educado is the participle of educar, a learned borrowing from Latin ēducāre "to bring up, to rear" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
grosero — rude, coarse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to offend, then the Spanish for polite.

You'll want to know: grosero.
grosero — "rude, coarse".
No seas grosero con la camarera. Do not be rude to the waitress.
Also vulgar: una palabra grosera.

The word, taken apart.
grosero is built on grueso "thick", which is inherited from Latin grossus "thick, coarse" (EtymDB); rough cloth became rough manners.
