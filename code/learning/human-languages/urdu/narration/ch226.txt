Urdu, chapter 226: Coriander, Mint, Aubergine, Pumpkin, Spinach.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دھنیا (dhaniyā) — coriander.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a rubber, then the Urdu for a file.

You'll want to know: دھنیا.
دھنیا — dhaniyā — "coriander".

The word, taken apart.
دھنیا (dhaniyā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dhāneyaka, "coriander" (CDIAL 6776). CDIAL lists it beside dhānaka, "coriander".

Lesson 3 of 5.
بینگن (baiṅgan) — an aubergine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for coriander, then the Urdu for mint.

You'll want to know: بینگن.
بینگن — baiṅgan — "an aubergine".

The word, taken apart.
بینگن (baiṅgan) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vātiṅgaṇa, "the egg-plant" (CDIAL 11503). CDIAL thinks the plant names go back to one source, perhaps Dravidian, and notes that Persian badingān came from here.
