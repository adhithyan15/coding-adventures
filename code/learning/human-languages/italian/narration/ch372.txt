Italian, chapter 372: Supponiamo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
supporre — to suppose, to assume.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for fundamental, then the Italian for the premise.

You'll want to know: supporre.
supporre — "to suppose, to assume".
Supponiamo che domani piova: che cosa facciamo? Let us suppose it rains tomorrow: what do we do?
Conjugated like porre: suppongo, supponi; past participle supposto. Supponiamo che takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
supporre comes from Latin suppōnere "to put under, to substitute" (Lewis & Short), built from sub "under" and pōnere "to put": an idea placed underneath the rest.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'ipotesi — the hypothesis, the possibility.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the premise, then the Italian for to suppose.

You'll want to know: l'ipotesi.
l'ipotesi — "the hypothesis, the possibility".
Nell'ipotesi che il treno sia in ritardo, prendiamo un taxi. If the train happens to be late, we will take a taxi.
The same in the plural: le ipotesi. Nell'ipotesi che takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
ipotesi is a learned borrowing from Late Latin hypothesis, from Greek hypóthesis "a placing under, a supposition", built from hypó "under" and thésis "a placing", the word behind Latin thesis "a proposition" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
presumere — to presume, to take for granted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to suppose, then the Italian for the hypothesis.

You'll want to know: presumere.
presumere — "to presume, to take for granted".
Presumo che tu sia stanco dopo il viaggio. I presume you are tired after the journey.
Past participle presunto. Presuntuoso means arrogant.

The word, taken apart.
presumere is a learned borrowing from Latin praesūmere "to take beforehand, to take for granted" (Lewis & Short), built from prae "before" and sūmere "to take".

Lesson 5 of 5.
il presupposto — the precondition, the assumption.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to presume, then the Italian for plausible.

You'll want to know: il presupposto.
il presupposto — "the precondition, the assumption".
Il presupposto è che tutti siano d'accordo. The assumption is that everyone agrees.
Partire dal presupposto che — to start from the assumption that.

The word, taken apart.
presupposto is the past participle of presupporre "to presuppose", built from pre- "before" and supporre, from Latin suppōnere "to put under" (Lewis & Short), on pōnere "to put".
