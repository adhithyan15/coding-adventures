Lesson 1 of 5.
नियमः (niyamaḥ) — a rule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dance, then the Sanskrit for painting.

You'll want to know: नियमः.
नियमः — niyamaḥ — "a rule".

The word, taken apart.
niyama is built on ni- and the root √yam, "to hold, restrain" (MW): first "restraint", then "fixed law" (CDIAL 7250). Hindi newā, "custom, method", comes from it.

कर्तव्यम् (kartavyam) — an obligation, a duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a rule, then the Sanskrit for righteousness.

You'll want to know: कर्तव्यम्.
कर्तव्यम् — kartavyam — "an obligation, a duty".

The word, taken apart.
kartavya is the future passive participle of the root √kṛ, "to do" (MW): "what is to be done", and so a duty (CDIAL 2859). It comes from the root of karomi, "I do", which you have already met.

Lesson 4 of 5.
अधिकारः (adhikāraḥ) — authority, a right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for righteousness, then the Sanskrit for an obligation.

You'll want to know: अधिकारः.
अधिकारः — adhikāraḥ — "authority, a right".

The word, taken apart.
adhikāra is built on adhi, "over", and the root √kṛ, "to do, make" (MW). The verb means "to place at the head, appoint", so the noun means "authority", and then a right that someone holds.

Lesson 5 of 5.
प्रश्नपत्रम् (praśnapatram) — a question paper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an obligation, then the Sanskrit for authority.

You'll want to know: प्रश्नपत्रम्.
प्रश्नपत्रम् — praśnapatram — "a question paper".

The word, taken apart.
praśnapatra is built from praśna, "question", from the root √prach, "to ask" (MW), and patra, "leaf, sheet of paper". MW does not list the compound, so it is a modern pairing. √prach is the root of pṛcchati, "asks", which you have already met.
