French, chapter 420: Escale et retard.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la turbulence — the turbulence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to land, then the French for to fly over.

You'll want to know: la turbulence.
la turbulence — "the turbulence".
Pendant les turbulences, tout le monde reste assis. During the turbulence everyone stays seated.
Often plural: des turbulences.

The word, taken apart.
turbulence is a learned borrowing from Late Latin turbulentia, from turba 'disorder, crowd' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'escale — the stopover, the port of call.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to fly over, then the French for the turbulence.

You'll want to know: l'escale.
l'escale — "the stopover, the port of call".
Nous avons fait une escale de trois heures. We had a three-hour stopover.
A feminine noun. Un vol sans escale — a non-stop flight.

The word, taken apart.
escale was borrowed from Italian scala 'landing stage', from Latin scāla 'ladder, steps' (EtymDB): the steps where a ship let people off.

Lesson 3 of 5.
décaler — to shift, to move (a time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the turbulence, then the French for the stopover.

You'll want to know: décaler.
décaler — "to shift, to move (a time)".
La compagnie a décalé le départ d'une heure. The airline moved the departure by an hour.
Décaler un rendez-vous — to move an appointment.

The word, taken apart.
décaler is built from dé- and caler 'to wedge': to take out the wedge and move something along. The origin of cale 'wedge' is usually traced to German Keil.

le décalage — "the gap, the shift; jet lag".
Avec le décalage horaire, il dort mal pendant trois jours. With jet lag he sleeps badly for three days.
Le décalage horaire — jet lag, the time difference.
