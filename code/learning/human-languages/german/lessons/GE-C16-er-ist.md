# er ist — "he is"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-BIST-01, GE-LEX-ES-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Bin* and *bist* both start with *b*. The third form does not, and
it is the one you will hear most.

## You'll want to know: er ist
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-IST-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **er ist** — "he is."

**er** is "he." The same **ist** serves three more subjects without changing at
all: **sie ist** ("she is"), **es ist** ("it is"), and **das ist** ("that is") —
*es* and *das* both already yours.

So one two-letter word covers everything that is not *ich* and not *du*, in the
singular. It is the workhorse of the whole verb.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-IST-01] -->

- `r-final` — the *r* at the end of *er* is not a full English *r*. It softens
  towards a vowel, close to *air* said without the tongue curling.
- `final-t` — *ist* ends in the same released **t** as *bist*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-IST-01, GE-LEX-SEIN-01] -->

*Ist* is the one form of *sein* whose relatives you can name on sight:

| language | "is" |
|---|---|
| German | **ist** |
| English | **is** |
| Latin | *est* |
| French | *est* |

All from Proto-Indo-European **\**h₁es-***, "to be" — and the infinitive
**sein** is built on that root too. So *sein* and *ist* really do belong to each
other; it is *bin* and *bist* that came from somewhere else.
