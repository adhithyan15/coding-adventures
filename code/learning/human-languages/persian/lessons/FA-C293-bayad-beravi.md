# باید بروی (bâyad beravi) — you should go

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-SUBJUNCTIVE-BE-01, FA-GRAMMAR-MIKHAHAM-SUBJ-01, FA-LEX-C198-CHERA198-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I want to go". (*Mikhâham beravam.*) Then the word for
"must, should". (*Bâyad.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Advice is a want that belongs to someone else. It takes the same
verb shape.

## Grammar Lens: bâyad, and the verb that carries the person
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-BAYAD-SUBJ-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C36-VERBS36-01, FA-LEX-C133-ACT133-04, FA-LEX-C66-TIME66-04] -->

**باید** does not change for the person. The verb after it does, in the
subjunctive:

> **باید بروی.** — *bâyad beravi* — You should go. You have to go.

> **باید بخوابم.** — *bâyad bekhâbam* — I must sleep.

One word covers "must", "have to" and "should"; the situation and your tone
decide how strong it is. With *na-* it forbids or warns:

> **نباید دیر برسی.** — *nabâyad dir beresi* — You mustn't arrive late.

## Grammar Lens: behtar ast, the softer one
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-BEHTAR-AST-SUBJ-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C164-SEFAT164-01, FA-LEX-C66-TIME66-03, FA-LEX-BE-NAZAR-E-MAN-01] -->

*Behtar*, "better", from chapter one hundred and sixty-four, with *ast*, "it
is", and the subjunctive after it:

> **بهتر است زود بخوابی.** — *behtar ast zud bekhâbi* — You'd better sleep
> early. It would be better to sleep early.

*Behtar ast* advises without pressing. With *be nazar-e man* in front it is
gentler still.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*bâyad* is usually traced to Middle Persian *abāyēd*, "it is needed". Its
last sound is usually read as the *-ad* of "he, she, it": a verb frozen in its
"it" form, "it is necessary". That fits what it does here. It takes no ending
of its own, so the person travels to the next verb.
