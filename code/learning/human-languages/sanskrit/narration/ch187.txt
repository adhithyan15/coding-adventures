Lesson 1 of 5.
दयालुः (dayāluḥ) — kind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for fine, then the Sanskrit for steady.

You'll want to know: दयालुः.
दयालुः — dayāluḥ — "kind".

The word, taken apart.
dayālu is dayā, "compassion", with the suffix -ālu, "inclined to" (MW). Dayā is built on the root √day, which MW glosses "to divide, impart" and also "to have pity on" (CDIAL 6185).

Lesson 2 of 5.
उदारः (udāraḥ) — generous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for steady, then the Sanskrit for kind.

You'll want to know: उदारः.
उदारः — udāraḥ — "generous".

The word, taken apart.
udāra is built on ud, "up", and the root √ṛ, "to rise, move" (MW): "raised, lofty". In the Rigveda it means "exciting", and later "lofty, noble" (CDIAL 1935), and from noble comes generous.

Lesson 3 of 5.
कृपणः (kṛpaṇaḥ) — stingy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for kind, then the Sanskrit for generous.

You'll want to know: कृपणः.
कृपणः — kṛpaṇaḥ — "stingy".

The word, taken apart.
kṛpaṇa is built on the root √kṛp, "to mourn, lament, implore" (MW). It first meant "pitiable, wretched", and later "miserly, stingy": the miser as a poor soul.

Lesson 4 of 5.
सत्यवादी (satyavādī) — truthful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for generous, then the Sanskrit for stingy.

You'll want to know: सत्यवादी.
सत्यवादी — satyavādī — "truthful".

The word, taken apart.
satyavādī is a compound of satya, "true", and vādin, "one who says", from the root √vad, "to speak" (MW). Satya lives on as Hindi saccā, "true" (CDIAL 13112).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: पटुः.
पटुः — paṭuḥ — "adept, skilful".

The word, taken apart.
paṭu first meant "sharp, pungent, keen", then "clever, skilful" (MW). Its origin is uncertain: MW suggests the root √paṭ, with a question mark.
