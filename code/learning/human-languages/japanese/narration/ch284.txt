Japanese, chapter 284: Election Day.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
せんきょ (senkyo) — an election.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for information, then the Japanese for a fact.

You'll want to know: せんきょ.
せんきょ — senkyo — "an election".
らいげつ せんきょが あります。 — raigetsu senkyo ga arimasu — "There is an election next month."

The word, taken apart.
せんきょ (senkyo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sen, "elect" (Middle Chinese sjwenX) and kyo, "raise" (kjoX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
あらそう (arasou) — to compete; to contend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the people (of a country), then the Japanese for the prime minister.

You'll want to know: あらそう.
あらそう — arasou — "to compete; to contend".
せんきょで ふたりが あらそっています。 — senkyo de futari ga arasotte imasu — "Two candidates are fighting the election."
Polite あらそいます, arasoimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ふたりが あらそいました。, futari ga arasoimashita.

The word, taken apart.
あらそう (arasou) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
