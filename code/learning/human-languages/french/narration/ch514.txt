French, chapter 514: Au théâtre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la troupe — (theatre) company, troupe; troop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for rehearsal, then the French for to improvise.

You'll want to know: la troupe.
la troupe — "(theatre) company, troupe; troop".
La troupe joue la même pièce depuis deux ans. The company has been playing the same play for two years.
Also a group of soldiers or animals.

The word, taken apart.
la troupe is usually traced to Frankish thorp "a gathering, a village" (EtymDB), a Germanic word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
les coulisses — wings, backstage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to improvise, then the French for (theatre) company.

You'll want to know: les coulisses.
les coulisses — "wings, backstage".
Avant le spectacle, les acteurs attendent dans les coulisses. Before the show the actors wait in the wings.
Mostly plural. Dans les coulisses — behind the scenes.

The word, taken apart.
les coulisses comes from coulisse "a groove that something slides in", built on couler "to flow, to slide", from Latin cōlāre "to strain" (Lewis & Short): the sets slid in grooves at the side of the stage.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le décor — set, scenery; decor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for (theatre) company, then the French for wings.

You'll want to know: le décor.
le décor — "set, scenery; decor".
Le décor représente une petite cuisine. The set shows a small kitchen.
In a theatre, often plural: les décors.

The word, taken apart.
le décor is built on décorer, a learned borrowing from Latin decorāre "to adorn", from decus "ornament, honour" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le costume — costume; suit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for wings, then the French for set.

You'll want to know: le costume.
le costume — "costume; suit".
Les costumes de la pièce sont magnifiques. The costumes in the play are magnificent.
Also a man's suit: un costume gris.

The word, taken apart.
le costume was borrowed from Italian costume "custom, way of dressing" (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin cōnsuētūdō "habit", from cōnsuēscere "to get used to" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'entracte — interval, intermission.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for set, then the French for costume.

You'll want to know: l'entracte.
l'entracte — "interval, intermission".
Pendant l'entracte, nous avons bu un café. During the interval we had a coffee.
Masculine. Literally between the acts.

The word, taken apart.
l'entracte is built from entre "between", from Latin inter, and acte "act", a learned borrowing from Latin āctus, from agere "to do, to drive" (Lewis & Short).
