Urdu, chapter 474: Illnesses That Spread.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جراثیم (jarāsīm) — germs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to limp, then the Urdu for an inflammation.

You'll want to know: جراثیم.
جراثیم — jarāsīm — "germs".
گندے ہاتھوں پر جراثیم ہوتے ہیں۔ gande hāthoṅ par jarāsīm hote haiṅ. There are germs on dirty hands.
Masculine, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
جراثیم (jarāsīm) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic jarāthīm, "germs, seeds of a thing".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for contagious, then the Urdu for an infection.

You'll want to know: خسرہ.
خسرہ — khasrā — "measles".
میرے بھائی کو خسرہ ہو گیا۔ mere bhāī ko khasrā ho gayā. My brother caught measles.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
خسرہ (khasrā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin khasrā, "scab, measles", under Sanskrit khasa, "itch, scab" (CDIAL 3854): the illness is named for its rash.
