# la sílaba — what you may break, and what you may not

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *chico* slowly, in pieces. Say where you would break it if the
line ran out — and note that the answer cannot be between the **c** and the
**h**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C425-05]; assesses=[] -->

**la sílaba** — "the syllable." Greek *syllabḗ*, "a taking together" — letters
taken together into one beat.

When a word will not fit at the end of a line, Spanish breaks it **between
syllables** and marks the break with *el guion*. What matters for a written
paper is which groups may **never** be split:

| never split | because |
|---|---|
| ch, ll, rr, gu, qu | each is one sound |
| a consonant + l or r | *bl, br, cl, cr, pl, pr, tr…* hold together |
| a diphthong | two vowels in one beat stay in it |

So *chi-co*, never *c-hico*. *Ha-blar*, never *hab-lar*. *Pe-rro*, never
*per-ro*.

## Grammar Lens: words Spanish spells two ways
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-05, ES-ORTH-C425-03] -->

A small set of words has two accepted spellings, and an exam will not mark
either wrong:

| both accepted | |
|---|---|
| sicología | psicología |
| setiembre | septiembre |
| quiosco | kiosco |

The pattern is a **simplified** form beside the older one that keeps a
consonant cluster the language stopped pronouncing. Spanish spelling follows the
mouth more closely than English does, so when a cluster goes quiet the spelling
is eventually allowed to follow.

One last rule a form actually tests: **a number written in figures is never
split across a line.** *2026* stays whole.
