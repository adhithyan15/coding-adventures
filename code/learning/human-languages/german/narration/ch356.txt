German, chapter 356: Karriere machen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Karriere — the career.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the cooperation, then the German for to organize.

You'll want to know: die Karriere.
die Karriere — "the career".
Sie war erst Kellnerin, dann machte sie Karriere. First she was a waitress, then she got on in her career.
Plural: die Karrieren. Karriere machen — to get on in your career.

The word, taken apart.
Karriere was borrowed from French carrière "a road, a racecourse", which came through Old Occitan carreira from Late Latin carrāria "a road for carts" (EtymDB), built on carrus "a two-wheeled wagon" (Lewis & Short): the track your working life runs along.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Aufstieg — the rise, the promotion; the climb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to organize, then the German for the career.

You'll want to know: der Aufstieg.
der Aufstieg — "the rise, the promotion; the climb".
Nach fünf Jahren kam der Aufstieg zur Chefin. After five years came her promotion to boss.
Plural: die Aufstiege. Also a climb up a mountain, and in sport the move up to a higher league.

The word, taken apart.
Aufstieg goes with aufsteigen "to climb up", built on steigen "to climb", inherited from Middle High German stîgen, Old High German stīgan and Proto-Germanic stīganą (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Herausforderung — the challenge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the career, then the German for the rise.

You'll want to know: die Herausforderung.
die Herausforderung — "the challenge".
Die neue Stelle ist eine große Herausforderung für mich. The new job is a big challenge for me.
Plural: die Herausforderungen. From herausfordern — to challenge.

The word, taken apart.
Herausforderung is the noun of herausfordern "to challenge", usually said to have first meant "to call out" to a fight, from heraus "out" and fordern "to demand". EtymDB has no history for fordern; it is usually traced to the family of vorder "front".

Lesson 4 of 5.
bewältigen — to cope with, to manage (a task).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the rise, then the German for the challenge.

You'll want to know: bewältigen.
bewältigen — "to cope with, to manage (a task)".
Allein kann ich die viele Arbeit nicht bewältigen. I cannot cope with all this work on my own.
Inseparable: sie bewältigt, sie hat bewältigt.

The word, taken apart.
bewältigen is built on the family of Gewalt "power" and walten "to rule", inherited from Old High German waltan and Proto-Germanic waldaną "to rule" (EtymDB): you get power over a task.

Lesson 5 of 5.
scheitern — to fail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the challenge, then the German for to cope with.

You'll want to know: scheitern.
scheitern — "to fail".
Der Plan ist am Geld gescheitert. The plan failed for lack of money.
Perfekt with sein: er ist gescheitert. An etwas scheitern — to fail because of something.

The word, taken apart.
scheitern is linked in EtymDB to Middle High German schīt "a split log", today Scheit. It is usually explained as first said of a ship that broke up into pieces of wood.
