German, chapter 465: Sturm und Hochwasser.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
der Sturm — the storm, the gale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for muggy, then the German for to dry out.

You'll want to know: der Sturm.
der Sturm — "the storm, the gale".
Der Sturm hat einen Baum auf unser Auto geworfen. The storm blew a tree down onto our car.
Plural: die Stürme. Stürmisch — stormy.

The word, taken apart.
Sturm is inherited from Old High German sturm 'storm, battle' and Proto-Germanic sturmaz (EtymDB); English storm is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 7.
die Böe — the gust (of wind).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to dry out, then the German for the storm.

You'll want to know: die Böe.
die Böe — "the gust (of wind)".
Eine starke Böe nimmt ihr den Regenschirm weg. A strong gust takes her umbrella away.
Plural: die Böen. Also spelled die Bö.

The word, taken apart.
Böe came from Low German and Dutch bui 'a sudden gust, a squall', a sailors' word; EtymDB does not list it, and the older origin is not settled.

You'll want to know: der Orkan.
der Orkan — "the hurricane".
Der Orkan hat in der Nacht viele Dächer beschädigt. The hurricane damaged many roofs in the night.
Plural: die Orkane. Stronger than a Sturm.

The word, taken apart.
Orkan was borrowed from Dutch orkaan, which came from Spanish huracán, a word taken from a Caribbean language (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 7.
überschwemmen — to flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the gust, then the German for the hurricane.

You'll want to know: überschwemmen.
überschwemmen — "to flood".
Der Fluss überschwemmt die Wiesen am Ufer. The river floods the meadows on its banks.
Inseparable: er überschwemmt, er hat überschwemmt.

The word, taken apart.
überschwemmen is built from über 'over' and schwemmen 'to float, to wash along', a causative of schwimmen 'to swim' (EtymDB lists the verb).

Lesson 5 of 7.
die Pfütze — the puddle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the hurricane, then the German for to flood.

You'll want to know: die Pfütze.
die Pfütze — "the puddle".
Das Kind springt mit den Stiefeln in jede Pfütze. The child jumps into every puddle in his boots.
Plural: die Pfützen.

The word, taken apart.
Pfütze is an early borrowing, Old High German pfuzzi, from Latin puteus 'well, pit' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); English pit goes back to the same Latin word.
