# писать — "to write"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A new letter, an English family you know on sight, and a stress
mistake worth naming before you make it.

## You'll want to know first — писать
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-PISAT]; assesses=[RU-LEX-YA, RU-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-FIRST-PERSON-U, RU-GRAMMAR-NO-PROGRESSIVE, RU-LEX-CHITAT] -->

> **писать** — *pisát'* — **to write**

Stress on the **last** syllable, **pi-SAT'** — not a detail you may skip.

> *ya pishú* — **I write**, and **I am writing**.

Two things moved: the **с** becomes *sh* in the present, and the stress that sat
on the ending for *I* jumps back to the stem elsewhere — *pishú*, but
*ty píshesh*. The pair runs as it did for *chitát'*: **писать** ongoing,
**написать** finished.

> **Careful.** *Писать* stressed at the end — *pisát'* — is **to write**.
> *Писать* stressed at the front — *písat'* — is a child's word for **to
> urinate**, whose *I* form is *písayu* beside *pishú*. The two differ only in
> where your voice lifts, and beginners land on the wrong one. Stress the
> **end** and say *pishú*.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-SCRIPT-SHA]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-YA-NOT-R] -->

*(Skim if you read Cyrillic.)* All of **писать** is familiar; the new sound
arrives in *pishú*.

- **"sh"**, the sound of *shoe*, has a letter of its own: three uprights on a
  bar, like a comb with the teeth up. You will write it in the letter chapters.

You have been hearing it without being told: *zhivyósh*, *znáyesh*, *gavarísh*
and the past *shol* all carry it. And note the kind of letter it is: **я** lies
to a Latin reader, looking like a mirrored *R* while being a vowel; the comb
resembles nothing Latin at all.

## The word, taken apart — writing was scratching
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-PISAT-PAINT]; assesses=[RU-ETYMON-CHITAT-COUNT-HONOUR, RU-ETYMON-DUMAT-DUMA, RU-ETYMON-PONIMAT-TAKE-HOLD, RU-LEX-PONIMAT, RU-LEX-DUMAT] -->

**писать** is Indo-European \**peyḱ-*, "**to cut, to scratch, to mark**" —
before ink there was a point and a surface. The root became, in Latin,
*pingere*, "to paint", and English took the results: **paint**, **picture**,
**pigment**. Russian's *pis'mó* (a letter) and *pisátel'* (a
writer) sit on the verb.

That closes the chapter's thread: *dúmat'* came in from Gothic, *ponimát'*
is a root meaning *take*, *chitát'* a root meaning *count* with no English
descendant, and **писать** a root meaning *scratch* that handed English a wall
of paint.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Say "I write." (*Ya pishú*.) Where does the stress go, and what if it
goes on the front? (On the **end**; *písat'* is a child's word for urinating.)
What sound does the comb-shaped letter make? (**Sh** — *zhivyósh*, *znáyesh*, *gavarísh*, *shol*.) Say the
chapter's four verbs in the *I* form, then their finished partners. (*Ya dúmayu,
ya panimáyu, ya chitáyu, ya pishú*; *podúmat'*, **понять**, *prochitát'*, **написать**.) And
what did *писать*'s root mean? (**To scratch** — hence *paint*.)
