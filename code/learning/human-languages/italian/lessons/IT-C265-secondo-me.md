# secondo me — following me, which is to say, in my opinion

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PENSO-CHE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the second". (*Il secondo.*) Then "I think that". (*Penso
che.*)

[PAUSE 1s] A story says what happened. Giving reasons starts one step earlier,
with what you think about it.

## You'll want to know: secondo me
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PER-ME-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PER-ME-03] -->

**secondo me** — "in my opinion", "if you ask me".

> **Secondo me** il treno è in ritardo. — If you ask me, the train is late.
> **Secondo me** è vero. — In my opinion it's true.

The *me* is the stressed pronoun of *per me*, and the others follow it:
**secondo te?**, "what do you think?", is the easiest way to hand the opinion
back to the other person.

And after *secondo me* the verb stays plain. Chapter thirty-one warned that
careful Italian bends the verb after *penso che* into the *congiuntivo*.
*Secondo me* asks for nothing of the kind: what follows is an ordinary
sentence. That makes it the safest opinion-opener you own.

## Grammar Lens: credo di sì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-CREDO-DI-SI-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C133-VERBI133-04, IT-LEX-SI-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PENSO-CHE-02] -->

You have *credere*, "to believe". Its "I" form is **credo**, and two short
answers built on it are worth more than the verb:

> **Credo di sì.** — I think so.
> **Credo di no.** — I don't think so.

The *di* has no English twin; Italian will not drop it. *Credo che …* also
exists and behaves exactly like *penso che*, careful *congiuntivo* and all.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**secondo** is inherited from Latin *secundum*, "following, according to"
(EtymDB 2.1), from the verb *sequī*, "to follow" (Lewis & Short, under
*secundus*). The ordinal **secondo** is the same word: the second is the one
that follows the first. *Secondo me* is "following me", the view as it looks
from where I stand. English **second**, **sequence** and **consequence** share
it.

**credo** comes from Latin *crēdere*, "to trust, to believe" (EtymDB 2.1).
