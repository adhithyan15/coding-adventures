# तुम्हारा — your, at the middle level of three

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *hamārā*. You have had *āpkā* for *your* since early on. This
lesson gives you the other one, and says when each is right.

## You'll want to know: तुम्हारा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03]; assesses=[] -->

**तुम्हारा** (*tumhārā*) — "your," said to someone you are familiar with.

Built on **तुम** (*tum*), the middle of Hindi's three *you*s.

## Grammar Lens: three levels, and the possessive that goes with each
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01] -->

| pronoun | possessive | who |
|---|---|---|
| आप *āp* | *āpkā* | a stranger, an elder, anyone respected |
| तुम *tum* | **tumhārā** | a friend, a sibling, a younger colleague |
| तू *tū* | *terā* | intimate, or rude — rarely the right choice for a learner |

The safe default for a learner is **आप**, and the reason is asymmetric: using
*āp* with a close friend sounds a little formal, while using *tum* with a
stranger or an elder sounds **rude**. One error is a small stiffness; the other
is an offence.

But *tumhārā* is not optional to know. A listening paper is full of friends
talking to each other, and it is the form they use.
