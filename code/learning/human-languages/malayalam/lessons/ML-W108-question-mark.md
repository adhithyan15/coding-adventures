# ? — the mark you have been reading all along

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PUNCT-FULL-STOP-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask somebody their name, out loud. Then picture how that sentence
was printed on the page you learned it from.

## Script you'll notice: ?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-PUNCT-QUESTION-01, ML-PUNCT-EXCLAM-01]; assesses=[] -->

**?** — the question mark, at the end, one space clear of the next sentence.

**You have been reading it since you first asked anybody their name.** That was
the headword `ML-C02-ninre-peru-entaanu`:

> **നിന്റെ പേര് എന്താണ്?**

It has been on the page ever since, never once named. Here it is.

**!** — the exclamation mark, in the same position, doing what it does in
English.

## Grammar Lens: one mark, at the end, and nothing at the front
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PUNCT-QUESTION-01, ML-PUNCT-EXCLAM-01] -->

**Spanish opens a question as well as closing it** — *¿cómo estás?* — with an
upside-down mark at the front. Malayalam has only the closing one:

| | |
|---|---|
| Spanish | ¿ … ? |
| Malayalam | … ? |

**The mark is not what makes it a question.** **എന്ത്**, **ആര്** and
**എപ്പോൾ** were doing that from your first questions onward, and
`ML-C03-sukhamaano` showed the other way: add **-ō** to the end and a statement
becomes a yes/no question.

## Writing: ? — where it goes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PUNCT-QUESTION-01] -->

Tight against the last letter, one space after. It closes the sentence by
itself; no full stop follows it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PUNCT-QUESTION-01, ML-PUNCT-EXCLAM-01, ML-PUNCT-FULL-STOP-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Where does a Malayalam question mark stand? (**At the end, and
nowhere else.**) Does a full stop follow it? (**No — it closes the sentence on
its own.**) What was making your questions questions before this lesson? (**The
question words, and the ending -ō.**)
