## You'll Want to Know — hear and say
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-YON]; assesses=[JA-SCRIPT-YO-01, JA-SCRIPT-N-01, JA-SCRIPT-SHI-01, JA-LEX-SAN] -->

> **よん** — *yon* — **four**

> **し** — *shi* — **four**, the same number said the other way

Both signs of **よん** are yours, and so is **し**. Two beats each: *yo* plus the
nasal beat, or *shi* alone.

## Grammar Lens — a native word inside a borrowed count
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-GRAMMAR-KUN-IN-THE-COUNT]; assesses=[JA-LEX-YON, JA-LEX-ICHI, JA-LEX-NI, JA-LEX-SAN, JA-LEX-GO] -->

Japanese has two ways of reading a sign: the reading it borrowed from Chinese
along with the sign, and the reading it already had for the thing the sign
means. Wiktionary's readings box for the kanji **four** lists them side by side:

| reading | kind | which |
|---|---|---|
| **し** *shi* | on'yomi, borrowed with the sign | four |
| **よん** *yon* | kun'yomi, the native word | four |

*Ichi*, *ni*, *san* and *go* are all borrowed readings, and none of them has a
native word standing beside it in the everyday count. **Four is the first place
in the run where the native word is used too** — and in ordinary speech
**よん** is the one you will hear far more often.

That split is coming back. It is the same seam the counters run along, and it
opens here.
