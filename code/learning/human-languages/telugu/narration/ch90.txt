Telugu, chapter 90: On the Way Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
జాగ్రత్త (jāgratta) — care, caution.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a wedding, then the Telugu for an address.

You'll want to know: జాగ్రత్త.
జాగ్రత్త (jāgratta) — "care, caution".
జాగ్రత్తగా వెళ్ళండి — "go carefully", what a host says at the door, where English says "take care". The -గా is the ending that makes a manner.

What you've built.
The kindest thing to say to someone leaving.

త్వరగా (tvaragā) రండి — "come back soon". The respectful raṇḍi you already have.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ఇంటికి (iṇṭiki) — home, to the house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for care, then the Telugu for soon.

You'll want to know: ఇంటికి.
ఇంటికి (iṇṭiki) — "home", as in "going home".
It is ఇల్లు, "a house", with the -కి "to" ending on it, and the house word changes shape to take it. నేను ఇంటికి (iṇṭiki) వెళ్తాను — "I am going home".

What you've built.
A reason to leave that needs no apology.

Lesson 4 of 5.
సెలవు (selavu) — leave, a holiday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for soon, then the Telugu for home.

You'll want to know: సెలవు.
సెలవు (selavu) — "leave", both a holiday and permission to go.
ఇవాళ సెలవు (selavu) — "today is a holiday". At a formal parting, సెలవు (selavu) alone asks leave to go, the way English once said "by your leave".

What you've built.
A holiday, and the most formal way out of a room.

Lesson 5 of 5.
పదండి (padaṇḍi) — let's go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for home, then the Telugu for leave.

You'll want to know: పదండి.
పదండి (padaṇḍi) — "let's go", said to the group you are leaving with.
It carries the respectful -అండి like raṇḍi and āgaṇḍi, so it stays polite even when it hurries people along.

What you've built.
Take care, come soon, I am going home, farewell, let's go: a whole exit.
