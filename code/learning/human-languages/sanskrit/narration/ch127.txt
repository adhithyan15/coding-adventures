Lesson 1 of 5.
दरिद्रः (daridraḥ) — poor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for wet, then the Sanskrit for dry.

You'll want to know: दरिद्रः.
दरिद्रः — daridraḥ — "poor".

The word, taken apart.
daridra comes from the intensive of the root √drā "run" (MW; CDIAL 6195): in the Vedas it means "roving, strolling", and later "poor, needy". Punjabi kept it as dariddar "poverty" (CDIAL 6195).

Lesson 2 of 5.
धनिकः (dhanikaḥ) — rich.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dry, then the Sanskrit for poor.

You'll want to know: धनिकः.
धनिकः — dhanikaḥ — "rich".

The word, taken apart.
dhanika is built from dhana "wealth" with the suffix -ika "having": "a rich man" (MW). In the Rigveda dhana means the prize of a contest, and then property and wealth (CDIAL 6717); Hindi dhan "wealth" is the same word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
तरुणः (taruṇaḥ) — young.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for poor, then the Sanskrit for rich.

You'll want to know: तरुणः.
तरुणः — taruṇaḥ — "young".

The word, taken apart.
taruṇa "young, tender" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-European: Greek terēn "tender" is a cousin (EtymDB). It lives on as Hindi tarun "a youth" (CDIAL 5712).

Lesson 4 of 5.
प्रसिद्धः (prasiddhaḥ) — famous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for rich, then the Sanskrit for young.

You'll want to know: प्रसिद्धः.
प्रसिद्धः — prasiddhaḥ — "famous".

The word, taken apart.
prasiddha is the past participle of pra-√sidh "be accomplished, be made clear", the root √sidh "succeed" with pra "forth" (MW; CDIAL 8842): "brought about", then "well known, famous".
