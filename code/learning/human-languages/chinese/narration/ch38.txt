Mandarin Chinese, chapter 38: Eating.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
吃 (chī) — to eat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for which one, then the Mandarin for that one.

You'll want to know: 吃.
吃 — chī — "to eat".
你吃什么 nǐ chī shénme — What are you having?
The new character, in pieces: 吃 (chī) — 口, the mouth piece, on the left, and on the right three strokes: a short fall, a short bar, and one long stroke that runs across, drops and hooks up. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
吃 (chī) is probably inherited from Middle Chinese khek, which the Guangyun glosses 'to eat' and writes with a different character; there 吃 itself is kj+t, 'to stammer' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the word and this character came together is not settled.

Script — a mouth, then three strokes.
吃 (chī).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 口, three strokes, small, as in 叫.
The right piece, three strokes. A short left-falling stroke. A short horizontal under it. Then one long stroke: across, down, bending right along the bottom and hooking up.
3 + 3 = six.

Script — the food piece, then four strokes.
饭 (fàn).
Two pieces, left before right.
The food piece, three strokes. A left-falling stroke. A short horizontal that hooks down at its end. A vertical that flicks up to the right at the foot.
The right piece, four strokes. A short left-falling stroke along the top. A long left-falling stroke down the left. A horizontal that turns and falls to the left. Then a right-falling stroke across it.
3 + 4 = seven.

You'll want to know: mǐfàn.
mǐfàn — "cooked rice".
wǒ xǐhuan chī mǐfàn — I like rice.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mǐfàn is built from mǐ 'husked grain' and 饭 (fàn) 'cooked grain', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mejX and bjonH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
