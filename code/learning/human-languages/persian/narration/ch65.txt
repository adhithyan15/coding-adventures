Persian, chapter 65: Minute, Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow, Now.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دقیقه (daghighe) — a minute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a week, then the Persian for a year.

You'll want to know: دقیقه.
دقیقه — daghighe — "a minute".

The word, taken apart.
daghighe is borrowed from Arabic daqīqa, "minute". Its Arabic root is d-q-q (Lane). The root means "fine, thin": a minute is a fine division of time.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: امروز.
امروز — emruz — "today".

The word, taken apart.
emruz is em-, "this", with ruz, "day": "this day". Both are native Persian; ruz continues Middle Persian rōz (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
دیروز (diruz) — yesterday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a minute, then the Persian for today.

You'll want to know: دیروز.
دیروز — diruz — "yesterday".

The word, taken apart.
diruz is di, the old Persian word for "yesterday" (usually traced to Middle Persian dīg), with ruz, "day". Both parts are native; ruz continues Middle Persian rōz (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
حالا (hâlâ) — now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for yesterday, then the Persian for tomorrow.

You'll want to know: حالا.
حالا — hâlâ — "now".

The word, taken apart.
hâlâ is borrowed from Arabic ḥālan, "now, at present". Its Arabic root is ḥ-w-l (Lane). It is the word ḥāl, "state", used as an adverb.
