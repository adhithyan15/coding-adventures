Portuguese, chapter 334: Até ficar dourado.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
escorrer — to drain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to grease, then the Portuguese for to sprinkle.

You'll want to know: escorrer.
escorrer — "to drain".
Ela escorreu a massa e juntou o molho. She drained the pasta and added the sauce.
Pôr a escorrer — to leave to drain.

The word, taken apart.
escorrer comes from Latin excurrere "to run out" (EtymDB), from ex "out" and currere "to run".

Lesson 2 of 7.
tapar — to cover (with a lid).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sprinkle, then the Portuguese for to drain.

You'll want to know: tapar.
tapar — "to cover (with a lid)".
O cozinheiro tapou o tacho e esperou. The cook put the lid on the pot and waited.
Also to cover anything: tapar os olhos.

The word, taken apart.
tapar is usually traced to a Germanic word for a stopper or plug, like English tap; the origin is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 3 of 7.
dourar — to brown, to turn golden; to gild.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to drain, then the Portuguese for to cover.

You'll want to know: dourar.
dourar — "to brown, to turn golden; to gild".
A cebola dourou em cinco minutos. The onion turned golden in five minutes.
Até dourar — until golden.

The word, taken apart.
dourar comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB), from Late Latin deaurāre "to gild", built on aurum "gold".

Lesson 4 of 7.
gratinar — to brown on top in the oven, to gratinate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to cover, then the Portuguese for to brown.

You'll want to know: gratinar.
gratinar — "to brown on top in the oven, to gratinate".
No fim, gratinamos o bacalhau no forno. At the end we brown the salt cod in the oven.
With cheese or breadcrumbs on top.

The word, taken apart.
gratinar was borrowed from French gratiner, from gratin "the crust", built on gratter "to scrape", a verb of Germanic origin (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 5 of 7.
rechear — to stuff, to fill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to brown, then the Portuguese for to brown on top in the oven.

You'll want to know: rechear.
rechear — "to stuff, to fill".
A avó recheou o frango com pão e chouriço. Grandmother stuffed the chicken with bread and chouriço.
O recheio — the stuffing, the filling.

The word, taken apart.
rechear is built from re- and cheio "full", which is inherited from Latin plēnus "full" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB has the word with no source.
