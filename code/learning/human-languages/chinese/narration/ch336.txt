Mandarin Chinese, chapter 336: Seeing Friends Off.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gàobié — to say goodbye; to take leave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a riddle, then the Mandarin for a string of firecrackers.

You'll want to know: gàobié.
gàobié — "to say goodbye; to take leave".
tā hé dàjiā gàobié yǐhòu jiù shàng chē le — After saying goodbye to everyone he got on board.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gàobié is built from gào 'to tell' and bié 'to part', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kawH and bjet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to report' and the second 'to differ' and 'to part'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
欢送 (huānsòng) — to give a send-off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a string of firecrackers, then the Mandarin for to take leave of someone.

You'll want to know: 欢送.
欢送 — huānsòng — "to give a send-off".
我们开了一个晚会欢送他 wǒmen kāi le yí ge wǎnhuì huānsòng tā — We held a party to see him off.
The new character, in pieces: 送 (sòng) — nine strokes: 关 with its last stroke shrunk to a dot, inside the walking piece of 这, which wraps it from the left and runs along the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
欢送 (huānsòng) is built from 欢 'glad' and sòng 'to send', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwan and suwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'joy' and the second 'to send away'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
送 (sòng) — 关 in the walking piece.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — 关 in the walking piece.
送 (sòng).
Two pieces: the inside first, then the piece that wraps it.
The inside piece, 关, six strokes, as you wrote it: two short strokes on top, two bars, a long stroke falling to the left, and last the stroke falling to the right, which here shrinks to a dot.
The walking piece, three strokes, as in 这: a dot, a small stroke that zigzags down, then a long sweep along the foot to the right.
6 + 3 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 送 (sòng), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 送 (sòng)? (Nine.) What sits inside? (关.) What is written last? (The long sweep along the foot.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 送 (sòng), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 4 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take leave of someone, then the Mandarin for to give a send-off.

You'll want to know: 送客.
送客 — sòngkè — "to see a guest out".
客人走的时候他去门口送客 kèrén zǒu de shíhou tā qù ménkǒu sòngkè — When the guests left, he saw them to the door.

The word, taken apart.
送客 (sòngkè) is built from 送 (sòng) 'to send' and 客 'a guest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, suwngH and khaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a guest'.
