Telugu, chapter 68: Sound and Voice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
శబ్దం (śabdaṁ) — a sound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kiṭikī mean?

You'll want to know: శబ్దం.
శబ్దం (śabdaṁ) — "a sound".
శబ్దం (śabdaṁ) is a sound — any sound at all, from a తలుపు closing to thunder over a పొలం.
It is Sanskrit, borrowed early and borrowed whole. It covers the smallest noise and the largest with no change: a చుక్క of water landing, or the crack that comes before rain.
A room with no శబ్దం (śabdaṁ) in it is not called quiet in Telugu so much as empty, and people say exactly that about a house whose children have grown up and gone.

What you've built.
One. The most general thing an ear can take in.

Lesson 2 of 5.
గొంతు (gontu) — a voice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did śabdaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: గొంతు.
గొంతు (gontu) — "a voice".
గొంతు (gontu) is a voice, and it is also the throat that makes one — the front of the మెడ you already know. Telugu lets the two meanings sit in the single word.
Somebody loud has a big గొంతు (gontu) and somebody who sings well has a sweet one. A person who speaks up for people who cannot is said to have lent them their గొంతు (gontu).
Losing it is ordinary. After a night of దగ్గు a villager will say the గొంతు (gontu) has gone, and mean the throat and the sound at once.

What you've built.
Two: శబ్దం (śabdaṁ) and గొంతు (gontu). Sound in general, and the sound a person makes.

Lesson 3 of 5.
పాట (pāṭa) — a song.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did gontu mean?

You'll want to know: పాట.
పాట (pāṭa) — "a song".
పాట (pāṭa) is a song. Watch the middle letter. పాత, with a plain letter, is the word for old that you already have; పాట (pāṭa) is a song. One has the tongue behind the teeth and the other has it curled back.
The word is inherited and it is everywhere: work songs at the కల్లం, lullabies, film songs a whole state can hum. A Telugu speaker will name a పాట (pāṭa) faster than a book.
Anything repeated past patience becomes a పాట (pāṭa) as an insult, in the way English reaches for the same old song.

What you've built.
Three. And a పాత పాట (pāṭa) is an old song, which is a phrase you have now earned.

Lesson 4 of 5.
అరుపు (arupu) — a shout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did pāṭa mean?

You'll want to know: అరుపు.
అరుపు (arupu) — "a shout".
అరుపు (arupu) is a shout: a cry raised on purpose, loud enough to carry the width of a పొలం.
It is inherited, built on the everyday word for shouting, and it is not rude in itself. Calling somebody in from work is an అరుపు (arupu), and so is the cry that warns a గ్రామం.
It turns rude only when there is a person on the far end of it. Somebody who was shouted at will say they got an అరుపు (arupu), and everybody hears the difference without being told.

What you've built.
Four. Sound, voice, song, and now sound sent a distance.

Lesson 5 of 5.
కబురు (kaburu) — a message, news.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did arupu mean?

You'll want to know: కబురు.
కబురు (kaburu) — "a message, news".
కబురు (kaburu) is a message: word sent, or news arriving.
It came into Telugu from Arabic by way of Persian, and it was worn in so long ago that it stopped feeling borrowed. It is the word for what an అరుపు (arupu) is for — getting something to somebody who is not there.
Sending కబురు (kaburu) is a fixed pair of words in the countryside, and the greeting at the head of a message is part of the message. Names travel this way too: a కబురు (kaburu) usually opens by saying whose it is.

What you've built.
Five: శబ్దం (śabdaṁ), గొంతు (gontu), పాట (pāṭa), అరుపు (arupu), కబురు (kaburu). Sound, and the whole way it gets from one person to another.
