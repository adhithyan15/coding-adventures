# માફ કરો (māf karo) — excuse me

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-PHA-01, GU-CONCEPT-C01-AABHAAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One lesson back, the new letter. And from the very first chapter, the
word for **thank you**.

- [YOU RECALL: **ફ**, and *ābhār*]

You have been able to thank someone since page one of this book. You have never
been able to apologise to them.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-MAAF-01, GU-SCRIPT-MAAF-01]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-PHA-01] -->

> **માફ કરો** — *māf karo* — "do forgiveness", that is, **forgive me**

**માફ** is the forgiveness itself, and **કરો** is *do* — the polite form you
already use for asking. Gujarati does not have a verb "to apologise"; it asks
the other person to **do** the forgiving.

Read the first word slowly. **મ** and the **ા** stroke you have had for a long
time; **ફ** you learned in the last lesson. Three signs, and only one of them
was new.

**માફ** is a traveller. It came from Arabic *muāf* by way of Persian, on the
same road that carried **બજાર** into the market chapter and **કાગળ** onto the
page two chapters ago. The apology in this book arrived the way the paper did.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-MAAF-01] -->

*Māf karo* does two jobs, and the second is the one a traveller needs most. It
apologises — and it is also how you **get someone's attention** before you ask
them anything: said to a stranger on the street, it means *excuse me*, not *I
have wronged you*. One phrase opens the conversation and repairs it.
