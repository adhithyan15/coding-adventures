## Two second-pass lessons: the letters one mark apart, and the layers a word arrives from

`UR-R22-second-pass-one-mark-apart` and `UR-R23-second-pass-three-layers`. Seven
atoms — three script points, two register points, **آرام سے**, and the
first-meeting reading page — had been revisited once or, for two of them, not at
all.

The first gathers the three pairs the eye has to separate and says how little
ink separates them: **ق** against **ک** is how far back in the throat, **ت**
against **ٹ** is two dots against a raised loop, **گ** against **ک** is one
stroke across the top. It is honest about which pair merges in relaxed speech
and which never does — **ت** and **ٹ** make different words.

The second sets three words beside the roads they took: **کوٹ** straight from
English one generation ago, **ہوا** handed Arabic to Persian to Urdu, **گرمی**
Persian on a root it shares with Greek *thermós*. The spelling announces none of
it, and **کوٹ** is the sharpest case, sharing its shape with an unrelated older
word for *fort*. It closes on **آرام سے**, *with ease* — worth more in a first
conversation than any of the three.

No new words and no new atoms. `urdu: reinforcement 7 -> 0`.
