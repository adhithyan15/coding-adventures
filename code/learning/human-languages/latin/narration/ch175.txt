Latin, chapter 175: At last, From now on, Still, Suddenly, Until.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tandem — at last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a voice, then the Latin for mind.

You'll want to know: tandem.
tandem — "at last".

The word, taken apart.
tandem is tam, "so far", with -dem, a pointing ending (Lewis & Short): so far and no further, at last.

Lesson 4 of 5.
subitō — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for from now on, then the Latin for still.

You'll want to know: subitō.
subitō — "suddenly".

The word, taken apart.
subitō comes from the participle of subeō, "to come up from under" (Lewis & Short), subitus, "sudden": coming up unseen.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dōnec — until; while.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for still, then the Latin for suddenly.

You'll want to know: dōnec.
dōnec — "until; while".

The word, taken apart.
dōnec comes from older dōnicum (Lewis & Short), but how that was formed is uncertain; Lewis & Short suggest an old word for "day" with cum, "when".
