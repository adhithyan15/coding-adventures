Latin, chapter 502: Jokes and wit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iocus, iocī — a joke, jest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for calm, then the Latin for free from care.

You'll want to know: iocus, iocī.
iocus, iocī — "a joke, jest".
Pater iocum nārrat, et omnēs rīdent. Father tells a joke, and everyone laughs.
Masculine, second declension; the plural is often ioca. Per iocum — as a joke. English joke and jocular.

The word, taken apart.
iocus is inherited through Proto-Italic from a Proto-Indo-European word that EtymDB traces to a root meaning "to speak", with Welsh iaith "language" as a cousin; Lewis & Short is less sure. English joke and jocular come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
iocōsus, iocōsa, iocōsum — humorous, playful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a joke, then the Latin for to joke.

You'll want to know: iocōsus, iocōsa, iocōsum.
iocōsus, iocōsa, iocōsum — "humorous, playful".
Avus meus senex iocōsus est. My grandfather is a humorous old man.
Built on iocus. English jocose.

The word, taken apart.
iocōsus is built on iocus "a joke" with -ōsus "full of" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English jocose comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
rīdiculus, rīdicula, rīdiculum — funny, laughable; absurd.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to joke, then the Latin for humorous.

rīdiculus, rīdicula, rīdiculum — "funny, laughable; absurd".
Histriō in theātrō rīdiculus est. The actor is funny in the theatre.
From rīdeō — to laugh. Funny in a good sense, absurd in a bad one. English ridiculous.

The word, taken apart.
rīdiculus is built on rīdeō "to laugh" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English ridiculous and ridicule come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
facētus, facēta, facētum — witty, clever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for humorous, then the Latin for funny.

You'll want to know: facētus, facēta, facētum.
facētus, facēta, facētum — "witty, clever".
Hospes facētus multa rīdicula dīcit. The witty guest says many funny things.
Elegant, good-humoured wit. English facetious comes from facētiae — jokes.

The word, taken apart.
facētus is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short and EtymDB link it with fax "torch" and a root meaning "to shine", but the origin is not settled. English facetious comes from facētiae "jokes", built on it.
