Mandarin Chinese, chapter 430: Seeing the Sights.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guānguāng — to go sightseeing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get about, then the Mandarin for travel costs.

You'll want to know: guānguāng.
guānguāng — "to go sightseeing".
wǒmen zhù zài zhè jiā jiǔdiàn měitiān qù guānguāng — We're staying at this hotel and going sightseeing every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guānguāng is built from guān 'to view' and guāng 'light', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwan and kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to view' and the second 'bright'. How the pair came to mean touring the sights is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jǐngqū — scenic area.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go sightseeing, then the Mandarin for a historic site.

You'll want to know: jǐngqū.
jǐngqū — "scenic area".
zhège jǐngqū zǎoshang bā diǎn kāimén — This scenic area opens at eight in the morning.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jǐngqū is built from jǐng 'a view' and qū 'an area', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaengX and khju (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'bright' and 'great' and gives the second as the name of a marsh; the senses 'a view' and 'an area' are not given there.
