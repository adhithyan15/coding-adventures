Latin, chapter 337: Palma et praemium.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
palma, palmae — the palm of victory; the palm of the hand; a palm tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hunt, then the Latin for a beast.

You'll want to know: palma, palmae.
palma, palmae — "the palm of victory; the palm of the hand; a palm tree".
Aurīga palmam et corōnam accēpit. The charioteer received the palm and a crown.
Feminine. The flat of the hand, then the palm tree, whose branch was given to winners.

The word, taken apart.
palma is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European form for "the flat of the hand" (EtymDB), matching Greek palamē. The palm tree was named from its hand-like leaves. English palm comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
praemium, praemiī — a prize, a reward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a beast, then the Latin for the palm of victory.

You'll want to know: praemium, praemiī.
praemium, praemiī — "a prize, a reward".
Praemium magnum erat: centum dēnāriī. The prize was large: a hundred denarii.
Neuter. First booty taken before others, then any reward. English premium.

The word, taken apart.
praemium is built on prae, "before", and emō, "to take, to buy" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): what one takes before others. English premium comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
certō, certāre — to compete, to contend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the palm of victory, then the Latin for a prize.

You'll want to know: certō, certāre.
certō, certāre — "to compete, to contend".
Duo aurīgae dē palmā certābant. Two charioteers were competing for the palm.
certō, certāre, certāvī, certātum. Do not confuse the adverb certō, 'for certain'.

The word, taken apart.
certō is built on cernō, "to decide" (Lewis & Short): to decide something by a contest.

Lesson 5 of 5.
plausus, plausūs — applause, clapping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to compete, then the Latin for a contest.

You'll want to know: plausus, plausūs.
plausus, plausūs — "applause, clapping".
Magnus plausus in theātrō erat. There was loud applause in the theatre.
Masculine, 4th declension, from plaudō, 'to clap' (taught). Plays ended with the call plaudite!

The word, taken apart.
plausus is built on plaudō, "to clap" (Lewis & Short). English applause comes from its compound applaudō.
