Japanese, chapter 212: Cold Nights and Warm Afternoons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きおん (kion) — air temperature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a small bird, then the Japanese for to increase.

You'll want to know: きおん.
きおん — kion — "air temperature".
あしたは きおんが たかいでしょう。 — ashita wa kion ga takai deshō — "The temperature will probably be high tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
きおん (kion) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "air" (Middle Chinese khj+jH) and on, "warm" ('won) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
あがる (agaru) — to rise; to go up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to increase, then the Japanese for air temperature.

You'll want to know: あがる.
あがる — agaru — "to rise; to go up".
あした きおんが あがります。 — ashita kion ga agarimasu — "The temperature will rise tomorrow."
Polite あがります, agarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
あがる (agaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
