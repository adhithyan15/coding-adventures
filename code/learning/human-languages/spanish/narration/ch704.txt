Spanish, chapter 704: Alegrías y penas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la alegría — joy, happiness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to seem, then the Spanish for to keep.

You'll want to know: la alegría.
la alegría — "joy, happiness".
Tu visita es una alegría. Your visit is a joy.
¡Qué alegría! — How lovely! It is built on alegre, cheerful.

The word, taken apart.
alegría is built on alegre "cheerful", which is usually traced to Latin alacer "lively, brisk" through a spoken form alecrem; English alacrity comes from the same Latin word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la melancolía — melancholy, gloom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to keep, then the Spanish for joy.

You'll want to know: la melancolía.
la melancolía — "melancholy, gloom".
El otoño me da melancolía. Autumn makes me melancholy.
A soft, lasting sadness. The adjective is melancólico.

The word, taken apart.
melancolía is a learned borrowing from Late Latin melancholia, from Greek melankholía "blackness of the bile" (EtymDB), built on mélas "black" and kholḗ "bile"; old medicine blamed gloom on black bile.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la dicha — happiness, good fortune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for joy, then the Spanish for melancholy.

You'll want to know: la dicha.
la dicha — "happiness, good fortune".
¡Qué dicha verte otra vez! What joy to see you again!
More literary than la alegría. Nunca es tarde si la dicha es buena — better late than never.

The word, taken apart.
dicha is inherited from Latin dicta "things said" (EtymDB), from dīcere "to say"; the usual reading is that good fortune was what fate had said for you.

Lesson 4 of 5.
emocionarse — to be moved, to get emotional.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for melancholy, then the Spanish for happiness.

You'll want to know: emocionarse.
emocionarse — "to be moved, to get emotional".
Mi madre se emociona con las bodas. My mother gets emotional at weddings.
Reflexive: me emociono, te emocionas. It is about being touched, not about being angry.

The word, taken apart.
emocionarse is built on emoción, which came from French émotion (EtymDB); the French word goes back to Latin ēmovēre "to move out, to stir up", from movēre "to move".

Lesson 5 of 5.
llorar — to cry, to weep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for happiness, then the Spanish for to be moved.

You'll want to know: llorar.
llorar — "to cry, to weep".
El bebé llora por la noche. The baby cries at night.
Llorar de alegría — to cry with joy.

The word, taken apart.
llorar is inherited from Latin plōrāre "to wail, to weep aloud" (EtymDB). Latin pl- at the start of a word became ll- in Spanish, the same change that turned plēnus into lleno.
