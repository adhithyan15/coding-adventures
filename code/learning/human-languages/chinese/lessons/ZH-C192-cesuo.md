## You'll want to know: cèsuǒ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C192-SIGN192-01]; assesses=[] -->

*cèsuǒ* — "toilet; lavatory".

> *qǐngwèn cèsuǒ zài nǎr* — Excuse me, where's the toilet?

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*cèsuǒ* is built from *cè* 'a privy' and *suǒ* 'a place', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsrhiH* and *srjoX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The modern *cè* is not the regular outcome of that form, and how it came is not settled.
