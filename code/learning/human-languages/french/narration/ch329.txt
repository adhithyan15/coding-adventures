French, chapter 329: Battre les œufs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le saladier — the salad bowl, the mixing bowl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to slice thinly, then the French for to grate.

You'll want to know: le saladier.
le saladier — "the salad bowl, the mixing bowl".
Elle met la farine dans un grand saladier. She puts the flour in a big bowl.
Built on salade.

The word, taken apart.
saladier is built on salade, which EtymDB traces to Italian salata "something salted" (Provençal salada is also proposed), built on Latin sal "salt" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

The word, taken apart.
verser is inherited from Latin versāre "to turn often" (EtymDB), built on vertere "to turn".

Lesson 3 of 5.
battre — to beat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the salad bowl, then the French for to pour.

You'll want to know: battre.
battre — "to beat".
Elle bat les œufs avec une fourchette. She beats the eggs with a fork.
Battre les blancs en neige — to beat egg whites until stiff. Also to beat someone at a game.

The word, taken apart.
battre is inherited from Late Latin battere "to beat" (EtymDB), from Latin battuere (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le fouet — the whisk; the whip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to pour, then the French for to beat.

You'll want to know: le fouet.
le fouet — "the whisk; the whip".
Il mélange la crème avec un fouet. He mixes the cream with a whisk.
Un coup de fouet — a boost.

The word, taken apart.
fouet is usually explained as a diminutive of Old French fou "beech", from Latin fāgus "beech" (Lewis & Short): first a beech switch.
