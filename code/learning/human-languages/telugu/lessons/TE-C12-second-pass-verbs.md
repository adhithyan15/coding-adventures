# The first three verbs again — one engine, and its negative

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C05-MAATLAADU-01, TE-LEX-C05-UNDU-01, TE-LEX-C05-PANI-CEYU-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The first three plain verbs, in any order: *to speak*, *to stay*,
*to work*. Say them before reading on.

## You'll want to know: the three verbs, side by side
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C05-MAATLAADU-01, TE-LEX-C05-UNDU-01, TE-LEX-C05-PANI-CEYU-01, TE-LEX-C05-NENU-TELUGU-MAATLAADATAANU-01] -->

| plain verb | the "I" form |
|---|---|
| **మాట్లాడు** *māṭlāḍu* — speak | **మాట్లాడతాను** *māṭlāḍatānu* |
| **ఉండు** *uṇḍu* — be, stay, live | **ఉంటాను** *uṇṭānu* |
| **పని చేయు** *pani cēyu* — work | **పని చేస్తాను** *pani cēstānu* |

Read the right-hand column downwards and listen to the last three sounds of
each. **‑ాను** every time. Three different verbs, one ending, and it is the
ending that means *I*.

## Grammar Lens: stem, then time, then person
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-GRAMMAR-C05-MAATLAADU-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C05-NENU-TELUGU-MAATLAADATAANU-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C05-PANI-CEYU-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C05-UNDU-03] -->

Every Telugu verb you meet from here is built in that order: the **stem** says
what happens, the next piece says **when**, the last piece says **who**. It is
the same gluing-on you met on the end of **ధన్యవాదములు**, applied to actions
rather than nouns.

Because the *who* sits at the end, **నేను** is optional — and because the
*when* sits in the middle, changing the time never disturbs the person.

## What you've built — ఉండు and its negative
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C05-UNDU-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C05-UNDU-03, TE-LEX-C03-PARAVAALEDU-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C03-PARAVAALEDU-03] -->

**ఉండు** is the verb of *being there*, and Telugu keeps a dedicated word for
its denial: **లేదు**, *there is not*. Learn the two as a pair rather than as a
verb and a stray word.

That pairing is what makes **పరవాలేదు** readable rather than memorised:
*paravā* is the bother, **‑లేదు** says there is none. A word you took whole
when you learned to answer *how are you* turns out to be a sentence built on
this verb.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] What ending do all three "I" forms share? (**‑ాను**.) In what order
does a Telugu verb stack its pieces? (**Stem, then time, then person**.) Which
word denies **ఉండు**? (**లేదు**.) And what is **పరవాలేదు** made of? (*Paravā*, the
bother, plus **‑లేదు** — there is none.)
