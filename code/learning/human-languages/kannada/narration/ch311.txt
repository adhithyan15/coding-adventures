Kannada, chapter 311: What I Think, and So.
3 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ಅಂತ ನನಗೆ ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ (anta nanage anisuttade) — what I think.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to seem, to feel (that)" (anisu) and the word that closes a quotation (anta). Then "clean" (svaccha). Which letter sits small inside it? (ಛ, cha.)

You'll want to know: … ಅಂತ ನನಗೆ ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ.
ಈ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ ಅಂತ ನನಗೆ ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ. ī pustaka cennāgide anta nanage anisuttade. — "I think this book is good."
The thought comes first, whole: ಈ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (ī pustaka cennāgide, "this book is good"). ಅಂತ closes it like a pair of quotation marks. Then ನನಗೆ ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ: "it seems to me". Kannada does not say "I think" here. The thought comes to you, so "me" takes the ನನಗೆ of ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು.
To ask for someone's view:
ನಿಮಗೆ ಏನು ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ? nimage ēnu anisuttade? — "What do you think?"
For what you thought then, the "it" past: ಅನಿಸಿತು, "it seemed".
ಅದು ಕಷ್ಟ ಅಂತ ನನಗೆ ಅನಿಸಿತು. adu kaṣṭa anta nanage anisitu. — "I felt that it was difficult."

Grammar Lens: ನನ್ನ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯದಲ್ಲಿ, in my opinion.
ನನ್ನ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯದಲ್ಲಿ ಈ ಹೋಟೆಲ್ ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು. nanna abhiprāyadalli ī hōṭel oḷḷeyadu. — "In my opinion this hotel is good."
ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯದಲ್ಲಿ is ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯ, "an opinion", with -ದಲ್ಲಿ, "in". ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು (oḷḷeyadu, "a good one; (it is) good"): ಒಳ್ಳೆಯ goes in front of a noun, and at the end of a sentence it takes -ದು.
In talk you will also hear ನನ್ನ ಪ್ರಕಾರ (nanna prakāra, "according to me"). ಯೋಚಿಸು stays the verb for thinking something over; for a view given in passing, ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ is the everyday one.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does the thought go? (First, closed by ಅಂತ.) Who takes ನನಗೆ? (The one the thought comes to: me.) "What do you think?" (ನಿಮಗೆ ಏನು ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ?) "In my opinion"? (ನನ್ನ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯದಲ್ಲಿ.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (hāgāgi) — so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think this book is good". (ī pustaka cennāgide anta nanage anisuttade.) Then "that is why" and "therefore". (adakkē, ādudarinda.)

You'll want to know: ಹಾಗಾಗಿ.
ನಿನ್ನೆ ಮಳೆ ಬಂತು. ಹಾಗಾಗಿ ನಾನು ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲೇ ಇದ್ದೆ. ninne maḷe bantu. hāgāgi nānu maneyallē idde. — "Yesterday it rained. So I stayed at home."
The reason comes first, then ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (hāgāgi), then the result. Taken apart, it is ಹಾಗೆ, "that way", and ಆಗಿ, the -ಇ form of ಆಗು: "that having happened". ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲೇ (maneyallē, "at home, nowhere else") is ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, "at home", with -ಏ, which leans on the word: that very place.
ನನಗೆ ಜ್ವರ ಇತ್ತು. ಹಾಗಾಗಿ ನಾನು ಶಾಲೆಗೆ ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ. nanage jvara ittu. hāgāgi nānu śālege hōgalilla. — "I had a fever. So I did not go to school."

You'll want to know: three words for 'so'.
ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (hāgāgi) — the everyday "so", in talk and in writing.
ಅದಕ್ಕೇ — "that is why", with weight on the reason: "that is exactly why".
ಆದುದರಿಂದ — "therefore", in careful writing and in speeches.
ಬಸ್ ಬರಲಿಲ್ಲ. ಅದಕ್ಕೇ ನಾನು ತಡವಾಗಿ ಬಂದೆ. bas baralilla. adakkē nānu taḍavāgi bande. — "The bus didn't come. That's why I came late."
ತಡವಾಗಿ (taḍavāgi, "late"). ಆದುದರಿಂದ is built from ಆಗು as well: "from what happened". All three stand at the start of the result, never in front of the reason.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I had a fever. So I did not go to school." — nanage jvara ittu. hāgāgi nānu śālege hōgalilla.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The bus didn't come. That's why I came late." — bas baralilla. adakkē nānu taḍavāgi bande.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"So", in talk? (ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (hāgāgi).) "That is why"? (ಅದಕ್ಕೇ.) "Therefore", in writing? (ಆದುದರಿಂದ.) Which comes first, the reason or the result? (The reason.) What is ಹಾಗಾಗಿ (hāgāgi) taken apart? (ಹಾಗೆ + ಆಗಿ: "that having happened".)
