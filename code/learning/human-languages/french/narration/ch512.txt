French, chapter 512: Chanter juste.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la chorale — choir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for accordion, then the French for to tune.

You'll want to know: la chorale.
la chorale — "choir".
Je chante dans une chorale depuis trois ans. I have been singing in a choir for three years.
An amateur choir; le chœur is a choir too, also in a church.

The word, taken apart.
la chorale comes from choral, built on Medieval Latin chorālis, from Latin chorus "a dance, a choir", a borrowing from Greek khorós.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fredonner — to hum.

Before the new one: say the French for to tune, then the French for choir.

You'll want to know: fredonner.
fredonner — "to hum".
Elle fredonne une chanson en faisant la cuisine. She hums a song while she cooks.
Humming with the lips closed or almost closed.

The word, taken apart.
fredonner is built on old fredon "a trill" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin fritinnīre "to twitter" (Lewis & Short); the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
siffloter — to whistle softly (a tune).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for choir, then the French for to hum.

You'll want to know: siffloter.
siffloter — "to whistle softly (a tune)".
Mon grand-père sifflote en marchant. My grandfather whistles softly as he walks.
Built on siffler with -oter, which makes the action light and repeated.

The word, taken apart.
siffloter is built on siffler "to whistle" with -oter; siffler goes back to Latin sībilāre "to hiss, to whistle" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le refrain — chorus, refrain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to hum, then the French for to whistle softly.

You'll want to know: le refrain.
le refrain — "chorus, refrain".
Tout le monde chante le refrain avec lui. Everyone sings the chorus with him.
The verses are les couplets. C'est toujours le même refrain — it is always the same old story.

The word, taken apart.
le refrain comes from Old French refraindre "to break off, to repeat" (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin refrangere, built on frangere "to break" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la mélodie — melody, tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to whistle softly, then the French for chorus.

You'll want to know: la mélodie.
la mélodie — "melody, tune".
Cette mélodie me rappelle mon enfance. This tune reminds me of my childhood.
The adjective is mélodieux "tuneful".

The word, taken apart.
la mélodie comes from Late Latin melōdia, a borrowing from Greek melōidía "singing", built on mélos "song" and ōidḗ "ode".
