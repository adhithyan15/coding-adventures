Mandarin Chinese, chapter 458: How I Feel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
qíngxù — emotions; spirits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to feel deeply grateful, then the Mandarin for a way to solve a problem.

You'll want to know: qíngxù.
qíngxù — "emotions; spirits".
zuìjìn wǒ de qíngxù bú tài hǎo — My mood hasn't been great lately.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qíngxù is built from qíng 'feeling' and xù 'the end of a thread', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjeng and zjoX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the first, after an older book, as what a person desires, and the second as the loose end of silk; the sense 'mood' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
心疼 (xīnténg) — to feel for someone; to love dearly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a way to solve a problem, then the Mandarin for one's spirits.

You'll want to know: 心疼.
心疼 — xīnténg — "to feel for someone; to love dearly".
孩子病了妈妈很心疼 háizi bìng le māma hěn xīnténg — The child is ill, and her mother's heart aches for her.

The word, taken apart.
心疼 (xīnténg) is built from 心 'heart' and 疼 'pain', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sim and downg (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'pain'. The regular sound change would give tóng for the second; why the vowel differs is not settled.

The word, taken apart.
xīngfèn is built from xīng 'to rise' and fèn 'to spread the wings', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xing and pjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and 'to flourish' and the second as a bird spreading its feathers. The sense 'excited' is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
qīngsōng — relaxed; light and easy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to feel for someone, then the Mandarin for thrilled.

You'll want to know: qīngsōng.
qīngsōng — "relaxed; light and easy".
kǎoshì yǐhòu wǒ gǎnjué hěn qīngsōng — After the exam I feel really relaxed.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qīngsōng is built from qīng 'light' and sōng 'loose', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khjieng and sowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as light against heavy, and lists the second under the spelling for loose, tousled hair; the sense 'relaxed' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fán — annoyed; fed up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for thrilled, then the Mandarin for relaxed and at ease.

You'll want to know: fán.
fán — "annoyed; fed up".
zhǎo bu dào wǒ de xínglixiāng wǒ hěn fán — I can't find my suitcase, and I'm really fed up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fán is inherited from Middle Chinese bjon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'weary', and explained after an older book as a hot, aching head. The same syllable is the first half of máfan 'trouble'.
