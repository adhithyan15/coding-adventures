Mandarin Chinese, chapter 230: Head to Toe.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
身上 (shēnshang) — on your person; on one's body.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to scratch, then the Mandarin for to stroke.

You'll want to know: 身上.
身上 — shēnshang — "on your person; on one's body".
我身上没有钱 wǒ shēnshang méiyǒu qián — I haven't got any money on me.
The new character, in pieces: 身 (shēn) — seven strokes: a short fall to the left, a short vertical, a bar that turns down into a long hook, two short bars inside, a long stroke rising to the right, and a long stroke falling to the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
身上 (shēnshang) is built from 身 (shēn) 'body' and 上 'on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syin and dzyangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'oneself' and 'the body'; in the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Script — a frame with two bars, crossed by two long strokes.
身 (shēn).
One piece, seven strokes. Count them in three groups.
The frame, three strokes. A short stroke falling to the left at the top. A short vertical on the left. Then a bar from its top that runs right and turns down into a long stroke to the foot, hooking left.
Inside, two short bars, one under the other.
Across it all, two long strokes. A stroke rising from the far left to the right, through the frame. Then a long stroke from the upper right falling down to the lower left.
3 + 2 + 2 = seven.

体重 — tǐzhòng — "body weight".
你的体重是多少 nǐ de tǐzhòng shì duōshao — How much do you weigh?
The new character, in pieces: 体 (tǐ) — seven strokes: the person piece 亻, then 本. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
体重 (tǐzhòng) is built from 体 (tǐ) 'body' and 重 'heavy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thejX and drjowngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the body' and the second 'much' and 'thick'.

Script — the person piece, then 本.
体 (tǐ).
Two pieces, left before right.
The person piece, 亻, two strokes, as in 你 and 他.
Then 本, five strokes, as in 本子: a bar across, a long vertical, a stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right, then a short bar low on the vertical.
2 + 5 = seven.

Lesson 6 of 7.
bízi — nose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for body weight, then the Mandarin for a tummy.

You'll want to know: bízi.
bízi — "nose".
tā de bízi hěn gāo — He has a high-bridged nose.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bízi is built from bí 'nose' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjijH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as drawing in breath. It is listed with a departing tone, so the rising tone of bí is not the regular outcome.
