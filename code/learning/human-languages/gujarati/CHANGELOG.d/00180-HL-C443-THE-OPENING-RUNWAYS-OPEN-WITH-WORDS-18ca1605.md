## HL-C443: the opening runways open with words

Chapters 1 and 3-7 used to teach letters before any word the reader could say.
Each now opens with short words. Every letter the chapter writes is a piece of
one of them, so writing a letter means taking apart a word the reader already
knows.

| chapter | words, read before the letters | letters they anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **આહાર** (*āhār*, food) | હ ા આ |
| 3 | **છીપ** (*chīp*, a seashell), **કણ** (*kaṇ*, a grain), **શક** (*śak*, a doubt) | છ ી ક ણ શ, and પ in chapter 5 |
| 4 | **અથાણું** (*athāṇũ*, pickle), **દિવાળી** (*divāḷī*, Diwali) | અ થ ળ િ |
| 5 | **ચપ્પલ** (*cappal*, sandals), **બગલો** (*bagalo*, a heron) | ચ લ બ, and ગ in chapter 6 |
| 6 | **ઉખાણું** (*ukhāṇũ*, a riddle), **એકલું** (*eklũ*, alone) | ઉ ખ એ |
| 7 | **ધૂળ** (*dhūḷ*, dust), **ઈંટ** (*ĩṭ*, a brick), **ઢોલ** (*ḍhol*, a drum) | ૂ ઈ ટ ઢ |

Each word is taught romanized. Its other letters are exposure only. Each word is
revisited by the letter lessons it anchors.

The inserted words moved twelve older atoms past the edge of a retrieval window:
ra, da, va, ya, bha, chha, ka, the vocalic-r sign and four chapter-2 courtesy
items. None of them lost a window. Each is now retrieved by name in a new
word's warm-up, at a distance inside its window. Later anchor words also
retrieve earlier ones, so the new words reach R2 and, from chapter 3 on, most
reach R3.

Every chapter stays within the twelve-atom budget: 1 → 12, 3 → 12, 4-6 → 6,
7 → 7. Chapter 2 is already at twelve and gets no words, so its four cold
letters remain: દ, ય, ધ and ૃ. Sequence numbers from chapter 1 on move up to
make room, and the session map follows.
