Urdu, chapter 512: In the Garden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
باغ (bāgh) — a garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to blow (on), then the Urdu for to give (someone) a taste.

You'll want to know: باغ.
باغ — bāgh — "a garden".
ہمارے گھر کے پیچھے ایک باغ ہے۔ hamāre ghar ke pīchhe ek bāgh hai. There is a garden behind our house.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
باغ (bāgh) is usually traced to Persian bāgh, "a garden, an orchard".

Lesson 2 of 5.
کیاری (kyārī) — a flower bed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to give (someone) a taste, then the Urdu for a garden.

You'll want to know: کیاری.
کیاری — kyārī — "a flower bed".
مالی نے نئی کیاری بنائی۔ mālī ne naī kyārī banāī. The gardener made a new flower bed.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
کیاری (kyārī) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin kyārī, "a garden bed", under Sanskrit kēdāra, "a field, especially a flooded one" (CDIAL 3463).

Lesson 4 of 5.
سینچنا (sīṅchnā) — to water (plants); to irrigate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a flower bed, then the Urdu for fertiliser.

You'll want to know: سینچنا.
سینچنا — sīṅchnā — "to water (plants); to irrigate".
مالی ہر شام باغ سینچتا ہے۔ mālī har shām bāgh sīṅchtā hai. The gardener waters the garden every evening.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کیاری سینچی, us ne kyārī sīṅchī.

The word, taken apart.
سینچنا (sīṅchnā) is inherited from Sanskrit siñcati, "pours out, sprinkles" (CDIAL 13394).

Lesson 5 of 5.
اکھاڑنا (ukhāṛnā) — to pull up; to uproot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for fertiliser, then the Urdu for to water (plants).

You'll want to know: اکھاڑنا.
اکھاڑنا — ukhāṛnā — "to pull up; to uproot".
مالی نے کیاری سے گھاس اکھاڑی۔ mālī ne kyārī se ghās ukhāṛī. The gardener pulled the grass out of the flower bed.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے پودا اکھاڑا, us ne paudā ukhāṛā.

The word, taken apart.
اکھاڑنا (ukhāṛnā) is inherited from utskarati, "digs out", a form CDIAL reconstructs, under which it lists the Hindi verb meaning "to uproot" (CDIAL 1895).
