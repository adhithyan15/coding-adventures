# सुंदर / बदसूरत — beautiful, ugly, and a prefix you already own

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You met **सुप्रभात** as "good morning." Say it again and listen to
the first syllable. You are about to meet it a second time, in a word that has
nothing to do with mornings.

## You'll want to know: सुंदर and बदसूरत
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C80-LOOKS-01, HI-LEX-C80-LOOKS-02]; assesses=[] -->

**सुंदर** (*sundar*) — "beautiful."
**बदसूरत** (*badsūrat*) — "ugly."

Neither ends in **-आ**, so neither bends. You can put both to work with what you
learned last lesson and nothing else.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C31-SUPRABHAT-01] -->

**सुंदर** opens with Sanskrit **सु-** — the prefix meaning "good, well." It is
the same सु- sitting at the front of **सुप्रभात** ("good dawn"). Once you have
noticed it, you will keep finding it: सु- marks the favourable version of
whatever follows.

**बदसूरत** is two borrowings glued together. **बद** is Persian for "bad," and
**सूरत** is Arabic *ṣūrat*, "face" or "form." Ugly, in Hindi, is literally
**"bad-faced."**

> **बद** and English *bad* look like the same word and are not related. The
> resemblance is one of the most famous coincidences in etymology — two
> unconnected languages landing on nearly the same sound for nearly the same
> meaning. Enjoy it, and do not build a rule on it.

So the pair is built from opposite halves of Hindi's vocabulary: सुंदर is
Sanskrit through and through, बदसूरत is Persian plus Arabic. The language names
beauty with its inherited words and ugliness with its borrowed ones, which is an
accident of history rather than a judgement, but it is a useful one to notice —
it tells you which dictionary each word's relatives live in.
