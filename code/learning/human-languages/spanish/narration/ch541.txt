Spanish, chapter 541: Cuánto duró.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la fase — the phase, the stage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for weekly, then the Spanish for yearly.

You'll want to know: la fase.
la fase — "the phase, the stage".
El proyecto está en la primera fase. The project is in its first phase.

The word, taken apart.
fase comes from Greek phásis 'an appearance' (EtymDB), the word for each new look of the moon.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el transcurso — the passing, the span (of time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for yearly, then the Spanish for the phase.

You'll want to know: el transcurso.
el transcurso — "the passing, the span (of time)".
En el transcurso de la noche llegó más gente. As the night went on, more people arrived.
En el transcurso de — during, over the span of.

The word, taken apart.
transcurso is a learned borrowing from Latin trānscursus 'a running through' (Lewis & Short), built on trāns 'across' and currere 'to run'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
prolongarse — to go on, to drag on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the passing, then the Spanish for the length.

You'll want to know: prolongarse.
prolongarse — "to go on, to drag on".
La reunión se prolongó hasta la medianoche. The meeting went on until midnight.
It takes gu before e: se prolongue. Without se, prolongar is to extend.

The word, taken apart.
prolongarse is built on prolongar, a learned borrowing from Late Latin prōlongāre 'to lengthen' (EtymDB), built on prō and longus 'long' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: transcurrir.
transcurrir — "to go by, to pass (of time); to take place".
Transcurrieron dos horas sin noticias. Two hours went by without news.
A written word; the story transcurre en Sevilla is set in Seville.

transcurrir is a learned borrowing from Latin trānscurrere 'to run across' (EtymDB), built on trāns 'across' and currere 'to run'.
