# (twelve consonants, four confusable sets) — sorted by ear, not by date

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-01, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-06] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **வ**, then **க**, and name each.

## Script: the three that are all n
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-02, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-03, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-04] -->

| letter | sound | where the tongue goes |
|---|---|---|
| **ந** | *na* | teeth — the soft one |
| **ண** | *ṇa* | curled back, on the roof |
| **ன** | *ṉa* | the ridge behind the teeth |

They arrived one per chapter, pages apart, and no page has put them side by side.
That is the whole reason this set is hard: **Tamil hears three different letters
where English hears one sound.** The difference is in where the tongue lands,
and the shapes carry no hint of it.

## Script: two l-letters and two r-letters
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-113, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-115, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-05, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-117] -->

| pair | letters |
|---|---|
| the l-sounds | **ல** *la*, ordinary · **ழ** the curled one Tamil is named for |
| the r-sounds | **ர** *ra*, a tap · **ற** *ṟa*, harder and further back |

**ழ** deserves the pause. It is the sound in the language's own name, it has no
English equivalent, and a reader who met it once, thirty chapters ago, will not
place it cold now.

## Script: five that stand alone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-01, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-101, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-07, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-06, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-116] -->

No confusable twin, no set to belong to: **வ** *va* · **ப** *pa* · **ம** *ma* ·
**க** *ka* · **ச** *ca*.

Each one carries a built-in *a*, as every bare Tamil consonant does. Reading one
aloud means saying two things — the consonant and the vowel inside it.
