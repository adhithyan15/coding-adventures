Lesson 1 of 5.
il cantante — a singer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for peace, then the Italian for an army.

You'll want to know: il cantante.
il cantante — "a singer".

The word, taken apart.
cantante is the present participle of cantare, "to sing", used as a noun: one who sings. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for it; the parts are plain to see.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lo spettacolo — a performance, a show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for an army, then the Italian for a singer.

You'll want to know: lo spettacolo.
lo spettacolo — "a performance, a show".

The word, taken apart.
spettacolo from Latin spectaculum, "a show, a sight" — a learned borrowing. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for spettacolo; it derives French spectacle and Portuguese espetáculo from spectaculum.

Lesson 5 of 5.
il romanzo — a novel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for an actor, then the Italian for an actress.

You'll want to know: il romanzo.
il romanzo — "a novel".

The word, taken apart.
romanzo from Old French romanz — borrowed; behind it is Vulgar Latin romanice, "in the Roman way". (EtymDB 2.1) A romanz was a tale told in the everyday tongue, not in learned Latin.
