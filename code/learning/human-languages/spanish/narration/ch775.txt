Lesson 1 of 6.
la nevera — the fridge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the mirror, then the Spanish for the blind.

You'll want to know: la nevera.
la nevera — "the fridge".
La nevera está casi vacía. The fridge is almost empty.
El frigorífico is also common in Spain; in parts of Latin America people say el refrigerador or la heladera.

The word, taken apart.
nevera comes from Latin nivārius 'of snow' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on nix, nivis 'snow', which also gave nieve. It was first a place where snow was stored to keep things cold.

Lesson 3 of 6.
la lata — the tin, the can; a nuisance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fridge, then the Spanish for the coffee maker.

You'll want to know: la lata.
la lata — "the tin, the can; a nuisance".
En el armario hay latas de atún. In the cupboard there are tins of tuna.
¡Qué lata! — what a pain! Dar la lata — to pester.

The word, taken apart.
lata is usually traced to Late Latin latta 'a lath, a thin board', probably of Germanic origin. EtymDB traces its French cousin latte to Frankish latta, the same Germanic word as English lath. The sense moved from a thin strip of wood to a thin sheet of tin.

You'll want to know: el trozo.
el trozo — "the piece, the bit".
Me queda un trozo de pastel en la nevera. I have a piece of cake left in the fridge.
Un trozo de pan — a piece of bread.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of trozo is not settled. One proposal links it to Latin thyrsus 'a stalk' (Lewis & Short), but no account is agreed.

You'll want to know: el olor.
el olor — "the smell".
Me encanta el olor del pan recién hecho. I love the smell of freshly baked bread.
Olor a — a smell of: olor a café.

The word, taken apart.
olor is usually traced to Latin olor 'a smell' (Lewis & Short), a rare by-form of odor shaped by the verb olēre 'to give off a smell'.
