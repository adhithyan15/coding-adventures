Lesson 1 of 5.
शिशुः (śiśuḥ) — a baby, an infant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for embraces, then the Sanskrit for kisses.

You'll want to know: शिशुः.
शिशुः — śiśuḥ — "a baby, an infant".

The word, taken apart.
śiśu is a Rigvedic word for "the young of an animal, a child" (CDIAL 12476). MW derives it from √śū, "to swell", that is, to grow. It lives on as Hindi sisu, "infant".

कुमारः (kumāraḥ) — a boy, a youth.

You'll want to know: कुमारः.
कुमारः — kumāraḥ — "a boy, a youth".

The word, taken apart.
kumāra is a Rigvedic word for "boy" (CDIAL 3303). CDIAL suggests a first sense "weak, tender", perhaps related to komala, "soft", which you have already met. MW offers two hedged derivations, so the origin is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
कुमारी (kumārī) — a maiden, a girl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a baby, then the Sanskrit for a boy.

You'll want to know: कुमारी.
कुमारी — kumārī — "a maiden, a girl".

The word, taken apart.
kumārī is the feminine of kumāra, "boy", made with the ending -ī (MW). The Atharvaveda already has it as "young girl" (CDIAL 3303), and Punjabi kãvārī, "virgin", comes from it. India's southern tip, Kanyakumari, is named after the maiden goddess.

Lesson 4 of 5.
युवतिः (yuvatiḥ) — a young woman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a boy, then the Sanskrit for a maiden.

You'll want to know: युवतिः.
युवतिः — yuvatiḥ — "a young woman".

The word, taken apart.
yuvati is the feminine of yuvan, "young" (MW), a Rigvedic word (CDIAL 10504). The stem is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Latin iuvenis and English young. Hindi joī, "wife", comes from yuvati.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पुरुषः (puruṣaḥ) — a person, a man.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a maiden, then the Sanskrit for a young woman.

You'll want to know: पुरुषः.
पुरुषः — puruṣaḥ — "a person, a man".

The word, taken apart.
puruṣa is a Rigvedic word for "man, male" (CDIAL 8289). MW derives it, probably, from √pṝ, "to fill", and connects it with puru, "much"; the derivation is not settled. In philosophy it names the conscious self.
