# था — one word swapped, and the sentence is behind you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-MAIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I am at home* in Hindi. Now try to say where you were an hour
ago, and watch the sentence stop at its last word.

## You'll want to know: था
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01]; assesses=[] -->

**था** (*thā*) — "was," spoken by or about a male subject.

Every other word in the sentence stays where it is. Take out the present-tense
word, put था in its place, and you have moved:

| now | earlier |
|---|---|
| मैं घर पर हूँ। | मैं घर पर था। |
| वह स्टेशन पर है। | वह स्टेशन पर था। |

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01] -->

था is worn down from Sanskrit **स्थित** (*sthita*), "standing, situated." To
have been somewhere was, once, to have been standing there.

English arrives at the same picture along a different road. *Station* comes from
Latin *stāre*, to stand — and Latin *stāre* and Sanskrit *sthā-* are the same
inherited word, separated long before either language was written down. The
Hindi for *was* and the English for *station* are distant relatives.

## Grammar Lens: two present words, one past word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-MAIN-01, HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-02] -->

| subject | now | earlier |
|---|---|---|
| मैं | हूँ | था |
| वह | है | था |

In the present, the verb changes to match **who** you are talking about. In the
past it stops caring about that. One word covers both rows — which is a small
mercy, and a short-lived one, because the next lesson shows what the past
listens to instead.
