Portuguese, chapter 302: A terra tremeu.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o sismo — the quake (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fire extinguisher, then the Portuguese for the burn.

You'll want to know: o sismo.
o sismo — "the quake (formal)".
O sismo foi sentido em todo o país. The quake was felt all over the country.
A news word in Portugal.

The word, taken apart.
sismo was borrowed from Greek seismós "a shaking, an earthquake" (EtymDB), from seíein "to shake".

Lesson 2 of 5.
o terramoto — the earthquake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the burn, then the Portuguese for the quake.

You'll want to know: o terramoto.
o terramoto — "the earthquake".
Em 1755, um terramoto destruiu a cidade. In 1755 an earthquake destroyed the city.
In Brazil: terremoto.

The word, taken apart.
terramoto goes back to Latin terrae mōtus "a movement of the earth", from terra "earth" and movēre "to move" (Lewis & Short).

The word, taken apart.
tremor comes from Latin tremor "a trembling" (EtymDB), from tremere "to tremble".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ruir — to collapse, to fall down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the earthquake, then the Portuguese for the tremor.

You'll want to know: ruir.
ruir — "to collapse, to fall down".
O velho prédio ruiu durante a noite. The old building collapsed during the night.
Mostly in the third person: ruiu, ruíram.

The word, taken apart.
ruir comes from Latin ruere "to rush down, to fall" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: os escombros.
os escombros — "the rubble, the ruins".
Os bombeiros procuram pessoas nos escombros. The firefighters are searching the rubble for people.
Almost always plural.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of escombros is not settled; it sits beside Spanish escombro, and EtymDB has no entry.
