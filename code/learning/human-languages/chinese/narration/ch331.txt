Mandarin Chinese, chapter 331: Monsters and Treasure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yāoguài — monster; demon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a king's wife, then the Mandarin for a kingdom ruled by a king.

You'll want to know: yāoguài.
yāoguài — "monster; demon".
chuánshuō shān lǐ yǒu yí ge yāoguài — Legend says there's a monster in the mountains.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yāoguài is built from yāo and guài 'strange', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jew and kweajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'bewitching' and the second 'strange'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
shénxiān — immortal; supernatural being.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a kingdom ruled by a king, then the Mandarin for a monster in a story.

You'll want to know: shénxiān.
shénxiān — "immortal; supernatural being".
zuìhòu yí ge shénxiān bāng le tā — In the end an immortal helped him.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shénxiān is built from shén 'a god' and xiān 'an immortal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zyin and sjen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a spirit' and says of the second that one who grows old and does not die is called so.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cáng — to hide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for magic in a fairy tale, then the Mandarin for a precious treasure.

You'll want to know: cáng.
cáng — "to hide".
yāoguài lái de shíhou tāmen dōu cáng zài mén hòubian — When the monster came, they all hid behind the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cáng is inherited from Middle Chinese dzang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'to conceal' and 'to keep out of sight'. The dictionary also lists dzangH, 'a storehouse', a different word.
