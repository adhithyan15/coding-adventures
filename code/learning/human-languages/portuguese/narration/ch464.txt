You'll want to know: a geada.
a geada — "the frost".
De manhã, a relva estava branca de geada. In the morning, the grass was white with frost.
Cair geada — to frost over.

The word, taken apart.
geada is inherited from Latin gelāta 'frozen' (EtymDB), from gelāre 'to freeze', on gelū 'frost'.

congelar is a learned borrowing from Latin congelāre 'to freeze together' (EtymDB), on gelū 'frost'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
derreter — to melt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to freeze, then the Portuguese for the freezer.

You'll want to know: derreter.
derreter — "to melt".
Com o sol, a neve derrete depressa. In the sun, the snow melts quickly.
Ice cream derrete too.

The word, taken apart.
derreter is usually traced to Latin dēterere 'to rub away' (Lewis & Short), but the origin is not settled; EtymDB has no entry for it.
