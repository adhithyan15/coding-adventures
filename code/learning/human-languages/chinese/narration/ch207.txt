Mandarin Chinese, chapter 207: Where and When.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
地点 (dìdiǎn) — place; venue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for office, then the Mandarin for reception desk.

You'll want to know: 地点.
地点 — dìdiǎn — "place; venue".
我们见面的地点离这儿两公里 wǒmen jiànmiàn de dìdiǎn lí zhèr liǎng gōnglǐ — The place where we're meeting is two kilometres from here.
The new character, in pieces: 地 (dì) — six strokes: on the left the earth piece, a short bar, a vertical and a stroke rising to the right, as at the lower right of 在; on the right 也. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
地点 (dìdiǎn) is built from 地 (dì) 'ground, place' and 点 'a point', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dijH and temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the earth'.

Script — the earth piece, then 也.
地 (dì).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, the earth piece, three strokes. A short bar, a vertical down through it, then a stroke along the foot that rises to the right. It is the piece at the lower right of 在, squeezed to the left.
The right piece, 也, three strokes, the same three as in 也 itself: a bar that turns down and hooks, a short vertical, and a long vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 地 (dì), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 地 (dì)? (Six.) Which stroke of the left piece rises? (The last, along the foot.) What is the right piece? (也.)

You'll want to know: jìhuà.
jìhuà — "plan".
nǐ zhōumò yǒu shénme jìhuà — What are your plans for the weekend?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìhuà is built from jì 'to reckon' and huà 'to mark out', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kejH and hweak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to count up' and the second 'to cut with an awl'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ānpái — to arrange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for venue, then the Mandarin for a plan.

You'll want to know: ānpái.
ānpái — "to arrange".
māma ānpáile míngtiān de shíjiān — Mum has planned out tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ānpái is built from ān 'to settle' and pái 'to set in a row'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese 'an (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'calm'; the dictionary glosses the second 'to push aside', so how the pair came to mean arranging is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.

jǐngdiǎn — "sight; place of interest".
nǐ qùguò zhège jǐngdiǎn ma — Have you been to this sight?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: jǐng is said with a rising tone before diǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
jǐngdiǎn is built from jǐng 'a view' and 点 'a point', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaengX and temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'bright' and 'great'.
