# el préstamo — what lending leaves behind

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04, ES-LEX-C353-WHERE-22, ES-LEX-DINERO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **prestar**, **el banco** and **el dinero**. You have the verb
for handing something over. Name the thing that exists afterwards.

## You'll want to know: el préstamo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04] -->

**el préstamo** — the loan.

*Pedir un préstamo* — to apply for a loan. *Devolver el préstamo* — to pay it
back, with the same **devolver** the lending cycle already gave you.

The stress is three syllables from the end — **PRÉS-ta-mo** — and the written
accent is what tells you so. Without it the word would be read *pres-TA-mo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-05, ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-04] -->

It is **prestar** with a noun ending, and both go back to Latin **praestāre** —
*prae-* in front, plus *stāre*, to stand. To *praestāre* was **to stand out in
front of** someone: to furnish, to supply, to put something forward.

So a **préstamo** is what has been *put forward*, and English keeps the same
idea in **to advance** a sum.

This completes a set you already had. **Prestar** hands it over,
**recoger** fetches it, **devolver** brings it back — and now **el préstamo** is
the name of the thing while it is out of your hands.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-05, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04] -->

**Préstamo** has a second life worth knowing, because you will meet it in this
kind of explanation rather than at a bank.

*Un préstamo lingüístico* is a **loanword** — a word one language has taken from
another. Spanish calls **garaje** and **sofá** *préstamos*, and it uses the
borrowing metaphor exactly as English does, down to the fact that nobody gives
them back.

| the word on the page | what it is a loan of |
|---|---|
| un préstamo del banco | dinero |
| un préstamo del francés | una palabra |

That is the sense in which **factura** is not a *préstamo* and **garaje** is:
*factura* was taken from the Latin that Spanish itself grew out of, which makes
it an inheritance claimed late rather than a borrowing from a neighbour.
