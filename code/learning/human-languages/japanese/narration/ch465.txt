Japanese, chapter 465: Paints and Brushes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
えのぐ (enogu) — paints.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to attack, then the Japanese for to snatch.

You'll want to know: えのぐ.
えのぐ — enogu — "paints".
あかい えのぐを つかいました。 — akai enogu o tsukaimashita — "I used red paint."

The word, taken apart.
えのぐ (enogu) is a mixed phrase: Sino-Japanese e, "picture", and gu, "tool" (Middle Chinese gjuH), both borrowed from Chinese, joined by the native particle no, "of" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: えがく.
えがく — egaku — "to paint a picture; to depict".

Lesson 4 of 5.
がか (gaka) — a painter; an artist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a brush, then the Japanese for to paint a picture.

You'll want to know: がか.
がか — gaka — "a painter; an artist".
おじさんは がかです。 — ojisan wa gaka desu — "My uncle is a painter."

The word, taken apart.
がか (gaka) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ga, "picture" (Middle Chinese hweaH) and ka, "expert" (kae) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
