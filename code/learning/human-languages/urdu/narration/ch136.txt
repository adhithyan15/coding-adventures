Urdu, chapter 136: Thirsty, Fresh, Raw, Ripe, Pink.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پیاسا (pyāsā) — thirsty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for tired, then the Urdu for hungry.

You'll want to know: پیاسا.
پیاسا — pyāsā — "thirsty".

The word, taken apart.
پیاسا (pyāsā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from pipāsaka, "thirsty", a form CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit pipāsita (CDIAL 8200).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for fresh, then the Urdu for raw.

You'll want to know: پکا.
پکا — pakkā — "ripe, firm".

The word, taken apart.
پکا (pakkā) goes back to Sanskrit pakva, "cooked, ripe" (CDIAL 7621). CDIAL marks the Hindi form as taken over from Punjabi; "firm, sure" grew out of "ripe, mature".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: گلابی.
گلابی — gulābī — "pink".

The word, taken apart.
گلابی (gulābī) is a loan, usually traced to Persian gulābī, "rose-coloured". It is built on gulāb, "rose-water", from gul, "rose", and āb, "water".
