Bengali, chapter 265: What Will Happen, and the End.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
আমি যাব (āmi jābô) — the future, person by person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "Yesterday I was at home". (kāl āmi bāṛite chhilām.) Then the goodbye from chapter seven: কাল দেখা হবে, "see you tomorrow". Its last word, হবে, is a future: "it will happen".

You'll want to know: the future, person by person.
কাল আমি স্টেশনে যাব। kāl āmi sṭeshône jābô. — "Tomorrow I will go to the station."
With a future, কাল is tomorrow. The future puts -ব- on the stem, then the person:
আমি / আমরা যাব (jābô) — I will go, we will go.
তুমি / সে যাবে (jābe) — you will go; he or she will go.
আপনি / তিনি যাবেন (jāben) — with respect.
তোমরা / তারা যাবে — you, to several; they.
তুমি and সে share -বে here; the past kept them apart (গেলে, গেল). The stem is the plain one, not the past's: করব (kôrbô), দেখব (dekhbô), খাব (khābô), আসব (āsbô), থাকব (thākbô); দেওয়া becomes দেব (debô), নেওয়া becomes নেব (nebô); হওয়া becomes হবে (hôbe), "will be, will happen". You have said one -বেন for a long time: মাফ করবেন, "excuse me".

You'll want to know: will not.
"Will not" puts না after the verb: আমি যাব না (āmi jābô nā), "I won't go".
কাল আমি বাজারে যাব না, বাড়িতে থাকব। kāl āmi bājāre jābô nā, bāṛite thākbô. — "Tomorrow I won't go to the market; I'll stay at home."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Tomorrow I will go to the station" — kāl āmi sṭeshône jābô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Will you come?", to a friend — tumi āsbe?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What will happen?" — kī hôbe?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say that you will go to the station tomorrow, and that she will not.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I will go"? (যাব.) "She will come"? (আসবে.) "You will give", with respect? (দেবেন.) "I won't go"? (যাব না.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
শেষ (shesh) — the end; last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I will go" and "I won't go". (jābô, jābô nā.)

You'll want to know: শেষ.
শেষ — shesh — "the end; last".
কাল আমার কাজ শেষ হবে। kāl āmār kāj shesh hôbe. — "My work will be finished tomorrow."
শেষ হওয়া is "to come to an end", and শেষ করা "to finish": আমি বইটা শেষ করলাম (āmi bôiṭā shesh kôrlām), "I finished the book". Before a noun, শেষ is "last":
শেষ ট্রেন রাতে ছাড়বে। shesh ṭren rāte chhāṛbe. — "The last train will leave at night."

The word, taken apart.
শেষ (shesh) is a tatsama: Sanskrit śeṣa, "what remains", which CDIAL (12611) and Monier-Williams both give. Monier-Williams also lists "end, conclusion" and "last" among its senses, so the Bengali uses were already Sanskrit's.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: shesh]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My work will be finished tomorrow" — kāl āmār kāj shesh hôbe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The last train will leave at night" — shesh ṭren rāte chhāṛbe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does শেষ (shesh) mean? ("The end", and before a noun, "last".) "To finish"? (শেষ (shesh) করা.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
ষ (sh) — the letter at the end of শেষ (shesh).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ষ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say শেষ (shesh), "the end". Its first letter is শ, which you write; its last is new.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ষ.
ষ — sh. Bengali says শ, ষ and স alike, as sh in most words, so the letter is a matter of spelling: শেষ (shesh) begins with শ and ends with ষ. Most of the words that carry ষ come from Sanskrit.
In the print of this book, ষ (sh) has a headline and an upright stem on the right, the full height of the letter, as ব and ঘ do. A long straight line lies on a slant between the left end of the headline, close under it, and the middle of the stem. A shorter slanting line joins the middle of that line to a pointed tip at the lower left, and a curve joins that tip to the foot of the stem.

Writing: ষ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ষ (sh) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the headline, the stem, the long slanting line, the short one to the tip, and the curve to the foot.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ষ (sh) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — find: ষ (sh) at the end of শেষ (shesh)]
[once you have stopped driving — write: শ, then ষ (sh), from the print, naming each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: শেষ (shesh)]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ঘ, from memory]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter ends শেষ (shesh)? (ষ.) How is it said? (sh, as শ is.) What lies across its middle? (A long slanting line, meeting the stem.)
[once you have stopped driving — question: With no model, write shesh, 'the end', with both of its letters.]
