Mandarin Chinese, chapter 391: When Plans Change.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
rìchéng — a day's schedule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for on the agreed date, then the Mandarin for approximately.

You'll want to know: rìchéng.
rìchéng — "a day's schedule".
míngtiān de rìchéng hěn mǎn — Tomorrow's schedule is full.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rìchéng is built from 日 'a day' and chéng 'a stage of a journey', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyit and drjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a set term' and 'a limit'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
láidejí — to still have time (to do something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: láidejí.
láidejí — "to still have time (to do something)".
rúguǒ xiànzài zǒu hái láidejí — If we leave now, there's still time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
láidejí is built from 来 'to come', 得 'can' and jí 'to reach', all inherited from Middle Chinese, loj, tok and gip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'to get to': you can still get there. It is the other side of láibují.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for at the last minute, then the Mandarin for to still have time.

You'll want to know: gǎnshàng.
gǎnshàng — "to catch in time; to make it onto a train".
yàoshi wǒmen kuài yìdiǎnr jiù néng gǎnshàng huǒchē — If we're a bit quicker, we can catch the train.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnshàng is built from gǎn 'to chase' and 上 'to go up'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese dzyangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to climb'; the dictionary has no entry for the first, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
到时候 (dào shíhou) — when the time comes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to still have time, then the Mandarin for to make it onto a train.

You'll want to know: 到时候.
到时候 — dào shíhou — "when the time comes".
到时候我一定来 dào shíhou wǒ yídìng lái — When the time comes, I'll be there for sure.

The word, taken apart.
到时候 (dào shíhou) is built from 到 'to arrive' and 时候 'the time', all inherited from Middle Chinese, tawH, dzyi and huwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'to wait for'.
