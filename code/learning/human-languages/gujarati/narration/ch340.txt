Gujarati, chapter 340: What It Is For.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઉદ્દેશ (uddeś) — a purpose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a situation, then the Gujarati for a circumstance.

You'll want to know: ઉદ્દેશ.
ઉદ્દેશ — uddeś — "a purpose".
આ યોજનાનો ઉદ્દેશ ગામમાં પાણી લાવવાનો છે. ā yojnāno uddeś gāmmā̃ pāṇī lāvvāno che. The aim of this plan is to bring water to the village.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઉદ્દેશ (uddeś) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit uddeśa, "pointing at, an aim" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ઇરાદો (irādo) — an intention.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a purpose, then the Gujarati for a motive.

You'll want to know: ઇરાદો.
ઇરાદો — irādo — "an intention".
અરુણનો ઇરાદો સારો હતો, પણ પરિણામ ખરાબ આવ્યું. Aruṇno irādo sāro hato, paṇ pariṇām kharāb āvyũ. Arun meant well, but the result was bad.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઇરાદો (irādo) is usually traced to Arabic irāda, "will, intention", which came to Gujarati through Persian.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an intention, then the Gujarati for an excuse.

You'll want to know: સાધવું.
સાધવું — sādhvũ — "to accomplish".
મહેનતથી તે પોતાનો હેતુ સાધશે. mahenatthī te potāno hetu sādhśe. With hard work he will achieve his aim.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે પોતાનો હેતુ સાધ્યો, teṇe potāno hetu sādhyo.

The word, taken apart.
સાધવું (sādhvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit sādhnōti, "accomplishes"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb (CDIAL 13339).
