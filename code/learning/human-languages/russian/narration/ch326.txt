Russian, chapter 326: Ancestors and chronicles.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
предок (prédok) — an ancestor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to conquer, then the Russian for to rule.

You'll want to know: предок.
предок (prédok) — "an ancestor".
Наши предки жили в этой деревне. Náshi prédki zhíli v étoy derévne. Our ancestors lived in this village.
Genitive предка. In slang предки are one's parents.

The word, taken apart.
предок (prédok) is a Church Slavonic word, prědŭkŭ (EtymDB), built on пред- "before", the church shape of native перед: the one who went before.

You'll want to know: потомок.
потомок (potómok) — "a descendant".
Он потомок известного художника. On potómok izvéstnovo khudózhnika. He is a descendant of a famous painter.
Genitive потомка.

The word, taken apart.
потомок (potómok) is usually traced to потом "afterwards", from по and том, a form of тот "that": the one who comes after. EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 6.
летопись (létopis) — a chronicle, annals.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an ancestor, then the Russian for a descendant.

You'll want to know: летопись.
летопись (létopis) — "a chronicle, annals".
Город впервые упоминается в летописи. Górod vpervýye upomináyetsya v létopisi. The town is first mentioned in a chronicle.
Feminine. The old chronicles of Rus were written year by year.

The word, taken apart.
летопись (létopis) joins лето "summer, year", Proto-Slavic lěto "year", and писать "to write", Proto-Slavic pisati (EtymDB): a record kept year by year.

Lesson 4 of 6.
князь (knyaz) — a prince of old Rus, a ruling prince.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a descendant, then the Russian for a chronicle.

You'll want to know: князь.
князь (knyaz) — "a prince of old Rus, a ruling prince".
Князь жил в большом доме на холме. Knyaz zhil v bolshóm dóme na kholmé. The prince lived in a big house on a hill.
Plural князья. Княгиня — a princess, a prince's wife.

The word, taken apart.
князь (knyaz) is inherited from Proto-Slavic kŭnędzĭ, an early borrowing from Proto-Germanic kuningaz "king" (EtymDB); English king is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 6.
основывать (osnóvyvat) — to found.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a chronicle, then the Russian for a prince of old Rus.

You'll want to know: основывать.
основывать (osnóvyvat) — "to found".
Город основали в двенадцатом веке. Górod osnováli v dvenádtsatom véke. The town was founded in the twelfth century.
Imperfective; perfective основать (osnovát). Основатель — a founder.

The word, taken apart.
основывать (osnóvyvat) is built on о- and основа "a base", inherited from Proto-Slavic osnova (EtymDB), first the warp threads on a loom, a cousin of сновать "to warp, to dart to and fro".
