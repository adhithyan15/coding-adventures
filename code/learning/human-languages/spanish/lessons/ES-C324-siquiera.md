## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C324-TRUE-05]; assesses=[] -->

**siquiera** — "even," or "at least."

Nothing new arrived. Spanish took *si* and *quiera* — both of them already
yours — and pushed them together until they stopped being two words. *Si quiera*
meant *if he should want it*, the shrug you make when the smallest version of
something would do. Said often enough, the seam closed and the meaning thinned
into *even*.

The Latin underneath *quiera* is **quaerere**, *to seek*, and English kept the
seeking rather than the wanting: to **query** is to seek an answer, to
**acquire** is to seek toward, an **inquest** is a seeking-into, and a **quest**
is the bare verb standing alone.

Two uses carry almost all of the weight. On its own, **siquiera** softens a
request down to the smallest acceptable size: *siquiera un poco* — *at least a
little*. With **ni** in front, it does the opposite and empties the sentence
out: **ni siquiera** is *not even*.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Five words for weighing a claim: true, false, perhaps, less — and not even.
