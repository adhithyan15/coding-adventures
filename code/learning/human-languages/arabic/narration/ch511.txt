Arabic, chapter 511: Doses and drops.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
جرعة (jurʿa) — a dose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to treat, then the Arabic for to cure.

You'll want to know: جرعة.
جرعة — jurʿa — "a dose".
شرب المريض جرعة من الدواء. shariba al-marīḍ jurʿa min ad-dawāʾ. The patient drank a dose of the medicine.

The word, taken apart.
جرعة (jurʿa) is built on the native Arabic root j-r-ʿ, "to gulp, to swallow" (Lane). Lane gives jurʿa as a single gulp: a dose is one swallow of medicine.

Lesson 3 of 6.
قطرة (qaṭra) — a droplet; a drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a dose, then the Arabic for a capsule.

You'll want to know: قطرة.
قطرة — qaṭra — "a droplet; a drop".
وضعت الممرضة قطرة في عيني. waḍaʿat al-mumarriḍa qaṭra fī ʿaynī. The nurse put a drop in my eye.

The word, taken apart.
قطرة (qaṭra) is built on the native Arabic root q-ṭ-r, "to drip, to fall in drops" (Lane). Qiṭār, a train, which you know, is from the same root: a line of camels following one another, like drops.

Lesson 5 of 6.
ضمد (ḍammada) — to bind up (a wound).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a droplet, then the Arabic for a splint.

You'll want to know: ضمد.
ضمد — ḍammada — "to bind up (a wound)".
ضمدت الممرضة جرح اللاعب. ḍammadat al-mumarriḍa jurḥ al-lāʿib. The nurse bound up the player's wound.
Present: يضمد, yuḍammid.
The noun ضمادة, a bandage, shares the root.

The word, taken apart.
ضمد (ḍammada) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-m-d, "to bind, to bandage" (Lane). Form II ḍammada is to bandage.
