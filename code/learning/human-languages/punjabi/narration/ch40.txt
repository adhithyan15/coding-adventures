5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਕਿ (ki) — that.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
From the chapter behind you, the two words for but. And from the naming lesson, the word for what.
[your turn — recall: par, lekin — then ਕੀ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: ate — from two chapters back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
Look at these two very carefully, because they are not the same word:
ਕੀ — kī — what, with the long bihari.
ਕਿ — ki — that, with the short sihari.
One stroke apart, and completely different jobs. You have had ਕੀ since you first asked someone's name. ਕਿ (ki) is new.
ਅਤੇ and ਪਰ put two sentences side by side. ਕਿ (ki) does something different: it lets one sentence be swallowed by another, as the thing a verb is about.
(verb of thinking, knowing or saying) ਕਿ (whole sentence).
English does the same with that, and like English, Punjabi often drops it in speech. Learn it in place first.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

The frame, taken apart.
ਮੈਂ ਸੋਚਦਾ ਹਾਂ ਕਿ … — maiṁ sochdā hāṁ ki … — "I think that …"
Three of the four pieces are old: ਮੈਂ, the habitual ending you met with bolda, and ਹਾਂ. Only ਕਿ (ki) is new, and it opens the space your opinion goes into.
A woman says ਸੋਚਦੀ — sochdī — with the feminine ending, exactly as with every habitual verb this book has taught. The sentence you use is the one that matches you.
Everything you can already say can now be said as an opinion rather than as a fact. That is a large change for one small word, and it is the difference between reporting the world and having a view about it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਕਿ (ki) reaches the page — that, on the page — and the one stroke that separates it from what.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one stroke is the whole lesson until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
One chapter back you wrote ਪਰ, standing alone. Write it once from memory.

Script — one stroke is the whole lesson.
Uncover both and put them side by side:
ਕਿ (ki) — the short ਿ sihari, written before the letter.
ਕੀ — the long ੀ bihari, written after it.
Same consonant. The vowel stroke sits on a different side, and that alone is the difference between that and what. Both signs have been yours for many chapters; what is new is that the pair now matters.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Copy ਕਿ (ki) once with the model in view, then ਕੀ beneath it. Check which side each stroke fell on before you go on.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write both. Which one is that, and how does its stroke sit? (ਕਿ (ki) — the sihari, before the letter.)

The word, taken apart.
ਕਿਉਂ — kyoṁ — why.
The same ਕ as every question word in the family. This one has been missing: the book named the family when it taught kivēṁ, listed its members, and ਕਿਉਂ (kyoṁ) was the one it left out — so a reader could ask several things and never why.
That is not a small omission. Why is the question that asks for a reason, and the next lesson is the word that answers it.

Warm-up.

[your turn — recall: kyoṁ, and ki]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: nā … nā … — from two chapters back]

The word, taken apart.
The answer is the question with the swallowing word stuck to the end of it:
ਕਿਉਂ (why) + ਕਿ (that) = ਕਿਉਂਕਿ (kyoṁki) — because.
Both halves are already yours from earlier in this chapter; the join is the only new thing. Punjabi answers why by saying, in effect, "why-that", and the reason then follows as a whole sentence.
(what happened) ਕਿਉਂਕਿ (kyoṁki) (the reason).
Note the ਕਿ (ki) at the end is the short sihari one — the that you learned, not the what. Reading the word slowly is its own revision of the pair.
