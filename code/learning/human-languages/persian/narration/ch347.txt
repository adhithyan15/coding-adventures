Persian, chapter 347: Legends and heroes.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
افسانه (afsâne) — a legend, a fable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to relate, then the Persian for to remind.

You'll want to know: افسانه.
افسانه (afsâne) — "a legend, a fable".
این افسانه درباره یک کوه بلند است. In afsâne darbâre-ye yek kuh-e boland ast. This legend is about a high mountain.
Also "a made-up story": afsâne ast "it is a fairy tale". Afsâne-i "legendary".

The word, taken apart.
afsâne is inherited from Middle Persian afsānag, "fable" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to remind, then the Persian for a legend.

You'll want to know: اسطوره.
اسطوره (osture) — "a myth".
در این اسطوره آتش مقدس است. Dar in osture âtash moqaddas ast. In this myth fire is sacred.
Broken plural asâtir. The word reached Arabic from Greek.

The word, taken apart.
osture is borrowed from Arabic usṭūra, "fable, myth", and EtymDB takes the Arabic word from Greek historía, "history, inquiry".

Lesson 5 of 6.
دیو (div) — a demon, an ogre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a hero, then the Persian for a dragon.

You'll want to know: دیو.
دیو (div) — "a demon, an ogre".
در قصه دیو دختر را دزدید. Dar qesse div dokhtar râ dozdid. In the tale the demon carried off the girl.
In Persian epics the div is a huge, evil being. Divâne "mad" is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
div is inherited: EtymDB traces it through Middle Persian dēw to Old Persian daiva-, "evil spirit", and on to an Indo-European word for a god. Persian turned the old gods into demons.
