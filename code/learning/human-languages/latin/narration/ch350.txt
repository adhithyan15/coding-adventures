Latin, chapter 350: In macellō.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
obsōnō, obsōnāre — to buy food, to shop for a meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for unbelievable, then the Latin for an invention.

You'll want to know: obsōnō, obsōnāre.
obsōnō, obsōnāre — "to buy food, to shop for a meal".
Coquus māne in macellō obsōnābat. In the morning the cook was buying food at the market.
obsōnō, obsōnāre, obsōnāvī, obsōnātum, also opsōnō. Comedies are full of cooks sent off to shop.

The word, taken apart.
obsōnō was borrowed from Greek opsōneō, "to buy fish or other dainties" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
obsōnium, obsōniī — food to go with bread, provisions.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an invention, then the Latin for to buy food.

You'll want to know: obsōnium, obsōniī.
obsōnium, obsōniī — "food to go with bread, provisions".
Obsōnium in culīnam portāvit: piscēs et holera. He carried the provisions into the kitchen: fish and vegetables.
Neuter. Anything eaten with bread, very often fish.

The word, taken apart.
obsōnium was borrowed from Greek opsōnion, "provisions, what is eaten with bread" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
abluō, abluere — to wash off, to wash clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to buy food, then the Latin for food to go with bread.

You'll want to know: abluō, abluere.
abluō, abluere — "to wash off, to wash clean".
Coquus holera aquā frīgidā abluit. The cook washed the vegetables in cold water.
abluō, abluere, abluī, ablūtum. English ablution.

The word, taken apart.
abluō joins ab, "away", and luō, "to wash", a cousin of lavō (EtymDB). English ablution comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
tergeō, tergēre — to wipe, to wipe clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for food to go with bread, then the Latin for to wash off.

You'll want to know: tergeō, tergēre.
tergeō, tergēre — "to wipe, to wipe clean".
Ancilla mēnsam linteō tersit. The maid wiped the dining table with a cloth.
tergeō, tergēre, tersī, tersum. Its compound dētergeō gives English detergent.

The word, taken apart.
tergeō is an inherited verb (EtymDB). English detergent comes from its compound dētergeō, "to wipe off".

Lesson 5 of 5.
cultellus, cultellī — a small knife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to wash off, then the Latin for to wipe.

You'll want to know: cultellus, cultellī.
cultellus, cultellī — "a small knife".
Coquus cultellum acūtum ē cistā sūmpsit. The cook took a sharp little knife from the box.
Masculine, a diminutive of culter (taught). English cutlass and cutlery come from this family.

The word, taken apart.
cultellus is a diminutive of culter, "knife" (Lewis & Short). English cutlass and cutlery come from it through French.
