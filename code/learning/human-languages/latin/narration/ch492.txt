Latin, chapter 492: Comfort and gentleness.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cōnsōlor, cōnsōlārī — to console, comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a groan, then the Latin for to sigh.

You'll want to know: cōnsōlor, cōnsōlārī.
cōnsōlor, cōnsōlārī — "to console, comfort".
Amīca puellam trīstem cōnsōlātur. Her friend comforts the sad girl.
Deponent: cōnsōlor, cōnsōlārī, cōnsōlātus sum, with the accusative. English console.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsōlor is built on con- and sōlor "to comfort" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English console and consolation come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cōnsōlātiō, cōnsōlātiōnis — consolation, comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sigh, then the Latin for to console.

You'll want to know: cōnsōlātiō, cōnsōlātiōnis.
cōnsōlātiō, cōnsōlātiōnis — "consolation, comfort".
Epistula amīcī mihi magna cōnsōlātiō fuit. My friend's letter was a great comfort to me.
Feminine, third declension. A favourite word of letter-writers.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsōlātiō is the action noun of cōnsōlor "to console", with -tiō (Lewis & Short). English consolation comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lēnis, lēne — gentle, mild, soft.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for consolation, then the Latin for solace.

You'll want to know: lēnis, lēne.
lēnis, lēne — "gentle, mild, soft".
Vōx mātris lēnis et dulcis est. Mother's voice is gentle and sweet.
Two endings: lēnis for masculine and feminine, lēne for neuter. Of voices, winds and people.

The word, taken apart.
lēnis is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short compares lentus "pliant, slow", and EtymDB lists a Proto-Indo-European parent, but the link is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lēniō, lēnīre — to soothe, soften.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for solace, then the Latin for gentle.

You'll want to know: lēniō, lēnīre.
lēniō, lēnīre — "to soothe, soften".
Medicus dolōrem aegrī medicāmentō lēnit. The doctor soothes the patient's pain with a medicine.
lēniō, lēnīre, lēnīvī, lēnītum, from lēnis. English lenient comes from its present participle.

The word, taken apart.
lēniō is built on lēnis "gentle, soft" (Lewis & Short). English lenient comes from its present participle lēniēns.
