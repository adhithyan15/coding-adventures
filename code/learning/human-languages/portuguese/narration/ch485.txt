Portuguese, chapter 485: Arranhões e nódoas negras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
arranhar — to scratch (and leave a mark).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for nausea, then the Portuguese for to vomit.

You'll want to know: arranhar.
arranhar — "to scratch (and leave a mark)".
O gato arranhou-me a mão. The cat scratched my hand.
Coçar is for an itch; arranhar leaves a mark.

The word, taken apart.
arranhar is an old Portuguese verb (EtymDB), but its origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
a nódoa negra — bruise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to scratch, then the Portuguese for scratch.

You'll want to know: a nódoa negra.
a nódoa negra — "bruise".
Bati com a perna na cama e fiquei com uma nódoa negra. I hit my leg on the bed and got a bruise.
Literally "a black stain". In Brazil: a mancha roxa.

The word, taken apart.
nódoa is inherited from Latin notula "a little mark" (EtymDB), a diminutive of nota "a mark"; negra comes from Latin niger "black" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
inchar — to swell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for scratch, then the Portuguese for bruise.

You'll want to know: inchar.
inchar — "to swell".
O tornozelo inchou depois da queda. The ankle swelled after the fall.
Regular: incha, inchou.

The word, taken apart.
inchar is inherited, through Old Portuguese enchar, from Latin inflāre "to blow into, to puff up" (EtymDB).
