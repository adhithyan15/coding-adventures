# suficiente — enough, and only enough

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-BASTANTE, ES-LEX-C352-COUNT-15] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **bastante** and **escaso**. You already own both ends of this.
The word coming next overlaps one of them, and the overlap is the lesson.

## You'll want to know: suficiente
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01] -->

**suficiente** — enough, sufficient.

> No producimos comida **suficiente** para todos.

*Comida suficiente* — enough food. *Lo suficiente* — enough of it, as a noun.
*Suficientemente* — sufficiently.

Like *bastante*, it has one form for both genders: *un plato suficiente*, *una
cantidad suficiente*.

Said of a person it turns unkind — *un tono suficiente* is a smug one.

## Grammar Lens: bastante already means this, and that is the problem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02, ES-LEX-BASTANTE] -->

*Bastante* was given to you as the middle of a scale — *poco*, **bastante**,
*muy* — and its lesson was firm that it does **not** mean *a lot*. So what is
*suficiente* for?

| word | what else it does |
|---|---|
| bastante | *quite*, *fairly* — *bastante caro* |
| suficiente | names a requirement, not a place on a scale |

*Bastante* has that second, adverbial job: *bastante caro* is **fairly**
expensive, a reading on the scale. *Suficiente* has no scale reading at all.
It measures a thing against what was needed, and reports whether the need was
met.

Both can say there is not enough — *no hay bastante* is the everyday phrasing.
A notice reaches for *no hay suficiente* because it names the requirement
rather than the quantity, and because it is the formal choice.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02] -->

Latin **sufficere**: **sub-**, up from under, plus **facere**, to make or do —
the *facere* that *la factura* called one of the hardest-working roots in the
language.

To *sufficere* was to put something **under** a thing: supply it from below,
prop it, make it stand. Adequacy came out of that, which is why sufficiency is
load-bearing rather than abundant — the amount that keeps the thing up.

*Suficiente* wears the **-ente** of *exigente*; *bastante* wears **-ante**.
They are the same ending choosing its vowel by verb family — *-ar* verbs take
*-ante*, *-er* and *-ir* verbs take *-ente*.

And the two words are one language at two speeds, the pattern you have from
*rueda* against *rotativo*. *Suficiente* is a real Latin present participle,
*sufficientem*, lifted off the page. *Bastante* was built inside Spanish on
the inherited verb *bastar* — there was never a Latin *bastans*.
