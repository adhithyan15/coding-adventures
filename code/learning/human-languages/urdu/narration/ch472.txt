Urdu, chapter 472: An Upset Stomach.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ہضم کرنا (hazm karnā) — to digest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for healing, then the Urdu for medical.

You'll want to know: ہضم کرنا.
ہضم کرنا — hazm karnā — "to digest".
معدہ کھانا ہضم کرتا ہے۔ medā khānā hazm kartā hai. The stomach digests food.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کھانا ہضم کیا, us ne khānā hazm kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
ہضم کرنا (hazm karnā) is built from ہضم, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic haḍm, "digestion", and کرنا, "to do".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ابکائی (ubkāī) — retching; feeling sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for digestion, then the Urdu for indigestion.

You'll want to know: ابکائی.
ابکائی — ubkāī — "retching; feeling sick".
بس میں اسے ابکائی آئی۔ bas meṅ use ubkāī āī. She felt sick on the bus.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ابکائی (ubkāī) is inherited: it is the noun of the Hindi verb ubaknā, "to vomit", which CDIAL lists under ubbakka, "vomited", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 2337).
