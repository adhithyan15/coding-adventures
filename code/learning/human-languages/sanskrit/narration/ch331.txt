Sanskrit, chapter 331: Down to the River.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
भक्तिः (bhaktiḥ) — devotion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a festival day, then the Sanskrit for a forehead mark.

You'll want to know: भक्तिः.
भक्तिः — bhaktiḥ — "devotion".
जनाः भक्त्या नदीं नमन्ति। janāḥ bhaktyā nadīṃ namanti. People bow to the river with devotion.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
भक्तिः (bhaktiḥ) comes from the root √bhaj, "to share, worship", as in bhajati; MW gives bhakti as a share, and also attachment, devotion, worship, faith (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
विसर्जयति (visarjayati) — immerses; lets go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for devotion, then the Sanskrit for sandalwood.

You'll want to know: विसर्जयति.
विसर्जयति — visarjayati — "immerses; lets go".
उत्सवस्य अन्ते जनाः दीपान् नद्यां विसर्जयन्ति। utsavasya ante janāḥ dīpān nadyāṃ visarjayanti. At the end of the festival people set lamps afloat on the river.
Past (he …): सः दीपं नद्यां विसर्जितवान्, saḥ dīpaṃ nadyāṃ visarjitavān.

The word, taken apart.
विसर्जयति (visarjayati) is built from vi and the causative of the root √sṛj, "to let go, send forth"; MW gives vi-sṛj as to send or pour forth, let go, and the immersion of an image is its festival use (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for immerses, then the Sanskrit for chants softly.

You'll want to know: पवित्रः.
पवित्रः — pavitraḥ — "holy; pure".
सा नदी पवित्रा अस्ति। sā nadī pavitrā asti. That river is holy.

The word, taken apart.
पवित्रः (pavitraḥ) MW gives pavitra first as a means of purification, a filter, and as an adjective purifying, pure, holy, sacred, without a root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).
