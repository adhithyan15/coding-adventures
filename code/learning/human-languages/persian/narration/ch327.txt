Persian, chapter 327: Help arrives.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
امدادگر (emdâdgar) — a rescue worker, a paramedic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to faint, then the Persian for to come round.

You'll want to know: امدادگر.
امدادگر (emdâdgar) — "a rescue worker, a paramedic".
امدادگرها زود به محل رسیدند. Emdâdgar-hâ zud be mahal residand. The rescue workers soon reached the scene.
emdâd "help, relief" + the native suffix -gar.

The word, taken apart.
emdâdgar joins emdâd "help, relief", borrowed from Arabic imdād (EtymDB lists the Arabic word), and the native Persian suffix -gar "one who does".

Lesson 2 of 5.
اورژانس (urzhâns) — the emergency services; an emergency room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to come round, then the Persian for a rescue worker.

You'll want to know: اورژانس.
اورژانس (urzhâns) — "the emergency services; an emergency room".
مادرم به اورژانس زنگ زد. Mâdaram be urzhâns zang zad. My mother called the emergency services.
Both the ambulance service and a hospital's emergency department. Written with ژ.

The word, taken apart.
urzhâns is borrowed from French urgence, "emergency" (EtymDB).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the emergency services, then the Persian for a siren.

You'll want to know: زنده ماندن.
زنده ماندن (zende mândan) — "to survive, to stay alive".
همه مسافران زنده ماندند. Hame-ye mosâferân zende mândand. All the passengers survived.
Literally "to remain alive". Present stem mân-: zende mimânad.

The word, taken apart.
zende "alive" is inherited from Middle Persian zīndag, and mândan "to remain" is inherited from Middle Persian too (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
غریق نجات (ghariq-e nejât) — a lifeguard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a siren, then the Persian for to survive.

You'll want to know: غریق نجات.
غریق نجات (ghariq-e nejât) — "a lifeguard".
غریق نجات (ghariq-e nejât) پسر را از آب بیرون آورد. Ghariq-e nejât pesar râ az âb birun âvard. The lifeguard pulled the boy out of the water.
Literally "rescue of the drowning": ghariq "a drowning person" + nejât "rescue".

The word, taken apart.
nejât "rescue" is borrowed from Arabic najāt (EtymDB); ghariq "drowning, a drowning person" is usually traced to Arabic gharīq. The phrase itself is Persian.
