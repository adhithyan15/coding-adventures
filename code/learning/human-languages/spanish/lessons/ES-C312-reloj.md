## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C312-ASK-11]; assesses=[] -->

**el reloj** — "the clock," and also the watch on your wrist.

Behind it is **horologium**, which Latin had from Greek: *hōra*, the **hour**,
plus a stem meaning **to tell**. A clock is an **hour-teller**. That is the entire
description, and it is still accurate.

Spanish reached *reloj* by way of Catalan *relotge*, and you can watch the word
lose weight at every step. *Horologium* has five syllables. *Relotge* has three.
*Reloj* has two. The *h-* went, the *-ium* went, the middle collapsed. What is
left is barely recognisable — until you know what to look for.

The *hōra* half is easy to find in English. An **hour** is one. **Horology** is
the study of timekeeping, and a **horologist** repairs watches. A **horoscope** is
an *hour-watcher*: the reading of which sky stood over the hour you were born.
