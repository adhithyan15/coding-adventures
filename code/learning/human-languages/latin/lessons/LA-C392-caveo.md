## You'll want to know: caveō, cavēre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C392-CAVEO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**caveō, cavēre** — "to beware, to be on one's guard".

> In mūrō scrīptum erat: CAVĒ CANEM.
>
> *On the wall was written: BEWARE OF THE DOG.*

*caveō, cavēre, cāvī, cautum*. *Cavēre* + accusative, to beware of; *cavē nē* + subjunctive, take care not to. English *caution* and *caveat*, let him beware.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*caveō* is inherited from Proto-Italic *\*kawēō* (EtymDB), from a root meaning "to watch, to heed". English *caution* and *caveat* come from it.
