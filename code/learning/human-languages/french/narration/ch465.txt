French, chapter 465: Tempête et inondations.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
la tempête — the storm, the gale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for clammy, then the French for to sweat.

You'll want to know: la tempête.
la tempête — "the storm, the gale".
La tempête a fait tomber des arbres sur la route. The storm brought trees down onto the road.
Stronger than an orage: a lot of wind.

The word, taken apart.
tempête comes from Old French tempeste, from Vulgar Latin tempesta, from Latin tempestās 'weather, storm', built on tempus 'time' (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: l'inondation.
l'inondation — "the flood".
Après l'inondation, la cave était pleine d'eau. After the flood the cellar was full of water.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
inondation is a learned borrowing from Latin inundātiō (EtymDB), from inundāre 'to overflow', built on unda 'wave'.

Lesson 4 of 7.
déborder — to overflow; to go beyond.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the flood, then the French for to flood.

You'll want to know: déborder.
déborder — "to overflow; to go beyond".
Après trois jours de pluie, le ruisseau déborde. After three days of rain the stream is overflowing.
Être débordé — to be swamped with work.

The word, taken apart.
déborder is built from dé- and bord 'edge, side' (EtymDB lists it), which comes from a Frankish word for the side of a ship.

Lesson 5 of 7.
la flaque — the puddle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to flood, then the French for to overflow.

You'll want to know: la flaque.
la flaque — "the puddle".
Le bus est passé dans une flaque et m'a mouillé. The bus went through a puddle and got me wet.
Une flaque d'eau.

The word, taken apart.
flaque came into French from Picard flaque (EtymDB), a northern dialect word usually linked to flache 'hollow, soft ground'; the deeper origin is not settled.
