# disfrutar — to take the fruit of it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C367-REF-37, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04, ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la fruta**, **descansar** and **celebrar**. Two of those three
share a prefix shape. Hold on to that.

## You'll want to know: disfrutar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C367-REF-37] -->

**disfrutar** — to enjoy.

*Disfruté mucho de la boda* — I really enjoyed the wedding. *¡Que disfrutes!* —
enjoy yourself, said as you hand somebody a ticket or wave them off.

It usually takes **de**: *disfrutar **de** algo*. Dropping the *de* is common in
speech and still reads as informal in writing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-04] -->

**dis-** plus **frut-**, and the second half is **la fruta** you already have.
Both come from Latin **frūctus**, which meant *produce, yield, the thing a
plant gives you*.

To **disfrutar** something is to **take the fruit of** it — to get the yield.
English has the same picture in *to enjoy the fruits of your labour*, which most
speakers hear as a metaphor and which Spanish has compressed into one verb.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C440-FIESTA-04, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04] -->

Now the trap, and it corrects the test you learned two chapters ago.

**descansar** is **des-** plus *cansar*, and the prefix **undoes**: it takes the
tiredness away. It would be natural to read **dis-** in *disfrutar* the same way
and land on *to un-fruit*, which would be the opposite of what the word means.

| word | what the prefix does |
|---|---|
| descansar | reverses — removes the tiredness |
| **disfrutar** | **intensifies — takes the yield fully** |

The piece-test still works: take **dis-** off and *frutar* stands, so the join is
real. What the test does **not** tell you is what the join *means*. A prefix that
usually reverses does not always reverse, and the only way to know is the word's
sense, not its shape.

That is the honest limit of taking words apart. It shows you the seams; it does
not read them for you.
