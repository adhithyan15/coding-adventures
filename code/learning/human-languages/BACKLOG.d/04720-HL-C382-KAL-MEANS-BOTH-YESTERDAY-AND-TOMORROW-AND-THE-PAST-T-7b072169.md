## HL-C382 — kal means both yesterday and tomorrow, and the past tense has just made the second sense teachable

**कल** (*kal*) is taught once in the Hindi track, inside `HI-C04-kal-milte-hain`
— *कल मिलते हैं*, "see you tomorrow." That is the only sense the corpus carries.

Hindi uses the same word for **the day before today and the day after it**. The
tense of the verb is what tells them apart, which is why the second sense was
genuinely unteachable until chapter 95: with no past tense in the corpus there
was nothing to disambiguate against. *कल मैं घर पर था* is yesterday and *कल मैं
घर पर हूँगा* is tomorrow, and neither sentence could be written before the past
copula existed.

**Now one of the two halves is available.** A lesson adding the *yesterday*
sense would be:

- a `introduces_senses` addition rather than a new headword — the machinery
  exists (`HI-C01-namaste` carries `HI-SENSE-NAMASTE-HELLO-GOODBYE-01` for the
  same hello/goodbye double duty), and it needs a registry entry, which is why
  it was left out of the chapter-95 tranche rather than bolted on;
- the natural payoff lesson for a past-tense chapter, because it turns the
  tense from a grammar fact into the thing that resolves an ambiguity;
- worth pairing with **परसों** (`HI-C49-day-after`), which does the same trick
  one day further out and is already taught with the same single-sense gap.

**Not urgent, high value per word.** It closes no inventory point on its own.
What it buys is that every past-tense sentence in the track can carry a time
adverb, which is what the `HI-A1-T-09` note ("nothing can be sequenced in time")
is really about — and **पहले** and **बाद** are the other half of that point.
Grouping कल, परसों, पहले and बाद into one short time-reference chapter after the
past tense would close `HI-A1-T-09` and make the past chapter's sentences
usable in a mock answer rather than only grammatical.


### Correction: the payoff named here has already happened

This entry proposed grouping **कल**, **परसों**, **पहले** and **बाद** into a time
chapter and said that would close `HI-A1-T-09`. **`HI-A1-T-09` closed at chapter
98**, on पहले and के बाद, without कल or परसों being touched.

So the stated payoff is gone, and what is left is the honest remainder: कल and
परसों each carry **two senses** and the corpus teaches one apiece. That is a real
gap in reading — *कल मैं घर पर था* and *कल मैं घर पर हूँगा* are different days —
but it **closes no inventory point**, and this entry should be ranked on that
basis rather than on a closure it can no longer deliver.

The `introduces_senses` machinery and the registry entry it needs are still the
right shape for the work.
