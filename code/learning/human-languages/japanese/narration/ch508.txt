Japanese, chapter 508: Good Luck and Bad Luck.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
うん (un) — luck; fortune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an ideal, then the Japanese for to want (said of someone else).

You'll want to know: うん.
うん — un — "luck; fortune".
もし うんが よかったら、あには しあいに かつでしょう。 — moshi un ga yokattara, ani wa shiai ni katsu deshō — "If he is lucky, my elder brother will probably win the match."

The word, taken apart.
うん (un) is Sino-Japanese (kango): one Chinese character read with its on'yomi, reading borrowed from Chinese. It is un, "luck" (Middle Chinese hjunH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

One more word for saying what happens if.

Lesson 4 of 5.
うんよく (un'yoku) — luckily.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for good fortune, then the Japanese for a misfortune.

You'll want to know: うんよく.
うんよく — un'yoku — "luckily".
うんよく でんしゃに まにあったら、ちちは ごじに つくでしょう。 — un'yoku densha ni maniattara, chichi wa goji ni tsuku deshō — "If my father is lucky and catches the train, he will arrive at five."

The word, taken apart.
うんよく (un'yoku) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese un, "luck" (Middle Chinese hjunH), borrowed from Chinese, and native yoku, "well", the adverb of yoi, "good" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
