Italian, chapter 426: I bagagli.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il trolley — the wheeled suitcase.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the shuttle, then the Italian for the time zone.

You'll want to know: il trolley.
il trolley — "the wheeled suitcase".
Porto solo un trolley piccolo. I am taking only a small wheeled case.
An English word with an Italian meaning: in English a trolley is a cart.

The word, taken apart.
trolley was borrowed from English trolley 'a small cart', and in Italian it came to mean a suitcase on wheels.

Le valigie grandi vanno in stiva. The big suitcases go in the hold.
Il bagaglio da stiva — checked luggage.

The word, taken apart.
stiva is built on stivare 'to stow, to load', which is inherited from Latin stīpāre 'to press together' (EtymDB).

il nastro — "the belt (for baggage); the ribbon".
Aspettiamo le valigie al nastro sei. We wait for the suitcases at belt six.
Il nastro trasportatore — conveyor belt; also a ribbon for hair or gifts.

The word, taken apart.
nastro was borrowed from Gothic nastilō 'strap, band' (EtymDB), a Germanic word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la bilancia — the scales.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the hold, then the Italian for the belt.

You'll want to know: la bilancia.
la bilancia — "the scales".
Metti la valigia sulla bilancia. Put the suitcase on the scales.
La bilancia pesapersone — bathroom scales.

The word, taken apart.
bilancia is inherited from Late Latin bilancia 'balance', from bilanx 'having two pans', built from bi- and lanx 'dish' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
imballare — to pack, to wrap (for transport).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the belt, then the Italian for the scales.

You'll want to know: imballare.
imballare — "to pack, to wrap (for transport)".
Imballiamo bene i piatti per il viaggio. We pack the plates carefully for the journey.
L'imballaggio — the packaging. For clothes in a case you say fare la valigia.

The word, taken apart.
imballare is built from in- and balla 'bale', which is usually traced to a Germanic word that came into Italian as a borrowing.
