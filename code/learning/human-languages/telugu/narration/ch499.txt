Telugu, chapter 499: Envy and Dislike.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
అసూయపడు (asūyapaḍu) — to be jealous; to envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to hold back politely, then the Telugu for to hang one's head.

You'll want to know: అసూయపడు.
అసూయపడు — asūyapaḍu — "to be jealous; to envy".
అతని కొత్త కారు చూసి స్నేహితులు అసూయపడ్డారు. atani kotta kāru cūsi snēhitulu asūyapaḍḍāru. Seeing his new car, his friends were jealous.
Past (I …): అసూయపడ్డాను, asūyapaḍḍānu.
Who you envy takes మీద: అతని మీద అసూయపడు (asūyapaḍu).

The word, taken apart.
అసూయపడు (asūyapaḍu) is built from అసూయ, "envy", from Sanskrit asūyā, resentment at another person's good fortune (Monier-Williams) and the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ద్వేషించు (dvēṣiñcu) — to hate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to hang one's head, then the Telugu for to be jealous.

You'll want to know: ద్వేషించు.
ద్వేషించు — dvēṣiñcu — "to hate".
అబద్ధాలను నేను ద్వేషిస్తాను. abaddhālanu nēnu dvēṣistānu. I hate lies.
Past (I …): ద్వేషించాను, dvēṣiñcānu.
The noun ద్వేషం is hatred; its opposite is ప్రేమ.

The word, taken apart.
ద్వేషించు (dvēṣiñcu) is built on Sanskrit dvēṣa, "hatred" (CDIAL 6696), from the root dviṣ, with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.

Lesson 3 of 5.
అసహ్యించుకో (asahyiñcukō) — to loathe; to be disgusted by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be jealous, then the Telugu for to hate.

You'll want to know: అసహ్యించుకో.
అసహ్యించుకో — asahyiñcukō — "to loathe; to be disgusted by".
మురికిగా ఉన్న వంటగదిని ఆమె అసహ్యించుకుంది. murikigā unna vaṇṭagadini āme asahyiñcukundi. She was disgusted by the dirty kitchen.
Past (I …): అసహ్యించుకున్నాను, asahyiñcukunnānu.
The noun is అసహ్యం, disgust: నాకు అసహ్యంగా ఉంది, it disgusts me.

The word, taken apart.
అసహ్యించుకో (asahyiñcukō) is built on Sanskrit asahya, "unbearable, not to be endured" (Monier-Williams), from a-, "not", and sah, "to bear"; the Telugu endings -ఇంచు and -కో make it a verb about one's own feeling.

Lesson 4 of 5.
పగ (paga) — a grudge; revenge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to hate, then the Telugu for to loathe.

You'll want to know: పగ.
పగ — paga — "a grudge; revenge".
ఆ రెండు కుటుంబాల మధ్య పాత పగ ఉంది. ā reṇḍu kuṭumbāla madhya pāta paga undi. There is an old grudge between those two families.
పగ (paga) తీర్చుకో, to take revenge.

The word, taken apart.
పగ (paga) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3808 lists Telugu paga, "enmity, hatred", and pagavāḍu, "an enemy", in the article of Tamil paku, "to be split, to be at variance".

Lesson 5 of 5.
చిన్నచూపు (cinnacūpu) — contempt; looking down on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to loathe, then the Telugu for a grudge.

You'll want to know: చిన్నచూపు.
చిన్నచూపు — cinnacūpu — "contempt; looking down on".
పేదవాళ్ళను చిన్నచూపు చూడకూడదు. pēdavāḷḷanu cinnacūpu cūḍakūḍadu. One must not look down on the poor.
చిన్నచూపు (cinnacūpu) చూడు, to look down on someone: literally a small look.

The word, taken apart.
చిన్నచూపు (cinnacūpu) is native Dravidian: చిన్న, "small, petty" (DEDR 2594), and cūpu, "sight, a look", the noun of cūcu, "to see" (DEDR 2735): a look that makes someone small.
