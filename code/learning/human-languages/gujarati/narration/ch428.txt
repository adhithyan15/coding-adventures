Gujarati, chapter 428: Debts and Moneylenders.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
દેવાળું (devāḷũ) — bankruptcy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a fixed term, then the Gujarati for to allot.

You'll want to know: દેવાળું.
દેવાળું — devāḷũ — "bankruptcy".
ધંધામાં ખોટ ગઈ અને વેપારીનું દેવાળું નીકળ્યું. dhandhāmā̃ khoṭ gaī ane vepārīnũ devāḷũ nīkaḷyũ. The business made a loss and the merchant went bankrupt.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
દેવાળું (devāḷũ) CDIAL lists Hindi diwālā and Punjabi davālā, "bankruptcy", under Sanskrit dīpāvali, "a row of lamps" (CDIAL 6358), the word behind દિવાળી; it gives no Gujarati form for દેવાળું, and how lamps came to mean ruin is not settled, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 2 of 5.
લેણું (leṇũ) — money owed to you; a sum due.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to allot, then the Gujarati for bankruptcy.

You'll want to know: લેણું.
લેણું — leṇũ — "money owed to you; a sum due".
તેની પાસે મારું સો રૂપિયાનું લેણું છે. tenī pāse mārũ so rūpiyānũ leṇũ che. He owes me a hundred rupees.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
લેણું (leṇũ) is built on લેવું, to take, a tadbhava of Sanskrit labhatē, "catches, takes" (CDIAL 10948): what you still have to take back from someone.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ઉછીનું (uchīnũ) — borrowed for a while.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for bankruptcy, then the Gujarati for money owed to you.

You'll want to know: ઉછીનું.
ઉછીનું — uchīnũ — "borrowed for a while".
મેં મિત્ર પાસેથી સો રૂપિયા ઉછીના લીધા. mẽ mitra pāsethī so rūpiyā uchīnā līdhā. I borrowed a hundred rupees from a friend.

The word, taken apart.
ઉછીનું (uchīnũ) goes back to ut-śrita-, "raised", a form CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit ucchrita; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "borrowed" (CDIAL 1862).

Lesson 4 of 5.
શાહુકાર (śāhukār) — a moneylender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for money owed to you, then the Gujarati for borrowed for a while.

You'll want to know: શાહુકાર.
શાહુકાર — śāhukār — "a moneylender".
ગામનો શાહુકાર ઘણું વ્યાજ લે છે. gāmno śāhukār ghaṇũ vyāj le che. The village moneylender charges a lot of interest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
શાહુકાર (śāhukār) is built on શાહુ, a banker, which goes back to Sanskrit sādhu, "good, honest" (CDIAL 13337), with -કાર, "one who does".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઠગવું (ṭhagvũ) — to swindle; to con.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for borrowed for a while, then the Gujarati for a moneylender.

You'll want to know: ઠગવું.
ઠગવું — ṭhagvũ — "to swindle; to con".
આ દુકાનદાર ગ્રાહકોને ઠગે છે. ā dukāndār grāhkone ṭhage che. This shopkeeper swindles his customers.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે વેપારીને ઠગ્યો, teṇe vepārīne ṭhagyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઠગવું (ṭhagvũ) goes back to ṭhagg, "to cheat", a form CDIAL reconstructs; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to cheat, to rob" (CDIAL 5489).
