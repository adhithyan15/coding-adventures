Gujarati, chapter 515: Cracks and Leaks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પોપડો (popḍo) — a flake (of plaster).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a wall peg, then the Gujarati for to hang (something).

You'll want to know: પોપડો.
પોપડો — popḍo — "a flake (of plaster)".
દીવાલ પરથી એક મોટો પોપડો પડ્યો. dīvāl parthī ek moṭo popḍo paḍyo. A big flake of plaster fell off the wall.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પોપડો (popḍo) comes from pōppa, a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word as a detached scaly crust (CDIAL 8405).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉખડવું (ukhaḍvũ) — to peel off (of plaster or paint).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to hang (something), then the Gujarati for a flake (of plaster).

You'll want to know: ઉખડવું.
ઉખડવું — ukhaḍvũ — "to peel off (of plaster or paint)".
ભેજને લીધે છત પરથી પોપડા ઉખડી ગયા. bhejne līdhe chat parthī popḍā ukhḍī gayā. Because of the damp, flakes peeled off the ceiling.
Past (it …): તે પોપડો ઉખડી ગયો, te popḍo ukhḍī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
ઉખડવું (ukhaḍvũ) comes from utskarati, "digs out", a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb as "to be rooted up" (CDIAL 1895). It is the intransitive partner of ઉખેડવું, to uproot.

Lesson 3 of 5.
કાણું (kāṇũ) — a hole.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a flake (of plaster), then the Gujarati for to peel off.

You'll want to know: કાણું.
કાણું — kāṇũ — "a hole".
બાલદીમાં એક કાણું છે. bāldīmā̃ ek kāṇũ che. There is a hole in the bucket.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
કાણું (kāṇũ) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit kāṇá, "one-eyed", with the Gujarati word glossed "full of holes; a hole" (CDIAL 3019): perhaps first an empty eye-hole, as CDIAL suggests.
