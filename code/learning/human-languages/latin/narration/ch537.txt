Latin, chapter 537: Quarrels and making up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
iūrgium, iūrgiī — a quarrel, dispute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a darling, then the Latin for a gift.

You'll want to know: iūrgium, iūrgiī.
iūrgium, iūrgiī — "a quarrel, dispute".
Inter frātrēs saepe iūrgium est. There is often a quarrel between the brothers.
Neuter, second declension. A quarrel of words, at home or between neighbours.

The word, taken apart.
iūrgium is built on iūrgō "to quarrel", which Lewis & Short takes from iūs "right, law" and agō "to drive"; EtymDB agrees on iūs.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēserō, dēserere — to abandon, desert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to give as a present, then the Latin for faithless.

You'll want to know: dēserō, dēserere.
dēserō, dēserere — "to abandon, desert".
Amīcus vērus amīcum in perīculō nōn dēserit. A true friend does not abandon his friend in danger.
dēserō, dēserere, dēseruī, dēsertum: dē- 'away' + serō 'to join' — to break a tie. English desert.

The word, taken apart.
dēserō is built on dē- "away" and serō "to join, link" (Lewis & Short): to break off a tie. English desert comes from its participle dēsertus.
