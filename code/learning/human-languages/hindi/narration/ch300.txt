Hindi, chapter 300: The Blaze.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
धधकना (dhadhaknā) — to blaze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to flare up, then the Hindi for dry straw.

You'll want to know: धधकना.
धधकना — dhadhaknā — "to blaze".
पूरा घर धधक रहा था। pūrā ghar dhadhak rahā thā. The whole house was ablaze.
Past (it …): वह आग धधकी, vah āg dhadhkī.

The word, taken apart.
धधकना (dhadhaknā) is inherited from dhagg, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 6704); the doubled dha-dha gives the roar of a big fire.

Lesson 2 of 5.
दहकना (dahaknā) — to glow red-hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for dry straw, then the Hindi for to blaze.

You'll want to know: दहकना.
दहकना — dahaknā — "to glow red-hot".
आग के बाद भी अंगारे दहक रहे थे। āg ke bād bhī aṅgāre dahak rahe the. Even after the fire the embers were still glowing.
Past (it …): वह कोयला दहका, vah koylā dahkā.

The word, taken apart.
दहकना (dahaknā) goes back to Sanskrit dahati, "burns" (CDIAL 6245). It is the glow of coals with no flame: embers दहकते हैं.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ज्वाला (jvālā) — a blaze; a flame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to blaze, then the Hindi for to glow red-hot.

You'll want to know: ज्वाला.
ज्वाला — jvālā — "a blaze; a flame".
आग की ज्वाला दूर से दिख रही थी। āg kī jvālā dūr se dikh rahī thī. The blaze could be seen from far away.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ज्वाला (jvālā) is Sanskrit jvālā, "a flame" (Monier-Williams), taken over whole. It is a word for writing and for big fires; the everyday word is लपट.

Lesson 4 of 5.
उजाला (ujālā) — light; brightness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to glow red-hot, then the Hindi for a blaze.

You'll want to know: उजाला.
उजाला — ujālā — "light; brightness".
आग का उजाला पूरे गाँव में फैल गया। āg kā ujālā pūre gā̃v meṁ phail gayā. The glow of the fire spread over the whole village.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
उजाला (ujālā) is inherited from ujjvālaka, "bright", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 1673). It is light that lets you see, the opposite of darkness: सुबह का उजाला, the light of morning.

Lesson 5 of 5.
तपिश (tapiś) — scorching heat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a blaze, then the Hindi for light.

You'll want to know: तपिश.
तपिश — tapiś — "scorching heat".
आग की तपिश से खिड़की का शीशा टूट गया। āg kī tapiś se khiṛkī kā śīśā ṭūṭ gayā. The heat of the fire cracked the window glass.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
तपिश (tapiś) is usually traced to Persian tapiš, "heat, a burning". It is stronger than गर्मी: the heat that comes off a fire or a road at noon.
