Gujarati, chapter 177: Again and again.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વારંવાર (vārãvār) — again and again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for three quarters, then the Gujarati for almost.

You'll want to know: વારંવાર.
વારંવાર — vārãvār — "again and again".
તે વારંવાર ફોન કરશે. — te vārãvār phon karśe — "She will phone again and again."

The word, taken apart.
વારંવાર (vārãvār) is built by doubling vār, "a turn, a time", which CDIAL traces to Sanskrit vāra, "appointed time, one's turn; day of the week" (CDIAL 11547).

Lesson 2 of 5.
અવારનવાર (avārnavār) — now and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for almost, then the Gujarati for again and again.

You'll want to know: અવારનવાર.
અવારનવાર — avārnavār — "now and then".
અમે અવારનવાર મળીશું. — ame avārnavār maḷīśũ — "We will meet now and then."

The word, taken apart.
અવારનવાર (avārnavār) is a rhyming compound built on vār, "a turn, a time", from Sanskrit vāra (CDIAL 11547); how its first part was formed is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
વખત (vakhat) — occasion, a time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for again and again, then the Gujarati for now and then.

You'll want to know: વખત.
વખત — vakhat — "occasion, a time".
હું બે વખત આવીશ. — hũ be vakhat āvīś — "I will come twice."
બે વખત (vakhat), twice.

The word, taken apart.
વખત (vakhat) is a loan from Arabic waqt, "a time, an appointed time", which reached Gujarati through Persian; EtymDB lists the Persian word. Its Arabic root is w-q-t, "to fix a time" (Lane).

રોજિંદું (rojindũ) — everyday, daily.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for occasion, then the Gujarati for regular.

You'll want to know: રોજિંદું.
રોજિંદું — rojindũ — "everyday, daily".
હું રોજિંદું કામ સવારે કરીશ. — hũ rojindũ kām savāre karīś — "I will do the everyday work in the morning."
Built on રોજ, roj, every day.

The word, taken apart.
રોજિંદું (rojindũ) is built on the taught roj, "every day", which is usually traced to Persian rōz, "day".
