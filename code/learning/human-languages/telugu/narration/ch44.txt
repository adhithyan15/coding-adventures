Telugu, chapter 44: Verbs of Seeing and Saying.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
చూడు (cūḍu) — look.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four verbs for taking things in and putting them out, one at a time.

You'll want to know: చూడు.
చూడు (cūḍu) — "look".
Also 'to see' and 'to look after', the same three-way stem as its Kannada cousin ನೋಡು — the languages are close enough that the pattern travels.

Lesson 2 of 4.
ఆలకించు (ālakiñcu) — listen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did cūḍu mean?

You'll want to know: ఆలకించు.
ఆలకించు (ālakiñcu) — "listen".
'Listen attentively' — a step above simply hearing. Telugu keeps listening and asking separate, where Kannada merges them into one stem.

Lesson 3 of 4.
మాట్లాడు (māṭlāḍu) — speak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ālakiñcu mean?

You'll want to know: మాట్లాడు.
మాట్లాడు (māṭlāḍu) — "speak".
From māṭa, 'word', plus āḍu, 'to play' — the same construction as Kannada's ಮಾತಾಡು, built from the same two pieces.

Lesson 4 of 4.
రాయి (rāyi) — write.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: రాయి.
రాయి (rāyi) — "write".
The everyday 'write'. Telugu and Kannada each kept their own Dravidian stem here rather than borrowing a shared one.
