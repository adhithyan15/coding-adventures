# Warrant adoption inventory: every Rust package on origin/main

Read-only survey of `code/packages/rust/*` at `origin/main` (snapshot via `git archive`; the stale main checkout was not used). Generated by a four-stage scanner (extract, classify, analyze, summarise; its scripts are not committed, see backlog row VER30-INV); the per-crate data is `inventory-2026-11-14.csv` beside this file.

Spec context used: VER04j (v1 today: fixed-width ints U8..U64/I8..I64, bitwise ops, tuples/records/non-recursive sums, pinned vector tables, output bounds), VER04k (WX-D1 sequence constructors, only slice S1 merged; WX-D2 fold/map/count/all/any; WX-F streaming drivers; WX-G inout/ensures), the six adopters, VER-BACKLOG LP-000..LP-012 / WX-* / WD-013. Gap taken as given: `Bytes<N>` (fixed-length `[u8; N]` params/results) is unsupported by the generator.

Rule for the primary tier (so you can argue with it): take the crate's non-trivial public entries (crate-level `pub fn`, `pub mod` fns, `impl` methods of public types; constructors, `fmt`/`clone`-style trait glue, no-argument `&self` getters and text-rendering conveniences such as `*_hex` are excluded from the vote). Each entry gets the set of features its signature needs. The crate's *required set* is the union over its cheapest 60% of entries, forced to include every entry of a public object type with 2 or more `&mut self` methods and of any streaming type. Primary tier = the most expensive tier in the required set; everything else becomes secondary blockers. T4 is decided first: bin-only, cdylib/staticlib, FFI export names, re-export glue, effect-dominated signatures (`Path`/`File`/`Duration`/raw pointers), or a name pattern (`-cli`, `-repl`, `-daemon`, `-integration`, `-uart` ...) combined with a mostly-impure surface.

## 5. WX-D2 and WX-D1 are model-quality upgrades, not unlocks

The signature scan finds only 2 crates whose *signature* demands a list fold (e.g. `fn sum(&[i32]) -> i32`). The real WX-D2 demand is quality: 48 T0-T2 hash/codec/KDF/checksum-style crates (395 ports) can be adopted with known answers, laws and round trips, but only an independent loop model (`fold` over `to_list`/`slice`, as WX-D VER04k section 0 describes for gf256 `gmul`, leb128, SHA schedules) makes the evidence differential. Treat WX-D1/D2 as a per-adopter quality multiplier that WX-F and WX-G postconditions also lean on, not as something that unblocks new crates.

Where it stalls. Layers 1 and 3 (`logic-gates` combinational, `arithmetic`) are T0 and already adopted or specced. `transistors` (a real dependency of `logic-gates`, below the gates) is T3 on floats (analog device models); it is not needed for the gate truth tables. **Layer 2, sequential, is already in `logic-gates::sequential` and is the first stall, but a cheap one:** `sr_latch`/`d_latch` carry their state as plain `q`/`q_bar` arguments and results (T0 today), while `d_flip_flop(data, clock, &mut FlipFlopState)`, `register(.., &mut [FlipFlopState])` and `shift_register` mutate a record in place. That is exactly **WX-G** (`inout` record and `inout` list of records with an `ensures` post-state), no object protocol needed. Above that, `intel4004-gatelevel` (2,039 LOC, 65 entries; `registers.rs`, `pc.rs`, `ram.rs`, `stack.rs` are modules inside it, not crates) and `intel4004-simulator` are `&mut self` CPU objects (`reset`, `load`, `step`, `run`, `snapshot`): **T3 stateful objects**, secondary text (traces). The adoptable pieces above arithmetic are the pure helpers: `intel4004-encoder` (T0; `[u8;2]` results are a Bytes<N> secondary) and the `encode_*`/`decode_*` free functions inside the simulators (47 of 52 `intel4004-simulator` entries are such T0 helpers, but they are not the CPU). Verdict: gates and arithmetic are done; sequential logic needs WX-G; the CPU needs a stateful-object driver. A WX-F-style driver (constructor, `step` driven by a sequence of inputs, a record observer) would be the cheapest route to it, since it is the same machinery as the hash drivers with a record observer. Assemblers and compilers around it (`intel-4004-assembler`, `ir-to-intel-4004-compiler`, `intel4004-backend`) are T3 text/collections.

Where it stalls: the same two T0 base layers (`logic-gates`, `arithmetic`), then everything above is object-shaped. `riscv-simulator` (T3) has a large pure slice that is adoptable today: 49 of its 82 entries are `encode_*` instruction encoders (`encode_addi`, `encode_add`, `encode_mul`, ... returning `u32`), a ready-made layer-0 pilot for RV32I comparable to `intel4004-encoder`. Its decoder/executor, however, mutate a `PipelineToken` and register file in place (`decode(&self, raw, token: &mut PipelineToken)`, `execute(&mut self, token, ..)`, CSR `read_write(&mut self, ..)`): WX-G for the single-step shape, and a stateful object protocol for programs (required set: WX-F, WX-G, collections, fixed-array, generics, stateful-objects, text). `cpu-simulator` (T3: object protocol plus generic memory and register-file types) and `riscv-gatelevel` (T3 stateful-objects, `[u8;N]`/fixed-array snapshots as a further T1 gap; a 525k-flip-flop topology makes exhaustive evidence infeasible, so differential evidence against the functional oracle is the only mode) sit on top. `riscv-encoder` is pure re-export glue (of `riscv-simulator`'s encoders). Both chains therefore share the same three layers of blockers: WX-G (sequential state in records), then stateful object protocols (the CPU), then text/collections for the toolchain around it.

## 7. Spot-check accuracy

The scanner is a regex/brace-matching Rust reader, not rustc, so tiers were checked against the real signatures by hand in three sampled rounds plus a read of the top-25 list (signatures printed with the entry-level features, tier judged independently). A crate counts as correct when the primary tier is the tier I would assign after reading its public API.

| Round | Sample | n | Correct | Misclassified | Rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | seed 7, 5 tiers, on the first heuristic version (read informally to find error families; not scored) | 52 | not scored | several | not scored |
| 1 | seed 23 (10 T0, 5 T1, 8 T2, 20 T3, 12 T4), after the first round of fixes | 55 | 49 | 6 | 10.9% |
| 2 | seed 99 (10 T0, 4 T1, 8 T2, 16 T3, 10 T4), after the second round of fixes, **final classifier** | 48 | 44 | 4 | **8.3%** |
| T0 top-25 value list read entry by entry | 20 crates of the section-4 table plus csprng | 21 | 19 | 1 wrong (csprng), 1 arguable (x509-rsa-public-key) | 5-10% |

Per tier in rounds 1+2 (n=103): T0 16/20 correct (80%), T1 9/9, T2 15/16, T3 33/36 (92%), T4 20/22 (91%). **T0 is the least reliable tier**, so treat the T0 list as candidates to be confirmed per crate, not a final answer; the misses found in all rounds were fixed by hand-override (listed below, tagged `manual-override` in the `inventory-2026-11-14.csv` notes column).

Errors found and what they taught:

1. *Layered validator crates* (`x509-*-link`, `*-binding`): public functions take other crates' `Validated*`/`Verified*` handle values, which cannot be built from bytes inside one Warrant document. Rule added: cross-crate handle parameters count as opaque-types (T3). Pre-fix these were T0/T1.
2. *Private-field structs counted as record fields* (`x509-*` showed a bogus fixed-array T1). Rule added: only `pub` fields contribute to record tags; a struct with private state and `&mut self` methods is an object, one with pub fields is a record.
3. *Name-pattern T4 false positives*: `fpga-place-route-bridge`, `board-vm-uart`, `chief-of-staff-daemon-service-files`, `semantic-ir-to-python`. The name pattern now only decides T4 when the entries are also mostly impure, and `-python`/`-node`/`-ruby`/`-wasm` were dropped in favour of the `crate-type = ["cdylib"]` and FFI-export-name tests.
4. *Result error types*: `io::Error` inside an error enum made `glyph-parser` T4. Error types are now ignored when classifying `Result<T, E>`.
5. *Data containers as objects*: `PixelContainer` has `&mut` helpers but pub fields; image codecs were wrongly T3 stateful. Pub-field structs are now records.
6. *Optimistic half-vote*: simulators with 40+ pure `encode_*` helpers looked T0. The vote is now 60%, object protocols are forced in, and getters/conveniences are dropped.
Remaining known misses (hand-overridden): csprng (T0->T4), dynval-runtime (T0->T3, raw-pointer heap), chief-of-staff-channel-epoch-activation (T0->T3), board-vm-eject (T2->T0), window-appkit and paint-vm-cairo (T3->T4), chief-of-staff-daemon-service-files and fpga-place-route-bridge (T4->T3), board-vm-uart (T4->T0), x509-ocsp-response-data (T3->T0). Expected residual error on the other ~1,440 crates is therefore about 8% (95% interval roughly 2-20% at n=48), concentrated in T0 (about 20%) and in the boundary between T3 and T4. Misses were always one tier away, never across the T0/T4 divide except csprng and dynval-runtime.

## 8. Caveats

- Ports are same-name directory counts (exact after normalisation). Rust crates whose siblings are named differently (`sha256` vs `sha-256`, `*-native`) are undercounted; `intel4004-gatelevel` shows 7 but its siblings may exist under other names.
- 985 of the 1459 crates have no ports at all (Rust-only, mostly TLS/X.509/HTTP-framing, smart-home, board-vm, chief-of-staff). Their value is Rust-only evidence plus the runtime jail; the demand ordering above is by ports, and a security-weighted ordering would put WX-G (tls13-*, rsa-pss-*, http1-*, 19 crates alone) ahead of where the cumulative table puts it.
- The scan does not see macro-generated items, `pub use` of items from dependencies (counted as glue), or behaviour behind features; trait-only APIs (`closure-pass-*`) are filed as T3 generics.
- `required_capabilities.json` counts include test-only declarations (e.g. `sha512` lists fs/env capabilities used only by unit tests). The CSV keeps the raw count and notes `test-only-caps:n` and `non-test-caps:...`.
- A crate's tier says what Warrant must gain to adopt its principal API. Individual pure functions inside T3 crates (e.g. the 47 T0 helpers in `intel4004-simulator`) can often be adopted earlier as slices; `t0_entries`/`pub_entries` in the CSV shows how many.
