Malayalam, chapter 172: Cook, Give, Bring, Call, Cry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
വേവിക്കുക (vēvikkuka) — to cook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to bathe, then the Malayalam for to wash.
[pause 1 second]
Say "in front of" and "behind." (Munnil, pinnil.)

You'll want to know: വേവിക്കുക.
വേവിക്കുക — vēvikkuka — "to cook".

The word, taken apart.
വേവിക്കുക (vēvikkuka) is native Dravidian, the causative of the verb "to be hot, to boil": "make boil"; DEDR 5517 lists the root with Tamil vē, Kannada bē and Telugu vēgu.

The word, taken apart.
കൊടുക്കുക (koṭukkuka) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil koṭu and Kannada koḍu (DEDR 2053). It is the "give" that goes to a third person, the partner of tarū.

Lesson 3 of 5.
കൊണ്ടുവരുക (koṇṭuvaruka) — to bring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to cook, then the Malayalam for to give.
[pause 1 second]
Say "with" two ways. (Oppaṁ, kūṭe.)

You'll want to know: കൊണ്ടുവരുക.
കൊണ്ടുവരുക — koṇṭuvaruka — "to bring".

The word, taken apart.
കൊണ്ടുവരുക (koṇṭuvaruka) is native Dravidian, "come having taken": koṇṭu, the past form of the verb "to take" (DEDR 2151, with Tamil koḷ), and varuka, "to come" (DEDR 5270).
