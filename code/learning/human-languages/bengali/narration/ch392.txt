Bengali, chapter 392: Traffic and Crossings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ট্রাফিক (ṭrāphik) — traffic (on the roads).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to overtake, then the Bengali for to apply (the brakes).

You'll want to know: ট্রাফিক.
ট্রাফিক — ṭrāphik — "traffic (on the roads)".
ট্রাফিক পুলিশ হাত তুলে গাড়ি থামালেন। ṭrāphik pulish hāt tule gāṛi thāmālen. The traffic policeman raised his hand and stopped the cars.
যানজট is a jam; ট্রাফিক (ṭrāphik) is the traffic itself, and ট্রাফিক (ṭrāphik) পুলিশ the officer at the crossing.

The word, taken apart.
ট্রাফিক (ṭrāphik) is borrowed from English traffic; Bengali has no f, so the English f is said with ফ.

পথচারী — pôthchārī — "a pedestrian".
পথচারীরা মোড়ে রাস্তা পার হল। pôthchārīrā moṛe rāstā pār hôlô. The pedestrians crossed the road at the corner.

পথচারী (pôthchārī) is built from পথ, "a way", and -cārin, "moving, going about" (Monier-Williams for both): one who goes on foot along the way.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ঘেঁষা (ghẽshā) — to keep close to; to brush against.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a flyover, then the Bengali for a signboard.

You'll want to know: ঘেঁষা.
ঘেঁষা — ghẽshā — "to keep close to; to brush against".
সাইকেলটা ফুটপাত ঘেঁষে চলল। sāikelṭā phuṭpāt ghẽshe chôllô. The bicycle kept close to the pavement.
Past (he/she …): সেই গাড়ি দেয়াল ঘেঁষল, sei gāṛi deyāl ghẽshlô.
Most often in the -এ form before a verb of motion: দেয়াল ঘেঁষে দাঁড়ানো, to stand close against the wall.

The word, taken apart.
ঘেঁষা (ghẽshā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali ghẽs, "a touch, a rub", under Sanskrit gharṣa, "friction" (CDIAL 4448); to brush past is to touch in passing.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.
