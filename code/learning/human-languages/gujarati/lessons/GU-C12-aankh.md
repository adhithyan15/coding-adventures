# આંખ — an eye, and a nasal that is not what it looks like

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-BAHEN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **બહેન** (*bahen*) closed a chapter about people. This chapter checks on how
someone is — starting with the parts of the body a friend might actually ask
about.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-AANKH]; assesses=[] -->

> **આંખ** — *āṅkh* — **eye**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-ANUSVARA-MEDIAL] -->

**આ** the independent long-*ā*, then the nasal dot **ં** sitting on top of it
— exactly the medial anusvāra you first read inside **વાંચવું**, doing the
same job here, in the middle of a noun rather than a verb — then **ખ**, the
aspirated *k*. **Note what this nasal is NOT**: it does not mark **આંખ** as
neuter — the word is feminine. A nasal dot in the middle of a word is a
**sound**, present or absent as pronunciation happens to need it; it is a
different thing entirely from the grammatical *-ũ* ending you will meet again
soon, and this chapter keeps the two apart on purpose.

## The word, taken apart — the same word as "eye," unbroken
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-ETYMON-AANKH-EYE]; assesses=[] -->

**āṅkh** is inherited from Sauraseni Prakrit *akkhi*, from Sanskrit **akṣi**,
"eye." The root is Indo-European \**h₃ekʷ-*, "eye; to see," and it survives
unbroken in English **eye** itself, in Latin *oculus* (behind **ocular**), and
in Greek *ósse*. This is *bhāī*'s kind of cousin, not *bahen*'s: a straight
line back to the same ancestor, no borrowing and no detour.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-AANKH, GU-ETYMON-AANKH-EYE, GU-SCRIPT-ANUSVARA-MEDIAL] -->
