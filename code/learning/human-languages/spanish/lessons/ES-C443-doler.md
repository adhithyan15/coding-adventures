# doler — the pain does the acting

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C286-BODY-04, ES-LEX-CABEZA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el dolor** and **la cabeza**. You have the noun for pain and
the part of you that has it. Name the verb.

## You'll want to know: doler
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C286-BODY-04] -->

**doler** — to hurt, to ache.

*Me duele la cabeza* — I have a headache. *Me duelen los pies* — my feet hurt.
*¿Le duele aquí?* — does it hurt here? That last one is what a doctor says, and
it is worth having ready before you need it.

## Grammar Lens: it is built like gustar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01, ES-GRAMMAR-GUSTAR-INVERSION-09, ES-LEX-CABEZA-01] -->

Look at *me duele la cabeza* and find the subject. It is not *me*. **La cabeza**
is the subject; the head is what does the hurting, and *me* is the person it
happens to.

That is the same machine as **gustar**, which you have had since early on.

| Spanish | what is the subject |
|---|---|
| me gusta el pan | **el pan** |
| me duele la cabeza | **la cabeza** |

So the verb agrees with the body part, not with you: *me duele la cabeza* but
*me **duelen** los pies*. Two feet, plural verb. This is the single most common
mistake English speakers make with the word, because English puts the person in
charge — *I have a headache* — and Spanish does not.

Nothing new is being learned here. You already owned the pattern; this is a
second verb that runs on it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01, ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-O-UE-06] -->

Latin **dolēre**, to hurt. **el dolor** is its noun and you have had it for a
long time; **doler** is the verb it was cut from.

The stem breaks under stress, exactly as *dormir* and *torcer* do: *doler*,
*dolemos*, but **duele** and **duelen**. Same condition, same result — when the
stress lands on the **o**, the **o** opens into **ue**.

English keeps *dolēre* in **condolence**, hurting *with* somebody, and in
**doleful**.
