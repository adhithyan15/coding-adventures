Gujarati, chapter 37: The Word That Carries a Thought.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કે (ke) — that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One chapter back: the word that sets one clause against another. Two chapters back, at the head of the repair chapter: the word that replaces chhe.
[your turn — recall: paṇ, then nathī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
The joining words so far put two sentences side by side. કે (ke) does something different: it lets one sentence be swallowed by another, as the thing a verb is about.
(verb of thinking or knowing) કે (ke) (whole sentence).
English does the same with that — "I know that it is late" — and, like English, Gujarati often lets it drop in speech. Learn it in place first.
કે (ke) is not a stranger. It is the old k- stem that also starts કેમ (why) — the same handful of sounds does the asking and the reporting, and that stem has more work to do before this run of chapters is finished.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ke, and hear how little it is. It carries an entire sentence.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: two sentences joined by ane, then one swallowed by ke.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: any sentence you can say, with ke in front of it, waiting.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does કે do that અને cannot? (ane sets two sentences side by side; ke puts one inside the other.) What other word does its k- begin? (kem.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
વિચારું છું કે (vichārũ chhũ ke) — I think that…

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back, the swallowing word. And from the verb chapter, long behind you, the verb to think.
[your turn — recall: ke, and vichārvũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The frame, taken apart.
વિચારું છું કે … — vichārũ chhũ ke … — "I think that …"
Three pieces, and two of them are old. વિચારું is the verb with the ending that means I; છું is the am you have had since the second chapter; કે (ke) opens the space where your opinion goes.
Everything you can already say can now be said as an opinion rather than as a fact, by putting it after કે (ke) and nothing else. That is a large change for one small word, and it is the difference between reporting the world and having a view about it.

Why it's said this way.
An A1 speaker who can only state facts sounds like a list. The frame above is what turns a list into a conversation — and it is also how you soften a disagreement, because I think that it is not so lands very differently from it is not so.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the bare sentence, then the same inside the frame.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: an opinion whose inside half is negative.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Give an opinion about anything you can name. Which of the three pieces was new today? (Only the way they combine — કે (ke) came last lesson and the other two are chapters old.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
કે (ke) reaches the page — that, on two signs and no new ones.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — two signs, neither new until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the swallowing word. Then a long reach back, to a word taught many chapters ago and not asked for since: what is શાળા?
[your turn — recall: shāḷā — school]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — two signs, neither new.
ક — ka, ે — the e stroke.
Uncover કે and name both pieces. Like અને last chapter, this word costs the hand nothing it did not already have.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Copy કે once with the model in view. Then write અને beside it from memory and compare the two ે strokes: the same sign, sitting on different letters.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write કે from memory. Which stroke does it share with અને? (The ે above.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
જાણું છું કે (jāṇũ chhũ ke) — I know that…

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back you wrote it. And from the verb chapter: to know.
[your turn — recall: કે (ke), and jāṇvũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The frame, taken apart.
જાણું છું કે … — jāṇũ chhũ ke … — "I know that …"
The same shape as the thinking frame, with a different verb in front. That is the point of કે (ke): it does not care which verb precedes it.
જાણવું and English know are the same ancient word — the gnō- that also gives knowledge, diagnosis and ignore. The kinship has been true since the verb chapter; what is new is that the verb finally has something to take.

Grammar Lens: and the half you need more.
Negate it with the word from two chapters back and you get the sentence a traveller actually uses:
જાણતો નથી — jāṇto nathī — "I don't know".
As with samajto nathī, the -તો is masculine and -તી feminine: a woman says jāṇtī nathī. Said alone, without any કે (ke) after it, this is a complete and very common reply.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: jāṇũ chhũ ke … with any sentence after it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jāṇto nathī — or jāṇtī nathī — flat, as a whole answer.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: samajto nathī and jāṇto nathī. Two different admissions.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say I don't know. Then say I don't understand. What is the difference you are drawing? (Not having the fact, against not having followed.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
The reporting frames, all together — the reporting frames, scored as one performance.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
No new words. One run through what કે (ke) made possible.

What you've built.
કે (ke) — the word that puts a whole sentence inside another.
વિચારું છું કે (vichārũ chhũ ke) … — an opinion rather than a fact.
જાણું છું કે (jāṇũ chhũ ke) … — and, negated, jāṇto nathī, "I don't know".
કે (ke) on the page, on two signs that were already yours.
Two chapters ago you could not say and. You can now hand an entire sentence to a verb.
