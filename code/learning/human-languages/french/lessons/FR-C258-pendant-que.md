# quand, pendant que — the scene, and what cut into it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-LEX-QUAND-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was sleeping", then "I slept". (*Je dormais*, then *j'ai
dormi.*)

[PAUSE 1s] This lesson joins a scene and an event into one sentence.

## Grammar Lens: quand, where the event comes in
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-QUAND-COUPE-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-LIRE-06, FR-LEX-C64-VERBES16-03, FR-LEX-C137-VERBES89-01] -->

You have **quand**, "when". It puts a scene and an event into one sentence:

> *Je lisais **quand** ma mère a téléphoné.*
> — I was reading when my mother phoned.
> *J'attendais le train **quand** il a commencé à pleuvoir.*
> — I was waiting for the train when it started to rain.

The first half is the scene, still running, in the imparfait. The *quand*
half is the event that cut into it, in the passé composé. Turn the sentence
round and the tenses keep their jobs: *Quand ma mère a téléphoné, je lisais.*

A *quand* can hold a scene too: *Quand j'avais vingt ans, j'habitais à
Paris.* A stretch of life is not an event, so both halves are scene.

## Grammar Lens: pendant que, two things at once
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-PENDANT-QUE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-FRERE-06, FR-LEX-C99-VILLE51-04, FR-LEX-DORMIR-04] -->

**pendant que** means "while". It holds a scene open so that something else
can happen inside it:

> ***Pendant que** je lisais, mon frère dormait.* — While I was reading, my
> brother was asleep.
> ***Pendant que** j'attendais le train, j'ai téléphoné à ma mère.* — While I
> was waiting for the train, I phoned my mother.

Two scenes side by side, then one event inside a scene.

French has one more way to hold a moment still: ***être en train de***, "to
be in the middle of". *J'étais en train de lire quand ma mère a téléphoné.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**pendant** comes from **pendre**, "to hang", which is inherited from Latin
*pendēre*, "to hang, to be suspended" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under
*pendeo*). *Pendant que je lisais* is, at root, "while my reading was still
hanging": open, not yet settled. English **pending** has the same picture.

**quand** is inherited from Latin *quandō*, "when" (EtymDB 2.1), and the *d*
it kept in spelling is the Latin one.
