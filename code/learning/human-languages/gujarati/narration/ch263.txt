Gujarati, chapter 263: Aunts and in-laws.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ભાભી (bhābhī) — sister-in-law, brother's wife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for picnic, then the Gujarati for to dive.

You'll want to know: ભાભી.
ભાભી — bhābhī — "sister-in-law, brother's wife".
શું તમારી ભાભી અહીં રહે છે? — śũ tamārī bhābhī ahī̃ rahe che? — "Does your sister-in-law live here?"

The word, taken apart.
The origin of ભાભી (bhābhī) is not settled. CDIAL lists the older Gujarati bhojāī, "brother's wife", under Sanskrit bhrāturjāyā (CDIAL 9660), but not bhābhī.

Lesson 2 of 5.
માસી (māsī) — maternal aunt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to dive, then the Gujarati for sister-in-law.

You'll want to know: માસી.
માસી — māsī — "maternal aunt".
મારી માસી શહેરમાં નથી રહેતી. — mārī māsī śahermā̃ nathī rahetī — "My aunt does not live in the city."
Your mother's sister.

The word, taken apart.
માસી (māsī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mātuḥṣvasṛ, "mother's sister" (CDIAL 10001).

ભત્રીજો (bhatrījo) — nephew, brother's son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for paternal aunt, then the Gujarati for mother-in-law.

You'll want to know: ભત્રીજો.
ભત્રીજો — bhatrījo — "nephew, brother's son".
શું તમારો ભત્રીજો શાળાએ જાય છે? — śũ tamāro bhatrījo śāḷāe jāy che? — "Does your nephew go to school?"

The word, taken apart.
ભત્રીજો (bhatrījo) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit bhrātrī́ya, "brother's son", built on bhrā́tṛ, "brother" (CDIAL 9672).
