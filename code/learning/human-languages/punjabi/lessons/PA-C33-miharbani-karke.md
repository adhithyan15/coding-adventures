# ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ ਕਰਕੇ — a god's name inside the word for "please"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-PHRASE-KIRPA-KARKE, PA-LEX-PAANI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask for water the way the drinks chapter taught, with *kirpā karke* in front
of it. That courtesy is Sanskritic. Punjabi has a second one, heard at least as
often, and it comes down the other road.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-MIHARBANI]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ** — *miharbānī* — **kindness, a favour**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-MA-01, PA-SCRIPT-SIHARI-01, PA-SCRIPT-HA-01, PA-SCRIPT-RA-01, PA-SCRIPT-BA-01, PA-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01] -->

**ਮ**, **ਿ**, **ਹ**, **ਰ**, **ਬ**, **ਾ**, **ਨ**, **ੀ**: **ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ**. Then
**ਕਰਕੇ**. Every shape is already yours, so this phrase is writable on sight.

## The word, taken apart — kindness, a keeper, and Mithra
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-MIHARBANI-MITHRA]; assesses=[PA-LEX-MIHARBANI, PA-GRAMMAR-NOUN-PLUS-KARNA, PA-LEX-DOST] -->

**ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ** is Persian *mihrbānī*, and it comes apart in three.

*-ī* is the quality-making ending, the one on *khushī*, met when you were pleased to meet someone. *-bān*
means **keeper**, the piece that turns *bāġ*, "garden," into *bāġbān*,
"gardener." And *mihr* means **love, affection**.

*Mihr* is the piece worth stopping on. It descends from Avestan *Mithra*, an
Indo-Iranian divinity who guaranteed covenants, whose name in the other branch
of the family is Sanskrit *mitra*, "friend" — the figure Roman
soldiers carried west as **Mithras**. Punjabi's commonest please carries, three
layers down, the name of the god who made promises binding.

You have met **ਦੋਸਤ**, a Persian word for friend. *Mitra* is what Sanskrit
called a friend, and both continue that same ancient name.

**ਕਰਕੇ** is "having done," from **ਕਰਨਾ**. So the phrase means **having done a
kindness** — exactly what the courtesy in front of *pāṇī* meant, with a Persian noun where
that one had a Sanskrit one.

## Writing: assemble ਚੰਗਾ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-CHANGA-01]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-CHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-GA-01, PA-SCRIPT-TIPPI-WRITING-01, PA-LEX-CHANGA, PA-SCRIPT-ACHCHHA-01, PA-SCRIPT-CHHA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Both letters that *changā* was waiting for have now been traced. Build it in
four moves: **ਚ**, the tippi **ੰ** above it, **ਗ**, and the upright **ਾ**.
Written out: **ਚੰਗਾ**.

Copy it once with the model visible. Cover the model, write it again, and
compare only whether the tippi sits above the first letter and not the second.

Then write **ਅੱਛਾ** once beside it, and look at what the two words share: a
first letter that hangs a mark above the line.
