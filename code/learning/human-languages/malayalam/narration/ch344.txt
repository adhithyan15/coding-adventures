Malayalam, chapter 344: Tickets, Seats and Trouble.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
രണ്ട് ടിക്കറ്റ് വേണം (raṇṭŭ ṭikkaṟṟŭ vēṇaṁ) — two tickets, please.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket", "a seat", and "at what time?" (ṭikkaṟṟŭ, sīṟṟŭ, etra maṇikkŭ.)

Grammar Lens: at the counter.
Name the place with -ലേക്ക്, "to", then how many, then വേണം:
കൊച്ചിയിലേക്ക് രണ്ട് ടിക്കറ്റ് വേണം. kocciyilēkkŭ raṇṭŭ ṭikkaṟṟŭ vēṇaṁ. — "Two tickets to Kochi, please."
For the way back, use മടങ്ങുക, "to return", in its -ആൻ form, with -ഉം, "too". At a counter you will also hear the English return ticket.
മടങ്ങാനും ടിക്കറ്റ് വേണം. maṭaṅṅānuṁ ṭikkaṟṟŭ vēṇaṁ. — "I need a ticket to come back too."
Then the price: എത്രയാണ്? (etrayāṇŭ?), "how much is it?"

Grammar Lens: times and seats.
ട്രെയിൻ എത്ര മണിക്ക് പുറപ്പെടും? ṭreyin etra maṇikkŭ puṟappeṭuṁ? — "What time does the train leave?"
പുറപ്പെടുക is "to set out"; the future പുറപ്പെടും asks about the timetable. So എപ്പോൾ എത്തും? (eppōḷ ettuṁ?), "when will it arrive?", and സീറ്റ് ഉണ്ടോ? (sīṟṟŭ uṇṭō?), "is there a seat?"

"To Kochi"? (കൊച്ചിയിലേക്ക്, kocciyilēkkŭ, with -ലേക്ക്.) "A ticket to come back too"? (maṭaṅṅānuṁ ṭikkaṟṟŭ vēṇaṁ.) "What time does the train leave?" (ṭreyin etra maṇikkŭ puṟappeṭuṁ?)


Lesson 2 of 5.
സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ) — convenience, facilities.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "two tickets to Kochi, please", then "what time does the train leave?"

You'll want to know: സൗകര്യം.
സൗകര്യം — saukaryaṁ — "convenience", and in the plural "facilities".
സ്റ്റേഷനിൽ നല്ല സൗകര്യങ്ങൾ ഉണ്ട്. sṟṟēṣanil nalla saukaryaṅṅaḷ uṇṭŭ. — "The station has good facilities."
With the dative it says what suits you:
നാളെ എനിക്ക് സൗകര്യമാണ്. nāḷe enikkŭ saukaryamāṇŭ. — "Tomorrow suits me."
നിങ്ങളുടെ സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ) പോലെ, "as suits you", is how a polite Malayali leaves the choice to you.
Its first syllable carries a vowel sign you have not written alone. The next lesson takes it out.

The word, taken apart.
സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ) from Sanskrit saukarya, "ease, facility", a tatsama with Malayalam's noun ending. Monier-Williams derives saukarya from su-kara, "easy to do": su-, "well, easily", and kara, "doing".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: saukaryaṁ, with au as in "cow"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The station has good facilities."]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Tomorrow suits me", then "as suits you"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ) mean? (Convenience; facilities.) "Tomorrow suits me"? (nāḷe enikkŭ saukaryamāṇŭ.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write െ and ൈ once each, and name them. (The e sign and the ai sign, both printed before their consonant.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ), "convenience". This lesson takes the sign in its first syllable and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ൗ.
ൗ — the vowel sign au. It is printed after its consonant: സ + ൗ becomes സൗ, sau.
Its formal name is the au length mark. Older books also put െ in front of the consonant for the same au, so you may meet both in print; they are read alike. The mark alone is the modern spelling.
You have written this shape already, as the tail of ഈ and ഔ: a small lobe, a notch, and a broader lobe sweeping back along its foot. Here it stands on its own.

Writing: ൗ.
ൗ (au) is the tail on its own: a small lobe on the left, a notch, and a broader lobe on the right whose foot sweeps back to the left. Write it after its consonant, the same way you wrote it on ഈ and ഔ.
Write ൗ (au) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Copy the printed shape.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — find: ൗ (au) inside സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ)]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ൗ three times, saying au as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: സൗ, then കൗ, then സൗകര്യം (saukaryaṁ)]

Lesson 4 of 5.
എനിക്ക് ബസ് കിട്ടിയില്ല (enikkŭ basŭ kiṭṭiyilla) — I missed the bus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to get", "to lose", and "it's late". (kiṭṭuka, naṣṭappeṭuka, vaiki.) Then the cry when something goes wrong. (ayyō!)

Grammar Lens: it did not come to me.
Malayalam usually turns "I missed the bus" round: the bus did not come to you, and you stand in the dative:
എനിക്ക് ബസ് കിട്ടിയില്ല. enikkŭ basŭ kiṭṭiyilla. — "I missed the bus."
Or the bus went: ബസ് പോയി (basŭ pōyi), "the bus has gone". Delays and cancellations use verbs you know:
ട്രെയിൻ ഒരു മണിക്കൂർ വൈകി. ṭreyin oru maṇikkūr vaiki. — "The train was an hour late."
എന്റെ ടിക്കറ്റ് റദ്ദാക്കി (enṟe ṭikkaṟṟŭ ṟaddākki), "my ticket was cancelled": no one is named, as in English.

Grammar Lens: lost and broken, and help.
അയ്യോ, എന്റെ ബാഗ് നഷ്ടപ്പെട്ടുപോയി. ayyō, enṟe bāgŭ naṣṭappeṭṭupōyi. — "Oh no, my bag has got lost."
പോയി after a past form says it went and is gone, and you are sorry about it: ഫോൺ കേടായിപ്പോയി, "the phone has broken down".
To ask for help, take the -ആം of വിളിക്കാം, "I'll call", and add -ഓ: "could you …?"
ക്ഷമിക്കണം, എന്നെ സഹായിക്കാമോ? kṣamikkaṇaṁ, enne sahāyikkāmō? — "Excuse me, could you help me?"
