Persian, chapter 34: Neighbour, Guest, University student, Gentleman, Lady.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
همسایه (hamsâye) — a neighbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a child, then the Persian for a family.

You'll want to know: همسایه.
همسایه — hamsâye — "a neighbour".

The word, taken apart.
hamsâye is built from ham-, "same, together", and sâye, "shade": the people who share your shade. Both parts are native Persian; sâye is usually traced to Middle Persian sāyag.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: دانشجو.
دانشجو — dâneshju — "a university student".

The word, taken apart.
dâneshju is built from dânesh, "knowledge" (from dânestan, "to know"), and -ju, "seeking" (from jostan, "to seek"): "knowledge-seeker". Both pieces are native Persian verbs, inherited from Middle Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
آقا (âghâ) — a gentleman; Mr.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a guest, then the Persian for a university student.

You'll want to know: آقا.
آقا — âghâ — "a gentleman; Mr".

The word, taken apart.
âghâ is a loan, usually traced to Turkic aqa, "elder brother". It became a title of respect, and Persian uses it as "Mr".
