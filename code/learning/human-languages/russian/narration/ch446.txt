Russian, chapter 446: Shop signs and windows.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
сквер (skver) — a small public garden, a square.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a kiosk, then the Russian for a flower bed.

You'll want to know: сквер.
сквер (skver) — "a small public garden, a square".
Возле театра есть маленький сквер. Vózle teátra yest málenkiy skver. Near the theatre there is a small public garden.
Masculine. A small green square with benches.

The word, taken apart.
сквер (skver) was borrowed from English square (EtymDB); in Russian it names a small garden on a square.

Lesson 2 of 5.
вывеска (výveska) — a shop sign, a signboard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a flower bed, then the Russian for a small public garden.

You'll want to know: вывеска.
вывеска (výveska) — "a shop sign, a signboard".
Над дверью висит новая вывеска. Nad dvéryu visít nóvaya výveska. A new sign hangs over the door.
Feminine; the stress is on вы- (vý-).

The word, taken apart.
вывеска (výveska) is built on вывесить 'to hang out', from вы- 'out' and весить, вешать 'to hang': a sign hung outside a shop. EtymDB does not list the word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
витрина (vitrína) — a shop window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a small public garden, then the Russian for a shop sign.

You'll want to know: витрина.
витрина (vitrína) — "a shop window".
В витрине стоят новые туфли. V vitríne stoyát nóvyye túfli. There are new shoes in the shop window.
Feminine. Also a glass display case in a museum.

The word, taken apart.
витрина (vitrína) is usually traced to French vitrine 'a shop window, a display case', which EtymDB builds on vitre 'a pane of glass'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
водосток (vodostók) — a drainpipe, a gutter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a shop sign, then the Russian for a shop window.

You'll want to know: водосток.
водосток (vodostók) — "a drainpipe, a gutter".
Во время дождя из водостока бежит вода. Vo vrémya dozhdyá iz vodostóka bezhít vodá. During the rain water runs out of the drainpipe.
Masculine. Водосточная труба — a drainpipe.

The word, taken apart.
водосток (vodostók) is built from вода 'water' and сток 'a draining off', on the root of течь 'to flow' (EtymDB lists водосток (vodostók)).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ларёк (laryók) — a stall, a booth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a shop window, then the Russian for a drainpipe.

You'll want to know: ларёк.
ларёк (laryók) — "a stall, a booth".
В ларьке у остановки продают пирожки. V larké u ostanóvki prodayút pirozhkí. The stall by the bus stop sells pies.
Masculine; the ё drops out: ларька, ларьки.

The word, taken apart.
ларёк (laryók) is a diminutive of ларь 'a chest, a bin', which came through Old East Slavic larĭ from Old Norse lárr 'a chest, a drawer' (EtymDB).
