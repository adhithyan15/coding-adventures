German, chapter 436: Kleine und große Straßen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Gasse — the lane, the alley.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to turn back, then the German for to get lost driving.

You'll want to know: die Gasse.
die Gasse — "the lane, the alley".
Die Gassen im alten Teil der Stadt sind eng. The lanes in the old part of town are narrow.
Plural: die Gassen. In Austria, die Gasse is an ordinary word for a street.

The word, taken apart.
Gasse is inherited from Old High German gazza and Proto-Germanic gatwōn (EtymDB). Old Norse gata 'road' is usually counted as a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Sackgasse — the dead end, the cul-de-sac.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: die Sackgasse.
die Sackgasse — "the dead end, the cul-de-sac".
Die Straße ist eine Sackgasse, wir müssen zurückfahren. The street is a dead end; we have to drive back.
Plural: die Sackgassen. Also figurative: a situation with no way out.

The word, taken apart.
Sackgasse is built from Sack 'sack', which goes back to Latin saccus (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), and Gasse 'lane': a lane closed at one end like a bag.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Einbahnstraße — the one-way street.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the dead end, then the German for the avenue.

You'll want to know: die Einbahnstraße.
die Einbahnstraße — "the one-way street".
In der Einbahnstraße fahren alle Autos in eine Richtung. In the one-way street all the cars go in one direction.
Plural: die Einbahnstraßen.

The word, taken apart.
Einbahnstraße is built from ein 'one', Bahn 'track, lane' and Straße 'street', which goes back to Latin (via) strāta 'paved road' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Passant — the passer-by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the avenue, then the German for the one-way street.

You'll want to know: der Passant.
der Passant — "the passer-by".
Ein Passant zeigt uns den Weg zum Museum. A passer-by shows us the way to the museum.
Weak noun: den Passanten. A woman: die Passantin.

The word, taken apart.
Passant was borrowed from French passant 'passing (by)' (EtymDB), from passer 'to pass', which goes back to Latin passus 'step'.
