# el carrito — a small cart, and a very large ending

## You'll want to know: el carrito
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SUPERMERCADO] -->

**el carrito** — the trolley, the cart.

*El carrito de la compra* — the shopping trolley. *Un carrito de bebé* — a
pushchair. On a website, *añadir al carrito* is **add to basket**, and that is
where you will meet the word most often.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-05] -->

**carro** plus **-ito**.

*El carro* is a cart, from Latin **carrus**, a wagon — a word Latin itself took
from Gaulish, and the same one behind English **car**, **carry** and **cargo**.
Add **-ito** and you have a small one.

## Grammar Lens: the ending that does more than shrink
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-05, ES-LEX-C363-WHERE-41] -->

**-ito** and **-ita** attach to almost any noun, and size is only its first job.

| word | with -ito | what it adds |
|---|---|---|
| un momento | un moment**ito** | *a moment* → *one moment, please* |
| ahora | ahor**ita** | now → right now, or soon |

*Momentito* is not a shorter moment. The ending softens the request, the way
English softens one by adding *a little* or *if you would*. Spanish speakers use it constantly for that,
not for measuring.

Two things to watch. **Spelling follows sound**: *poco* becomes *poquito*,
because *c* before *i* would go soft and the word must keep its **k**. And the
ending has a cousin, **-illo**, which shrinks less kindly — *un librillo* is
faintly dismissive where *un librito* is affectionate.

So when a Spanish speaker adds *-ito* to something that is not small, they are
usually being warm rather than inaccurate.
