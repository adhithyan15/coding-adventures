# One possessor or several?

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-MAMA, SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01, SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give the three forms you can now retrieve directly: “my”, “our”, and
“your, of you all”. (*Mama; asmākam; yuṣmākam.*)

## Grammar Lens: the choice

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C259-PLURAL-POSSESSORS-03]; assesses=[] -->

Choose by counting the possessors or addressees:

- one possessor speaking: **मम** (*mama*);
- several possessors speaking: **अस्माकम्** (*asmākam*);
- one familiar addressee: **तव** (*tava*);
- several addressees: **युष्माकम्** (*yuṣmākam*).

All four answer “whose?” In this paradigm they are fixed genitive forms, **not
possessive adjectives** that you inflect to agree with the thing possessed.
Whitney §493 records the older adjectival history of the two plural forms; that
history does not change the learner's choice here.

## Guided Practice — choose before you speak

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-MAMA, SA-PATTERN-WHATS-YOUR-NAME, SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01, SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02, SA-LEX-C16-PLACE-01, SA-LEX-KUTUMBAM-FAMILY, SA-GRAMMAR-C259-PLURAL-POSSESSORS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s each]
- [YOU SAY: one speaker, one family: “my family” — *mama kuṭumbam*]
- [YOU SAY: several speakers, one family: “our family” — *asmākam kuṭumbam*]
- [YOU SAY: to one familiar person: “your house” — *tava gṛham*]
- [YOU SAY: to several people: “your house” — *yuṣmākam gṛham*]
- [YOU SAY: the rule — choose by whether the possessor or addressee is one or several]

## Wrap-up Recall

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C259-ASMAKAM-01, SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C259-PLURAL-POSSESSORS-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Without looking back, give “our family” and “your house” when you
address several people. Then say why the first word changes. (*Asmākam
kuṭumbam; yuṣmākam gṛham; the possessor or addressee is several, not one.*)
