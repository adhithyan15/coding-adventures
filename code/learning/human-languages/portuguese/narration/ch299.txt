Portuguese, chapter 299: Um acidente na estrada.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o acidente — the accident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the inmate, then the Portuguese for to set free.

You'll want to know: o acidente.
o acidente — "the accident".
Houve um acidente na autoestrada. There was an accident on the motorway.
Ter um acidente — to have an accident; por acidente — by chance.

The word, taken apart.
acidente is a learned borrowing from Latin accidēns "happening, chance" (EtymDB), from accidere "to fall upon, to happen", built on cadere "to fall".

Lesson 3 of 5.
chocar — to crash, to run into; to shock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the accident, then the Portuguese for the crash.

You'll want to know: chocar.
chocar — "to crash, to run into; to shock".
O autocarro chocou contra um muro. The bus crashed into a wall.
Chocar com or chocar contra. Also ‘to shock’: a notícia chocou o país.

The word, taken apart.
chocar is built on choque "an impact" (EtymDB), from French choc, with the verb ending -ar.

Lesson 4 of 5.
colidir — to collide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the crash, then the Portuguese for to crash.

You'll want to know: colidir.
colidir — "to collide".
Dois camiões colidiram na ponte. Two lorries collided on the bridge.
Formal, a news word; in speech, bater or chocar.

The word, taken apart.
colidir is a learned borrowing from Latin collīdere "to strike together", built on laedere "to strike, to hurt" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o sinistro — the mishap; an accident (in insurance language).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to crash, then the Portuguese for to collide.

You'll want to know: o sinistro.
o sinistro — "the mishap; an accident (in insurance language)".
O seguro pagou o sinistro. The insurance paid for the accident.
An insurance and news word. As an adjective, sinistro means ‘sinister’.

The word, taken apart.
sinistro was borrowed from Latin sinister "on the left; unlucky" (EtymDB): the left side was the side of bad omens.
