Hindi, chapter 463: Calm Again.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सुकून (sukūn) — peace of mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to strut, then the Hindi for fussiness.

You'll want to know: सुकून.
सुकून — sukūn — "peace of mind".
गाँव की शांत शाम में बहुत सुकून है। gā̃v kī śānt śām meṁ bahut sukūn hai. There is a great peace in a quiet village evening.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सुकून (sukūn) is usually traced to Arabic sukūn, "stillness, rest", which reached Hindi through Persian.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for contentment, then the Hindi for reassurance.

You'll want to know: अघाना.
अघाना — aghānā — "to eat one's fill; to be content".
दादी के हाथ का खाना खाकर बच्चे अघा गए। dādī ke hāth kā khānā khākar bacce aghā gae. After my grandmother's home cooking, the children were full and content.
Past (he …): वह खाकर अघा गया, vah khākar aghā gayā.

The word, taken apart.
अघाना (aghānā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi aghānā, "to be full; satiated", under Sanskrit āghrāpayati (CDIAL 1062). One is aghā when hunger, or a wish, is fully met.

Lesson 5 of 5.
सांत्वना (sāntvanā) — consolation; words of comfort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for reassurance, then the Hindi for to eat one's fill.

You'll want to know: सांत्वना.
सांत्वना — sāntvanā — "consolation; words of comfort".
दोस्तों की सांत्वना से मीरा का मन शांत हुआ। dostoṁ kī sāntvanā se Mīrā kā man śānt huā. Her friends' words of comfort calmed Mira's mind.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
सांत्वना (sāntvanā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit sāntvanā, which Monier-Williams explains as calming someone with gentle words, or consolation (Monier-Williams). It is what one offers a person in grief: sāntvanā denā.
