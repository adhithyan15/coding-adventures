Arabic, chapter 535: Companions and quarrels.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
رفيق (rafīq) — a companion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for esteem, then the Arabic for to pardon.

You'll want to know: رفيق.
رفيق — rafīq — "a companion".
كان الكلب رفيق جدي في الجبل. kāna al-kalb rafīq jaddī fī al-jabal. The dog was my grandfather's companion in the mountains.
Plural: رفاق, rifāq.

The word, taken apart.
رفيق (rafīq) is built on the native Arabic root r-f-q, "to be gentle; to be a companion" (Lane). A rafīq goes along with you.

Lesson 2 of 6.
عدو (ʿaduww) — an enemy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to pardon, then the Arabic for a companion.

You'll want to know: عدو.
عدو — ʿaduww — "an enemy".
القط والفأر عدوان في القصص. al-qiṭṭ wa-l-faʾr ʿaduwwān fī al-qiṣaṣ. The cat and the mouse are enemies in stories.
Plural: أعداء, aʿdāʾ.

The word, taken apart.
عدو (ʿaduww) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-d-w, "to run, to pass beyond" (Lane). An ʿaduww is one who runs against you, who goes beyond what is right.

Lesson 5 of 6.
هجر (hajara) — to abandon, to leave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for betrayal, then the Arabic for to fall out.

You'll want to know: هجر.
هجر — hajara — "to abandon, to leave".
هجر الشاب قريته وسكن في المدينة. hajara ash-shābb qaryatahu wa-sakana fī al-madīna. The young man left his village and lived in the city.
Present: يهجر, yahjur.

The word, taken apart.
هجر (hajara) is built on the native Arabic root h-j-r, "to cut off, to forsake" (Lane). Lane gives hajara, aor. yahjuru, as to cut oneself off from someone.
