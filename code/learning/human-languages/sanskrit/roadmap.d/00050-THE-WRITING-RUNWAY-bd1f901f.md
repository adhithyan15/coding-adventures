## The writing runway

Recognition and a copied isolated shape are not the end of the ladder. The
first cumulative stage on **न** proves observe/trace, guided copy, delayed copy,
and dictation. Chapter 3 now reuses only already-known meaning and already-taught
shapes to widen that evidence without a jump:

- **मम** moves from a visible two-letter copy to delayed recall and then
  sound-to-word dictation;
- **मम नाम** repeats the same withdrawal of support for a two-word phrase and
  makes the long-ā sign and the word boundary explicit.
- **◌ः → नमः** now gives Sanskrit's first distinctive mark the same
  cumulative treatment. The existing visarga lesson supplies observe/trace;
  three short lessons then move the already-known *namas* “a bow” through a
  visible copy, a delayed copy, and sound-and-meaning dictation. The written
  answer must be Devanagari: romanization remains a prompt, never credit.

The next writing tranche must earn a full introduction only after every shape
in it has been taught. Later tranches still owe vocalic ṛ, conjuncts,
connected text, and independent recall; this word-and-mark bridge does not
pretend that one pre-A1 stage marker completed those jobs.
