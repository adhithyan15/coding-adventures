# uno... otro — splitting a pair

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-UNUS, ES-GRAMMAR-INDEFINITE-PLURAL, ES-GRAMMAR-INDEFINITE-PLURAL-AGAINST-BARE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Un libro* — one book. *Unos libros* — some books.

[PAUSE 1s] Now: two books, and you want to say something different about each.

## The word, taken apart: otro
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-OTRO, ES-ETYMON-OTRO-ALTER]; assesses=[ES-LEX-LIBRO] -->

***Otro*** — *the other*. Latin ***alter***, which did not mean *any other*: it
meant **the other of two**, the second of a pair.

[PAUSE 2s] English keeps that sense in *alternative* and *alternate* — one, then
the other, back and forth.

[PAUSE 2s] And it takes no article. *Otro libro*, never *un otro libro*.

## Grammar Lens: one at each end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DISTRIBUTIVE-UNO-OTRO]; assesses=[ES-LEX-OTRO] -->

> ***Uno*** *es grande,* ***otro*** *es bueno.* — One is big, the other is
> good.

[PAUSE 2s] The pattern splits a pair across the two halves of the sentence:
*uno* takes the first, *otro* takes the second. Neither needs a noun after it,
because the noun was already named or is obvious.

[PAUSE 2s] It agrees, like everything else: *una... otra* for two feminine
things, *unos... otros* for two groups.
