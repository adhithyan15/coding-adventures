Lesson 1 of 8.
找回 (zhǎohuí) — to get back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A new chapter, and it opens on the happy end of a bad day.
[your turn — recall: say 我们坐八点的火车 — wǒmen zuò bā diǎn de huǒchē — R1, the chapter you have finished]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 找 — zhǎo, to look for — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 找到 — zhǎodào, to find — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 回 — huí, to go back — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 找回.
找回 — zhǎohuí — "to get back", something you had lost.
我找回了我的手机 wǒ zhǎohuí le wǒ de shǒujī — I got my phone back.
Both characters are ones you write.

The word, taken apart.
找回 (zhǎohuí) is built from 找 'to look for' and 回 'to go back'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese hwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to turn round'; the dictionary has no entry for 找, so the history of that part is not settled.

What you've built.
找 is to look for, 找到 is to find, and 找回 (zhǎohuí) is to find what you had lost and have it again.
了 after it says it is done: 我找回了我的手机.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 找, then 找到, then 找回 (zhǎohuí)]

Lesson 2 of 8.
我的手机不见了 (wǒ de shǒujī bú jiàn le) — it's gone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word in it is one you have; together they report a problem.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 找回, aloud — zhǎohuí]
[your turn — recall: say diū, to lose — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 手机 — shǒujī, a phone — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: …不见了, it has gone missing.
Name the thing, then say 不见了: it has gone missing.
我的手机不见了 wǒ de shǒujī bú jiàn le — My phone's gone missing. 我的车票不见了 你看到了吗 wǒ de chēpiào bú jiàn le, nǐ kàndào le ma — My ticket's disappeared. Have you seen it?
不见 is 'not seen', and 了 says that is new: it was there, and now it is not. 不 before the fall of jiàn is bú.
diū le says you lost it yourself: wǒ de qiánbāo diū le. huài le says it broke. qǐng bāng wǒ, please help me, asks for help.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: zhèngjiàn.
zhèngjiàn — "papers; an identity document".
wǒ de zhèngjiàn bú jiàn le — My papers have gone missing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhèngjiàn is built from zhèng 'proof' and jiàn 'an item', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyingH and gjenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, under its traditional form, 'to verify' and the second 'a portion'.

What you've built.
hùzhào and shēnfènzhèng are both zhèngjiàn: it is the word for any of them, the one a desk asks to see.

Lesson 4 of 8.
guàshī — to report a loss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: last lesson's word, and two words that share its sounds.
[your turn — recall: say zhèngjiàn, papers — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say guà, to hang — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say shīwàng, disappointed — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: guàshī.
guàshī — "to report the loss of" a card or a document.
wǒ yào guàshī wǒ de xìnyòngkǎ — I need to report my credit card lost.
wǒ děi qù guàshī — I have to go and report it lost.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guàshī is built from guà 'to hang' and shī 'to lose', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kweaH and syit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hang' and the second 'to let slip'. 'To report a loss' for the pair is its own sense, which neither gloss gives.

What you've built.
guàshī is what you do at a bank or a police desk once a card or a passport is gone, so nobody else can use it.
The thing follows it: guàshī wǒ de xìnyòngkǎ.

[your turn — recall: say guàshī, to report a loss — R1, one lesson back]

You'll want to know: liánxì.
liánxì — "to get in touch with; to contact".
wǒ zěnme liánxì nǐ — How can I get in touch with you?
qǐng gēn wǒ liánxì — Please get in touch with me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liánxì is built from lián 'to join' and xì 'to tie'. The Guangyun has no entry for the first under the traditional forms we look it up by, so the history of that part is not settled; the second is inherited from Middle Chinese kejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'joined together' under one traditional form and 'to tie up' under another.

What you've built.
打电话 is one way to liánxì; a message is another. The person you reach follows it, or comes before it with gēn: qǐng gēn wǒ liánxì.

Lesson 6 of 8.
找不到 (zhǎo bu dào) — can't find it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Verbs with a result after them, which you have; and a way to say the result is out of reach.
[your turn — recall: say liánxì, to get in touch — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 找到 — zhǎodào, to find — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say tīngdǒng, to understand what you hear — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 看到 — kàndào, to see — R3]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 找不到 and 找得到, can't and can.
不 between a verb and its result says you can't manage it; 得 there says you can.
我找不到我的手机 wǒ zhǎo bu dào wǒ de shǒujī — I can't find my phone. wǒ tīng bu dǒng — I can't understand what's being said.
找到, 看到, tīngdǒng: you know verbs with a result after them. Put 不 in the middle, said light, and the result is out of reach: 找不到 (zhǎo bu dào). Put 得 there, also light, and it is within reach: 你找得到吗 nǐ zhǎo de dào ma?
Keep to the results dào, dǒng and wán for now: zuò bu wán, more than you can finish.

Lesson 7 of 8.
A note to the desk, four lines that lean on each other — four connected lines to a hotel desk about something lost: what, when, what you need, how to reach you.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You are staying at a hotel, and something of yours has gone missing. The front desk, qiántái, can help, if you tell them clearly.
[pause 1 second]
This lesson writes to them.
[your turn — recall: say liánxì, to get in touch — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say qiántái, the front desk — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A note is connected when each line leans on the one before. Four short lines can hang together; four long ones can fall apart.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three things do the leaning, and you own all three. The time words lead: 早上, 回来的时候, 明天. The subject is said once: 我 in the first line, and the lines after it start on the time or the verb. Each line answers the question the last one raised: gone, when, what now, how to reach you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Your reader is the desk. Your purpose is to get the thing back.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four lines to the front desk. No name at the top: begin as you would at the desk.
Line 1 says what is gone, with 不见了.
Line 2 says when you last had it, with 的时候.
Line 3 says what you need, with 请, or what you think you should do, with 我应该.
Line 4 says how to reach you: liánxì, and a time or a room number.
[pause 2 seconds]
A word whose characters you have not written goes in pinyin, inside the line, with its tone marks: liánxì, qiántái, hùzhào. Every character comes from the ones you have learned, each in the order you learned it, each in the same size of square.
[pause 2 seconds]
No Chinese commas or full stops: leave a space between clauses, as this book prints them.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the note. Then read it once as the desk would, top to bottom: could they act on it?

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read your second line on its own. Does it stand up without the first? (It should not quite: it says when, and the first says what.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Count the lines that start with 我. If every one does, you wrote a list, not a note.
[once you have stopped driving — question: Write the words for 'when I came back', in characters.]


Lesson 8 of 8.
我的车票不见了 (wǒ de chēpiào bú jiàn le) — a traveller reports a lost ticket and papers, and is told what to do.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The chapter closes here, on a train, with two things missing.
[your turn — recall: say guàshī, to report a loss — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say zhèngjiàn, papers — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 我找回了我的手机 — wǒ zhǎohuí le wǒ de shǒujī — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange.
A: 我的车票不见了 wǒ de chēpiào bú jiàn le — My ticket's gone. B: 你找了吗 nǐ zhǎo le ma — Have you looked for it? A: 找了 我找不到 zhǎo le, wǒ zhǎo bu dào — I have. I can't find it. B: 你上车的时候有吗 nǐ shàngchē de shíhou yǒu ma — Did you have it when you got on? A: 有 我坐在这儿的时候还有 yǒu, wǒ zuò zài zhèr de shíhou hái yǒu — Yes. I still had it when I sat down here. B: nǐ yīnggāi qù qiántái — You should go to the desk. A: wǒ de zhèngjiàn yě bú jiàn le — My papers are gone too. B: nà nǐ děi mǎshàng guàshī — Then you'll have to report them lost straight away.
A answers with the verb alone, 找了, and B leads with the moment, 你上车的时候. 还有 says A still had it then.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
When did A still have the ticket?

What you've built — when something goes missing.
What to say, from the bad news to the end of it:
我的车票不见了 wǒ de chēpiào bú jiàn le — it's gone.
我找不到 wǒ zhǎo bu dào — I can't find it.
guàshī, liánxì — report it, and leave a way to reach you.
我找回了我的手机 wǒ zhǎohuí le wǒ de shǒujī — and, with luck, it comes back.
Listen. Nǐ de shénme bú jiàn le?
Speak. Report something missing at a desk, in four sentences: what, when, what you need, how to reach you.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 不见了 and 找不到 (zhǎo bu dào)]
