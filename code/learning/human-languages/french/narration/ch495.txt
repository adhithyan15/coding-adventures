French, chapter 495: Disputes et trahisons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la dispute — quarrel, argument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to disobey, then the French for day nursery.

You'll want to know: la dispute.
la dispute — "quarrel, argument".
Ils ont eu une dispute à propos de l'argent. They had an argument about money.
A real row, not a debate.

The word, taken apart.
la dispute is built on the verb disputer, from Latin disputāre "to discuss, to argue" (EtymDB), built from dis- "apart" and putāre "to reckon" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
brouiller — to mix up, to blur; (se brouiller) to fall out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for day nursery, then the French for quarrel.

You'll want to know: brouiller.
brouiller — "to mix up, to blur; (se brouiller) to fall out".
Les deux frères se sont brouillés à cause d'une maison. The two brothers fell out over a house.
Se brouiller avec quelqu'un — to fall out with someone. Des œufs brouillés — scrambled eggs.

The word, taken apart.
brouiller goes back to Old French brouiller, from Vulgar Latin brodiculāre "to jumble together" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to a Germanic word for broth.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tromper — to deceive; to be unfaithful to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: tromper.
tromper — "to deceive; to be unfaithful to".
Elle a appris que son compagnon la trompait. She found out that her partner was cheating on her.
Se tromper — to make a mistake, a very common sense.

The word, taken apart.
tromper goes back to Old French tromper (EtymDB). The origin is not settled; a link with trompe "horn" is often proposed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
trahir — to betray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to mix up, then the French for to deceive.

You'll want to know: trahir.
trahir — "to betray".
Elle a trahi le secret de son amie. She betrayed her friend's secret.
A regular -ir verb: nous trahissons.

The word, taken apart.
trahir is inherited from Latin trādere "to hand over" (Lewis & Short), built from trāns and dare "to give".

You'll want to know: la trahison.
la trahison — "betrayal; treason".
Pour lui, ce mensonge est une trahison. For him, this lie is a betrayal.
Built on trahir.

The word, taken apart.
la trahison comes from Old French traison (EtymDB), built on trair "to betray", from Latin trādere "to hand over", built on dare (Lewis & Short).
