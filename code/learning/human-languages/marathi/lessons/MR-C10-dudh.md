# दूध (dūdh) — back to Sanskrit, and a surprising English cousin

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-CHAHA, MR-LEX-PAANI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **चहा** broke the pattern — a loanword with no Sanskrit behind it.
This next word rejoins **पाणी**'s family: inherited, traceable, Indo-European.

## You'll want to know: दूध
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-DUDH]; assesses=[] -->

> **दूध** — *dūdh* — **milk**

> **दूध, कृपया.** — *dūdh, kṛpayā.* — **Milk, please.**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One new mark: **ू**, the **long *ū*** vowel sign — a longer hook under the
consonant than any you've written so far. **द** you have from *dhanyavād* and
*madat*; **ध** from *dhanyavād* too. *Dū-dh*.

## The word, taken apart — milk, and the root behind "doughty"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-DUDH-DUGDHA]; assesses=[] -->

**दूध** continues Maharastri Prakrit **duddha**, from Sanskrit **दुग्ध**
*dugdha*, "milked, milk" — literally the past participle of **दुह्** *duh-*,
"to milk." That root traces to Indo-European ***\*dʰewgʰ-***, "to be fit, to be
of use, to produce" — milking as *producing something useful*. Germanic kept
the same root for a different sense of "useful, capable": English **doughty**,
"brave and able," is *dūhtig* in Old English, built on this exact root. Milk
and doughtiness, four thousand years apart, share one ancestor.
