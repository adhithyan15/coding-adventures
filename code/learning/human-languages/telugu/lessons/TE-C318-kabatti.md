# కాబట్టి (kābaṭṭi) — so: the reason, then the result

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C108-WANT108-04, TE-LEX-C109-WHY109-01, TE-LEX-C74-LINK-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "therefore" (*anduvalla*), "that is exactly why" (*andukē*) and "because"
(*endukaṇṭē*).

## You'll want to know: a reason + కాబట్టి
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C318-KABATTI-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C105-LOC105-02, TE-LEX-C149-ACT149-01] -->

> **వర్షం పడుతోంది కాబట్టి నేను ఇంట్లో ఉన్నాను.**
>
> *varṣaṁ paḍutōndi kābaṭṭi nēnu iṇṭlō unnānu.* — "It is raining, so I am at home."

> **బస్సు లేదు కాబట్టి మేము నడిచాము.**
>
> *bassu lēdu kābaṭṭi mēmu naḍicāmu.* — "There was no bus, so we walked."

**కాబట్టి** follows the reason directly, inside the same sentence; the result comes after it. Read it as
"since it is raining, …" or "it is raining, so …". The order is the reverse of **ఎందుకంటే**, where the result
comes first and the reason follows.

## You'll want to know: అందుకే, a new sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C318-ANDUKE-RESULT-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C84-UNWELL-01, TE-LEX-C103-LOC103-02, TE-LEX-C109-WHY109-01, TE-LEX-C108-WANT108-04] -->

> **నాకు జ్వరం వచ్చింది. అందుకే నేను బడికి వెళ్ళలేదు.**
>
> *nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi. andukē nēnu baḍiki veḷḷalēdu.* — "I had a fever. That is why I did not go to school."

When the reason is a whole sentence, the next sentence opens on **అందుకే**, "that is exactly why", or, more
formally, **అందువల్ల**, "therefore".

**వెళ్ళలేదు** (*veḷḷalēdu*, "did not go") is how a story says "did not": the verb's base with **లేదు**. **చేయలేదు**,
"did not do"; **రాలేదు**, "did not come".
