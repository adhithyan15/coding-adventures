Japanese, chapter 421: Haggling at the Market.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ねぎる (negiru) — to haggle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pay a bill, then the Japanese for to refund.

You'll want to know: ねぎる.
ねぎる — negiru — "to haggle".
いちばで ねぎって、やすく かいました。 — ichiba de negitte, yasuku kaimashita — "I haggled at the market and bought it cheaply."
Polite ねぎります, negirimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが いちばで ねぎりました。, haha ga ichiba de negirimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ねぎる (negiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ne, "price" and giru, "cutting" (from kiru); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ていか (teika) — the list price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to refund, then the Japanese for to haggle.

You'll want to know: ていか.
ていか — teika — "the list price".
わりびきで ていかより やすく かいました。 — waribiki de teika yori yasuku kaimashita — "With the discount I bought it below the list price."

The word, taken apart.
ていか (teika) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tei, "fix" (Middle Chinese dengH) and ka, "value" (kaeH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
