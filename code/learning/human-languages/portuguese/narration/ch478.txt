Portuguese, chapter 478: Solidão e saudade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a nostalgia — nostalgia.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for sigh, then the Portuguese for burst of laughter.

You'll want to know: a nostalgia.
a nostalgia — "nostalgia".
A avó olha para as fotografias antigas com nostalgia. Grandma looks at the old photographs with nostalgia.
Close to a saudade, but more about a time that is gone.

The word, taken apart.
nostalgia comes from New Latin nostalgia (EtymDB), a modern coinage from Greek nóstos "return home" and álgos "pain".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: a melancolia.
a melancolia — "melancholy".
Os dias de chuva enchem-me de melancolia. Rainy days fill me with melancholy.
The adjective is melancólico.

The word, taken apart.
melancolia goes back, through Old Portuguese melanconia (EtymDB), to Latin melancholia (Lewis & Short), from Greek melankholía "black bile", which old medicine blamed for sadness.

solidão is inherited from Latin sōlitūdō "loneliness, a lonely place" (EtymDB), built on sōlus "alone".

É um homem solitário que vive no campo. He is a solitary man who lives in the country.

The word, taken apart.
solitário is a learned borrowing from Latin sōlitārius "lonely, solitary" (EtymDB), from sōlus "alone".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o desgosto — grief, sorrow; heartbreak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for loneliness, then the Portuguese for lonely.

You'll want to know: o desgosto.
o desgosto — "grief, sorrow; heartbreak".
A morte do cão foi um grande desgosto para as crianças. The dog's death was a great sorrow for the children.
Stronger than tristeza. Ter um desgosto — to suffer a heartbreak.

The word, taken apart.
desgosto is built from des- and gosto "taste, pleasure", which is inherited from Latin gustus "a tasting, taste" (Lewis & Short).
