# The name exchange, with somebody else in the room

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-ISMUKA-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Ask a woman her name, answer with your own, and close politely. This
chapter has given you a neighbour, a friend, a son and a daughter — so run the
exchange, then introduce one of them.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-PRACTICE-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISMII-02] -->

- [YOU SAY: ask a man — *mā ismuka?*]
- [YOU SAY: ask a woman — *mā ismuki?*]
- [YOU SAY: answer — *ismī …*]
- [YOU SAY: close — *tasharrafnā*]
- [YOU SAY: now name your neighbour, your friend, your son, your daughter into the same frame]

Every line above is missing a verb, and none of them feels short. That is the
zero copula doing its work: *my-name Arun*, with the *is* understood.

## Grammar Lens: the suffix set, whole
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-II-MY-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-II-MY-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-ISMUKA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISM-01] -->

| noun | glued on | result |
|---|---|---|
| **اسم** *ism* | **ـي** *-ī* | **اسمي** my name |
| **اسم** *ism* | **ـك** *-ka* | **اسمك** your name, to a man |
| **اسم** *ism* | **ـك** *-ki* | **اسمك** your name, to a woman |

**اسم** is three letters, all from the first writing set: *alif · sīn · mīm*. What
follows it is never a separate word. Arabic has no free-standing *my* — it glues
**ي** on, the same habit that glues **الـ** for *the* and **بـ** for *in*.

The two *your* forms are **identical on the page**. Only the unwritten vowel
separates them, which is why the ear does the work the eye cannot.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ISM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-TASHARRAFNA-02] -->

**ما** *mā* is *mīm* plus *alif* and means **what** — the form you want before a
noun, where *māḏā* is the one for verbs.

Two of these words have histories worth holding onto. **اسم** shares its root
**s–m–w** with Hebrew *shem* and has no tie to English *name* at all. And
**تشرفنا** comes from **sh–r–f**, honour: *sharaf*, *sharīf*, and at a long
distance English *sheriff*. It does not mean *pleased to meet you*. It means **we
have been honoured**.

## Grammar Lens: which axis splits you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-01, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-02, AR-CONCEPT-C02-ANTA-ANTI-03] -->

**أنت** is one skeleton — hamza-seated *alif*, *nūn*, *tāʾ* — for both genders.
**أنتَ** *anta* addresses a man, **أنتِ** *anti* a woman, and the split is Semitic:
the same *t* marks *you* in Hebrew *atta* and *att*.

The European and Dravidian tracks split *you* by **register**. Arabic splits it by
**who is listening**. Two languages can make the same distinction in form and mean
something entirely different by it.
