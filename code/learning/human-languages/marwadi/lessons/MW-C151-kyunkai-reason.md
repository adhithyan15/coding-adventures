# क्यूंकै — give one short reason

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C117-WHY117-05, MW-LEX-BAATI-01, MW-LEX-C118-THINGS118-02, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-01, MW-LEX-HAI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Bring back **क्यूंकै** (*kyū̃kai*, because), **बाटी** (*bāṭī*),
**तवो** (*tavo*, a griddle), and **गरम है** (*garam hai*, is hot).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C117-WHY117-05] -->

Chapter 117 takes **क्यूंकै** apart as *kyū̃* plus *kai*. CDIAL lists Old
Marwari *kyu* under Sanskrit *kim* “what” (CDIAL 3164); the origin of the
second part remains not settled. This lesson reuses that word and adds only
the two-clause reason frame.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-CAUSAL-KYUNKAI-01]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C117-WHY117-05, MW-LEX-BAATI-01, MW-LEX-C118-THINGS118-02, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-01, MW-LEX-HAI-01] -->

Chapter 117 taught the word **क्यूंकै**. A word meaning “because” is not yet a
reason. Put one complete familiar statement before it and another after it:

> **बाटी गरम है क्यूंकै तवो गरम है।**
>
> *bāṭī garam hai kyū̃kai tavo garam hai.*
>
> The bati is hot because the griddle is hot.

The first part says what is so. The second gives one reason. Both small
statements keep **है** at their ends.
