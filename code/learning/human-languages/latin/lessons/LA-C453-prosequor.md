## You'll want to know: prōsequor, prōsequī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C453-PERITUS-03]; assesses=[] -->

**prōsequor, prōsequī** — "to escort, to see off, to accompany; to pursue".

> Amīcī viātōrem ad portam urbis prōsequuntur.
>
> *Friends escort the traveller as far as the city gate.*

Deponent: *prōsequor, prōsequī, prōsecūtus sum*. Escorting a departing friend part of the way was a duty. English *prosecute* and *pursue* come from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*prōsequor* is built from *prō-* 'forward, onward' and *sequor* 'to follow' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *prosecute* comes from its participle, and *pursue* from it through French.
