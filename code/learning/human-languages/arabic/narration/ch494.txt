Arabic, chapter 494: Feeling low.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
كآبة (kaʾāba) — gloom, low spirits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to reproach, then the Arabic for despair.

You'll want to know: كآبة.
كآبة — kaʾāba — "gloom, low spirits".
يسبب الشتاء الطويل الكآبة لبعض الناس. yusabbibu ash-shitāʾ aṭ-ṭawīl al-kaʾāba li-baʿḍ an-nās. The long winter causes gloom for some people.

The word, taken apart.
كآبة (kaʾāba) is built on the native Arabic root k-ʾ-b, "to be sad, broken in spirit" (Lane). Kaʾāba is that low, heavy state.

شعر جدي بالحنين إلى قريته. shaʿara jaddī bi-l-ḥanīn ilā qaryatihi. My grandfather felt homesick for his village.
It takes إلى for what you long for.

The word, taken apart.
حنين (ḥanīn) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-n-n, "to yearn, to feel tenderness" (Lane). Ḥanīn is a yearning, often for home or for the past.

Lesson 5 of 5.
مل (malla) — to get bored (of).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for frustration, then the Arabic for frustrated.

You'll want to know: مل.
مل — malla — "to get bored (of)".
مل الأطفال من الانتظار في السيارة. malla al-aṭfāl min al-intiẓār fī as-sayyāra. The children got bored of waiting in the car.
Present: يمل, yamall.
It takes من. The noun ملل, boredom, shares the root.

The word, taken apart.
مل (malla) is built on the native Arabic root m-l-l, "to be weary of a thing" (Lane). Its last two root letters are the same, so they write as one doubled letter.
