Mandarin Chinese, chapter 420: Parking.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiàzhào — driving licence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for international, then the Mandarin for inside the country.

You'll want to know: jiàzhào.
jiàzhào — "driving licence".
wǒ de jiàzhào bú jiàn le — My driving licence has gone missing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiàzhào is built from jià 'to drive' and zhào, short for a word for a licence; both are inherited from Middle Chinese, kaeH and tsyewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to drive a team' and the second 'bright'.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 2 of 5.
车道 (chēdào) — traffic lane.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for inside the country, then the Mandarin for a driving licence.

You'll want to know: 车道.
车道 — chēdào — "traffic lane".
wǎng yòubian de chēdào kāi — Move over into the right-hand lane.

The word, taken apart.
车道 (chēdào) is built from 车 'vehicle' and 道 'a road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhae and dawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a road' and 'reason'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
wéizhāng — to break the traffic rules.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a parking space, then the Mandarin for to reverse the car.

You'll want to know: wéizhāng.
wéizhāng — "to break the traffic rules".
zhèr bù néng tíngchē tíng le jiù wéizhāng — You can't park here: if you do, you're breaking the rules.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wéizhāng is built from wéi 'to go against' and zhāng 'a rule', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjw+j and tsyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to turn your back on' and the second as a section of writing; the sense 'a rule' is not given there.
