## You'll want to know: there is nothing to conjugate
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-GRAMMAR-VERB-INVARIANT-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **A Mandarin verb has exactly one form. It never changes — not for who is
> doing it, not for how many of them there are, and not for when.**

You have been given 看 *kàn*, "to look", and you have used it in exactly one
shape. That was not the lesson being brief. That is the whole verb.

| | |
|---|---|
| **我看书** | *wǒ kàn shū* — I read |
| **你看书** | *nǐ kàn shū* — you read |
| **人看书** | *rén kàn shū* — people read |

The verb is 看 three times. Nothing has been added, dropped or swapped.

This is worth stating out loud because of what a reader coming from English or
Spanish does otherwise: they wait for the ending, decide the book has not
reached it yet, and go on quietly expecting one. Spanish has *hablo, hablas,
habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan*. English has three forms and an irregular verb
for every occasion. **Mandarin has one shape and no exceptions.**

Every verb in this book behaves this way — 看, 是, 上学 — and so will every verb
in every chapter after it.
