German, chapter 351: Die Arbeitszeit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Teilzeit — part-time (work).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to employ, then the German for fixed-term.

You'll want to know: die Teilzeit.
die Teilzeit — "part-time (work)".
Seit der Geburt ihres Sohnes arbeitet sie in Teilzeit. Since the birth of her son she has been working part-time.
In Teilzeit arbeiten or Teilzeit arbeiten. Der Teilzeitjob — the part-time job.

The word, taken apart.
Teilzeit joins Teil "part", Old High German teil, Proto-Germanic dailą (EtymDB), to Zeit "time", Old High German zīt, Proto-Germanic tīdiz (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Schicht — the shift (at work); the layer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for part-time, then the German for full-time.

You'll want to know: die Schicht.
die Schicht — "the shift (at work); the layer".
Ihre Schicht beginnt um sechs Uhr morgens. Her shift starts at six in the morning.
Plural: die Schichten. Die Nachtschicht — the night shift. Also a layer: eine Schicht Schnee.

The word, taken apart.
Schicht is usually traced to Middle Low German schicht, a word of the mines for a layer of rock and for a stint of work. EtymDB gives no history for it, but lists English shift as a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Überstunde — the hour of overtime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for full-time, then the German for the shift.

You'll want to know: die Überstunde.
die Überstunde — "the hour of overtime".
Letzte Woche habe ich zehn Überstunden gemacht. Last week I worked ten hours of overtime.
Mostly plural: Überstunden machen — to work overtime.

The word, taken apart.
Überstunde joins über "over" to Stunde "hour", from Old High German stunta "a while"; EtymDB lists English stound "a while" as a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Feierabend — the end of the working day; the evening off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the shift, then the German for the hour of overtime.

You'll want to know: der Feierabend.
der Feierabend — "the end of the working day; the evening off".
Nach Feierabend gehe ich gern schwimmen. After work I like to go swimming.
Feierabend machen — to finish work for the day. Schönen Feierabend! — Enjoy your evening!

The word, taken apart.
Feierabend joins Feier, from Old High German fīra and Latin fēriae "holidays" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), to Abend "evening", inherited from Old High German āband (EtymDB). It is usually said to have first meant the evening before a holiday.
