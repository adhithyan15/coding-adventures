Lesson 1 of 5.
भयंकरः (bhayaṅkaraḥ) — terrible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for famous, then the Sanskrit for safe.

You'll want to know: भयंकरः.
भयंकरः — bhayaṅkaraḥ — "terrible".

The word, taken apart.
bhayaṅkara is built from bhaya "fear", from the root √bhī "be afraid" (MW), and -kara "making", from √kṛ "do, make", with an ṃ between them: "fear-making", so "terrible" (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
मिथ्या (mithyā) — false.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for safe, then the Sanskrit for terrible.

You'll want to know: मिथ्या.
मिथ्या — mithyā — "false".

The word, taken apart.
mithyā is an old adverb, "invertedly, wrongly", and later "falsely": MW contracts it from mithūyā, and CDIAL 10130 sets it under the root √mith "alternate, clash". It lives on as Bengali michā "untrue" (CDIAL 10130).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कटुः (kaṭuḥ) — pungent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for terrible, then the Sanskrit for false.

You'll want to know: कटुः.
कटुः — kaṭuḥ — "pungent".

The word, taken apart.
kaṭu "pungent, sharp, bitter" is old, in the Rigveda; MW tentatively links it with √kṛt "cut", but its origin is uncertain, and DEDR 1135 compares a Dravidian word. It lives on as Marathi kaḍū and Hindi kaṛuā "bitter, pungent" (CDIAL 2641).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अम्लः (amlaḥ) — sour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for false, then the Sanskrit for pungent.

You'll want to know: अम्लः.
अम्लः — amlaḥ — "sour".

The word, taken apart.
amla "sour, acid" appears from Manu on, and MW gives no derivation for it, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Bengali āmbal "sour" and Pali ambila (CDIAL 579).

Lesson 5 of 5.
तिक्तः (tiktaḥ) — bitter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for pungent, then the Sanskrit for sour.

You'll want to know: तिक्तः.
तिक्तः — tiktaḥ — "bitter".

The word, taken apart.
tikta "bitter, pungent" comes from the root √tij "be sharp" (CDIAL 5806): bitterness as a sharp taste. MW compares √tij with Greek stizō "prick" and Latin distinguo, and it lives on as Bengali titā "bitter" (CDIAL 5806).
