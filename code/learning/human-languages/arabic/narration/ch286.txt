Arabic, chapter 286: Must, Should, Will, Because.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أن أذهب (an adhhaba) — to go: the verb after an.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think that the exam is hard". (Aʿtaqidu anna al-imtiḥāna ṣaʿb.) Then "in my opinion". (Fī raʾyī.)
[pause 1 second]
You have said anwī an azūra jaddatī, "I intend to visit my grandmother". Listen to the end of azūra. It is not the azūru of "I visit", and this lesson says why.

Grammar Lens: -u becomes -a.
After أن — an, "to", "that" before a verb — the present changes its last vowel. Adhhabu, I go, becomes an adhhaba, to go:
أريد أن أذهب إلى السوق. — urīdu an adhhaba ilā as-sūq — I want to go to the market.
This shape of the present is the subjunctive: the verb for a thing wanted, planned or needed, not yet a fact. Urīdu, I want, is the present of arāda.
Where the present ends in -ūna or -īna, the -na falls away: yadhhabūna, they go, becomes an yadhhabū; tadhhabīna, you go (to a woman), becomes an tadhhabī.

Grammar Lens: an and anna.
قررنا أن نسافر بالقطار. — qarrarnā an nusāfira bi-l-qiṭār — We decided to travel by train.
An takes a verb: what someone wants or decides to do. Anna, with the doubled n, takes a sentence: what someone thinks is so. Aʿtaqidu anna al-qiṭāra sarīʿ; urīdu an arkaba al-qiṭār.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: adhhabu … an adhhaba]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I want to go to the market" — urīdu an adhhaba ilā as-sūq]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We decided to travel by train" — qarrarnā an nusāfira bi-l-qiṭār]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "They want to go" — yurīdūna an yadhhabū]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: anwī an azūra jaddatī, and hear the -a]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
After an, what happens to -u? (It becomes -a: an adhhaba.) And to -ūna? (The -na falls away: an yadhhabū.) Which particle takes a verb, and which a sentence? (An a verb, anna a sentence.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
يجب أن (yajibu an) — must, should.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I want to go". (Urīdu an adhhaba.) Then the Arabic for homework, a duty. (Wājib.)
[pause 1 second]
Wanting is one reason to act. Having to is another, and a piece of advice is a third.

Grammar Lens: yajibu an, it is necessary.
يجب — yajibu — "it is necessary". It does not change for the person. The person is in the verb after an, in the subjunctive:
يجب أن أذهب الآن. — yajibu an adhhaba al-ān — I must go now.
So yajibu an tadhhaba, you must go; yajibu an nadhhaba, we must go. Put kāna in front for "had to": kāna yajibu an adhhaba, I had to go.
Yajibu comes from the root of wājib, "a duty". The root's w drops in the present, as it does in yaṣilu, he arrives, from waṣala.

Grammar Lens: yanbaghī an, you ought to.
ينبغي — yanbaghī — "it is right, it is fitting". It is softer than yajibu: advice, not an order.
ينبغي أن تزور الطبيب. — yanbaghī an tazūra aṭ-ṭabīb — You ought to visit the doctor.
Yanbaghī an tastarīḥa, you should rest. The verb after it behaves exactly as after yajibu.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I must go now" — yajibu an adhhaba al-ān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We must go" — yajibu an nadhhaba]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I had to go" — kāna yajibu an adhhaba]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You ought to visit the doctor" — yanbaghī an tazūra aṭ-ṭabīb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: advice with a reason — fī raʾyī yanbaghī an tastarīḥa, li-annaka marīḍ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Does yajibu change for the person? (No: the verb after an does.) The softer one, for advice? (Yanbaghī.) "I had to go"? (Kāna yajibu an adhhaba.) Which noun shares the root of yajibu? (Wājib, a duty.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
بسبب (bi-sabab) — because of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I must go now". (Yajibu an adhhaba al-ān.) Then ask "why?". (Limādhā?)
[pause 1 second]
The answer can be a whole sentence, or a single thing. Arabic uses a different word for each.

Grammar Lens: a thing, or a sentence.
بسبب — bi-sabab — "because of". It is bi-, "by", with sabab, "a reason", and a noun follows it:
تأخرت بسبب المطر. — taʾakhkhartu bi-sabab al-maṭar — I was late because of the rain.
Where the reason is a whole sentence, with its own verb, it takes li-anna:
تأخرت لأن الطريق كان مزدحما. — taʾakhkhartu li-anna aṭ-ṭarīqa kāna muzdaḥiman — I was late because the road was busy.
So bi-sabab al-maṭar, a thing; li-anna aṭ-ṭarīqa kāna muzdaḥiman, a sentence.

Grammar Lens: the reason is that.
To give the reason as an answer of its own, say السبب هو أن — as-sababu huwa anna — "the reason is that". Huwa, "he, it", stands where English has "is":
السبب هو أن القطار تأخر. — as-sababu huwa anna al-qiṭāra taʾakhkhara — The reason is that the train was late.
Natīja, "a result", is its partner: as-sabab looks back, an-natīja looks forward.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I was late because of the rain" — taʾakhkhartu bi-sabab al-maṭar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "because the road was busy" — li-anna aṭ-ṭarīqa kāna muzdaḥiman]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The reason is that the train was late" — as-sababu huwa anna al-qiṭāra taʾakhkhara]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a question and its answer — limādhā taʾakhkharta? … bi-sabab al-qiṭār]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What follows bi-sabab? (A noun: bi-sabab al-maṭar.) What follows li-anna? (A sentence.) "The reason is that"? (As-sababu huwa anna.) Which word sits inside bi-sabab? (Sabab, a reason.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
سوف (sawfa) — will, for a plan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "because of the rain". (Bi-sabab al-maṭar.) Then "the reason is that". (As-sababu huwa anna.)
[pause 1 second]
You have heard sa-usāfiru al-usbūʿa al-qādim, "I will travel next week". Reasons are often reasons for a plan, and a plan needs a future.

Grammar Lens: sa- and sawfa.
Arabic builds the future on the present. Put سـ — sa- — onto its front, with no space, or put سوف — sawfa — before it as a word of its own:
سأسافر غدا. — sa-usāfiru ghadan — I will travel tomorrow.
سوف نسافر في العطلة القادمة. — sawfa nusāfiru fī al-ʿuṭla al-qādima — We will travel in the next holiday.
The two say the same "will". Sawfa is the fuller one, and it is often heard for something further off or said with weight.

Grammar Lens: lan, will not.
For "will not", the sa- goes and لن — lan — takes its place. After lan the verb is in the subjunctive, as after an:
لن أسافر في العطلة. — lan usāfira fī al-ʿuṭla — I won't travel in the holiday.
Lan adhhaba, I won't go; lan nadhhaba, we won't go.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I will travel tomorrow" — sa-usāfiru ghadan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We will travel in the next holiday" — sawfa nusāfiru fī al-ʿuṭla al-qādima]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I won't go" — lan adhhaba]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a plan with its reason — sa-usāfiru li-anna ʿindī waqtan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the opposite — lan usāfira li-anna ʿindī imtiḥānan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
The short "will"? (سـ, sa-, joined to the verb.) The long one? (Sawfa.) "Will not"? (Lan, with the subjunctive: lan adhhaba.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
Review — a plan, some advice, and why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I have a question", then "I think that it is easy". (ʿIndī suʾāl, aʿtaqidu annahu sahl.) Then "so", the one-letter way. (Fa-.)

What you've built.
Four lessons, and one vowel at the end of a verb. After an, after yajibu an and yanbaghī an, and after lan, the present ends in -a: the shape for a thing wanted, needed or refused, and not yet a fact. Sa- and sawfa keep the plain present. Bi-sabab gives a reason that is a thing, li-anna one that is a sentence.
[pause 2 seconds]
A need and its reason in one breath:
يجب أن أزور الطبيب غدا، لأن عندي سؤالا. — yajibu an azūra aṭ-ṭabība ghadan, li-anna ʿindī suʾālan — I must visit the doctor tomorrow, because I have a question.
