Italian, chapter 413: Incidenti e code.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tamponare — to rear-end, to bump into (the car in front).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to run over, then the Italian for the fine.

You'll want to know: tamponare.
tamponare — "to rear-end, to bump into (the car in front)".
Un furgone ci ha tamponato al semaforo. A van ran into the back of us at the lights.
Also to stop a leak or a wound: tamponare una ferita.

The word, taken apart.
tamponare is built on tampone 'plug, pad', which was borrowed from French tampon.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'ingorgo — the traffic jam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to rear-end, then the Italian for the rear-end collision.

You'll want to know: l'ingorgo.
l'ingorgo — "the traffic jam".
Siamo rimasti in un ingorgo per un'ora. We were stuck in a traffic jam for an hour.
Worse than la coda: nothing moves.

The word, taken apart.
ingorgo is built from in- and gorgo 'whirlpool', which is inherited from Latin gurges 'whirlpool' (EtymDB): traffic swirling and stuck.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la deviazione — the diversion, the detour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the rear-end collision, then the Italian for the traffic jam.

You'll want to know: la deviazione.
la deviazione — "the diversion, the detour".
Per i lavori c'è una deviazione. Because of the roadworks there is a diversion.
Seguire la deviazione — to follow the diversion.

The word, taken apart.
deviazione is a learned borrowing from Late Latin dēviātiō, from dēviāre 'to turn aside', built on via 'way' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: deviare.
deviare — "to divert, to turn off".
Hanno deviato il traffico verso il centro. They diverted the traffic towards the centre.
Also without an object: la strada devia a destra.

The word, taken apart.
deviare is a learned borrowing from Late Latin dēviāre 'to turn aside', from dē 'off' and via 'way' (Lewis & Short).
