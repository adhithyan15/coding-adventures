Hindi, chapter 75: Where You Come From.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
देश (desh) — a country.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two lessons back you learnt to ask with nothing but your voice, and one lesson back to hang a check on the end: say चाय ठीक है flat, then with the end lifted, then say चाय ठीक है ना? and name what each one does. You have also filled in a नाम line without a model, and a form asks for more than a name. The next five words are what it asks for.

You'll want to know: देश.
देश (desh) — "a country".
Sanskrit देश (desh) (deśa) meant a place, a region, a stretch of land — and it comes from the root दिश् (diś-), "to point out, to show". A देश (desh) began as whatever a pointing hand took in.
That root travelled. Latin dīcere, "to say", and index, the pointing finger, grew from the same ancient root as दिश्. The finger you use to show someone where you are from is the finger the word is named after.
Every Hindi form you will ever fill in has a देश (desh) line on it, and every conversation that starts with a greeting reaches it within a minute.

What you've built.
The first of five words for saying where you are from.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: say ghās, then read कुआँ and say what it means]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — hear: desh, said once slowly and once at speed]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: desh]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yah ek desh hai nā? — last chapter's tag, on your new word]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: देश (desh), then say what it means]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say kyoṁ, then read कब]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does देश (desh) mean? ("a country".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
भारत (Bhārat) — India.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say desh, then write देश from memory. Three letters and one matra: द, then े on top of it, then श. You have been taught all three.

You'll want to know: भारत.
भारत (Bhārat) — "India", the country's name in its own language.
The name is older than the country. भरत (Bharata) is a figure the Sanskrit epics name, and भारत (Bhārat) is the form meaning "belonging to Bharata" — the land of his people. English India took a different route entirely, through Greek and Persian from सिंधु (Sindhu), the river.
So one country carries two names that started as two different things: a people and a river. In Hindi you will hear भारत (Bhārat) on the news and हिंदुस्तान in the street, and both mean the same place.

What you've built.
A देश (desh) and the name of one: भारत (Bhārat) एक देश (desh) है.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: read कुआँ, then say it without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Bhārat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Bhārat ek desh hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same sentence twice — flat, then with the end lifted — and then Bhārat ek desh hai nā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: a नाम line with no model, then देश (desh) on the line under it]
[once you have stopped driving — write: देश (desh) once more, without looking]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does भारत (Bhārat) mean? ("India".) And देश (desh)? ("a country".)


Lesson 3 of 5.
शहर (shahar) — a city, a town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of the two words you have is the name of a country, and which is the word for any country at all?

You'll want to know: शहर.
शहर (shahar) — "a city, a town".
Persian شهر (shahr), "city", and behind it Old Persian xšaça, "kingdom, dominion" — a शहर (shahar) was first the seat of a king rather than a crowd of people. The same word ends dozens of place names across the region.
Set it against गाँव (gā̃v), the village you already own. गाँव and शहर (shahar) are the pair Hindi uses for the whole of settled life, and a speaker will place themselves in one or the other within a sentence of meeting you.
A form asks for your शहर (shahar) on the line under your देश (desh).

What you've built.
Three: देश (desh), भारत (Bhārat), शहर (shahar). A country, its name, and the place in it you live.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — hear: shahar, then gā̃v]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: shahar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: shahar and gā̃v, and say which one is bigger]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does शहर (shahar) mean? ("a city".) Say it once more.


Lesson 4 of 5.
भाषा (bhāshā) — a language.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write शहर (shahar) from memory: श, then ह, then र. No matras at all in this one — three bare letters in a row.

You'll want to know: भाषा.
भाषा (bhāshā) — "a language".
Sanskrit भाषा (bhāshā) (bhāṣā), "speech, what is spoken", from भाष् (bhāṣ-), "to speak". The word names the act before it names the thing: a भाषा (bhāshā) is speaking, turned into a noun.
You have been saying मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ for a long time. भाषा (bhāshā) is the word that lets you talk about हिंदी rather than only in it — and it is a printed line on both of the writing papers you will one day sit, right under देश (desh) and शहर (shahar).

What you've built.
Four of the five lines a form asks for: देश (desh), भारत (Bhārat), शहर (shahar), भाषा (bhāshā).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: bhāshā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hindī ek bhāshā hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: शहर (shahar) once more, without looking]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does भाषा (bhāshā) mean? ("a language".) Say it once more.


Lesson 5 of 5.
अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) — English, the language.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the four words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: अंग्रेज़ी.
अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) — "English", the language.
The route is long and worth following. Portuguese traders said inglês; Persian took it as انگریز (angrez), "an Englishman"; Hindi added the ending -ी that turns a people into their language, and अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) came out. The nuqta under ज़ is the z you learnt with the letters.
Set it beside हिंदी, which is built the same way: हिंद, the land, plus the same ending. भाषा (bhāshā) is the word for the class of thing; हिंदी and अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) are two members of it.
Both listening papers you will one day sit key on somebody speaking two of these, so the pair matters more than either word alone.

What you've built.
Five: देश (desh), भारत (Bhārat), शहर (shahar), भाषा (bhāshā), अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī). Enough to say what country you are in, what city you live in, and what you speak.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: angrezī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hindī aur angrezī, do bhāshā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: भाषा (bhāshā) from memory, then अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) beside the printed model]
[your turn — say: all five in order]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does अंग्रेज़ी (angrezī) mean? ("English".) And what is the word for a language itself? (bhāshā.)
