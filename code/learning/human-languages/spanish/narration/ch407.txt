Spanish, chapter 407: Middle, Below, Outside and Beside.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
en medio — in the middle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall la puerta, the door. Now place it at the centre: la puerta está en medio.

The word, taken apart.
Medio comes from Latin medius, “middle.” English kept the same root in median, medium and intermediate. Spanish uses en medio for a position between the edges or between two things.

Grammar Lens: the frame en medio de.
Say en medio when the reference is understood. Add de before the thing that frames the middle: en medio de la puerta, in the middle of the door.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: en medio]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: en medio de la puerta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: la puerta está en medio]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
In the middle? (En medio.)
Source: DLE: medio.


Lesson 2 of 6.
debajo — underneath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall la mesa. Something lower than the tabletop can be debajo de la mesa.

The word, taken apart.
The history is visible: de + bajo. Bajo is low; debajo names the lower position. The DLE defines it as a place below another higher one.

Grammar Lens: debajo de.
Before a reference object, use debajo de: debajo de la mesa. With no object stated, está debajo is complete.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: debajo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: debajo de la mesa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: está debajo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Underneath? (Debajo.) Under the table? (Debajo de la mesa.)
Source: DLE: debajo.


Lesson 3 of 6.
fuera — outside.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall la casa. Inside it is one location; beyond it is fuera, outside.

The word, taken apart.
Fuera comes from Latin foras, “outward.” The core picture is crossing the boundary and standing beyond it. The opening fue- is one syllable: say FWE-ra, not fu-E-ra.

Grammar Lens: fuera and fuera de.
Está fuera means it is outside. Add de for the boundary: fuera de la casa, outside the house.

Source: DLE: fuera.


Lesson 4 of 6.
el lado — the side.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A door has a left edge and a right edge. Each edge belongs to un lado, a side.

The word, taken apart.
Lado continues Latin latus, “side.” English lateral is the learned cousin: a lateral movement goes toward one side.

Grammar Lens: al lado de.
The useful location frame is al lado de, beside or next to: al lado de la puerta. The al is a + el, literally “at the side of.”

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el lado]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: al lado]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: al lado de la puerta]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
The side? (El lado.) Beside the door? (Al lado de la puerta.)
Source: DLE: lado.
