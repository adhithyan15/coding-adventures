## You'll want to know: 比如
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C281-BIRU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**比如** — *bǐrú* — "for instance; such as".

> **我喜欢中国菜 比如包子** *wǒ xǐhuan Zhōngguó cài, bǐrú bāozi* — I like Chinese food, steamed buns for instance.

> **我喜欢喝茶 比如中国茶** *wǒ xǐhuan hē chá, bǐrú Zhōngguó chá* — I like tea, Chinese tea for instance.

The next lesson puts the second character in your hand. The first is the 比 you write.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**比如** (*bǐrú*) is built from 比 'to compare' and 如 'like', both inherited from Middle Chinese. The Guangyun lists 比 under five readings, of which *pjijX* is glossed 'to compare', and 如 under two, *nyo* and *nyoH*; it glosses *nyo* with several words, among them 'to resemble' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'For instance' for the pair is the word's own sense.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C153-BI-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-BI-COMPARE-01, ZH-LEX-C38-EAT38-05, ZH-LEX-C70-FOOD70-02, ZH-LEX-C39-DRINK39-05] -->

比如 comes after the general point and before the example: 中国菜, then 比如包子.

It is the 比 of comparing, 比 as in 我比他大, with an example set beside the point.
