# ◌़ — the dot you have been reading and nobody named

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-DA-RETROFLEX-01, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-137] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Two letters you can draw: **ड** and **ढ**. Say each one.

[PAUSE 2s] Now look at **बड़ा** and **पढ़ना**. Something is under the letter in
each of them that is not under the bare letter above.

## Script you'll notice: ◌़
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-140]; assesses=[] -->

**◌़** — a single dot, sitting **below** the consonant.

It is **not a vowel sign**. A vowel sign changes the vowel a consonant carries.
This one changes **the consonant itself** into a different sound, and the letter
underneath keeps its shape.

Worked through: **ड** + **◌़** = **ड़**.

> The order is **carrier first, dot second**. The reference that defines this
> mark describes where the dot sits and what it combines with, not the order a
> hand should move in, so take carrier-first as a convention of this book rather
> than a rule handed down. Source: The Unicode Consortium, The Unicode Standard,
> Version 17.0, Core Specification, section 12.1.3 ‘Combining Marks’, U+093C Devanagari
> Sign Nukta, 2025.

## Script: the two that flap
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-140] -->

| letter | with the dot | sound |
|---|---|---|
| ड | ड़ | *ṛa* |
| ढ | ढ़ | *ṛha* |

The tongue taps the roof of the mouth once and flicks forward. It is a flap
rather than a held sound.

Every one of these was yours long before this page:

- **बड़ा** — big
- **सड़क** — road
- **खिड़की** — window
- **पढ़ना** — to read
- **सीढ़ी** — stair

Read them again and this time **the dot is part of what you are reading**. Look
at where in each word the dotted letter falls.

## Writing: ◌़ — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-140] -->

Draw the consonant the way you already draw it. Then put one dot below it,
under the middle of the letter, clear of the line.

Put your pen on **ड़** and follow it. Copy the shape slowly and larger than it
is printed.

> This book does not tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
