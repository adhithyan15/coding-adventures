Portuguese, chapter 393: No banco.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o saldo — the balance (of an account).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the rate, then the Portuguese for the inflation.

You'll want to know: o saldo.
o saldo — "the balance (of an account)".
Vi o saldo da conta no multibanco. I checked my account balance at the cash machine.
Os saldos in the plural are the sales in shops.

The word, taken apart.
saldo was borrowed from Italian saldo "balance, settled" (EtymDB), built on saldare "to settle", from Latin solidus "solid, whole" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the inflation, then the Portuguese for the balance.

You'll want to know: o depósito.
o depósito — "the deposit; the (fuel) tank".
Fiz um depósito de cem euros. I made a deposit of a hundred euros.
Also a fuel tank: encher o depósito — to fill up the tank.

The word, taken apart.
depósito is a learned borrowing from Latin dēpositum "a thing laid down for safekeeping" (Lewis & Short), from dēpōnere "to lay down", on pōnere "to put".

Lesson 4 of 5.
transferir — to transfer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the deposit, then the Portuguese for to deposit.

You'll want to know: transferir.
transferir — "to transfer".
Transferi o dinheiro da renda para a conta da minha mãe. I transferred the rent money to my mother's account.
Conjugated transfiro, transferes, transfere.

The word, taken apart.
transferir is a learned borrowing from Latin trānsferre "to carry across" (EtymDB), built from trāns "across" and ferre "to carry" (Lewis & Short).
