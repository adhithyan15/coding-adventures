# cubrir — and the lid that comes off again

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ABRIR-01, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-03, ES-LEX-C444-CASA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **abrir** and **cerrado**. Say **plantar**. You have put a
cutting in the ground and the frost is coming. Name what you do to it.

## You'll want to know: cubrir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ABRIR-01] -->

**cubrir** — to cover.

*Cubre la olla* — cover the pot. *El seguro no lo cubre* — the insurance does
not cover it, which is the sense you will meet on paper rather than in a
kitchen. **el cubierto** is a place setting at table, the knife and fork laid
out — what *covers* your place.

## Grammar Lens: a third irregular participle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04, ES-GRAMMAR-ABIERTO-03, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-03] -->

**abrir** gave you **abierto** and **cerrar** gave you **cerrado**. *Cubrir*
follows *abrir*.

| verb | participle |
|---|---|
| cerrar | cerrado — regular |
| abrir | **abierto** — irregular |
| cubrir | **cubierto** — irregular |

*Abrir* and *cubrir* both end in **-brir**, and both break the same way. That is
not a coincidence you have to memorise separately: the handful of irregular
participles come in small families, and finding one member gives you the others.
*Descubrir* makes **descubierto**, because it is *cubrir* with something on the
front.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-04, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04] -->

Latin **cooperīre** — **co-** together, plus **operīre**, to shut or cover over.
*Operīre* is itself **ob-** plus a root meaning *to close*, and its opposite,
**aperīre**, gave you **abrir**. So *cubrir* and *abrir* are not merely alike in
shape; they are two halves of one Latin pair.

And now the prefix that earns its keep:

| word | what des- does |
|---|---|
| cubrir | put the cover on |
| **descubrir** | **take it off — to discover** |

**Descansar** taught you *des-* as an undoing, and this is the same job done
plainly: to *discover* something is to **uncover** it. Compare *disfrutar*,
where the prefix did not reverse at all. Same shape, two behaviours, and only
the meaning of the whole word tells you which you have.
