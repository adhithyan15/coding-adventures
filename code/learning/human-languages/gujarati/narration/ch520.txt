Gujarati, chapter 520: Spices in Hot Oil.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વઘારવું (vaghārvũ) — to temper (with hot spiced oil).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for steam, then the Gujarati for to let cool.

You'll want to know: વઘારવું.
વઘારવું — vaghārvũ — "to temper (with hot spiced oil)".
માતાએ રાઈ અને જીરાથી દાળ વઘારી. mātāe rāī ane jīrāthī dāḷ vaghārī. Mother tempered the dal with mustard seed and cumin.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે દાળ વઘારી, teṇe dāḷ vaghārī.

The word, taken apart.
વઘારવું (vaghārvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit vyághārayati, "sprinkles over", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to season" (CDIAL 12191): hot oil with spices is poured over the dish.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ચપટી (capṭī) — a pinch (of something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to temper, then the Gujarati for to sauté.

You'll want to know: ચપટી.
ચપટી — capṭī — "a pinch (of something)".
શાકમાં એક ચપટી હળદર નાખો. śākmā̃ ek capṭī haḷdar nākho. Put a pinch of turmeric in the vegetables.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ચપટી (capṭī) is as much as fits between finger and thumb. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: છાંટવું.
છાંટવું — chā̃ṭvũ — "to sprinkle (a liquid)".
મીરાએ ગરમ તવા પર થોડું પાણી છાંટ્યું. Mīrāe garam tavā par thoḍũ pāṇī chā̃ṭyũ. Mira sprinkled a little water on the hot griddle.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે પાણી છાંટ્યું, teṇe pāṇī chā̃ṭyũ.

The word, taken apart.
છાંટવું (chā̃ṭvũ) goes back to a reconstructed chaṇṭ, "scatter", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to sprinkle" (CDIAL 4970).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ભૂંજવું (bhū̃jvũ) — to parch (in a dry pan).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a pinch, then the Gujarati for to sprinkle.

You'll want to know: ભૂંજવું.
ભૂંજવું — bhū̃jvũ — "to parch (in a dry pan)".
દાદીએ તવા પર ચણા ભૂંજ્યા. dādīe tavā par caṇā bhū̃jyā. Grandmother parched chickpeas on the griddle.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ચણા ભૂંજ્યા, teṇe caṇā bhū̃jyā.

The word, taken apart.
ભૂંજવું (bhū̃jvũ) comes from bhṛñjati, "roasts", a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to roast" (CDIAL 9586); it is said of grain roasted without oil.
