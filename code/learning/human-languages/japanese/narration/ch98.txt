Lesson 1 of 5.
どうして (doushite) — why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a method, then the Japanese for a way of doing.

You'll want to know: どうして.
どうして — doushite — "why".

The word, taken apart.
どうして (doushite) (dōshite) is native: dō, "how", built on the do- of "which", with shite, the linking form of suru, "to do": "having done how" (JMdict). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
りゆう (riyuu) — a reason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a way of doing, then the Japanese for why.

You'll want to know: りゆう.
りゆう — riyuu — "a reason".

The word, taken apart.
りゆう (riyuu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ri, "reason" (Middle Chinese liX) and yū, "cause" (yuw) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ため (tame) — for the sake of; because of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for why, then the Japanese for a reason.

You'll want to know: ため.
ため — tame — "for the sake of; because of".

The word, taken apart.
ため (tame) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
