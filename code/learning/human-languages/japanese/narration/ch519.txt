Japanese, chapter 519: Giving Up and Thinking Again.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おもいなおす (omoinaosu) — to think again; to change one's mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to slip up, then the Japanese for a fault.

You'll want to know: おもいなおす.
おもいなおす — omoinaosu — "to think again; to change one's mind".
もし いま やめたら、かれは あとで おもいなおすでしょう。 — moshi ima yametara, kare wa ato de omoinaosu deshō — "If he quits now, he will probably think again later."
Polite おもいなおします, omoinaoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが ひっこしを おもいなおしました。, chichi ga hikkoshi o omoinaoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おもいなおす (omoinaosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from omou, "to think", and naosu, "to fix"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ぶなん (bunan) — safe (as a choice); acceptable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to give up on (someone), then the Japanese for to betray.

You'll want to know: ぶなん.
ぶなん — bunan — "safe (as a choice); acceptable".
もし まよったら、この みちが ぶなんです。 — moshi mayottara, kono michi ga bunan desu — "If you are unsure, this road is the safe choice."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
ぶなん (bunan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are bu, "nothingness" (Middle Chinese mju) and nan, "difficult" (nan) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
