## You'll want to know: 口味
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C449-TASTE449-02]; assesses=[] -->

**口味** — *kǒuwèi* — "one's taste in food".

> *nánfāng rén hé běifāng rén de kǒuwèi bù yíyàng* — People in the south and the north have different tastes in food.

The new character, in pieces: 味 — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left 口 kǒu, small; on the right a short bar, a longer bar under it, a vertical through both, and a stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**口味** (*kǒuwèi*) is built from 口 'the mouth' and 味 'a flavour', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *khuwX* and *mj+jH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the five flavours, sour, salty, sweet, bitter and pungent.
