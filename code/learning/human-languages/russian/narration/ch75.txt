Lesson 1 of 5.
уже (uzhé) — already.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for now, then the Russian for later.

You'll want to know: уже.
уже (uzhé) — "already".

The word, taken apart.
уже (uzhé) — Vasmer finds two old words fused in it: Proto-Slavic ju, "already", a cousin of Lithuanian jaũ and Gothic ju, "already", and a particle u; -же adds weight.

Lesson 2 of 5.
быстро (býstro) — quickly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for later, then the Russian for already.

You'll want to know: быстро.
быстро (býstro) — "quickly". The adverb of быстрый.

The word, taken apart.
быстро (býstro) is быстрый, "fast", made into an adverb with -о. быстрый is an old Slavic word whose deeper origin is not settled (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
одиннадцать (odínnadtsat) — eleven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for already, then the Russian for quickly.

You'll want to know: одиннадцать.
одиннадцать (odínnadtsat) — "eleven". "One on ten": один + на + десять.

The word, taken apart.
одиннадцать (odínnadtsat) is один на десять, "one on ten", worn down: Old Russian одинадесять, from Proto-Slavic jedinŭ na desęte (Trubachev, in Vasmer). All the teens are built this way.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ноль (nol) — zero.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for eleven, then the Russian for twelve.

You'll want to know: ноль.
ноль (nol) — "zero".

The word, taken apart.
ноль (nol), also нуль, probably came through German Null from Italian nulla, "nothing", from Latin nūllus, "none" (Vasmer). The о may reflect the short German u.
