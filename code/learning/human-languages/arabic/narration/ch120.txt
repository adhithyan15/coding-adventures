Arabic, chapter 120: Jam, Pomegranate, Strawberries, Lettuce, Aubergine.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مربى (murabbā) — jam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for dinner, then the Arabic for vinegar.

You'll want to know: مربى.
مربى — murabbā — "jam".

The word, taken apart.
مربى (murabbā) is fruit preserved in rubb, a thickened fruit juice: Lane gives murabbab and murabbā for ginger so preserved, under the root r-b-b (Lane). Jam is the later use.

Lesson 2 of 5.
رمان (rummān) — a pomegranate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for vinegar, then the Arabic for jam.

You'll want to know: رمان.
رمان — rummān — "a pomegranate".

The word, taken apart.
رمان (rummān) is built on the native Arabic root r-m-n, "the pomegranate" (Lane). Lane gives it under r-m-n and r-m-m; Hebrew rimmon, "pomegranate", is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
فراولة (farāwila) — strawberries.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for jam, then the Arabic for a pomegranate.

You'll want to know: فراولة.
فراولة — farāwila — "strawberries".

The word, taken apart.
فراولة (farāwila) is a modern loan, usually traced to Italian fragola, "strawberry"; EtymDB links it with Greek fraoula, a borrowing of the same Italian word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
خس (khass) — lettuce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a pomegranate, then the Arabic for strawberries.

You'll want to know: خس.
خس — khass — "lettuce".

The word, taken apart.
خس (khass) is listed by Lane under kh-s-s. Aramaic ḥassā, "lettuce", is the same word (EtymDB), and the Arabic is usually taken as borrowed from it.

باذنجان — bādhinjān — "an aubergine".

باذنجان (bādhinjān) is a loan: Lane derives it from Persian bādinkān. English aubergine comes from the Arabic word through Catalan and French.
