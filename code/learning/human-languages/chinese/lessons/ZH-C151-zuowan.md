## You'll want to know: zuòwán
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C151-ZUOWAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

*zuòwán* — "to finish (doing)".

> *wǒ zuòwán le* — I've finished.

> *nǐ zuòwán le ma* — Have you finished?

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*zuòwán* is built from 做 'to do', the 做 you write, and wán 'to finish'. 做 has no reading in the Guangyun, so its history is not settled. wán is inherited from Middle Chinese *hwan* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'whole; complete'; the regular sound change from *hwan* would give huán, so why it is said wán is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-RESULT-COMPLEMENT-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-LE-CHANGED-STATE-01] -->

做 is the doing; wán is the result: it is done. 了 at the end makes it news: *wǒ zuòwán le*.

Not finished yet takes 没: *wǒ méi zuòwán*.
