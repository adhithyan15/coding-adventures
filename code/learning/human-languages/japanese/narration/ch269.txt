Japanese, chapter 269: A Thief in the Night.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
どろぼう (dorobō) — a thief; a burglar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to help each other, then the Japanese for safe.

You'll want to know: どろぼう.
どろぼう — dorobō — "a thief; a burglar".
どろぼうが うちに はいりました。 — dorobō ga uchi ni hairimashita — "A burglar broke into our house."

The word, taken apart.
どろぼう (dorobō) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: doro, "mud", is native Japanese, and bō, "stick", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese baewngX) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). How the two came together is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ぬすむ (nusumu) — to steal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for safe, then the Japanese for a thief.

You'll want to know: ぬすむ.
ぬすむ — nusumu — "to steal".
どろぼうは なにも ぬすみませんでした。 — dorobō wa nanimo nusumimasen deshita — "The burglar took nothing."
Polite ぬすみます, nusumimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): どろぼうが さいふを ぬすみました。, dorobō ga saifu o nusumimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぬすむ (nusumu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
