Mandarin Chinese, chapter 423: Going South.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
lùkuàng — road conditions.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to head up north, then the Mandarin for high-speed.

You'll want to know: lùkuàng.
lùkuàng — "road conditions".
lùkuàng bù hǎo dehuà wǒmen zuò dìtiě qù — If the roads are bad, we'll take the metro.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lùkuàng is built from 路 'a road' and kuàng 'a state of things', both inherited from Middle Chinese, luH and xjwangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to compare'; the sense 'state of things' is not given there.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
东南 (dōngnán) — southeast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for high-speed, then the Mandarin for road conditions.

You'll want to know: 东南.
东南 — dōngnán — "southeast".
huǒchēzhàn zài jīchǎng de dōngnán fāngxiàng — The railway station is southeast of the airport.
The new character, in pieces: 南 (nán) — nine strokes: 十 on top; under it a frame open at the foot, a vertical down the left and a bar that turns down the right side and hooks; inside it a dot and a short stroke falling to the left, two bars, and a vertical down through them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
东南 (dōngnán) is built from 东 'east' and 南 (nán) 'south', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tuwng and nom (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the direction of fire.

Lesson 3 of 6.
南 (nán) — 十 over a frame.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 十 over a frame and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say dōngnán — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
十 东.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shí, dōng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 十 over a frame.
南 (nán).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The top piece, 十, two strokes. A short bar, then a vertical down through it.
The frame, two strokes. A vertical down the left. Then a bar across that turns down the right side and hooks to the left at the foot: the frame is open at the bottom.
Inside, five strokes. A dot and a short stroke falling to the left, side by side. Two bars, one under the other. Then a vertical from the upper bar down through the lower one.
2 + 2 + 5 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 南 (nán), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 南 (nán)? (Nine.) What sits on top? (十.) Is the frame closed at the foot? (No, it is open.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 南 (nán), pinned stroke paths and medians.

南下 (nánxià) is built from 南 (nán) 'south' and 下 'to go down', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nom and haeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'low' and 'to come down'.
