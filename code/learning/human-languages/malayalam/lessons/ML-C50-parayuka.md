# പറയുക (paṟayuka) — the verb behind the goodbye

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C33-CINTIKKUKA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *ñān cintikkunnu*. One word for the present, whoever is doing it.

[PAUSE 2s] Today's verb runs on the same machine, and you have met it before
without being told what it was.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C50-PARAYUKA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **പറയുക** — *paṟayuka* — **to say, to tell**

> **ഞാൻ പറയുന്നു.** — *ñān paṟayunnu* — "I say."

Past **പറഞ്ഞു** (*paṟaññu*), future *paṟayuṁ*. And like *cintikkunnu*,
**പറയുന്നു is the whole present tense** — one word for *ñān*, *nī* and *avar*
alike.

**You have met this verb once already without being told.** The politeness
chapter printed *paṟayū*, "please tell me" — the respectful request form, stem
plus **-ൂ**. The stem under it is today's word.

It arrives here because the rest of this chapter needs it: Malayalam does not
have a single word for *to say goodbye*. It names the thing being given — a
journey, a leave — and hangs this verb off it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C50-PARAYUKA-01] -->

**പറ** (*paṟa*) is the stem, and the **റ** in it is the hard *ṟ* — made further
back in the mouth than the ordinary *r*, and one of the letters this script
keeps apart where English has a single shape.

Where the other three verbs in this strand came from Sanskrit nouns —
*cintikkuka* from **ചിന്ത**, *cōdikkuka* from a Sanskrit word for pushing — this
one did not. **പറ** is a native Dravidian stem, and it is the one this language reaches for
when somebody speaks, with no other job attached.

**-ഉക** (*-uka*) is the dictionary ending you have seen on every verb in this
book: *aṟiyuka*, *cintikkuka*, *cōdikkuka*, *cērkkuka*.
