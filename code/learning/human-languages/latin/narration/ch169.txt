Latin, chapter 169: To kill, To get used to, To make haste, To move back, To approach.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
interficiō, interficere — to kill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to lend, then the Latin for to propose.

You'll want to know: interficiō, interficere.
interficiō, interficere — "to kill".

The word, taken apart.
interficiō is inter, "between", with faciō, "to make" (Lewis & Short): first to put out of the way, then to kill.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cōnsuēscō, cōnsuēscere — to get used to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to propose, then the Latin for to kill.

You'll want to know: cōnsuēscō, cōnsuēscere.
cōnsuēscō, cōnsuēscere — "to get used to".

The word, taken apart.
cōnsuēscō is con-, "thoroughly", with suēscō, "to become used to" (EtymDB 2.1). suēscō is usually traced to the reflexive swe-, "one's own": to make something one's own.

Lesson 3 of 5.
properō, properāre — to make haste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to kill, then the Latin for to get used to.

You'll want to know: properō, properāre.
properō, properāre — "to make haste".

The word, taken apart.
properō is built on properus, "quick, hasty" (Lewis & Short), which is often explained as pro-, "forward", plus the root of parō, "to make ready".
