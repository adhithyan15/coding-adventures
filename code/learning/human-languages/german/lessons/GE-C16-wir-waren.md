# wir waren — "we were"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-SIND-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-WAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One more form, and the verb is finished — present and past, all of
it.

## You'll want to know: wir waren
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-WAREN-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-SIE-SIND-01] -->

> **wir waren** — "we were."

*War* plus the plural ending **-en**, the one *wir* takes on every verb. And
just as *sind* served three subjects at once, *waren* covers **sie waren**
("they were") and **Sie waren** ("you were," politely) with no change.

The one plural form left over is **ihr wart**, for a group of friends. It takes
the plural-*you* **-t**, and it is the rarest form in the whole verb.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-WAREN-01] -->

English does the identical thing and nobody notices:

| | German | English |
|---|---|---|
| singular | *war* | **was** |
| plural | *waren* | **were** |

An **s** in the singular, an **r** in the plural, in both languages, inherited
from the same ancestor. English *was/were* and German *war/waren* are the same
pair of words.
