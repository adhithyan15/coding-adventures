Persian, chapter 335: The wedding.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
عروسی ('arusi) — a wedding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an engagement, then the Persian for the marriage contract.

You'll want to know: عروسی.
عروسی ('arusi) — "a wedding".
عروسی ('arusi) دوستم تا نیمه شب طول کشید. 'Arusi-ye dustam tâ nime shab tul keshid. My friend's wedding went on until midnight.
Built on 'arus "bride". In speech 'arusi kardan is "to get married".

The word, taken apart.
'arusi adds the Persian suffix -i to 'arus "bride", which is borrowed from Arabic 'arūs (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
عروس ('arus) — a bride; a daughter-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the marriage contract, then the Persian for a wedding.

You'll want to know: عروس.
عروس ('arus) — "a bride; a daughter-in-law".
عروس ('arus) لباس سفید پوشیده بود. 'Arus lebâs-e sefid pushide bud. The bride was wearing a white dress.
It also means "daughter-in-law". The groom is dâmâd.

The word, taken apart.
'arus is borrowed from Arabic 'arūs (EtymDB), which can name either the bride or the groom; in Persian it is the bride.

Lesson 3 of 5.
حنابندان (hanâbandân) — a henna night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a wedding, then the Persian for a bride.

You'll want to know: حنابندان.
حنابندان (hanâbandân) — "a henna night".
شب حنابندان زنها آواز میخواندند. Shab-e hanâbandân zan-hâ âvâz mikhândand. On the henna night the women sang.
The night before the wedding, when henna is put on the bride's hands. hanâ "henna" + band "binding".

The word, taken apart.
hanâbandân joins hanâ "henna", borrowed from Arabic ḥinnā' (EtymDB), and the native Persian band- "to bind" with the suffix -ân. EtymDB lists no entry.

Lesson 4 of 5.
مهریه (mehriye) — a mahr, the bride's marriage gift.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a bride, then the Persian for a henna night.

You'll want to know: مهریه.
مهریه (mehriye) — "a mahr, the bride's marriage gift".
مهریه (mehriye) او صد سکه طلا بود. Mehriye-ye u sad sekke-ye talâ bud. Her mahr was a hundred gold coins.
Promised by the groom in the marriage contract and owed to the wife.

The word, taken apart.
mehriye is built on mehr, the marriage gift, with the ending -iye. mehr is usually traced to Arabic mahr, "marriage portion" (EtymDB lists the Arabic word), though EtymDB also links the Persian word to an Old Persian form, so its origin is not fully settled.

The furniture and household things that the bride's family provides.

The word, taken apart.
jahiziye is built on jahiz "a trousseau", which EtymDB derives from Arabic jihāz, "equipment, outfit", with the ending -iye.
