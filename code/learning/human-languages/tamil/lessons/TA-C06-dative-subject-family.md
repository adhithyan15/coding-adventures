# -ukku, -ku, -ge, -ikku — the family shows its bones

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Tamil says knowing happens **to** you. Its three closest sisters make
the same grammatical choice.

## Grammar Lens: Four sentences, one construction
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-FAMILY-01]; assesses=[] -->

| language | "I know [the language]" | dative |
|---|---|---|
| Tamil | *enakku tamiḻ teriyum* | **-ukku** |
| Telugu | *nāku telugu vaccu* | **-ku** |
| Kannada | *nanage kannaḍa gottu* | **-ge** |
| Malayalam | *enikku malayāḷam aṟiyām* | **-ikku** |

The verbs differ, but the experiencer sits in a dative whose forms visibly
belong together: *-ukku / -ku / -ge / -ikku*. This is family evidence, just as
*blanc/bianco/branco* exposes Romance kinship.

The comparison also makes cross-language practice useful: once you recognize
"to me," you can search for the known or apparent thing rather than forcing an
English subject onto the sentence.

## Guided Practice — the ending underneath
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-UKKU-01, TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-STACKING-01, TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-02] -->

- Say "for the name", then "for work". (***peyarukku***, ***vēlaikku*** — one
  case, two shapes, picked by the noun's ending.) And "to me"? (***enakku*** —
  *nāṉ* changes its body to *en-* first.)
- How is *-ukku* unlike Latin *-īs*? (**One** meaning, seam visible — Tamil
  **stacks**; Latin **fuses** case, number and class.)
- Why the dative for knowing? (Knowing **happens to** you; speaking you **do**.)

## Script — வ, ம and ண
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-01, TA-SCRIPT-MA-RETROFLEX-NA-01, TA-SCRIPT-MA-RETROFLEX-NA-02, TA-SOUND-MA-RETROFLEX-NA-03] -->

> வணக்கம்

- [YOU POINT: வ, then ண, then ம]
- [YOU SAY: plain *n*, then curl the tongue back — **ṇ**]

(**வ**: *va*, one motion, no pen lift. **ம**: upright on the **left**, arch on
the right. **ண**: **two** arches; its dot-*ṇ* is **retroflex**, tongue curled back.)

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01, TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-FAMILY-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Do the four sisters agree on dative experiencers? (**Yes**.) Which
four suffix shapes reveal the relationship? (***-ukku, -ku, -ge, -ikku***.)
What stays plain in the sentence? (The thing **known or experienced**.)
