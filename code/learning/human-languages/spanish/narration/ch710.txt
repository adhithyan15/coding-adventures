Spanish, chapter 710: Los nervios.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
los nervios — nerves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for nostalgia, then the Spanish for to long for.

You'll want to know: los nervios.
los nervios — "nerves".
Antes del examen tengo nervios. I get nervous before the exam.
Tener nervios — to be nervous. ¡Qué nervios! — I am so nervous!

The word, taken apart.
nervios is the plural of nervio, which comes from Latin nervus "sinew" (EtymDB); the sense nerve came later, as in English nerve.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el estrés — stress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to long for, then the Spanish for nerves.

You'll want to know: el estrés.
el estrés — "stress".
El trabajo me causa estrés. Work causes me stress.
Estar estresado — to be stressed. Spanish adds an e- before s + consonant.

The word, taken apart.
estrés was borrowed from English stress, usually dated to the twentieth century; Spanish put an e- in front, as it does with esnob and escáner.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: la ansiedad.
la ansiedad — "anxiety".
La ansiedad no me deja dormir. Anxiety does not let me sleep.
Also eagerness: esperar con ansiedad.

The word, taken apart.
ansiedad is a learned borrowing from Latin anxietās "anxiety" (EtymDB), built on anxius "troubled"; English anxious comes from the same Latin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la tensión — tension; blood pressure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for stress, then the Spanish for anxiety.

You'll want to know: la tensión.
la tensión — "tension; blood pressure".
Había mucha tensión en la reunión. There was a lot of tension in the meeting.
Tomar la tensión — to take someone's blood pressure (Spain); in Latin America la presión.

The word, taken apart.
tensión is a learned borrowing from Latin tēnsiō "a stretching" (EtymDB), from tendere "to stretch".

Lesson 5 of 5.
calmar — to calm, to soothe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for anxiety, then the Spanish for tension.

You'll want to know: calmar.
calmar — "to calm, to soothe".
Esta música me calma. This music calms me.
Calmarse — to calm down. ¡Cálmate! — Calm down!

The word, taken apart.
calmar is built on calma, which comes from Late Latin cauma, from Greek kauma "burning heat" (EtymDB); at sea the hottest hours were the windless ones.
