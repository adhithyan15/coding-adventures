## Added — the first 401 word lessons say where their word comes from

HL24 §9 asks every lesson that introduces a word to say where it comes from, from a cited source, or to say plainly that the origin is not recorded. The first 401 lessons in sequence order that lacked an `etymology_hook`, visible etymology, or both (IT-C01-ciao through IT-C110-pepe) now have both (human-language-data: Italian word lessons without a hook 1126 → 745, without visible etymology 1135 → 745).

- **Sources.** EtymDB 2.1 (Fourrier & Sagot 2020, "Methodological Aspects of Developing and Managing an Etymological Lexical Resource: Introducing EtymDB-2.0", LREC 2020), licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. Where EtymDB has no row for a textbook word, the Latin form and its meaning are cited from Lewis & Short, *A Latin Dictionary* (Perseus Digital Library `lexica`, CC BY-SA 4.0), and only when the derivation is certain. Each headword was looked up, and its inherited, borrowed, derived or compound link was followed one step, and one more where that step was Latin. Every link was checked by hand against the reviewer's own knowledge before it was written. Where the two disagreed, only the agreed part was kept, or the lesson says the origin is not recorded. No dataset file is in the repository. Each statement is a one-line sentence of our own and names its source: "(EtymDB 2.1)", "(Lewis & Short)", "(EtymDB 2.1, French *restaurant*)" when the row that supports it belongs to the donor or sister word, or, where neither source has the word, "EtymDB 2.1 has no row for …" or "its origin is not recorded in EtymDB 2.1". Four well-established origins that neither source confirms carry no source tag: *grigio* ← Frankish *gris*, *regalo* ← Spanish *regalo*, and *semaforo* and *televisore* built from their parts.
- **The visible statement.** A `## The word, taken apart` block (or `## The phrase, taken apart`) follows the block that introduces the word, for example "**braccio** ← Latin *bracchium*, "arm" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1)". The heading uses the corpus's existing etymology prefix because the parser has no block type for "Where it comes from".
- **What the 401 say.** 271 are inherited, 60 borrowed (from French, Old French, Old Provençal, Frankish, Arabic, Spanish, Portuguese, Lombardic, English, and learned borrowings from Latin), and 58 built from parts (*occhiali* from *occhio*, *telefonino* from *telefono*, *panino* from *pane*, *biscotto* from *bis-* and "cooked", phrases from their words). 12 still say "Its origin is not recorded in EtymDB 2.1". All 12 are genuinely uncertain: *stanco*, *bambino*, *bambina*, *mucca*, *piccolo*, *vasca*, *pentola*, *scarpe*, *manzo* and *cetriolo*, plus *scatola* and *poltrona*, where EtymDB's account could not be confirmed. *piccolo* adds the cognate EtymDB lists, Portuguese *pequeno*.
- **How the origins EtymDB lacks were filled.** EtymDB has no row for 19 of these words (*malato* among them), but it derives their sister words from the same Latin etymon, and the lesson says so. An example: "**fame** ← Latin *fames*, "hunger" — inherited. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for *fame*; it derives French *faim* and Romanian *foame* from *fames*." 26 cite the Latin form from Lewis & Short, for example *settimo* ← *septimus*, *penna* ← *penna*, *pesca* ← *persicum* and *dentifricio* ← *dentifricium*. Ten built words with no row say "the parts are plain to see".
- **Rows rejected.** Forty-one EtymDB rows or labels were rejected rather than repeated. Some named the wrong parent: *volere* ← *volare* "to fly", *vecchio* ← *vitulus* "calf", *contento* ← *contemptus*, *gatto* ← the prefix *re-*, *ballare* ← *ballo* "I throw" (the source is *ballizein*), and *gonna* ← Greek *gouna* (the Greek came from Latin). Some labelled a loan as inherited: *burro* (from Old French *burre*), *albergo* (Germanic *hariberg-*), *limone*, *tazza*, *cioccolato*, and the learned *economico* and *colazione*. *biscotto* is labelled built from parts, not inherited. The rest were junk glosses.
- **Hooks.** Each lesson's `etymology_hook` (≤120 characters) says the same thing as its visible block. A cognate is added only where it is certain (English *bracelet* for *braccio*). Eleven opening lessons that already told the story in their body (*ciao*, *buono*, *giorno*, the three chapter practices and others) gained only the hook. Twenty that had a hook but nothing printed gained only the block.
- **`roots:`** 236 lessons gained a Latin etymon slug. 87 reuse a slug that another lesson already uses, so cousins join (`fortis-latin`, `latin-camera`, `filius`, `novus-latin`). 149 are new `<lemma>-latin` slugs. `check:root-slug-splits` reports no new split.
- **Durations.** The new blocks add about 9 narrated seconds per lesson. Three statements were kept short to stay under 300 s. Nine lessons had `max_seconds` raised to their computed length, and nothing was cut: IT-C02-come-sta 218 → 245, IT-C16-passato-prossimo-essere 233 → 250, IT-C02-come-va 250 → 275, IT-C29-vero 250 → 290, IT-C30-non-capisco 250 → 295, IT-C31-che-buono 250 → 280, IT-C34-signore-signora 250 → 299, IT-C34-per-me 250 → 295, IT-C03-practice 237 → 245. Only come-sta and passato-prossimo-essere were pushed over by the new block. The other seven were already computed above their declared budget, and IT-C03-practice gained only a hook.
