Gujarati, chapter 344: Proof.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
દાવો (dāvo) — a claim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for in agreement, then the Gujarati for to lean towards.

You'll want to know: દાવો.
દાવો — dāvo — "a claim".
દુકાનદારનો દાવો છે કે માલ અસલી છે. dukāndārno dāvo che ke māl aslī che. The shopkeeper claims the goods are genuine.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
દાવો (dāvo) is usually traced to Arabic daʿwā, "a claim, a lawsuit", through Persian.

તમારા દાવાની સાબિતી ક્યાં છે? tamārā dāvānī sābitī kyā̃ che? Where is the proof of your claim?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a claim, then the Gujarati for proof.

ખાતરી — khātrī — "an assurance; being sure".
મને ખાતરી છે કે તે સમયસર આવશે. mane khātrī che ke te samaysar āvśe. I am sure he will come on time.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ખાતરી (khātrī) is usually traced to Persian khātir, "the mind, regard", a word of Arabic origin, with the Gujarati ending -ī; "being sure" is the Gujarati sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for proof, then the Gujarati for an assurance.

You'll want to know: સંદેહ.
સંદેહ — sandeh — "uncertainty; a lingering doubt".
આ પરિણામ સાચું છે તેમાં કોઈ સંદેહ નથી. ā pariṇām sācũ che temā̃ koī sandeh nathī. There is no doubt that this result is right.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંદેહ (sandeh) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit saṃdeha, "a doubt, uncertainty" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
દર્શાવવું (darśāvvũ) — to indicate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an assurance, then the Gujarati for uncertainty.

You'll want to know: દર્શાવવું.
દર્શાવવું — darśāvvũ — "to indicate".
આ ફોટો દર્શાવે છે કે દુકાન રાત્રે ખુલ્લી હતી. ā phoṭo darśāve che ke dukān rātre khullī hatī. This photo shows that the shop was open at night.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે સાબિતી દર્શાવી, teṇe sābitī darśāvī.

The word, taken apart.
દર્શાવવું (darśāvvũ) is built on Sanskrit darśana, "seeing, showing" (Monier-Williams), with the Gujarati causative ending -āvvũ: to make something seen.
