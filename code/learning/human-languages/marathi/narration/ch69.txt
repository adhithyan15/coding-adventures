Marathi, chapter 69: This One and That One.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
हा / ही / हे (hā / hī / he) — "this".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three nouns, three genders. मित्र is masculine, खोली is feminine, घर is neuter. Say all three before you turn the page.

You'll want to know: हा, ही, हे.
हा hā, ही hī, हे he — this.
English has one word for this. Marathi has three, and which one you use is decided by the noun, not by you:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
हा मित्र hā mitra, this friend.
ही खोली hī khoolī, this room.
हे घर he ghar, this house.
You have made this choice before. माझा and माझी split the same way, on the same two endings — -आ for a masculine noun, -ई for a feminine one. So the first two rows are a habit you already have, pointed at a new word.

Grammar Lens: it goes in front, and it never stands alone here.
हा sits before its noun — hā mitra, never mitra hā. That is the same slot माझा and डावा take, so the front of a Marathi noun is becoming a place you know: the words that describe it queue up there.
And because the ending is chosen by the noun, the word tells you the gender of anything that follows it. Meet a noun you have never read, with ही in front of it, and you already know one true thing about it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: hā mitra, hī khoolī, he ghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which of the three endings matches माझा]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: where the word sits — before the noun or after it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Say this house and this room, then say which word in each told you the gender.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you say this room? (ही खोली.) Who chooses between हा, ही and हे — you, or the noun? (The noun.) And where does the word go? (In front.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
तो / ती / ते (to / tī / te) — "that".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say this house, this room, this friend. Keep the three endings in your ear; only their front is about to change.

You'll want to know: तो, ती, ते.
तो to, ती tī, ते te — that.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
near: हा मित्र. far: तो मित्र.
near: ही खोली. far: ती खोली.
near: हे घर. far: ते घर.
Only the first letter moved. ह- became त-, and the three endings did not budge, because they were never about distance — they were about the noun.
That is the whole of the second lesson. You learned six words by learning four.

Why it's said this way.
One warning worth having before you meet ते on a page.
You have already read ते in this book, and it was not this word. In एक ते पाच — ek te pāc — it means "one to five", a stretch between two numbers. Same three letters, different job.
Nothing in the shape tells them apart. What tells them apart is what sits beside it: a noun after it makes it that; two numbers around it make it to. Marathi will do this to you again, and the habit of reading the neighbours rather than the word alone is what gets you through it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: to mitra, tī khoolī, te ghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: each near word and its far partner, as a pair]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the other job ते does, and how you would tell which is which]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Say this room, then that room, then say what changed.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you say that house? (ते घर.) What is the only difference between this room and that room? (The first letter.) And what else can ते mean? (To, between two numbers.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
हा, तो — two rows, and what the far one is really for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the six words, near row first, in the order masculine, feminine, neuter.

Grammar Lens: two rows, not three.
Marathi points in two directions and stops. ह- is here; त- is not here. There is no third word for that one over there, further away.
Spanish needs three — este, ese, aquel — and English is unstable about it, keeping yonder alive only in corners. Marathi draws the line once, between what is at hand and what is not, and everything not at hand shares one row.
That is fewer words to learn and one fewer decision to make while speaking.

Grammar Lens: the far row is also he, she and it.
Here is the part that matters most, and it is not about pointing at all.
तो — that — is also he. ती — that — is also she. ते — that — is also it.
Marathi does not keep a separate set of third-person pronouns. It reuses the far row. Whoever is not you and not the person you are speaking to is, by default, the one over there.
So this chapter did more than teach pointing:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
मी, I.
तू / तुम्ही, you.
तो / ती / ते (to / tī / te), he / she / it.
That is the whole set of people a sentence can be about, and until now you had the first two rows and not the third.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: how many directions Marathi points in]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: to, and then both things it can mean]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three rows of people — I, you, and the third]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say I, then you, then he and she — using the words this chapter gave you for the third.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many directions does Marathi point in? (Two — here and not here.) How do you say she? (ती — the same word as that.) And does Marathi have a separate word for he? (No — it reuses the far row.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
(this one and that one) (hā ani to) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Draw the grid from memory: two rows, three columns.

Grammar Lens: four letters, six words.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
near. masculine: हा. feminine: ही.
far. masculine: तो. feminine: ती.
The neuter completes each row — हे and ते — and the grid is done.
Two fronts and three endings make six words. The front carries the distance and is yours to choose; the ending carries the gender and is the noun's. Neither decision touches the other, which is why six words cost you four.
All six sit in front of their noun, in the slot माझा and डावा already use.

What you've built.
Pointing, and the third person.
तो, ती and ते are he, she and it as well as that. Before this chapter a Marathi sentence could be about you or about the person you were speaking to, and about nobody else. Now it can be about anyone.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the near row, then the far row]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: this room and that room]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: he, she, it — without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which part of each word the noun decided]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
From cold: this house and that house, then say how you would say she.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pointing rows does Marathi have? (Two.) Say that room. (ती खोली.) And what else do तो, ती and ते do? (They are he, she and it.)
