# माम् (mām) — me, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE, SA-REL-SAH-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **सः पश्यति** (*saḥ paśyati*), “he sees”. Keep the seeing
verb. Now make the person seen be the speaker.

## You'll want to know: माम्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01]; assesses=[] -->

**माम्** (*mām*) means “me” when I am the object of the action.

> **सः माम् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ mām paśyati.*
>
> **He sees me.**

The verb still says who does the seeing. **माम्** names the person the seeing
reaches.

Whitney's *Sanskrit Grammar* §491 lists **माम्** as the independent accusative
singular of the first-person pronoun. “Accusative” is the case label; “the
object” is the job you can hear in this sentence.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **माम्** comes from the first-person pronoun paradigm;
it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit Grammar*
§491 records it as the independent accusative singular. This lesson makes no
deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
