German, chapter 462: Ein Unwetter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Unwetter — the severe weather, the violent storm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the sunny spell, then the German for to clear up.

You'll want to know: das Unwetter.
das Unwetter — "the severe weather, the violent storm".
Nach dem Unwetter liegen Äste auf der Straße. After the storm there are branches lying in the road.
Same form in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
Unwetter is inherited from Middle High German unweter 'bad weather' (EtymDB), built from un- 'bad, not' and Wetter 'weather'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
aufziehen — to come up, to gather (of a storm); to bring up (a child).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to clear up, then the German for the severe weather.

You'll want to know: aufziehen.
aufziehen — "to come up, to gather (of a storm); to bring up (a child)".
Am Abend zieht ein Gewitter auf. In the evening a thunderstorm comes up.
Separable; Perfekt with sein for weather. Kinder aufziehen — to bring up children (with haben).

The word, taken apart.
aufziehen is built from auf 'up' and ziehen 'to pull, to move', inherited from Proto-Germanic teuhaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Hagel — the hail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the severe weather, then the German for to come up.

You'll want to know: der Hagel.
der Hagel — "the hail".
Der Hagel hat viele Autos beschädigt. The hail damaged lots of cars.
No plural. Die Hagelkörner — hailstones.

The word, taken apart.
Hagel is inherited from Old High German hagal, and English hail is a cousin. EtymDB lists the verb hageln from Middle High German hagelen.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Es blitzt und donnert die ganze Nacht. There is thunder and lightning all night.

donnern is built on Donner 'thunder' (EtymDB lists the verb), inherited from Proto-Germanic þunraz; English thunder is a cousin.
