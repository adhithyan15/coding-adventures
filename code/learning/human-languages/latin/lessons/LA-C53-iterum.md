## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C53-SCHOOL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**iterum** — "**again**."

This one word closes the largest hole in this book. The *quaesō* lesson gave you "please"; the eight-verb chapter gave you **dīcō**,
"I say." Fifty-two chapters went by without a way of joining them, and here it is:

> **Iterum, quaesō.** — Again, please.
> **Quid dīcis?** — What are you saying?
> **Lentē, quaesō.** — Slowly, please.

That is a **repair**: the move you make when you did not catch what somebody said.
Every language teaches it in its first week, and this book — which could greet,
thank, apologise and say please within four chapters — could not until now ask anyone to
repeat anything.

*Iterum* is built on the same ***\*i-*** stem as **is** from the pointing chapter, with an
old comparative suffix: literally "the other of the two," hence "a second time."
English **iterate** and **reiteration** are loans from it.
