5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बकरी (bakrī) — a goat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a cow, then the Marwadi for a buffalo.

You'll want to know: बकरी.
बकरी — bakrī — "a goat".

The word, taken apart.
बकरी (bakrī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from barkara, "kid, lamb; goat". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 9153); the match is through Hindi bakrī.

Lesson 4 of 5.
नाहर (nāhar) — a lion, a tiger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a sheep, then the Marwadi for a sparrow.

You'll want to know: नाहर.
नाहर — nāhar — "a lion, a tiger".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Hindi nāhar, "tiger", under two reconstructions, nakhadara, "tearing with the claws" (CDIAL 6919), and nakharin, "having claws" (CDIAL 6921). Both are built on Sanskrit nakha, "claw"; which one is the source is not settled, and CDIAL gives no Marwari form.

Lesson 5 of 5.
हाथी (hāthī) — an elephant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a sparrow, then the Marwadi for a lion.

You'll want to know: हाथी.
हाथी — hāthī — "an elephant".

The word, taken apart.
हाथी (hāthī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from hastin, "having hands; elephant". CDIAL lists Marwari hāthī under it (CDIAL 14039). CDIAL asks whether the Sanskrit word reshaped an older karin, perhaps of non-Aryan origin, tied to hasta, "hand", by popular etymology.
