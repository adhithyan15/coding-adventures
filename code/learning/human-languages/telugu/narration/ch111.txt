Telugu, chapter 111: Comb, Soap, Towel, Glasses, Clock.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
దువ్వెన (duvvena) — a comb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a nail, then the Telugu for scissors.

You'll want to know: దువ్వెన.
దువ్వెన — duvvena — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
దువ్వెన (duvvena) is native Dravidian. DEDR 689 lists duvvu, "to comb", and duvvena, "a comb", with Tamil uḻu, "to arrange the hair".

Lesson 4 of 5.
కళ్ళజోడు (kaḷḷajōḍu) — glasses, spectacles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for soap, then the Telugu for a towel.

You'll want to know: కళ్ళజోడు.
కళ్ళజోడు — kaḷḷajōḍu — "glasses, spectacles".

The word, taken apart.
కళ్ళజోడు (kaḷḷajōḍu) is native kaḷḷu, "eyes", the plural of kannu (DEDR 1159), and jōḍu, "pair", a word from the north (Hindi joṛ): "a pair for the eyes".

Lesson 5 of 5.
గడియారం (gaḍiyāraṁ) — a clock, a watch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a towel, then the Telugu for glasses.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the first Telugu month, చైత్రం, and the last, ఫాల్గుణం. Then say ఆదివారం, the day of the beginning.

You'll want to know: గడియారం.
గడియారం — gaḍiyāraṁ — "a clock, a watch".

The word, taken apart.
గడియారం (gaḍiyāraṁ) is a loan from Hindustani ghaṛiyāl, "gong, clock", from Sanskrit ghaṭikā, the little pot that measured time as a water-clock.
