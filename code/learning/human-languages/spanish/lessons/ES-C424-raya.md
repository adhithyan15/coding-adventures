## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C424-04]; assesses=[] -->

**la raya** — "the dash." A *raya* is a line or a stripe, and it is longer than
the hyphen, which is a different mark with a different job.

In printed Spanish dialogue, a **raya opens each turn of speech**, and nobody is
named:

> — ¿Cómo estás?
> — Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?
> — Muy bien.

Three turns, two speakers, no *he said* and no quotation marks. The reader
tracks who is talking by alternation alone.

## Grammar Lens: what replaces the quotation marks
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-04] -->

Set the two traditions side by side, because a reading paper prints the Spanish
one and an English-trained eye looks for the wrong thing:

| | English | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| opens a turn | "Hello," | — Hola. |
| names the speaker | he said | often nobody |
| new speaker | new paragraph | new paragraph |

An English reader hunting for quotation marks in a Spanish story finds none and
concludes there is no dialogue. There is; it is marked with **la raya**.
