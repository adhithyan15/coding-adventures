Sanskrit, chapter 461: Good People and Bad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
महात्मा (mahātmā) — a great soul; a noble person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a virtue, then the Sanskrit for wicked.

You'll want to know: महात्मा.
महात्मा — mahātmā — "a great soul; a noble person".
महात्मा दरिद्रस्य साहाय्यं करोति। mahātmā daridrasya sāhāyyaṃ karoti. A noble soul helps the poor.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
महात्मा (mahātmā) is built from महत्, "great", and आत्मा, "the self, the soul"; MW gives "high-souled, magnanimous, noble" (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
खलः (khalaḥ) — a scoundrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for wicked, then the Sanskrit for a great soul.

You'll want to know: खलः.
खलः — khalaḥ — "a scoundrel".
खलः सज्जनं पीडयति। khalaḥ sajjanaṃ pīḍayati. A scoundrel torments a good man.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
खलः (khalaḥ) MW's first khala is "a threshing-floor"; a later sense is "a mischievous man", and for it MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
सौजन्यम् (saujanyam) — goodness; kindness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a great soul, then the Sanskrit for a scoundrel.

You'll want to know: सौजन्यम्.
सौजन्यम् — saujanyam — "goodness; kindness".
तस्य सौजन्येन सर्वे प्रसन्नाः भवन्ति। tasya saujanyena sarve prasannāḥ bhavanti. Everyone is pleased by his kindness.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
सौजन्यम् (saujanyam) is built on su-jana, "a good person" (su, "good", and जनः), with the lengthening of its first vowel; MW gives "goodness, kindness, benevolence" (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
निष्ठुरः (niṣṭhuraḥ) — heartless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a scoundrel, then the Sanskrit for goodness.

You'll want to know: निष्ठुरः.
निष्ठुरः — niṣṭhuraḥ — "heartless".
निष्ठुरः स्वामी सेवकं ताडयति। niṣṭhuraḥ svāmī sevakaṃ tāḍayati. The heartless master beats his servant.

The word, taken apart.
निष्ठुरः (niṣṭhuraḥ) MW asks, with a question mark, whether it comes from ni and the root √sthā, "to stand" (the root of तिष्ठति), so its origin is uncertain; it gives "hard, harsh, severe, cruel" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
द्रुह्यति (druhyati) — bears malice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for goodness, then the Sanskrit for heartless.

You'll want to know: द्रुह्यति.
द्रुह्यति — druhyati — "bears malice".
खलः सज्जनाय द्रुह्यति। khalaḥ sajjanāya druhyati. The scoundrel bears malice towards the good man.
Past (he …): सः मित्राय द्रुग्धवान्, saḥ mitrāya drugdhavān.

The word, taken apart.
द्रुह्यति (druhyati) comes from the root √druh, which takes the dative of the person harmed (MW).
