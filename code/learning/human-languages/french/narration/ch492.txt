French, chapter 492: Familles d'aujourd'hui.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
recomposé — blended (of a family).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to break up, then the French for break-up.

You'll want to know: recomposé.
recomposé — "blended (of a family)".
Nous sommes une famille recomposée avec cinq enfants. We are a blended family with five children.
Une famille recomposée — parents and children from earlier unions living together.

The word, taken apart.
recomposé is built from re- and composé, from composer "to put together", which goes back to Latin compōnere, reshaped after poser; both rest on pōnere "to put" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: la fratrie.
la fratrie — "siblings (as a group)".
Dans ma fratrie, nous sommes quatre. There are four of us siblings.
A useful word with no plain everyday English match: all the brothers and sisters of one family.

The word, taken apart.
la fratrie is a modern learned word built on Latin frāter "brother" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la famille nombreuse — large family.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for adoption, then the French for siblings.

You'll want to know: la famille nombreuse.
la famille nombreuse — "large family".
Elle vient d'une famille nombreuse de sept enfants. She comes from a large family of seven children.
In France, from three children on; such families get some discounts.

The word, taken apart.
la famille nombreuse joins famille, a learned borrowing from Latin familia "household", and nombreux "numerous", from Latin numerōsus, built on numerus "number" (Lewis & Short).
