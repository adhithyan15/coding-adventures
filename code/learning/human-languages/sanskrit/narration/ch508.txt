Sanskrit, chapter 508: Bricks and Mortar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
इष्टका (iṣṭakā) — a brick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for blows on a fire, then the Sanskrit for an ornament.

You'll want to know: इष्टका.
इष्टका — iṣṭakā — "a brick".
कर्मकरः इष्टकाः आनयति। karmakaraḥ iṣṭakāḥ ānayati. The worker brings the bricks.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
इष्टका (iṣṭakā) is the word MW records for a brick, first a brick of the sacrificial altar; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
चिनोति (cinoti) — piles up; builds up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an ornament, then the Sanskrit for a brick.

You'll want to know: चिनोति.
चिनोति — cinoti — "piles up; builds up".
कर्मकरः इष्टकाः चिनोति। karmakaraḥ iṣṭakāḥ cinoti. The worker stacks the bricks.
Past (he …): सः भित्तिं चितवान्, saḥ bhittiṃ citavān.

The word, taken apart.
चिनोति (cinoti) comes from the root √ci, "to arrange in a row, to pile up, to build"; its past participle is cita (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a brick, then the Sanskrit for piles up.

You'll want to know: निर्माणम्.
निर्माणम् — nirmāṇam — "construction".
नगरे नवस्य सेतोः निर्माणं भवति। nagare navasya setoḥ nirmāṇaṃ bhavati. A new bridge is being built in the city.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
निर्माणम् (nirmāṇam) is built on nis, "out", and the root √mā, "to measure, to form"; MW gives nirmāṇa as forming, making, building (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सन्दधाति (sandadhāti) — mends; puts together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for piles up, then the Sanskrit for construction.

You'll want to know: सन्दधाति.
सन्दधाति — sandadhāti — "mends; puts together".
सः जीर्णं कटं सन्दधाति। saḥ jīrṇaṃ kaṭaṃ sandadhāti. He mends the old mat.
Past (he …): सः जीर्णं कटं संहितवान्, saḥ jīrṇaṃ kaṭaṃ saṃhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
सन्दधाति (sandadhāti) is built on sam, "together", and the root √dhā, "to put"; MW gives saṃdhā as to put together, to join, and also to mend, to restore; its past participle is saṃhita (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सुधा (sudhā) — lime plaster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for construction, then the Sanskrit for mends.

You'll want to know: सुधा.
सुधा — sudhā — "lime plaster".
भित्तिः सुधया श्वेता भवति। bhittiḥ sudhayā śvetā bhavati. The wall turns white with lime plaster.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सुधा (sudhā) is built on su, "good", and the root √dhe, "to drink": MW gives sudhā first as nectar, the drink of the gods, and also as whitewash, plaster and mortar (MW).
