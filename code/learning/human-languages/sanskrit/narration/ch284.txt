Sanskrit, chapter 284: If It Rains.
4 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
यदि … तर्हि … (yadi … tarhi …) — if, then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "if" and "then, in that case". (yadi, tarhi.) You have both words; this chapter puts them to work.

You'll want to know: यदि … तर्हि.
यदि (yadi), "if", opens the condition; तर्हि (tarhi), "then", opens what follows from it:
यदि वर्षा भवति, yadi varṣā bhavati, तर्हि अहं गृहे तिष्ठामि। tarhi ahaṃ gṛhe tiṣṭhāmi. — "If it rains, I stay at home."
यदि सः आगच्छति, yadi saḥ āgacchati, तर्हि वयं खादामः। tarhi vayaṃ khādāmaḥ. — "If he comes, we eat."

You'll want to know: if not, and the condition second.
"If not" puts न before the verb of the यदि half:
यदि सः न आगच्छति, yadi saḥ na āgacchati, तर्हि अहं गच्छामि। tarhi ahaṃ gacchāmi. — "If he does not come, I go."
The condition can also come second, with no तर्हि:
अहं गच्छामि, यदि सः न आगच्छति। ahaṃ gacchāmi, yadi saḥ na āgacchati. — "I go if he does not come."

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If he comes, we read" — yadi saḥ āgacchati, tarhi vayaṃ paṭhāmaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If he comes, we play" — yadi saḥ āgacchati, tarhi vayaṃ krīḍāmaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that if it rains you stay at home, then that you go if he does not come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If"? (यदि.) "Then"? (तर्हि.) "If he does not come"? (यदि सः न आगच्छति.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
यदि वर्षा भविष्यति (yadi varṣā bhaviṣyati) — if it happens, it will follow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if it rains, I stay at home". (yadi varṣā bhavati, tarhi ahaṃ gṛhe tiṣṭhāmi.) That is what happens every time. Now say what will happen tomorrow.

You'll want to know: the future in both halves.
For one real thing ahead, both halves take the future, as Lanman's vocabulary shows for यदि:
यदि वर्षा भविष्यति, yadi varṣā bhaviṣyati, तर्हि अहं गृहे स्थास्यामि। tarhi ahaṃ gṛhe sthāsyāmi. — "If it rains, I will stay at home."
यदि समयः भविष्यति, yadi samayaḥ bhaviṣyati, तर्हि अहम् आगमिष्यामि। tarhi aham āgamiṣyāmi. — "If there is time, I will come."
यदि भवान् आगमिष्यति, yadi bhavān āgamiṣyati, तर्हि मीरा प्रसन्ना भविष्यति। tarhi mīrā prasannā bhaviṣyati. — "If you come, Mira will be pleased."

You'll want to know: if …, then do this.
After a condition in the present, the second half can ask or advise, with -तु or -एत्:
यदि भवान् रुग्णः अस्ति, yadi bhavān rugṇaḥ asti, तर्हि वैद्यं गच्छतु। tarhi vaidyaṃ gacchatu. — "If you are ill, please go to the doctor."
यदि धूमयानं गतम्, yadi dhūmayānaṃ gatam, तर्हि भवान् लोकयानेन गच्छेत्। tarhi bhavān lokayānena gacchet. — "If the train has gone, you should go by bus."
लोकयानेन (lokayānena) is "by bus": -ena, "by", as in मतेन.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If there is time, I will read" — yadi samayaḥ bhaviṣyati, tarhi ahaṃ paṭhiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If there is time, I will write" — yadi samayaḥ bhaviṣyati, tarhi ahaṃ lekhiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Say that if it rains tomorrow you will stay at home, then advise a friend's father to go to the doctor if he is ill.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home"? (यदि श्वः वर्षा भविष्यति, तर्हि अहं गृहे स्थास्यामि.) Which form goes in both halves? (The future.) "If you are ill, please go to the doctor"? (यदि भवान् रुग्णः अस्ति, तर्हि वैद्यं गच्छतु.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
अन्यथा (anyathā) — otherwise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "if there is time, I will come". (yadi samayaḥ bhaviṣyati, tarhi aham āgamiṣyāmi.) Then say "quickly". (śīghram.)

You'll want to know: अन्यथा.
अन्यथा — anyathā — "otherwise": if not, then this.
शीघ्रं गच्छतु, śīghraṃ gacchatu, अन्यथा धूमयानं गमिष्यति। anyathā dhūmayānaṃ gamiṣyati. — "Go quickly, otherwise the train will leave."
कृपया शीघ्रम् आगच्छतु, kṛpayā śīghram āgacchatu, अन्यथा अहं गमिष्यामि। anyathā ahaṃ gamiṣyāmi. — "Please come quickly, otherwise I will leave."

The word, taken apart.
अन्यथा (anyathā) is built on अन्य (anya), "other", with -thā, "in the way of": "in another way". Monier-Williams gives it as "otherwise, in a different manner", and also "in the contrary case", the sense here.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does अन्यथा (anyathā) mean? ("Otherwise".) "Otherwise I will leave"? (अन्यथा (anyathā) अहं गमिष्यामि.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — if, and otherwise.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.

Say "the train goes faster than the bus". (dhūmayānaṃ lokayānāpekṣayā śīghraṃ gacchati.) Then "from morning until night". (prātaḥ ārabhya rātriparyantam.)

यदि or तर्हि, to open the condition? (यदि.) For tomorrow, which form in both halves? (The future.) Now the other way round: what does अन्यथा (anyathā) add? ("Otherwise": what happens if not.)
