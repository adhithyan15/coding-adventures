# पण (paṇ) — but

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-JOIN-AANI, MR-JOIN-KINVA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two joining words are yours: **आणि** adds, **किंवा** offers. Say
**चहा आणि दूध**, then **चहा किंवा दूध**. Both are friendly. Neither can
disagree with anything.

## You'll want to know: पण
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-JOIN-PAN]; assesses=[MR-LEX-NAHI-01] -->

> **पण** — *paṇ* — **but**

> **चहा आहे पण दूध नाही.** — *chahā āhe paṇ dūdh nāhī.* —
> **There is tea but there is no milk.**

**पण** stands in the same slot as the other two and does the one thing they
cannot: it lets the second half push back against the first. Until you have it,
every sentence you can build only agrees with itself.

**पण** and **पुन्हा** — the *again* in **पुन्हा भेटू** — are the same Sanskrit
word, **पुनर्** (*punar*), "again, back." One descendant kept the time meaning;
the other kept only the *back*, and turned it into contradiction. English does
this too: *but* began as *be-utan*, "outside."
