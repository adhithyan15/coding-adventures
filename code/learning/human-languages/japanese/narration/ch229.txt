Japanese, chapter 229: At the Company.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
かいしゃ (kaisha) — a company; a workplace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to quit, then the Japanese for graduation.

You'll want to know: かいしゃ.
かいしゃ — kaisha — "a company; a workplace".
ちちは まいにち かいしゃに いきます。 — chichi wa mainichi kaisha ni ikimasu — "My father goes to the company every day."

The word, taken apart.
かいしゃ (kaisha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "meet" (Middle Chinese hwajH) and sha, "company, association" (dzyaeX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
つとめる (tsutomeru) — to be employed; to work for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a meeting, then the Japanese for an office.

You'll want to know: つとめる.
つとめる — tsutomeru — "to be employed; to work for".
あには らいねん ぎんこうに つとめます。 — ani wa rainen ginkō ni tsutomemasu — "Next year my elder brother will work for a bank."
Polite つとめます, tsutomemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
つとめる (tsutomeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
