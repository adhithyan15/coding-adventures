Persian, chapter 449: Following a route.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مسیریاب (masiryâb) — a satnav, a GPS; a route-finding app.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an underpass, then the Persian for a flyover.

You'll want to know: مسیریاب.
مسیریاب (masiryâb) — "a satnav, a GPS; a route-finding app".
بدون مسیریاب در این شهر گم میشویم. Bedun-e masiryâb dar in shahr gom mishavim. Without a satnav we get lost in this town.
masir 'route' + yâb 'finding', from yâftan.

The word, taken apart.
masiryâb is a modern compound of masir 'route', a loan usually traced to Arabic masīr, and native yâb, the present stem of yâftan 'to find'.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: دنبال کردن.
دنبال کردن (donbâl kardan) — "to follow".
ما ماشین دوستمان را تا روستا دنبال کردیم. Mâ mâshin-e dustemân râ tâ rustâ donbâl kardim. We followed our friend's car to the village.
donbâl 'behind, the tail' + kardan. Also to look for: donbâl-e kelid migardam.

The word, taken apart.
donbâl kardan is built from native donbâl 'tail, the back', whose older history is not settled, and kardan 'to do', inherited from Middle Persian kardan (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
گذشتن (gozashtan) — to pass (by, through); to go by (of time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to follow, then the Persian for to approach.

You'll want to know: گذشتن.
گذشتن (gozashtan) — "to pass (by, through); to go by (of time)".
اتوبوس از کنار دریا گذشت. Otobus az kenâr-e daryâ gozasht. The bus went past the sea.
Present stem gozar-: migozarad. Of time: do sâl gozasht — two years went by.

The word, taken apart.
gozashtan is inherited: EtymDB traces it to a Middle Persian verb (written wtštn') meaning 'to pass, to cross, to die'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
کوچه پس کوچه (kuche-pas-kuche) — the back streets, a maze of lanes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to approach, then the Persian for to pass.

You'll want to know: کوچه پس کوچه.
کوچه پس کوچه (kuche-pas-kuche) — "the back streets, a maze of lanes".
در کوچه پس کوچه های شهر قدیمی قدم زدیم. Dar kuche-pas-kuche-hâ-ye shahr-e qadimi qadam zadim. We walked through the back streets of the old town.
Literally 'lane behind lane'. Usually in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
kuche-pas-kuche is a native compound of kuche 'lane', from ku 'street', and pas 'behind', inherited from Middle Persian pas (EtymDB): lane after lane.
