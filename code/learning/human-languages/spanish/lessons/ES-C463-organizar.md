# organizar — to put it together

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C349-CLOCK-20, ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la agenda** and **encargar**. Somebody has to put the whole
thing together, and that is the verb next.

## You'll want to know: organizar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C463-GUIA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03] -->

**organizar** — to organise, to arrange, to put on.

*Organizar una excursión* — to arrange a trip. *Lo organiza el ayuntamiento* —
the town hall is putting it on. *Organízate* — get yourself sorted.

**La organización** is both the act and the body that does it, the same double
sense as *la instalación*.

## Grammar Lens: the z that becomes c
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C463-GUIA-01, ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02] -->

You met this with *rechacé*. Here it is as a rule you can apply everywhere:

| verb | I did it |
|---|---|
| organizar | organi**c**é |
| empezar | empe**c**é |
| rechazar | recha**c**é |

Spanish almost never writes **ze** — outside a short list of oddities like
*zeta* and *zigzag* — and in a verb ending it never does. Wherever an ending
would put an *e* after the *z*, the *z* becomes a **c**, and the sound does not
change at all, because *z* and *c*-before-*e* are the same sound already.

It is a spelling rule and nothing more, which is why every *-zar* verb in the
language obeys it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C463-GUIA-01] -->

Greek **órganon**, a tool or an instrument — from *érgon*, **work**.

So an *organ* is a **working part**, and to organise is to arrange the working
parts so they function together. The body's **órganos** are named the same way:
each one is a tool doing a job.

*Érgon* is worth keeping, because it is in English **energy** (work within),
**surgery** — through *kheirourgía*, hand-work — and **ergonomic**. And the
musical organ is the same Greek noun: an instrument, a tool for making sound.

Spanish took the word from Greek by way of Latin, which is why it kept its
shape rather than wearing down.
