# ई — the long ī, standing alone

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C283-IDRSAH-01, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-210, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-224] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: say **ईदृशः** (*īdṛśaḥ*), "such". Then name the sign **ी** and the letter **इ**. (*ī*, *i*.)

[PAUSE 2s] The long sign **ी** has a standing form, and **ईदृशः** opens with it.

## Script you'll notice: ई
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-229]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-210, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-224] -->

**ई** — *ī*.

It is an **independent vowel**: **इ** (*i*) made long. On a consonant the same vowel is the sign **ी**, as in **नदी** (*nadī*).

What it is made of:

- the continuous body of इ
- a separate upper curl
- the top shirorekhā

You have met it here:

- **ईदृशः** *īdṛśaḥ* — such

## Writing: ई
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-229] -->

- **1.** descend the upright, turn through both bowls, and finish down-right through the tail without lifting
- **2.** lift at the headline junction and sweep the upper curl upward, left, and around to the right
- **3.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 2.** The pen comes up 2 times and no more.

> Verified modern printed teaching form; everyday handwriting may simplify the curves.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari ई stroke order.svg’, panels 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
