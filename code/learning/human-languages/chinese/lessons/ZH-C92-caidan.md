## You'll want to know: càidān
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C92-CAIDAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

*càidān* — "menu".

> *qǐng gěi wǒ càidān* — The menu, please.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*càidān* is built from cài 'a dish', the *cài* you know, and dān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshojH* and *tan* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'greens fit to eat' and 'single'. A sheet that lists the dishes is the modern sense of the pair.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C89-GEI-01, ZH-LEX-QING] -->

*qǐng gěi wǒ càidān*: 请, then 给 + the person + the thing, the pattern of the chapter on giving.
