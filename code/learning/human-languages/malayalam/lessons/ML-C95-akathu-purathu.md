# അകത്ത് (akattŭ) — inside

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C95-OPEN-01, ML-LEX-C95-CLOSE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *open it* and *close it*. A door is worth opening because of what
is on the other side of it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C95-INSIDE-01, ML-LEX-C95-OUTSIDE-01]; assesses=[] -->

Two words, and they are the two halves of the same line:

| | |
|---|---|
| **അകത്ത്** *akattŭ* | inside |
| **പുറത്ത്** *puṟattŭ* | outside |

> **ഞാൻ അകത്ത് ആണ്.** — *ñān akattŭ āṇŭ* — "I am inside."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C95-INSIDE-01, ML-LEX-C95-OUTSIDE-01] -->

**Compare the ends and not the fronts.** The two words end the same way as each
other, and what differs is only the front — where the whole meaning sits. One
opens onto a room, the other onto a street.

The chapter before this had a pair the other way round: **ഇന്ന്** and
**ഇന്നലെ** shared their *opening* and parted at the tail. Here the tail matches
and the fronts part. **Both pairs are easy for the same reason** — one end holds
still while you listen to the other.

**Where the words come from.** Both are native Dravidian: *akam*, "inside, home" (DEDR 7, with Tamil *akam*), and *puṟam*, "the outside" (DEDR 4333, with Tamil *puṟam*), each with the place ending.
