Japanese, chapter 480: Making Things.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
つくる (tsukuru) — to make; to build.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for serious, then the Japanese for selfish.

You'll want to know: つくる.
つくる — tsukuru — "to make; to build".
ちちは いすを つくります。 — chichi wa isu o tsukurimasu — "My father makes chairs."
Polite つくります, tsukurimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが たなを つくりました。, chichi ga tana o tsukurimashita.

The word, taken apart.
つくる (tsukuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
でし (deshi) — an apprentice; a pupil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for selfish, then the Japanese for to make.

You'll want to know: でし.
でし — deshi — "an apprentice; a pupil".
だいくの でしは まいにち はたらきます。 — daiku no deshi wa mainichi hatarakimasu — "The carpenter's apprentice works every day."

The word, taken apart.
でし (deshi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are de, "younger brother" (Middle Chinese dejX) and shi, "child" (tsiX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
