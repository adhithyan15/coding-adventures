# دوسرا and تیسرا — second, third, and the ending

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-PEHLA, UR-LEX-DO, UR-LEX-TEEN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **pehlā** and why it is not built from **ek**. Then say **do**
and **tīn**.

## You'll want to know first — two words and an ending
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-DUSRA, UR-LEX-TISRA, UR-MORPH-ORDINAL-RA]; assesses=[UR-LEX-DO, UR-LEX-TEEN, UR-LEX-PEHLA] -->

> **دوسرا** — *dūsrā* — **second**
>
> **تیسرا** — *tīsrā* — **third**

Look at what came back. **دو** is inside *dūsrā*; **تین** is inside *tīsrā*.
The number returns, and an ending *-rā* is added to it.

So the series breaks at *first* and mends at *second* — one word out of line,
then a pattern. English breaks one word later and mends at *third*; the shape is
the same and only the seam moved.
