Lesson 1 of 7.
quēdiǎn — weak point; shortcoming.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the purpose behind it, then the Mandarin for a strong point.

You'll want to know: quēdiǎn.
quēdiǎn — "weak point; shortcoming".
wǒ juéde měi ge rén dōu yǒu quēdiǎn — I think everyone has their faults.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
quēdiǎn is built from quē and 点 'a point', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khjwiet and temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'too little' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'a broken vessel', and the second 'a dot'.

Lesson 4 of 7.
天真 (tiānzhēn) — naive; innocent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a benefit, then the Mandarin for a downside.

You'll want to know: 天真.
天真 — tiānzhēn — "naive; innocent".
虽然你说得有理但是你太天真了 suīrán nǐ shuō de yǒulǐ dànshì nǐ tài tiānzhēn le — You may have a point, but you're too naive.

The word, taken apart.
天真 (tiānzhēn) is built from 天 'the sky' and 真 'true', both inherited from Middle Chinese, then and tsyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the dark heaven above' and the second 'true, as against false'. How the pair came to mean naive is not settled.
