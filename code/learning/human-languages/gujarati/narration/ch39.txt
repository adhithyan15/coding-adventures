Every joining word so far has taken two things that are true and put them together. This one takes something that might not be.

The word, taken apart.
જો (jo) (something that might be so) …
જો (jo) stands at the front of the condition, where English also puts if. That is unusual for this book: most of the small words you have met — માટે, પણ in its also sense, the postpositions — come after what they apply to. જો (jo) comes first, and that alone tells your ear a condition is starting.

Lesson 2 of 5.
જો … તો … (jo … to …) — if … then …

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

[your turn — recall: jo, and nathī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The pattern, taken apart.
Gujarati usually marks both ends of a condition:
જો (jo) (condition), તો (result).
English says if it is late, I will go and marks only the front. Gujarati puts તો at the head of the result as well, so the sentence announces its two halves. Beginners often leave તો out; it is understood, but the full pair is what you will hear.
તો is a worn-down demonstrative — the sentence says, in effect, if … that … — which is why it sits at the head of the result rather than inside it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
જો (jo) reaches the page — if, on two signs — and a warning about a word it is not.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — two signs, and a caution until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Three chapters back you wrote the joining word. Write અને once from memory before anything is uncovered.

Script — two signs, and a caution.
જ — ja, ો — the o stroke.
Both old. But જો (jo) is worth extra care for a reason the other words in this run did not have: the same two letters appear inside words you already know that have nothing to do with if. They turn up at the end of the farewell આવજો, and at the start of the verb જોવું, to see.
So when you meet જો (jo) on a page, check that it is standing alone and at the front of its clause. Inside a longer word it is not this word at all.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Copy જો (jo) once with the model in view. Then write it again with a space after it, at the front of a line, which is where it belongs.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you tell the if જો (jo) from the same two signs inside a longer word? (It stands alone, at the front of its clause.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
જ્યારે (jyāre) — when.

Warm-up.

[your turn — recall: જો (jo), and kemke]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
જ્યારે (jyāre) (one event), (the other).
જ્યારે (jyāre) sets two events against each other in time — when this, that. Like જો (jo), it opens its clause rather than following it.
It is not a random shape. The જ્- at its front belongs to a large family: Gujarati builds a whole set of clause-openers on that one stem, and you are learning a single member of it here rather than the pattern all at once.
