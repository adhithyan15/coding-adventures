Bengali, chapter 419: Want and Waste.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
অভাব (ôbhāb) — want; lack; need.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a moneylender, then the Bengali for the rest (remaining).

You'll want to know: অভাব.
অভাব — ôbhāb — "want; lack; need".
গরমে গ্রামে জলের অভাব হয়। gôrôme grāme jôler ôbhāb hôy. In summer the village runs short of water.
Also hardship itself: অভাবের দিন, hard times; টাকার অভাব (ôbhāb), a shortage of money.

The word, taken apart.
অভাব (ôbhāb) is Sanskrit abhāva, "non-existence, absence" (Monier-Williams), from a-, not, and bhāva, being; Bengali uses it for the want of what one needs.

অপচয় (ôpôchôy) — waste; wastefulness.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Bengali for the rest (remaining), then the Bengali for want (lack).

You'll want to know: অপচয়.
অপচয় — ôpôchôy — "waste; wastefulness".
আলো জ্বালিয়ে রাখা বিদ্যুতের অপচয়। ālo jvāliye rākhā bidyuter ôpôchôy. Leaving the lights on is a waste of electricity.

The word, taken apart.
অপচয় (ôpôchôy) is Sanskrit apacaya, "diminution, decrease" (Monier-Williams); the sense of wasting is the Bengali use.

কিপটে (kipṭe) is an everyday, rather rude word for a stingy person; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
বন্ধক (bôndhôk) — a pledge; a mortgage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for waste (of something useful), then the Bengali for stingy.

You'll want to know: বন্ধক.
বন্ধক — bôndhôk — "a pledge; a mortgage".
দরকারে বাবা জমিটা বন্ধক রাখলেন। dôrkāre bābā jômiṭā bôndhôk rākhlen. In his need my father mortgaged the land.
বন্ধক (bôndhôk) রাখা, to pawn or to mortgage; বন্ধক (bôndhôk) ছাড়ানো, to redeem a pledge.

The word, taken apart.
বন্ধক (bôndhôk) is Sanskrit bandhaka, "pledging, a pledge" (Monier-Williams), from bandh, to bind: a thing bound as a surety.

Lesson 5 of 5.
সম্বল (sômbôl) — one's means; what one has to live on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for stingy, then the Bengali for a pledge (for a loan).

You'll want to know: সম্বল.
সম্বল — sômbôl — "one's means; what one has to live on".
ওই টাকাটাই ছিল আমাদের পথের সম্বল। oi ṭākāṭāi chhilô āmāder pôther sômbôl. That money was all we had for the journey.

The word, taken apart.
সম্বল (sômbôl) is Sanskrit śambala, also written sambala, "provisions for a journey" (Monier-Williams): পথের সম্বল keeps the old sense, and the word has grown to mean all one has to live on.
