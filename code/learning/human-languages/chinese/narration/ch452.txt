Mandarin Chinese, chapter 452: After the Meal.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
xiāohuà — to digest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a meatless dish, then the Mandarin for to be left over.

You'll want to know: xiāohuà.
xiāohuà — "to digest".
chī le fàn mǎshàng shuìjiào duì xiāohuà bù hǎo — Going to bed right after a meal is bad for your digestion.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiāohuà is built from xiāo 'to melt away' and huà 'to change', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjew and xwaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to use up' and the second 'to change'.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

fúwùyuán qǐng gěi wǒ jǐ zhāng cānjīnzhǐ — Excuse me, could I have a few napkins, please?
