Latin, chapter 359: Weighing a Question.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ponderō, ponderāre — to weigh (up), to ponder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for agreement, then the Latin for a dispute.

You'll want to know: ponderō, ponderāre.
ponderō, ponderāre — "to weigh (up), to ponder".
Verba eius dīligenter ponderāvī. I weighed his words carefully.
ponderō, ponderāre, ponderāvī, ponderātum. Built on pondus, a weight: you weigh words as a merchant weighs goods. English ponder.

The word, taken apart.
ponderō is built on pondus, ponderis "a weight" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB lists ponderō "to weigh"). English ponder comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dēlīberō, dēlīberāre — to deliberate, to think over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dispute, then the Latin for to weigh.

You'll want to know: dēlīberō, dēlīberāre.
dēlīberō, dēlīberāre — "to deliberate, to think over".
Diū dēlīberāvimus; tandem cōnstituimus. We deliberated for a long time; at last we decided.
dēlīberō, dēlīberāre, dēlīberāvī, dēlīberātum. Dē rē dēlīberāre, to think a matter over. English deliberate.

The word, taken apart.
dēlīberō is built from dē and a verb made on lībra "a balance, a pair of scales" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to weigh a decision on the scales. English deliberate comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dubitātiō, dubitātiōnis — doubt, hesitation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to weigh, then the Latin for to deliberate.

You'll want to know: dubitātiō, dubitātiōnis.
dubitātiō, dubitātiōnis — "doubt, hesitation".
Sine ūllā dubitātiōne tibi crēdō. I believe you without any doubt.
dubitātiō, dubitātiōnis f., the noun of dubitō, to doubt. Sine dubitātiōne, without hesitation. English doubt comes through French from dubitāre.

The word, taken apart.
dubitātiō is the noun built on dubitō "to doubt" (EtymDB), which belongs with dubius "wavering between two" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
probābilis, probābile — likely, credible; worth approving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to deliberate, then the Latin for doubt.

You'll want to know: probābilis, probābile.
probābilis, probābile — "likely, credible; worth approving".
Tua causa mihi probābilis vidētur. Your case seems credible to me.
probābilis, probābile: two endings. Built on probō, to approve, to test: a thing you can approve. For a view that is likely but not certain. English probable.

The word, taken apart.
probābilis is built on probō "to approve, to test" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from probus "good, upright". English probable comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vērisimilis, vērisimile — probable, like the truth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for doubt, then the Latin for likely.

You'll want to know: vērisimilis, vērisimile.
vērisimilis, vērisimile — "probable, like the truth".
Nōn est vērisimile eum hoc fēcisse. It is not likely that he did this.
vērisimilis, vērisimile, also written as two words, vērī similis, like the truth. Vērisimile est + accusative and infinitive. English verisimilitude.

The word, taken apart.
vērisimilis grew out of the phrase vērī similis "like the truth" (Lewis & Short), from vērus "true" and similis "like". English verisimilitude comes from it.
