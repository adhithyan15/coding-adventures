Arabic, chapter 347: A kidnapping.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اختطف (ikhtaṭafa) — to kidnap; to snatch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to sneak in, then the Arabic for to chase.

You'll want to know: اختطف.
اختطف — ikhtaṭafa — "to kidnap; to snatch".
اختطف رجلان ابن التاجر. ikhtaṭafa rajulān ibn at-tājir. Two men kidnapped the merchant’s son.
Present: يختطف, yakhtaṭif.

The word, taken apart.
اختطف (ikhtaṭafa) is built on the native Arabic root kh-ṭ-f (Lane), "to snatch away quickly", as a bird snatches its prey. It is Form VIII.

Present: يلحق, yalḥaq.

The word, taken apart.
لحق (laḥiqa) is built on the native Arabic root l-ḥ-q (Lane), "to overtake, to come up with", the root of the taught lāḥiqan, later.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: رهينة.
رهينة — rahīna — "a hostage".
بقيت الرهينة ثلاثة أيام في البيت. baqiyat ar-rahīna thalāthat ayyām fī al-bayt. The hostage stayed in the house for three days.

The word, taken apart.
رهينة (rahīna) is built on the native Arabic root r-h-n (Lane), "to give a thing as a pledge"; rahn is a pawn. A rahīna is a person held as one.

Lesson 5 of 5.
خاطف (khāṭif) — a kidnapper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hostage, then the Arabic for a ransom.

You'll want to know: خاطف.
خاطف — khāṭif — "a kidnapper".
هرب الخاطف بالسيارة. haraba al-khāṭif bi-s-sayyāra. The kidnapper got away in the car.
Plural خاطفون. As an adjective it is swift: زيارة خاطفة, a flying visit.

The word, taken apart.
خاطف (khāṭif) is built on the native Arabic root kh-ṭ-f (Lane), "to snatch". Khāṭif is the active participle, "one who snatches".
