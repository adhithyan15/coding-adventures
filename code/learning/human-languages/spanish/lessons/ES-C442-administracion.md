# la administración — the lesser one, serving

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-14, ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO, ES-LEX-SERVIR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la oficina**, **el documento** and **servir**. Put the three
together and you have named the thing that asks you for papers.

## You'll want to know: la administración
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-14] -->

**la administración** — the administration, and in Spain usually **the state**:
*la Administración* with a capital letter is officialdom as a whole.

*Trabaja en la administración* — he works in the public sector. *Trámites con la
administración* — dealings with officialdom. The verb is **administrar**, to
manage or to administer.

The ending is **-ción**, which you have had since the chapter on paperwork, so
the word is *the act of administering* made into a thing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-01, ES-LEX-SERVIR] -->

**ad-** towards, plus **ministr-** from Latin **minister**, a servant or
attendant, plus **-ción**.

And *minister* comes from **minus**, less. A *minister* was **the lesser one**,
the person who serves — which is why the word for a government's most senior
officials is built on the word for *less*.

The pair is the point:

| Latin word | built on | what it named |
|---|---|---|
| **minister** | *minus*, less | the one who serves |
| **magister** | *magis*, more | the one who directs |

*Magister* is where **el maestro** comes from. So *maestro* and *ministro* are
the same shape twice over, once on *more* and once on *less*, and Spanish kept
both.

You already own the smaller half of that pair outright: **menos** is *minus*,
unchanged in meaning and barely changed in sound. The administration is named
with the same word you use to say *less*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-01, ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO] -->

That etymology is not a curiosity here; it tells you the register.

**La administración** is what a notice calls itself when it wants to sound like
an institution rather than a person. A letter from it will not say *le pedimos*
— it will say *se requiere*, and it will ask for **documentación** rather than
papers.

So when you see the word on a form, expect the impersonal grammar that comes
with it. The vocabulary and the sentence shape travel together, and recognising
one tells you to expect the other.
