# ¿Cómo se escribe? — the task the letter names are for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-01, ES-ORTH-C425-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Somebody on a telephone asks how your surname is written. Spell it
aloud in Spanish before reading on.

## Grammar Lens: the question, and what it is not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-01] -->

| question | what it asks for |
|---|---|
| ¿Cómo se escribe? | how it is **written** — spell it |
| ¿Cómo se dice? | how it is **said** — the word itself |

A form-filling exchange uses the first. An exam's listening paper uses it to
dictate a name at you letter by letter, which is the one situation where knowing
*hache* and *jota* and *equis* is the difference between hearing a name and
writing it.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-01, ES-ORTH-C425-02, ES-ORTH-C425-04] -->

Read this exchange aloud:

> — ¿Cómo se escribe su apellido?
> — Vázquez. Uve, a con tilde, zeta, cu, u, e, zeta.
> — ¿Uve o be?
> — Uve. Ve corta.

The last two turns are the reason the second names exist. The listener could
not tell **b** from **v** by ear, asked, and got an answer that settles it.
