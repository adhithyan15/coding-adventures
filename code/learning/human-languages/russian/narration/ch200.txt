You'll want to know: секрет.
секрет (sekrét) — "a secret".

The word, taken apart.
секрет (sekrét) — from Peter the Great's time; it perhaps came through Polish sekret, or from French secret, from Latin sēcrētus, "set apart, hidden" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
шутка (shútka) — a joke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a text, then the Russian for a secret.

You'll want to know: шутка.
шутка (shútka) — "a joke".

The word, taken apart.
шутка (shútka) comes from шутить, "to joke", and шут, "a jester", recorded from 1076. Vasmer reports a suggested link with Lithuanian siaũsti, "to rage, to frolic", and doubts a loan from German Schaute, "a fool".

Lesson 3 of 5.
смех (smekh) — laughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a secret, then the Russian for a joke.

You'll want to know: смех.
смех (smekh) — "laughter".

The word, taken apart.
смех (smekh) from Proto-Slavic směxŭ, with смеяться, "to laugh" — inherited (Vasmer). Vasmer relates the root to Sanskrit smáyate, "smiles", and Middle English smilen, "to smile".

Lesson 4 of 5.
слёзы (slyózy) — tears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a joke, then the Russian for laughter.

You'll want to know: слёзы.
слёзы (slyózy) — "tears".

The word, taken apart.
слёзы (slyózy) is the plural of слеза, "a tear", Proto-Slavic slĭza — inherited. Vasmer thinks it more likely belongs with слизь, "slime", and Middle Low German slîk, "slime".

Lesson 5 of 5.
улыбка (ulýbka) — a smile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for laughter, then the Russian for tears.

You'll want to know: улыбка.
улыбка (ulýbka) — "a smile".

The word, taken apart.
улыбка (ulýbka) goes with улыбаться, "to smile", from dialect лыбить, "to smile". Vasmer calls лыбить unclear and finds the proposed links, with луб, "bast", or Latin labō, "I totter", unconvincing.
