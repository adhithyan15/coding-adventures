## You'll want to know: 关机
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C198-PHONE198-01]; assesses=[] -->

**关机** — *guānjī* — "to switch off (a phone or a computer)".

> **我的手机关机了** *wǒ de shǒujī guānjī le* — My phone is switched off.

The new character, in pieces: 关 — six strokes: two short strokes leaning toward each other, then 天 underneath. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**关机** (*guānjī*) is built from 关 'to shut' and 机 'machine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kwaen* and *kj+j* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the bar that shuts a door, and explains the second as the catch that sets off a crossbow.
