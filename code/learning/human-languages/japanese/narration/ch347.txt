Japanese, chapter 347: Visiting Someone.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ほうもんする (hōmon suru) — to pay a visit; to call on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the next occasion, then the Japanese for a period of time.

You'll want to know: ほうもんする.
ほうもんする — hōmon suru — "to pay a visit; to call on".
もし じかんが あったら、あした ほうもんします。 — moshi jikan ga attara, ashita hōmon shimasu — "If I have time, I will call on you tomorrow."
Polite ほうもんします, hōmonshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せんせいが うちを ほうもんしました。, sensei ga uchi o hōmon shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ほうもんする (hōmon suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, hōmon, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: hō, "visit" (Middle Chinese phjangH) and mon, "question" (mjunH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ついでに (tsuideni) — while you are at it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the matter in hand, then the Japanese for in person.

You'll want to know: ついでに.
ついでに — tsuideni — "while you are at it".
ゆうびんきょくに いったら、ついでに きってを かって ください。 — yūbinkyoku ni ittara, tsuideni kitte o katte kudasai — "If you go to the post office, buy some stamps while you are at it."

The word, taken apart.
ついでに (tsuideni) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (JMdict).
