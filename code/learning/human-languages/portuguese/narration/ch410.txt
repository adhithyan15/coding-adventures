Portuguese, chapter 410: Estradas do campo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a curva — the bend, the curve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to crash into, then the Portuguese for the diversion.

You'll want to know: a curva.
a curva — "the bend, the curve".
Depois da curva, a estrada desce até ao rio. After the bend, the road goes down to the river.
Uma curva perigosa — a dangerous bend.

The word, taken apart.
curva comes from Latin curvus 'bent, crooked' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB has no entry for the Portuguese word.

What you've built.

lomba is built on lombo 'back, loin', which is inherited from Latin lumbus 'loin' (Lewis & Short): a hump like an animal's back.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o atalho — the shortcut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bend, then the Portuguese for the speed bump.

You'll want to know: o atalho.
o atalho — "the shortcut".
Conheço um atalho pelo campo. I know a shortcut through the fields.
Ir por um atalho — to take a shortcut.

The word, taken apart.
atalho is built on atalhar 'to cut short', on talhar 'to cut', which goes back to Late Latin taleāre, from tālea 'cutting, stick' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o alcatrão — tarmac, tar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the speed bump, then the Portuguese for the shortcut.

You'll want to know: o alcatrão.
o alcatrão — "tarmac, tar".
A estrada nova tem alcatrão; a velha é de terra. The new road is tarmac; the old one is dirt.
In Brazil: o asfalto.

The word, taken apart.
alcatrão comes from Arabic qaṭrān 'tar' with the article al- (EtymDB), like Spanish alquitrán.

serpentear — "to wind, to snake (of a road or river)".
A estrada serpenteia pela montanha. The road winds up the mountain.
Built on a serpente, the snake.

The word, taken apart.
serpentear is built on serpente 'snake', from Latin serpēns 'creeping thing', from serpere 'to creep' (Lewis & Short).
