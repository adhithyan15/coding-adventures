Lesson 1 of 5.
चक्रवाकः (cakravākaḥ) — a ruddy goose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lizard, then the Sanskrit for a crocodile.

You'll want to know: चक्रवाकः.
चक्रवाकः — cakravākaḥ — "a ruddy goose".

The word, taken apart.
cakravāka is the ruddy goose, already in the Rigveda (CDIAL 4551). MW lists it as a compound of cakra, "wheel", but does not explain the second part or the name. Poets made the pair a symbol of lovers parted at night, and Hindi cakwā continues it.

The word, taken apart.
śyena is a hawk or eagle, the bird that brings the Soma down in the Rigveda (MW). MW gives it no derivation, so its origin is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रातराशः (prātarāśaḥ) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a ruddy goose, then the Sanskrit for a hawk.

You'll want to know: प्रातराशः.
प्रातराशः — prātarāśaḥ — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
prātarāśa is a compound of prātar, "early in the morning", and āśa, "eating, food", from the root √aś, "to eat" (MW): the morning meal. MW compares prātar with Greek prōi and German früh, "early".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: रात्रिभोजनम्.
रात्रिभोजनम् — rātribhojanam — "dinner".

The word, taken apart.
rātribhojana is a compound of rātri, "night", and bhojana, "eating, food", from the root √bhuj, "to enjoy, eat" (CDIAL 9630). MW lists the compound as "eating at night"; its use for the evening meal is the modern one.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पक्वान्नम् (pakvānnam) — cooked food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for breakfast, then the Sanskrit for dinner.

You'll want to know: पक्वान्नम्.
पक्वान्नम् — pakvānnam — "cooked food".

The word, taken apart.
pakvānna is a compound of pakva, "cooked, ripe", from the root √pac, "to cook", and anna, "food" (MW). Pakva lives on as Hindi pakkā, "cooked, ripe, real" (CDIAL 7621), and anna is the food word you have already met.
