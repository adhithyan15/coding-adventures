You'll want to know: полночь.

The word, taken apart.
полночь (pólnoch) is пол-, "half", with ночь, "night". ночь from Proto-Slavic noťĭ, a cousin of Lithuanian naktìs, Latin nox and English night (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: рано.

The word, taken apart.
рано (ráno) from Proto-Slavic rano, "early" — inherited, but Vasmer calls it hard to explain: some link it with Lithuanian rýtas, "morning", others with Greek órthros, "dawn".

Lesson 4 of 5.
поздно (pózdno) — late.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for midnight, then the Russian for early.

You'll want to know: поздно.
поздно (pózdno) — "late".

The word, taken apart.
поздно (pózdno) is the adverb of поздний, "late", Proto-Slavic pozdŭ — inherited, from the PIE pos, "after, behind", which Vasmer also finds in Lithuanian pàs, "at", and Latin post, "after".

Lesson 5 of 5.
утром (útrom) — in the morning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for early, then the Russian for late.

You'll want to know: утром.
утром (útrom) — "in the morning".

The word, taken apart.
утром (útrom) is утро, "morning", in the form that says "by, during": "in the morning". утро from Proto-Slavic jutro; Vasmer calls its origin difficult, with links to уже, "already", or to Latin iugum, "yoke", as "the time of yoking".
