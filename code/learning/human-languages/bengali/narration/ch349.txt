Bengali, chapter 349: Raising a Voice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
প্রতিবাদ করা (prôtibād kôrā) — to protest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to collect dues, then the Bengali for to sanction.

You'll want to know: প্রতিবাদ করা.
প্রতিবাদ করা — prôtibād kôrā — "to protest".
ছাত্ররা নতুন নিয়মের প্রতিবাদ করল। chhātrôrā nôtun niyômer prôtibād kôrlô. The students protested against the new rule.
Past (he/she …): তিনি অন্যায়ের প্রতিবাদ করলেন, tini ônyāyer prôtibād kôrlen.

The word, taken apart.
প্রতিবাদ করা (prôtibād kôrā) is Sanskrit prativāda, speaking against, contradiction (Monier-Williams), with করা. What is protested takes -এর.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to sanction, then the Bengali for to protest.

You'll want to know: অবরোধ.
অবরোধ — ôbôrodh — "a blockade; a sit-in (blocking a road)".
অবরোধের জন্য রাস্তায় বাস নেই। ôbôrodher jônyô rāstāy bās nei. There are no buses on the road because of the blockade.

The word, taken apart.
অবরোধ (ôbôrodh) is Sanskrit avarodha, hindrance or obstruction (Monier-Williams). In Bengali news it is a protest that blocks a road or a railway.

Lesson 3 of 5.
জনমত (jônômôt) — public opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to protest, then the Bengali for a blockade.

You'll want to know: জনমত.
জনমত — jônômôt — "public opinion".
জনমত সরকারের বিরুদ্ধে। jônômôt sôrkārer biruddhe. Public opinion is against the government.

The word, taken apart.
জনমত (jônômôt) is a modern compound of Sanskrit jana, people, and mata, an opinion (Monier-Williams): what the public thinks.
