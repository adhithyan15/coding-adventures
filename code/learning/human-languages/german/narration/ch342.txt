German, chapter 342: Ursache und Wirkung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Wirkung — the effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for nevertheless, then the German for in fact.

You'll want to know: die Wirkung.
die Wirkung — "the effect".
Der Tee hat eine gute Wirkung: Ich schlafe besser. The tea has a good effect: I sleep better.
Plural: die Wirkungen. Ursache und Wirkung — cause and effect.

The word, taken apart.
Wirkung is the noun of wirken, inherited from Old High German wirken and Proto-Germanic wirkijaną "to work, to make" (EtymDB), of the family of Werk "work".

Lesson 2 of 5.
wirken — to work, to take effect; to seem.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: wirken.
wirken — "to work, to take effect; to seem".
Die Tabletten wirken schon nach einer halben Stunde. The tablets take effect after only half an hour.
It also means to seem: Er wirkt müde — he seems tired.

The word, taken apart.
wirken is inherited from Old High German wirken and Proto-Germanic wirkijaną "to work, to make" (EtymDB), of the same family as Werk "work" and Greek érgon.

Lesson 3 of 5.
verursachen — to cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the effect, then the German for to work.

You'll want to know: verursachen.
verursachen — "to cause".
Der starke Regen hat viele Probleme verursacht. The heavy rain caused a lot of problems.
Inseparable: es verursacht, es hat verursacht.

The word, taken apart.
verursachen is built with ver- on Ursache "cause" (EtymDB), which joins the old prefix ur- "first, original" to Sache "thing, matter", from Old High German sahha.

Lesson 4 of 5.
auslösen — to trigger, to set off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to work, then the German for to cause.

You'll want to know: auslösen.
auslösen — "to trigger, to set off".
Seine Bemerkung hat einen großen Streit ausgelöst. His remark set off a big argument.
Separable: es löst aus, es hat ausgelöst. Also of machines: den Alarm auslösen — to set off the alarm.

The word, taken apart.
auslösen is built from aus "out" and lösen "to loosen, to release", inherited from Old High German lōsen and Proto-Germanic lausijaną, from lausaz "loose" (EtymDB): you release something that then runs by itself.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Auslöser — the trigger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to cause, then the German for to trigger.

You'll want to know: der Auslöser.
der Auslöser — "the trigger".
Der Auslöser für den Streit war eine kleine Nachricht. The trigger for the argument was a small message.
Plural: die Auslöser. On a camera, der Auslöser is the shutter button.

The word, taken apart.
Auslöser is formed from auslösen "to set off" with the ending -er; the verb is built on lösen "to release", from Proto-Germanic lausijaną (EtymDB).
