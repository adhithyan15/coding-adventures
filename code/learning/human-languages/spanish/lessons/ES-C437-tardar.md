# tardar — how long a thing takes

## Warm-up

## You'll want to know: tardar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-03] -->

**tardar** — to take time, to be long about something.

*Tardo una hora* — it takes me an hour. *¿Cuánto tarda?* — how long does it
take?

## Grammar Lens: the delay and the duration
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-04, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-03] -->

**el retraso** and **tardar** are not interchangeable, and the difference is
worth having exactly:

| you mean | you say |
|---|---|
| it is later than promised | lleva retraso |
| it takes this long | tarda una hora |

A journey that **tarda** two hours is not late — two hours is what the journey
costs. A train with **retraso** has broken a promise. English blurs both into
*late* and *takes*, and a reader who maps one to one will misread a station
announcement.

Both come from **tardus**, slow, so the shared root is real and the split is
recent.
