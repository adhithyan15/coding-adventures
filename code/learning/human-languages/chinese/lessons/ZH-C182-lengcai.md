## You'll want to know: 冷菜
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C182-EATOUT182-02]; assesses=[] -->

**冷菜** — *lěngcài* — "cold starter; cold dishes (on a menu)".

> **我们要两个冷菜** *wǒmen yào liǎng ge lěngcài* — We'd like two cold starters.

The new character, in pieces: 冷 — seven strokes: the ice piece on the left (a dot, then a stroke rising to the right), and on the right 今 as you write it, with one more dot under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**冷菜** (*lěngcài*) is built from 冷 'cold' and 菜 'dish', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *lengX* and *tshojH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'cold' and calls the second any plant fit to eat; 'a dish' is the later sense. The dishes a meal starts with, served cold.
