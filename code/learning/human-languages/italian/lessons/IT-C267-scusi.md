# scusi, per andare a… — the question that finds the way

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-ANDARE-02, IT-LEX-C82-CITTA2-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to go". (*Andare.*) Say "the station". (*La stazione.*)

[PAUSE 1s] You are in a town you do not know, and the station is somewhere.

## You'll want to know: scusi
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-SCUSI-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C143-VERBI143-04] -->

**scusi** — "excuse me", said to someone you do not know.

You met **scusarsi**, "to apologise"; *scusi* comes from the plain verb
inside it, *scusare*, "to excuse", made a polite request. It is the form for **Lei**, the polite "you" of *come sta*, which a
stranger in the street is owed. Its *-i* comes back in the next lesson.

## Grammar Lens: per andare a…
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-PER-ANDARE-A-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C84-CITTA4-01, IT-LEX-C83-CITTA3-04] -->

> **Scusi, per andare alla stazione?** — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?

Literally, "for going to the station". There is no verb of asking in it at all:
**per** plus the infinitive *andare*, then the place, and your voice rises at
the end.

The *a*, "to", joins the article in front of the place, and the two become one
word:

> **alla** stazione · **al** museo · **all'**albergo

*a* + *la* is **alla**, *a* + *il* is **al**, and *a* + *l'*, before a vowel,
is **all'**.

There is a second way to ask, with *dove*, "where": **Dov'è la stazione?**,
"where is the station". Both work. *Per andare a…* asks for the **way**;
*dov'è* asks for the **place**.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**scusare** is inherited from Latin *excūsāre* (EtymDB 2.1), built on *ex-*,
"out of", and *causa*, "a case at law": to excuse someone was to get them out
of the charge (Lewis & Short, under *excuso*). English **excuse** is the same
word, borrowed through French.
