Urdu, chapter 156: Festival, New Year, Era, Every week, Every year.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
تہوار (tahvār) — a festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for February, then the Urdu for March.

You'll want to know: تہوار.
تہوار — tahvār — "a festival".

The word, taken apart.
تہوار (tahvār) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from tithivāra, "a festival", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5811). It is built from tithi, "lunar day", and vāra, "day".

Lesson 2 of 5.
نیا سال (nayā sāl) — New Year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for March, then the Urdu for a festival.

You'll want to know: نیا سال.
نیا سال — nayā sāl — "New Year".

The phrase, taken apart.
نیا سال (nayā sāl) joins nayā, "new", and sāl, "year". nayā comes from Sanskrit naviya, a variant of navya, "new" (CDIAL 7025); sāl is usually traced to Persian sāl, "year".

Lesson 3 of 5.
زمانہ (zamānā) — an era, the times.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a festival, then the Urdu for New Year.

You'll want to know: زمانہ.
زمانہ — zamānā — "an era, the times".

The word, taken apart.
زمانہ (zamānā) is a loan from Arabic zamān, "time, a period", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is z-m-n (Lane).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ہر ہفتے (har hafte) — every week.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for New Year, then the Urdu for an era.

You'll want to know: ہر ہفتے.
ہر ہفتے — har hafte — "every week".

The phrase, taken apart.
ہر ہفتے (har hafte) joins har, "every", and hafte, a form of haftā, "week", both usually traced to Persian. Persian hafta is built on haft, "seven".
