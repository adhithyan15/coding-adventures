Sanskrit, chapter 130: Foolish, Lazy, Strong, Weak, Clear.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मूर्खः (mūrkhaḥ) — foolish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for brave, then the Sanskrit for clever.

You'll want to know: मूर्खः.
मूर्खः — mūrkhaḥ — "foolish".

The word, taken apart.
mūrkha "stupid, foolish" is old, in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā. MW files it under the root √mūrch "thicken, congeal, faint" without deriving it from there, so its source is uncertain. Hindi uses it unchanged as mūrkh (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
अलसः (alasaḥ) — lazy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for clever, then the Sanskrit for foolish.

You'll want to know: अलसः.
अलसः — alasaḥ — "lazy".

The word, taken apart.
alasa "inactive, lazy" has a disputed origin: MW divides it as a- "not" + lasa "lively", CDIAL 708 reports EWA's view that it is an l-form of arasa "sapless", and DEDR 236 takes it from Dravidian. Pali kept it as alasa "lazy" (CDIAL 708).

Lesson 4 of 5.
दुर्बलः (durbalaḥ) — weak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lazy, then the Sanskrit for strong.

You'll want to know: दुर्बलः.
दुर्बलः — durbalaḥ — "weak".

The word, taken apart.
durbala is built from dus- "bad, ill" and bala "strength" (CDIAL 6438): "of little strength". It lives on as Hindi dublā "thin, weak" (CDIAL 6438).
