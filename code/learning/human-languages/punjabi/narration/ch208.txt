Lesson 2 of 5.
One bounded past-necessity form — have to; had to, masculine singular perfective.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve familiar ਮੈਨੂੰ, “to me,” and ਜਾਣਾ, “to go.” Then listen for one new auxiliary base and its one past form.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਪੈਣਾ — paina — to have to ਪਿਆ — piaa — had to; masculine singular perfective form.
Hear and say the two whole forms before reading them. Keep ਪਿਆ (piaa) whole. This chapter uses it only after one familiar infinitive in one past-necessity frame. It does not open the wider agreement system.

The form, taken apart — source boundary.
Punjabi University Patiala's reference grammar describes the -ਣਾ ਪੈ- construction as “have to,” says that the person uses ਨੂੰ, and gives both present or future and past examples. AQA's official Panjabi grammar lists the infinitive, past and perfective forms, and auxiliary ਪੈ. Sources: Punjabi University reference grammar and AQA Panjabi grammar.
Those sources support this modern construction. Keep the exact form whole; do not invent a separate etymology for it. This chapter teaches only the masculine singular perfective in one “had to” frame, not a present or future obligation, a feminine or plural form, a full agreement paradigm, or any other sense of the auxiliary.
