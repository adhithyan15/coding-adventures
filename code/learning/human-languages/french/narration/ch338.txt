French, chapter 338: Je n'y crois pas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
douter — to doubt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to give in, then the French for to concede.

You'll want to know: douter.
douter — "to doubt".
Je doute qu'il vienne demain. I doubt that he will come tomorrow.
Douter que takes the subjunctive: je doute qu'il vienne. Se douter de means the opposite: to suspect.

The word, taken apart.
douter is inherited, through Old French, from Latin dubitāre "to waver, to be uncertain" (Lewis & Short). Lewis & Short link the Latin verb to duo "two": a mind going two ways.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: sceptique.
sceptique — "sceptical".
Mon frère reste sceptique: il ne croit pas les journaux. My brother remains sceptical: he does not believe the newspapers.
One form for both genders. The c is silent: say septique.

The word, taken apart.
sceptique is a learned word, usually traced through Latin Sceptici "the Sceptics" (Lewis & Short) to Greek skeptikós "inquiring, thoughtful", which is built on sképtesthai "to look about, to consider".

Lesson 5 of 5.
discutable — debatable, questionable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to deny, then the French for absurd.

You'll want to know: discutable.
discutable — "debatable, questionable".
Ta décision est discutable, mais c'est ton choix. Your decision is debatable, but it is your choice.
From discuter + -able: it can be argued about. C'est discutable is a polite way to disagree.

The word, taken apart.
discutable is built from discuter and the suffix -able (EtymDB). discuter comes from Latin discutere "to strike apart, to shatter" (Lewis & Short), built on quatere "to shake".
