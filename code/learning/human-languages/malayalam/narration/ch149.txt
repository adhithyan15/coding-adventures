Malayalam, chapter 149: Garden, Leaf, Mango, Banana, Grapes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പൂന്തോട്ടം (pūntōṭṭaṁ) — a garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for an island, then the Malayalam for a shore.
[pause 1 second]
Does vaikunnēraṁ mean afternoon or evening? (A dictionary lists both; phrasebooks use it for evening.)

You'll want to know: പൂന്തോട്ടം.
പൂന്തോട്ടം — pūntōṭṭaṁ — "a garden".

The word, taken apart.
പൂന്തോട്ടം (pūntōṭṭaṁ) is native Dravidian, "the flower garden": pū, "flower" (DEDR 4345), and tōṭṭam, "garden" (DEDR 3549, with Tamil tōṭṭam). DEDR 4345 compares Sanskrit puṣpa, with a query.

Lesson 3 of 5.
മാമ്പഴം (māmbaḻaṁ) — a mango.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a garden, then the Malayalam for a leaf.
[pause 1 second]
Which sense of vaikunnēraṁ do phrasebooks give? (Evening.)

You'll want to know: മാമ്പഴം.
മാമ്പഴം — māmbaḻaṁ — "a mango".

The word, taken apart.
മാമ്പഴം (māmbaḻaṁ) is native Dravidian, "mango fruit": mā, "mango tree" (DEDR 4782, with Tamil mā), and paḻaṁ, "ripe fruit" (DEDR 4004). DEDR 4782 compares Sanskrit mākanda. DEDR 4004 compares Sanskrit phala.

വാഴപ്പഴം (vāḻappaḻaṁ) is native Dravidian, "plantain fruit": the word for the plantain plant (DEDR 5373, with Tamil vāḻai), and paḻaṁ, "ripe fruit" (DEDR 4004). DEDR 5373 compares Sanskrit vārabuṣā. DEDR 4004 compares Sanskrit phala.
