Telugu, chapter 520: Wearing Down and Bouncing Back.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
కుదుటపడు (kuduṭapaḍu) — to steady; to settle down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a bandage, then the Telugu for to visit the sick.

You'll want to know: కుదుటపడు.
కుదుటపడు — kuduṭapaḍu — "to steady; to settle down".
మందులతో అతని ఆరోగ్యం కుదుటపడింది. mandulatō atani ārōgyaṁ kuduṭapaḍindi. With medicines his health settled down.
Past (I …): కుదుటపడ్డాను, kuduṭapaḍḍānu.
Health, a situation or a mind settles after trouble.

The word, taken apart.
కుదుటపడు (kuduṭapaḍu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1709 lists Telugu kuduru, "to become settled, to become calm, to be cured", and kuduṭabaḍu itself, with Tamil kutir, "to be settled".

Lesson 2 of 5.
బలపడు (balapaḍu) — to grow strong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to visit the sick, then the Telugu for to steady.

You'll want to know: బలపడు.
బలపడు — balapaḍu — "to grow strong".
వ్యాయామంతో నా కాళ్ళు బలపడ్డాయి. vyāyāmantō nā kāḷḷu balapaḍḍāyi. With exercise my legs grew strong.
Past (I …): బలపడ్డాను, balapaḍḍānu.
బలం, strength, + పడు. Also of friendships: స్నేహం బలపడింది.

The word, taken apart.
బలపడు (balapaḍu) is built from బలం, from Sanskrit bala, "power, strength" (CDIAL 9161) and the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853).

Lesson 3 of 5.
నలిగిపోవు (naligipōvu) — to be worn out; to get crumpled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to steady, then the Telugu for to grow strong.

You'll want to know: నలిగిపోవు.
నలిగిపోవు — naligipōvu — "to be worn out; to get crumpled".
రోజంతా పనిచేసి అమ్మ నలిగిపోయింది. rōjantā panicēsi amma naligipōyindi. After working all day, mum was worn out.
Past (I …): నలిగిపోయాను, naligipōyānu.
Of cloth, to crumple; of people, to be worn down by work or worry.

The word, taken apart.
నలిగిపోవు (naligipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3611 lists Telugu nalāgu, naliyu, "to be crushed, bruised, crumpled", and the verb ends in pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

చీదు — cīdu — "to blow one's nose".
జలుబుతో అతను పదే పదే ముక్కు చీదాడు. jalubutō atanu padē padē mukku cīdāḍu. With his cold he blew his nose again and again.
Past (I …): చీదాను, cīdānu.
With ముక్కు, nose: ముక్కు చీదు (cīdu).

The word, taken apart.
చీదు (cīdu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2618 lists the Telugu verb with Tamil cīntu, "to blow, as the nose".

Lesson 5 of 5.
పుంజుకో (puñjukō) — to pick up; to regain strength.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be worn out, then the Telugu for to blow one's nose.

You'll want to know: పుంజుకో.
పుంజుకో — puñjukō — "to pick up; to regain strength".
జ్వరం తగ్గాక పాప మళ్ళీ పుంజుకుంది. jvaraṁ taggāka pāpa maḷḷī puñjukundi. After the fever went down, the little girl picked up again.
Past (I …): పుంజుకున్నాను, puñjukunnānu.
Said of a person getting their strength back, and of a team or a business that rallies.

The word, taken apart.
పుంజుకో (puñjukō) is a Telugu verb ending in the reflexive -కో; the origin of its stem is not settled.
