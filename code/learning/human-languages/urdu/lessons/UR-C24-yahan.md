# یہاں (yahāṅ) — here

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KAHAN, UR-LEX-YIH] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask **where?** Then say **this**, and look at how the two words
begin.

## You'll want to know: یہاں
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-YAHAN]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KAHAN, UR-LEX-YIH, UR-LEX-MAIN] -->

> **میں یہاں ہوں** — *maiṅ yahāṅ hūṅ* — **I am here.**

Chapter seventeen taught **کہاں** — *where?* — and this book has had no way to
answer it ever since. A learner could ask the question and could not receive the
reply.

Look at the two words together. **کہاں** and **یہاں** share their ending, and
differ only in the head: **ک-** asks, **یہ-** points near. That is not a
coincidence to be memorised — it is a frame.

## Grammar Lens: the frame, two thirds filled
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-YAHAN, UR-LEX-KAHAN, UR-LEX-YIH, UR-LEX-VOH, UR-ETYMON-K-QUESTION-FAMILY] -->

| head | frame | word |
|---|---|---|
| **ک-** asking | thing | **کیا** |
| **ک-** asking | place | **کہاں** |
| **یہ-** near | thing | **یہ** |
| **یہ-** near | place | **یہاں** |
| **وہ-** far | thing | **وہ** |
| **وہ-** far | place | *next lesson* |

Chapter seventeen already showed you that **کیا**, **کون** and **کہاں** are one
family. This is the other half of that observation: the *answers* are a family
too, built on the pointing heads, and the ending is shared across both.

One cell is still empty, and you can already guess what goes in it.
