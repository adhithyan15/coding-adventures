## You'll want to know: 有望
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C532-HOPE532-01]; assesses=[] -->

**有望** — *yǒuwàng* — "with good hopes of it; likely to happen".

> **如果天气好飞机就有望正点起飞** *rúguǒ tiānqì hǎo fēijī jiù yǒuwàng zhèngdiǎn qǐfēi* — If the weather is good, the plane should take off on time.

The new character, in pieces: 望 — eleven strokes in three pieces: on the top left a dot, a bar and a short vertical that turns right, three strokes; on the top right 月, four strokes; and 王 at the foot, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**有望** (*yǒuwàng*) is built from 有 'to have' and *wàng*, both inherited from Middle Chinese, *hjuwX* and *mjangH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to look out for' and, after an older dictionary, as watching for someone far away to come back.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for saying what would happen.
