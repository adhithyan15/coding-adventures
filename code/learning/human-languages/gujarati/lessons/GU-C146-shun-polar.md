# શું …? — the yes-or-no question

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-SHUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C01-HAANAA-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NEGATE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **શું**, *what*, as in *your name*: **તમારું નામ શું છે**. Then
the two oldest answers in the book, **હા** and **ના**. And one sentence from the
last chapter: *I don't speak Gujarati*.

## Grammar Lens: શું at the front
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POLAR-SHUN-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-SHUN-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-O-01] -->

> **શું તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો છો?** — *śũ tame gujarātī bolo cho?* — Do you speak Gujarati?

Inside a sentence **શું** means *what*. At the very front it means nothing at
all: it marks the sentence as a yes-or-no question, and not one other word
moves. English rebuilds the sentence around *do*; Gujarati sets a flag at the
front.

- **તમારું નામ શું છે** — *what* is your name — inside;
- **શું તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો છો** — *do* you speak Gujarati — in front.

## You'll want to know: the voice can carry it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POLAR-RISE-01]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-O-01] -->

In speech the flag often drops, and the voice does its work: **તમે ગુજરાતી બોલો
છો?** with the pitch rising on the last word asks the same question. On the page
the question mark carries the rise.

## Grammar Lens: answering
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-HAANAA-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NEGATE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-HUNGUJARATIBOLUNCHHUN-01] -->

Gujarati answers with **હા** or **ના**, and then, usually, with the verb again:

> **હા, હું ગુજરાતી બોલું છું.** — *hā, hũ gujarātī bolũ chũ.*
> **ના, હું ગુજરાતી નથી બોલતો.** — *nā, hũ gujarātī nathī bolto.*
