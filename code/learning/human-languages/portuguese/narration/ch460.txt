Portuguese, chapter 460: Animais selvagens.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o javali — the wild boar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to tan, then the Portuguese for to dive.

You'll want to know: o javali.
o javali — "the wild boar".
À noite, os javalis descem até às hortas. At night, the wild boar come down to the vegetable gardens.
Feminine: a javalina.

The word, taken apart.
javali comes from Andalusian Arabic jabalī 'wild boar' (EtymDB), literally 'of the mountain', short for 'mountain pig'.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to dive, then the Portuguese for the wild boar.

You'll want to know: o veado.
o veado — "the deer".
Vimos um veado à beira da estrada. We saw a deer at the side of the road.
O cervo is the red deer; veado covers most deer.

The word, taken apart.
veado is inherited through Old Portuguese veado (EtymDB) from Latin vēnātus 'hunting, game' (Lewis & Short): the animal people hunted.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: o falcão.
o falcão — "the falcon".
Um falcão voa por cima do campo. A falcon is flying over the field.
Very fast when it dives.

The word, taken apart.
falcão is inherited from Late Latin falcō, falcōnis 'falcon' (EtymDB), often linked with falx 'sickle' for its curved claws.
