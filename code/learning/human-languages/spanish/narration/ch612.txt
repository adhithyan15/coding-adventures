Spanish, chapter 612: Si no es así.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a no ser que — except if; unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for provided that, then the Spanish for in case.

You'll want to know: a no ser que.
a no ser que — "except if; unless".
Salimos a las ocho, a no ser que llueva. We leave at eight, unless it rains.
Means the same as a menos que, with the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
a no ser que is built on ser, usually explained as a merger of Latin esse 'to be' and sedere 'to sit'; EtymDB takes it back to Old Spanish seer. The phrase means 'unless it be that'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: de lo contrario.
de lo contrario — "otherwise".
Date prisa; de lo contrario, perdemos el tren. Hurry up; otherwise, we will miss the train.
In speech people also say si no.

The word, taken apart.
de lo contrario is built on contrario, a learned borrowing from Latin contrarius 'opposite', from contra 'against' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
en ese caso — in that case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for on condition that, then the Spanish for otherwise.

You'll want to know: en ese caso.
en ese caso — "in that case".
—No tengo coche. —En ese caso, vamos en tren. I do not have a car. In that case, we will go by train.
It answers a condition that someone has given.

The word, taken apart.
en ese caso is built on caso, a learned borrowing from Latin casus 'an event', from cadere 'to fall' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
en todo caso — in any case; if anything.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for otherwise, then the Spanish for in that case.

You'll want to know: en todo caso.
en todo caso — "in any case; if anything".
En todo caso, te llamo mañana. In any case, I will call you tomorrow.
Close to de todas formas.

The word, taken apart.
en todo caso is built on todo, inherited from Latin totus 'whole', and caso, borrowed from Latin casus 'an event' (EtymDB).
