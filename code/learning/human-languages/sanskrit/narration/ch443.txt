Sanskrit, chapter 443: Sunset and Night.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सूर्यास्तः (sūryāstaḥ) — sunset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the horizon, then the Sanskrit for dawns.

You'll want to know: सूर्यास्तः.
सूर्यास्तः — sūryāstaḥ — "sunset".
सूर्यास्ते काकः नीडं गच्छति। sūryāste kākaḥ nīḍaṃ gacchati. At sunset the crow goes to its nest.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सूर्यास्तः (sūryāstaḥ) is built from sūrya, "the sun", and asta, "home, setting"; MW gives the compound as sunset (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
अस्तं गच्छति (astaṃ gacchati) — sets in the west; goes down (of the sun).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dawns, then the Sanskrit for sunset.

You'll want to know: अस्तं गच्छति.
अस्तं गच्छति — astaṃ gacchati — "sets in the west; goes down (of the sun)".
सूर्यः पश्चिमायाम् अस्तं गच्छति। sūryaḥ paścimāyām astaṃ gacchati. The sun sets in the west.
Past (it …): सूर्यः अस्तं गतवान्, sūryaḥ astaṃ gatavān.

The word, taken apart.
अस्तं गच्छति (astaṃ gacchati) is built from astam, "home, the setting of the sun", and the root √gam, "to go": to go home; MW gives astam with √gam as to set, of the sun; CDIAL 976 gives astam eti, "goes home, sets", from the Rigveda on (MW; CDIAL 976).

Lesson 3 of 5.
गोधूलिः (godhūliḥ) — dusk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sunset, then the Sanskrit for sets in the west.

You'll want to know: गोधूलिः.
गोधूलिः — godhūliḥ — "dusk".
गोधूलौ गोपालः धेनुं गृहम् आनयति। godhūlau gopālaḥ dhenuṃ gṛham ānayati. At dusk the cowherd brings the cow home.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गोधूलिः (godhūliḥ) is built from go, "a cow, the earth", and dhūli, "dust": MW glosses it "earth-dust", the time of day when a haze seems to rise from the ground, and gives it as a time of twilight (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
तमिस्रा (tamisrā) — a dark night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sets in the west, then the Sanskrit for dusk.

You'll want to know: तमिस्रा.
तमिस्रा — tamisrā — "a dark night".
तमिस्रायां दीपं विना गन्तुं कठिनम्। tamisrāyāṃ dīpaṃ vinā gantuṃ kaṭhinam. On a dark night it is hard to go without a lamp.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तमिस्रा (tamisrā) is built on tamas, "darkness", with an ending Pāṇini (5.2.114) gives; MW gives tamisrā as a dark night (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
नक्तम् (naktam) — by night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dusk, then the Sanskrit for a dark night.

You'll want to know: नक्तम्.
नक्तम् — naktam — "by night".
उलूकः नक्तं चरति। ulūkaḥ naktaṃ carati. The owl moves about by night.

The word, taken apart.
नक्तम् (naktam) is the word MW gives for by night, often set against divā, "by day"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
