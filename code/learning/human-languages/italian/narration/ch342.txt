Italian, chapter 342: Trovare un accordo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ammettere — to admit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to answer back, then the Italian for to contradict.

You'll want to know: ammettere.
ammettere — "to admit".
Devo ammettere che il tuo piano era migliore. I have to admit that your plan was better.
Conjugated like mettere; past participle ammesso. It also means to let someone in: ammettere a un corso.

The word, taken apart.
ammettere is inherited from Latin admittere "to let in, to allow" (EtymDB), built from ad "to" and mittere "to send": you let a fact in.

Lesson 2 of 5.
concordare — to agree (on), to settle together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to contradict, then the Italian for to admit.

You'll want to know: concordare.
concordare — "to agree (on), to settle together".
Abbiamo concordato il prezzo con il proprietario. We agreed on the price with the owner.
Concordare con qualcuno — to agree with someone; concordare una data — to agree on a date. A little more formal than essere d'accordo.

The word, taken apart.
concordare is a learned borrowing from Latin concordāre "to agree, to be of one mind" (Lewis & Short), from concors "of one heart", built from con- "together" and cor, cordis "heart".

Lesson 3 of 5.
il consenso — the consent; the agreement (of many).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to admit, then the Italian for to agree.

You'll want to know: il consenso.
il consenso — "the consent; the agreement (of many)".
Non puoi usare la mia foto senza il mio consenso. You cannot use my photo without my consent.
Dare il proprio consenso — to give your consent. It can also be broad agreement in a group.

The word, taken apart.
consenso is a learned borrowing from Latin cōnsēnsus "agreement, accord" (Lewis & Short), from cōnsentīre "to agree", built from con- "together" and sentīre "to feel".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il compromesso — the compromise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to agree, then the Italian for the consent.

You'll want to know: il compromesso.
il compromesso — "the compromise".
Alla fine abbiamo trovato un compromesso: un film e poi una pizza. In the end we found a compromise: a film and then a pizza.
Arrivare a un compromesso — to reach a compromise. It is also the preliminary contract when you buy a house.

The word, taken apart.
compromesso comes from Latin compromissum "a mutual promise to abide by an arbiter's decision" (Lewis & Short), from compromittere, built on prōmittere "to promise" and mittere "to send".

Lesson 5 of 5.
cedere — to give in, to give way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the consent, then the Italian for the compromise.

You'll want to know: cedere.
cedere — "to give in, to give way".
Dopo un'ora di discussione, mia sorella ha ceduto. After an hour of discussion, my sister gave in.
Cedere il posto — to give up your seat. Non cedere — to hold firm.

The word, taken apart.
cedere is inherited from Latin cēdere "to go, to withdraw, to yield" (EtymDB): you step back and let the other side pass.
