# ചികിത്സ — treatment

## Warm-up

a person. Neither is what happens between them.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C107-TREATMENT-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C107-MEDICINE-01] -->

**ചികിത്സ** (*cikitsa*) — **treatment**.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **വൈദ്യൻ** | *vaidyan* | the person |
| **ചികിത്സ** | *cikitsa* | what the person does |
| **മരുന്ന്** | *marunnŭ* | what the person gives |

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C107-TREATMENT-01, ML-LEX-C107-MEDICINE-01] -->

**Sanskrit, where the last word was Dravidian.** That split is ordinary in
Malayalam and you have seen it before: the everyday thing keeps its native name,
and the formal practice around it takes a borrowed one.

The Sanskrit word has an unusual shape. Its grammarians build it from a root
meaning **to perceive**, in a form that means **wanting to** — so the word is put
together as something like *a wish to see clearly*. Treat that as the traditional
account rather than a settled fact; what is certain is the borrowing, not the
story inside it.
