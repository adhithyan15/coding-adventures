# tenho falado — the compound that means something else

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Portuguese **does** have *ter* + participle. It looks exactly like the
French and Italian compound past, and it means **something different**. This is
one of the most common mistakes English speakers make in Portuguese.

## The form

> **ter** (conjugated) + **past participle**

| infinitive | participle |
|---|---|
| fal**ar** | fal**ado** |
| com**er** | com**ido** |
| part**ir** | part**ido** |

→ **tenho falado**, **tens falado**, **tem falado**…

## What it actually means

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| **falei** | "I spoke" — one finished event |
| **tenho falado** | "I **have been** speaking" — **repeatedly, lately** |

*Tenho falado com ele* does **not** mean "I have spoken to him [once]." It means
"I **have been** speaking to him" — **more than once, over a recent stretch of
time, and it may still be going on**.

For a single completed act, Portuguese uses the simple past from the last lesson:

> **Falei com ele ontem.** — "I spoke with him yesterday."

## Why the meanings diverged

French and Italian let their compound take over the plain past, so *j'ai parlé*
and *ho parlato* now just mean "I spoke."

Portuguese never did — ***falei*** was already occupying that slot and never left.
So when Portuguese assembled the same *have* + participle machinery, there was **no
vacancy**, and the construction drifted into a different job: **repeated or
continuing** action.

Same parts, same era, same family. Different outcome, because of what was already
in the way.

| language | have + participle means |
|---|---|
| French | *j'ai parlé* — **I spoke** |
| Italian | *ho parlato* — **I spoke** |
| German | *ich habe gesagt* — **I said** |
| **Portuguese** | *tenho falado* — **I have been speaking (repeatedly)** |
