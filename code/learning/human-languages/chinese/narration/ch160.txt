Mandarin Chinese, chapter 160: Doing the Chores.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sǎodì — to sweep the floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a balcony, then the Mandarin for a window.

You'll want to know: sǎodì.
sǎodì — "to sweep the floor".
wǒ sǎodì nǐ xǐ cài — I'll sweep the floor; you wash the vegetables.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sǎodì is built from 扫 'to sweep' and dì 'ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sawX and dijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for saying what I do.

Lesson 2 of 5.
xǐwǎn — to do the washing-up; to wash the dishes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a window, then the Mandarin for to sweep the floor.

You'll want to know: xǐwǎn.
xǐwǎn — "to do the washing-up; to wash the dishes".
chī fàn yǐhòu wǒ xǐwǎn — After we eat, I'll do the dishes.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: xǐwǎn is said xíwǎn, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
xǐwǎn is built from 洗 'to wash' and wǎn 'bowl', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sejX and 'wanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which writes the second another way and glosses it 'a vessel'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
gànhuó — to get the jobs done; to work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do the washing-up, then the Mandarin for to make the bed.

You'll want to know: gànhuó.
gànhuó — "to get the jobs done; to work".
nǐmen dōu zài gànhuó ma — Are you all busy working?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gànhuó is built from gàn and huó, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kanH and hwat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a stem' and 'strong' and the second 'alive'; how the pair came to mean doing the work is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zhàogù — to look after; to take care of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: zhàogù.
zhàogù — "to look after; to take care of".
māma bìng le wǒ yào zhàogù tā — Mum is ill, so I'm going to look after her.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhàogù is built from zhào 'bright, to shine' and gù 'to turn and look'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the second is listed there as kuH, glossed 'to look back' and 'to care for'.
