Hindi, chapter 356: Fair and Unfair.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बराबर (barābar) — equal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a command, then the Hindi for to suggest.

You'll want to know: बराबर.
बराबर — barābar — "equal".
क़ानून के सामने सब लोग बराबर हैं। qānūn ke sāmne sab log barābar haiṁ. Everyone is equal before the law.

The word, taken apart.
बराबर (barābar) is usually traced to Persian barābar, "equal, level".

Lesson 2 of 5.
भेदभाव (bhedbhāv) — discrimination.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to suggest, then the Hindi for equal.

You'll want to know: भेदभाव.
भेदभाव — bhedbhāv — "discrimination".
स्कूल में लड़कियों के साथ भेदभाव नहीं होना चाहिए। skūl meṁ laṛkiyoṁ ke sāth bhedbhāv nahīṃ honā cāhiye. There should be no discrimination against girls at school.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
भेदभाव (bhedbhāv) joins two Sanskrit words, bheda, "a split, a distinction", and bhāva, "a state, an attitude" (Monier-Williams): treating people as split apart.

Lesson 5 of 5.
उकसाना (uksānā) — to provoke, to stir up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for bias, then the Hindi for fairness.

You'll want to know: उकसाना.
उकसाना — uksānā — "to provoke, to stir up".
कुछ लोग भीड़ को उकसा रहे थे। kuch log bhīṛ ko uksā rahe the. Some people were stirring up the crowd.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने भीड़ को उकसाया, usne bhīṛ ko uksāyā.

The word, taken apart.
उकसाना (uksānā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit utkasati, "opens" (CDIAL 1718). CDIAL glosses the Hindi verb "to draw out, to raise, to excite".
