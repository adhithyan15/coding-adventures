Portuguese, chapter 526: Séries e telenovelas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o episódio — episode.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for translation, then the Portuguese for translator.

You'll want to know: o episódio.
o episódio — "episode".
Vi três episódios seguidos. I watched three episodes in a row.
Also an incident: um episódio estranho.

The word, taken apart.
episódio comes from French épisode (EtymDB), from Greek epeisódion "an added part", built on hodós "a way".

Lesson 3 of 5.
a telenovela — soap opera.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for episode, then the Portuguese for season.

You'll want to know: a telenovela.
a telenovela — "soap opera".
A minha avó não perde a telenovela das nove. My grandmother never misses the nine o'clock soap.
Often shortened to a novela.

The word, taken apart.
telenovela is built from tele-, from Greek tēle "far", and novela, from Italian novella "a tale" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a dobragem — dubbing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for soap opera, then the Portuguese for subtitled.

You'll want to know: a dobragem.
a dobragem — "dubbing".
Os desenhos animados para crianças têm dobragem. Children's cartoons are dubbed.
In Brazil: a dublagem. In Portugal most films for adults are subtitled.

The word, taken apart.
dobragem is built on dobrar "to fold, to double, to dub", which goes back to Late Latin duplāre "to double" (EtymDB), from duplus.
