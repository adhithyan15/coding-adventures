Arabic, chapter 404: Conditions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شرط (sharṭ) — a condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for most probably, then the Arabic for in general.

You'll want to know: شرط.
شرط — sharṭ — "a condition".
لي شرط واحد. lī sharṭ wāḥid. I have one condition.
Plural: شروط, shurūṭ.

The word, taken apart.
شرط (sharṭ) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-ṭ, "to set a mark; to stipulate" (Lane). A sharṭ is a mark set down in a bargain: a condition. شرطة, the police, is from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
حتى لو (ḥattā law) — even if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a condition, then the Arabic for to stipulate.

You'll want to know: حتى لو.
حتى لو — ḥattā law — "even if".
سأذهب حتى لو أمطرت. sa-adhhabu ḥattā law amṭarat. I will go even if it rains.

The word, taken apart.
حتى لو (ḥattā law) joins the native Arabic particles ḥattā, "even, until", and law, "if": even if. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ضمن (ḍamina) — to ensure; to guarantee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for even if, then the Arabic for as if.

You'll want to know: ضمن.
ضمن — ḍamina — "to ensure; to guarantee".
ضمن الأب مستقبل ابنه. ḍamina al-ab mustaqbal ibnih. The father secured his son's future.
Present: يضمن, yaḍman.
Followed by أن: ضمن (ḍamina) أن …, made sure that ….

The word, taken apart.
ضمن (ḍamina) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-m-n, "to be surety for" (Lane). Ḍamina is a Form I verb, to stand surety: to guarantee, to ensure.
