# surgir — to arise

## You'll want to know: surgir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-02] -->

**surgir** — to arise, to come up, to emerge.

> La idea **surgió** de una encuesta.

*Ha surgido un problema* — a problem has come up. *Surgir de* — to arise
*from* something, which is the shape you will meet most.

The **g** is the throaty one before *i*: *sur-JIR*. Before *a* or *o* it is
written **j** — *surjo*, I arise — so the sound stays put while the spelling
moves.

## Grammar Lens: the verb that reports an origin
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-03] -->

*Surgir* says something appeared **without anyone deciding it would**. That is
its whole job, and it is why a question about where an idea came from uses it.

| what you say | what it implies |
|---|---|
| la idea **surgió de** | it emerged, nobody planned it |
| alguien tuvo la idea | a person had it |

So *¿de dónde surgió la idea?* is not asking who is responsible. It is asking
what the idea **came out of** — a conversation, a complaint, a survey.

Answer it with *de* and a source, the way the question is built.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-03] -->

Latin **surgere**, to rise — itself **sub-** plus **regere**, to direct: to
straighten oneself up from below.

The picture is physical and the word has kept it. English took the same verb
for a **surge**, a rising of water, and for **resurgent** and **insurgent**,
things that rise again or rise against.

So *surgir* is not *appear*. It is **rise**, and what rises came from
somewhere underneath — which is exactly why it wants *de*.
