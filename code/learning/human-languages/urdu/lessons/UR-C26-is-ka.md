# اس کا (is kā) — his, her

[PAUSE 2s] What is the oblique of **یہ**? Then say **our room**.

- [YOU RECALL: two chapters back, the shape a noun takes before a postposition — *kamre*]

## You'll want to know: اس کا
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-IS-KA]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-IS-OBLIQUE, UR-LEX-KAMRA] -->

> **اس کا کمرہ** — *is kā kamrā* — **his room**, or **her room.**

> **ان کا کمرہ** — *un kā kamrā* — **their room**, or **his room**, respectfully.

Urdu has no word for *his* and none for *her*. It takes the demonstrative in the
oblique shape you learned two chapters ago and puts **کا** after it — which is
why this could not be taught earlier. The third-person possessive is built on
the oblique, and until the oblique existed there was nothing to build it on.

## Grammar Lens: the column, finally full
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-IS-KA, UR-GRAMMAR-HAMARA, UR-GRAMMAR-AAP-KA, UR-LEX-MERA-NAAM-HAI, UR-PRAGMATICS-YOU-REGISTER] -->

| person | possessive | written |
|---|---|---|
| I | **میرا** | fused |
| we | **ہمارا** | fused |
| you, respectfully | **آپ کا** | apart |
| he, she, it | **اس کا** | apart |
| they, or he respectfully | **ان کا** | apart |

Five of the six an A1 candidate needs, and the shape of the sixth — *your*, to
somebody close — follows the same rule.

Every one of them ends in **-ا** and agrees like an adjective, so **اس کی ماں**
is *his mother* and **اس کے کمرے میں** is *in his room*.
