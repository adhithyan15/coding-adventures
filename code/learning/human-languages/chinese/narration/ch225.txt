Mandarin Chinese, chapter 225: In the Country.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nóngcūn — the countryside; a farming village.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a butterfly, then the Mandarin for a bee.

You'll want to know: nóngcūn.
nóngcūn — "the countryside; a farming village".
wǒ lǎojiā zài nóngcūn — My home town is out in the countryside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nóngcūn is built from nóng 'farming' and cūn 'village', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nowng and tshwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'farming the fields' and the second 'a farmstead'.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lùyíng — to camp out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to work the land, then the Mandarin for to pick fruit.

You'll want to know: lùyíng.
lùyíng — "to camp out".
zhōumò wǒmen qù lùyíng — We're going camping at the weekend.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lùyíng is built from lù 'dew, the open air' and yíng 'a camp'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese yweng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to build'; the dictionary explains the first, luH there, as dew. How the pair came to mean camping is not settled.
