Sanskrit, chapter 407: Drivers and Reins.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
यन्ता (yantā) — a driver of horses; a charioteer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a queue, then the Sanskrit for free space.

You'll want to know: यन्ता.
यन्ता — yantā — "a driver of horses; a charioteer".
यन्ता रथं मार्गे चालयति। yantā rathaṃ mārge cālayati. The charioteer drives the chariot along the road.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
यन्ता (yantā) is built on the root √yam, "to hold, to restrain", with the agent ending -tṛ; MW gives yantṛ as a driver of horses or elephants, a charioteer (MW).

कृषकः प्रतोदेन वृषभं नुदति। kṛṣakaḥ pratodena vṛṣabhaṃ nudati. The farmer drives the bull on with a goad.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतोदः (pratodaḥ) is built from pra, "forth", and the root √tud, "to strike, to push"; MW gives pratoda as a goad or long whip (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
युनक्ति (yunakti) — harnesses; yokes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a whip, then the Sanskrit for a goad.

You'll want to know: युनक्ति.
युनक्ति — yunakti — "harnesses; yokes".
यन्ता रथे अश्वं युनक्ति। yantā rathe aśvaṃ yunakti. The charioteer harnesses the horse to the chariot.
Past (he …): सः रथे अश्वं युक्तवान्, saḥ rathe aśvaṃ yuktavān.

The word, taken apart.
युनक्ति (yunakti) comes from the root √yuj, "to yoke, to join", which also gives yojayati, "joins"; MW gives its participle yukta as yoked or harnessed; MW compares Greek zeugnumi and Latin jungo (MW).
