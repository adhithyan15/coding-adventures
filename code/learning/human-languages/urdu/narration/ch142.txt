Lesson 8 of 8.
Your first complete A1 writing paper — a complete 20-minute A1 form and named-reader message under assessment conditions.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is an attempt, not a model. You will write the answers, and this page will not show you any copyable answer.
Put away Roman Urdu, Devanagari, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers. Set one timer for 20 minutes. Keep it running through both tasks. Write in Urdu script; either approved Nastaliq or accessibility Naskh is fine. Ordinary legible handwriting is enough: imitation Nastaliq calligraphy is not scored.

Writing — timed assessment production.
Task 1 — practical form: 7 minutes, 40 points.
Complete all six fields in Urdu script for a new neighbourhood activity group. Short answers are enough.
Your name.
Your city or village.
A language you speak.
One place you like.
One drink or food you like.
One everyday action or kind of work you do.
At seven minutes, move on even if a field is unfinished. Do not restart the timer and do not polish the form with time meant for the message.
Task 2 — message: 13 minutes, 60 points.
Reader: Amina. Purpose: help Amina recognise you and arrange your first meeting tomorrow.
Write 30–40 words in Urdu script. Greet Amina, introduce yourself, say where you live and which language you speak, mention one place and one drink or food you like, ask where Amina lives, and close politely. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose and connect the Urdu yourself.
When the 20-minute timer rings, stop, even if a line is unfinished. Record the number of completed form fields and the message word count. An honest timed attempt is more useful than a perfect untimed one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Only after you have stopped, review the same attempt. Do not rewrite it yet. Mark one strength and one next step under each assessment criterion:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — six filled fields; the named reader, meeting purpose, and requested message points are present.
Comprehensibility and organisation — Amina can follow the message and see how its ideas connect.
Urdu vocabulary, grammar, and register — familiar words and sentence patterns carry the intended meaning in a suitable everyday tone.
Urdu orthographic control — Urdu letters, joining, spacing, and punctuation remain readable. Optional short vowels or diacritics earn no bonus and receive no penalty unless a tested contrast depends on them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Keep the paper. On another day, repeat the same two-task shape with new personal details. The goal is not to memorise this prompt; it is to make a complete Urdu attempt calmly inside the real limit.
