Japanese, chapter 146: Sleeping Soundly and Waking Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ねむる (nemuru) — to fall asleep; to sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for open for business, then the Japanese for not yet open.

You'll want to know: ねむる.
ねむる — nemuru — "to fall asleep; to sleep".
こどもは よく ねむります。 — kodomo wa yoku nemurimasu — "The child sleeps well."
Polite ねむります, nemurimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ねむる (nemuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
さめる (sameru) — to wake up; to come round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to fall asleep, then the Japanese for soundly.

You'll want to know: さめる.
さめる — sameru — "to wake up; to come round".
あさ めが さめます。 — asa me ga samemasu — "I wake up in the morning."
Polite さめます, samemasu; an ichidan verb. With め, me, it means one's eyes open from sleep.

The word, taken apart.
さめる (sameru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おこす (okosu) — to wake someone; to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for soundly, then the Japanese for to wake up.

You'll want to know: おこす.
おこす — okosu — "to wake someone; to raise".
ははは まいあさ こどもを おこします。 — haha wa maiasa kodomo o okoshimasu — "My mother wakes the children every morning."
Polite おこします, okoshimasu; a godan verb. Its intransitive partner is おきます, okimasu.

The word, taken apart.
おこす (okosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as okiru, "to get up"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
すいみん (suimin) — sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to wake up, then the Japanese for to wake someone.

You'll want to know: すいみん.
すいみん — suimin — "sleep".
すいみんは たいせつです。 — suimin wa taisetsu desu — "Sleep is important."

The word, taken apart.
すいみん (suimin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "drowsy, sleep" (Middle Chinese dzyweH) and min, "sleep" (men) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
