# wohnen — "to live," and your first regular verb

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-ENDINGS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Until now you have assembled fixed phrases. This chapter hands you
the machine itself — and the first verb to run through it.

## You'll want to know: wohnen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-WOHNEN-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-ENDINGS-01] -->

> **wohnen** — "to live, to dwell."

Not *to be alive* — that is a different verb. *Wohnen* is where your bed is.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-SOUND-WOHNEN-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WOHNEN-01, GE-SOUND-H-LENGTHEN-01] -->

- `w-as-v` — the **w** is an English *v*.
- `h-silent-lengthening` — the **h** only stretches the *o*, exactly as in
  *gehen* and *sehen*.

So: *VOH-nen*, with a long open *oh*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-WOHNEN-WONT-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WOHNEN-01] -->

**wohnen** comes from Old High German *wonēn*, "to dwell, to be accustomed, to
be content" — because dwelling somewhere and *being used to* it were the same
idea.

That root survives in English, just barely, in **wont**: "as is his *wont*"
means "as he is accustomed." It is also kin to **win** and **wish** — all from
one root about striving for something, or being content with it.

So *wohnen* is not a stranger. It is the word behind *wont*, still doing the
older job.
