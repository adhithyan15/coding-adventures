Gujarati, chapter 486: Staying Well.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
આરોગ્ય (ārogya) — good health.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a dose, then the Gujarati for a diet restriction.

You'll want to know: આરોગ્ય.
આરોગ્ય — ārogya — "good health".
રોજ ચાલવું આરોગ્ય માટે સારું છે. roj cālvũ ārogya māṭe sārũ che. Walking every day is good for your health.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
આરોગ્ય (ārogya) is a tatsama, Sanskrit ārogya, "freedom from disease, health", as Monier-Williams records it; CDIAL lists an older spoken form, ārog (CDIAL 1330).

તાજો ખોરાક શરીર માટે સારો છે. tājo khorāk śarīr māṭe sāro che. Fresh food is good for the body.

The word, taken apart.
ખોરાક (khorāk) is usually traced to Persian xurāk, "food", built on xurdan, "to eat".

Lesson 5 of 6.

You'll want to know: સુકાવું.
સુકાવું — sukāvũ — "to waste away; to dry up".
લાંબા તાવમાં બાળક સુકાઈ ગયું. lā̃bā tāvmā̃ bāḷak sukāī gayũ. The child wasted away during the long fever.
Past (it …): તે છોડ સુકાઈ ગયો, te choḍ sukāī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
સુકાવું (sukāvũ) goes back to a reconstructed śuṣkati, "dries"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to become emaciated" (CDIAL 12552).
