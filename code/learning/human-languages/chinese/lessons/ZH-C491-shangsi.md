## You'll want to know: 上司
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C491-BOSS491-02]; assesses=[] -->

**上司** — *shàngsi* — "one's superior at work".

> **我的新上司比以前的好** *wǒ de xīn shàngsi bǐ yǐqián de hǎo* — My new boss is better than the last one.

The new character, in pieces: 司 — five strokes: one long stroke for the frame, a bar along the top that turns down the right side and hooks; a short bar under it; then 口 *kǒu* inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The second syllable is light: shàngsi, with no tone of its own.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**上司** (*shàngsi*) is built from 上 'above' and 司 'to be in charge', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *dzyangH* and *si* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a ruler' and the second 'to be in charge'.
