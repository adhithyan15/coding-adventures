# सः पठति स्म (saḥ paṭhati sma) — what was going on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-TAVAT-01, SA-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-TAVAT-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "he read the book". (*saḥ pustakaṃ paṭhitavān*.) That tells one finished event. This lesson tells the scene around it.

## You'll want to know: the present with स्म
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C276-ONGOING-PAST-01]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PATHATI-READ, SA-LEX-PLAY, SA-LEX-C20-PERSON-03, SA-LEX-C240-TIME240-03] -->

> **सः पठति स्म।**
>
> *saḥ paṭhati sma.* — "He was reading."

**स्म** (*sma*) after a present puts it in the past: "he was reading", or "he used to read". The verb keeps its present ending. Lanman's vocabulary describes it as a present that tells the past (he cites Whitney §778).

> **बालाः क्रीडन्ति स्म।**
>
> *bālāḥ krīḍanti sma.* — "The children were playing."

> **सा प्रतिदिनं पठति स्म।**
>
> *sā pratidinaṃ paṭhati sma.* — "She used to read every day."

## You'll want to know: यदा … तदा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C276-ONGOING-PAST-02]; assesses=[SA-LEX-AHAM, SA-LEX-KHADATI-EAT, SA-LEX-AGACCHATI-COME] -->

**यदा** (*yadā*) is "when", opening the scene; **तदा** (*tadā*), "then", opens what broke into it. The scene takes **स्म**, the event the past:

> **यदा अहं पठामि स्म,**
>
> *yadā ahaṃ paṭhāmi sma,*
>
> **तदा मीरा आगतवती।**
>
> *tadā mīrā āgatavatī.* — "When I was reading, Mira came."

> **यदा सः खादति स्म,**
>
> *yadā saḥ khādati sma,*
>
> **तदा अहं गतवान्।**
>
> *tadā ahaṃ gatavān.* — "While he was eating, I left."

| the sentence | what it tells |
|---|---|
| **सः पठितवान्** | one event: he read |
| **सः पठति स्म** | the scene: he was reading |
