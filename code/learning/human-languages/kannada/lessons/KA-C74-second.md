## You'll want to know: ಎರಡನೆಯ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C74-ORDINAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ಎರಡನೆಯ** (*eraḍaneya*) — "**second**".

**ಒಂದು, ಎರಡು, ಮೂರು** answer **how many**. This word answers **which one in the
line**, and Kannada keeps a second set of words for that job.

It goes straight in front of what it counts, and nothing on the noun changes:
**ಎರಡನೆಯ ದಿನ** (*eraḍaneya dina*), the second day; **ಎರಡನೆಯ ಬಾಗಿಲು**
(*eraḍaneya bāgilu*), the second door.

## Grammar Lens: the ending, and why the chapter opens on two
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C74-ORDINAL-02]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C74-ORDINAL-01, KA-LEX-C07-ERADU-01, KA-LEX-C07-ONDU-01] -->

Look at the word against the number it comes from:

| cardinal | ordinal |
|---|---|
| **ಎರಡು** (*eraḍu*) | **ಎರಡನೆಯ** (*eraḍaneya*) |

The final **-ು** goes, and **-ಅನೆಯ** (*-aneya*) goes on. That is the rule, and
in Kannada it has no exceptions at all — every number you know already takes it.

So the chapter does not open on **one**. It opens on **two**, because *eraḍu* is
the first number that shows the ending working, and because the one word that
will surprise you is *first* — which is not built on **ಒಂದು** at all. An
exception is only worth looking at once you can see what it is an exception to.
