Arabic, chapter 484: Heat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
موجة حر (mawjat ḥarr) — a heatwave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for frost, then the Arabic for to get cold.

You'll want to know: موجة حر.
موجة حر — mawjat ḥarr — "a heatwave".
وصلت موجة حر إلى المدينة. waṣalat mawjat ḥarr ilā al-madīna. A heatwave reached the city.

The word, taken apart.
موجة حر (mawjat ḥarr) is built from two native Arabic words: mawja, "a wave", on the root m-w-j (Lane), and ḥarr, "heat", on ḥ-r-r (Lane).

خانق — khāniq — "stifling; suffocating".
كان الحر خانقا في الحافلة. kāna al-ḥarr khāniqan fī al-ḥāfila. The heat was stifling on the bus.

The word, taken apart.
خانق (khāniq) is built on the native Arabic root kh-n-q, "to strangle" (Lane). It is the active participle, "choking".

تبخر — tabakhkhara — "to evaporate".

The word, taken apart.
تبخر (tabakhkhara) is built on the native Arabic root b-kh-r, "vapour, steam" (Lane). It is Form V, "to turn to vapour".
