Mandarin Chinese, chapter 216: Something Has Gone Wrong.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zāogāo — oh no; what a mess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for laundry shop, then the Mandarin for to dry-clean.

You'll want to know: zāogāo.
zāogāo — "oh no; what a mess".
zāogāo wǒ de qiánbāo diū le — Oh no, I've lost my wallet!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zāogāo is built from zāo 'dregs' and gāo 'cake', both inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): tsaw, glossed 'dregs', and probably the kaw that the dictionary writes with another form of the character, glossed with a word for gruel. How the pair came to mean a mess is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for saying no and asking.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: zāogāo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zāogāo, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zāogāo wǒ de qiánbāo diū le — Oh no, I've lost my wallet!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for laundry shop, then the Mandarin for to dry-clean, then say zāogāo again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does zāogāo mean? ("Oh no; what a mess".) Say it once more.

qíguài — "strange; odd".

The word, taken apart.
qíguài is built from qí and guài, both inherited from Middle Chinese, gje and kweajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'different' and the second 'strange'.
