Spanish, chapter 434: El Taller Y La Máquina.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
el taller — where things get mended.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el coche. Say el trabajo. The car has stopped working. Name the place it goes.

You'll want to know: el taller.
el taller — the workshop, the garage in the sense of repair shop.
Careful with that English word: el garaje is where you park; el taller is where it gets fixed. English uses garage for both and Spanish does not.

The word, taken apart.
el taller also means a workshop in the other sense — a short practical class. Un taller de cocina, un taller de reparación de bicicletas.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
the phrase: llevar el coche al taller. what it is: the repair shop.
the phrase: un taller de cocina. what it is: a hands-on class.
The two meanings share a core that English keeps as well: a place where people work with their hands, rather than sit and listen. If a notice offers you a taller, expect to do something, not to watch.

Lesson 2 of 7.
la máquina — the word above the others.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la lavadora. Say el ordenador. You can name particular machines. Name the kind.

You'll want to know: la máquina.
la máquina — the machine.
Stressed on the first syllable, MÁ-qui-na, and the written accent marks it.

The word, taken apart.
Notice what el ordenador is: ordenar plus the doer-ending, the thing that orders. Spanish named the computer after what it does, where English named it after counting.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
language: Spanish. the computer is: the orderer.
language: English. the computer is: the counter.
la máquina sits above all of these as the general term, and it is the word you need for the ones with no special name: la máquina de hacer pan, la máquina del billete. When you do not know an appliance's name, la máquina de plus what it does will be understood every time.

Lesson 3 of 7.
la pieza — the part that is missing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la máquina and el taller. The machine is in the workshop and one small thing is wrong with it. Name that thing.

You'll want to know: la pieza.
la pieza — the part, the piece, the component.
Falta una pieza — a part is missing. Era una pieza pequeña — it was a small part. Both are what a repair shop says to you.

The word, taken apart.
la pieza is wider than English part. It also names a room in some regions, and a piece of music almost everywhere.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
una pieza de la lavadora means a component.
una pieza de Mozart means a piece of music.
A word that covers several English words is not being vague. It is naming something English happens to split: a discrete thing that belongs to a larger whole. Hold that idea and all three uses follow from it.

Lesson 4 of 7.
la batería — the ending that is not one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say la tubería. You learned that -ería collects. Here is a word that ends the same way. Guess what it collects, then read on.

You'll want to know: la batería.
la batería — the battery. The one in a car, a phone, a torch.
It also names a drum kit, which is the same idea of a set of things acting together.

The word, taken apart.
Here the guess fails, and it is worth knowing why.
There is no Spanish word bate that batería collects. The word arrived whole from French batterie, where it had been built from battre, to beat — a battery of guns, a set of things striking together.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
word: la tubería. where the building happened: in Spanish, from tubo.
word: la estantería. where the building happened: in Spanish, from estante.
word: la batería. where the building happened: in French, from battre.
So the resemblance is real but it is inherited, not made here. That is the honest shape of the rule you have been building for three chapters: look for the join, and accept that sometimes the join happened in another language and the pieces never entered this one.
A guess that fails costs you nothing. Not guessing costs you every word you could have read.

Lesson 5 of 7.
el cargador — the one that loads.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el móvil. Say la batería. The battery is empty. Name the thing you plug in — and try the ending before you are told.

You'll want to know: el cargador.
el cargador — the charger.
cargar — to load, to charge. cargar el móvil is to charge the phone.

The word, taken apart.
Fourth time for -dor, and by now it should arrive before the lesson does:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
verb: proveer. the doer: el proveedor.
verb: lavar. the doer: la lavadora.
verb: contar. the doer: el contador.
verb: cargar. the doer: el cargador.
cargar is worth more than the charger. It means to load, and it reaches much further than electricity: cargar el coche is to load the car, la carga is the load itself, and no se cobra from earlier meets it in hacerse cargo de — to take charge of something.
The same verb also turns up wherever a shop charges you, which is why cobrar and cargar feel adjacent without being the same word.

Lesson 6 of 7.
(taller, máquina, pieza, batería, cargador) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say the workshop, the machine and the charger.

Grammar Lens: the guess, and the one that failed.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: el cargador. comes apart?: yes — cargar + -dor.
word: la batería. comes apart?: no — built in French, not here.
Four chapters have now handed you -dor, -ería and -ero, and batería is the first word that wore an ending and did not come apart. Do not read that as a warning against guessing. A guess that fails costs nothing; not guessing costs every word you could have read without help.
el taller and el garaje are the pair to keep straight: English calls both garage, Spanish does not. You park in one and your car is repaired in the other.

What you've built.
You could name machines and could not say anything about one that had stopped. La lavadora and el ordenador were yours; the place they go, the general word for the kind, the part that is missing, the battery that is flat and the charger you lent somebody were not.
That is most of what a person actually says about a device — and almost none of it is in a beginner's dialogue, because beginners' dialogues are about things that work.
