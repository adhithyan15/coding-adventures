Spanish, chapter 564: El héroe de la historia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el personaje — the character (in a story).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to relate, then the Spanish for the narrator.

You'll want to know: el personaje.
el personaje — "the character (in a story)".
Mi personaje favorito es el abuelo. My favourite character is the grandfather.
Always masculine, even for a woman: el personaje principal.

The word, taken apart.
personaje was borrowed from French personnage (EtymDB), built on Latin persōna 'an actor's mask, a role'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el protagonista — the main character, the lead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the narrator, then the Spanish for the character.

You'll want to know: el protagonista.
el protagonista — "the main character, the lead".
La protagonista de la película es una médica. The main character in the film is a doctor.
The same form for a woman: la protagonista.

The word, taken apart.
protagonista is usually traced to Greek prōtagōnistḗs 'the first actor', built on prôtos 'first' and agōnistḗs 'a competitor', from agṓn 'a contest'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el héroe — the hero.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the character, then the Spanish for the main character.

You'll want to know: el héroe.
el héroe — "the hero".
El bombero fue el héroe del día. The firefighter was the hero of the day.
A woman is la heroína.

The word, taken apart.
héroe is a learned borrowing from Latin hērōs, which comes from Greek hḗrōs 'a demigod, a hero' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la leyenda — the legend; the caption.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the main character, then the Spanish for the hero.

You'll want to know: la leyenda.
la leyenda — "the legend; the caption".
Según la leyenda, aquí vivía un dragón. According to the legend, a dragon lived here.
On a map or a photo it is the key or caption.

The word, taken apart.
leyenda comes from Medieval Latin legenda 'things to be read' (EtymDB), from legere 'to read'. The first legends were the lives of saints read aloud.
