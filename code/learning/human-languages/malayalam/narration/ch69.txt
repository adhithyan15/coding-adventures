Malayalam, chapter 69: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Passage.
4 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
(reading) — six words, and the mark that ends them.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have already met. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them to you first.

Reading.
ഇത് അത് ആണ് ഉണ്ട് പൂവ് പേര്.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time look at how every one of them ends.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
All six end in the same small hook, the chandrakkala, and it is the most common mark on any Malayalam page.
[pause 2 seconds]
It does two different jobs, and where it sits tells you which. Inside a word it stacks two consonants into a single shape — that is the mark you learned when you first wrote the greeting.
[pause 2 seconds]
At the END of a word it does something else entirely: it is a faint half-u, breathed rather than said. പൂവ് does not stop at the v; there is a breath after it, and that breath is what the hook is for.
[pause 2 seconds]
So a Malayalam word can end in a mark that is not silence, and a reader who treats it as silence will sound wrong on five words out of six.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which word is the flower?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What does the chandrakkala do at the end of a word? (A faint half-u — not silence.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
(reading) — six lines, two verbs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words. These put them somewhere.
[pause 1 second]
Six lines, three things, and no word you have not met.

Reading.
ഇത് വാതിൽ ആണ്. ഇവിടെ വാതിൽ ഉണ്ട്. ഇത് പൂവ് ആണ്. ഇവിടെ പൂവ് ഇല്ല. അത് വിളക്ക് ആണ്. അവിടെ വിളക്ക് ഉണ്ട്.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read the six once, straight down.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read only the last word of each line.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
English would translate ആണ് and ഉണ്ട് with the same word. It is, it is. Malayalam keeps them apart, and the difference is worth having.
[pause 2 seconds]
ആണ് NAMES. It puts an equals sign between two things: this thing, that word. It says nothing at all about where either of them is.
[pause 2 seconds]
ഉണ്ട് PLACES. It says a thing exists somewhere, and the somewhere is usually the first word on the line.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why the last word of the line is worth reading first. It tells you whether the line is about to name something or point at it, before you have read the middle.
[pause 2 seconds]
And the pair that does NOT match: ഇല്ല is the negative of ഉണ്ട്, never of ആണ്. It is the one line here that takes a thing away.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines aloud, once, straight down]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which verb tells you WHERE a thing is?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which verb names, and which places? (ആണ് names, ഉണ്ട് places.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of the two does ഇല്ല cancel? (ഉണ്ട്.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
(reading) — the first passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now twenty-four of them together.
[pause 1 second]
Still not one new word.

Reading.
ഇത് എന്റെ വാതിൽ ആണ്. ഇത് പഴയ വാതിൽ ആണ്. ഇവിടെ കോലം ഉണ്ട്. ഇത് നല്ല കോലം ആണ്. അവിടെ വലിയ മരം ഉണ്ട്. ഇവിടെ പൂവ് ഇല്ല. എന്നാൽ ഇവിടെ മാല ഉണ്ട്. പിന്നെ ഇവിടെ വിളക്ക് ഉണ്ട്. ഇത് പുതിയ വിളക്ക് ആണ്. അവിടെ ചെറിയ കസേര ഉണ്ട്. കസേര പഴയ കസേര ആണ്. അവിടെ പുസ്തകം ഉണ്ട്. ഇപ്പോൾ വെയിൽ ഉണ്ട്. ആകാശം നീല ആണ്. ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഉണ്ട്. അവിടെ ചായ ഇല്ല. എന്നാൽ ഇവിടെ പാൽ ഉണ്ട്. ഇവിടെ നായ ഉണ്ട്. അവിടെ പൂച്ച ഉണ്ട്. നായ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്, പൂച്ച അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. ഇത് പഴയ പുസ്തകം ആണ്. എന്നാൽ അത് നല്ല പുസ്തകം ആണ്. ഇപ്പോൾ ഇവിടെ വലിയ മാല ഉണ്ട്. ഇപ്പോൾ ഇവിടെ നോക്കൂ.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the lines arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and watch ആണ് and ഉണ്ട് take turns.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Everything near is ഇവിടെ; everything far is അവിടെ. ആണ് names; ഉണ്ട് places.
[pause 2 seconds]
എന്നാൽ joins absence and presence: ഇവിടെ പൂവ് ഇല്ല. എന്നാൽ ഇവിടെ മാല ഉണ്ട്. It is the passage's one explicit connection.
[pause 2 seconds]
Order carries the rest.
[pause 2 seconds]
The first twenty-three lines describe. The last asks you to look.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
What is not here?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word joins two thoughts? (എന്നാൽ — but.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which line speaks to the reader rather than describing? (The last one — ഇവിടെ നോക്കൂ.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
(ക ര ന മ ത ഴ ് ി ാ) (onnaam onpath) — nine, and three kinds of thing.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: six letters, one killer, two hangers until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Two consonants you have been reading since the book's opening pages. Read ക, then ന, and say each one's name.

Script: six letters, one killer, two hangers.
These arrived one per chapter, and no page has put them side by side. Sorted by what kind of thing each one is rather than by when you met it:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
kind: letters — each carries a built-in a. pieces: ക ka, ര ra, ന na, മ ma, ത ta, ഴ ḻa.
kind: the killer. pieces: ് — takes the built-in a away.
kind: hangers. pieces: ി i, ാ ā — replace the built-in a with another vowel.
ഴ is the one to look at twice. It is the sound Malayalam is named for and that almost no other language has; a reader who has not seen it since the chapter that taught it will not place it at all.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
The three marks, cold, and what each does to the letter it touches.
[your turn — read: ് — say what it removes]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: ി — say what it replaces]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: ാ — say the same, and say how it differs from the one above]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
A letter on its own already has a vowel in it. That is the fact all three of these depend on: the killer takes it away, and the hangers swap it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What vowel is inside a bare Malayalam letter? (A short a.) Which of the nine takes it away? (്, the virama.) And which one is the sound the language is named for? (ഴ.)
