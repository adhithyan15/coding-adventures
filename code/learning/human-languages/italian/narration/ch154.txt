Lesson 1 of 5.
intorno — around.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for meanwhile, then the Italian for by now.

You'll want to know: intorno.
intorno — "around".

The word, taken apart.
intorno is in torno, "in a turn": torno goes back to Latin tornus, "a lathe" (Lewis & Short), which Latin took from Greek tornos. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dappertutto — everywhere.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for by now, then the Italian for around.

You'll want to know: dappertutto.
dappertutto — "everywhere".

The word, taken apart.
dappertutto is "from through all": the word for "from", plus per from Latin per, and tutto, "all" from Latin totus. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
da nessuna parte — nowhere.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for around, then the Italian for everywhere.

You'll want to know: da nessuna parte.
da nessuna parte — "nowhere".

The phrase, taken apart.
da nessuna parte is "from no part": da, "from"; nessuna, "not one", from Latin ne ipse una, "not even one"; and parte, "part" from Latin partem. (EtymDB 2.1 records da and parte, and derives Neapolitan nisciuno from ne ipse unus.)

Lesson 4 of 5.
altrove — elsewhere.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for everywhere, then the Italian for nowhere.

You'll want to know: altrove.
altrove — "elsewhere".

The word, taken apart.
altrove is "other where", from a Latin phrase ending in ubi, "where". EtymDB 2.1 gives it as aliter ubi; other accounts give alter ubi, "the other where".

laggiù is là, "there" from Latin illac, "that way", plus giù, "down", from Old Italian giuso. (EtymDB 2.1).
