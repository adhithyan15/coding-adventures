German, chapter 325: Der Teig.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
der Teig — the dough, the batter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to crack open, then the German for the whisk.

You'll want to know: der Teig.
der Teig — "the dough, the batter".
Der Teig muss eine Stunde ruhen. The dough has to rest for an hour.
Plural: die Teige.

The word, taken apart.
Teig is inherited from Old High German teig, from Proto-Germanic daigaz (EtymDB), the same source as English dough.

Lesson 2 of 6.
die Hefe — the yeast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the whisk, then the German for the dough.

You'll want to know: die Hefe.
die Hefe — "the yeast".
Für das Brot braucht man frische Hefe. For the bread you need fresh yeast.
No plural. In Austria: die Germ.

The word, taken apart.
Hefe is inherited from Old High German hevo, from Proto-Germanic hafjô "yeast" (EtymDB), related to heben "to lift": what makes dough rise.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ausrollen — to roll out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the yeast, then the German for to knead.

You'll want to know: ausrollen.
ausrollen — "to roll out".
Sie rollte den Teig dünn aus. She rolled the dough out thin.
Separable: sie rollt aus, sie hat ausgerollt.

The word, taken apart.
ausrollen is built on rollen, from Rolle "roll", which came through Old French rolle from Latin rotulus "a little wheel" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
das Nudelholz — the rolling pin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to knead, then the German for to roll out.

You'll want to know: das Nudelholz.
das Nudelholz — "the rolling pin".
Mit dem Nudelholz rollte er den Teig aus. He rolled out the dough with the rolling pin.
Plural: die Nudelhölzer. In Austria: der Nudelwalker.

The word, taken apart.
Nudelholz joins Nudel "noodle" and Holz "wood", Old High German holz, Proto-Germanic hultą (EtymDB): the wooden tool for noodle dough.
