Japanese, chapter 470: Songs and Tunes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きょく (kyoku) — a piece of music; a tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to play music, then the Japanese for the tone of an instrument.

You'll want to know: きょく.
きょく — kyoku — "a piece of music; a tune".
ははは この きょくが すきです。 — haha wa kono kyoku ga suki desu — "My mother likes this tune."

The word, taken apart.
きょく (kyoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): one Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese. It is kyoku, "melody" (Middle Chinese khjowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
くちぶえ (kuchibue) — whistling; a whistle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a folk song, then the Japanese for an enka ballad.

You'll want to know: くちぶえ.
くちぶえ — kuchibue — "whistling; a whistle".
かれは くちぶえを ふきました。 — kare wa kuchibue o fukimashita — "He whistled."

The word, taken apart.
くちぶえ (kuchibue) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: kuchi, "mouth" and bue, "flute (fue)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
