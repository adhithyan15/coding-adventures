Urdu, chapter 312: Rebellion.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
رعایا (riāyā) — the subjects (of a ruler).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a martyr, then the Urdu for a peace settlement.

You'll want to know: رعایا.
رعایا — riāyā — "the subjects (of a ruler)".
رعایا بادشاہ سے ناراض تھی۔ riāyā bādshāh se nārāz thī. The king's subjects were angry with him.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رعایا (riāyā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic raʿāyā, the plural of raʿiyya, "a flock, the people in one's care". Urdu treats it as one feminine body: رعایا ناراض تھی.

پہلے یہاں غلام کام کرتے تھے۔ pehle yahāṅ ghulām kām karte the. Slaves used to work here long ago.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
غلام (ghulām) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ghulām, "a youth, a servant"; in Urdu it is one who is owned. غلامی is slavery.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: للکارنا.
للکارنا — lalkārnā — "to challenge; to call out (an enemy)".
صبح ایک سپاہی نے بادشاہ کو للکارا۔ subah ek sipāhī ne bādshāh ko lalkārā. In the morning a soldier challenged the king.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے شیر کو للکارا, us ne sher ko lalkārā.

The word, taken apart.
للکارنا (lalkārnā) is built on lalkār, "a challenge, a defiance", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 10973). The one challenged takes کو.
