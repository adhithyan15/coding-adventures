# Review — one name, four different jobs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A-WHY, ES-GRAMMAR-VOCATIVE-NOT-OBJECT] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "I see María." (*Veo a María*.) "María, come." (*María, ven*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A-WHY, ES-GRAMMAR-VOCATIVE-NOT-OBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-PROPER-NOUN-NO-ARTICLE, ES-GRAMMAR-TITLE-TAKES-ARTICLE] -->

The name does not change. *María* is *María* in both. Everything that
distinguishes them sits **outside** the word.

> *Veo* ***a*** *María.* — she is what the seeing lands on.
> *María****,*** *ven.* — she is the one being spoken to.

[PAUSE 2s] A preposition in the first, a comma in the second. Latin did both
with endings on *María* herself, and had a whole case for each.

The article does a third job: a name points at one person already, so *el* has
nothing left to do — *María*, not *la María*. Give her a **title** and it comes
back: *la señora García*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-QUANTIFIER-AGREES-WITH-NOUN, ES-GRAMMAR-QUANTIFIER-FROZEN-AFTER-VERB, ES-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-BEFORE-NOUN, ES-GRAMMAR-CIRCUMSTANTIAL-MOVES, ES-GRAMMAR-WORD-ORDER-EMPHASIS, ES-GRAMMAR-EXCLAMATIVE-QUE] -->

The fourth job went to **position**, which decides form as well as meaning.

> *Mucha agua.* — leaning on a noun, so it agrees.
> *Come mucho.* — leaning on a verb, so it freezes.

Same word, two shapes, and the only difference is what it stands next to. The
ordinal does this one slot over: *el primer libro*, in front, shortened.

[PAUSE 2s] And because the verb's ending already says who did what, the pieces
that say **when** and **where** can move:

> *Hoy como en casa.*  ·  *Como en casa hoy.*  ·  *En casa como hoy.*

Three placements, one meaning — and the front slot is the emphatic one. Latin
bought that freedom with case endings; Spanish lost them and bought it back with
word order. Same trade in *¡**Qué** grande!*: the force sits at the front.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A-WHY, ES-GRAMMAR-WORD-ORDER-EMPHASIS, ES-GRAMMAR-QUANTIFIER-FROZEN-AFTER-VERB] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What replaced Latin's ending for the person on the receiving end? (A
little ***a***.) Which slot carries emphasis? (The **front** one.) And why does
*mucho* refuse to agree in *come mucho*? (It leans on a **verb**.)
