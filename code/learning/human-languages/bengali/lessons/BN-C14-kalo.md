# কালো (kalo) — "black," and a look-alike for "time"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C14-NIL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Blue carried a false friend. This color has a look-alike too: the
Sanskrit word for time, and for death, sounds the same.

## You'll want to know: কালো
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C14-KALO-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **কালো** — *kālo* — **black**

কালো চা (*kālo chā*) — black tea.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**কালো** is **ক** (*kô*) with the vowel sign **া** (*ā*), plus **ল** (*l*)
with the vowel sign **ো** (*o*): *kā-lo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**কালো** comes from Sanskrit **কাল** (*kāla*), **"black, dark"** (CDIAL
3083), which CDIAL takes from a Dravidian word. Hindi's *kālā* comes from the
same word, and so does the name of the goddess **Kālī**, "the dark one."

Sanskrit has a second **কাল** (*kāla*), **"time,"** and so "fate, death" (CDIAL
3084); as Time the destroyer it is linked with **Yama**, the god of death.
CDIAL gives the two separate entries, and Monier-Williams lists them as two
words. MW wonders whether "black" shares the root *kal-*, "to count," of
"time," and marks the guess as uncertain. The dictionaries keep the two words
apart.
