German, chapter 288: Ein Zusammenstoß.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zusammenstoßen — to collide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to disappear, then the German for to flee.

You'll want to know: zusammenstoßen.
zusammenstoßen — "to collide".
Zwei Autos stießen an der Kreuzung zusammen. Two cars collided at the crossroads.
Separable, Perfekt with sein: Sie sind zusammengestoßen.

The word, taken apart.
zusammenstoßen joins zusammen "together" and stoßen "to push, to bump", from Old High German stōzan, Proto-Germanic stautaną (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: prallen.
prallen — "to crash (into), to hit hard".
Das Auto prallte gegen einen Baum. The car crashed into a tree.
Perfekt with sein: Es ist gegen die Wand geprallt.

The word, taken apart.
prallen is usually traced to Middle High German prellen "to bounce off"; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 4 of 5.
verunglücken — to have an accident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the collision, then the German for to crash.

You'll want to know: verunglücken.
verunglücken — "to have an accident".
Mein Onkel verunglückte mit dem Fahrrad. My uncle had an accident on his bike.
Perfekt with sein: Er ist verunglückt. Built on das Unglück, misfortune.

The word, taken apart.
verunglücken is built on Unglück "misfortune", Middle High German unglück (EtymDB), from Glück "luck", Middle High German gelücke.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Schaden — the damage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to crash, then the German for to have an accident.

You'll want to know: der Schaden.
der Schaden — "the damage".
Der Schaden am Auto war groß. The damage to the car was great.
Plural: die Schäden. Schade! — what a pity — comes from the same word.

The word, taken apart.
Schaden is usually traced to Old High German scado "damage", which EtymDB derives from Proto-Germanic skaþô; EtymDB lists English scathe as a cousin.
