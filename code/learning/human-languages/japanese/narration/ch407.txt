Lesson 1 of 6.
さんぱい (sanpai) — a visit to worship (at a shrine or temple).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a Shinto priest, then the Japanese for to offer up.

You'll want to know: さんぱい.
さんぱい — sanpai — "a visit to worship (at a shrine or temple)".
かぞくで てらに さんぱいに いきました。 — kazoku de tera ni sanpai ni ikimashita — "My family went to the temple to worship."

The word, taken apart.
さんぱい (sanpai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are san, "visit" (Middle Chinese tshom) and hai, "worship" (peajH), joined as sanpai (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
おがむ (ogamu) — to pray with joined hands.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a great statue of the Buddha, then the Japanese for the main hall of a temple.

You'll want to know: おがむ.
おがむ — ogamu — "to pray with joined hands".
ほんどうの まえで おがみました。 — hondō no mae de ogamimashita — "I put my hands together in front of the main hall."
Polite おがみます, ogamimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): そぼが ほんどうで おがみました。, sobo ga hondō de ogamimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おがむ (ogamu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
