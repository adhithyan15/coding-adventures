Lesson 1 of 6.
буря (búrya) — a storm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a puddle, then the Russian for to pour.

You'll want to know: буря.
буря (búrya) — "a storm".
Ночью была сильная буря. Nóchyu bylá sílnaya búrya. There was a strong storm in the night.
Feminine. Буря is a storm of wind; гроза is a thunderstorm.

The word, taken apart.
буря (búrya) is inherited from Proto-Slavic buřa (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 6.
порыв (porýv) — a gust (of wind); an impulse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a storm, then the Russian for a hurricane.

You'll want to know: порыв.
порыв (porýv) — "a gust (of wind); an impulse".
Сильный порыв ветра сорвал с него шляпу. Sílnyy porýv vétra sorvál s negó shlyápu. A strong gust of wind blew his hat off.
Masculine. Порыв ветра — a gust of wind.

The word, taken apart.
порыв (porýv) is built from по- and рыв 'a jerk, a tearing', on the root of рвать 'to tear'. EtymDB lists порыв (porýv) without a deeper source.

Lesson 4 of 6.
греметь (gremét) — to rumble, to thunder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a hurricane, then the Russian for a gust.

You'll want to know: греметь.
греметь (gremét) — "to rumble, to thunder".
Всю ночь гремел гром. Vsyu noch gremél grom. Thunder rumbled all night.
Imperfective; perfective прогреметь. Present гремлю, гремишь.

The word, taken apart.
греметь (gremét) is inherited from Proto-Slavic grĭměti (EtymDB), which EtymDB takes back to the PIE root gʰrem- 'to resound, to thunder'; гром 'thunder' is its noun.
