German, chapter 221: Slim, Dreadful, Shy, Independent, Sunny.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
schlank — slim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for salty, then the German for sour.

You'll want to know: schlank.
schlank — "slim".

The word, taken apart.
schlank first meant "flexible, thin" and spread from Low German. Its origin is uncertain; EtymDB 2.1 has the word but no source for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
schrecklich — dreadful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for sour, then the German for slim.

You'll want to know: schrecklich.
schrecklich — "dreadful".

The word, taken apart.
schrecklich is the word for "fright" + -lich. It comes from the verb Old High German skreckōn, "to jump up in fright" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
schüchtern — shy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for slim, then the German for dreadful.

You'll want to know: schüchtern.
schüchtern — "shy".

The word, taken apart.
schüchtern came into standard German from Middle Low German schüchter, "shy, easily scared" (EtymDB 2.1) — a northern word, related to English shy.

Lesson 4 of 5.
selbstständig — independent, self-employed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for dreadful, then the German for shy.

You'll want to know: selbstständig.
selbstständig — "independent, self-employed".

The word, taken apart.
selbstständig is the word for "self", which EtymDB 2.1 lists beside English self, + a word built on the verb "to stand", Old High German stān: standing on one's own.
