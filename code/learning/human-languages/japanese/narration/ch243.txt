Japanese, chapter 243: Grandparents and Siblings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りょうしん (ryōshin) — parents; both parents.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a close friend, then the Japanese for to go out with.

You'll want to know: りょうしん.
りょうしん — ryōshin — "parents; both parents".
りょうしんは げんきですか。 — ryōshin wa genki desu ka — "Are our parents well?"

The word, taken apart.
りょうしん (ryōshin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ryō, "both" (Middle Chinese ljangX) and shin, "parent, intimate" (tshin) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
そだてる (sodateru) — to bring up; to raise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for my grandmother, then the Japanese for siblings.

You'll want to know: そだてる.
そだてる — sodateru — "to bring up; to raise".
あねは こどもを ひとりで そだてますか。 — ane wa kodomo o hitori de sodatemasu ka — "Will my elder sister bring up her child on her own?"
Polite そだてます, sodatemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
そだてる (sodateru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
