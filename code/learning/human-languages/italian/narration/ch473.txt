Italian, chapter 473: Sinceri e bugiardi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sincero — sincere, honest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for mean, then the Italian for to boast.

You'll want to know: sincero.
sincero — "sincere, honest".
Sii sincero: ti piace il mio vestito? Be honest: do you like my dress?
The noun is la sincerità.

The word, taken apart.
sincero is a learned borrowing from Latin sincērus "pure, clean, whole" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
il bugiardo — liar; lying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to boast, then the Italian for sincere.

You'll want to know: il bugiardo.
il bugiardo — "liar; lying".
Non credergli, è un bugiardo. Do not believe him, he is a liar.
Built on la bugia "a lie". Dire bugie — to tell lies.

The word, taken apart.
bugiardo is built on bugia "a lie" with -ardo (EtymDB). Bugia is usually traced to Old Occitan bauzia "deceit", itself from a Germanic word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ingenuo — naive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sincere, then the Italian for liar.

You'll want to know: ingenuo.
ingenuo — "naive".
Ero così ingenuo che credevo a tutto. I was so naive that I believed everything.
Do not mix it up with ingegnoso "clever, ingenious": ingenuo means naive, like English ingenuous.

The word, taken apart.
ingenuo is a learned borrowing from Latin ingenuus "native, freeborn", and so "frank, open" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
furbo — sly, crafty; clever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for liar, then the Italian for naive.

You'll want to know: furbo.
furbo — "sly, crafty; clever".
Il gatto è furbo: apre la porta da solo. The cat is crafty: it opens the door by itself.
Often a compliment in speech: che furbo! — how clever!

The word, taken apart.
furbo was borrowed from French fourbe "a cheat" (EtymDB), which is built on fourbir "to polish", a verb of Germanic origin.
