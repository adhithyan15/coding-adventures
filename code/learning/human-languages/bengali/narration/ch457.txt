Bengali, chapter 457: Generous and Mean.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
উদার (udār) — generous; broad-minded.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for conduct, then the Bengali for to master.

You'll want to know: উদার.
উদার — udār — "generous; broad-minded".
আমার কাকা উদার, সবসময় গরিবদের সাহায্য করেন। āmār kākā udār, sôbsômôy gôribder sāhājjô kôren. My uncle is generous: he always helps the poor.

The word, taken apart.
উদার (udār) is a tatsama, Sanskrit udāra, "high, lofty, noble" and "generous" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
কৃপণ (kripôn) — miserly; a miser.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to master, then the Bengali for generous.

You'll want to know: কৃপণ.
কৃপণ — kripôn — "miserly; a miser".
লোকটার অনেক টাকা আছে, কিন্তু সে খুব কৃপণ। lokṭār ônek ṭākā āchhe, kintu she khub kripôn. The man has plenty of money, but he is a real miser.

The word, taken apart.
কৃপণ (kripôn) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kṛpaṇa, "miserable, pitiable" (CDIAL 3436), which Monier-Williams also gives as "niggardly"; that is the sense Bengali kept.

আন্তরিক — āntôrik — "warm-hearted; sincere".
আমাদের নতুন প্রতিবেশীরা আন্তরিক। āmāder nôtun prôtibeshīrā āntôrik. Our new neighbours are warm-hearted.

The word, taken apart.
আন্তরিক (āntôrik) is Sanskrit antara, "interior, intimate" (Monier-Williams), with the adjective ending -ika: from the inside, from the heart.

পরোপকারী ছেলেটা সবসময় বুড়োদের সাহায্য করে। pôropôkārī chheleṭā sôbsômôy buṛoder sāhājjô kôre. The kind-hearted boy always helps old people.

পরোপকারী (pôropôkārī) is a tatsama, Sanskrit paropakārin, "assisting others, beneficent" (Monier-Williams): para "another" with upakāra "help".

Lesson 5 of 5.
মহৎ (môhôt) — noble; great-hearted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for sincere, then the Bengali for helpful to others.

You'll want to know: মহৎ.
মহৎ — môhôt — "noble; great-hearted".
গরিব ছাত্রটির পড়ার খরচ দিয়ে তিনি একটা মহৎ কাজ করলেন। gôrib chhātrôṭir pôṛār khôrôch diye tini ekṭā môhôt kāj kôrlen. By paying for the poor student's studies, he did a noble thing.

The word, taken apart.
মহৎ (môhôt) is a tatsama, Sanskrit mahat, "great, high, eminent" (Monier-Williams); Bengali uses it for greatness of heart. It ends in the new letter ৎ.
