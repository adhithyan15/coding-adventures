# bonne nuit — good night, and the feminine side of *bon*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last greeting of the day — and the one that finally shows *bon*
in its feminine coat.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [bon / bonne](./FR-C01-bon.md) · [nuit](./FR-C01-nuit.md)

## The word, taken apart — by assembling it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-BONNE-NUIT-11]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-ADJ-AGREEMENT-04, FR-LEX-BON-03, FR-LEX-NUIT-10, FR-LEX-BONJOUR-07, FR-LEX-BONSOIR-09] -->

**bonne nuit** = **bonne** ("good," feminine) + **nuit** ("night," feminine)
= "good night." Here it is at last: because *nuit* is **feminine**, *bon*
becomes **bonne** — and, from the *bon* lesson, remember they even *sound*
different: *bon* is nasal *bõ*, but *bonne nuit* is *bun nwee*, with a real
*n*.

So the three evening-ish greetings line up and show the whole agreement rule
in one breath:

- *bon**jour*** — *jour* masculine → *bon*
- *bon**soir*** — *soir* masculine → *bon*
- *bon**ne** nuit* — *nuit* feminine → *bonne*

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Unlike *bonsoir* (an evening *greeting*), *bonne nuit* is a **farewell for
bed** — "goodnight," said when parting for sleep. It maps onto the Spanish
split you may feel forming: Spanish overloads *buenas noches* for both, while
French keeps *bonsoir* (evening hello/bye) separate from *bonne nuit*
(off-to-sleep).
