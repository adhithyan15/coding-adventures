Lesson 1 of 5.
лев (lev) — a lion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for lips, then the Russian for the chest.

You'll want to know: лев.
лев (lev) — "a lion".

The word, taken apart.
лев (lev) from Old High German lëwo, from Latin leō, Greek léōn (Vasmer). Vasmer thinks it came through books, since a spoken loan would have given лёв, and that the word's ultimate source is probably Semitic.

обезьяна (obezyána) — Old Russian обезьяна (obezyána), recorded by Afanasy Nikitin; it comes from Turkish and Persian abuzine, "monkey", reshaped to look like Russian words with о- and без- (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
слон (slon) — an elephant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a lion, then the Russian for a monkey.

You'll want to know: слон.
слон (slon) — "an elephant".

The word, taken apart.
слон (slon) — origin disputed. It is usually read as a bookish coinage from прислониться, "to lean", after the old Physiologus, which says the elephant sleeps leaning on a tree; Vasmer thinks that link is folk etymology and finds a loan from Turkic arslan, "lion", acceptable in its sounds.

Lesson 4 of 5.
пчела (pchelá) — a bee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a monkey, then the Russian for an elephant.

You'll want to know: пчела.
пчела (pchelá) — "a bee".

The word, taken apart.
пчела (pchelá) from Proto-Slavic bĭčela — inherited; б turned to п before ч. Vasmer relates it to Irish bech, Lithuanian bìtė and Old High German bîa, all "bee": English bee is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
муравей (muravéy) — an ant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an elephant, then the Russian for a bee.

You'll want to know: муравей.
муравей (muravéy) — "an ant".

The word, taken apart.
муравей (muravéy) from Proto-Slavic morvĭjĭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Irish moirb, Avestan maoiri- and Old Norse maurr, all "ant"; Latin formīca, "ant", is related too.
