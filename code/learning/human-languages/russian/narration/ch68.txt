Russian, chapter 68: Day, Week, Month, Year, Evening.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
день (den) — a day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for cheap, then the Russian for badly.

You'll want to know: день.
день (den) — "a day".

The word, taken apart.
день (den) from Proto-Slavic dĭnĭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Sanskrit dínam, Lithuanian dienà and Latin diēs, all "day", from the PIE root dyew-, "to shine".

Lesson 2 of 5.
неделя (nedélya) — a week.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for badly, then the Russian for a day.

You'll want to know: неделя.
неделя (nedélya) — "a week".

The word, taken apart.
неделя (nedélya) is не, "not", with дело, "work": first "the day of no work", Sunday, and then the week that runs from it (Vasmer). Vasmer thinks it most likely copies Latin fēria, "holiday".

You'll want to know: месяц.
месяц (mésyats) — "a month".

The word, taken apart.
месяц (mésyats) from Proto-Slavic měsęcĭ, "moon; month" — inherited. Vasmer lists Sanskrit mās-, Greek mḗn and Latin mēnsis, "month", and Lithuanian mėnuo, "moon": English moon and month are cousins. A month is one round of the moon.

Lesson 4 of 5.
год (god) — a year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a week, then the Russian for a month.

You'll want to know: год.
год (god) — "a year".

The word, taken apart.
год (god) from Proto-Slavic godŭ, "a fitting time, a season" — inherited; Old Russian godŭ meant "time, term" (Vasmer). Vasmer relates it to Latvian gadīgs, "capable", and Old High German gigat, "fitting"; the same root is in годиться, "to be fit".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a month, then the Russian for a year.

You'll want to know: вечер.
вечер (vécher) — "the evening".

The word, taken apart.
вечер (vécher) from Proto-Slavic večerŭ — inherited, a cousin of Lithuanian vãkaras, "evening" (Vasmer). Vasmer thinks Greek hespéra and Latin vespera, "evening", probably belong here too, with a changed first sound.
