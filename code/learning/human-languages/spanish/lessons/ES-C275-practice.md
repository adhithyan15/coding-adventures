# Four differences a translation loses

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DEBER-DE, ES-LEX-SI-QUE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Each of these four survives translation into English as the *same
sentence*. That is what makes them the last things you learn.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DEBER-DE, ES-SEMANTIC-MODAL-EPISTEMIC, ES-LEX-COMETER-UN-ERROR, ES-SEMANTIC-COLLOCATION, ES-LEX-SI-QUE, ES-SEMANTIC-EMPHASIS, ES-LEX-DELGADO-FLACO, ES-SEMANTIC-CONNOTATION] -->

[PAUSE 1s] For each pair, say both and then say what English does with the
difference.

- [YOU SAY: *debe estar* and *debe de estar* — English gives both **must**, and
  leaves you to guess which]
- [YOU SAY: *cometer un error* and the *hacer* version — English cannot even see
  the error; both translate to *make a mistake*]
- [YOU SAY: *habla español* and *sí que habla español* — English needs the voice,
  or italics, and loses it in plain text]
- [YOU SAY: *delgado* and *flaco* — English gives **thin** for both, and the
  dictionary removes exactly the information you needed]

[PAUSE 2s]
- [YOU SAY: which of the four could get you into trouble socially, and where
  (*flaco* — warm in much of Latin America, harder elsewhere)]
- [YOU SAY: which one no rule can teach you (the collocation — only having met it)]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SEMANTIC-MODAL-EPISTEMIC, ES-SEMANTIC-COLLOCATION, ES-SEMANTIC-EMPHASIS, ES-SEMANTIC-CONNOTATION] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the four: a guess marked in the verb, a verb the noun insists on,
an emphasis that implies a doubt, and a synonym that is not one. What do all four
have in common? (English translates them identically.)
