Spanish, chapter 524: El incendio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el incendio — the fire (big, destructive).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to testify, then the Spanish for to free.

You'll want to know: el incendio.
el incendio — "the fire (big, destructive)".
El incendio empezó en la cocina. The fire started in the kitchen.
El fuego is fire in general; un incendio is a fire that burns a building or a forest.

The word, taken apart.
incendio is a learned borrowing from Latin incendium 'a fire, a blaze' (EtymDB), built on incendere 'to set on fire'. English incendiary comes from the same verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
arder — to burn, to be on fire.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fire, then the Spanish for to set on fire.

You'll want to know: arder.
arder — "to burn, to be on fire".
El edificio ardió toda la noche. The building burned all night.
Arder is to be burning; to burn something is quemar.

The word, taken apart.
arder is inherited from Latin ārdēre 'to be on fire, to blaze' (EtymDB). English ardent comes from the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la alarma — the alarm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to set on fire, then the Spanish for to burn.

You'll want to know: la alarma.
la alarma — "the alarm".
Sonó la alarma a las tres de la mañana. The alarm went off at three in the morning.
Dar la alarma — to raise the alarm.

The word, taken apart.
alarma comes from the Italian cry all'arme 'to arms' (EtymDB), and arme goes back to Latin arma 'weapons'. An alarm was first a call to take up your weapons.
