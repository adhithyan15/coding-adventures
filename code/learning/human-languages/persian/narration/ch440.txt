Persian, chapter 440: When things go wrong.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سر و صدا (sar-o sedâ) — noise, a din.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for central heating, then the Persian for a view.

You'll want to know: سر و صدا.
سر و صدا (sar-o sedâ) — "noise, a din".
اتاق ما شب پر از سر و صدا بود. Otâq-e mâ shab por az sar-o sedâ bud. Our room was full of noise at night.
Sar-o sedâ kardan — to make a racket. Also a fuss: sar-o sedâ-ye ziyâdi kard — it caused a stir.

The word, taken apart.
sar-o sedâ is a pair of native sar 'head', from Middle Persian sar (EtymDB), and sedâ 'sound', which EtymDB takes from Arabic ṣadā 'echo'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
کثیف (kasif) — dirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a view, then the Persian for noise.

You'll want to know: کثیف.
کثیف (kasif) — "dirty".
حوله های حمام کثیف بود. Hole-hâ-ye hammâm kasif bud. The bathroom towels were dirty.
The opposite of tamiz. Note the letter ث, said s.

The word, taken apart.
kasif was borrowed from Arabic kaṯīf (EtymDB). In Arabic the word usually means 'thick, dense'; in Persian the sense moved to 'dirty'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
صدا دادن (sedâ dâdan) — to make a noise, to squeak, to rattle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for noise, then the Persian for dirty.

You'll want to know: صدا دادن.
صدا دادن (sedâ dâdan) — "to make a noise, to squeak, to rattle".
در اتاق موقع باز شدن صدا میدهد. Dar-e otâq mowqe'-e bâz shodan sedâ midahad. The room's door squeaks when it opens.
sedâ 'sound' + dâdan 'to give'. Present stem dah-: sedâ midahad.

The word, taken apart.
sedâ dâdan is built from sedâ 'sound', which EtymDB takes from Arabic ṣadā 'echo', and dâdan 'to give', which EtymDB traces to the PIE root deh₃- 'to give'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
بدبو (badbu) — smelly, foul-smelling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for dirty, then the Persian for to make a noise.

You'll want to know: بدبو.
بدبو (badbu) — "smelly, foul-smelling".
آب لوله بدبو بود. Âb-e lule badbu bud. The tap water smelled bad.
bad 'bad' + bu 'smell'; the opposite of khoshbu.

The word, taken apart.
badbu is a native compound of bad 'bad' and bu 'smell', the word behind buyidan 'to smell'. EtymDB does not list the compound.

Lesson 5 of 5.
آب گرمکن (âbgarmkon) — a water heater, a boiler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to make a noise, then the Persian for smelly.

You'll want to know: آب گرمکن.
آب گرمکن (âbgarmkon) — "a water heater, a boiler".
آب گرمکن (âbgarmkon) خراب بود و آب حمام سرد بود. Âbgarmkon kharâb bud va âb-e hammâm sard bud. The water heater was broken and the bath water was cold.
âb 'water' + garm 'hot' + kon 'making'.

The word, taken apart.
âbgarmkon is a native compound: âb 'water' goes back to Middle Persian āb (EtymDB), garm 'warm' is usually traced to Middle Persian garm, and kon is the present stem of kardan 'to do'.
