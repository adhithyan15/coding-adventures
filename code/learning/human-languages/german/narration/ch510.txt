German, chapter 510: Auf der Tribüne.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Tribüne — stand, grandstand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for medal, then the German for award ceremony.

You'll want to know: die Tribüne.
die Tribüne — "stand, grandstand".
Wir saßen auf der Tribüne direkt hinter dem Tor. We sat in the stand right behind the goal.
Plural: die Tribünen.

The word, taken apart.
Tribüne came from French tribune, from Italian tribuna (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Latin tribūnal, the raised platform of the magistrates (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Zuschauer — spectator; viewer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for award ceremony, then the German for stand.

You'll want to know: der Zuschauer.
der Zuschauer — "spectator; viewer".
Im Stadion waren fünfzigtausend Zuschauer. There were fifty thousand spectators in the stadium.
Plural: die Zuschauer. Also a TV viewer.

The word, taken apart.
Zuschauer is built on zuschauen "to watch", from schauen "to look", inherited from Old High German scouwōn, from Proto-Germanic skawwōną (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
anfeuern — to cheer on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for stand, then the German for spectator.

You'll want to know: anfeuern.
anfeuern — "to cheer on".
Die Fans feuern ihre Mannschaft laut an. The fans loudly cheer on their team.
Separable: wir feuern an, hat angefeuert.

The word, taken apart.
anfeuern is built on an "on" and feuern "to fire", from Feuer "fire", inherited from Old High German fiur (EtymDB): to fire someone up.

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Jubel — cheering, jubilation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for spectator, then the German for to cheer on.

You'll want to know: der Jubel.
der Jubel — "cheering, jubilation".
Nach dem Tor war der Jubel groß. After the goal there was huge cheering.
No plural. The verb: jubeln — to cheer.

The word, taken apart.
Jubel comes from Middle High German jūbel, from Latin iūbilus (EtymDB); Lewis & Short gives iūbilum "a wild cry, a shout".

Lesson 5 of 5.
buhen — to boo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to cheer on, then the German for cheering.

You'll want to know: buhen.
buhen — "to boo".
Nach dem schlechten Spiel buhten die Zuschauer. After the poor game the spectators booed.
Regular: hat gebuht. From the cry buh!

The word, taken apart.
buhen is built on the cry buh!; EtymDB lists the verb without a source, and it is usually explained as imitative.
