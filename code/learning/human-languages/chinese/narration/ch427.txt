Mandarin Chinese, chapter 427: Comings and Goings.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gébì — next door.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the hotel star rating, then the Mandarin for a power socket.

You'll want to know: gébì.
gébì — "next door".
wǒ zhù zài gébì — I'm staying next door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gébì is built from gé 'to separate' and bì 'a wall', both inherited from Middle Chinese, keak and pek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to block' and the second 'a wall'.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

Lesson 2 of 6.
来往 (láiwǎng) — to come and go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a power socket, then the Mandarin for right next door.

You'll want to know: 来往.
来往 — láiwǎng — "to come and go".
路口来往的车很快你要小心 lùkǒu láiwǎng de chē hěn kuài nǐ yào xiǎoxīn — The cars coming and going at the junction are fast: be careful.
The new character, in pieces: 往 (wǎng) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left the left piece of 很, three strokes, two short strokes falling to the left and a vertical; on the right a dot, two bars, a vertical through them and a long bar at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
来往 (láiwǎng) is built from 来 'to come' and 往 (wǎng) 'to go', both inherited from Middle Chinese, loj and hjwangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to go'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
往 (wǎng) — the left piece of 很, then a dot over three bars.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the left piece of 很, then a dot over three bars and Guided Practice.

很 来.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — hěn, lái]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the left piece of 很, then a dot over three bars.
往 (wǎng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, three strokes, as in 很: two short strokes falling to the left, one under the other, then a vertical down from the second.
The right piece, five strokes. A dot on top. A bar, then a second bar. A vertical down through them. Then a long bar across the foot.
3 + 5 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 往 (wǎng), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 往 (wǎng)? (Eight.) What is the left piece? (The left piece of 很.) What closes the right piece at the foot? (A long bar.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 往 (wǎng), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 4 of 6.
zìzhùcān — buffet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for right next door, then the Mandarin for to come and go.

You'll want to know: zìzhùcān.
zìzhùcān — "buffet".
zhè jiā jiǔdiàn de zǎofàn shì zìzhùcān — Breakfast at this hotel is a buffet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zìzhùcān is built from zì 'oneself', zhù 'to help' and cān 'a meal', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dzijH, dzrjoH and tshan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the third 'to swallow'.
