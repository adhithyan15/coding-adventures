Latin, chapter 391: Even If and Otherwise.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
etiamsī — even if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for only, then the Latin for however much.

You'll want to know: etiamsī.
etiamsī — "even if".
Etiamsī pluet, ambulābimus. Even if it rains, we shall walk.
Etiamsī = etiam + sī: even if. It takes the mood a sī clause would take.

The word, taken apart.
etiamsī is built from etiam "even, also" and sī "if" (Lewis & Short), and is often written as two words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
tametsī — although, even though.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for however much, then the Latin for even if.

You'll want to know: tametsī.
tametsī — "although, even though".
Tametsī fessus erat, labōrāvit. Although he was tired, he worked.
Tametsī = tamen + etsī, and like etsī it mostly takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
tametsī is built from tamen "yet" and etsī "even if" (Lewis & Short), with the end of tamen worn away.

Lesson 3 of 5.
aliōquī — otherwise; in other respects.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for even if, then the Latin for although.

You'll want to know: aliōquī.
aliōquī — "otherwise; in other respects".
Festīnāre dēbēmus; aliōquī nāvem āmittēmus. We must hurry; otherwise we shall miss the ship.
Aliōquī (also aliōquīn): otherwise, or else; also, in other respects.

The word, taken apart.
aliōquī is built from a form of alius "other" and the old adverb quī "in some way" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): in another way.

Lesson 4 of 5.
secus — differently, otherwise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for although, then the Latin for otherwise.

You'll want to know: secus.
secus — "differently, otherwise".
Rēs secus accidit atque spērābāmus. Things turned out otherwise than we hoped.
Secus is an adverb: nōn secus ac, not otherwise than, in the same way as; haud secus, in no other way.

The word, taken apart.
secus comes from the root of sequor "to follow" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): coming after, so less well, otherwise.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sīve — or if; whether … or.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for otherwise, then the Latin for differently.

You'll want to know: sīve.
sīve — "or if; whether … or".
Sīve pluit sīve sōl lūcet, ambulō. Whether it rains or the sun shines, I go for a walk.
Sīve (or seu) = sī + -ve, or: sīve … sīve, whether … or.

The word, taken apart.
sīve is built from sī "if" and the particle -ve "or" (Lewis & Short).
