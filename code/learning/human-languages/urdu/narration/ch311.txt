Urdu, chapter 311: Victory and Defeat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ہرانا (harānā) — to defeat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a bow for arrows, then the Urdu for an army on the march.

You'll want to know: ہرانا.
ہرانا — harānā — "to defeat".
ہماری فوج نے انہیں ہرایا۔ hamārī fauj ne unheṅ harāyā. Our army defeated them.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بادشاہ کو ہرایا, us ne bādshāh ko harāyā.

The word, taken apart.
ہرانا (harānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousins hārnā, "to lose", and harānā, "to defeat", under Sanskrit hārayati (CDIAL 14061). It is the causing partner of ہارنا.

Lesson 3 of 5.
فتح (fateh) — a victory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to defeat, then the Urdu for to throw down.

You'll want to know: فتح.
فتح — fateh — "a victory".
فتح کے بعد جشن ہوا۔ fateh ke bād jashn huā. After the victory there was a celebration.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
فتح (fateh) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic fatḥ, "an opening", and so the taking of a city. It is feminine.

Lesson 4 of 5.
شہید (shahīd) — a martyr.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to throw down, then the Urdu for a victory.

You'll want to know: شہید.
شہید — shahīd — "a martyr".
جنگ میں دو سو سپاہی شہید ہوئے۔ jang meṅ do sau sipāhī shahīd hue. Two hundred soldiers fell in the war.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
شہید (shahīd) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic shahīd, "a witness", and so one who dies for a cause. شہید ہوئے is said of soldiers killed in battle.
