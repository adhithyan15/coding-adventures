Latin, chapter 95: Hot, Cold, Dry, Wet, Sweet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
calidus, calida, calidum — hot, warm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fast, then the Latin for slow.

You'll want to know: calidus, calida, calidum.
calidus, calida, calidum — "hot, warm".

The word, taken apart.
calidus is built on caleō, "to be warm" (EtymDB 2.1), with -idus, the ending of state adjectives. caleō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kelh1-, "warm" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
frīgidus, frīgida, frīgidum — cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for slow, then the Latin for hot.

You'll want to know: frīgidus, frīgida, frīgidum.
frīgidus, frīgida, frīgidum — "cold". English frigid.

The word, taken apart.
frīgidus is built on frīgeō, "to be cold" (Lewis & Short), from frīgus, "cold", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European sriHgos-, "cold" (EtymDB 2.1). Greek rhigos, "frost", is a cousin.

ūmidus is built on ūmeō, "to be moist" (Lewis & Short), with -idus, the ending of state adjectives. The spelling with h came later.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dulcis, dulce — sweet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dry, then the Latin for wet.

You'll want to know: dulcis, dulce.
dulcis, dulce — "sweet". Honey, mel, is the sweetest thing.

The word, taken apart.
dulcis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dlku-, "sweet" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek glykys, "sweet", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).
