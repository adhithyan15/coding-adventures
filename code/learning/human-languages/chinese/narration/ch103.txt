Mandarin Chinese, chapter 103: Using Things.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
用 (yòng) — to use.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for beer, then the Mandarin for biscuit.

You'll want to know: 用.
用 — yòng — "to use".
我用水洗菜 wǒ yòng shuǐ xǐ cài — I wash the vegetables in water.
The new character, in pieces: 用 (yòng) — the frame of the moon piece in 明, open at the foot, with two bars inside and a vertical down the middle. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
用 (yòng) is inherited from Middle Chinese yowngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to employ' and 'by means of'.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Lesson 2 of 6.
用 (yòng) — the moon frame, two bars, a vertical.

Script — the moon frame, two bars, a vertical.
用 (yòng).
The frame, two strokes, as on the right of 明: a left-falling stroke down the left side, then a stroke across the top that turns down the right side and hooks.
Two horizontals inside, one above the other.
A vertical down the middle, from the top through both bars to the foot.
2 + 2 + 1 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 用 (yòng), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 用 (yòng)? (Five.) Which character you know has the same frame? (明, on its right.) Which stroke comes last? (The vertical down the middle.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
ná — to take; to hold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for biscuit, then the Mandarin for to use.

You'll want to know: ná.
ná — "to take; to hold".
nǐ ná wǒ de yàoshi le ma — Did you take my keys?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ná is probably inherited from Middle Chinese nrae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary lists under an older form of the character, glossed 'to pull'. That the two are one word is likely; how 'to pull' became 'to take' is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 6.
rēng — to throw; to throw away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to use, then the Mandarin for to take hold of.

You'll want to know: rēng.
rēng — "to throw; to throw away".
bú yào rēng wǒ de shū — Don't throw my books away.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rēng is inherited from Middle Chinese nying (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to pull'. The regular change from nying gives réng, with a rising tone, not the level rēng of today, and throwing is not given there; how the word came to its sound and sense is not settled.
