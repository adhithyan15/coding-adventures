Marathi, chapter 2: Hear Thanks, Then Meet Six Signs.
7 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
धन्यवाद (dhanyavad) (dhanyavād) — "thank you".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The formal "thanks" — Sanskrit, shared across the Indian languages.

You'll want to know — hear the word before reading it.
Keep the spelling in the heading as a sign you are not yet asked to decode. Listen first: dhan-ya-vād. Say it once for "thank you." The next two tiny chapters will give you one missing sign at a time; only then will you read and write the whole word.

The word, taken apart.
Dhanyavād = Sanskrit dhanya ("worthy") + vāda ("a saying") — "an utterance of '[you are] worthy.'" The same Sanskrit word behind Hindi dhanyavād, Kannada dhanyavāda, Telugu dhanyavādamulu. Warmer, more spoken Marathi often says ābhārī āhe ("[I] am grateful"), on the root ābhār, "gratitude."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "dhanyavād" — no reading yet]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the warmer spoken form — "ābhārī āhe"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the two Sanskrit pieces of dhanyavād, and a warmer Marathi alternative? (dhanya "worthy" + vāda "a saying"; ābhārī āhe.)


Lesson 2 of 7.
ः (ḥ) — a small breath after a vowel.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You already know the greeting namaskār and its older first piece, namaḥ, "a bow." The final breath in namaḥ has its own mark.

Script.
ः (ḥ).
The visarga is two dots written vertically to the right of a syllable. It marks a quiet breath after the vowel. For this first encounter, place the upper dot, lift, then place the lower dot. Stop after this one sign.

Writing — visible model.
Keep ः (ḥ) visible. Point to the upper dot, then the lower dot. Finger-trace the two positions once and copy the sign once.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name it: visarga, a breath after a vowel.
Source: Unicode Devanagari chart, U+0903.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Copy the visible two-dot breath sign once.


Lesson 3 of 7.
आ (ā) — long aa standing alone.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Set one blank line aside for a single new vowel sign.

Script.
आ (ā).
This is independent ā, used when the vowel begins a syllable. Curve around the joined left body; lift for the middle shoulder; descend the inner stem; descend the trailing stem; finish with the headline from left to right.

Writing — trace, then guided copy.
Keep आ visible. Finger-trace the five runs once. Copy it once, looking back after every run. Say ā only when the pen stops.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Is this a vowel sign attached to a consonant? (No. It stands alone.)
Source: Saurmandal, Devanagari आ (ā) stroke order, frames 1–5.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
Copy the visible independent long-aa sign once, finishing with the headline.


Lesson 4 of 7.
भ — bha, with a puff of breath.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Make room for one consonant and one careful copy.

Script.
भ (bha).
Say ba, then bha with a small puff against your palm. For the shape, sweep the upper loop into the trunk, lower bowl, and crossbar without lifting; descend the right stem; finish the headline left to right.

Writing — guided copy.
Keep भ visible. Copy one run at a time, looking back before each. Say bha once after the headline is complete.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which has the puff: ba or bha? (bha.)
Source: JackPotte, Devanagari bh stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
Copy the visible breathy-b consonant once.


Lesson 5 of 7.
े — e above the headline.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Leave a small space above the next practice headline.

Script.
े (e).
This mātrā replaces the inherent vowel with e. Add its short rising stroke above the consonant's headline; it cannot stand alone as a spoken vowel here.

Writing — guided copy and first retrieval.
Copy े once beside the visible model. Then cover the previous three models and write, from their names, visarga, independent ā, and bha. Uncover and repair only one differing part.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Where does e sit? (Above the headline.)
Source: Unicode Devanagari chart, U+0947.
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Copy the e mark, then retrieve the three preceding signs by name.


Lesson 6 of 7.
ं (ṃ) — one nasal dot.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Prepare to place one mark above a headline.

Script.
ं (ṃ).
The anusvāra is one dot above the headline. It marks a nasal sound whose exact place follows the sound after it. Place one dot; do not turn it into the two-dot visarga.

Writing — guided copy.
Keep ं (ṃ) visible and copy it once. Point above the imaginary headline where the dot belongs.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
One dot or two? (One.)
Source: Unicode Devanagari chart, U+0902.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Copy the visible one-dot nasal sign once.


Lesson 7 of 7.
त — dental ta.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
Touch the tongue lightly behind the upper teeth once.

Script.
त (ta).
Touch the tongue lightly behind the upper teeth: ta. Sweep left across the shoulder and curve down to the open tip; descend the right stem; finish the headline left to right.

Writing — look, hide, write.
Look at त (ta) for five seconds. Cover it, wait five seconds, and write it once. Uncover and compare body, stem, then headline.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ta once with the tongue at the teeth.
Source: Opiaterein, Devanagari ta stroke animation.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
After a five-second delay, write dental ta from memory.
