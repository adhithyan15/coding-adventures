Gujarati, chapter 430: Scales and Packets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ત્રાજવું (trājvũ) — a pair of scales.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be robbed, then the Gujarati for to be affordable.

You'll want to know: ત્રાજવું.
ત્રાજવું — trājvũ — "a pair of scales".
દુકાનદાર ત્રાજવામાં ખાંડ તોલે છે. dukāndār trājvāmā̃ khā̃ḍ tole che. The shopkeeper weighs sugar on the scales.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ત્રાજવું (trājvũ) is usually traced to Persian tarāzū, "a balance, a pair of scales".

Lesson 2 of 5.
મણ (maṇ) — a maund (an old measure of weight).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be affordable, then the Gujarati for a pair of scales.

You'll want to know: મણ.
મણ — maṇ — "a maund (an old measure of weight)".
વેપારીએ બજારમાં દસ મણ ઘઉં વેચ્યા. vepārīe bajārmā̃ das maṇ ghaũ vecyā. The trader sold ten maunds of wheat in the market.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મણ (maṇ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit maṇa, "a measure of grain"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "a maund" (CDIAL 9730). Farmers still sell grain by the maund.

You'll want to know: પડીકું.
પડીકું — paḍīkũ — "a small packet (wrapped in paper)".
દુકાનદારે ચાનું પડીકું વાળીને આપ્યું. dukāndāre cānũ paḍīkũ vāḷīne āpyũ. The shopkeeper folded up a packet of tea and handed it over.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
પડીકું (paḍīkũ) seems to belong with Sanskrit puṭa, "a fold, a pocket", under which CDIAL lists a Gujarati word for a packet (CDIAL 8253).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ઘરાક (gharāk) — a customer (everyday); a regular buyer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a maund, then the Gujarati for a small packet.

You'll want to know: ઘરાક.
ઘરાક — gharāk — "a customer (everyday); a regular buyer".
આ દુકાનના ઘરાક વરસોથી અહીં આવે છે. ā dukānnā gharāk varsothī ahī̃ āve che. This shop's customers have come here for years.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઘરાક (gharāk) is usually traced to Sanskrit grāhaka, "one who takes" (Monier-Williams), worn down in speech; ગ્રાહક keeps the learned form of the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
જોખવું (jokhvũ) — to weigh out (goods).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a small packet, then the Gujarati for a regular buyer.

You'll want to know: જોખવું.
જોખવું — jokhvũ — "to weigh out (goods)".
દુકાનદારે બે કિલો ચોખા જોખ્યા. dukāndāre be kilo cokhā jokhyā. The shopkeeper weighed out two kilos of rice.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ખાંડ જોખી, teṇe khā̃ḍ jokhī.

The word, taken apart.
જોખવું (jokhvũ) goes back to yōkṣati, a form CDIAL reconstructs; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to examine, to weigh" (CDIAL 10525).
