## Grammar Lens: two endings and one exception
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-03, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-05] -->

| word | built from |
|---|---|
| **la lavadora** | lavar + **-dora** |
| **el contador** | contar + **-dor** |
| **la estantería** | estante + **-ería** |
| **la tubería** | tubo + **-ería** |
| **el sofá** | nothing — borrowed whole |

Four of five came apart. Do not read a rule into it — the next five household
words might all be whole. What this chapter is for is the **question**, asked
before you reach for a dictionary: *is there a piece in here I already have?*

The two endings answer different questions. **-dor** and **-dora** name the one
that does it. **-ería** names where the thing is kept or collected.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-04, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-02, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-04] -->

**You could name a house and a chair, and almost nothing inside either.**
*La casa*, *la silla* and *el agua* have been yours for a long time. What was
missing was everything a notice on a door actually talks about: the meter
somebody comes to read, the pipework they are repairing, the machine that broke.

You now hold the vocabulary of the **building**, not only of the home.
