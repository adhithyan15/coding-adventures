Italian, chapter 434: Il bagno non funziona.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il rubinetto — the tap, the faucet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bar of soap, then the Italian for the hair dryer.

You'll want to know: il rubinetto.
il rubinetto — "the tap, the faucet".
Il rubinetto dell'acqua calda non funziona. The hot tap does not work.
Aprire il rubinetto — to turn on the tap.

The word, taken apart.
rubinetto was borrowed from French robinet 'tap' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
intasato — blocked, clogged.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the tap, then the Italian for to drip.

You'll want to know: intasato.
intasato — "blocked, clogged".
Il tubo è intasato. The pipe is blocked.
Roads too: le strade sono intasate.

The word, taken apart.
intasato is the past form of intasare 'to clog', built on taso, the crust that wine leaves in a barrel. EtymDB links taso to the name of a Greek island known for its wine, but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lo scarico — the drain, the plughole; the flush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to drip, then the Italian for blocked.

You'll want to know: lo scarico.
lo scarico — "the drain, the plughole; the flush".
L'acqua non va giù nello scarico. The water will not go down the drain.
Il tubo di scarico — the exhaust pipe of a car.

The word, taken apart.
scarico is built on scaricare 'to unload', from s- and caricare 'to load', which is inherited from Late Latin carricāre, built on carrus 'cart'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sturare — to unblock, to unclog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for blocked, then the Italian for the drain.

You'll want to know: sturare.
sturare — "to unblock, to unclog".
L'idraulico ha sturato lo scarico. The plumber unblocked the drain.
Also to uncork a bottle.

The word, taken apart.
sturare is built from s- and turare 'to stop up', which is usually traced to Latin obtūrāre 'to stop up' (Lewis & Short), an inherited word.
