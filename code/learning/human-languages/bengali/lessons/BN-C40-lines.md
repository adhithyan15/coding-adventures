# (reading) — six lines, and a bow nobody makes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words. These are things you would say.

[PAUSE 1s] You have said every one of them.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SKILL-READ-LINES]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C01-NOMOSHKAR-01, BN-CONCEPT-C01-DHONNOBAD-01] -->

> নমস্কার।
> ধন্যবাদ।
> হ্যাঁ।
> না।
> দয়া করে।
> স্বাগতম।

[PAUSE 3s] Read down once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and notice the mark at the end of each line. It is not a
full stop but a **দাঁড়ি**, a single upright stroke.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C13-DOYAKORE-01, BN-LEX-C13-SBAGOTOM-01, BN-SKILL-READ-LINES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **নমস্কার** is a bow written down: *namas* plus *kara*, the making
of a bow. The commonest word in the language is a gesture that stopped being
made.

[PAUSE 2s] **দয়া করে** is two words and means *having done kindness*. Bengali
asks for things by describing what the other person has already generously
done, which is a habit worth noticing on the page where it sits complete.

[PAUSE 2s] Six lines, and four of them are a single word.
