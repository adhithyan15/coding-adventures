Marathi, chapter 54: A Little, Enough, Quite a Lot.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
थोडा (thoḍā) — a little, some.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
जरा softens an adjective. It cannot measure out a quantity of something: jarā water is not Marathi.
[your turn — recall: say jarā kaṭhīṇ, then double a word to strengthen it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: थोडा.
थोडा — thoḍā — a little, some, a small amount of.
थोडं पाणी — thoḍaṁ pāṇī — a little water.
The ending is where the difference lives. थोडा (thoḍā) ends in -आ, so it agrees: थोडा (thoḍā) चहा (masculine), थोडं पाणी (neuter). जरा never changes. The two are close in meaning and behave in opposite ways, and the reason is that they are different parts of speech — थोडा (thoḍā) measures a NOUN, जरा softens an adjective or a verb.
थोडा (thoḍā) चहा — a little tea. जरा कठीण — slightly difficult.
Its ancestor is Sanskrit स्तोक (stoka), "a little, a drop," worn down through the Prakrits. Hindi's thoṛā is the same word by the same road.
Against खूप it is the other end of the same scale: खूप पाणी is a lot of water, थोडं पाणी is a little.

[your turn — say: jarā — and hear that it never would]

Lesson 2 of 5.
पुरेसा (puresā) — enough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A little and a lot are both amounts. Neither one says the amount is right.

You'll want to know: पुरेसा.
पुरेसा — puresā — enough, sufficient.
पुरेसं पाणी — puresaṁ pāṇī — enough water.
Another -आ word, so it agrees and you already have all three forms: पुरेसा (puresā) चहा, पुरेसं पाणी.
Said on its own it closes a transaction. पुरे! is that's enough, and a Marathi host pouring tea will stop when you say it — which is a more useful thing to own than it sounds, because the alternative is नको, and नको refuses the whole offer rather than the next spoonful.
That पुरे is the word underneath पुरेसा (puresā), and it comes from the Sanskrit root पूर् (pūr-), "to fill." Enough is filled. The same ancient root gives Latin plenus and English full, so the idea has travelled a long way without changing.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: puresaṁ pāṇī, puresā chahā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: pure! — that's enough]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: thoḍaṁ, puresaṁ, khūp — three amounts, smallest first]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say there is enough water, then stop somebody pouring, then refuse the offer entirely.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does पुरे stop, and what does नको stop? (पुरे stops the pouring; नको refuses the offer.) What does the root पूर् mean? (To fill.)

Lesson 3 of 5.
बराच (barāch) — quite a lot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A little, enough, a lot. There is a step between enough and a lot that Marathi bothers to name.

You'll want to know: बराच.
बराच — barāch — quite a lot, a fair amount, rather.
बरंच पाणी — baraṁch pāṇī — quite a lot of water.
You already own both halves of this word. बरा is the fine, all right of chapter four — the बरं you answer how are you with — and -च is Marathi's little emphatic tail, the one that means precisely that. Glue them together and a fair amount is literally a good-ish bit.
It agrees, because the बरा underneath it ends in -आ: बराच (barāch) चहा, बरंच पाणी.
Set the four amounts in order and the scale is complete:
थोडं a little, पुरेसं enough, बरंच quite a lot, खूप a lot.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 4 of 5.
किती मोठं! (kitī moṭhaṁ!) — how big!

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
किती has asked for a number since chapter thirty-seven. Put it in front of an adjective instead of a noun and it stops asking.

You'll want to know: किती with an adjective.
किती मोठं! — kitī moṭhaṁ! — how big! किती सुंदर! — kitī sundar! — how beautiful!
Same word, different neighbour, different job. In front of a noun — किती भाऊ? — किती asks for a number. In front of an adjective it exclaims, and no number is wanted or given.
Two things tell the listener which one you mean. On the page it is the! you learned in chapter thirty-three, where a question would have carried?. Out loud it is the tune: the exclamation falls at the end where a question rises. Say the pair back to back and hear your own voice do it.
English works exactly this way — how many? against how lovely! — so this is a construction you can feel rather than one you have to install.
The adjective still agrees, and there is nothing else to remember: किती मोठं! (kitī moṭhaṁ!) of a house, किती मोठी! of a room.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kitī sundar! — and let the voice fall]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī bhāū? — and let it rise]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kitī moṭhaṁ! of a house, then kitī moṭhī! of a room]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
