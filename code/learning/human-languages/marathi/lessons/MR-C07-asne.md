# असणे (asṇe) — "to be," and the word your sentence ends on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have already said it: *mājhaṁ nāv … āhe*. Now meet the verb that
form belongs to, and notice where Marathi insists on putting it.

## You'll want to know: असणे and its present आहे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-ASNE-AAHE]; assesses=[] -->

> **असणे** — *asṇe* — **to be**

The ending **-णे** (*-ṇe*) is the Marathi infinitive, the same ending as *bolṇe*
"to speak." Its present tense is short, irregular, and already yours: **आहे**
(*āhe*) — "am, is, are."

| who | Marathi | said |
|---|---|---|
| मी (I) | **आहे** | *āhe* |
| तू (you, familiar) | **आहेस** | *āhes* |
| तुम्ही (you, respectful) | **आहात** | *āhāt* |

## Sounds you'll need: अ · स · णे and आ · हे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**अ** is the bare vowel *a*; **स** is *sa*; **णे** is the retroflex **ण** *ṇa*
carrying the *-e* stroke — *a-sa-ṇe*. **आ** is that first letter with an upright
stroke added, the long *ā*. **हे** is **ह** *ha* with the same *-e* stroke:
*ā-he*.

## The word, taken apart — the oldest verb in the family
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Sanskrit's word for "is" was **अस्ति** *ásti*, sitting on the stem *as-*, and
that stem goes back to the Indo-European root **\*h₁es-**. It surfaces almost
everywhere the family reaches: Latin *est*, Greek *estí*, German *ist*, and
English **is**. *Asṇe* is built straight on it.

**आहे** itself is the contested piece. Some trace it to *ásti* directly; others
route it through a Middle Indo-Aryan verb *acchai* "stays, remains." The family
is not in doubt, the exact line of descent is — so treat it as an open question
rather than a settled fact.

## Grammar Lens: the sentence ends on आहे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-AAHE-LAST]; assesses=[] -->

English slots *is* into the middle of a sentence: "My name **is** Sara." Marathi
holds it back to the very end:

**माझं नाव सारा आहे** — *mājhaṁ nāv Sārā āhe* — piece by piece, "my name Sara
is."

That is the rule for every Marathi verb, and *āhe* is where it becomes
unmissable. Whatever else the sentence carries, the verb closes it.
