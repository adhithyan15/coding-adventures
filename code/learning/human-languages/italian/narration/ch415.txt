Italian, chapter 415: A noleggio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
noleggiare — to hire, to rent (a car or a bike).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the pothole, then the Italian for the dirt road.

You'll want to know: noleggiare.
noleggiare — "to hire, to rent (a car or a bike)".
In vacanza noleggiamo una macchina. On holiday we hire a car.
For a car, a bike or a boat; for a flat, affittare.

The word, taken apart.
noleggiare is built on nolo 'hire, freight charge', which is usually traced through Medieval Latin naulum to Greek naûlon 'the fare for a passage by ship'; it came into Italian as a borrowing.

You'll want to know: la targa.
la targa — "the number plate; the plaque".
Hai visto la targa di quella macchina? Did you see that car's number plate?
Also a plaque on a wall, in memory of someone.

The word, taken apart.
targa first meant a shield. It is usually traced to a Germanic word for a shield, but the exact path is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il libretto — the logbook (of a car); the booklet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the hire, then the Italian for the number plate.

You'll want to know: il libretto.
il libretto — "the logbook (of a car); the booklet".
Il vigile chiede patente e libretto. The warden asks for the licence and the logbook.
Il libretto di circolazione is the car's registration document; at university, il libretto records your exams.

The word, taken apart.
libretto is a little libro 'book', which is inherited from Latin liber 'book' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'assicurazione — the insurance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the number plate, then the Italian for the logbook.

You'll want to know: l'assicurazione.
l'assicurazione — "the insurance".
L'assicurazione della macchina scade a maggio. The car insurance runs out in May.
Assicurare — to insure, and to assure.

The word, taken apart.
assicurazione is built on assicurare 'to make safe', from a- and sicuro 'safe', which is inherited from Latin sēcūrus 'free from care' (Lewis & Short).
