# se piove, resto a casa — the condition that really can happen

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SE-01, IT-LEX-C178-VERBI178-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if". (*Se.*) Say "it was raining". (*Pioveva.*) Now the
present: "it's raining". (*Piove.*)

## Grammar Lens: both halves in the present
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-SE-PRESENTE-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C135-VERBI135-03, IT-LEX-C82-CITTA2-02] -->

> **Se piove, resto a casa.** — If it rains, I stay at home.
> **Se il treno è in ritardo, prendo l'autobus.** — If the train is late, I take
> the bus.

This is a **real** condition: it may well happen, and if it does, the result
follows. Italian puts both halves in the plain present, as English does. There
is nothing new to build. *se* plus a sentence you could already say, then
another sentence you could already say.

The result half can also be advice, with the *dovresti* of chapter two hundred
and sixty-six:

> *Se sei stanco, **dovresti** dormire.* — If you're tired, you should sleep.

And it can be a plain instruction, as in the last lesson: *se sei stanco,
dormi*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-HO-PERSO-01, IT-GRAMMAR-HO-UN-PROBLEMA-02, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01] -->

A real condition is a plan with a branch in it. Travel is full of them, and
the station gives you two at once:

> *Se perdo il treno, prendo l'autobus. Se perdo l'autobus, resto qui.*

Compare *quindi*. *Piove, quindi resto a casa* says it **is** raining, and here
is the consequence. *Se piove, resto a casa* says it **might**, and here is
what happens if so. Same consequence; the second one has not decided yet
whether the cause is true.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**piovere** is inherited from spoken Latin *plŏvere* (EtymDB 2.1), a
reshaping of classical *pluere*, "to rain" (Lewis & Short, under *pluo*). The Latin *pl* became Italian *pi*, as in **più** from *plūs* and
**piegare** from *plicāre*: three words, one sound change.
