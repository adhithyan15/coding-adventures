Urdu, chapter 296: The Rescue.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نجات (najāt) — deliverance; release (from danger or trouble).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to pull out, then the Urdu for in a sorry state.

You'll want to know: نجات.
نجات — najāt — "deliverance; release (from danger or trouble)".
صبح تک سب کو پانی سے نجات ملی۔ subah tak sab ko pānī se najāt milī. By morning everyone had been delivered from the water.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
نجات (najāt) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic najāt, "deliverance, escape". The one set free takes کو and the danger takes سے: پانی سے نجات ملی. It is feminine.

Lesson 3 of 5.
چھڑانا (chhuṛānā) — to set free; to pull free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for deliverance, then the Urdu for a helicopter.

You'll want to know: چھڑانا.
چھڑانا — chhuṛānā — "to set free; to pull free".
اس نے رسی سے اپنا ہاتھ چھڑایا۔ us ne rassī se apnā hāth chhuṛāyā. He freed his hand from the rope.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بکری چھڑائی, us ne bakrī chhuṛāī.

The word, taken apart.
چھڑانا (chhuṛānā) is usually taken as the causing partner of چھوٹنا, to come loose, whose Hindi cousin chūṭnā, "escapes", CDIAL lists under kṣuṭyatē, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 3707): to make a thing come loose. What it is freed from takes سے: رسی سے ہاتھ چھڑایا.

وہ پانی کی طرف لپکا۔ voh pānī kī taraf lapkā. He darted towards the water.
Past (she …): وہ لپکی, voh lapkī.

The word, taken apart.
لپکنا (lapaknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin lapaknā, "to leap up", under a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 10939).
