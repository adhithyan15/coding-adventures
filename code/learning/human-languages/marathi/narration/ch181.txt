Lesson 1 of 5.
सून (sūn) — a daughter-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a journalist, then the Marathi for an architect.

You'll want to know: सून.
सून — sūn — "a daughter-in-law".

The word, taken apart.
सून (sūn) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from snuṣā, "son's wife" (CDIAL 13801). The same ancient word as Latin nurus.

Lesson 3 of 5.
नवरी (navrī) — a bride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a daughter-in-law, then the Marathi for a bridegroom.

You'll want to know: नवरी.
नवरी — navrī — "a bride".

The word, taken apart.
नवरी (navrī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from navavarikā, "newly wed (woman)" (CDIAL 7013). It is nava, "new", and vara, "bridegroom". CDIAL glosses the Marathi word "bride".
