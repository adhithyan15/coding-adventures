Telugu, chapter 76: Very Good, Very Bad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
చాలా మంచి (cālā manci) — very good.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did saṁvatsaraṁ mean?

You'll want to know: చాలా మంచి.
చాలా మంచి (cālā manci) — "very good". The new word in it is చాలా, "very, a lot".
Until now every adjective you had was flat: పెద్ద was big and there was no bigger. చాలా is the dial.
It stands in front of what it strengthens: చాలా పెద్ద "very big", చాలా కొత్త "very new". It also works on its own for "a lot", where కొంచెం was "a little".

What you've built.
A degree, on any adjective you have.

Lesson 2 of 5.
చెడ్డ (ceḍḍa) — bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did cālā mean?

You'll want to know: చెడ్డ.
చెడ్డ (ceḍḍa) — "bad".
మంచి, "good", has stood without an opposite ever since the adjectives chapter. చెడ్డ (ceḍḍa) is the opposite.
It sits in front of its noun and does not change, exactly as మంచి does. Do not reach for చేదు from the last chapter: that is bitter, a taste, and it does not judge anything.

What you've built.
Both ends of the judgement: మంచి and చెడ్డ (ceḍḍa).

Lesson 3 of 5.
మంచిది (mancidi) — it is a good one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ceḍḍa mean?

You'll want to know: మంచిది.
మంచిది (mancidi) — "it is a good one".
You could already say మంచి పుస్తకం, "a good book". You could not yet say that the book is good, because a Telugu adjective cannot end a sentence by itself.
Add -ది and it becomes a thing: మంచి + ది = "a good one". పుస్తకం మంచిది (mancidi) — "the book is a good one", which is how Telugu says "the book is good".

Grammar Lens: -ది turns a describing word into a naming word.
Telugu will not let a bare adjective be the predicate. -ది makes it one — a pronoun-like ending meaning "the one that is". మంచిది (mancidi) "a good one", చెడ్డది "a bad one", కొత్తది "a new one". For a person the ending is -వాడు or -ఆమె instead. Learn -ది now and every adjective you already have can finish a sentence.

What you've built.
An adjective that can be the whole comment.

Lesson 4 of 5.
సులభం (sulabhaṁ) — easy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mancidi mean?

You'll want to know: సులభం.
సులభం (sulabhaṁ) — "easy".
Sanskrit సు- ("well") plus లభ ("got"): what is easily got. The same సు- is the శు- of శుభోదయం, softened.
తెలుగు సులభం (sulabhaṁ) — "Telugu is easy." A -ం noun like this needs no -ది; it is already a naming word.

What you've built.
A verdict you can pass on a task.

Lesson 5 of 5.
కష్టం (kaṣṭaṁ) — difficult; trouble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did sulabhaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: కష్టం.
కష్టం (kaṣṭaṁ) — "difficult", and also "trouble, hardship".
The pair to సులభం (sulabhaṁ), and a Sanskrit tatsama shared right across India.
It carries more weight than English "difficult": కష్టం (kaṣṭaṁ) said alone about someone's situation means real hardship, not an awkward crossword.

What you've built.
Both ends of a task: సులభం (sulabhaṁ) and కష్టం (kaṣṭaṁ).
