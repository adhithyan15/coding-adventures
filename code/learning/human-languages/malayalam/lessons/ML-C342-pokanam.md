# ഞാൻ പോകണം (ñān pōkaṇaṁ) — I must go

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C58-WANT-01, ML-LEX-C58-ANSWER-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I want tea". (*enikkŭ cāya vēṇaṁ*.) Then "no need, I don't want it". (*vēṇṭa*.)

## Grammar Lens: -അണം on the verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C342-ANAM-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C58-WANT-01, ML-CONCEPT-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01, ML-CONCEPT-C32-POKUKA-01] -->

Take a verb's dictionary form, drop **-ഉക**, and add **-അണം**:

> പോകുക → **പോകണം**, *pōkaṇaṁ* · വരുക → **വരണം**, *varaṇaṁ* · പഠിക്കുക → **പഠിക്കണം**, *paṭhikkaṇaṁ*

Grammars explain **-അണം** as **വേണം** itself, worn down and joined to the verb. Who stands in front
decides what it means:

> **ഞാൻ നാളെ പോകണം.**
>
> *ñān nāḷe pōkaṇaṁ.* — "I must go tomorrow."

> **എനിക്ക് നാളെ പോകണം.**
>
> *enikkŭ nāḷe pōkaṇaṁ.* — "I want to go tomorrow."

With **ഞാൻ** it is a duty; with the dative **എനിക്ക്**, as with **വേണം** alone, it is a wish
or a need.

## Grammar Lens: ought to, need not, must not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C342-ENDATANU-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C58-ANSWER-04, ML-LEX-C127-CARE-02, ML-LEX-C171-ACT171-02] -->

**-ഏണ്ടതാണ്** says what is right to do: "ought to".

> **നീ നേരത്തെ ഉറങ്ങേണ്ടതാണ്.**
>
> *nī nēratte uṟaṅṅēṇṭatāṇŭ.* — "You ought to sleep early."

**-ഏണ്ട**, the **വേണ്ട** you know, says "need not": **നീ വരേണ്ട** (*nī varēṇṭa*), "you need not come".
**-അരുത്** forbids: **നീ വൈകരുത്** (*nī vaikarutŭ*), "you must not be late".
