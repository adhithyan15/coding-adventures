Lesson 1 of 5.
मञ्जूषा (mañjūṣā) — a trunk, a chest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for spotless, then the Sanskrit for bright.

You'll want to know: मञ्जूषा.
मञ्जूषा — mañjūṣā — "a trunk, a chest".

The word, taken apart.
mañjūṣā may be borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 9721 says "possibly", and DEDR 4640 compares Tamil mañcikai, "chest, box", with a question mark. Pali has it as mañjūsā, "casket".

Lesson 2 of 5.
शिक्यम् (śikyam) — a hanging basket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for bright, then the Sanskrit for a trunk.

You'll want to know: शिक्यम्.
शिक्यम् — śikyam — "a hanging basket".

The word, taken apart.
śikya is a rope loop hung from a pole or yoke to carry a load (MW), and so a hanging basket. Neither MW nor CDIAL 12427 gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain; CDIAL compares śic, "sling, net". It lives on as Hindi chīkā, "hanging net".

Lesson 3 of 5.
पिटकम् (piṭakam) — a basket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a trunk, then the Sanskrit for a hanging basket.

You'll want to know: पिटकम्.
पिटकम् — piṭakam — "a basket".

The word, taken apart.
piṭaka is probably borrowed from a non-Indo-Aryan language: CDIAL 8164 calls it "non-Aryan", while MW derives it from a root √piṭ, "to gather". The Buddhist canon is the Tipiṭaka, "three baskets".

Lesson 4 of 5.
कटः (kaṭaḥ) — a mat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a hanging basket, then the Sanskrit for a basket.

You'll want to know: कटः.
कटः — kaṭaḥ — "a mat".

The word, taken apart.
kaṭa is a twist of straw and so a straw mat. MW thinks it may stand for karta from √kṛt, "to spin, twist", and CDIAL 2629 supports a derivation of that kind. In Hindi the word became kaṛā, "ring, bracelet".

Lesson 5 of 5.
आस्तरणम् (āstaraṇam) — a carpet, a spread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a basket, then the Sanskrit for a mat.

You'll want to know: आस्तरणम्.
आस्तरणम् — āstaraṇam — "a carpet, a spread".

The word, taken apart.
āstaraṇa is built on ā- and the root √stṛ, "to spread, strew" (MW): a carpet is what is spread out. In the Atharvaveda it is a layer of sacred grass (CDIAL 1506), and Marathi keeps it as ãthrūṇ, "bedspread".
