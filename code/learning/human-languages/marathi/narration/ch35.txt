Marathi, chapter 35: A Sentence Inside a Sentence.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
की (kī) — that.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Retrieve the two items before this one by name: कारण, which puts a reason under a claim, and the comma that carries a written list. Then say मला वाटतं — it seems to me. You have had the verb for a long time and have never once been able to say WHAT seems.

Grammar Lens: की opens the room where the thought goes.
मला वाटतं की चहा आहे. — malā vāṭtaṁ kī chahā āhe. — I think there is tea.
Word for word that is to-me it-seems that tea is. Everything after की (kī) is an ordinary complete Marathi sentence — चहा आहे — doing the job of an object.
That is the trick worth seeing: you do not need a new grammar to report a thought. You need one word and a sentence you could already say. Every sentence in this book just became something you can think, know or say about.
मला माहीत आहे की (kī) चहा आहे. — I know that there is tea.
Negate the outer half and the inner clause survives untouched: मला माहीत नाही की (kī) चहा आहे — I don't know whether there is tea. Only the main verb was denied.
कारण and की (kī) are cousins in shape and different in job: कारण puts a clause underneath as a REASON, की (kī) puts one underneath as the CONTENT. Both keep the main clause first, which is Marathi's steady habit.
की (kī) comes from the same Sanskrit question stem क- that gave you का and काय. Persian does exactly the same thing with its own ke, and for the same reason: a question word is already a placeholder, so it is the natural thing to put where a whole sentence is about to arrive.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: chahā āhe on its own]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: malā vāṭtaṁ kī chahā āhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what changed inside the second half when you put it after की (kī) — nothing]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Say that you think there is tea, then that you know there is tea.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What goes after की (kī)? (A whole ordinary sentence, unchanged.) How does की (kī) differ from कारण? (कारण gives the reason; की (kī) gives the content.)

Lesson 2 of 3.
-ण्यासाठी (-ṇyāsāṭhī) — in order to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every Marathi verb in this book ends in -णे: शिकणे, वाचणे, बोलणे. That ending is what makes the verb usable as a thing. Now it becomes usable as a purpose.

Grammar Lens: swap -णे for -ण्यासाठी.
मी मराठी शिकण्यासाठी वाचतो. — mī marāṭhī shikṇyāsāṭhī vācto. — I read in order to learn Marathi.
One swap does it — take -णे off, put -ण्यासाठी (-ṇyāsāṭhī) on:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
dictionary form: शिकणे. purpose form: शिकण्यासाठी.
dictionary form: वाचणे. purpose form: वाचण्यासाठी.
The purpose clause then goes BEFORE the main verb, which is where Marathi keeps everything that modifies it.
Two honest notes. First, साठी is a postposition meaning for the sake of — it can sit after a plain noun too. Second, the -ण्या- in front of it is not decoration: Marathi bends a word into an oblique shape before any postposition, and this is one instance of a rule that runs through the whole language. That general rule is still owed to you. Learn this one swap now; the rule it belongs to comes later.
Where कारण answers why by giving a cause, -ण्यासाठी (-ṇyāsāṭhī) answers it by giving an aim. Marathi keeps those apart and you now have both.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: shikṇe becomes shikṇyāsāṭhī, then vācṇe becomes vācṇyāsāṭhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mī marāṭhī shikṇyāsāṭhī vācto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the difference between a cause and an aim — kāraṇ against -ṇyāsāṭhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Turn shikṇe into a purpose and say that you read in order to learn Marathi.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What do you take off the verb, and what do you put on? (Take off -णे, put on -ण्यासाठी (-ṇyāsāṭhī).) Where does the purpose clause stand? (Before the main verb.)
Sources: Wiktionary: साठी.


Lesson 3 of 3.
Three clauses underneath, three jobs — the two ways a whole clause can go inside another one.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Three things can now go underneath a Marathi main clause: a reason, a content, and an aim. Name the word or ending that carries each.
Then reach further back and say the frame that shares a pair out — एक in front of one, दुसरा in front of the other — and notice that it puts its two halves side by side as equals, which is exactly what these three do not do.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the की (kī) sentence]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 3 seconds]
Which of the three goes BEFORE the main verb rather than after it? (The -ण्यासाठी (-ṇyāsāṭhī) purpose clause.) Then stop.
