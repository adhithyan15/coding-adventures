Sanskrit, chapter 473: Arms and Legs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अंगम् (aṅgam) — a limb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for spits, then the Sanskrit for a taste.

You'll want to know: अंगम्.
अंगम् — aṅgam — "a limb".
व्यायामेन अंगानि दृढानि भवन्ति। vyāyāmena aṅgāni dṛḍhāni bhavanti. Exercise makes the limbs strong.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
अंगम् (aṅgam) is traced to a root √am in the Uṇādi list that MW follows; MW gives "a limb of the body, a member" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
आकुञ्चयति (ākuñcayati) — bends a limb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the heel, then the Sanskrit for a fist.

You'll want to know: आकुञ्चयति.
आकुञ्चयति — ākuñcayati — "bends a limb".
वृद्धः शनैः जानु आकुञ्चयति। vṛddhaḥ śanaiḥ jānu ākuñcayati. The old man slowly bends his knee.
Past (he …): सः जानु आकुञ्चितवान्, saḥ jānu ākuñcitavān.

The word, taken apart.
आकुञ्चयति (ākuñcayati) is built from ā and the root √kuñc, "to bend"; MW gives the causative "to bend (as a limb)" and the ppp ākuñcita "bent (as the arm or the knee)" (MW).
