Lesson 1 of 5.
gotear — to drip, to leak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to beat, then the Spanish for the ingredient.

You'll want to know: gotear.
gotear — "to drip, to leak".
El grifo goteó toda la noche. The tap dripped all night.

The word, taken apart.
gotear is built on gota 'a drop' with the ending -ear. Gota is inherited from Latin gutta 'a drop' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la gotera — the leak (in a roof).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the ingredient, then the Spanish for to drip.

You'll want to know: la gotera.
la gotera — "the leak (in a roof)".
Con la lluvia salió una gotera en el techo. With the rain a leak appeared in the ceiling.
Used for water coming through a roof or ceiling; a leaking pipe is una fuga.

The word, taken apart.
gotera is built on gota 'a drop' (EtymDB), which comes from Latin gutta 'a drop', with the ending -era.

Lesson 3 of 5.
averiado — broken down, out of order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to drip, then the Spanish for the leak.

You'll want to know: averiado.
averiado — "broken down, out of order".
El ascensor está averiado desde el lunes. The lift has been out of order since Monday.
For machines, with estar. Una avería is a breakdown.

The word, taken apart.
averiado is built on avería 'a breakdown, damage'. Avería is usually traced to Arabic ʿawāriyya 'damaged goods', through Catalan or Italian avaria, a word of sailors and merchants.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el fontanero — the plumber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the leak, then the Spanish for broken down.

You'll want to know: el fontanero.
el fontanero — "the plumber".
El fontanero arregló la tubería en una hora. The plumber fixed the pipe in an hour.
This is the word in Spain; in Latin America people say el plomero.

The word, taken apart.
fontanero is built on old fontana 'a fountain', from Late Latin fontāna (EtymDB), from fōns 'a spring', with the ending -ero.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el electricista — the electrician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for broken down, then the Spanish for the plumber.

You'll want to know: el electricista.
el electricista — "the electrician".
Llamamos a un electricista porque no había luz. We called an electrician because there was no power.
The same form for a woman: la electricista.

The word, taken apart.
electricista is built on eléctrico with the ending -ista. Eléctrico goes back to Greek ḗlektron 'amber' (Lewis & Short, under electrum), because rubbed amber pulls in light things.
