## You'll want to know: स्टेशन
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C69-GOING-03]; assesses=[] -->

**स्टेशन** (*sṭeshan*) — "a station".

English *station*, and it shows its seams. Hindi does not open a word with two
consonants stuck together, so the writing does it the Devanagari way instead:
स with the vowel-killer ् underneath, then ट. That stack is the same virama you
met inside नमस्ते.

The retroflex ट rather than dental त is deliberate. When Hindi borrows an
English *t*, it hears the English tongue as curled back, so *station* becomes
स्टेशन and not स्तेशन. Every English *t* you meet in a Hindi loan will do this.

The word is painted on every platform board in the country, in Devanagari and in
Latin letters side by side.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Three: गाड़ी, रेल, स्टेशन. A vehicle, a network, and the place you wait.
