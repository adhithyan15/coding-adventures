## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,200 words in chapters 300–539; Persian attains B1

- **240 new chapters (300–539) teach 1,200 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 358 of them are verbs, most of them compound verbs built on *kardan*, *shodan*, *dâdan* or *zadan*. Each lesson gives:
  - the word in Persian script with its romanization;
  - a short example in Persian, its romanization word for word, and its translation;
  - a note with the present stem, an Arabic broken plural, or the parts of a compound verb;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 300–359, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (300 words): time and sequence, news and reports, crime, accidents and emergencies, life events, Nowruz and other celebrations, stories and history, and cooking step by step.
  - 360–419, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (300 words): opinion and argument, connectors and what each takes (*be shart-e inke* and *magar inke* with the subjunctive, *bâ vojud-e inke* with a clause, *be dalil-e* with a noun), cause and consequence, work, society, conditions and likelihood, study and exams, rules and the law, politics, money, and the internet.
  - 420–479, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (300 words): the road and the car, trains, planes, taxis and boats, lodging and complaints, finding the way, the town and its services, shops and the bazaar, nature, and weather.
  - 480–539, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (300 words): feelings and character, health and the body, family and relationships, hobbies, sport, music, poetry and the arts, reading and the screen, and life at home.
- **Persian now teaches 2,585 headwords at or below B1**, past the level gate's target of 2,500. With the B1 core (chapters 290–299), Persian attains B1.
- **Romanization follows the track's later lessons:** *q* for ق and *gh* for غ, as chapters 150 onward write them, and *ow* for the diphthong (*nowbat*, *dowr*).
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1, and Latin parents against Lewis & Short. Words EtymDB lacks are hedged as "usually traced to …" or marked not settled. Arabic loans are told apart from inherited Iranian words, and European and Turkic loans say where they came from. An independent review of each tranche fixed errors in 125 of the 1,200 words (10.4%), mostly etymology claims EtymDB does not support and romanizations that differed from the track's taught spelling.
- **No forward references and no hidden duplicates.** Every candidate headword was screened against every earlier lesson's text, and words that are only an inflected form of a taught word (مدتی for مدت) were replaced. Persian's forward-reference count stays at its pin of 2. Middle Persian forms are written with *gh*, not ɣ, which the book font lacks. Arabic roots use the track's three-letter slugs (*a-l-l* for *'illa*, so it does not join Dravidian *illa* "not").
