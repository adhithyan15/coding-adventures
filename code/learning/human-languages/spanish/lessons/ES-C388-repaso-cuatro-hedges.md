# Review — four verbs, four kinds of "related"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-GRITAR, ES-LEX-LANZAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "I shout." (*Grito*.) "I throw." (*Lanzo*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-EMPUJAR, ES-LEX-LANZAR, ES-LEX-BORRAR, ES-LEX-GRITAR] -->

Four ordinary *-ar* verbs. Each one sits next to an English word that looks like
it — and each one is related to that English word in a **different** way.

| Spanish | English lookalike | the truth |
|---|---|---|
| *lanzar* | launch | **the same word** |
| *gritar* | cry | **the same word**, meaning moved |
| *empujar* | push | **a cousin**, not the same word |
| *borrar* | borrow | **no relation at all** |

[PAUSE 2s] Four verbs is a small chapter. Four different answers is a whole
method.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-EMPUJAR, ES-LEX-GRITAR, ES-LEX-BORRAR] -->

*Push* misses being the same word as *empujar* by one piece. Both go back to
Latin *pulsare*, but Spanish took the form with *in-* stuck on the front and
English took the plain one. Same root, different word.

*Cry* really is *gritar*. Latin *quiritare* went into French and on into English,
and then **English alone** slid the meaning from shouting to weeping. Spanish
never did — which is why *gritar* is not the word for tears.

And *borrar* has nothing to do with *borrow*, which is Germanic. *Borrar* comes
from *borra*, coarse wool: the pad you rubbed writing out with. A rough draft is
still *un borrador* — the thing meant to be rubbed out.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the part worth keeping. Look at how the dictionaries actually
talk. The Academy writes *quizá* in front of *empujar*; Corominas writes
*probablemente*. On *lanzar* they part company outright — the Academy calls its
root Celtiberian, Corominas only says *quizá*. On *borrar* neither will choose
between two candidates.

A hedge is not a failure. It is a dictionary telling you exactly how much it
knows, and a claim with no hedge on it has not been made more certain by leaving
the hedge off.
