Persian, chapter 56: Mouse, Tiger, Elephant, Monkey, Snake.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
موش (mush) — a mouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a sheep, then the Persian for a bird.

You'll want to know: موش.
موش — mush — "a mouse".

The word, taken apart.
mush is a native Persian word, inherited rather than borrowed; it is usually traced to Middle Persian mūš, "mouse". English mouse and Latin mūs are the same Indo-European word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
فیل (fil) — an elephant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a mouse, then the Persian for a tiger.

You'll want to know: فیل.
فیل — fil — "an elephant".

The word, taken apart.
fil has made a round trip. Middle Persian had pīl, "elephant" (EtymDB), usually traced to Akkadian pīru. Arabic, with no p, took it as fīl (root f-y-l, Lane), and Persian took the Arabic form back.

Lesson 4 of 5.
میمون (meymun) — a monkey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a tiger, then the Persian for an elephant.

You'll want to know: میمون.
میمون — meymun — "a monkey".

The word, taken apart.
meymun is borrowed from Arabic maymūn, "blessed, fortunate" (root y-m-n; EtymDB). The monkey sense is usually explained as a euphemism: an animal thought ill-omened was given a lucky name.
