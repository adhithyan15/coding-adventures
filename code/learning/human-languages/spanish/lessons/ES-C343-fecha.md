## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C343-CLOCK-06]; assesses=[] -->

**la fecha** — "the date."

Latin **facta** meant *done, made* — the feminine past participle of
**facere**, *to do*.

The date meaning came off the bottom of letters. A scribe finished a document
and wrote **facta in...** — *done at* such a place on such a day. The word
sitting where the day was written slowly became the name of the day itself.
Spanish still says **fecha en Madrid** in formal documents, which is the
original line, unchanged.

English did exactly the same trick with a different Latin word: **date** is
*data*, *given*, from the *datum* that closed a Roman letter. Two languages,
two closing formulas, both promoted to mean the day.

*Facere* is one of the biggest roots in English. A **fact** is a thing done. A
**feat** is a deed. A **factory** makes things. **Perfect** is thoroughly made.
And to **affect** something is to do something to it.
