Arabic, chapter 375: Contrast and addition.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مع ذلك (maʿa dhālika) — even so; however.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for given that, then the Arabic for in order to.

You'll want to know: مع ذلك.
مع ذلك — maʿa dhālika — "even so; however".
كان متعبا، مع ذلك أكمل عمله. kāna mutʿaban, maʿa dhālika akmala ʿamalah. He was tired; even so, he finished his work.
Often with و: ومع ذلك, and yet.

The word, taken apart.
مع ذلك (maʿa dhālika) joins two native Arabic words, maʿa, "with", and dhālika, "that": with all that, even so. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
إلا أن (illā anna) — yet; but.

You'll want to know: إلا أن.
إلا أن — illā anna — "yet; but".
الفندق جميل، إلا أن الغرف صغيرة. al-funduq jamīl, illā anna al-ghuraf ṣaghīra. The hotel is beautiful, but the rooms are small.

The word, taken apart.
إلا أن (illā anna) joins the native Arabic particles illā, "except", and anna, "that": except that, and so yet, but. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
أما (ammā) — as for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for even so, then the Arabic for yet.

You'll want to know: أما.
أما — ammā — "as for".
أما أخي فيحب الرياضة. ammā akhī fa-yuḥibbu ar-riyāḍa. As for my brother, he loves sport.
The next part starts with فـ: أما (ammā) … فـ ….

The word, taken apart.
أما (ammā) is an old native Arabic particle that sets one topic apart from the others. Its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
علاوة على (ʿilāwatan ʿalā) — moreover; on top of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for as for, then the Arabic for likewise.

You'll want to know: علاوة على.
علاوة على — ʿilāwatan ʿalā — "moreover; on top of".
علاوة على الراتب، يحصل الموظف على سيارة. ʿilāwatan ʿalā ar-rātib, yaḥṣulu al-muwaẓẓaf ʿalā sayyāra. On top of the salary, the employee gets a car.
Also علاوة alone: a bonus on a salary.

The word, taken apart.
علاوة على (ʿilāwatan ʿalā) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-l-w, "to be high, to rise above" (Lane). ʿIlāwa is what is laid on top of a load: something extra, and so moreover.
