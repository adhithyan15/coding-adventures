Sanskrit, chapter 422: Walls and Gates.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्राकारः (prākāraḥ) — a town wall; a rampart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a mansion, then the Sanskrit for converses.

You'll want to know: प्राकारः.
प्राकारः — prākāraḥ — "a town wall; a rampart".
नगरं परितः उन्नतः प्राकारः अस्ति। nagaraṃ paritaḥ unnataḥ prākāraḥ asti. Around the city there is a high rampart.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्राकारः (prākāraḥ) is built from prā, a form of pra, and the root √kṝ, "to pour out, to heap"; MW gives prākāra as a wall or enclosure, a rampart (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
गोपुरम् (gopuram) — a town gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for converses, then the Sanskrit for a town wall.

You'll want to know: गोपुरम्.
गोपुरम् — gopuram — "a town gate".
यात्रिकाः गोपुरेण नगरं प्रविशन्ति। yātrikāḥ gopureṇa nagaraṃ praviśanti. The pilgrims enter the city by the town gate.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
गोपुरम् (gopuram) is built from go, "a cow", and pura, "a town"; MW gives gopura as a town gate, and in modern use it also names the tower over a temple gate (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
परिखा (parikhā) — a moat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a town wall, then the Sanskrit for a town gate.

You'll want to know: परिखा.
परिखा — parikhā — "a moat".
प्राकारात् बहिः गभीरा परिखा अस्ति। prākārāt bahiḥ gabhīrā parikhā asti. Outside the rampart there is a deep moat.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
परिखा (parikhā) is built from pari, "around", and the root √khan, "to dig", which also gives khanati, "digs"; MW gives parikhā as a moat or trench round a town or fort (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सुरंगा (suraṅgā) — a tunnel; an underground passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a town gate, then the Sanskrit for a moat.

You'll want to know: सुरंगा.
सुरंगा — suraṅgā — "a tunnel; an underground passage".
दुर्गस्य अधः सुरंगा अस्ति। durgasya adhaḥ suraṅgā asti. There is a tunnel beneath the fort.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सुरंगा (suraṅgā) is the word MW gives for a hole cut in a wall and an underground passage; MW cites the view that it is Greek syrinx, "a pipe", so its origin is uncertain; it lives on as Hindi suraṅg, a burglar's hole or a mine (MW; CDIAL 13506).

Lesson 5 of 5.
खातम् (khātam) — a ditch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a moat, then the Sanskrit for a tunnel.

You'll want to know: खातम्.
खातम् — khātam — "a ditch".
कृषकः क्षेत्रस्य समीपे खातं खनति। kṛṣakaḥ kṣetrasya samīpe khātaṃ khanati. The farmer digs a ditch near the field.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
खातम् (khātam) comes from the root √khan, "to dig": it is the participle, "dug", and MW gives khāta as dug, excavated and, as a noun, a ditch (MW).
