Sanskrit, chapter 51: More of the Family.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पतिः (patiḥ) — a husband.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did ślokaḥ mean?

You'll want to know: पतिः.
पतिः (patiḥ) — "a husband".
पतिः (patiḥ) is a husband, and more widely the master of a household.
It is one of the oldest words the family holds: Latin potis, "able, in command", behind English possible and power, and Greek posis, a husband. The -pati that ends a great many Indian names and titles is this word doing the same work.
Beside kuṭumbam, the household you can already name, पतिः (patiḥ) is the one who answers for it. Ends in -iḥ.

What you've built.
One end of a household, named.

Lesson 2 of 5.
पत्नी (patnī) — a wife.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did patiḥ mean?

You'll want to know: पत्नी.
पत्नी (patnī) — "a wife".
पत्नी (patnī) is a wife, the mistress of the household.
It is पतिः (patiḥ) with a feminine ending laid on it, and the two have travelled together since before Sanskrit was Sanskrit: Greek potnia, "lady", is the very same formation made in the very same way.
So the second word costs almost nothing once the first is yours. Feminine, ending in -ī.

What you've built.
The pair that a household is built on.

Lesson 3 of 5.
पितामहः (pitāmahaḥ) — a grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did patnī mean?

You'll want to know: पितामहः.
पितामहः (pitāmahaḥ) — "a grandfather".
पितामहः (pitāmahaḥ) is a grandfather — and, precisely, the father's father.
Both halves are already yours. पिता is the father, and महत् is big; the ending shifts a little in the joining, but the sense does not. A grandfather is the great father.
Sanskrit keeps the two sides of a family apart, so this word does not stretch to the mother's side. Ends in -अः.

What you've built.
A new word made of two old ones.

Lesson 4 of 5.
मातामही (mātāmahī) — a grandmother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did pitāmahaḥ mean?

You'll want to know: मातामही.
मातामही (mātāmahī) — "a grandmother".
मातामही (mātāmahī) is a grandmother — and, precisely, the mother's mother.
It is built exactly as पितामहः (pitāmahaḥ) is, with माता standing in front instead of पिता. Having watched the joining once, you can read this one before it is explained.
The pair also shows the line Sanskrit draws down the middle of a family: one word for each side, and no word covering both. Feminine, ending in -ī.

What you've built.
The same joining, worked a second time.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मातुलः (mātulaḥ) — a mother's brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the last word of the chapter: what did pitāmahaḥ mean, and what did mātāmahī mean?

You'll want to know: मातुलः.
मातुलः (mātulaḥ) — "a mother's brother".
मातुलः (mātulaḥ) is a mother's brother.
Sanskrit has no single word covering both uncles, and this is the one for the mother's side, with माता standing plainly inside it. The line drawn by पितामहः (pitāmahaḥ) and मातामही (mātāmahī) is drawn here too.
Latin drew it in the same place: avunculus was the mother's brother, and it is the word English wore down into uncle. Two languages, one distinction, one border apart. Ends in -अः.

What you've built.
Five more of a household: a husband, a wife, a grandfather, a grandmother, a mother's brother.
