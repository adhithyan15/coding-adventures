## Guided Practice: the distant band — the whole book, in the plural

[PAUSE 4s] Sort every noun this book owns into its group, then pluralise it the
way its group demands. Cold, from memory.

- [YOU SAY: the living ones — *bhāirā*, *bônerā*, *bôndhurā*]
- [YOU SAY: the things — *jāmāgulo*, *kāpôṛgulo*, *chokhgulo*, *nākgulo*]
- [YOU SAY: the two that catch people out, and why — *nāk* and *hridôy* are **জিনিস**]
- [YOU SAY: the food, definite and plural — *bhātgulo*, *chāgulo*]
- [YOU SAY: with a colour and a pointer — *ei lāl jāmāgulo*, *oi kālo kāpôṛgulo*]
- [YOU SAY: one, another, then all of them — *ekṭā jāmā*, *ārekṭā jāmā*, *jāmāgulo*]
- [YOU SAY: the family, singular and plural — *pôribārṭā*, then *bhāirā*]
- [YOU SAY: asking about a pile of them — *eṭā ki?*]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C22-ANIMACY-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C22-PLURAL-RA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C22-PLURAL-GULO-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C22-ADJ-ORDER-01, BN-SCRIPT-TTA-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Which ending does a living noun take, and which does a thing take?
(**রা** and **গুলো**.) Which of the two also means *the*? (**গুলো**.) Where does
an adjective stand, and does it change? (**In front**, and **no**.) And which
letter, three lessons before this chapter, made all of it writable? (**ট**.)
