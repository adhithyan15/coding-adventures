# 人 — two strokes, and a person walking

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have heard Mandarin pitch. Now you meet the writing, and this
book will hand it to you **one piece at a time** — never a character you have
not been shown the parts of first.

This is the whole first piece:

> 人

Two strokes. That is the entire character.

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-SCRIPT-REN-01]; assesses=[] -->

**人** *rén* means **person**.

Look at it before you read another word about it. A stroke leaning down-left, a
stroke leaning down-right, meeting at the top. **It is a figure standing on two
legs, seen from the side.** You are not being asked to take that on faith — the
oldest surviving forms of this character, scratched into ox bone three thousand
years ago, are recognisably a standing person, and the two legs are the part
that lasted.

Write it in this order, and the order is not a suggestion:

1. the **left** stroke, from the top down to the lower left — a falling sweep
2. the **right** stroke, starting near the top of the first and pressing down to
   the lower right

**Left before right.** Almost every character you meet will obey it, and it is
worth meeting here — on a character with two strokes — rather than later on one
with fifteen.

The pitch is **second tone**: *rén* rises, like the end of an English question.
