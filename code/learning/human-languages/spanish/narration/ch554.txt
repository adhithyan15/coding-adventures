Spanish, chapter 554: Hacerse mayor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
graduarse — to graduate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to dismiss, then the Spanish for to resign.

You'll want to know: graduarse.
graduarse — "to graduate".
Se graduó en Medicina en 2020. She graduated in Medicine in 2020.
It takes an accent: me gradúo.

The word, taken apart.
graduarse is usually traced to Medieval Latin graduāre 'to give a degree', built on Latin gradus 'a step' (Lewis & Short), the word behind grado. To graduate is to take the next step.

Lesson 3 of 5.
licenciarse — to get a degree, to graduate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to graduate, then the Spanish for the graduation.

You'll want to know: licenciarse.
licenciarse — "to get a degree, to graduate".
Me licencié en Historia. I got my degree in History.
Mostly in Spain: la licenciatura is the degree.

The word, taken apart.
licenciarse is built on licencia, from Latin licentia 'freedom, leave to act' (Lewis & Short), from licet 'it is allowed'. A degree was the licence to teach.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la mayoría de edad — the age of majority, adulthood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the graduation, then the Spanish for to get a degree.

You'll want to know: la mayoría de edad.
la mayoría de edad — "the age of majority, adulthood".
Al cumplir la mayoría de edad, sacó el carné. When she came of age, she got her licence.
In Spain it is eighteen: ser mayor de edad — to be an adult.

The word, taken apart.
la mayoría de edad is built on mayoría, from mayor, which comes from Latin maior 'greater', and edad, from Latin aetās, aetātem 'age, lifetime' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
envejecer — to grow old, to age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get a degree, then the Spanish for the age of majority.

You'll want to know: envejecer.
envejecer — "to grow old, to age".
Mi perro ha envejecido mucho. My dog has aged a lot.
It takes a z: envejezco.

The word, taken apart.
envejecer is built on viejo 'old' with en- and -ecer. Viejo is inherited from Latin vetulus 'little old', from vetus 'old' (Lewis & Short).
