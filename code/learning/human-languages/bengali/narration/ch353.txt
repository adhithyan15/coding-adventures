Bengali, chapter 353: Customs and Change.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
রেওয়াজ (reoyāj) — a custom; a common practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for deprived, then the Bengali for to include.

You'll want to know: রেওয়াজ.
রেওয়াজ — reoyāj — "a custom; a common practice".
বিয়েতে এই রেওয়াজ পুরনো। biyete ei reoyāj purôno. This wedding custom is old.

The word, taken apart.
রেওয়াজ (reoyāj) is Persian rivāj, currency and so a custom in general use, usually traced to Arabic: what people commonly do.

সংস্কার — sôngskār — "a reform".
আইনের সংস্কার দরকার। āiner sôngskār dôrkār. The law needs reform.

The word, taken apart.
সংস্কার (sôngskār) is Sanskrit saṃskāra, putting together well, perfecting (Monier-Williams). The sense 'a reform', a remaking of laws or customs, is a modern one.

Lesson 5 of 6.
মানা (mānā) — to obey; to keep (a custom, a rule).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a reform, then the Bengali for in common use.

You'll want to know: মানা.
মানা — mānā — "to obey; to keep (a custom, a rule)".
আমরা আমাদের পুরনো রীতি মানি। āmrā āmāder purôno rīti māni. We keep our old customs.
Past (he/she …): তিনি নিয়ম মানলেন, tini niyôm mānlen.

The word, taken apart.
মানা (mānā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit mányatē, thinks or considers; CDIAL 9857 lists Bengali mānā 'to consider, obey'. To hold a rule worth heeding is to obey it.
