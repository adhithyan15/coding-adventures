Sanskrit, chapter 265: A gentle order at a meal.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
A meal in a familiar place — retrieve familiar food, drink, meal, and restaurant words.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “armour,” then contrast “my” with “our,” and finish with “I will do.” (Kavacam; mama, asmākam; kariṣyāmi.)

Grammar Lens: bring the meal words back.
Nothing here is new. भोजनालयः (bhojanālayaḥ) is a restaurant; भोजनम् (bhojanam) is a meal or food. You already know खादति (khādati), “eats,” along with अन्नम् (annam), food, पानीयम् (pānīyam), water, and क्षीरम् (kṣīram), milk.

Lesson 2 of 6.
ददातु (dadātu) — please give.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “flag,” then “me” as an object, then “I will read.” (Dhvajaḥ; mām; paṭhiṣyāmi.)

Grammar Lens: one useful request form.
You know ददाति (dadāti), “he or she gives.” For a gentle request, use the complete form ददातु (dadātu): “let him or her give,” or in this exchange, “please give.”
पानीयम् ददातु। Pānīyaṃ dadātu. Please give water.
Here ददातु (dadātu) is the third-person singular imperative. Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar §§617 and 668–669, records the present imperative and this reduplicating root. Keep the whole form together. This is not a complete imperative paradigm: other persons, numbers, and roots wait for later.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — contrast: gives; please give — dadāti; dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Please give water” — pānīyaṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the request form once more — dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Change ददाति, “gives,” into the one taught request form. (ददातु (dadātu).)


Lesson 3 of 6.
Give me one familiar item — ask for a familiar food or drink with mahyam and dadātu.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “drum,” then “you” as one object, then make one plan for tomorrow. (Paṭahaḥ; tvām; śvaḥ plus a taught first-person future form.)

Grammar Lens: receiver, item, request.
Build the request in three small pieces:
मह्यम् + familiar item + ददातु (dadātu) to me + item + please give.
So मह्यम् पानीयं ददातु (mahyaṃ pānīyaṃ dadātu) asks, “Please give me water.” The item changes; the frame stays still.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: “Give me food” — mahyaṃ annaṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Give me water” — mahyaṃ pānīyaṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Give me milk” — mahyaṃ kṣīraṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the three pieces? (Receiver मह्यम्, a familiar item, then ददातु (dadātu).)


Lesson 4 of 6.
Name the meal or make the order — distinguish naming food from politely asking for it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “treasury,” then explain the choice between माम् and त्वाम्: is the one person used as object the speaker or the addressee?

Grammar Lens: name or request.
भोजनम् names a meal. It does not itself order anything. Put the courtesy word before the frame when you make the request:
कृपया मह्यम् अन्नं ददातु। Kṛpayā mahyam annaṃ dadātu. Please give me food.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Please give me water” — kṛpayā mahyaṃ pānīyaṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Please give me milk” — kṛpayā mahyaṃ kṣīraṃ dadātu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 5 of 6.
Choose and order — choose a familiar item and request it politely.

Warm-up.

[your turn — say: Politely ask for milk.]

What you've built.
Possible answers are कृपया मह्यं पानीयं ददातु (dadātu), कृपया मह्यं क्षीरं ददातु (dadātu), and कृपया मह्यम् अन्नं ददातु (dadātu). The place is भोजनालयः; the meal is भोजनम्; “eats food” is अन्नं खादति.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Choose a different item from your last answer and order it again.


Lesson 6 of 6.
Make the order yourself — independently make a polite request for familiar food or drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “rupee,” then say the independent object form for “you two.” (Rūpyakam; yuvām.)

Guided Practice — final retrieval.
No Sanskrit model is shown before you answer. Imagine you are in a भोजनालयः. Choose अन्नम्, पानीयम्, or क्षीरम्, and make the complete polite request from memory.
[pause 5 seconds]
Now choose a second item and make a different request.
[pause 5 seconds]
Finally, name the meal and say that someone eats food.

What you've built.
Your request should contain कृपया, मह्यम्, the chosen item, and ददातु (dadātu). For example, after answering: कृपया मह्यं पानीयं ददातु (dadātu) is the track's water request. It preserves the chapter-24 distinction between जलम्, water as a substance, and पानीयम्, a drink offered to someone. भोजनम्; अन्नं खादति completes the last prompt.

Grammar Lens: the boundary.
This chapter gives you one useful request with familiar items. It is not a complete imperative paradigm, a complete restaurant vocabulary, or evidence that every possible meal exchange is covered.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Without looking back, make one final polite order for a familiar food or drink.
