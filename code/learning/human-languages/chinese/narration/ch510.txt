Mandarin Chinese, chapter 510: Grandparents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wàipó — grandma on the mother's side.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for one's mother tongue, then the Mandarin for a stepmother.

You'll want to know: wàipó.
wàipó — "grandma on the mother's side".
wǒ zài wàipó jiā zhù le yí ge yuè — I stayed at my grandma's for a month.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wàipó is built from 外 'outside' and pó 'an old woman', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwajH and ba (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a name for an old mother'.

sūnzi — "grandson".

The word, taken apart.
sūnzi is built from sūn 'a grandchild' and 子 'a son', both inherited from Middle Chinese, swon and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, 'the son of a son'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
疼爱 (téng'ài) — to dote on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a grandson, then the Mandarin for a granddaughter.

You'll want to know: 疼爱.
疼爱 — téng'ài — "to dote on".
wàipó hěn téng'ài wǒ — Grandma really dotes on me.

The word, taken apart.
疼爱 (téng'ài) is built from 疼 'to ache' and ài 'to love', both inherited from Middle Chinese, downg and 'ojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'pain' and the second 'to pity'; the sense 'to love dearly' is not given for the first there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
hǒng — to soothe a child; to coax.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a granddaughter, then the Mandarin for to dote on.

You'll want to know: hǒng.
hǒng — "to soothe a child; to coax".
wàipó zuì huì hǒng háizi — Grandma is the best at soothing children.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǒng is written with a character the Guangyun lists only as Middle Chinese huwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the sound of singing', which would regularly give hòng. The sense 'to soothe a child' is not given there and the third tone does not fit, so how the word came is not settled.
