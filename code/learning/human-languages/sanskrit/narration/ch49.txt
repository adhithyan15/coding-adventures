Sanskrit, chapter 49: Five Small Answers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तर्हि (tarhi) — then, in that case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did haṁsaḥ mean?

You'll want to know: तर्हि.
तर्हि (tarhi) — "then, in that case".
तर्हि (tarhi) — "then, in that case".
Said on its own it answers a condition. Somebody tells you how matters stand; तर्हि (tarhi) is your "then, all right".
It is built on the same t- that points for you in तत् and in तत्र. Those two point at a thing and at a place; तर्हि (tarhi) points at a moment — at that time, and so, therefore.

What you've built.
A one-word answer to how things stand.

Lesson 2 of 5.
यथा (yathā) — as, in the way that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did tarhi mean?

You'll want to know: यथा.
यथा (yathā) — "as, in the way that".
यथा (yathā) — "as, in the way that".
You already say तथा, "so, in that way". यथा is its partner, and the two travel together: yathā... tathā, "as... so". Holding one of the pair, you now hold both.
Said back to somebody on its own, it works as "just so, as you say" — an agreement rather than a description.

What you've built.
The other half of a pair you already had half of.

Lesson 3 of 5.
केवलम् (kevalam) — only, merely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did yathā mean?

You'll want to know: केवलम्.
केवलम् (kevalam) — "only, merely".
केवलम् (kevalam) — "only, merely, nothing but".
Put it in front of what you are naming and it narrows the claim: केवलम् (kevalam) दुग्धम्, "only milk". It is the ordinary Sanskrit "only", not a poetic one.
It also stands alone. Asked how much of a thing there is, केवलम् (kevalam) on its own is a complete and slightly rueful answer.

What you've built.
A word that narrows whatever it stands in front of.

Lesson 4 of 5.
मुहुः (muhuḥ) — again and again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: what did kevalam mean?

You'll want to know: मुहुः.
मुहुः (muhuḥ) — "again and again".
मुहुः (muhuḥ) — "again and again, over and over".
You have पुनः for "again", meaning one more time. मुहुः is the stronger one: not once more but repeatedly, and doubled as muhur muhuḥ it is stronger still.
It belongs with muhūrta, a short measured stretch of time. The picture underneath is a thing happening moment after moment after moment.

What you've built.
A stronger "again" than the one you had.

Lesson 5 of 5.
अहो (aho) — oh!

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the last word of the chapter: what did kevalam mean, and what did muhuḥ mean?

You'll want to know: अहो.
अहो (aho) — "oh!".
अहो (aho) — "oh!".
It is the sound of being struck by something: delight, surprise or dismay, with the tone deciding which. अहो (aho) on its own is a whole reply, and one of the few you can give without knowing what comes next.
It is two small cries stuck together rather than a word with parts, which is why there is no history to trace under it. Sanskrit wrote its exclamations down, and this one has been in the language for as long as anything in it.

What you've built.
Five more replies: then, as, only, again and again, and oh.
