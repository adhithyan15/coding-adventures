### Fourteen points from twenty-one items, and the ratio is one rule

The oblique is not vocabulary and it is not optional. Every postposition this
book has ever shown — **کا** since chapter three, **کے لیے** since chapter
twenty-one — has been silently demanding the shape-change, and no lesson said
so. Once it is stated, **کو**, **میں** and **پر** cost one lesson each, the
demonstratives get their oblique shapes, and the third-person possessive falls
out of the two together.

The plural does the same trick a second time: the plural of a masculine noun in
*-ā* is spelled **exactly** like its oblique singular, and the chapter teaches
the collision rather than hiding it — the postposition is what settles the
reading, and **-وں** is the one ending in Urdu that is never ambiguous.

### The script debt did not move, and that is why this tranche is the oblique

Violations held at 4, glyphs taught at 15 of 35 shown, never-taught at 20 and
headwordsWithoutRomanization at 0. **All twenty-one new headwords are spellable
with the fifteen letters the ladder has actually taught**, and every one was
tested against that set — including the worked examples — before it was written.

That constraint chose the tranche. The time column (آج, دن, رات, وقت, کب) is the
other big cluster in this file and needs five letters the ladder has not reached:
a vocabulary tranche cannot buy them, and a script tranche has to. Where a needed
word could not be spelled it is in romanization with the blocking letter named —
*mujhe* and *pasand* in UR-C23-ko, *ṭhīk* and *jī* in UR-C26-hai-na, *garm* and
*sard* in UR-C27-lahori.

One correction to existing content: the corpus spelled the word for *room* both
ways. UR-W05-he showed **کمرہ**; this tranche's first draft wrote **کمرا**. The
standard spelling is **کمرہ** and the book now uses it everywhere, with the
chapter-23 lesson stopping to say that a final **ہ** here writes the vowel *-ā*
rather than an *h*.

### Forward references: one cleared, one made attributable

    2 -> 4 -> 3

UR-W05-he teaches the gol he by showing three words that use it in three
positions, and emphasised two of them — **ہم** and **کمرہ** — which the detector
reads as teaching those words. Both are now taught as vocabulary in chapters 25
and 23, so the emphasis had become a claim the lesson does not mean to make. The
Urdu is still on the page as a shape specimen; the bold is not, and ہم clears.

**کمرہ** remains, at 80 lessons early, and it is not new debt: the script lesson
already showed the word and glossed it "room" while no lesson taught it. Teaching
it — late — is what made the earlier use measurable.
