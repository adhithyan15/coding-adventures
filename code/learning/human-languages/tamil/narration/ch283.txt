Lesson 1 of 1.
A connected Tamil message about your familiar day — plan and write a 70–90 word Tamil message about a familiar day for a named reader and purpose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A connected message is not a pile of vocabulary and not one long sentence. It is a short route for one reader. Before writing, draw eight empty boxes and give them these jobs:
reader and greeting;
reason for writing;
where or when your day begins;
two familiar actions;
what happens afterwards and a second time anchor;
one contrast or reason;
one direct question about tomorrow;
a close.
Write only one or two English cue words in each box. Do not draft Tamil yet.

Writing — connected composition.
There is no Tamil model, sentence bank, word bank, romanization, or copyable answer on this page.
Reader: your friend Meena. Purpose: help Meena understand your familiar day and choose a good time to visit you tomorrow.
Write 70–90 words in Tamil script. Use every planning box. Include at least four familiar actions, two time anchors, one sequence link, one contrast or reason, and one direct question for Meena. Make every detail help with tomorrow's visit. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose only words and forms this book has taught, and connect them yourself.
This first connected attempt is untimed. Put away dictionaries, grammar references, translators, generative assistants, and spell-checkers. Do not copy from an earlier lesson. Ordinary legible Tamil handwriting is enough.
When the whole message exists, count its words once. If it is short, add one useful taught detail to a planning box. If it is long, remove a detail that does not help Meena. Do not replace the message with a memorised model.

Wrap-up Recall.
Review the same attempt in four separate passes:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — Meena, the familiar day, tomorrow's visit, and every requested content point are present.
Organisation and cohesion — the eight jobs form a route rather than a list of disconnected sentences.
Tamil vocabulary and grammar — every form and connector comes from the taught standard-colloquial Tamil track; a simpler familiar sentence is better than an invented structure.
Tamil-script control — letter shapes, vowel signs, conjuncts, word boundaries, and punctuation remain readable.
[pause 2 seconds]
Mark one strength and one next step. Repair only the next step; keep the original attempt so the next 70–90-word message can show real growth.
