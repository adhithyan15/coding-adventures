# Every way to leave, asked and denied

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C04-PRACTICE-01, AR-CONCEPT-C04-PRACTICE-02, AR-CONCEPT-C04-MAA-SALAMA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C04-ILA-LIQAA-01] -->

- [YOU SAY: the whole arc — greet, give your name, ask after them, part]
- [YOU SAY: part with safety — *maʿa s-salāma*]
- [YOU SAY: part until the meeting — *ilā l-liqāʾ*]
- [YOU RECALL: the one root that opens the greeting and closes the farewell]

Everything above stands on a handful of three-consonant roots, and the farewell
uses the same one the first greeting did. **s–l–m** gives *salām*, *salāma*,
*islām*, *muslim* — peace, safety, wholeness, one family.

## Grammar Lens: with, until, and the pattern between them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C04-MAA-WITH-01, AR-CONCEPT-C04-MAA-WITH-02, AR-CONCEPT-C04-AL-SALAMA-02] -->

**مع** *maʿa* is *mīm* plus *ʿayn* and means **with**. It is a preposition that
**stands apart** — unlike **الـ** and **بـ**, which fasten onto the word after
them. Its Hebrew cousin is *ʿim*.

**السلامة** is **الـ** + *salām* + **ة**, and the middle piece has been poured into
the pattern *faʿāla*, which converts a thing into **the state of** that thing:
*peace* becomes *safety*. Then the sun-letter rule bites, so **الس** is read
**as-**, and the phrase is *as-salāma*, never *al-salāma*.

## You'll want to know: until the meeting
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C04-ILA-LIQAA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C04-ILA-LIQAA-02] -->

**إلى اللقاء** is **إلى** (*until*) plus **اللقاء** (*the meeting*), root **l–q–y**,
to meet. Arabic does not name the parting; it names **the next meeting** and
points at it.

Spanish does the same thing with *hasta luego*, *until later*, and both leave the
listener holding a future rather than an ending. The **إ** with its hamza below and
the **ق** *qāf* both sit outside the writing set, so read them.
