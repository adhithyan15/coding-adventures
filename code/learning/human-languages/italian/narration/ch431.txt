Italian, chapter 431: Prenotare e disdire.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la caparra — the deposit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the camper van, then the Italian for the mosquito net.

You'll want to know: la caparra.
la caparra — "the deposit".
Per la camera chiedono una caparra del trenta per cento. For the room they ask for a thirty per cent deposit.
If you cancel, you may lose la caparra.

The word, taken apart.
caparra is built from capo 'head, chief', inherited from Latin caput, and arra 'pledge', from Latin arrha, which came into Latin from Greek (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
disdire — to cancel (a booking).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the deposit, then the Italian for the advance.

You'll want to know: disdire.
disdire — "to cancel (a booking)".
Abbiamo dovuto disdire la prenotazione. We had to cancel the booking.
Conjugated like dire: ho disdetto.

The word, taken apart.
disdire is built from dis- 'un-' and dire 'to say', which is inherited from Latin dīcere (Lewis & Short): to unsay what was agreed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la penale — the cancellation fee, the penalty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the advance, then the Italian for to cancel.

You'll want to know: la penale.
la penale — "the cancellation fee, the penalty".
Se disdici tardi, paghi una penale. If you cancel late, you pay a fee.
As an adjective, penale means criminal: il codice penale.

The word, taken apart.
penale is a learned borrowing from Latin poenālis 'of punishment', from poena 'penalty' (Lewis & Short).
