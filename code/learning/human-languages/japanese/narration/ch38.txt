Lesson 1 of 5.
ゆか (yuka) — the floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a desk, then the Japanese for a roof.

You'll want to know: ゆか.
ゆか — yuka — "the floor".

The word, taken apart.
ゆか (yuka) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ふとん (futon) — a futon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the floor, then the Japanese for a mirror.

You'll want to know: ふとん.
ふとん — futon — "a futon".

The word, taken apart.
ふとん (futon) is usually traced to a Chinese word for a round cushion woven of rushes: fu, "a kind of rush" (Middle Chinese bu), and ton, "a round mass" (Middle Chinese dwan), ton being a later Chinese reading. JMdict marks the common spelling with "cloth" as ateji.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a futon, then the Japanese for chopsticks.

You'll want to know: だいどころ.
だいどころ — daidokoro — "a kitchen".

The word, taken apart.
だいどころ (daidokoro) mixes the two layers: dai, "stand; platform", borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese doj), and native tokoro, "place", voiced to dokoro (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
