Mandarin Chinese, chapter 135: Sport.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zúqiú — football; soccer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for empty, then the Mandarin for full.

You'll want to know: zúqiú.
zúqiú — "football; soccer".
wǒ xǐhuan zúqiú — I love football.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zúqiú is built from zú 'foot' and qiú 'ball', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjowk and gjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses gjuw as 'fine jade', and under another way of writing it as a ball of fur that is struck.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lánqiú — basketball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for full, then the Mandarin for football.

You'll want to know: lánqiú.
lánqiú — "basketball".
wǒ gēge hěn gāo tā xǐhuan lánqiú — My brother is tall, and he loves basketball.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lánqiú is built from lán 'basket' and qiú 'ball'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese lam (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a basket', a different word from the lán of blue; the second is the qiú of football.

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for football, then the Mandarin for basketball.

You'll want to know: tī.
tī — "to kick".
wǒmen qù tī zúqiú — We're off to play football.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tī The character is in the Guangyun as thek (in Baxter's transcription), but only in the name of a beast; the verb is not given there. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
bǐsài — match; competition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for basketball, then the Mandarin for to kick.

You'll want to know: bǐsài.
bǐsài — "match; competition".
jīntiān yǒu zúqiú bǐsài — There's a football match today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bǐsài is built from bǐ 'to compare' and sài, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjijX and sojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'to repay'; how the pair came to mean a match is not settled.

The word, taken apart.
duànliàn is built from duàn 'to forge iron' and liàn 'to refine metal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, twanH and lenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The pair said of the body is not given there.
