Japanese, chapter 377: Delays and Cancellations.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ちえん (chien) — a delay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a route, then the Japanese for to skip.

You'll want to know: ちえん.
ちえん — chien — "a delay".
たいふうで でんしゃに ちえんが ありました。 — taifū de densha ni chien ga arimashita — "The typhoon delayed the trains."

The word, taken apart.
ちえん (chien) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are chi, "late" (Middle Chinese drij) and en, "prolong" (yen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
はなれる (hanareru) — to move away; to be apart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a timetable, then the Japanese for a direction (of travel).

You'll want to know: はなれる.
はなれる — hanareru — "to move away; to be apart".
でんしゃが えきを はなれました。 — densha ga eki o hanaremashita — "The train pulled away from the station."
Polite はなれます, hanaremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ふねが みなとを はなれました。, fune ga minato o hanaremashita.

The word, taken apart.
はなれる (hanareru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
