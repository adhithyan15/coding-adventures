# ಮೂಗು (mūgu) — "nose," where Telugu is the one that drifts

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C37-KIVI-01, KA-ETYMON-C37-KIVI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Kivi* split the family down a register line. This word splits
it a different way.

## You'll want to know: ಮೂಗು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C37-MOOGU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ಮೂಗು** — *mūgu* — **nose**

> **ನನ್ನ ಮೂಗು.** — *nanna mūgu.* — "My nose."

## The word, taken apart: Telugu drifts this time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C37-MOOGU-02]; assesses=[] -->

**ಮೂಗು** continues Proto-Dravidian **\*mūnk(k)u**, "nose." Tamil **மூக்கு**
(*mūkku*) and Malayalam **മൂക്ക്** (*mūkkŭ*) sit close beside Kannada's own
form. Telugu is the outlier this round: **ముక్కు** (*mukku*) is treated as a
slightly irregular reflex, likely blended with a separate word for "face."
Ear put Telugu with the group and split Tamil's registers; nose does the
opposite — Telugu drifts, and Tamil and Malayalam hold the line.
