Spanish, chapter 681: De compras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el mercadillo — the street market, the flea market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the launderette, then the Spanish for the ice-cream shop.

You'll want to know: el mercadillo.
el mercadillo — "the street market, the flea market".
Los domingos hay mercadillo en la plaza. On Sundays there is a street market in the square.
Smaller than el mercado, often outdoors and once a week.

The word, taken apart.
mercadillo is a little mercado "market", which is inherited from Latin mercātus (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el centro comercial — the shopping centre, the mall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the ice-cream shop, then the Spanish for the street market.

You'll want to know: el centro comercial.
el centro comercial — "the shopping centre, the mall".
El centro comercial abre hasta las diez. The shopping centre is open until ten.
In parts of Latin America also el mall or el shopping.

The word, taken apart.
centro comes from Latin centrum, Greek kéntron "a sharp point"; comercial is built on comercio, a learned borrowing from Latin commercium "trade".

Lesson 3 of 5.
los grandes almacenes — the department store.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the street market, then the Spanish for the shopping centre.

You'll want to know: los grandes almacenes.
los grandes almacenes — "the department store".
Compré la maleta en unos grandes almacenes. I bought the suitcase in a department store.
Always plural, even for one store.

The word, taken apart.
almacén comes from Andalusian Arabic al-maxzán "the storehouse", from Arabic makhzan; grande is inherited from Latin grandis "big".

Lesson 4 of 5.
festivo — holiday (of a day: día festivo); festive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the shopping centre, then the Spanish for the department store.

You'll want to know: festivo.
festivo — "holiday (of a day: día festivo); festive".
El museo cierra los días festivos. The museum is closed on public holidays.
On timetables: domingos y festivos — Sundays and public holidays.

The word, taken apart.
festivo is a learned borrowing from Latin fēstīvus "festive, merry" (Lewis & Short), from fēstum "a holiday".

Lesson 5 of 5.
atender — to serve (a customer); to attend to; to pay attention.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the department store, then the Spanish for holiday.

You'll want to know: atender.
atender — "to serve (a customer); to attend to; to pay attention".
¿Ya le atienden? Are you being served?
The e becomes ie under stress: atiendo, atiende.

The word, taken apart.
atender comes from Latin attendere "to stretch towards, to give heed" (EtymDB), built on tendere "to stretch".
