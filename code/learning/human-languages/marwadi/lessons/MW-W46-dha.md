# ध (dha) — the letter that ends दूध

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-DA-01, MW-LEX-C155-FOOD155-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write **द** once, and name it.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **दूध** (*dūdh*), "milk". It opens on **द** and ends on a **द**
with a breath after it: *dh*. This lesson puts that letter in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ध
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-SCRIPT-C155-DHA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ध** — *dha*, the breathed *d*.

It ends **दूध**. It sits beside **द** as **घ** sits beside **ग**: the same place in
the mouth, with a breath. On the page it is an upper spiral that ends in a
shoulder, a lower bowl, and a right stem under the headline.

## Writing: ध
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-SCRIPT-C155-DHA-01] -->

- **1.** start at the upper spiral's inner crossing, curl around the small opening, widen left and down around the outer loop, then sweep right through the shoulder
- **2.** without lifting, turn back left along the shoulder to the left waist
- **3.** sweep down and around the lower bowl to its right junction
- **4.** climb up the right stem to the headline
- **5.** descend the right stem top-to-bottom
- **6.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 1.**

> The source shows four movements; native writers draw ध as two pen-down strokes (81% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 64 of 79). This ductus follows the four movements in order and direction, lifting only before the headline: after the shoulder the pen turns back along it into the lower bowl, then climbs the right stem before descending it.

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ध-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
