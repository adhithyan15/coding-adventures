Lesson 1 of 5.
pōmārium, pōmāriī — an orchard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to shear, then the Latin for to milk.

You'll want to know: pōmārium, pōmāriī.
pōmārium, pōmāriī — "an orchard".
In pōmāriō arborēs plēnae pōmōrum sunt. In the orchard the trees are full of fruit.
Second declension, neuter: pōmārium, pōmāriī n., from pōmum 'fruit'. A pōmārius sells fruit.

The word, taken apart.
pōmārium is built on pōmum 'fruit' with the suffix -ārium 'place for' (Lewis & Short, under pōmārius).

In colle multae oleae sunt. On the hill there are many olive trees.
First declension, feminine: olea, oleae f. A variant of olīva.

The word, taken apart.
olea was borrowed from Greek elaia 'olive, olive tree' (EtymDB), like olīva and oleum 'oil'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prēlum, prēlī — a press (for wine or oil).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an orchard, then the Latin for an olive tree.

You'll want to know: prēlum, prēlī.
prēlum, prēlī — "a press (for wine or oil)".
Servī ūvās in prēlō premunt. The slaves press the grapes in the press.
Second declension, neuter: prēlum, prēlī n., also praelum. The heavy beam that squeezed grapes and olives.

The word, taken apart.
prēlum is built on premō 'to press' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from an older prem-lom. English press comes from the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
īnserō, īnserere — to graft, to plant in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an olive tree, then the Latin for a press.

You'll want to know: īnserō, īnserere.
īnserō, īnserere — "to graft, to plant in".
Agricola rāmum pirī in mālum īnserit. The farmer grafts a pear branch onto an apple tree.
īnserō, īnserere, īnsēvī, īnsitum, from serō, serere, sēvī 'to sow'. A different verb īnserō, īnserere, īnseruī, īnsertum 'to put in' gives English insert.

The word, taken apart.
īnserō 'to graft' is built from in- 'in' and serō, serere, sēvī 'to sow, to plant' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). It must be kept apart from īnserō 'to put in', from serō, seruī 'to join'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vindēmia, vindēmiae — the grape harvest, the vintage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a press, then the Latin for to graft.

You'll want to know: vindēmia, vindēmiae.
vindēmia, vindēmiae — "the grape harvest, the vintage".
Autumnō vindēmia est; tōta familia ūvās legit. In autumn it is the grape harvest; the whole household gathers grapes.
First declension, feminine: vindēmia, vindēmiae f. English vintage comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
vindēmia is built from vīnum 'wine, grapes' and dēmō 'to take off, to remove' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): taking the grapes off the vine. English vintage comes from it through French.
