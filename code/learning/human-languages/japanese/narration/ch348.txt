Japanese, chapter 348: If You Cannot Come.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
けっせきする (kesseki suru) — to be absent; to stay away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for while you are at it, then the Japanese for a caller.

You'll want to know: けっせきする.
けっせきする — kesseki suru — "to be absent; to stay away".
ねつが あったら、がっこうを けっせきして ください。 — netsu ga attara, gakkō o kesseki shite kudasai — "If you have a fever, stay away from school."
Polite けっせきします, kessekishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): むすこが がっこうを けっせきしました。, musuko ga gakkō o kesseki shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
けっせきする (kesseki suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, kesseki, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: ketsu, "lack" and seki, "seat" (Middle Chinese zjek) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as kes-seki.

Lesson 2 of 5.
おそい (osoi) — slow; late.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a caller, then the Japanese for to be absent.

You'll want to know: おそい.
おそい — osoi — "slow; late".
おそく なったら、でんわして ください。 — osoku nattara, denwa shite kudasai — "If it gets late, please phone."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
おそい (osoi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ぎりぎり (girigiri) — at the last minute; barely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be absent, then the Japanese for slow.

You'll want to know: ぎりぎり.
ぎりぎり — girigiri — "at the last minute; barely".
いま でたら、ぎりぎり まにあうでしょう。 — ima detara, girigiri maniau deshō — "If you leave now, you will barely make it."

The word, taken apart.
ぎりぎり (girigiri) is a native mimetic word, one of the Japanese words that picture a manner or a feeling in sound; JMdict marks it as mimetic. It is inherited, not borrowed, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for slow, then the Japanese for at the last minute.

You'll want to know: じこく.
じこく — jikoku — "the hour; the time of day".
でんしゃの じこくが かわったら、おしえて ください。 — densha no jikoku ga kawattara, oshiete kudasai — "If the train times change, please tell me."

The word, taken apart.
じこく (jikoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "time" (Middle Chinese dzyi) and koku, "time" (khok) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
