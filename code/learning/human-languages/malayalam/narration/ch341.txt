Malayalam, chapter 341: What I Think, and So.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
വിചാരിക്കുക (vicārikkuka) — to think, to suppose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for "a thought". (vicāraṁ.) Then "I think it is good". (nallatāṇŭ ennŭ ñān cintikkunnu.)

You'll want to know: വിചാരിക്കുക.
വിചാരിക്കുക — vicārikkuka — "to think, to suppose".
It is the everyday verb for what you think is so, and it takes what you think in front of it, closed with എന്ന്, the way ചിന്തിക്കുക did when you met it.
ഇത് ഒരു ചെറിയ പ്രശ്നമാണ് എന്ന് ഞാൻ വിചാരിച്ചു. itŭ oru ceṟiya praśnamāṇŭ ennŭ ñān vicāriccu. — "I thought this was a small problem."
അവൻ വരും എന്ന് ഞാൻ വിചാരിച്ചു. avan varuṁ ennŭ ñān vicāriccu. — "I thought he would come."
The past, വിചാരിച്ചു, often carries a hint that you were wrong: he did not come after all. ചിന്തിക്കുക leans the other way, towards thinking something over.

The word, taken apart.
വിചാരിക്കുക (vicārikkuka) is വിചാരം, "a thought", from Sanskrit vicāra, "pondering" (CDIAL 11683), made into a verb with -ഇക്കുക. That ending turns many Sanskrit nouns into Malayalam verbs: സഹായം, "help", gives സഹായിക്കുക, "to help".

Lesson 2 of 6.
എന്ന് ഞാൻ കരുതുന്നു (ennŭ ñān karutunnu) — I think that …

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to think, to suppose". (vicārikkuka.) Then "to feel, to seem". (tōnnuka.) Then "an opinion". (abhiprāyaṁ.)

Grammar Lens: … എന്ന് ഞാൻ വിചാരിക്കുന്നു / കരുതുന്നു.
An opinion has the same frame as a report: what you think, then എന്ന്, then you, then the verb. Two verbs fill the last slot:
ഇത് നല്ലതാണ് എന്ന് ഞാൻ വിചാരിക്കുന്നു. itŭ nallatāṇŭ ennŭ ñān vicārikkunnu. — "I think this is good."
അത് ശരിയാണ് എന്ന് ഞാൻ കരുതുന്നു. atŭ śariyāṇŭ ennŭ ñān karutunnu. — "I consider that right."
കരുതുക, "to consider, to hold", is a little weightier than വിചാരിക്കുക (vicārikkuka): the view is thought through, not a guess.

Grammar Lens: എനിക്ക് … എന്ന് തോന്നുന്നു.
The softest way puts you in the dative, as with എനിക്ക് അറിയാം: the thought comes to you.
ഇത് നല്ലതാണ് എന്ന് എനിക്ക് തോന്നുന്നു. itŭ nallatāṇŭ ennŭ enikkŭ tōnnunnu. — "It seems to me this is good."
For a view you stand behind, open with എന്റെ അഭിപ്രായത്തിൽ (enṟe abhiprāyattil), "in my opinion":
എന്റെ അഭിപ്രായത്തിൽ ഇത് ഒരു നല്ല തീരുമാനമാണ്. enṟe abhiprāyattil itŭ oru nalla tīrumānamāṇŭ. — "In my opinion, this is a good decision."

Lesson 3 of 6.
ഐക്യം (aikyaṁ) — unity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I think this is good", then "it seems to me this is good".

You'll want to know: ഐക്യം.
ഐക്യം — aikyaṁ — "unity, togetherness": people holding together as one.
ഞങ്ങളുടെ ഗ്രാമത്തിൽ നല്ല ഐക്യം ഉണ്ട്. ñaṅṅaḷuṭe grāmattil nalla aikyaṁ uṇṭŭ. — "There is real unity in our village."
കുടുംബത്തിൽ ഐക്യം വേണം എന്ന് ഞാൻ കരുതുന്നു. kuṭumbattil aikyaṁ vēṇaṁ ennŭ ñān karutunnu. — "I think a family needs unity."
It is a word for speeches and newspapers as much as for kitchens, and it opens with a letter you have not written yet. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ഐക്യം (aikyaṁ) from Sanskrit aikya, "oneness", taken over whole as a tatsama with Malayalam's noun ending (CDIAL 2533). aikya is built on eka, "one": Sanskrit strengthens the first vowel, e to ai, to make "the state of being one". Hindi ek, "one", is the same root.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ഐക്യം (aikyaṁ) mean? (unity, togetherness.) Which Sanskrit number is inside it? (eka, "one".)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write എ and ൈ once each, and name them. (e at the start of a word; the ai sign, printed before its consonant, as in കൈ.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ഐക്യം (aikyaṁ), "unity". This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ഐ.
ഐ — ai, at the start of a word.
After a consonant, ai is the sign ൈ, printed before the consonant it follows in speech: കൈ, kai. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own, ഐ.
Its left half has the shape of എ. The right half is a second piece: a small loop, a short base, and a tall loop that drops below the line.

Writing: ഐ.
One recorded hand writes ഐ (ai) in two strokes. First the left half, as you write എ: from the inner tip, round the small loop and out over the arch to its foot. Then, after one lift, the right half in one run: up and over its small loop, along the base, up the short stem, down below the line, and up the tall loop, over its top and down the right side.
Write ഐ (ai) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Copy the printed shape and keep the order above.

Lesson 5 of 6.
അതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല (atukoṇṭŭ ñān vannilla) — so I did not come.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "so, therefore". (atukoṇṭŭ.) Then "a reason; because". (kāraṇaṁ.)

Grammar Lens: the reason first, then അതുകൊണ്ട്.
അതുകൊണ്ട് is അത്, "that", with കൊണ്ട്, "with": "with that", "so". It points forward, from a reason to what it caused.
എനിക്ക് പനിയായിരുന്നു. അതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല. enikkŭ paniyāyirunnu. atukoṇṭŭ ñān vannilla. — "I had a fever. So I did not come."
കാരണം points the other way, back from the result to its reason:
ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല, കാരണം എനിക്ക് പനിയായിരുന്നു. ñān vannilla, kāraṇaṁ enikkŭ paniyāyirunnu. — "I did not come, because I had a fever."
Same facts, opposite order. Pick the one that puts first what your listener needs first.

Grammar Lens: അതുകൊണ്ടാണ് — that is why.
Add -ആണ് and the result is singled out: "that is why". The verb after it takes -അത്, "the …-ing", so the whole sentence says "it is with that that the …-ing happened":
മഴയായിരുന്നു. അതുകൊണ്ടാണ് ഞാൻ വൈകിയത്. maḻayāyirunnu. atukoṇṭāṇŭ ñān vaikiyatŭ. — "It was raining. That is why I was late."
വൈകി gives വൈകിയത്, and വന്നു gives വന്നത്, the same way.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[your turn — say: "I think the village needs unity. So I am going to the meeting." — grāmattinŭ aikyaṁ vēṇaṁ ennŭ ñān karutunnu. atukoṇṭŭ ñān yōgattinŭ pōkunnu.]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word points forward to a result? (അതുകൊണ്ട്.) Which points back to a reason? (കാരണം.) "That is why I was late"? (atukoṇṭāṇŭ ñān vaikiyatŭ.)
