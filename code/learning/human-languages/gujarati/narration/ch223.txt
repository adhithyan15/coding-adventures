Gujarati, chapter 223: How are you feeling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
તબિયત (tabiyat) — health.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for high tide, then the Gujarati for low tide.

You'll want to know: તબિયત.
તબિયત — tabiyat — "health".
શું તમારી તબિયત સારી છે? — śũ tamārī tabiyat sārī che? — "Are you keeping well?"
Word for word, is your health good.

The word, taken apart.
તબિયત (tabiyat) is a loan from Arabic ṭabīʿa, "nature, temperament", which reached Gujarati through Persian (EtymDB lists the Persian word). Its Arabic root is ṭ-b-ʿ (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

મારું શરીર નબળું નથી. — mārũ śarīr nabaḷũ nathī — "My body is not weak."

The word, taken apart.
શરીર (śarīr) is Sanskrit śarīra, "body", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama. CDIAL lists the inherited Old Gujarati saïra, "body", beside it (CDIAL 12335).

Lesson 3 of 5.
દુખવું (dukhvũ) — to ache, to hurt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for health, then the Gujarati for body.

You'll want to know: દુખવું.
દુખવું — dukhvũ — "to ache, to hurt".
શું તમારું માથું દુખે છે? — śũ tamārũ māthũ dukhe che? — "Does your head ache?"
The noun for the pain is દુખાવો.

The word, taken apart.
દુખવું (dukhvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from duḥkhati, "pains", built on duḥkha, "pain" (CDIAL 6376).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for body, then the Gujarati for to ache.

શરદી — śardī — "common cold, a chill".
મને શરદી નથી. — mane śardī nathī — "I don't have a cold."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
શરદી (śardī) is a loan, usually traced to Persian sardī, "coldness", from sard, "cold"; EtymDB lists the Persian word. CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
છીંકવું (chī̃kvũ) — to sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to ache, then the Gujarati for common cold.

You'll want to know: છીંકવું.
છીંકવું — chī̃kvũ — "to sneeze".
હું આજે નથી છીંકતો. — hũ āje nathī chī̃kto — "I am not sneezing today."
The sneeze is છીંક, chī̃k.

The word, taken apart.
છીંકવું (chī̃kvũ) is inherited, built on an imitative word for the sound. CDIAL lists Gujarati chī̃k and chī̃kvũ under Sanskrit chikkā, "sneeze" (CDIAL 5032).
