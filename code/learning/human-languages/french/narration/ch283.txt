Lesson 1 of 6.
diffuser — to broadcast; to spread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the editorial, then the French for short news items.

You'll want to know: diffuser.
diffuser — "to broadcast; to spread".
La télé a diffusé le match en direct. The TV broadcast the match live.
Also to spread news or a smell: diffuser une information.

The word, taken apart.
diffuser is built on diffus "spread out" (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin diffūsus, the past participle of diffundere "to pour out" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
interviewer — to interview.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the broadcasting, then the French for to film.

You'll want to know: interviewer.
interviewer — "to interview".
La journaliste a interviewé le maire. The journalist interviewed the mayor.
It ends in the French -er sound of parler. The noun is l'interview, feminine; in Québec, l'entrevue.

The word, taken apart.
interviewer was borrowed from English interview, which itself comes from French entrevue "a meeting face to face" (EtymDB), built on voir, from Latin vidēre.

Lesson 5 of 6.
l'antenne — the aerial, the antenna; being on air.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to film, then the French for to interview.

You'll want to know: l'antenne.
l'antenne — "the aerial, the antenna; being on air".
Le chanteur a parlé pendant dix minutes à l'antenne. The singer spoke on air for ten minutes.
Être à l'antenne — to be on air. Also an insect's feeler.

The word, taken apart.
antenne is a learned borrowing from Latin antemna "a sail yard" (EtymDB), first said of an insect's feelers, then of a radio mast.
