Japanese, chapter 501: Moon Viewing and Autumn Leaves.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
つきみ (tsukimi) — moon viewing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a riddle, then the Japanese for a slide (in a playground).

You'll want to know: つきみ.
つきみ — tsukimi — "moon viewing".
あきの ばん、つきみを します。 — aki no ban, tsukimi o shimasu — "On autumn evenings we go moon viewing."

The word, taken apart.
つきみ (tsukimi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from tsuki, "moon" and mi, "looking" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ふうりん (fūrin) — a wind chime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for flower viewing, then the Japanese for autumn leaves.

You'll want to know: ふうりん.
ふうりん — fūrin — "a wind chime".
なつに ふうりんの おとが します。 — natsu ni fūrin no oto ga shimasu — "In summer you hear the wind chimes."

The word, taken apart.
ふうりん (fūrin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are fū, "wind" (Middle Chinese pjuwng) and rin, "bell" (leng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ちらす (chirasu) — to scatter (something); to strew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for autumn leaves, then the Japanese for a wind chime.

You'll want to know: ちらす.
ちらす — chirasu — "to scatter (something); to strew".
かぜが さくらの はなを ちらしました。 — kaze ga sakura no hana o chirashimashita — "The wind scattered the cherry blossoms."
Polite ちらします, chirashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かぜが はなを ちらしました。, kaze ga hana o chirashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ちらす (chirasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, the transitive partner of chiru, "to fall (of leaves)"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
