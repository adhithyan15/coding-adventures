## You'll want to know: 球场
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C498-PITCH498-02]; assesses=[] -->

**球场** — *qiúchǎng* — "sports ground; pitch; court".

> *wǒmen zài qiúchǎng tī le yí ge xiàwǔ zúqiú* — We played football on the pitch all afternoon.

The new character, in pieces: 球 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the jade piece of 理 *lǐ*, four strokes; on the right seven strokes, a bar, a long vertical with a hook, a dot and a rising stroke on the left, a short and a long falling stroke on the right, and a last dot at the top right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**球场** (*qiúchǎng*) is built from *qiú* 'a ball' and 场 'a ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *gjuw* and *drjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a threshing floor'. It glosses the first character 'a fine jade'; the sense 'a ball' belongs to a second character with the same sound, glossed 'a ball of hair to be struck', which modern writing spells the same way.
