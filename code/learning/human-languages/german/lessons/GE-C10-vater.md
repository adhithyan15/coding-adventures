# der Vater — "father"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-NATIVE-VS-LOAN-01, GE-SOUND-V-AS-F-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The months were bought from Rome. Nothing in this chapter was.

## You'll want to know: der Vater
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-VATER-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **der Vater** — "father."

A *der*-word, two syllables, and the first letter is a trap for an English eye.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-V-AS-F-01, GE-LEX-VATER-01] -->

- `v-as-f` — German *v* is an **f**. You were given this on *vier*, "four," and
  it has not changed: *Vater* opens *FAH-*, never *VAH-*.
- `vowel-a-long` — the *a* is long and open, the *ah* of English *father*.
- `r-final` — and it closes on the soft *r* you have been ending words with
  since *Uhr*.

*FAH-ter*. Say it beside English **father** and the only real difference is the
middle consonant.
