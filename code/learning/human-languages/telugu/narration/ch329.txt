Telugu, chapter 329: A Break-In.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
దొంగతనం (doṅgatanaṁ) — a theft; a burglary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for the next day, then the Telugu for to recall.

You'll want to know: దొంగతనం.
దొంగతనం — doṅgatanaṁ — "a theft; a burglary".
నిన్న రాత్రి మా వీధిలో దొంగతనం జరిగింది. ninna rātri mā vīdhilō doṅgatanaṁ jarigindi. Last night there was a theft in our street.
దొంగ, a thief, with -తనం, the ending that makes a noun of a state or a deed.

The word, taken apart.
దొంగతనం (doṅgatanaṁ) is native Dravidian: doṅga, "a thief" (DEDR 2982), with the Telugu noun ending -tanaṁ.

Lesson 2 of 5.
చొరబడు (corabaḍu) — to break in; to intrude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to recall, then the Telugu for a theft.

You'll want to know: చొరబడు.
చొరబడు — corabaḍu — "to break in; to intrude".
దొంగలు కిటికీ నుంచి ఇంట్లోకి చొరబడ్డారు. doṅgalu kiṭikī nuñci iṇṭlōki corabaḍḍāru. The thieves broke into the house through the window.
Past (I …): చొరబడ్డాను, corabaḍḍānu.
The place you enter takes -లోకి, into: ఇంట్లోకి చొరబడు (corabaḍu).

The word, taken apart.
చొరబడు (corabaḍu) is a native compound: the stem cor- of coccu, "to enter, penetrate" (DEDR 2778), with paḍu, the helping verb of things that befall.

Lesson 3 of 5.
బద్దలు కొట్టు (baddalu koṭṭu) — to break open; to smash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a theft, then the Telugu for to break in.

You'll want to know: బద్దలు కొట్టు.
బద్దలు కొట్టు — baddalu koṭṭu — "to break open; to smash".
దొంగ తలుపు తాళం బద్దలు కొట్టాడు. doṅga talupu tāḷaṁ baddalu koṭṭāḍu. The thief smashed the lock on the door.
Past (I …): బద్దలు కొట్టాను, baddalu koṭṭānu.
బద్దలు means pieces: to beat something to pieces is to break it open.

The word, taken apart.
బద్దలు కొట్టు (baddalu koṭṭu) is a Telugu phrase: koṭṭu, "to beat, strike", is native Dravidian (DEDR 2063); the origin of baddalu, "pieces", is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఎత్తుకుపోవు (ettukupōvu) — to make off with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to break in, then the Telugu for to break open.

You'll want to know: ఎత్తుకుపోవు.
ఎత్తుకుపోవు — ettukupōvu — "to make off with".
దొంగలు బంగారం అంతా ఎత్తుకుపోయారు. doṅgalu baṅgāraṁ antā ettukupōyāru. The thieves made off with all the gold.
Past (I …): ఎత్తుకుపోయాను, ettukupōyānu.
Three pieces in one: ఎత్తు, to lift, -కు, for oneself, and పోవు, to go.

The word, taken apart.
ఎత్తుకుపోవు (ettukupōvu) is a native compound: ettu, "to lift up, take away" (DEDR 796), with the self-ending -ku and pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 5 of 5.
పసిగట్టు (pasigaṭṭu) — to sense; to detect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to break open, then the Telugu for to make off with.

You'll want to know: పసిగట్టు.
పసిగట్టు — pasigaṭṭu — "to sense; to detect".
కుక్క దొంగను వెంటనే పసిగట్టింది. kukka doṅganu veṇṭanē pasigaṭṭindi. The dog sensed the thief at once.
Past (I …): పసిగట్టాను, pasigaṭṭānu.
A dog senses a stranger, and so does a sharp mind: పసిగట్టు (pasigaṭṭu) works for both.

The word, taken apart.
పసిగట్టు (pasigaṭṭu) is a Telugu compound whose first part is pasi, "smell, scent" (DEDR 3826); the origin of the whole word is not settled.
