Portuguese, chapter 420: Chegar a terra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a boia — the buoy; the float, the rubber ring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bow, then the Portuguese for the stern.

You'll want to know: a boia.
a boia — "the buoy; the float, the rubber ring".
As crianças nadam com boias nos braços. The children swim with armbands on.
Boias on the arms are armbands; uma boia in the sea marks a safe area.

The word, taken apart.
boia was borrowed from Old French; EtymDB traces the French word to a Frankish word related to beacon 'sign'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a âncora — the anchor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the stern, then the Portuguese for the buoy.

You'll want to know: a âncora.
a âncora — "the anchor".
O barco lançou a âncora perto da praia. The boat dropped anchor near the beach.
In Brazil, o âncora is also a TV news anchor; Portugal says o pivô.

The word, taken apart.
âncora is inherited from Latin ancora, which came from Greek ánkyra 'anchor' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
atracar — to moor, to dock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the buoy, then the Portuguese for the anchor.

You'll want to know: atracar.
atracar — "to moor, to dock".
O ferry atracou no cais às dez. The ferry docked at the quay at ten.
The opposite is desatracar, to cast off.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of atracar is not settled. EtymDB links it with an Arabic verb meaning 'to advance', but that link is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o enjoo — seasickness, nausea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the anchor, then the Portuguese for to moor.

You'll want to know: o enjoo.
o enjoo — "seasickness, nausea".
Tomei um comprimido para o enjoo. I took a pill for the seasickness.
Also car sickness or morning sickness.

The word, taken apart.
enjoo is built on enjoar 'to feel sick', whose own origin is not settled (EtymDB lists enjoar without a source).

Lesson 5 of 5.
enjoar — to feel sick (on a boat or in a car); to get sick of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to moor, then the Portuguese for seasickness.

You'll want to know: enjoar.
enjoar — "to feel sick (on a boat or in a car); to get sick of".
Enjoo sempre que viajo de barco. I always feel sick when I travel by boat.
Informal: Já enjoei esta música — I am sick of this song.

The word, taken apart.
enjoar is usually traced to Latin in odiō 'in hatred', the phrase behind Spanish enojar, but the origin is not settled; EtymDB has no source for it.
