# ottenere — "to get," and a b that flattened into a t

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-GIOCARE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson was about **doing** — playing a game, playing an
instrument. This one is about **having**. English *get* means almost anything;
Italian has nothing of the sort, and for the sense that matters, **coming to
have** a thing, the verb is this one.

## You'll want to know: the word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-OTTENERE-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PRENDERE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PRENDERE-04, IT-LEX-PORTARE-02] -->

> **ottenere** — to get, to obtain, to come to have.

Line it up with what you have. *Prendere* **seizes** a thing, *portare*
**carries** it. *Ottenere* **obtains** it: it was not lying there for the
taking.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-OTTENERE-03]; assesses=[IT-ETYMON-PRENDERE-03, IT-ETYMON-PORTARE-03, IT-ETYMON-MANO-04, IT-LEX-ETA-02, IT-GRAMMAR-ETA-05] -->

**ottenere** comes from Latin **obtinēre**: **ob-**, "toward, up against," plus
**tenēre**, *"to hold."* To obtain is to get a hold on something:

- **obtain** — the compound borrowed whole.
- **tenacious**, **tenant** and **tenure** (a holding), **tenet** ("he holds"),
  **tenor**.
- **contain**, **retain**, **maintain** — which Chapter 17 gave you as "hold in
  the hand."

English stacked prefixes on *tenēre* exactly as it stacked them on the last
chapter's *portāre*: one Latin verb, a rack of prefixes.

*Tenēre* also explains Chapter 14, where Italian counted years with *avere*:
**Ho venti anni**. **Spanish** says *tengo veinte años*, **Portuguese** *tenho vinte anos*,
and both verbs are this same *tenēre*. Where Italian **has** its years, Spain and
Portugal **hold** theirs — and English and German simply **are** twenty.

## Grammar Lens: bt lands on tt, and a stem that bends
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-OTTENERE-04]; assesses=[IT-ETYMON-NUMERI-6-10-03, IT-GRAMMAR-SCRIVERE-03, IT-GRAMMAR-PASSATO-PROSSIMO-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PORTARE-04, IT-GRAMMAR-GIOCARE-04] -->

*Obtinēre* has a **b** and a **t** side by side, and Italian will not say them
that way. It does to *-bt-* what it did to *-ct-* and *-pt-*: the first consonant
flattens into the second.

| Latin | Italian |
|---|---|
| *octō* | **otto** |
| *scriptum* | **scritto** |
| *obtinēre* | **ottenere** |

The forms bend under stress: **ottengo, ottieni, ottiene**. A **g** surfaces in
the *io* form; the stem's *e* breaks into *ie* in the other two. Where *giocare*
spelled in an **h** to hold a consonant still, this verb lets the vowel move
instead. The past is ordinary — **ottenuto**, regular the way *portato* was, so
**ho ottenuto**. That *-ngo* is the shape Spanish shows in *tengo*.
