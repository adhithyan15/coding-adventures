# ض (zuād) — the letter that ends قمیض

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C37-FE-01, UR-SCRIPT-C37-QAF-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write ف and ق once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **قمیض** (*qamīz*), "shirt".
You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts
it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ض
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-SCRIPT-C37-ZUAD-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ض** — *zuād*, the sound *z*.

It ends **قمیض** (*qamīz*). An oval with a short tooth, a bowl, and **one dot
above**. Urdu writes *z* with more than one letter; **ض** is the one that came with
Arabic words like this.

## Writing: ض
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-C37-ZUAD-01] -->

- **1.** start here: close the elongated upper oval clockwise and finish its short tooth
- **2.** after one lift, restart below the oval and sweep through the lower bowl from right to left
- **3.** after another lift, place the dot above last — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 2.**

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary.
> Source: Zer o Zabar, Khe, ze, zal, swad, and zwad, independent ض calligraphic and handwriting animations and Zwad instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).
