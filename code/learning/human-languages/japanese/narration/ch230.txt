Japanese, chapter 230: Bosses, Seniors and Juniors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しゃちょう (shachō) — a company president.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be employed, then the Japanese for a colleague.

You'll want to know: しゃちょう.
しゃちょう — shachō — "a company president".
しゃちょうは いつも かいぎで はなします。 — shachō wa itsumo kaigi de hanashimasu — "The company president always speaks at meetings."

The word, taken apart.
しゃちょう (shachō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sha, "company, association" (Middle Chinese dzyaeX) and chō, "leader, senior" (trjangX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
がんばる (ganbaru) — to do one's best; to keep at it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a senior, then the Japanese for a junior.

You'll want to know: がんばる.
がんばる — ganbaru — "to do one's best; to keep at it".
こうはいは まいにち しごとを がんばります。 — kōhai wa mainichi shigoto o ganbarimasu — "The junior does his best at work every day."
Polite がんばります, ganbarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
がんばる (ganbaru) is written with two characters meaning "stubborn" and "stretch", read as one verb in -ru; its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
