Latin, chapter 398: Keeping My Word.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
violō, violāre — to violate, to break; to wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a pact, then the Latin for to bargain for.

You'll want to know: violō, violāre.
violō, violāre — "to violate, to break; to wrong".
Hostēs foedus violāvērunt. The enemy broke the treaty.
violō, violāre, violāvī, violātum, kin to vīs, force. Fidem violāre, to break one's word. English violate and violence.

The word, taken apart.
violō is built on vīs "force" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to treat with force. English violate and violence come from its family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
praestō, praestāre — to make good, to fulfil; to excel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to bargain for, then the Latin for to violate.

You'll want to know: praestō, praestāre.
praestō, praestāre — "to make good, to fulfil; to excel".
Fidem praestitit. He kept his word.
praestō, praestāre, praestitī, praestitum. Fidem praestāre, to make one's word good; with the dative, to stand out above: omnibus praestat.

The word, taken apart.
praestō is built from prae "before, in front" and stō "to stand" (Lewis & Short): to stand out in front, then to stand good for a promise.

obligō, obligāre, obligāvī, obligātum, ob + ligō, to tie. English oblige and obligation.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ratus, rata, ratum — fixed, settled, valid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to make good, then the Latin for to oblige.

You'll want to know: ratus, rata, ratum.
ratus, rata, ratum — "fixed, settled, valid".
Pactum ratum est. The pact is valid.
ratus, rata, ratum, the participle of reor, to reckon. Ratum habēre, to ratify; prō ratā parte, in proportion, gives English pro rata.

The word, taken apart.
ratus is the participle of reor "to reckon, to think" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): reckoned, so settled. English pro rata and rate come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
irritus, irrita, irritum — void, invalid; in vain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to oblige, then the Latin for fixed.

You'll want to know: irritus, irrita, irritum.
irritus, irrita, irritum — "void, invalid; in vain".
Cōnsilia eius irrita fuērunt. His plans came to nothing.
irritus, irrita, irritum (also inritus): in- not + ratus, fixed. Irritum fierī, to come to nothing.

The word, taken apart.
irritus is built from in- "not" and ratus "fixed, valid" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): not settled, so void.
