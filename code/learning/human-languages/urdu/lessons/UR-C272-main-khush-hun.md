# میں خوش ہوں (maiṅ khush hūṅ) — how I feel

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C272-DAR-01, UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-02, UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I'm afraid". (*mujhe ḍar lagtā hai*.) Then "happy" and "sad". (*khush*, *udās*.) Urdu says a feeling two ways.

## You'll want to know: I am happy, she was sad
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C272-FEELING-01]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-02, UR-LEX-C42-HEALTH42-03, UR-LEX-C193-SIFAT193-05, UR-GRAMMAR-C264-WAS-01, UR-GRAMMAR-C267-KAR-01] -->

> **میں خوش ہوں۔**
>
> *maiṅ khush hūṅ.* — "I'm happy."

> **کل وہ اداس تھی۔**
>
> *kal voh udās thī.* — "Yesterday she was sad."

> **میں پریشان تھا۔**
>
> *maiṅ pareshān thā.* — "I was worried."

The feeling word stays as it is; **ہوں**, **تھی**, **تھا** follow the person. With **ہوا** (*huā*) the feeling arrives:

> **یہ سن کر وہ خوش ہوئی۔**
>
> *yih sun kar voh khush huī.* — "Hearing this, she was pleased."

## You'll want to know: what strikes you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C272-FEELING-02]; assesses=[UR-LEX-C268-LAGNA-01, UR-LEX-SHAHR, UR-LEX-C228-CHIZ228-04, UR-LEX-KAISE-KAISI] -->

> **مجھے ڈر لگا۔**
>
> *mujhe ḍar lagā.* — "I got scared."

> **مجھے یہ شہر اچھا لگا۔**
>
> *mujhe yih shahr achchhā lagā.* — "I liked this city."

A feeling that strikes you, or a thing that seems good, takes **مجھے** and **لگنا**, and the verb follows the thing: **شہر** is masculine, **اچھا لگا**. To ask:

> **آپ کو فلم کیسی لگی؟**
>
> *āp ko film kaisī lagī?* — "How did you like the film?"
