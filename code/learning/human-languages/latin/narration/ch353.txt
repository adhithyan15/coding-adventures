Latin, chapter 353: Aqua fervet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ferveō, fervēre — to boil, to be hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for leaven, then the Latin for a baking pot.

You'll want to know: ferveō, fervēre.
ferveō, fervēre — "to boil, to be hot".
Aqua in ōllā fervēbat. The water was boiling in the pot.
ferveō, fervēre, ferbuī, also an older fervō, fervere. English fervent.

The word, taken apart.
ferveō is an inherited verb (EtymDB). English fervent comes from it, and ferment from its noun fermentum.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Caccabus super focum pendēbat. A cooking pot was hanging over the hearth.
Masculine, a Greek loan: a round pot of bronze or clay.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ēlixus, ēlixa, ēlixum — boiled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to boil, then the Latin for a cooking pot.

You'll want to know: ēlixus, ēlixa, ēlixum.
ēlixus, ēlixa, ēlixum — "boiled".
Carō ēlixa mollis et dulcis erat. The boiled meat was soft and sweet.
Boiled right through. A slave in Plautus jokes that he tastes better boiled than roasted.

The word, taken apart.
ēlixus is built, says Lewis & Short, on ē, "out, thoroughly", and lix, an old word for water or lye: thoroughly boiled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
spūma, spūmae — foam, froth, scum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cooking pot, then the Latin for boiled.

You'll want to know: spūma, spūmae.
spūma, spūmae — "foam, froth, scum".
Spūma alba in aquā ferventī natābat. White foam was floating on the boiling water.
Feminine. English spume.

The word, taken apart.
spūma is linked by Lewis & Short with spuō, "to spit". English spume comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēspūmō, dēspūmāre — to skim off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for boiled, then the Latin for foam.

You'll want to know: dēspūmō, dēspūmāre.
dēspūmō, dēspūmāre — "to skim off".
Coquus ōllam dīligenter dēspūmāvit. The cook carefully skimmed the pot.
dēspūmō, dēspūmāre, dēspūmāvī, dēspūmātum. Vergil's farmer skims the boiling grape juice with leaves.

The word, taken apart.
dēspūmō is built on spūma, "foam", with dē-, "off" (EtymDB): to take the foam off.
