# जाणे (jāṇe) — "to go," and a present tense that gives you away

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-ASNE-AAHE, MR-GRAMMAR-AAHE-LAST] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "my name is …" once more, and hear where *āhe* lands. Every verb
from here obeys that same rule. This one adds a second rule English has no use
for.

## You'll want to know: जाणे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-JANE]; assesses=[] -->

> **जाणे** — *jāṇe* — **to go**

Strip the infinitive ending **-णे** and the stem is **जा-** *jā-*. On that stem
Marathi builds the everyday present: **जातो** (*jāto*) and **जाते** (*jāte*).

Both mean "I go." Neither is more correct than the other. Which one you may say
is decided by something the English sentence never mentions.

## Sounds you'll need: जा · णे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ज** is *ja*, and the upright stroke after it is the long *ā* — together **जा**
*jā*. **णे** you met last lesson: retroflex **ण** with the *-e* stroke. Two
syllables, *jā-ṇe*, with the tongue curled back for that second one.

## Grammar Lens: the ending declares who is speaking
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-GENDER]; assesses=[] -->

English "I go" hides the speaker completely — a man and a woman say the same two
words. Marathi will not let you:

| speaking of | Marathi | said |
|---|---|---|
| a man, of himself | मी जातो | *mī jāto* |
| a woman, of herself | मी जाते | *mī jāte* |
| a man, "he goes" | तो जातो | *to jāto* |
| a woman, "she goes" | ती जाते | *tī jāte* |

The **-तो** / **-ते** swap is the whole mechanism, and it runs through every
verb: *bolto / bolte*, *yeto / yete*. Hindi marks gender on its verbs too, but
Marathi makes it unavoidable in the plainest sentence a beginner can build. From
your first "I go," you are choosing.

Notice also what is missing: no separate word for "am." The ending carries the
action, the person, and the gender at once.
