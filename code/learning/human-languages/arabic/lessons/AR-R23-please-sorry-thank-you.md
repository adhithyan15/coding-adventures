# Please, sorry, thank you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C05-NAAM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Say yes, please, sorry and thank you in Arabic, in that order, before
reading on. Four short words, each taught once, none asked for since.

## You'll want to know: three of the four bend
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C05-NAAM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-02, AR-CONCEPT-C07-ASIF-01, AR-CONCEPT-C07-ASIF-02] -->

| word | to a man | to a woman |
|---|---|---|
| please | **من فضلك** *min faḍlak* | **من فضلك** *min faḍlik* |
| sorry | **آسف** *āsif* | **آسفة** *āsifa* |
| yes | **نعم** *naʿam* | **نعم** *naʿam* |

*Naʿam* is the clear standard **yes**, safe anywhere, and it has spoken
neighbours you will also hear. *Min faḍlik* is the only one of the three whose
bend is **invisible**: the written form does not change at all, because only the
unwritten short vowel moves.

**آسف** is stranger than English *sorry*, because it is not a fixed word — it is
a **gendered adjective**, from the verb *asifa*, to grieve or regret. So a woman
apologising says a different word from a man. For a quick, ungendered *excuse me*,
Arabic reaches for **معذرة** *maʿdhira* instead.

## Script: the two letters inside شكرا
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-SHIN-KAF-20, AR-SCRIPT-BA-FAMILY-07] -->

**ش** *shīn* is not a new shape at all: it is **س** *sīn* with **three dots** on
top — the same trick the boat-bowl plays, where **ب** takes one dot below, **ت**
two above and **ث** three above. The dots do the work; the skeleton is shared.

The other new letter in the word is **ك** *kāf*.

## Writing: what changes when letters join
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-SCRIPT-JOINING-FORMS-04] -->

- [YOU WRITE: **سلام** right to left — *sīn · lām · alif · mīm*]
- [YOU WRITE: **شكرا** — *shīn* with its three dots, then *kāf*, *rāʾ*, *alif*]

What changes when a letter joins is the **connector at each edge**. What stays is
the **skeleton** — which is why a letter you learned standing alone is still
readable buried in the middle of a word.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CULTURE-SHUKRAN-22] -->

The reply to thanks is **عفوا** *ʿafwan* — *it is nothing*, *you are pardoned* —
from a root meaning to forgive or waive. English answers gratitude by accepting
it. Arabic answers by **waving it away**: you thank, and they erase the debt.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C07-ASIF-01, AR-CULTURE-SHUKRAN-22, AR-SCRIPT-SHIN-KAF-20, AR-CONCEPT-C06-MIN-FADLIK-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Give the two forms of *sorry* and what makes them differ. (**آسف** and
**آسفة** — a gendered adjective.) Give the reply to **شكرا** and its literal
force. (**عفوا** — it is nothing, you are pardoned.) Say what **ش** is made of.
(**س** plus three dots.) And say which courtesy word bends without the spelling
changing. (**من فضلك**.)
