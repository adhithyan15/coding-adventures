Lesson 1 of 5.
ごご (gogo) — the afternoon, p.m.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a nephew, then the Japanese for a niece.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — read: もういちど、おねがいします (Once more, please.) without stopping]

You'll want to know: ごご.
ごご — gogo — "the afternoon, p.m.".

The word, taken apart.
ごご (gogo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are go, "noon" (Middle Chinese nguX) and go, "after" (huwX) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

You'll want to know: よなか.
よなか — yonaka — "the middle of the night".

The word, taken apart.
よなか (yonaka) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: yo, "night", and naka, "middle", both kun'yomi of its kanji (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ゆうはん (yuuhan) — the evening meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the middle of the night, then the Japanese for early morning.

You'll want to know: ゆうはん.
ゆうはん — yuuhan — "the evening meal".

The word, taken apart.
ゆうはん (yuuhan) mixes the two layers: native yū, "evening", read as a kun'yomi, and han, "cooked rice; a meal", the on'yomi of its second character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese bjonX; JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
