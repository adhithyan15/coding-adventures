# keinen — for the thing you do *not* have

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-EINEN-01, GE-LEX-HABEN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-HABEN-REGULAR-01, GE-LEX-BRUDER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I have a brother" — **Ich habe einen Bruder**. That lesson
handed you *einen* as a single word and promised the system behind it would come
later. Some of it arrives now, and it costs nothing, because you are about to
watch a word you met yesterday do the identical thing.

## You'll want to know: keinen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-KEINEN-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-EINEN-01, GE-LEX-KEIN-01, GE-LEX-BRUDER-01] -->

> **keinen** — "no," "not a," for a *der*-word that is the thing being had.

> **Ich habe einen Bruder.** → **Ich habe keinen Bruder.**

The *-en* is the one from *einen*, unchanged. You are not learning a second
ending; you are watching the ending you already own ride along on the negative
word, because *kein* is *ein* with two letters welded to the front and nothing
else touched.

## Grammar Lens: three shapes, and the one that moves
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KEINEN-01, GE-LEX-KEIN-01, GE-LEX-KEINE-01, GE-LEX-HUND-02, GE-LEX-SCHWESTER-01, GE-LEX-BROT-01] -->

Line the three up against what they stand in front of and one thing jumps out:

| in front of | naming it | having it |
|---|---|---|
| a *der*-word (*Hund*) | **kein** Hund | **keinen** Hund |
| a *die*-word (*Schwester*) | **keine** Schwester | **keine** Schwester |
| a *das*-word (*Brot*) | **kein** Brot | **kein** Brot |

Only the *der* line changes. *Die* and *das* words look identical whether they
are the thing doing or the thing done to — which is why you have got this far
without meeting the question at all, and why *einen* could be handed over as a
plain word for twenty-three chapters.

That is the whole of what German asks of you here, and it is much less than the
reputation suggests: **one line out of three moves, and it is the *der* line.**
