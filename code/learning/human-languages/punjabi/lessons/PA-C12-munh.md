# ਮੂੰਹ — a consonant that wore all the way down to a tone

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-KANN, PA-CONTRAST-KANN-KARNA, PA-SOUND-TONE-HIGH-LEVEL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **ਚਾਹ**'s final **ਹ** was not really a consonant — it marked a
level high tone, because the word ended right there. This word ends the same
way, from a consonant that used to be something else entirely. And unlike
**ਕੰਨ**, which only *looks* like it hides **ਕਰਨਾ** inside it, this word hides
no false friend at all.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-MUNH]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਮੂੰਹ** — *mūnh* — **mouth**, also **face**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SOUND-TONE-HIGH-LEVEL] -->

**ਮ** *m*, **ੂ** long *ū*, **ੰ** the nasal *ṭippī*, **ਹ** — again not spoken as
*h*, marking the same level high tone you first read in **ਚਾਹ**: *mū́h̃*.

## The word, taken apart — kh wore down to h, then h wore down to nothing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-ETYMON-MUNH-MUKHA]; assesses=[PA-LEX-CHA, PA-LEX-KANN] -->

**mūnh** is inherited from Sanskrit **mukha**, "face, mouth" — Punjabi also
kept the same word a second time, whole, as the learned borrowing **ਮੁਖ**
*mukh*, the way it kept both *ਪਸੰਦ* and its own inherited relatives side by
side before.

On the inherited path, *mukha*'s middle **kh** did not simply vanish. Middle
Indo-Aryan first softened intervocalic *kh* down to a plain **h** — a
regular, well-worn move, nothing to do with the breathy-voiced letters that
gave you *bharā*'s low tone or *samajhṇā*'s falling one. Punjabi then lost
**that** *h* too, the same way it lost *cāh*'s Persian-final one, and let its
pitch land on the vowel before it: level and high, because — exactly as in
*cāh* — nothing follows it in the word.

So *mūnh* and *cāh* reach a level high tone from different histories — one
from a worn-down *kh*, one from a borrowed word's own *h* — and arrive at the
identical sound.
