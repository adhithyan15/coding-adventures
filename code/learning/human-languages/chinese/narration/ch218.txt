Mandarin Chinese, chapter 218: Wind and Warm Days.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
风衣 (fēngyī) — a windbreaker; a light coat against the wind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to ask around, then the Mandarin for to solve.

You'll want to know: 风衣.
风衣 — fēngyī — "a windbreaker; a light coat against the wind".
chūntiān fēng hěn dà wǒ chángcháng chuān fēngyī — It's windy in spring, so I often wear a windbreaker.
The new character, in pieces: 风 (fēng) — four strokes: a long stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns and hooks down to the right, and inside them a short stroke to the left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
风衣 (fēngyī) is built from 风 (fēng) 'wind' and 衣 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjuwng and 'j+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the first, after an older book, as the messenger of heaven and earth.

What you've built.

Script — a frame, and two short strokes inside.
风 (fēng).
Two pieces: a frame, then what sits inside it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The frame, two strokes. A long stroke falling to the left, from the top down to the foot. Then a bar from its top that runs right, turns and falls to the lower right, flicking up at its end.
Inside, two strokes. A short stroke falling to the left, then a dot falling to the right across it, like a small cross.
2 + 2 = four.

Lesson 3 of 6.
nuǎnhuo — pleasantly warm; mild.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to solve, then the Mandarin for a windbreaker.

You'll want to know: nuǎnhuo.
nuǎnhuo — "pleasantly warm; mild".
jīntiān hěn nuǎnhuo — It's lovely and warm today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nuǎnhuo is built from nuǎn 'warm' and huo 'mild', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nwanX and hwa (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first beside another character glossed 'warm', and glosses the second 'in harmony' and 'neither hard nor soft'; in the word the second loses its tone and its vowel changes.

Lesson 4 of 6.
mēnrè — hot and stuffy; muggy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a windbreaker, then the Mandarin for pleasantly warm.

You'll want to know: mēnrè.
mēnrè — "hot and stuffy; muggy".
jīntiān tài mēnrè le — It's so muggy today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mēnrè is built from mēn 'stifled' and rè 'hot', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mwonH and nyet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'vexed' and explains the second as burning, as if by fire. The first is listed with a departing tone, so the level tone of mēn is not the regular outcome.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gānzào is built from gān 'dry' and zào 'parched'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese kan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second, sawX there, with this very word, 'dry'.
