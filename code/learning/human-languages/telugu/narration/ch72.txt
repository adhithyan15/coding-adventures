Telugu, chapter 72: The Market.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) — a shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did alluḍu mean?

You'll want to know: అంగడి.
అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) — "a shop".
అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) is a shop — one counter, one family behind it, and everything a గ్రామం needs on the shelves.
It is inherited, and it is the older and homelier of the two words available. City Telugu will as often reach for a borrowed word for a store, but the అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) is the one at the end of the బాట.
You are known in an అంగడి (aṅgaḍi). Nobody there opens with a price; they open by asking whether your household is well.

What you've built.
One. The place the rest of this chapter happens in.

Lesson 2 of 5.
డబ్బు (ḍabbu) — money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did aṅgaḍi mean?

You'll want to know: డబ్బు.
డబ్బు (ḍabbu) — "money".
డబ్బు (ḍabbu) is money. You have said it once already without being told what it was: డబ్బు (ḍabbu) లేదు, there is no money, turned up early as an example of how లేదు denies that a thing exists. Here is the word itself.
It is a borrowing, from the name of a small copper coin, and it travelled across the region on the coin's back.
It is blunt and it is not rude. Asking whether somebody has డబ్బు (ḍabbu) today is an ordinary question in an అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) where the account is settled by the month.

What you've built.
Two. And one of them you had already used once.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ధర (dhara) — a price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ḍabbu mean?

You'll want to know: ధర.
ధర (dhara) — "a price".
ధర (dhara) is a price — what a thing costs, said out loud.
It is Sanskrit, from a sense of what holds a thing up, which is a fair picture of value. Telugu uses it for one thing's price and for prices in general: when people say the ధర (dhara) has gone up, they mean everything has.
Asking is direct and expected. Nobody in an అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) minds the question, and asking before you pick a thing up is politer than asking after.

What you've built.
Three. The question you are allowed to ask first.

Lesson 4 of 5.
తూకం (tūkaṁ) — weight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did dhara mean?

You'll want to know: తూకం.
తూకం (tūkaṁ) — "weight".
తూకం (tūkaṁ) is weight — what the scales say, and also the act of weighing.
Almost everything in an అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) is sold by it: బియ్యం, ఉప్పు, చక్కెర, మిరప. The ధర (dhara) you are quoted is a ధర (dhara) for a తూకం (tūkaṁ), so the two words travel together.
It carries fairness as well. Somebody who speaks with తూకం (tūkaṁ) is somebody whose words are measured, and short తూకం (tūkaṁ) is a serious accusation.

What you've built.
Four. What the ధర (dhara) is attached to.

Lesson 5 of 5.
బేరం (bēraṁ) — a bargain, haggling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did tūkaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: బేరం.
బేరం (bēraṁ) — "a bargain, haggling".
బేరం (bēraṁ) is the haggle — the back and forth between the ధర (dhara) asked and the ధర (dhara) paid, and also the deal that comes out of it.
It is expected in an open market crowd and out of place in a fixed-price అంగడి (aṅgaḍi), and telling those two apart is most of what a newcomer gets wrong.
However the బేరం (bēraṁ) ends, it ends with ధన్యవాదములు, and in a small అంగడి (aṅgaḍi) it ends with both sides fairly sure they did well out of it.

What you've built.
Five: అంగడి (aṅgaḍi), డబ్బు (ḍabbu), ధర (dhara), తూకం (tūkaṁ), బేరం (bēraṁ). Enough to buy something and thank the person who sold it.
