Arabic, chapter 322: Castles and walls.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قلعة (qalʿa) — a castle, a citadel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to rob, then the Arabic for to loot.

You'll want to know: قلعة.
قلعة — qalʿa — "a castle, a citadel".
صعدنا إلى قلعة على الجبل. ṣaʿidnā ilā qalʿa ʿalā al-jabal. We climbed up to a castle on the mountain.
Plural: قلاع, qilāʿ.

The word, taken apart.
قلعة (qalʿa) is usually filed under the native Arabic root q-l-ʿ, "to pull out by the roots" (Lane). Lane's article does not give the castle sense, and how a fortress took the name is not settled.

بنى الجنود حصنا قرب البحر. banā al-junūd ḥiṣnan qurba al-baḥr. The soldiers built a fort near the sea.

The word, taken apart.
حصن (ḥiṣn) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-ṣ-n (Lane), "to be inaccessible, well guarded"; the taught ḥiṣān, a horse, is the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
سور (sūr) — a city wall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a castle, then the Arabic for a fort.

You'll want to know: سور.
سور — sūr — "a city wall".
حول المدينة القديمة سور طويل. ḥawla al-madīna al-qadīma sūr ṭawīl. There is a long wall round the old city.
Plural: أسوار, aswār.
A wall round a garden or a school is also سور (sūr); the wall of a room is جدار.

The word, taken apart.
سور (sūr) is built on the native Arabic root s-w-r (Lane), where Lane gives sūr as the wall of a town, and sāra, to climb over it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a fort, then the Arabic for a city wall.

You'll want to know: حاصر.
حاصر — ḥāṣara — "to besiege, to surround".
حاصر الجيش القلعة شهرا. ḥāṣara al-jaysh al-qalʿa shahran. The army besieged the castle for a month.
Present: يحاصر, yuḥāṣir.

The word, taken apart.
حاصر (ḥāṣara) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-ṣ-r (Lane), "to confine, to hem in". It is Form III, "to hem in an enemy".

Lesson 5 of 5.
دافع (dāfaʿa) — to defend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a city wall, then the Arabic for to besiege.

You'll want to know: دافع.
دافع — dāfaʿa — "to defend".
دافع الجنود عن المدينة. dāfaʿa al-junūd ʿan al-madīna. The soldiers defended the city.
Present: يدافع, yudāfiʿ.
It takes عن: دافع (dāfaʿa) عن صديقه.

The word, taken apart.
دافع (dāfaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root d-f-ʿ (Lane), "to push", the root of the taught dafaʿa. It is Form III, "to push back against someone": to defend.
