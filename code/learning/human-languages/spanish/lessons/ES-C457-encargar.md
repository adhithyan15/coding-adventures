# encargar — ask them to get it in

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-02, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el cargo** and **el pedido**. Put *en-* on the front of the
first and you get a verb.

## You'll want to know: encargar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-01] -->

**encargar** — to order something in, to ask for a thing to be got.

*¿Me lo puede encargar?* — can you order it in for me? *Lo tengo encargado* —
it's on order. *Encargar una tarta* — to order a cake.

With **-se de** it means to take charge of something: *yo me encargo de la
comida* — I'll see to the food. *De eso se encarga la empresa* — the company
handles that.

## Grammar Lens: encargar is not pedir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-01] -->

| what is happening | the verb |
|---|---|
| asking for what is there | **pedir** |
| asking them to **get** what is not | **encargar** |

In a restaurant you *pides* a salad, because it is on the menu. At a chemist's
you *encargas* a medicine, because it has to be brought in. *Pedir* takes
something off a list; *encargar* creates a job for somebody.

That is also why *encargarse de* means taking responsibility: the load has been
put on you.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C457-GUARDIA-02, ES-LEX-C456-CUENTA-02] -->

**en-** plus **cargar**, and *cargar* is the *carricare* from the lesson on
**el cargo** — to load a cart.

So *encargar* is to **load a task onto somebody**, and every sense follows
from that one picture. Order a cake: the baker now carries it. Take charge:
you are carrying it. *El encargado* — the person in charge — is the one with
the load on.

English has the same metaphor in *charge*: you are *in charge*, you *take
charge*, somebody is *charged with* a duty. Two languages reached for a cart.
