## You'll want to know: yǒushì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C95-YOUSHI-01]; assesses=[] -->

*yǒushì* — "to have something on; to be busy".

> *wǒ jīntiān yǒushì* — I've got something on today.

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*yǒushì* is built from yǒu 'to have', the 有 you write, and shì 'a matter'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *hjuwX* and *dzriH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun glosses *dzriH* 'to employ; to set up', and 'a matter' is the everyday sense, not the gloss.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-MANG-01] -->

*yǒushì* is the polite reason that gives nothing away: something has come up. *máng* says you are busy; *yǒushì* says there is a particular thing.
