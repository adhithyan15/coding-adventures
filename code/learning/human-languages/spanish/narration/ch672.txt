Spanish, chapter 672: Carteles y flechas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el indicador — the signpost, the sign; the gauge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for forward, then the Spanish for the detour.

You'll want to know: el indicador.
el indicador — "the signpost, the sign; the gauge".
Sigue los indicadores hacia el centro. Follow the signs for the centre.
In a car, el indicador also shows the fuel or the speed.

The word, taken apart.
indicador is built on indicar "to point out", a learned borrowing from Latin indicāre (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

You'll want to know: el letrero.
el letrero — "the sign (with words), the notice".
El letrero de la tienda es azul. The shop sign is blue.
Close to el cartel; a letrero is the one made of letters.

The word, taken apart.
letrero is built on letra "letter" with -ero (EtymDB), and letra is inherited from Latin littera.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the signpost, then the Spanish for the sign.

You'll want to know: la flecha.
la flecha — "the arrow".
Sigue las flechas amarillas. Follow the yellow arrows.
On a map or a sign, the arrow shows the way.

The word, taken apart.
flecha is usually traced to French flèche "arrow", a word of Germanic origin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la señalización — the signs, the signposting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sign, then the Spanish for the arrow.

You'll want to know: la señalización.
la señalización — "the signs, the signposting".
La señalización de la ciudad es muy clara. The signposting in the city is very clear.
All the road signs together.

The word, taken apart.
señalización is built on señalizar (EtymDB), from señal "sign", inherited from Late Latin signāle, from signum "a mark".

Lesson 5 of 5.
el desvío — the diversion, the detour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the arrow, then the Spanish for the signs.

You'll want to know: el desvío.
el desvío — "the diversion, the detour".
Hay un desvío por obras. There is a diversion because of roadworks.
The verb is desviar, to divert.

The word, taken apart.
desvío is built on desviar "to divert", from Late Latin dēviāre "to leave the road", built on via "road".
