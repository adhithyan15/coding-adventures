# donc, alors — the arrow that points forward

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PARCEQUE-01, FR-GRAM-JE-PENSE-QUE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give an opinion with a reason: "I think it's going to rain,
because there's no sun." (*Je pense qu'il va pleuvoir, parce qu'il n'y a pas
de soleil.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Parce que* runs from the claim back to its reason. This lesson
runs the other way.

## Grammar Lens: the two arrows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-DEUX-FLECHES-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C158-POURQUOI158-01, FR-LEX-C106-TRAJET58-02, FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01] -->

The same two facts, in opposite orders:

> J'ai pris le métro **parce qu'**il pleuvait. — I took the metro
> **because** it was raining.
> Il pleuvait, **donc** j'ai pris le métro. — It was raining, **so** I took
> the metro.

**Parce que** points **back**, from what happened to its cause. **Donc**
points **forward**, from the cause to what followed, which is why the cause
has to come first. Which one you reach for depends on which fact you want
to say first: a choice about emphasis, not about grammar.

The tenses keep their jobs. *Il pleuvait* is the scene, the reason; *j'ai
pris* is the event, the consequence.

## Grammar Lens: alors, the chatty one
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-ALORS-DONC-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C187-TEMPS187-01] -->

**alors** can stand in the same place: *Il pleuvait, **alors** j'ai pris le
métro.* It is lighter and more conversational, the "so" of a friend telling
you about their day. **Donc** is the neutral one, as at home in a letter as
in speech.

> chatty: *alors* · neutral: *donc*

Both point forward. *Alors* also keeps its first meaning, "then, at that
time", so in a story it can do both jobs at once. And when *donc* opens a
clause, its *c* is usually said: *Donc, je reste.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**alors** is *à* plus Old French *lors*, "then", which goes back to Latin
*illā hōrā*, "at that hour" (EtymDB 2.1): a word about time that became a
word about consequence, as English "then" did. **donc** is usually traced to
Late Latin *dunc*, "then, so", whose own making is debated; EtymDB 2.1
follows it only as far as Middle French *donc*.

que* **back** to the cause; *donc* **forward** to the consequence.) Which of

*illā hōrā* mean? (**At that hour.**)
