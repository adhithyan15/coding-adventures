## You'll want to know: tiānqì yùbào
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C147-TIANQIYUBAO-01]; assesses=[] -->

*tiānqì yùbào* — "weather forecast".

> *tiānqì yùbào shuō míngtiān huì xiàyǔ* — The forecast says it'll rain tomorrow.

Its last two characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*tiānqì yùbào* is built from 天气 'weather' and yùbào. yùbào is built from yù and bào, inherited from Middle Chinese *yoH* and *pawH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'beforehand' and 'to report'. A report given beforehand is the plain reading of the two; 'forecast' for the whole is the modern compound's own sense.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-HUI-FUTURE-01, ZH-LEX-C147-HOUTIAN-01, ZH-LEX-C66-WEATHER66-01] -->

*tiānqì yùbào shuō …*, the forecast says, and then the forecast itself, with 会:

> *tiānqì yùbào shuō hòutiān bú huì xiàyǔ* — The forecast says it won't rain the day after tomorrow.
