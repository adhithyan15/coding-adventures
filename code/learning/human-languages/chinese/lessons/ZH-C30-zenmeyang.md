## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-ZENMEYANG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*zěnmeyàng* — **a dip, a light syllable, then a fall.**

*zěn* is third tone. *me* is the same toneless syllable that ends 什么. *yàng* is
fourth tone, and it carries the weight of the word.

> *nǐ zěnmeyàng* — How are you doing?

Say it once slowly and once at speed: **zěn me yàng**.

## Why it's said this way — two ways to ask
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-ZENMEYANG-01, ZH-LEX-NIHAOMA-01] -->

You already have **你好吗** *nǐ hǎo ma*, and it is correct. It is the question
textbooks open with.

Between people who know each other, the one you will hear far more often is
*nǐ zěnmeyàng*: "how are things with you?" On its own, *zěnmeyàng* asks how
something is, and putting 你 in front makes the something you.

So keep both. 你好吗 is the careful one; *nǐ zěnmeyàng* is the one a friend asks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Mandarin builds *zěnmeyàng* from three syllables. *me* is the light 么 from
什么, a character you know. The characters for *zěn* and *yàng* come later; this
chapter keeps them to the ear.

No dictionary we use records an older form of *zěn* or *yàng*, so the history of
the word is not settled, and where 么 comes from is not settled either.
