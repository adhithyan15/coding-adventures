Japanese, chapter 249: Heart and Character.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
こころ (kokoro) — the heart; the mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a secret, then the Japanese for to doubt.

You'll want to know: こころ.
こころ — kokoro — "the heart; the mind".
そぼは こころが やさしいですか。 — sobo wa kokoro ga yasashii desu ka — "Is my grandmother kind-hearted?"

The word, taken apart.
こころ (kokoro) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: きもち.
きもち — kimochi — "a feeling; a mood".
かのじょの きもちが わかりますか。 — kanojo no kimochi ga wakarimasu ka — "Do you understand her feelings?"

The word, taken apart.
きもち (kimochi) is a mixed word: Sino-Japanese ki, "spirit" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), borrowed from Chinese, joined to native mochi, "holding", from motsu (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 7.
せいかく (seikaku) — personality; character.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the heart, then the Japanese for a feeling.

You'll want to know: せいかく.
せいかく — seikaku — "personality; character".
かれは せいかくが いいですか。 — kare wa seikaku ga ii desu ka — "Does he have a good personality?"

The word, taken apart.
せいかく (seikaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sei, "nature" (Middle Chinese sjengH) and kaku, "status, rank" (kaek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

まじめ — majime — "serious; earnest".
こうはいは しごとに まじめですか。 — kōhai wa shigoto ni majime desu ka — "Is the junior serious about work?"

The word, taken apart.
まじめ (majime) is written with three characters meaning "true", "face" and "eye", read as a whole word; its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 7.
わがまま (wagamama) — selfish; wilful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for personality, then the Japanese for serious.

You'll want to know: わがまま.
わがまま — wagamama — "selfish; wilful".
あの こどもは わがままですか。 — ano kodomo wa wagamama desu ka — "Is that child selfish?"

The word, taken apart.
わがまま (wagamama) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from waga, "one's own", and mama, "as it is", and it is usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
