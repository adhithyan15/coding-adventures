Italian, chapter 519: A teatro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il palcoscenico — stage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for verse, then the Italian for to hum.

You'll want to know: il palcoscenico.
il palcoscenico — "stage".
Gli attori salgono sul palcoscenico. The actors go up on stage.
Often shortened to il palco.

The word, taken apart.
palcoscenico is built from palco "platform", usually traced to a Germanic word for a beam (EtymDB lists it beside German Balken), and scenico "of the stage", from Latin scaena, from Greek skēnḗ "a tent, a stage" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
il sipario — curtain (in a theatre).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to hum, then the Italian for stage.

You'll want to know: il sipario.
il sipario — "curtain (in a theatre)".
Si alza il sipario e comincia lo spettacolo. The curtain rises and the show begins.
Only for the theatre; at home, la tenda.

The word, taken apart.
sipario comes from Latin sīparium "a stage curtain" (EtymDB), from Greek síparos "a topsail".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la platea — stalls; audience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for stage, then the Italian for curtain.

You'll want to know: la platea.
la platea — "stalls; audience".
Abbiamo i posti in platea, in quinta fila. We have seats in the stalls, in the fifth row.
Also the audience as a whole.

The word, taken apart.
platea is a learned borrowing from Latin platēa "a broad street, a courtyard" (EtymDB), from Greek plateîa "broad".

Lesson 4 of 5.
il loggione — gallery, the gods (top seats).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for curtain, then the Italian for stalls.

You'll want to know: il loggione.
il loggione — "gallery, the gods (top seats)".
Dal loggione si vede poco, ma costa meno. From the gods you see little, but it costs less.
The cheap seats at the top; the people there are i loggionisti.

The word, taken apart.
loggione is a big loggia "a gallery", which came from Old French loge, from Frankish laubija "a pergola" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'applauso — applause, round of applause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for stalls, then the Italian for gallery.

You'll want to know: l'applauso.
l'applauso — "applause, round of applause".
Alla fine c'è stato un lungo applauso. At the end there was long applause.
Fare un applauso — to give a round of applause.

The word, taken apart.
applauso is a learned borrowing from Latin applausus "a clapping" (EtymDB), from applaudere "to strike upon, to clap".
