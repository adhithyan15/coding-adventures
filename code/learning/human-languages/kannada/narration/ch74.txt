You'll want to know: ಎರಡನೆಯ.
ಎರಡನೆಯ (eraḍaneya) — "second".
ಒಂದು, ಎರಡು, ಮೂರು answer how many. This word answers which one in the line, and Kannada keeps a second set of words for that job.
It goes straight in front of what it counts, and nothing on the noun changes: ಎರಡನೆಯ ದಿನ (eraḍaneya dina), the second day; ಎರಡನೆಯ ಬಾಗಿಲು (eraḍaneya bāgilu), the second door.

Grammar Lens: the ending, and why the chapter opens on two.
Look at the word against the number it comes from:
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
cardinal: ಎರಡು (eraḍu). ordinal: ಎರಡನೆಯ (eraḍaneya).
The final -ು goes, and -ಅನೆಯ (-aneya) goes on. That is the rule, and in Kannada it has no exceptions at all — every number you know already takes it.
So the chapter does not open on one. It opens on two, because eraḍu is the first number that shows the ending working, and because the one word that will surprise you is first — which is not built on ಒಂದು at all. An exception is only worth looking at once you can see what it is an exception to.

You'll want to know: ಮೂರನೆಯ.
ಮೂರನೆಯ (mūraneya) — "third".
You have already met the machine that made it. ಮೂರು loses its final -ು and takes -ಅನೆಯ:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
cardinal: ಎರಡು (eraḍu). ordinal: ಎರಡನೆಯ (eraḍaneya).
cardinal: ಮೂರು (mūru). ordinal: ಮೂರನೆಯ (mūraneya).
ಮೂರನೆಯ ದಿನ (mūraneya dina), the third day. ಮೂರನೆಯ ಮರ (mūraneya mara), the third tree.
This is the second word and it needed nothing new. That is what an exceptionless ending buys you: from here on, a number you can say is a position you can say.

You'll want to know: ಮೊದಲನೆಯ.
ಮೊದಲನೆಯ (modalaneya) — "first".
The ending is the one you already have. What is new is the word underneath it, because there is no ಒಂದು anywhere in modalaneya — and there was never going to be.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
cardinal: ಒಂದು (ondu). ordinal: ಮೊದಲನೆಯ (modalaneya).
cardinal: ಎರಡು (eraḍu). ordinal: ಎರಡನೆಯ (eraḍaneya).
cardinal: ಮೂರು (mūru). ordinal: ಮೂರನೆಯ (mūraneya).
ಮೊದಲನೆಯ ದಿನ (modalaneya dina), the first day. ಮೊದಲನೆಯ ದಾರಿ (modalaneya dāri), the first road.

Across the family: ಮೊದಲು, "a beginning".
Under it sits ಮೊದಲು (modalu), "a beginning" — so the word means "of the beginning", not "one-th".
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
language: Kannada. "one": ondu. "first" is built on: ಮೊದಲು modalu, a beginning.
language: Tamil. "one": oṉṟu. "first" is built on: முதல் mudal, a beginning.
language: Telugu. "one": okaṭi. "first" is built on: మొదలు modalu, a beginning.
Three sisters, three different words for one, and the same word for the start of a line. Counting and ordering were never the same idea in this family, and first is where the seam shows.
Note what is and is not irregular here. The ending is perfectly regular — modalu drops its final -ು and takes -ಅನೆಯ exactly like eraḍu did. It is only the stem that is a surprise. Kannada asks you to learn one extra word, not one extra rule.

ನಾಲ್ಕನೆಯ (nālkaneya) — "fourth".
ನಾಲ್ಕು was the first word in this book to carry a stacked consonant, the ಲ್ಕ riding under the line. The ending does not disturb it: the stack stays exactly where it was and -ಅನೆಯ is added after it.

You'll want to know: ಐದನೆಯ.
ಐದನೆಯ (aidaneya) — "fifth". ಐದು drops its -ು, takes -ಅನೆಯ, and the set is complete.
ಐದನೆಯ ದಿನ (aidaneya dina), the fifth day.

What you've built.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
1. cardinal: ಒಂದು ondu. ordinal: ಮೊದಲನೆಯ modalaneya.
2. cardinal: ಎರಡು eraḍu. ordinal: ಎರಡನೆಯ eraḍaneya.
3. cardinal: ಮೂರು mūru. ordinal: ಮೂರನೆಯ mūraneya.
4. cardinal: ನಾಲ್ಕು nālku. ordinal: ನಾಲ್ಕನೆಯ nālkaneya.
5. cardinal: ಐದು aidu. ordinal: ಐದನೆಯ aidaneya.
Count the work. Four of those five were built, from numbers you had learned when you learned to count and an ending you learned in one lesson. Exactly one, modalaneya, was a word to learn — and even it took the regular ending.
English asks you to learn first, second, third before the pattern starts; Latin asks for ten separate words. Kannada asks for one, and it is not a number word at all.
