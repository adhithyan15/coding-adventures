# ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു — right now, I am going

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C104-ONE-PRESENT-01, ML-LEX-C50-LEAVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I am going*. Then say the word for **now**.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C104-NOW-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C50-LEAVE-01] -->

The last lesson left one thing open: if **ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു** can mean either, how do
you say you mean **this moment**?

**You put the moment in front of it.**

> **ഇപ്പോൾ ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു.** — *ippōḷ ñān pōkunnu* — "**Right now** I am going."

**The verb did not change.** Not one letter of it. What changed is that a word
naming the moment is standing at the front, and it is a word you have owned for
a long while.

## Grammar Lens: the slot again
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C104-NOW-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C104-ONE-PRESENT-01] -->

That front position is not new either. **ഇന്ന്** has stood there, and **നാളെ**,
and a named day, and an hour with an ending on it. **ഇപ്പോൾ** is the same slot
doing the narrowest version of its job: not today, not this Monday — *now*.

**This is how Malayalam answers a question English answers with the verb.**
English changes *go* into *am going*. Malayalam leaves the verb alone and adds a
word — and a word is easier than a form.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C104-NOW-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C104-ONE-PRESENT-01] -->

happened to the verb? (**Nothing** — it is unchanged.) Where did **ഇപ്പോൾ** go?
(**At the front**, in the slot that says *when*.)
