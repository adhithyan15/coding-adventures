French, chapter 271: Peu à peu.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
peu à peu — little by little.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for at the time, then the French for straight after.

You'll want to know: peu à peu.
peu à peu — "little by little".
Peu à peu, la ville s'est réveillée. Little by little, the town woke up.
The same as petit à petit.

The word, taken apart.
peu is inherited from Latin paucum "a little", the neuter of paucus "few" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
progressivement — gradually, progressively.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for straight after, then the French for little by little.

You'll want to know: progressivement.
progressivement — "gradually, progressively".
Il fait progressivement plus chaud. It is gradually getting warmer.
Built on progressif; a neutral word, common in reports.

The word, taken apart.
progressivement is built on progressif, a learned borrowing from Latin prōgressīvus (EtymDB), built on prōgressus "a going forward", from prōgredī "to step forward" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
au fur et à mesure — as one goes along, bit by bit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for little by little, then the French for gradually.

You'll want to know: au fur et à mesure.
au fur et à mesure — "as one goes along, bit by bit".
Il apprend la langue au fur et à mesure. He learns the language as he goes along.
With a verb: au fur et à mesure que: au fur et à mesure qu'on avance.

The word, taken apart.
mesure is inherited from Latin mēnsūra "a measuring" (EtymDB). Fur is an old word for "rate, price", which EtymDB traces to Latin forum "market place".

Lesson 4 of 5.
à la longue — in the long run, eventually.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for gradually, then the French for as one goes along.

You'll want to know: à la longue.
à la longue — "in the long run, eventually".
À la longue, on s'habitue au bruit. In the long run, you get used to the noise.
Here la longue is a noun: a long stretch of time.

The word, taken apart.
longue is the feminine of long, inherited from Latin longus "long" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
tôt ou tard — sooner or later.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for as one goes along, then the French for in the long run.

You'll want to know: tôt ou tard.
tôt ou tard — "sooner or later".
Tôt ou tard, il va comprendre. Sooner or later, he is going to understand.
The order is fixed: tôt first.

The word, taken apart.
tard is inherited from Latin tardē "slowly, late" (Lewis & Short), from tardus "slow" (EtymDB); tôt is usually traced to Latin tostum "scorched".
