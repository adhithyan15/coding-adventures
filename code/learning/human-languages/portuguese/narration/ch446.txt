Portuguese, chapter 446: Festas da terra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o arraial — the open-air party (at a festival).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to collapse, then the Portuguese for to knock down.

You'll want to know: o arraial.
o arraial — "the open-air party (at a festival)".
No verão, há arraiais em todos os bairros. In summer, there are street parties in every neighbourhood.
In June the city quarters fill with arraiais, music and grilled sardines.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of arraial is not settled; it first meant a military camp and is usually linked with real 'army camp', but EtymDB has no entry for it.

a romaria — "the pilgrimage festival, the pilgrimage".
A aldeia inteira vai à romaria. The whole village goes to the pilgrimage festival.
A trip to a chapel, then food, music and dancing.

romaria is built on romeiro 'pilgrim' with -ia (EtymDB lists romaria without giving a source).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a procissão.
a procissão — "the procession".
A procissão passa pela rua principal. The procession goes along the main street.
A procissão ainda vai no adro — it is early days yet.

The word, taken apart.
procissão comes from Latin prōcessiō 'marching forward, religious procession' (EtymDB), from prōcēdere 'to go forward'.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: o padroeiro.
o padroeiro — "the patron saint, the patron".
A festa do padroeiro é em agosto. The patron saint's festival is in August.
Feminine: a padroeira.

The word, taken apart.
padroeiro goes back to Latin patrōnus 'protector' (EtymDB has patrono), built on pater 'father'.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: desfilar.
desfilar — "to parade, to march past".
As bandas desfilam pela avenida. The bands parade along the avenue.
Models desfilam on a catwalk too.

The word, taken apart.
desfilar was borrowed from French défiler 'to march in file', built on fil 'thread', from Latin fīlum (Lewis & Short).
