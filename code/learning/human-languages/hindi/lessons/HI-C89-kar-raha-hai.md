# कर रहा है — two words doing two different jobs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I am doing*. Now point at somebody else and change only what has
to change.

## You'll want to know: कर रहा है
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03]; assesses=[] -->

**वह कर रहा है।** — He is doing (it).
**वह कर रही है।** — She is doing (it).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02] -->

Only the **last** word moved when the subject changed from *I* to *he*:

| subject | middle | last |
|---|---|---|
| मैं | रहा / रही | हूँ |
| वह | रहा / रही | है |

Two independent dials. The middle word answers *what is the subject like* and
the last word answers *which person is it*. Turning one does nothing to the
other, which is why you can build any combination without a table to memorise.

## Grammar Lens: it works on things as well as people
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-02] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| वह जा रहा है। | He is going. |
| वह आ रही है। | She is coming. |

A noun that is not a person still has a gender, and the middle word follows the
noun rather than any person. That is how the weather sentence you learned long
ago was put together — the rain is feminine, so the middle word ended in **-ई**
— and the review lesson takes it apart.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-03, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which word answers *which person*? (The last one.) Which answers
*what is the subject like*? (The middle one.) Do the two ever interfere? (No —
they are separate dials.)
