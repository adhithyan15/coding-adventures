Russian, chapter 525: Poems and Rhymes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
поэт (poét) — a poet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a novella, then the Russian for memoirs.

You'll want to know: поэт.
поэт (poét) — "a poet".
Этот поэт писал о любви и природе. Étot poét pisál o lyubví i priróde. This poet wrote about love and nature.
Masculine; feminine поэтесса, though many women prefer поэт (poét).

The word, taken apart.
поэт (poét) was borrowed through German Poet from Latin poēta, which comes from Greek poiētḗs 'a maker, a poet' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
стихотворение (stikhotvoréniye) — a poem.

You'll want to know: стихотворение.
стихотворение (stikhotvoréniye) — "a poem".
В школе мы учили это стихотворение. V shkóle my uchíli éto stikhotvoréniye. We learnt this poem at school.
Neuter. Стихи — verse, poems.

The word, taken apart.
стихотворение (stikhotvoréniye) is a compound of стих 'a line of verse' and творить 'to create'; EtymDB has no entry.

Feminine.

Lesson 4 of 5.
рифмовать (rifmovát) — to rhyme.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a poem, then the Russian for a rhyme.

You'll want to know: рифмовать.
рифмовать (rifmovát) — "to rhyme".
Мой сын любит рифмовать слова. Moy syn lyúbit rifmovát slová. My son likes making words rhyme.
Imperfective; present рифмую. Рифмоваться — to rhyme (of words).

The word, taken apart.
рифмовать (rifmovát) is built on рифма (rífma) 'a rhyme' with -овать; EtymDB has no entry, and рифма (rífma) is usually traced to French rime.

Lesson 5 of 5.
наизусть (naizúst) — by heart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a rhyme, then the Russian for to rhyme.

You'll want to know: наизусть.
наизусть (naizúst) — "by heart".
Бабушка знает наизусть много стихов. Bábushka znáyet naizúst mnógo stikhóv. Grandma knows a lot of poems by heart.
Adverb. Учить наизусть (naizúst) — to learn by heart.

The word, taken apart.
наизусть (naizúst) is usually explained as на, из and the old word уста 'lips, mouth', so it means 'from the mouth'; EtymDB has no entry for наизусть (naizúst), and уста is inherited from Proto-Slavic usta (EtymDB).
