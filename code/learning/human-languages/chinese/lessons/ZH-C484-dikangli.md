## You'll want to know: dǐkànglì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C484-WELL484-05]; assesses=[] -->

*dǐkànglì* — "resistance to illness".

> *chángcháng duànliàn de rén dǐkànglì bǐ biérén hǎo* — People who exercise regularly fight off illness better than others.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǐkànglì* is built from *dǐ* 'to push against', *kàng* 'to resist' and 力 'strength', all inherited from Middle Chinese, *tejX*, *khangH* and *lik* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to push' and lists the second under two readings, glossing *khangH* as lifting with the hand.
