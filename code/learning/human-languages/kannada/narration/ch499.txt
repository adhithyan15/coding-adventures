Kannada, chapter 499: Changing Moods.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊ (munisiko) — to sulk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for compassion, then the Kannada for sympathy.

You'll want to know: ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊ.
ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊ — munisiko — "to sulk".
ತಿಂಡಿ ಸಿಗಲಿಲ್ಲ ಅಂತ ತಂಗಿ ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊಂಡಳು. tiṇḍi sigalilla anta taṅgi munisikoṇḍaḷu. My little sister sulked because she did not get a snack.
Past (I …): ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊಂಡೆ, munisikoṇḍe.
With whom: … ಮೇಲೆ ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊ (munisiko). ಮುನಿಸು is a sulk.

The word, taken apart.
ಮುನಿಸಿಕೊ (munisiko) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5021 lists Kannada muni, "to become angry", and munisu, "anger"; -ko adds "for oneself", so the anger is turned inward.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಮುಗುಳ್ನಗು (muguḷnagu) — to smile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for sympathy, then the Kannada for to sulk.

You'll want to know: ಮುಗುಳ್ನಗು.
ಮುಗುಳ್ನಗು — muguḷnagu — "to smile".
ಹಳೆಯ ಫೋಟೋ ನೋಡಿ ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಮುಗುಳ್ನಕ್ಕರು. haḷeya phōṭō nōḍi ajji muguḷnakkaru. Looking at the old photo, grandmother smiled.
Past (I …): ಮುಗುಳ್ನಕ್ಕೆ, muguḷnakke.
ಮುಗುಳು, a bud + ನಗು, to laugh: a laugh still in the bud. The noun is ಮುಗುಳ್ನಗೆ, a smile.

The word, taken apart.
ಮುಗುಳ್ನಗು (muguḷnagu) is a native Dravidian compound: muguḷ, "a bud; to close like a bud" (DEDR 4893), and nagu, "to laugh" (DEDR 3569).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಹಿಂಜರಿ (hiñjari) — to hesitate; to hold back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to sulk, then the Kannada for to smile.

You'll want to know: ಹಿಂಜರಿ.
ಹಿಂಜರಿ — hiñjari — "to hesitate; to hold back".
ಸಹಾಯ ಕೇಳಲು ಅವನು ಹಿಂಜರಿದನು. sahāya kēḷalu avanu hiñjaridanu. He hesitated to ask for help.
Past (I …): ಹಿಂಜರಿದೆ, hiñjaride.
What you shrink from takes the -ಅಲು form: ಕೇಳಲು ಹಿಂಜರಿ (hiñjari), to hesitate to ask.

The word, taken apart.
ಹಿಂಜರಿ (hiñjari) is native Kannada, built on hin-, "back, behind" (DEDR 4205), as in ಹಿಂದೆ; the origin of the second part is not settled. The picture is stepping back.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು (hoṭṭekiccu) — envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to smile, then the Kannada for to hesitate.

You'll want to know: ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು.
ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು — hoṭṭekiccu — "envy".
ಸ್ನೇಹಿತನ ಹೊಸ ಕಾರು ನೋಡಿ ಅವನಿಗೆ ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು ಆಯಿತು. snēhitana hosa kāru nōḍi avanige hoṭṭekiccu āyitu. Seeing his friend's new car, he felt envious.
Literally belly-fire: ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ, belly + ಕಿಚ್ಚು, fire. Plainer and more everyday than ಅಸೂಯೆ.

The word, taken apart.
ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು (hoṭṭekiccu) is a native Dravidian compound: hoṭṭe, "belly" (DEDR 4494), and kiccu, "fire" (DEDR 1514). Envy burns in the stomach.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಆತ್ಮವಿಶ್ವಾಸ (ātmaviśvāsa) — self-confidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to hesitate, then the Kannada for envy.

You'll want to know: ಆತ್ಮವಿಶ್ವಾಸ.
ಆತ್ಮವಿಶ್ವಾಸ — ātmaviśvāsa — "self-confidence".
ತರಬೇತಿಯ ನಂತರ ಅವಳಿಗೆ ಆತ್ಮವಿಶ್ವಾಸ ಬಂದಿತು. tarabētiya nantara avaḷige ātmaviśvāsa banditu. After the training she gained self-confidence.
ಆತ್ಮ, self + ವಿಶ್ವಾಸ, trust: trust in yourself.

The word, taken apart.
ಆತ್ಮವಿಶ್ವಾಸ (ātmaviśvāsa) is a learned compound built from Sanskrit: ātman, "self", and viśvāsa, "trust, confidence" (CDIAL 11966).
