Punjabi, chapter 25: Requests, People, and Body Words at R4.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
Water and tea requests return at R4 — retrieve water and tea requests with their old source and tone cues at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 6 seconds]
Ask for water, then tea, using the same known polite close each time.

Guided Practice.
Two remembered routes.
Read ਪਾਣੀ, ਚਾਹ once. This is recognition and spoken retrieval, not a writing checkpoint.
[question — say your answer, then pause 40 seconds]
Say Water, please and Tea, please in Punjabi. Keep cāh high and level rather than pronouncing a final h consonant.
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Which drink is inherited from the old root meaning drink, and which came overland from Chinese through Persian?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Make the two polite requests once more from meaning.


Lesson 2 of 4.
Milk and bread return at R4 — retrieve milk and bread with honest source limits at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Hear dudh, roṭī and say “milk, bread” aloud.

Guided Practice.
Read ਦੁੱਧ, ਰੋਟੀ once. No copying is requested, and no independent writing evidence is awarded.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say the meanings aloud in order: dudh / roṭī.
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Which word descends from dugdha, that which has been milked, and which is inherited from roṭikā while its deeper root remains unsettled?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Ask for either one using the already-known kirpā karke.


Lesson 3 of 4.
Friend and family return at R4 — retrieve friend and family with their distinct source stories at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
Say dost, “friend,” and parivār, “family.”

Guided Practice.
Read ਦੋਸਤ, ਪਰਿਵਾਰ once. The models support reading only; do not count them as independent writing.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say the meanings aloud in order: dost / parivār.
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Match the old images: the chosen one from Persian; pari around plus the root cover or surround.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say “friend; family” from meaning alone.


Lesson 4 of 4.
Brother, sister, and eye return at R4 — retrieve brother, sister, and eye with tone and doubling contrasts at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 7 seconds]
Say bharā, bhaiṇ, and akkh from memory: brother, sister, eye. Keep the first two low; hold the doubled consonant in the third.

Guided Practice.
Family trees.
Read ਭਰਾ, ਭੈਣ, ਅੱਖ once. This lesson deliberately stops at reading and speech; it does not award independent Gurmukhi writing evidence.
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Say brother / sister / eye in Punjabi. Which two begin with the already-taught low-tone outcome, and which keeps a real doubled consonant?
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Match the old histories: true cousin of English brother; Indo-Aryan share-root replacement rather than cousin of sister; old eye word that also reaches English eye.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say the three meanings once more, then stop. No new item follows.
