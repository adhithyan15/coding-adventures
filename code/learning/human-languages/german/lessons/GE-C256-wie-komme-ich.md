# wie komme ich zum Bahnhof — asking the way

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ENTSCHULDIGUNG-02, GE-PRAGMATICS-SORRY-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] To get a stranger's attention in the street, say *Entschuldigung!*

## You'll want to know: wie komme ich zum Bahnhof
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-WIE-KOMME-ICH-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KOMMEN-01, GE-LEX-C96-STADT1-03, GE-LEX-C95-WO-04, GE-LEX-DU-SIE-02] -->

**Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?** — "Excuse me, how do I get
to the station?"

German asks with *kommen*: literally "how do I come to the station?"
English says "get"; German keeps the plain verb of coming.

Two short follow-ups help:

> *Ist das weit?* — Is it far?
> *Danke schön!* — Thank you.

To a stranger, keep to *Sie*: *Können Sie mir helfen?*

## Grammar Lens: zum or zur
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ZUM-ZUR-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C104-ORT104-05, GE-LEX-C104-ORT104-03, GE-LEX-C97-STADT2-03, GE-LEX-C98-STADT3-03] -->

**zu**, "to", takes the dative, and it fuses with the article:

> **zum** = *zu dem*, for *der* and *das* words: *zum Bahnhof*, *zum Museum*
> **zur** = *zu der*, for *die* words: *zur Post*, *zur Apotheke*, *zur
> Haltestelle*

So the gender you learned with each noun decides the little word in front
of it. *Der Bahnhof*, *zum Bahnhof*. *Die Post*, *zur Post*.

In Switzerland you will hear *Exgüsi* in dialect for "excuse me", from
French *excusez*; *Entschuldigung* is understood everywhere.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-KOMMEN-01] -->

**kommen** ← Old High German *kweman* ← Proto-Germanic *\*kwemaną*, "to
come" (EtymDB 2.1): the same verb as English *come*. **zu** is usually
traced to Old High German *zuo*, a cousin of English *to*; EtymDB lists
*zu* without a parent, so that is given as the usual account.
