6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
なくなる (nakunaru) — to pass away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to see (someone) off, then the Japanese for new.

You'll want to know: なくなる.
なくなる — nakunaru — "to pass away".
きょねん、そぼが なくなりました。 — kyonen, sobo ga nakunarimashita — "My grandmother passed away last year."
Polite なくなります, nakunarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): そふが なくなりました。, sofu ga nakunarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
なくなる (nakunaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 6.
そうしき (sōshiki) — a funeral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for new, then the Japanese for to pass away.

You'll want to know: そうしき.
そうしき — sōshiki — "a funeral".
あした そうしきが あります。 — ashita sōshiki ga arimasu — "The funeral is tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
そうしき (sōshiki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sō, "bury" (Middle Chinese tsangH) and shiki, "ceremony" (syik) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
