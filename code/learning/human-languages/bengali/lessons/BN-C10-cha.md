# চা (chā) — "tea," and the first word you can politely offer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C09-BHALOLAGA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Last lesson you said *āmār chā bhālo lāge*, "I like tea," without চা
ever being taught on its own. Here it is, by itself — and a new job for a form
you already own.

## You'll want to know: চা
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **চা** — *chā* — **tea**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**চা** is **চ** (*chô*) with the vowel sign **া** (*ā*). **চ** is a plain,
unaspirated *ch*, the sound at the start of English *church* with no puff of
breath behind it — Bengali also has a breathy partner, **ছ**, for later.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**চা** is not Sanskrit at all. It came into Bengali from **Persian** *čā*,
which took it from **Chinese** *chá*. That is the overland route: China to
Persia to Bengal, the same road Hindi and Punjabi travelled. The
words that reached Europe — English *tea*, French *thé* — went the opposite way,
by sea, from Hokkien Chinese *tê*, carried on Dutch trading ships. One drink,
two ends of the same word, arriving by two different roads.

## Grammar Lens: an offer, built on a form you already have
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-GRAMMAR-C10-KHAN-REQUEST-02]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C07-KHAOWA-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C07-KHAOWA-02] -->

Chapter 7 taught you **আপনি খান** (*āpni khān*), the respectful form of
খাওয়া. Put a noun in front of it and the very same words become a polite
offer or request:

| Bengali | what it does |
|---|---|
| আপনি খান | you eat / you drink (respectful) |
| **চা খান** | (please) have some tea |

No new ending to learn — চা খান reuses exactly what Chapter 7 already gave you.
