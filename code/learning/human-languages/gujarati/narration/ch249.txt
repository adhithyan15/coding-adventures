Gujarati, chapter 249: Calls and messages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
મોબાઈલ (mobāīl) — mobile phone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to hand over, then the Gujarati for to note down.

You'll want to know: મોબાઈલ.
મોબાઈલ — mobāīl — "mobile phone".
મારો મોબાઈલ ખિસ્સામાં છે. — māro mobāīl khissāmā̃ che — "My mobile phone is in my pocket."

The word, taken apart.
મોબાઈલ (mobāīl) is a loan from English mobile, short for a mobile phone.

Lesson 3 of 5.
સંદેશો (sandeśo) — message.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for mobile phone, then the Gujarati for number.

You'll want to know: સંદેશો.
સંદેશો — sandeśo — "message".
મારો ભાઈ રોજ સંદેશો મોકલે છે. — māro bhāī roj sandeśo mokle che — "My brother sends a message every day."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંદેશો (sandeśo) comes from Sanskrit saṁdēśa, "message, information" (CDIAL 12904). It keeps the Sanskrit shape with the Gujarati masculine ending -o, a semi-tatsama.

Lesson 5 of 5.
મેળવવું (meḷavvũ) — to obtain, to get.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for message, then the Gujarati for voice.

You'll want to know: મેળવવું.
મેળવવું — meḷavvũ — "to obtain, to get".
હું મિત્ર પાસેથી નંબર મેળવું છું. — hũ mitra pāsethī nambar meḷavũ chũ — "I get the number from a friend."

The word, taken apart.
મેળવવું (meḷavvũ), to bring something to oneself, is usually explained as the causative of the inherited maḷvũ, "to meet, to be got", with the vowel raised to e. CDIAL derives maḷvũ from Sanskrit miláti, "meets" (CDIAL 10133), but lists no Gujarati form under the Sanskrit causative mēlayati (CDIAL 10332).
