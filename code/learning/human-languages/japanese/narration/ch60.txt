Lesson 1 of 5.
まん (man) — ten thousand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for thirty, then the Japanese for a thousand.

You'll want to know: まん.
まん — man — "ten thousand".

The word, taken apart.
まん (man) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the on'yomi of a character meaning "ten thousand", a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mjonH; Unihan, KANJIDIC2, Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 2 of 5.
じゅうに (juuni) — twelve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a thousand, then the Japanese for ten thousand.

You'll want to know: じゅうに.
じゅうに — juuni — "twelve".

The word, taken apart.
じゅうに (juuni) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jū, "ten" (Middle Chinese dzyip) and ni, "two" (nyijH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 3 of 5.
なす (nasu) — an aubergine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for ten thousand, then the Japanese for twelve.

You'll want to know: なす.
なす — nasu — "an aubergine".

The word, taken apart.
なす (nasu) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. JMdict writes it with the characters for "eggplant" and the noun ending "child", read as a whole, and marks it as usually written in kana. Its older origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
うた (uta) — a song.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for twelve, then the Japanese for an aubergine.

You'll want to know: うた.
うた — uta — "a song".

The word, taken apart.
うた (uta) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
