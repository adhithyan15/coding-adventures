# કે નહીં? and ખરું? — two tags at the end

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POLAR-SHUN-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POLAR-RISE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NAHI-01, GU-LEX-C136-QUAL136-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask *do you speak Gujarati?* twice: once with **શું**, once with your
voice alone. Then say **નહીં**, the *not* for one word, and **સાચું**, *true*.

## Grammar Lens: … કે નહીં?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-KE-NAHI-01]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NAHI-01, GU-JOIN-KE-01, GU-LEX-AAVVU] -->

> **તમે આવો છો કે નહીં?** — *tame āvo cho ke nahī̃?* — Are you coming or not?

The **કે** you know as *that* also means *or* between two choices. The tag offers
both answers and asks the listener to pick one. It is firmer than
**શું**: a parent at the door, a friend whose bus is leaving.

## You'll want to know: …, ખરું?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C146-KHARUN-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C136-QUAL136-03] -->

**ખરું** (*kharũ*) means *true, real*, a close neighbour of **સાચું**. As a tag it
asks for agreement:

> **તમે અહીં રહો છો, ખરું?** — *tame ahī̃ raho cho, kharũ?* — You live here, right?

The speaker already expects **હા** and checks it. So the three sound different:
**શું** in front opens the question, **કે નહીં** at the end asks for a pick, and
**ખરું** at the end asks for a yes.
