Portuguese, chapter 256: Writing --- My Own Line.
1 lesson. It needs your eyes or your hands, so save it for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
Your own line — the first writing with no model.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Everything you have written in Portuguese so far had something to reach for — a shape to trace, a word to copy, a sound to take down.
[pause 1 second]
This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed: only what this book has taught you.

Writing — controlled composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write one line about yourself: your name, where you live, and a language you speak. The verbs are ones you already hold — chamo-me, moro, falo — and the -o already says "I", so no eu is needed.
[pause 2 seconds]
One thing to decide on purpose before you start: the marks are letters, not decoration.
[pause 2 seconds]
The marks over a Portuguese vowel do two different jobs. The accent in português marks the vowel that carries the stress. The til in pão and irmã is not an accent at all: it marks a nasal vowel, a sound rather than a stress.
[pause 2 seconds]
A hand that writes pao for pão has not written Portuguese untidily. It has written a word Portuguese does not have, and left the reader to guess.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the line. Then read it back and check every mark before you check anything else.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Is the til on pão an accent? (No — it marks a nasal vowel, not the stress.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And what did you choose here that the earlier writing lessons chose for you? (The words.)
