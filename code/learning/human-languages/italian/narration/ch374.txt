Italian, chapter 374: Gli imprevisti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'imprevisto — the unexpected problem, the hitch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for or else, then the Italian for possible.

You'll want to know: l'imprevisto.
l'imprevisto — "the unexpected problem, the hitch".
Scusa il ritardo, ho avuto un imprevisto. Sorry I am late, something came up.
As an adjective: unexpected. Salvo imprevisti — barring the unexpected.

The word, taken apart.
imprevisto is built from in- "not" and previsto "foreseen", past participle of prevedere, from Latin praevidēre "to foresee" (Lewis & Short), on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'alternativa — the alternative, the other option.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for predictable, then the Italian for unpredictable.

You'll want to know: l'alternativa.
l'alternativa — "the alternative, the other option".
Se il ristorante è pieno, qual è l'alternativa? If the restaurant is full, what is the alternative?
Non c'è alternativa — there is no other way.

The word, taken apart.
alternativa is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin alternātīvus, from alternāre "to do by turns" (Lewis & Short), built on alter "the other of two".

Lesson 5 of 5.
adattarsi — to adapt, to get used to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for unpredictable, then the Italian for the alternative.

You'll want to know: adattarsi.
adattarsi — "to adapt, to get used to".
Se cambiano i piani, mi adatto. If the plans change, I will adapt.
Adattarsi a qualcosa. Without -si, adattare is to adapt something.

The word, taken apart.
adattarsi is built on adattare, a learned borrowing from Latin adaptāre "to fit to" (Lewis & Short), built from ad "to" and aptus "fitted".
