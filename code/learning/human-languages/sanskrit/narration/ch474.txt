Sanskrit, chapter 474: Veins and Muscles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सिरा (sirā) — a vein.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a fist, then the Sanskrit for bends a limb.

You'll want to know: सिरा.
सिरा — sirā — "a vein".
रक्तं सिरासु वहति। raktaṃ sirāsu vahati. Blood flows in the veins.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सिरा (sirā) comes from the root √sṛ, "to flow": MW's first sense is "a stream", then "any tubular vessel of the body, a vein, an artery" (MW).

स्नायुः अस्थि बध्नाति। snāyuḥ asthi badhnāti. Sinew binds the bone.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्नायुः (snāyuḥ) MW compares, with a question mark, German Sehne and English sinew, and reports that some trace it to a root meaning "to bind"; its origin is not settled (MW).

पेशी (peśī) MW's first sense is "a piece of flesh", then "a muscle"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
