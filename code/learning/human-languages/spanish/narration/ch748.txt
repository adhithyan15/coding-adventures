Spanish, chapter 748: Familias de hoy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
adoptar — to adopt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the son-in-law, then the Spanish for the daughter-in-law.

You'll want to know: adoptar.
adoptar — "to adopt".
Adoptaron a una niña. They adopted a little girl.
Also for pets and ideas: adoptar un perro, adoptar una medida.

The word, taken apart.
adoptar is a learned borrowing from Latin adoptāre "to choose for oneself" (EtymDB), built on optāre "to choose".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
acoger — to take in, to foster; to welcome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the daughter-in-law, then the Spanish for to adopt.

You'll want to know: acoger.
acoger — "to take in, to foster; to welcome".
Acogieron a dos niños durante un año. They fostered two children for a year.
Spelling: acojo. Una familia de acogida — a foster family.

The word, taken apart.
acoger is inherited from a spoken Latin accolligere (EtymDB), built on colligere "to gather": to gather someone in.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la madrastra — the stepmother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to take in, then the Spanish for adoption.

You'll want to know: la madrastra.
la madrastra — "the stepmother".
Se lleva bien con su madrastra. She gets on well with her stepmother.
Fairy tales give it a bad name; today people often say la mujer de mi padre.

The word, taken apart.
madrastra is inherited from spoken Latin mātrastra (EtymDB), built on māter "mother" with a belittling ending.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: el padrastro.
el padrastro — "the stepfather; the hangnail".
Mi padrastro me enseñó a nadar. My stepfather taught me to swim.
Also a hangnail beside a fingernail.

The word, taken apart.
padrastro is usually traced to a spoken Latin patraster, built on pater "father" with the belittling ending -aster.
