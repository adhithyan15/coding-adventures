Gujarati, chapter 174: October to Maha.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઓક્ટોબર (okṭobar) — October.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for last, then the Gujarati for whole.

You'll want to know: ઓક્ટોબર.
ઓક્ટોબર — okṭobar — "October".
ઓક્ટોબરમાં દિવાળી આવશે. — okṭobarmā̃ divāḷī āvśe — "Diwali will come in October."

The word, taken apart.
ઓક્ટોબર (okṭobar) is a loan from English October, the name of the tenth month.

Lesson 3 of 5.
મહા (mahā) — Maha, a winter month.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for October, then the Gujarati for December.

You'll want to know: મહા.
મહા — mahā — "Maha, a winter month".
મહા મહિનામાં લગ્ન હશે. — mahā mahināmā̃ lagna haśe — "The wedding will be in the month of Maha."

The word, taken apart.
મહા (mahā) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit māgha, "the month January-February", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati mahā, māh for that month (CDIAL 9993).

Lesson 4 of 5.
તારીખિયું (tārīkhiyũ) — wall calendar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for December, then the Gujarati for Maha.

You'll want to know: તારીખિયું.
તારીખિયું — tārīkhiyũ — "wall calendar".
હું નવું તારીખિયું ખરીદીશ. — hũ navũ tārīkhiyũ kharīdīś — "I will buy a new calendar."
Built on તારીખ, tārīkh, date.

The word, taken apart.
તારીખિયું (tārīkhiyũ) is built on the taught tārīkh, "date", with the ending -iyũ. Tārīkh is a loan from Arabic tārīkh, "a dating, a date", through Persian; EtymDB lists the Persian word. Its Arabic root is ʾ-r-kh, "to date a letter" (Lane).

Lesson 5 of 5.
શરૂઆત (śarūāt) — beginning, start.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for Maha, then the Gujarati for wall calendar.

You'll want to know: શરૂઆત.
શરૂઆત — śarūāt — "beginning, start".
મહિનાની શરૂઆતમાં પગાર આવશે. — mahinānī śarūātmā̃ pagār āvśe — "The salary will come at the start of the month."

The word, taken apart.
શરૂઆત (śarūāt) is a loan from Arabic shurūʿ, "a beginning", which reached Gujarati through Persian, with the ending -āt; EtymDB lists the Arabic word. Its root is sh-r-ʿ, "to enter upon" (Lane).
