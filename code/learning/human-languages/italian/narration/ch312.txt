6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'antenato — the ancestor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bouquet, then the Italian for the wedding favour.

You'll want to know: l'antenato.
l'antenato — "the ancestor".
I miei antenati erano contadini. My ancestors were farmers.
Plural gli antenati.

The word, taken apart.
antenato is usually traced to Late Latin antenātus "born before", a learned word built on ante "before" and nātus "born" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
la discendenza — the descendants, the line of descent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the ancestor, then the Italian for posterity.

You'll want to know: la discendenza.
la discendenza — "the descendants, the line of descent".
Ha una discendenza numerosa: dieci nipoti. He has many descendants: ten grandchildren.
Built on discendere, ‘to go down’.

The word, taken apart.
discendenza is built on discendere "to descend", from Latin dēscendere (EtymDB), from dē and scandere "to climb".

Lesson 5 of 6.
l'albero genealogico — the family tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the descendants, then the Italian for the lineage.

You'll want to know: l'albero genealogico.
l'albero genealogico — "the family tree".
Mio padre ha disegnato l'albero genealogico della famiglia. My father drew the family tree.
Genealogico — of the family line.

The word, taken apart.
albero is inherited from Latin arbor "tree" (EtymDB); genealogico comes from Greek genealogía, an account of a family line, which Latin took over as geneālogia (Lewis & Short).
