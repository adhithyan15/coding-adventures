# তিন — three, read

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-TIN-READ-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-TA-01, BN-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01, BN-SCRIPT-NA-01] -->

> **ত** + **ি** + **ন** → **তিন**

Read it the way the mouth needs it, not the way the eye meets it:

- **ত** — the dental *t*, tongue on the teeth
- **ি** — leaning on that **ত** from the left, heard after it
- **ন** — *nô*, its inherent vowel almost silent at the end

**tin** — *three*, counted since the numbers chapter.

The hook in front of **ত** is the same trap as **আমি**, and it will keep being
the same trap until the hand has written it twenty times. On the page: hook,
t, n. In the mouth: t, i, n.

This word is also about as old as words get. Sanskrit *tri*, Latin *trēs*, Greek
*treîs*, English *three* — the numbers chapter set that out, and the point here
is only that you can now meet it on a page in Kolkata instead of in a table.
