# హ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-124] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ఎ — what does it do?

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time — and you have been saying it for pages
without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: హ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-125]; assesses=[] -->

**హ** — *ha*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *h*, it is **ha**.

You already say these, and both have హ somewhere inside them:

- **సహాయం** *sahāyam* — help
- **హైదరాబాద్** *Hyderābād* — the city from the sentence about where you live

Look at where those two came from. *Sahāyam* is Sanskrit; *Hyderābād* reached
Telugu through Persian and Urdu. In the words this book has given you so far, హ
sits on borrowings, and the word for noon — the one you say every time you greet
somebody in the middle of the day — carries it for the same reason.

## Writing: హ — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-125] -->

Put your pen on హ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
