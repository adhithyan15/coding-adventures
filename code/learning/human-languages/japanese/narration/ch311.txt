Lesson 1 of 6.
おもいで (omoide) — a memory; a keepsake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to come true, then the Japanese for shortly.

You'll want to know: おもいで.
おもいで — omoide — "a memory; a keepsake".
なつやすみは いい おもいでに なりました。 — natsuyasumi wa ii omoide ni narimashita — "The summer holidays became a happy memory."

The word, taken apart.
おもいで (omoide) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from omoi, "thinking" (from omou), and de, "coming out" (from deru) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

しょうねん — shōnen — "a boy; boyhood".
しょうねんの ころ、そふは やまで はたらいていました。 — shōnen no koro, sofu wa yama de hataraite imashita — "As a boy, my grandfather worked in the mountains."

The word, taken apart.
しょうねん (shōnen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "little" (Middle Chinese syewH) and nen, "year" (nen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ふりかえる (furikaeru) — to look back (on).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a boy, then the Japanese for to lose for good.

You'll want to know: ふりかえる.
ふりかえる — furikaeru — "to look back (on)".
そふは ときどき むかしを ふりかえります。 — sofu wa tokidoki mukashi o furikaerimasu — "Grandfather sometimes thinks back to the old days."
Polite ふりかえります, furikaerimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが ふりかえりました。, haha ga furikaerimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ふりかえる (furikaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from furi, "swinging" (from furu), and kaeru, "to turn back", the intransitive partner of kaesu, "to give back" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
