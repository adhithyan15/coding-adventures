Japanese, chapter 148: Hair, Beards and Make-up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かみのけ (kaminoke) — the hair on one's head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for hot water, then the Japanese for soap.

You'll want to know: かみのけ.
かみのけ — kaminoke — "the hair on one's head".
いもうとの かみのけは ながいです。 — imōto no kaminoke wa nagai desu — "My younger sister's hair is long."

The word, taken apart.
かみのけ (kaminoke) is native Japanese, built from kami, "hair", the linking no, and ke, "hair, fur", all inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

ひげ (hige) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; the kanji JMdict lists for it were attached later (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
けしょう (keshō) — make-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a beard, then the Japanese for to grow a beard.

You'll want to know: けしょう.
けしょう — keshō — "make-up".
あねは かがみで けしょうを なおします。 — ane wa kagami de keshō o naoshimasu — "My elder sister fixes her make-up in the mirror."

The word, taken apart.
けしょう (keshō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ke, "change, take a form" (Middle Chinese xwaeH) and shō, "cosmetics, adorn", whose Middle Chinese reading the sources here do not give (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
