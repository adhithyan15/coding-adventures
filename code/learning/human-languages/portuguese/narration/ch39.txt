Portuguese, chapter 39: Child, Neighbour, Guest, Boy, Girl.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a criança — a child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a grandson, then the Portuguese for a granddaughter.

You'll want to know: a criança.
a criança — "a child".

The word, taken apart.
criança was built in Old Portuguese from the verb "to raise, to bring up" and the suffix -ança: a child is one being raised (EtymDB 2.1). The verb from Latin creare, "to create" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o vizinho — a neighbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a granddaughter, then the Portuguese for a child.

You'll want to know: o vizinho.
o vizinho — "a neighbour".

The word, taken apart.
vizinho from Latin vicinus, "neighbouring", from vicus, "village" — inherited. EtymDB 2.1 traces it only to Old Portuguese vizinno; it derives Spanish vecino from vicinus.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o convidado — a guest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a child, then the Portuguese for a neighbour.

You'll want to know: o convidado.
o convidado — "a guest".

The word, taken apart.
convidado is "invited": the participle of the verb "to invite", used as a noun. That verb from Vulgar Latin convitare (EtymDB 2.1), a reshaping of Latin invitare, "to invite".
