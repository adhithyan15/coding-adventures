Hindi, chapter 6: Numbers One to Five.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
एक, दो, तीन, चार, पाँच (pāṁch) — the five forms.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Five words, and a story about how languages age.

You'll want to know — The five.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
1. Hindi: एक. said: ek.
2. Hindi: दो. said: do.
3. Hindi: तीन. said: tīn.
4. Hindi: चार. said: chār.
5. Hindi: पाँच. said: pāṁch.
If you did the writing track you'll recognise some pieces: क (Lesson 2), त (Lesson 2), न (Lesson 1), र (Lesson 4), and the ा mātrā (Lesson 3).
Plenty here you haven't been taught to draw yet — ए, च, द, प, the ी and ो mātrās, and the ँ. Read them now; you'll draw them when the writing track reaches them.
The ँ over pāṁch is a chandrabindu, "moon-dot" — it nasalises the vowel, the way French does in bon.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ek, do, tīn, chār, pāṁch"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the familiar क, त, न, र and ा inside the five words]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: the ँ moon-dot over pāṁch]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Count to five in Hindi. (Ek, do, tīn, chār, pāṁch.) Which mark nasalises the vowel in पाँच? (The chandrabindu, "moon-dot.") Do you need to draw every unfamiliar letter today? (No — read them now and draw them when their stroke lessons arrive.) Next: watch the Sanskrit forms wear smooth.


Lesson 2 of 3.
तीन and चार — weight moves from consonants to vowels.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The five modern forms did not jump straight from Sanskrit. The Prakrits did most of the wearing-down in between.

You'll want to know — Put the stages side by side.
[a table of 5 rows, read aloud]
Sanskrit: éka-. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: ekka. Hindi: ek.
Sanskrit: dvá-. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: do. Hindi: do.
Sanskrit: neuter trī́ṇi. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: tiṇṇi. Hindi: tīn.
Sanskrit: neuter catvā́ri. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: cattāri. Hindi: chār.
Sanskrit: páñca. becomes Prakrit, which gives:: pañca. Hindi: pāṁch.
Three and four come from the neuter forms. Sanskrit also had masculine tráyaḥ, but Hindi tīn continues trī́ṇi. The same choice gives catvā́ri becomes chār.

You'll want to know — The two-step weight trade.
trī́ṇi becomes tiṇṇi: r is lost and ṇ doubles to make up the weight. Long ī shortens because the doubled consonant already makes the syllable heavy.
tiṇṇi becomes tīn: the double simplifies and the vowel lengthens to pay for the lost consonant weight.
The vowel ends long again after being short in the middle. Ekka becomes ek shows the second half of the same trade.
This is not decay in a bad sense; it is what heavy use does over two thousand years. The same ancient number wore down on another continent:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
Latin becomes Spanish. ancient: quattuor. modern: cuatro.
Sanskrit becomes Hindi. ancient: catvā́ri. modern: chār.
Same PIE word, two continents, two ordinary histories.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "trī́ṇi … tiṇṇi … tīn"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "catvā́ri … cattāri … chār"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — compare: "quattuor becomes cuatro, catvā́ri becomes chār"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which Sanskrit forms underlie tīn and chār? (Neuter trī́ṇi and catvā́ri.) What sits between Sanskrit and Hindi? (The Prakrits.) In trī́ṇi becomes tiṇṇi becomes tīn, what trades places? (The lost r is paid for by a double consonant, then the simplified double by a long vowel.) Next: follow the nasal that stayed inside pāṁch.


Lesson 3 of 3.
पाँच (pāṁch) — the nasal moved into the vowel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Tīn moved weight between consonants and vowels. Pāṁch moves a whole nasal sound into the vowel itself.

You'll want to know — The nasal that stayed.
Sanskrit pañca becomes Hindi pāṁch. The n did not vanish; Sanskrit's ñ consonant became Hindi's nasalised ā. The ँ chandrabindu records that nasality above the word.
Say pāṁch and pañca one after the other. You can hear the nasal migrating backward from its own consonant into the vowel.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "pañca … pāṁch"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — hum: hold the nasal through the vowel in pāṁch]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: ँ, the moon-dot that records it]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Did the nasal in pañca disappear? (No — it moved into the vowel.) What records it in पाँच? (The chandrabindu.) Count one to five once more. (Ek, do, tīn, chār, pāṁch.)
