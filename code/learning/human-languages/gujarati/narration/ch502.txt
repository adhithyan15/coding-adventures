Gujarati, chapter 502: In the Carpenter's Shop.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કરવત (karvat) — a saw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to know how, then the Gujarati for a profession.

You'll want to know: કરવત.
કરવત — karvat — "a saw".
સુથાર કરવતથી લાકડું કાપે છે. suthār karvatthī lākḍũ kāpe che. The carpenter cuts the wood with a saw.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
કરવત (karvat) is inherited from Sanskrit karapattra, "a saw", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 2795).

Lesson 2 of 5.
કોરવું (korvũ) — to hollow out (wood).

You'll want to know: કોરવું.
કોરવું — korvũ — "to hollow out (wood)".
સુથારે લાકડું કોરીને એક વાટકી બનાવી. suthāre lākḍũ korīne ek vāṭkī banāvī. The carpenter hollowed out the wood and made a bowl.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે લાકડું કોર્યું, teṇe lākḍũ koryũ.

The word, taken apart.
કોરવું (korvũ) goes back to a reconstructed kōrati, "scrapes", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb for scooping out, carving or boring a hole (CDIAL 3530).

Lesson 3 of 5.
હથોડી (hathoḍī) — a hammer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a saw, then the Gujarati for to hollow out.

You'll want to know: હથોડી.
હથોડી — hathoḍī — "a hammer".
સુથારની હથોડી ટેબલ પર છે. suthārnī hathoḍī ṭebal par che. The carpenter's hammer is on the table.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
હથોડી (hathoḍī) comes from hastakūṭa, "a hand hammer", a form CDIAL builds from hásta, "hand", and kūṭa, "a hammer"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 14028).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઠોકવું (ṭhokvũ) — to hammer in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a hammer, then the Gujarati for a nail.

You'll want to know: ઠોકવું.
ઠોકવું — ṭhokvũ — "to hammer in".
અરુણે હથોડીથી દીવાલમાં મેખ ઠોકી. Aruṇe hathoḍīthī dīvālmā̃ mekh ṭhokī. Arun hammered a nail into the wall.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે મેખ ઠોકી, teṇe mekh ṭhokī.

The word, taken apart.
ઠોકવું (ṭhokvũ) comes from ṭhōkk, "to knock", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to strike" (CDIAL 5513). The past agrees with what is hammered in: મેખ (mekh) ઠોકી.
