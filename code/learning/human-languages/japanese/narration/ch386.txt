Japanese, chapter 386: Passport and Customs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りょけん (ryoken) — a passport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a time difference, then the Japanese for to aim for.

You'll want to know: りょけん.
りょけん — ryoken — "a passport".
りょけんを なくさないで ください。 — ryoken o nakusanaide kudasai — "Please do not lose your passport."

The word, taken apart.
りょけん (ryoken) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ryo, "trip" (Middle Chinese ljoX) and ken, "ticket" (khjwonH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
とりだす (toridasu) — to take something out; to produce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for entry into a country, then the Japanese for departure from a country.

You'll want to know: とりだす.
とりだす — toridasu — "to take something out; to produce".
かばんから りょけんを とりだしました。 — kaban kara ryoken o toridashimashita — "I took my passport out of my bag."
Polite とりだします, toridashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが かばんから りょけんを とりだしました。, chichi ga kaban kara ryoken o toridashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とりだす (toridasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tori, "taking" (from toru) and dasu, "to put out"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
