Italian, chapter 524: Un bel romanzo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il romanziere — novelist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for fresco, then the Italian for to restore.

You'll want to know: il romanziere.
il romanziere — "novelist".
Il romanziere presenta il suo nuovo libro in libreria. The novelist is presenting his new book in the bookshop.
A woman is la romanziera.

The word, taken apart.
romanziere is built on romanzo "novel", from Old French romanz "a story told in the common tongue", from Vulgar Latin rōmānicē "in the Roman manner" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: ambientare.
ambientare — "to set (a story)".
Il romanzo è ambientato in un piccolo paese di montagna. The novel is set in a small mountain village.
Mostly essere ambientato — to be set (in).

The word, taken apart.
ambientare is built on ambiente "surroundings", a learned borrowing from Latin ambiēns "going round" (Lewis & Short), from ambīre.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il colpo di scena — twist (in a story).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for chapter, then the Italian for to set.

You'll want to know: il colpo di scena.
il colpo di scena — "twist (in a story)".
Alla fine c'è un colpo di scena incredibile. At the end there is an incredible twist.
Literally "a blow of the stage".

The word, taken apart.
colpo di scena is built from colpo "blow", inherited from Vulgar Latin colpus, from Latin colaphus "a punch" (EtymDB), and scena "scene", from Latin scaena, from Greek skēnḗ "a stage" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'epilogo — ending, epilogue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to set, then the Italian for twist.

You'll want to know: l'epilogo.
l'epilogo — "ending, epilogue".
L'epilogo del libro mi ha sorpreso. The ending of the book surprised me.
Also the end of a real story: l'epilogo della vicenda.

The word, taken apart.
epilogo is a learned borrowing, through Latin epilogus, from Greek epílogos "a conclusion" (EtymDB, under English epilogue), from légein "to speak".
