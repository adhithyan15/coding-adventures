Spanish, chapter 740: Me siento mal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
las náuseas — nausea, sickness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to operate, then the Spanish for surgery.

You'll want to know: las náuseas.
las náuseas — "nausea, sickness".
El barco me da náuseas. Boats make me feel sick.
Usually plural: tener náuseas, dar náuseas.

The word, taken apart.
náuseas is a learned borrowing from Latin nausea, from Greek nausía "seasickness" (EtymDB), built on naûs "ship".

Lesson 4 of 5.
desmayarse — to faint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to relieve, then the Spanish for to vomit.

You'll want to know: desmayarse.
desmayarse — "to faint".
Se desmayó por el calor. She fainted because of the heat.
El desmayo — the faint.

The word, taken apart.
desmayarse is the reflexive of desmayar, which is usually traced to Old French esmaier "to trouble, to dismay"; desmayo "a faint" is built on the verb, and English dismay is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la alergia — the allergy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to vomit, then the Spanish for to faint.

You'll want to know: la alergia.
la alergia — "the allergy".
Tengo alergia al polen. I am allergic to pollen.
Tener alergia a algo; the adjective is alérgico.

The word, taken apart.
alergia was borrowed from German Allergie (EtymDB), a word an Austrian doctor coined in 1906 from Greek állos "other" and érgon "work".
