## You'll want to know: 救火
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C298-FIRE298-02]; assesses=[] -->

**救火** — *jiùhuǒ* — "to fight a fire; to put out a fire".

> **大家都来救火了** *dàjiā dōu lái jiùhuǒ le* — Everyone came to fight the fire.

The new character, in pieces: 救 — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left seven strokes, a bar, a vertical with a hook, a dot and a rising stroke on its left, a short sweep and a dot on its right, and a dot at the top right; on the right the striking piece of 故, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**救火** (*jiùhuǒ*) is built from 救 'to save' and 火 'fire', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kjuwH* and *xwaX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to protect' and 'to stop': to stop a fire.
