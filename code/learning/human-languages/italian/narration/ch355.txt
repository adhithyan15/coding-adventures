Italian, chapter 355: Porre rimedio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il rimedio — the remedy, the cure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to favour, then the Italian for to allow.

You'll want to know: il rimedio.
il rimedio — "the remedy, the cure".
Il tè con il miele è un buon rimedio per la tosse. Tea with honey is a good remedy for a cough.
Porre rimedio a — to put right. Non c'è rimedio — there is nothing to be done.

The word, taken apart.
rimedio is a learned borrowing from Latin remedium "a cure, a remedy" (Lewis & Short), built from re- and medērī "to heal", the verb behind medico.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
sistemare — to sort out, to fix; to put in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to allow, then the Italian for the remedy.

You'll want to know: sistemare.
sistemare — "to sort out, to fix; to put in order".
Non preoccuparti, sistemo tutto io. Do not worry, I will sort everything out.
Very frequent in speech. Sistemarsi — to settle down, to find a job or a home.

The word, taken apart.
sistemare is built on sistema "system", a learned borrowing from Late Latin systēma, from Greek sýstēma "a whole made of parts" (EtymDB), from synistánai "to set together".

You'll want to know: compensare.
compensare — "to make up for, to compensate".
Il panorama compensa la fatica del viaggio. The view makes up for the tiring journey.
Compensare la mancanza di qualcosa. The noun compenso is a fee for work.

The word, taken apart.
compensare is a learned borrowing from Latin compēnsāre "to weigh one thing against another, to balance" (EtymDB), built on pēnsāre "to weigh", from pendere "to hang, to weigh".
