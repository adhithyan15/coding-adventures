## You'll want to know: two ways of ordering
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-TASK-C77-ORDER-02]; assesses=[] -->

You have ten ordinals, an ending that makes the rest, and **एक … दूसरा**. They do
two different jobs, and the difference is easiest to feel on one small set.

Three books on a shelf:

- **पहली किताब**, **दूसरी किताब**, **तीसरी किताब** — the first, second and third.
  Each one is named by **where it sits**, and the naming works however many there
  are.
- **एक किताब नई है, दूसरी पुरानी।** — One is new, the other old. Nothing is
  numbered; two things are **sorted against each other**, and this only works when
  there are two.

So: **पहले** the first, and after it the second. Say the three books in order,
then say the two-book version, and you have used both systems on the same shelf.

**पहला दिन**, **दूसरा दिन**, **तीसरा दिन** — and if you can say those, you can say
the fortieth, because the ending does not stop.
