Italian, chapter 385: Si può o no.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vietato — forbidden, not allowed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for optional, then the Italian for the obligation.

You'll want to know: vietato.
vietato — "forbidden, not allowed".
È vietato fumare in tutto l'edificio. Smoking is forbidden in the whole building.
On signs: vietato l'ingresso — no entry; vietato fumare — no smoking.

The word, taken apart.
vietato is the past participle of vietare "to forbid", inherited from Latin vetāre "to forbid" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
proibire — to ban, to prohibit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the obligation, then the Italian for forbidden.

You'll want to know: proibire.
proibire — "to ban, to prohibit".
Il comune ha proibito le macchine in centro. The town council has banned cars in the centre.
Conjugated with -isc-: proibisco. Stronger and more official than vietare.

The word, taken apart.
proibire is a learned borrowing from Latin prohibēre "to hold back, to forbid" (Lewis & Short), built from prō "before" and habēre "to hold".

Lesson 4 of 5.
autorizzare — to authorise, to allow officially.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to ban, then the Italian for to tolerate.

You'll want to know: autorizzare.
autorizzare — "to authorise, to allow officially".
Se il preside ci autorizza, facciamo la festa a scuola. If the head teacher authorises it, we will have the party at school.
Autorizzare qualcuno a fare qualcosa.

The word, taken apart.
autorizzare was borrowed from French autoriser, from Medieval Latin auctōrizāre (EtymDB), built on Latin auctor "author, guarantor", from augēre "to increase".
