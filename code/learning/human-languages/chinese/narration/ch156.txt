You'll want to know: 元.
元 — yuán — "yuan", the unit of money, as it is written.
一本书十元 yì běn shū shí yuán — A book costs ten yuan.
On a price list it is written 元 (yuán); aloud, most people say kuài: shí kuài. The next lesson puts the character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
元 (yuán) is inherited from Middle Chinese ngjwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'great; the beginning; the head'. The unit of money is a modern sense, assumed here, not quoted.

What you've built.
The number, then 元 (yuán): 十元. A thing's price follows the thing: 一本书, 十元.
When you say it, swap in kuài: yì běn shū shí kuài. 一 falls before the dip of běn: yì běn.

Script — 二, then 儿 under it.
元 (yuán).
Two characters you own, one above the other.
二, two strokes: the short upper bar, then the longer lower bar.
儿, two strokes, under them: the left-falling stroke, then the vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up.
2 + 2 = four.

You'll want to know: jiàqián.
jiàqián — "price".
jiàqián tài guì le — The price is too high.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiàqián is built from jià 'price' and qián 'money', the word you know. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, kaeH and dzjen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'price; value' and 'coin'. The jià of fàngjià, a holiday, sounds the same and is a different word.

What you've built.
jiàqián names the price as a fact, the figure on the list; duōshao qián asks it aloud.
太 … 了 is still the complaint: jiàqián tài guì le.

You'll want to know: miǎnfèi.
miǎnfèi — "free (of charge)".
shuǐ miǎnfèi — The water's free.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
miǎnfèi is built from miǎn 'to be spared' and fèi 'cost'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, mjenX and phj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to escape; to remove' and 'to use up; to spend'. Spared the spending: free.

What you've built.
miǎnfèi follows the thing, as a price does: shuǐ miǎnfèi, where a price list would say 水 一元.
Its first syllable dips: miǎn.

You'll want to know: guǎnggào.
guǎnggào — "advert; advertisement".
zhè shì shǒujī de guǎnggào — This is an advert for a phone.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guǎnggào is built from guǎng 'wide' and gào 'to tell'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, kwangX and kawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'broad' and 'to report': telling widely. 'An advert' is the pair's modern sense.

What you've built.
的 joins the thing to the advert: shǒujī de guǎnggào, an advert for a phone.
An advert often has a price and a day on it, and little else.

The exchange.
A: zhè běn shū duōshao qián — How much is this book? B: 一本书十元 yì běn shū shí yuán — Ten yuan a book. A: jiàqián tài guì le — That's too dear. B: 星期六和星期天 两本书十五元 xīngqīliù hé xīngqītiān, liǎng běn shū shíwǔ yuán — At the weekend, two books for fifteen. A: nǐ kàndào guǎnggào le ma — Did you see the advert? B: kàndào le, shuǐ miǎnfèi — Yes, and the water's free. A: 本子呢 běnzi ne — And notebooks? B: 两元一本 liǎng yuán yì běn — Two yuan each.
The list says 元 (yuán) and the speakers read it as written; at a counter, most people would say kuài. Both are right.
