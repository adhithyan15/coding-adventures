Italian, chapter 483: Piccoli incidenti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il livido — bruise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for temple, then the Italian for jaw.

You'll want to know: il livido.
il livido — "bruise".
Sono caduto e ho un livido sul ginocchio. I fell and I have a bruise on my knee.
Also an adjective: livido — bluish, or pale with anger.

The word, taken apart.
livido is a learned borrowing from Latin līvidus "bluish, black and blue" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from līvēre "to be bluish".

The verb is graffiare — to scratch.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of graffio is not settled; it is often linked to a Germanic word for a hook or claw.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la scottatura — burn; sunburn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for bruise, then the Italian for scratch.

You'll want to know: la scottatura.
la scottatura — "burn; sunburn".
Sono stato al sole troppo a lungo e ora ho una scottatura. I stayed in the sun too long and now I have sunburn.
From scottarsi — to get burnt.

The word, taken apart.
scottatura is built on scottare "to scald", usually traced to Latin excoctāre, from coquere "to cook" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
inciampare — to trip, to stumble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for scratch, then the Italian for burn.

You'll want to know: inciampare.
inciampare — "to trip, to stumble".
Sono inciampato sulle scale e sono caduto. I tripped on the stairs and fell.
It takes essere: sono inciampato. Inciampare in something.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of inciampare is not settled; it may be built on an old dialect word ciampa "paw, foot".

Lesson 5 of 5.
tagliarsi — to cut oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for burn, then the Italian for to trip.

You'll want to know: tagliarsi.
tagliarsi — "to cut oneself".
Mi sono tagliato con il coltello. I cut myself with the knife.
Tagliarsi un dito — to cut a finger.

The word, taken apart.
tagliarsi is the reflexive of tagliare "to cut", inherited from Late Latin tāleāre, from tālea "a rod, a cutting" (Lewis & Short).
