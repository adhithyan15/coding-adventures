Italian, chapter 306: Salvati in mare.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il salvataggio — the rescue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the avalanche, then the Italian for to drown.

You'll want to know: il salvataggio.
il salvataggio — "the rescue".
Il salvataggio è durato tutta la notte. The rescue went on all night.
Il giubbotto di salvataggio — the life jacket.

The word, taken apart.
salvataggio is built on salvare "to save", from Late Latin salvāre, built on salvus "safe" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: il naufragio.
il naufragio — "the shipwreck".
Dopo il naufragio sono arrivati su un'isola. After the shipwreck they reached an island.
Also a failure: il naufragio di un progetto.

The word, taken apart.
naufragio is a learned borrowing from Latin naufragium (EtymDB), built on nāvis "ship" and frangere "to break".

Lesson 4 of 5.
disperso — missing (person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the lifeguard, then the Italian for the shipwreck.

You'll want to know: disperso.
disperso — "missing (person)".
Due pescatori sono ancora dispersi. Two fishermen are still missing.
The past participle of disperdere, ‘to scatter’.

The word, taken apart.
disperso is the past participle of disperdere "to scatter, to lose"; the form is usually traced to Latin dispersus, the past participle of dispergere "to scatter" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
sopravvivere — to survive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the shipwreck, then the Italian for missing.

You'll want to know: sopravvivere.
sopravvivere — "to survive".
Tutti sono sopravvissuti al naufragio. Everyone survived the shipwreck.
With essere: è sopravvissuto; sopravvivere a — to survive something.

The word, taken apart.
sopravvivere joins sopra "over" and vivere "to live", modelled on Latin supervīvere (Lewis & Short).
