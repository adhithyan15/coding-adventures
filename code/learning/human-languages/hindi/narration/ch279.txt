Hindi, chapter 279: Telling It in Order.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
खाना खाकर (khānā khākar) — one action, then the next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You met -कर with देखकर and करके: मैं किताब देखकर आता हूँ, "I'll look at the book and come". Say it once. Then "she came". (vah āī.)
[pause 1 second]
This lesson takes -कर into the past, where stories are told.

You'll want to know: -कर in a story.
मैंने हाथ धोकर खाना खाया। maiṁne hāth dhokar khānā khāyā. — "I washed my hands and ate."
One doer, two actions, in the order they happened. The first drops its -ना and takes -कर; only the last verb carries the past. करना still gives करके.
The last verb decides ने. If it takes a thing, the doer takes ने: मैंने हाथ धोकर खाना खाया. If it does not, there is no ने, though something was eaten on the way: मैं खाना खाकर सो गया (maiṁ khānā khākar so gayā), "I ate and fell asleep".
वह सुबह उठकर नहाया। (vah subah uṭhkar nahāyā.) — He got up in the morning and bathed.
मीरा ने घर आकर चाय पी। (Mīrā ne ghar ākar cāy pī.) — Mira came home and drank tea.

You'll want to know: after doing.
नाश्ते के बाद हम बाज़ार गए। nāśte ke bād ham bāzār gae. — "After breakfast we went to the market."
के बाद, "after", follows a noun in its oblique form: नाश्ते के बाद. It follows a verb too, as के लिए does in करने के लिए: the -ना becomes -ने.
खाना खाने के बाद (khānā khāne ke bād) — after eating.
काम करने के बाद मैं घर आया। (kām karne ke bād maiṁ ghar āyā.) — After work I came home.
-कर needs one doer for both actions. When the doers differ, tell it in two sentences, or with जब … तब ….

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I washed my hands and ate" — maiṁne hāth dhokar khānā khāyā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Mira came home and drank tea" — Mīrā ne ghar ākar cāy pī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you got up and bathed, then that after breakfast you went to the market.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many verbs in a -कर sentence carry the past? (One: the last.) Which verb decides ने? (The last: मैंने हाथ धोकर खाना खाया, but मैं खाना खाकर (khānā khākar) सो गया.) "After breakfast"? (नाश्ते के बाद.) "After eating"? (खाना खाने के बाद.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
आख़िर (ākhir) — at last, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I ate and fell asleep". (maiṁ khānā khākar so gayā.) A story needs a word for its last step, and for a wait that ends.

You'll want to know: आख़िर.
आख़िर — ākhir — "at last, finally".
दो घंटे के बाद आख़िर ट्रेन आई। do ghaṇṭe ke bād ākhir ṭren āī. — "After two hours the train came at last."
With में it closes a story: आख़िर में (ākhir meṁ), "in the end".
आख़िर में हम घर पहुँचे। ākhir meṁ ham ghar pahũce. — "In the end we reached home."

The word, taken apart.
आख़िर (ākhir) is usually traced to Arabic ākhir, "last, the end", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its ख़, with the dot below, is the letter Hindi writes in many words that came this way.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ākhir, then ākhir meṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After two hours the train came at last" — do ghaṇṭe ke bād ākhir ṭren āī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In the end we reached home" — ākhir meṁ ham ghar pahũce]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does आख़िर (ākhir) mean? ("At last, finally".) "In the end"? (आख़िर (ākhir) में.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
पहले … फिर … (pahle … phir …) — first, then, after that, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You know पहले, "first, earlier", and फिर, "again, then". Say both. Then "in the end". (ākhir meṁ.)

You'll want to know: the order words.
पहले मैं स्टेशन गया, फिर मैंने टिकट लिया। pahle maiṁ sṭeśan gayā, phir maiṁne ṭikaṭ liyā. — "First I went to the station, then I bought a ticket."
A story marks its steps:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
step: the first. the word: पहले (pahle).
step: the next. the word: फिर (phir).
step: the one after. the word: उसके बाद (uske bād).
step: the last. the word: आख़िर में (ākhir meṁ).
Each one opens its sentence, and the verb after it is the plain past. टिकट is masculine, so लिया.

You'll want to know: उसके बाद.
उसके बाद is "after that": उस, the form वह takes before a postposition, as in उसका, then के बाद. इसके बाद (iske bād), "after this", is built the same way.
उसके बाद हम गाड़ी में बैठे। uske bād ham gāṛī meṁ baiṭhe. — "After that we got on the train."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "First we ate, then we went to the market" — pahle hamne khānā khāyā, phir ham bāzār gae]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After that I went home" — uske bād maiṁ ghar gayā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Tell three steps: first you went to the station, after that you got on the train, and in the end you reached the village.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word opens the first step? (पहले.) The next? (फिर.) "After that"? (उसके बाद.) The last? (आख़िर (ākhir) में.) Which past follows them? (The plain past.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
(reading) — a summer holiday in the village.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Eighty-nine words, every one met before. Mira is writing. She went to her village last summer, and she tells it in order.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
पिछले साल गर्मी की छुट्टी में मैं अपने गाँव गई। मुझे गाँव बहुत पसंद है। पहले मैंने स्टेशन पर टिकट लिया। फिर मैं गाड़ी में बैठी। गाड़ी में मैं एक किताब पढ़ रही थी। अचानक तेज़ बारिश हुई। शाम को मैं गाँव पहुँची। वहाँ दादी खाना बना रही थीं। मैंने हाथ धोकर दादी के साथ खाना खाया। खाना खाने के बाद दादी ने मुझे एक कहानी सुनाई। उसके बाद मैं छत पर गई। आसमान में बहुत तारे थे। आख़िर (ākhir) में मैं थककर सो गई। वह दिन बहुत अच्छा था।.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage once without stopping; do not translate]
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage again, and sort every past verb: a scene or an event]

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
गई, बैठी and पहुँची end in -ई: Mira is a woman, and these verbs have no thing. मैंने टिकट लिया and मैंने … खाना खाया follow the thing, and टिकट and खाना are masculine. दादी ने मुझे एक कहानी सुनाई follows कहानी, which is feminine; मुझे is the one it was told to.
[pause 2 seconds]
पढ़ रही थी and बना रही थीं are the scene. थीं, with the dot, is for the grandmother, said with respect. तेज़ बारिश is heavy rain: तेज़ is "fast, sharp", and rain can be sharp.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: what Mira did first, at the station]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what her grandmother was doing when she arrived]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: how the day ended, with ākhir meṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
What was Mira doing on the train when the rain came?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Why is बना रही थीं in the scene form? (The cooking was going on when Mira arrived.) Which words keep the steps in order? (पहले, फिर, उसके बाद, आख़िर (ākhir) में.)
