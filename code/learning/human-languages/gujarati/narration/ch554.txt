Gujarati, chapter 554: Dancing All Night.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
રાસ (rās) — raas (a stick dance).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to chime, then the Gujarati for to be itching to dance.

You'll want to know: રાસ.
રાસ — rās — "raas (a stick dance)".
જો આજે રાત્રે વરસાદ નહીં પડે તો આપણે રાસ રમીશું. jo āje rātre varsād nahī̃ paḍe to āpaṇe rās ramīśũ. If it doesn't rain tonight, we'll dance raas.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
રાસ (rās) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit rāsa, a country dance of cowherds (Monier-Williams). Gujarati "plays" it: રાસ રમવો.

Lesson 2 of 7.
દાંડિયા (dā̃ḍiyā) — dandiya sticks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be itching to dance, then the Gujarati for raas.

You'll want to know: દાંડિયા.
દાંડિયા — dā̃ḍiyā — "dandiya sticks".
જો તારી પાસે દાંડિયા નહીં હોય તો હું તને મારા આપીશ. jo tārī pāse dā̃ḍiyā nahī̃ hoy to hũ tane mārā āpīś. If you don't have dandiya sticks, I'll give you mine.
A masculine noun, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
દાંડિયા (dā̃ḍiyā) is built on dā̃ḍo, "a pole, a stick", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit daṇḍá, "a stick" (CDIAL 6128); dancers strike them together in the raas.

જો તું પગમાં ઘૂઘરા પહેરીશ તો નાચતી વખતે મીઠો અવાજ આવશે. jo tũ pagmā̃ ghūghrā paherīś to nāctī vakhte mīṭho avāj āvśe. If you wear ankle bells, they'll ring sweetly as you dance.

The word, taken apart.
ઘૂઘરો (ghūghro) comes from ghuṅghura, a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "a jingling bell" (CDIAL 4477).

Lesson 4 of 7.
ઝૂમવું (jhūmvũ) — to sway (to music).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for dandiya sticks, then the Gujarati for an ankle bell.

You'll want to know: ઝૂમવું.
ઝૂમવું — jhūmvũ — "to sway (to music)".
જો સંગીત સારું હશે તો બધા ઝૂમશે. jo sangīt sārũ haśe to badhā jhūmśe. If the music is good, everyone will sway to it.
Past (he …): તે ઝૂમ્યો, te jhūmyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઝૂમવું (jhūmvũ) is to sway with the music, the body and the head together. CDIAL lists Hindi jhūmnā, "to shake", with a query, under a reconstructed kṣumbhati, "shakes" (CDIAL 3726); it gives no Gujarati form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 7.
નચાવવું (nacāvvũ) — to make (someone) dance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an ankle bell, then the Gujarati for to sway.

You'll want to know: નચાવવું.
નચાવવું — nacāvvũ — "to make (someone) dance".
જો તું ઢોલ વગાડીશ તો તું બધાને નચાવીશ. jo tũ ḍhol vagāḍīś to tũ badhāne nacāvīś. If you play the drum, you'll get everyone dancing.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બધાને નચાવ્યા, teṇe badhāne nacāvyā.

The word, taken apart.
નચાવવું (nacāvvũ) is built from નાચવું, "to dance", with the causative -āv- and a shortened vowel. CDIAL files નાચવું under Sanskrit nṛtyati, "dances" (CDIAL 7583).
