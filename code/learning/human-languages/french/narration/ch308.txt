French, chapter 308: Une naissance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la naissance — the birth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the disaster victim, then the French for the scratch.

You'll want to know: la naissance.
la naissance — "the birth".
Nous avons fêté la naissance de notre fille. We celebrated the birth of our daughter.
La date de naissance — date of birth; de naissance — from birth.

The word, taken apart.
naissance is built on the stem of naître "to be born", which is inherited from Vulgar Latin nāscere, from Latin nāscī "to be born" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the French for the scratch, then the French for the birth.

You'll want to know: enceinte.
enceinte — "pregnant".
Ma sœur est enceinte de six mois. My sister is six months pregnant.
Enceinte de six mois — six months pregnant. As a noun, une enceinte is a loudspeaker or a city wall.

The word, taken apart.
enceinte is inherited from Late Latin incincta (EtymDB), usually read as "without a belt", from cingere "to gird" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'accouchement — the delivery, giving birth.

You'll want to know: l'accouchement.
l'accouchement — "the delivery, giving birth".
L'accouchement s'est bien passé. The delivery went well.
Un accouchement à domicile — a home birth.

The word, taken apart.
accouchement is built on a verb from Old French acouchier "to lie down", itself built on coucher, which is inherited from Latin collocāre "to place, to lay" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
allaiter — to breastfeed.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: allaiter.
allaiter — "to breastfeed".
Elle a allaité son bébé pendant six mois. She breastfed her baby for six months.
Built on lait, milk.

The word, taken apart.
allaiter is usually traced to Late Latin allactāre "to suckle", built on lac, lactis "milk" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists its Catalan and Italian cousins.

You'll want to know: le berceau.
le berceau — "the cradle".
Le bébé dort dans son berceau. The baby is sleeping in its cradle.
Figurative: le berceau d'une civilisation.

The word, taken apart.
berceau comes from Old French berçuel, inherited from Vulgar Latin berciolum "cradle" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to Gaulish.
