# HL97 — Punjabi A2 `ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ` prerequisite

Issue: #18095

After chapter 196 merged, the confidential Punjabi A2 mock audit against exact
merged main `1230cd7ef1f2e800724a7e802449e4cd82622410` finds **ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ**
without an instructional owner. It occurs four times in confidential examiner
material and does not occur in the confidential learner material. This spec
records counts only; neither confidential body belongs in the public corpus.

Chapter 197 closes that single-word gap through one deliberately narrow
identity frame: **ਇਹ ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ ਹੈ** — “this is a daily routine.” The voice-first
opener retrieves only familiar **ਇਹ** and **ਹੈ**. One lesson directly owns exact
**ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ**, an immediate lesson retrieves its sound, meaning, and script, a
bounded frame lesson places it between the two familiar words, and a four-skill
payoff retrieves the complete label again. The target has one direct owner and
three later retrievals. It must not leak into authored, generated-book, or
narration copy before its owner.

The new atom is `PA-LEX-DINCHARIYA-197`. The lesson keeps the Punjabi form whole
and owns only the neutral noun sense “daily routine” or “the usual ordered
activities of a day.” It does not derive the word from smaller Punjabi parts,
assign it a Punjabi gender, teach plural or oblique forms, introduce a full
routine narrative, extend it to an Ayurvedic regimen, or teach the separate
meeting-agenda sense.

The Australian Government's Punjabi Medicare Mental Health Check In page uses
**ਤੁਹਾਡੀ ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ** for a person's routine:
<https://www.medicarementalhealthcheckin.gov.au/pa>.
This source supports the bounded present-day sense only. The learner note must
not claim that the Punjabi University dictionary attests this spelling: its
exact lookup currently returns “Match Not Found.”

The Punjabi origin of **ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ** remains unknown in the sources used here. As
a narrow memory comparison, Apte records the matching Sanskrit form
*dinacaryā* as “daily occupation” or “daily routine of business” on p. 0813-1:
<https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/APScan/2020/web/webtc/getword.php?key=dinacaryA&filter=roman&accent=no&transLit=slp1>.
The lesson may report that comparison, but it must not claim a Sanskrit-to-
Punjabi transmission or that the two visible pieces form a productive Punjabi
derivation.

This is the twentieth prerequisite tranche exposed by Punjabi A2 mock drafting.
It does not by itself unblock mock 1 and is not mock-readiness, attainment,
external-equivalence, or human-validation evidence. Authored and generated
