# reír — the prefix that is not there

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C315-BODY-10, ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la risa**, the noun for the sound itself. Say

ridiculous. Name the verb *risa* belongs to.

## You'll want to know: reír
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C315-BODY-10] -->

**reír** — to laugh. **reírse de** — to laugh *at* something.

*Nos reímos mucho* — we laughed a lot. *No te rías* — do not laugh. And
**sonreír**, with *son-* from Latin *sub-*, under: a laugh kept low, which is
exactly what a smile is.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-05] -->

Here is the trap, and it is worth setting off deliberately.

**reír** looks like **re-** plus **ír**. It is neither. There is no prefix and
there is no *ir*. The whole word is Latin **rīdēre**, to laugh, arriving in one
piece — the *r-e* at the front is nothing more than the first two letters of the root.

You met **batería**, a word that wears an *ending* it never earned.
This is the other half of that lesson: a word that wears a *prefix* it never
had. Spanish has both kinds, and neither announces itself.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C438-CUERPO-05] -->

The written accent on **reír** is physical evidence, and you can read it.

*Rīdēre* had a **d** between its vowels: *ri-d-ere*. Spanish wore intervocalic
**d** away in word after word, and when it went here it left the **i** and the
**e** standing next to each other with nothing between them.

Two vowels side by side would normally collapse into one syllable. These do not,
because they were never together — a consonant used to hold them apart. The
accent on the **í** is how Spanish writes down that refusal: *re-ír*, two
syllables, the join still visible where the **d** used to be.

The family keeps the evidence too: **la risa** lost the same **d**, while
**ridículo** was borrowed back from Latin late enough to keep it. One root, one
missing consonant, and a word on each side of the loss.
