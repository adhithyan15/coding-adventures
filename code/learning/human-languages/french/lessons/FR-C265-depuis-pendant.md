# depuis, pendant — how long, and still going

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-PLUS-QUE-01, FR-LEX-HABITER-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the train is faster than the car". (*Le train est plus
rapide que la voiture.*) Then "I live in Paris". (*J'habite à Paris.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a length, and French asks whether it is over.

## You'll want to know: depuis, and the present
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-DEPUIS-PRESENT-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C187-TEMPS187-05, FR-LEX-AN-09, FR-LEX-TROIS-04, FR-LEX-JANVIER-01] -->

> **J'habite** ici **depuis** trois ans. — I have been living here for three
> years.
> **J'habite** ici **depuis** janvier. — I have lived here since January.

Listen to the verb. English says *have been living*; French says
**j'habite**, the plain **present**, because the living is still going on.
**Depuis** takes a length, "for three years", or a starting point, "since
January", with the same present. The question is *depuis quand*, since when,
or *depuis combien de temps*, for how long.

Everyday speech everywhere has a second way to say it, with the length in
front: ***Ça fait trois ans que** j'habite ici.*

## Grammar Lens: pendant, and il y a
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-PENDANT-DUREE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-ILYA-01, FR-LEX-DEUX-03, FR-GRAM-PASSE-COMPOSE-ETRE-01] -->

When the stretch has **ended**, the verb goes into the passé composé and the
length takes **pendant**, "for, during":

> **J'ai habité** à Paris **pendant** deux ans. — I lived in Paris for two
> years. *(and then I left)*

One English word, *for*, splits in two. *Depuis* with the present: still
going on. *Pendant* with the passé composé: over.

And the **il y a** you know as "there is" also means "ago", with an event:

> *Je suis arrivé ici **il y a** trois ans.* — I arrived here three years
> ago.

So one life can carry all three: *Je suis arrivé il y a trois ans. J'habite
ici depuis trois ans. Avant, j'ai habité à Paris pendant deux ans.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-PENDANT-QUE-02] -->

**depuis** is *de*, "from", plus **puis**, "then" (EtymDB 2.1): "from then
on". *Puis* goes back to a spoken Latin *postius*, built on *post*, "after".
**pendant** is the participle of **pendre**, "to hang", from Latin
*pendēre* (EtymDB 2.1): *pendant deux ans* is, at root, "two years hanging",
the stretch while the matter hung open.
