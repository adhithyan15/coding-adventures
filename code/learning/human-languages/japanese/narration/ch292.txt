Japanese, chapter 292: A Long Life.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おいる (oiru) — to grow old; to age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a grave, then the Japanese for to pray.

You'll want to know: おいる.
おいる — oiru — "to grow old; to age".
ちちも すこし おいました。 — chichi mo sukoshi oimashita — "My father has aged a little too."
Polite おいます, oimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): そふが おいました。, sofu ga oimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おいる (oiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

としより — toshiyori — "an old person; the elderly".
その まちには としよりが おおいです。 — sono machi ni wa toshiyori ga ōi desu — "There are many old people in that town."

The word, taken apart.
としより (toshiyori) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from toshi, "years", and yori, "gathering" (from yoru) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
じんせい (jinsei) — a life; one's lifetime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to grow old, then the Japanese for an old person.

You'll want to know: じんせい.
じんせい — jinsei — "a life; one's lifetime".
日本人の じんせいは ながく なりました。 — nihonjin no jinsei wa nagaku narimashita — "Japanese people's lives have grown longer."

The word, taken apart.
じんせい (jinsei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jin, "person" (Middle Chinese nyin) and sei, "life" (srjaeng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
