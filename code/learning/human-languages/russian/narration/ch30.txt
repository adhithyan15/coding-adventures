Russian, chapter 30: Hair, Finger, Neck, Shoulder, Knee.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
волосы (vólosy) — hair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the belly, then the Russian for a tooth.

You'll want to know: волосы.
волосы (vólosy) — "hair". Plural, like English "locks".

The word, taken apart.
волосы (vólosy) is the plural of волос, "a hair", from Proto-Slavic volsŭ — inherited. Vasmer compares Avestan varəsa-, "hair". Russian -оло- faces Church Slavonic -ла- again: the church form is власы, an old word for "hair".

Lesson 2 of 5.
палец (pálets) — a finger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tooth, then the Russian for hair.

You'll want to know: палец.
палец (pálets) — "a finger".

The word, taken apart.
палец (pálets) from Proto-Slavic palĭcĭ — inherited; older Russian texts use it for "thumb" (Sokolova, in Trubachev's note to Vasmer). It is usually tied to Latin pollex, "thumb", though Vasmer notes doubts about that link.

Lesson 3 of 5.
шея (shéya) — the neck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for hair, then the Russian for a finger.

You'll want to know: шея.
шея (shéya) — "the neck".

The word, taken apart.
шея (shéya) from Proto-Slavic šija — inherited. Vasmer thinks it probably first meant "collar", from the root of шить, "to sew"; the links proposed with Latin and Albanian words he calls unreliable.

Lesson 4 of 5.
плечо (plechó) — a shoulder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a finger, then the Russian for the neck.

You'll want to know: плечо.
плечо (plechó) — "a shoulder".

The word, taken apart.
плечо (plechó) from Proto-Slavic pleťe — inherited. Vasmer compares Latvian plecs, "shoulder", and words for "flat" such as Greek plax, "a level plain", but he also gives a second possible source, so the deeper origin is open.
