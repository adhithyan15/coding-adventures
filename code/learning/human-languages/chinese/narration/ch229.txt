Mandarin Chinese, chapter 229: Keeping a Pet.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
chǒngwù — pet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for serious (of illness), then the Mandarin for to nod.

You'll want to know: chǒngwù.
chǒngwù — "pet".
nǐ jiā yǒu chǒngwù ma — Have you got a pet at home?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chǒngwù is built from chǒng 'to dote on' and wù 'thing', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trhjowngX and mjut (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to love and favour'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what I do.

Lesson 3 of 6.
liùgǒu — to walk the dog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a pet, then the Mandarin for to keep an animal.

You'll want to know: liùgǒu.
liùgǒu — "to walk the dog".
wǒ měitiān wǎnshang liùgǒu — I walk the dog every evening.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liùgǒu is built from liù 'to stroll' and gǒu 'dog'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese kuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'dog'; the dictionary glosses the first, ljuw there, 'to linger', and the falling tone of liù is not the regular outcome of that form.

Lesson 4 of 6.
zhuā — to scratch; to grab.

You'll want to know: zhuā.
zhuā — "to scratch; to grab".
bié pà wǒ de māo bù zhuā rén — Don't worry, my cat doesn't scratch.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhuā is inherited from Middle Chinese tsraew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to scratch and pinch'; the vowel of zhuā is not the regular outcome of that form.

Lesson 5 of 6.
mō — to stroke; to touch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to walk the dog, then the Mandarin for to scratch.

You'll want to know: mō.
mō — "to stroke; to touch".
wǒ kěyǐ mō nǐ de gǒu ma — Can I stroke your dog?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mō is inherited from Middle Chinese mu (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to feel with the hand'. The vowel and the level tone of mō are not the regular outcome of that form.
