Japanese, chapter 434: Thanks and Sympathy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かんしゃする (kansha suru) — to be grateful; to thank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an interest, then the Japanese for admiration.

You'll want to know: かんしゃする.
かんしゃする — kansha suru — "to be grateful; to thank".
りょうしんに かんしゃしています。 — ryōshin ni kansha shite imasu — "I am grateful to my parents."
Polite かんしゃします, kanshashimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): がくせいが せんせいに かんしゃしました。, gakusei ga sensei ni kansha shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
かんしゃする (kansha suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango) kansha with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kan, "emotion" (Middle Chinese komX) and sha, "thank" (zjaeH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
きのどく (kinodoku) — pitiable; unfortunate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be grateful, then the Japanese for to feel sympathy.

You'll want to know: きのどく.
きのどく — kinodoku — "pitiable; unfortunate".
ともだちは さいふを なくして、きのどくでした。 — tomodachi wa saifu o nakushite, kinodoku deshita — "My friend lost his wallet, poor thing."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
きのどく (kinodoku) is a mixed phrase: Sino-Japanese ki, "spirit" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), and doku, "poison" (dowk), both borrowed from Chinese, joined by the native particle no, "of" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
みじめ (mijime) — miserable; wretched.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: みじめ.
みじめ — mijime — "miserable; wretched".
あめの なかの ねこは みじめでした。 — ame no naka no neko wa mijime deshita — "The cat out in the rain was miserable."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
みじめ (mijime) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).
