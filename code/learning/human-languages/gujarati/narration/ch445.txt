Gujarati, chapter 445: Nests and Night Sounds.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
માળો (māḷo) — a nest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a cluster, then the Gujarati for to be fragrant.

You'll want to know: માળો.
માળો — māḷo — "a nest".
ચકલીએ બારી પાસે માળો બનાવ્યો. caklīe bārī pāse māḷo banāvyo. The sparrow built a nest by the window.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
માળો (māḷo) goes back to māḍa, "an upper storey", a form CDIAL reconstructs; CDIAL lists a Gujarati word for a loft (CDIAL 9996). How it came to mean a nest is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
આગિયો (āgiyo) — a firefly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be fragrant, then the Gujarati for a nest.

You'll want to know: આગિયો.
આગિયો — āgiyo — "a firefly".
ચોમાસાની રાતે ખેતરમાં આગિયા ચમકે છે. comāsānī rāte khetarmā̃ āgiyā camke che. On monsoon nights fireflies glow in the fields.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
આગિયો (āgiyo) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit agnika, "fiery"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "a glow-worm" (CDIAL 57): the little insect with a fire in it.

ચોમાસામાં મોર ટહુકે છે. comāsāmā̃ mor ṭahuke che. In the monsoon the peacock calls.
Past (it …): તે મોર ટહુક્યો, te mor ṭahukyo.

The word, taken apart.
ટહુકવું (ṭahukvũ) sounds like the call it names. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.
