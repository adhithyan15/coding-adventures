French, chapter 446: Chez le cordonnier.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le cordonnier — the cobbler, the shoe mender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the haberdasher’s, then the French for the wine merchant.

You'll want to know: le cordonnier.
le cordonnier — "the cobbler, the shoe mender".
Le cordonnier a réparé mes bottes en une journée. The cobbler mended my boots in a day.
Les cordonniers sont toujours les plus mal chaussés — the cobbler's children go barefoot.

The word, taken apart.
cordonnier is an altered form of an older word for a worker in fine leather, named after a city famous for it. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le serrurier — the locksmith.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to resole, then the French for to polish.

You'll want to know: le serrurier.
le serrurier — "the locksmith".
Elle a perdu ses clés et elle a appelé un serrurier. She lost her keys and called a locksmith.
La serrure — the lock.

The word, taken apart.
serrurier is built on serrure 'lock', from serrer 'to close tight', which goes back to Vulgar Latin serrāre, built on Latin sera 'bar for a door' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
recoudre — to sew back on, to stitch up.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: recoudre.
recoudre — "to sew back on, to stitch up".
La dame a recousu le bouton de ma veste. The lady sewed the button back on my jacket.
Irregular: je recouds, il a recousu.

The word, taken apart.
recoudre is built from re- 'again' and coudre 'to sew', which goes back to Latin consuere, from suere 'to sew' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.
