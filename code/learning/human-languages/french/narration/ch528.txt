French, chapter 528: Faire le ménage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
balayer — to sweep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for wardrobe, then the French for to hang.

You'll want to know: balayer.
balayer — "to sweep".
Tous les soirs, il balaie la cuisine. Every evening he sweeps the kitchen.
Je balaie or je balaye; both are correct.

The word, taken apart.
balayer is built on balai "broom" (EtymDB), a word of Celtic origin, first the name of the broom plant.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: épousseter.
épousseter — "to dust".
Elle époussette les meubles du salon. She dusts the furniture in the living room.
Spelling: j'époussette. Built on la poussière.

The word, taken apart.
épousseter is built on an old word pousse "dust", which goes back to Latin pulvis "dust" (Lewis & Short); la poussière has the same source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
frotter — to rub, to scrub.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to sweep, then the French for to dust.

You'll want to know: frotter.
frotter — "to rub, to scrub".
J'ai frotté la tache, mais elle est encore là. I scrubbed the stain, but it is still there.
Se frotter les mains — to rub one's hands.

The word, taken apart.
frotter goes back to Old French froter. The origin is not settled; a link with Latin fricāre "to rub" is often proposed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'éponge — sponge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to dust, then the French for to rub.

You'll want to know: l'éponge.
l'éponge — "sponge".
Il nettoie la table de la cuisine avec une éponge. He wipes down the kitchen table with a sponge.
Feminine. Passer l'éponge — to let bygones be bygones.

The word, taken apart.
l'éponge is inherited, through Old French esponge, from Latin spongia (EtymDB), a borrowing from Greek spongiá "a sponge".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la serpillière — floor cloth, mop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to rub, then the French for sponge.

You'll want to know: la serpillière.
la serpillière — "floor cloth, mop".
Après le repas, je passe la serpillière. After the meal I mop the floor.
Passer la serpillière — to mop. In the north of France and in Belgium also la wassingue; in Québec la moppe. Also spelt serpillère.

The word, taken apart.
la serpillière goes back to Old French sarpilliere "a coarse cloth". The origin is not settled.
