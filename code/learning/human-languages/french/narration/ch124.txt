Lesson 1 of 5.
le boulanger — a baker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a shop assistant, then the French for a farmer.

You'll want to know: le boulanger.
le boulanger — "a baker". The person behind la boulangerie.

The word, taken apart.
boulanger from Picard boulenc, probably from Middle Dutch bolle, "a round loaf": a maker of round loaves. (EtymDB 2.1 has its Old French form.)

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'avocat — a lawyer; an avocado.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a student, then the French for a driver.

You'll want to know: l'avocat.
l'avocat — "a lawyer; an avocado".

The word, taken apart.
avocat — two words in one. The lawyer from Latin advocatus, "one called in to help" (EtymDB 2.1) — a learned borrowing. The fruit from Spanish aguacate, from Nahuatl ahuacatl (EtymDB 2.1), reshaped to look like the lawyer word.

The word, taken apart.
ingénieur — built from Old French engin, "a clever device, a war machine", from Latin ingenium, "talent" (EtymDB 2.1), with -eur, later respelt after the Latin.
