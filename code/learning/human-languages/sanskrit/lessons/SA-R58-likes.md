# मह्यं … रोचते — the liking frame, reviewed

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-FRAME-ROCATE-01, SA-PRON-DATIVE-ME-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I like the fruit" in Sanskrit, and name the subject of the
sentence you said.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-FRAME-ROCATE-01, SA-LEX-ICCHATI-WANT-01, SA-LEX-SHAKNOTI-CAN-01] -->

Three sentences about what a person is able to do, wants to do, and likes — and
two of the three share a shape while the third breaks it deliberately.

- **बालः पठितुम् शक्नोति** — the boy **can** read
- **बालः पठितुम् इच्छति** — the boy **wants to** read
- **मह्यं फलं रोचते** — I **like** the fruit

The first two put the person in the nominative and an infinitive before the
verb. The third puts the person in the **dative** and makes the liked thing the
subject.

That is not an inconsistency to be smoothed over — it is the fact about Sanskrit
worth carrying out of this chapter. Ability and desire belong to the person.
Pleasing is something the **thing** does.
