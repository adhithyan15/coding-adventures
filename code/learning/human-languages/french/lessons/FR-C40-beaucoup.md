# beaucoup — "a beautiful blow"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You can say *some* four ways and *none* one way. You have no way at
all to say **a lot**.

- [YOU RECALL: two chapters back, the four partitives collapsing after a negative
  — *pas de*]

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-BEAUCOUP-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-DE-PREP-01, FR-PHON-CONSONNE-FINALE-01, FR-LEX-CAFE-01, FR-LEX-PAIN-01] -->

> **beaucoup** — a lot, much, many.

> **beaucoup de café** — a lot of coffee. · **beaucoup d'amis** — a lot of
> friends.

Note what follows it: a bare **de**, exactly as after a negative. Any word of
quantity in French takes *de* and never *du* or *des*, for the same reason — the
quantity word is already doing the measuring, so the measuring article drops
out.

**beaucoup** is **beau** + **coup**, "a **beautiful blow**." *Coup* is Latin
**colaphus**, a punch on the ear, and English borrowed it far more politely as
*coup d'état* and *a coup*. A word from a fistfight became the ordinary French
for *a lot* — the promotion any word earns by carrying emphasis often enough.

The **p** is silent, as final consonants are: *boh-KOO*.
