# Unit 12: Months & Seasons

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Recall the days of the week and their Roman-god etymology.
Months follow the exact same pattern — literally the same calendar.

## New Material

**enero** — January
> ← Latin *Ianuarius*, "month of Janus" (the two-faced god of doorways and
> beginnings — cognate with English *January*, *janitor* [door-keeper]).

**marzo** — March
> ← Latin *Martius*, "month of Mars" — the same *Martis* root as *martes*
> (Tuesday).

**octubre** — October
> ← Latin *October*, "the eighth month" (*octo*, from the old Roman
> calendar — same root you met in Unit 4's numbers).

**la primavera** (fem.) — spring
> ← Latin *prima vera*, literally "first spring/green."

**el verano** (masc.) — summer
> ← Latin *veranum tempus* ("spring-time") — curiously, the word drifted
> from meaning spring to meaning summer over the centuries.

**el otoño** (masc.) — autumn
> ← Latin *autumnus*, borrowed directly into English unchanged as *autumn*.

**el invierno** (masc.) — winter
> ← Latin *hibernus* (→ English *hibernate*, literally "to winter over").

## Grammar Lens

Spanish months and seasons are **not capitalized** — *enero*, not *Enero*
— unlike English, which always capitalizes month names. Days of the week
(Chapter 1) follow the same lowercase rule. This is a simple orthographic
difference, but a persistent one to unlearn from English writing habits.
