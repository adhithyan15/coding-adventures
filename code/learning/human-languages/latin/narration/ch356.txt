Latin, chapter 356: Cēna appōnitur.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
appōnō, appōnere — to serve up, to put before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to pour in, then the Latin for to strain.

You'll want to know: appōnō, appōnere.
appōnō, appōnere — "to serve up, to put before".
Servī cēnam amīcīs appōnēbant. The slaves were serving dinner to the friends.
appōnō, appōnere, apposuī, appositum: ad + pōnō (taught), to set food before someone.

The word, taken apart.
appōnō is built on pōnō, "to put", with ad-, "to, beside" (Lewis & Short): to put before someone.

Lesson 2 of 7.
ministrō, ministrāre — to wait at table, to serve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to strain, then the Latin for to serve up.

You'll want to know: ministrō, ministrāre.
ministrō, ministrāre — "to wait at table, to serve".
Puerī vīnum ministrābant. The slave boys were serving the wine.
ministrō, ministrāre, ministrāvī, ministrātum, from minister, 'servant'. English minister.

The word, taken apart.
ministrō is built on minister, "servant, attendant" (EtymDB), itself from minus, "less", as magister is from magis. English minister comes from it.

triclīnium, triclīniī — "a dining room; a dining couch".
Hospitēs in triclīnium intrāvērunt. The guests went into the dining room.
Neuter, a Greek loan: three couches round a table, three guests on each.

The word, taken apart.
triclīnium was borrowed from Greek triklinion, from treis, "three", and klinē, "couch" (EtymDB): the room with three couches.

Lesson 4 of 7.
discumbō, discumbere — to recline at table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to wait at table, then the Latin for a dining room.

You'll want to know: discumbō, discumbere.
discumbō, discumbere — "to recline at table".
Omnēs discubuērunt, et cēna incēpit. Everyone took their places at table, and the dinner began.
discumbō, discumbere, discubuī, discubitum. Romans ate lying on the left elbow.

The word, taken apart.
discumbō is built on -cumbō, "to lie down", with dis-, "apart" (EtymDB): each guest lying down in his place.

Lesson 5 of 7.
ferculum, ferculī — a course, a dish; a tray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dining room, then the Latin for to recline at table.

You'll want to know: ferculum, ferculī.
ferculum, ferculī — "a course, a dish; a tray".
Prīmum ferculum ōva et olīvae erant. The first course was eggs and olives.
Neuter, from ferō: a tray that carries food, and so a course. In a triumph, a ferculum carried the spoils.

The word, taken apart.
ferculum is built on ferō, "to carry", with the tool ending -culum (EtymDB): a carrier, a tray.
