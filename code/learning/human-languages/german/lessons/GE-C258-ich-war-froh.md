# ich war froh — how it felt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-LEX-MUEDE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was tired". (*Ich war müde.*) That *war* is a Präteritum
German says out loud, in speech as well as in books.

## You'll want to know: froh
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-FROH-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C60-GEFUEHLE1-05] -->

**froh** — "glad": *froh*, with a long *o*.

> *Ich war froh.* — I was glad.

*Froh* and *glücklich* are close. *Glücklich* is happy, a state that can
fill a day or a life. *Froh* is often glad about one thing, and relieved:
the train came after all, the exam is over.

## Grammar Lens: the feeling, then the reason
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-GEFUEHL-DASS-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C60-GEFUEHLE1-04, GE-LEX-C169-QUAL169-04, GE-LEX-C169-QUAL169-03, GE-LEX-C169-QUAL169-05, GE-GRAMMAR-DASS-VERB-ENDE-01, GE-LEX-WEIL-01] -->

*Ich war* takes any feeling you own: *froh*, *glücklich*, *traurig*,
*stolz*, *nervös*, *wütend*, *müde*. Then the reason, with **dass** or
**weil**, and the verb at the end:

> *Ich war froh, dass der Zug endlich* **kam**. — I was glad the train
> came at last.
> *Ich war traurig, weil meine Großmutter krank* **war**. — I was sad
> because my grandmother was ill.
> *Ich war nervös, weil der Zug Verspätung* **hatte**. — I was nervous
> because the train was late.

*Dass* gives the thing you feel about; *weil* gives the cause. After
*froh*, *dass* is the usual choice. In the present it works the same way:
*Ich bin froh, dass du kommst.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-GESPROCHEN-01] -->

**froh** ← Middle High German *vrō* ← Old High German *frō* (EtymDB 2.1).
You met it inside *freut mich* in chapter two. EtymDB also sets English
*frolic* beside it and links *frolic* to Dutch *vrolijk*, "cheerful"; the
usual account is that English borrowed the word from Dutch.
