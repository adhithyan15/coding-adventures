# la arroba — Spanish has a word where English has a noise

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CORREO-ELECTRONICO] -->

aloud in Spanish, and stop at the symbol in the middle.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C428-01]; assesses=[] -->

**la arroba** — the **@** sign.

English calls it *at*, which is a preposition pressed into service. Spanish has
a **noun** for it, and the noun is much older than e-mail.

An *arroba* was a unit of weight — about eleven and a half kilos — from Arabic
**ar-rubʿ**, "the quarter," because it was a quarter of a *quintal*. Merchants
wrote it with a looped **a**, and that shorthand is the symbol on your keyboard.

So when a Spanish speaker reads out an address, they are naming a **medieval
unit of weight**:

> ana **arroba** ejemplo punto example

You have the other two pieces already: **punto** for the dot, from the
punctuation chapter, and **guion** if the address carries one.

## Grammar Lens: reading an address aloud
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-01, ES-ORTH-C424-07] -->

| symbol | said aloud |
|---|---|
| @ | arroba |
| . | punto |
| - | guion |

A listening paper dictates an address using exactly these words, and the whole
task is unreadable without the first one. Note that the symbol is **feminine**:
*la arroba*, because the unit of weight was.
