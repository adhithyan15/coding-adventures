- [YOU RECALL: *ane*, *athavā*]

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-JOIN-PAN-01]; assesses=[GU-JOIN-ANE-CLAUSE-01, GU-NEG-NATHI-01] -->

**પણ** sets one thing against another. It joins two clauses the way *ane* does,
and it warns that the second will pull against the first:

> *(first sentence)* **પણ** *(second sentence)* — "… **but** …"

It works especially well with what you learned last chapter, because the
sharpest contrast a beginner can draw is between something that is and something
that **નથી**.

## Grammar Lens: the same word, moved, means "also"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-JOIN-PAN-ALSO-01]; assesses=[GU-JOIN-PAN-01] -->

Put **પણ** between two clauses and it is *but*. Put it directly **after a
noun** and it becomes **also**:

> **ચા પણ** — *chā paṇ* — "**tea too**"

Nothing about the word changes. Only where it stands changes, and position
alone carries the difference. This is the first time in this book that a word's
job has depended on its place in the sentence rather than on its ending.
