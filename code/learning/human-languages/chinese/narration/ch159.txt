Mandarin Chinese, chapter 159: Rooms at Home.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
wòshì — bedroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to stand up, then the Mandarin for to be used to.

You'll want to know: wòshì.
wòshì — "bedroom".
wǒ de wòshì hěn xiǎo — My bedroom is small.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wòshì is built from wò 'to lie down' and shì 'room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwaH and syit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a room to lie down in.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
书房 (shūfáng) — study; a room for books and work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bedroom, then the Mandarin for a living room.

You'll want to know: 书房.
书房 — shūfáng — "study; a room for books and work".
我的书都在书房里 wǒ de shū dōu zài shūfáng lǐ — All my books are in the study.
The new character, in pieces: 房 (fáng) — eight strokes in two pieces: on top a door of four strokes (a dot, a horizontal that turns down, a horizontal closing it, a long stroke falling to the left), and under it the four strokes of the piece read fāng. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
书房 (shūfáng) is built from 书 'book' and 房 (fáng) 'room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syo and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a room': the room for books.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a door over fāng.
房 (fáng).
Two pieces, top over bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The door, four strokes. A dot on top. A horizontal that turns down at its right end. A horizontal under it, closing the box. Then a long stroke from the top left falling down to the lower left: the side of the door.
The piece read fāng, four strokes, under the door. A dot. A long horizontal. A horizontal that turns down and hooks back to the left. Last, a stroke falling to the left through the middle.
4 + 4 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 房 (fáng), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 房 (fáng)? (Eight.) Which piece is on top? (The door, four strokes.) Which stroke of the lower piece hooks? (The third, the one that turns down.)

Lesson 5 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a living room, then the Mandarin for a study at home.

You'll want to know: yángtái.
yángtái — "balcony".
wǒmen jiā de yángtái hěn dà — Our balcony is big.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yángtái is built from yáng 'the sunny side' and a word for a platform, both inherited from Middle Chinese, yang and doj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary writes the second another way and glosses it as raised square ground: a platform in the sun.

The word, taken apart.
chuānghu is built from chuāng 'window' and hu 'door', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsrhaewng and huX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary writes the first another way and glosses it 'an opening right through'; it calls the second half of a door. In the word the second loses its tone.
