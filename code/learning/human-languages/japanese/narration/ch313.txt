Japanese, chapter 313: What Remains.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きろく (kiroku) — a record; an account.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for formerly, then the Japanese for at that time.

You'll want to know: きろく.
きろく — kiroku — "a record; an account".
むかしの きろくが のこっています。 — mukashi no kiroku ga nokotte imasu — "Accounts from long ago survive."

The word, taken apart.
きろく (kiroku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "account" (Middle Chinese kiH) and roku, "record" (ljowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
さかえる (sakaeru) — to prosper; to flourish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for at that time, then the Japanese for a record.

You'll want to know: さかえる.
さかえる — sakaeru — "to prosper; to flourish".
むかし この まちは さかえていました。 — mukashi kono machi wa sakaete imashita — "Long ago this town was prosperous."
Polite さかえます, sakaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): まちが さかえました。, machi ga sakaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
さかえる (sakaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
