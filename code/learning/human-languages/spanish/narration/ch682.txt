Spanish, chapter 682: Lo que hay en la calle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la farola — the street lamp, the lamp post.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for holiday, then the Spanish for to serve.

You'll want to know: la farola.
la farola — "the street lamp, the lamp post".
Las farolas se encienden a las nueve. The street lamps come on at nine.
A Spain word for a tall street lamp.

The word, taken apart.
farola is built on farol "lantern". The word is usually linked to faro "lighthouse", from Latin pharus, though the details are not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: el bordillo.
el bordillo — "the kerb, the curb".
El autobús para junto al bordillo. The bus stops next to the kerb.
A Spain word; elsewhere el borde de la acera or el cordón.

The word, taken apart.
bordillo is built on borde "edge", which comes from French bord (EtymDB), a word of Germanic origin.

Esta calle es peatonal. This is a pedestrian street.
Calle peatonal or zona peatonal — no cars.

The word, taken apart.
peatonal is built on peatón "pedestrian", which came from French piéton, built on pied "foot", from Latin pēs.
