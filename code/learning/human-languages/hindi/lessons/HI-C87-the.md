# थे — the third ending, and the politeness you have already paid for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have था and थी for one person. Name the pronoun for *we*, and
guess which ending a group will want.

## You'll want to know: थे
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-03]; assesses=[] -->

**थे** (*the*) — "were."

| subject | earlier |
|---|---|
| हम | हम घर पर थे। |
| वे | वे स्टेशन पर थे। |
| आप | आप कहाँ थे? |

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-03, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01] -->

**-ए** is the plural ending on the same family of words as **-आ** and **-ई**, so
थे sits beside था and थी without being a new thing to learn.

The row worth pausing on is the last one. **आप** takes थे even when you are
speaking to exactly one person, for the same reason it takes हैं for one person:
the polite level in Hindi is built out of plural forms. Asking a single guest
*आप कहाँ थे?* is grammatically a question to a crowd, and that is the courtesy.

For a group of women, थे adds a nasal and becomes **थीं** — worth recognising on
a page now, and not worth drilling yet.

## Grammar Lens: the level decision, once more
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-03, HI-CONCEPT-C02-AAPTUM-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-02] -->

| system | at the तुम level | at the आप level |
|---|---|---|
| copula now | हो | हैं |
| command | बोलो | बोलिए |
| copula earlier | थे | थे |

The past does not split the two levels at all: तुम and आप both take थे. One
fewer decision, in the one place you might have expected another.
