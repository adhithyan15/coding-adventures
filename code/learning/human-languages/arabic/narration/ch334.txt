Arabic, chapter 334: A trial.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
محاكمة (muḥākama) — a trial.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a pirate, then the Arabic for to capture.

You'll want to know: محاكمة.
محاكمة — muḥākama — "a trial".
بدأت محاكمة (muḥākama) الرجل أمس. badaʾat muḥākamat ar-rajul ams. The man’s trial began yesterday.

The word, taken apart.
محاكمة (muḥākama) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-m (Lane), "to judge". Muḥākama is the verbal noun of Form III, "to bring someone to judgment".

You'll want to know: جلسة.
جلسة — jalsa — "a hearing, a session".
تأجلت جلسة (jalsa) المحكمة أسبوعا. taʾajjalat jalsat al-maḥkama usbūʿan. The court hearing was put off for a week.
Any sitting: جلسة (jalsa) عمل, a working session.

The word, taken apart.
جلسة (jalsa) is built on the native Arabic root j-l-s (Lane), the root of the taught jalasa, to sit. The faʿla pattern gives one sitting.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أدان (adāna) — to convict; to condemn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hearing, then the Arabic for the defence.

You'll want to know: أدان.
أدان — adāna — "to convict; to condemn".
أدانت المحكمة الرجل بالسرقة. adānat al-maḥkama ar-rajul bi-s-sariqa. The court convicted the man of theft.
Present: يدين, yudīn.
Of words: أدان (adāna) الهجوم, he condemned the attack.

The word, taken apart.
أدان (adāna) is built on the native Arabic root d-y-n (Lane), the root of dayn, a debt, and of a judgment that is owed. Modern Arabic gives Form IV adāna the sense "to find guilty".

You'll want to know: براءة.
براءة — barāʾa — "innocence".

The word, taken apart.
براءة (barāʾa) is built on the native Arabic root b-r-ʾ (Lane), "to be free, clear of a thing". Barāʾa is being clear of blame.
