# spērō — I hope

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C112-WANT112-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C287-AFFECTUS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was happy". (*Laetus eram.*) Then the noun for "hope".
(*Spēs.*)

[PAUSE 1s] A feeling looks back. A hope looks forward, and Latin has a verb
for it built on the noun you own.

## You'll want to know: spērō
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C287-SPERO-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-VALEO-FAMILY-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

**spērō, spērāre** — "to hope". It takes three shapes.

> **Pācem spērō.** — I hope for peace.
> **Ut spērō, valēs.** — As I hope, you are well.
> **Spērō tē valēre.** — I hope you are well.

[PAUSE 2s] The first hopes for a thing, in the accusative. The second tucks
*ut spērō*, "as I hope", into a sentence; Cicero writes it in his letters.
The third is the accusative and infinitive of *putō*: *tē*, then *valēre*.

[PAUSE 1s] When the hope is about later, Latin prefers an infinitive made
for the future, a form still to come. For now, hope for things, and for
how things are.

## Grammar Lens: cupiō, volō + infinitive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C287-CUPIO-INF-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C111-WANT111-02, LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-02, LA-LEX-C111-WANT111-03] -->

What you long **to do** goes with **cupiō**, "I long", or **volō**, "I
want", and a plain infinitive:

> **Rōmam vidēre cupiō.** — I long to see Rome.
> **Mercātor esse volō.** — I want to be a merchant.

[PAUSE 2s] *Mercātor* stays nominative: it is the same person as the one
who wants. And **optō**, "to wish for, to choose", hopes for a thing, like
*spērō*: *nāvem optō*.

## The word, taken apart: spērō
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C112-WANT112-02] -->

**spērō** is the verb of *spēs*, "hope", and EtymDB 2.1 traces it, like
the noun, to Proto-Indo-European *\*speh₁-*, "to prosper, to turn out well".
Put *dē-*, "away", in front and you have *dēspērō*, "to give up hope"
(Lewis & Short), which reached English as *despair* and *desperate*.
