# अर (ar) — put two familiar things together

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-02, MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **कुत्तो** (*kutto*, a dog). Then say **बिल्ली** (*billī*, a
cat). Keep the two words separate for one more breath.

## You'll want to know: अर
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-CONJ-AR-01]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-02, MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-03] -->

> **अर** — *ar* — **and**, joining this thing with that thing

The Government of India's *Linguistic Survey of India — Rajasthan, Part I*
records Marwari **अर** as the particle that links words or groups of words. Its
own example joins a dog and a cat. This lesson keeps the book's already-taught
word **बिल्ली** for cat.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

The official survey attests the Marwari form and its “and / or” use, but does
not derive it. Its origin remains unknown in the source used for this lesson.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-COORDINATE-AND-01]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-AR-01, MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-02, MW-LEX-C70-NATURE70-03] -->

Put **अर** between the two things:

> **कुत्तो अर बिल्ली** — *kutto ar billī* — a dog and a cat

Nothing changes on either noun. The small joining word does the new work.
