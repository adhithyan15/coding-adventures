Gujarati, chapter 148: What I did.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
મેં ચા પીધી — the past that agrees with the thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I went and she came. Then three old words: પીવું, to drink; ચા, tea; પાણી, water.

Grammar Lens: હું becomes મેં.
હું ગયો keeps I in its plain form. A verb that does something to something, like drinking tea or eating rice, changes that in the past: the doer is marked, and હું becomes મેં (mẽ).
મેં ચા પીધી. — mẽ cā pīdhī. — I drank tea.
મેં is the same for a man and a woman. The verb has stopped listening to the drinker.

You'll want to know: the verb follows the tea.
પીધી ends in -ી because ચા is feminine. Change the drink and the verb changes with it:
મેં ચા પીધી. — mẽ cā pīdhī — tea, feminine;
મેં પાણી પીધું. — mẽ pāṇī pīdhũ — water, neuter;
મેં દૂધ પીધું. — mẽ dūdh pīdhũ — milk, neuter.
This is the agreement મારી ચા showed you: the ending follows the thing. English has nothing like it, so it is worth saying slowly a few times.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: mẽ cā pīdhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mẽ dūdh pīdhũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: મેં તમારી ચા પીધી! — mẽ tamārī cā pīdhī!, I drank your tea!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: hũ gayo and mẽ cā pīdhī: which verb follows the doer, and which the drink]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that you drank water, then that you drank tea.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does હું become before a past verb with an object? (મેં.) What does that verb agree with? (The object: ચા gives પીધી.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
તેણે, તેમણે — who did it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I drank tea and I drank water, and say why the two verbs end differently.

Grammar Lens: one person.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
who: I. plain: હું. in the object past: મેં.
who: you (familiar). plain: તું. in the object past: તેં.
who: he, she. plain: તે. in the object past: તેણે.
તેણે ચા પીધી. — teṇe cā pīdhī. — He drank tea. She drank tea.
તેણે is the form CDIAL records for Gujarati under the old Sanskrit stem ta- (CDIAL 5612), the stem behind તે and તેઓ.

Grammar Lens: more than one.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
who: we. plain: અમે. in the object past: અમે.
who: you (respectful). plain: તમે. in the object past: તમે.
who: they. plain: તેઓ. in the object past: તેમણે.
અમે and તમે do not change at all: અમે ચા પીધી, તમે પાણી પીધું. Only તેઓ moves, to તેમણે, built on the તેમ- of તેમને.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: teṇe pāṇī pīdhũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તમે ચા પીધી? — tame cā pīdhī?, did you drink tea?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તેમણે દૂધ પીધું — temṇe dūdh pīdhũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which two doers keep their plain form? (અમે and તમે.) What does તેઓ become? (તેમણે.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
કર્યું — the regular past: stem and -્યું.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say she drank tea and they drank milk. Then કામ કરવું, to work, to do work.

Grammar Lens: -્યું on the stem.
Most verbs build this past in one move: take the stem and add -્યું, a virama and then યું, so the stem's last consonant joins the ય.
કર- becomes કર્યું — karyũ — did (the ર rides on top as a small hook);
આપ- becomes આપ્યું — āpyũ — gave;
લખ- becomes લખ્યું — lakhyũ — wrote;
કહ- becomes કહ્યું — kahyũ — said;
જો- becomes જોયું — joyũ — saw (a stem ending in a vowel takes યું with no virama).
મેં કામ કર્યું. — mẽ kām karyũ. — I did the work. તેણે પત્ર લખ્યો. — teṇe patra lakhyo. — She wrote a letter. મેં તેને પૈસા આપ્યા. — mẽ tene paisā āpyā. — I gave him the money. તેણે મને પત્ર આપ્યો. — teṇe mane patra āpyo. — She gave me a letter. મેં બે ફોન કર્યા. — mẽ be phon karyā. — I made two phone calls.
The four endings again: -્યો, -ી, -્યું, -્યા. પત્ર is masculine, so લખ્યો; પૈસા is plural, so આપ્યા. In the feminine the ય drops: કરી, આપી, જોઈ.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: mẽ kām karyũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: teṇe patra lakhyo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mẽ tene paisā āpyā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: તેમણે શું કહ્યું? — temṇe śũ kahyũ?, what did they say?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is added to a stem like કર- to make this past? (-્યું.) Why લખ્યો in she wrote a letter? (પત્ર is masculine.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
લીધું, ખાધું, પીધું — three pasts in -ધું.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I did the work and she wrote a letter. Then લેવું, to take, and ખાવું, to eat.

Grammar Lens: -ધું.
પીધું has been doing this since the first lesson of the chapter. Three short, common verbs share it: instead of -્યું they take -ધું.
લેવું becomes લીધું — līdhũ — took;
ખાવું becomes ખાધું — khādhũ — ate;
પીવું becomes પીધું — pīdhũ — drank.
Their endings are the usual four: ખાધો, ખાધી, ખાધું, ખાધા.
મેં ભાત ખાધો. — mẽ bhāt khādho. — I ate rice. તેણે રોટલી ખાધી. — teṇe roṭlī khādhī. — She ate a roṭlī. મેં ટિકિટ લીધી. — mẽ ṭikiṭ līdhī. — I took a ticket. તેણે ભાત લીધો. — teṇe bhāt līdho. — He took some rice. તેણે દવા લીધી. — teṇe davā līdhī. — He took the medicine.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: mẽ bhāt khādho]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: teṇe roṭlī khādhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mẽ ṭikiṭ līdhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that you ate rice and took the medicine.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which three verbs take -ધું? (લેવું, ખાવું, પીવું.) Why ખાધો with ભાત? (ભાત is masculine.)
