Mandarin Chinese, chapter 358: Real Manners.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
lǐmào — good manners; politeness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to criticize, then the Mandarin for to praise.

You'll want to know: lǐmào.
lǐmào — "good manners; politeness".
zhège háizi hěn yǒu lǐmào — This child has very good manners.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lǐmào is built from lǐ and mào, both inherited from Middle Chinese, lejX and maewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, quoting an older dictionary, 'that by which the spirits are served and blessing is brought', and gives the second only as an older spelling. How the pair came to mean manners is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

Lesson 2 of 6.
真正 (zhēnzhèng) — real; genuine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to praise, then the Mandarin for good manners.

You'll want to know: 真正.
真正 — zhēnzhèng — "real; genuine".
我觉得这是真正的中国菜 wǒ juéde zhè shì zhēnzhèng de Zhōngguó cài — I think this is real Chinese food.
The new character, in pieces: 真 (zhēn) — ten strokes: 十 shí on top, a narrow box with two bars inside it under that, a long bar across, and two short dots under it, left then right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
真正 (zhēnzhèng) is built from 真 (zhēn) 'true' and 正 'proper', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyin and tsyengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'true, as against false' and the second 'proper'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
真 (zhēn) — ten, a box, a bar and two dots.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — ten, a box, a bar and two dots and Guided Practice.

十 正.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shí, zhèng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — ten, a box, a bar and two dots.
真 (zhēn).
One piece, top to bottom. Count it in four parts, not ten strokes.
十 on top, two strokes: a horizontal, then a vertical down through it.
A narrow box, five strokes: a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, two short horizontals inside, then one closing the foot of the box.
A long horizontal across the whole width under the box.
Two short strokes at the foot: one falling to the left, then one falling to the right.
2 + 5 + 1 + 2 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 真 (zhēn), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What sits on top of 真 (zhēn)? (十.) How many bars inside the box? (Two, and one closing it.) What comes last? (Two short strokes, left then right.)

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: 面子.
面子 — miànzi — "face; your standing in others' eyes".
他很要面子 tā hěn yào miànzi — He cares a lot about saving face.

The word, taken apart.
面子 (miànzi) is built from 面 'the face' and 子, a noun ending, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjienH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to face' and the second 'a child'. The second is said light here.
