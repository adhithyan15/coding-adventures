しょくりょう (shokuryō) — food supplies; provisions.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: しょくりょう.
しょくりょう — shokuryō — "food supplies; provisions".
しょくりょうを かばんに いれました。 — shokuryō o kaban ni iremashita — "I put the food supplies in the bag."

The word, taken apart.
しょくりょう (shokuryō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shoku, "eat" (Middle Chinese zyik) and ryō, "materials" (lew) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.
じすいする (jisui suru) — to cook for oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for food supplies, then the Japanese for food costs.

You'll want to know: じすいする.
じすいする — jisui suru — "to cook for oneself".
あには まいにち じすいしています。 — ani wa mainichi jisuishiteimasu — "My elder brother cooks for himself every day."
Polite じすいします, jisuishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): あにが じすいしました。, ani ga jisuishimashita.

The word, taken apart.
じすいする (jisui suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun jisui with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "oneself" (Middle Chinese dzijH) and sui, "cook" (tsyhwe) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
まるめる (marumeru) — to roll into a ball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for food costs, then the Japanese for to cook for oneself.

You'll want to know: まるめる.
まるめる — marumeru — "to roll into a ball".
ごはんを まるめて、おにぎりを つくります。 — gohan o marumete, onigiri o tsukurimasu — "I roll the rice into balls to make rice balls."
Polite まるめます, marumemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): こどもが ねんどを まるめました。, kodomo ga nendo o marumemashita.

The word, taken apart.
まるめる (marumeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
