# भवान् (bhavān) — the respectful "you"

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NAMA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] To ask someone's name you need a word for "you" — and for a first
meeting, Sanskrit reaches for the polite one.

## Sounds you'll need

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-BHAVAN]; assesses=[] -->

*bha · vān*. The *bh* is one sound — a *b* released with breath behind it. The
second beat carries a long *ā* and closes on *n*.

## The word, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*Bhavān* ("you," respectful) is not really a second-person word at all: it is a
third-person honorific — "your honour," from *bhavat* ("being, gracious one") —
and so it takes a *third-person* verb (*bhavān asti*, "your honour is"). For a
first meeting, use *bhavān*.

## Grammar Lens: politeness by grammatical person

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-POLITENESS-BY-PERSON]; assesses=[] -->

Where Hindi and Punjabi make respect *plural* (*āp … haiṁ*), Sanskrit makes it
*third-person*: to be polite you address someone as though speaking *about* them
— "is your honour well?" rather than "are you well?" Different trick, same
courtesy.
