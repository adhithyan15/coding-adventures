Russian, chapter 266: A Ticket, and a Missed Train.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
билет туда и обратно (bilét tudá i obrátno) — a return ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket", "a train" and "the till". (Bilét. Póyezd. Kássa.) Then ask the way to the till. (Kak proytí k kásse?)

You'll want to know: туда и обратно.
туда и обратно — tudá i obrátno — "there and back". At the till it is a return ticket:
— Один билет до Москвы, пожалуйста. — Туда и обратно? — odín bilét do Moskvý, pozhálusta. tudá i obrátno? — One ticket to Moscow, please. Return? — Да, туда и обратно. — da, tudá i obrátno — Yes, a return.
A single is в одну сторону — v odnú stóronu — "in one direction".

Grammar Lens: до a place, на a train.
A ticket goes до the place, as far as it, and the place changes as it did after до in the street: светофор, до светофора; Москва, до Москвы.
A ticket is на the train or the bus, and поезд and автобус keep their plain form: билет на поезд, билет на автобус — bilét na póyezd, bilét na avtóbus.

The exchange.
— Сколько стоит билет? — Тысяча рублей.
Skólko stóit bilét? — "How much is the ticket?" Týsyacha rubléy — "A thousand roubles." After тысяча, рубль takes the counting form рублей.

The word, taken apart.
обратно is the adverb of обратный, "reverse, return". EtymDB 2.1 lists обратный without a parent; by the usual account it is об-, "around", with the root of вертеть, "to turn", from Proto-Indo-European wert-: a ticket that turns round. туда is "to that place", on the same pointing stem as тот.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Tudá i obrátno. Then a single: V odnú stóronu.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Dva biléta na póyezd.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: Skólko stóit bilét? Týsyacha rubléy.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"A return ticket"? (Билет туда и обратно.) "A ticket to Moscow"? (Билет до Москвы.) "A ticket for the train"? (Билет на поезд.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
я опоздал на поезд (ya opozdál na póyezd) — I've missed the train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to be late" and "to lose". (Opázdyvat. Teryát.) Then "a return ticket for the train". (Bilét na póyezd tudá i obrátno.)

You'll want to know: я опоздал на поезд.
Я опоздал на поезд. — ya opozdál na póyezd — I've missed the train. A woman says Я опоздала на поезд.
Russian says "I was late for the train", with на, as on a ticket. Опоздать is the perfective partner of опаздывать: one lateness, and the train is gone.

Grammar Lens: a problem is a result.
English often reports a problem with "I have": I've lost, it has gone. Russian uses the perfective past, because the result is still true now:
К сожалению, я потерял билет. — k sozhaléniyu, ya poteryál bilét — Unfortunately I've lost my ticket. Поезд уже ушёл. — póyezd uzhé ushól — The train has already left.
Потерял is the partner of терять; ушёл, of уходить.

The exchange.
— Извините, у меня проблема. Я опоздал на поезд. — Ничего. Есть поезд через час.
U menyá probléma — "I have a problem", literally "at me, a problem". Nichevó. Yest póyezd chérez chas — "Never mind. There's a train in an hour." Есть, "there is", is the present of быть that Russian does say aloud. When it is serious: Помогите, пожалуйста!

The word, taken apart.
опоздать is о- with the root of поздно, "late". EtymDB 2.1 lists опоздать without a parent; the usual account takes поздно back to a Proto-Slavic pozdŭ and relates it to Latin post, "after".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Izvinítye, u menyá probléma. Póyezd uzhé ushól.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I've missed the train"? (Я опоздал на поезд.) "I've lost my ticket": which aspect? (Perfective, потерял: it is still lost.) "I have a problem"? (У меня проблема.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — a ticket, and a train that left without you.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask for one return ticket to Moscow. (Odín bilét do Moskvý, tudá i obrátno, pozhálusta.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and the station can be handled out loud. A ticket goes до a place and на a train; a return is туда и обратно. And when it goes wrong, the perfective past says what happened and is still true: я опоздал на поезд (ya opozdál na póyezd), я потерял билет.

The exchange.
— Извините, я опоздала на поезд. Как пройти к кассе? — Идите прямо, потом налево.
The whole trip so far in two lines: a problem in the perfective, a question about the way, and an answer in two steps.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Izvinítye, ya opozdála na póyezd. Kak proytí k kásse?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Dva biléta na póyezd, tudá i obrátno.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: K sozhaléniyu, ya poteryál bilét.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: Pochemú ty opozdál? Explain what happened, then what followed, with poétomu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: the cashier asks Tudá i obrátno? Answer for a single: Nyet, v odnú stóronu.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"She's missed the train"? (Она опоздала на поезд.) "Two tickets for the bus"? (Два билета на автобус.)
