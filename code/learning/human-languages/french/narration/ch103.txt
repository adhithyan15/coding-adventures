Lesson 1 of 5.
le quartier — a district, a neighbourhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a bakery, then the French for the town hall.

You'll want to know: le quartier.
le quartier — "a district, a neighbourhood".

The word, taken apart.
quartier from Latin quartarius, "a fourth part", from quartus, "fourth" — first a quarter of something, then a part of a town. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
les vêtements — clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a district, then the French for an airport.

You'll want to know: les vêtements.
les vêtements — "clothes".

The word, taken apart.
vêtements is the plural of the word for "a garment" from Old French vestement from Latin vestimentum, "clothing", from vestire, "to clothe" (Lewis & Short) — inherited. The circumflex marks the lost s.

robe from Old French robe, "booty, a garment", from Frankish rauba, "plunder" (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed: clothes were the first spoils of a raid.
