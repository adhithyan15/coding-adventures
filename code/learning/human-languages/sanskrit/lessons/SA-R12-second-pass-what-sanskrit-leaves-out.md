# अस्मि · असि · अस्ति — and the words you are allowed to drop

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-ASMI-FIRST-PERSON, SA-PATTERN-I-AM-WELL] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say that you are well. Then say it again with the word for **I**
taken out, and notice that nothing was lost.

## Grammar Lens: one verb, three shapes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-THREE-PERSONS, SA-GRAMMAR-ASMI-FIRST-PERSON, SA-GRAMMAR-POLITENESS-BY-PERSON] -->

One verb, and the person is in its ending rather than in front of it:

| person | the verb | with a subject |
|---|---|---|
| I | **अस्मि** *asmi* | *ahaṁ kuśalī asmi* |
| you (familiar) | **असि** *asi* | *tvam kuśalī asi* |
| he, she, your honour | **अस्ति** *asti* | *bhavān kathaṁ asti?* |

The third row is why the respectful word costs more than a swap. *Bhavān* is a
third-person word, so it takes the third-person verb — the courtesy reaches the
end of the sentence.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-PRONOUN-DROPPING, SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-IS-KEPT, SA-GRAMMAR-RESPECTFUL-QUESTION] -->

Two rules that look alike and are opposites:

- **The pronoun may go.** *Asmi* already says *I*. Putting *aham* in front of it
  adds emphasis, not information.
- **The copula usually stays.** *Mama nāma … asti* keeps its *asti* where many
  of India's modern languages would still need theirs and where Russian would
  drop it outright.

And one place where the second rule bends: in *tava nāma kim?* the *asti* goes,
because the question-word is doing the work alone.

So the test is not *does Sanskrit like short sentences*. It is **what is already
carried elsewhere in the sentence** — by a verb ending, or by a question word.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-I-AM-WELL, SA-PATTERN-NA-CINTA, SA-GRAMMAR-ONE-WORD-ONE-DAY] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: you are well, the short way — *kuśalī asmi*]
- [YOU SAY: wave away a thank-you — *na cintā*]
- [YOU SAY: tomorrow — *śvaḥ*]

*Śvaḥ* is the pattern's limit case: one word, and it means the day after this
one. No preposition, no counting, nothing to hang it on. Some meanings are
lexical, and no amount of grammar predicts them.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-PRONOUN-DROPPING, SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-IS-KEPT] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which is safer to drop in Sanskrit — the pronoun, or the verb *to
be*? (**The pronoun.**) What licenses dropping *asti* in *tava nāma kim?*
(**The question word carries it.**)
