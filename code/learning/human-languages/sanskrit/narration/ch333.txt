Sanskrit, chapter 333: The Curse and the Boon.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शापः (śāpaḥ) — a curse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for penance, then the Sanskrit for curses.

You'll want to know: शापः.
शापः — śāpaḥ — "a curse".
ऋषेः शापेन राजा दुःखी भवति। ṛṣeḥ śāpena rājā duḥkhī bhavati. The seer's curse makes the king unhappy.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
शापः (śāpaḥ) comes from the root √śap, "to curse", as in śapati; MW gives śāpa as a curse, malediction, oath (MW).

देवः ऋषये वरं ददाति। devaḥ ṛṣaye varaṃ dadāti. The god gives the seer a boon.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वरः (varaḥ) comes from the root √vṛ, "to choose", like the vara who is chosen as a bridegroom; MW gives vara as choosing, a wish, and a boon or gift (MW).

तपः दृष्ट्वा देवः प्रसीदति। tapaḥ dṛṣṭvā devaḥ prasīdati. Seeing the penance, the god shows favour.
Past (he …): सः अन्ते प्रसन्नवान्, saḥ ante prasannavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रसीदति (prasīdati) is built from pra and the root √sad, "to sit, settle"; MW gives pra-sad as to settle down, grow clear and calm, and to be gracious (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
अमरः (amaraḥ) — immortal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a boon, then the Sanskrit for shows favour.

You'll want to know: अमरः.
अमरः — amaraḥ — "immortal".
वरेण ऋषिः अमरः भवति। vareṇa ṛṣiḥ amaraḥ bhavati. Through the boon the seer becomes immortal.

The word, taken apart.
अमरः (amaraḥ) is built from a, "not", and mara, "dying", from the root √mṛ, as in mriyate; MW gives amara as undying, immortal, and as a noun a god (MW).
