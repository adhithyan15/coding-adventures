Marathi, chapter 44: Right and Left.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
उजवा (ujvā) — right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A direction needs a side, and in front of is only one of them.
[your turn — recall: say lāmb, then samor, and put a room in front of a house]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: उजवा.
उजवा — ujvā — right (the side).
उजवा डोळा — ujvā ḍoḷā — the right eye.
उजवा (ujvā) is an adjective, so it comes BEFORE its noun and it agrees with it: उजवा (ujvā) with a masculine noun, उजवी with a feminine, उजवं with a neuter. You have seen that three-way ending before, on माझा / माझी / माझं, and it is the same three endings doing the same job.
Its history is a good one to keep. The Sanskrit word ṛju meant "straight, upright," and the right hand was the straight one — the hand that does the proper thing. The very same idea sits inside Latin rectus and therefore inside English right, which is both a side and a correctness. Two languages made the same joke independently, out of the same root.
To send somebody rightwards you add the -कडे you will meet later, giving उजवीकडे. For now, the side itself is enough.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ujvā — right]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ujvā ḍoḷā — the right eye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ujvī khoolī — and hear the ending follow the noun]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'the right eye', then put the adjective on a feminine noun.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does उजवा (ujvā) stand relative to its noun? (Before it.) What did its Sanskrit ancestor mean? (Straight — the same idea as English right.)
Sources: Wiktionary: उजवा (ujvā).


Lesson 2 of 3.
डावा (ḍāvā) — left.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One side is not a pair. Say उजवा (ujvā) once and then take the other hand.

You'll want to know: डावा.
डावा — ḍāvā — left (the side).
डावा कान — ḍāvā kān — the left ear.
Everything you learned one lesson ago transfers whole: डावा (ḍāvā) is an adjective, it stands before its noun, and it agrees — डावा (ḍāvā), डावी, डावं. Learning a pair of opposites together is cheaper than learning two words, because the second one costs only its sound.
It opens with ड, the retroflex d, so the tongue curls back the way it does in डोळा. Keep it apart from the dental द of दूध: the two sound close to an English ear and Marathi treats them as different letters entirely.
Now you can take somebody through a door: उजवा (ujvā) or डावा (ḍāvā), then a landmark with समोर or जवळ, and the direction is complete.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ḍāvā — left, with the tongue curled on the ड]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ḍāvā kān, then ujvā kān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ḍāvī khoolī — and hear the feminine ending]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say the left ear and the right ear, then swap the noun for a feminine one.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter does डावा (ḍāvā) start with, and which taught word shares it? (ड, as in डोळा.) How much of उजवा (ujvā)'s grammar did डावा (ḍāvā) need you to learn again? (None of it.)
Sources: Wiktionary: डावा (ḍāvā).
