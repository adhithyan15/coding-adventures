## You'll want to know: caleō, calēre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C488-BRUMA-05]; assesses=[] -->

**caleō, calēre** — "to be warm, to be hot".

> Aestāte saxa sub sōle calent.
>
> *In summer the stones are hot under the sun.*

*caleō, calēre, caluī*, second conjugation. *Calidus* 'hot' and *calor* 'heat' come from it; English *calorie* comes from *calor*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*caleō* is inherited from Proto-Italic *kalēō* 'to be hot', from PIE *ḱelh₁-* 'to heat' (EtymDB). *calidus* 'hot' and *calor* 'heat', the source of English *calorie*, are built on it (Lewis & Short).
