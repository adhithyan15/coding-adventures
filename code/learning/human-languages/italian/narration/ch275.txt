Italian, chapter 275: Cosa è accaduto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
accadere — to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for at a certain point, then the Italian for all of a sudden.

You'll want to know: accadere.
accadere — "to happen".
È accaduto tutto in pochi secondi. It all happened in a few seconds.
Takes essere: è accaduto. A little more formal than succedere.

The word, taken apart.
accadere is usually traced to Vulgar Latin accadĕre, a remake of Latin accidere "to fall upon, to happen", from ad and cadere "to fall" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
verificarsi — to occur, to take place (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to happen, then the Italian for to happen to someone.

You'll want to know: verificarsi.
verificarsi — "to occur, to take place (formal)".
Ieri sera si è verificato un incidente in centro. Last night an accident occurred in the centre.
A news word. Without -si, verificare means ‘to check’.

The word, taken apart.
verificarsi comes from verificare "to check, to show true", usually traced to Medieval Latin vērificāre, built on vērus "true" and facere "to make" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
avvenire — to take place, to come about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to happen to someone, then the Italian for to occur.

You'll want to know: avvenire.
avvenire — "to take place, to come about".
Il fatto è avvenuto di notte. The incident took place at night.
Conjugated like venire: è avvenuto. As a noun, l'avvenire is the future.

The word, taken apart.
avvenire is inherited from Latin advenīre "to come to, to arrive" (EtymDB), from ad and venīre "to come".

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'accaduto — what happened, the incident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to occur, then the Italian for to take place.

You'll want to know: l'accaduto.
l'accaduto — "what happened, the incident".
Mi ha raccontato l'accaduto. He told me what had happened.
The past participle of accadere used as a noun; common on the news.

The word, taken apart.
l'accaduto is the past participle of accadere used as a noun, "the thing that happened"; the verb goes back to Latin cadere "to fall" (Lewis & Short).
