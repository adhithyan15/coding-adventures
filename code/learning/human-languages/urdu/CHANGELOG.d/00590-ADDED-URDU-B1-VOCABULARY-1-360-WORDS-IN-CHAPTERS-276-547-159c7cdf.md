## Added — Urdu B1 vocabulary, 1,360 words in chapters 276–547

- Added 272 chapters of five words each (1,360 headwords, 370 of them
  verbs), one word per lesson, with eight reviews in each of five tranches:
  - 276–330: narrating (a theft and the police, crashes and fire,
    earthquakes, storms and floods, rescue and relief, the news, kings and
    wars, Eid and the fair, tales and buried treasure);
  - 331–385: reasons and conditions (causes and consequences, argument and
    proof, rights and the courts, government and citizens, values, research,
    then possibility, conditions, danger, effort and success);
  - 386–439: travel (trains, roads and traffic, flights and security, a
    place to stay, the way, the town, money and the market, the weather and
    nature);
  - 440–493: experience (feelings and character, the body and health, the
    family);
  - 494–547: work and crafts, repairs and the kitchen, free time, the arts
    and wrestling, then plans, luck and choices.
- Every verb shows its past with نے where it takes one and the right
  agreement (پولیس نے رپورٹ لکھی); every noun's gender is checked
  against every word that agrees with it. Examples use only words taught
  earlier and only the grammar the core teaches: no subjunctive, no -یں
  request, no passive, no بولا.
- The first words of chapters 276–283 practise the core's last grammar
  points again, two words each; ؤ ع غ ذ ث ڈ ص each come back twice in
  chapters 284–312.
- Every word has a cited origin: CDIAL (with its Hindi cousin), or a named
  Persian, Arabic or English donor hedged as the usual tracing; where no
  source settles a word, its note says so. No Arabic root letters, and no
  etymology is invented. Root slugs reuse the corpus spelling of each etymon.
- Echo words, a taught word plus a light verb, plain derivations of taught
  words, near-synonym clusters beyond two, and homographs of inflected forms
  were replaced during review.
