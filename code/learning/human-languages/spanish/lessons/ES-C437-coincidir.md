## You'll want to know: coincidir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C436-GENTE-02] -->

**coincidir** — to coincide, and in everyday use **to clash**.

*Me coincide con el trabajo* — it clashes with work. That is the ordinary reason
somebody gives for not taking a place.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-02, ES-LEX-C437-PLAN-01] -->

**co-** together, plus the same **-incidir** family as *decidir* — things
falling together.

Your English eye can read it without help, and that is the point worth taking:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| coincidir | coincide |
| decidir | decide |
| dividir | divide |

**But the everyday meaning has drifted.** English *coincide* is neutral or
lucky; Spanish **coincidir**, said about a timetable, is almost always a
complaint. The form is transparent and the use is not, which is exactly where a
confident reader gets caught.
