Russian, chapter 213: Border, Customs, Capital, Flat, Floor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
граница (granítsa) — a border.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for September, then the Russian for October.

You'll want to know: граница.
граница (granítsa) — "a border".

The word, taken apart.
граница (granítsa) is built on грань, "an edge, a facet" (Vasmer), which first probably meant "a point"; Vasmer relates it to Old High German grana, "whisker". German Grenze, "border", was borrowed from this Slavic word.

таможня (tamózhnya) comes from Old Russian тамга, "a stamp, a customs duty" (Vasmer), from Turkic tamga, "an owner's brand, a seal, a tax".

You'll want to know: столица.
столица (stolítsa) — "a capital (city)".

The word, taken apart.
столица (stolítsa) is built on стол, which in Old Russian meant "a throne, a princely seat" as well as "a table" (Vasmer): the capital is where the ruler sits.

Lesson 4 of 5.
квартира (kvartíra) — a flat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for customs, then the Russian for a capital.

You'll want to know: квартира.
квартира (kvartíra) — "a flat".

The word, taken apart.
квартира (kvartíra) — from 1704, also folk фатера; it came through German Quartier or Dutch kwartier from Old French quartier, "a quarter, lodgings", Latin quartārius, "a fourth" (Vasmer).
