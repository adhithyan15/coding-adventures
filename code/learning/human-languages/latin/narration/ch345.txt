Latin, chapter 345: Rēgēs et dictātōrēs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rēx, rēgis — a king.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an origin, then the Latin for great age.

You'll want to know: rēx, rēgis.
rēx, rēgis — "a king".
Ōlim rēgēs hanc urbem tenēbant. Once kings held this city.
Masculine, 3rd declension. After the last king was driven out, the Romans hated the very name rēx.

The word, taken apart.
rēx is inherited from Proto-Indo-European h₃rḗǵs, "ruler, king" (EtymDB), a cousin of Sanskrit rāj-. English regal and royal come from its family.

You'll want to know: rēgnō, rēgnāre.
rēgnō, rēgnāre — "to reign, to be king".
Senex rēx multōs annōs rēgnāvit. The old king reigned for many years.
rēgnō, rēgnāre, rēgnāvī, rēgnātum, from rēgnum (taught). English reign.

The word, taken apart.
rēgnō is built on rēgnum, "kingship, kingdom" (EtymDB). English reign comes from its family through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tyrannus, tyrannī — a tyrant, a despot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a king, then the Latin for to reign.

You'll want to know: tyrannus, tyrannī.
tyrannus, tyrannī — "a tyrant, a despot".
Cīvēs tyrannum ex urbe pepulērunt. The citizens drove the tyrant out of the city.
Masculine. For a Roman, a ruler who seized power and ruled without law.

The word, taken apart.
tyrannus was borrowed from Greek tyrannos, "absolute ruler" (EtymDB). English tyrant comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
plēbs, plēbis — the common people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to reign, then the Latin for a tyrant.

You'll want to know: plēbs, plēbis.
plēbs, plēbis — "the common people".
Plēbs in forō clāmābat. The common people were shouting in the forum.
Feminine, 3rd declension, singular. The citizens outside the old noble families. English plebeian.

The word, taken apart.
plēbs is linked by Lewis & Short with a root meaning "to fill", as in plēnus, "full": the multitude. English plebeian comes from its family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dictātor, dictātōris — a dictator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tyrant, then the Latin for the common people.

You'll want to know: dictātor, dictātōris.
dictātor, dictātōris — "a dictator".
In magnō perīculō senātus dictātōrem creāvit. In great danger the senate appointed a dictator.
Masculine, 3rd declension. The Roman dictator held full power in an emergency, for six months only.

The word, taken apart.
dictātor is built on dictō, "to say often, to order" (Lewis & Short), from dīcō, "to say". English dictator is the same word.
