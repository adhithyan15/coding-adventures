## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C54-BODY-03]; assesses=[] -->

**oculus** — "**an eye**."

The third body word this book has, after *caput* and *manus*.

English **eye** is an **inherited cousin** — Proto-Indo-European ***\*h₃ekʷ-***
gave Latin *oculus* and Germanic *augon*, which is *eye*. And the loans are
everywhere: **ocular**, **binoculars**, **monocle**, **oculist**.

The best of them is **inoculate**. Latin *inoculāre* was a **gardening** word: to
graft a bud — an *oculus*, an "eye" — into a plant. Eighteenth-century physicians
borrowed the gardener's word for grafting a little disease into a person, and that
is why an injection is named after an eye.

Italian *occhio* and Spanish *ojo* both descend from it, by the same *-cl-* to *-cchi-* change Italian makes on *occhio*.
