Latin, chapter 454: To the right, To the left, Slanting, To go round, Detour.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
dextrōrsum — to the right, towards the right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to find, then the Latin for to track.

You'll want to know: dextrōrsum.
dextrōrsum — "to the right, towards the right".
Post pontem via dextrōrsum it. After the bridge the road goes to the right.
An adverb, also dextrōrsus, from dexter 'right' and vorsum 'turned'.

The word, taken apart.
dextrōrsum is a shortened dextrō-vorsum, built from dexter 'right' and vorsum, an old form of versum 'turned', from vertō 'to turn' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
oblīquus, oblīqua, oblīquum — slanting, sideways, at an angle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to the right, then the Latin for to the left.

You'll want to know: oblīquus, oblīqua, oblīquum.
oblīquus, oblīqua, oblīquum — "slanting, sideways, at an angle".
Sēmita oblīqua per collem ascendit. The path climbs across the hill at a slant.
An adjective of three endings. Oblīquō itinere, by a sideways route. English oblique comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
oblīquus is built from ob- and an old root meaning 'bent, slanting' (Lewis & Short); the exact source of that root is not settled. English oblique comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
circumeō, circumīre — to go round, to walk round; to visit in turn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to the left, then the Latin for slanting.

You'll want to know: circumeō, circumīre.
circumeō, circumīre — "to go round, to walk round; to visit in turn".
Via mūrōs oppidī circumit. The road goes round the walls of the town.
Irregular like eō: circumeō, circumīre, circumiī, circumitum. Often written circueō.

The word, taken apart.
circumeō is built from circum 'around' and eō 'to go' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
circuitus, circuitūs — a detour, a way round; a circuit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for slanting, then the Latin for to go round.

You'll want to know: circuitus, circuitūs.
circuitus, circuitūs — "a detour, a way round; a circuit".
Pōns frāctus est; itaque longō circuitū ad oppidum pervenīmus. The bridge is broken; so we reach the town by a long detour.
Fourth declension, masculine: circuitus, circuitūs m., from circumeō. English circuit comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
circuitus is built on circumeō 'to go round' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the noun ending -tus. English circuit comes from it.
