Bengali, chapter 264: What Happened, and Where I Was.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
আমি গেলাম (āmi gelām) — the past, person by person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a female singer and a female dancer. (gāyikā, nôrtôkī.) Then the line from your first meeting: আলাপ করে ভালো লাগলো, "pleased to meet you".
[pause 1 second]
Its last word is a past, "it felt good". This book now writes that ending -ল: লাগল (lāglô). You will meet -লো in print too; they sound the same.

You'll want to know: the past, person by person.
কাল আমি বাজারে গেলাম। kāl āmi bājāre gelām. — "Yesterday I went to the market."
With a past, কাল is yesterday. গেলাম is যাওয়া in the past: the stem গে-, then -ল-, then the person.
আমি / আমরা গেলাম (gelām) — I went, we went.
তুমি / তোমরা গেলে (gele) — you went, to a friend or to several.
সে / তারা গেল (gelô) — he or she went; they went.
আপনি / তিনি গেলেন (gelen) — you went, he or she went, with respect.
They are the present's four slots: যাই, যাও, যান, and one not written before, সে যায় (she jāy), "he or she goes": -য় after a vowel, -এ after a consonant, সে বসে.

You'll want to know: the past stems.
Most verbs put -ল- on the stem: বসা becomes বসলাম (bôslām), দেখা becomes দেখলাম (dekhlām), with a plain e for the æ of দেখা. An এ said e rises to ি, an ও to ু: কেনা becomes কিনলাম (kinlām), ফেরা becomes ফিরলাম (phirlām), শোনা becomes শুনলাম (shunlām).
Eight verbs change the stem:
যাওয়া becomes গেলাম (gelām) — went.
আসা becomes এলাম (elām), সে এল (she elô) — came.
খাওয়া becomes খেলাম (khelām) — ate, drank.
নেওয়া becomes নিলাম (nilām) — took.
দেওয়া (of জল দেওয়া) becomes দিলাম (dilām) — gave.
হওয়া becomes হলাম (hôlām); কী হল? (kī hôlô?) — what happened?
চাওয়া becomes চাইলাম (chāilām), সে চাইল (she chāilô) — wanted, asked for.
পাওয়া becomes পেলাম (pelām) — got, found.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Yesterday I went to the market" — kāl āmi bājāre gelām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In the evening she came back home" — sôndhyāy she bāṛi phirlô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you went to the market yesterday, then that she came back home.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I went"? (গেলাম.) "He came"? (এল.) "You went", with respect? (গেলেন.) "What happened?" (কী হল?)


Lesson 2 of 5.
আমি ছিলাম (āmi chhilām) — was, and was not.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I went" and "she came". (gelām, she elô.) Then আছি, "I am, I am there": this lesson puts it in the past.

You'll want to know: ছিলাম.
কাল আমি বাড়িতে ছিলাম। kāl āmi bāṛite chhilām. — "Yesterday I was at home."
আছি has a past of its own, ছিলাম, with the endings of the last lesson:
আমি / আমরা ছিলাম (chhilām) — I was, we were.
তুমি ছিলে (chhile) — you were.
সে / তারা ছিল (chhilô) — he or she was; they were.
আপনি / তিনি ছিলেন (chhilen) — with respect.
In the present, Bengali says "is" with no verb at all: সে ভালো. In the past it needs one: সে ক্লান্ত ছিল (she klāntô chhilô), "she was tired". ছিল also says "there was": গ্রামে একটা বড় পুকুর ছিল (grāme ekṭā bôṛo pukur chhilô), "there was a big pond in the village".

You'll want to know: ছিল না.
নেই, "there is not", which you know, has no past of its own. Its past is ছিল না, with না after the verb, as in the present:
আজ বাজারে মাছ নেই। কালও ছিল না। āj bājāre māchh nei. kālo chhilô nā. — "There is no fish at the market today. There was none yesterday either."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Yesterday I was at home" — kāl āmi bāṛite chhilām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She was tired" — she klāntô chhilô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "There was a pond in the village, but no river" — grāme ekṭā pukur chhilô, kintu nôdī chhilô nā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say that you were at home yesterday, and that he was not.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I was"? (ছিলাম.) "They were"? (ছিল.) "There was not"? (ছিল না.) What does the past need that the present leaves out? (The verb: সে ভালো, but সে ক্লান্ত ছিল.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ঘটা (ghôṭā) — to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: ask "What happened?" the everyday way. (কী হল?, kī hôlô?)

You'll want to know: ঘটা.
ঘটা — ghôṭā — "to happen".
কাল রাতে পাড়ায় কী ঘটল? kāl rāte pāṛāy kī ghôṭlô? — "What happened in the neighbourhood last night?"
কী হল? is the everyday question. ঘটা (ghôṭā) is for something that takes place, an event, and it suits a story. Its past is regular: ঘটল (ghôṭlô), "it happened".
এটা কখন ঘটল? eṭā kôkhôn ghôṭlô? — "When did this happen?"

The word, taken apart.
ঘটা (ghôṭā) is usually traced to the Sanskrit verb ghaṭate, which CDIAL (4407) glosses "is busy with" and, in a later text, "happens". CDIAL lists no Bengali form under that entry, so the line from one to the other is the usual tracing, not a recorded row.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ghôṭā, then its past, ghôṭlô]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What happened in the neighbourhood last night?" — kāl rāte pāṛāy kī ghôṭlô?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When did this happen?" — eṭā kôkhôn ghôṭlô?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ঘটা (ghôṭā) mean? ("To happen".) Its past? (ঘটল, ghôṭlô.) Which question is the everyday one? (কী হল?)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ঘ (ghô) — the letter at the start of ঘটা (ghôṭā).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ঘ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say ঘটা (ghôṭā), "to happen", and its past, ঘটল. Then read ব aloud (bô); you will want its shape in a moment.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ঘ.
ঘ — ghô: a g with a breath after it, as খ (khô) is a k with a breath. It opens ঘটা (ghôṭā).
In the print of this book, ঘ (ghô) has the frame of ব: a headline along the top, an upright stem on the right, the full height of the letter, a pointed tip at the lower left, and a curve that joins that tip to the foot of the stem. Where ব has one straight diagonal, ঘ (ghô) has two pieces. Under the left end of the headline hangs a rounded curl, open toward the stem, its end a short point aimed up and to the right. Below the curl, a short straight piece joins it to the pointed tip.

Writing: ঘ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ঘ (ghô) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the headline, the stem, the curl with its point, the tip at the lower left, and the curve to the foot.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ঘ (ghô) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.
