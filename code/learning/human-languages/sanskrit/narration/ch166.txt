5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अश्वत्थः (aśvatthaḥ) — a sacred fig tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a live coal, then the Sanskrit for iron.

You'll want to know: अश्वत्थः.
अश्वत्थः — aśvatthaḥ — "a sacred fig tree".

The word, taken apart.
aśvattha "the sacred fig tree" has a disputed origin: MW reads it as aśva "horse" + -stha "standing", "the tree horses stand under", but CDIAL 922 reports Mayrhofer's view that it is non-Aryan and unconnected with aśva. It is old, in the Atharvaveda.

The word, taken apart.
nimba "the neem tree" appears in the ritual manuals, and neither MW nor CDIAL 7245 gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi nīm and Punjabi nimb (CDIAL 7245).

You'll want to know: आम्रवृक्षः.
आम्रवृक्षः — āmravṛkṣaḥ — "a mango tree".

The word, taken apart.
āmravṛkṣa is built from āmra "mango" and vṛkṣa "tree" (MW). Neither MW nor CDIAL gives a derivation for āmra, so its origin is uncertain; it lives on as Hindi ām "mango" (CDIAL 1268).

वटः — vaṭaḥ — "a banyan tree".

The word, taken apart.
vaṭa "the banyan" appears from the Mahābhārata on. MW thinks it perhaps a Prakrit form of vṛta "surrounded, covered", but marks that as a guess, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi baṛ "banyan" (CDIAL 11211).

Lesson 5 of 5.
तालः (tālaḥ) — a palm tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a mango tree, then the Sanskrit for a banyan tree.

You'll want to know: तालः.
तालः — tālaḥ — "a palm tree".

The word, taken apart.
tāla "the palmyra, the fan-palm" appears from Manu on (CDIAL 5750). DEDR 3180 sets it beside Dravidian words for the palm, noting that some of those may be borrowed from Indo-Aryan, so the direction is not settled. Punjabi kept it as tāṛ "fan-palm" (CDIAL 5750).
