Tamil, chapter 286: What I Think and So.
3 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
… என்று தோன்றுகிறது (… eṉṟu tōṉṟukiṟatu) (eṉṟu tōṉṟukiṟatu) — it seems to me, in my opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think this is a good house." (itu nalla vīṭu eṉṟu niṉaikkiṟēṉ.) What closes the opinion before the verb? (என்று.) Then say "dampness". (īram.)

You'll want to know: … என்று தோன்றுகிறது.
எனக்கு நாளை மழை பெய்யும் என்று தோன்றுகிறது. eṉakku nāḷai maḻai peyyum eṉṟu tōṉṟukiṟatu. — "It seems to me it will rain tomorrow."
தோன்றுகிறது (tōṉṟukiṟatu, "it appears, it occurs"). The person it occurs to takes -க்கு, as in எனக்குத் தமிழ் தெரியும்: "to me, '(it) will rain tomorrow', so it appears". பெய்யும் (peyyum, "it will fall") is the "it" form of the future.
நினைக்கிறேன் says you have weighed it; தோன்றுகிறது says it is an impression, and sounds softer. Both take the whole opinion first, closed by என்று.
Two more shapes of the same நினை: … என்று நினைத்தேன் (… eṉṟu niṉaittēṉ, "I thought that …"), and the negative, அவன் வருவான் என்று நான் நினைக்கவில்லை (avaṉ varuvāṉ eṉṟu nāṉ niṉaikkavillai, "I don't think he will come"). வருவான் (varuvāṉ, "he will come").

You'll want to know: என் கருத்தில், 'in my opinion'.
என் கருத்தில், ரயில் பயணம் நல்லது. eṉ karuttil, rayil payaṇam nallatu. — "In my opinion, travelling by train is good."
கருத்து is "an opinion"; கருத்தில் is "in (the) opinion". நல்லது (nallatu, "a good thing; good") is நல்ல made into a thing you can point at. என் கருத்துப்படி (eṉ karuttuppaṭi, "according to my opinion") says the same with -படி, "according to".
This one sits at the front, apart, so the listener knows that what follows is yours.

Lesson 2 of 3.
எனவே (eṉavē) — so, that is why, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "so, because of that". (ataṉāl.) Then "It seems to me it will rain tomorrow." (eṉakku nāḷai maḻai peyyum eṉṟu tōṉṟukiṟatu.)

You'll want to know: அதனால் and அதனால்தான்.
நேற்று மழை பெய்தது. அதனால் நான் வீட்டில் இருந்தேன். nēṟṟu maḻai peytatu. ataṉāl nāṉ vīṭṭil iruntēṉ. — "It rained yesterday. So I stayed at home."
அதனால் is அதன், the shape of அது that carries endings, with -ஆல், "by, because of": "because of that". The cause comes first, in its own sentence; அதனால் opens the result.
Add -தான், "exactly, that very", and it points back hard: "that is why".
அவனுக்குக் காய்ச்சல். அதனால்தான் அவன் வரவில்லை. avaṉukkuk kāyccal. ataṉāltāṉ avaṉ varavillai. — "He has a fever. That is why he did not come."

You'll want to know: எனவே and ஆகையால், the written 'therefore'.
பேருந்து வரவில்லை. எனவே நாங்கள் நடந்து போனோம். pēruntu varavillai. eṉavē nāṅkaḷ naṭantu pōṉōm. — "The bus did not come. Therefore we walked."
In written Tamil, எனவே (eṉavē) and ஆகையால் (ākaiyāl, "therefore") do the job of அதனால்: a notice, a letter, the news. They are the formal side of the two Tamils. In talk, அதனால் carries the result.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It rained yesterday. So I stayed at home." — nēṟṟu maḻai peytatu. ataṉāl nāṉ vīṭṭil iruntēṉ.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He has a fever. That is why he did not come." — avaṉukkuk kāyccal. ataṉāltāṉ avaṉ varavillai.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The bus did not come. Therefore we walked." — pēruntu varavillai. eṉavē nāṅkaḷ naṭantu pōṉōm.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"So"? (அதனால்.) "That is why"? (அதனால்தான்.) "Therefore", in writing? (எனவே (eṉavē), or ஆகையால்.) Which comes first, the cause or the result? (The cause.)
