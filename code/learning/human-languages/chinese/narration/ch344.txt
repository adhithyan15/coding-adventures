Mandarin Chinese, chapter 344: Keeping Memories.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bǎocún — to keep; to save.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for good wishes sent to someone, then the Mandarin for to share good things.

You'll want to know: bǎocún.
bǎocún — "to keep; to save".
tā de xìn wǒ dōu bǎocún zài shūfáng lǐ — I've kept all his letters in the study.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bǎocún is built from bǎo and cún, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pawX and dzwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to keep safe' and the second 'to be there'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
xiàngcè — photo album.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a memory of the past, then the Mandarin for past events.

You'll want to know: xiàngcè.
xiàngcè — "photo album".
wǒ zài xiàngcè lǐ kàndào le xiǎoshíhou de zhàopiàn — In the album I came across photos of myself as a child.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàngcè is built from xiàng 'a likeness' and cè 'a volume', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjangH and tsrheak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to look at' and the second 'bound bamboo slips'. The sense 'a photograph' for the first is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
yǒngyuǎn — forever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for past events, then the Mandarin for a photo album.

You'll want to know: yǒngyuǎn.
yǒngyuǎn — "forever".
wǒ huì yǒngyuǎn jìde nàge Zhōngqiū — I'll always remember that moon festival.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: yǒng is said with a rising tone before yuǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
yǒngyuǎn is built from yǒng and yuǎn 'far', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjwaengX and hjwonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'long' and the second 'distant'.
