French, chapter 509: Dans l'eau.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
plonger — to dive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for relay, then the French for to time.

You'll want to know: plonger.
plonger — "to dive".
Les enfants plongent du haut du rocher. The children dive off the top of the rock.
Spelling: nous plongeons. La plongée — scuba diving.

The word, taken apart.
plonger is inherited from Old French plongier (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin plumbicāre "to sink like lead", built on plumbum "lead" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le maillot de bain — swimsuit, swimming trunks.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for to dive, then the French for dive.

You'll want to know: le maillot de bain.
le maillot de bain — "swimsuit, swimming trunks".
J'ai oublié mon maillot de bain à l'hôtel. I left my swimsuit at the hotel.
Often le maillot alone.

The word, taken apart.
le maillot de bain joins maillot, built on maille "a stitch, a mesh", from Latin macula "a spot, a mesh", and bain "bath", from Latin balneum (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: la brasse.
la brasse — "breaststroke".
Ma grand-mère nage la brasse tous les matins. My grandmother swims breaststroke every morning.
Front crawl is le crawl; backstroke is le dos crawlé.

The word, taken apart.
la brasse is inherited from Latin bracchia "arms" (EtymDB), the plural of bracchium "arm" (Lewis & Short): first the span of two outstretched arms.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ramer — to row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for swimsuit, then the French for breaststroke.

You'll want to know: ramer.
ramer — "to row".
Nous avons ramé jusqu'à l'île. We rowed to the island.
Informally, to struggle: j'ai ramé pour trouver un appartement.

The word, taken apart.
ramer is linked to rame "oar". EtymDB traces it to Old French raim "branch", from Latin rāmus "a branch"; Latin rēmus "an oar" (Lewis & Short) probably shaped the sense, and the origin is not fully settled.
