# 好 — one character, and three things it is not

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-HAO-FOND, ZH-SCRIPT-HAO-BUILD-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say 好 twice, once in each tone you have met, and say what each one
means. One character, two words.

## You'll want to know: four cautions, and 好 carries three of them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-COMPOUND-WORD, ZH-LEX-HAO-FOND, ZH-SCRIPT-HAO-BUILD-01] -->

These chapters have warned you four separate times about reading too much
into what you see. **好** is where three of the four meet:

- **A character is not a word.** 你好 is two characters and one word. A
  character writes a syllable and usually a meaningful piece — not a word.
- **A component is not always a meaning.** 好 is 女 beside 子, woman beside
  child, and the lesson that built it warned you against reading a story into
  that. The pieces are how the character is *assembled*, not what it argues.
- **A tone is not a decoration.** *hǎo* is good; *hào* is to be fond of. Same
  character, same strokes, different word.

## Script: the component that carries a sound
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-SCRIPT-ER-01, ZH-SCRIPT-PHONETIC, ZH-LEX-KOU, ZH-LEX-RI] -->

> 尔

The fourth caution is the other way round. **尔** is in 你 to say how it
**sounds**, not what it means — a component doing phonetic work. Once you expect
that, a great many characters stop looking arbitrary.

Against it, 口 *kǒu* (mouth) and 日 *rì* (sun, and also day) are components that
do mean what they draw. **Both kinds exist, and the character does not announce
which you are looking at.** That is the caution worth carrying forward.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-COMPOUND-WORD, ZH-LEX-HAO-FOND, ZH-SCRIPT-ER-01, ZH-SCRIPT-HAO-BUILD-01, ZH-LEX-KOU, ZH-LEX-RI, ZH-SCRIPT-PHONETIC] -->

[PAUSE 3s] How many words is 你好, and how many characters? (**One word, two characters**.) What are the two halves of 好, and what do they tell you about its meaning? (**女 and 子** — and **nothing** you should rely on.) What does 尔 contribute to 你? (**Its sound**, not its meaning.) And which of 口 and 日 means what it draws? (**Both** — which is why the two kinds have to be told apart.)
