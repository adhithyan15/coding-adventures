## You'll want to know: नौवाँ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C76-ORDINAL-04]; assesses=[] -->

**नौवाँ** (*nauvā̃*) — "**ninth**".

**नौवाँ दिन** (*nauvā̃ din*), **नौवीं किताब** (*nauvī̃ kitāb*).

**नौ** ends in a vowel where *sāt* and *āṭh* ended in consonants, and **-वाँ**
attaches anyway. That is worth one sentence of attention: an ending that does not
care what the word before it ends in is a **suffix**, not a reshaping — it is
added, not fused.

Compare *cauthā*, where *cār* had to change shape to accept its ending. That is a
fused form. This is not. Same language, two different ages of word-making, and
you can hear which is which.
