Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 230: Burning Sand, Setting Sun.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बळणो (baḷṇo) — to be scorching; to burn (by itself).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for white, then the Marwadi for to shine.

You'll want to know: बळणो.
बळणो — baḷṇo — "to be scorching; to burn (by itself)".
दोपार मांय रेत बळै है। dopār mā̃y ret baḷai hai. At midday the sand is scorching.
Past (it …): वो दीयो बळ्यो, vo dīyo baḷyo.

The word, taken apart.
बळणो (baḷṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed dvalati, "burns"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari verb balaï, "burns" (CDIAL 6654). बाळणो, to burn something, is its partner.

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to shine, then the Marwadi for to be scorching.

You'll want to know: ढळणो.
ढळणो — ḍhaḷṇo — "to go down (of the sun); to slope".
सूरज आथण ढळै है। sūraj āthaṇ ḍhaḷai hai. The sun goes down in the evening.
Past (it …): वो सूरज ढळ्यो, vo sūraj ḍhaḷyo.

The word, taken apart.
ढळणो (ḍhaḷṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed ḍhalati, "bends over, falls"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari verb ḍhalaï, "falls, declines" (CDIAL 5581).

Lesson 3 of 5.
धरती (dhartī) — the earth; the ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to be scorching, then the Marwadi for to go down (of the sun).

You'll want to know: धरती.
धरती — dhartī — "the earth; the ground".
आज धरती ताती है। āj dhartī tātī hai. Today the ground is hot.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
धरती (dhartī) is inherited from Sanskrit dharitrī, "she who carries", a name for the earth; CDIAL lists Old Marwari dharatī, "the earth" (CDIAL 6750).

Lesson 4 of 5.
आक (āk) — swallow-wort (a desert shrub).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to go down (of the sun), then the Marwadi for the earth.

You'll want to know: आक.
आक — āk — "swallow-wort (a desert shrub)".
रेत मांय आक है। ret mā̃y āk hai. There's swallow-wort in the sand.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आक (āk) is inherited from Sanskrit arka, the name of the plant Calotropis; CDIAL lists Marwari āk (CDIAL 625). It grows wild in the sand.

Lesson 5 of 5.
कांटो (kā̃ṭo) — a thorn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for the earth, then the Marwadi for swallow-wort.

You'll want to know: कांटो.
कांटो — kā̃ṭo — "a thorn".
पग मांय कांटो है। pag mā̃y kā̃ṭo hai. There's a thorn in my foot.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कांटो (kā̃ṭo) is inherited from Sanskrit kaṇṭaka, "a thorn"; CDIAL lists Hindi kā̃ṭā and a Gujarati cousin, "a thorn". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 2668).
