Gujarati, chapter 225: Not so well today.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ચક્કર (cakkar) — dizziness, a turn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to throb, then the Gujarati for breath.

You'll want to know: ચક્કર.
ચક્કર — cakkar — "dizziness, a turn".
શું તમને ચક્કર આવે છે? — śũ tamne cakkar āve che? — "Do you feel dizzy?"
A neuter noun. Word for word, does a turn come to you.

The word, taken apart.
ચક્કર (cakkar) is usually traced to Sanskrit cakra, "wheel", so dizziness is the head going round. CDIAL lists only the inherited Gujarati cāk, "wheel", under cakra, not cakkar itself (CDIAL 4538).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઊલટી થવી (ūlṭī thavī) — to vomit, to be sick.

You'll want to know: ઊલટી થવી.
ઊલટી થવી — ūlṭī thavī — "to vomit, to be sick".
શું તમને ઊલટી થાય છે? — śũ tamne ūlṭī thāy che? — "Are you being sick?"
Word for word, vomiting happens: ઊલટી, ūlṭī, and થવું, thavũ.

The word, taken apart.
ઊલટી થવી (ūlṭī thavī) is built from ūlṭī, "vomiting", the noun of ūlṭũ, "turned over" (EtymDB), and thavũ, "to become". CDIAL traces the family to ullaṭati, "turns over", a reconstructed form (CDIAL 2368).

Lesson 3 of 5.
પચવું (pacvũ) — to digest, to be digested.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for dizziness, then the Gujarati for to vomit.

You'll want to know: પચવું.
પચવું — pacvũ — "to digest, to be digested".
મને દૂધ પચતું નથી. — mane dūdh pactũ nathī — "Milk does not agree with me."
Word for word, milk is not digested to me.

The word, taken apart.
પચવું (pacvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from pacyate, "is cooked, is digested" (CDIAL 7654).

You'll want to know: બગાસું ખાવું.
બગાસું ખાવું — bagāsũ khāvũ — "to yawn".
શું બાળક બગાસું ખાય છે? — śũ bāḷak bagāsũ khāy che? — "Is the child yawning?"
Word for word, to eat a yawn: બગાસું, bagāsũ, and ખાવું, khāvũ.

The word, taken apart.
બગાસું ખાવું (bagāsũ khāvũ) is built from bagāsũ, "a yawn", and the inherited verb khāvũ, "to eat". The origin of bagāsũ is not settled: CDIAL lists no Gujarati form for it.
