Punjabi, chapter 27: Ear, Mouth, Nose, and Heart at R4.
2 lessons. All 2 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 2.
Ear and mouth return at R4 — retrieve ear and mouth with their unrelated lookalike and worn-consonant histories at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 7 seconds]
Say kann and mūnh from memory: ear, mouth. Keep the two meanings separate before looking at the script.

Guided Practice.
Read, then stop.
Read ਕੰਨ, ਮੂੰਹ once. This lesson measures listening, speech, and recognition. It does not award independent Gurmukhi writing evidence.
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Say ear and mouth in Punjabi. Which word only sounds as though it belongs with karnā, to do?
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Match the old histories: karṇa without any link to karnā; mukha whose kh weakened and disappeared into a level high tone.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say both meanings and one history for each, then stop. No new body word follows.


Lesson 2 of 2.
Nose and heart return at R4 — retrieve nose and heart with their inherited and borrowed histories at R4.

Warm-up.
[pause 7 seconds]
Say nakk and dil from memory: nose, heart. Hold the doubled consonant in the first word.

Guided Practice.
Read, then stop.
Read ਨੱਕ, ਦਿਲ once. This lesson measures listening, speech, and recognition. It does not award independent Gurmukhi writing evidence.
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Say nose and heart in Punjabi, in that order.
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Which word is inherited and cognate with English nose, and which is a Persian loan with a deeper shared ancestor behind English heart?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 4 seconds]
Say both meanings and contrast their routes once: inherited nakk, borrowed dil. Then stop.
