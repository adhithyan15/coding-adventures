# pintar — to paint

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VERDE-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **verde** and **arreglar**. One way of fixing a room is to
change its colour.

## You'll want to know: pintar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VERDE-01] -->

**pintar** — to paint, both walls and pictures.

*Voy a pintar la cocina de verde* — I'm going to paint the kitchen green.
*Recién pintado* — wet paint, the wording on the sign. *¿Quién pintó esto?* —
who painted this?

Note the **de** in *pintar de verde*. Spanish marks the resulting colour with
*de*, where English puts the adjective straight after the object.

**La pintura** is both the paint in the tin and the painting on the wall.

## Grammar Lens: recién plus a participle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01] -->

**Recién** means "newly" or "a moment ago", and it attaches only to
participles:

| sign | what it means |
|---|---|
| *recién pintado* | wet paint |
| *recién llegado* | newly arrived |
| *recién casados* | newlyweds |

In Spain it never stands alone and never modifies a normal adjective —
*recién limpio* is not said. Take it as half of a two-word unit whose other
half ends in *-ado* or *-ido*.

Much of Latin America is freer with it. *Recién llegué* — I arrived a moment
ago — and *recién ayer* are ordinary in the Southern Cone, where *recién*
behaves like a plain adverb of time.

The participle agrees: *recién pintada* for *la pared*, *recién pintados* for
*los bancos*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01] -->

Latin **pingere**, to paint — a verb that also meant to **embroider**, and
behind both of those an older word for **pricking or cutting a mark** into a
surface.

So painting and needlework were one verb in Latin, because both put a design
onto cloth. The relatives come off the painting side: English **picture**,
**pigment**, **paint** and **pictorial**, and Spanish **pintoresco** —
worth-painting — which English borrowed through French as **picturesque**.

The Latin participle *pictus* is where the harder consonants survived:
Spanish kept the soft, spoken *pintar* and borrowed the bookish *pictórico*
alongside it. Another pair arriving twice, by the two roads.
