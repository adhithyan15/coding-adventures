# meā sententiā, mihi vidētur — in my opinion

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C283-PUTO-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I think my friend is ill". (*Putō amīcum aegrum esse.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Putō* turns the whole sentence into what you think. Latin also
has ways to leave the sentence as it is and hang the opinion on it.

## You'll want to know: meā sententiā
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C283-MEA-SENTENTIA-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C194-RES194-01] -->

**meā sententiā** — "in my opinion". You own **sententia**, "an opinion";
this is its ablative with *meā*, "my": word for word, "by my opinion".

> **Meā sententiā, Rōma pulchra est.** — In my opinion, Rome is beautiful.

[PAUSE 1s] Nothing else in the sentence changes: *Rōma* stays nominative,
*est* stays as it was. Plautus says it, and so does Cicero.

## You'll want to know: mihi vidētur
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C283-MIHI-VIDETUR-02]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C232-ACT232-03, LA-LEX-C68-AEGER-02] -->

**mihi vidētur** — "it seems to me". You own **videor**, "to seem"; *mihi*
is "to me".

> **Mārcus mihi fessus vidētur.** — Marcus seems tired to me.
> **Pluit, ut mihi vidētur.** — It is raining, as far as I can tell.

[PAUSE 1s] Marcus is the subject, so *fessus* stays nominative. And *ut
mihi vidētur*, "as it seems to me", is a phrase Cicero tucks into the middle
of a sentence.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**sententia** is built on *sentiō*, "to feel, to think" (Lewis & Short), so
*meā sententiā* is "by what I feel about it". **vidētur** is the passive of
*videō*, "to see": "it is seen". So *mihi vidētur* is, word for word, "it is
seen, to me", which is how a Roman said "it seems to me". *Videō* is
inherited from Proto-Indo-European *\*weyd-*, "to see" (EtymDB 2.1).

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C283-MEA-SENTENTIA-01, LA-LEX-C283-MIHI-VIDETUR-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "In my opinion"? (***Meā sententiā.***) "It seems to me"?
(***Mihi vidētur.***) After *putō*, *Rōma* becomes what? (***Rōmam***, with
*esse*.) After *meā sententiā*? (It stays ***Rōma***.)
