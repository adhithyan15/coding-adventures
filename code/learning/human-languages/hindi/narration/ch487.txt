Hindi, chapter 487: Catching Something.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
छूत (chūt) — contagion; infection by touch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for the spine, then the Hindi for to swell (of the body).

You'll want to know: छूत.
छूत — chūt — "contagion; infection by touch".
छूत की बीमारी में मरीज़ को अलग रखते हैं। chūt kī bīmārī meṁ marīz ko alag rakhte haiṁ. With a contagious illness, the patient is kept apart.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
छूत (chūt) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi chūt, "touch of anything impure", under chupti, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5057), from the same root as chūnā, "to touch". chūt kī bīmārī is a disease that spreads by contact.

Lesson 2 of 5.
संक्रमण (saṅkramaṇ) — an infection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to swell (of the body), then the Hindi for contagion.

You'll want to know: संक्रमण.
संक्रमण — saṅkramaṇ — "an infection".
गंदे घाव में संक्रमण हो सकता है। gande ghāv meṁ saṅkramaṇ ho saktā hai. A dirty wound can become infected.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
संक्रमण (saṅkramaṇ) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit saṁkramaṇa, "going together, entrance into, transference" (Monier-Williams). The medical sense, a disease passing into the body, is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पकना (paknā) — to fester; to ripen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for measles, then the Hindi for smallpox.

You'll want to know: पकना.
पकना — paknā — "to fester; to ripen".
पुराना घाव पक गया और बहुत दर्द हुआ। purānā ghāv pak gayā aur bahut dard huā. The old wound festered and hurt a lot.
Past (it …): वह घाव पक गया, vah ghāv pak gayā.

The word, taken apart.
पकना (paknā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi paknā, "to be cooked, become ripe, suppurate", under Sanskrit pakvá, "cooked, ripe" (CDIAL 7621). Fruit ripens and a wound festers with the same verb; pakānā, "to cook", is its causative.
