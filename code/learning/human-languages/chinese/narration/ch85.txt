You'll want to know: 在.
在 — zài — "to be at; in, at".
妈妈在家吗 māma zài jiā ma — Is Mum at home?
我在这儿 wǒ zài zhèr — I'm here.
The next lesson puts the character in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
在 (zài) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to dwell' and 'to exist'.

What you've built.
The place comes straight after 在 (zài): 妈妈, 在 (zài), 家. English needs is and at; Mandarin needs 在 (zài) alone.
That is the whole sentence pattern of the chapter: a person or a thing, then 在 (zài), then the place.

Script — the top of 有, a vertical, then 土.
在 (zài).
Three pieces, and two of them you already write.
The top of 有, two strokes: a long horizontal, then a long left-falling stroke from above it, down to the lower left.
A short vertical under them, on the left, down from the falling stroke.
土, three strokes: a short horizontal, a vertical down through it, and a long bar along the foot. It is 十 standing on a bar.
2 + 1 + 3 = six.

Lesson 3 of 6.
那儿 (nàr) — there.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: the character from the last lesson, then a word it pairs with.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 在家, aloud — zài jiā, at home]
[your turn — recall: say 这儿 — zhèr, here]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 那儿.
那儿 — nàr — "there".
他在那儿 tā zài nàr — He's over there.
我在这儿 wǒ zài zhèr — I'm here.

The word, taken apart.
那儿 (nàr) is built from 那 'that' and 儿, the ending that runs into the syllable before it as an r: the same two pieces as 这儿. The Guangyun records 那 as na (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'how, what'; how it came to mean 'that' is not settled.

What you've built.
这儿 and 那儿 (nàr), here and there, answer most of the place questions this chapter asks. Both take the r ending, and both follow 在 (zài).

Lesson 4 of 6.

You'll want to know: lǐbian.
lǐbian — "inside; in".
shū zài bāo lǐbian — The book is in the bag.
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lǐbian is built from lǐ 'inside', the 里 you write, and bian 'side', the syllable that ends zuǒbian and pángbiān. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, liX and pen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the inside of a garment' and 'an edge; a side'.

What you've built.
lǐbian comes after the thing whose inside it names: bāo lǐbian, inside the bag. Put 在 in front and it is a place: zài bāo lǐbian.
里 on its own does the same job in a quicker sentence, as in shān lǐ. lǐbian can also stand alone: zài lǐbian, inside.

The exchange.
A: 我在家 你呢 wǒ zài jiā, nǐ ne — I'm at home. And you? B: wǒ zài xuéxiào — I'm at school. A: 你的书呢 nǐ de shū ne — Where's your book? B: zài bāo lǐbian — In my bag. A: māo ne — And the cat? B: māo zài wàibian — The cat's outside. A: 他呢 tā ne — And him? B: 他在那儿 tā zài nàr — He's over there.
nǐ de shū ne asks where the book is: 呢 after a thing, with nothing else, means and where is it? The answer drops the book and starts at 在 (zài).

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Where is the cat?

What you've built — one pattern, four places.
One pattern, a person or a thing, then 在 (zài), then the place:
我在这儿 wǒ zài zhèr — I'm here.
他在那儿 tā zài nàr — he's there.
shū zài bāo lǐbian — the book is in the bag.
māo zài wàibian — the cat is outside.
Listen. Nǐ de shǒujī ne?
Speak. Place yourself, then somebody you know, then your phone.
