## You'll want to know: cónglái
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C150-CONGLAI-01]; assesses=[] -->

*cónglái* — "ever"; before 没, "never once".

> *wǒ cónglái méi qù guo* — I've never once been there.

Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*cónglái* is built from cóng 'from' and 来 'to come': from then until now. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *dzjowng* and *loj* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), *dzjowng* glossed 'to go to; to follow'. 'Ever' is the pair's own sense, not the gloss's.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-GUO-EXPERIENCE-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-BU-MEI-01] -->

*cónglái* stands in front of 没 … 过 and makes it stronger: 我没去过, I've not been; *wǒ cónglái méi qù guo*, not once.

It needs 没 after it to say never; on its own it is rare.
