Latin, chapter 403: Learned and Unlearned.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
doctus, docta, doctum — learned, skilled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to think over, then the Latin for to call to mind.

You'll want to know: doctus, docta, doctum.
doctus, docta, doctum — "learned, skilled".
Vir doctus et prūdēns est. He is a learned and wise man.
doctus, docta, doctum, the participle of doceō: taught.

The word, taken apart.
doctus is the participle of doceō "to teach" (Lewis & Short): one who has been taught.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ērudītus, ērudīta, ērudītum — educated, learned.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for learned, then the Latin for unlearned.

You'll want to know: ērudītus, ērudīta, ērudītum.
ērudītus, ērudīta, ērudītum — "educated, learned".
Fēmina ērudīta multōs librōs lēgerat. The educated woman had read many books.
ērudītus, ērudīta, ērudītum, the participle of ērudiō. English erudite.

The word, taken apart.
ērudītus is the participle of ērudiō "to educate" (Lewis & Short), from ē and rudis "rough". English erudite comes from it (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
sciēns, scientis — knowing; on purpose; skilled (in).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for unlearned, then the Latin for educated.

You'll want to know: sciēns, scientis.
sciēns, scientis — "knowing; on purpose; skilled (in)".
Sciēns hoc fēcit, nōn imprūdēns. He did this knowingly, not by mistake.
sciēns, scientis, the present participle of sciō: knowing, on purpose. With the genitive, skilled in: bellī sciēns.

The word, taken apart.
sciēns is the present participle of sciō "to know" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English science and conscience come from its family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
imperītus, imperīta, imperītum — inexperienced, unskilled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for educated, then the Latin for knowing.

You'll want to know: imperītus, imperīta, imperītum.
imperītus, imperīta, imperītum — "inexperienced, unskilled".
Imperītus nauta nāvem frēgit. The unskilled sailor wrecked the ship.
imperītus, imperīta, imperītum: the opposite of the word for experienced, with in-, not. With the genitive: bellī imperītus, without experience of war.

The word, taken apart.
imperītus is built from in- "not" and perītus "experienced" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from the root of experior "to try, to test".
