Japanese, chapter 322: Saying Yes.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
しょうちする (shōchi suru) — to consent; to agree to a request.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for rather, then the Japanese for to nod.

You'll want to know: しょうちする.
しょうちする — shōchi suru — "to consent; to agree to a request".
あにが たのんだので、しょうちしました。 — ani ga tanonda node, shōchi shimashita — "My brother asked me, so I agreed."
Polite しょうちします, shōchishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): てんいんが しょうちしました。, ten'in ga shōchi shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しょうちする (shōchi suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, shōchi, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: shō, "acquiesce" (Middle Chinese dzying) and chi, "know" (trje) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
うけいれる (ukeireru) — to accept; to take in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to nod, then the Japanese for to consent.

You'll want to know: うけいれる.
うけいれる — ukeireru — "to accept; to take in".
みんなが さんせいしたので、かいしゃは ていあんを うけいれました。 — minna ga sansei shita node, kaisha wa teian o ukeiremashita — "Everyone agreed, so the company accepted the proposal."
Polite うけいれます, ukeiremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): かいしゃが うけいれました。, kaisha ga ukeiremashita.

The word, taken apart.
うけいれる (ukeireru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from ukeru, "to receive" and ireru, "to put in", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to consent, then the Japanese for to take in.

You'll want to know: もちろん.
もちろん — mochiron — "of course".
じかんが あるから、もちろん てつだいます。 — jikan ga aru kara, mochiron tetsudaimasu — "I have time, so of course I will help."

The word, taken apart.
もちろん (mochiron) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are mochi, "not" and ron, "argument" (Middle Chinese lwon) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana.
