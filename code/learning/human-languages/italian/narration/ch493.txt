Italian, chapter 493: Parenti acquisiti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il cognato — brother-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to grow fond of, then the Italian for affectionate.

You'll want to know: il cognato.
il cognato — "brother-in-law".
Mio cognato è il fratello di mia moglie. My brother-in-law is my wife's brother.
The husband of your sister, or the brother of your husband or wife.

The word, taken apart.
cognato is inherited from Latin cognātus "related by birth" (EtymDB), from co- "with" and nātus "born"; in Italian it moved to relatives by marriage.

Lesson 3 of 5.
il bisnonno — great-grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for brother-in-law, then the Italian for sister-in-law.

You'll want to know: il bisnonno.
il bisnonno — "great-grandfather".
Il mio bisnonno è nato in campagna. My great-grandfather was born in the countryside.
La bisnonna — great-grandmother; i bisnonni — great-grandparents.

The word, taken apart.
bisnonno is built from bis- "twice", from Latin bis (Lewis & Short), and nonno "grandfather", from Late Latin nonnus "a monk, a tutor" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la parentela — relatives, kinship.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: la parentela.
la parentela — "relatives, kinship".
Tutta la parentela è venuta alla festa. All the relatives came to the party.
All your relatives together; grado di parentela — degree of kinship.

The word, taken apart.
parentela is built on parente "relative", from Latin parēns, parentis "a parent" (Lewis & Short), from parere "to give birth".

Lesson 5 of 5.
assomigliare — to look like, to resemble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for great-grandfather, then the Italian for relatives.

You'll want to know: assomigliare.
assomigliare — "to look like, to resemble".
Assomigli molto a tuo padre. You look a lot like your father.
Assomigliare a qualcuno; reflexive assomigliarsi — to look alike.

The word, taken apart.
assomigliare is built on somigliare "to resemble", usually traced to Vulgar Latin similiāre, from Latin similis "like" (Lewis & Short).
