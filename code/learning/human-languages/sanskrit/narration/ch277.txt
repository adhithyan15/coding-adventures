Sanskrit, chapter 277: One Thing, Then the Next.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आगत्य (āgatya) — one action, then the next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "the boy, having gone home, reads". (bālaḥ gṛhaṃ gatvā paṭhati.) Then say "he went home". (saḥ gṛhaṃ gatavān.) Now put the two together.

You'll want to know: -त्वा with the past.
सः खादित्वा गतवान्। saḥ khāditvā gatavān. — "He ate, then he left."
The -त्वा form finishes the first action, and the last verb carries the past for both. खादित्वा (khāditvā) is "having eaten", and कृत्वा (kṛtvā) "having done":
सा गृहं गत्वा पठितवती। sā gṛhaṃ gatvā paṭhitavatī. — "She went home and read."
ते कार्यं कृत्वा गतवन्तः। te kāryaṃ kṛtvā gatavantaḥ. — "They did the work and left."
Both actions belong to one doer, and the -त्वा action comes first.

You'll want to know: -य after a prefix.
A verb with a prefix, such as आगच्छति (āgacchati), "comes", or प्रविशति (praviśati), "goes in", takes -य (-ya) in place of -त्वा: one short ending, after a prefix (Pāṇini 7.1.37).
सः आगत्य पठति। saḥ āgatya paṭhati. — "He comes and reads."
मीरा गृहं प्रविश्य उपविशति। mīrā gṛhaṃ praviśya upaviśati. — "Mira goes into the house and sits down."
सा उपविश्य खादितवती। sā upaviśya khāditavatī. — "She sat down and ate."
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
the verb: गच्छति. the action finished: गत्वा (gatvā).
the verb: आगच्छति. the action finished: आगत्य (āgatya).
the verb: प्रविशति. the action finished: प्रविश्य (praviśya).
the verb: उपविशति. the action finished: उपविश्य (upaviśya).

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Having come, he eats" — saḥ āgatya khādati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Having come, he sits down" — saḥ āgatya upaviśati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Having gone in"? (प्रविश्य.) Why -ya and not -tvā? (The verb has a prefix.) "She ate and then left"? (सा खादित्वा गतवती.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
प्रथमम्, ततः, अन्ते (prathamam, tataḥ, ante) — first, then, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "she went home and read". (sā gṛhaṃ gatvā paṭhitavatī.) Two actions in one sentence. A story needs more sentences, and words to order them.

You'll want to know: प्रथमम्, ततः, अनन्तरम्.
प्रथमम् (prathamam), "first", is प्रथमः (prathamaḥ), "first", said as an adverb. ततः (tataḥ), "then", and अनन्तरम् (anantaram), "afterwards", you know.
प्रथमं सः खादितवान्। prathamaṃ saḥ khāditavān. — "First he ate."
ततः सः पठितवान्। tataḥ saḥ paṭhitavān. — "Then he read."
अनन्तरं सः गतवान्। anantaraṃ saḥ gatavān. — "Afterwards he left."

You'll want to know: अन्ते.
अन्ते (ante), "in the end", closes the story.
अन्ते सः गृहम् आगतवान्। ante saḥ gṛham āgatavān. — "In the end he came home."
Four lines, in order. क्रीतवती (krītavatī) is the past of क्रीणाति (krīṇāti), "buys":
प्रथमं मीरा आपणं गतवती। prathamaṃ mīrā āpaṇaṃ gatavatī. — "First Mira went to the shop."
ततः सा फलं क्रीतवती। tataḥ sā phalaṃ krītavatī. — "Then she bought a fruit."
अनन्तरं सा गृहम् आगतवती। anantaraṃ sā gṛham āgatavatī. — "Afterwards she came home."
अन्ते सा फलं खादितवती। ante sā phalaṃ khāditavatī. — "In the end she ate the fruit."

[your turn — say: "First I ate" — prathamam ahaṃ khāditavān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Then I ate" — tataḥ ahaṃ khāditavān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In the end I ate" — ante ahaṃ khāditavān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"First"? (प्रथमम्.) "Afterwards"? (अनन्तरम्.) "In the end"? (अन्ते.) Which of them opens a story? (प्रथमम्.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
शीघ्रम् (śīghram) — quickly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "first he ate, then he left". (prathamaṃ saḥ khāditavān, tataḥ saḥ gatavān.)

You'll want to know: शीघ्रम्.
शीघ्रम् — śīghram — "quickly".
शीघ्रम् आगच्छतु। śīghram āgacchatu. — "Please come quickly."
सः शीघ्रं धावति। saḥ śīghraṃ dhāvati. — "He runs quickly."
सा शीघ्रं गृहं गतवती। sā śīghraṃ gṛhaṃ gatavatī. — "She went home quickly."

The word, taken apart.
शीघ्रम् (śīghram) is the neuter of शीघ्र (śīghra), "quick, swift", said as an adverb, as प्रथमम् is. Monier-Williams calls its derivation doubtful, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Sindhi sigho, "quick" (CDIAL 12484).

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Please come quickly" — śīghram āgacchatu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "First he came quickly" — prathamaṃ saḥ śīghram āgatavān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does शीघ्रम् (śīghram) mean? ("Quickly".) "She went home quickly"? (सा शीघ्रं गृहं गतवती.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
(reading) — Mira's day in the city.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Eighty words, every one of them met before: Mira's day in the city, told in order.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
ह्यः मीरा नगरं गतवती। hyaḥ mīrā nagaraṃ gatavatī.
प्रथमं सा स्थानकं गतवती। prathamaṃ sā sthānakaṃ gatavatī.
तत्र सा एकं यानपत्रं क्रीतवती। tatra sā ekaṃ yānapatraṃ krītavatī.
ततः सा धूमयानं प्रविष्टवती। tataḥ sā dhūmayānaṃ praviṣṭavatī.
धूमयाने सा पुस्तकं पठति स्म। dhūmayāne sā pustakaṃ paṭhati sma.
यदा सा पठति स्म, तदा वर्षा आगतवती। yadā sā paṭhati sma, tadā varṣā āgatavatī.
धूमयानं शीघ्रं गच्छति स्म। dhūmayānaṃ śīghraṃ gacchati sma.
अनन्तरं सा नगरम् आगतवती। anantaraṃ sā nagaram āgatavatī.
नगरे सा आपणं गत्वा फलानि क्रीतवती। nagare sā āpaṇaṃ gatvā phalāni krītavatī.
तत्र अरुणः अपि आगतवान्। tatra aruṇaḥ api āgatavān.
मीरा अरुणं दृष्ट्वा प्रसन्ना अभवत्। mīrā aruṇaṃ dṛṣṭvā prasannā abhavat.
अरुणः मीरा च उद्यानं गतवन्तौ। aruṇaḥ mīrā ca udyānaṃ gatavantau.
उद्याने बालाः क्रीडन्ति स्म। udyāne bālāḥ krīḍanti sma.
अरुणः तत्र उपविश्य फलं खादितवान्। aruṇaḥ tatra upaviśya phalaṃ khāditavān.
ततः वर्षा पुनः आगतवती। tataḥ varṣā punaḥ āgatavatī.
अन्ते मीरा शीघ्रं गृहम् आगतवती। ante mīrā śīghraṃ gṛham āgatavatī.
श्वः सा पुनः नगरं गमिष्यति। śvaḥ sā punaḥ nagaraṃ gamiṣyati.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage a second time with the romanization covered, once through, without stopping; do not translate as you go]

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
How this book writes: Pāṇini (1.4.109) calls words said close together saṃhitā, and there the sounds at the joins change. Where this book has bālaḥ gacchati, a classical page has बालो गच्छति (bālo gacchati), and saḥ gacchati is स गच्छति (sa gacchati; Pāṇini 6.1.132). This book writes each word whole, with the one join you know, gṛhaṃ gacchati. Recognise the joined forms; there is nothing here to apply.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: what Mira did first, at the station]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: how the day ended, with ante]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
What was Mira doing on the train when the rain came?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Why paṭhati sma in the train? (The reading was going on when the rain came.) Which words keep the steps in order? (prathamam, tataḥ, anantaram, ante.)
