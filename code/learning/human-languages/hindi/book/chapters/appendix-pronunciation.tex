\markboth{Devanagari}{Devanagari}

Not to be read before the lessons --- they teach reading through real words,
each letter introduced as a word needs it. This page just gathers the system.

\section*{The three facts}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Consonants carry a built-in ``a''}: \hi{क} = ka, \hi{न} = na,
    \hi{म} = ma.
  \item \textbf{A vowel sign (\emph{m\=atr\=a}) changes that vowel}: \hi{ा} =
    \=a (\hi{का} k\=a), \hi{ि} = i (written before, read after), \hi{े} = e
    (\hi{के} ke), and \hi{ी ु ू ै ो ौ} for \=\i, u, \=u, ai, o, au.
  \item \textbf{A \emph{halant} (\hi{्}) removes the built-in vowel}: \hi{क्}
    = bare k. A vowel-less consonant leans onto the next as a \textbf{conjunct}:
    \hi{स्} + \hi{त} $\to$ \hi{स्त} (st), \hi{न्} + \hi{य} $\to$ \hi{न्य} (ny).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Independent vowels (word-initial)}

\hi{अ} a · \hi{आ} \=a · \hi{इ} i · \hi{ई} \=\i · \hi{उ} u · \hi{ऊ} \=u ·
\hi{ए} e · \hi{ऐ} ai · \hi{ओ} o · \hi{औ} au.

\section*{Worth flagging}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Aspiration} distinguishes letters: \hi{क} k / \hi{ख} kh,
    \hi{त} t / \hi{थ} th, \hi{द} d / \hi{ध} dh --- the ``h'' is a real puff.
  \item \textbf{Retroflex} (\hi{ट ड ण}) vs.\ \textbf{dental} (\hi{त द न}):
    tongue curled back vs.\ on the teeth.
  \item \textbf{Nukta} (a dot below) marks Perso-Arabic/English sounds:
    \hi{ज़} z, \hi{फ़} f, \hi{क़} q.
\end{itemize}

\section*{The two vocabularies}

Many everyday ideas have two Hindi words --- a \textbf{Sanskrit} one, often
formal (\emph{dhanyav\=ad}, \emph{namaste}), and a \textbf{Perso-Arabic} one,
often everyday (\emph{shukriy\=a}, \emph{alvid\=a}). The lessons trace which is
which.
