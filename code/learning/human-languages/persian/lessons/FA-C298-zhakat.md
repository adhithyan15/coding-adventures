# ژاکت (zhâkat) — a cardigan, in case it turns cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-AGAR-SUBJ-01, FA-LEX-C120-THINGS120-01, FA-LEX-C204-CHIZ204-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if the weather is good". (*Agar havâ khub bâshad.*) Then a
padded jacket and a pullover. (*Kâpshan*, *poliver*.)

[PAUSE 1s] A condition is easiest to practise on the weather, and on what to
wear for it.

## You'll want to know: ژاکت
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-C298-ZHAKAT-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C39-VERBS39-03, FA-LEX-C59-ADJ59-04, FA-GRAMMAR-POLITE-IMPERATIVE-01, FA-LEX-C208-CHIZ208-01] -->

**ژاکت** — *zhâkat* — "a cardigan", a knitted jacket that buttons down the
front.

> **اگر هوا سرد باشد، ژاکت بپوشید.** — *agar havâ sard bâshad, zhâkat
> bepushid* — If it's cold, put on a cardigan.

*Bepushid* is *pushidan*, "to put on, to wear", on its stem *push-*. A
*zhâkat* is lighter than a *kâpshan*, the padded jacket from chapter one
hundred and twenty, and it opens at the front where a *poliver* goes over the
head.

Say *zhâ-kat*. The *zh* is the sound in the middle of English *measure*, and
its letter is one Persian added to the Arabic alphabet. The next lesson puts
it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

EtymDB 2.1 has no entry for *zhâkat*. It is usually traced to French
*jaquette*, a jacket. EtymDB takes English **jacket** back to Middle French *jacquet*, from the same French family.
