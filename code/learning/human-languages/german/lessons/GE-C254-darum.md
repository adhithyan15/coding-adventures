# darum — that's why

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C250-LINK250-02, GE-LEX-WARUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the German for "therefore", from chapter two hundred and
fifty. (*Deshalb.*) And the question "why?" (*Warum?*)

## You'll want to know: darum
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-DARUM-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C116-DINGE116-05, GE-LEX-SPAET-01, GE-LEX-WETTER-02] -->

**darum** — "that's why, so": *da-RUM*, with a short *u*; when you point
hard at the reason, *DA-rum*.

> *Es regnet, darum nehme ich den Regenschirm.* — It's raining, so I'm
> taking the umbrella.
> *Der Zug hatte Verspätung, darum kam ich spät.* — The train was late,
> that's why I came late.

*Darum* and *deshalb* do the same job, and either fits in both sentences.
*Darum* has one more use: on its own, it is the curt answer to *warum*.
"*Warum?*" "*Darum!*", because.

## Grammar Lens: two directions, two word orders
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-WEIL-DARUM-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WEIL-01, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-LETZT-01, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-SECOND-02, GE-GRAMMAR-DANN-VERB-ZWEI-02] -->

One fact, told two ways:

> *Ich nehme den Regenschirm, weil es* **regnet**.
> *Es regnet, darum* **nehme** *ich den Regenschirm.*

**weil** points back to the cause, and its verb goes to the end.
**darum** points forward to the result. It takes the first place of its
clause, like *dann*, so its verb comes straight after it: *darum nehme
ich*, not *darum ich nehme*.

The comma before *darum* is where one clause stops and the next begins. In
a longer stretch, *darum* can open a fresh sentence: *Es regnet. Darum
nehme ich den Regenschirm.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-ETYMON-WARUM-WAR-UM-02] -->

**darum** is *da(r)*, "there, that", + **um**, "around, about": about
that. You took *warum* apart the same way, "about what". EtymDB 2.1 lists
*darum* without its parts, but traces *um* through Old High German *umbi*
to Proto-Germanic *\*umbi*, beside Greek *amphí* and Latin *ambi-*,
"around".
