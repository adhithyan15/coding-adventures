Latin, chapter 325: Nōmen et bulla.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bulla, bullae — an amulet (worn by a child); a bubble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for childhood, then the Latin for to bring up.

You'll want to know: bulla, bullae.
bulla, bullae — "an amulet (worn by a child); a bubble".
Puer bullam auream in collō gerēbat. The boy wore a golden amulet round his neck.
Feminine. First a bubble, then the round locket a freeborn boy wore until he came of age. English bullet and the papal bull come from it.

The word, taken apart.
bulla first meant "a bubble", anything swelling into a round shape (Lewis & Short); where the word comes from is not settled. English bull, a papal letter with a round seal, comes from it, and bullet through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
crepundia, crepundiōrum — a rattle; childhood toys.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to bring up, then the Latin for an amulet.

You'll want to know: crepundia, crepundiōrum.
crepundia, crepundiōrum — "a rattle; childhood toys".
Nūtrīx īnfantī crepundia dedit. The nurse gave the baby a rattle.
Neuter, plural only. In comedies a lost child is recognised by the little toys kept in a box.

The word, taken apart.
crepundia is built on crepō, "to rattle, to creak" (Lewis & Short): things that rattle.

Lesson 3 of 5.
praenōmen, praenōminis — a first name.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an amulet, then the Latin for a rattle.

You'll want to know: praenōmen, praenōminis.
praenōmen, praenōminis — "a first name".
Pater fīliō praenōmen avī dedit. The father gave his son his grandfather's first name.
Neuter, 3rd declension: the name before the family name. Romans had few first names and wrote them short.

The word, taken apart.
praenōmen joins prae, "before", and nōmen, "name" (Lewis & Short): the name in front of the family name.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cognōmen, cognōminis — an extra family name, a surname.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rattle, then the Latin for a first name.

You'll want to know: cognōmen, cognōminis.
cognōmen, cognōminis — "an extra family name, a surname".
Ob magnam victōriam cognōmen novum accēpit. Because of a great victory he received a new surname.
Neuter, 3rd declension. The third name, which often told something about a person or a branch of the family.

The word, taken apart.
cognōmen joins co-, "together with", and nōmen, "name" (EtymDB): the name added to the family name.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tūtor, tūtōris — a guardian, a protector.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a first name, then the Latin for an extra family name.

You'll want to know: tūtor, tūtōris.
tūtor, tūtōris — "a guardian, a protector".
Avunculus tūtor puerōrum factus est. Their uncle became the boys' guardian.
Masculine, 3rd declension. A child with no father had a tūtor to keep his property safe. English tutor.

The word, taken apart.
tūtor is built on tueor, "to watch over, to protect" (Lewis & Short): a protector. English tutor comes from it.
