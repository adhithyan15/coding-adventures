## Added — chapters 287-289 prove Persian's missing A1 writing stages

- Three writing lessons on SPINE-SAY-WHY, where چون (chapter 92) and ولی
  (chapter 158) already live, appended after chapter 286 at sequences
  14850-14870. None introduces a word, a letter or a knowledge atom.
- Chapter 287, FA-W287-khat-e-khodam, "my own line": the first controlled
  composition, one line about yourself with no model, word bank or
  romanization. The Persian-specific check is the dots: ب and پ share one body
  and differ only by one dot below or three, and ی drops its dots at the end of
  a word, so the line is read back dot by dot before anything else. A name
  that needs a letter the ramp has not yet written is written without it and
  checked only on the letters the learner owns.
- Chapter 288, FA-W288-ba-saat, "with a clock": a three-minute, six-field form
  in Persian script, stopped when the timer rings, with a one-minute review cap.
  It says plainly that this book found no widely used international A1
  Persian writing paper: SAMFA, the track's external capstone, is one academic test
  (two essays in one hour) that its provider maps to no CEFR level. The A1
  paper is therefore the track's own contract in `assessment-spec.md`, modelled
  on the CEFR A1 writing expectation (a form with personal details and a short
  simple message): 20 minutes, a practical form and a 30-40 word message,
  60/100 on every paper with no averaging, and Persian orthographic control as one of
  four scored writing dimensions. The lesson rehearses only the form and states
  that it is not the full paper.
- Chapter 289, FA-W289-chahar-khat, "four lines": a connected composition to a
  named reader using ولی and چون. Both carry و, which never joins to its left,
  so each word has a gap inside it; the check is that the space between words
  stays wider than the break inside one.
- The cumulative writing-stage measure now counts controlled-composition,
  timed-assessment-production and connected-composition for Persian at A1.
