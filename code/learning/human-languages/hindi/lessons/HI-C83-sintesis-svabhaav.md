# वह कैसी है? — the question the seven were assembled to answer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **होशियार** and **मेहनती**. Two words no reference letter could
do without, and the track had neither until now.

## Grammar Lens: the question word bends too
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

**कैसा** is itself an **-आ** word, so the question agrees before the answer
does:

| about | question | a possible answer |
|---|---|---|
| a man | वह कैसा है? | वह गंभीर है। |
| a woman | वह कैसी है? | वह शर्मीली है। |

Notice what that means in practice. The gender is announced **in the question**,
before any adjective arrives. A listener who catches only *kaisī* already knows a
woman is being asked about — and knows that any bending adjective in the answer
must end in **-ई**.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-02, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-05, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06] -->

Read aloud. Every word has been taught:

> — आपकी बहन कैसी है?
> — वह होशियार है। वह मेहनती भी है। वह शर्मीली है।
> — आपका भाई कैसा है?
> — वह मिलनसार है। वह गंभीर भी है।

Five adjectives in that exchange, and only **शर्मीली** had to change its ending.
The other four were decided by their own last letters rather than by who was
being described — which is the entire point of the chapter, put into one
conversation.
