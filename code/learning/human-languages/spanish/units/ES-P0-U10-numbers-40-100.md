# Unit 10: Numbers 40-100

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Count *once* to *treinta*. Today finishes the round-number set.

## New Material

**cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa** — 40-90
> ← Latin *quadraginta, quinquaginta, sexaginta, septuaginta, octoginta,
> nonaginta*. Same *-ginta* ("tens") ending as *veinte* and *treinta*.
> Combine the same way: *cuarenta y cinco* (45).

**cien / ciento** — 100
> ← Latin *centum* — an enormous English cognate family: *cent, century,
> percent, centipede, centennial*.

## Grammar Lens

*cien* and *ciento* are the same number with a usage split English doesn't
have: **cien** stands alone or directly before a noun (*cien dólares*, "one
hundred dollars"), while **ciento** is used when another number follows it
(*ciento diez*, "one hundred ten"). English "hundred" doesn't change form
either way — this is a real, small grammar rule with no direct English
parallel to lean on.
