Sanskrit, chapter 266: A small market exchange.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.

Lesson 2 of 6.
क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi) — I buy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “a fee,” then “us” as an object, then make one complete polite request for a familiar item. (Śulkam; asmān; the frame ending in dadātu.)

Grammar Lens: the buyer speaks.
You know क्रीणाति (krīṇāti), “he or she buys.” When the buyer speaks for themselves, keep the whole new form together:
अहं फलं क्रीणामि। Ahaṃ phalaṃ krīṇāmi. I buy fruit.
Here क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi) is the first-person singular present. The final -मि (-mi) points to the speaker, as it does in forms you already know. Both checked-in A1 mocks use this complete form. This is not a complete present paradigm: the other persons, numbers, and class-nine forms wait for later.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — contrast: buys; I buy — krīṇāti; krīṇāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “I buy fruit” — ahaṃ phalaṃ krīṇāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the buyer form once more — krīṇāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Change क्रीणाति, “buys,” into the one taught speaker form. (क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi).)


Lesson 3 of 6.
Ask the price and hear the answer — ask how much an item costs and understand a short answer in rupees.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “news,” then “you two” as an object. (Vārtā; yuṣmān.)

Grammar Lens: one price turn.
Put two known words together to ask the question used in the A1 mocks:
मूल्यं कियत्? Mūlyaṃ kiyat? How much is the price?
One short answer is:
विंशतिः रूप्यकाणि। Viṃśatiḥ rūpyakāṇi. Twenty rupees.
You already know रूप्यकम्, one rupee. Keep रूप्यकाणि together inside this useful price answer. This small exchange does not teach the complete neuter plural system or every way to state a price.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: ask the price — mūlyaṃ kiyat?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: answer “twenty rupees” — viṃśatiḥ rūpyakāṇi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the question and answer as one turn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ask one price, then answer with twenty rupees.


Lesson 4 of 6.
Complete one small transaction — choose, buy, ask a price, request the item, and close politely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “a drama,” then explain how the taught object forms change for person and number. (Nāṭakam; use the object-person-and-number contrast.)

Guided Practice: five turns.
Build the exchange one complete turn at a time:
अहं फलं क्रीणामि। — Ahaṃ phalaṃ krīṇāmi. — I buy fruit.
मूल्यं कियत्? — Mūlyaṃ kiyat? — How much is the price?
विंशतिः रूप्यकाणि। — Viṃśatiḥ rūpyakāṇi. — Twenty rupees.
कृपया मह्यं फलं ददातु। — Kṛpayā mahyaṃ phalaṃ dadātu. — Please give me fruit.
धन्यवादः। — Dhanyavādaḥ. — Thank you.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the five turns once without breaking the exchange apart.

What you've built.
The vocabulary was already yours. Now it carries one complete A1 transaction.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the five jobs in order: choose and buy, ask the price, hear the price, request the item, close politely.


Lesson 5 of 6.
Choose and complete the exchange — independently complete a familiar-item transaction in a shop or market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “a poem.” (Kāvyam.)

Guided Practice: independent round one.
No Sanskrit model is shown before you answer.
[pause 12 seconds]
You are speaking to a shopkeeper at a market. Choose one familiar item. Say that you buy it. Ask the price. The shopkeeper says that the price is twenty rupees. Request the item politely, then close with thanks.

What you've built: check after speaking.
One complete answer with fruit is अहं फलं क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi)। मूल्यं कियत्? विंशतिः रूप्यकाणि। कृपया मह्यं फलं ददातु। धन्यवादः। Your item may differ; the five jobs must remain.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which two new pieces held the old vocabulary together? (The buyer form क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi) and the price-exchange pattern.)


Lesson 6 of 6.
Retrieve the whole market transaction — independently choose a setting and complete a second familiar-item transaction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say “a film,” then “I will go.” (Citrapaṭaḥ; gamiṣyāmi.)

Guided Practice: independent round two.
No Sanskrit model is shown before you answer.
[pause 12 seconds]
Choose a shop or market and a different familiar item. Say that you buy it. Ask its price. The shopkeeper says that the price is one hundred rupees. Request the item politely and close with thanks.

What you've built: check after speaking.
A complete answer still has five jobs: say what you buy; ask the price; hear the rupee amount; request the item; thank the shopkeeper. Your chosen item may differ from the first round.

Wrap-up Recall: boundary.
You can now complete one small A1 market exchange with familiar language. This is not complete commerce, currency, bargaining, consumer-rights, or present paradigm coverage, and it is not evidence of exam readiness or learner success.
