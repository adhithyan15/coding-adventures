Mandarin Chinese, chapter 460: Feeling Low.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yùmèn — down; gloomy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for what is really on one's mind, then the Mandarin for hurt by unfair blame.

You'll want to know: yùmèn.
yùmèn — "down; gloomy".
wǒ bìng le yí ge xīngqī hěn yùmèn — I've been ill for a week, and I'm feeling really down.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yùmèn is built from yù 'dense' and mèn 'vexed', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jut and mwonH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as thick and blocked and the second 'vexed'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
难为情 (nánwéiqíng) — embarrassed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for down in the dumps, then the Mandarin for to sigh.

You'll want to know: 难为情.
难为情 — nánwéiqíng — "embarrassed".
大家都笑我我很难为情 dàjiā dōu xiào wǒ wǒ hěn nánwéiqíng — Everyone laughed at me, and I was so embarrassed.

The word, taken apart.
难为情 (nánwéiqíng) is built from 难 'hard', 为 'to make' and 情 'feeling', all inherited from Middle Chinese, nan, hjwe and dzjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'hard to do'; the sense 'embarrassed' is not given there.

同情 — tóngqíng — "to sympathize with".
nàge háizi gēn māma zǒusàn le wǒ hěn tóngqíng tā — That boy got separated from his mother, and I really feel for him.

The word, taken apart.
同情 (tóngqíng) is built from 同 'the same' and 情 'feeling', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwng and dzjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'equal' and 'together'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
xiànmù — to envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for embarrassed, then the Mandarin for to sympathize with.

You'll want to know: xiànmù.
xiànmù — "to envy".
nǐ qù guo hěn duō guójiā wǒ hěn xiànmù nǐ — You've been to so many countries; I really envy you.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiànmù is built from xiàn 'to covet' and mù 'to long for', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zjenH and muH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under three readings and glosses this one 'to covet'; it glosses the second as thinking of someone with longing.
