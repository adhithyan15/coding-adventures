Spanish, chapter 88: Nos --- Us, and the We the Verb Already Says.
2 lessons. Both can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 2.
nos — "us".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you love me." (Me quieres.) And "I love you." (Te quiero.) Both of those are about one person on the receiving end. Here is more than one.
[pause 2 seconds]
Podemos keeps the plain stem; pueden breaks it again. Say both.

Grammar Lens: the third person in the room.
nos — us.
Nos quieren. — They love us.
Same slot as always, in front of the verb. Me is one person speaking; nos is that person and somebody else.
Me quieren. — They love me. Nos quieren. — They love us.
One letter of difference, and the difference is how many people you are speaking for.

The word, taken apart.
nos is Latin nos, "us" and "we", unchanged in two thousand years. The next lesson shows what Spanish built on it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Me quieren" then "Nos quieren"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Te quiero" then "Nos quieren" — one person, then a group]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
Say they love us.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Me quieren and nos quieren differ in one thing. What?
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Where does nos stand in nos quieren?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "they love us." (Nos quieren.) And which slot did nos take? (The one in front of the verb.) Next: where nos came from, and the verb ending that has been saying "we" without it.


Lesson 2 of 2.
nosotros — the "we" the verb already says.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "they love us." (Nos quieren.)
[pause 1 second]
Nos is "us." Today it becomes "we" — and you will find you have been saying "we" for a long time without it.

The word, taken apart.
Nos is Latin nōs, which meant both we and us — the language had not yet split them into two words.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have been saying "we" without it. The -mos ending on hablamos, comemos, tenemos already means we — it is the verb's own "we" ending, Latin -mus (amāmus, "we love"), a different word from nōs that happens to mean the same person. That is why hablamos alone is a whole sentence, and the pronoun is extra.
And the word for "we" itself:
nosotros — we, literally nos + otros, "we others."
[pause 2 seconds]
Spanish added otros to the plain nos somewhere in the Middle Ages, to make clear the speaker meant this group and not the person being addressed. Catalan (nosaltres) and Occitan (nosautres) did the same; standard French and Italian did not, and in Spanish the seam is still visible in the word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "hablamos" — "we speak," with no pronoun at all]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "nosotros hablamos" — "we speak," said twice]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Which verb ending already says we, without nosotros?
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What are the two pieces inside nosotros, and what do they mean together?
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Nosotros hablamos says we in two places. Name them.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is nosotros made of? (Nos + otros — "we others.") And which ending already says "we" without it? (-mos, from Latin -mus.) Next: the plural of lo and la, and you will not need to be told what they are.
