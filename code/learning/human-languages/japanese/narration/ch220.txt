Japanese, chapter 220: Weight and Staying Healthy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
けんこう (kenkō) — health.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to collapse, then the Japanese for an injury.

You'll want to know: けんこう.
けんこう — kenkō — "health".
けんこうは たいせつです。 — kenkō wa taisetsu desu — "Health is important."

The word, taken apart.
けんこう (kenkō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ken, "strong" (Middle Chinese gjonH) and kō, "at ease" (khang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ふとる (futoru) — to gain weight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for health, then the Japanese for robust.

You'll want to know: ふとる.
ふとる — futoru — "to gain weight".
いぬは ふとりましたか。 — inu wa futorimashita ka — "Has the dog put on weight?"
Polite ふとります, futorimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ふとる (futoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed and built on the same stem as futoi, "thick"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

やせる (yaseru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
