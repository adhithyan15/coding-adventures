quàn — to urge; to try to persuade.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: quàn.
quàn — "to urge; to try to persuade".
wǒ quàn tā zuìhǎo bié hējiǔ — I urged him not to drink.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
quàn is inherited from Middle Chinese khjwonH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'to encourage' and 'to teach'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
不准 (bùzhǔn) — to not allow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a person's character and upbringing, then the Mandarin for to urge someone.

You'll want to know: 不准.
不准 — bùzhǔn — "to not allow".
上课的时候不准吃东西 shàngkè de shíhou bùzhǔn chī dōngxi — No eating in class.
The new character, in pieces: 准 (zhǔn) — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left the ice piece of 冷, two strokes; on the right a bird piece, eight strokes: a left-falling stroke, a long vertical, a dot, three short bars, a vertical down through them, and a long bar at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
The tone of bù stays falling here, because the next syllable is not a falling tone.

The word, taken apart.
不准 (bùzhǔn) is built from 不 'not' and zhǔn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut and tsywinX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the second as the everyday spelling of a word glossed 'even' and 'level'. How it came to mean allowing is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

Script — the ice piece, then the bird piece.
准 (zhǔn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the ice piece, two strokes, as in 冷: a dot, then a stroke rising from the bottom left.
The right piece, the bird piece, eight strokes: a left-falling stroke, a long vertical down the left side, a dot at the top right, three short horizontals, a vertical down through them, then a long horizontal at the foot.
2 + 8 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 准 (zhǔn), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 准 (zhǔn)? (Two.) Which character you know has the same left piece? (冷.) What closes the bird piece? (A long horizontal at the foot.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
biāozhǔn — standard to measure by.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: biāozhǔn.
biāozhǔn — "standard to measure by".
wǒ juéde zhège biāozhǔn tài gāo le — I think the standard is set too high.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biāozhǔn is built from biāo and 准 (zhǔn) 'level', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjiew and tsywinX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to raise' and 'the tip of a tree', and the second 'level'.
