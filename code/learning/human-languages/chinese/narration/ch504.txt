Mandarin Chinese, chapter 504: Songs.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gēcí — the words of a song; lyrics.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to create (art or writing), then the Mandarin for a new year print.

You'll want to know: gēcí.
gēcí — "the words of a song; lyrics".
wǒ tīng le liǎng tiān cái jìzhù gēcí — It took two days of listening before I knew the words.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gēcí is built from gē 'a song' and cí 'words', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ka and zi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the voice of a person singing' and the second 'to speak'.

You'll want to know: 儿歌.
儿歌 — érgē — "nursery rhyme; children's song".
这是我小时候最喜欢的儿歌 zhè shì wǒ xiǎoshíhou zuì xǐhuan de érgē — This was my favourite nursery rhyme when I was little.
The new character, in pieces: 歌 (gē) — fourteen strokes in two pieces: on the left 可 kě twice, one over the other, ten strokes; on the right four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
儿歌 (érgē) is built from 儿 'a child' and 歌 (gē) 'a song', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nye and ka (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'an infant' and the second 'the voice of a person singing'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
歌 (gē) — kě twice, then four strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — kě twice, then four strokes and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kě, ér]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — kě twice, then four strokes.
歌 (gē).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, ten strokes: 可 kě twice, one over the other. The upper 可 is small: a bar, 口, and a vertical with no hook. The lower 可 is wider: a long bar, 口, and a vertical that hooks to the left.
The right piece, four strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns down to the left, a long stroke falling to the left, and a long stroke falling to the right.
5 + 5 + 4 = fourteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 歌 (gē), all fourteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 歌 (gē) have? (Fourteen.) What is the left piece? (可, twice.) Which of the two has a hook? (The lower one.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 歌 (gē), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: 国歌.
国歌 — guógē — "national anthem".
tīng guógē de shíhou wǒ gǎnjué hěn jīdòng — When I hear the national anthem, I feel very moved.

The word, taken apart.
国歌 (guógē) is built from 国 'a country' and 歌 (gē) 'a song', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwok and ka (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
