Mandarin Chinese, chapter 250: Hobbies.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
àihào — hobby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for marks at school, then the Mandarin for hard-working.

You'll want to know: àihào.
àihào — "hobby".
nǐ yǒu shénme àihào — What hobbies do you have?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
àihào is built from ài 'to love' and hào 'to be fond of', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'ojH and xawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to cherish' and the second with this very word. The second is 好 said with a falling tone.

Lesson 2 of 5.
xìngqù — interest; interest in something.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hard-working, then the Mandarin for a hobby.

You'll want to know: xìngqù.
xìngqù — "interest; interest in something".
wǒ méiyǒu xìngqù — I'm not interested.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xìngqù is built from xìng and qù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, xingH and tshjuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'to tend towards'; it gives no sense for the first in this tone, and its level-tone reading means 'to rise'. How the pair came to mean an interest is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
小说 (xiǎoshuō) — novel; story (in a book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a hobby, then the Mandarin for an interest.

You'll want to know: 小说.
小说 — xiǎoshuō — "novel; story (in a book)".
我喜欢读小说 wǒ xǐhuan dú xiǎoshuō — I like reading novels.

The word, taken apart.
小说 (xiǎoshuō) is built from 小 'small' and 说 'to tell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX and sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to tell'. How 'small tellings' came to mean a novel is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
xiàqí — to play chess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a novel, then the Mandarin for a story.

You'll want to know: xiàqí.
xiàqí — "to play chess".
wǒmen xiàwǔ yìqǐ xiàqí — We're playing chess together this afternoon.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàqí is built from 下 'to go down' and qí 'a board game', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX and gi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to descend' and lists the second under another spelling of the character, quoting an older book on who first made the board game weiqi.
