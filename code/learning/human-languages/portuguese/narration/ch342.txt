Portuguese, chapter 342: Dizer o que se pensa.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
afirmar — to state, to assert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for indisputable, then the Portuguese for to refute.

You'll want to know: afirmar.
afirmar — "to state, to assert".
O jornal afirma que o preço do pão vai subir. The newspaper states that the price of bread is going to rise.
Afirmar que takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
afirmar is a learned borrowing from Latin affirmāre "to present as firm, to assert" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from ad and firmāre "to make firm", from firmus "firm".

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
fiável — reliable, trustworthy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to state, then the Portuguese for the statement.

You'll want to know: fiável.
fiável — "reliable, trustworthy".
Este carro é velho, mas é muito fiável. This car is old, but it is very reliable.
European Portuguese; in Brazil confiável. Uma fonte fiável — a reliable source.

The word, taken apart.
fiável is built on fiar "to trust, to give credit" with -ável; fiar is inherited from Vulgar Latin fīdāre, a form of Latin fīdere "to trust" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
credível — credible, believable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the statement, then the Portuguese for reliable.

You'll want to know: credível.
credível — "credible, believable".
A história dele não é muito credível. His story is not very credible.
Of a person or a story. Pouco credível — hard to believe.

The word, taken apart.
credível is a learned formation on Latin crēdibilis "worthy of belief, credible" (Lewis & Short), from crēdere "to believe, to trust".

Lesson 5 of 6.
alegar — to claim, to allege.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for reliable, then the Portuguese for credible.

You'll want to know: alegar.
alegar — "to claim, to allege".
Ele alegou que estava doente. He claimed that he was ill.
Often of an excuse that may not be true.

The word, taken apart.
alegar is a learned borrowing from Latin allēgāre "to send with a commission, to bring forward, to cite" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from ad and lēgāre "to send, to depute".
