Lesson 1 of 5.
さかな (sakana) — a fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to move, then the Japanese for rice; a meal.

You'll want to know: さかな.
さかな — sakana — "a fish".

The word, taken apart.
さかな (sakana) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

にく (niku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the on'yomi of a character meaning "flesh, meat", a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese nyuwk in Baxter's transcription; Unihan, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
やさい (yasai) — vegetables.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for meat, then the Japanese for an egg.

You'll want to know: やさい.
やさい — yasai — "vegetables".

The word, taken apart.
やさい (yasai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ya, "field" (Middle Chinese yaeX) and sai, "vegetables" (tshojH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
くだもの (kudamono) — fruit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an egg, then the Japanese for vegetables.

You'll want to know: くだもの.
くだもの — kudamono — "fruit".

The word, taken apart.
くだもの (kudamono) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. The usual account takes it apart as ku, an old form of ki, "tree", an old linking da, and mono, "thing": "tree things". JMdict's kanji, "fruit" and "thing", are read for their meaning.
