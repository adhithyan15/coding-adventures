Hindi, chapter 292: Robbers on the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
डाकू (ḍākū) — a bandit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for suspicion, then the Hindi for to stare.

You'll want to know: डाकू.
डाकू — ḍākū — "a bandit".
रात को दरवाज़ा बंद रखो, नहीं तो डाकू आ जाएँगे। rāt ko darvāzā band rakho, nahīṃ to ḍākū ā jāeṅge. Keep the door shut at night, or the bandits will get in.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
comes from ḍākka, "robbery", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5543). A डाकू (ḍākū) belongs to a gang that robs on the roads and in villages, not a single चोर who steals in secret.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: लूटना.
लूटना — lūṭnā — "to rob".
डाकुओं ने पूरा गाँव लूटा। ḍākuoṁ ne pūrā gā̃v lūṭā. The bandits robbed the whole village.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दुकान लूटी, usne dukān lūṭī.

The word, taken apart.
लूटना (lūṭnā) is inherited from luṭṭati, "plunders", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 11078). English loot was taken from this family of words.

Lesson 3 of 5.
डकैती (ḍakaitī) — a robbery (by a gang).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a bandit, then the Hindi for to rob.

You'll want to know: डकैती.
डकैती — ḍakaitī — "a robbery (by a gang)".
अगर फिर डकैती हुई तो गाँव के लोग पुलिस को बुलाएँगे। agar phir ḍakaitī huī to gā̃v ke log pulis ko bulāeṅge. If there is another robbery, the villagers will call the police.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
डकैती (ḍakaitī) is ḍakait, a member of a robber gang, with the noun ending -ī; CDIAL 5543 lists ḍakait beside डाकू (ḍākū) under ḍākka, "robbery". English dacoit and dacoity were taken from these words.

Lesson 4 of 5.
बंदूक़ (bandūq) — a gun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to rob, then the Hindi for a robbery (by a gang).

You'll want to know: बंदूक़.
बंदूक़ — bandūq — "a gun".
एक डाकू के हाथ में बंदूक़ थी। ek ḍākū ke hāth meṁ bandūq thī. One of the bandits had a gun in his hand.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
बंदूक़ (bandūq) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic bunduq, a hazelnut and then a small round pellet; the step from pellet to gun is said to have come through the pellet-bow. Hindi also writes it without the dot.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बाँधना (bā̃dhnā) — to tie up; to bind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a robbery (by a gang), then the Hindi for a gun.

You'll want to know: बाँधना.
बाँधना — bā̃dhnā — "to tie up; to bind".
डाकुओं ने चौकीदार को रस्सी से बाँधा। ḍākuoṁ ne caukīdār ko rassī se bā̃dhā. The bandits tied the watchman up with rope.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने रस्सी बाँधी, usne rassī bā̃dhī.

The word, taken apart.
बाँधना (bā̃dhnā) goes back to Sanskrit bandhati, "binds" (CDIAL 9139). A person tied up takes को and the verb stays masculine: चौकीदार को बाँधा; a thing tied agrees: रस्सी बाँधी.
