Japanese, chapter 318: Explaining Why.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
せつめいする (setsumei suru) — to explain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to propose, then the Japanese for a conclusion.

You'll want to know: せつめいする.
せつめいする — setsumei suru — "to explain".
わかりませんから、もういちど せつめいして ください。 — wakarimasen kara, mō ichido setsumei shite kudasai — "I don't understand, so please explain it once more."
Polite せつめいします, setsumeishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せんせいが せつめいしました。, sensei ga setsumei shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
せつめいする (setsumei suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, setsumei, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: setsu, "explanation" (Middle Chinese sywet) and mei, "bright" (mjaeng) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.

The word, taken apart.
なっとくする (nattoku suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". Lesson 5 of 5.
なぜ (naze) — for what reason; why.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for grounds for a claim, then the Japanese for a meaning.

You'll want to know: なぜ.
なぜ — naze — "for what reason; why".
なぜ はんたいしますか。 — naze hantai shimasu ka — "Why are you against it?"

The word, taken apart.
なぜ (naze) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (JMdict).
