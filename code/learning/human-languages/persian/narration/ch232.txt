Lesson 1 of 5.
کاشی (kâshi) — a tile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for homeland, then the Persian for birthplace.

You'll want to know: کاشی.
کاشی — kâshi — "a tile".

The word, taken apart.
kâshi is usually said to be named after the city of Kâshân, long famous for its glazed tiles. EtymDB gives a second account, linking it with an old Persian word for glass, kâsh.

Lesson 2 of 5.
زیرزمین (zirzamin) — a basement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for birthplace, then the Persian for a tile.

You'll want to know: زیرزمین.
زیرزمین — zirzamin — "a basement".

The word, taken apart.
zirzamin is zir, "under", with zamin, "ground": under the ground. Both are native Persian; zamin is usually traced to Middle Persian zamīg.

Lesson 4 of 5.
پاگرد (pâgard) — a landing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a basement, then the Persian for a stairwell.

You'll want to know: پاگرد.
پاگرد — pâgard — "a landing".

The word, taken apart.
pâgard is built from pâ, "foot", and -gard, "turning" (from gardidan, "to turn"): where the foot turns. pâ is inherited; Old Persian had pād-, "foot".
