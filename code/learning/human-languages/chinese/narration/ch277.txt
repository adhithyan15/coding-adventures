Mandarin Chinese, chapter 277: Likes and Dislikes.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
yǒuqù — fun; amusing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to retire, then the Mandarin for a big deal.

You'll want to know: yǒuqù.
yǒuqù — "fun; amusing".
zhège gùshi hěn yǒuqù — This story is a lot of fun.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǒuqù is built from 有 'to have' and qù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwX and tshjuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to tend towards'. How the pair came to mean amusing is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 7.
tǎoyàn — to dislike; to hate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a big deal, then the Mandarin for amusing.

You'll want to know: tǎoyàn.
tǎoyàn — "to dislike; to hate".
wǒ tǎoyàn xiàyǔ tiān — I hate rainy days.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tǎoyàn is built from tǎo and yàn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, thawX and 'jiemH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to govern' and 'to punish', and for the second quotes the Analects, on not tiring of fine food. How the pair came to mean disliking is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 7.
xiǎngshòu — to enjoy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to dislike, then the Mandarin for to admire.

You'll want to know: xiǎngshòu.
xiǎngshòu — "to enjoy".
wǒmen zài xiǎngshòu jiàqī — We're enjoying the holidays.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiǎngshòu is built from xiǎng and shòu, both inherited from Middle Chinese, xjangX and dzyuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the first under an older spelling and glosses it 'to offer' and 'to sacrifice', and glosses the second 'to take in'. How the first came to mean enjoying is not settled.
