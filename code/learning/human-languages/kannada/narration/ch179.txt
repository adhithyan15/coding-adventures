ತಾಜಾ (tājā) is a loan from Persian tāza, "fresh", which came through Hindustani.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಹಳಸಿದ (haḷasida) — stale, spoiled (food).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for by the way, then the Kannada for fresh.

You'll want to know: ಹಳಸಿದ.
ಹಳಸಿದ — haḷasida — "stale, spoiled (food)".

The word, taken apart.
ಹಳಸಿದ (haḷasida) is native Dravidian, from haḷasu, "to go stale", which DEDR 3999 lists as older Kannada paḻasu, "to become old, decay, be spoiled"; the entry also has Tamil paḻa.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಹಸಿ (hasi) — raw, uncooked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for fresh, then the Kannada for stale.

You'll want to know: ಹಸಿ.
ಹಸಿ — hasi — "raw, uncooked".

The word, taken apart.
ಹಸಿ (hasi) is native Dravidian, of the root for "green", and DEDR 3821 lists older Kannada pasi, "greenness, unripeness, rawness", itself; the entry also has Tamil paca.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಸಪ್ಪೆ (sappe) — bland, tasteless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for stale, then the Kannada for raw.

You'll want to know: ಸಪ್ಪೆ.
ಸಪ್ಪೆ — sappe — "bland, tasteless".

The word, taken apart.
ಸಪ್ಪೆ (sappe) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil cappai and Telugu cappa (DEDR 2337). DEDR lists cappe, sappe, "flatness, insipidness". DEDR 2337 compares Sanskrit capaṭa.
