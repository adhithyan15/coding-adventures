Telugu, chapter 476: Talking About the Climate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
శీతోష్ణస్థితి (śītōṣṇasthiti) — the climate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for irrigation, then the Telugu for a marsh.

You'll want to know: శీతోష్ణస్థితి.
శీతోష్ణస్థితి — śītōṣṇasthiti — "the climate".
ఆ ప్రాంతం శీతోష్ణస్థితి వ్యవసాయానికి బాగుంటుంది. ā prāntaṁ śītōṣṇasthiti vyavasāyāniki bāguṇṭundi. The climate of that region is good for farming.
శీత, cold, + ఉష్ణ, hot, + స్థితి, state: the state of cold and heat. In speech people often say వాతావరణం.

The word, taken apart.
శీతోష్ణస్థితి (śītōṣṇasthiti) is built from three Sanskrit words: śīta, "cold" (CDIAL 12485), uṣṇa, "hot" (CDIAL 2389), and sthiti, "a state" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఉక్కపోత (ukkapōta) — muggy heat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for temperature, then the Telugu for humidity.

You'll want to know: ఉక్కపోత.
ఉక్కపోత — ukkapōta — "muggy heat".
వర్షం ముందు ఉక్కపోత భరించలేకపోయాం. varṣaṁ mundu ukkapōta bhariñcalēkapōyāṁ. Before the rain we could not bear the muggy heat.
ఉక్క, closeness of the air, + పోత. The heat that makes you sweat without any breeze.

The word, taken apart.
ఉక్కపోత (ukkapōta) is a Telugu compound: ఉక్క, "sultriness, closeness of the weather" (DEDR 568), native Dravidian, and -పోత, whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
వేడెక్కు (vēḍekku) — to heat up; to grow hot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for humidity, then the Telugu for muggy heat.

You'll want to know: వేడెక్కు.
వేడెక్కు — vēḍekku — "to heat up; to grow hot".
మధ్యాహ్నానికి గది బాగా వేడెక్కింది. madhyāhnāniki gadi bāgā vēḍekkindi. By midday the room had got very hot.
Past (it …): వేడెక్కింది, vēḍekkindi.
వేడి, heat, + ఎక్కు, to climb: heat climbing. Also said of a heated argument. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: వేడెక్కింది, it got hot.

The word, taken apart.
వేడెక్కు (vēḍekku) is native Dravidian: వేడి, "heat" (DEDR 5517), and ఎక్కు, "to climb, to rise" (DEDR 766).
