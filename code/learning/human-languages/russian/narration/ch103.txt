Russian, chapter 103: Hat, Jacket, Shirt, Trousers, Dress.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
шапка (shápka) — a hat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for shoes, then the Russian for socks.

You'll want to know: шапка.
шапка (shápka) — "a hat".

The word, taken apart.
шапка (shápka) — recorded from 1327. It is usually taken through Middle High German schapël from Old French chape, chapel, from Latin cappa, "a hooded cloak"; Vasmer notes that West Slavic has only č-, as in Polish czapka, which troubles that account.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: рубашка.
рубашка (rubáshka) — "a shirt".

The word, taken apart.
рубашка (rubáshka) is рубаха, "a shirt", with the fond -шка. Vasmer derives рубаха from руб, "a cut piece of cloth", and рубить, "to chop".

Lesson 4 of 5.
брюки (bryúki) — trousers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a jacket, then the Russian for a shirt.

You'll want to know: брюки.
брюки (bryúki) — "trousers".

The word, taken apart.
брюки (bryúki) — from Peter the Great's time, from Low German brôk or Dutch broek, "breeches"; the Germanic word goes with Latin-Gaulish brāca, "trousers" (Vasmer). English breeches is a cousin of the Dutch word.

платье (plátye) — "a dress".

The word, taken apart.
платье (plátye) from Old Russian платие, "clothing", an old collective noun built on плат, "a piece of cloth" (Vasmer).
