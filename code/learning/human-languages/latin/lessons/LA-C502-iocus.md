## You'll want to know: iocus, iocī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C502-IOCU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**iocus, iocī** — "a joke, jest".

> Pater iocum nārrat, et omnēs rīdent.
>
> *Father tells a joke, and everyone laughs.*

Masculine, second declension; the plural is often *ioca*. *Per iocum* — as a joke. English *joke* and *jocular*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**iocus** is inherited through Proto-Italic from a Proto-Indo-European word that EtymDB traces to a root meaning "to speak", with Welsh *iaith* "language" as a cousin; Lewis & Short is less sure. English *joke* and *jocular* come from it.
