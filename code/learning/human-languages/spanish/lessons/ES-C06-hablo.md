# hablo — "I speak"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HABLAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] What is the word for "to speak"? (*Hablar*.) Say it once.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HABLAR] -->

- [hablar](./ES-C06-hablar.md) — the verb, and the *fābula* behind it.

## Grammar Lens: change the ending, change who is speaking
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-1SG]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HABLAR, ES-GRAMMAR-FIRST-PERSON-VERB] -->

*Hablar* is the naming form — "to speak." It is not yet about anybody.

To make it about **you**, take off the **-ar** and put **-o** in its place:

> **hablar** → **hablo** — *I speak.*

That is the whole operation. One ending, swapped.

And notice what you did not have to do: you did not add a word for *I*. The
**-o** is already carrying it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-1SG] -->

The **-o** is old and it is nearly untouched. Latin marked "I" on a verb with
**-ō**, a long *o*: *amō*, "I love." Two thousand years later Spanish still
marks "I" with exactly that letter.

Most of Latin's endings wore down on the way to Spanish. This one did not. When
you say *hablo*, the last sound in your mouth is the same one a Roman used to
say *I*.

> **Worth noticing.** English gave up. *Speak* is *speak* whoever is doing it,
> which is why English cannot let go of its pronouns. You will meet that
> difference properly in the very next lesson.
