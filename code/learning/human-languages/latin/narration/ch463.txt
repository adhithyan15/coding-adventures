Lesson 1 of 5.
holitor, holitōris — a vegetable grower, a market gardener.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for goods, then the Latin for a hawker.

You'll want to know: holitor, holitōris.
holitor, holitōris — "a vegetable grower, a market gardener".
Holitor holera in forum portat et vendit. The market gardener carries vegetables to the forum and sells them.
Third declension, masculine: holitor, holitōris m., also spelled olitor. From holus 'vegetables'.

The word, taken apart.
holitor is built on holus, holeris 'vegetables, greens' with the suffix -tor 'one who' (Lewis & Short, under olitor).

Lesson 2 of 5.
piscārius, piscāriī — a fishmonger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hawker, then the Latin for a vegetable grower.

You'll want to know: piscārius, piscāriī.
piscārius, piscāriī — "a fishmonger".
Piscārius piscēs recentēs clāmat. The fishmonger calls out his fresh fish.
Second declension, masculine: piscārius, piscāriī m. Also an adjective: forum piscārium, the fish market.

The word, taken apart.
piscārius is built on piscis 'fish' with the suffix -ārius (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); piscis comes from PIE peysḱ-, and English fish is a Germanic cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sporta, sportae — a plaited basket, a hamper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a vegetable grower, then the Latin for a fishmonger.

You'll want to know: sporta, sportae.
sporta, sportae — "a plaited basket, a hamper".
Ancilla pānem et cāseum in sportā portat. The maid carries bread and cheese in a hamper.
First declension, feminine: sporta, sportae f. Its diminutive sportula was the food basket, later the cash, a patron gave his clients.

The word, taken apart.
sporta is usually traced to Greek spyris 'plaited basket' (Lewis & Short compare it); EtymDB links it with PIE sper- 'to twist, to turn'. The path is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
corbis, corbis — a wicker basket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fishmonger, then the Latin for a plaited basket.

You'll want to know: corbis, corbis.
corbis, corbis — "a wicker basket".
Servī ūvās in corbēs pōnunt. The slaves put the grapes into wicker baskets.
Third declension, masculine or feminine: corbis, corbis. A big basket of woven twigs, for the field or the vineyard.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of corbis is not settled: an ancient writer explained it from the bent (curvātae) twigs it is woven of, while Lewis & Short compare Greek kolpos 'fold'. EtymDB gives no parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
canistrum, canistrī — a bread basket, a reed basket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a plaited basket, then the Latin for a wicker basket.

You'll want to know: canistrum, canistrī.
canistrum, canistrī — "a bread basket, a reed basket".
In cēnā canistrum plēnum pānis est. At dinner there is a basket full of bread.
Second declension, neuter: canistrum, canistrī n., mostly plural. English canister comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
canistrum was borrowed from Greek kanastron 'basket of reeds' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from kanna 'reed'. English canister comes from it.
