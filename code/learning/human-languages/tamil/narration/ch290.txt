Tamil, chapter 290: Feelings and Hopes.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
எனக்குக் கோபம் வந்தது (eṉakkuk kōpam vantatu) — how I felt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the words for anger, fear and gladness. (kōpam, payam, cantōṣam.) Then "I know Tamil". (eṉakkut tamiḻ teriyum.) Then say "dampness". (īram.)

You'll want to know: a feeling comes to you.
எனக்குக் கோபம் வந்தது. eṉakkuk kōpam vantatu. — "I got angry."
Word for word: "to me, anger came". The feeling is what came, so the verb is the "it" past, வந்தது, and the person it came to takes -க்கு, as in எனக்குத் தமிழ் தெரியும். The first letter of the feeling doubles in writing: எனக்குக் கோபம், அவனுக்குப் பயம்.
அவனுக்குப் பயம் வந்தது. avaṉukkup payam vantatu. — "He got frightened."
அவனுக்கு (avaṉukku, "to him"), அவளுக்கு (avaḷukku, "to her"), உங்களுக்கு (uṅkaḷukku, "to you").

You'll want to know: the state you are in, -ஆக இருக்கிறது.
எனக்குச் சந்தோஷமாக இருக்கிறது. eṉakkuc cantōṣamāka irukkiṟatu. — "I am happy."
For a feeling you are in, the feeling takes -ஆக, the -ஆக of நன்றாக, and the verb is இருக்கிறது: "to me, it is in gladness". In the past: இருந்தது.
எனக்குப் பயமாக இருந்தது. eṉakkup payamāka iruntatu. — "I was afraid."
அவளுக்கு ஏமாற்றமாக இருந்தது. avaḷukku ēmāṟṟamāka iruntatu. — "She was disappointed."
The same feelings also go with a plain subject: நான் சந்தோஷமாக இருக்கிறேன் (nāṉ cantōṣamāka irukkiṟēṉ, "I am happy").

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I got angry"? (எனக்குக் கோபம் வந்தது (eṉakkuk kōpam vantatu).) Who takes -க்கு? (The person who feels.) "I was afraid"? (எனக்குப் பயமாக இருந்தது.) Which verb says the feeling arrived, and which says you were in it? (வந்தது arrived; இருந்தது was.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ஆசை (ācai) — a wish, a longing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I am happy." (eṉakkuc cantōṣamāka irukkiṟatu.) Then "to dream" and "the sea". (kaṉavu kāṇ, kaṭal.)

You'll want to know: ஆசை.
ஆசை — ācai — "a wish, a longing, a desire".
எனக்கு ஒரு ஆசை இருக்கிறது. eṉakku oru ācai irukkiṟatu. — "I have a wish."
எனக்குக் கடலில் நீந்த ஆசை. eṉakkuk kaṭalil nīnta ācai. — "I long to swim in the sea."
ஆசை (ācai) goes the way of the feelings: the one who wishes takes -க்கு. What you long to do comes as a verb's to-form before it: நீந்த ஆசை (ācai), "a longing to swim". It covers a child's wish for a sweet and a grown person's hope for a whole life; the sentence tells you which.

The word, taken apart.
ஆசை (ācai) is a loan from Sanskrit āśā, which Monier-Williams glosses "wish, desire, hope". The final -ā of the Sanskrit word became -ஐ in Tamil, the change you can hear in கதை, "a story", from Sanskrit kathā.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ācai, slowly, then at speed]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I have a wish" — eṉakku oru ācai irukkiṟatu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I long to swim in the sea" — eṉakkuk kaṭalil nīnta ācai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ஆசை (ācai) mean? (A wish, a longing.) "I long to swim in the sea"? (எனக்குக் கடலில் நீந்த ஆசை (ācai).) Which language is it from? (Sanskrit: āśā.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
இலக்கு (ilakku) — a goal, a target.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I have a wish." (eṉakku oru ācai irukkiṟatu.) Then the words for a doctor and a teacher. (maruttuvar, āciriyar.)

You'll want to know: இலக்கு.
இலக்கு — ilakku — "a goal, a target".
என் இலக்கு மருத்துவர் ஆவது. eṉ ilakku maruttuvar āvatu. — "My goal is to become a doctor."
ஆவது (āvatu, "becoming"). Where ஆசை (ācai) is a longing, இலக்கு (ilakku) is a mark you aim at and work towards. The same word names the target an arrow is shot at.
இந்த ஆண்டு என் இலக்கு: மாதம் ஒரு புத்தகம். inta āṇṭu eṉ ilakku: mātam oru puttakam. — "My goal this year: a book a month."
இந்த (inta, "this"): the i- of இது and இங்கே, in front of a noun.

The word, taken apart.
இலக்கு (ilakku) is usually traced to Sanskrit lakṣya or lakṣa, "mark, aim": CDIAL 10881 lists lakṣá "mark, sign" and "aim", and lakṣya- "aim". The இ in front is the vowel older Tamil set before a borrowed word that began with l.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does இலக்கு (ilakku) mean? (A goal, a target.) And ஆசை (ācai)? (A wish, a longing.) "My goal"? (என் இலக்கு (ilakku).)


Lesson 4 of 5.
போக ஆசை (pōka ācai) — what I hope for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I long to swim in the sea." (eṉakkuk kaṭalil nīnta ācai.) Then "My goal is to become a doctor." (eṉ ilakku maruttuvar āvatu.) Then "I must go." (nāṉ pōka vēṇṭum.)

You'll want to know: a wish, with the to-form.
எனக்கு ஒரு நாள் விமானத்தில் பயணம் செய்ய ஆசை. eṉakku oru nāḷ vimāṉattil payaṇam ceyya ācai. — "I long to travel by plane one day."
விமானத்தில் (vimāṉattil, "by plane"); பயணம் செய் (payaṇam cey, "to travel"), "do a journey". The to-form that came before வேண்டும் comes before ஆசை (ācai): செய்ய ஆசை (ācai).
நான் தமிழ் நன்றாகப் பேச விரும்புகிறேன். nāṉ tamiḻ naṉṟākap pēca virumpukiṟēṉ. — "I want to speak Tamil well."
விரும்பு, "to wish, to like", takes a plain subject, நான், and the same to-form. It is plainer than ஆசை (ācai), and fits a letter or a form as well as talk.

You'll want to know: what you want to become, ஆக.
நான் ஆசிரியர் ஆக விரும்புகிறேன். nāṉ āciriyar āka virumpukiṟēṉ. — "I want to become a teacher."
ஆக (āka, "to become") is the to-form of ஆகு. The thing you will become stands bare in front of it: ஆசிரியர் ஆக, மருத்துவர் ஆக.
Three ways to say the same hope: எனக்கு ஆசிரியர் ஆக ஆசை (ācai); நான் ஆசிரியர் ஆக விரும்புகிறேன்; என் இலக்கு (ilakku) ஆசிரியர் ஆவது. And the biggest of them: என் கனவு (eṉ kaṉavu, "my dream").

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I long to go"? (எனக்குப் போக ஆசை (pōka ācai).) "I want to speak"? (நான் பேச விரும்புகிறேன்.) "To become"? (ஆக.) Which one takes -க்கு, and which one takes நான்? (ஆசை takes -க்கு; விரும்புகிறேன் takes நான்.)
