Latin, chapter 354: Assum et frīctum.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
assus, assa, assum — roasted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for foam, then the Latin for to skim off.

You'll want to know: assus, assa, assum.
assus, assa, assum — "roasted".
Carō assa iūcunda erat. The roast meat was delicious.
Assus is roasted, ēlixus boiled: Horace and Celsus set the two side by side.

The word, taken apart.
assus is inherited (EtymDB); Lewis & Short connect it with āreō, "to be dry", and ārdeō, "to burn".

lūcānica, lūcānicae — "a smoked sausage".
Senex lūcānicam et pānem edēbat. The old man was eating a sausage and bread.
Feminine. A smoked pork sausage from southern Italy; Martial sends one as a little present.

The word, taken apart.
lūcānica is the sausage of Lucania, a region of southern Italy, named after the people who made it (EtymDB).

Piscēs in crāticulā super carbōnēs coquēbantur. The fish were cooking on a grill over the coals.
Feminine, a small grid of iron. Martial makes a small present of one.

The word, taken apart.
crāticula is a diminutive of crātis, "wickerwork, a hurdle" (Lewis & Short): a little grid.

frīgō, frīgere — "to fry, to parch".
Coquus piscēs in oleō frīxit. The cook fried the fish in oil.
frīgō, frīgere, frīxī, frīctum, long ī, in a sartāgō (taught). English fry comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
frīgō is compared by Lewis & Short with Greek phrygō, "to roast, to parch". English fry comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 6.
torreō, torrēre — to roast, to toast, to parch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a gridiron, then the Latin for to fry.

You'll want to know: torreō, torrēre.
torreō, torrēre — "to roast, to toast, to parch".
Ad focum pānem torrēbat. He was toasting bread by the hearth.
torreō, torrēre, torruī, tostum. English toast comes from tostum, and torrid from the verb.

The word, taken apart.
torreō is inherited from Proto-Italic torzeō, "to scorch" (EtymDB), a cousin of English thirst. English toast and torrid come from it.
