# دست راست (dast-e râst) — on the right, and how directions are given

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C69-PLACE69-03, FA-LEX-C69-PLACE69-04, FA-LEX-C230-MAKAN230-04, FA-GRAMMAR-CHETOR-MITAVANAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "right", "left" and "straight ahead". (*Râst*, *chap*,
*mostaqim*.) Then ask the way to the station. (*Bebakhshid, chetor mitavânam
be istgâh beravam?*)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the answer, which arrives fast, so it pays to know its shape.

## You'll want to know: دست راست
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-DAST-E-RAST-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-DAST, FA-GRAMMAR-EZAFE-OWNER, FA-LEX-C70-TOWN70-02] -->

**دست راست** — *dast-e râst* — "on the right": word for word, "the right
hand". **دست چپ**, *dast-e chap*, is "on the left".

> **ایستگاه دست راست است.** — *istgâh dast-e râst ast* — The station is on
> the right.

The ezafe joins the hand to its side, the same *-e* as in *esm-e man*.

## Grammar Lens: the polite instruction
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-POLITE-IMPERATIVE-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C133-ACT133-03, FA-LEX-C266-MAKAN266-01, FA-LEX-C27-BODY27-01, FA-GRAMMAR-BAD-THEN-01, FA-GRAMMAR-SUBJUNCTIVE-BE-01] -->

A stranger gives directions in the subjunctive with the *shomâ* ending, said
as an instruction. You hold the shape already:

> **مستقیم بروید.** — *mostaqim beravid* — Go straight on.

> **سر چهارراه به دست چپ بپیچید.** — *sar-e chahârrâh be dast-e chap bepichid*
> — At the crossroads, turn left.

*Bepichid* is *pichidan*, "to turn", from chapter one hundred and
thirty-three, on its stem *pich-*. *Sar-e chahârrâh* is "at the head of the
crossroads". To a friend, someone you call *to*, the ending drops: *bepich*,
turn.

So a whole answer runs: *mostaqim beravid, ba'd be dast-e râst bepichid.
istgâh dast-e chap ast.*

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Both words are inherited. *dast*, "hand", continues Old Persian *dasta*,
on the same Indo-European root as Sanskrit *hasta*, "hand" (EtymDB 2.1).
*râst* continues Old Persian *rāsta*, "right, straight", and EtymDB sets it
beside English **right**: both go back to an Indo-European word for "moved in
a straight line".
