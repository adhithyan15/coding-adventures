Mandarin Chinese, chapter 120: Takeaway.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wàimài — takeaway; food delivered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for carrier bag, then the Mandarin for luggage.

You'll want to know: wàimài.
wàimài — "takeaway; food delivered".
wǒmen jīntiān wǎnshang chī wàimài — We're having a takeaway tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wàimài is built from 外 'outside' and mài 'to sell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwajH and meaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): food sold to be eaten somewhere else.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dìng — to make a booking; to order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for luggage, then the Mandarin for takeaway.

You'll want to know: dìng.
dìng — "to make a booking; to order".
wǒ xiǎng dìng yí ge fángjiān — I'd like to book a room.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dìng is inherited from Middle Chinese dengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to discuss and settle', which regularly gives dìng. Booking is the sense today, probably by way of settling an arrangement, but the step is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
打包 (dǎbāo) — to pack food to take away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to make a booking, then the Mandarin for address.

You'll want to know: 打包.
打包 — dǎbāo — "to pack food to take away".
我想打包 wǒ xiǎng dǎbāo — Could I take this away?

The word, taken apart.
打包 (dǎbāo) is built from 打 'to strike, to do' and 包 'to wrap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to strike'; dǎ is not the sound taengX would regularly give, and its everyday sense of doing is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
点心 (diǎnxin) — dim sum; light snacks and pastries.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for address, then the Mandarin for to pack food to take away.

You'll want to know: 点心.
点心 — diǎnxin — "dim sum; light snacks and pastries".
我们去外边吃点心 wǒmen qù wàibian chī diǎnxin — We're going out for dim sum.

The word, taken apart.
点心 (diǎnxin) is built from 点 'a dot, a little' and 心 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, temX and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as a dot made with the brush. How the pair came to mean a light snack is not settled.
