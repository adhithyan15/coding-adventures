# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-SVO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-ET-01, FR-GRAM-SVO-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What France Éducation international publishes.** The DELF A1 writing paper
runs **30 minutes** and holds **two** parts: a form to complete, and a personal
message of **at least forty words**.

Those numbers are read out of `task-shapes/a1.json`, which transcribes the
published materials. Note what is *not* there: the paper publishes a **minimum**
for the message and no maximum. Forty is a floor, not a target.

[PAUSE 2s] **Ten minutes** for the form, starting now. Fill every field.

[PAUSE 3s] Now **twenty minutes** for the message. Forty words is the floor;
write past it rather than up to it.

[PAUSE 2s] Stop when the time is up, even mid-word.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-SVO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Count your words. **Under forty and the task has not been done** —
this is the one number on the paper that is a hard floor rather than guidance.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson, because it changes
how you should study rather than how you should write today.

[PAUSE 2s] DELF A1 passes on **50 out of 100 overall** — and **any single skill
below 5 out of 25 is eliminatory.** A candidate with 45 across three papers and
4 in the fourth does not pass, whatever the total says.

[PAUSE 2s] So the paper that decides your result is **the one you are worst at**,
not the average of four. If that is writing, no amount of reading practice
repairs it.
