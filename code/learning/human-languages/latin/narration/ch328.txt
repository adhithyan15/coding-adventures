Latin, chapter 328: Nūptiae.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
flammeum, flammeī — a bridal veil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bride, then the Latin for marriage.

You'll want to know: flammeum, flammeī.
flammeum, flammeī — "a bridal veil".
Spōnsa flammeum gerēbat. The bride wore a flame-coloured veil.
Neuter, from flammeus, 'flame-coloured': the bride's orange-red veil.

The word, taken apart.
flammeum is the neuter of flammeus, "flame-coloured", with vēlum, "veil", understood (Lewis & Short); flammeus is built on flamma, "flame".

Lesson 2 of 5.
prōnuba, prōnubae — a matron of honour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for marriage, then the Latin for a bridal veil.

You'll want to know: prōnuba, prōnubae.
prōnuba, prōnubae — "a matron of honour".
Prōnuba dextrās spōnsī et spōnsae iūnxit. The matron of honour joined the right hands of groom and bride.
Feminine. A woman married only once led the bride and joined the couple's hands.

The word, taken apart.
prōnuba is built on nūbō, "to marry", with prō (Lewis & Short): she who helps on the marrying.

Lesson 3 of 5.
nūptiālis, nūptiāle — of a wedding, bridal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bridal veil, then the Latin for a matron of honour.

You'll want to know: nūptiālis, nūptiāle.
nūptiālis, nūptiāle — "of a wedding, bridal".
Post cēnam nūptiālem spōnsa ad domum novam ducta est. After the wedding feast the bride was led to her new home.
Two endings, from nūptiae (taught). Cēna nūptiālis: the wedding feast. English nuptial.

The word, taken apart.
nūptiālis is built on nūptiae, "wedding" (EtymDB), and so on nūbō, "to marry". English nuptial comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mātrōna, mātrōnae — a married woman, a lady of the house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a matron of honour, then the Latin for of a wedding.

You'll want to know: mātrōna, mātrōnae.
mātrōna, mātrōnae — "a married woman, a lady of the house".
Mātrōna servōs et domum cūrābat. The lady of the house looked after the slaves and the home.
Feminine. The stola (taught) was the dress of a mātrōna. English matron.

The word, taken apart.
mātrōna is built on māter, "mother" (EtymDB): a married woman of standing. English matron comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dīvortium, dīvortiī — a divorce; a separation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for of a wedding, then the Latin for a married woman.

You'll want to know: dīvortium, dīvortiī.
dīvortium, dīvortiī — "a divorce; a separation".
Post decem annōs dīvortium fēcērunt. After ten years they divorced.
Neuter. Dīvortium facere: to divorce. Also a place where roads or rivers part.

The word, taken apart.
dīvortium is built on dīvertō, "to turn aside, to separate" (EtymDB): a parting of ways. English divorce comes from it through French.
