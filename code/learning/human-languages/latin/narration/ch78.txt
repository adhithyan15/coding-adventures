Latin, chapter 78: Live, Enter, Return, Fear, Rejoice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vīvō, vīvere — to live.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to put, then the Latin for to find.

You'll want to know: vīvō, vīvere.
vīvō, vīvere — "to live". English vivid.

The word, taken apart.
vīvō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European gweih3w-, "to live" (EtymDB 2.1). Greek bios, "life", and English quick, which once meant "alive", are cousins (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
redeō, redīre — to go back, to return.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to live, then the Latin for to enter.

You'll want to know: redeō, redīre.
redeō, redīre — "to go back, to return". re-, back, with eō, go.

The word, taken apart.
redeō is built from red-, "back", and eō, "to go" (EtymDB 2.1); red- is the older form of the prefix, kept before a vowel.
