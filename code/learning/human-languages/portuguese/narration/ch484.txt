Portuguese, chapter 484: Dores de cabeça e náuseas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a enxaqueca — migraine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for itch, then the Portuguese for to scratch an itch.

You'll want to know: a enxaqueca.
a enxaqueca — "migraine".
Quando tenho enxaqueca, fico às escuras no quarto. When I have a migraine, I stay in my room in the dark.
Stronger than uma dor de cabeça.

The word, taken apart.
enxaqueca is usually traced to Arabic aš-šaqīqa, a word for a headache on one side of the head.

Lesson 2 of 5.
tonto — dizzy; silly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to scratch an itch, then the Portuguese for migraine.

You'll want to know: tonto.
tonto — "dizzy; silly".
Depois da viagem de barco, fiquei tonto. After the boat trip, I felt dizzy.
It also means silly: não sejas tonto.

The word, taken apart.
tonto is inherited from Latin attonitus "thunderstruck, stunned" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: a tontura.
a tontura — "dizziness".
Levantei-me depressa e senti uma tontura. I stood up quickly and felt dizzy.
Built on tonto.

The word, taken apart.
tontura is built on tonto "dizzy" with -ura (EtymDB); tonto is inherited from Latin attonitus "stunned".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a náusea — nausea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for dizzy, then the Portuguese for dizziness.

You'll want to know: a náusea.
a náusea — "nausea".
O cheiro do peixe deu-lhe náuseas. The smell of the fish made her feel sick.
Often plural: ter náuseas.

The word, taken apart.
náusea is a learned borrowing from Latin nausea (EtymDB), from Greek nausía "seasickness", built on naûs "ship".
