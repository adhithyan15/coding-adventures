Latin, chapter 485: Downpour, To be soaking wet, Rainy, Stormy, Turbulent.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
imber, imbris — a downpour, a heavy shower of rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an ash tree, then the Latin for shady.

You'll want to know: imber, imbris.
imber, imbris — "a downpour, a heavy shower of rain".
Subitō imber magnus cadit, et viātōrēs ad caupōnam currunt. Suddenly a heavy downpour falls, and the travellers run to the inn.
Third declension, masculine: imber, imbris m., genitive plural imbrium. Heavier than pluvia.

The word, taken apart.
imber is inherited; Lewis & Short list Sanskrit abhra 'cloud', Greek ombros 'rain' and Latin umbra 'shade' as kin. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
madeō, madēre — to be soaking wet, to drip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for shady, then the Latin for a downpour.

You'll want to know: madeō, madēre.
madeō, madēre — "to be soaking wet, to drip".
Post imbrem vestēs viātōrum madent. After the downpour the travellers' clothes are soaking wet.
madeō, madēre, maduī, second conjugation, no supine. Also, in comedy, to be drunk.

The word, taken apart.
madeō is inherited from PIE meh₂d- 'to drip, to ooze' (EtymDB); Lewis & Short compare Greek madaō 'to be moist'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
pluviālis, pluviāle — rainy, of rain, bringing rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a downpour, then the Latin for to be soaking wet.

You'll want to know: pluviālis, pluviāle.
pluviālis, pluviāle — "rainy, of rain, bringing rain".
Ventus pluviālis ā marī venit. The rainy wind comes from the sea.
An adjective of two endings, from pluvia 'rain'. Aqua pluviālis, rainwater. English pluvial comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
pluviālis is built on pluvia 'rain' with the suffix -ālis (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), and pluvia comes from pluit 'it rains'.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to be soaking wet, then the Latin for rainy.

You'll want to know: nimbōsus, nimbōsa, nimbōsum.
nimbōsus, nimbōsa, nimbōsum — "stormy, full of rain clouds".
Caelum nimbōsum est; nautae in portū manent. The sky is stormy; the sailors stay in the harbour.
An adjective of three endings, from nimbus 'rain cloud' with -ōsus 'full of'.

The word, taken apart.
nimbōsus is built on nimbus 'rain cloud, storm' with the suffix -ōsus 'full of' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
turbidus, turbida, turbidum — turbulent, wild; cloudy, muddy (of water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rainy, then the Latin for stormy.

You'll want to know: turbidus, turbida, turbidum.
turbidus, turbida, turbidum — "turbulent, wild; cloudy, muddy (of water)".
Post tempestātem flūmen turbidum est. After the storm the river is cloudy and wild.
An adjective of three endings, from turba 'disorder'. Of water, weather or a crowd. English turbid comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
turbidus is built on turba 'disorder, commotion' with the suffix -idus (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English turbid comes from it.
