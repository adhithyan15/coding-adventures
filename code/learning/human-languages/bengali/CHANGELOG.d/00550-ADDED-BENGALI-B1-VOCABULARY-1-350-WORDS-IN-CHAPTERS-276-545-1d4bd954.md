## Added — Bengali B1 vocabulary, 1,350 words in chapters 276–545

- Added 270 chapters of five words each (1,350 headwords, 380 of them verbs),
  one word per lesson, with eight reviews in each of five tranches:
  - 276–329: narrating (accidents, fire and rescue, crime and the police,
    cyclones, floods and drought, the news, festivals and weddings, kingdoms
    and wars, legends and books);
  - 330–383: reasons and conditions (making a case, rights and the law,
    government and public money, values and customs, research and
    measurement, then likelihood, conditions, limits and goals);
  - 384–437: travel (trains, roads and breakdowns, boats and ferries, a place
    to stay, the town, the bank and the market, the weather and nature);
  - 438–491: experience (feelings and character, the body and health, the
    family from birth to old age);
  - 492–545: work, machines and the home, the kitchen, free time, sport and
    the arts, then means, plans, chance, obstacles and choices.
- Every verb is shown with its past in a third-person clause (সেই দেয়াল ধসে
  পড়ল, "that wall collapsed"; তিনি পুকুরে ডুব দিলেন).
- Every example uses only words taught earlier and only the grammar the core
  teaches: no passive or impersonal forms.
- The first words of chapters 276–283 practise the core's last grammar points
  again (… -এর ওপর নির্ভর করে, -তাম, the -লে form, যদি … তাহলে), two words
  each; four words bring back the new letters ঢ and ঙ.
- Every word has a cited origin: CDIAL, Monier-Williams, or a named donor
  word hedged as the usual tracing. English loans say "borrowed from
  English"; imitative words with no recorded older form say that their
  history is not settled. No etymology is invented.
- Echo words, reduplications, a taught word plus a light verb, plain
  derivations of taught words and homographs of inflected forms were
  replaced during review; causative partners and same-root words with their
  own senses stay.
