Sanskrit, chapter 297: Putting the Fire Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
इन्धनम् (indhanam) — fuel; firewood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dense, then the Sanskrit for spreads.

You'll want to know: इन्धनम्.
इन्धनम् — indhanam — "fuel; firewood".
शुष्कम् इन्धनं शीघ्रं ज्वलति। śuṣkam indhanaṃ śīghraṃ jvalati. Dry fuel burns fast.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
इन्धनम् (indhanam) comes from the root √indh, "to kindle, light"; MW gives indhana as kindling and as fuel (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
निर्वापयति (nirvāpayati) — puts out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a water jar, then the Sanskrit for a stream of water.

You'll want to know: निर्वापयति.
निर्वापयति — nirvāpayati — "puts out".
जनाः जलेन अग्निं निर्वापयन्ति। janāḥ jalena agniṃ nirvāpayanti. People put out the fire with water.
Past (he …): सः जलेन अग्निं निर्वापितवान्, saḥ jalena agniṃ nirvāpitavān.

The word, taken apart.
निर्वापयति (nirvāpayati) is built from nir, "out", and the causative of the root √vā, "to blow"; MW gives nir-vāpayati as to put out, extinguish (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
शाम्यति (śāmyati) — dies down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a stream of water, then the Sanskrit for puts out a fire.

You'll want to know: शाम्यति.
शाम्यति — śāmyati — "dies down".
शनैः अग्निः शाम्यति। śanaiḥ agniḥ śāmyati. Slowly the fire dies down.
Past (it …): अग्निः अन्ते शान्तवान्, agniḥ ante śāntavān.

The word, taken apart.
शाम्यति (śāmyati) comes from the root √śam, "to be quiet, come to rest"; MW gives it as to cease and to be extinguished (MW).
