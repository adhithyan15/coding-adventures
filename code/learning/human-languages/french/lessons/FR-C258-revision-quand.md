# Review — the scene, and what cut into it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPARFAIT-01, FR-LEX-CETAIT-01, FR-LEX-CHIEN-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Build "I was reading" from *lire*. (*Je lisais.*) Then say "I had
a dog". (*J'avais un chien.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-IL-Y-AVAIT-02, FR-LEX-IL-FAISAIT-01, FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-GRAM-PENDANT-QUE-02] -->

Five lessons, and every piece of a scene is in your hands. The recipe, the
*nous* stem plus **-ais**, on almost any verb: *je parlais*, *je finissais*,
*je prenais*. The one verb that refuses it, *être*, with *c'était*. The
scene-setter *il y avait*. The weather, made rather than being: *il faisait
froid*. And the division of labour: the imparfait paints, the passé composé
drops the events in.

[PAUSE 2s] The joints between the two are **quand** and **pendant que**.

> *Je lisais quand ma mère a téléphoné.*
> *J'attendais le train quand il a commencé à pleuvoir.*

The second one is worth hearing twice. The rain is an event here, because it
**began**: *il a commencé*. Once it is falling, it is scene again: *il
pleuvait*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-02, FR-GRAM-QUAND-COUPE-01, FR-LEX-CETAIT-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] After *quand*, in these sentences, which past? (The **passé
composé** — the event.) Why is *il a commencé à pleuvoir* in the passé
composé? (The rain **began**: that is an event.) What does *imparfait* mean,
taken apart? (**Not carried through.**) And which stem does *c'était* use?
(***Ét-***, from the standing verb.)
