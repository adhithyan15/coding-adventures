# ‑ండి (-aṇḍi) — where Telugu actually keeps "please"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C08-DAYACHESI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You now have **దయచేసి** for *please*. Now the honest part:
most of the time, a Telugu speaker asking politely does not say it.

## You'll want to know: The ending does the work
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-PRAGMATICS-C08-DAYACHESI-03]; assesses=[] -->

Telugu politeness rides on the **respectful command ending ‑ండి** (*-aṇḍi*).
It is not a separate word you add — it is a **tail on the verb**, and it is
already "please."

| respectful form | |
|---|---|
| **కూర్చోండి** *kūrcōṇḍi* | please sit |
| **చెప్పండి** *ceppaṇḍi* | please tell me |

Each of these is a verb with **‑ండి** on the end, and each is already polite —
no extra word required. There is a shorter form of every one of them, used with
a friend or a child; you will meet those verbs properly later, so take the
‑ండి forms as wholes for now. The short form is not rude, it is *close*, and
using it with a stranger is the mistake. Leaving out **దయచేసి** is not.

## Why it's said this way: Where దయచేసి actually goes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-PRAGMATICS-C08-DAYACHESI-03, TE-LEX-C08-DAYACHESI-01] -->

The standalone **దయచేసి** is **formal or emphatic**. It is not the everyday
"please" — it is the one you reach for to *warm a request further*, on top of an
ending that is already polite:

- **కూర్చోండి** — please sit. Complete, polite, ordinary.
- **దయచేసి కూర్చోండి** — *do* please sit. Warmer, more insistent, more formal.

So the two stack. What they do not do is substitute: **దయచేసి** in front of the
*short* form reads as oddly as "kindly sit down, mate."

English keeps politeness in a word you add; Telugu keeps it in the shape of the
verb. A learner hunting for a word to translate *please* will reach for
**దయచేసి** far too often and still sound abrupt — because the abruptness was
never in the missing word. It was in the ending.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-PRAGMATICS-C08-DAYACHESI-03, TE-LEX-C08-DAYACHESI-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What carries "please" in ordinary Telugu speech? (**The ending ‑ండి**
*-aṇḍi*, on the verb.) Is **కూర్చోండి** already polite without **దయచేసి**?
(**Yes** — the ending is the politeness.) When do you add **దయచేసి**? (To make a
request **warmer or more formal**, on top of ‑ండి.) What is wrong with putting
**దయచేసి** in front of the short form? (The **short** ending undercuts the
polite word.)
