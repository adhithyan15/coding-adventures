# હું બોલીશ — the future, built on મળીશું

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C148-ERGATIVE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C148-DHUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C04-MALISHUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C04-KAALE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I drank tea* and *I ate rice*. Then the goodbye from the first
week: **કાલે મળીશું**, *see you tomorrow*, word for word *tomorrow we will meet*.

## Grammar Lens: -ીશું, we will
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C149-ISHUN-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C04-MALISHUN-01, GU-PRON-AME-01, GU-PRON-AAPNE-01] -->

**મળીશું** is **મળ-**, *meet*, plus **-ીશું**, *we will*. Gujarati needs no
separate word for *will*: the future is an ending on the stem.

> **આપણે કાલે મળીશું.** — *āpṇe kāle maḷīśũ.* — We'll meet tomorrow.
> **અમે ગુજરાતી બોલીશું.** — *ame gujarātī bolīśũ.* — We will speak Gujarati.

## You'll want to know: -ીશ, I will
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C149-ISH-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C03-HUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C02-TUTAME-01] -->

Take the last **-ું** off and you have *I will*:

> **હું ગુજરાતી બોલીશ.** — *hũ gujarātī bolīś.* — I will speak Gujarati.

The familiar *you* takes the same form in the standard language: **તું બોલીશ**,
*you will speak*. There is no copula and no gender: a man and a woman both say
**હું બોલીશ**.

The **-શ** of these endings is usually traced to the old Sanskrit future, the
*-iṣya-* of *kariṣyati*, "will do".
