Portuguese, chapter 9: Months and Seasons.
2 lessons. Both can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 2.
os meses — the gods and numbers of the calendar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The Portuguese months are the Roman procession of gods and Caesars, worn down the Portuguese way. And two things you know pay off: março is the war-god, and setembro–dezembro are the Latin numbers 7–10.

The months, taken apart.
[a table of 12 rows, read aloud]
Portuguese from Latin: janeiro from Januarius. who / what: Janus, god of beginnings. Spanish twin: enero.
Portuguese from Latin: fevereiro from Februarius. who / what: the Februa purification. Spanish twin: febrero.
Portuguese from Latin: março from Martius. who / what: Mars, the war-god. Spanish twin: marzo.
Portuguese from Latin: abril from Aprilis. who / what: aperire "to open". Spanish twin: abril.
Portuguese from Latin: maio from Maius. who / what: Maia, growth-goddess. Spanish twin: mayo.
Portuguese from Latin: junho from Junius. who / what: Juno, queen of gods. Spanish twin: junio.
Portuguese from Latin: julho from Julius. who / what: Julius Caesar. Spanish twin: julio.
Portuguese from Latin: agosto from Augustus. who / what: emperor Augustus. Spanish twin: agosto.
Portuguese from Latin: setembro from september. who / what: "7th" (septem). Spanish twin: septiembre.
Portuguese from Latin: outubro from october. who / what: "8th" (octo). Spanish twin: octubre.
Portuguese from Latin: novembro from november. who / what: "9th" (novem). Spanish twin: noviembre.
Portuguese from Latin: dezembro from december. who / what: "10th" (decem). Spanish twin: diciembre.
Portuguese tells: janeiro/fevereiro kept the -eiro ending ( from -arius, the same suffix in feira), and setembro/dezembro echo the numbers sete/dez you learned. Two payoffs:
março = Mars. The war-god's month, the same Mars behind English March.
setembro…dezembro = 7…10. Still the Latin numbers, because the Roman year began in March; then julho (Julius) and agosto (Augustus) were inserted and pushed the count down two.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which god is março named for? (Mars, the war-god.) Why is dezembro Latin for "ten"? (The Roman year began in March.) Which two months honour real people? (Julho — Julius; agosto — Augustus.) Next: the seasons.


Lesson 2 of 2.
as estações — the seasons, and a shifting summer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four seasons — and a small mystery in one of them. Portuguese's word for summer, verão, didn't come from a "heat" word like French été. It grew out of the word for spring. Here's how.

The four seasons, taken apart.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
Portuguese: a primavera (spring). from Latin: prīma vēra. literally: "first spring / first green" (prima + ver "spring").
Portuguese: o verão (summer). from Latin: vēranum (tempus). literally: "the spring-like / warm season" ( from vēr "spring"!)
Portuguese: o outono (autumn). from Latin: autumnus. literally: "autumn" (ct/tumn becomes ut: autumnus becomes outono).
Portuguese: o inverno (winter). from Latin: hibernum. literally: "the wintry season" (cousin of hibernate).
The twist is in the first two, both born from Latin vēr, "spring":
primavera = prima + ver: "the first spring / first green" — the year's opening season (shared with Spanish and Italian).
verão from vēranum, which meant "spring-like, of the growing season." Warmth crept forward on the calendar, and the word slid with it — so Portuguese's "summer" literally grew out of "spring." (Spanish did the same: verano.)
And outono from autumnus shows a familiar Portuguese move: the au…t smoothed to ou, the same softening you saw in oito and noite.

[pause 3 seconds]
