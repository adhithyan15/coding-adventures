Portuguese, chapter 415: No comboio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a carruagem — the carriage (of a train); the coach (horse-drawn).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the ticket inspector, then the Portuguese for the fine.

You'll want to know: a carruagem.
a carruagem — "the carriage (of a train); the coach (horse-drawn)".
O nosso lugar é na carruagem cinco. Our seat is in carriage five.
In Brazil a train carriage is o vagão.

The word, taken apart.
carruagem came from Spanish carruaje, which came from Catalan carruatge (EtymDB); the family goes back to Latin carrus 'cart'.

You'll want to know: o compartimento.
o compartimento — "the compartment".
Partilhamos o compartimento com uma família simpática. We share the compartment with a nice family.
Also a compartment in a bag or a drawer.

The word, taken apart.
compartimento is a learned formation on Late Latin compartīrī 'to share out', built on pars 'part' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o beliche — the bunk (bed).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the carriage, then the Portuguese for the compartment.

You'll want to know: o beliche.
o beliche — "the bunk (bed)".
No comboio da noite, durmo no beliche de cima. On the night train, I sleep in the top bunk.
At home, two children often share um beliche.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of beliche is not settled. It first meant a sleeping place on a ship, and EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o maquinista — the train driver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the compartment, then the Portuguese for the bunk.

You'll want to know: o maquinista.
o maquinista — "the train driver".
O maquinista avisou que o comboio ia parar. The train driver announced that the train was going to stop.
A bus driver is o motorista.

The word, taken apart.
maquinista is built on máquina 'machine, engine' with -ista; máquina is a learned borrowing from Latin māchina 'machine, device' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
a locomotiva — the engine, the locomotive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bunk, then the Portuguese for the train driver.

You'll want to know: a locomotiva.
a locomotiva — "the engine, the locomotive".
A locomotiva antiga está agora no museu. The old engine is now in the museum.
An older word: a máquina of the train.

The word, taken apart.
locomotiva is a learned coinage from Latin locō 'from a place' and Medieval Latin mōtīvus 'moving', from movēre 'to move' (Lewis & Short has movēre).
