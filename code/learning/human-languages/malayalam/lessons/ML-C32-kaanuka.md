# കാണുക (kāṇuka) — "to see," and everything else the second slot can hold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-UNDU-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two slots, and the second one has only carried tense so far. It holds
a good deal more — and you have used one of its other endings since Chapter 4.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-KAANUKA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **കാണുക** — *kāṇuka* — **to see, to meet**

Present *kāṇunnu*, future *kāṇuṁ*, and a past that reshapes the way *vannu* did:
**കണ്ടു** (*kaṇṭu*), "saw."

You have said this verb in a goodbye. **നാളെ കാണാം** (*nāḷe kāṇāṁ*) — "see you
tomorrow" — is *nāḷe* ("tomorrow") plus this stem with a different ending.

## Sounds you'll need: the letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**കാ** (*ka* with the long-*ā* sign) + **ണു** (retroflex *ṇu*) + **ക** (*ka*) →
**കാണുക**. That **ണ** is the letter that ended *uṇṭŭ*: tongue curled back to the
roof of the mouth.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*Kāṇ-* is the family's own see-root, inherited from Proto-Dravidian — and here
the four sisters part company. **Tamil** says **பார்** (*pār*), keeping **காண்**
(*kāṇ*) for formal writing; **Kannada** says **ನೋಡು** (*nōḍu*), keeping **ಕಾಣು**
(*kāṇu*) alongside; **Telugu** says **చూడు** (*cūḍu*).

Malayalam simply kept the inherited word in daily use. Twice now — *tinnuka* and
*kāṇuka* — the youngest of the four sisters turns out to be the conservative one.

## Grammar Lens: tense is only one thing that ending can be
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C32-KAANUKA-02]; assesses=[] -->

Hold *kāṇ-* still and change what follows it:

| ending on *kāṇ-* | what it says |
|---|---|
| *kāṇunnu* | sees, is seeing |
| *kāṇuṁ* | will see |
| *kāṇāṁ* | may see; let's see |
| *kāṇaṇaṁ* | must see |
| *kāṇarutŭ* | must not see |

Time, permission, obligation, prohibition — all in the same single slot. This is
where the person ending went: Malayalam gave that slot up and spent the room on
**mood** instead.

And the payoff lands on every line. *Kāṇaṇaṁ* is "I must see," "you must see"
and "they must see" without changing a letter. In Tamil each of those five ideas
must still pick a person ending before it can be said aloud.
