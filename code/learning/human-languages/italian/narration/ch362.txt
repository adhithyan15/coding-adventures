Lesson 1 of 6.
stressato — stressed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the training, then the Italian for the consultant.

You'll want to know: stressato.
stressato — "stressed".
In questo periodo sono molto stressata. I am very stressed at the moment.
A woman says stressata. The noun is lo stress.

The word, taken apart.
stressato is built on stress, which was borrowed from English stress "strain, pressure" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 6.
esaurito — worn out, burnt out; sold out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the consultant, then the Italian for stressed.

You'll want to know: esaurito.
esaurito — "worn out, burnt out; sold out".
Dopo sei mesi senza ferie sono esaurito. After six months without holidays I am burnt out.
Of tickets or goods: i biglietti sono esauriti — sold out.

The word, taken apart.
esaurito is the past participle of esaurire "to use up", a learned borrowing from Latin exhaurīre "to draw out, to drain" (Lewis & Short), built on haurīre "to draw water": drained dry.

Con il nuovo computer l'efficienza è aumentata. With the new computer, efficiency has gone up.

The word, taken apart.
efficienza is a learned borrowing from Latin efficientia "efficiency" (EtymDB), from efficere "to bring about", built on facere "to do".

You'll want to know: efficiente.
efficiente — "efficient".
La nuova segretaria è molto efficiente. The new secretary is very efficient.

efficiente is a learned borrowing from Latin efficiēns (EtymDB), present participle of efficere "to bring about", built from ex and facere "to do".

Lesson 5 of 6.
la produttività — the productivity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the efficiency, then the Italian for efficient.

You'll want to know: la produttività.
la produttività — "the productivity".
Dormire poco abbassa la produttività. Sleeping too little lowers productivity.
The adjective is produttivo.

The word, taken apart.
produttività is built on produttivo, a learned borrowing from Late Latin prōductīvus, from prōdūcere "to bring forth" (Lewis & Short), built on dūcere "to lead".
