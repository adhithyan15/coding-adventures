# One conversation, from hello to goodbye

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PRACTICE-02, IT-LEX-C03-PRACTICE-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] You have practised three exchanges one at a time. Nothing has yet
asked you to run them together, which is what a real conversation is.

## You'll want to know: the three, joined up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PRACTICE-02, IT-LEX-C03-PRACTICE-02, IT-LEX-C04-PRACTICE-02, IT-PRAGMATIC-PREGO-COME-AGAIN-06] -->

One formal meeting, first word to last:

> — Buongiorno. Come sta?
> — Bene, grazie. E Lei? Come si chiama?
> — **Prego?**
> — Come si chiama?
> — Mi chiamo Anna. Piacere.
> — Piacere. Arrivederci, a domani!

The third line is the one to notice. **Prego?** on a rising voice is not the
*prego* that answers *grazie* — it is the repair for a sentence you did not
catch, and a first conversation in a new language needs it more than any other
line in the exchange.

## What you've built — three chapters in one view
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-NOTICE-PRACTICE-03, IT-NOTICE-C03-PRACTICE-03, IT-NOTICE-C04-PRACTICE-03] -->

| informal · formal | |
|---|---|
| *Come stai* · *Come sta* | asking how someone is |
| *Come ti chiami* · *Come si chiama* | asking a name |
| *Ciao* · *Arrivederci* | leaving |

The first two rows differ in the **verb ending**, not in an added polite word.
The third row is the exception that proves it: **arrivederci** is "until we
re-see each other", **a domani** "until tomorrow", **a presto** "until soon".
Italian takes its leave by naming the next meeting, as *au revoir* and *auf
Wiedersehen* do.
