Spanish, chapter 722: Valientes y cobardes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el valor — courage; value.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fault, then the Spanish for to value.

You'll want to know: el valor.
el valor — "courage; value".
Hace falta valor para decir la verdad. It takes courage to tell the truth.
Two senses: courage and value (el valor de una casa).

The word, taken apart.
valor comes from Late Latin valor, valōrem (EtymDB), from valēre "to be strong, to be worth"; strength and worth stayed in one word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el coraje — guts, courage; anger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to value, then the Spanish for courage.

You'll want to know: el coraje.
el coraje — "guts, courage; anger".
Tuvo el coraje de hablar. She had the courage to speak.
In Mexico and other countries me da coraje means it makes me angry.

The word, taken apart.
coraje was borrowed from Old Occitan coratge (EtymDB), built on Latin cor "heart"; English courage is the French form of the same word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la valentía — bravery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for courage, then the Spanish for guts.

You'll want to know: la valentía.
la valentía — "bravery".
Todos admiran su valentía. Everyone admires her bravery.
Built on valiente, brave.

The word, taken apart.
valentía is built on valiente "brave", usually explained as the old participle of valer, from Latin valēns "strong", from valēre "to be strong".

Lesson 4 of 5.
cobarde — cowardly; the coward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for guts, then the Spanish for bravery.

You'll want to know: cobarde.
cobarde — "cowardly; the coward".
No seas cobarde y llámala. Do not be a coward, call her.
Same form for men and women: es una cobarde.

The word, taken apart.
cobarde was borrowed from Old French cuard "coward" (EtymDB), built on coue "tail", from Latin cauda: the animal that runs with its tail down.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for bravery, then the Spanish for cowardly.

You'll want to know: atreverse.
atreverse — "to dare".
No me atrevo a decírselo. I do not dare to tell him.
Atreverse a + infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
atreverse is inherited from Latin tribuere "to grant, to assign" (EtymDB), used with se: to grant oneself the right to do something.
