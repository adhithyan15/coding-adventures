# Unit 2: Subject Pronouns

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Out loud: how do you say "my name is..." in Spanish? ... Good —
today's unit gives you the words for "I," "you," "he/she," and more, so you
can start building full sentences instead of memorized phrases.

## New Material

**yo** — I
> Etymology: ← Latin *ego* ("I"). Note: English "I" is *not* a cognate of
> *yo* (English "I" is Germanic) — but *ego* itself was later borrowed
> straight into English: *ego*, *egotist*, *egocentric*.

**tú** — you (informal, singular)
> Etymology: ← Latin *tu*. Cognate with English *thou* — the old informal
> "you" that English used to have and lost, while Spanish kept the
> informal/formal distinction alive (*tú* vs. *usted*, below).

**él / ella** — he / she
> Etymology: ← Latin demonstrative *ille* / *illa* ("that one," masc./fem.).
> Third-person pronouns across the Romance languages descend from this same
> Latin demonstrative — French *il/elle*, Italian *egli/ella* are cousins of
> *él/ella*, not independent inventions.

**usted** — you (formal, singular)
> Etymology: a contraction of *vuestra merced* ("your grace/mercy"). *merced*
> ← Latin *merces* ("payment, reward, wages" — cognate with English *mercy*,
> *mercenary*, *commerce*). Formal "you" in Spanish literally started as a
> title of respect, then wore down into a pronoun.

**nosotros** — we
> Etymology: ← Latin *nos* ("we") + *alteros* ("others"), i.e. "we others."
> This compound is a Romance-era innovation, not a word Latin itself had.

**ellos / ellas** — they (masc./fem.)
> Etymology: plural of *él/ella* — same *ille/illa* root as above.

## Guided Practice

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "yo"]
- [YOU SAY: "tú"]
- [YOU SAY: "él" then "ella"]
- [YOU SAY: "nosotros"]

[REPEAT x2] Now point (mentally) at yourself, then the road ahead, then say:
*yo... tú... nosotros.*
