Mandarin Chinese, chapter 115: Careful!.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
小心 (xiǎoxīn) — to be careful; to mind (something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay by card, then the Mandarin for to give change.

You'll want to know: 小心.
小心 — xiǎoxīn — "to be careful; to mind (something)".
出门小心车 chūmén xiǎoxīn chē — Mind the traffic when you go out.
The new character, in pieces: 心 (xīn) — four strokes: a dot, a long hook lying along the bottom, and two dots, the foot of 想. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
小心 (xiǎoxīn) is built from 小 'small' and 心 (xīn) 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How a small heart came to mean care is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for signs and notices.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: xiǎoxīn, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chūmén xiǎoxīn chē — Mind the traffic when you go out.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to pay by card, then the Mandarin for to give change, then say xiǎoxīn again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 6.
心 (xīn) — a dot, a lying hook, two dots.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a dot, a lying hook, two dots and Guided Practice.

Script — a dot, a lying hook, two dots.
心 (xīn).
A dot on the left, slanting down.
A long hook lying along the bottom, curving up at the right and hooking in.
Two dots above it, the middle one, then the right one.
1 + 1 + 2 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 心 (xīn), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 心 (xīn)? (Four.) Which stroke is the long one? (The hook lying along the bottom, second.) Where have you written it before? (At the foot of 想.)

Lesson 6 of 6.
wēixiǎn — dangerous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to forbid, then the Mandarin for to pay attention.

You'll want to know: wēixiǎn.
wēixiǎn — "dangerous".
zhèr hěn wēixiǎn — It's dangerous here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wēixiǎn is built from wēi and xiǎn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwe and xjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'unsafe' and 'perilous, hard to pass'. The regular change from ngjwe gives wéi, with a rising tone; the level wēi of today is not settled.
