## Chapter 76 — reading: words, lines, and a first passage

Kannada's reading rung. No task shapes are declared, so it moves no number.

Three lessons, 6 -> 12 -> 31 tokens, no new word and no new sign in any: the
script ladder has taught 50, and everything here is built from those.

- **Words**: the lesson is about the CURVES. Kannada rounds nearly every letter
  because the script was cut into palm leaf for a thousand years and a straight
  stroke splits a leaf along its grain. The curves are not decoration; they are
  what a scribe had to do to keep the page from tearing, kept long after the
  page became paper. A reader gains from it too -- rounded shapes are easier to
  tell apart at a glance, which is most of what reading a word cold is.
- **Lines**: naming needs no verb, locating does. **ಇದು ಬಾಗಿಲು** is two words
  side by side and finished, but **ಇದೆ** appears the moment a line says WHERE.
  That distinction is visible on the page before anyone names it.
- **Passage**: the whole thing stands in one spot and points -- **ಇಲ್ಲಿ** near,
  **ಅಲ್ಲಿ** far -- and ends with the one line that says a thing is not here.
  **ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಇಲ್ಲ** is two words one letter apart doing opposite work, the hardest
  pair on the page to read quickly.

Two joiners carry it: **ಮತ್ತು** adds, **ಅಥವಾ** offers. Cut by the check: **ಮರದ**,
the genitive, which the track has never written.
