# j'espère revenir — saying what you are reaching for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C138-VERBES90-03, FR-GRAM-SENTIMENT-ETAIS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to hope". (*Espérer.*) Then "I was pleased". (*J'étais
content.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An experience has a past and a future. Last lesson was how it
felt; this one is what you want next.

## You'll want to know: j'espère + an infinitive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-ESPERER-INFINITIF-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PARLER-01, FR-LEX-JOUR-06] -->

**j'espère** + infinitive — "I hope to".

> **J'espère revenir** à Paris. — I hope to come back to Paris.
> **J'espère parler** français un jour. — I hope to speak French one day.

**espérer** is a regular *-er* verb whose *é* opens to *è* where the ending
goes quiet: *j'espère*, *tu espères*, but *nous espérons*. An infinitive
straight after it, with nothing in between, makes the hope your own.
Italian puts a small *di* there; French puts nothing.

When someone else is the one who will do it, the frame is **j'espère
que…**, and the verb after it stays plain: *J'espère que tu vas bien.* — I
hope you're well.

## Grammar Lens: mon rêve, c'est de…
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-MON-REVE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C155-SANTE155-03, FR-LEX-C149-VERBES149-05, FR-GRAMMAR-MODAL-CHAIN-04, FR-LEX-C82-NATURE34-01, FR-LEX-C188-LIEU188-01] -->

An ambition is bigger than a hope, and it has its own frame:

> **Mon rêve, c'est de** vivre près de la mer. — My dream is to live by the
> sea.

You have **le rêve**, "the dream", and **rêver**, "to dream", which takes the
same *de*: *je rêve de partir*, I dream of leaving.

Three strengths, then, for the future: ***je voudrais** partir*, I'd like
to; ***j'espère** partir*, I hope to; ***mon rêve, c'est de** partir*, it is
my dream. Only the last needs a *de*.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**espérer** is inherited from Latin *spērāre*, "to hope, to look for, to
expect" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under *spero*), with an *e-* that French
grew in front of *sp-*, as it grew an *é-* in front of the *sc-* of
*écrire*. English **desperate** is the
same verb with *dē-* in front: hope taken away. **rêver** goes back to Old
French *resver*, "to be delirious" (EtymDB 2.1), and further than that
the sources do not reach.
