Japanese, chapter 165: The Seventh, the Tenth and a Birthday.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
なのか (nanoka) — the seventh of the month; seven days.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for Friday, then the Japanese for Sunday.

You'll want to know: なのか.
なのか — nanoka — "the seventh of the month; seven days".
らいげつの なのかに うみに いきます。 — raigetsu no nanoka ni umi ni ikimasu — "I will go to the sea on the seventh of next month."

The word, taken apart.
なのか (nanoka) is native Japanese, built from nano-, a form of nana, "seven", and ka, "day", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

ようか — yōka — "the eighth of the month; eight days".
ようかは にちようびでしょう。 — yōka wa nichiyōbi deshō — "The eighth is probably a Sunday."

The word, taken apart.
ようか (yōka) is native Japanese, built from ya, "eight", which takes the form yō here, and ka, "day", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たんじょうび (tanjōbi) — a birthday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the tenth of the month, then the Japanese for the twentieth of the month.

You'll want to know: たんじょうび.
たんじょうび — tanjōbi — "a birthday".
あしたは いもうとの たんじょうびです。 — ashita wa imōto no tanjōbi desu — "Tomorrow is my younger sister's birthday."

The word, taken apart.
たんじょうび (tanjōbi) is a mixed word: Sino-Japanese tan, "birth" (Middle Chinese danX), and jō, "life, be born" (srjaeng), borrowed from Chinese, with native hi, "day", voiced to bi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
