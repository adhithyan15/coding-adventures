Hindi, chapter 149: Mosquito, Ant, Frog, Tortoise, Mouse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मच्छर (macchar) — a mosquito.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a butterfly, then the Hindi for a fly.

You'll want to know: मच्छर.
मच्छर — macchar — "a mosquito".

The word, taken apart.
मच्छर (macchar) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from matsara, "mosquito, fly" (CDIAL 9757). The Sanskrit word is known only from the old word-lists; CDIAL suggests it was named for its humming.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a mosquito, then the Hindi for an ant.

You'll want to know: मेंढक.
मेंढक — meṇḍhak — "a frog".

The word, taken apart.
मेंढक (meṇḍhak) is usually traced to Sanskrit maṇḍūka, "frog" (CDIAL 9746). CDIAL does not list the Hindi form, and notes that words for "frog" took many irregular shapes, partly through taboo.

चूहा — cūhā — "a mouse".

The word, taken apart.
चूहा (cūhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from cūha, "rat, mouse", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4899).
