Hindi, chapter 546: Fate and Chance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
शगुन (śagun) — an omen (a good sign).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a piece (in chess or a board game), then the Hindi for defeat.

You'll want to know: शगुन.
शगुन — śagun — "an omen (a good sign)".
दादी मानती हैं कि यह एक अच्छा शगुन है। Dādī māntī haiṁ ki yah ek acchā śagun hai. Grandmother believes that this is a good omen.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
शगुन (śagun) is probably a later borrowing of Sanskrit śakuna, "a bird", which also meant "an omen", read from birds. The forms Hindi inherited, saun and son, "a good omen", are the ones CDIAL lists (CDIAL 12245); śagun itself is not among them.

Lesson 2 of 5.
आसार (āsār) — signs; prospects (of something coming).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for defeat, then the Hindi for an omen (a good sign).

You'll want to know: आसार.
आसार — āsār — "signs; prospects (of something coming)".
आसमान में बादल हैं, बारिश के आसार हैं। Āsmān meṁ bādal haiṁ, bāriś ke āsār haiṁ. There are clouds in the sky: rain looks likely.
Masculine.
Used in the plural: बारिश के आसार (āsār) हैं.

The word, taken apart.
आसार (āsār) is usually traced to Arabic āthār, "traces, signs", through Persian.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for signs, then the Hindi for unlucky.

You'll want to know: सौभाग्य.

आपसे मिलना मेरा सौभाग्य है। Āpse milnā merā saubhāgya hai. It is my good fortune to meet you.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सौभाग्य (saubhāgya) is a tatsama, taken whole from Sanskrit saubhāgya, "welfare, good fortune" (Monier-Williams).
