Latin, chapter 102: Far, Above, Below, Inside, Outside.

Lesson 1 of 5.
procul — far away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for at once, then the Latin for then.

You'll want to know: procul.
procul — "far away".

The word, taken apart.
procul probably begins with prō, "forward, forth", but the rest is unclear; Lewis & Short's link with procellō, "to throw down", is uncertain.

Lesson 2 of 5.
suprā — above.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for then, then the Latin for far away.

You'll want to know: suprā.
suprā — "above". English supra and super-.

The word, taken apart.
suprā comes from superus, "upper" (Lewis & Short), as if "on the upper side". Old Latin wrote suprād (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
intrā — inside, within.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for above, then the Latin for below.

You'll want to know: intrā.
intrā — "inside, within".

The word, taken apart.
intrā is contracted from interā, "on the inner side", with parte, "side", understood (Lewis & Short); it belongs with inter, "between".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for below, then the Latin for inside.

You'll want to know: extrā.
extrā — "outside, beyond". English extra.

The word, taken apart.
extrā is contracted from exterā, "on the outer side" (Lewis & Short), from exter, "being on the outside" (EtymDB 2.1). Old Latin wrote extrād (Lewis & Short).
