## You'll want to know: 头疼
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C228-ACHE228-01]; assesses=[] -->

**头疼** — *tóuténg* — "having a headache; aching in the head".

> **我头疼不想吃饭** *wǒ tóuténg bù xiǎng chīfàn* — I've got a headache, so I don't feel like eating.

The new character, in pieces: 头 — five strokes: two dots, a long bar, a long stroke falling to the left, and a dot at the lower right; 疼 — ten strokes: the illness frame of 病, five strokes, then inside it five more: a short fall to the left, a bar that turns and falls to the left, a long stroke to the right, and two dots. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**头疼** (*tóuténg*) is built from 头 'head' and 疼 'sore', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *duw* and *downg* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the head' and the second 'pain'.
