Lesson 1 of 5.
कीलः (kīlaḥ) — a nail (to hammer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a curtain, then the Sanskrit for a lock.

You'll want to know: कीलः.
कीलः — kīlaḥ — "a nail (to hammer)".

The word, taken apart.
kīla "a stake, a peg, a pin" has no settled origin: CDIAL 3202 notes an Indo-European account (EWA) but also a proposal of non-Aryan origin, since forms with kh- stand beside it. It lives on as Hindi kīl "nail" (CDIAL 3202).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: मुद्गरः.
मुद्गरः — mudgaraḥ — "a hammer".

The word, taken apart.
MW takes mudgara "a hammer, a mallet" as probably formed from mudga "mung bean", but gives no further account, so its source is uncertain. It lives on as Marathi and Gujarati mogar "mallet" (CDIAL 10199).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कर्तरी (kartarī) — scissors.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a nail, then the Sanskrit for a hammer.

You'll want to know: कर्तरी.
कर्तरी — kartarī — "scissors".

The word, taken apart.
kartarī "scissors" comes from the root √kṛt "cut" (CDIAL 2858), which MW compares with Latin curtus "cut short", the source of English curt. It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi katarnī "scissors" (CDIAL 2858).

Lesson 4 of 5.
सूचिः (sūciḥ) — a needle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a hammer, then the Sanskrit for scissors.

You'll want to know: सूचिः.
सूचिः — sūciḥ — "a needle".

The word, taken apart.
MW connects sūci "needle" with sūtra "thread" and the root √siv "sew", though CDIAL 13551 notes that some forms point to an older śūci-, so the link is not settled. It lives on as Hindi sūī and Bengali sui "needle" (CDIAL 13551).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सूत्रम् (sūtram) — thread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for scissors, then the Sanskrit for a needle.

You'll want to know: सूत्रम्.
सूत्रम् — sūtram — "thread".

The word, taken apart.
sūtra "thread" comes from the root √siv "sew" (MW), an Indo-European root (EtymDB) that also gave English sew and Latin suere. It lives on as Hindi sūt "thread" (CDIAL 13561), and a sūtra of rules is a "thread" of short lines.
