Portuguese, chapter 29: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Passage.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six words off a counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have met. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them first.

Reading.
água pão café leite livro amigo.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time say the article you would put in front of each one. A água. O pão.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Portuguese hangs an article on nearly every noun, and the article is the gender said out loud.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why a written list like this one is harder than a real Portuguese page. On a page the article is already there, doing the remembering for you. Stripped of it, each word makes you do the work yourself.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, and the hardest thing about them is what is missing.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words with their articles, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which word is milk?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What is missing from this list that a real page would have? (The article — and it carries the gender.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines, and one that depends on you.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words. These are things you would say.
[pause 1 second]
You have said every one of them.

Reading.
Olá! Bom dia. Boa noite. Obrigado. Sim. Adeus!
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice that Bom dia and Boa noite differ by one letter, and that the letter is doing grammar.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Bom and Boa are the same word agreeing with dia and noite. Read the two lines together and the agreement is visible without anybody naming it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Obrigado is the strange one. It means obliged, and it is a participle, so it agrees with the person SAYING it, not the person thanked. A woman writes obrigada.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the only line on this page whose spelling depends on who is reading it aloud.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which line changes spelling depending on who says it?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Why is it Bom dia but Boa noite? (Agreement — and you read it before it was explained.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Who does obrigado agree with? (The speaker.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now twelve of them together.
[pause 1 second]
Forty-two words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
Olá! Bom dia. Eu tenho um livro. O livro é muito bom. Eu leio e falo. Tenho um amigo. O amigo é o primeiro. A família é boa. Hoje eu como pão. Tenho água e café. Amanhã leio o livro. Obrigado. Adeus!
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and count the joining words.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
One. E, twice.
[pause 2 seconds]
There is no but and no because here, and that is not a simplification — the book has not taught either yet. What holds the paragraph together instead is the order of the sentences: a thing, then what it is like; today, then tomorrow.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is worth knowing before the joiners arrive. A paragraph can be held together by sequence alone, and every language lets you do it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice also that eu appears and then stops. Portuguese keeps the person in the verb, so Tenho on its own is already I have, and the paragraph drops the pronoun once it is established.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
How many different joining words are in the passage?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What holds this paragraph together, if not joining words? (The order of the sentences.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Why does eu disappear halfway? (The verb already carries it.)
