Italian, chapter 330: Sotto la terra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'epoca — the age, the era, the period.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to surrender, then the Italian for to free.

You'll want to know: l'epoca.
l'epoca — "the age, the era, the period".
In quell'epoca non c'era la luce elettrica. In those days there was no electric light.
All'epoca — at the time.

The word, taken apart.
epoca is a learned word usually traced to Greek epokhḗ "a pause, a fixed point in time", taken in through Medieval Latin (EtymDB has no Italian entry).

Lesson 2 of 5.
il medioevo — the Middle Ages.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to free, then the Italian for the age.

You'll want to know: il medioevo.
il medioevo — "the Middle Ages".
Il castello è del medioevo. The castle dates from the Middle Ages.
Often written Medioevo; the adjective is medievale.

The word, taken apart.
medioevo joins medio "middle", from Latin medius, and evo "age", from Latin aevum (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
gli scavi — the excavations, the dig.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the Middle Ages, then the Italian for the ruins.

You'll want to know: gli scavi.
gli scavi — "the excavations, the dig".
Negli scavi hanno trovato monete antiche. At the dig they found ancient coins.
Singular lo scavo, built on scavare, ‘to dig’.

The word, taken apart.
scavi is built on scavare "to dig out", from Late Latin excavāre "to hollow out", built on cavus "hollow" (Lewis & Short).
