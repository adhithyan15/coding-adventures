Latin, chapter 411: Going to Law.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tribūnal, tribūnālis — the judgement seat, a raised platform.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to make sacred and binding, then the Latin for to lay down.

You'll want to know: tribūnal, tribūnālis.
tribūnal, tribūnālis — "the judgement seat, a raised platform".
Praetor in tribūnālī sedēbat. The praetor sat on the judgement seat.
tribūnal, tribūnālis n.: the raised platform where a magistrate sat to judge. English tribunal.

The word, taken apart.
tribūnal is built on tribūnus "a tribune" (Lewis & Short), who takes his name from tribus "a tribe": the platform of the tribunes. English tribunal comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
līs, lītis — a lawsuit, a dispute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to lay down, then the Latin for the judgement seat.

You'll want to know: līs, lītis.
līs, lītis — "a lawsuit, a dispute".
Inter frātrēs līs dē hērēditāte erat. There was a lawsuit between the brothers about the inheritance.
līs, lītis f., old Latin stlīs. Lītem intendere, to bring a suit. English litigation is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
līs was stlīs in old Latin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); its deeper origin is not settled. English litigation comes from its family.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

lītigō, lītigāre — "to go to law, to quarrel".
Vīcīnī dē fīnibus lītigant. The neighbours are going to law about the boundaries.
lītigō, lītigāre, lītigāvī, lītigātum, built on līs and agō: to drive a suit. English litigate.

The word, taken apart.
lītigō is built from līs, lītis "a lawsuit" and agō "to drive, to conduct" (Lewis & Short). English litigate comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
vadimōnium, vadimōniī — a promise to appear in court, bail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lawsuit, then the Latin for to go to law.

You'll want to know: vadimōnium, vadimōniī.
vadimōnium, vadimōniī — "a promise to appear in court, bail".
Reus vadimōnium prōmīsit. The accused gave bail to appear.
vadimōnium, vadimōniī n., built on vas, vadis, a surety: a promise, backed by money, to appear in court on a set day.

The word, taken apart.
vadimōnium is built on vas, vadis "a surety, a bail" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -mōnium; it is a different word from vās "a vessel".

Lesson 5 of 5.
iūriscōnsultus, iūriscōnsultī — a legal expert, a jurist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to go to law, then the Latin for a promise to appear in court.

You'll want to know: iūriscōnsultus, iūriscōnsultī.
iūriscōnsultus, iūriscōnsultī — "a legal expert, a jurist".
Iūriscōnsultum dē hāc rē cōnsuluī. I consulted a legal expert about this matter.
iūriscōnsultus, iūriscōnsultī m., also written as two words, iūris cōnsultus: one consulted on the law. Romans came to his house in the morning for advice.

The word, taken apart.
iūriscōnsultus is built from iūris "of the law", the genitive of iūs, and cōnsultus, the participle of cōnsulō "to consult" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
