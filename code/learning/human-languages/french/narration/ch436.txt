French, chapter 436: Raccourcis et détours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le raccourci — the shortcut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the marker post, then the French for the corner.

You'll want to know: le raccourci.
le raccourci — "the shortcut".
Il connaît un raccourci par le parc. He knows a shortcut through the park.
Built on raccourcir 'to shorten'.

The word, taken apart.
raccourci is the past participle of raccourcir 'to shorten', used as a noun (EtymDB); the verb is built on court 'short', from Latin curtus (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le détour — the detour, the roundabout way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the corner, then the French for the shortcut.

You'll want to know: le détour.
le détour — "the detour, the roundabout way".
Nous avons fait un détour pour voir le château. We made a detour to visit the castle.
Sans détour — straight out, frankly.

The word, taken apart.
détour comes from Old French destour, built on détourner 'to turn away' (EtymDB), from tourner, which goes back to Latin tornāre 'to turn on a lathe'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'itinéraire — the route, the itinerary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the shortcut, then the French for the detour.

You'll want to know: l'itinéraire.
l'itinéraire — "the route, the itinerary".
Avant de partir, elle prépare son itinéraire. Before leaving, she plans her route.
A masculine noun. Un itinéraire bis — an alternative route.

The word, taken apart.
itinéraire is a learned borrowing from Late Latin itinerārium 'travel guide' (EtymDB), from iter, itineris 'journey'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
contourner — to go round, to bypass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the detour, then the French for the route.

You'll want to know: contourner.
contourner — "to go round, to bypass".
La route contourne la ville par le nord. The road goes round the town to the north.
Figurative: contourner un problème — to get round a problem.

The word, taken apart.
contourner goes back to Vulgar Latin contornāre (EtymDB), from Latin tornāre 'to turn'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rebrousser — to turn back (rebrousser chemin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the route, then the French for to go round.

You'll want to know: rebrousser.
rebrousser — "to turn back (rebrousser chemin)".
La route était fermée, alors nous avons rebroussé chemin. The road was closed, so we turned back.
Almost always in rebrousser chemin — to retrace one's steps.

The word, taken apart.
rebrousser is usually traced to rebours 'against the grain', from Late Latin reburrus 'with hair standing up'; the details are not settled, and EtymDB does not list it.
