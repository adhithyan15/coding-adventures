Spanish, chapter 707: Cuando me enfado.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
enfadado — angry, annoyed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for panic, then the Spanish for to fear.

You'll want to know: enfadado.
enfadado — "angry, annoyed".
Mi jefe está enfadado conmigo. My boss is angry with me.
The usual word in Spain: estar enfadado con alguien. Latin America mostly uses another word, coming up next.

The word, taken apart.
enfadado is built on enfadar, which is usually traced to Portuguese enfadar, made from fado "fate", from Latin fātum.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: enojado.
enojado — "cross, angry".
¿Por qué estás enojada? Why are you cross?
The everyday word in Latin America; in Spain people mostly say enfadado.

The word, taken apart.
enojado is built on enojar, which is usually traced to spoken Latin inodiāre, from the phrase in odiō "in hatred"; odio comes from the same Latin word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la rabia — rage, fury; rabies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for angry, then the Spanish for cross.

You'll want to know: la rabia.
la rabia — "rage, fury; rabies".
Me da rabia perder el autobús. It makes me furious to miss the bus.
Me da rabia — it makes me mad. The same word names the disease rabies.

The word, taken apart.
rabia is inherited from Latin rabiēs "rage, madness" through a spoken form rabia (EtymDB); English rabies and rabid share it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fastidiar — to annoy, to bother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for rage, then the Spanish for anger.

You'll want to know: fastidiar.
fastidiar — "to annoy, to bother".
Me fastidia el ruido de la calle. The street noise annoys me.
Works like gustar: me fastidia. ¡No fastidies! — You are kidding!

The word, taken apart.
fastidiar is a learned borrowing from Latin fastīdiāre (fastīdiō) "to feel disgust, to loathe" (EtymDB), built on fastīdium "loathing"; English fastidious comes from the same family.
