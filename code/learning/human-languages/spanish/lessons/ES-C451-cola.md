## You'll want to know: la cola
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ESPERAR-01] -->

**la cola** — the queue, the line of people.

*Hay mucha cola* — there's a long queue. *Hacer cola* — to queue: *llevo media
hora haciendo cola*. *Ponte a la cola* — get in line. *El último de la cola* —
the last person in the queue.

Spanish says **hacer** cola, never *estar en* cola. You *make* a queue by
standing in it, which puts the work where it belongs.

## Grammar Lens: two colas, and one of them is glue
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-01, ES-LEX-C313-ASK-19] -->

There are three separate words spelled **cola** in Spanish, and none of them
is related to the others:

| the word | where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **la cola** — tail, queue | Latin *cauda* |
| **la cola** — glue | Greek *kólla* |
| **la cola** — the drink | a West African word for the nut |

*La cola del perro* is the dog's tail. *Un bote de cola* is a pot of glue.
*Una cola* at a bar is the drink. Nothing but context separates them, and
context always does: nobody queues in a tube and nobody glues at the entrance.

Words that collide like this are called homonyms, and Spanish has fewer of
them than English because its spelling is so regular that unrelated words
rarely arrive at the same shape. When they do, it is worth knowing, because a
dictionary will list them as two entries and you might otherwise think you had
missed a connection.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C451-TURNO-01] -->

Latin **cauda**, tail.

The jump from tail to queue is one picture: a line of people trailing behind
something, the way a tail trails behind an animal. French made the same jump
with *queue*, which English then borrowed — and *queue* is also from *cauda*.
So the English and Spanish words for a line of people are the same Latin word,
arrived at independently.

The *au* closing to *o* is regular: *causa* gave *cosa*, *paucum* gave *poco*,
*taurum* gave *toro*. The **l** is not. Spanish inherited the word through a
spoken form **coda**, and the step from *coda* to *cola* is one nobody has a
tidy rule for — which is worth saying, because most of the sound changes in
this material do have one.
