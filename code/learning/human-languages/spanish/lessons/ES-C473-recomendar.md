# recomendar — to recommend

## You'll want to know: recomendar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01] -->

**recomendar** — to recommend.

> Les **recomienda** empezar con pocos objetos.

*Te recomiendo este* — I recommend this one. *¿Qué me recomienda?* — what do
you recommend? That last one is what you say to a waiter, and it is the most
useful sentence in the lesson.

## Grammar Lens: the e that breaks, and the twin it works beside
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02, ES-LEX-PENSAR-01] -->

You said *recomienda*, not *recomenda*. That is
[pensar](./ES-C34-pensar.md)'s rule: under stress the **e** breaks open into
**ie**.

| stress on the stem | stress on the ending |
|---|---|
| recom**ie**ndo, recom**ie**ndas | recomend**a**mos |
| recom**ie**nda, recom**ie**ndan | recomend**á**is |

Only the **second** e breaks — the one that carries the stress. The first stays
put, which is why the infinitive keeps both.

And the twin. *Aconsejar* and *recomendar* overlap almost completely, with one
lean:

| verb | what it leans toward |
|---|---|
| **aconsejar** | what would be wise for you |
| **recomendar** | what is good, of its kind |

You *recomiendas* a restaurant. You *aconsejas* somebody to rest. Either verb
will be understood in either sentence, and a careful speaker picks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02] -->

**re-** plus **commendare**: **com-**, wholly, plus **mandare**, to **entrust**
— to put a thing into somebody's hand.

So recommending is not praising. It is **handing over**: you put the thing in
the other person's care and let them judge it.

That *mandare* is busy in English — **command**, **mandate**, **demand**,
**commend** — and the hand inside it is *manus*, the same hand you met in **el
mango**.
