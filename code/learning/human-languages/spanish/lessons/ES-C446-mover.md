# mover — and the phone that is named after it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MOVIL, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-02, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el móvil** and **trasladar**. Say **mirar**. Your phone is
called *móvil* for a reason. Name the verb inside it.

## You'll want to know: mover
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MOVIL] -->

**mover** — to move something. **moverse** — to move yourself.

*¿Me ayudas a mover la mesa?* — will you help me move the table? *No te muevas*
— do not move. *El niño no para de moverse* — the child will not sit still.

The stem breaks under stress, like *doler* and *dormir*: **muevo**, **mueves**,
**mueve**, but *movemos*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-02, ES-LEX-MOVIL] -->

Latin **movēre**, to move. **El móvil** is *mōbilis* — **movable** — from the
same verb, so a mobile phone is literally *the movable one*.

| word | the movement in it |
|---|---|
| mover | to move it |
| **el móvil** | the movable thing |
| el automóvil | the **self**-moving thing |

That last one is the whole of *automobile* explained: *auto-* from Greek
*autós*, self, on a Latin stem. A car is a thing that moves without being
pulled.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-02, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-02] -->

**Mover** and **trasladar** are not the same act, and a notice picks carefully.

*Mover* is physical and local: shift the table, shift your arm. *Trasladar* is
institutional and from-somewhere-to-somewhere: the office was moved, the patient
was transferred. You *mueves* a chair across a room; a company *traslada* its
headquarters.

Use *mover* for a thing you could push with your hands, and *trasladar* when an
address changes. Getting it backwards is not a grammar mistake — it is a
register mistake, and it is the kind a reader notices.
