Spanish, chapter 699: Rayos y truenos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el rayo — the lightning bolt; the ray.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to cloud over, then the Spanish for to clear.

You'll want to know: el rayo.
el rayo — "the lightning bolt; the ray".
Un rayo cayó en el árbol. Lightning struck the tree.
Los rayos del sol — the sun’s rays.

The word, taken apart.
rayo is inherited from Latin radius "a staff, a spoke, a ray".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: relampaguear.
relampaguear — "to flash with lightning".
Toda la noche estuvo relampagueando. There was lightning all night.
Used in the third person: relampaguea.

The word, taken apart.
relampaguear is built on relámpago "flash of lightning" (EtymDB), from Latin lampas "a torch", Greek lampás.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el granizo — the hail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the lightning bolt, then the Spanish for to flash with lightning.

You'll want to know: el granizo.
el granizo — "the hail".
El granizo rompió los cristales. The hail broke the windows.
One hailstone is un grano de granizo or una piedra.

The word, taken apart.
granizo is built on grano "grain", which is inherited from Latin grānum (EtymDB): hail falls like grains of ice.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tronar — to thunder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the hail, then the Spanish for to hail.

You'll want to know: tronar.
tronar — "to thunder".
Truena mucho esta noche. It is thundering a lot tonight.
The o becomes ue: truena. El trueno is thunder.

The word, taken apart.
tronar is inherited from Latin tonāre "to thunder" (EtymDB), like trueno "thunder".
