Lesson 1 of 6.
きもの (kimono) — a kimono.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to tie, then the Japanese for a ring.

You'll want to know: きもの.
きもの — kimono — "a kimono".
まつりで きものを きます。 — matsuri de kimono o kimasu — "I wear a kimono at the festival."

The word, taken apart.
きもの (kimono) is native Japanese, built from ki, "wearing", from kiru, and mono, "thing": a thing to wear. Both parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

まつりで しろい たびを はきます。 — matsuri de shiroi tabi o hakimasu — "I wear white split-toe socks at the festival."

The word, taken apart.
たび (tabi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its two characters, "foot" and "bag", are read together as one word rather than one by one (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
にあう (niau) — to suit; to look good on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for split-toe socks, then the Japanese for a sleeve.

You'll want to know: にあう.
にあう — niau — "to suit; to look good on".
この きものは ははに にあいます。 — kono kimono wa haha ni niaimasu — "This kimono suits my mother."
Polite にあいます, niaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
にあう (niau) is native Japanese, built from ni, the stem of niru, "to resemble", and au, "to match", both inherited rather than borrowed; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
