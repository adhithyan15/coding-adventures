# मित्र (mitra) — a friend, and a very old bond

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-BHAKARI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can ask for **bhākarī** now. This chapter turns from what you'd
request to who you'd share it with.

## You'll want to know: मित्र
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-MITRA]; assesses=[] -->

> **मित्र** — *mitra* — **friend** (masculine)

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Two things worth pausing on. **ि**, the **short *i*** vowel sign, is new —
written *before* the consonant it belongs to but said *after* it, so **मि** is
read *mi*, not *im*. And **त्र** is **त** joined to **र** by a *halant*, the
same conjunct mechanism as *punhā*'s **न्हा**, just a different pair of
letters. *Mi-tra*.

## The word, taken apart — a friend, and a covenant before that
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-MITRA-BIND]; assesses=[] -->

**मित्र** is a **learned borrowing** straight from Sanskrit **मित्र** *mitra* —
taken up whole, not worn down by centuries of sound change the way most
everyday words are. Its root is Indo-European ***\*mey-***, "to bind," and the
same word named a god: Avestan **Mithra** and Old Persian **Mithra** were
divine witnesses to oaths — the bond itself, personified, before the word
settled into ordinary Sanskrit as "friend."

## Grammar Lens: taken up whole, not worn down
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-TATSAMA-BORROWING]; assesses=[MR-LEX-BHAKARI] -->

Sanskrit-descended Marathi words come in two kinds: a **tatsama**, borrowed
directly with its Sanskrit shape mostly intact — **मित्र** is one — and a
**tadbhava**, one that passed through centuries of Prakrit sound change and
came out reshaped. **भाकरी** was neither: it never touched Sanskrit at all.
This chapter will show both a tatsama and a tadbhava for family words, so you
can hear the difference.
