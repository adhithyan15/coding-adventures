Mandarin Chinese, chapter 514: When the Weather Turns.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shǎndiàn — lightning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to struggle, then the Mandarin for with one's own hands.

You'll want to know: shǎndiàn.
shǎndiàn — "lightning".
wǒ zuì pà dǎléi hé shǎndiàn — Thunder and lightning are what I'm most afraid of.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shǎndiàn is built from shǎn and diàn 'lightning', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syemX and denH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the look of something coming out of a door, and explains the second as the flash when yin and yang clash; the sense 'to flash' is not given for the first.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rónghuà — to melt; to thaw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a thunderstorm, then the Mandarin for to be frozen stiff.

You'll want to know: rónghuà.
rónghuà — "to melt; to thaw".
tiānqì nuǎnhuo le xuěrén dōu rónghuà le — It turned warm, and the snowmen all melted.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rónghuà is built from róng and huà 'to change', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yuwng and xwaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'harmonious' and 'bright' and, after an older book, as steam rising from a cooking pot; the second it glosses 'to change, to transform'. How the pair came to mean melting is usually explained as warmth changing a thing, but it is not settled.
