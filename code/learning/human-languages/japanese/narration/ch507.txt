Japanese, chapter 507: Hopes and Wishes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きぼう (kibō) — a hope; a wish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a defeat, then the Japanese for to defeat.

You'll want to know: きぼう.
きぼう — kibō — "a hope; a wish".
もし きぼうが あったら、おしえて ください。 — moshi kibō ga attara, oshiete kudasai — "If you have any wishes, please tell me."

The word, taken apart.
きぼう (kibō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "hope" (Middle Chinese xj+j) and bō, "ambition" (mjangH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
のぞむ (nozomu) — to hope for; to desire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to defeat, then the Japanese for a hope.

You'll want to know: のぞむ.
のぞむ — nozomu — "to hope for; to desire".
もし かれが のぞんだら、この しごとを まかせます。 — moshi kare ga nozondara, kono shigoto o makasemasu — "If he wants it, I will leave this job to him."
Polite のぞみます, nozomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かれが ちいさい うちを のぞみました。, kare ga chiisai uchi o nozomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
のぞむ (nozomu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しぼうする (shibō suru) — to aspire to; to apply for (a school).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a hope, then the Japanese for to hope for.

You'll want to know: しぼうする.
しぼうする — shibō suru — "to aspire to; to apply for (a school)".
もし この がっこうを しぼうしたら、しけんが あるでしょう。 — moshi kono gakkō o shibōshitara, shiken ga aru deshō — "If you apply for this school, there will probably be an exam."
Polite しぼうします, shibōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): むすめが いしゃを しぼうしました。, musume ga isha o shibōshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しぼうする (shibō suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun shibō with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "intention" and bō, "ambition" (Middle Chinese mjangH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ほしがる (hoshigaru) — to want (said of someone else).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to aspire to, then the Japanese for an ideal.

You'll want to know: ほしがる.
ほしがる — hoshigaru — "to want (said of someone else)".
もし こどもが ほしがったら、かって ください。 — moshi kodomo ga hoshigattara, katte kudasai — "If the child wants it, please buy it."
Polite ほしがります, hoshigarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): いもうとが にんぎょうを ほしがりました。, imōto ga ningyō o hoshigarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ほしがる (hoshigaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built on hoshii, "wanted", with the ending -garu, "to show signs of"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
