Marathi, chapter 276: What Happened, Who Did It, What There Was.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
मी गेलो (mī gelo) — the past, person by person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the last two things you named, dance and a stage. (nṛtya, raṅgmañc.) Then the line you have said since your first meeting: भेटून आनंद झाला (bheṭūn ānand jhālā).
[pause 1 second]
Its last word, झाला, is a past: "(gladness) came about".

You'll want to know: the past, person by person.
काल मी पुण्याला गेलो. kāl mī puṇyālā gelo. — "Yesterday I went to Pune."
काल (kāl, "yesterday"). गेलो is जाणे in the past: the stem गे-, then -ल-, then an ending for person and gender, as in बोलतो / बोलते:
मी गेलो (mī gelo) (gelo) — I went; a woman says मी गेले (gele).
तू गेलास (gelās) — you went, to a friend; to a woman, गेलीस (gelīs).
तो गेला (gelā) — he went; ती गेली (gelī) — she went.
आम्ही (āmhī, "we") गेलो — we went.
तुम्ही गेलात (gelāt) — you went, with respect or to several.
ते गेले (gele) — they went; त्या (tyā, "they, all women") गेल्या (gelyā).
-ा, -ी, -ं are the endings of माझा, माझी, माझं. The Marathi past behaves like an adjective that agrees with whoever went.

You'll want to know: the past stems.
Most verbs put -ल- straight onto the stem: बसणे becomes मी बसलो (mī baslo), झोपणे becomes मी झोपलो (mī jhoplo), पडणे becomes तो पडला (to paḍlā), "he fell". A few add a vowel first: राहणे becomes मी राहिलो (mī rāhilo), "I stayed"; निघणे becomes मी निघालो (mī nighālo), "I set out".
Two more change the stem itself:
येणे becomes मी आलो (mī ālo), ती आली (tī ālī) — came.
होणे (hoṇe, "to become, to happen") becomes तो झाला (to jhālā), ते झालं (te jhālaṁ) — became, happened.
"Did not" keeps the past form and puts नाही last, as you do with the present: मी गेलो नाही (mī gelo nāhī), "I didn't go".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
घरी (gharī, "home, at home").
[your turn — say: "Yesterday I went to Pune" — kāl mī puṇyālā gelo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She came home in the evening" — tī sandhyākāḷī gharī ālī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We sat. Then we slept." — āmhī baslo. mag āmhī jhoplo.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you went to Pune yesterday, then that she came home.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I went"? (गेलो, or गेले.) "He came"? (आला.) "It happened"? (झालं.) Which ending says "she"? (-ी: गेली.) "I didn't go"? (मी गेलो (mī gelo) नाही.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
त्याने काम केलं (tyāne kām kelaṁ) — the past that agrees with the thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I went" and "she came". (mī gelo, tī ālī.) Then "I see with my eye", with the -ने of a tool. (mī ḍoḷyāne pāhto.)
[pause 1 second]
That -ने was promised a second job. This is it.

Grammar Lens: the doer takes -ने.
त्याने काम केलं. tyāne kām kelaṁ. — "He did the work."
A verb with an object, such as eating, reading or doing, changes the past. The doer is marked: तो becomes त्याने (tyāne), ती becomes तिने (tine), and ते, "they", becomes त्यांनी (tyānnī). A name takes -ने too: मीराने (mīrāne).
मी, तू, आम्ही and तुम्ही take nothing: मी काम केलं (mī kām kelaṁ), "I did the work".

You'll want to know: the verb follows the thing.
मी आंबा खाल्ला. mī āmbā khāllā. — "I ate a mango."
आंबा is masculine, so the verb ends in -ा, whoever ate it. Change the thing and the verb changes:
मी पोळी खाल्ली (khāllī) — I ate a chapati (feminine).
मी पुस्तक वाचलं (vāclaṁ) — I read a book (neuter).
तिने चित्रपट पाहिला (pāhilā) — she watched a film.
After तू the verb adds -स: तू पुस्तक वाचलंस (tū pustak vāclaṁs); after तुम्ही, -त: तुम्ही काय केलंत? (tumhī kāy kelant?), "What did you do?"
Pasts to learn: केलं, खाल्लं, घेतलं (ghetlaṁ), दिलं (dilaṁ), सांगितलं (sāṅgitlaṁ), and from पिणे, प्यायलं (pyāylaṁ). With no object, -ं: त्याने सांगितलं (tyāne sāṅgitlaṁ), "he said so". Formal print writes this -ं as -े (केले). A few verbs with an object still follow the doer: मी मराठी शिकलो (mī marāṭhī śiklo).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "He did the work" — tyāne kām kelaṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I ate a mango" — mī āmbā khāllā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Meera read the news" — mīrāne bātmī vāclī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you ate a chapati, then that he watched a film.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Who takes -ने? (त्याने, तिने, त्यांनी, and a name.) Who takes nothing? (मी, तू, आम्ही, तुम्ही.) Why खाल्ली in मी पोळी खाल्ली? (पोळी is feminine, and the verb follows the thing.) "I read a book"? (मी पुस्तक वाचलं.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
होता (hotā) — was, and there was.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I am well": mī barā āhe. Then "I went": mī gelo.
[pause 1 second]
आहे has a past of its own, and it takes the endings of गेलो.

You'll want to know: होता, 'was'.
काल मी घरी होतो. kāl mī gharī hoto. — "Yesterday I was at home."
मी होतो (hoto) — I was; a woman says होते (hote).
तू होतास (hotās) / होतीस (hotīs) — you were, to a friend.
तो होता (hotā), ती होती (hotī), ते होतं (hotaṁ) — he, she, it was.
आम्ही होतो, तुम्ही होतात (hotāt), ते होते (hote) — we, you, they were.
It is also "there was":
गावात एक नदी होती. gāvāt ek nadī hotī. — "There was a river in the village."

You'll want to know: नव्हता, 'was not'.
"Was not" is one word, with the same endings: नव्हतो (navhto), नव्हता (navhtā), नव्हती (navhtī), नव्हतं (navhtaṁ).
तो शाळेत नव्हता. to śāḷet navhtā. — "He was not at school."
It is to होता (hotā) what नाही is to आहे.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Yesterday I was at home" — kāl mī gharī hoto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "There was a big shop in the village" — gāvāt ek moṭhaṁ dukān hotaṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He was not at school" — to śāḷet navhtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that there was a river in the village, then that he was not at school.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I was"? (होतो, or होते.) "It was"? (होतं.) "There was a river"? (एक नदी होती.) "She was not"? (ती नव्हती.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — what happened.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "Yesterday I went to Pune". (kāl mī puṇyālā gelo.) Then "she came" and "we sat". (tī ālī, āmhī baslo.)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: I came home in the evening, and then I slept.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Meera ate a mango. Arun read a book.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: What did you do yesterday? I was at home.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: There was a big shop in the village.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I didn't go to school. He was not at home either.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which past follows the one who went? (गेलो, आली: no object.) Which follows the thing? (The past with an object: मीराने आंबा खाल्ला.) "There was"? (होता (hotā), होती, होतं.) "Was not"? (नव्हता.)
