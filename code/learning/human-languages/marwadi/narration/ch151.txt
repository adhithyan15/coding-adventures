Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 151: And, Or, But, and One Reason.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
अर (ar) — put two familiar things together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say कुत्तो (kutto, a dog). Then say बिल्ली (billī, a cat). Keep the two words separate for one more breath.

You'll want to know: अर.
अर — ar — and, joining this thing with that thing.
The Government of India's Linguistic Survey of India — Rajasthan, Part I records Marwari अर (ar) as the particle that links words or groups of words. Its own example joins a dog and a cat. This lesson keeps the book's already-taught word बिल्ली for cat.

The word, taken apart.
The official survey attests the Marwari form and its “and / or” use, but does not derive it. Its origin remains unknown in the source used for this lesson.

What you've built.
Put अर (ar) between the two things:
कुत्तो अर बिल्ली — kutto ar billī — a dog and a cat.
Nothing changes on either noun. The small joining word does the new work.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: कुत्तो अर (ar) बिल्ली]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — switch: cat first — बिल्ली अर (ar) कुत्तो]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say only the joining word — अर (ar)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say कुत्तो अर बिल्ली once without looking. What is the one new piece? (अर, ar, and.)
Source: Government of India, Linguistic Survey of India — Rajasthan, Part I, section 4.5, “Conjunctive particle”, pp. 125–126.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Join kutto and billi with the new Marwadi word for and.


Lesson 2 of 4.
चाय अर पाणी? (chāy ar pāṇī?) — offer two familiar choices.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say चाय (chāy, tea), then पाणी (pāṇī, water). Join them once with अर (ar) as “tea and water”.

What you've built.
The official survey gives अर (ar) both “and” and “or” value. When somebody offers two alternatives with a question contour, the same small word carries the choice:
चाय अर पाणी? — chāy ar pāṇī? — Tea or water?
The setting and the rising question do the separating. Do not import a Hindi connector to make the choice look more familiar.

The phrase, taken apart.
This lesson adds a new use, not a new connector. The official survey attests अर (ar) with “and / or” value but does not derive it; its deeper origin remains unknown in that source.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — ask: चाय अर पाणी? (chāy ar pāṇī?)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: पाणी।]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — ask: reverse the alternatives — पाणी अर (ar) चाय?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: चाय।]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
One short item is enough for the answer. This lesson teaches how to hand over a real choice; it does not yet teach “I prefer”.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Offer tea or water. Answer with one item. This closes a tiny two-turn choice, not a general preference statement.
Source: Government of India, Linguistic Survey of India — Rajasthan, Part I, section 4.5, “Conjunctive particle”, pp. 125–126.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Offer the choice tea or water in Marwadi.


Lesson 3 of 4.
पण (paṇ) — set one statement against another.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say चाय गरम है। Tea is hot. Then say पाणी ठंडो है। Water is cold. Keep both short statements intact.

You'll want to know: पण.
पण — paṇ — but, yet.
The Government of India's survey records Marwari पण (paṇ) as the particle for “yet/but”. A Marwari dictionary independently prints the same Devanagari form and labels it a conjunction meaning “but”.

The word, taken apart.
The two sources attest the Marwari form and meaning but do not give an etymology. Its origin remains unknown in the sources used for this lesson.

What you've built.
Keep the two familiar statements and put पण (paṇ) at their turning point:
चाय गरम है, पण पाणी ठंडो है। chāy garam hai, paṇ pāṇī ṭhaṇḍo hai. Tea is hot, but water is cold.
The first statement stays true. पण (paṇ) tells the listener that the second one pulls in a different direction.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: चाय गरम है]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — add: पण (paṇ) पाणी ठंडो है]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the whole contrast once, keeping पण (paṇ) in the middle]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What single word marks the turn? (पण, paṇ, but/yet.) Say the whole contrast once more.
Sources: Government of India, Linguistic Survey of India — Rajasthan, Part I, section 4.5, pp. 125–126; Marwari–Urdu–English Dictionary, entry पण (paṇ).
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Join 'tea is hot' and 'water is cold' as a contrast.


Lesson 4 of 4.
क्यूंकै — give one short reason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Bring back क्यूंकै (kyū̃kai, because), बाटी (bāṭī), तवो (tavo, a griddle), and गरम है (garam hai, is hot).

The word, taken apart.
Chapter 117 takes क्यूंकै apart as kyū̃ plus kai. CDIAL lists Old Marwari kyu under Sanskrit kim “what” (CDIAL 3164); the origin of the second part remains not settled. This lesson reuses that word and adds only the two-clause reason frame.

What you've built.
Chapter 117 taught the word क्यूंकै. A word meaning “because” is not yet a reason. Put one complete familiar statement before it and another after it:
बाटी गरम है क्यूंकै तवो गरम है। bāṭī garam hai kyū̃kai tavo garam hai. The bati is hot because the griddle is hot.
The first part says what is so. The second gives one reason. Both small statements keep है at their ends.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: बाटी गरम है]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — add: क्यूंकै तवो गरम है]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the complete reason line once]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
Now retrieve the chapter in four small moves:
join कुत्तो and बिल्ली with अर (ar);
offer चाय अर पाणी? (chāy ar pāṇī?) and answer with one;
contrast hot tea with cold water using पण (paṇ); and.
give the bati-and-griddle reason using क्यूंकै.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the whole reason once without looking. This is one small curriculum frame against a partial inventory: it is not an A1 mock, not A1 readiness, not an attainment claim, and not human validation.
Source for क्यूंकै: the existing chapter-117 Marwadi lesson and its cited dictionary/CDIAL trail. No new lexical source is claimed here.
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Say that the bati is hot because the griddle is hot.
