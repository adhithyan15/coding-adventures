## Grammar Lens: paperwork is a set of physical acts
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-01, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-02, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-03] -->

Nothing in this chapter began as an abstraction. Every one of the three binding
words is a thing done with the hands.

| word | the act underneath |
|---|---|
| **contrato** | pull the two sides together |
| **firmar** | make the result solid |
| **fijo** | drive it in so it cannot move |

Read a Spanish contract with those three in mind and the vocabulary stops
looking like jargon. *Contratar*, *firmar*, *fijar* — draw, harden, fasten. The
law borrowed its whole vocabulary from carpentry and rope.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-02, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-03, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-05] -->

**You can now read the four pieces of paper that decide where your money goes**
— what you agreed to, that you agreed to it, what you owe, and what you owe it
on. That is the administrative half of living somewhere, and it is what an A2
reading paper is mostly made of.

You also have two endings that keep paying. **-ura** names the thing an action
produces, so *factura*, *escritura* and *lectura* stop being three separate words
to learn. And **fijo** showed that a participle used long enough stops feeling
like one at all.
