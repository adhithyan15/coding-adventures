# ஏ — the letter that opens ஏழு

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-134, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-135] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: read ஐ and ◌ூ aloud, and write each once.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ஏழு** (*ēḻu*), "seven". You
have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and
puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ஏ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-136]; assesses=[] -->

**ஏ** — *ē*.

It opens **ஏழு** (*ēḻu*), "seven". You already write its sign ◌ே from பேசு; ஏ is
the independent shape of the same long vowel, for the front of a word.

## Writing: ஏ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-136] -->

- **1.** start here: sweep from the inner curl around the broad left loop
- **2.** without lifting, climb the outer left side into the crown
- **3.** without lifting, carry the top bar to the right
- **4.** without lifting, retrace left and draw the right upright down
- **5.** without lifting, continue down-left along the diagonal foot
- **6.** without lifting, turn at the foot and finish back up-right — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 0.** The pen never leaves the paper.

> Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard —
> Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source:
> Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Module 7, ஏ, with Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frame 7 (University of Texas at Austin), p. 193.
