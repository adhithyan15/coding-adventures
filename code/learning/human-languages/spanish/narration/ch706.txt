Spanish, chapter 706: Sustos y miedos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
asustar — to frighten, to scare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for ashamed, then the Spanish for to be ashamed.

You'll want to know: asustar.
asustar — "to frighten, to scare".
El perro me asustó. The dog frightened me.
Asustarse is to get scared: me asusté.

The word, taken apart.
asustar is built on susto "fright" with the prefix a-. Where susto itself comes from, the origin is not settled; it may be an expressive word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el susto — the fright, the scare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to frighten, then the Spanish for frightened.

You'll want to know: el susto.
el susto — "the fright, the scare".
¡Qué susto me diste! What a fright you gave me!
Dar un susto — to give someone a fright.

The word, taken apart.
For susto the origin is not settled. It is old in Spanish and Portuguese, and it may have begun as an expressive word imitating a sharp breath.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el pánico — panic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for frightened, then the Spanish for the fright.

You'll want to know: el pánico.
el pánico — "panic".
Me da pánico hablar en público. Speaking in public terrifies me.
Me da pánico + infinitive — something terrifies me.

The word, taken apart.
pánico is usually traced to Greek panikós "of Pan", the country god said to cause sudden fear; it came into Spanish through French and Latin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
temer — to fear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fright, then the Spanish for panic.

You'll want to know: temer.
temer — "to fear".
Temo que llegue tarde. I fear he will be late.
More formal than tener miedo. Temer que takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
temer is inherited from Latin timēre "to fear" (EtymDB); English timid comes from the same Latin family.
