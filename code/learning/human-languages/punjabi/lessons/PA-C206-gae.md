# One bounded past form of to go

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-JANA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Retrieve the familiar infinitive **ਜਾਣਾ**, “to go,” then listen for
one irregular past form.

## Grammar Lens: direct owner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-GRAM-GAE-206]; assesses=[PA-LEX-JANA] -->

> **ਗਏ** — *gae* — went, masculine plural

Hear and say the form before reading it. Punjabi also uses this masculine
plural form for one respectfully mentioned masculine person. Keep the
irregular form whole. This chapter practises only the small “they went” frame;
it does not open the wider perfective or agreement system.

## The word, taken apart — source boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-GRAM-GAE-206, PA-LEX-JANA] -->

Punjabi University Patiala's lexical statistics identifies **ਗਏ** as the
masculine plural perfect form of the verb “go.” Its verb reference places the
same form in the perfective family of that verb, and its reference grammar
independently labels it masculine plural.
Sources: [Punjabi University lexical statistics](https://www.learnpunjabi.org/statistics.html),
[Punjabi University verb reference](https://www.learnpunjabi.org/Verb.html),
and [Punjabi University reference grammar](https://pt.learnpunjabi.org/assets/a%20reference%20grammar_final.pdf).

Those sources support the modern form. Reuse the history already attached to
**ਜਾਣਾ**; do not invent a second etymology for an inflected form. For this
lesson, remember the whole irregular form and one bounded meaning.
