Mandarin Chinese, chapter 428: Moving On.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
通往 (tōngwǎng) — to lead to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bed sheet, then the Mandarin for to be bound for a place.

You'll want to know: 通往.
通往 — tōngwǎng — "to lead to".
这个门通往停车场 zhège mén tōngwǎng tíngchēchǎng — This door leads to the car park.

The word, taken apart.
通往 (tōngwǎng) is built from 通 'to go through' and 往 'to go towards', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thuwng and hjwangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be bound for a place, then the Mandarin for to lead to a place.

You'll want to know: 前往.
前往 — qiánwǎng — "to set out for a place".
前往机场的旅客请上车 qiánwǎng jīchǎng de lǚkè qǐng shàng chē — Passengers for the airport, please get on.

The word, taken apart.
前往 (qiánwǎng) is built from 前 'forward' and 往 'to go', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzen and hjwangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'before'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
huíchéng — return journey; the journey back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lead to a place, then the Mandarin for to set out for a place.

You'll want to know: huíchéng.
huíchéng — "return journey; the journey back".
huíchéng wǒmen zuò gāotiě — On the way back we're taking the fast train.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huíchéng is built from 回 'to go back' and chéng 'a stage of a journey', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwoj and drjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a set period' and 'a rule'; the sense 'a stretch of road' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
发票 (fāpiào) — official receipt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the journey back, then the Mandarin for the plan for a trip.

You'll want to know: 发票.
发票 — fāpiào — "official receipt".
我的发票不见了 wǒ de fāpiào bú jiàn le — My receipt has gone missing.

The word, taken apart.
发票 (fāpiào) is built from fā 'to send out' and 票 'a slip'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese pjot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to start' and 'to send out'; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.
