## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C299-FARM-04]; assesses=[] -->

**la cosecha** — "the harvest," both the gathering and the crop gathered.

You have already done the hard part of this one. When *tomar* was taken apart,
the word Latin actually left behind turned out to be *coger*, from **colligere**
— *com-*, together, and *legere*, to pick. That is where **collect** comes from,
and **coil**, and the *leer* you read with.

*Cosecha* is the same word wearing its finished-and-done form: **collecta**, "the
gathered-in." So the harvest is not named for cutting or for work. It is named
for the picking-up afterwards, which is the part that counts.

One use worth knowing. On a wine label, *una buena cosecha* is a good vintage —
English does the same thing, since a **vintage** is only a harvest with a year
attached to it.
