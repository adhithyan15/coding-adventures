Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 266: Please Come In.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हाजर (hājar) — present; at your service.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for brave, then the Marwadi for crazy.

You'll want to know: हाजर.
हाजर — hājar — "present; at your service".
हूं हाजर हूं सा। hū̃ hājar hū̃ sā. I'm here, at your service.

The word, taken apart.
हाजर (hājar) is usually traced, through Persian hāzir, to Arabic ḥāḍir, "present". With सा it tells a host or an elder that you are here and ready to help.

मदद (madad) is usually traced to Persian madad, "help". It is feminine: म्हारी मदद, my help.

Lesson 4 of 5.
अरज (araj) — a petition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for help, then the Marwadi for a kindness.

You'll want to know: अरज.
अरज — araj — "a petition".
आप म्हारी अरज सुणो सा। āp mhārī araj suṇo sā. Please hear my petition.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अरज (araj) is usually traced, through Persian ʿarz, to Arabic ʿarḍ, "presenting": a request set before someone. It is feminine: म्हारी अरज.

Lesson 5 of 5.
आसीस (āsīs) — a blessing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a kindness, then the Marwadi for a petition.

You'll want to know: आसीस.
आसीस — āsīs — "a blessing".
दादी आसीस देवै है। dādī āsīs devai hai. Grandmother gives her blessing.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आसीस (āsīs) goes back to Sanskrit āśis, "a wish, a prayer; a blessing" (CDIAL 1457; Monier-Williams). CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, and no other modern form either: the Marwadi word keeps the Sanskrit shape, with स for श. It is feminine, and an elder gives it: आसीस देवै है.
