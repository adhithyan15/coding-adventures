Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 287: Teachers and Pupils.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सिखावणो (sikhāvṇo) — to teach a skill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a class at school, then the Marwadi for to mark schoolwork.

You'll want to know: सिखावणो.
सिखावणो — sikhāvṇo — "to teach a skill".
दादी सीवणो सिखावै है। dādī sīvṇo sikhāvai hai. Grandmother teaches sewing.
Past (I …): म्हैं गीत सिखायो, mhaĩ gīt sikhāyo.

The word, taken apart.
सिखावणो (sikhāvṇo) is built on सीखणो, to learn, as its causative: "to make learn". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi sikhānā, "teaches", under Sanskrit śikṣate (CDIAL 12430).

Lesson 3 of 5.
गुरु (guru) — a guru; a master teacher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to teach a skill, then the Marwadi for to teach lessons.

You'll want to know: गुरु.
गुरु — guru — "a guru; a master teacher".
गुरु संगीत सिखावै है। guru saṅgīt sikhāvai hai. The guru teaches music.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गुरु (guru) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit guru, "heavy", and so "venerable"; Monier-Williams also gives it as a teacher (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
चेलो (chelo) — a pupil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to teach lessons, then the Marwadi for a guru.

You'll want to know: चेलो.
चेलो — chelo — "a pupil".
यो म्हारो चेलो है। yo mhāro chelo hai. This is my pupil.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
चेलो (chelo) is inherited from a reconstructed cēlla, "boy"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi celā, "pupil, apprentice", Gujarati celo, "pupil", and Sindhi celo, "disciple" (CDIAL 4911).

Lesson 5 of 5.
अखाड़ो (akhāṛo) — a wrestling ground; a training ground.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a guru, then the Marwadi for a pupil.

You'll want to know: अखाड़ो.
अखाड़ो — akhāṛo — "a wrestling ground; a training ground".
गांव मांय एक अखाड़ो है। gā̃v mā̃y ek akhāṛo hai. There's a wrestling ground in the village.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अखाड़ो (akhāṛo) is inherited from Sanskrit akṣavāṭa, "a wrestling ground"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi akhāṛā, "wrestling ground", and Gujarati akhāṛo, "wrestling place" (CDIAL 39).
