Russian, chapter 38: Work, Sing, Dance, Laugh, Cry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
работать (rabótat) — to work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pupil, then the Russian for work.

You'll want to know: работать.
работать (rabótat) — "to work". The verb of работа.

The word, taken apart.
работать (rabótat) is работа, "work", made into a verb with -ать. работа goes back to a Proto-Slavic word for "toil, slavery", built on the root of раб, "slave" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
петь (pet) — to sing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for work, then the Russian for to work.

You'll want to know: петь.
петь (pet) — "to sing".

The word, taken apart.
петь (pet) from Proto-Slavic pěti, with pojǫ, "I sing" — inherited. The deeper origin is unclear: Vasmer finds the proposed links unreliable, and Trubachev, in a note to Vasmer, explains it from пить, "to drink", since пою means both "I sing" and "I give to drink".

Lesson 4 of 5.
смеяться (smeyátsya) — to laugh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to sing, then the Russian for to dance.

You'll want to know: смеяться.
смеяться (smeyátsya) — "to laugh".

The word, taken apart.
смеяться (smeyátsya) from Proto-Slavic smĭjati sę — inherited; -ся is the old "oneself". Vasmer relates it to Latvian smiêt, "to laugh", Sanskrit smáyate, "smiles", Greek meidiáō, "I smile", and Middle English smilen, so English smile is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
плакать (plákat) — to cry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to dance, then the Russian for to laugh.

You'll want to know: плакать.
плакать (plákat) — "to cry".

The word, taken apart.
плакать (plákat) from Proto-Slavic plakati — inherited. Vasmer gives the first Slavic sense as "to beat one's breast" in mourning, and relates it to Lithuanian plàkti, "to beat", Greek plēssō, "I strike", and Latin plangō, "I beat my breast, lament".
