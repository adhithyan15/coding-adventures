Japanese, chapter 484: Needle and Thread.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
さいほう (saihō) — sewing; needlework.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to harden, then the Japanese for to set hard.

You'll want to know: さいほう.
さいほう — saihō — "sewing; needlework".
そぼは さいほうが じょうずです。 — sobo wa saihō ga jōzu desu — "My grandmother is good at sewing."

The word, taken apart.
さいほう (saihō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sai, "tailor" and hō, "sew" (Middle Chinese bjowng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
あむ (amu) — to knit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to set hard, then the Japanese for sewing.

You'll want to know: あむ.
あむ — amu — "to knit".
ははは ふゆの ぼうしを あみます。 — haha wa fuyu no bōshi o amimasu — "My mother knits a winter hat."
Polite あみます, amimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが てぶくろを あみました。, haha ga tebukuro o amimashita.

The word, taken apart.
あむ (amu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ほころびる (hokorobiru) — to come apart at the seams.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to knit, then the Japanese for knitting wool.

You'll want to know: ほころびる.
ほころびる — hokorobiru — "to come apart at the seams".
うわぎの そでが ほころびました。 — uwagi no sode ga hokorobimashita — "The sleeve of my jacket came apart at the seam."
Polite ほころびます, hokorobimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ふくの そでが ほころびました。, fuku no sode ga hokorobimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ほころびる (hokorobiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and usually written in kana; its older origin is not settled (JMdict).
