# ഇവിടെ (iviṭe) — here — where I am

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-01, ML-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can already name things. This is how you POINT at them.

## You'll want to know: ഇവിടെ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-03]; assesses=[] -->

**ഇവിടെ** — *iviṭe* — here — where I am.

Say it, and picture the thing it lands on as you say it. That is the whole word:
it does not mean anything on its own, it means whatever the speaker is pointing at.

Its partner, the far one, comes in the next lesson. The two of them
differ by one sound at the front — **i-** for near — and that is not a
coincidence. It is the whole system, and the last lesson of this chapter says so.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ഇവിടെ** (*iviṭe*) is native Dravidian: the near-pointing base of *itŭ* with a tail meaning "place". DEDR 410 gathers the near-pointing words, with Tamil *iṅkē*, "here".
