# My own line — the first Chinese with no model

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C97-XIAOSHI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Everything you have written so far had something to reach for: a model to trace, a word somebody said, a sentence printed above the space.

[PAUSE 1s] This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed: what this book has given you.

- [YOU RECALL: say 两个小时 — *liǎng ge xiǎoshí*, two hours]

## Writing — controlled composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C97-XIAOSHI-01, ZH-SCRIPT-SHI-TIME-01, ZH-LEX-C85-ZAI-01, ZH-LEX-C91-XIANGYAO-01, ZH-SCRIPT-YAO-01, ZH-LEX-C92-YE-01, ZH-LEX-C97-MEITIAN-01, ZH-LEX-C97-YIHOU-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **one line** of your own about your day. Three parts, in your own words: where you are, with 在; what you want, with 想要 or 要; and for how long, with 一个小时 or 两个小时. If you want a second thing, 也 brings it in, in front of its verb.

[PAUSE 2s] Some words you will want have characters you have not written yet: *měitiān*, every day; *yǐhòu*, after. Write those in pinyin, inside the line, with their tone marks. This task allows that, and it allows nothing else: a character you can read but have not learned to write is not one you produce, so it goes in pinyin too.

[PAUSE 2s] Three checks belong to the hand. Write each character in the stroke order you learned: 时 is 日 first, narrow, then the hooked vertical and the dot. Give every character **the same size of square**, two-stroke 了 as much as ten-stroke 都. And leave a space between clauses: no Chinese comma or full stop, as this book prints them.

[PAUSE 3s] Write the line. Then read it back, and check that every pinyin word has its tone marks before you check anything else.
