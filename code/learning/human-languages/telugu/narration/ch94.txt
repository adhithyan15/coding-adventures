Telugu, chapter 94: Offers, Apologies, Introductions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
మన్నించండి (manniñcaṇḍi) — please pardon me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for gladly, then the Telugu for the body.

You'll want to know: మన్నించండి.
మన్నించండి (manniñcaṇḍi) — "please pardon me".
It does the work of క్షమించండి, and is the gentler, native word beside it. Either answers a mistake; మన్నించండి (manniñcaṇḍi) is the one to use with an elder.

What you've built.
A second apology, softer than the first.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say manniñcaṇḍi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Telugu for gladly, then the Telugu for the body, then say manniñcaṇḍi again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does మన్నించండి (manniñcaṇḍi) mean? ("Please pardon me".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
క్షమాపణ (kṣamāpaṇa) — an apology.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: క్షమాపణ.
క్షమాపణ (kṣamāpaṇa) — "an apology", the noun.
క్షమాపణ (kṣamāpaṇa) చెప్పండి — "say sorry". It comes from the same root as క్షమించండి: to forgive, and to ask forgiveness.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
కావాలా? (kāvālā?) — do you want it?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for pardon me, then the Telugu for an apology.

You'll want to know: కావాలా?
కావాలా? (kāvālā?) — "do you want it?".
It is కావాలి, "is wanted", with the question -ఆ. టీ కావాలా? (kāvālā?) — "would you like tea?". The answer is కావాలి or వద్దు, both yours already.

What you've built.
An offer, and both of its answers.

Lesson 5 of 5.
పరిచయం (paricayaṁ) — an introduction, acquaintance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for do you want it, then the Telugu for need.

You'll want to know: పరిచయం.
పరిచయం (paricayaṁ) — "an introduction, an acquaintance".
మీతో పరిచయం (paricayaṁ) సంతోషం — "glad to have met you", literally "the acquaintance with you is a joy". A fuller form of the సంతోషం you said at your first meeting.

What you've built.
Pardon, an apology, an offer, a refusal, and a fuller pleased-to-meet-you.
