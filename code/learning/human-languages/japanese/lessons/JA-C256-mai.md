# まい (mai) — the counter for flat things

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-GRAMMAR-C256-DIRECTION-01, JA-LEX-ANCHOR-KIPPU, JA-LEX-NIN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new word: say "where is the station?" (*eki wa doko desu ka*.) Then "a ticket". (*kippu*.) People are counted with **にん**; flat things have a counter of their own.

## You'll want to know: まい
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C256-MAI-01]; assesses=[JA-LEX-ICHI, JA-LEX-NI, JA-LEX-SAN] -->

**まい** — *mai* — the counter for flat things: tickets, stamps, sheets, plates. The number goes in front: **いちまい** (*ichimai*), one; **にまい** (*nimai*), two; **さんまい** (*sanmai*), three.

> **きっぷを にまい ください。**
>
> *kippu o nimai kudasai.* — "Two tickets, please."

> **きってを さんまい ください。**
>
> *kitte o sanmai kudasai.* — "Three stamps, please."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**まい** (*mai*) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the on'yomi of a character KANJIDIC2 glosses "sheet of …" and "counter for flat thin objects or sheets", a reading borrowed from Chinese (KANJIDIC2). JMdict lists it as a counter, "for thin, flat objects (e.g. sheets of paper, plates, coins)" (JMdict).
