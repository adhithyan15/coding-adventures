Portuguese, chapter 262: Devia --- What You Should Do, and Explaining Why.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
devia — what you ought to do about it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I would like". (Gostaria.) Then run a reason forward: "I've got a cold, so I'm not going out." (Estou constipado, por isso não saio.)
[pause 1 second]
An explanation often ends on a third move: what somebody ought to do. That move has its own form.

You'll want to know: devia.
You have dever, "to have to, must": devo estudar, I must study. Put it in the imperfeito and it softens into advice:
Devia estudar. — I should study. Devias descansar. — You should rest. (to a friend) Devia descansar. — You should rest. (to o senhor, a senhora or você).
A bare infinitive follows, with nothing in between. Devo estudar has no way out; devia estudar says I know I ought to, and have not promised. That softness is the register advice usually wants.
This is the imperfeito doing a present job, and in Portugal it is the everyday way to say "should". The same verb has a second soft form, deveria, with the ending of your gostaria:
Deveria descansar mais. — I ought to rest more.
Deveria is a little more careful, and Brazil uses it more than Portugal. Its -ia belongs to the would-form of Portuguese, which the last chapter of this run builds properly.

The word, taken apart.
dever is inherited from Latin dēbēre, "to owe" (EtymDB 2.1), and dēbēre is a compound: dē, "from", plus habēre, "to have" (Lewis & Short, under debeo). To owe is to have something from someone: you are holding what is theirs. a dívida, "a debt", which you have met, is the same family, and English took it as debt, due and duty.

[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: devia, devias, devia — then deveria]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the hard one, then the soft one — Devo estudar. then Devia estudar.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: advice to a friend with a cold — Estás constipado, então devias descansar.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is the difference between devo and devia? (Devo is "I must"; devia is "I should", softer.) Which soft form is more usual in Brazil? (Deveria.) What are the two Latin pieces of dēbēre? (Dē and habēre — to have from someone.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
explicar — to unfold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four pieces are in your hands: acho que for an opinion, porque and por isso for the two arrows, devia for advice. This lesson puts them in a row, under the verb that names the act.

Grammar Lens: the shape of an explanation.
explicar, "to explain", is a regular -ar verb with one spelling catch. Its event past for "I" is expliquei: the c becomes qu before an e, so that it keeps its hard sound. The rest is plain: explico, explicas, explica, explicou.
Here is a whole explanation, every word of it taught:
— Porque é que não sais? — Acho que vai chover. E estou constipado, porque ontem fazia frio. Por isso não saio. Devia descansar. — Why aren't you going out? — I think it's going to rain. And I've got a cold, because it was cold yesterday. So I'm not going out. I ought to rest.
Four moves, and a word for each: an opinion (acho que), a cause pointing back (porque), a consequence pointing forward (por isso), and advice (devia). With both arrows, you can build it cause first or claim first.

The word, taken apart: folded, and unfolded.
explicar is a learned borrowing from Latin explicāre, "to unfold, to spread out" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under explico), which is ex-, "out", plus plicāre, "to fold". To explain is to unfold: something was folded shut, and you open it out.
The same plicāre also came down into Portuguese the slow way, by mouth, and wore into chegar, "to arrive" (EtymDB 2.1): Latin pl became Portuguese ch. The usual story of how folding became arriving is the sailor's, folding the sails at the end of a voyage; neither source tells it, so it is offered as the usual story only. English kept the Latin in explicit, and complicated is the opposite: folded together.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: explico, explicas, explica — then expliquei]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the four moves — Acho que vai chover, porque não há sol. Por isso não saio. Devia ler.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, consequence first — Não saio porque chove.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: explicar, chegar, then English explicit]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the four moves of an explanation and a word for each. (Opinion acho que, cause porque, consequence por isso, advice devia.) Why is there a qu in expliquei? (To keep the c hard.) What is explicar, taken apart? (Ex- and plicāre: to unfold.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — explain yourself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you should rest" to a friend. (Devias descansar.) Which English word for owing shares its Latin? (Debt, or due, or duty.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, four moves. You can say what you think, why, what follows from it, and what ought to be done. And because chapter two hundred and fifty-nine gave you the scene, your reasons can reach into the past:
— Porque é que não foste ao mercado? — Fazia frio e eu estava constipado, por isso não saí. Acho que devia sair mais.
The reason is scene, fazia and estava; the consequence is an event, não saí; the advice is about now. Three times in one answer.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the question — Porque é que não foste ao mercado?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the answer, all four moves — Fazia frio e eu estava constipado, por isso não saí. Acho que devia sair mais.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a different reason — Chovia, então estudei.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same answer, claim first — Não saí porque fazia frio.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: an explanation of your own: para mim, a porque, a por isso, a devia]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
In fazia frio, por isso não saí, which half is the scene and which the event? (Fazia frio is the scene; não saí is the event.) What does explicar do, literally? (It unfolds.) And what is softer than devo? (Devia.)
