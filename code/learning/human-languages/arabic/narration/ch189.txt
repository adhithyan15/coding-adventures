Arabic, chapter 189: Accountant, Journalist, Worker, Translator, Interview.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
محاسب (muḥāsib) — an accountant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a customer, then the Arabic for a cashier; a money changer.

You'll want to know: محاسب.
محاسب — muḥāsib — "an accountant".

The word, taken apart.
محاسب (muḥāsib) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-s-b, "to count, to reckon" (Lane). Muḥāsib is the Form III participle, "one who settles accounts".

Lesson 4 of 5.
مترجم (mutarjim) — a translator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a journalist, then the Arabic for a worker.

You'll want to know: مترجم.
مترجم — mutarjim — "a translator".

The word, taken apart.
مترجم (mutarjim) is the participle of tarjama, "to translate", which Lane gives with tarjumān, "interpreter". Tarjumān is usually traced to Aramaic targmānā; English dragoman comes from it.
