Tamil, chapter 103: Grandchildren, Neighbours, Names.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
பேரன் (pēraṉ) — a grandson.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a daughter-in-law, then the Tamil for a son-in-law.

You'll want to know: பேரன்.
பேரன் (pēraṉ) — "a grandson".
-அன் marks a man, as in மருமகன்.

What you've built.
A grandson.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say eṉ pēraṉ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Tamil for a daughter-in-law, then the Tamil for a son-in-law, then say pēraṉ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does பேரன் (pēraṉ) mean? ("A grandson".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
பக்கத்து வீட்டுக்காரர் (pakkattu vīṭṭukkārar) — a neighbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a grandson, then the Tamil for a granddaughter.

You'll want to know: பக்கத்து வீட்டுக்காரர்.
பக்கத்து வீட்டுக்காரர் (pakkattu vīṭṭukkārar) — "a neighbour", literally "the person of the house beside".
பக்கம் is "side"; வீடு is "house".

What you've built.
A neighbour, spelled out as the house beside.

Lesson 4 of 5.
குடும்பப் பெயர் (kuṭumpap peyar) — a family name.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a granddaughter, then the Tamil for a neighbour.

You'll want to know: குடும்பப் பெயர்.
குடும்பப் பெயர் (kuṭumpap peyar) — "a family name".
பெயர் is the "name" of your first introductions; குடும்பம் is "family". Many Tamil names use a father's initial where English uses a surname.

What you've built.
A family name, and what often stands in for it.
