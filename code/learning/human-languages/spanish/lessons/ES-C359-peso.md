## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C359-COUNT-20]; assesses=[] -->

**el peso** — "the weight."

Latin **pendere** meant two things at once: to hang, and to weigh. That is not
two meanings — it is one, because the way you weighed a thing was to hang it
from a balance. **Pēnsum** is what came off the scale: a portion, weighed out.

Then money got involved. Before coins were trusted, you paid in metal and you
weighed it, so paying and weighing became one act. English is still full of the
consequences:

- a **pension** is a payment weighed out at intervals;
- to **spend** is *expendere*, to weigh out;
- something **expensive** cost a lot of weighing;
- to **compensate** is to weigh one thing against another.

The hanging survived too, in a **pendant** and a **pendulum**, both of which
dangle. And to **ponder** is *ponderāre*, to weigh a thing in your mind — which
is also what **pensive** does.

Spanish then named a coin after the weight and shipped it across an ocean, which
is why so much of the Americas still counts in **pesos**.
