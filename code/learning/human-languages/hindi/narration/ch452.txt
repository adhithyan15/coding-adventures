Hindi, chapter 452: Naming a Feeling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
भावना (bhāvnā) — a feeling; an emotion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for turning over (in bed), then the Hindi for a stretch (of the body).

You'll want to know: भावना.
भावना — bhāvnā — "a feeling; an emotion".
हर इंसान की भावना अलग होती है। har insān kī bhāvnā alag hotī hai. Every person's feelings are different.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
भावना (bhāvnā) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit bhāvanā, which Monier-Williams gives with senses such as imagining and a feeling of devotion (Monier-Williams). Hindi uses it for any feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a feeling, then the Hindi for an awareness.

You'll want to know: अनुभूति.
अनुभूति — anubhūti — "a deep feeling; an inner sense".
गाना सुनकर मीरा को शांति की अनुभूति हुई। gānā sunkar Mīrā ko śānti kī anubhūti huī. Listening to the song, Mira felt a deep sense of peace.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
अनुभूति (anubhūti) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit anubhūti, "perception, knowledge" (Monier-Williams). It is a formal word for a feeling sensed deep inside; anubhav is the experience itself.

Lesson 4 of 5.
महसूस करना (mahsūs karnā) — to feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an awareness, then the Hindi for a deep feeling.

You'll want to know: महसूस करना.
महसूस करना — mahsūs karnā — "to feel".
मीरा ने कंधे में दर्द महसूस किया। Mīrā ne kandhe meṁ dard mahsūs kiyā. Mira felt a pain in her shoulder.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दर्द महसूस किया, usne dard mahsūs kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
महसूस करना (mahsūs karnā) joins mahsūs, usually traced to Arabic maḥsūs, "perceived, felt", which came through Persian, with karnā, "to do": to do the feeling of something. The thing felt is the object, so the perfective agrees with it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
उमड़ना (umaṛnā) — to well up; to surge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a deep feeling, then the Hindi for to feel.

You'll want to know: उमड़ना.
उमड़ना — umaṛnā — "to well up; to surge".
बेटी को देखकर माँ की आँखों में आँसू उमड़ आए। beṭī ko dekhkar māṁ kī ā̃khoṁ meṁ ā̃sū umaṛ āe. When she saw her daughter, tears welled up in the mother's eyes.
Past (it …): वह नदी उमड़ पड़ी, vah nadī umaṛ paṛī.

The word, taken apart.
उमड़ना (umaṛnā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi umaḍnā, "to swell, heave", under ummaḍ, a form it reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 2344). Feelings, tears, rivers and crowds all well up with it.
