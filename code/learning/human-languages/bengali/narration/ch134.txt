বেঁটে (beṭe) — short (of height).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Bengali for a vase, then the Bengali for a cup.

You'll want to know: বেঁটে.
বেঁটে — beṭe — "short (of height)".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of বেঁটে (beṭe) is not settled: CDIAL has no entry for it, and a search by form and by the glosses "short" and "stunted" finds nothing of this shape in Bengali.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ঝাল (jhāl) — spicy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a cup, then the Bengali for short.

You'll want to know: ঝাল.
ঝাল — jhāl — "spicy".

The word, taken apart.
ঝাল (jhāl) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from jhāla, "burning, pungent", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5379). Late Sanskrit has jhāli, a dish of fried green mango.

Lesson 3 of 5.
নোনতা (nontā) — salty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for short, then the Bengali for spicy.

You'll want to know: নোনতা.
নোনতা — nontā — "salty".

The word, taken apart.
নোনতা (nontā) is non, "salt", with the ending -tā. The word for salt goes back to Sanskrit lavaṇa, "salt": CDIAL lists Oriya nuṇa under it (CDIAL 10978), and Bengali alonā, "not salty", under alavaṇa, "not salty" (CDIAL 707).

Lesson 4 of 5.
শক্ত (shôktô) — hard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for spicy, then the Bengali for salty.

You'll want to know: শক্ত.
শক্ত — shôktô — "hard".

The word, taken apart.
শক্ত (shôktô) is spelled as Sanskrit śakta, "able, strong"; its sense "hard" also matches Persian sakht, "hard, firm", and the two may have run together.
