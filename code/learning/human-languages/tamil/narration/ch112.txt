Lesson 1 of 6.
ஞ (ña) — the letter that opens ஞாயிறு.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ஞ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read ◌ூ and ஏ aloud, and write each once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ஞாயிறு (ñāyiṟu), "Sunday". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ஞ.
ஞ — ña.
It opens ஞாயிறு (ñāyiṟu), "Sunday", the last day in the week you learned. ஞ (ña) is a rare letter: few everyday words use it, and Sunday is the one you will see most.

Writing: ஞ.
1. start here: curve down into the compact left inner turn.
2. without lifting, circle the left inner loop and return upward, then lift.
3. put the pen down again and carry the long top bar straight to the right, then lift.
4. put the pen down again and curve left from the upper-right shoulder.
5. without lifting, descend the central upright, then lift.
6. put the pen down again and sweep around the broad outer-right curve.
7. without lifting, continue around the broad bottom bowl.
8. without lifting, return up the outer-left side — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frame 8, ஞ (ña) (University of Texas at Austin), p. 194.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at ஞாயிறு and point at ஞ (ña) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ஞ three times, saying ña as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ஏ, then ஞ (ña), from memory]
[your turn — read: ஞாயிறு aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 6.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read ஏ and ஞ (ña) aloud, and write each once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ஊர் (ūr), "town, home place". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ஊ.
ஊ — ū.
It opens ஊர் (ūr), "town", the place a Tamil introduction asks you about. It is built on உ (u), which you already write, with a second part added to its right: short vowel, then more pen for the long one.

Writing: ஊ.
1. start here: sweep outward around the compact upper spiral.
2. without lifting, descend through the broad outer curve and turn left onto the baseline.
3. without lifting, carry the long baseline straight to the right, then lift.
4. put the pen down again and curl around the added left loop.
5. without lifting, turn inward through the added loop.
6. without lifting, descend around the added inner curl, then lift.
7. put the pen down again and rise on the added adjoining upright.
8. without lifting, carry the added top bar right, then lift.
9. put the pen down again and descend the added separate right upright — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Module 17, ஊ (ū) construction, with Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frames 17, 16, and 12 (University of Texas at Austin), pp. 195–196.

Script you'll notice: ஒ.
ஒ — o.
It opens ஒரு (oru), "a, one", the form ஒன்று takes in front of a noun. You already read its long partner ஓ (ō). Put them side by side: the long one carries more at its foot.

Writing: ஒ.
1. start here: circle the small left loop and climb into the crown.
2. without lifting, sweep through the large right loop and curl inward, then lift.
3. put the pen down again and draw the separate lower bowl and return left — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Module 14, ஒ, with Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frame 14 (University of Texas at Austin), p. 195.

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say சந்தோஷம் (santōṣam), "gladness". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ஷ.
ஷ — ṣa.
It is inside சந்தோஷம் (santōṣam), "gladness". Like ஸ, it is a Grantha letter, kept for words Tamil took from Sanskrit.

Writing: ஷ.
1. start here: curl around the inner-left loop and descend to the lower-left join, then lift.
2. put the pen down again and climb around the rounded outer-left body and descend to the lower join, then lift.
3. put the pen down again and draw the lower joining bar from the left body to the right, then lift.
4. put the pen down again and loop around the right body and finish with its descending tail — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Ratnakar Narale, Learn Tamil Through English/Hindi, Reading and Writing Tamil Granthakshars, Third Tamil Granthakshar ஷ (ṣa), Sanskrit Hindi Research Institute, p. 13.
