Telugu, chapter 321: Tickets, Seats and Trouble.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
సీటు (sīṭu) — a seat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a bus" (bassu) and "a train" (railu). Then ask "how do I get to the station?" (sṭēṣanuki elā veḷḷāli?)

You'll want to know: సీటు.
సీటు — sīṭu — "a seat", on a bus or a train, or in a hall.
ఈ సీటు ఖాళీగా ఉందా? ī sīṭu khāḷīgā undā? — "Is this seat free?"
నాకు కిటికీ దగ్గర సీటు కావాలి. nāku kiṭikī daggara sīṭu kāvāli. — "I would like a seat by the window."
ఖాళీగా (khāḷīgā, "empty, free") with ఉందా?, "is it?": the -ఆ question again. Two seats are రెండు సీట్లు (reṇḍu sīṭlu), the way two tickets are టిక్కెట్లు.

The word, taken apart.
సీటు (sīṭu) is a loan from English seat, with the -ఉ that Telugu gives most borrowed nouns, as in బస్సు and ఫోను.

[your turn — say: "Is this seat free?" — ī sīṭu khāḷīgā undā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I would like a seat by the window" — nāku kiṭikī daggara sīṭu kāvāli]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does సీటు (sīṭu) mean? (A seat.) "Is this seat free?" (ఈ సీటు (sīṭu) ఖాళీగా ఉందా?) "Two seats"? (రెండు సీట్లు.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
ఒక టిక్కెట్టు ఇవ్వండి (oka ṭikkeṭṭu ivvaṇḍi) — at the ticket counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket" (ṭikkeṭṭu) and "give", politely (ivvaṇḍi). Then "is this seat free?" (ī sīṭu khāḷīgā undā?)

You'll want to know: buying a ticket.
విజయవాడకి రెండు టిక్కెట్లు ఇవ్వండి. vijayavāḍaki reṇḍu ṭikkeṭlu ivvaṇḍi. — "Two tickets to Vijayawada, please."
The place takes -కి, "to"; the number comes before the ticket; ఇవ్వండి, "give", closes the request.
టిక్కెట్టు ధర ఎంత? ṭikkeṭṭu dhara enta? — "How much is the ticket?"
ఎంత (enta, "how much"). For the way back:
నేను రేపు తిరిగి వస్తాను. nēnu rēpu tirigi vastānu. — "I will come back tomorrow."
తిరిగి, "back", is the one in తిరిగి ఇవ్వు, "to give back".

You'll want to know: when does it leave?
రైలు ఎన్ని గంటలకు బయలుదేరుతుంది? railu enni gaṇṭalaku bayaludērutundi? — "At what time does the train leave?"
ఆరు గంటలకు బయలుదేరుతుంది. āru gaṇṭalaku bayaludērutundi. — "It leaves at six."
ఎన్ని గంటలకు (enni gaṇṭalaku, "at how many o'clock"): times of day take -కు, "at": ఆరు గంటలకు, "at six".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "At what time does the train leave?" — railu enni gaṇṭalaku bayaludērutundi?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 3 of 6.
ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ) — a remedy; medication.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "medicine" (mandu), "a fever" (jvaraṁ) and "a doctor" (vaidyuḍu).

You'll want to know: ఔషధం.
ఔషధం — auṣadhaṁ — "a remedy; medication".
ప్రయాణంలో నాకు జ్వరం వచ్చింది. అక్కడ ఒక వైద్యుడు నాకు ఔషధం ఇచ్చాడు. prayāṇaṁlō nāku jvaraṁ vaccindi. akkaḍa oka vaidyuḍu nāku auṣadhaṁ iccāḍu. — "On the trip I got a fever. A doctor there gave me a remedy."
నవ్వు మంచి ఔషధం. navvu mañci auṣadhaṁ. — "Laughter is a good remedy."
Everyday Telugu says మందు. ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ) is the careful, learned word: you will read it on the signs of chemists' shops (ఔషధాలు, "medicines"), on labels, and in sentences about what heals in a wider sense, like the one about laughter.

The word, taken apart.
ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit auṣadha, which CDIAL describes as "made of herbs" and as "a herb" (CDIAL 2571), with Telugu's noun ending -ం. A remedy was first a plant.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the everyday word for medicine, then the learned one]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ) mean? (A remedy; medication.) Which word is the everyday one? (మందు.) Where will you read ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ)? (On chemists' signs and labels.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
ఔ (au) — the letter that opens ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ఔ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ఒ and ఓ once each. Then the sign ౌ, which you know from గౌరవం, "respect".
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ). This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ఔ.
ఔ — au, at the start of a word: one vowel that glides from a toward u, the way the middle of English now does.
After a consonant, au is the sign ౌ: గౌరవం. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own, ఔ. Few words open with it, and most of them come from Sanskrit.
In print ఔ (au) is built on the body of ఒ, with a long stroke laid along its top that runs out to the right and ends in a small loop.

Writing: ఔ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ఔ (au) and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Write the body of ఒ the way you learned it, then copy the long top stroke and its loop.
This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

[once you have stopped driving — write: ఒ, ఓ, then ఔ (au), from memory, naming each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ఔషధం (auṣadhaṁ)]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ఔ? (au, at the start of a word.) Which word did it come from? (ఔషధం, auṣadhaṁ, "a remedy".) Which sign says au after a consonant? (ౌ, as in గౌరవం.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
పోయింది (pōyindi) — when something goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "At what time does the train leave?" (railu enni gaṇṭalaku bayaludērutundi?) Then say "a remedy". (auṣadhaṁ.)
[once you have stopped driving — write: ఔ (au), once]

You'll want to know: the bus has gone.
అయ్యో! బస్సు వెళ్ళిపోయింది. ayyō! bassu veḷḷipōyindi. — "Oh no! The bus has gone."
అయ్యో (ayyō, "oh no"). వెళ్ళిపోయింది is వెళ్ళి, "having gone", with పోయింది (pōyindi), "went away": gone, and not coming back. Telugu says what the bus did; English says what you missed.
నేను బస్సు అందుకోలేకపోయాను. nēnu bassu andukōlēkapōyānu. — "I could not catch the bus."
అందుకోలేకపోయాను (andukōlēkapōyānu, "I could not catch") is అందుకో, "to catch", with -లేకపోయాను, "I could not".

You'll want to know: it is lost.
నా సంచి పోయింది. nā sañci pōyindi. — "My bag is lost."
పోవు, "to go", says "to be lost" too: the bag "went". Then say what was in it and when it went:
సంచిలో నా ఫోను, నా డబ్బు ఉన్నాయి. sañcilō nā phōnu, nā ḍabbu unnāyi. — "My phone and my money were in the bag."
నిన్న సాయంత్రం స్టేషనులో పోయింది. నేను పోలీసులకు ఫిర్యాదు ఇవ్వాలి. ninna sāyantraṁ sṭēṣanulō pōyindi. nēnu pōlīsulaku phiryādu ivvāli. — "It went yesterday evening, at the station. I must make a complaint to the police."
ఉన్నాయి (unnāyi, "they are, they were") is the plural of ఉంది. A report answers three questions: what, where, when.
