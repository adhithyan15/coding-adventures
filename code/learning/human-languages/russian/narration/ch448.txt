Russian, chapter 448: Laundry and mending.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
прачечная (práchechnaya) — a laundry (shop), a launderette.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a locksmith, then the Russian for repairs.

You'll want to know: прачечная.
прачечная (práchechnaya) — "a laundry (shop), a launderette".
В прачечной стирают бельё. V práchechnoy stiráyut belyó. At the laundry they wash linen.
An adjective used as a noun. Traditionally чн is pronounced шн here.

The word, taken apart.
прачечная (práchechnaya) is built on прачка 'a washerwoman' with an adjective ending: the washerwomen's place. EtymDB lists прачечная (práchechnaya) beside прачка.

Lesson 2 of 5.
химчистка (khimchístka) — a dry cleaner's; dry cleaning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for repairs, then the Russian for a laundry.

You'll want to know: химчистка.
химчистка (khimchístka) — "a dry cleaner's; dry cleaning".
Мы отдали пальто в химчистку. My otdáli paltó v khimchístku. We took the coat to the dry cleaner's.
Feminine; short for химическая чистка — chemical cleaning.

The word, taken apart.
химчистка (khimchístka) is a short form of химическая чистка 'chemical cleaning', from химия 'chemistry' and чистка 'cleaning', on чистый 'clean', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic čistŭ (EtymDB).

штопать (shtópat) — "to darn".
Бабушка штопает носки. Bábushka shtópayet noskí. Grandma darns socks.
Imperfective; perfective заштопать.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
зашивать (zashivát) — to sew up, to mend (by sewing).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a dry cleaner's, then the Russian for to darn.

You'll want to know: зашивать.
зашивать (zashivát) — "to sew up, to mend (by sewing)".
Мама зашивает дыру на брюках. Máma zashiváyet dyrú na bryúkakh. Mum is sewing up the hole in the trousers.
Imperfective; perfective зашить (зашью, зашьёшь).

The word, taken apart.
зашивать (zashivát) is built from за- and -шивать, from шить 'to sew', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic šiti 'to sew' (EtymDB).
