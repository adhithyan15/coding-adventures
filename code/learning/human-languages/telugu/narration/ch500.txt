Telugu, chapter 500: Joy and Delight.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఆనందించు (ānandiñcu) — to enjoy; to rejoice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a grudge, then the Telugu for contempt.

You'll want to know: ఆనందించు.
ఆనందించు — ānandiñcu — "to enjoy; to rejoice".
సెలవుల్లో పిల్లలు సముద్రం దగ్గర ఆనందించారు. selavullō pillalu samudraṁ daggara ānandiñcāru. In the holidays the children enjoyed themselves by the sea.
Past (I …): ఆనందించాను, ānandiñcānu.
The noun is ఆనందం, joy; ఆనందంగా, happily.

The word, taken apart.
ఆనందించు (ānandiñcu) is built on Sanskrit ānanda, "happiness" (CDIAL 1172), from the root nand, with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.

Lesson 2 of 5.
మురిసిపోవు (murisipōvu) — to be delighted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for contempt, then the Telugu for to enjoy.

You'll want to know: మురిసిపోవు.
మురిసిపోవు — murisipōvu — "to be delighted".
మనవడిని చూసి తాతయ్య మురిసిపోయారు. manavaḍini cūsi tātayya murisipōyāru. Seeing his grandson, grandpa was delighted.
Past (I …): మురిసిపోయాను, murisipōyānu.
A warm word for the glow of a parent or a grandparent.

The word, taken apart.
మురిసిపోవు (murisipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5011 lists Telugu muriyu, "to exult, to be in great glee", and the verb ends in pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 3 of 5.
పొంగిపోవు (poṅgipōvu) — to be overjoyed; to swell with pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to enjoy, then the Telugu for to be delighted.

You'll want to know: పొంగిపోవు.
పొంగిపోవు — poṅgipōvu — "to be overjoyed; to swell with pride".
బహుమతి గెలిచినప్పుడు ఆమె పొంగిపోయింది. bahumati gelicinappuḍu āme poṅgipōyindi. When she won the prize, she was overjoyed.
Past (I …): పొంగిపోయాను, poṅgipōyānu.
పొంగు is to boil over, like milk: joy that overflows.

The word, taken apart.
పొంగిపోవు (poṅgipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 4469 lists Telugu poṅgu, "to bubble up, to boil; to rejoice, to be elated", with Tamil poṅku; pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572) ends it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
పులకించు (pulakiñcu) — to thrill; to be thrilled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be delighted, then the Telugu for to be overjoyed.

You'll want to know: పులకించు.
పులకించు — pulakiñcu — "to thrill; to be thrilled".
ఆ పాట విని నా మనసు పులకించింది. ā pāṭa vini nā manasu pulakiñcindi. Hearing that song, my heart thrilled.
Past (I …): పులకించాను, pulakiñcānu.
Said of the body or the heart: the hair stands on end with joy.

The word, taken apart.
పులకించు (pulakiñcu) is built on Sanskrit pulaka, the hair of the body standing on end, a sign of rapture rather than fear (Monier-Williams); the Telugu ending -ఇంచు makes it a verb.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఉల్లాసం (ullāsaṁ) — cheerfulness; gaiety.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be overjoyed, then the Telugu for to thrill.

You'll want to know: ఉల్లాసం.
ఉల్లాసం — ullāsaṁ — "cheerfulness; gaiety".
పండుగ రోజు ఇల్లంతా ఉల్లాసంగా ఉంది. paṇḍuga rōju illantā ullāsaṅgā undi. On the festival day the whole house is cheerful.
ఉల్లాసంగా, cheerfully, in high spirits.

The word, taken apart.
ఉల్లాసం (ullāsaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit ullāsa, both "light" and "joy, merriness" (Monier-Williams).
