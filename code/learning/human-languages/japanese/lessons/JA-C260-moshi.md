# もし (moshi) — if; supposing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-GRAMMAR-C260-TARA-01, JA-LEX-C231-PHONE231-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new word: say "if it rains, I won't go". (*ame ga futtara, ikimasen*.) Then say "hello", on the telephone. (*moshimoshi*.)

## You'll want to know: もし
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C260-MOSHI-01]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C54-TIME54-02, JA-LEX-C207-WEATHER207-02] -->

**もし** — *moshi* — "if", "supposing". It opens the sentence and warns the listener that a condition is coming; the **-たら** at the end of the clause still carries the "if".

> **もし あめが ふったら、いきません。**
>
> *moshi ame ga futtara, ikimasen.* — "If it rains, I won't go."

It is not the **もし** of **もしもし**, the telephone "hello".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**もし** (*moshi*) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana and glosses it "if; in case; supposing" (JMdict).
