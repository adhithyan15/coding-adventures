# Is the one person me or you?

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give the two singular objects: “me” and “you”. (*Mām; tvām.*)

## Grammar Lens: choose by person

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE] -->

There is one person in either sentence. Choose by who that person is:

- the speaker as object: **माम्** (*mām*);
- one familiar addressee as object: **त्वाम्** (*tvām*).

Both are independent full forms and both take the same place before
**पश्यति**. The choice changes the person seen, not the verb.

## Guided Practice

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE, SA-REL-SAH-01, SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s each]
- [YOU SAY: “he sees me” — *saḥ mām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: “he sees you” — *saḥ tvām paśyati*]
- [YOU SAY: the rule — choose *mām* for the speaker, *tvām* for one familiar addressee]
- [YOU CONTRAST: *mām; tvām; mām; tvām*]

## Wrap-up Recall

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C260-SINGULAR-OBJECTS-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say both sentences without looking back, then name what changes.
(*Saḥ mām paśyati; saḥ tvām paśyati; the person used as the object changes.*)
