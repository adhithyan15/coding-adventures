Spanish, chapter 714: Cariño y ternura.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el cariño — affection; darling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get bored, then the Spanish for boredom.

You'll want to know: el cariño.
el cariño — "affection; darling".
Le tengo mucho cariño a mi abuela. I am very fond of my grandmother.
Tener cariño a alguien — to be fond of someone. Cariño is also a pet name: darling.

The word, taken apart.
cariño was borrowed from Galician-Portuguese cariño (EtymDB). Further back the origin is not settled; it is often linked to Latin carēre "to lack", as longing for someone absent.

You'll want to know: cariñoso.

Used with ser for character, with estar for how someone is acting now.

cariñoso is built on cariño "affection" with the ending -oso; cariño was borrowed from Galician-Portuguese (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
tierno — tender, soft; sweet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for affectionate, then the Spanish for tenderness.

You'll want to know: tierno.
tierno — "tender, soft; sweet".
La carne está muy tierna. The meat is very tender.
Used for food (tender) and for people (sweet, gentle).

The word, taken apart.
tierno is inherited from Latin tener, tenerum "soft, delicate" (EtymDB); the stressed e became ie, as in tiempo.

The word, taken apart.
adorar was borrowed from Latin adōrāre "to address, to pray to" (EtymDB), built on ad "to" and ōrāre "to speak, to pray".
