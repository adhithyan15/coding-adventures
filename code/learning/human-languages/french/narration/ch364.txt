French, chapter 364: Juste et injuste.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la justice — the justice; the courts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the banner, then the French for to protest.

You'll want to know: la justice.
la justice — "the justice; the courts".
La famille demande justice. The family is asking for justice.
La justice is also the legal system: aller en justice.

The word, taken apart.
justice comes through Old French from Latin iūstitia "fairness, justice" (EtymDB), from iūstus "right, fair" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
injuste — unfair, unjust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to protest, then the French for the justice.

You'll want to know: injuste.
injuste — "unfair, unjust".
C'est injuste: il a eu la même note que moi sans travailler. It is unfair: he got the same mark as me without working.
One form for both genders.

The word, taken apart.
injuste is a learned borrowing from Latin iniūstus "unjust" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from in- "not" and iūstus "right, fair".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le préjugé — the prejudice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for unfair, then the French for the injustice.

You'll want to know: le préjugé.
le préjugé — "the prejudice".
Elle a beaucoup de préjugés sur les jeunes. She has a lot of prejudices about young people.
Literally judged in advance: pré- + jugé.

The word, taken apart.
préjugé is built from pré- "before" and jugé "judged", after Latin praeiūdicium "a judgement made beforehand" (Lewis & Short), built on iūdex "judge".

Lesson 5 of 5.
le stéréotype — the stereotype.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the injustice, then the French for the prejudice.

You'll want to know: le stéréotype.
le stéréotype — "the stereotype".
Ce film est plein de stéréotypes. This film is full of stereotypes.
First a word from printing: a solid metal plate that copies a page.

The word, taken apart.
stéréotype is usually explained as a French formation from Greek stereós "solid" and týpos "mark, impression": first a printing plate, then a fixed idea that is copied without thought.
