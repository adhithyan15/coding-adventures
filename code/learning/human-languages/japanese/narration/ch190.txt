Japanese, chapter 190: Buildings and Shopping Streets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
たてもの (tatemono) — building.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hand over, then the Japanese for souvenir.

You'll want to know: たてもの.
たてもの — tatemono — "building".
あの たかい たてものは ぎんこうです。 — ano takai tatemono wa ginkō desu — "That tall building is a bank."

The word, taken apart.
たてもの (tatemono) is native Japanese, built from tate, the stem of tateru, "to build", and mono, "thing" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
しょうてんがい (shōtengai) — shopping street; shopping district.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for souvenir, then the Japanese for building.

You'll want to know: しょうてんがい.
しょうてんがい — shōtengai — "shopping street; shopping district".
えきの まえに しょうてんがいが あります。 — eki no mae ni shōtengai ga arimasu — "There is a shopping street in front of the station."

The word, taken apart.
しょうてんがい (shōtengai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "trade" (Middle Chinese syang), ten, "shop" (temH) and gai, "street" (kea) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
はいる (hairu) — to enter; to go in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for shopping street, then the Japanese for department store.

You'll want to know: はいる.
はいる — hairu — "to enter; to go in".
ともだちと ひゃっかてんに はいります。 — tomodachi to hyakkaten ni hairimasu — "I go into the department store with a friend."
Polite はいります, hairimasu; a godan verb. It ends in -iru, but it is godan.

The word, taken apart.
はいる (hairu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed; JMdict also gives a spelling that points to hau, "to crawl", and iru, "to enter" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
