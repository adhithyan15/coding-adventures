Hindi, chapter 72: When, and Then.
3 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
कब (kab) — when?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Run the k- words you now hold: क्या, कहाँ, क्यों, and kaun. One question a traveller asks more than any of those is still missing.

You'll want to know: कब.
कब — kab — when?
आप कब आ सकते हैं? — āp kab ā sakte haiṁ? — When can you come?
Like every Hindi question word, कब (kab) stays in the sentence's own slot in front of the verb; nothing is fronted and nothing is reordered. Two letters, both of which you can already write.
You have had कल since chapter four — tomorrow, and also yesterday, the same word for both. कब (kab) is how you ask which day somebody means, and it is the question the whole timetable half of a listening paper is made of.
कब (kab) is Sanskrit कदा, worn down. Its two partners are coming in the next lesson, and they are the same word with different first letters.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: āp kab ā sakte haiṁ?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same question with kyoṁ instead — āp kyoṁ ā sakte haiṁ?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the five k- words together — kyā, kaun, kahāṁ, kyoṁ, kab]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say saknā, then read चाहना]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Ask when the listener can come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does कब (kab) ask? (When.) Where does it stand? (In the sentence's own slot, in front of the verb.)

Lesson 2 of 3.
जब … तब … (jab … tab …) — when, then.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Retrieve the two items before this one by name: क्योंकि, and कब (kab). Then say the pair the deixis lesson gave you — यह and वह, this and that, the य- shape and the व- shape. That machine is about to run again.

Grammar Lens: three first letters, one word.
जब मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ, तब आप सुनते हैं. — jab maiṁ hindī boltā hūṁ, tab āp sunte haiṁ. — When I speak Hindi, then you listen.
Look at the three words side by side:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
कब (kab), when? — the question.
जब, when — the clause that sets the time.
तब, then — the clause that answers it.
One word, three first letters. क- asks, ज- points back at something, त- points at it again. It is exactly the machine the deixis lesson taught with यह and वह, and Hindi is unusually tidy about it: once you have seen the pattern once you can hear which job a word is doing from its first letter alone.
Hindi says both halves. English drops the then and leaves when I speak Hindi, I feel happiness; Hindi keeps तब, and it earns its place — it is what tells the listener the first clause has finished, which matters far more in a language where every clause ends on a verb.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kab, jab, tab]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jab maiṁ hindī boltā hūṁ, tab āp sunte haiṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which of the three asks a question — कब (kab)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Say the three time words in order — question, when-clause, then-clause — and then use the frame in one sentence.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What must follow a जब clause? (तब.) What do the three first letters do? (क- asks, ज- sets the time, त- answers it.)
Sources: Wiktionary: जब.


Lesson 3 of 3.

Warm-up.
