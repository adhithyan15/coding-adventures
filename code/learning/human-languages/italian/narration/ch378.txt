Italian, chapter 378: Che cosa voglio fare.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
lo scopo — the purpose, the aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to commit yourself, then the Italian for flexible.

You'll want to know: lo scopo.
lo scopo — "the purpose, the aim".
Lo scopo del viaggio è imparare la lingua. The aim of the trip is to learn the language.
Allo scopo di — in order to. A che scopo? — what for?

The word, taken apart.
scopo is a learned borrowing from Greek skopós "a watcher; a mark to aim at", through Latin scopos "a mark to aim at" (Lewis & Short), which EtymDB gives as the source of scopo (EtymDB); it is related to sképtesthai "to look carefully".

You'll want to know: l'intenzione.
l'intenzione — "the intention".
Non ho intenzione di cambiare lavoro. I have no intention of changing jobs.
Avere intenzione di + infinitive — to intend to.

The word, taken apart.
intenzione is a learned borrowing from Latin intentiō "a stretching, an effort, a purpose" (Lewis & Short), from intendere "to stretch towards", built on tendere "to stretch".

Lesson 3 of 6.
programmare — to plan; to program.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the purpose, then the Italian for the intention.

You'll want to know: programmare.
programmare — "to plan; to program".
Abbiamo programmato le vacanze con sei mesi di anticipo. We planned the holidays six months in advance.
Also to program a computer or a machine.

The word, taken apart.
programmare is built on programma "programme", a learned borrowing from Late Latin programma "a proclamation" (EtymDB), from Greek prógramma "a public notice", from prográphein "to write before", on gráphein "to write".

Lesson 4 of 6.
il proposito — the resolution, the intention.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the intention, then the Italian for to plan.

You'll want to know: il proposito.
il proposito — "the resolution, the intention".
Il mio proposito per l'anno nuovo è fare più sport. My New Year's resolution is to do more sport.
A proposito! — by the way! Di proposito — on purpose.

The word, taken apart.
proposito is a learned borrowing from Latin prōpositum "a plan, a purpose" (Lewis & Short), from prōpōnere "to set forth", built on pōnere "to put".

Lesson 5 of 6.
la meta — the goal; the destination.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to plan, then the Italian for the resolution.

You'll want to know: la meta.
la meta — "the goal; the destination".
La nostra meta è la cima della montagna. Our goal is the top of the mountain.
Stress on the first syllable: mèta. Not to be mixed up with metà "half".

The word, taken apart.
meta is a learned borrowing from Latin mēta "a cone-shaped turning post in a racecourse; a limit, a goal" (Lewis & Short).
