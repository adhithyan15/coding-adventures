# Change one familiar noun before a postposition

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-PLACE-LOK-PEOPLE-01, PA-POSTPOSITION-NAAL-COMITATIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Retrieve **ਲੋਕ**, then the familiar companion postposition **ਨਾਲ**.

## Grammar Lens: direct owner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-FORM-LOKAAN-OBLIQUE-192]; assesses=[PA-PLACE-LOK-PEOPLE-01, PA-POSTPOSITION-NAAL-COMITATIVE-01] -->

> **ਲੋਕਾਂ ਨਾਲ** — *lokā̃ nāl* — with people

The standalone form is **ਲੋਕ**. Before a postposition, this noun becomes
**ਲੋਕਾਂ**. Keep the step narrow: this lesson follows one noun and does not say
that every Punjabi noun changes in the same way.

## Grammar Lens: source boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-LOKAAN-OBLIQUE-192] -->

Johal explains the oblique case before a postposition and shows that this
class of masculine plural adds the nasal ending **-ਾਂ**. Here that
source rule is applied only to **ਲੋਕ**. Source: Ranbir Johal, *Speaking and
Writing Punjabi* (2018), pp. 76–78, [oblique nouns before
postpositions](https://openlibrary-repo.ecampusontario.ca/jspui/bitstream/123456789/492/2/2018Pun1100CoursePackage.pdf).
