# (neither … nor) — from cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C109-NEITHER-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C109-NEITHER-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Close the lessons before this one. Say *neither tea nor coffee*, then
*neither a teacher nor a doctor*.

## Grammar Lens: one joining, two endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C109-NEITHER-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C109-NEITHER-02, ML-CONCEPT-C70-UM-01] -->

| | |
|---|---|
| **ചായയും കാപ്പിയും ഇല്ല** | neither is there |
| **അധ്യാപകനും വൈദ്യനും അല്ല** | neither is so |

**The left half of those two sentences is built the same way.** Every item takes
the joining ending, and the pair is denied as a pair. What differs is the last
word, and picking it is the same decision you have been making since you owned
two negatives.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-ILLA-01, ML-LEX-C77-ALLA-01, ML-CONCEPT-C77-NEGATION-SPLIT-01] -->

**You could join things and you could deny things, and you had never been shown
that doing both at once needs nothing further.** English keeps a special pair of
words for this and teaches them as their own lesson. Malayalam had already given
you every piece; what was missing was the sentence that puts them together.
