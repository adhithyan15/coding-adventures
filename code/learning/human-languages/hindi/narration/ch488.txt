Hindi, chapter 488: At the Doctor's.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जाँच (jā̃c) — a check-up; a test.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for smallpox, then the Hindi for to fester.

You'll want to know: जाँच.
जाँच — jā̃c — "a check-up; a test".
डाक्टर ने मीरा की आँखों की जाँच की। ḍākṭar ne Mīrā kī ā̃khoṁ kī jā̃c kī. The doctor examined Mira's eyes.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
जाँच (jā̃c) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi jā̃c, "examination", under Sanskrit yācñā, "request, asking" (CDIAL 10448): asking questions of a thing. jā̃c karnā is to examine.

Lesson 2 of 5.
नुस्ख़ा (nuskhā) — a prescription.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to fester, then the Hindi for a check-up.

You'll want to know: नुस्ख़ा.
नुस्ख़ा — nuskhā — "a prescription".
डाक्टर ने नया नुस्ख़ा लिखा। ḍākṭar ne nayā nuskhā likhā. The doctor wrote out a new prescription.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
नुस्ख़ा (nuskhā) is usually traced to Arabic nuskha, "a copy, a written text", which reached Hindi through Persian; a recipe for medicine is a written text. It is also a household remedy.

Lesson 4 of 5.
दिखवाना (dikhvānā) — to have (something) looked at.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a prescription, then the Hindi for a nurse.

You'll want to know: दिखवाना.
दिखवाना — dikhvānā — "to have (something) looked at".
अरुण ने अपनी रिपोर्ट डाक्टर को दिखवाई। Aruṇ ne apnī riporṭ ḍākṭar ko dikhvāī. Arun had the doctor look at his report.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने आँख दिखवाई, usne ā̃kh dikhvāī.

The word, taken apart.
दिखवाना (dikhvānā) is the second causative of dekhnā, "to see", which CDIAL lists under dṛkṣati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 6507): dikhānā is "to show", and dikhvānā is "to have something seen to", usually by a doctor.

Lesson 5 of 5.
कराना (karānā) — to have something done.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a nurse, then the Hindi for to have (something) looked at.

You'll want to know: कराना.
कराना — karānā — "to have something done".
अरुण ने अस्पताल में ख़ून की जाँच कराई। Aruṇ ne aspatāl meṁ khūn kī jā̃c karāī. Arun had a blood test done at the hospital.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने जाँच कराई, usne jā̃c karāī.

The word, taken apart.
कराना (karānā) is the causative partner of karnā, "to do": CDIAL lists Hindi karānā, "to cause to be made", under Sanskrit karṓti, "does" (CDIAL 2814). Someone else does the work for you.
