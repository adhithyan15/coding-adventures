Lesson 1 of 5.
గీత (gīta) — a line.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a necklace, then the Telugu for a braid.

You'll want to know: గీత.
గీత — gīta — "a line".

The word, taken apart.
గీత (gīta) is native Dravidian. DEDR 1623 lists a nasalised gīta, "stroke, line", under gīṟu, "to scratch", with Tamil kīṟṟu and Kannada gīṭu, "a line, stroke". DEDR compares, with a query, Sanskrit kiraka, "scribe" (CDIAL 3170). CDIAL 4167 gīta, "singing", is a different word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
వృత్తం (vṛttaṁ) — a circle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a braid, then the Telugu for a line.

You'll want to know: వృత్తం.
వృత్తం — vṛttaṁ — "a circle".

The word, taken apart.
వృత్తం (vṛttaṁ) is Sanskrit vṛtta, "turned; rounded", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama, with the Telugu noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 12069). The sense "circle" is the one Telugu gives it.
