Tamil, chapter 499: Getting Married.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
வரதட்சணை (varataṭcaṇai) — a dowry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a prospective groom, then the Tamil for consent.

You'll want to know: வரதட்சணை.
வரதட்சணை — varataṭcaṇai — "a dowry".
அவர்கள் வரதட்சணை வாங்காமல் திருமணம் செய்தார்கள். avarkaḷ varataṭcaṇai vāṅkāmal tirumaṇam ceytārkaḷ. They married without taking a dowry.
வரதட்சணை (varataṭcaṇai) வாங்கு, to take a dowry.

The word, taken apart.
வரதட்சணை (varataṭcaṇai) is borrowed from Sanskrit, built from vara, "bridegroom" (CDIAL 11309), and dakṣiṇā, "a gift" (CDIAL 6120); Tamil writes kṣ as ட்ச.

Lesson 3 of 5.
மண (maṇa) — to marry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a dowry, then the Tamil for a blessing.

You'll want to know: மண.
மண — maṇa — "to marry".
அவர் ஒரு மருத்துவரை மணந்தார். avar oru maruttuvarai maṇantār. He married a doctor.
Past (I …): மணந்தேன், maṇantēṉ.
Formal and literary; the everyday phrase is திருமணம் செய். The one married takes -ஐ.

The word, taken apart.
மண (maṇa) is native Dravidian (DEDR 4667). DEDR keeps மண "to emit fragrance" as a separate entry (4668).

Lesson 4 of 5.
தம்பதி (tampati) — a married couple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a blessing, then the Tamil for to marry.

You'll want to know: தம்பதி.
தம்பதி — tampati — "a married couple".
அந்தத் தம்பதி ஐம்பது வருடமாக ஒன்றாக வாழ்கிறார்கள். antat tampati aimpatu varuṭamāka oṉṟāka vāḻkiṟārkaḷ. That couple has lived together for fifty years.
One word for the pair; the verb is plural.

The word, taken apart.
தம்பதி (tampati) is borrowed from Sanskrit dampati, "lord of the house", whose dual names the married pair (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
இல்லறம் (illaṟam) — married life.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to marry, then the Tamil for a married couple.

You'll want to know: இல்லறம்.
இல்லறம் — illaṟam — "married life".
அவர்களுடைய இல்லறம் மகிழ்ச்சியாக இருந்தது. avarkaḷuṭaiya illaṟam makiḻcciyāka iruntatu. Their married life was happy.
இல் "house" + அறம் "duty, virtue".

The word, taken apart.
இல்லறம் (illaṟam) is a Tamil compound of native இல் il, "house, home" (DEDR 494), and அறம், "virtue, duty".
