Russian, chapter 102: Boots, Skirt, Coat, Shoes, Socks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
сапоги (sapogí) — boots.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for gloves, then the Russian for a scarf.

You'll want to know: сапоги.
сапоги (sapogí) — "boots".

The word, taken apart.
сапоги (sapogí) is the plural of сапог, "a boot", Old Church Slavonic sapogŭ. Vasmer reports the usual link with сопеть, "to wheeze", through "a pipe" and so "a boot shaft"; Trubachev, in a note, takes it from early Bulgar or Turkic sapag, "a boot with a shaft", from a word for "stem".

Lesson 2 of 5.
юбка (yúbka) — a skirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a scarf, then the Russian for boots.

You'll want to know: юбка.
юбка (yúbka) — "a skirt".

The word, taken apart.
юбка (yúbka) — older юпка, from 1701; it came through Polish jupa, "a jacket", from Middle High German joppe, which goes back through Old French jupe to Italian giuppa and Arabic jubba, "an undergarment" (Vasmer).

You'll want to know: пальто.
пальто (paltó) — "a coat".

The word, taken apart.
пальто (paltó) comes from French paletot, "an overcoat"; Vasmer traces it further to Spanish palletoque, "a hooded cloak", and Latin palla, "a mantle".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a skirt, then the Russian for a coat.

You'll want to know: туфли.
туфли (túfli) — "shoes".

The word, taken apart.
туфли (túfli) — recorded from 1714; it comes from Low German tuffel, "a shoe", from older pantuffel, from Italian pantofola, "a slipper" (Vasmer). Its earlier source is disputed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
носки (noskí) — socks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a coat, then the Russian for shoes.

You'll want to know: носки.
носки (noskí) — "socks".

The word, taken apart.
носки (noskí) is the plural of носок, "a sock", built on нос, "nose" (Vasmer): the tip of the foot or shoe, then the short stocking that covers it.
