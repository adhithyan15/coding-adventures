Latin, chapter 539: Dice and board games.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ālea, āleae — dice-playing, a game of chance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to relax, then the Latin for relaxation.

You'll want to know: ālea, āleae.
ālea, āleae — "dice-playing, a game of chance".
Mīlitēs in castrīs āleā lūdunt. The soldiers play dice in the camp.
Feminine, first declension. Gambling at dice was forbidden except at festival time. Ālea iacta est — the die is cast.

The word, taken apart.
ālea is a word of uncertain origin; Lewis & Short reports a guess linking it with a word for bone, but nothing is settled. English aleatory, depending on chance, comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fritillus, fritillī — a dice-box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for relaxation, then the Latin for dice-playing.

You'll want to know: fritillus, fritillī.
fritillus, fritillī — "a dice-box".
Senex tālōs ē fritillō iacit. The old man throws the knucklebones from the dice-box.
Masculine, second declension. A little cup for shaking and throwing the dice.

The word, taken apart.
fritillus is a native Latin noun whose origin is not settled (Lewis & Short gives none). The flower fritillary is named after it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
latrunculus, latrunculī — a piece in a board game; a robber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dice-playing, then the Latin for a dice-box.

You'll want to know: latrunculus, latrunculī.
latrunculus, latrunculī — "a piece in a board game; a robber".
Duo amīcī latrunculīs lūdunt. Two friends play the board game of soldiers.
Masculine, second declension. First a little robber or hired soldier, then a piece in a war game played on a board.

The word, taken apart.
latrunculus is a diminutive of latrō "robber, hired soldier" (Lewis & Short): the pieces of the game were little soldiers.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lūsus, lūsūs — play, a game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dice-box, then the Latin for a piece in a board game.

You'll want to know: lūsus, lūsūs.
lūsus, lūsūs — "play, a game".
Lūsus puerōrum in viā clāmōsus est. The children's play in the street is noisy.
Masculine, fourth declension, from lūdō — to play.

The word, taken apart.
lūsus is the action noun of lūdō "to play" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English illusion comes from the family, through illūdō "to mock".

Lesson 5 of 5.
luctor, luctārī — to wrestle, struggle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a piece in a board game, then the Latin for play.

You'll want to know: luctor, luctārī.
luctor, luctārī — "to wrestle, struggle".
Iuvenēs in campō luctantur. The young men wrestle on the field.
Deponent: luctor, luctārī, luctātus sum. Also to struggle against something. English reluctant comes from reluctor.

The word, taken apart.
luctor is built on lucta "wrestling" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB links it with a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to bend, twist". English reluctant comes from the compound reluctor.
