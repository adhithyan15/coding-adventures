Mandarin Chinese, chapter 111: In Front and Behind.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
前边 (qiánbian) — in front; ahead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to paint pictures, then the Mandarin for to dance.

You'll want to know: 前边.
前边 — qiánbian — "in front; ahead".
前边是火车站 qiánbian shì huǒchēzhàn — The railway station is up ahead.
The new character, in pieces: 边 (biān) — lì, the foot of 男, inside the walking piece that runs down the left and along the foot of 这 and 进. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
前边 (qiánbian) is built from 前 'front' and 边 (biān) 'side', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzen and pen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'first, before' and the second 'an edge'.

What you've built.
One more word for asking where things are.

Lesson 2 of 6.
边 (biān) — the foot of 男, inside the walking piece.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the foot of 男, inside the walking piece and Guided Practice.

男 这 进.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — nán, zhè, jìn]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the foot of 男, inside the walking piece.
边 (biān).
The inside first, two strokes, the foot of 男: a stroke across that turns down and hooks, then a left-falling stroke through it.
The walking piece, three strokes, written last, as in 这 and 进: a dot, a small zigzag down the left, and a long flat sweep along the foot.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 边 (biān), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which comes first in 边 (biān), the inside or the walking piece? (The inside.) Where have you written that inside piece? (At the foot of 男.) Which characters you know end with the walking piece? (这 and 进.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 边 (biān), pinned stroke paths and medians.

guǎi — "to turn a corner".
zhèr néng guǎi ma — Can we turn here?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guǎi is inherited from Middle Chinese kweaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as a stick, a thing for the hands and feet, probably a crutch; how it came to mean turning a corner is not settled.
