Latin, chapter 436: Stern, Prow, Keel, Rowing bench, For cargo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
puppis, puppis — the stern (of a ship); a ship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a punt, then the Latin for a raft.

You'll want to know: puppis, puppis.
puppis, puppis — "the stern (of a ship); a ship".
Gubernātor in puppī stat et nāvem regit. The helmsman stands at the stern and steers the ship.
Third declension, feminine, with accusative puppim and ablative puppī. English poop deck comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of puppis is not settled: Lewis & Short call it doubtful and EtymDB gives no parent. English poop, the raised deck at the stern, comes from it through French poupe.

Lesson 2 of 5.
prōra, prōrae — the prow, the bow (of a ship).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a raft, then the Latin for the stern.

You'll want to know: prōra, prōrae.
prōra, prōrae — "the prow, the bow (of a ship)".
Nauta in prōrā stat et lītus spectat. A sailor stands in the prow and watches the shore.
First declension, feminine: prōra, prōrae f. Prōra et puppis, the whole of a thing, front to back. English prow comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
prōra was borrowed from Greek prōira 'prow' (EtymDB), built on the root of pro 'forward'. English prow comes from it through French proue.

Lesson 3 of 5.
carīna, carīnae — the keel (of a ship); a boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the stern, then the Latin for the prow.

You'll want to know: carīna, carīnae.
carīna, carīnae — "the keel (of a ship); a boat".
Nāvis nova carīnam longam et validam habet. The new ship has a long, strong keel.
First declension, feminine: carīna, carīnae f. First a nutshell, then the keel. English careen 'to tip a ship on its side' comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
carīna is inherited and is usually traced to PIE ḱerh₂- (EtymDB); Lewis & Short compare Greek karyon 'nut', since its first sense is a nutshell. English careen comes from it through French carène.

Lesson 4 of 5.
trānstrum, trānstrī — a rowing bench, a thwart; a crossbeam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the prow, then the Latin for the keel.

You'll want to know: trānstrum, trānstrī.
trānstrum, trānstrī — "a rowing bench, a thwart; a crossbeam".
Rēmigēs in trānstrīs sedent. The rowers sit on the benches.
Second declension, neuter: trānstrum, trānstrī n. The bench runs across the ship. English transom is usually traced to it.

The word, taken apart.
trānstrum is built on trāns 'across' with the suffix -trum (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a beam laid across a ship. English transom is usually traced to it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
onerārius, onerāria, onerārium — for cargo, for carrying loads.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the keel, then the Latin for a rowing bench.

You'll want to know: onerārius, onerāria, onerārium.
onerārius, onerāria, onerārium — "for cargo, for carrying loads".
Nāvēs onerāriae frūmentum ad urbem vehunt. Cargo ships carry grain to the city.
An adjective of three endings. Nāvis onerāria, a merchant ship, or simply onerāria; iūmentum onerārium, a pack animal.

The word, taken apart.
onerārius is built on onus, oneris 'a load' with the suffix -ārius (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English onerary 'fit for loads' is a rare borrowing of it.
