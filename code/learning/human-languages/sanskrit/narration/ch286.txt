Sanskrit, chapter 286: A Theft in the Village.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चौर्यम् (cauryam) — a theft.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for radio, then the Sanskrit for the internet.

You'll want to know: चौर्यम्.
चौर्यम् — cauryam — "a theft".
ह्यः रात्रौ ग्रामे चौर्यं घटितम्। hyaḥ rātrau grāme cauryaṃ ghaṭitam. A theft took place in the village last night.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
चौर्यम् (cauryam) is built on caura, "a thief", with the ending -ya that makes a noun of the deed; MW gives caurya as the act of a thief, theft (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
अपहरति (apaharati) — makes off with; snatches away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a theft, then the Sanskrit for a night watchman.

You'll want to know: अपहरति.
अपहरति — apaharati — "makes off with; snatches away".
चौरः धनम् अपहरति। cauraḥ dhanam apaharati. The thief makes off with the money.
Past (he …): सः धनम् अपहृतवान्, saḥ dhanam apahṛtavān.

The word, taken apart.
अपहरति (apaharati) is built from the prefix apa, "away", and the root √hṛ, "to take"; MW gives apa-hṛ as to snatch away or plunder (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
साक्षी (sākṣī) — a witness.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a night watchman, then the Sanskrit for makes off with.

You'll want to know: साक्षी.
साक्षी — sākṣī — "a witness".
साक्षी चौरं न दृष्टवान्। sākṣī cauraṃ na dṛṣṭavān. The witness did not see the thief.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
साक्षी (sākṣī) is the nominative of sākṣin, which MW gives as one who sees with his own eyes, an eye-witness, and a witness in law; it sits beside MW's sākṣāt, "with one's own eyes", but MW gives no derivation in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
पलायते (palāyate) — runs away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for makes off with, then the Sanskrit for a witness.

You'll want to know: पलायते.
पलायते — palāyate — "runs away".
चौरः शीघ्रं पलायते। cauraḥ śīghraṃ palāyate. The thief runs away fast.
Past (he …): सः शीघ्रं पलायितवान्, saḥ śīghraṃ palāyitavān.
Future: पलायिष्यते, palāyiṣyate.

The word, taken apart.
पलायते (palāyate) is built on palā, a form of parā, "away", and the root √ay, "to go": to go away, flee (MW).
