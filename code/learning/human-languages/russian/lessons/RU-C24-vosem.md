# восемь — eight, and the seven inside it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-SEM-01, RU-ETYMON-SEM-SEPTM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **семь**, and which sound it lost that Latin kept.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-VOSEM-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-SEM-01] -->

> **восемь** — *vósem* — **eight**

Say **семь** and **восемь** one after the other. Seven is audible at the end of
eight, and the unstressed **о** at the front reduces towards *a* — the reduction
this book named long ago.

## The word, taken apart — a neighbour's ending
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-VOSEM-OKTOW]; assesses=[RU-LEX-VOSEM-01, RU-LEX-SEM-01] -->

**восемь** continues Indo-European \**oḱtṓw*, "eight" — Latin **octō**, Greek
**oktṓ**, English **eight**.

There is no *s* and no *m* anywhere in that history, so neither is inherited.
They came from **семь**, standing next to it. Seven and eight are said together
more often than almost any other pair of words in any language, and the pair
pulls each other into shape: Persian's **hasht** borrowed its *h*- from
**haft** for exactly the same reason, in a different family, with no contact
between them.
