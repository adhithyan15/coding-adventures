Mandarin Chinese, chapter 521: Keeping House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shuā — to scrub; to brush clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for central heating, then the Mandarin for to use little electricity.

You'll want to know: shuā.
shuā — "to scrub; to brush clean".
wǒ shuā le yí ge xiǎoshí de guō — I spent an hour scrubbing the pot.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shuā is inherited from Middle Chinese srwaet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to wipe clean'; the dictionary also lists srjwet, glossed 'to sweep'. The level tone of shuā cannot be read off srwaet: words that once ended in t, as this one did, are spread over all four Mandarin tones.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: féng.
féng — "to sew; to stitch".
māma bāng wǒ féng kùzi féng le bàntiān — Mum sewed up my trousers for me, and it took her ages.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
féng is inherited from Middle Chinese bjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to stitch'; its departing-tone reading, bjowngH, is glossed 'a seam'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
bùzhì — to lay out a room; to decorate a room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to scrub clean, then the Mandarin for to sew by hand.

You'll want to know: bùzhì.
bùzhì — "to lay out a room; to decorate a room".
xīn fángzi wǒmen bùzhì le liǎng tiān — We spent two days arranging the new flat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bùzhì is built from bù 'to spread' and zhì 'to place', both inherited from Middle Chinese, puH and triH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to spread everywhere' and the second 'to set in place'.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sew by hand, then the Mandarin for to lay out a room.

zhěngqí — "neat; orderly".
tā de fángjiān bǐ wǒ de zhěngqí — His room is tidier than mine.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhěngqí is built from zhěng and qí, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyengX and dzej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses each with the other: 'upright, even'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
wèishēng — hygiene; cleanliness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lay out a room, then the Mandarin for neat and orderly.

You'll want to know: wèishēng.
wèishēng — "hygiene; cleanliness".
chúfáng yídìng yào zhùyì wèishēng — A kitchen has to be kept really clean.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wèishēng is built from wèi 'to guard' and 生 'life', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjwejH and srjaeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to protect' and the second 'to grow'. Guarding life as a word for hygiene is modern, and its path is not settled.
