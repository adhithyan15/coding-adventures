Arabic, chapter 457: The sales.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
تخفيض (takhfīḍ) — a price cut; a reduction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to fit, then the Arabic for to be too tight.

You'll want to know: تخفيض.
تخفيض — takhfīḍ — "a price cut; a reduction".
اشتريت المعطف بتخفيض كبير. ishtaraytu al-miʿṭaf bi-takhfīḍ kabīr. I bought the coat with a big price cut.
The plural takhfīḍāt is the sales.

The word, taken apart.
تخفيض (takhfīḍ) is built on the native Arabic root kh-f-ḍ, "to be low; to lower" (Lane). It is the verbal noun of Form II.

Lesson 2 of 5.
قسيمة (qasīma) — a voucher; a coupon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be too tight, then the Arabic for a price cut.

You'll want to know: قسيمة.
قسيمة — qasīma — "a voucher; a coupon".
استعملت أمي قسيمة في المتجر. istaʿmalat ummī qasīma fī al-matjar. My mother used a voucher in the shop.

The word, taken apart.
قسيمة (qasīma) is built on the native Arabic root q-s-m, "to divide" (Lane). It shares its root with qism, "a portion, a share" (Lane); the sense of a voucher or a coupon is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
أنفق (anfaqa) — to spend money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to lower, then the Arabic for to get cheaper.

You'll want to know: أنفق.
أنفق — anfaqa — "to spend money".
أنفقنا كل مالنا في السوق. anfaqnā kulla mālinā fī as-sūq. We spent all our money at the market.
Present: ينفق, yunfiq.

The word, taken apart.
أنفق (anfaqa) is built on the native Arabic root n-f-q, "to pass away, to run out" (Lane). It is Form IV, "to make money run out"; Lane gives nafaq, "a tunnel", on the same root.
