# Review — the accent is doing the work

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PORQUE-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "I get up because I run." (*Me levanto porque corro*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PORQUE-THREE-05, ES-LEX-PORQUE-03] -->

Three spellings that a listener cannot tell apart, and a reader always can.

| written | job |
|---|---|
| *porque* | **because** — gives the reason |
| *por qué* | **why** — asks for it |
| *el porqué* | **the reason** — a thing you can own |

[PAUSE 2s] *¿**Por qué** te levantas?* — *Me levanto **porque** corro.* — *No
entiendo **el porqué**.*

Ask, answer, and then name the answer.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PORQUE-THREE-05] -->

The accent on *qué* is not there to look nice, and it is not there at random. It
is the same mark that sits on every question word: *qué*, *quién*, *dónde*,
*cuándo*, *cómo*. Spanish uses one mark, consistently, for one job — **this word
is the one doing the asking**.

So *por qué* is two words because it is literally *for what*, with the accent
marking *what* as the question. And *porque*, fused into one word with no accent,
has nothing left to ask with.

[PAUSE 2s] *El porqué* is the odd one, and it is odd in a way that makes sense:
put *el* in front of anything in Spanish and it becomes a thing. It keeps the
accent because the question word is still inside it — the reason is, after all,
the answer to a *why*.
