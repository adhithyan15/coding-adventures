# क्रीणामि (krīṇāmi) — I buy

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C248-THG248-05, SA-PRON-C262-ASMAN-01, SA-PATTERN-C265-POLITE-ORDER-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say “a fee,” then “us” as an object, then make one complete polite
request for a familiar item. (*Śulkam; asmān; the frame ending in dadātu.*)

## Grammar Lens: the buyer speaks

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-VERB-C266-KRINAMI-01]; assesses=[SA-LEX-AHAM, SA-LEX-C24-ASK-03, SA-LEX-C74-MARKET-01] -->

You know **क्रीणाति** (*krīṇāti*), “he or she buys.” When the buyer speaks for
themselves, keep the whole new form together:

> **अहं फलं क्रीणामि।**
>
> *Ahaṃ phalaṃ krīṇāmi.*
>
> I buy fruit.

Here **क्रीणामि** is the **first-person singular present**. The final **-मि**
(*-mi*) points to the speaker, as it does in forms you already know. Both
checked-in A1 mocks use this complete form. This is **not a complete present
paradigm**: the other persons, numbers, and class-nine forms wait for later.
