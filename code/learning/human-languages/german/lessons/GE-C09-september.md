# der September — "September"

## You'll want to know: der September
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-SEPTEMBER-01]; assesses=[GE-CULTURE-ROMAN-YEAR-BEGAN-IN-MARCH-01, GE-LEX-MAERZ-01] -->

> **der September** — "September."

Latin *september*, from ***septem*** — **seven**. Count from *März*, where the
Roman year started, and September lands seventh.

The year was later moved to start in January. The name was not moved with it, so
a word meaning "seventh" now sits in the ninth slot, in German exactly as in
English, and has done for two thousand years.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SEPTEMBER-01] -->

- `vowel-e-german` — three clear short *e*s: *sep-TEM-ber*.
- `r-final` — closing on the soft *r*.

The stress is on the middle syllable, as in English.
