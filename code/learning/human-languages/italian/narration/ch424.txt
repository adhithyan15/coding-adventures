Italian, chapter 424: Arrivare in porto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la boa — the buoy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bow, then the Italian for the stern.

You'll want to know: la boa.
la boa — "the buoy".
Non nuotare oltre le boe. Do not swim beyond the buoys.
On beaches the buoys mark the safe swimming area.

The word, taken apart.
boa is usually traced to a Germanic word for a sign or marker, through Old French, but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
approdare — to come ashore, to land (a boat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the stern, then the Italian for the buoy.

You'll want to know: approdare.
approdare — "to come ashore, to land (a boat)".
Siamo approdati su una piccola isola. We came ashore on a small island.
Figuratively: approdare a un'idea — to arrive at an idea.

The word, taken apart.
approdare is built from a- and proda 'shore, edge', which is usually traced to Latin prōra 'prow' (Lewis & Short), an inherited word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il salvagente — the lifebelt, the life jacket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to come ashore, then the Italian for to moor.

You'll want to know: il salvagente.
il salvagente — "the lifebelt, the life jacket".
Sulla nave ogni posto ha un salvagente. On the ship every seat has a life jacket.
Built from salva and gente: it saves people. Plural unchanged.

The word, taken apart.
salvagente is built from salvare 'to save', inherited from Late Latin salvāre (EtymDB), and gente 'people', inherited from Latin gēns.

You'll want to know: la nausea.
la nausea — "the nausea, the feeling sick".
Con le onde ho avuto la nausea. With the waves I felt sick.
Avere la nausea; il mal di mare is seasickness.

The word, taken apart.
nausea is a learned borrowing from Latin nausea, from Greek nausía 'seasickness', built on naûs 'ship' (EtymDB).
