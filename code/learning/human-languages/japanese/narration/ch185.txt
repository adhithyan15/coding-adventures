Japanese, chapter 185: Sale, Discount and Sold Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
わりびき (waribiki) — discount; reduction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for all of it, then the Japanese for exactly.

You'll want to know: わりびき.
わりびき — waribiki — "discount; reduction".
この くつは わりびきです。 — kono kutsu wa waribiki desu — "These shoes are discounted."

The word, taken apart.
わりびき (waribiki) is native Japanese, built from wari, the stem of waru, "to divide", and biki, from hiku, "to pull, to take off" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
うりきれ (urikire) — sold out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for discount, then the Japanese for bargain sale.

You'll want to know: うりきれ.
うりきれ — urikire — "sold out".
やすい たまごは もう うりきれです。 — yasui tamago wa mō urikire desu — "The cheap eggs are already sold out."

The word, taken apart.
うりきれ (urikire) is native Japanese, built from uri, "selling", and kire, "running out", from kireru, a verb on the same stem as kiru, "to cut" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かかる (kakaru) — to cost; to take (time), to hang.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for bargain sale, then the Japanese for sold out.

You'll want to know: かかる.
かかる — kakaru — "to cost; to take (time), to hang".
くるまは おかねが かかります。 — kuruma wa okane ga kakarimasu — "A car costs money."
Polite かかります, kakarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
かかる (kakaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
