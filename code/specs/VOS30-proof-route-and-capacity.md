## 0. How to read this document

VOS00 promised a third proof stage: symbolic execution of each compiled kernel
entry, mitered against the model and DRAT-checked, labelled `proved` (bar
X3). It also admitted that "solver capacity at kernel-entry size is unmeasured"
(section 10.2). This document does three things.

1. It specifies the **route**: what an X3 proof is made of, as a set of
   obligations of known shapes, and how a long program is cut into pieces a SAT
   solver can swallow (sections 2 to 7).
2. It records the **capacity estimate** that decides whether the route is worth
   building (section 8), and **specifies the probe** that replaces the estimate
   with committed, reproducible numbers (section 9). The probe is the work of
   VOS-028b.
3. It states what the executor row, VOS-029, must deliver so that the route is
   designed with cut points from its first line (section 10).

The most important sentence of the document is in section 2.3: **X3 is
compositional, never one monolithic miter.** VOS00 section 10.1 drew the proof
as a single arrow from trap vector to `mret`. The calibration of section 8 shows
that a single miter over a realistic entry sits past the cliff of what the
repository's solver and proof checker can do. The route is therefore redrawn,
and VOS00 is corrected in the same PR.

Words used strictly:

* **Obligation:** one propositional formula whose UNSAT is one claim, with its
  own DRAT certificate (section 6).
* **Miter:** the formula "the two sides differ"; UNSAT means they cannot.
* **Cut point:** a place in a program where the proof stops tracking how the
  machine got there and starts again from a stated relation (section 4.2).
* **Collapsed:** a miter the AIG reduced to a trivial formula, so UNSAT proves
  nothing about the code (section 4.5).
* **Green:** within the thresholds of section 8.3.

Everything here is about the *machine* proof. X1 and X2 (host-level, by-value)
are unchanged and are VOS01's business.

## 1. Scope and reading guide

### 1.1 What an X3 claim is

VOS00 section 9.2 defines X3 as "proved on the image, per instance, with a
certificate". Precisely, for one kernel entry `e` (one trap vector path, one
component step reachable from the spine) in one compiled image `I` built for
one board `B` at one configuration `C` (N, P, table sizes):

> For every machine state satisfying the entry precondition, running the
> instructions of `e` in `I` under the RV32I model of `riscv-simulator` ends
> in a state `m'` with `alpha(m')` equal to the step the Warrant model
> prescribes, within the proven step bound, touching nothing outside the stated
> footprint.

Each phrase of that sentence is an obligation kind (section 3). The claim
carries every label of VOS00 section 9.3: `proved` or `bounded-proof(k=K)`,
with the instance constants and the image and board digests printed (section 5).

### 1.2 What it does not claim

VOS00 section 1.3 and section 10.3 stand. In addition:

* It proves nothing about silicon, about `rustc` (the compiler is trusted in
  the host-rustc stack, section 5.3), about trap entry and CSR side effects
  until the executor models them, or about any entry the probe did not size.
* The probe of section 9 bounds *solver capacity per obligation shape*. A green
  row says a shape of that size can be solved and certified. It does not say any
  particular kernel entry is verifiable.
* A hand-written instruction sequence is not compiler output (section 8.4).

### 1.3 Why a SAT route at all

By-value components are checked exhaustively on the host (X1). That is
enumeration over a reduced instance, 400,000 cases at most per claim (VOS01
section 5.6). It cannot reach the shipped instance (N = 64 or 256), and it checks
the Rust source as compiled for the host, not the RV32 image that boots. The
solver route answers both: a miter over *all* 2^64 masks is one formula whose
size is linear in the width, and it runs over the actual image bytes. The cost
is that it is only as good as the solver's capacity, which is the subject of
this document.

## 2. The route

### 2.1 Three stages, restated

```
   X1   by-value enumeration on the host, reduced instance, exhaustive
          what it checks: the Rust step against the Warrant model, all cases
          where it stops: the 400,000-case and 64 MiB budgets (VOS01 5.6)

   X2   X1 plus shipped-size evidence (property, fuzz) and the image-level
        refinement monitor (VOS00 10.1): boot the image, abstract at every
        trap through alpha, check against the kernel model
          label: randomized (samples traces); resource claims: example

   X3   proof on the compiled image: symbolic RV32I execution of each entry,
        cut into obligations, each bit-blasted and DRAT-checked
          label: proved, or bounded-proof(k=K)
```

X1 and X2 exist now (VOS00 section 9.2). X3 needs the executor of VOS-029.

### 2.2 Why not one big miter

A symbolic executor turns code into formulas. If you run an entry of a few
hundred instructions symbolically and also evaluate the model on the same
inputs, you get two big circuits and ask "can their outputs differ?". That is
the natural design, and it fails for a reason worth understanding.

A SAT solver does not "see" that two circuits compute the same thing; it
searches. If the two circuits are *structurally identical*, the AIG builder
hashes identical sub-expressions to the same node (VER10 section 2.2) and the
miter collapses to nothing: the solver is asked a question whose answer is
visible by inspection. If they are *structurally different* (the compiler
computed the same value another way: `x*5` as `(x<<2)+x`, a sum re-associated,
a lookup unrolled as a comparison chain), the solver must discover the
equivalence by search. For multiplication-like and addition-chain structure that
search is exponential in the number of independent pieces: about 8x per extra
32-bit term in a re-associated sum (section 8.2). A real compiled kernel is
structurally different from its model everywhere. The conclusion of the
calibration is that instruction count is not the cost; **divergence is**.

Two things make memory worse. First, the symbolic executor of `warrant-ir`
(VER19 section 3) models memory by concrete offsets only; a symbolic index into
a table becomes an if-then-else chain over every word. Second, each further
symbolic access multiplies what the solver must disentangle: the circuit grows
linearly but solving cost grows exponentially in the number of chained accesses
(section 8.2, tables D). So the route avoids symbolic offsets where it can
(concrete frame addresses, index case-splitting, section 4.3 and 4.4).

### 2.3 The route is compositional

> **Decision.** X3 is a set of small obligations tied together by a
> composition argument and a manifest. A proof is never one miter over a whole
> entry, unless the probe shows an entry is small enough to be one obligation
> with a collapse-free non-trivial certificate.

The ingredients, each specified below:

1. **Per-path splitting.** The executor enumerates paths by *concrete PC*:
   a branch whose condition is symbolic forks, and each path becomes its own
   obligation set (section 4.1).
2. **Cut points** with a **composition checker**: along a path, the proof is
   chopped into short segments; each segment is a separate miter that *assumes*
   the relation established at the previous cut and *proves* the relation at the
   next (section 4.2).
3. **Index case-splitting** where a table index is symbolic (section 4.3).
4. **Concrete frame addresses** for the stack and fixed structures
   (section 4.4).
5. **Cone-of-influence reduction**: only the state a segment reads is variable
   (section 4.4).
6. **Certified, content-addressed certificates and a composition manifest**
   (section 6).

Whether the compositional route is affordable is the open question the probe
answers (B3 and D3 in section 9). Section 8.4 states the current decision
state: the monolithic form already fails; the compositional form is pending.

### 2.4 What changes in VOS00

VOS00 section 10.1 drew X3 as "symbolic RV32 execution of each compiled kernel
entry, trap vector to mret, mitered against the bit-blasted model". Corrected in
the same PR: the proof is compositional (this section), and section 10.2's
sentence that fixed-size arrays are "within reach of inlining plus
bit-blasting" is true only for concrete or case-split indices; with symbolic
indices the calibrated limit is about two chained accesses into an eight-word
table (section 8.2). VOS00 gains a pointer to this document.

## 3. Obligation kinds

Each obligation has a formula *shape*, because the shape is what the probe
measures and what a reader needs to predict cost. Notation: `m` is machine
state (registers, `pc`, memory image), `s` the model state, `alpha` the
abstraction of VOS01 section 1.5 (ghost code in the host build; here a
bit-vector function over machine state), `inv` the invariant, `pre` the entry
precondition.

### 3.1 Path feasibility and coverage

Enumeration by concrete PC yields paths `p_1 .. p_k`, each with a path
condition `PC_i(m0)` over the initial machine state `m0`. Two facts are needed,
and the second is the one people forget:

* **Per-path soundness** (sections 3.2 to 3.4) is proved *under* `PC_i`.
* **Coverage**: `pre(m0) => PC_1 \/ ... \/ PC_k`, and the conditions are pairwise
  disjoint. Miter: `pre(m0) /\ not(OR PC_i)` is UNSAT, and for each pair
  `PC_i /\ PC_j` is UNSAT.

Why it matters: if the executor silently dropped a path (an unsupported
instruction, an exhausted fork budget), every per-path proof could pass and the
entry still be unproved. Coverage turns "the executor saw everything" from an
assumption into an obligation. A path that is infeasible (its `PC_i` is UNSAT)
is recorded as such with its certificate, never dropped.

Shape: a disjunction of path conditions, each a conjunction of branch tests over
at most a few symbolic words. Cheap in the calibration (clz-like, section 8.2
tables A) but it grows with the path count, so it is sized by the probe.

### 3.2 Per-path functional refinement

For each path `p_i` ending at `mret` (or the return instruction):

```
  pre(m0) /\ PC_i(m0) /\ m' = exec_i(m0) /\ s' = model_step(alpha(m0), in)
        /\ not( alpha(m') = s'  /\  out(m') = model_out )          is UNSAT
```

`exec_i` is the straight-line machine semantics along the path, built by the
executor. This is the heart of X3 and the obligation that explodes when
monolithic (section 2.2). With cut points it is replaced by the chained
obligations of section 4.2.

### 3.2a Model decomposition (the model side is obligated too)

Cut points split the *model* step as well: the chain of section 4.2 uses
`step_seg_1 .. step_seg_n` (model fragments) between model points `s_0 .. s_n`.
If those fragments did not compose to the real model step, every segment could
be proved against a fragment that is not the Warrant model and the chain would
prove a different statement. So the decomposition is itself an obligation:

```
  for the path's input state s_0 and input in:
      step_seg_n( ... step_seg_2( step_seg_1(s_0, in) ) ... )  =  model_step(s_0, in)
  (and the same for the output), under PC_i
```

This is a miter between the fragment composition and the whole model step; it is
UNSAT with its own DRAT certificate, listed in the manifest as kind
`model-decomposition`. When the fragments are literally the model's own
sub-expressions (the usual case) the two sides are the same AIG node and the
miter collapses legitimately; the obligation is then recorded with
`collapsed_reason = "identical-structure"` (section 4.5) and the checker confirms
the identity structurally (section 6.2). It is never omitted: an omitted
decomposition obligation is a rejected manifest.

### 3.3 Closure of `inv`

The contract's invariant must hold after the step: `inv(alpha(m0)) /\ pre /\
PC_i` implies `inv(alpha(m'))`. This is the machine twin of the X1 closure
conjunct (VOS01 section 2.2). It is stated separately from refinement so a
failure points at the invariant, not the arithmetic. When `alpha(m')` equals the
model's `s'` and the model's own closure is a host-level lemma, this obligation
is a corollary, but it is cheap and is kept explicit: `inv` evaluated on
`alpha(m')`, which catches an `alpha` that is wrong in a way refinement
alone does not see.

### 3.4 Frame

Everything the entry is not allowed to change must be unchanged. For each
machine location outside the declared footprint (VOS01 section 2.7), the final
value equals the initial value. Formula shape: a conjunction of equalities over
locations, which is usually trivial because untouched memory is the same AIG
node (the structural collapse of section 4.5, *legitimate* here). The obligation
that is not trivial is the converse: that every store's *address* lies inside
the footprint. A store through a computed address is an address obligation:
`addr(store) in footprint` under `PC_i`. With a symbolic address that is a
range check over a bit-vector sum; with a concrete address it is constant-folded
and free (section 4.4).

Registers are part of the frame: callee-saved registers and `sp` must be
restored; the contract says which registers are outputs.

Memory-access conditions are explicit obligations, not by-products of the miter
(an RV32 store can overwrite the code, and a misaligned or out-of-range access is
a fault the model does not describe):

* **Store address outside code.** Every store's address range is disjoint from
  the image's executable range (and from every read-only region): the machine
  does not rewrite the instructions the proof read. Without it, the proof of a
  fixed image says nothing about a run that modifies itself.
* **Alignment.** Every `lw` and `sw` address satisfies the alignment the model
  of `riscv-simulator` requires (or the model's behaviour on a misaligned access
  is the stated fault path).
* **Fault conditions.** Every access address lies in a mapped region of the
  board; a path that reaches an access fault is a declared fault path with its
  own path condition, never silently dropped.

Each is a per-access obligation under `PC_i` with kind `access` in the manifest.

### 3.5 Step bound

The maximum number of RV32 instructions over all paths, as a number `K`. Two
outcomes, matching VOS00 section 9.3:

* every path is loop-free (concrete-PC enumeration terminates without unrolling):
  the bound is the maximum path length and the label is `proved`;
* a loop was unrolled to a limit: the label is `bounded-proof(k=K)` and the
  proof says so (VER20 section 4.2 is the model).

The residual of a `bounded-proof(k=K)` is part of the claim and of the label:
**executions that run longer than K steps are not covered**. The label prints
`bounded-proof(k=K; executions longer than K unproved)` and the manifest records
the unwinding limit and the loop or back-edge that was cut. A bound that is only
`bounded-proof` cannot discharge a "terminates" or "step bound" claim of the
contract; it can discharge only a claim stated at k.

The bound is *derived from the paths*, not a separate formula, but coverage
(section 3.1) is what makes it a bound: it is a bound over *all* paths only
because the paths are all of them. The bound is then compared with the
contract's resource claim (VOS01 section 2.5).

### 3.6 Two-safety (constant-time and timing)

Where the contract carries a timing or flow label (VOS01 section 2.6), the
obligation is self-composition, as VER20 section 2: two copies of the machine,
public inputs shared, secret inputs fresh, and the observable trace (the `pc`
sequence, every load and store address and size, and the retired-instruction
count) must be equal. This is exactly the event set of VER13 section 4.3 item 4.
Miter form (VER20 section 2.3):

```
  pre(m1) /\ pre(m2) /\ public(m1) = public(m2) /\ not( trace(m1) ~ trace(m2) )
```

with `prove_with_custom_inputs` in `warrant-ir/src/ct_proof.rs:222` as the
existing pattern. Self-composition doubles the circuit, so it is probed
separately (section 9, experiments C1 and C2). It also needs a *negative*
control that must be SAT: a secret-indexed load (a table lookup on a secret).

**Relational cut points are deferred.** Cutting a *pair* of runs needs relational
hypotheses (the two copies' cut states related by public equality plus the
secret-independence of the trace so far) and a composition rule for relations,
which this document does not specify. Until a later revision does, two-safety
obligations are **monolithic per path** (no cuts) and are sized by C1 and C2;
an entry whose self-composition obligation is not green is reported red, not
cut. VOS-029 does not include two-safety on a real entry (section 10).

On a concrete-PC executor the pc trace of a path is constant, so the `pc` part
of the observation is free; the address and count parts are where the work is.

### 3.7 Image checks

Two properties of the *whole image* rather than one entry. They are the
companions deferred by VOS03 (the lexical half of the shape checker, backlog
VOS-D002) and VOS02's appendix:

* **No panic path.** No reachable instruction sequence in a class K entry jumps
  to the panic handler. Checked on the compiled image by reachability over the
  decoded control-flow graph from each entry; an edge to the handler's address
  range is a finding. **The check fails closed**: an unresolved indirect jump
  (`jalr` whose target is not a proved constant or a declared table), a word
  that fails to decode, data inside an executable range, or a branch target
  outside the image makes the check *fail*, never be skipped.
* **Stack bound.** A static maximum stack depth per entry, from the `sp`
  adjustments **read from the disassembled image** (not from recorded frame
  sizes, which a compiler or a hand edit can falsify) summed along the acyclic
  call graph SH5 guarantees. An `sp` write the analysis cannot interpret (a
  computed `sp`) fails closed.

These are graph checks, not SAT obligations, but they are certificates of the
same family: each is recorded in the manifest with its image digest and tool
version. They belong to VOS-026 and VOS-029 (the image exists then); this spec
only fixes that they are part of the X3 label (a theorem is not X3 without
them).

## 4. Decomposition rules

### 4.1 Per-path splitting

Branches in kernel entries are mostly forward and data-dependent. A symbolic
executor with *concrete PC* forks at each conditional branch whose condition
involves symbolic state and keeps the PC concrete along each fork. Why concrete
PC and not a symbolic PC with a big ite over the program counter: a symbolic PC
turns instruction fetch into a table lookup indexed by a symbolic value (the
worst case of section 2.2), and loses the natural unit of proof, the path.

Path explosion is bounded by the entry's structure (2^b paths for b independent
branches). The probe does not measure path enumeration cost (it uses a concrete
PC, forward-branch stepper); VOS-029 must report path counts per entry and fail
closed above a declared limit (section 10).

### 4.2 Cut points, and the composition argument

A *cut point* is a program location `c` (after instruction `k`) together with a
**cut relation** `R_c(m, s)` between machine state and model state. Most cuts are
the plainest relation: `s = alpha_c(m)` for the model's intermediate state at
that point, together with `pc = c` and the registers that are live.

Replace the single path obligation of section 3.2 with a chain over cuts
`c_0 < c_1 < ... < c_n`:

```
  base     :  pre(m0)                                   =>  R_{c_0}(m0, s0)
  segment j:  R_{c_{j-1}}(m, s)  /\  m' = exec_{seg j}(m)
              /\ s' = step_{seg j}(s)                   =>  R_{c_j}(m', s')
  final    :  R_{c_n}(m, s)                             =>  alpha(m) = s_final
                                                          (and sections 3.3, 3.4)
  model    :  step_seg_n o ... o step_seg_1  =  model_step   (section 3.2a)

  Every R_c contains, as conjuncts: pc = c; the live registers related to the
  model state; and  FRAME(m, m0): every location outside the footprint equals
  its value at entry.
```

Each line is its own miter: the segment's *input* is a set of **fresh symbolic
variables** (the cut state is cut loose from how it was reached), constrained
by the hypothesis relation `R_{c_{j-1}}`; the formula's negated conclusion is
`not R_{c_j}`. The segment is a handful of instructions, so structure
divergence cannot accumulate: this is why 25 dependent steps went from 6.5 s to
31 ms in the calibration (section 8.2).

**What a cut buys.** The solver never has to prove that the two sides stay equal
*across* many instructions; it proves each hop, and the equalities carried
between hops are explicit.

**What the composition argument must establish.** Splitting is only sound if the
pieces really add up. A proof of the chain is an induction over `j`, and each of
the following has to be true *by construction*, not by luck, and has to be checked
by the composition checker (section 6.2):

1. **Hypothesis equals conclusion, as instances of one relation.** The checker
   re-derives the cut relation `R_j` **once** from the regenerated path, and
   checks that the predecessor's conclusion and the successor's hypothesis are
   **both instances of it** under a **version-indexed mapping** `(location, bit,
   state-version index)`: version `j` names the state at cut `j`, so the
   predecessor's output variables and the successor's input variables are the
   same variables by construction of the mapping, and `m0` (for FRAME) is version
   0 in every segment. Not a digest the manifest asserts, and not raw clause-set
   equality (section 6.2 explains why). If it were weaker than what was proved,
   the segment would prove its conclusion from less than we know and be sound but
   incomplete (it fails loud). If it were *stronger* than what was proved, the
   segment would assume something never established, and the chain is unsound.
   The checker rejects any hypothesis not discharged by the previous conclusion.
2. **Complete state, with the frame carried, not declared.** The cut variables
   must cover everything segment `j` can read. A register or memory word the
   segment reads but the relation does not mention is silently treated as
   unconstrained fresh (sound, but possibly spuriously SAT) or, worse, if it is
   *not* made fresh and instead takes the value from the executor's single run,
   unsound for other paths. The rule: every location in the segment's read set is
   constrained by `R_{c_{j-1}}`. In particular, "unchanged since entry" is **a
   conjunct `FRAME(m, m0)` of every cut relation**: segment `j` assumes it in its
   hypothesis and *re-concludes* it for the state it produces (its stores are
   proved to hit only footprint locations). It is never a side declaration
   discharged only by the final obligation, which would be circular (the segments
   would rely on a frame fact whose proof itself relies on the segments). The
   checker verifies that every `R_c` contains the FRAME conjunct and that read
   sets are covered (the cone-of-influence of section 4.4).
3. **Same model point.** `s` at the cut is the model state after exactly the
   model steps that correspond to the machine segment. The *model-step
   boundaries* are part of the cut definition and are recorded in the manifest
   (a segment that straddles an incomplete model step has no meaningful `R`).
4. **No gap or overlap.** The segments partition the path: segment `j` starts
   where `j-1` ended, covers a contiguous instruction range, the ranges
   concatenate to the whole path, and base and final obligations exist.
5. **Path condition carried.** Each segment inherits `PC_i`; a segment proved
   under a weaker condition than its path's is fine, under a stronger one it
   silently narrows coverage.
6. **The model side composes.** The model-decomposition obligation of section 3.2a
   exists, is UNSAT, and its fragments are the ones the segments used.
7. **Inputs are bound.** Each obligation's CNF is a deterministic function of
   `(image slice, cut relation, model fragment, path condition)` and the image
   digest is among those inputs (section 6.2). A manifest whose digests do not
   match a regenerated CNF is rejected.
8. **Every access is obligated.** For every memory access in every path's
   instruction range there is an `access` obligation (section 3.4: outside code,
   alignment, mapped region, within footprint) and it is UNSAT. The checker
   enumerates the accesses from the **regenerated path**, not from the manifest,
   and matches them one to one with the manifest's `access` obligations (no
   extra, none missing). The FRAME re-conclusion of condition 2 depends on this:
   a store the access obligations did not bound could change a location FRAME says
   is untouched.

FRAME carries `m0` across all cuts through the version-indexed mapping. For a
large table that is a conjunct over every untouched word at every cut; the
builder must represent it as one shared equality over the unmodified region (a
range, not one conjunct per word), or the obligations grow with `cuts x table
size`. This is a performance requirement on VOS-029's representation, not a
change to the argument.

Soundness theorem of the composition (to be written and checked in the
composition checker's own tests): if the base, every segment, the final
obligation, the model-decomposition obligation and every `access` obligation are
UNSAT and conditions 1 to 8 hold, then for every initial state satisfying `pre`
and `PC_i`, the whole-path obligation of section 3.2 holds. Proof by induction
on `j`: `R_{c_j}` holds of the actual run because the actual states satisfy the
hypothesis (induction) and the segment obligation says the hypothesis implies the
conclusion. **Premises of the entry-level claim, not of this theorem**: `PC_i` is
the *exact path-following condition under the stepper* (the run takes path `i`
iff `PC_i` holds), the coverage and disjointness obligations of section 3.1, and
the base obligation. The theorem is about one path; the entry claim needs all
three. Model decomposition by slicing `model_step` into fragments is accepted
**only when condition 6 is checked on regenerated CNFs**.

**Where cuts go.** Per instruction is the strongest and the most obligations
(the probe's per-instruction row), per block every 8 or 32 instructions is the
trade-off between obligation count and difficulty (section 9 experiment B3
sweeps both). The right place is *source-aligned*: after each model-level
operation, because there `R` is a clean equation. That is why the owned-compile
lane (VER13 section 4.3 item 1, which emits a source map) gives cuts "by
construction" and is the fallback of section 8.5.

**A cut relation is a trusted definition.** `R` for each cut is written
(generated) by the tool; a wrong `R` that is too weak yields SAT (loud), one that
is too strong yields an unprovable *previous* segment (loud). Only the pairing
rule (condition 1) can make it silent, and that is checked on regenerated CNFs. The relation family
is part of the trusted base (section 7).

### 4.3 Index case-splitting in place of arrays

Today's solver stack has no array theory (VOS00 section 10.2, section 12 row 9).
A load from `table[i]` with symbolic `i` and `T` entries becomes
`ite(i=0, w0, ite(i=1, w1, ...))`, an eq/ite chain; the AIG is linear in `T`
but the solver pays for the interaction of consecutive accesses (section 8.2
tables D). The replacement is to split the *obligation* on the index:

```
  for each v in 0..T-1:   obligation_v = obligation with  i := v  and PC /\ (i = v)
  plus one more:           PC /\ i >= T   is UNSAT or leads to the declared fault path
```

Each `obligation_v` has a *concrete* offset, which the executor handles exactly
(VER19 section 3). Total cost is the sum over `v`; worst-case is the max.
This is not free (T obligations for T entries and the product for several
independent indices), so it is sized by the probe (D3: sum and max). For
table-indexed state such as a thread table, plan for `T <= 8-16` per obligation
or one obligation per index (VOS00 section 14.3 D4, section 8.3 below).

The coverage obligation (section 3.1) must include the split: the cases
`i = 0 .. T-1` and the out-of-range case partition the precondition.

### 4.4 Concrete frame addresses and cone-of-influence

**Concrete frame addresses.** The kernel's stack and its static structures are
at link-time-known addresses (the image is hermetic, VOS22). `sp` at entry is
either a constant or a base plus a symbolic *delta that the contract bounds*.
The executor treats `sp`-relative accesses as concrete offsets into a frame
whose base is a fresh symbolic word with the alignment constraint; the loads and
stores then become plain wires, not ite chains. A store through a pointer that
is not frame- or table-relative is the address obligation of section 3.4.

**Cone of influence.** Only the variables a segment's conclusion depends on
matter; the executor drops everything else from the formula (it builds the
formula from the conclusion's cone, not from the whole machine state). This is
standard, but the *recording* is what matters here: the read set is part of the
manifest (section 6.2) because the composition argument's condition 2 is
precisely "the cone is covered by the hypothesis".

### 4.5 Legitimate structural collapse, and why a collapsed miter is not evidence

A miter whose two sides are the same AIG node reduces to the constant
"different = false". The CNF is two clauses or fewer, the solver reports UNSAT
with 0 conflicts and an empty or trivial DRAT. That is sound and uninformative.
There are two cases and they must not be confused:

* **Legitimate collapse.** The compiled code *is* the model's computation
  (a bitwise-and with the same operands; an untouched register in the frame).
  The collapse proves, correctly, that nothing differs. This is expected in the
  frame obligations and in unchanged state.
* **Vacuous collapse.** The proof is trivial because the *inputs were wrong*:
  the executor read a constant instead of a symbolic value, or the generated
  program lost entropy (every value folded to a constant, section 9.4 guard G2).
  Then UNSAT means nothing and the VER-P030 audit found exactly this failure in
  earlier miters.

The rule is to **label it, never hide it**. Every row (probe) and every
obligation (VOS-029) records the number of AND nodes in the miter's cone and a
`collapsed` flag (CNF of at most two clauses, or zero conflicts). A collapsed
*capacity* row is reported `collapsed` and never counted as a capacity datum. A
collapsed
*proof* obligation is allowed only when the manifest gives a `collapsed_reason`
from a closed enum **and the checker verifies it structurally** on the regenerated
obligation (it never reads the reason as a claim):

| Reason | Allowed when |
|---|---|
| `identical-structure` | the two sides are the same AIG node, **and** (for a refinement obligation: base, segment, final) the machine-side cone was built from the image bytes by the checker's own builder with **symbolic, bound input bits** (section 6.2) |
| `constant-fold` | the same condition; both sides fold to the same constant *from symbolic inputs*, never because an input was a constant |
| `frame-untouched` | the location is **absent from the regenerated write set** of the segment |
| `propagation-only` | a valid zero-conflict segment: the CNF is not trivial but unit propagation alone refutes it. It still requires the machine-side input-binding check; it exists so a correct fast segment is not pushed into a wrong label |

Any other reason is rejected. `identical-structure` and `constant-fold` on a
refinement obligation without the input-binding check are **forbidden**: that is
exactly how an executor that read a constant would look. The rule is **per path**,
not per entry: every segment that writes a live or output value is either
non-collapsed or carries a checker-verified independent identity (the
input-binding check plus an identity the checker re-derives). One honest
non-collapsed segment does not excuse a vacuous one beside it. The `pick` proof of
VOS-029 must carry a non-collapsed certificate and a SAT sabotage control
(section 10).

### 4.6 Vacuity: an UNSAT proof needs witnesses that the premises can hold

An UNSAT miter proves "no state satisfying the premises differs". If the premises
are contradictory the miter is UNSAT for a worthless reason (the VER-P030 failure
in another form). So the premises are shown *satisfiable*, by witnesses, and a
witness is run on the concrete machine:

1. **`pre` is satisfiable**: a SAT witness for `pre(m0)` alone.
2. **Every feasible path has a replayed witness.** "Feasible" means *SAT with a
   replayed model*: the path condition is satisfied by a witness replayed on
   `riscv-simulator` (the real simulator runs the image from the witness state and
   takes the path). A path with no replayed witness is not feasible. (VOS-029 may
   justify one witness per entry instead, in its own spec, if path counts make
   per-path replay impractical, **never for a path whose PC is claimed
   infeasible**; the default is per path.) An entry with no
   feasible path proves nothing. **An infeasibility claim is accepted only if the
   checker's regenerated path condition is UNSAT** (with a certificate); the
   manifest's "infeasible" flag is never enough.
3. **A witness for each cut hypothesis**: `R_{c_{j-1}}` is satisfiable together with
   `PC_i` (a hypothesis that no state satisfies makes segment `j` vacuous).
4. At least one witness (per entry) is replayed end to end through all segments on
   the concrete simulator and its final state is checked against the model step,
   computed by an evaluator independent of the executor.

Witnesses are recorded in the manifest (obligation kind `witness`, the model, its
digest); the composition checker verifies each against its CNF and replays at
least one.

### 5.1 The X3 instance may differ from the X1 instance

X1 is enumerated at a *reduced* configuration (N = 8, VOS01 section 5.6). X3 for
a bitmap component is *plausibly* width-insensitive: a clz-based `pick` miter over
64 bits solves in milliseconds on hand-written code (section 8.2, table A), so the
X3 instance may turn out to be the shipped N, **pending the compiled-image re-run**
of VOS-029 (section 10 item 7); until then the label prints the instance it was
proved at and claims nothing wider. The label prints both: an X1 instance and an X3 instance, so nobody
reads "N = 8 exhaustive" as "N = 64 proved" or the reverse. For table-indexed
state the X3 instance is *smaller* than the shipped table unless the case-split
total is affordable (section 4.3), and the label says so.

### 5.2 Board and image digests

The image digest is the content digest of the linked image the executor read.
The board digest is the emulator board definition (VOS00 section 10.3, row 2).
Both are pinned in every certificate; a change to either invalidates the
manifest and the check fails.

### 5.3 Which stack

VER13 distinguishes two stacks for any guarantee (section 4.3 "Important
framing", section 5.2 item 6, and rule AC-7 in section 6): `host-rustc` (the
Rust as the host compiler builds it) and `owned-rv32i` (the output of the
owned compile lane). X3 on an image compiled by `rustc` is about **a binary
`rustc` produced, with `rustc` trusted**: it carries `stack = host-rustc` and
cannot retire any assumption that VER13 scopes to `owned-rv32i` (such as
`compiler.preserves_timing`). Only the owned-compile lane can print
`owned-rv32i`. The label always prints the stack.

## 6. Certificates

### 6.1 One DRAT per obligation

Each obligation is a *separate CNF* with its own DRAT proof, produced by
`cdcl-sat` with proof logging and checked by `drat-check` (VER11).
Obligations are content-addressed with a **length-prefixed** digest, so no
(CNF, proof) pair can be re-split into another:

```
  certificate_id = sha256( u64be(len(DIMACS)) || DIMACS || u64be(len(DRAT)) || DRAT )
```

(plain `sha256(DIMACS || DRAT)` would let bytes migrate across the boundary and
give two different pairs one id). The manifest lists `cnf_sha256` (of the DIMACS
alone), `drat_sha256` (of the DRAT alone) and the combined id.

Why separate CNFs, not one incremental solver with assumptions. UNSAT under
assumptions does *not* end in an empty clause (VER11 section 4: such proofs
"are not certificates of anything yet"). `cdcl-sat` exposes
`solve_with_assumptions` and `failed_assumptions` (`solver.rs:1077`, `:1137`)
and states that the clause of the negated failed assumptions is *implied*, but
logs no DRAT lemma for it. So an incremental run is fast for exploration (the
calibration used it) but yields no checkable proof. Two routes to certified
composition:

* **Route A, per-obligation CNFs (works today).** The hypothesis relation is
  added as clauses of the segment's own formula; each segment has a plain
  UNSAT with a plain DRAT. Costs: each segment re-pays its own cost and proofs
  are many. No solver change. This is the route VOS-029 can start on and the one
  the probe measures as "certified" (section 9, B3).
* **Route B, lemma logging in `cdcl-sat` (needs a change).** After an UNSAT
  under assumptions, the solver logs the failed-assumption clause as a DRAT
  lemma. Three rules keep it sound:
  1. **Bind the pair.** The certificate is for `(original CNF, stated clause)`:
     its id covers the digest of the CNF *without* any hypothesis clauses and the
     stated clause. A proof of a clause is meaningless detached from the formula it
     was derived from.
  2. **Hypotheses are assumptions, never permanent clauses.** In an incremental
     solver, a segment's hypothesis added as a permanent clause would stay in the
     database for every later segment and silently strengthen them. Segment
     hypotheses are passed as assumptions only, and the stated clause is the
     negation of exactly those assumptions.
  3. **Do not grow the trusted kernel.** The checker does not get a new "derives
     this clause" accept mode. Instead the *caller* appends the negated stated
     clause as unit clauses to the original formula and runs the existing
     `drat-check` for the empty clause (the clause is implied iff the formula plus
     its negation is refuted). Any genuinely new accept mode needs its own review
     of the kernel (VER11 section 3.4).
  This is VOS-D045 with VER-BACKLOG row VER-SAT2.

### 6.2 The composition manifest and what the checker recomputes

A machine-readable manifest per X3 label lists, for the entry:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `entry`, `image_sha256`, `board_sha256`, `model_digest` | what was proved, about what |
| `paths[]` | id, path-condition digest, feasible flag with its witness id, instruction range |
| `obligations[]` | id, kind (section 3, including `model-decomposition`, `access`, `witness`), path id, segment range, `cnf_sha256`, `drat_sha256`, `certificate_id`, `ands`, `collapsed`, `collapsed_reason` (the enum of section 4.5), conflicts, check work |
| `inputs` per obligation | digests of the **inputs the CNF was generated from**: the image slice (bytes and range), the cut relation, the model fragment, the path condition; `image_sha256` is among them |
| `hypotheses` per obligation | the ids of the obligations whose conclusions it assumed |
| `read_set` per obligation | the locations the segment can read (cone of influence) |
| `coverage` | the coverage obligation id for the entry, and the path list it covers |
| `step_bound` | `K`, whether `proved` or `bounded-proof(k=K)`, and the residual statement (section 3.5) |

**The composition checker does not trust the manifest.** The manifest is a
claim; the checker is a tool that re-derives it. A digest the manifest states is
only data; nothing is accepted because a field says so. The checker:

1. **Regenerates each obligation's CNF** from `(image slice, cut relation, model
   fragment, path condition)` with a builder that is *separate from the
   executor's*. What the checker and the executor **share, and only this**: the
   RV32I **decoder** (`riscv-simulator::decode`), the `Aig`, hashing, and
   `Tseitin`. That shared set is kept minimal and separately tested: a decoder
   differential against `riscv-simulator`, and Aig and Tseitin faithfulness tests
   (the pattern of `bit-blaster/tests/faithfulness.rs`). The checker's builder has
   **its own per-opcode semantics table**, written independently of the
   stepper's. **Both the stepper and the checker's builder go through a randomized
   differential against `riscv-simulator`** (section 7). Obligations are compared
   by a **canonical AIG hash of the output cone** (structural hashing with a
   canonical input order and the version-indexed names), not by raw clause sets:
   two correct Tseitin encodings of one circuit differ in variable numbering and
   clause order, and a raw comparison would reject them or invite a loose one. The
   CNF is a deterministic function of its inputs and the image digest is bound
   into them: a CNF built from another image, or by hand, does not match.
   **What survives both builders:** a *shared misreading of the RV32I semantics*
   (both tables wrong the same way) passes. That is why the `riscv-simulator`
   differential and the gate-level twin (`riscv-gatelevel`, VER13 section 4.3)
   exist; independent code is not claimed to rule it out.
   **Binding of the refuted CNF.** The CNF that `drat-check` refutes is the
   checker's own Tseitin encoding of its regenerated AIG (canonical variable
   numbering derived from the canonical AIG order), or is verified clause-for-clause
   equal to it up to a stated renaming. An AIG hash alone, without this binding, is
   not accepted: it would show the AIG is right and say nothing about the formula
   the certificate is about.
   **Structural parsing instead of regeneration** is allowed for exactly these
   kinds and no others: `witness` (the model is evaluated against the regenerated
   formula), `step-bound` and the image checks of section 3.7. Every
   machine-segment, `base`, `final`, `model-decomposition`, `access` and
   **`coverage`** kind **must be regenerated** (coverage from the regenerated path
   conditions and `pre`, with the binding above); a kind not listed is rejected.
2. **Verifies the hypothesis pairing** as instances of one relation under the
   version-indexed mapping (section 4.2 condition 1): it re-derives `R_j` once and
   checks that the predecessor's negated conclusion and the successor's hypothesis
   are both its instances, by canonical AIG hash. Equality of *digests the manifest
   provides* is never enough.
3. Verifies conditions 1 to 8 of section 4.2 (condition 8 enumerates accesses
   from the regenerated path): read sets covered, the FRAME
   conjunct in every cut relation, model boundaries and model decomposition,
   segments partition the path, path conditions carry, coverage lists every path
   exactly once, witnesses of section 4.6 present and replayed.
4. Re-runs `drat-check` on every certificate under the limits of section 6.3.
5. Verifies every `collapsed_reason` structurally, including the machine-side
   input-binding check (section 4.5).

It does not trust the executor's claim that it saw everything; the coverage
obligation and the witnesses are the evidence.

### 6.3 Default `drat-check` limits are a requirement

`drat-check` ships with `DEFAULT_MAX_VARS = 2^20` and `DEFAULT_MAX_WORK = 2^32`
(`drat-check/src/lib.rs:66`, `:73`). Running out only ever *rejects*. In the
calibration those defaults are exceeded by roughly 105k to 237k conflicts
(section 8.2). The temptation is to raise them until things pass. That is
forbidden here:

> The trusted kernel's limits are never raised to make a certificate pass.
> A proof the default limits reject is an obligation that is too big; the
> remedy is a better decomposition (a cut, a split), not a bigger budget. Raising
> a limit is an explicit, reviewed decision recorded in the backlog, and the
> label then prints the raised limit.

Every obligation must pass under `drat_check::Limits::default()`. The probe
reports it per row (`pass_default_limits`) and counts it in "green".

### 6.4 Replay of counterexamples

Every UNSAT obligation of a capacity or proof run has a paired SAT negative
control (section 9.4, guard G3): a one-instruction mutation whose counterexample
is *replayed* by an **independent concrete evaluator** (the concrete
`riscv-simulator` for the code side; a separately written evaluator for the
reference side, never the stepper's own circuit evaluated again). The control
shows the obligation is not UNSAT merely because the formula is unsatisfiable for
an unrelated reason. It does **not** by itself show the stepper agrees with the
simulator in general (one counterexample covers one input); that is the job of the
randomized differential of section 7.

## 7. Trusted base

VOS00 section 10.3 stands (ISA model, board, compiler, HAL asm, the Warrant
toolchain). X3 adds:

| Item | Why it is trusted | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| The symbolic stepper | It decides what formula stands for an instruction | A **randomized differential** against `riscv-simulator` on random concrete states, instruction by instruction, over every opcode and operand shape (this, not one replayed counterexample, is what ties it to the simulator); SAT negative controls replayed by an independent evaluator (section 6.4); the same differential covers the checker's own builder (section 6.2); the stepper is *not* the simulator and never shares code with it beyond the decoder |
| The composition checker | It is the only thing that makes the chain add up | It **regenerates the CNFs and the hypothesis pairing itself** (section 6.2) and trusts no manifest field; its own unit tests carry a negative control per condition of section 4.2 (hypothesis too strong, FRAME conjunct missing, read set uncovered, an access without its obligation, an illegal collapse label, gap, overlap, dropped path, model fragments not composing, a CNF built from a different image) |
| `alpha` on machine state | The refinement is about its image; **it is trusted, and only randomized-differential evidence backs it** (the label says so, section 5) | Differential against the host `alpha` on sampled and exhaustive reduced states (the VOS01 section 1.5 ghost code, evaluated on the same data) |
| The cut relations | They define what is carried between segments | Generated, digested, and cross-checked by condition 1 |
| The bit-blasted model's faithfulness | The model circuit stands for the Warrant model | The pattern of `bit-blaster/tests/faithfulness.rs`: the circuit evaluated exhaustively or on corner cases against a reference semantics |
| `drat-check` and its limits | It is the kernel | VER11 section 3.4; limits never raised (section 6.3) |

The probe's stepper (section 9.1) is explicitly **not** in this list: it is a
measurement instrument for capacity only, private to the probe, and no
certificate it produces is used as evidence of anything about the OS.

### 8.3 Thresholds

Stated in conflicts and checker work first, seconds second (seconds depend on
the machine, conflicts do not).

**Seeds.** Every knob is run with **at least 5 seeds**. The row that drives a
verdict and the table is the **worst seed's own row**, chosen by a fixed rule
(non-green first, then the most conflicts of one obligation, then the longest
solve, then the lowest seed) so that conflicts, DRAT size and the checker's verdict
are from one seed; a median would let one hard instance hide. The table adds the
maximum solve time and resident memory over the seeds in one group-level column, so
the single-row rule hides neither. The seed count and the number of distinct
instances are recorded as well.

An obligation is **GREEN** only if all of, in its worst seed:

* UNSAT, with its SAT negative control passing and replayed;
* at most 100,000 conflicts and at most 10 s solve;
* DRAT at most 32 MB;
* `drat-check` accepts under the **default** `Limits` (work at most 2^32);
* peak resident memory at most 1 GB;
* not `collapsed`, `unknown`, `killed`, `oom` or `crashed`.

An entry is **feasible** when it decomposes into at most about 1,000 green
obligations totalling under 10 minutes.

**Which numbers drive which row.** The monolithic experiment B2 is the baseline
and *drives no row*: its job is to show the cliff. The compositional numbers
drive every row: B3 (certified mode, section 9.5; the incremental mode is
informational), D3 and D4 for memory, A1 and A2 for the bitmap shapes, and C1 and
C2 for two-safety. B4 constrains the SH lint only.

**Evaluation order and precedence.** Rows are evaluated **worst case first**; the
first row whose condition holds is the outcome, so a later (better) row can never
override an earlier (worse) one.

|---|---|---|---|

**The table is total.** Any non-green result that no row names (a new result
kind, a guard failure, an experiment missing from the results) **falls through to
row 1**: unclassified is red, not green.

**A proof the default limits reject can never be "reduced".** A *required*
obligation whose certificate is rejected by `drat-check` under its default limits
is a row-1 failure at that decomposition. The remedy is a finer decomposition
(which is then tested, and may place the entry in row 2 *if the finer obligations
are green*), never a raised limit (section 6.3) and never the "reduced" row on the
strength of the same oversized obligation.

**The 100,000 to 1,000,000 gap.** The earlier draft left a worst step between
100k and 1M conflicts unclassified. It is classified: a step above the green cap
of 100,000 conflicts at the finest cut is **row 1**. (Row 2 is reached only by
steps at or below 100,000 conflicts that need a finer cut or smaller instance to
get there.) The 1M figure of the earlier text is dropped.

Why "worst step" and not only the total: a chain is only as strong as its
hardest hop, and one hop that is not green blocks the whole entry regardless of
how cheap the others are.

### 8.4 Validity limits of the calibration, and of the probe

Print these with every number, because each one limits what it supports:

1. **Hand-written code is not compiler output.** No register allocation, no
   spills, no instruction fusion, no inlining decisions. Real code may be
   easier (more shared structure) or harder (more mixing).
2. **The divergence is synthetic.** The reference side was made to differ from
   the code in controlled proportions (5%, 25%, 100% of instructions encoded
   differently). It is not the divergence a real compiler introduces.
3. **Trap entry, CSRs and `mret` are not modelled.** Only the ALU, loads and
   stores and forward branches with a concrete PC.
4. **The probe bounds solver capacity per obligation shape.** It does not prove
   any entry verifiable.
5. **One machine, one solver build.** Seconds are host-specific; the
   conflicts and work are the portable part.
6. **The advisor numbers raised the `drat-check` limits**, so every proof there
   was checkable; under the shipped limits many are not (the REJECT rows). The
   probe never raises a limit: a proof the default limits reject is a red row.
7. **The D instances share one symbolic base index** (the fields of one table
   entry, the shape of a thread-table operation): `k` accesses at word offsets
   `i + j` of a T-word table. `k` *independent* symbolic indices (a product of
   `T^k` cases when split) are not measured, and `i` has `log2 T` bits, so the
   out-of-range case of section 4.3 does not arise.
8. **Liveness is exact only for small cones.** Guard G2 decides a declared bit
   that random flips did not find sensitive by a SAT query when the cone has at
   most 4,000 AND nodes; for a larger cone the count rests on 256 random
   assignments and the row says so in its `note`.
9. **Both sides run on one stepper.** The code side and the reference side are
   executed by the same symbolic stepper; only the *circuits* of the diverged
   instructions differ. A stepper bug that affects both sides alike is invisible
   to the miter; the checks against it are the probe's differential of the
   stepper against the concrete simulator, the independent concrete evaluator of
   the replay, and the replay of every control on the real `riscv-simulator`.
10. **"Five seeds" means five distinct instances only where the table's Seeds
    column says so.** A deterministic shape may build one circuit for several
    seeds (the seed then varies only the variable numbering; for C1 on an A1 base
    all five seeds have the same conflicts), and 18 groups of the committed file
    (15 solver groups and the three B1 groups, which are identical by design) have
    fewer than five distinct `instance_sha256`. Each group's line is its worst
    seed's own row, every cell from that one row.

### 9.2 Entry points

```
vos-capacity-probe list
vos-capacity-probe run --tier quick|full [--only A1,B3] [--jobs N] [--seeds N] --out results.json
vos-capacity-probe check results.json [--jobs N]
vos-capacity-probe merge --base A.json --fresh B.json --experiment E1 [--where field=value] [--ignore-outside] --out C.json

vos-capacity-probe decide [--results FILE]
vos-capacity-probe row --spec JSON --wall SECONDS          (the child process of run)
```

Exit status 0 is success, 1 means the command ran and found violations, 2 means a
malformed command line or an unreadable input (a gate never confuses the two).

* `list` prints each experiment, its knob counts per tier and its guards.
* `run` executes the selected experiments, **one child process per row** so peak
  resident memory is attributable (each child is wrapped with `/usr/bin/time -l`
  on macOS or `-v` on Linux; the tool parses it), and records one row per
  `(experiment, knob, seed)`. At least five seeds per knob (`--seeds` may raise
  it, never lower it). `--tier quick` also applies the BUILD gate of section 9.3.
* `check` validates a results file against the schema and the guards, **re-runs
  every quick-tier row** and compares digests, verdicts, controls and conflict
  counts, requires that the file contain every quick row of the current plan, and
  exits 1 on any violation; full-tier rows are validated for schema and guards
  only and are regenerated by a reviewer (section 9.6).
* `merge` is the only sanctioned way to refresh a SUBSET of the committed rows (the file is never
  edited by hand): it replaces the base rows of one experiment (optionally those whose knob string
  field equals a value, `--where fixture=vos-sched-fp-bitmap`) by the same-identity rows of a fresh
  `run`, leaves every other row byte-identical and in place, and records the refresh.
  **Provenance is per row, so the base `tool`, `host` and `revisions` are kept** as the file's
  original provenance and the merge appends one entry to a top-level `merges` list (omitted while
  empty): `{ "rev", "binary_sha256", "dirty_tree", "experiment", "where", "rows": [row ids], "at" }`,
  with `rev` the fresh run's `repo` revision. `validate` checks every entry (a 40-hex `rev`, a
  64-hex binary digest, `dirty_tree` false, a known experiment, every listed row id present in the

  applies unchanged to a partial run: it needs only non-empty rows, five seeds per knob and
  the row rules); a merged result for which `validate` reports anything; a `dirty_tree` that is not
  the JSON boolean `false` (in `merge` and in `validate`: a string or `null` never reads as clean);
  a fresh `repo` revision that is not HEAD or an ancestor of it (`git merge-base --is-ancestor`);
  duplicate row ids in either file; fresh rows outside the selected subset (unless `--ignore-outside`

### 9.3 BUILD

The BUILD file runs, in release mode (SAT is slow in a debug build): the unit and

### 9.4 Validity guards, on every row

These are the lessons of the VER-P030 audit and of the calibration; a row that
fails a guard is *invalid*, never a capacity datum.

**G1. Collapse accounting, measured from the AIG.** Record the number of AND
nodes in the miter's **output cone** (`ands`), computed by walking the `Aig` from
the miter output, not taken from the generator, and a `collapsed` flag: the CNF
has at most two clauses, or the solver used zero conflicts. A collapsed row is
reported with `result = "collapsed"` and excluded from every threshold and table
statistic. Experiment B1 (a sequence against itself, divergence 0%) *must* report
collapsed; it measures build time and memory only. `collapsed_reason` is the
four-value closed enum of the section 4.5 table (`identical-structure`,
`constant-fold`, `frame-untouched`, `propagation-only`) and `check` verifies it
structurally, by the allowed-when rules of that table, from the instance.

**G2. Liveness of generated programs, measured semantically.** Generators keep
programs live: inject a fresh symbolic load every `6` instructions, and cap the
share of entropy-killing operations (`sltu`, shifts, `and` with small constants)
at `20%`. The guard is *measured*, and on **behaviour**, not graph structure (a
cone can mention an input bit and still ignore it):

* **Declared bits** come from the generator's *input list* (the symbolic registers
  and loads it created), enumerated by the tool, never a self-reported count.
* **Sensitivity** is defined on **each side's own output cone** (the code side
  and the reference side separately), **not on the miter output**: an UNSAT miter
  is constant 0 and would wrongly mark every row collapsed. It is measured by
  **random simulation of the cone with per-input-bit flips**: for each declared bit, over `256` random assignments flip
  that bit and see whether any output bit changes; a bit is *sensitive* if it ever
  does. (Where an instance is small, a SAT query "two inputs differing only in
  this bit give different outputs" replaces the sampling.) `entropy_bits` is the
  number of sensitive bits. **As built:** the sampling is the first stage and
  the SAT query decides every bit it did not find sensitive, for a cone of at
  most 4,000 ANDs (and `undecided bits x ANDs` at most 400,000); a larger cone is
  measured by sampling alone, and a very large one by a sample of the bits with
  the count scaled, and the row says so in its `note`. (Sampling alone calls a
  64-bit `pick` dead: a bit matters only when no higher bit is set, which random
  assignments reach for about nine bits of any width.) In the cut form (inputs
  `...@c` and `...@r`, one per side) each side is measured against *its own*
  declared inputs. For a **self-composition** (C1, C2) the output is
  secret-independent by design, so the two cones measured are the two sides of
  the equivalence that cancels the secret (`code(s) XOR ref(s)` taken apart), not
  the output. For a **chain** the counts are summed over the segments that have
  any AND at all; a chain with none is `collapsed`.
* A row is `collapsed` if, on **either** side's output cone (the rule must hold for
  both sides, so it fails closed), fewer than **50% of the declared bits are sensitive**, or
  if the AND count of the miter cone is below **25% of the AND count of the same
  generator at the same length with divergence 100%**. These are the defaults; a
  change to any is a reviewed change.
* The G3 control proves sensitivity **at one site only** (the mutated
  instruction); it does not substitute for this per-bit measure.

This is the guard against the n of 200 or more rows of table B2.

**G3. SAT negative control, per experiment and per mode.** Every UNSAT experiment
has a paired SAT control. The rules:

* the control is the same instance with **one mutated instruction** (or, for
  memory experiments D1 to D4, one mutated access: a wrong index, a dropped
  store, a wrong stride; for E1 and E2, one mutated generated claim that the
  harness must report as failing, and a record whose width bound is violated that
  must be refused);
* it must be **SAT**, and the model is validated: the SAT model **satisfies the
  CNF** (re-evaluated by a small CNF evaluator, not trusting the solver's flag);
* its counterexample is **replayed**: the code side on the concrete
  `riscv-simulator`, the reference side on an **independent concrete evaluator**
  (written separately from the stepper; it must not call into the stepper's
  circuit), and the two outputs must differ;
* a **control per mode**: the cut experiments (B3, D4) have a control in the
  incremental mode and another in the certified mode, and the self-composition
  experiments (C1, C2) have a control that is a leaking variant, replayed by
  running two concrete runs that agree on public inputs and differ in trace;
* a control that is UNSAT, or whose model does not satisfy the CNF, or whose
  counterexample does not replay, fails the row. The control instance has its own
  digest (`control_instance_sha256`).

A single counterexample does not show the stepper agrees with the simulator; the
real test is the randomized differential of section 7 (the probe has a quick-tier
differential on 1,000 random states per opcode for the same reason).

**G4. Determinism.** The generator is seeded; the seed is a recorded field. A row
reproduces bit-for-bit (same DIMACS digest) from `(experiment, knob, seed)`.

### 9.5 Measurements per row, limits and the timeout

Per row (see the schema of section 9.6): `ands`, `vars`, `clauses`, `result`
(`unsat`, `sat`, `unknown`, `collapsed`, `refused`, `killed`, `oom`, `crashed`),
`conflicts`, `propagations`, `solve_ms` (wall), `drat_bytes`, `check_ms` (wall)
and `check_work` (the `Accepted.work` of `drat-check`), `pass_default_limits`
(checked under `Limits::default()`; the probe never raises limits, so a row too
big for the defaults records `false` and the reason), `peak_rss_kb`.

**Resource limits** (the probe runs hostile-sized instances by design; these keep
it from taking the machine down, and a limit that trips is a red row, never a
silent drop):

1. **Conflict cap with proof logging.** Whenever proof logging is on, the solve
   stops at **1,000,000 conflicts** (the proof is held in memory, about 350 MB per
   million conflicts); past it the row is `unknown`.
2. **Wall timeout, 120 s per row**, as a loop over `set_conflict_budget(Some(20_000))`
   with a clock check between slices (`cdcl-sat/src/solver.rs:422`; the budget
   counts down per slice, so it is reset each iteration; as built a slice also
   ends at 4 x 10^8 watch-list visits, `Solver::set_work_budget`, so the clock is
   read about every second even on a million-clause formula where 20,000
   conflicts would take minutes). A timeout is `result = "unknown"`, cap `wall`,
   with the conflicts used so far. A chain also stops between two obligations.
3. **Whole-process-group kill.** The row runs as a child in its **own process
   group**; the parent kills the *group* on the wall timeout, covering **every
   stage**: instance generation and CNF writing, the solve, and `drat-check`, not
   only the solver loop. A stage that never returns (a pathological generator, a
   checker running to its work limit) is thereby bounded too. Result `killed`.
   As built the kill is at **300 s**: 120 s of solving, 30 s for the control
   stage and 150 s of slack, because the checker's own work limit (2^32 visits)
   bounds one check to about two minutes; a row that merely needs its check is
   red for the right reason, not `killed`.
4. **Enforced memory cap per child**: the parent polls the child's resident set
   **every 50 ms** (`/usr/bin/time` output is only a final figure) and kills the
   group above **2 GB** (result `oom`). The row records **which cap tripped**
   (`cap`: `conflicts`, `wall`, `rss` or `none`), so a proof-logging run that hits
   the 1M-conflict cap near 1.8 GB resident (`unknown`) is not confused with the
   2 GB RSS cap (`oom`). `RLIMIT_AS` is *not* relied on: it is unreliable on
   macOS (it does not bound resident memory the way it does on Linux).
5. **A crash** (a panic, a signal) is `crashed`, with the exit status in `note`.
6. **Peak RSS must be parsed.** If the `/usr/bin/time` figure cannot be parsed the
   row **fails**; it is never defaulted to 0.

`unknown`, `killed`, `oom` and `crashed` are all **red** and are never dropped
from the table: they are the data that says "does not fit".

**Two modes for cut experiments.** Where a cut-point experiment is measured,
both are run and recorded:

* `mode = "incremental"`: one solver, assumptions per segment, no proof logging
  (fast; the form of the advisor's table B3); informational;
* `mode = "certified"`: one fresh CNF per segment with the hypothesis as clauses,
  DRAT logged and checked under default limits (the form that would ship;
  section 6.1 route A). **The decision uses the certified numbers.**

`dirty_tree`, `binary_sha256` and the regeneration banner are **self-reported by
the tool**: nothing stops an edited tool or file from writing the values it
likes. Reviewer regeneration is therefore a **process control**, not a technical
one; the results file is evidence only after a named person has regenerated it.

**What `check` verifies, and what it cannot.**

* Schema and guards, always, on every row: every UNSAT row has a replayed control
  whose model satisfies the CNF; no `collapsed`, `unknown`, `killed`, `oom` or
  `crashed` row is `green`; `green` equals the section 8.3 predicate recomputed
  from the other fields; peak RSS present; seeds at least 5 per knob;
  `dirty_tree` is `false` for a committed file (a dirty-tree run may not be
  committed).
* **Re-runs**: `check` **re-runs every quick-tier row** (the smallest knob of each
  experiment, deterministic from the recorded seed) and compares the instance
  digest, the verdict, the control result and the **conflict count** (equal, since
  the solver is deterministic); any difference fails. It also re-checks every
  recorded DRAT of those rows with `drat-check`.
* **Full-tier rows are not verifiable by `check`** (they take hours and their
  seconds are host-dependent). It validates their schema and guards only. A
  reviewer **regenerates the full tier** on a stated host before the numbers are
  accepted, and the PR says so. A result file that a reviewer has not regenerated
  carries the banner "full tier not independently regenerated" in the table.

### 9.7 The experiments

Each experiment lists its shape, sweep and what a verdict is. "Equal knobs" mean
the quick tier uses the smallest value only.

| Id | Shape | Sweep |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | *Pick miter*: the RV32 `clzN` binary-search sequence against the "highest set member" priority chain | N = 8, 16, 32, 64 |
| **A2** | *Insert/remove miter*: `mask | (1 << p)` against a per-`Bool` update with the `p >= N` guard | N = 8 up to 64 |
| **B1** | A straight-line sequence against itself (divergence 0%) | lengths 200, 500, 1000; **must report `collapsed`**; measures build time and memory only |

| **B4** | *Arithmetic with no cut point*: n-term re-association; `x*5` against `(x<<2)+x`; `base + i*12` against shift-add | n = 3 to 7 at W = 32 (n = 2 is one adder against itself); W = 8, 16, 32 |

| **C2** | A secret-dependent load index (**must be SAT**) and a padded constant-time variant (UNSAT) | T = 8, 16, 64 |
| **D1** | *Memory mux*: T words, k chained symbolic load-modify-store at offsets `i + j` of one symbolic base index, the `sym.rs` eq/ite chain against a mux tree | T = 4, 8, 16, 64, 256, k = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 |
| **D2** | D1 with concrete offsets | same grid |
| **D3** | D1 case-split on the index: T obligations, reported as **sum and max** | same grid |
| **D4** | Both sides share the mux structure, with cut points after each access | same grid |
| **E1** | *Enumeration throughput* of the generated by-value harness: the real `vos-sched-fp-bitmap` at N = 8 (binder products 256 and 65,536) and the fixture `vos-probe-ready` (65,536 in the 8-`Bool` form and in the `Impl(mask: U8)` form, 262,144 for insert at N = 10, 393,216); **N = 11 must be refused (W0706)**. Run-only cases per second (the harness is compiled into the probe: compile time is the probe's build). G3 control: the same claim against a broken adapter, which the harness must report failing; the refusal row's control is the same document at N = 10, which must be accepted | as stated |

G3 control: a binder wider than the 4096 cap must be refused (W0707) | as stated |

Notes on a few of them, because the shape explains the choice:

* **A1** is where "CLZ is not a cost driver" is confirmed (table A); it is the
  entry-level `pick` shape.
* **B1** is a *negative* result by design: it demonstrates guard G1, that an
  identical sequence collapses, and measures only what building a large
  sequence costs.
* **B2 against B3** is the pair that decides the route. B2 is the cliff; B3 is
  what a cut does about it, at three cut granularities, with certification.
* **B4** isolates arithmetic structure (re-association, strength reduction)
  from instruction count, which is the cost driver identified in section 2.2.
* **D1, D2, D3, D4** triangulate the memory story: chained symbolic accesses
  (red), concrete offsets (the target), case-split (the substitute for arrays)
  and cut points between accesses.
* **E1** sits beside the solver rows because the X1 bar is enumeration, and
  its throughput and its refusal at N = 11 are the X1 reduced-instance
  evidence (VOS01 section 5.6). The fixture is committed so the numbers come from
  the real generated harness.

### 9.8 The generated table and its check mode

`vos-capacity-probe table` reads the committed results file and emits, for each
experiment-knob group, one line that is the **worst seed's own row** (section 8.3;
the tie-break is the lowest seed), with two group-level cells: the seeds (counted
of total, and the distinct instances among the counted) and the maximum solve time
and resident memory over the seeds:

```
| Experiment | Knob | Mode | ANDs | Vars | Clauses | Entropy bits | Seeds | Conflicts (total / worst) | Solve | DRAT | Check work | Default limits | RSS | Control | Max over seeds (solve / RSS) | Verdict |
```

`Control` is `replayed` or the failure; `Verdict` is `GREEN`, `red (reason)`,
`collapsed`, `unknown (timeout)`, `killed`, `oom` or `crashed`, computed from
section 8.3. The generated block **begins with a fixed banner and the validity
limits** (section 8.4, all of them), so a table can never be read without them:

```
PROXY: synthetic hand-written code with synthetic divergence, not compiler
output. These numbers bound solver capacity per obligation shape; they do not
prove any entry verifiable. Seeds >= 5; each line is its worst seed's own row. <full-tier regeneration
banner if applicable>
```

The table is placed in this document between a pair of HTML-comment markers
named `vos30-table:begin` and `vos30-table:end` (they are written out only once,
around the block itself, because the checker finds the first pair; the markers
were not present in the spec until a generated table existed, because an empty
block would look like a result). `table --check`
regenerates the block and compares it byte for byte with the spec, failing on
**drift**. It **also fails if section 8.5 states a decision** (one of the three
rows of section 8.3, after the words `Decision (VOS-028b)` in bold) **while the
generated block is absent or empty**, if the stated row is not the row the results
give, if the results file is absent while the block is present, and if the results
file exists but the block does not. **No number in the table is hand-typed.**
VOS-028b keeps the tables of section 8.2 as history under a *superseded* banner
(so that the comparison with the advisor stays readable) and states the decision of
section 8.5 from the generated table.

### 9.10 Implementation decisions of VOS-028b

Everything below is what the implementation fixed where this section left a choice
open. Each item is binding on `check` and on a later re-run.

**Instances.** One seed makes one program; `(experiment, knob, seed)` fixes the
DIMACS text (G4). For the deterministic shapes (A, C, D) the seed shuffles the
**order in which the AIG inputs are created**, i.e. the variable numbering, which
is the one honest source of solver variance they have; for the generated programs
(B) it also selects the program, the divergence sites and the control mutation.

| Id | Code side | Reference side |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | the branch-free RV32I `clz32` binary search (five `srli/sltiu/slli/sll/add` rounds and a zero fix-up), then `r = 32 - clz` (N = 64: two words and a mask select) | a priority chain `ite(bit N-1, N, ite(bit N-2, N-1, ... ite(bit 0, 1, 0)))`, the position of the highest set member plus one, 0 when empty |
| A2 | branch-free insert and remove on an N-bit mask (`sltiu` guard, `sll` of 1, mask select; N = 64: two words), both outputs compared | per-`Bool` update `ite(p == i, b, m_i)`; `p >= N` changes nothing |
| B | a seeded straight-line RV32I sequence over `x1..x8` with a fresh symbolic `lw` every 6 instructions (G2), at most 20% entropy-killing operations | the same sequence as an `Op` list built through the same `bv` operations, except that a seeded share of the instructions that have an alternative is encoded differently (adder identities, reversed barrel stages, borrow-bit comparisons, commuted multiplier) |
| B3 | segments of 1, 8 or 32 instructions, each in its own AIG; every live-in register and memory word is **two fresh inputs, one per side, joined by an equality premise** (the hypothesis as clauses); all registers and words the segment writes are compared | as B |
| B4 | n-term re-association `((a+b)+c)+d` against `a+(b+(c+d))`, `x*5` against `(x<<2)+x` (the multiplier by a symbolic register premised equal to 5), and `base + i*12` against shift-add; width W by comparing the low W bits of zero-extended W-bit inputs | as stated |
| C1 | two copies of an A1 or B body with public inputs shared and secrets fresh; the public output is `pub_chain XOR (f_code(s) XOR f_ref(s))`, secret-independent only *semantically*, so the proof is a real cancellation, not a structural one | the same with `f_code(s)` alone (a leak), the SAT control |
| C2 | the padded variant loads all T words at constant addresses after a semantically cancelled secret perturbation of the address (UNSAT); the leaking variant loads `table[s & (T-1)]` (SAT) | observable = the address trace |
| D | `k` load-add-store accesses at word offsets `i + j` (mod T) of one symbolic base index `i` over a T-word table: the code side is the `sym.rs`-style eq/ite chain over a store log, the reference a per-word array with a binary mux tree; the data operation is the code's `add` against the reference's alternative encoding of it (so that the concrete-offset rows are not collapsed) | as stated |
| D2, D3, D4 | D2: `i` a constant (concrete offsets). D3: D2 for every `i = 0 .. T-1` as T obligations, `conflicts` the sum and `worst_step_conflicts` the max. D4: per access, fresh code and reference tables joined by equality premises, both sides built with the same mux structure; both modes | |

**Limits of the D shape** (item 7 of section 8.4): the k accesses share one
symbolic base index, as the fields of one thread-table entry do; k *independent*
symbolic indices (a product of T^k cases when split) are not measured, and `i` has
log2 T bits, so the out-of-range case of section 4.3 does not arise.

**Which part runs in CI.** BUILD runs the unit tests, `run --tier quick` and
`check` of the committed results file. The quick tier is the smallest knob of
every experiment at all recorded seeds (at least 5); `check` re-runs exactly those
rows and compares the digest, verdict, control and conflict count (section 9.6).
The full tier is a recorded offline run, committed by a person who regenerated it
(section 9.6); BUILD never runs it.

**Fields added to the row** (section 9.6): `tier` (`quick` or `full`), `steps`
(the number of obligations of a chain or split), `worst_step_solve_ms`, and
`metrics` (an object of experiment-specific numbers: E1 and E2 counts and rates, the
divergence actually applied). For a chain or split row `solve_ms` and `check_ms`
are totals, `drat_bytes`, `check_work` and `pass_default_limits` describe the
**worst obligation** (the green predicate of section 8.3 is per obligation), and the
solve-time clause of green uses `worst_step_solve_ms`.

**Timeouts.** The solver loop stops itself at 120 s (cap `wall`, result `unknown`);
the control stage has its own 30 s; the parent kills the process group at 300 s
if the child is still running in any stage (result `killed`, cap `wall`).

**Deviations from the text above, with the reason.**

* E1 and E2 rows have no solver fields (`null`); their "seeds" are repetitions
  (the counts are deterministic, the rates are samples, and the table shows the
  slowest). Their `result` is `pass`, `fail` or `refused` (the closed set of
  section 9.6 gains `pass` and `fail` for them) and their control is `caught`:
  a broken adapter that the harness reports failing, or a binder width over the
  cap that the generator refuses. A refused E1 row carries `result = "refused"`
  and `green = false`; BUILD expects exactly that row (section 9.3).
* **B4 starts at three terms.** `n = 2` is `a + b` against `a + b`: one adder,
  hash-consed into itself, a collapse by construction and not a re-association.
* **D sweep.** `T` in 4, 8, 16, 64, 256 and `k` in 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 (a grid of 25
  knobs per experiment); D4 and B3 run both modes.
* **The incremental mode is informational** and logs no proof: its `green` is the
  same predicate without the DRAT and default-limit clauses, and the decision
  never reads it.
* **A chain's `green`** is the section 8.3 predicate per obligation: the worst
  obligation's conflicts and solve time, the largest DRAT, the checker accepting
  every obligation. The total is in `conflicts` and `solve_ms`.
* **E1 digests the document.** An E1 row's `instance_sha256` is the SHA-256 of the
  Warrant document the harness was generated from, not of the generated text: a
  regeneration of a harness by a newer generator must not break a probe whose
  claim set is unchanged, while a changed claim changes the document and the
  case counts.
* **The real scheduler's harness installs its allocation observer as the
  probe's global allocator** (a generated `unsafe impl GlobalAlloc`, inert
  outside an observed call: one thread-local check per allocation). It changes no
  conflict or work count; the probe cannot avoid it without editing a generated
  file (backlog VOS-D091).

### 9.11 The decision procedure, as built

`decide` (and the line printed under the generated table) applies section 8.3 to
the rows. The definitions it fixes, where 8.3 left them to the implementation:

* A **group** is every seed of one `(experiment, mode, knob)`. A **counted** row
  is one that is not `collapsed`. A group is **green** iff at least one row is
  counted and every counted row is green. A group that is missing from the file,
  or whose every seed is collapsed, is **not green** (unclassified is red).
* **Row 1** (infeasible) holds iff any of these required shapes is not green, at
  the finest decomposition tested: A1 at N = 32; D3 at T = 8 with k = 16 (one
  obligation per index); B3 in certified mode with a cut after every instruction
  at 200 instructions and 25% divergence; C1 on a B body of 200 instructions at
  25% divergence.
* **Row 2** (feasible, reduced and mitigated) holds, if row 1 does not, iff any
  of: B3 (certified, 25% divergence) is green at 1,000 instructions with a cut
  after every instruction but not with a cut every 32 (green only with cuts at 8
  instructions or fewer), or is green at 200 instructions but not at 1,000 (only
  up to about 200); D3 at k = 16 is not green at T = 64 or at T = 256 (green only
  for T at most 16); B4 re-association is not green at some n of 5, 6, 7 (only n at
  most 4).
* **Row 3** (feasible as planned) holds, if neither row 1 nor row 2 does, iff all
  of: A1 and A2 are green at N = 32 and 64; B3 (certified, 25% divergence) is
  green at 1,000 instructions for some cut granularity, with a worst step of at
  most 10,000 conflicts and a total (solve plus check) of at most 60 s; D2 and D3
  are green at T = 64, k = 16; C1 on a 1,000-instruction body at 25% divergence is
  green.
* If none of the three holds, the outcome is row 1 and the printed reasons say
  which row-3 condition failed.

The unit tests of `results.rs` carry a negative control for every clause.

## 10. What VOS-029 must deliver

**Status after VOS-028b: not funded.** The probe's decision is row 1 of section 8.3
(section 8.5), so VOS-029 is a *design for a re-plan*, not a work item: what
follows records what the route would need, and the probe's numbers say which parts
are cheap (certified per-instruction cuts, A1 and A2, a cut between accesses on small
tables) and which are not (monolithic miters from 50 instructions, self-composition
without relational cuts, a whole table compared after every access above 16 words).

VOS-029 is the symbolic RV32I executor and the first machine proof (VER13
section 4.3 item 2). It is **designed around the compositional route**, with cut
points, per-path splitting and concretised addresses from its first line, never
added as later mitigations. It is funded only if VOS-028b's decision is one of the
first two rows of section 8.3.

1. **An RV32I symbolic executor** over `riscv-simulator::decode`, with
   concrete-PC path enumeration (section 4.1), a fork limit that fails closed,
   and a **differential against the concrete simulator** (section 7): random
   states, instruction by instruction, plus the SAT-control replay.
2. **Per-path obligations plus a coverage obligation** (section 3.1): every
   path has its refinement, closure and frame obligations, and the entry has one
   coverage obligation listing every path, feasible or not.
3. **Cut-point support with certified composition.** This needs either:
   * route A of section 6.1 (a CNF per segment with the hypotheses as clauses
     and the manifest recording them), which works today; or
   * route B: `cdcl-sat` logging the failed-assumption clause as a DRAT lemma,
     bound to `(original CNF, stated clause)`, hypotheses as assumptions only,
     and checked by appending the negated clause as units and running the
     existing `drat-check` (no new accept mode; VOS-D045, VER-SAT2).
   Today a `solve_with_assumptions` UNSAT leaves no checkable lemma. The
   composition checker of section 6.2 (which regenerates CNFs and the hypothesis
   pairing and trusts no manifest field) is part of the deliverable, together with
   the model-decomposition obligation (section 3.2a), the FRAME conjunct in every
   cut relation (section 4.2), the witnesses of section 4.6 (one replayed end to
   end on the concrete simulator), and the access obligations of section 3.4.
4. **Index case-splitting** (section 4.3) and concrete frame addresses
   (section 4.4).
5. **A streaming proof sink.** `cdcl-sat` keeps the whole DRAT in memory
   (`solver.rs:375`); about 1.7 GB of resident memory per million conflicts
   makes proof memory the first resource to run out. Either the obligations stay
   under 100k conflicts (about 175 MB) or a streaming sink lands first
   (VOS-D044).
6. **`pick` proved on the compiled image at the shipped N**, with a
   **non-collapsed certificate**, a **SAT sabotage control** (a mutated image
   that must fail with a replayed counterexample) and **manifest-derived
   labels** (section 5).
7. **A re-run of probe tiers B and D on the real compiled `pick`, `insert` and
   `remove`**, replacing the proxy rows with real ones. Until then, every number in
   this document is a proxy number (the validity limits of section 8.4).

Not in VOS-029: trap-entry and CSR modelling (a later slice, with
VOS-020/VOS-021), the image checks of section 3.7 beyond `pick`'s own image,
two-safety on a real entry (VOS-026 and after), and the owned-compile lane.

## 11. Open questions and findings

### 11.1 Open questions

1. **Cut relation generation.** Cuts are easy at source-aligned points and hard
   at arbitrary instruction boundaries of compiled code. Which `rustc` output
   shapes admit a clean `R`? The probe cannot answer it (synthetic code);
   VOS-029 will, per entry.
2. **Does `alpha` itself fit a cone?** `alpha` over a 64-entry table is itself a
   circuit with table structure. The calibration did not measure the model-side
   cost separately.
3. **Memory models.** Byte-addressed loads and stores (`lbu`, `sb`) into a
   word-organized table add alignment cases the probe covers only
   lightly (D1 uses word accesses; `lbu`/`sb` appear in A and B).
4. **Incremental versus per-CNF.** If route B lands, is one incremental certified
   solver faster than many CNFs, or does the learned-clause database of an
   incremental run slow later segments? **Answered in part by B3 (VOS-028b):** at
   1,000 per-instruction segments one incremental solver was about forty times
   slower than a fresh CNF per segment (about two minutes against about three
   seconds); the incremental mode logs no proof, so route B's certified form is
   still unmeasured.
5. **Equivalence sweeping.** VOS00 section 12 row 9 (arrays, incremental solving,
   k-induction, and the planned equivalence-sweeping work) would make the
   monolithic route cheaper. The probe's decision table keeps it as the
   `Infeasible` row's escape hatch.
6. **The owned-compile lane.** If the decision is the third row, the lane with a
   source map (VER13 section 4.3 item 1) is the way to get cuts by construction.
   Its cost (a new back end) is outside this program's current plan.
