You'll want to know: ちゅうい.
ちゅうい — chūi — "caution", "take care". It heads warning signs and notices. The small ゅ joins ち into one beat, chu, and う holds it long: chū, then i. On a real sign this word is usually printed in kanji, which this book has not reached yet; the reading is the same.
くるまに ちゅうい — kuruma ni chūi — "Watch out for cars."
Read a notice like this and act on it: stop, look, and cross with care.

You'll want to know: きんえん.
きんえん — kin'en — "no smoking". The apostrophe in the romanization keeps kin and en apart: four beats, ki–n–e–n. On a real sign this word is usually printed in kanji, which this book has not reached yet; the reading is the same.
きんえん (kin'en) ここで たばこを すわないで ください。 — koko de tabako o suwanaide kudasai — "Please do not smoke here."
すう, suu, the verb you know for breathing in, also means to smoke. Its negative stem すわ with ないで ください asks you politely not to do it.

You'll want to know: うけつけ.
うけつけ — uketsuke — "reception", "a reception desk": where you check in at a hospital, an office or a school. On a real sign this word is usually printed in kanji, which this book has not reached yet; the reading is the same.
うけつけで なまえを かいて ください。 — uketsuke de namae o kaite kudasai — "Please write your name at reception."
かく, kaku, "to write", becomes かいて before ください. The notice tells you what to do: go to the desk, and write your name.

You'll want to know: えいぎょうちゅう.
えいぎょうちゅう — eigyōchū — "open", "open for business", on a small sign hung on a shop or restaurant door. ちゅう at the end means "in the middle of": the shop is in the middle of doing business. Eight signs make six beats, e–i–gyo–o–chu–u: each small sign joins the one before it, and each う holds the sound before it long. On a real sign this word is usually printed in kanji, which this book has not reached yet; the reading is the same.
Read it and act on it: the door says えいぎょうちゅう (eigyōchū), so you can go in.

You'll want to know: じゅんびちゅう.
じゅんびちゅう — junbichū — "getting ready", "not yet open": the other side of the same door sign. じゅんび is preparation, and ちゅう again means "in the middle of". On a real sign this word is usually printed in kanji, which this book has not reached yet; the reading is the same.

いまは じゅんびちゅうです。 — ima wa junbichū desu — "We are getting ready now; not open yet."
