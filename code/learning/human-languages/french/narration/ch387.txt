French, chapter 387: Au tribunal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le procès — the trial.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the complaint, then the French for the misdemeanour.

You'll want to know: le procès.
le procès — "the trial".
Le procès commence la semaine prochaine. The trial starts next week.
Faire un procès à quelqu'un — to sue someone.

The word, taken apart.
procès comes from Latin prōcessus "a going forward, progress" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from prōcēdere "to go forward", built on cēdere "to go".

You'll want to know: le verdict.
le verdict — "the verdict".
Le verdict tombe demain. The verdict comes tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
verdict was borrowed from English verdict, from Medieval Latin vērēdictum "a thing truly said", built from vērē "truly" and dictum "said" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: coupable.
coupable — "guilty".
Pour le juge, il est coupable. For the judge, he is guilty.
Also a noun: le coupable — the culprit.

The word, taken apart.
coupable is inherited from Latin culpābilis "blameworthy" (EtymDB), from culpa "fault, blame" (Lewis & Short).
