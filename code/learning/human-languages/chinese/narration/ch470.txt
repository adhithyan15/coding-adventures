Mandarin Chinese, chapter 470: Faults.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zìsī — selfish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for steady and reliable, then the Mandarin for an impatient sort.

You'll want to know: zìsī.
zìsī — "selfish".
wǒ zuì bù xǐhuan zìsī de rén — Selfish people are the ones I like least.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zìsī is built from 自 'self' and sī 'private', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzijH and sij (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'not public'.

Lesson 2 of 5.

gùzhí — "stubborn".

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gùzhí is built from gù 'firm' and zhí 'to hold', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuH and tsyip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'firm' and the second 'to hold' and 'to keep': holding on to one's view.

Lesson 3 of 5.
jiāo'ào — proud; full of oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for selfish, then the Mandarin for stubborn.

You'll want to know: jiāo'ào.
jiāo'ào — "proud; full of oneself".
tā hěn cōngming kěshì yǒudiǎnr jiāo'ào — He's very clever, but a bit full of himself.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiāo'ào is built from jiāo and ào 'haughty', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjew and ngawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as a horse six feet tall and the second 'arrogant'; the sense 'proud' for the first is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mǎhu — sloppy; slapdash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for stubborn, then the Mandarin for full of oneself.

You'll want to know: mǎhu.
mǎhu — "sloppy; slapdash".
tā zuò zuòyè hěn mǎhu — He's slapdash about his homework.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mǎhu is built from 马 'horse' and hǔ 'tiger', both inherited from Middle Chinese, maeX and xuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How a horse and a tiger came to mean 'careless' is not settled; the second syllable is said light.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bèn — slow-witted; clumsy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for full of oneself, then the Mandarin for slapdash.

You'll want to know: bèn.
bèn — "slow-witted; clumsy".
wǒ gǎnjué zìjǐ hěn bèn — I feel really stupid.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bèn is inherited from Middle Chinese bwonX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the inside of a bamboo stem; the dictionary also records a fat man nicknamed with this word. The sense 'stupid' is not given there, so how it came is not settled.
