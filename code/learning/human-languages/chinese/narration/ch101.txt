Lesson 1 of 6.
chúfáng — kitchen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for library, then the Mandarin for post office.

You'll want to know: chúfáng.
chúfáng — "kitchen".
māma zài chúfáng — Mum's in the kitchen.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chúfáng is built from chú and fáng 'room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drju and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as the house where food is cooked.

Lesson 2 of 6.
白菜 (báicài) — Chinese cabbage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for post office, then the Mandarin for kitchen.

You'll want to know: 白菜.
白菜 — báicài — "Chinese cabbage".
我去洗白菜 wǒ qù xǐ báicài — I'll go and wash the cabbage.
The new character, in pieces: 菜 (cài) — the grass piece on top, three strokes; under it a hand reaching down, four strokes; and at the foot the tree piece of 机, standing alone. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
白菜 (báicài) is built from 白 'white' and 菜 (cài) 'vegetable', both inherited from Middle Chinese, baek and tshojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary says every plant that can be eaten is called tshojH; the white one is probably named for its pale stalks.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Script — the grass piece, a hand, the tree piece.
菜 (cài).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The grass piece, three strokes: a horizontal across the top, then two short verticals through it, left before right.
The hand, four strokes: a left-falling stroke across, two dots under it, left then middle, and a short left-falling stroke on the right.
The tree piece, four strokes, full width at the foot: a horizontal, a vertical down through it, a left-falling stroke and a long right-falling one.
3 + 4 + 4 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 菜 (cài), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 菜 (cài)? (Three — the grass piece, a hand, the tree piece.) Which character you know has the tree piece? (机, on its left.) How does 菜 (cài) end? (With a long right-falling stroke.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
kuàizi — chopsticks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for kitchen, then the Mandarin for cabbage.

You'll want to know: kuàizi.
kuàizi — "chopsticks".
wǒ de kuàizi ne — Where are my chopsticks?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kuàizi is built from kuài and the noun ending zi. The ending is inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.
