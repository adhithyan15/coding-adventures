Portuguese, chapter 348: Causas e efeitos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o efeito — the effect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to rebut, then the Portuguese for relevant.

You'll want to know: o efeito.
o efeito — "the effect".
O remédio fez efeito depressa. The medicine took effect quickly.
Fazer efeito — to take effect; com efeito — indeed.

The word, taken apart.
efeito is inherited from Latin effectus "an effect, a result" (EtymDB), from efficere "to bring about", built from ex and facere "to do, to make" (Lewis & Short); -ct- became -it- as in feito.

Lesson 2 of 5.
provocar — to bring about, to cause; to provoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for relevant, then the Portuguese for the effect.

You'll want to know: provocar.
provocar — "to bring about, to cause; to provoke".
A chuva forte provocou muitos acidentes. The heavy rain caused many accidents.
Also to provoke or tease a person.

The word, taken apart.
provocar is a learned borrowing from Latin prōvocāre "to call forth, to call out, to challenge" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built from prō and vocāre "to call".

You'll want to know: causar.

The word, taken apart.
causar is built on causa "cause" with the verb ending -ar (EtymDB); causa comes from Latin causa "reason, cause, lawsuit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o fator — the factor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to bring about, then the Portuguese for to cause.

You'll want to know: o fator.
o fator — "the factor".
O preço é um fator importante na escolha. The price is an important factor in the choice.
European fator (AO90); older spelling factor.

The word, taken apart.
fator is a learned borrowing from Latin factor "a maker, a doer, a performer" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from facere "to make"; Portuguese also has the inherited form feitor "steward".

Lesson 5 of 5.
a consequência — the consequence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a consequência.
a consequência — "the consequence".
Ele não pensou nas consequências. He did not think about the consequences.
Em consequência de — as a result of.

The word, taken apart.
consequência is a learned borrowing from Latin cōnsequentia "a consequence, a natural succession" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from cōnsequī "to follow after", built on sequī "to follow".
