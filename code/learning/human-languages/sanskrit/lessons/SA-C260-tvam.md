# त्वाम् (tvām) — you, one familiar person, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C260-MAM-01, SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say “he sees me”: **सः माम् पश्यति** (*saḥ mām paśyati*). Keep
the frame and turn the object toward one familiar person.

## You'll want to know: त्वाम्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02]; assesses=[] -->

**त्वाम्** (*tvām*) means “you” when one familiar addressee is the object.

> **सः त्वाम् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ tvām paśyati.*
>
> **He sees you.**

The opening **tv-** also appears in familiar **tvam** and **tava**. Here the
long **ā** and final **m** belong to the independent accusative singular.
Whitney §491 places **त्वाम्** beside **माम्** in the first- and second-person
paradigms.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **त्वाम्** comes from the second-person pronoun
paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit
Grammar* §491 records it as the independent accusative singular. This lesson
makes no deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
