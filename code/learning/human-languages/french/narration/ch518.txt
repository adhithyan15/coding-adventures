French, chapter 518: Une exposition.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'exposition — exhibition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for marble, then the French for sculptor.

You'll want to know: l'exposition.
l'exposition — "exhibition".
Nous avons vu une exposition de photos. We saw an exhibition of photos.
Feminine. Informally une expo.

The word, taken apart.
l'exposition is a learned borrowing from Latin expositiō "a setting out" (EtymDB), from expōnere "to put out", built on pōnere "to put" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le vernissage — private view, opening (of an exhibition).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sculptor, then the French for exhibition.

You'll want to know: le vernissage.
le vernissage — "private view, opening (of an exhibition)".
Elle nous a invités au vernissage de son exposition. She invited us to the opening of her exhibition.
First the last coat of varnish, put on the day before the show.

The word, taken apart.
le vernissage is built on vernir "to varnish" with -age (EtymDB): painters once varnished their works on the eve of the show. The origin of vernis is usually traced to Medieval Latin veronix.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la galerie — gallery; arcade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for exhibition, then the French for private view.

You'll want to know: la galerie.
la galerie — "gallery; arcade".
Cette galerie vend les tableaux de jeunes artistes. This gallery sells paintings by young artists.
Une galerie marchande — a shopping arcade.

The word, taken apart.
la galerie was borrowed from Italian galleria, from Medieval Latin galeria. The earlier origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'œuvre — work (of art).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for private view, then the French for gallery.

You'll want to know: l'œuvre.
l'œuvre — "work (of art)".
Le musée garde les œuvres d'un grand peintre. The museum holds the works of a great painter.
Feminine for a single work: une œuvre d'art. Un chef-d'œuvre — a masterpiece.

The word, taken apart.
l'œuvre is inherited from Latin opera "work, effort" (EtymDB), related to opus "a work" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
encadrer — to put in a frame; to supervise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for gallery, then the French for work.

You'll want to know: encadrer.
encadrer — "to put in a frame; to supervise".
J'ai fait encadrer une vieille photo de mes parents. I had an old photo of my parents framed.
Also to supervise: encadrer une équipe.

The word, taken apart.
encadrer is built from en- and cadre "frame", borrowed from Italian quadro, from Latin quadrum "a square" (Lewis & Short).
