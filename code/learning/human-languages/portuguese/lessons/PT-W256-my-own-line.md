# Your own line — the first writing with no model

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-MORAR-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Everything you have written in Portuguese so far had something to
reach for — a shape to trace, a word to copy, a sound to take down.

[PAUSE 1s] This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed:
only what this book has taught you.

## Writing — controlled composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-ME-CHAMO-04, PT-GRAMMAR-MORAR-04, PT-LEX-FALO-PORTUGUES-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **one line** about yourself: your name, where you live, and a
language you speak. The verbs are ones you already hold — **chamo-me**,
**moro**, **falo** — and the *-o* already says "I", so no **eu** is needed.

[PAUSE 2s] One thing to decide on purpose before you start: **the marks are
letters, not decoration.**

[PAUSE 2s] The marks over a Portuguese vowel do two different jobs. The accent
in **português** marks the vowel that carries the stress. The **til** in **pão**
and **irmã** is not an accent at all: it marks a **nasal** vowel, a sound
rather than a stress.

[PAUSE 2s] A hand that writes **pao** for **pão** has not written Portuguese
untidily. It has written a word Portuguese does not have, and left the reader
to guess.

[PAUSE 3s] Write the line. Then read it back and check every mark **before** you
check anything else.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-FALO-PORTUGUES-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Is the til on **pão** an accent? (**No** — it marks a nasal vowel,
not the stress.)

[PAUSE 2s] And what did you choose here that the earlier writing lessons chose
for you? (**The words.**)
