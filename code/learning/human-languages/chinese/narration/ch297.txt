Lesson 1 of 6.
bǎoxiǎn — insurance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a beginner at the wheel, then the Mandarin for to pay for damage.

You'll want to know: bǎoxiǎn.
bǎoxiǎn — "insurance".
wǒ de chē yǒu bǎoxiǎn — My car is insured.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: bǎo is said with a rising tone before xiǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
bǎoxiǎn is built from bǎo 'to guard' and xiǎn 'danger', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pawX and xjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to guard' and 'safe' and the second 'danger': a guard against danger.

Lesson 2 of 6.
事故 (shìgù) — accident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay for damage, then the Mandarin for insurance for the car.

You'll want to know: 事故.
事故 — shìgù — "accident".
这个路口常常发生事故 zhège lùkǒu chángcháng fāshēng shìgù — Accidents often happen at this crossing.
The new character, in pieces: 故 (gù) — nine strokes in two pieces, the right two pieces of 做 without its person piece: on the left 十 over 口, five strokes; on the right the striking piece, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
事故 (shìgù) is built from 事 'a matter' and 故 (gù), both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzriH and kuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'old' and 'a matter'; how the pair came to mean an accident is not settled.

Script — 十 over 口, then the striking piece.
故 (gù).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, five strokes. 十: a bar and a vertical through it. Under it 口: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, and the bar that closes the foot.
The right piece, four strokes. The striking piece of 做: a sweep down to the left, a bar across to the right, a long sweep down to the lower left, and a long stroke falling to the right across it.
5 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 故 (gù), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 故 (gù)? (Two.) Which character has the same two pieces on its right? (做.) What is on the left? (十 over 口.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
zérèn — responsibility; blame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for insurance for the car, then the Mandarin for a serious accident.

You'll want to know: zérèn.
zérèn — "responsibility; blame".
shìgù fāshēng yǐhòu, sījī shuō shì tā de zérèn — After the accident, the driver said it was his fault.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zérèn is built from zé 'to demand' and rèn 'to bear', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsreak and nyim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to seek' and the second 'able to bear', and lists the second also in the departing tone, the source of rèn.

后果 — hòuguǒ — "consequences; what comes of it".
kāichē tài kuài, hòuguǒ huì hěn yánzhòng — If you drive too fast, the consequences will be serious.
