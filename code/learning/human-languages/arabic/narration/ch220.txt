Arabic, chapter 220: Card, Loan, Goods, Account, Butcher.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بطاقة (biṭāqa) — a card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for copper, then the Arabic for silk.

You'll want to know: بطاقة.
بطاقة — biṭāqa — "a card".

The word, taken apart.
بطاقة (biṭāqa) is listed by Lane under b-ṭ-q. He reports a native explanation that his sources already rejected, and suggests, after Freytag, that it probably comes from Greek pittakion, "label, ticket".

Lesson 2 of 5.
قرض (qarḍ) — a loan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for silk, then the Arabic for a card.

You'll want to know: قرض.
قرض — qarḍ — "a loan".

The word, taken apart.
قرض (qarḍ) is built on the native Arabic root q-r-ḍ, "to cut off; to lend" (Lane). Lane's qarḍ is a loan, a portion cut off from one's goods.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: حساب.
حساب — ḥisāb — "an account".

The word, taken apart.
حساب (ḥisāb) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-s-b, "to count, to reckon" (Lane). Ḥisāb is the verbal noun, "reckoning".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

جزار — jazzār — "a butcher".

The word, taken apart.
جزار (jazzār) is built on the native Arabic root j-z-r, "to cut; to slaughter" (Lane). Jazzār is the faʿʿāl trade noun, "slaughterer".
