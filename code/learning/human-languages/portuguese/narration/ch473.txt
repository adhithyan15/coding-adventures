Portuguese, chapter 473: Gentis e grosseiros.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a gentileza — kindness, courtesy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to suspect, then the Portuguese for suspicious.

You'll want to know: a gentileza.
a gentileza — "kindness, courtesy".
Foi uma gentileza do vizinho ajudar-nos com as malas. It was kind of the neighbour to help us with the cases.
Por gentileza — please, kindly (a little formal).

The word, taken apart.
gentileza is built on gentil "kind, courteous", from Latin gentīlis "of the same clan" (Lewis & Short), built on gēns "a clan, a family".

Foi muito delicado da tua parte telefonar. It was very thoughtful of you to phone.
Of things it means delicate or fragile: uma situação delicada.

The word, taken apart.
delicado is a learned borrowing from Latin dēlicātus "charming, dainty, delicate" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for kindness, then the Portuguese for polite.

You'll want to know: grosseiro.
grosseiro — "rude, coarse".
O empregado foi grosseiro com os clientes. The waiter was rude to the customers.
Of cloth or work: rough, badly made.

The word, taken apart.
grosseiro is built on grosso "thick, coarse", which is inherited from Late Latin grossus "thick" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
antipático — unfriendly, unpleasant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for polite, then the Portuguese for rude.

You'll want to know: antipático.
antipático — "unfriendly, unpleasant".
O novo vizinho é um pouco antipático. The new neighbour is a bit unfriendly.
The opposite of simpático.

The word, taken apart.
antipático is built on antipatia "dislike", a learned borrowing from Greek antipátheia "antipathy" (EtymDB), built on anti "against" and páthos "feeling".
