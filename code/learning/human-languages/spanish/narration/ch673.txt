Spanish, chapter 673: Hacia dónde.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dirigirse — to head (for), to make your way; to speak to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the signs, then the Spanish for the diversion.

You'll want to know: dirigirse.
dirigirse — "to head (for), to make your way; to speak to".
Nos dirigimos a la salida. We are heading for the exit.
Dirigirse a alguien — to address someone. The g becomes j: me dirijo.

The word, taken apart.
dirigirse is built on dirigir, a learned borrowing from Latin dīrigere "to lay straight, to direct" (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
acortar — to shorten; to cut short.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the diversion, then the Spanish for to head.

You'll want to know: acortar.
acortar — "to shorten; to cut short".
Por el parque acortamos camino. Going through the park shortens the way.
Acortar camino — to take a short cut; el atajo is the short cut itself.

The word, taken apart.
acortar is built on corto "short", which is inherited from Latin curtus "shortened, incomplete" (EtymDB); EtymDB also links it with cortar "to cut", from the same Latin word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
retroceder — to go back, to back up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to head, then the Spanish for to shorten.

You'll want to know: retroceder.
retroceder — "to go back, to back up".
Tuvimos que retroceder dos kilómetros. We had to go back two kilometres.
In a car, dar marcha atrás is to reverse.

The word, taken apart.
retroceder is a learned borrowing from Latin retrōcēdere "to go back, to retire" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from retrō "back" and cēdere "to go".

rodear — "to go round; to surround".
Rodeamos el lago a pie. We walked round the lake.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bordear — to go along the edge of, to skirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to go back, then the Spanish for to go round.

You'll want to know: bordear.
bordear — "to go along the edge of, to skirt".
El camino bordea la costa. The path runs along the coast.
Close to rodear, but along the edge rather than all the way round.

The word, taken apart.
bordear is built on borde "edge", which came from French bord (EtymDB), a word of Germanic origin.
