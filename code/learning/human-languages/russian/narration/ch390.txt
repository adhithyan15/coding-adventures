Russian, chapter 390: Keeping the rules.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
соблюдать (soblyudát) — to observe, to keep (a rule).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for permission, then the Russian for to let.

You'll want to know: соблюдать.
соблюдать (soblyudát) — "to observe, to keep (a rule)".
Все должны соблюдать правила. Vse dolzhný soblyudát právila. Everyone must observe the rules.
Imperfective; perfective соблюсти, rare. Соблюдать закон, диету, тишину.

The word, taken apart.
соблюдать (soblyudát) is built from со- and блюсти "to guard, to keep", an old Slavic verb also behind наблюдать "to observe".

Lesson 2 of 5.
обязательный (obyazátelnyy) — compulsory, obligatory.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Russian for to let, then the Russian for to observe.

You'll want to know: обязательный.
обязательный (obyazátelnyy) — "compulsory, obligatory".
Этот экзамен обязательный для всех. Étot ekzámen obyazátelnyy dlya vsekh. This exam is compulsory for everyone.
Of a person it means reliable: он очень обязательный (obyazátelnyy).

The word, taken apart.
обязательный (obyazátelnyy) is built on обязать "to oblige", from о- and вязать "to tie", Proto-Slavic vęzati: what you are bound to do.

Lesson 3 of 5.
порядок (poryádok) — order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to observe, then the Russian for compulsory.

You'll want to know: порядок.
порядок (poryádok) — "order".
В его комнате всегда порядок. V yegó kómnate vsegdá poryádok. His room is always tidy.
Masculine; genitive порядка. Всё в порядке — everything is all right. По порядку — in order.

The word, taken apart.
порядок (poryádok) is built from по- and ряд "a row, a line", inherited from Proto-Slavic rędŭ: order is things set in rows.

Lesson 4 of 5.
право (právo) — a right; law (as a subject).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for compulsory, then the Russian for order.

You'll want to know: право.
право (právo) — "a right; law (as a subject)".
Каждый имеет право на отдых. Kázhdyy iméyet právo na ótdykh. Everyone has the right to rest.
Neuter. Иметь право (právo) на + accusative, or + infinitive. It is also law as a subject: изучать право (právo).

The word, taken apart.
право (právo) is built on правый "right, correct", inherited from Proto-Slavic pravŭ "right, correct" (EtymDB): a right is what is due by what is correct.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ответственность (otvétstvennost) — responsibility.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for order, then the Russian for a right.

You'll want to know: ответственность.
ответственность (otvétstvennost) — "responsibility".
Родители несут ответственность за детей. Rodíteli nesút otvétstvennost za detéy. Parents bear responsibility for their children.
Feminine. Нести ответственность (otvétstvennost) за + accusative — to bear responsibility for.

The word, taken apart.
ответственность (otvétstvennost) is built on ответственный "responsible", from ответ "an answer", from от- "back" and Old Russian větŭ "counsel": having to answer for something.
