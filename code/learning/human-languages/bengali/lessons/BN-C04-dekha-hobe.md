# দেখা হবে (dækhā hôbe) — "(we) will meet"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A future-tense phrase — the other half of "see you again," built in a
way English never would.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-CONCEPT-C04-DEKHAHOBE-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01] -->

**দেখা** (*dækhā*, "seeing") = **দে** (*dæ*) + **খা** (*khā*). **হবে** (*hôbe*,
"will be/happen") = **হ** (*hô*) + **বে** (*be*).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01, BN-CONCEPT-C04-DEKHAHOBE-01] -->

**দেখা হবে** literally means "**[a] seeing will happen**" — *dækhā* (the noun
"seeing," from **দেখা** *dækhā* "to see") + *hôbe* ("will be/happen," the future
of **হওয়া** *hôwā*, "to be, to become"). Bengali says "a meeting will occur"
rather than "we will meet" — an impersonal, gentle way to promise reunion. The
verb *hôwā* ("to be/become") is the same one behind *āchhā*'s cousin, and its
root **ভূ / হ** ties (distantly) to English **be**.

## Grammar Lens: the impersonal future
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-TUMIKEMONACHHO-01, BN-CONCEPT-C04-DEKHAHOBE-01] -->

Many Bengali "we'll…" expressions use this **noun + hôbe** ("…will happen")
shape: *dækhā hôbe* ("[a] seeing will happen"), *kôthā hôbe* ("[a] talk will
happen"). No "we," no active verb — the event itself is the subject.
