Sanskrit, chapter 451: Distress and Comfort.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
व्यथते (vyathate) — is pained; is distressed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tears, then the Sanskrit for a sigh.

You'll want to know: व्यथते.
व्यथते — vyathate — "is pained; is distressed".
मित्रस्य दुःखेन मीरा व्यथते। mitrasya duḥkhena mīrā vyathate. Mira is pained by her friend's sorrow.
Past (she …): सा व्यथितवती, sā vyathitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
व्यथते (vyathate) comes from the root √vyath, "to tremble"; MW's ppp vyathita is "disquieted, distressed, afflicted" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for coaxes, then the Sanskrit for troubled.

You'll want to know: सन्तापः.
सन्तापः — santāpaḥ — "anguish".
मम हृदये महान् सन्तापः अस्ति। mama hṛdaye mahān santāpaḥ asti. There is great anguish in my heart.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सन्तापः (santāpaḥ) is built from sam and the root √tap, "to burn", the root of तापः: a burning through and through; MW gives "affliction, sorrow, anguish" (MW).
