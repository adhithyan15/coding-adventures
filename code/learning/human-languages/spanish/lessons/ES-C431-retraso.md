# el retraso — put further behind

## You'll want to know: el retraso
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**el retraso** — the delay.

**con retraso** — late, running behind. That is the phrase you will hear on a
platform: *el tren sale con veinte minutos de retraso*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-03] -->

**re-** again, plus **tras**, behind. Something put further behind than it
should be.

**tras** is worth a moment on its own, because it is hiding in words you will
meet all week:

| word | the behind in it |
|---|---|
| detrás | behind, in place |
| atrasar | to put back |
| el retraso | the being-behind |

Spanish also has **tardar**, to take time, from a different Latin word for
*slow*. Two families, one idea, and a speaker picks whichever fits: a train
*lleva retraso*, a journey *tarda* two hours.
