Urdu, chapter 143: A connected A2 message.
1 lesson. It needs your eyes or your hands, so save it for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
A connected message about your day — plan and write a 70–90 word Urdu message about a familiar day for a named reader and purpose.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A connected message is not one long sentence. It is a short route for one reader. Before writing, draw eight empty boxes and give them these jobs:
reader and greeting;
reason for writing;
where your day begins;
two things you usually do;
what happens next;
one contrast or reason;
one question for the reader;
a polite close.
Write only one or two English cue words in each box. Do not draft Urdu yet.

Writing — connected composition.
There is no model, sentence bank, or copyable answer on this page.
Reader: Sana. Purpose: help Sana understand your usual weekday and choose a good time to visit you tomorrow.
Write 70–90 words in Urdu script. Use every planning box. Include at least four familiar actions, two time anchors, one preference, one contrast or reason, one sequence link, and one direct question for Sana. Keep the reader visible by making every detail help with the visit. The instructions give content, not sentences: choose and connect the Urdu yourself.
This first connected attempt is untimed. Put away Roman Urdu, Devanagari, dictionaries, translators, and spell-checkers. Either approved Nastaliq or accessibility Naskh is fine. Ordinary legible Urdu handwriting is enough; imitating Nastaliq calligraphic slope or ligatures is not part of the task.
When the whole message exists, count its words once. If it is short, add one useful detail to a planning box. If it is long, remove a detail that does not help Sana. Do not replace the message with a memorised model.

Wrap-up Recall.
Review the same attempt in four separate passes:
Task fulfilment and relevant content — Sana, the weekday, tomorrow's visit, and every requested content point are present.
Comprehensibility and organisation — the eight jobs form a route rather than a list of disconnected sentences.
Urdu vocabulary, grammar, and register — connectors make addition, sequence, contrast, and reason clear in a polite everyday tone.
Urdu orthographic control — letters, dots, joins, non-joins, word boundaries, and punctuation remain readable. Unwritten short vowels and optional diacritics receive neither bonus nor penalty unless they change the intended word.
[pause 2 seconds]
Mark one strength and one next step. Repair only the next step; keep the original attempt so the next 70–90 word message can show real growth.
