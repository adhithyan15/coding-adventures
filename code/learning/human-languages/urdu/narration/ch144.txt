Urdu, chapter 144: Recently, Already, the Past, Last, To Happen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حال ہی میں (hāl hī meṅ) — recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to be saved, to avoid, then the Urdu for to grow, to increase.

You'll want to know: حال ہی میں.
حال ہی میں — hāl hī meṅ — "recently". With a verb like پڑھنا, Urdu's past puts نے after the doer: میں نے حال ہی میں ایک کتاب پڑھی۔ — maiṅ ne hāl hī meṅ ek kitāb paṛhī — "I read a book recently." پڑھی agrees with کتاب.

The phrase, taken apart.
حال ہی میں (hāl hī meṅ) is "in the very present state". hāl is a loan from Arabic ḥāl, "state, condition", through Persian (Lane, root ḥ-w-l); hī is the emphatic "very"; and meṅ, "in", comes from Sanskrit madhye, "in the middle" (CDIAL 9804).

Lesson 2 of 5.
پہلے سے (pehle se) — already, beforehand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to grow, to increase, then the Urdu for recently.

You'll want to know: پہلے سے.
پہلے سے — pehle se — "already, beforehand". It is پہلے, "before", with سے: میں پہلے سے جانتا ہوں۔ — maiṅ pehle se jāntā hūṅ — "I already know." A woman says جانتی.

The phrase, taken apart.
پہلے سے (pehle se) joins pehle, "first, before", and se, "from". pehle is the adverb of pahlā, which CDIAL lists under prathilla, a variant of a reconstructed prathila, "first" (CDIAL 8652). CDIAL lists se under Sanskrit sahita, "together with" (CDIAL 13310), and sama, "equal", has also been suggested (CDIAL 13173).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ماضی (māzī) — the past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for recently, then the Urdu for already.

You'll want to know: ماضی.
ماضی — māzī — "the past". Grammar books use it for the past tense too. It came into Urdu from Arabic.

The word, taken apart.
ماضی (māzī) is a loan from Arabic māḍī, "past, gone by", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is m-ḍ-y (Lane). Grammar books use it for the past tense.

Lesson 4 of 5.
گزشتہ (guzashtā) — last, previous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for already, then the Urdu for the past.

You'll want to know: گزشتہ.
گزشتہ — guzashtā — "last, previous". گزشتہ سال — guzashtā sāl — "last year". It is the formal word; everyday speech also says پچھلا.

The word, taken apart.
گزشتہ (guzashtā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian guzashta, "passed, gone by". It is the past participle of guzashtan, "to pass".

Lesson 5 of 5.
پیش آنا (pesh ānā) — to happen, to occur.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the past, then the Urdu for last.

You'll want to know: پیش آنا.
پیش آنا — pesh ānā — "to happen, to occur". Said of events: کل ایک مسئلہ پیش آیا۔ — kal ek mas'alā pesh āyā — "A problem came up yesterday." Its past is پیش آیا, or پیش آئی with a feminine subject.

The phrase, taken apart.
پیش آنا (pesh ānā) is "to come to the front". pesh, "front, before", is usually traced to Persian pesh; ānā, "to come", comes from Sanskrit āpayati, "causes to reach" (CDIAL 1200).
