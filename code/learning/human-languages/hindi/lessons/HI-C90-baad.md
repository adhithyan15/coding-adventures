# के बाद — the other end of the pair

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have **पहले** for *first*. Guess its partner, and then read on to
see what Hindi makes you put in front of it.

## You'll want to know: के बाद
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06]; assesses=[] -->

**बाद** (*bād*) — after. In a sentence it almost always arrives as **के बाद**.

**काम के बाद** — after work.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-01] -->

**बाद** comes from Arabic **بعد** (*baʿd*), "after" — a plain borrowing that
kept its job exactly.

What is Hindi's own is the **के** in front of it. A two-part postposition like
this one is built out of the possessive you already have: *the after **of**
work*. Hindi has a shelf of these, and they all share the shape:

| shape | example |
|---|---|
| noun + के + a word | काम के बाद |

So **के बाद** is not a new kind of thing. It is the possessive **का / के / की**
you met with *uskā* and *hamārā*, put to work as glue.

## Grammar Lens: the pair, and a day in order
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04] -->

| what you mean | the shape |
|---|---|
| first | पहले |
| before X | X से पहले |
| after X | X के बाद |

Notice the asymmetry: *before* takes **से** and *after* takes **के**. There is no
tidy reason to give you; it is a pair of fixed habits, and the way to keep them
apart is to say each one a few times rather than to reason about it.
