# आपको क्या चाहिए? — answering at a counter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04, HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say *to you* in its respectful form, and the word that marks a
question. Both have been yours for a while.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-02, HI-CONCEPT-C37-CHAI-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04] -->

| speaker | Hindi |
|---|---|
| A | आपको क्या चाहिए? |
| B | मुझे चाय चाहिए। |

The two lines are the **same sentence with the person swapped** — **आपको** for
**मुझे** — and **क्या** standing where the answer will go.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-02, HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01] -->

A listening item built on this keys on **the noun between the two fixed
words**. Everything else in B's reply is predictable:

1. The first word is the **dative person** — it tells you who, not what.
2. The last word is **चाहिए** — it never changes, so it carries no information.
3. **The answer is whatever sits between them.**

That is why the item is answerable even if the middle word is the only one you
catch. Listen to the middle.

## Grammar Lens: the chapter in one sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01, HI-LEX-C95-KNOW-02] -->

Hindi puts the person on the **receiving end** of wanting, and gives the wanting
word **no endings at all** — so the sentence's whole meaning lives in the noun
in the middle and the case of the word in front.

**क्या आप जानते हैं?** works the other way round: there the endings do all the
agreeing, because somebody is genuinely doing the knowing.
