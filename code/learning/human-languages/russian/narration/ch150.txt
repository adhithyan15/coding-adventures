Russian, chapter 150: Rarely, Every, At first, At last, Still.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
редко (rédko) — rarely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for dawn, then the Russian for sunset.

You'll want to know: редко.
редко (rédko) — "rarely".

The word, taken apart.
редко (rédko) is редкий, "sparse, rare", made into an adverb. Vasmer relates редкий to Lithuanian rẽtas, "sparse, rare", and Latin rēte, "a net": a net is all gaps.

Lesson 2 of 5.
каждый (kázhdyy) — every, each.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for sunset, then the Russian for rarely.

You'll want to know: каждый.
каждый (kázhdyy) — "every, each".

The word, taken apart.
каждый (kázhdyy) from Proto-Slavic kŭžĭdo — inherited: the old question stem of кто, "who", with же, a stressing particle, and -де, as in где, "where" (Vasmer). Gothic hvarjis, "which", is built in a similar way.

Lesson 3 of 5.
сначала (snachála) — at first.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for rarely, then the Russian for every.

You'll want to know: сначала.
сначала (snachála) — "at first".

The word, taken apart.
сначала (snachála) is с, "from", with начала, a form of начало, "the beginning": "from the start". Начало comes from начать, "to begin", which Vasmer puts in one family with конец, "end".

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Russian for every, then the Russian for at first.

You'll want to know: наконец.
наконец (nakonéts) — "at last, finally".

The word, taken apart.
наконец (nakonéts) is на, "at, on", with конец, "the end". Vasmer relates конец to Latin recēns, "fresh", and Old Irish cinim, "I arise".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ещё (yeshchyó) — still; more.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for at first, then the Russian for at last.

You'll want to know: ещё.
ещё (yeshchyó) — "still; more".

The word, taken apart.
ещё (yeshchyó) from Old Russian още, Old Church Slavonic ješte. Vasmer allows a link with Greek éti, "still", and Latin et, "and", plus an old kʷe, "and", but notes the sounds are hard to explain; Grappin traced it instead to jest-je.
