Latin, chapter 389: Duty and Freedom.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
servitūs, servitūtis — slavery, servitude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a companion, then the Latin for an association.

You'll want to know: servitūs, servitūtis.
servitūs, servitūtis — "slavery, servitude".
Lībertās servitūte melior est. Freedom is better than slavery.
servitūs, servitūtis f., built on servus, a slave. English servitude.

The word, taken apart.
servitūs is built on servus "a slave" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -tūs that makes a state.

Lesson 2 of 5.
manūmittō, manūmittere — to set free (a slave).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an association, then the Latin for slavery.

You'll want to know: manūmittō, manūmittere.
manūmittō, manūmittere — "to set free (a slave)".
Dominus servum fidēlem manūmīsit. The master freed his faithful slave.
manūmittō, manūmittere, manūmīsī, manūmissum: manū from the hand + mittō, to let go. The freed slave became a lībertus. English manumit.

The word, taken apart.
manūmittō is built from manū "from the hand", the ablative of manus, and mittō "to let go" (Lewis & Short): to release from one's power. English manumit comes from it.

Fīlius magnā pietāte patrem cūrābat. The son looked after his father with great devotion.
pietās, pietātis f., built on pius, dutiful: the loyalty owed to gods, parents and country. English piety and pity.

The word, taken apart.
pietās is built on pius "dutiful" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -tās that makes a quality. English piety and pity both come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
onus, oneris — a load, a burden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to set free, then the Latin for dutifulness.

You'll want to know: onus, oneris.
onus, oneris — "a load, a burden".
Asinus onus grave portat. The donkey carries a heavy load.
onus, oneris n. A load on a back, and a burden of duty or tax. English onus and onerous.

The word, taken apart.
onus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European noun meaning "a burden" (EtymDB). English took it over as onus, and onerous comes from its adjective.

Lesson 5 of 5.
obsequium, obsequiī — compliance, obedience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for dutifulness, then the Latin for a load.

You'll want to know: obsequium, obsequiī.
obsequium, obsequiī — "compliance, obedience".
Obsequium amīcōs, vēritās odium parit. Giving way wins friends; truth breeds hatred.
obsequium, obsequiī n., built on obsequor, to give way to someone. English obsequious.

The word, taken apart.
obsequium is built on obsequor "to comply" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from ob and sequor "to follow". English obsequious comes from its adjective.
