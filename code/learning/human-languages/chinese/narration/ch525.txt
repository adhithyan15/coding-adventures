Lesson 1 of 7.
zhōudào — attentive and thorough (of service).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to offer a lower price back, then the Mandarin for the cost of living.

You'll want to know: zhōudào.
zhōudào — "attentive and thorough (of service)".
zhè jiā fàndiàn de fúwùyuán bǐ nà jiā zhōudào — The waiters in this restaurant are more attentive than in that one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhōudào is built from zhōu and 到 'to reach', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyuw and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'all round', 'complete' and 'close' and the second 'to arrive': reaching all round.

Lesson 2 of 7.
格外 (géwài) — more than usual; unusually.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the cost of living, then the Mandarin for attentive and thorough.

You'll want to know: 格外.
格外 — géwài — "more than usual; unusually".
今天的菜格外好吃 jīntiān de cài géwài hǎochī — The food is especially good today.
The new character, in pieces: 格 (gé) — ten strokes in three pieces: on the left the tree piece, four strokes, as in 机; on the right the walking foot, three strokes, as in the middle of 客; and 口 under it, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
格外 (géwài) is built from 格 (gé) and 外 'outside', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaek and ngwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a pattern' and 'a measure', and lists another reading, kak, for a branch; outside the measure is beyond the usual, though how the pair came to mean 'especially' is not settled.

Script — the tree piece, then the walking foot over 口.
格 (gé).
Three pieces: the tree piece on the left, then the walking foot, then 口 under it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the tree piece, four strokes, narrow, as in 机: a short bar, a vertical down through it, a stroke falling to the left, then a short dot to the right.
The walking foot, three strokes, as in 客: a short stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns into a long stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right.
口, three strokes, at the foot: a vertical, a bar that turns down, and a bar closing it.
4 + 3 + 3 = ten.

Lesson 4 of 7.
shòuhuòyuán — shop assistant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for attentive and thorough, then the Mandarin for more than usual.

You'll want to know: shòuhuòyuán.
shòuhuòyuán — "shop assistant".
wǒ juéde zhèr de shòuhuòyuán zuì rèqíng — I think the shop assistants here are the friendliest.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shòuhuòyuán is built from shòu 'to sell', huò 'goods' and yuán 'a member of staff', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyuwH, xwaH and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to sell goods', the second 'wealth' and the third as the number of things; the sense 'staff' is not given there.
