## You'll want to know: नारी
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-C20-PERSON-02]; assesses=[] -->

**नारी** (*nārī*) — "woman."

Look at it beside नरः and the relationship is visible: the same *nar-*, a
different ending. Sanskrit builds a great many feminine nouns with a long
*-ī*, and this is your first.

So the map has grown again: *-am* neuter, *-aḥ* masculine, *-iḥ* a third family,
and now *-ī* feminine. Four endings, four families. You are not being asked to
produce them — only to hear them, because everything Sanskrit does with a noun
starts here.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*Nārī* is built from the stem of *nara*, "man", with the feminine ending *-ī*
(MW). It is already in the Rigveda and lives on as Hindi *nār*, "woman" (CDIAL
7078).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

A second person and a fourth ending. The system is bigger than two, exactly as
the night lesson warned.
