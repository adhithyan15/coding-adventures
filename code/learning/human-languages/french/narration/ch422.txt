5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le ferry — the ferry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to cancel, then the French for the overbooking.

You'll want to know: le ferry.
le ferry — "the ferry".
Nous prenons le ferry pour aller sur l'île. We take the ferry to go over to the island.
In Québec: le traversier.

The word, taken apart.
ferry was borrowed from English ferry (EtymDB), from a verb meaning 'to carry over water'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la traversée — the crossing (by sea).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the overbooking, then the French for the ferry.

You'll want to know: la traversée.
la traversée — "the crossing (by sea)".
La traversée dure quatre heures. The crossing takes four hours.
Built on traverser 'to cross'.

The word, taken apart.
traversée is built on traverser 'to cross', which goes back to Latin trānsversus 'lying across', from vertere 'to turn' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the noun.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la cabine — the cabin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the ferry, then the French for the crossing.

You'll want to know: la cabine.
la cabine — "the cabin".
Pour la nuit, ils ont pris une cabine avec deux lits. For the night they took a cabin with two beds.
Une cabine d'essayage — a fitting room.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of cabine is not settled; it is usually linked to a northern dialect word and to English cabin. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le gilet — the waistcoat; the cardigan; the life jacket (gilet de sauvetage).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the crossing, then the French for the cabin.

You'll want to know: le gilet.
le gilet — "the waistcoat; the cardigan; the life jacket (gilet de sauvetage)".
Sous chaque siège, il y a un gilet de sauvetage. Under every seat there is a life jacket.
Le gilet de sauvetage — the life jacket. Un gilet can also be a cardigan.

The word, taken apart.
gilet goes back to Turkish yelek 'waistcoat', which reached French through Arabic (EtymDB), probably by way of Spanish.

Lesson 5 of 5.
débarquer — to get off (a boat or plane), to land.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cabin, then the French for the waistcoat.

You'll want to know: débarquer.
débarquer — "to get off (a boat or plane), to land".
Les passagers débarquent sur le quai. The passengers get off onto the quay.
Informal: il débarque — he turns up out of the blue.

The word, taken apart.
débarquer is built from dé- and barque 'boat' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin barca (Lewis & Short).
