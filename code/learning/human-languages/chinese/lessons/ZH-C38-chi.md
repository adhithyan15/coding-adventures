## You'll want to know: 吃
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C38-EAT38-01]; assesses=[] -->

**吃** — *chī* — "to eat".

> **你吃什么** *nǐ chī shénme* — What are you having?

The new character, in pieces: 吃 — 口, the mouth piece, on the left, and on the right three strokes: a short fall, a short bar, and one long stroke that runs across, drops and hooks up. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**吃** (*chī*) is probably inherited from Middle Chinese *khek*, which the Guangyun glosses 'to eat' and writes with a different character; there 吃 itself is *kj+t*, 'to stammer' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the word and this character came together is not settled.
