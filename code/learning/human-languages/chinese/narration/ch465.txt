Mandarin Chinese, chapter 465: Spoiling the Fun.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tòngkuài — delighted; to one's heart's content.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take it out on someone, then the Mandarin for furious.

You'll want to know: tòngkuài.
tòngkuài — "delighted; to one's heart's content".
wǒmen wán le yì tiān wán de hěn tòngkuài — We spent the whole day having fun and enjoyed every minute of it.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tòngkuài is built from tòng 'to hurt' and 快 'glad', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thuwngH and khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'illness' and 'to hurt' and the second as what pleases the heart. The sense 'thoroughly' for the first is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
扫兴 (sǎoxìng) — deflated; with the fun spoiled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for furious, then the Mandarin for to one's heart's content.

You'll want to know: 扫兴.
扫兴 — sǎoxìng — "deflated; with the fun spoiled".
下雨了真扫兴 xiàyǔ le zhēn sǎoxìng — It's raining. What a let-down.
The new character, in pieces: 兴 (xìng) — six strokes in three pieces: at the top a dot, a second dot and a short stroke falling to the left; a long bar across; and under it 八 bā, two strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
扫兴 (sǎoxìng) is built from 扫 'to sweep' and 兴 (xìng) 'high spirits', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sawX and xingH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to sweep away'; it gives the second reading no gloss of its own, so 'high spirits' is the usual explanation, not a settled one.

Lesson 3 of 6.
兴 (xìng) — three short strokes over a bar, and 八 under it.

Script — three short strokes over a bar, and 八 under it.
兴 (xìng).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top, three strokes. A dot on the left, a second dot in the middle, both falling to the right, then a short stroke on the right falling to the left.
The bar, one stroke. A long bar across, from left to right, wider than everything above it.
The bottom, 八, two strokes. A stroke falling to the left, then a short stroke falling to the right.
3 + 1 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 兴 (xìng), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 兴 (xìng) have? (Three: three strokes, one and two.) What is under the bar? (八.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
lèguān — optimistic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to one's heart's content, then the Mandarin for the fun spoiled.

You'll want to know: lèguān.
lèguān — "optimistic".
wǒ nǎinai shì wǒmen jiā zuì lèguān de rén — My grandmother is the most optimistic person in our family.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lèguān is built from 乐 'joy' and guān 'to view', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lak and kwan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'joy' and the second 'to look': looking at things with joy.
