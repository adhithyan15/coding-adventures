Japanese, chapter 11: Seven Body Words, No New Guessing.
12 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 12.
っ (small tsu) — one small held beat.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say yukkuri and itte kudasai. Hold the silence before each second consonant; do not add a vowel.

Script — the shape.
っ — small tsu — one beat of consonant closure.
It has the same family shape as full-sized つ, but it is smaller and sits lower. Copy only the small form today. In ゆっくり, it holds before k; in いって, it holds before t.

Guided Practice.
Trace っ (small tsu) once and copy it once.
Hide it; write the small sign after ten seconds.
Read ゆっくり and いって with one silent beat at っ (small tsu).

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate small form.
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart.

Lesson 2 of 12.
て — close itte.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the shape until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say itte kudasai. Write the small っ (small tsu) that holds before t.

Script — the shape.
て — te.
One continuous stroke: begin across the top, turn back, then sweep down and right. Keep the lower curve open.

Guided Practice.
Trace て (te) once.
Copy it once, then retrieve it after ten seconds.
Join the earned ending: い | っ (small tsu) | て (te).

Wrap-up Recall.
Read いって once without romanization.

Lesson 3 of 12.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Say wakarimashita. Write と and notice that today's ta belongs to the same consonant row but has its own shape.

Script — the shape.
た — ta.
Four strokes: a short top stroke, a crossing vertical curve, then two small right-side strokes. Keep the right pair separate.

Guided Practice.

Add it to the known final block し | た (ta).

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 4 of 12.
だ (da) — voice the known base.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the change until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 12 seconds]
Write た (ta), then add the two-stroke dakuten without changing the base. Say the da in kudasai.

Script — the change.
た + dakuten becomes だ — ta becomes da.
The voicing mark does the same job it did before. This is transfer, not a new complex shape.

Guided Practice.
Write た (ta) once.
Add dakuten to make だ.
Point to だ while saying the middle of kudasai.

Wrap-up Recall.
Read ください once, attending to だ (da).
Source: Unicode Hiragana chart.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Voice た (ta) with dakuten. Write the result.


Lesson 5 of 12.
て (te) — hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 10 seconds]
Write て once and say its mora.

You'll want to know.
て — te — hand.
The one-sign word names the hand. Sound, meaning, reading, and writing can meet without adding another shape.

Guided Practice.

See て (te); say “hand.”
Hide the word and write its one sign.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 12 of 12.
Seven readable body words — hand, ear, mouth, foot or leg, nose, face, eye.

Warm-up.
[pause 25 seconds]
Write っ (small tsu), て, た (ta), だ (da). Read ゆっくり, いって, わかりました, and ください once; the old repair exposure is now closed.

You'll want to know.
て (te) ・ みみ (mimi) ・ くち (kuchi) ・ あし (ashi) ・ はな (hana) ・ かお (kao) ・ め (me).
This is a reading-and-writing map, not a new-word dump. Every word arrived in its own lesson, and every sign was already earned.

Guided Practice.
Listen: point to the named body part.
Speak: name any three without reading.
Read: read all seven from left to right.
Write: choose any three and write them from memory.

Wrap-up Recall.
Stop after one accurate three-word dictation.
Source: Japan Foundation Marugoto A1 vocabulary database.
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Write the Japanese words for hand, ear, and mouth, in that order.
