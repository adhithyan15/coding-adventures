Tamil, chapter 64: The Harvest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
நெல் (nel) — paddy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nambikkai mean, and what did paṇivu mean?

You'll want to know: நெல்.
நெல் (nel) — "paddy".
Rice still in its husk — standing in the வயல், cut, carried, stored, but not yet அரிசி. The two words do not trade places: நெல் (nel) goes into the field and அரிசி comes out of the kitchen, and the husking is the border between them.
Kannada ಭತ್ತ (bhatta) and Telugu వరి (vari) each have their own word for it, so this is one the three do not share.

What you've built.
The first of five field words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
புல் (pul) — grass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nel mean?

You'll want to know: புல்.
புல் (pul) — "grass".
Grass, of the kind cut and carried home. Kannada makes it ಹುಲ್ಲು (hullu) — the p-for-h trade a third time, and by now you can guess a Kannada word before you meet it. Telugu went elsewhere, to గడ్డి (gaḍḍi).
புல் (pul) and பல் sit one mark apart, and the mark is the whole difference: keep the உ round and low and the word stays grass. A பசு and an ஆடு are put out for புல் (pul).

What you've built.
Two.

Lesson 3 of 5.
பயிர் (payir) — a standing crop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did pul mean?

You'll want to know: பயிர்.
பயிர் (payir) — "a standing crop".
What is growing in the வயல் at this moment, green and still rooted. It is not the வயல், which stays there when nothing is on it, and it is not the நெல் (nel) in the sack, which has stopped growing.
So the run has three separate things now where English leans on one word: the ground, the standing green, and the grain. Kannada calls the standing green ಬೆಳೆ (beḷe).

What you've built.
Three, and the three are carefully apart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
களம் (kaḷam) — a threshing floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did payir mean?

You'll want to know: களம்.
களம் (kaḷam) — "a threshing floor".
The beaten flat ground at the edge of a village where cut நெல் (nel) is spread, trodden and winnowed. Telugu has the same word as కల్లం (kallaṁ), which makes it old.
It is not காலம், the season word you already have. This one is short at the front and takes the ள; காலம் opens long and takes the ல. One mark and one letter, and they are unrelated.

What you've built.
Four.

Lesson 5 of 5.
அறுவடை (aṟuvaṭai) — the harvest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did kaḷam mean?

You'll want to know: அறுவடை.
அறுவடை (aṟuvaṭai) — "the harvest".
The cutting and the gathering together, and the stretch of the year named after them. Kannada ಸುಗ್ಗಿ (suggi) and Telugu పంట (paṇṭa) each named the same weeks their own way.
The front of it is the reaping, not the ஆறு you counted with: this one opens short on அ and takes the hard ற straight away, where six opens long on ஆ. Close together on the page, far apart in the mouth.

What you've built.
Five, and the run is closed: paddy, grass, a standing crop, a threshing floor, and the harvest.
