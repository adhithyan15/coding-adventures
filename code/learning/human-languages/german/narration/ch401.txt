German, chapter 401: Sicher unterwegs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Hupe — the horn (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the rear-view mirror, then the German for to indicate.

You'll want to know: die Hupe.
die Hupe — "the horn (of a car)".
Die Hupe von unserem alten Auto ist sehr laut. The horn on our old car is very loud.
Feminine: eine laute Hupe.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of Hupe is not settled. It is usually explained as an imitation of the sound itself; EtymDB does not list the word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hupen — to hoot, to sound the horn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to indicate, then the German for the horn.

You'll want to know: hupen.
hupen — "to hoot, to sound the horn".
Der Taxifahrer hupt zweimal vor unserem Haus. The taxi driver hoots twice in front of our house.
Regular: er hupt, er hat gehupt.

The word, taken apart.
hupen is built on Hupe 'horn', which is usually explained as a sound word. EtymDB lists neither, so the origin stays hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Sicherheitsgurt — the seat belt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the horn, then the German for to hoot.

You'll want to know: der Sicherheitsgurt.
der Sicherheitsgurt — "the seat belt".
Im Taxi trägt sie immer den Sicherheitsgurt. In a taxi she always wears her seat belt.
Plural: die Sicherheitsgurte. Often shortened to der Gurt.

The word, taken apart.
Sicherheitsgurt is built from Sicherheit 'safety', from sicher, an early borrowing from Latin sēcūrus 'free from care' (Lewis & Short), and Gurt 'belt', inherited from Middle High German gurt (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
anschnallen — to buckle up, to fasten a seat belt (mostly reflexive: sich anschnallen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hoot, then the German for the seat belt.

You'll want to know: anschnallen.
anschnallen — "to buckle up, to fasten a seat belt (mostly reflexive: sich anschnallen)".
Vor der Fahrt schnallen sich alle Kinder an. Before the journey all the children buckle up.
Mostly reflexive and separable: ich schnalle mich an, wir haben uns angeschnallt.

The word, taken apart.
anschnallen is built from an 'on' and Schnalle 'buckle', which is inherited from Middle High German snalle (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Beifahrer — the front-seat passenger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the seat belt, then the German for to buckle up.

You'll want to know: der Beifahrer.
der Beifahrer — "the front-seat passenger".
Der Beifahrer liest die Karte und sagt den Weg. The passenger reads the map and says which way to go.
Same form in the plural. A woman: die Beifahrerin. Der Beifahrersitz — the passenger seat.

The word, taken apart.
Beifahrer is built from bei 'beside, with' and Fahrer 'driver', from the inherited verb fahren, Proto-Germanic faraną (EtymDB).
