# Synthesis — the choices English never makes you make

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TRAER-01, ES-LEX-CONOCER-10] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new here. Two verbs to say aloud first: *traer*, *conocer*.

## Grammar Lens: one English word, two Spanish verbs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-TRAER-LLEVAR-03, ES-GRAMMAR-CONOCER-SABER-12, ES-GRAMMAR-ASK-SPLIT-04] -->

Three times now you have met a pair of verbs where English makes do with one.
They arrived chapters apart and have never been put side by side:

| English gives you | Spanish makes you choose | the question you must answer |
|---|---|---|
| **to ask** | *preguntar* · *pedir* | asking a **question**, or asking **for a thing**? |
| **to bring / take** | *traer* · *llevar* | toward the speaker, or away? |
| **to know** | *conocer* · *saber* | a **person or place**, or a **fact**? |

None of these is a vocabulary problem. In each one, the word is easy and the
**decision** is the work — and the decision is information English speakers are
simply never required to supply.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONOCER-SABER-12, ES-GRAMMAR-TRAER-LLEVAR-03] -->

When you pick *traer* over *llevar*, you have told your listener **where you
are standing**. When you pick *conocer* over *saber*, you have told them
whether you have *met* the thing or only heard of it. The grammar is carrying
meaning that English would need a whole clause to add.

You are not translating any more. You are choosing.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-CONOCER-SABER-12, ES-GRAMMAR-TRAER-LLEVAR-03, ES-LEX-CONOCER-10] -->

> — ¿**Conoces** Madrid?
> — Sí, **conozco** Madrid. Pero no **sé** dónde está el café.

Two "knows" in two lines, and not interchangeable. The first is acquaintance —
*I have been there*. The second is a fact. English uses **know** for both and
loses the difference.

## Wrap-up Recall

(*Preguntar* and *pedir* — long before the other two.)
