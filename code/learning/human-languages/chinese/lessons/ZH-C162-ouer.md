## You'll want to know: ǒu'ěr
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C162-OFTEN162-04]; assesses=[] -->

*ǒu'ěr* — "now and then; occasionally".

> *wǒ ǒu'ěr hē kāfēi* — I drink coffee now and then.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones: ǒu'ěr is said óu'ěr, the first rising.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*ǒu'ěr* is built from *ǒu* 'by chance' and the old ending 尔, both inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the first as *nguwH*, glossed 'unexpected', though today's third tone goes with its other reading, *nguwX* 'a pair'; the second as *nyeX*, glossed 'you', here an ending.
