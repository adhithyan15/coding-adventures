Italian, chapter 513: La gara.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gareggiare — to compete, to race.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for medal, then the Italian for podium.

You'll want to know: gareggiare.
gareggiare — "to compete, to race".
Gareggio nei cento metri. I compete in the hundred metres.
Built on la gara "competition".

The word, taken apart.
gareggiare is built on gara "a competition", whose origin is not settled; an Arabic source is often suggested.

Lesson 2 of 5.
il cronometro — stopwatch.

Before the new one: say the Italian for podium, then the Italian for to compete.

You'll want to know: il cronometro.
il cronometro — "stopwatch".
L'allenatore ferma il cronometro all'arrivo. The coach stops the stopwatch at the finish.
Una gara a cronometro — a time trial.

The word, taken apart.
cronometro is a modern learned word built from Greek khrónos "time" and métron "measure" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la maratona — marathon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to compete, then the Italian for stopwatch.

You'll want to know: la maratona.
la maratona — "marathon".
Ho corso la mia prima maratona a quarant'anni. I ran my first marathon at forty.
Also a long session: una maratona di film.

The word, taken apart.
maratona comes from Greek, from the name of a town, which is built on márathon "fennel" (EtymDB): the story goes that a runner carried news of a victory from there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'atleta — athlete.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for stopwatch, then the Italian for marathon.

You'll want to know: l'atleta.
l'atleta — "athlete".
Gli atleti si preparano per la gara. The athletes are getting ready for the race.
The same form for men and women: l'atleta; plural gli atleti, le atlete.

The word, taken apart.
atleta is a learned borrowing from Latin āthlēta (EtymDB), from Greek athlētḗs "one who competes for a prize", from âthlos "a contest".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ansimare — to pant, to gasp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for marathon, then the Italian for athlete.

You'll want to know: ansimare.
ansimare — "to pant, to gasp".
All'arrivo ansimavo e non riuscivo a parlare. At the finish I was panting and could not speak.
Breathing hard after an effort.

The word, taken apart.
ansimare is usually traced to Latin anhēlāre "to pant" (Lewis & Short), through a Vulgar Latin form; the details are not settled.
