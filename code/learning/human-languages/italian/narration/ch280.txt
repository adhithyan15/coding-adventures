Italian, chapter 280: Da allora a oggi.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
finora — so far, until now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for early, then the Italian for the stage.

You'll want to know: finora.
finora — "so far, until now".
Finora è andato tutto bene. So far everything has gone well.
Also written fino ad ora.

The word, taken apart.
finora fuses fino "up to", built on Latin fīnis "end, limit", and ora "now", from Latin hōra "hour" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
da allora — since then, ever since.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the stage, then the Italian for so far.

You'll want to know: da allora.
da allora — "since then, ever since".
Ci siamo conosciuti a scuola e da allora siamo amici. We met at school and we have been friends ever since.
Da allora in poi — from then on.

The word, taken apart.
allora is inherited from Latin ad illam hōram "at that hour" (EtymDB); da "from" is usually traced to Latin dē ab.

Lesson 3 of 6.
tuttora — still, to this day.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for so far, then the Italian for since then.

You'll want to know: tuttora.
tuttora — "still, to this day".
Mio nonno abita tuttora nella stessa casa. My grandfather still lives in the same house.
Stronger than ancora: true then and true now.

The word, taken apart.
tuttora fuses tutta "all" and ora "hour, now", from Latin hōra (EtymDB): all the hours up to now.

Lesson 5 of 6.
precedentemente — previously, beforehand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for still, then the Italian for lately.

You'll want to know: precedentemente.
precedentemente — "previously, beforehand".
Come ho detto precedentemente, il negozio è chiuso. As I said previously, the shop is closed.
Formal; in speech, prima.

The word, taken apart.
precedentemente is built on precedente "previous", from Latin praecēdēns, the present participle of praecēdere "to go before" (Lewis & Short).
