# あした いく (ashita iku) — the future, with a time word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C54-TIME54-02, JA-LEX-C26-FAMILY26-03, JA-LEX-C141-RAISHUU] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "tomorrow" and "next week". (*ashita*, *raishū*.) Then say "I go", politely. (*ikimasu*.)

## You'll want to know: no future form
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-GRAMMAR-C251-FUTURE-01]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C41-SCHOOL41-05, JA-LEX-C138-HATARAKIMASU, JA-LEX-C141-RAINEN] -->

> **あした いきます。**
>
> *ashita ikimasu.* — "I will go tomorrow."

Japanese has no separate form for the future. **いきます** (*ikimasu*) is "I go" and also "I will go"; the time word says which.

> **あした やまに いきます。**
>
> *ashita yama ni ikimasu.* — "I will go to the mountains tomorrow."

> **らいしゅう はたらきます。**
>
> *raishū hatarakimasu.* — "I will work next week."

Without a time word, the same verb is what you do as a rule:

> **まいにち はたらきます。**
>
> *mainichi hatarakimasu.* — "I work every day."

## You'll want to know: the plain future
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-GRAMMAR-C251-FUTURE-02]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C141-TSUMORI, JA-LEX-C141-DESHOU, JA-LEX-C207-WEATHER207-02, JA-LEX-C44-NATURE44-05] -->

To a friend, the dictionary form does the same job:

> **えいがを みる。**
>
> *eiga o miru.* — "I will see a film."

> **あした えいがを みる。**
>
> *ashita eiga o miru.* — "I will see a film tomorrow."

That plain form is the one you already put before **つもり** (*tsumori*), "a plan", and it goes before **でしょう** (*deshō*), "probably", too:

> **らいねん うみに いく つもりです。**
>
> *rainen umi ni iku tsumori desu.* — "I plan to go to the sea next year."

> **あした あめが ふるでしょう。**
>
> *ashita ame ga furu deshō.* — "It will probably rain tomorrow."
