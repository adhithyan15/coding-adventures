Hindi, chapter 386: Leaning on Others.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
खिसकना (khisaknā) — to slip away, to shift.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to set, then the Hindi for unfinished.

You'll want to know: खिसकना.
खिसकना — khisaknā — "to slip away, to shift".
अगर तुम दोनों थोड़ा खिसकोगे तो मैं भी बैठ सकूँगा। agar tum donõ thoṛā khiskoge to maiṁ bhī baiṭh sakūṅgā. If you two shift up a little, I'll be able to sit too.
Past (he …): वह पीछे खिसक गया, vah pīche khisak gayā.

The word, taken apart.
खिसकना (khisaknā) is inherited from khis, "slip", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 3888).

Lesson 2 of 5.
खलना (khalnā) — to be missed, to bother (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for unfinished, then the Hindi for to slip away.

You'll want to know: खलना.
खलना — khalnā — "to be missed, to bother (someone)".
अगर तुम चले जाओगे तो मुझे बहुत खलेगा। agar tum cale jāoge to mujhe bahut khalegā. If you go away, I'll really miss you.
Past (it …): वह बात उसे बहुत खली, vah bāt use bahut khalī.

The word, taken apart.
खलना (khalnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit skhalati, "stumbles" (CDIAL 13663). CDIAL glosses the Hindi verb "to be bad, to be inimical"; the sense of being missed is not among its glosses.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ज़िद (zid) — stubbornness, insistence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to be missed, then the Hindi for someone to lean on.

You'll want to know: ज़िद.
ज़िद — zid — "stubbornness, insistence".
ज़िद मत करो, वरना माँ को ग़ुस्सा आएगा। zid mat karo, varnā māṁ ko ġussā āegā. Don't be stubborn, or your mother will get angry.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ज़िद (zid) is usually traced to Arabic ḍidd, "the opposite", which reached Hindi through Persian.
