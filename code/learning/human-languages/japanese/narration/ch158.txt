Japanese, chapter 158: Keys, Windows and Lights.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かぎ (kagi) — a key; a lock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hang, then the Japanese for to put.

You'll want to know: かぎ.
かぎ — kagi — "a key; a lock".
かぎを かばんに いれます。 — kagi o kaban ni iremasu — "I put the key in my bag."

The word, taken apart.
かぎ (kagi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
でんき (denki) — electric light; electricity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a key, then the Japanese for to switch off.

You'll want to know: でんき.
でんき — denki — "electric light; electricity".
へやの でんきを けします。 — heya no denki o keshimasu — "I switch off the light in the room."

The word, taken apart.
でんき (denki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are den, "electricity" (Middle Chinese denH) and ki, "air, spirit" (khj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
