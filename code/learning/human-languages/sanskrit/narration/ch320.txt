Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रजा (prajā) — the subjects of a king; the people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for retreats, then the Sanskrit for overpowers.

You'll want to know: प्रजा.
प्रजा — prajā — "the subjects of a king; the people".
प्रजाः राज्ये सुखेन वसन्ति। prajāḥ rājye sukhena vasanti. The subjects live happily in the kingdom.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रजा (prajā) is built from pra and the root √jan, "to be born"; MW gives prajā as offspring, and also as people, the subjects of a prince (MW).

A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दासः (dāsaḥ) MW's oldest senses are a fiend and the name of certain foes in the Veda, and it also gives dāsa as a slave or servant; MW gives no root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

प्रजाः क्रूरं सेनापतिं निष्कासयन्ति। prajāḥ krūraṃ senāpatiṃ niṣkāsayanti. The people drive out the cruel general.
Past (he …): सः शत्रुं नगरात् निष्कासितवान्, saḥ śatruṃ nagarāt niṣkāsitavān.

The word, taken apart.
निष्कासयति (niṣkāsayati) is built from niṣ, "out", and the causative of the root √kas, "to go"; MW gives niṣ-kāsayati as to drive or turn out, expel (MW).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: स्वातन्त्र्यम्.
स्वातन्त्र्यम् — svātantryam — "freedom; independence".
प्रजाः स्वातन्त्र्यम् इच्छन्ति। prajāḥ svātantryam icchanti. The people want freedom.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्वातन्त्र्यम् (svātantryam) is built on sva-tantra, "self-dependent" (from sva, "own", and tantra, "rule"), with the lengthened first vowel; MW gives svātantrya as following one's own will, independence (MW).
