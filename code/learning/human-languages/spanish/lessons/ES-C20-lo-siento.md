# lo siento — "I'm sorry," i.e. "I feel it"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-POR-FAVOR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You already know **por favor**, "please." The next courtesy phrase
is **lo siento**. Spanish does not say "I *am* sorry" as an adjective; it
says something closer to **"I feel this."**

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-LO-SIENTO-01, ES-ETYMON-SENTIR-02]; assesses=[] -->

- **lo** = "it" — a **neuter** pronoun. It does not point at any noun; it
  points at the **situation itself**. Spanish keeps this one leftover neuter
  slot precisely for "the whole business, unnamed."
- **siento** = "I feel," from **sentir**, "to feel," from Latin **sentīre**.
  That root gave English a whole family: **sentiment**, **sense**,
  **consent** ("feel together"), **resent** ("feel back at"), and
  **sentence** (originally an *opinion*, something felt).

So **lo siento** is literally *"I feel it."* You are naming a feeling —
regret, sympathy — rather than declaring a state about yourself. That is why
it stretches so easily: *lo siento mucho*, "I feel it a lot," works both for
stepping on someone's foot and for hearing that their father died.
