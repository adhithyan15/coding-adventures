Sanskrit, chapter 145: Is glad, Cries out, Roars, Coos, Chatters.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नन्दति (nandati) — is glad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for puts in, then the Sanskrit for joins.

You'll want to know: नन्दति.
नन्दति — nandati — "is glad".

The word, taken apart.
nandati comes from the root √nand "rejoice, be pleased" (MW; CDIAL 6950), already in the Rigveda. ānanda "joy", a word you already know, is built on the same root.

krandati comes from the root √krand "roar, neigh, lament" (CDIAL 3574), which MW sets beside Gothic grētan "lament". It lives on as Bengali kāṁdā "to cry" (CDIAL 3574).

गर्जति — garjati — "roars, thunders".

The word, taken apart.
garjati comes from the root √garj "roar, growl, thunder" (CDIAL 4046), inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB); MW compares Latin garrire "chatter". garjana "thunder" is the noun of the same root.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कूजति (kūjati) — coos, warbles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for cries out, then the Sanskrit for roars.

You'll want to know: कूजति.
कूजति — kūjati — "coos, warbles".

The word, taken apart.
kūjati comes from the root √kūj "make an inarticulate sound, coo, warble" (MW), used of birds such as the pigeon and the cuckoo. MW gives no outside cousins for it, so its source is uncertain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
जल्पति (jalpati) — chatters.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for roars, then the Sanskrit for coos.

You'll want to know: जल्पति.
जल्पति — jalpati — "chatters".

The word, taken apart.
jalpati comes from the root √jalp "murmur, chatter" (CDIAL 5163), already in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa. MW suggests, with a query, that it is a reduplicated form of √lap "prate, talk".
