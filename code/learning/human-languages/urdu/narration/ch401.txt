Lesson 1 of 6.
ٹرالی (ṭrālī) — a luggage trolley.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to spend (time), then the Urdu for a return.

You'll want to know: ٹرالی.
ٹرالی — ṭrālī — "a luggage trolley".
میں نے اپنا سامان ٹرالی پر رکھا۔ maiṅ ne apnā sāmān ṭrālī par rakhā. I put my luggage on a trolley.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ٹرالی (ṭrālī) is borrowed from English trolley.

Lesson 2 of 6.
منتظر (muntazir) — waiting (for someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a return, then the Urdu for a luggage trolley.

You'll want to know: منتظر.
منتظر — muntazir — "waiting (for someone)".
ہمارا دوست باہر ہمارا منتظر تھا۔ hamārā dost bāhar hamārā muntazir thā. Our friend was outside, waiting for us.

The word, taken apart.
منتظر (muntazir) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic muntaẓir, "one who waits"; it is a cousin of انتظار, "waiting".

Lesson 4 of 6.
گلدستہ (guldastā) — a bouquet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for waiting (for someone), then the Urdu for a welcome.

You'll want to know: گلدستہ.
گلدستہ — guldastā — "a bouquet".
سارا نے اپنی بہن کو گلدستہ دیا۔ sārā ne apnī bahan ko guldastā diyā. Sara gave her sister a bouquet.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
گلدستہ (guldastā) is built from Persian gul, "a flower", and dasta, "a handful": it is usually traced to Persian guldasta, "a bunch of flowers".

Lesson 5 of 6.
ہلانا (hilānā) — to wave; to shake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a welcome, then the Urdu for a bouquet.

You'll want to know: ہلانا.
ہلانا — hilānā — "to wave; to shake".
دوستوں نے ہمیں دیکھ کر ہاتھ ہلائے۔ dostoṅ ne hameṅ dekh kar hāth hilāe. Our friends saw us and waved.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے ہاتھ ہلایا, us ne hāth hilāyā.

The word, taken apart.
ہلانا (hilānā) is built on hilnā, "to shake", which CDIAL lists under hillati, "moves, shakes", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 14120): this is the form for shaking something.
