## Unreleased — Chapter 86: the writing paper, with a clock

The seventh and last writing stage, and the only one Hindi had never proven. One
lesson, no new atoms, and the track is now complete at **every level** from
pre-A1 to C2.

`timed-assessment-production` is first required at A1 and inherited by every
level above, so a track missing it fails A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 **and** C2 at once —
and one lesson clears all six.

**Every number in the lesson is read out of `task-shapes/a1.json`**, the same
file the assessment contract points at, rather than invented for the page: 20
minutes, two tasks, a practical form of six to eight fields worth 40 points, and
a 30–40 word message to a named reader worth 60.

The lesson makes the learner read that split before writing a word: **the
message is worth more than the form and is the shorter of the two**, so time
spent perfecting a field is time taken from the part that scores. Seven minutes
for the form, thirteen for the message.

Two habits it drills that no untimed lesson can: stop when the time is up even
mid-line — *an unfinished paper is information, an untimed one is not* — and
check the form for **blanks before mistakes**, because a wrong field can still
earn something and an empty one cannot.
