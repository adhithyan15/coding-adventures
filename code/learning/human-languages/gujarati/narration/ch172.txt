Gujarati, chapter 172: Stale, burnt and fresh juice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ફીકું (phīkũ) — bland, pale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for salty, then the Gujarati for sweet.

You'll want to know: ફીકું.
ફીકું — phīkũ — "bland, pale".
આ શાક ફીકું છે. — ā śāk phīkũ che — "This curry is bland."

The word, taken apart.
ફીકું (phīkũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from phikka, "defective", a reconstructed form, and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "pale, tasteless" (CDIAL 9037).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Gujarati for bland, then the Gujarati for stale.

You'll want to know: બળવું.
બળવું — baḷvũ — "to get burnt, to burn".
રોટલી તવા ઉપર બળે છે. — roṭlī tavā upar baḷe che — "The rotli burns on the griddle."

નારંગી ખાટી અને મીઠી છે. — nārangī khāṭī ane mīṭhī che — "The orange is sour and sweet."

Lesson 5 of 5.
રસ (ras) — juice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to get burnt, then the Gujarati for orange.

You'll want to know: રસ.
રસ — ras — "juice".
હું સવારે નારંગીનો રસ પીઉં છું. — hũ savāre nārangīno ras pīũ chũ — "I drink orange juice in the morning."

The word, taken apart.
રસ (ras) is inherited from Sanskrit rasa, "sap or juice of plants; taste", with only its final vowel lost, and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "sap, juice" (CDIAL 10650).
