Portuguese, chapter 469: Teimosos e sovinas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
teimoso — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for selfish, then the Portuguese for selfishness.

You'll want to know: teimoso.
teimoso — "stubborn".
O meu avô é teimoso e nunca muda de ideias. My grandfather is stubborn and never changes his mind.
Of things too: uma tosse teimosa — a cough that will not go away.

The word, taken apart.
teimoso is built on teima "obstinacy", which is usually traced to Latin thema "a theme, a topic", from Greek (EtymDB); the path of meaning is not fully settled.

O miúdo teima em não comer a sopa. The kid insists on not eating his soup.
Teimar em + infinitive — to insist on doing something.

The word, taken apart.
teimar is built on teima "obstinacy", usually traced to Latin thema "a theme", from Greek (EtymDB); the origin is not fully settled.

O velho avarento guardava o dinheiro debaixo da cama. The miserly old man kept his money under the bed.
The vice is a avareza.

The word, taken apart.
avarento is built on avaro "greedy", which goes back to Latin avārus "greedy, covetous" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
sovina — stingy; skinflint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to insist, then the Portuguese for miserly.

You'll want to know: sovina.
sovina — "stingy; skinflint".
O meu tio é sovina: nunca paga um café a ninguém. My uncle is stingy: he never buys anyone a coffee.
Informal. The same form for a man and a woman.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of sovina is not settled; dictionaries offer several guesses and none is certain.
