Mandarin Chinese, chapter 530: Winning a Prize.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cǎipiào — lottery ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fortunate, then the Mandarin for fate.

You'll want to know: cǎipiào.
cǎipiào — "lottery ticket".
tā měi ge xīngqī dōu mǎi cǎipiào — He buys lottery tickets every week.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cǎipiào is built from cǎi and 票 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tshojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'brilliant colour'; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Lesson 2 of 5.
chōujiǎng — to draw for prizes; to hold a prize draw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fate, then the Mandarin for a lottery ticket.

You'll want to know: chōujiǎng.
chōujiǎng — "to draw for prizes; to hold a prize draw".
zhǐyào mǎi yìbǎi kuài qián de dōngxi jiù kěyǐ chōujiǎng — Spend a hundred yuan and you can enter the prize draw.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōujiǎng is built from chōu 'to pull out' and jiǎng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, trhjuw and tsjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to encourage' and 'to praise'; the sense 'a prize' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 5.
zhòngjiǎng — to win in a draw; to win a prize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a lottery ticket, then the Mandarin for to draw for prizes.

You'll want to know: zhòngjiǎng.
zhòngjiǎng — "to win in a draw; to win a prize".
yàoshi wǒ zhòngjiǎng le wǒ jiù qù lǚyóu — If I win a prize, I'll go travelling.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhòngjiǎng is built from 中 zhòng 'to hit the mark' and jiǎng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjuwngH and tsjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hit' and the second 'to encourage'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
jiǎngpǐn — a prize you win; an award.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to draw for prizes, then the Mandarin for to win in a draw.

You'll want to know: jiǎngpǐn.
jiǎngpǐn — "a prize you win; an award".
rúguǒ nǐ zhòngjiǎng le jiù kěyǐ ná dào jiǎngpǐn — If you win, you'll get a prize.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: jiǎng is said with a rising tone before pǐn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎngpǐn is built from jiǎng and pǐn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjangX and phimX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to encourage' and the second 'a rank' and 'a kind'.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tóujiǎng — first prize; the jackpot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: tóujiǎng.
tóujiǎng — "first prize; the jackpot".
zhè cì chōujiǎng de tóujiǎng shì yìbǎi wàn — The first prize in this draw is a million.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tóujiǎng is built from 头 'the head' and jiǎng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, duw and tsjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to encourage': the reward at the head of the list.
