Persian, chapter 20: Which One: the Ordinals.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
اول (avval) — first, and the one ordinal Persian did not build.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read ۱, ۵ and ۱۰, then say yek.

You'll want to know first — one word.
اول — avval — first.
Counting says how many. Ordering says which one, and a floor number, a date on a notice, a chapter heading and a queue all need the second.
Three letters — ا, و, ل — and every one of them was taught in the script chapter, so this is the third word in the book you can write from memory without meeting a new sign.

The word, taken apart — a borrowing at the head of a native series.
اول (avval) is Arabic, from the root ʾ-w-l. Persian did not build it from yek.
Every other ordinal in the language is built from its own number, as the next lesson shows. So the series opens with a borrowed word and continues with native ones — the same shape English has in first and second, which are not built from one and two either, before third, fourth, fifth fall into line.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — say: avval — first]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: اول (avval) — alef, vâv, lam]
[your turn — contrast: yek counts, اول (avval) orders]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Wiktionary: اول (avval); Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Is avval built from the Persian word for one?


Lesson 2 of 4.
دوم and سوم — second, third, and one ending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say avval, and where it came from. Then say do and se.

You'll want to know first — two words and a rule.
دوم — dovvom — second سوم — sevvom — third.
The rule is one ending: ـم, -om, on the cardinal. do + -om becomes dovvom; se + -om becomes sevvom. The consonant doubles at the join, which is the only thing you have to hear.
سوم is written س، و، م — three letters, all yours from the script chapter. That makes it the fourth word in this book you can write unaided, after se, noh and avval, whose written forms the script chapter already gave.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — say: dovvom — second; sevvom — third]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — split: do + -om, se + -om]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: سوم — sin, vâv, mim]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Wiktionary: سوم; Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What ending turns a Persian cardinal into an ordinal?


Lesson 3 of 4.
چهارم (chahârom) — fourth, with nothing bending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the ordinal ending, then dovvom and sevvom.

You'll want to know first — one word.
چهارم — chahârom — fourth.
chahâr + -om, and nothing happens at the join. panjom, "fifth," is the same: the cardinal ends in a consonant, so the ending simply follows it.
dovvom and sevvom doubled a consonant only because their cardinals end in a vowel. Two and three are the exception; four onwards is the rule, all the way up. dahom is "tenth", bistom is "twentieth", sadom is "hundredth."
So a reader who has twelve cardinals and one ending has twelve ordinals, minus the borrowed first.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — say: chahârom — fourth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: panj + -om becomes panjom, fifth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: dah + -om becomes dahom, tenth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Wiktionary: چهارم (chahârom); Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Build tenth and twentieth from dah and bist.


Lesson 4 of 4.
Payoff — which one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count one to twenty, then read ۷, ۱۲ and ۱۰۰.

Guided Practice.
Say the first four in order, then out of order.
Build, do not recall: fifth, seventh, tenth, twentieth, hundredth, from the cardinals and the ending.
Say which one of the whole series is not built that way, and where it came from.
Write اول (avval) and سوم from memory. Both are made entirely of letters the script chapter taught.
Use one: name the floor a room is on, and the day of the month a notice gives.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 16 seconds]
Say first, second, third, fourth in Persian.
