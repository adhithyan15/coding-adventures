Punjabi, chapter 47: Writing --- Four Lines That Hold Together.
1 lesson. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
Four lines to one person — where the second needs the first.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson asked for writing that was finished on time. This one asks for writing that holds together, which is a different thing.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one person who will read this. A real one.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Connected writing is not longer writing. Four short lines can be connected, and one long line can fail to be.
[pause 2 seconds]
What makes writing connected is that a sentence stops standing on its own — line two is about line one, and a reader who skipped line one would not follow it.
[pause 2 seconds]
You already hold the two words that do this, and they do opposite jobs. ਅਤੇ (ate) adds one thing to another. ਪਰ sets one thing against another.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the difference between a list and a paragraph. A list can be read in any order. A paragraph cannot.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four lines to the person you picked. Greet them, tell them two things, and close.
At least one line must use ਅਤੇ (ate), and at least one must use ਪਰ.
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary — the whole exercise is the joining, not the reaching.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Cover your first line and read the second on its own. Does it still make sense without the first?
[pause 2 seconds]
If it does, you wrote a list. That is not a failure — it is the stage you were at one lesson ago — but go back and make one line depend on another before you stop.
[pause 2 seconds]
And read it as the person you named would. Writing for a reader you can picture is what makes this a task rather than an exercise, and it is what every level above this one keeps asking for.
