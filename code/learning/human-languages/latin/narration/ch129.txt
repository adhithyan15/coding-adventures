Lesson 1 of 5.
rāmus, rāmī — a branch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a rose, then the Latin for a lily.

You'll want to know: rāmus, rāmī.
rāmus, rāmī — "a branch".

The word, taken apart.
rāmus is probably related to rādīx, "root", from an older rādmus (Lewis & Short); both are traced to Proto-Indo-European wreh2d-, "root, branch" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
truncus, truncī — a tree trunk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lily, then the Latin for a branch.

You'll want to know: truncus, truncī.
truncus, truncī — "a tree trunk".

The word, taken apart.
truncus is the adjective truncus, "cut off, maimed" (Lewis & Short), used as a noun: the stem of a tree, without its branches. English trunk comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sēmen, sēminis — a seed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a branch, then the Latin for a tree trunk.

You'll want to know: sēmen, sēminis.
sēmen, sēminis — "a seed".

The word, taken apart.
sēmen is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European seh1mn, "seed", from seh1-, "to sow" (EtymDB 2.1); serō, "to sow", belongs to the same family (Lewis & Short), and English seed is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.

The word, taken apart.
saxum is often traced to Proto-Indo-European sek-, "to cut" (EtymDB 2.1), the root of secāre, "to cut" (Lewis & Short), but the link is not certain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rīpa, rīpae — a river bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a seed, then the Latin for a rock.

You'll want to know: rīpa, rīpae.
rīpa, rīpae — "a river bank".

The word, taken apart.
rīpa is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European h1reypeh2, "steep slope", from a root for tearing (EtymDB 2.1); Greek ereipō, "to tear down", is often compared. Lewis & Short call it doubtful.
