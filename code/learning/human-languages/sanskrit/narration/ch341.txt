Sanskrit, chapter 341: What It Is For.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रयोजनम् (prayojanam) — a purpose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for comes about, then the Sanskrit for gives rise to.

You'll want to know: प्रयोजनम्.
प्रयोजनम् — prayojanam — "a purpose".
एतस्य कार्यस्य प्रयोजनं किम्? etasya kāryasya prayojanaṃ kim? What is the purpose of this work?
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रयोजनम् (prayojanam) is built on pra and the root √yuj, "to join, to use"; MW gives pra-yojana the senses "occasion, motive, purpose, aim" (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
उद्देश्यम् (uddeśyam) — an aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for gives rise to, then the Sanskrit for a purpose.

You'll want to know: उद्देश्यम्.
उद्देश्यम् — uddeśyam — "an aim".
मम उद्देश्यं स्पष्टम् अस्ति। mama uddeśyaṃ spaṣṭam asti. My aim is clear.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
उद्देश्यम् (uddeśyam) is built on ud, "up", and the root √diś, "to point out"; MW gives ud-deśya as "anything which one has in view", and "an aim" is the sense modern Sanskrit gives it (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
प्रेरयति (prerayati) — urges on; inspires.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an aim, then the Sanskrit for a use.

You'll want to know: प्रेरयति.
प्रेरयति — prerayati — "urges on; inspires".
आचार्यः शिष्यान् प्रेरयति। ācāryaḥ śiṣyān prerayati. The preceptor inspires his pupils.
Past (he …): सः मां प्रेरितवान्, saḥ māṃ preritavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रेरयति (prerayati) is built on pra, "forth", and the root √īr, "to set in motion", in its causative form; MW gives prerita "urged, impelled, sent" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
कारयति (kārayati) — has something done.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a use, then the Sanskrit for urges on.

You'll want to know: कारयति.
कारयति — kārayati — "has something done".
राजा मन्दिरं कारयति। rājā mandiraṃ kārayati. The king has a temple built.
Past (he …): सः गृहं कारितवान्, saḥ gṛhaṃ kāritavān.

The word, taken apart.
कारयति (kārayati) comes from the root √kṛ, "to do, to make", in its causative form; MW gives kārita "caused to be made or done" (MW).
