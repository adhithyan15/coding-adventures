Lesson 1 of 5.
व्याधः (vyādhaḥ) — a hunter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lawyer, then the Sanskrit for a writer.

You'll want to know: व्याधः.
व्याधः — vyādhaḥ — "a hunter".

The word, taken apart.
vyādha is built on the root √vyadh, "to pierce, wound" (MW). MW glosses it "one who pierces or wounds", and so a hunter who lives by killing deer (CDIAL 12199).

You'll want to know: चौरः.
चौरः — cauraḥ — "a thief".

The word, taken apart.
caura is a variant of cora, "thief", and MW links both to the root √cur, "to steal". CDIAL 4931 lists the two forms together, and the word lives on as Hindi cor.

Lesson 3 of 5.
याचकः (yācakaḥ) — a beggar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a hunter, then the Sanskrit for a thief.

You'll want to know: याचकः.
याचकः — yācakaḥ — "a beggar".

The word, taken apart.
yācaka is built on the root √yāc, "to ask, beg, entreat", with the agent suffix -aka (MW): "one who asks". The verb itself is old; CDIAL 10447 finds yācate already in the Rigveda.

Lesson 4 of 5.
पण्डितः (paṇḍitaḥ) — a scholar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a thief, then the Sanskrit for a beggar.

You'll want to know: पण्डितः.
पण्डितः — paṇḍitaḥ — "a scholar".

The word, taken apart.
paṇḍita is of uncertain origin: CDIAL 7718 says it has no certain etymology. MW files it beside paṇḍā, "wisdom", and notes the view that it stands for spandita. CDIAL lists Hindi paṇḍā, "temple priest", among its descendants, and English took the word as pundit.

Lesson 5 of 5.
विद्वान् (vidvān) — a learned person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a beggar, then the Sanskrit for a scholar.

You'll want to know: विद्वान्.
विद्वान् — vidvān — "a learned person".

The word, taken apart.
vidvān is the nominative of vidvas, "one who knows", the old perfect participle of the root √vid, "to know" (MW). The root is inherited from Indo-European; MW compares Latin vidēre, "to see".
