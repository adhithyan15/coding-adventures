Latin, chapter 170: To slip, To lighten, To admire, To suspect, To press.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lābor, lābī — to slip, to glide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to move back, then the Latin for to approach.

You'll want to know: lābor, lābī.
lābor, lābī — "to slip, to glide".

The word, taken apart.
lābor is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root (s)leh2b-, "to hang loosely, to droop" (EtymDB 2.1). English lapse comes from its participle lāpsus, "slipped".

Lesson 2 of 5.

levō, levāre — "to lighten, to lift".

The word, taken apart.
levō is built on levis, "light" (Lewis & Short): to make light, to lift. It is a different word from lēvō, "to make smooth".

Lesson 3 of 5.
admīror, admīrārī — to admire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to slip, then the Latin for to lighten.

You'll want to know: admīror, admīrārī.
admīror, admīrārī — "to admire".

The word, taken apart.
admīror is ad, "at", with mīror, "to wonder" (EtymDB 2.1), from mīrus, "wonderful", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European (s)meyh2-, "to smile, to be astonished" (EtymDB 2.1). English smile is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
suspicor, suspicārī — to suspect.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to lighten, then the Latin for to admire.

You'll want to know: suspicor, suspicārī.
suspicor, suspicārī — "to suspect".

The word, taken apart.
suspicor belongs with suspiciō, "to look up at, to suspect" (Lewis & Short), built from sub, "up from under", and speciō, "to look": to look at someone from below.
