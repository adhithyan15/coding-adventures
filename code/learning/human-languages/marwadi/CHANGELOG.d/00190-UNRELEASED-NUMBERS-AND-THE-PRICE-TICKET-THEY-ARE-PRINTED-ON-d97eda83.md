## Unreleased -- numbers, and the price ticket they are printed on

- Added fifty-five <=5-minute sessions across Chapters 32--36. Before this
  tranche the track was a market that taught no number: Chapters 26--31 could
  ask **ये कितणे कू है?** four different ways and understand no answer to any of
  them, and **एक, दो, तीन, चार, पांच** returned ZERO occurrences across 257
  lesson files.
- Taught twelve cardinals -- **एक, दो, तीन, चार, पांच, छ, सात, आठ, नो, दस,
  बीस, सो** -- one to a lesson, each heard before it is written and each scored
  in four skills.
- **Added exactly ONE Devanagari letter to do it.** Independent **ए** is
  isolated in `MW-W32-e` against the **े** mātrā the hand had been writing
  since Chapter 9; every other sign in all twelve words was already in the
  track. The block was never the script, which is why no per-track reading
  found this gap.
- Taught **twenty before the teens, deliberately.** Marwari builds nineteen by
  subtraction -- *ughaṇīs*, one short of twenty, the same shape as Turner's
  *ekuṇcālīs*, one short of forty, for thirty-nine -- so the round number has to
  exist before the number under it. Teaching eleven to nineteen first would
  have printed **बीस** in bold in a lesson that had not taught it.
- Added the ten Devanagari digits **० १ २ ३ ४ ५ ६ ७ ८ ९**, three at a time
  across four writing lessons, each set cashed immediately against the words it
  writes. A price ticket, a fare and a form are written in these shapes and not
  in words, so this is a reading gap as much as a counting one. Chapter 35 is
  built around the four a Latin-trained eye reads wrongly -- **४** taken for an
  eight, **५** for a four, **७** for a nine, **८** for a letter -- drilled
  beside the six it guesses correctly.
- Closed the price loop the market chapters left open. **बीस।** is the seller's
  one-word answer; **दस करो।** is the buyer's counter-offer, and it is Chapter
  28's **करो** with a figure standing where **थोड़ु सस्तो** stood. The
  chapter-31 exchange is re-run in Chapter 36 with six turns instead of five,
  two of them numbers, over the shopping, food and transport words in turn.
- Took the track from 71 to 84 distinct taught headwords and from 228 to 272
  atoms, against the 300-word pre-A1 target.
- Kept the strict gentle-ramp report at ALL ZERO across 312 lessons, which took
  design rather than luck: growing the track from 257 to 312 lessons makes 42
  R4 windows and 11 R3 windows JUDGEABLE for the first time, all of them on
  Chapter 18--25 atoms. Each new chapter's warm-ups therefore sweep that older
  vocabulary in the order it was taught, so the last two lessons of the tranche
  retrieve **लो**, the bargaining request and **करो**, which are the atoms whose
  windows close last.
- Left **ordinals uncovered on purpose**, and recorded WHICH KIND of gap it is:
  *pahlo, dūjo, tījo, chautho* need no sign this track has not taught, so it is
  a sourcing gap and not a script one. No citable Marwari-specific ordinal
  series was found; the Rajasthani numeral list this tranche cites prints
  cardinals only.
- Left eleven to nineteen uncovered with the reason in the inventory: they need
  **इ** (igyārā), **अ** (aṭhārā), **उ** (ughaṇīs) and **ळ** (soḷā), four
  independent vowels and one consonant with no lesson. Five script lessons, not
  a numeral gap.
- Updated the A1 exam inventory IN THIS COMMIT. `MW-A1-Q-01` (cardinal
  numerals) and `MW-A1-LIP-13` (the Devanagari digits) move from `probe: null`
  to probes naming real atoms; coverage moves 74/197 (38%) to 76/197 (39%).
  Both halves were falsified before this shipped: a fabricated atom id and a
  removed probe each break the suite.
