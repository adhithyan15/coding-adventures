Latin, chapter 159: To feel, To hang, To be silent, To keep, To change.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sentiō, sentīre — to feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to dine, then the Latin for to taste.

You'll want to know: sentiō, sentīre.
sentiō, sentīre — "to feel".

The word, taken apart.
sentiō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European sent-, "to head for, to go" (EtymDB 2.1). English send is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pendeō, pendēre — to hang.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to taste, then the Latin for to feel.

You'll want to know: pendeō, pendēre.
pendeō, pendēre — "to hang".

The word, taken apart.
pendeō is the intransitive partner of pendō, "to weigh, to hang up" (Lewis & Short): to be hanging. The pair is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European (s)pend-, "to stretch, to pull" (EtymDB 2.1).

taceō, tacēre — "to be silent".

The word, taken apart.
taceō is inherited from Proto-Italic takēō, "to be silent" (EtymDB 2.1), but Lewis & Short call its deeper origin doubtful. Gothic þahan, "to be silent", is often compared.

servō, servāre — "to keep, to save".

The word, taken apart.
servō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European ser-, "to watch over, to protect" (EtymDB 2.1). It is probably not the same word as servus, "a slave".

Lesson 5 of 5.
mūtō, mūtāre — to change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be silent, then the Latin for to keep.

You'll want to know: mūtō, mūtāre.
mūtō, mūtāre — "to change".

The word, taken apart.
mūtō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European mey-, "to change, to exchange", the root of mūtuus, "borrowed, mutual"; Lewis & Short's link with moveō, "to move", is less likely.
