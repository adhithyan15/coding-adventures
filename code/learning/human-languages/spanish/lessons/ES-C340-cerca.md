## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C340-WHERE-01]; assesses=[] -->

**cerca** — "near," "nearby."

Latin **circā** meant *around, round about* — the preposition of a ring drawn
about a thing. What lies around you lies close, and Spanish let the word slide
from *around* to *near*.

English took **circa** whole and left it in Latin, where it still marks a date
given loosely: *circa 1500*, somewhere in the ring around that year. The same
ring is in **circle**, in a **circuit** that goes round and comes back, and in
the **circus**, which was a ring long before it was a show.

So when you say *cerca*, the picture underneath is a circle with you at the
centre, and everything inside it counted as near.
