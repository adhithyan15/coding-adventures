Telugu, chapter 170: Time, Sometimes, Often, Date, Weekend.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
సమయం (samayaṁ) — time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a scar, then the Telugu for a sprain.

You'll want to know: సమయం.
సమయం — samayaṁ — "time".

The word, taken apart.
సమయం (samayaṁ) is Sanskrit samaya, "meeting; rule; agreement; time", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama, with the Telugu noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 13185).

Lesson 3 of 5.
తరచుగా (taracugā) — often.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for time, then the Telugu for sometimes.

You'll want to know: తరచుగా.
తరచుగా — taracugā — "often".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of taracu, "often", the word in తరచుగా (taracugā), is uncertain: DEDR and CDIAL list no word of this shape with this sense. The ending -gā makes it an adverb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
తేదీ (tēdī) — a date.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for sometimes, then the Telugu for often.

You'll want to know: తేదీ.
తేదీ — tēdī — "a date".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of తేదీ (tēdī) is not settled. Sanskrit tithi, "a lunar day" (CDIAL 5810), is the usual suggestion, but neither CDIAL nor DEDR lists the Telugu word.

వారాంతం (vārāntaṁ) is Sanskrit: vāra, "day of the week" (CDIAL 11547), and anta, "end" (CDIAL 347), with the Telugu ending -ṁ.
