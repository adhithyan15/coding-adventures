# es geht — the German shrug: "it goes"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-LEX-GEHEN-01, GE-LEX-WIE-GEHTS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Someone asks *Wie geht's?* You are neither great nor terrible. German
has an answer for exactly that, and it is made of two words you already own.

## You'll want to know: es geht
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ES-GEHT-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-01, GE-LEX-GEHEN-01, GE-LEX-GUT-02] -->

> **es geht** — "it goes." → "So-so. I'm getting by."

Nothing has been added. That is the whole trick.

*Es geht mir gut* says it goes **well**. Take the *gut* away and German does not
become vague — it becomes *pointed*. The listener was waiting for a *gut* or a
*schlecht*, and you gave neither. The **absence** is the meaning.

English does this too, with tone rather than deletion: "It's… going."

## Grammar Lens: the answer ladder
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-PRAGMATICS-ES-GEHT-SHRUG-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ES-GEHT-01, GE-LEX-SEHR-01, GE-LEX-NICHT-01, GE-LEX-SO-LALA-01] -->

You now hold the whole range, top to bottom, with no word left unexplained:

| Answer | Sense |
|---|---|
| *Sehr gut!* | very well |
| *Gut, danke.* | well, thanks |
| *Es geht.* | so-so, getting by |
| *So lala.* | so-so, sung with a shrug |
| *Nicht so gut.* | not so well |

*Es geht* and *so lala* sit on the same rung and differ only in mood: *es geht*
is dry and a little stoic, *so lala* is light and musical. Choose by the face
you want to make.
