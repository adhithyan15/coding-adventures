Latin, chapter 471: Lathe, To turn on a lathe, To polish, To sharpen, Stonecutter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tornus, tornī — a lathe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a file, then the Latin for to hew.

You'll want to know: tornus, tornī.
tornus, tornī — "a lathe".
Faber in officīnā tornum habet et pōcula lignea facit. The craftsman has a lathe in his workshop and makes wooden cups.
Second declension, masculine: tornus, tornī m. Also the finish of a poem, as if turned on a lathe.

The word, taken apart.
tornus was borrowed from Greek tornos 'lathe, a tool for drawing circles' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from PIE terh₁- 'to rub, to twist'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
tornō, tornāre — to turn on a lathe, to round off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to hew, then the Latin for a lathe.

You'll want to know: tornō, tornāre.
tornō, tornāre — "to turn on a lathe, to round off".
Faber lignum tornat, et pōculum rotundum fit. The craftsman turns the wood, and a round cup takes shape.
tornō, tornāre, tornāvī, tornātum. English turn comes from it, borrowed early into Old English.

The word, taken apart.
tornō is built on tornus 'lathe' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), a Greek borrowing. English turn comes from tornāre, borrowed early into Old English and later into French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
poliō, polīre — to polish, to smooth; to refine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lathe, then the Latin for to turn on a lathe.

You'll want to know: poliō, polīre.
poliō, polīre — "to polish, to smooth; to refine".
Servus vāsa argentea polit. The slave polishes the silver dishes.
poliō, polīre, polīvī, polītum. Polītus is polished, also refined in manner. English polish and polite come from it.

The word, taken apart.
poliō is usually traced to PIE pelh₂- (EtymDB), though Lewis & Short suggest another root and the origin is not settled. English polish comes from it through French, and polite from its participle polītus.

Lesson 4 of 5.
acuō, acuere — to sharpen, to whet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to turn on a lathe, then the Latin for to polish.

You'll want to know: acuō, acuere.
acuō, acuere — "to sharpen, to whet".
Mīles gladium ante bellum acuit. The soldier sharpens his sword before the war.
acuō, acuere, acuī, acūtum. Its participle acūtus 'sharp' gives English acute.

The word, taken apart.
acuō is inherited from PIE h₂eḱ- 'sharp' (EtymDB), the root of acus 'needle'. English acute comes from its participle acūtus, and acumen from a noun built on it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lapicīda, lapicīdae — a stonecutter, a quarryman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to polish, then the Latin for to sharpen.

You'll want to know: lapicīda, lapicīdae.
lapicīda, lapicīdae — "a stonecutter, a quarryman".
Lapicīda saxum magnum in forō caedit. The stonecutter cuts a large stone in the forum.
First declension, masculine: lapicīda, lapicīdae m. Like agricola, a masculine noun in -a.

The word, taken apart.
lapicīda is built from lapis, lapidis 'stone' and caedō 'to cut' (Lewis & Short; an older form is lapidicīda). EtymDB lists it without a parent.
