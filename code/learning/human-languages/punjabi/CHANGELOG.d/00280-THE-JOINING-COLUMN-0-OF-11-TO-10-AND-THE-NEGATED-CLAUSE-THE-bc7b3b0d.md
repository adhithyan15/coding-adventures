## The joining column, 0 of 11 to 10, and the negated clause the book never had

Chapters 37-43 — *The Sentence That Says No*, *And, Or, and Neither*, *But,
Twice Over*, *That, and Why*, *If, and When*, *He, We, and the One Who*, and
*Who, When, Where, How Many* — seven five-lesson chapters, one new item per
lesson, writing lesson third in each. Punjabi: **36 chapters and 226 lessons ->
43 and 261**.

### What the inventory measured

`Jorr` stood at **0 of 11**. Not one of *te/ate*, *jaan*, *par/lekin*,
*kyunki*, *je*, the complementiser *ki*, *jadon* or *jo* occurred anywhere in 226
lessons, so the longest structure the track taught was a four-slot single clause
and the A1 writing paper asked for a message the corpus could not produce.

Separately, `nahin` and `na` were both taught and **no negated clause pattern
was**, so nothing could be contradicted and *I don't understand* could not be
said.

### Eleven of eleven needed no new sign

Every joining device is spelled in Gurmukhi the track taught long ago — checked
against the union of glyphs in every script lesson before a single lesson was
designed. The one new letter in seven chapters is **ਥ**, and it was bought for a
question word (*kitthe*), not for a joining word. Even the apology was already
spellable, and already covered.

### Punjabi's own material carried two of the chapters

*par* and *lekin* are themselves a Sanskritic / Perso-Arabic doublet — the same
shape as the *dhanvaad* / *shukriya* pair this book has taught side by side since
its first chapter without ever saying which to choose. One lesson states the rule
on both pairs and closes `PA-A1-REG-05`, which the inventory said one lesson
would close.

And *asi* answers the oddest gap in the file: the corpus teaches the first-person
**plural** future ending four separate times, so a reader has been saying *we
will meet* for chapters without ever owning the word for *we*.

### Left uncovered, each with its reason in the file

The distributive (`JOIN-04`), the tag question (`Q-09`), the reflexive *aap*
(`PRON-09`) and the exclamative (`PRON-10`) all need **no new glyph** — they are
one-lesson jobs that one-new-item-per-lesson left no slot for. Unlike Gujarati,
nothing in this track is blocked by the script.

### Reinforcement, decomposed against the same corpus without chapters 37-43

428 -> 507:

- **+105** the tranche's own atoms, whose R3 and R4 windows fall past the last
  lesson of the book.
- **+20** pre-existing atoms whose windows did not exist until the track grew.
- **-1** a pre-existing miss closed outright (`PA-LEX-KI-02` reaches its R4).

That +105 was **+124** on the first measurement. Every chapter already opened by
retrieving the two preceding items, which is why R1 moved by only 6 across 36 new
atoms — but those reach-backs were one chapter deep, which is distance 1-4 and
lands in R1 rather than R2. Adding a same-position retrieval **two** chapters
back (distance 10, inside R2) and, in the last two chapters, **five** chapters
back (distance 25, inside R3) took it 124 -> 114 -> 105 and R2 137 -> 127. The
residue is structural: a book's last chapters have no later lessons to be
retrieved from.

### Two defects only the compiled page showed

- **Consecutive `>` lines join into one run-on paragraph.** Seven lessons used a
  two-line blockquote for a contrast, and in every one the contrast *was* the
  lesson — the two streams of the doublet, **ਕਿ** against **ਕੀ**, **ਤ** against
  **ਥ**, the two admissions. All seven now use bullets and render apart. Three
  more instances shipped in the Gujarati tranche and are filed for a follow-up.
- Ten lessons said "chapter 2" or "chapter 3". Punjabi is pinned at 34
  cross-chapter prose references and this took it to 44. All ten now name the
  thing — "the *kivēṁ* lesson", "when you first asked a name" — which is the rule
  the French and German tracks were cleared to zero under.
