Latin, chapter 447: Bedbug, To bite, To scratch, Sleepless, To lie down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cīmex, cīmicis — a bedbug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a pillow, then the Latin for a blanket.

You'll want to know: cīmex, cīmicis.
cīmex, cīmicis — "a bedbug".
In lectō caupōnae multī cīmicēs sunt. In the inn's bed there are lots of bedbugs.
Third declension, masculine: cīmex, cīmicis m. An old enemy of travellers; also a rude word for a small, annoying man.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of cīmex is not settled: Lewis & Short give the sense, a bug, but no etymology, and EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
mordeō, mordēre — to bite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a blanket, then the Latin for a bedbug.

You'll want to know: mordeō, mordēre.
mordeō, mordēre — "to bite".
Tōtam noctem cīmicēs hospitem mordent. All night the bedbugs bite the guest.
mordeō, mordēre, momordī, morsum, with a reduplicated perfect. English remorse (a biting back) and mordant come from it.

The word, taken apart.
mordeō is inherited from Proto-Italic mordeō, from PIE (s)merd- 'to bite, to sting' (EtymDB). English smart 'to sting' is a cousin, and remorse and mordant come from the Latin verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
scalpō, scalpere — to scratch; to carve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bedbug, then the Latin for to bite.

You'll want to know: scalpō, scalpere.
scalpō, scalpere — "to scratch; to carve".
Hospes caput et bracchia scalpit. The guest scratches his head and arms.
scalpō, scalpere, scalpsī, scalptum. Also to carve or engrave. English scalpel comes from scalpellum, a little knife, from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
scalpō is inherited from PIE (s)kel(H)p-, from a root meaning 'to split, to cut' (EtymDB). English scalpel comes from scalpellum, a diminutive of scalprum 'knife', built on it (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
īnsomnis, īnsomne — sleepless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to bite, then the Latin for to scratch.

You'll want to know: īnsomnis, īnsomne.
īnsomnis, īnsomne — "sleepless".
Propter cīmicēs viātor īnsomnis in lectō iacet. Because of the bedbugs the traveller lies sleepless in bed.
An adjective of two endings, from in- 'not' and somnus 'sleep'. English insomnia comes from a noun built on it.

The word, taken apart.
īnsomnis is built from in- 'not' and somnus 'sleep' (Lewis & Short). English insomnia comes from the related noun īnsomnia.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cubō, cubāre — to lie down, to lie (in bed); to sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to scratch, then the Latin for sleepless.

You'll want to know: cubō, cubāre.
cubō, cubāre — "to lie down, to lie (in bed); to sleep".
Fessus viātor in lectulō cubat, sed nōn dormit. The tired traveller lies on the little bed, but he does not sleep.
cubō, cubāre, cubuī, cubitum. English incubate and cubicle come from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
cubō is inherited from Proto-Italic kubāō, from PIE ḱewb- 'to lie down' (EtymDB). English incubate comes from incubāre, built on it (Lewis & Short).
