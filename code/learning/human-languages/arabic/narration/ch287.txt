Arabic, chapter 287: Asking The Way.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
كيف أصل إلى (kayfa aṣilu ilā) — how do I get to …?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I will travel tomorrow". (Sa-usāfiru ghadan.) Then "I want to go to the market". (Urīdu an adhhaba ilā as-sūq.)
[pause 1 second]
You are in a city you do not know. This is what you say to the first person who stops.

You'll want to know: كيف أصل إلى.
كيف أصل إلى — kayfa aṣilu ilā — "how do I get to", word for word "how do I arrive at".
من فضلك، كيف أصل إلى المحطة؟ — min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-maḥaṭṭa? — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?
Aṣilu, I arrive, is the present of waṣala, and the root's w has dropped, as it did in yajibu. To a woman, say min faḍliki.

Grammar Lens: the questions around it.
Two shorter questions often come with it. أين — ayna, "where": ayna al-matḥaf?, where is the museum? And هل with baʿīd or qarīb: hal al-matḥaf baʿīd?, is the museum far? The answer may be qarīb min hunā, near here, or baʿīd qalīlan, a little far.
The answer to kayfa aṣilu is a set of orders, which the next lesson builds.

The phrase, taken apart.
أصل (aṣilu) is built on the native Arabic root w-ṣ-l, "to reach, to join" (Lane), the root of waṣala, "he arrived", and of ittaṣala, "he phoned", a call that joins two people. كيف (kayfa) and إلى (ilā) are inherited particles, not built on three-letter roots.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-maḥaṭṭa?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, to a woman, for the museum — min faḍliki, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-matḥaf?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Is the museum far?" — hal al-matḥaf baʿīd?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"How do I get to the station?" (Kayfa aṣilu ilā al-maḥaṭṭa?) Which verb is inside aṣilu? (Waṣala, to arrive.) Which letter of its root has dropped? (The w.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
اذهب مستقيما (idhhab mustaqīman) — go straight on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask the way to the station. (Min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-maḥaṭṭa?) Then ask someone to say it again. (Karrir min faḍlik.)
[pause 1 second]
Karrir was an order. The answer to your question will be a string of them.

Grammar Lens: an order from the present.
Start from "you do it": tadhhabu, you go. Take off the ta- and the final vowel, and -dhhab is left, which cannot open with two consonants. Arabic puts a short vowel in front: u- when the stem's vowel is u, and i- otherwise.
tadhhabu becomes اذهب idhhab, go, taʿburu becomes اعبر uʿbur, cross, taktubu becomes اكتب uktub, write.
To a woman, add -ī: idhhabī, uʿburī. One common order is short and irregular: خذ khudh, take, from akhadha; to a woman, khudhī.

Grammar Lens: the way, in orders.
اذهب مستقيما، ثم خذ الشارع الأول على اليمين. — idhhab mustaqīman, thumma khudh ash-shāriʿa al-awwala ʿalā al-yamīn — Go straight on, then take the first street on the right.
اعبر الجسر، والمحطة على اليسار. — uʿbur al-jisr, wa-l-maḥaṭṭa ʿalā al-yasār — Cross the bridge, and the station is on the left.
Mustaqīman, with -an, says how you go. ʿAlā al-yamīn and ʿalā al-yasār say where a thing is. Thumma strings the steps together, as it did in a story.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tadhhabu … idhhab; taʿburu … uʿbur]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Go straight on" — idhhab mustaqīman]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Take the first street on the right" — khudh ash-shāriʿa al-awwala ʿalā al-yamīn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Cross the bridge", to a woman — uʿburī al-jisr]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the whole answer — idhhab mustaqīman, thumma uʿbur al-jisr, wa-l-maḥaṭṭa ʿalā al-yasār]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does an order come from? (The present, with ta- taken off.) "Go"? (Idhhab.) "Take"? (Khudh, the short one.) "On the left"? (ʿAlā al-yasār.) What do you add for a woman? (-ī: idhhabī.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — asking the way, and following it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I must go now" and "you ought to rest". (Yajibu an adhhaba al-ān, yanbaghī an tastarīḥa.) Then "I won't go, because of the rain". (Lan adhhaba, bi-sabab al-maṭar.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and both halves of a conversation in the street. Your half: min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā …?, with ayna and hal … baʿīd? beside it. Their half: orders, the present with its ta- taken off, strung together with thumma.
[pause 2 seconds]
One exchange, whole:
— min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-funduq? — idhhab mustaqīman, thumma khudh ash-shāriʿa al-awwala ʿalā al-yasār. — hal huwa baʿīd? — lā, qarīb min hunā.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Excuse me, how do I get to the hotel?" — min faḍlik, kayfa aṣilu ilā al-funduq?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Where is the museum?" — ayna al-matḥaf?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Go straight on, then take the first street on the left"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The station is at the corner" — al-maḥaṭṭa ʿinda az-zāwiya]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the way from your door to the centre of your own city, in three orders]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"How do I get to …?" (Kayfa aṣilu ilā …?) "Cross"? (Uʿbur.) "Take", to a woman? (Khudhī.) "On the right"? (ʿAlā al-yamīn.)
