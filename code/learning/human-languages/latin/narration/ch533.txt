Latin, chapter 533: Close friends.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
familiāris, familiāris — a close friend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for free-born, then the Latin for a felt cap.

You'll want to know: familiāris, familiāris.
familiāris, familiāris — "a close friend".
Hic vir familiāris patris meī est. This man is a close friend of my father's.
As an adjective (familiāris, familiāre), of the household; as a noun, an intimate friend. English familiar.

The word, taken apart.
familiāris is built on familia "household" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English familiar comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
necessitūdō, necessitūdinis — a close bond, tie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a close friend, then the Latin for close friendship.

You'll want to know: necessitūdō, necessitūdinis.
necessitūdō, necessitūdinis — "a close bond, tie".
Magna necessitūdō mihi cum frātre tuō est. I have a close bond with your brother.
Feminine, third declension, from necesse. Also need, but in letters it often means a tie of family or friendship.

The word, taken apart.
necessitūdō is built on necesse "necessary" (Lewis & Short): first need, then the ties that bind people.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dīligō, dīligere — to hold dear, esteem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for close friendship, then the Latin for a close bond.

You'll want to know: dīligō, dīligere.
dīligō, dīligere — "to hold dear, esteem".
Parentēs līberōs suōs dīligunt. Parents hold their children dear.
dīligō, dīligere, dīlēxī, dīlēctum. Love by choice and esteem, calmer than amō. Dīligēns, already known, comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
dīligō is built on dis- "apart" and legō "to choose" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): to single out. English diligent and predilection come from the family.
