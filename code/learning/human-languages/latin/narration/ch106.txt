Latin, chapter 106: Thirty to a Thousand.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
trīgintā — thirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for much, then the Latin for too little.

You'll want to know: trīgintā.
trīgintā — "thirty".

The word, taken apart.
trīgintā is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European trid-komt, "three tens" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek triakonta, "thirty", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
mīlle — a thousand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fifty, then the Latin for a hundred.

You'll want to know: mīlle.
mīlle — "a thousand". English mile: a thousand paces.

The word, taken apart.
mīlle is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European sm-gheslo-, "one thousand" (EtymDB 2.1), whose first part meant "one".
