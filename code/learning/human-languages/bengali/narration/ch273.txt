Bengali, chapter 273: Than, Most, and How Long.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
দৈনিক (doinik) — of each day; a daily paper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I want to be a doctor". (āmi ḍāktār hôte chāi.) Then রোজ, "daily, every day".

You'll want to know: দৈনিক.
দৈনিক — doinik — "of each day; a daily paper".
আমার দৈনিক খরচ একশো টাকা। āmār doinik khôrôch eksho ṭākā. — "My daily spending is a hundred taka."
রোজ tells you when: "every day". দৈনিক describes a thing: দৈনিক খরচ, "daily spending", দৈনিক কাজ, "daily work". On its own it is a newspaper that comes out every day: বাবা রোজ একটা দৈনিক পড়েন (bābā roj ekṭā doinik pôṛen), "Father reads a daily every day".

The word, taken apart.
দৈনিক (doinik) is a tatsama: Sanskrit dainika, "daily" (Monier-Williams). It belongs with dina, "day", Bengali দিন: CDIAL (6566) lists the related daina, "relating to a day", under dina, and dainika has the same strengthened first vowel, ai, with -ika, "of, belonging to", added.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My daily spending is a hundred taka" — āmār doinik khôrôch eksho ṭākā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Father reads a daily every day" — bābā roj ekṭā doinik pôṛen]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does দৈনিক (doinik) describe? (A thing of each day.) Which word says "every day", when? (রোজ.)

[pause 2 seconds]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ৈ.
ৈ — -oi, the vowel of দৈনিক (doinik). Like ে, it is written in front of its consonant, though it is said after it.
Set the print of দৈ beside দে. দে has ে in front: an upright curve with a small knob at its foot. দৈ has the same, and above it a hook stands on the headline, bent over to the left, its tip at the upper left: the same hook as the one on the stroke of ৌ.

Writing: ৈ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside দৈ and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large: the ে with its hook above the headline, then the দ. Check the copy against দে: only the hook differs.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ৈ (-oi) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 5.
-এর চেয়ে / সবচেয়ে (-er cheye / sôbcheye) — than, and most.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "my daily spending". (āmār doinik khôrôch.) Then বড় and ছোট, "big" and "small".

You'll want to know: than.
নদী পুকুরের চেয়ে বড়। nôdī pukurer cheye bôṛo. — "A river is bigger than a pond."
The thing you measure against takes the -র form and চেয়ে (cheye), "than". The describing word does not change: বড় is "big" and "bigger". ট্রেন বাসের চেয়ে তাড়াতাড়ি যায় (ṭren bāser cheye tāṛātāṛi jāy), "a train goes faster than a bus".

You'll want to know: most.
সব (sôb) is "all", so সবচেয়ে (sôbcheye) is "than all": "most, the -est".
আমার সবচেয়ে প্রিয় খাবার মাছ। āmār sôbcheye priyô khābār māchh. — "My favourite food of all is fish."
এটা সবচেয়ে দামি বই (eṭā sôbcheye dāmi bôi), "this is the most expensive book".

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 4 of 5.
… ধরে / থেকে … পর্যন্ত (… dhôre / theke … pôrjôntô) (dhôre / theke … pôrjôntô) — how long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "A river is bigger than a pond". (nôdī pukurer cheye bôṛo.) Then ধরা, "to hold".

You'll want to know: for a stretch of time.
আমি দুই বছর ধরে বাংলা শিখছি। āmi dui bôchhôr dhôre bānglā shikhchhi. — "I have been learning Bengali for two years."
ধরে is ধরা in the -e form: "holding" a stretch of time. With the going-on present, it says what started then and is still going on: English says "have been", Bengali says "am". In the past it is a stretch that is over: আমরা তিন দিন ধরে হাঁটলাম (āmrā tin din dhôre hā̃ṭlām), "we walked for three days".

You'll want to know: from … until.
থেকে, "from", which you have met, is থাকা in the -e form; পর্যন্ত (pôrjôntô) is "until, as far as". They frame a stretch with two ends:
দোকান সকাল থেকে রাত পর্যন্ত খোলা থাকে। dokān sôkāl theke rāt pôrjôntô kholā thāke. — "The shop is open from morning until night."
পর্যন্ত measures places too: বাড়ি থেকে স্টেশন পর্যন্ত (bāṛi theke sṭeshôn pôrjôntô), "from the house as far as the station".

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
