Latin, chapter 473: Countryside, Farm, Ox stall, Cottage, Hut.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rūs, rūris — the countryside, the country; a farm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to spin, then the Latin for wool-working.

You'll want to know: rūs, rūris.
rūs, rūris — "the countryside, the country; a farm".
Aestāte multī cīvēs rūs eunt. In summer many citizens go to the country.
Third declension, neuter: rūs, rūris n. Like domus, it takes no preposition: rūs 'to the country', rūre 'from the country', rūrī 'in the country'. English rural and rustic come from it.

The word, taken apart.
rūs is inherited from Proto-Italic rowos, from PIE rewh₁- 'open space, field' (EtymDB). English rural and, through rūsticus, rustic come from it; German Raum 'space' is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fundus, fundī — a farm, an estate; the bottom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wool-working, then the Latin for the countryside.

You'll want to know: fundus, fundī.
fundus, fundī — "a farm, an estate; the bottom".
Fundus noster parvus est sed bonus. Our farm is small but good.
Second declension, masculine: fundus, fundī m. First the bottom of a thing, then the ground itself, a piece of land. English fund and foundation go back to the same word.

The word, taken apart.
fundus is inherited from PIE bʰudʰmḗn 'bottom' (EtymDB), with Greek pythmēn 'bottom' as a cousin. English fund and profound come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
būbīle, būbīlis — an ox stall, a cowshed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the countryside, then the Latin for a farm.

You'll want to know: būbīle, būbīlis.
būbīle, būbīlis — "an ox stall, a cowshed".
Bovēs hieme in būbīlī manent. In winter the oxen stay in the cowshed.
Third declension, neuter: būbīle, būbīlis n., from bōs, bovis 'ox'. A word of the farm writers.

The word, taken apart.
būbīle is built on bōs, bovis 'ox' with the suffix -īle 'place for animals', as in ovīle 'sheepfold' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
casa, casae — a cottage, a small house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a farm, then the Latin for an ox stall.

You'll want to know: casa, casae.
casa, casae — "a cottage, a small house".
Pastor in casā parvā prope silvam habitat. The shepherd lives in a small cottage near the wood.
First declension, feminine: casa, casae f. In Italian and Spanish it became the everyday word for 'house'.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of casa is not settled: Lewis & Short link it with a root meaning 'to cover', but EtymDB gives no parent. It became casa 'house' in Italian, Spanish and Portuguese.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tugurium, tuguriī — a hut, a shed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an ox stall, then the Latin for a cottage.

You'll want to know: tugurium, tuguriī.
tugurium, tuguriī — "a hut, a shed".
Pauper agricola in tuguriō cum familiā vīvit. The poor farmer lives in a hut with his family.
Second declension, neuter: tugurium, tuguriī n. A rough hut, poorer than a casa.

The word, taken apart.
tugurium is usually linked with tegō 'to cover' (Lewis & Short), so a shelter with a roof; EtymDB traces it to PIE (s)teg- 'to cover with a roof', with Greek stegō 'to cover' as a cousin.
