Spanish, chapter 732: Cuidarse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
saludable — healthy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for optimistic, then the Spanish for pessimistic.

You'll want to know: saludable.
saludable — "healthy".
Como de forma saludable. I eat in a healthy way.
For food and habits; a healthy person is sano.

The word, taken apart.
saludable is built on salud "health" with the ending -able; salud comes from Latin salūs, salūtem "health, safety" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
cuidarse — to look after oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for pessimistic, then the Spanish for healthy.

You'll want to know: cuidarse.
cuidarse — "to look after oneself".
Cuídate mucho. Take good care of yourself.
¡Cuídate! is a common goodbye.

The word, taken apart.
cuidarse is the reflexive of cuidar, which is inherited from Latin cōgitāre "to think" (EtymDB); in old Spanish cuidar meant to think, then to watch over.

Lesson 4 of 5.
enfermarse — to fall ill, to get sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to look after oneself, then the Spanish for illness.

You'll want to know: enfermarse.
enfermarse — "to fall ill, to get sick".
Me enfermé en las vacaciones. I got sick on holiday.
Common in Latin America; in Spain people say ponerse enfermo or enfermar.

The word, taken apart.
enfermarse is built on enfermo "ill", which is inherited from Latin īnfirmus "weak" (EtymDB).
