# hacemos, hacen — and the club has no plural at all

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-YO-GO-CONTRAST-LEARNED, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-PRESENT-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the six *-go* verbs. And say where in each one the *-go*
appears.

## Grammar Lens: an oddity that was only ever in one slot
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-YO-GO-PLURAL-REGULAR]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-YO-GO-CONTRAST-LEARNED, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-CHANGE-STRESS-RULE] -->

You were told, when the club closed, that the **-go** lives in the *yo* form and
nowhere else. Here is what that is worth:

> **hacemos** · **hacéis** · **hacen** — plain *-er* endings.
> **ponemos** · **ponéis** · **ponen** — plain *-er* endings.
> **salimos** · **salís** · **salen** — plain *-ir* endings.

Nothing to learn. The *-go* club has no plural forms of its own, because the
thing that made it a club was never in the plural to begin with.

Two of the six do something in the plural, and it is **not** the *-go*:

> **decimos** but **dicen** · **venimos** but **vienen**

Those are stem changes, and they follow the rule you already have — the stem
breaks where it is stressed, and *nosotros* is never stressed on the stem. So
even the exceptions are not exceptions.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-YO-GO-PLURAL-REGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-STEM-CHANGE-STRESS-RULE] -->

Two rules — **the *-go* is yo-only**, and **stems break under stress** — now
generate the entire present tense of every irregular verb in this book, in every
person.

That is what a rule buys that a table does not: the table stops at the forms
printed on it.
