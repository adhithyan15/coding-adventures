French, chapter 295: Les aveux.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
avouer — to confess, to admit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for police custody, then the French for to question.

You'll want to know: avouer.
avouer — "to confess, to admit".
Il a fini par avouer. In the end he confessed.
Je dois avouer que… — I must admit that…

The word, taken apart.
avouer is inherited through Old French avoer from Latin advocāre "to call to, to call upon" (EtymDB).

l'aveu — "the confession, the avowal".
Ses aveux ont surpris tout le monde. His confession surprised everyone.
Often plural: passer aux aveux — to confess.

aveu is built on avouer "to confess", which is inherited from Latin advocāre "to call to" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le complice — the accomplice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to confess, then the French for the confession.

You'll want to know: le complice.
le complice — "the accomplice".
Il avait un complice qui l'attendait dans la voiture. He had an accomplice who was waiting for him in the car.
Also an adjective: un sourire complice — a knowing smile.

The word, taken apart.
complice is a learned borrowing from Late Latin complex, complicem "partner, one folded in with" (EtymDB), built on plicāre "to fold".

Lesson 4 of 5.
acquitter — to acquit; to pay off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the confession, then the French for the accomplice.

You'll want to know: acquitter.
acquitter — "to acquit; to pay off".
Le tribunal a acquitté le jeune homme. The court acquitted the young man.
Also to pay a debt: s'acquitter d'une dette.

The word, taken apart.
acquitter is built on quitte "free of debt", which comes from Medieval Latin quitus, from Latin quiētus "at rest" (Lewis & Short).
