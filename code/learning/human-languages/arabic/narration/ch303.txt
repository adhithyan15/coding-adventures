Arabic, chapter 303: Legends and heroes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أسطورة (usṭūra) — a legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to host, then the Arabic for a spokesperson.

You'll want to know: أسطورة.
أسطورة — usṭūra — "a legend".
سمعنا أسطورة عن جبل قديم. samiʿnā usṭūra ʿan jabal qadīm. We heard a legend about an old mountain.
Plural: أساطير, asāṭīr.

The word, taken apart.
أسطورة (usṭūra) is filed by Lane under the root s-ṭ-r, "a line of writing", and its plural asāṭīr is old. It was borrowed from Greek historia, "an inquiry, a story" (EtymDB); others see a native word, so the origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
خرافة (khurāfa) — a myth, a superstition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a spokesperson, then the Arabic for a legend.

You'll want to know: خرافة.
خرافة — khurāfa — "a myth, a superstition".
قال جدي إن القصة خرافة. qāla jaddī inna al-qiṣṣa khurāfa. My grandfather said the story was a myth.
Plural خرافات. Of a belief it means a superstition.

The word, taken apart.
خرافة (khurāfa) comes from a name, as Lane records under the root kh-r-f (Lane): Khurāfa, a man of the tribe of ʿUdhra, told what he had seen among the jinn and was called a liar, and khurāfāt became tales deemed pretty but untrue.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بطل (baṭal) — a hero; a champion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a legend, then the Arabic for a myth.

You'll want to know: بطل.
بطل — baṭal — "a hero; a champion".
كان جدي بطلا في قريته. kāna jaddī baṭalan fī qaryatihi. My grandfather was a hero in his village.
Plural: أبطال, abṭāl.
In sport بطل (baṭal) is a champion, and the hero of a film or novel is its بطل (baṭal) too.

The word, taken apart.
بطل (baṭal) is built on the native Arabic root b-ṭ-l (Lane), "to come to nothing". Lane explains baṭal, a brave man, as one whose wound goes for nothing, so that it does not hold him back.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: شرير.
شرير — shirrīr — "a villain; wicked".
كان الملك في الحكاية شريرا. kāna al-malik fī al-ḥikāya shirrīran. The king in the tale was wicked.

The word, taken apart.
شرير (shirrīr) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-r (Lane), the root of sharr, evil. The fiʿʿīl pattern marks a quality held strongly: "very evil".

Lesson 5 of 5.
تخيل (takhayyala) — to imagine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a hero, then the Arabic for a villain.

You'll want to know: تخيل.
تخيل — takhayyala — "to imagine".
تخيلت البنت أنها تطير فوق المدينة. takhayyalat al-bint annahā taṭīru fawqa al-madīna. The girl imagined that she was flying over the city.
Present: يتخيل, yatakhayyal.
It often takes أن: تخيلت أنها …, she imagined that she ….

The word, taken apart.
تخيل (takhayyala) is built on the native Arabic root kh-y-l (Lane), "to think, to fancy", the root of khayāl, imagination. It is Form V, "to picture to oneself".
