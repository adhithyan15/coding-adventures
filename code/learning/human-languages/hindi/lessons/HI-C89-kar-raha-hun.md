# कर रहा हूँ — one word slid into the middle

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-KARNA-01, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I do* — the habit. Now try to say what you are doing **at this
moment**, and notice the habit form does not quite mean it.

## You'll want to know: कर रहा हूँ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**कर रहा हूँ** (*kar rahā hū̃*) — "I am doing," right now, said by a man.

Three words: the bare stem, then **रहा**, then the copula you have had since
your third chapter.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-HUN-01] -->

| piece | what it carries |
|---|---|
| कर | the action, bare |
| रहा | that it is in progress |
| हूँ | who, and that it is now |

**रहा** is the past participle of **रहना**, "to stay, to remain." So the
sentence says, almost literally, *doing — having stayed — I am*. The action is
pictured as one you are **still inside**.

English does the same thing from a different angle: *I am doing* uses a
participle too, and *I remain at work* is the same metaphor spelled out.

## Grammar Lens: the stem is bare, and that is the point
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

Look at what happens to the verb in each tense:

| what you say | the verb |
|---|---|
| I do (habit) | करता |
| I am doing (now) | कर |

The habit puts an ending on the stem. The continuous **takes the ending off**
and puts a separate word after it instead. Nothing else in the sentence moves.

That means the pattern works on **any** verb you know. Take the stem, add
**रहा**, keep the copula: *बोल रहा हूँ*, *जा रहा हूँ*, *खा रहा हूँ*.
