Lesson 1 of 4.
जवळ (javaḷ) — near, close to.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last chapter stacked things above and below each other. Real directions are almost never that tidy.
[your turn — recall: say khālī, then var, then bend घर and glue each on]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: जवळ.
जवळ — javaḷ — near, close to.
माझं घर पुण्याजवळ आहे. — mājhaṁ ghar puṇyājavaḷ āhe. — my house is near Pune.
This is the answer a real person gives when you ask कुठे? and the true answer is not a single named point. It takes the same oblique stem as everything else in this column — पुणे becomes पुण्या- — so nothing new has to be learned about the noun.
जवळ (javaḷ) carries the ळ, the retroflex l that is Marathi's own letter and that Hindi does not have. Say it with the tongue curled back, the way you learned it in chapter three, or the word will sound like a different one.
It has a second job you will need in the next chapter: X जवळ (javaḷ) can also mean in X's keeping — near enough to reach — which is how Marathi gets close to saying somebody HAS something.

Lesson 2 of 4.
समोर (samor) — in front of, opposite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
जवळ (javaḷ) says close by, which does not say which side.

You'll want to know: समोर.
समोर — samor — in front of, opposite, facing.
घरासमोर — gharāsamor — in front of the house.
This is the postposition a set of directions is actually made of. Opposite the station, in front of the shop — a Marathi speaker giving you a landmark reaches for समोर (samor) before anything else.
Take the word apart and it pays you back. It is Sanskrit सम्मुख (sammukha) — सम् together plus मुख face. Chapter nineteen told you that मुख is the formal doublet of तोंड, the everyday mouth. So समोर (samor) is, quite literally, face-to-face: the thing whose face is turned toward yours.
That is why it means opposite as well as in front of. English keeps those apart; Marathi does not need to, because a face has only one direction.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: samor — in front of]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: gharāsamor, then gharājavaḷ — a side, then a distance]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which body part is inside this word — मुख, the formal tond]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'in front of the house' and 'near the house', and name the difference.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What two pieces is समोर (samor) built from? (सम् together and मुख face.) Why does one word cover both in front of and opposite? (A face points one way; both meanings are that.)

Lesson 3 of 4.
लांब (lāmb) — far, and long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
जवळ (javaḷ) answers near. Nothing yet answers the other half of the question.

You'll want to know: लांब.
लांब — lāmb — far; long.
माझं घर लांब आहे. — mājhaṁ ghar lāmb āhe. — my house is far away.
लांब (lāmb) is जवळ (javaḷ)'s opposite and does not behave like it. जवळ (javaḷ) is a postposition and wants a noun bent in front of it; लांब (lāmb) is an ordinary word that can simply stand in the sentence, which is why the example above needs no oblique stem at all.
The second meaning is the one to hold onto, because it explains the first. Sanskrit लम्ब (lamba) means "hanging down, extended" — so लांब (lāmb) is long before it is far, and far is only long applied to a distance. A Marathi speaker uses the one word for a long rope and a distant town without feeling any jump.
Pair the two and you can answer a real question:
जवळ, की लांब? — javaḷ, kī lāmb? — near, or far?

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of जवळ (javaḷ) and लांब (lāmb) needs the oblique stem? (जवळ (javaḷ) — it is a postposition; लांब (lāmb) is not.) What does लांब (lāmb) mean before it means far? (Long.)
