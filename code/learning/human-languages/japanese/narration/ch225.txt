Japanese, chapter 225: Practice and Revision.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おぼえる (oboeru) — to memorize; to learn by heart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to teach, then the Japanese for to take lessons.

You'll want to know: おぼえる.
おぼえる — oboeru — "to memorize; to learn by heart".
せんせいは がくせいの なまえを おぼえます。 — sensei wa gakusei no namae o oboemasu — "The teacher learns the students' names by heart."
Polite おぼえます, oboemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
おぼえる (oboeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

[pause 2 seconds]

れんしゅう — renshū — "practice; training".
まいあさ うたの れんしゅうを します。 — maiasa uta no renshū o shimasu — "I do singing practice every morning."

The word, taken apart.
れんしゅう (renshū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ren, "drill, train" (Middle Chinese lenH) and shū, "learn" (zip) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
