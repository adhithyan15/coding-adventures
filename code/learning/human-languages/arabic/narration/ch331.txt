Arabic, chapter 331: Rising empires.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شيد (shayyada) — to erect, to build up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a shackle, then the Arabic for to release.

You'll want to know: شيد.
شيد — shayyada — "to erect, to build up".
شيد الملك قصرا كبيرا. shayyada al-malik qaṣran kabīran. The king built a great palace.
Present: يشيد, yushayyid.
Of grand buildings; بنى is the everyday verb.

The word, taken apart.
شيد (shayyada) is built on the native Arabic root sh-y-d (Lane), where shīd is plaster and Form II shayyada is "to plaster a building, to raise it high".

Lesson 2 of 5.
سيطر (sayṭara) — to control, to take control.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to release, then the Arabic for to erect.

You'll want to know: سيطر.
سيطر — sayṭara — "to control, to take control".
سيطر رجال الإطفاء على الحريق. sayṭara rijāl al-iṭfāʾ ʿalā al-ḥarīq. The firefighters brought the fire under control.
Present: يسيطر, yusayṭir.
It takes على: سيطر (sayṭara) على.

The word, taken apart.
سيطر (sayṭara) is filed under the root s-ṭ-r (Lane), the root of saṭr, a line, with muṣayṭir, an overseer. Its earlier history, perhaps through Aramaic, is not settled.

Present: يمتد, yamtadd.

The word, taken apart.
امتد (imtadda) is built on the native Arabic root m-d-d (Lane), "to stretch out". It is Form VIII, "to be stretched, to reach".

ازدهر (izdahara) is built on the native Arabic root z-h-r (Lane), "to shine; to blossom", the root of zahra, a flower. It is Form VIII, where the t turns to d after z.

Lesson 5 of 5.
إمبراطور (imbarāṭūr) — an emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to extend, then the Arabic for to flourish.

You'll want to know: إمبراطور.
إمبراطور — imbarāṭūr — "an emperor".
حكم الإمبراطور بلادا واسعة. ḥakama al-imbarāṭūr bilādan wāsiʿa. The emperor ruled wide lands.
An empire is إمبراطورية.

The word, taken apart.
إمبراطور (imbarāṭūr) is usually traced to Latin imperator, "a commander", which reached Arabic through a European language.
