## You'll want to know: īnsula, īnsulae
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C457-INSULA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**īnsula, īnsulae** — "a block of flats, an apartment house; an island".

> In urbe multī pauperēs in īnsulīs altīs habitant.
>
> *In the city many poor people live in tall blocks of flats.*

First declension, feminine: *īnsula, īnsulae f.* First an island, then a block of rented flats standing alone like an island among streets. English *isle* and *insulate* come from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

The origin of *īnsula* is not settled: Lewis & Short offer a doubtful analysis as *in-* + *sul-*, and EtymDB gives no older parent. English *isle* comes from it through French, and *insulate* and *peninsula* from Latin.
