6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
il malinteso — the misunderstanding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for credible, then the Italian for reliable.

You'll want to know: il malinteso.
il malinteso — "the misunderstanding".
Mi dispiace, è stato un malinteso. I am sorry, it was a misunderstanding.
C'è stato un malinteso — there has been a misunderstanding.

The word, taken apart.
malinteso is built from male "badly", from Latin male, and inteso, past participle of intendere "to understand", from Latin intendere "to stretch towards, to direct the mind" (Lewis & Short), built on tendere.

You'll want to know: fraintendere.
fraintendere — "to misunderstand".
Forse mi hai frainteso: non volevo criticarti. Maybe you misunderstood me: I did not want to criticise you.
Past participle frainteso. Built like intendere, which means to understand.

The word, taken apart.
fraintendere is built from fra "between, partly" and intendere "to understand", from Latin intendere "to direct the mind" (Lewis & Short), built on tendere "to stretch": to understand halfway.

Lesson 3 of 6.
chiarire — to clarify, to clear up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the misunderstanding, then the Italian for to misunderstand.

You'll want to know: chiarire.
chiarire — "to clarify, to clear up".
Vorrei chiarire una cosa prima di cominciare. I would like to clarify one thing before we start.
Conjugated with -isc-: chiarisco, chiarisci. Chiarirsi — to sort things out with someone.

The word, taken apart.
chiarire is built on chiaro "clear", which is inherited from Latin clārus "bright, loud, clear" (Lewis & Short): to make something clear.

Lesson 4 of 6.
precisare — to specify, to make clear (a detail).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to misunderstand, then the Italian for to clarify.

You'll want to know: precisare.
precisare — "to specify, to make clear (a detail)".
Voglio precisare che non è colpa mia. I want to make clear that it is not my fault.
Ci tengo a precisare che — I want to point out that. Built on preciso "precise".

The word, taken apart.
precisare is built on preciso "precise", a learned borrowing from Latin praecīsus "cut off, abrupt" (Lewis & Short), from praecīdere "to cut off in front", built on caedere "to cut": what is left after cutting away the extra.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Before the new one: say the Italian for to clarify, then the Italian for to specify.

You'll want to know: il chiarimento.
il chiarimento — "the clarification, the explanation".
Ho chiesto un chiarimento al professore. I asked the teacher for a clarification.
Chiedere un chiarimento — to ask for a clarification. Between friends it can also be a frank talk that sorts things out.

The word, taken apart.
chiarimento is built on chiarire "to clarify" with the noun ending -mento; the verb comes from chiaro, inherited from Latin clārus "bright, clear" (Lewis & Short).
