Lesson 1 of 6.
じょげん (jogen) — advice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to adopt an idea, then the Japanese for scattered.

You'll want to know: じょげん.
じょげん — jogen — "advice".
せんぱいの じょげんを きいた ほうが いいです。 — senpai no jogen o kiita hō ga ii desu — "You should listen to your senior's advice."

The word, taken apart.
じょげん (jogen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jo, "help" (Middle Chinese dzrjoH) and gen, "word" (ngjon) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 6.
ちゅうこくする (chūkoku suru) — to warn; to advise against.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for scattered, then the Japanese for advice.

You'll want to know: ちゅうこくする.
ちゅうこくする — chūkoku suru — "to warn; to advise against".
あぶないから、いしゃが ちゅうこくしました。 — abunai kara, isha ga chūkoku shimashita — "It is dangerous, so the doctor warned me."
Polite ちゅうこくします, chūkokushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): いしゃが ちゅうこくしました。, isha ga chūkoku shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ちゅうこくする (chūkoku suru) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb suru, "to do". The noun, chūkoku, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: chū, "loyalty" and koku, "tell" (Middle Chinese kowk) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.
こころがける (kokorogakeru) — to keep in mind; to make a point of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for advice, then the Japanese for to warn.

You'll want to know: こころがける.
こころがける — kokorogakeru — "to keep in mind; to make a point of".
けんこうの ために、はやく ねる ことを こころがけて ください。 — kenkō no tame ni, hayaku neru koto o kokorogakete kudasai — "For your health, make a point of going to bed early."
Polite こころがけます, kokorogakemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あには あさの うんどうを こころがけました。, ani wa asa no undō o kokorogakemashita.

The word, taken apart.
こころがける (kokorogakeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from kokoro, "heart" and kakeru, "to hang", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
