# plus … que — more than

## You'll want to know: plus … que, moins … que
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-PLUS-QUE-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C99-VILLE51-04, FR-LEX-C99-VILLE51-05, FR-LEX-C100-VILLE52-01, FR-LEX-C92-ADJ44-03, FR-LEX-MOI-01] -->

To compare two things, put **plus**, "more", in front of the adjective and
**que**, "than", in front of the second thing:

> Le train est **plus** rapide **que** la voiture. — The train is faster than
> the car.
> L'avion est **plus** cher **que** le train. — The plane is dearer than the
> train.
> Tu es **plus** jeune **que** moi. — You are younger than me.

English often builds the comparison into the adjective, *faster*,
*younger*; French builds it with *plus*. After *que* a person takes the
strong form you know: *que moi*, *que toi*, *que lui*.

Its opposite is **moins**, "less": *Le vélo est **moins** cher **que** la
voiture.* And for "as … as", **aussi … que**: *Le train est **aussi** cher
**que** l'avion.*

## Grammar Lens: le plus, and the one that refuses
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-MEILLEUR-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-BON-03, FR-LEX-THE-03, FR-GRAM-ADJ-APRES-NOM-10] -->

For the most of all, give *plus* the article as well:

> *le train **le plus** rapide* — the fastest train
> *la voiture **la plus** chère* — the dearest car

These adjectives stay after the noun, as in *le vin rouge*, and the
adjective takes its own article: *le train le plus rapide*, the article
twice.

The commonest adjective of all refuses *plus*. **Bon** becomes
**meilleur**, "better": *Le café est **meilleur** que le thé.* Its
adverb, *bien*, becomes **mieux**: *Tu parles **mieux** que moi.* —
You speak better than me.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**plus** is inherited from Latin *plūs*, "more" (EtymDB 2.1), which English
kept whole as **plus**. **moins** is inherited, through Old French *meins*,
from Latin *minus*, "less" (EtymDB 2.1), English **minus**. **meilleur** is
inherited, through Old French *meillor*, from Latin *melior*, "better"
(EtymDB 2.1), which English kept in **ameliorate**, "to make better"; and
**mieux** goes back to its neighbour *melius*.
