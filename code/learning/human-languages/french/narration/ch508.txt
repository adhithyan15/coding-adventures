French, chapter 508: La course.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le coureur — runner; rider (in a race).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for champion, then the French for to face.

You'll want to know: le coureur.
le coureur — "runner; rider (in a race)".
Le coureur a battu son propre record. The runner beat his own record.
A woman is la coureuse. Also a cyclist in a race: un coureur cycliste.

The word, taken apart.
le coureur is built on courir "to run", inherited from Latin currere "to run" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la ligne d'arrivée — finishing line.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to face, then the French for runner.

You'll want to know: la ligne d'arrivée.
la ligne d'arrivée — "finishing line".
Elle a franchi la ligne d'arrivée en premier. She crossed the finishing line first.
The start is la ligne de départ.

The word, taken apart.
la ligne d'arrivée joins ligne, from Latin līnea "a line", and arrivée, built on arriver, from Vulgar Latin arrīpāre "to come to the shore", built on rīpa "bank" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le marathon — marathon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for runner, then the French for finishing line.

You'll want to know: le marathon.
le marathon — "marathon".
Mon père a couru son premier marathon à cinquante ans. My father ran his first marathon at fifty.
Also a long, tiring task: un marathon de réunions.

The word, taken apart.
le marathon takes its name from a Greek town (EtymDB), whose own name comes from Greek márathon "fennel"; the race recalls a runner's long journey from there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le relais — relay; relay race.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for finishing line, then the French for marathon.

You'll want to know: le relais.
le relais — "relay; relay race".
Notre équipe a gagné le relais quatre fois cent mètres. Our team won the four by one hundred metres relay.
Prendre le relais — to take over.

The word, taken apart.
le relais comes from Old French relaier "to leave behind" (EtymDB), built from re- and laier "to leave": fresh horses were left waiting along the road.

Lesson 5 of 5.
chronométrer — to time (with a stopwatch).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for marathon, then the French for relay.

You'll want to know: chronométrer.
chronométrer — "to time (with a stopwatch)".
L'entraîneur chronomètre chaque coureur. The coach times each runner.
Un chronomètre, informally un chrono — a stopwatch.

The word, taken apart.
chronométrer is built on chronomètre "stopwatch", a learned word made from Greek khrónos "time" and métron "measure".
