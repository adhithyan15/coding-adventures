## HL-C190 — पाँव joins the Hindi foot headword

After **में**, the ranked Hindi queue returns to already classified script and
morphology fragments and to historical forms supplied for etymology. **पाँव**
is the next independent everyday word whose lesson directly defines and
assesses it.

Chapter 38 teaches **पैर** as “foot,” then introduces **पाँव** as Hindi's other
everyday word for the same body part. The historical contrast is the point of
the lesson: **पाँव** descends from Sanskrit **पाद**, while **पैर** descends from
the shorter stem **पद्**. Guided practice and wrap-up recall both ask learners
to distinguish the two modern forms by those ancestors. The old headword named
only **पैर**, hiding the assessed synonym.

The headword is now **पैर / पाँव**, with a gloss that names both modern words
and their contrasting stems. This is a metadata repair for existing teaching,
not a new vocabulary claim. Rerun the ranked report next and continue past the
explicitly classified script, morphology, and historical-source rows to the
next independent learner-facing form.
