# Nine moves, sorted by how much the page is helping

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-NAME-NO-MODEL-01, PA-FORM-LANGUAGE-NO-MODEL-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two fields, no model, nothing on the page but a label and a blank.
Write both before reading on.

## Guided Practice: across the three fields, one rung at a time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-NAME-SUPPORTED-ENTRY-01, PA-FORM-LANGUAGE-SUPPORTED-ENTRY-01, PA-FORM-RESIDENCE-SUPPORTED-ENTRY-01, PA-FORM-NAME-DELAYED-ENTRY-01, PA-FORM-LANGUAGE-DELAYED-ENTRY-01, PA-FORM-RESIDENCE-DELAYED-ENTRY-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s] The book taught these field by field. Take them rung by rung instead.

- [YOU WRITE: all three fields with a bank in front of you — the **supported** rung]
- [YOU WRITE: all three after the bank is covered — the **delayed** rung]
- [YOU WRITE: all three from a label alone — the **no-model** rung]

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-NAME-NO-MODEL-01, PA-FORM-LANGUAGE-NO-MODEL-01, PA-FORM-RESIDENCE-NO-MODEL-01] -->

Read across rather than down and the ladder stops being about forms at all. It is
about **what is in the room when you produce something**: a page that shows you
the answer, a page that showed you and stopped, and a page that never did.

Three fields multiplied by three rungs is nine separate things you can now do,
and the only three that count as writing evidence are the bottom row. The other
six are the scaffolding that got you there, and scaffolding is worth naming
rather than mistaking for the building.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-RESIDENCE-NO-MODEL-01, PA-FORM-NAME-DELAYED-ENTRY-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Of the nine moves, how many are writing evidence? (**Three** — the
no-model row.) What is the difference between the supported and delayed rungs?
(**The bank is visible, then covered.**)
