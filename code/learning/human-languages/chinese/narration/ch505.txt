Mandarin Chinese, chapter 505: Dancing.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
wǔdǎo — the art of dance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a folk song, then the Mandarin for to perform a song (on stage).

You'll want to know: wǔdǎo.
wǔdǎo — "the art of dance".
wǒ nǚ'ér xué le wǔ nián wǔdǎo — My daughter has studied dance for five years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: wǔ is said with a rising tone before dǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
wǔdǎo is built from wǔ 'to dance' and dǎo 'to tread', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjuX and dawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'singing and dancing' and the second 'to tread'. The modern third tone of dǎo is irregular: dawH would regularly give dào.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǔdǎo, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ nǚ'ér xué le wǔ nián wǔdǎo — My daughter has studied dance for five years.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 6.
舞会 (wǔhuì) — dance party; ball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to perform a song (on stage), then the Mandarin for the art of dance.

You'll want to know: 舞会.
舞会 — wǔhuì — "dance party; ball".
zhè shì wǒ dìyī cì cānjiā wǔhuì — This is the first time I've been to a dance.
The new character, in pieces: 舞 (wǔ) — fourteen strokes: on top eight, a short stroke falling left, two bars with four short verticals through the lower one, and a long bar across; below, on the left the top of 多 duō, three strokes, and on the right three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
舞会 (wǔhuì) is built from 舞 (wǔ) 'to dance' and 会 'a gathering', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjuX and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'singing and dancing' and the second 'to meet'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
舞 (wǔ) — eight strokes on top, then two pieces below.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — eight strokes on top, then two pieces below and Guided Practice.

The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say wǔhuì — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
多 会.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — duō, huì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — eight strokes on top, then two pieces below.
舞 (wǔ).
The top, eight strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. A short bar, then a longer bar under it. Four short verticals through it, from left to right. Then a long bar across the whole width.
The lower left, three strokes, as on top of 多: a stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns and falls to the left, and a dot.
The lower right, three strokes: a bar, a stroke that drops and turns to the right, then a long vertical down through them.
8 + 3 + 3 = fourteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 舞 (wǔ), all fourteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 舞 (wǔ) have? (Fourteen.) How many short verticals in the top? (Four.) Which character you know has the lower left piece on top? (多.)

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the art of dance, then the Mandarin for a dance party.

You'll want to know: wǔbàn.
wǔbàn — "dance partner".
tā shì wǒ zuì hǎo de wǔbàn — She's my best dance partner.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wǔbàn is built from 舞 (wǔ) 'to dance' and bàn 'a companion', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjuX and banX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a companion'.
