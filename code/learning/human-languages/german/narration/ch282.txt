German, chapter 282: Der Überfall.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
überfallen — to hold up, to attack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to pinch, then the German for the loot.

You'll want to know: überfallen.
überfallen — "to hold up, to attack".
Zwei Männer überfielen eine Tankstelle. Two men held up a petrol station.
Not separable: sie überfielen, sie haben überfallen.

The word, taken apart.
überfallen is inherited from Middle High German übervallen (EtymDB), built on über "over" and fallen "to fall": to fall upon someone.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
rauben — to rob, to steal by force.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hold up, then the German for the hold-up.

You'll want to know: rauben.
rauben — "to rob, to steal by force".
Der Mann raubte einer Frau die Tasche. The man robbed a woman of her bag.
Jemandem etwas rauben: the person goes in the dative. Der Raub — the robbery.

The word, taken apart.
rauben is inherited from Old High German roubōn, which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic raubōną and further to a root meaning "to snatch".

Lesson 4 of 5.
bedrohen — to threaten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the hold-up, then the German for to rob.

You'll want to know: bedrohen.
bedrohen — "to threaten".
Er bedrohte den Verkäufer mit einem Messer. He threatened the shop assistant with a knife.
Jemanden mit etwas bedrohen. The plain verb drohen takes the dative: er drohte ihm.

The word, taken apart.
bedrohen is built on drohen "to threaten", from Old High German drouwen (EtymDB), with the prefix be-.
