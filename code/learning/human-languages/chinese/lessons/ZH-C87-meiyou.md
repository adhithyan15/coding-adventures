## You'll want to know: 没有
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C87-MEIYOU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**没有** — *méiyǒu* — "not have; there is no".

> **我没有手机** *wǒ méiyǒu shǒujī* — I don't have a phone.

> **这儿没有人** *zhèr méiyǒu rén* — There's nobody here.

The next lesson puts the new character in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**没有** (*méiyǒu*) is built from 没 'not' and 有 'to have'. 有 is inherited from Middle Chinese *hjuwX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 没 is there as *mwot*, glossed 'to sink'; the Guangyun does not give 'not', and the regular sound change from *mwot* gives mò, not méi, so how 没 came to mean 'not' and to be said méi is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-YOU-EXISTENCE-01] -->

有 has its own negative, and it is 没, not 不: 没有. Both jobs of 有 go with it, 'to have' and 'there is': 我没有手机, 这儿没有人.
