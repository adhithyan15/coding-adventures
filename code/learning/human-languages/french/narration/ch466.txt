French, chapter 466: Timides et sociables.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la timidité — shyness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a team, then the French for a birthday.

You'll want to know: la timidité.
la timidité — "shyness".
Avec le temps, il a perdu sa timidité. With time, he lost his shyness.
The adjective timide "shy" is the everyday word; la timidité names the quality.

The word, taken apart.
la timidité is a learned borrowing from Latin timiditās "fearfulness" (EtymDB), built on timidus "fearful", from timēre "to fear" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: sociable.
sociable — "sociable, outgoing".
Ma cousine est très sociable: elle parle à tout le monde. My cousin is very sociable: she talks to everyone.
The same form for a man and a woman. The opposite is peu sociable.

The word, taken apart.
sociable is a learned borrowing from Latin sociābilis "that can be joined together", built on socius "a companion, a partner" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
réservé — reserved, quiet (of a person); booked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for shyness, then the French for sociable.

You'll want to know: réservé.
réservé — "reserved, quiet (of a person); booked".
Mon frère est un garçon réservé; il parle peu de lui. My brother is a reserved boy; he says little about himself.
Of a table or a seat it means booked: une table réservée.

The word, taken apart.
réservé is the past participle of réserver "to keep back", which comes from Latin reservāre (Lewis & Short), built on servāre "to keep, to watch over".

Lesson 4 of 5.
bouder — to sulk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sociable, then the French for reserved.

You'll want to know: bouder.
bouder — "to sulk".
Le petit garçon boude parce qu'il ne peut pas sortir. The little boy is sulking because he cannot go out.
Bouder quelqu'un means to give someone the cold shoulder.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of bouder is not settled. It is usually traced to an expressive root bod- for something swollen, the picture being pushed-out lips.

Lesson 5 of 5.
taquiner — to tease (playfully).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for reserved, then the French for to sulk.

You'll want to know: taquiner.
taquiner — "to tease (playfully)".
Mon oncle aime taquiner les enfants pendant le dîner. My uncle likes teasing the children at dinner.
Gentle, friendly teasing; stronger mockery is se moquer de.

The word, taken apart.
taquiner is built on the adjective taquin, which first meant "stingy" and later "fond of teasing". The origin of taquin is not settled.
