Lesson 1 of 6.
el combate — the fight, the combat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the record, then the Spanish for the novelty.

You'll want to know: el combate.
el combate — "the fight, the combat".
Murieron cien hombres en el combate. A hundred men died in the fighting.
Fuera de combate — out of action, knocked out.

The word, taken apart.
combate is built on combatir 'to fight', which comes from spoken Latin combattere (EtymDB), built on com- and battuere 'to beat'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
la tregua — the truce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the novelty, then the Spanish for the fight.

You'll want to know: la tregua.
la tregua — "the truce".
Los dos países firmaron una tregua. The two countries signed a truce.
Also a break in anything hard: La lluvia no dio tregua.

The word, taken apart.
tregua comes from Gothic triggwa 'a treaty, a covenant' (EtymDB), a Germanic word of the same family as English true and truce.

Lesson 4 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the truce, then the Spanish for the battle.

You'll want to know: el soldado.
el soldado — "the soldier".
Los soldados volvieron a casa. The soldiers came home.
A woman is la soldado.

The word, taken apart.
soldado is usually traced to Italian soldato 'paid', from soldo 'pay', from Latin solidus, a gold coin; EtymDB lists the two words as cousins. A soldier was a man paid to fight.
