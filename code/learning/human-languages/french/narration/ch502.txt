French, chapter 502: Prendre des photos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
flou — blurred, out of focus; vague.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for model-making, then the French for to put together.

You'll want to know: flou.
flou — "blurred, out of focus; vague".
Toutes mes photos de la fête sont floues. All my photos from the party are blurred.
Feminine floue. Also vague: des idées floues.

The word, taken apart.
flou goes back to Old French flou "faded, weak, soft" (EtymDB). Its earlier origin is not settled.

You'll want to know: net.
net — "sharp, in focus; clean, clear".
Sur cette photo, le visage est bien net. In this photo the face is nice and sharp.
Feminine nette. Also net (of money): un salaire net.

The word, taken apart.
net is inherited from Latin nitidus "shining, clean" (EtymDB), from nitēre "to shine" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le cliché — snapshot, photo; cliché.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for blurred, then the French for sharp.

You'll want to know: le cliché.
le cliché — "snapshot, photo; cliché".
Le photographe a pris de beaux clichés du port. The photographer took some lovely shots of the harbour.
Also a worn-out idea, as in English.

The word, taken apart.
le cliché is the participle of clicher "to make a printing plate" (EtymDB), a word probably imitating the sound of the die striking the metal.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cadrer — to frame (a picture).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sharp, then the French for snapshot.

You'll want to know: cadrer.
cadrer — "to frame (a picture)".
Elle a mal cadré la photo: la tour est coupée. She framed the photo badly: the tower is cut off.
Also to fit: ça ne cadre pas avec ses idées.

The word, taken apart.
cadrer is built on cadre "frame", borrowed from Italian quadro, from Latin quadrum "a square" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for snapshot, then the French for to frame.

You'll want to know: le trépied.
le trépied — "tripod".
Pour les photos de nuit, il utilise un trépied. For night photos he uses a tripod.
Three feet: tré- "three" and pied "foot".

The word, taken apart.
le trépied comes from Latin tripēs "three-footed" (EtymDB), built from trēs "three" and pēs "foot" (Lewis & Short).
