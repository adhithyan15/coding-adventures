Sanskrit, chapter 327: A Festival of Colours.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
होलिका (holikā) — the festival of colours; Holi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for festive excitement, then the Sanskrit for splendour.

You'll want to know: होलिका.
होलिका — holikā — "the festival of colours; Holi".
वसन्ते होलिका भवति। vasante holikā bhavati. Holi comes in spring.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
होलिका (holikā) MW gives holikā as the Holi festival, beside holākā, the spring festival near the equinox, which it takes perhaps from a cry or shout in singing, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

सिञ्चति (siñcati) comes from the root √sic, "to pour out, sprinkle", the root of abhiṣeka too (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for saffron, then the Sanskrit for sprinkles.

You'll want to know: विकिरति.
विकिरति — vikirati — "scatters".
जनाः मार्गे पुष्पाणि विकिरन्ति। janāḥ mārge puṣpāṇi vikiranti. People scatter flowers on the road.
Past (he …): सः पुष्पाणि विकीर्णवान्, saḥ puṣpāṇi vikīrṇavān.

The word, taken apart.
विकिरति (vikirati) is built from vi and the root √kṝ, "to scatter"; MW gives vi-kṝ as to scatter, throw about, disperse (MW).

उत्क्षिपति — utkṣipati — "tosses up".
बालः कन्दुकम् उपरि उत्क्षिपति। bālaḥ kandukam upari utkṣipati. The boy tosses the ball up.
Past (he …): सः कन्दुकम् उत्क्षिप्तवान्, saḥ kandukam utkṣiptavān.

The word, taken apart.
उत्क्षिपति (utkṣipati) is built from ud, "up", and the root √kṣip, "to throw", as in kṣipati; MW gives ut-kṣip as to throw up, raise (MW).
