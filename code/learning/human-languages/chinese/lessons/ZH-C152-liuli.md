## You'll want to know: liúlì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C152-LIULI-01]; assesses=[] -->

*liúlì* — "fluent".

> *tā Hànyǔ shuō de hěn liúlì* — He speaks Chinese fluently.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*liúlì* is built from liú 'to flow' and lì. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *ljuw* and *lijH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the Guangyun explains *ljuw*, after an older dictionary, as water moving, and glosses *lijH* 'favourable' and, after the same dictionary, 'sharp'. 'Fluent' is the pair's own sense.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-DE-COMPLEMENT-01, ZH-GRAMMAR-TOPIC-COMMENT-01] -->

*liúlì* sits after 得 with 很, as the other adjectives do: *shuō de hěn liúlì*.

Here the topic, Hànyǔ, goes first, so the verb is said only once.
