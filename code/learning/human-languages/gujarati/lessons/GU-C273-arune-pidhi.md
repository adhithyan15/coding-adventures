# અરુણે ચા પીધી (Aruṇe cā pīdhī) — a name as the doer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C148-ERGATIVE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C148-OBJECT-AGREE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C148-ERG-SG-01, GU-LEX-CHA, GU-LEX-PAANI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "I drank tea" and "he drank water". (*mẽ cā pīdhī*, *teṇe pāṇī pīdhũ*.) Both times the doer was a pronoun. This lesson puts a name in its place.

## Grammar Lens: the doer takes -એ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C273-NOUN-AGENT-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-BAHEN, GU-LEX-C84-TOWN-03, GU-LEX-CHHOKRO-01, GU-LEX-C55-HOUSEWORK-05] -->

> **અરુણે ચા પીધી.**
>
> *Aruṇe cā pīdhī.* — "Arun drank tea."

With a verb that has an object, a name or a noun that does it takes **-એ** (*-e*), the way **હું** becomes **મેં**:

| the doer | with -એ |
|---|---|
| **અરુણ** | **અરુણે** (*Aruṇe*) |
| **મીરા** | **મીરાએ** (*Mīrāe*) |
| **બહેન** | **બહેને** (*bahene*) |
| **પોલીસ** | **પોલીસે** (*polīse*) |
| **છોકરો** | **છોકરાએ** (*chokrāe*) |

After a consonant it is the sign **ે**; after a vowel, the letter **એ**. **છોકરો** first turns its **-ો** into **-ા**, as it does before **-ને**.

## You'll want to know: the verb follows the thing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C273-NE-OBJECT-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C88-CARRY-04, GU-LEX-C255-STORY255-01, GU-LEX-PUSTAK-01, GU-LEX-LAKHVU, GU-LEX-VANCHVU, GU-LEX-JOVU] -->

> **મીરાએ પત્ર લખ્યો.**
>
> *Mīrāe patra lakhyo.* — "Mira wrote a letter."

**પત્ર** is masculine, so **લખ્યો**. The doer leaves the ending alone; the thing sets it: **મીરાએ વાર્તા લખી** (*Mīrāe vārtā lakhī*), for a feminine story, and **અરુણે પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું** (*Aruṇe pustak vā̃cyũ*), for a neuter book.

When the thing is a person, it takes **-ને**, and in Gujarati the verb follows that person too:

> **અરુણે મીરાને જોઈ.**
>
> *Aruṇe Mīrāne joī.* — "Arun saw Mira."

**જોઈ** is feminine, for Mira. Turn it round, **મીરાએ અરુણને જોયો** (*Mīrāe Aruṇne joyo*): masculine, for Arun. Some speakers say **જોયું** in both; this book follows the person.
