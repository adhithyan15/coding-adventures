Lesson 1 of 5.
حلوہ (halvā) — halwa, a sweet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a paratha, then the Urdu for a samosa.

You'll want to know: حلوہ.
حلوہ — halvā — "halwa, a sweet".

The word, taken apart.
حلوہ (halvā) is a loan from Arabic ḥalwā, "a sweetmeat", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is ḥ-l-w (Lane).

Before the new one: say the Urdu for a samosa, then the Urdu for halwa.

You'll want to know: کھیر.
کھیر — khīr — "rice pudding".

The word, taken apart.
کھیر (khīr) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kṣīrī, "a dish prepared with milk" (CDIAL 3705). It is built on kṣīra, "milk".

Lesson 3 of 5.
چٹنی (chaṭnī) — a chutney.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for halwa, then the Urdu for rice pudding.

You'll want to know: چٹنی.
چٹنی — chaṭnī — "a chutney".

The word, taken apart.
چٹنی (chaṭnī) is usually tied to chāṭnā, "to lick". CDIAL lists that verb, and chaṭnā, "to be tasted", made from it, under a reconstructed caṭṭ, "lick, taste" (CDIAL 4573), but it does not list chaṭnī itself. English took the word as chutney.

Lesson 4 of 5.
کباب (kabāb) — a kebab.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for rice pudding, then the Urdu for a chutney.

You'll want to know: کباب.
کباب — kabāb — "a kebab".

The word, taken apart.
کباب (kabāb) is a loan from Arabic kabāb, "meat cut up and roasted", which reached Urdu through Persian. Lane lists it under the root k-b-b, and records that some Arab scholars took the word to be Persian.
