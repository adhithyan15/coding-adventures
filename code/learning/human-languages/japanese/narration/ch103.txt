Lesson 1 of 5.
おたま (otama) — a ladle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a rice pot, then the Japanese for a chopping board.

You'll want to know: おたま.
おたま — otama — "a ladle".

The word, taken apart.
おたま (otama) is native: the polite o- and tama, "ball", the ladle's round bowl. JMdict marks it as a shortened word. It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
すいどう (suidou) — the water supply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a chopping board, then the Japanese for a ladle.

You'll want to know: すいどう.
すいどう — suidou — "the water supply".

The word, taken apart.
すいどう (suidou) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "water" (Middle Chinese sywijX) and dō, "way, path" (dawX) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 3 of 5.
やかん (yakan) — a kettle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a ladle, then the Japanese for the water supply.

You'll want to know: やかん.
やかん — yakan — "a kettle".

The word, taken apart.
やかん (yakan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yaku, "medicine" (Middle Chinese yak) and kan, "jar" (kwanH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart). It was a pot for brewing medicine, and yaku shortened to ya; JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.

おぼん (obon) is the native polite prefix o- with bon, "tray, basin", the on'yomi of its character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese bwon; JMdict, Unihan).

Lesson 5 of 5.
かん (kan) — a can, a tin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a kettle, then the Japanese for a tray.

You'll want to know: かん.
かん — kan — "a can, a tin".

The word, taken apart.
かん (kan) is a loanword: JMdict gives its source as Dutch kan, or English can. Its kanji, a character for an earthen jar, is marked as ateji, chosen for its sound.
