# Chapter 13 checkpoint — head, hand, and நன்றி from sound

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Nothing new here. The two body words out loud first, then two
letters, a vowel sign and the word *thank you* on the page.

## Guided Practice — head and hand
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-UDAL-URUPPUGAL-01, TA-SOUND-I-SIGN-WRITE-NANDRI-03] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "head", then "hand", in Tamil]

(*Talai*; *kai*.)

- Are either of them Sanskrit loans, the way Hindi's *sir* and *hāth* are? (No:
  both are native Dravidian.)
- Why do people spell *thank you* as *nandri* when no *d* is written? (The
  nasal voices the stop after it: *ṉ* plus *ṟ* is said close to *ndr*.)

## Script — க, ர and the i-sign
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-06, TA-SCRIPT-I-SIGN-WRITE-NANDRI-01, TA-SCRIPT-I-SIGN-WRITE-NANDRI-02, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-117] -->

- What are the three parts of **க**, and how many times does the pen lift?
  (An upper frame, a lower-left bowl, a lower-right bowl; no lift at all.)
- Where does **ி** attach, and what does it do to the consonant's *a*? (Above
  and to the right; it replaces the *a*, so **ற** becomes **றி**.)
- Which three pieces build **நன்றி**? (**ந** · **ன்** · **றி**.)
- **சரி** is *okay*. Which letter in it carries *ra*? (**ர**.)

[YOU READ: **நன்றி** and **சரி** aloud, and point to the ர]
