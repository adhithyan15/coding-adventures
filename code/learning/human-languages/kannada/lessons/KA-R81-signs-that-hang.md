# (ಾ ಿ ೆ ೊ ೋ ೃ ಂ ಃ) — eight that cannot stand alone

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Two of these you met in the book's first handful of pages. Name the
one that lengthens a vowel, and the one that makes it *i*.

## Script: six signs and two marks
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-08, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-04, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-111, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-114, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-131] -->

Six vowel signs, cold. Each attaches to a consonant and replaces the *a* built
into it:

| sign | sound |
|---|---|
| **ಾ** | *ā* |
| **ಿ** | *i* |
| **ೆ** | *e* |
| **ೊ** | *o* |
| **ೋ** | *ō* |
| **ೃ** | *ṛ* |

The last is the odd one. **ೃ** writes a vowel that behaves like a consonant in
the mouth, inherited from Sanskrit and rare enough that a reader can go pages
without meeting it — which is exactly why it is here.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-123, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-132, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-114] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Two marks now, and they are not vowel signs at all.

- [YOU READ: **ಂ** — say what it adds to the syllable it follows]
- [YOU READ: **ಃ** — say the same for this one]
- [YOU READ: **ೊ** and **ೋ** back to back, and say what separates them]

**ಂ** and **ಃ** ride *after* a syllable rather than reshaping its vowel. That is
the division worth keeping: a vowel sign changes the sound already there, a mark
adds one behind it.
