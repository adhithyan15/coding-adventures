Persian, chapter 517: Musical Instruments.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پیانو (piyâno) — a piano.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for energetic, then the Persian for to be lazy.

You'll want to know: پیانو.
پیانو (piyâno) — "a piano".
دخترم از هفت سالگی پیانو میزند. dokhtaram az haft sâlegi piyâno mizanad. My daughter has played the piano since she was seven.
Persian strikes an instrument: piyâno zadan 'to play the piano'.

The word, taken apart.
piyâno is a modern loan, usually traced to French or Italian piano. EtymDB has no entry for the Persian word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تار (târ) — a tar (a long-necked lute); a string.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a piano, then the Persian for a violin.

You'll want to know: تار.
تار (târ) — "a tar (a long-necked lute); a string".
پدربزرگم تار میزد. pedarbozorgam târ mizad. My grandfather used to play the tar.
târ also means 'string, thread': the instrument is 'the string'.

The word, taken apart.
târ is a native Persian word for a string or thread, and the instrument takes its name from it. EtymDB lists it without a source, so its older origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: سنتور.
سنتور (santur) — "a santur (a hammered dulcimer)".
صدای سنتور خیلی آرام است. sedâ-ye santur kheyli ârâm ast. The sound of the santur is very soft.
Played with two light hammers: santur zadan.

The word, taken apart.
santur is the name of a Persian instrument, but EtymDB lists it without a source, and its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تنبک (tombak) — a tombak (a goblet drum).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a tar, then the Persian for a santur.

You'll want to know: تنبک.
تنبک (tombak) — "a tombak (a goblet drum)".
او در گروه موسیقی تنبک میزند. u dar goruh-e musiqi tombak mizanad. He plays the tombak in a music group.
Spelled with n but said with m before b: tombak. Also tonbak.

The word, taken apart.
tombak is inherited: EtymDB traces it to a Middle Persian word for 'drum' (EtymDB).
