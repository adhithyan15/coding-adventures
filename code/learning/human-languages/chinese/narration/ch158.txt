Mandarin Chinese, chapter 158: Waking Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiàoxǐng — to wake someone up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for news, then the Mandarin for to reply.

You'll want to know: jiàoxǐng.
jiàoxǐng — "to wake someone up".
māma měitiān qī diǎn jiàoxǐng wǒ — Mum wakes me at seven every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiàoxǐng is built from 叫 'to call' and xǐng 'to wake', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kewH and sengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as sobering up after wine: to call someone awake.

The word, taken apart.
zuòmèng is built from 做 'to do' and mèng 'a dream'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese mjuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the spirit wandering in sleep; 做 has no form in that dictionary, so the history of the first part is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
起来 (qǐlai) — to get up; to stand up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to fall asleep, then the Mandarin for to have a dream.

You'll want to know: 起来.
起来 — qǐlai — "to get up; to stand up".
七点了你快起来 qī diǎn le nǐ kuài qǐlai — It's seven o'clock: up you get, quick.

The word, taken apart.
起来 (qǐlai) is built from 起 'to rise' and 来 'to come', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khiX and loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and 'to stand'. In the word the second loses its tone.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xíguàn is built from xí and guàn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, zip and kwaenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to learn' and explains the second with the first: practised.
