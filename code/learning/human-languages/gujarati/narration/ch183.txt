Gujarati, chapter 183: At the grocer's counter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ગ્રાહક (grāhak) — customer, buyer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to believe, then the Gujarati for to change.

You'll want to know: ગ્રાહક.
ગ્રાહક — grāhak — "customer, buyer".
દુકાનમાં એક ગ્રાહક છે. — dukānmā̃ ek grāhak che — "There is one customer in the shop."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગ્રાહક (grāhak) is Sanskrit grāhaka, "one who takes, a buyer", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama. It is built on the root grah-, "to seize, to take", which CDIAL lists in its causative grāhayati, "causes to be taken" (CDIAL 4383).

Lesson 2 of 5.
દુકાનદાર (dukāndār) — shopkeeper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to change, then the Gujarati for customer.

You'll want to know: દુકાનદાર.
દુકાનદાર — dukāndār — "shopkeeper".
આ દુકાનદાર સારો છે. — ā dukāndār sāro che — "This shopkeeper is a good one."
Built on દુકાન, dukān, 'shop'.

The word, taken apart.
દુકાનદાર (dukāndār) is built from dukān, "shop", a loan that reached Gujarati through Persian, and the Persian suffix -dār, "holder, keeper". Lane lists Arabic dukkān, "a shop", under the root d-k-n, but notes that some Arabic scholars held it to be a Persian word taken into Arabic, so its first source is not settled (Lane).

Lesson 3 of 5.
કરિયાણું (kariyāṇũ) — groceries.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for customer, then the Gujarati for shopkeeper.

You'll want to know: કરિયાણું.
કરિયાણું — kariyāṇũ — "groceries".
તે બજારથી કરિયાણું લાવે છે. — te bajārthī kariyāṇũ lāve che — "She brings the groceries from the market."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
કરિયાણું (kariyāṇũ) comes from Sanskrit krayāṇaka, "goods for sale", built on krī, "to buy". CDIAL lists the Gujarati word as kiriyāṇũ, "groceries, drugs", among the forms it marks as loan-words (CDIAL 3584).

Lesson 4 of 5.
તોલવું (tolvũ) — to weigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for shopkeeper, then the Gujarati for groceries.

You'll want to know: તોલવું.
તોલવું — tolvũ — "to weigh".
દુકાનદાર ચોખા તોલે છે. — dukāndār cokhā tole che — "The shopkeeper weighs the rice."

The word, taken apart.
તોલવું (tolvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from tōláyati, "lifts, weighs, considers", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb as toḷvũ, "to weigh" (CDIAL 5979).
