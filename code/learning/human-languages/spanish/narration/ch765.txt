Spanish, chapter 765: Ilusiones y deseos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la ilusión — excitement, hope; illusion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to relive, then the Spanish for the diary.

You'll want to know: la ilusión.
la ilusión — "excitement, hope; illusion".
Me hace mucha ilusión verte. I am really looking forward to seeing you.
Me hace ilusión — I am excited about it. It is usually a happy word in Spanish.

The word, taken apart.
ilusión is a learned borrowing from Latin illūsiō "mockery, deception" (EtymDB), from illūdere, built on lūdere "to play"; Spanish turned it into hope and excitement.

You'll want to know: anhelar.
anhelar — "to yearn for, to long for".
Anhela una vida tranquila. She longs for a quiet life.
Literary; in conversation people say desear mucho.

The word, taken apart.
anhelar is a learned borrowing from Latin anhēlāre "to pant, to gasp" (EtymDB); to long for something is to be out of breath for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el capricho — the whim, the treat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for excitement, then the Spanish for to yearn for.

You'll want to know: el capricho.
el capricho — "the whim, the treat".
Me di un capricho y compré chocolate. I treated myself and bought chocolate.
Darse un capricho — to treat yourself. Caprichoso — fussy, impulsive.

The word, taken apart.
capricho was borrowed from Italian capriccio "whim, fancy" (EtymDB). The Italian word is often explained as capo "head" plus riccio "hedgehog", hair on end, but that is not certain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
aspirar — to aspire; to breathe in; to vacuum.

You'll want to know: aspirar.
aspirar — "to aspire; to breathe in; to vacuum".
Aspira a ser médica. She aspires to be a doctor.
Aspirar a — to aspire to. Also to vacuum: aspirar la alfombra.

The word, taken apart.
aspirar is a learned borrowing from Latin aspīrāre "to breathe upon" (EtymDB), built on spīrāre "to breathe".
