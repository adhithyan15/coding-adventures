# el instituto — the building between the two you already know

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-11, ES-LEX-UNIVERSIDAD] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say *la escuela* and *la universidad*. A Spanish fifteen-year-old
goes to neither. Where do they go?

## You'll want to know: el instituto
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-01]; assesses=[] -->

**el instituto** — the secondary school, for students of about twelve to
eighteen.

On a sign it is often shortened to **IES** — *Instituto de Educación
Secundaria*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-01] -->

Latin **instituere** — *in-* plus **statuere**, "to set up, to stand a thing
upright." An **institutum** was **something that had been set up**: a custom, an
arrangement, a foundation. A Spanish *instituto* is a **thing that was
established**, and the school sense is one narrow use of a very wide word.

*Statuere* is behind a crowd of words you can already half-read: *estatua*,
*estado*, *estatura*, *constituir*. Every one of them is about **standing**.

## Grammar Lens: the false friend, and the ladder it breaks
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-01, ES-LEX-C346-WHERE-11, ES-LEX-UNIVERSIDAD] -->

English *institute* names a research body — the Institute of Physics. Spanish
*instituto* can mean that too, but its everyday sense is **the school**, and
that is the one a reading paper uses.

The three buildings in order:

| age | building |
|---|---|
| about 6 to 12 | la escuela |
| about 12 to 18 | el instituto |
| after 18 | la universidad |

Without the middle rung the ladder has a gap in exactly the years an exam
candidate is most likely to be asked about.

The stress is *ins-ti-TU-to*, on the second-last syllable, and no accent is
written because a word ending in a vowel is stressed there by default.
