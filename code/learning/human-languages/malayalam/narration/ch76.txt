Malayalam, chapter 76: Asking For It.
2 lessons. All 2 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 2.
വേണോ (vēṇō) — "do you want?"

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say wanted. Then say the one letter Malayalam puts on the end of something to turn it into a question.
[pause 2 seconds]
Put them together before you read on.

You'll want to know: വേണോ.
ചായ വേണോ? cāya vēṇō? — "Do you want tea?"
If you built വേണോ (vēṇō) in the warm-up, this lesson taught you nothing, which is the point. വേണം loses its ending and takes the question letter you already had, and that is the whole derivation.

Grammar Lens: a three-way set you now own.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
വേണോ (vēṇō)?, do you want it?
വേണം, yes — wanted.
വേണ്ട, no — not wanted.
That is a whole exchange. One person offers, the other accepts or declines, and every word in it is a form of the same root.
Notice which one arrived first. The track gave you the refusal long before the request, so for a while you could say no to an offer you had no way of making. Both halves are yours now.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: both answers — vēṇaṁ, then vēṇṭa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the offer with water instead of tea]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which letter turned the statement into the question]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you offer somebody tea? (ചായ വേണോ (vēṇō)?) How do you accept? (വേണം.) And how do you decline? (വേണ്ട.)


Lesson 2 of 2.
(wanted, and not) (vēṇaṁ ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say wanted and not wanted, and hear how much of the two words is the same.

Grammar Lens: one root, a whole exchange.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
ചായ വേണോ (vēṇō)?, do you want tea?
വേണം, yes.
വേണ്ട, no.
Three words, one root, and the shortest complete conversation this book has given you. Only one of the three was new this chapter — the question form you can build yourself, and the acceptance the refusal had been pointing at all along.

What you've built.
The ability to ask for something, which needs one more thing said about it.
എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണം.
To me, water is wanted. You are not the subject of your own wanting — the water is. That is the same shape knowing and age already took, and it is the habit to carry forward: when something happens to a person in Malayalam, the person goes in the dative and the thing goes to the front.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Offer somebody water. (വെള്ളം വേണോ (vēṇō)?) Accept it. (വേണം.) And in enikku veḷḷaṁ vēṇaṁ, who is the subject? (The water.)
