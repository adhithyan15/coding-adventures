# tenemos — and the stem change disappears

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-1PL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I have" and "you have." (*Tengo, tienes*.) And "we eat."
(*Comemos*.)

## Grammar Lens: the break is gone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRESENT-2PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-1PL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-2PL, ES-LEX-VOSOTROS] -->

> **tenemos** — *we have.*  ·  **tenéis** — *you all have.*

Not *tienemos*. Look at what happened: *tienes* had the broken stem **ie**, and
here it is plain **e** again — the ordinary *-er* endings, on the ordinary
stem.

**You already know why.** It is the same reason `-er` and `-ir` looked identical
in the singular and parted in the plural: **stress**.

*Tienes* stresses the stem, and a stressed **e** in that position breaks into
**ie**. *Tenemos* stresses the *ending*, so the stem is unstressed and its vowel
stays whole.

The stem change was never a property of the verb. It is a property of **where
the stress lands**, and these two forms are where the stress lands elsewhere.
