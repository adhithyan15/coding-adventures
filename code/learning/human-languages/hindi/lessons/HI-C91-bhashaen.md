# भाषाएँ — an -आ ending that does not behave like the others

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01, HI-LEX-C68-ORIGIN-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **भाषा** ends in **-आ**, exactly like **बच्चा**. Guess its plural
from that, and then read on to find out why the guess is wrong.

## You'll want to know: भाषाएँ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03]; assesses=[] -->

**भाषाएँ** (*bhāṣāẽ*) — languages.

**दो भाषाएँ** — two languages. That phrase is printed in a practice paper, in a
personal account: *she speaks two languages*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-01] -->

**भाषा** is **feminine**, and that is the whole story. The **-आ → -ए** swap
belongs to **masculine** nouns; a feminine noun **adds** instead, exactly as
**किताब** did.

| noun | gender | plural |
|---|---|---|
| बच्चा | masculine | बच्चे |
| किताब | feminine | किताबें |
| भाषा | feminine | भाषाएँ |

So the question to ask a noun is **not** "how does it end" on its own. It is
**gender first, ending second** — and the two feminine rows do the same thing
despite ending differently.

The **ए** is written as a full letter here rather than as a mark, because
**भाषा** ends in a vowel and there is nothing for a mark to sit on. That is the
same rule that gave you **आऊँगा** and **आओगे**.

## Grammar Lens: the three shapes, in the order to ask them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-03, HI-LEX-C91-NOUN-02, HI-CONCEPT-C06-NUMBERS-1-5-01] -->

| ask this | then this |
|---|---|
| is it masculine? | -आ swaps to -ए |
| is it feminine? | it adds a nasal ending |

Two questions and you have the plural of any A1 noun. The number in front —
**दो**, **तीन** — does not change either way; Hindi marks the plural on the
noun, not on the numeral.
