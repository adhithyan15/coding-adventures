Latin, chapter 277: Helmet, Spear, Bow, Arrow, Trumpet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
galea, galeae — a helmet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a worm, then the Latin for a tortoise.

You'll want to know: galea, galeae.
galea, galeae — "a helmet".

The word, taken apart.
galea is probably a loan from Greek galeē, "a weasel, a marten", for a cap of animal skin; Lewis & Short's comparison with Sanskrit is doubtful.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: hasta, hastae.

The word, taken apart.
hasta is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European ghast-, "a rod, a branch" (EtymDB 2.1). Gothic gazds, "a goad", is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
arcus, arcūs — a bow; an arch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a helmet, then the Latin for a spear.

You'll want to know: arcus, arcūs.
arcus, arcūs — "a bow; an arch".

The word, taken apart.
arcus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2erkwo-, "bow, arch" (EtymDB 2.1); English arrow is a cousin. English arc and arch come from arcus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tuba, tubae — a trumpet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bow; an arch, then the Latin for an arrow.

You'll want to know: tuba, tubae.
tuba, tubae — "a trumpet".

The word, taken apart.
tuba belongs with tubus, "a pipe, a tube" (Lewis & Short); where the pair comes from is uncertain. English tube comes from tubus.
