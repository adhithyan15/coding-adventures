## Chapter 84 — reading: words, lines, and a first passage

Tamil's reading rung. No task shapes are declared, so it moves no number.

Three lessons, 6 -> 8 -> 30 tokens, no new word in any.

- **Words**: the lesson counts SHAPES rather than sounds. Tamil joins a
  consonant and its vowel into one shape, so **வீடு** is four sounds and two
  shapes. A learner met these words as sounds and wrote them as shapes, and
  reading asks for the shape whole rather than unpacked back into sounds.
- **Lines**: the question word goes LAST. **உன் பெயர் என்ன?** is *your name
  what?*, so a reader does not know a line is a question until it is over. In
  speech intonation gives it away early; in writing only the final word does,
  which is exactly why meeting it on a page is worth a lesson.
- **Passage**: four of its twelve lines have no verb at all. Tamil needs no word
  for *is*, so a noun and what is said about it stand side by side and the
  sentence is finished. A reader has to accept a line ending without anything
  verb-shaped.

The one joiner is **ஆனால்**, and the lesson that taught it also taught **ஆனா**
— the same word as people say it. This passage sits on the written side of that
split, which is where reading lives.

Cut by the check: a practice name. Tamil teaches **என் பெயர் …** with an
ellipsis and no concrete name anywhere, so the passage introduces nobody and
asks the reader's name instead.
