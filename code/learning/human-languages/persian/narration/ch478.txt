Persian, chapter 478: Dust, drought and fog.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ریزگرد (rizgard) — fine dust (in the air), dust haze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for mild, then the Persian for to sunbathe.

You'll want to know: ریزگرد.
ریزگرد (rizgard) — "fine dust (in the air), dust haze".
به خاطر ریزگرد مدرسه ها تعطیل شدند. Be khâter-e rizgard madrese-hâ ta'til shodand. Because of the dust haze the schools closed.
Dust from the deserts is a serious problem in the west and south of the country.

The word, taken apart.
rizgard is a modern native compound of riz 'fine, tiny' and gard 'dust', which EtymDB lists.

Lesson 2 of 5.
گرد و خاک (gard-o khâk) — dust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to sunbathe, then the Persian for fine dust.

You'll want to know: گرد و خاک.
گرد و خاک (gard-o khâk) — "dust".
باد گرد و خاک را به خانه آورد. Bâd gard-o khâk râ be khâne âvard. The wind blew dust into the house.
gard 'dust' + o 'and' + khâk 'earth'.

The word, taken apart.
gard-o khâk is a native pair: gard 'dust', which EtymDB lists, and khâk 'earth', inherited from Middle Persian xāk (EtymDB).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Persian for dust, then the Persian for a drought.

You'll want to know: خشک شدن.
خشک شدن (khoshk shodan) — "to dry up; to dry".
در تابستان نهر خشک شد. Dar tâbestân nahr khoshk shod. In summer the stream dried up.
khoshk 'dry' + shodan. Of washing: lebâs-hâ khoshk shod.

The word, taken apart.
khoshk shodan is built from native khoshk 'dry', which EtymDB lists, and the native verb shodan 'to become'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
مه آلود (mehâlud) — foggy, misty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a drought, then the Persian for to dry up.

You'll want to know: مه آلود.
مه آلود (mehâlud) — "foggy, misty".
صبح جاده مه آلود بود و آهسته رانندگی کردیم. Sobh jâdde mehâlud bud va âheste rânandegi kardim. In the morning the road was foggy and we drove slowly.
meh 'fog' + âlud 'covered with', from âludan.

The word, taken apart.
mehâlud is a native compound of inherited words: meh 'fog', usually traced to Middle Persian mēgh, and âlud 'covered, smeared', the past stem of âludan 'to soil, to smear', as in âlude 'polluted'.
