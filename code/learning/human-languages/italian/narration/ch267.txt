Italian, chapter 267: Scusi, Per Andare A --- Asking the Way.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
scusi, per andare a… — the question that finds the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to go". (Andare.) Say "the station". (La stazione.)
[pause 1 second]
You are in a town you do not know, and the station is somewhere.

You'll want to know: scusi.
scusi — "excuse me", said to someone you do not know.
You met scusarsi, "to apologise"; scusi comes from the plain verb inside it, scusare, "to excuse", made a polite request. It is the form for Lei, the polite "you" of come sta, which a stranger in the street is owed. Its -i comes back in the next lesson.

Grammar Lens: per andare a…
Scusi, per andare alla stazione? — Excuse me, how do I get to the station?
Literally, "for going to the station". There is no verb of asking in it at all: per plus the infinitive andare, then the place, and your voice rises at the end.
The a, "to", joins the article in front of the place, and the two become one word:
alla stazione, al museo, all'albergo.
a + la is alla, a + il is al, and a + l', before a vowel, is all'.
There is a second way to ask, with dove, "where": Dov'è la stazione?, "where is the station". Both work. Per andare a… asks for the way; dov'è asks for the place.

The phrase, taken apart.
scusare is inherited from Latin excūsāre (EtymDB 2.1), built on ex-, "out of", and causa, "a case at law": to excuse someone was to get them out of the charge (Lewis & Short, under excuso). English excuse is the same word, borrowed through French.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Scusi, per andare alla stazione? — and let the voice rise]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same, to the museum — Scusi, per andare al museo?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: all'albergo, then al museo, then alla stazione]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask for the place instead — Scusi, dov'è la stazione? È lontano?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Who gets scusi? (A stranger, anyone you call Lei.) Ask the way to the hotel. (Scusi, per andare all'albergo?) What is a + la? (Alla.) What did Latin excūsāre mean? (To get someone out of a charge.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
a destra, a sinistra — following the answer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Stop a stranger and ask the way to the station. (Scusi, per andare alla stazione?)
[pause 1 second]
Asking was the easy half. Now the answer comes back, and it comes fast.

You'll want to know: a destra, a sinistra.
a destra — "on the right, to the right". a sinistra — "on the left, to the left". With dritto, "straight on", which you already have, that is every direction a street can send you.
La stazione è a destra. — The station is on the right. La prima a sinistra. — The first on the left.
That second one is a whole answer in four words: la prima, "the first", is the ordinal you learned in chapter twenty-six, agreeing with the street it leaves unsaid.

Grammar Lens: vada dritto, giri a destra.
A stranger answering a Lei question answers in the Lei form, and the directions are instructions:
Vada dritto, poi giri a destra al semaforo. — Go straight on, then turn right at the traffic lights.
giri is girare, "to turn", with the same -i as scusi: the polite instruction of an -are verb. vada is andare's irregular one, "go". You need to understand these two, not build them; the rest of the answer is words you know: dritto, poi, al semaforo, all'incrocio.
A long answer comes in the order you will walk it, and poi marks each new piece.

The phrase, taken apart.
destra goes back to Latin dextera, "the right hand", from dexter, "right" (Lewis & Short). English dexterous is the same word: skilled, as the right hand is. sinistra is Latin sinistra, "the left hand"; Lewis & Short call the origin of sinister doubtful. Roman augurs counted the left as the lucky side, but Latin poets already used sinister for "unlucky, adverse" (Lewis & Short), and that is the sense English sinister kept. EtymDB 2.1 lists both Italian words but gives neither a direct Latin parent.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: a destra, a sinistra, dritto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: La prima a sinistra. — then La seconda a destra.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the stranger's answer — Vada dritto, poi giri a destra al semaforo.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: repeat the answer back to check it — Dritto, poi a destra al semaforo?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word is "left", and which English word shares its Latin? (Sinistra — sinister.) What does giri share with scusi? (The -i of a polite instruction.) What does la prima a destra leave unsaid? (The street — la prima agrees with it.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — ask, listen, check, thank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "on the left", then "on the right". (A sinistra, a destra.)

What you've built.
Asking the way has four steps, and you now have Italian for every one.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask: Scusi, per andare alla stazione? Listen: Vada dritto, poi giri a sinistra all'incrocio. Check, by saying the answer back as a question: Dritto, poi a sinistra? Thank: Grazie!
The check is the step travellers skip, and it is the one that saves the walk. A stranger who hears their own directions said back will correct the one piece you got wrong.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ask — Scusi, per andare al museo?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hear the answer and say it back — Vada dritto, poi giri a destra. … Dritto, poi a destra?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: thank — Grazie!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: now be the stranger: send someone to the station — straight on, then the first on the left]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the four steps? (Ask, listen, check, thank.) Which one do travellers skip? (The check.) And if you think a friend is lost, how do you advise them to ask, with a reason? (Secondo me dovresti chiedere, perché la stazione è lontana.)
