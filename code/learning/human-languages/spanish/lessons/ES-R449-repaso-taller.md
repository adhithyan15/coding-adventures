# (arreglar, el freno, la rueda, el taladro, acercar) — from cold

[PAUSE 3s] Close the lessons before this one. Say all five: *to fix*, *the
brake*, *the wheel*, *the drill*, *to pass*.

## Grammar Lens: each noun brought a verb with it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-02, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-04] -->

Three of these five came in pairs, and the second half of each pair cost you
nothing:

| the noun | its verb |
|---|---|
| el freno | **frenar** — to brake |
| la rueda | **rodar** — to roll |
| el taladro | **taladrar** — to drill |

Note that *rodar* does not keep the *ue*. The **ue** appears only where the
stress lands on it, so the noun has it and the infinitive does not. If you try
to say *ruedar* you will be corrected; if you say *rueda* and *rodamos* you
will not.

That one condition — stress — accounts for most of what looks irregular in
Spanish verbs, and you now have a noun that shows it standing still.

## Grammar Lens: the -se you keep meeting
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01] -->

Three verbs in two chapters have turned on the same small word:

| without -se | with -se |
|---|---|
| *mudar* — change a thing | *mudarse* — move house |
| *acercar* — bring it closer | *acercarse* — come closer |
| *arreglar* — fix a thing | *arreglarse* — get ready |

Read the right-hand column and one thing is constant: the subject is on the
receiving end. Read the left and the verb is reaching out to an object. When
you meet a new verb in both shapes, that question sorts them before you look
anything up.
