Hindi, chapter 544: A Stroke of Luck.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
क़िस्मत (qismat) — luck; fortune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an oath (formal), then the Hindi for bound (by a rule or a time).

You'll want to know: क़िस्मत.
क़िस्मत — qismat — "luck; fortune".
उसकी क़िस्मत अच्छी है, उसे हमेशा सीट मिल जाती है। Uskī qismat acchī hai, use hameśā sīṭ mil jātī hai. He is lucky: he always gets a seat.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
क़िस्मत (qismat) is usually traced to Arabic qisma, "a share, a portion", through Persian. One's share, handed out in advance, is one's luck.

Lesson 2 of 5.
संयोग (saṁyog) — a coincidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for bound (by a rule or a time), then the Hindi for luck.

You'll want to know: संयोग.
संयोग — saṁyog — "a coincidence".
क्या संयोग है, हम दोनों का जन्मदिन एक ही दिन है! Kyā saṁyog hai, ham donõ kā janmdin ek hī din hai! What a coincidence: we both have our birthday on the same day!
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
संयोग (saṁyog) is a tatsama, taken whole from Sanskrit saṃyoga, "conjunction, connection" (Monier-Williams). Hindi uses it for things that come together by chance.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

मुझे मंच पर गाने का मौक़ा मिला। Mujhe mañc par gāne kā mauqā milā. I got the chance to sing on stage.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
मौक़ा (mauqā) is usually traced to Arabic mauqiʿ, "a place where something falls", through Persian. Hindi and Urdu use it for the moment when something can happen.

Lesson 5 of 5.
दाँव (dā̃v) — a turn (in a game); a stake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a chance, then the Hindi for an opportunity (formal).

You'll want to know: दाँव.
दाँव — dā̃v — "a turn (in a game); a stake".
अगर इस बार मेरा दाँव लग गया, तो मैं जीत जाऊँगा। Agar is bār merā dā̃v lag gayā, to maiṁ jīt jāūṅgā. If my luck holds this time, I will win.
Masculine.
दाँव (dā̃v) लगना: one's turn or one's gamble comes off.

The word, taken apart.
दाँव (dā̃v) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit dātu, "a share" (CDIAL 6258). CDIAL gives the Hindi word as "a turn": one's share of the play.
