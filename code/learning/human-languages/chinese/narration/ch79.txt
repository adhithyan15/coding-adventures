Lesson 1 of 7.
上课 (shàngkè) — to have class; to start class.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for age, then the Mandarin for in childhood.

You'll want to know: 上课.
上课 — shàngkè — "to have class; to start class".
今天有汉语课我们一会儿上课 jīntiān yǒu hànyǔ kè wǒmen yíhuìr shàngkè — There's a Chinese class today; it starts in a moment.
The new character, in pieces: 课 (kè) — 讠 on the left, and on the right guǒ, the fruit of shuǐguǒ: a box over the tree piece. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
上课 (shàngkè) is built from 上 'to go up to' and 课 (kè) 'lesson', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangX and khwaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as 'a tax' and 'a test'; the sense lesson is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 7.
课 (kè) — the speech piece, then guǒ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the speech piece, then guǒ.
课 (kè).
Two pieces, left before right.
讠, two strokes, as in 说 and 话.
Guǒ, eight strokes: a box like 日, its left side, the top that turns down, the middle bar and the bar that closes it; then a long horizontal, a vertical down through the box and the bar, a left-falling stroke and a stroke falling to the right.
2 + 8 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 课 (kè), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 课 (kè)? (Two.) Which characters you know start the same way? (说 and 话.) What runs down through the box? (The vertical, the eighth stroke.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 课 (kè), pinned stroke paths and medians.
