Urdu, chapter 440: Naming a Feeling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جذبہ (jazbā) — an emotion; a strong feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a fruit seller, then the Urdu for a greengrocer.

You'll want to know: جذبہ.
جذبہ — jazbā — "an emotion; a strong feeling".
ماں کا پیار ایک گہرا جذبہ ہے۔ māṅ kā pyār ek gahrā jazbā hai. A mother's love is a deep emotion.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
جذبہ (jazbā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic jadhba, "a pull, an attraction"; the sense "a strong feeling" is the one Urdu gives it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
محسوس کرنا (mahsūs karnā) — to sense; to feel (inside).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an emotion, then the Urdu for a feeling.

You'll want to know: محسوس کرنا.
محسوس کرنا — mahsūs karnā — "to sense; to feel (inside)".
میں نے اس کا درد محسوس کیا۔ maiṅ ne us kā dard mahsūs kiyā. I felt his pain.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے تھکن محسوس کی, us ne thakan mahsūs kī.

The word, taken apart.
محسوس کرنا (mahsūs karnā) is built from محسوس, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic maḥsūs, "perceived by the senses", and کرنا, "to do".

کیفیت (kaifiyat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic kayfiyya, "how a thing is"; Urdu uses it for the state a person is in.
