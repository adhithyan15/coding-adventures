## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-17]; assesses=[] -->

**el sur** — "the south."

Like *norte*, this came in with Atlantic sailors rather than down from Rome.
Germanic **sunthaz** became Old French **sud**, and Spanish took it as
**sur**. It is the same word as English **south**.

What is inside it is a guess, but a good one: **sun**. Stand anywhere in
northern Europe and the sun is never overhead — it swings across the southern
half of the sky, all day, all year. The sunward quarter is the south, and so
the sun gave its name to a direction.

Spanish has both shapes. **Sur** on its own, and **sud-** when it joins onto
another word, as in *Sudamérica*.

English does the same trick and never notices: **south**, but **southern**
with a different vowel, and **Sussex** for the *South Saxons*.
