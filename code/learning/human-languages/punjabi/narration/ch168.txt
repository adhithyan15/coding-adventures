Punjabi, chapter 168: Return to the Date Line.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
The old date label, once more — the familiar date label, recalled after a long gap.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

This is a gentle return to one familiar field on a fictional form. No real personal date is needed, and this is not a scored writing mock.

Warm-up.
Look at ਤਾਰੀਖ਼: (tārīkh). Recall that it labels the date line, not an age or phone line. The blank after it is for a date.

Guided Practice.
Trace ਤਾਰੀਖ਼ (tārīkh): with a finger, then copy only the label and its colon once. Leave the value blank. Compare one mark at a time with the visible model.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model. Which familiar label asks for a date? Write ਤਾਰੀਖ਼ (tārīkh):, then uncover and repair one mark if needed. This is retrieval practice, not a scored form submission.


Lesson 2 of 3.
Two old month positions — the two taught month positions on the fictional form.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Only the two previously taught months return. This is a tiny closed bank, not a new month list or a scored form task.

Warm-up.
Look at ੦੧ for January (ਜਨਵਰੀ) and ੦੨ for February (ਫ਼ਰਵਰੀ). The first place is zero in both; the second changes.

Guided Practice.
With both codes visible, copy ੦੧ under January and ੦੨ under February. Say the month, cover the code, and try its two digits once. Uncover and repair only the differing digit.

Wrap-up Recall.
From the old English cue February, write its month position ੦੨. Pause before looking. These are month positions, not new Punjabi words.


Lesson 3 of 3.
The old printed date order — the printed three-box order on the fictional date form.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

The fictional form still prints day / month / year. Another form may choose another order; follow what this one prints. No complete personal date or scored A1 response is requested here.

Warm-up.
Look at the printed order from left to right: day, month, year. The familiar model __/__/____ (day/month/year) has two places, two places, then four places. Slashes separate the boxes; they are not digits.

Guided Practice.
Copy only the empty frame __/__/____ (day/month/year). Label its boxes in English: day, month, year. Keep the two slash marks visible; do not fill a real date.

Wrap-up Recall.
Cover the model. Draw the empty frame and point to the middle month box. Uncover and check its order and two separators. This is a no-stakes retrieval, not independent scored writing.
