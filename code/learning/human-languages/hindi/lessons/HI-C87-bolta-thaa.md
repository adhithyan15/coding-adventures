# बोलता था — what you used to do

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I speak Hindi*. Then look at the last word of it and ask what
you would put there instead.

## You'll want to know: बोलता था
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-05]; assesses=[] -->

**बोलता था** (*boltā thā*) — "used to speak."

| now | earlier |
|---|---|
| मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ। | मैं हिंदी बोलता था। |

One word replaced, and the habit is one you no longer have.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-05, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

Two pieces, two jobs:

| piece | its job |
|---|---|
| बोलता | this happens repeatedly |
| हूँ / था | and it happens **now** / it happened **then** |

**-ता** carries the habit. The word after it carries the time. They were never
welded together, which is why moving the sentence into the past costs a single
swap rather than a new conjugation.

English needs a whole phrase for this — *used to* — and Hindi needs one letter's
worth of change.

## Grammar Lens: both halves bend
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-05, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-02, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01] -->

| speaker | earlier |
|---|---|
| a man | मैं हिंदी बोलता था। |
| a woman | मैं हिंदी बोलती थी। |

Both words end in **-आ** for a man and both swap to **-ई** for a woman. They
move together, so you are still tracking one decision rather than two.
