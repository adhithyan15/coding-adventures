Mandarin Chinese, chapter 304: Starting School.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
rùxué — to start school.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to start the school term, then the Mandarin for a new student at school.

You'll want to know: rùxué.
rùxué — "to start school".
tā liù suì rùxué — He started school at six.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rùxué is built from rù 'to enter' and 学 'to study', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyip and haewk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'inside' and 'to take in'.

The new character, in pieces: 记 (jì) — five strokes in two pieces: the speech piece of 说 on the left, two strokes; on the right the right piece of 起, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
日记 (rìjì) is built from 日 'a day' and 记 (jì) 'to note down', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyit and kiH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to record'.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Script — the speech piece, then the right piece of 起.
记 (jì).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, two strokes. The speech piece of 说: a dot, then a short stroke that runs right, turns down, and rises to the right at the foot.
The right piece, three strokes, as on the right of 起: a bar that turns down, a bar across the middle, then a vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 记 (jì), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 记 (jì)? (Two.) What is the left piece? (The speech piece, as in 说.) Where have you made the right piece? (On the right of 起.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
报到 (bàodào) — to report in on arrival; to sign in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to start school as a child, then the Mandarin for a diary.

You'll want to know: 报到.
报到 — bàodào — "to report in on arrival; to sign in".
新生明天来学校报到 xīnshēng míngtiān lái xuéxiào bàodào — The new students sign in at school tomorrow.
It sounds the same as bàodào 'to report the news'; this one means arriving and signing in.

The word, taken apart.
报到 (bàodào) is built from 报 'to report' and 到 'to arrive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pawH and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to reach': to report that you have arrived.
