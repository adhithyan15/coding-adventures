# три — three

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-DVA-01, RU-ETYMON-DVA-DWOH] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **два** and its English cousin.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-TRI-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **три** — *tri* — **three**

Three letters, and every one of them is a Cyrillic shape this book taught long
ago. Say it and read it at the same time: **т-р-и**.

## The word, taken apart — three, tres, tri
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-TRI-TREYES]; assesses=[RU-LEX-TRI-01, RU-LEX-DVA-01] -->

**три** continues Indo-European \**tréyes*, "three." English **three**, Latin
**trēs**, Greek **treîs** and Sanskrit **tri** are the same word.

This is the plainest of the whole set. Nothing has shifted: the *t*, the *r* and
the vowel are all where they were, which is why **три** and **three** sound like
each other and **сто** and **hundred**, later in this slice, will not.
