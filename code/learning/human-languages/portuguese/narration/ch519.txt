Portuguese, chapter 519: Antes do espetáculo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o ensaio — rehearsal; essay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to hum, then the Portuguese for to whistle.

You'll want to know: o ensaio.
o ensaio — "rehearsal; essay".
O ensaio da peça é às oito. The rehearsal for the play is at eight.
Also a written essay: um ensaio sobre arte.

The word, taken apart.
ensaio is inherited, through Old Portuguese eisaio, from Late Latin exagium "a weighing, a trial" (EtymDB), related to exigere "to weigh out".

Lesson 4 of 5.
a plateia — audience; the stalls.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to rehearse, then the Portuguese for stage.

You'll want to know: a plateia.
a plateia — "audience; the stalls".
A plateia estava cheia. The auditorium was full.
Also the seats on the ground floor of a theatre.

The word, taken apart.
plateia comes from Latin platēa "a broad street, a courtyard" (EtymDB), from Greek plateîa "broad".

Lesson 5 of 5.
o aplauso — applause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for stage, then the Portuguese for audience.

You'll want to know: o aplauso.
o aplauso — "applause".
No fim, os músicos receberam um grande aplauso. At the end, the musicians got a big round of applause.
Often plural: os aplausos.

The word, taken apart.
aplauso comes from Latin applausus "applause" (EtymDB), from applaudere, built on plaudere "to clap".
