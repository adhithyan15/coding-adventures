Lesson 1 of 5.
la sábana — the sheet (on a bed).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the refund, then the Spanish for to refund.

You'll want to know: la sábana.
la sábana — "the sheet (on a bed)".
Cambian las sábanas cada día. They change the sheets every day.
Do not mix it up with la sabana, savannah, with no written accent.

The word, taken apart.
sábana is inherited from Latin sabana, the plural of sabanum "a linen cloth, a towel" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from Greek sábanon.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: el balcón.
el balcón — "the balcony".
La habitación tiene un balcón al mar. The room has a balcony over the sea.
Salir al balcón — to go out on the balcony.

The word, taken apart.
balcón was borrowed from Italian balcone, which comes from Langobardic balko "beam", a Germanic word (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: la vista.
la vista — "the view; sight, eyesight".
Desde aquí hay una vista preciosa. There is a lovely view from here.
Con vistas al mar — with a sea view. Hasta la vista — see you.

The word, taken apart.
vista is built on visto "seen", the past form of ver "to see", which comes from Latin vidēre (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
el enchufe — the socket, the plug; connections (influence).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the balcony, then the Spanish for the view.

You'll want to know: el enchufe.
el enchufe — "the socket, the plug; connections (influence)".
No hay enchufe al lado de la cama. There is no socket next to the bed.
Colloquially, tener enchufe is to have connections that get you a job.

The word, taken apart.
enchufe is built on enchufar "to fit one pipe into another" (EtymDB). The origin of the verb is not settled; it may be imitative.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el grifo — the tap, the faucet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the view, then the Spanish for the socket.

You'll want to know: el grifo.
el grifo — "the tap, the faucet".
El grifo del baño gotea. The bathroom tap is dripping.
Latin America says la llave or la canilla.

The word, taken apart.
grifo is the same word as grifo "griffin", from Latin gryphus and Greek grýps (EtymDB); old taps were often shaped like a griffin head.
