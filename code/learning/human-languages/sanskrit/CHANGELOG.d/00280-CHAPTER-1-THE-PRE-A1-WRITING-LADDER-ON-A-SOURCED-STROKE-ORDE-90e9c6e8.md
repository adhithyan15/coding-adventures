## Chapter 1 — the pre-A1 writing ladder, on a sourced stroke order

Sanskrit had **no writing-stage evidence at all**: fifty script lessons and not
one directive. All four stages now sit on **न**, and Sanskrit gets the richest
guided copy of any track in the corpus, because it is the one track whose letter
lesson already carries a **cited** stroke order.

- **observe-trace** — the existing `SA-S02-letter-na` already traced न three
  times with the model visible. Only the marker was missing.
- **guided-copy** — new: copy the letter in its three strokes and **count the pen
  lifts**. There should be two, after the shoulder and after the stem. Three or
  four lifts means you drew a picture of the letter rather than the letter. The
  **śirorekhā** goes last, left to right, and it is not a lid on one letter — in
  running Devanagari it joins across a whole word, so it is the line the word
  will hang from. The lesson repeats the source's own caution: one attested
  teaching order, not a national standard.
- **delayed-copy** — new. Check the śirorekhā's **length**, not its presence: a
  hand writing from memory draws it only as wide as the letter's own body, and a
  short bar is the one mistake that looks right on one letter and fails on two.
- **dictation-transcription** — new. You are asked for **na**, not *n*. One shape
  came out for two sounds and nobody wrote the vowel, which is why the letters of
  this script cannot be counted the way an alphabet's can: **न** is not a letter
  plus a silent vowel, it is a syllable with a consonant you can change.
