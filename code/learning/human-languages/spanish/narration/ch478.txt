Lesson 1 of 5.
el equipaje — the luggage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to earn, then the Spanish for three hundred.

You'll want to know: el equipaje.
el equipaje — "the luggage".
El equipaje de mano no puede pesar más de diez kilos. Hand luggage cannot weigh more than ten kilos.
It is singular, like English luggage: mi equipaje.

The word, taken apart.
equipaje from French équipage, "a ship's crew and gear" (EtymDB 2.1), from équiper, "to fit out a ship", a word of Germanic origin related to English ship.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el calzado — the footwear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for three hundred, then the Spanish for the luggage.

You'll want to know: el calzado.
el calzado — "the footwear".
Lleva calzado cómodo. Wear comfortable footwear.
A notice word for shoes in general; one shoe is un zapato.

The word, taken apart.
calzado is the participle of calzar, "to put on shoes", from Latin calceare (EtymDB 2.1), built on calceus, "a shoe" — inherited: the Latin -ce- became -z-.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: el sitio.
el sitio — "the place; the room, the space".
No hay sitio en el coche. There is no room in the car.
Un sitio web is a website.

The word, taken apart.
sitio goes back to Latin situs, "a position, a site" (EtymDB 2.1); how it came by its ending -io is not settled. English site comes from the same Latin word.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: el espacio.
el espacio — "the space".
El espacio es pequeño pero tiene luz. The space is small but it has light.
More formal than sitio, and the word for outer space too.

The word, taken apart.
espacio from Latin spatium, "room, extent" (EtymDB 2.1) — a learned borrowing, with the e- Spanish puts before sp-.

Lesson 5 of 5.
disponible — available.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the place, then the Spanish for the space.

You'll want to know: disponible.
disponible — "available".
La habitación está disponible desde el lunes. The room is available from Monday.
Used with estar.

The word, taken apart.
disponible is built from disponer, "to arrange, to have at hand", with -ible, "able to be". Disponer is Latin disponere, "to set in order": dis-, "apart", and ponere, "to put".
