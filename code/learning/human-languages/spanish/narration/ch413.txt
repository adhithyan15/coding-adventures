Spanish, chapter 413: Music and Concert.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
la música — another art of the Muses.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall el museo, a place whose name once meant a seat of the Muses. Their art also gives Spanish la música.

The word, taken apart.
Greek mousikḗ, the art of the Muses, travelled into Latin mūsica and then Spanish música. English music followed the same road through French. The root is already anchored in museo, so this lesson extends that family rather than assigning it a second owner.

Grammar Lens: the written accent.
The stress falls on the first beat: MÚ-si-ca. Spanish writes the accent because that antepenultimate stress must always be marked. The noun is feminine: la música, música clásica.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: la música]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: música clásica]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: me gusta la música]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Music? (La música.) Classical music? (Música clásica.)
Source: DLE: música.


Lesson 2 of 4.
el concierto — music brought together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall la música. A scheduled event where people perform music is el concierto.

The word, taken apart.
Concierto belongs to concertar, to bring things into agreement, from Latin concertāre. Italian and French helped the musical event-sense travel, and English concert is a close relative. Think of performers bringing their parts together.

Grammar Lens: concert of what?
Use de to name the kind: un concierto de música clásica. The event is masculine: el concierto, un concierto.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el concierto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un concierto de música]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un concierto de música clásica]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Concert? (El concierto.)
Source: DLE: concierto.
