Lesson 1 of 6.
shénhuà — myth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go into battle, then the Mandarin for a war between countries.

You'll want to know: shénhuà.
shénhuà — "myth".
wǒ xiǎoshíhou tīng guo yí ge shénhuà — When I was little I heard a myth.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shénhuà is built from shén 'a god' and 话 'talk', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zyin and hwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a spirit' and the second 'talk'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
传说 (chuánshuō) — legend; folk tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a war between countries, then the Mandarin for a myth about the gods.

You'll want to know: 传说.
传说 — chuánshuō — "legend; folk tale".
zhège dìfang yǒu yí ge gǔlǎo de chuánshuō — This place has an old legend.
The new character, in pieces: 传 (chuán) — six strokes: the person piece 亻 on the left; on the right two bars, a stroke that runs down, turns right and sweeps down to the left, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
传说 (chuánshuō) is built from 传 (chuán) 'to pass on' and 说 'to tell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjwen and sywet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to turn over, to pass along' and the second 'to tell'.

Script — the person piece, then two bars and a turning sweep.
传 (chuán).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes, as in 代.
The right piece, four strokes. A short bar, then a longer bar under it. Then one stroke that starts above the bars, runs down through them, turns right, and sweeps down to the left. Last, a dot at the foot.
2 + 4 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
liúchuán — to be handed down; to spread.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a myth about the gods, then the Mandarin for an old folk tale.

You'll want to know: liúchuán.
liúchuán — "to be handed down; to spread".
zhège chuánshuō liúchuán le yìqiān nián — This legend has been handed down for a thousand years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liúchuán is built from liú 'to flow' and 传 (chuán) 'to pass on', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ljuw and drjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, quoting an older dictionary, 'water moving'.
