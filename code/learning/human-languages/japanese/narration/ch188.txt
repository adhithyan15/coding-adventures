Japanese, chapter 188: At the Post Office.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ゆうびんきょく (yūbinkyoku) — post office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to borrow, then the Japanese for money exchange.

You'll want to know: ゆうびんきょく.
ゆうびんきょく — yūbinkyoku — "post office".
ゆうびんきょくは えきの ちかくです。 — yūbinkyoku wa eki no chikaku desu — "The post office is near the station."

The word, taken apart.
ゆうびんきょく (yūbinkyoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yū, "post station" (Middle Chinese hjuw), bin, "convenience" (bjienH) and kyoku, "office, bureau" (gjowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
きって (kitte) — stamp; postage stamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for money exchange, then the Japanese for post office.

You'll want to know: きって.
きって — kitte — "stamp; postage stamp".
ゆうびんきょくで きってを かいます。 — yūbinkyoku de kitte o kaimasu — "I buy stamps at the post office."

The word, taken apart.
きって (kitte) is native Japanese, built from kiri, the stem of kiru, "to cut", and te, "hand", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
はがき (hagaki) — postcard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for post office, then the Japanese for stamp.

You'll want to know: はがき.
はがき — hagaki — "postcard".
ともだちに はがきを かきます。 — tomodachi ni hagaki o kakimasu — "I write a postcard to a friend."

The word, taken apart.
はがき (hagaki) is native Japanese, built from ha and kaki, "writing", from kaku, "to write"; JMdict spells the first part as either "leaf" or "edge", so which came first is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おくる (okuru) — to send; to see off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for stamp, then the Japanese for postcard.

You'll want to know: おくる.
おくる — okuru — "to send; to see off".
ははに てがみを おくります。 — haha ni tegami o okurimasu — "I send a letter to my mother."
Polite おくります, okurimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
おくる (okuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
