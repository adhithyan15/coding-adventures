Spanish, chapter 491: Pagar en la tienda.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el recibo — the receipt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the strike, then the Spanish for the business.

You'll want to know: el recibo.
el recibo — "the receipt".
Guarde el recibo para cambiar el producto. Keep the receipt to exchange the item.

The word, taken apart.
recibo is the noun of recibir, "to receive", from Latin recipere, "to take back" — inherited: the -p- became -b-.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the business, then the Spanish for the receipt.

You'll want to know: la oferta.
la oferta — "the offer, the special offer".
Las naranjas están de oferta. The oranges are on special offer.
De oferta — on offer.

The word, taken apart.
oferta goes back to Latin offerre, "to bring before, to offer" (Lewis & Short): ob-, "towards", and ferre, "to carry" — the verb behind ofrecer, "to offer".

Lesson 3 of 5.
el cajero — the cashier; the cash machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the receipt, then the Spanish for the offer.

You'll want to know: el cajero.
el cajero — "the cashier; the cash machine".
El cajero no funciona. The cash machine is not working.
La caja is the till.

The word, taken apart.
cajero is caja, "a box, a cash box", with -ero. Caja came through Catalan caixa from Latin capsa, "a box" (EtymDB 2.1 traces it to capsa).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la propina — the tip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the cashier, then the Spanish for the wallet.

You'll want to know: la propina.
la propina — "the tip".
Le dejamos una propina al camarero. We left the waiter a tip.
In much of Latin America a waiter is el mesero.

The word, taken apart.
propina from Medieval Latin propina, from Latin propinare, "to drink to someone's health" (Lewis & Short), itself from Greek: a tip was money for a drink.
