Portuguese, chapter 413: Na paragem.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o abrigo — the shelter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to pedal, then the Portuguese for the parking meter.

You'll want to know: o abrigo.
o abrigo — "the shelter".
Esperamos o autocarro no abrigo da paragem. We wait for the bus in the bus shelter.
Ficar ao abrigo da chuva — to stay out of the rain.

The word, taken apart.
abrigo is built on abrigar 'to shelter' (EtymDB). Abrigar is usually traced to Latin aprīcārī 'to warm oneself in the sun' (Lewis & Short), but that is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
a camioneta — the coach, the long-distance bus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the shelter, then the Portuguese for the terminus.

You'll want to know: a camioneta.
a camioneta — "the coach, the long-distance bus".
Fomos para o sul de camioneta. We went south by coach.
In Portugal a camioneta runs between towns and o autocarro inside a town. In Brazil: o ônibus.

The word, taken apart.
camioneta is a small form of camião 'lorry', which was borrowed from French camion 'truck' (EtymDB); the French word's origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o itinerário — the route, the itinerary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the terminus, then the Portuguese for the coach.

You'll want to know: o itinerário.
o itinerário — "the route, the itinerary".
O itinerário da carreira passa pelo hospital. The route of the bus line goes past the hospital.
O itinerário of a trip is also its plan, day by day.

The word, taken apart.
itinerário is a learned borrowing from Late Latin itinerārium 'travel guide', built on iter, itineris 'journey' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
apear — to get off (a vehicle); to dismount.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the coach, then the Portuguese for the route.

You'll want to know: apear.
apear — "to get off (a vehicle); to dismount".
Apeei-me na paragem do mercado. I got off at the market stop.
Mostly reflexive and a bit formal: apear-se do comboio. Everyday speech also says sair do autocarro.

The word, taken apart.
apear is built from a-, pé 'foot' and -ar (EtymDB): to put your feet on the ground. Pé is inherited from Latin pedem.
