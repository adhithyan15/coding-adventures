# അല്ലേ (allē) — "isn't it?"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C77-ALLA-01, ML-CONCEPT-C70-O-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *is not so*. Then say the letter Malayalam adds to turn a
statement into a genuine question.

## You'll want to know: അല്ലേ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C77-ALLE-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C48-ROLE-01] -->

> **നിങ്ങൾ അധ്യാപകൻ ആണ്, അല്ലേ?**
>
> *niṅṅaḷ adhyāpakan āṇ, allē?* — "**You are a teacher, aren't you?**"

Look at the front of it. **അല്ലേ** opens on **അല്ല**, the word from the last
lesson, and that containment is the whole of why it means what it means: it
hangs *"or is it not so?"* on the end of a statement you have already made.

## Grammar Lens: asking, and asking for agreement
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C77-ALLE-01, ML-CONCEPT-C70-O-01] -->

Malayalam has **two ways to end a sentence with a question**, and they want
different things back:

| | |
|---|---|
| **-ഓ** | a genuine question — *I do not know; tell me* |
| **അല്ലേ** | a tag — *I think I know; agree with me* |

**ചായ വേണോ?** genuinely does not know whether you want tea. **നിങ്ങൾ അധ്യാപകൻ
ആണ്, അല്ലേ?** is fairly sure you are one and is checking.

English hides this difference inside intonation and a repeated verb — *you are,
aren't you*. Malayalam puts it in one word and does not change the sentence in
front of it.
