Arabic, chapter 310: Calling for help.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
استغاث (istaghātha) — to call for help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a throne, then the Arabic for to crown.

You'll want to know: استغاث.
استغاث — istaghātha — "to call for help".
استغاثت المرأة من نافذة البيت. istaghāthat al-marʾa min nāfidhat al-bayt. The woman called for help from the window of the house.
Present: يستغيث, yastaghīth.

The word, taken apart.
استغاث (istaghātha) is built on the native Arabic root gh-w-th (Lane), where ghawth is help given to one in distress. It is Form X, "to ask for help".

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

Lesson 2 of 5.
نجا (najā) — to survive, to escape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to crown, then the Arabic for to call for help.

You'll want to know: نجا.
نجا — najā — "to survive, to escape".
نجا كل الركاب من الحادث. najā kull ar-rukkāb min al-ḥādith. All the passengers survived the accident.
Present: ينجو, yanjū.
It takes من: نجا (najā) من الموت.

The word, taken apart.
نجا (najā) is built on the native Arabic root n-j-w (Lane), "to escape, to be safe"; najāt, rescue, is its verbal noun.

صاح (ṣāḥa) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-y-ḥ (Lane), "to cry out, to raise the voice"; ṣayḥa is a shout.

Lesson 4 of 5.
نادى (nādā) — to call out to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to survive, then the Arabic for to cry out.

You'll want to know: نادى.
نادى — nādā — "to call out to".
نادت الأم ابنها من الشرفة. nādat al-umm ibnahā min ash-shurfa. The mother called to her son from the balcony.
Present: ينادي, yunādī.
Not the taught نادي, a club: the final letter tells them apart.

The word, taken apart.
نادى (nādā) is built on the native Arabic root n-d-w (Lane), "to call; to meet together", the root of the taught nādī, a club. It is Form III, "to call to someone".

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: منقذ.
منقذ — munqidh — "a rescuer; a lifeguard".
سبح المنقذ نحو الطفل بسرعة. sabaḥa al-munqidh naḥwa aṭ-ṭifl bi-surʿa. The lifeguard swam quickly towards the child.

The word, taken apart.
منقذ (munqidh) is built on the native Arabic root n-q-dh (Lane), the root of the taught anqadha, to rescue. Munqidh is its Form IV participle, "one who rescues".
