Japanese, chapter 106: Gate, Wood, Bud, Rock, Pebble.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
もん (mon) — a gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a lamp, then the Japanese for light.

You'll want to know: もん.
もん — mon — "a gate".

The word, taken apart.
もん (mon) is Sino-Japanese (kango): the on'yomi of a character meaning "gate", a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mwon in Baxter's transcription; Unihan, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
はやし (hayashi) — a wood, a grove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for light, then the Japanese for a gate.

You'll want to know: はやし.
はやし — hayashi — "a wood, a grove".

The word, taken apart.
はやし (hayashi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

こいし — koishi — "a pebble".

The word, taken apart.
こいし (koishi) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: ko, "small", and ishi, "stone", both kun'yomi of its kanji (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
