Sanskrit, chapter 335: Magic and Disguise.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
माया (māyā) — illusion; magic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for kills, then the Sanskrit for swallows.

You'll want to know: माया.
माया — māyā — "illusion; magic".
एतत् सर्वं राक्षसस्य माया अस्ति। etat sarvaṃ rākṣasasya māyā asti. All this is the demon's illusion.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
माया (māyā) MW gives māyā first as art, wisdom, extraordinary power, and later as illusion, deception, trickery and magic, without a root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वेषः (veṣaḥ) — a disguise; dress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for swallows, then the Sanskrit for illusion.

You'll want to know: वेषः.
वेषः — veṣaḥ — "a disguise; dress".
राक्षसः यात्रिकस्य वेषं धारयति। rākṣasaḥ yātrikasya veṣaṃ dhārayati. The demon takes on the disguise of a pilgrim.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वेषः (veṣaḥ) comes from the root √viṣ, "to be active, work", by MW's account; MW gives veṣa as work, and as dress, an artificial exterior, an assumed appearance (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
छलम् (chalam) — a trick; deceit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for illusion, then the Sanskrit for a disguise.

You'll want to know: छलम्.
छलम् — chalam — "a trick; deceit".
छलेन सः जनान् वञ्चयति। chalena saḥ janān vañcayati. By a trick he cheats people.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
छलम् (chalam) comes from the root √skhal, "to stumble", as MW explains it, as in skhalati; MW gives chala as fraud, deceit, sham, guise, a trick (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मोहयति (mohayati) — bewilders; deludes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a disguise, then the Sanskrit for a trick.

You'll want to know: मोहयति.
मोहयति — mohayati — "bewilders; deludes".
राक्षसः मायया जनान् मोहयति। rākṣasaḥ māyayā janān mohayati. The demon bewilders people with illusion.
Past (he …): सः जनान् मोहितवान्, saḥ janān mohitavān.

The word, taken apart.
मोहयति (mohayati) comes from the root √muh, "to be confused, go astray", in its causative form: to bewilder, delude (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
आकर्षति (ākarṣati) — draws; attracts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a trick, then the Sanskrit for bewilders.

You'll want to know: आकर्षति.
आकर्षति — ākarṣati — "draws; attracts".
मधुरं गीतं जनान् आकर्षति। madhuraṃ gītaṃ janān ākarṣati. The sweet song attracts people.
Past (it …): गीतं जनान् आकृष्टवत्, gītaṃ janān ākṛṣṭavat.

The word, taken apart.
आकर्षति (ākarṣati) is built from ā, "towards", and the root √kṛṣ, "to drag, pull", as in karṣati; MW gives ā-kṛṣ as to draw towards oneself, attract (MW).
