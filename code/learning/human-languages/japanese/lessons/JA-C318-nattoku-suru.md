## You'll want to know: なっとくする
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C318-SETSUMEI318-02]; assesses=[] -->

**なっとくする** — *nattoku suru* — "to be convinced; to accept a reason".

> **りゆうが わかったので、なっとくしました。** — *riyū ga wakatta node, nattoku shimashita* — "Once I understood the reason, I was convinced."

Polite **なっとくします**, *nattokushimasu*; a する verb. Past (polite): **ちちは なっとくしました。**, *chichi wa nattoku shimashita*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**なっとくする** (*nattoku suru*) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb *suru*, "to do". The noun, *nattoku*, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: *nō*, "settlement" (Middle Chinese *nop*) and *toku*, "gain" (*tok*) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as *nat-toku*.
