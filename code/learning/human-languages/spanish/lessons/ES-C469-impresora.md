# la impresora — the printer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01, ES-LEX-IMPRESO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la lavadora** and **el impreso**. One lends the ending, the
other lends the stem, and together they make the machine.

## You'll want to know: la impresora
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01] -->

**la impresora** — the printer.

> La **impresora** de la sala 2 está fuera de servicio.

*Una impresora en color* — a colour printer.

You could have reached this word before being told, which is what
[la lavadora](./ES-C432-lavadora.md) promised: a machine is often its verb
plus **-dora**.

## Grammar Lens: the article says which one you mean
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01] -->

[El contador](./ES-C432-contador.md) settled one thing already: the ending
does not decide the article, since *la lavadora* is feminine and *el contador*
masculine off the same **-dor**. Treat that as known.

What this word adds is a case where the article is not merely a fact to
memorise but is **carrying the meaning**. *Lavar* has no human washer in
ordinary speech, so **-dora** was free to mean the machine and nothing else.
*Imprimir* is not like that. Printing was a trade long before it was a box on
a desk, and the stem kept both.

| Spanish | what it is |
|---|---|
| el impresor | the person who prints |
| la impresora | the machine that prints |

Say *el impresor* about the box and you have promoted it to a tradesman. With
*contador* and *lavadora* the article was something to learn; here it is
something to choose.

English will not help you: it calls both of them a **printer** and leaves the
sentence to sort it out. Spanish has decided in advance, in one letter.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-02, ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01] -->

Same *imprimere* as the verb: *in-* plus *premere*, to press into.

Why feminine rather than masculine? *El contador* told you the ending does not
decide; this is the mechanism underneath. *Impresora* started life as an
adjective leaning on a noun — *la máquina impresora*, the printing machine.
The noun dropped away and left its gender behind. So the article you cannot
predict from the ending was fixed by a word that is no longer said.
