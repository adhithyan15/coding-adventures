Gujarati, chapter 503: Smooth and Straight.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
રંદો (rando) — a carpenter's plane.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a nail, then the Gujarati for to hammer in.

You'll want to know: રંદો.
રંદો — rando — "a carpenter's plane".
સુથાર રંદાથી લાકડું સીધું કરે છે. suthār randāthī lākḍũ sīdhũ kare che. The carpenter makes the wood straight with a plane.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
રંદો (rando) is usually traced to Persian randa, "a carpenter's plane". Before થી it becomes રંદાથી.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: મઠારવું.
મઠારવું — maṭhārvũ — "to smooth (a surface)".
સુથારે રંદાથી બારણું મઠાર્યું. suthāre randāthī bārṇũ maṭhāryũ. The carpenter smoothed the door with a plane.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બારણું મઠાર્યું, teṇe bārṇũ maṭhāryũ.

The word, taken apart.
મઠારવું (maṭhārvũ) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit mṛṣṭá, "rubbed, washed, pure", and CDIAL gives the Gujarati verb as "to smooth" (CDIAL 10299).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ગુંદર (gundar) — glue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a carpenter's plane, then the Gujarati for to smooth.

You'll want to know: ગુંદર.
ગુંદર — gundar — "glue".
આ ગુંદર લાકડા પર સારું ચોંટે છે. ā gundar lākḍā par sārũ cõṭe che. This glue sticks well to wood.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ગુંદર (gundar) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit gundra, the name of a reed, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati word with the sense "gum" (CDIAL 4199); how the reed's name came to mean gum is not explained there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
તિરાડ (tirāḍ) — a crack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to smooth, then the Gujarati for glue.

You'll want to know: તિરાડ.
તિરાડ — tirāḍ — "a crack".
જૂના બારણામાં એક મોટી તિરાડ છે. jūnā bārṇāmā̃ ek moṭī tirāḍ che. There is a big crack in the old door.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
તિરાડ (tirāḍ) is perhaps related to taraṛ, "a crack", which CDIAL files under traṭ (CDIAL 5988), but CDIAL does not list this form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ચીરવું (cīrvũ) — to split (wood).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for glue, then the Gujarati for a crack.

You'll want to know: ચીરવું.
ચીરવું — cīrvũ — "to split (wood)".
અરુણે મોટું લાકડું બે ભાગમાં ચીર્યું. Aruṇe moṭũ lākḍũ be bhāgmā̃ cīryũ. Arun split the big piece of wood in two.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે લાકડું ચીર્યું, teṇe lākḍũ cīryũ.

The word, taken apart.
ચીરવું (cīrvũ) comes from cīrayati, "splits", a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to slit, crack, cut" (CDIAL 4844).
