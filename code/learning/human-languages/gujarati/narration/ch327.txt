Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉજવવું (ujavvũ) — to celebrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to found, then the Gujarati for a celebration.

You'll want to know: ઉજવવું.
ઉજવવું — ujavvũ — "to celebrate".
અમે દિવાળી ઘરે ઉજવી. ame divāḷī ghare ujvī. We celebrated Diwali at home.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે તહેવાર ઉજવ્યો, teṇe tahevār ujavyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઉજવવું (ujavvũ) comes from udyāpayati, a form CDIAL reconstructs, where it lists the Gujarati verb, "to conclude a religious vow" (CDIAL 2053): the feast at the end of a vow became any celebration. It agrees with the festival: દિવાળી ઉજવી.

Lesson 4 of 5.
રિવાજ (rivāj) — a custom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to celebrate, then the Gujarati for a tradition.

You'll want to know: રિવાજ.
રિવાજ — rivāj — "a custom".
લગ્નનો આ રિવાજ ખૂબ જૂનો છે. lagnano ā rivāj khūb jūno che. This wedding custom is very old.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
રિવાજ (rivāj) is usually traced to Persian rivāj, "currency, custom", a word Persian took from Arabic: what is current among people. It is masculine: જૂનો રિવાજ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
પર્વ (parva) — a holy day; a festival day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a tradition, then the Gujarati for a custom.

You'll want to know: પર્વ.
પર્વ — parva — "a holy day; a festival day".
દિવાળી પ્રકાશનું પર્વ છે. divāḷī prakāśnũ parva che. Diwali is the festival of light.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
પર્વ (parva) is a tatsama, Sanskrit parvan, "a knot, a joint", and then a fixed point in the year, the days of the moon's changes and a festival (Monier-Williams). It is neuter: પ્રકાશનું પર્વ.
