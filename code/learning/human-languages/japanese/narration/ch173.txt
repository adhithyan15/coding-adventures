Japanese, chapter 173: Food, Drinks and Leftovers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
たべもの (tabemono) — food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dinner, then the Japanese for to grill.

You'll want to know: たべもの.
たべもの — tabemono — "food".
にほんの たべものは おいしいです。 — nihon no tabemono wa oishii desu — "Japanese food is tasty."

The word, taken apart.
たべもの (tabemono) is native Japanese, built from tabe, the stem of taberu, "to eat", and mono, "thing" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
おべんとう (obentō) — boxed lunch; bento.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for food, then the Japanese for beverage.

You'll want to know: おべんとう.
おべんとう — obentō — "boxed lunch; bento".
こうえんで おべんとうを たべます。 — kōen de obentō o tabemasu — "I eat a boxed lunch in the park."

The word, taken apart.
おべんとう (obentō) is built from polite o- and Sino-Japanese bentō, two characters read with their on'yomi, ben and tō, readings borrowed from Chinese; the older history of the compound is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
のこる (nokoru) — to be left over; to remain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for beverage, then the Japanese for boxed lunch.

You'll want to know: のこる.
のこる — nokoru — "to be left over; to remain".
ごはんが すこし のこります。 — gohan ga sukoshi nokorimasu — "A little rice is left over."
Polite のこります, nokorimasu; a godan verb. Its transitive partner のこす is taught already.

The word, taken apart.
のこる (nokoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it shares its stem with nokosu, "to leave behind" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
