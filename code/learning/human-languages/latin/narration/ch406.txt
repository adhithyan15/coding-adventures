Latin, chapter 406: Good and Bad Pupils.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ingeniōsus, ingeniōsa, ingeniōsum — clever, talented.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to say repeatedly, then the Latin for a task.

You'll want to know: ingeniōsus, ingeniōsa, ingeniōsum.
ingeniōsus, ingeniōsa, ingeniōsum — "clever, talented".
Puer ingeniōsus omnia celeriter discit. The clever boy learns everything quickly.
ingeniōsus, ingeniōsa, ingeniōsum, built on ingenium, natural talent. English ingenious.

The word, taken apart.
ingeniōsus is built on ingenium "inborn talent" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -ōsus "full of". English ingenious comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
attentus, attenta, attentum — attentive, intent; careful with money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a task, then the Latin for clever.

You'll want to know: attentus, attenta, attentum.
attentus, attenta, attentum — "attentive, intent; careful with money".
Discipulī attentī magistrum audiunt. The attentive pupils listen to the teacher.
attentus, attenta, attentum, the participle of attendō, to stretch towards. Attentus ad rem, careful with money. English attentive.

The word, taken apart.
attentus is the participle of attendō "to stretch towards, to give heed" (Lewis & Short), from ad and tendō "to stretch". English attentive and attention come from its family.

Lesson 3 of 5.
tardus, tarda, tardum — slow; slow-witted.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for clever, then the Latin for attentive.

You'll want to know: tardus, tarda, tardum.
tardus, tarda, tardum — "slow; slow-witted".
Tardus est, sed dīligēns. He is slow, but hard-working.
tardus, tarda, tardum: slow of foot and slow of mind. English tardy and retard.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of tardus is not settled; Lewis & Short give no sure account. English tardy comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
sēdulus, sēdula, sēdulum — painstaking, busy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for attentive, then the Latin for slow.

You'll want to know: sēdulus, sēdula, sēdulum.
sēdulus, sēdula, sēdulum — "painstaking, busy".
Sēdula ancilla domum pūrgat. The busy maid cleans the house.
sēdulus, sēdula, sēdulum. The adverb sēdulō is busily, on purpose. English sedulous.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of sēdulus is not settled: EtymDB links it with sedeō "to sit", and Lewis & Short call it doubtful. English sedulous comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
neglegēns, neglegentis — careless, negligent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for slow, then the Latin for painstaking.

You'll want to know: neglegēns, neglegentis.
neglegēns, neglegentis — "careless, negligent".
Discipulus neglegēns pēnsum nōn perfēcit. The careless pupil did not finish his task.
neglegēns, neglegentis, the participle of neglegō, nec + legō: not to pick up. English negligent and neglect.

The word, taken apart.
neglegēns is the present participle of neglegō "not to heed" (Lewis & Short), from nec "not" and legō "to pick up, to gather". English negligent and neglect come from it.
