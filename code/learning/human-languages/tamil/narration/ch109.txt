Tamil, chapter 109: Letters From Your First Words.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
இ (i) — the letter that opens இல்லை.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: இ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach them.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read உ and ஓ aloud, and write each once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say இல்லை (illai), "no". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: இ.
இ — i.
It is the letter at the front of இல்லை (illai), "no", one of the first words in this book. You have been writing its sign, ◌ி, since நன்றி. This is the independent shape of the same vowel: the one a word uses when it begins with the sound.

Writing: இ.
1. start inside the upper curl and sweep around it.
2. without lifting, sweep down the inner right curve.
3. without lifting, carry left down the lower diagonal and turn around the lower-left loop.
4. without lifting, climb the loop's outer side back to the left crossing.
5. without lifting, carry right across the middle and turn around the lower-right loop.
6. without lifting, climb the other diagonal back across the middle and on up the outer left side.
7. without lifting, arch over the top and down the far right — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order, not a national standard — Tamil handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Sankaran Radhakrishnan, Tamil Script Learners Manual, Appendix I: Hand-movements, Frame 4, இ (Univ. of Texas at Austin), p. 192.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at இல்லை and point at இ inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: இ three times, saying i as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ஓ, then இ (i), from memory]
[once you have stopped driving — read: இல்லை aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which character is this — இ? (i.) Which word did it come from? (இல்லை, illai, "no".)

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say இல்லை (illai), "no". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one character out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ◌ை.
◌ை — ai.
It is a vowel sign, not a letter. It attaches to a consonant and replaces the built-in a with ai. Look at the end of இல்லை: the sign sits on the left of ல, although you say it after. Tamil writes this sign first and sounds it second.

Writing: ◌ை.
Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ◌ை (ai) yourself — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
The strip shows where to start the character and which way to travel, in one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school, so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the only one.

Script you'll notice: ஸ.
ஸ — sa.
It is inside நமஸ்காரம் (namaskāram), the borrowed bow-word you met beside வணக்கம். ஸ (sa) is a Grantha letter: Tamil added it to write words that came from Sanskrit, so you will see it mostly in borrowed words.
