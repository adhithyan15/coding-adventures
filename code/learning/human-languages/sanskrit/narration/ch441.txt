Sanskrit, chapter 441: Bad Weather.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दुर्दिनम् (durdinam) — a day of bad weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for huddles up, then the Sanskrit for a cloak.

You'll want to know: दुर्दिनम्.
दुर्दिनम् — durdinam — "a day of bad weather".
दुर्दिने यात्रा कठिना भवति। durdine yātrā kaṭhinā bhavati. On a day of bad weather a journey is hard.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
दुर्दिनम् (durdinam) is built from dur, "bad, ill", and dina, "a day"; MW gives the compound as a rainy or cloudy day, bad weather (MW).

A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
तुहिनम् (tuhinam) is the word MW gives for frost, cold, mist, dew and snow; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

आवृणोति — āvṛṇoti — "veils; hides from view".
अभ्रं सूर्यम् आवृणोति। abhraṃ sūryam āvṛṇoti. A cloud veils the sun.
Past (it …): अभ्रं सूर्यम् आवृतवत्, abhraṃ sūryam āvṛtavat.

The word, taken apart.
आवृणोति (āvṛṇoti) is built from ā and the root √vṛ, "to cover"; MW gives ā-vṛ as to cover, hide, conceal, with the participle āvṛta, "covered" (MW).
