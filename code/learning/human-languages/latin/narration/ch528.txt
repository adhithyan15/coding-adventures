Lesson 1 of 6.
gēns, gentis — a clan, family line; a people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sleeplessness, then the Latin for to recline.

You'll want to know: gēns, gentis.
gēns, gentis — "a clan, family line; a people".
Haec gēns antīqua et nōbilis est. This clan is old and noble.
Feminine, third declension. A Roman's family name showed his gēns. English gentle and genteel come from it.

The word, taken apart.
gēns is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "birth, family", built on the root of gignō "to beget" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English gentle and gentile come from it, and English kin and kind are cousins.

Lesson 3 of 6.
affīnis, affīne — related by marriage; an in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a clan, then the Latin for near.

You'll want to know: affīnis, affīne.
affīnis, affīne — "related by marriage; an in-law".
Socer meus affīnis meus est. My father-in-law is my relative by marriage.
Two endings. As a noun, a relative by marriage, as against a cognātus by blood. English affinity.

The word, taken apart.
affīnis is built on ad- "at" and fīnis "boundary" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): first a neighbour, then a relative by marriage. English affinity comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
cōnsōbrīnus, cōnsōbrīnī — a cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for near, then the Latin for related by marriage.

You'll want to know: cōnsōbrīnus, cōnsōbrīnī.
cōnsōbrīnus, cōnsōbrīnī — "a cousin".
Cōnsōbrīnus meus in vīllā avī habitat. My cousin lives in grandfather's country house.
Masculine; cōnsōbrīna is a female cousin. First the child of a mother's sister. English cousin comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsōbrīnus is built on con- and sōbrīnus "maternal cousin", from soror "sister" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English cousin comes from it through Old French.
