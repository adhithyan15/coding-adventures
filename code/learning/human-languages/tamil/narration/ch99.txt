5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — please drink.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please come in, then the Tamil for please eat.

You'll want to know: குடியுங்கள்.
குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — "please drink".
தண்ணீர் குடியுங்கள் (kuṭiyuṅkaḷ) — "please have some water".
Look at தண்ணீர்: it is the thing being drunk, and it carries no -ஐ. A thing can do without the ending that a person must have.

What you've built.
Food, and now drink.

Lesson 2 of 5.
அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) — a gift given with affection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please eat, then the Tamil for please drink.

You'll want to know: அன்பளிப்பு.
அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) — "a present", literally "a giving of love".
அன்பு is "love"; அளிப்பு is "a giving". பரிசு can be a prize; அன்பளிப்பு (aṉpaḷippu) is always from the heart.

What you've built.
A second word for gift, with love in it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
நல்வரவு (nalvaravu) — welcome (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for please drink, then the Tamil for a present.

You'll want to know: நல்வரவு.
நல்வரவு (nalvaravu) — "welcome", the formal word, literally "good arrival".
You will see it on banners and doorways; வாருங்கள் is what you say aloud.

What you've built.
The written welcome.

Lesson 4 of 5.
மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள் (maṉṉittuviṭuṅkaḷ) — please forgive me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a present, then the Tamil for a formal welcome.

You'll want to know: மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள்.
மன்னித்துவிடுங்கள் (maṉṉittuviṭuṅkaḷ) — "please forgive me".
It is fuller and more earnest than மன்னிக்கவும்: for a real mistake, not a bumped elbow.

What you've built.
A fuller apology.
