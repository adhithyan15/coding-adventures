Hindi, chapter 337: Buried Treasure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ख़ज़ाना (khazānā) — a treasure; a treasury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to regret, then the Hindi for to sulk.

You'll want to know: ख़ज़ाना.
ख़ज़ाना — khazānā — "a treasure; a treasury".
कहानी में राजा का ख़ज़ाना एक पुराने क़िले में था। kahānī meṁ rājā kā khazānā ek purāne qile meṁ thā. In the story the king's treasure was in an old fort.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ख़ज़ाना (khazānā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic khizāna, "a storehouse". It is a king's treasury and, in stories, gold hidden underground.

Lesson 2 of 5.
गाड़ना (gāṛnā) — to bury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to sulk, then the Hindi for a treasure.

You'll want to know: गाड़ना.
गाड़ना — gāṛnā — "to bury".
राजा ने ख़ज़ाना एक पेड़ के नीचे गाड़ा। rājā ne khazānā ek peṛ ke nīce gāṛā. The king buried the treasure under a tree.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने पैसा गाड़ा, usne paisā gāṛā.

The word, taken apart.
गाड़ना (gāṛnā) is inherited from gaḍḍ, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 3979): to drive something into the ground, a post or a treasure.

Lesson 5 of 5.
डसना (ḍasnā) — to bite (of a snake).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a cobra, then the Hindi for a jewel.

You'll want to know: डसना.
डसना — ḍasnā — "to bite (of a snake)".
नाग ने चोर को डसा। nāg ne cor ko ḍasā. The cobra bit the thief.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने किसान को डसा, usne kisān ko ḍasā.

The word, taken apart.
डसना (ḍasnā) goes back to Sanskrit daśati, "bites, stings" (CDIAL 6230). It is the bite of a snake or a scorpion, not of a dog.
