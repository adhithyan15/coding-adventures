Italian, chapter 478: Orgoglio e vergogna.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'orgoglio — pride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for regret, then the Italian for remorse.

You'll want to know: l'orgoglio.
l'orgoglio — "pride".
Mio padre parla con orgoglio del suo lavoro. My father speaks with pride about his work.
The adjective is orgoglioso; it can be good or bad, like English pride.

The word, taken apart.
orgoglio comes from Frankish urgol "pride" (EtymDB), a Germanic word, probably through Old French.

Lesson 2 of 6.
vergognarsi — to be ashamed, to be embarrassed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for remorse, then the Italian for pride.

You'll want to know: vergognarsi.
vergognarsi — "to be ashamed, to be embarrassed".
Mi vergogno di cantare davanti agli altri. I am embarrassed to sing in front of other people.
Vergognarsi di + noun or infinitive. The noun is la vergogna.

The word, taken apart.
vergognarsi is built on vergogna "shame", inherited from Latin verēcundia "modesty, shame" (Lewis & Short), from verērī "to respect, to fear".

Lesson 3 of 6.
l'imbarazzo — embarrassment; awkwardness.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: l'imbarazzo.
l'imbarazzo — "embarrassment; awkwardness".
Per l'imbarazzo non sapevo cosa dire. I was so embarrassed I did not know what to say.
Mettere in imbarazzo — to embarrass. Imbarazzato — embarrassed.

The word, taken apart.
imbarazzo comes from Spanish embarazo "hindrance" (EtymDB), built on a word for a bar or barrier: at first it meant an obstacle.

Nella sua voce c'era un po' di invidia. There was a little envy in his voice.
The adjective is invidioso.

The word, taken apart.
invidia is a learned borrowing from Latin invidia "envy, ill will" (EtymDB), from invidēre "to eye with ill will", built on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 5 of 6.
invidiare — to envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for embarrassment, then the Italian for envy.

You'll want to know: invidiare.
invidiare — "to envy".
Ti invidio: vai in vacanza al mare! I envy you: you are going on holiday to the seaside!
Invidiare qualcosa a qualcuno: gli invidio la casa — I envy him his house.

The word, taken apart.
invidiare is built on invidia "envy", from Latin invidia (Lewis & Short), and so goes back to vidēre "to see".
