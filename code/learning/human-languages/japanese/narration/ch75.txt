You'll want to know: まいとし.
まいとし — maitoshi — "every year".

The word, taken apart.
まいとし (maitoshi) mixes the two layers: mai, "every", borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mwojX), and native toshi, "year", read as a kun'yomi; mainen, with the Sino-Japanese nen, means the same (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ことし (kotoshi) — this year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for half past, then the Japanese for every year.

You'll want to know: ことし.
ことし — kotoshi — "this year".

The word, taken apart.
ことし (kotoshi) is a native word, usually taken apart as ko, "this", and toshi, "year". JMdict writes it with characters for "now" and "year", read as a whole for their meaning. It is inherited rather than borrowed.

You'll want to know: こんしゅう.
こんしゅう — konshuu — "this week".

The word, taken apart.
こんしゅう (konshuu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kon, "now, this" (Middle Chinese kim) and shū, "week" (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おととし (ototoshi) — the year before last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for this year, then the Japanese for this week.

You'll want to know: おととし.
おととし — ototoshi — "the year before last".

The word, taken apart.
おととし (ototoshi) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed, built like ototoi with toshi, "year", in place of the day. JMdict writes it with characters for "one", "past" and "year", read as a whole; its older origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
あさねぼう (asanebou) — oversleeping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for this week, then the Japanese for the year before last.

You'll want to know: あさねぼう.
あさねぼう — asanebou — "oversleeping".

The word, taken apart.
あさねぼう (asanebou) mixes the two layers: native asa, "morning", and ne, "sleep", from neru, "to sleep", with bō, "monk's lodging; boy", the on'yomi of its last character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese bjang): a morning sleeper (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
