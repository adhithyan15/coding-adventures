Spanish, chapter 543: Más tarde de lo previsto.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
reanudar — to resume, to start again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the start, then the Spanish for to end.

You'll want to know: reanudar.
reanudar — "to resume, to start again".
Después de la lluvia reanudaron el partido. After the rain they resumed the match.
Reanudarse — to start up again: Se reanudó el servicio.

The word, taken apart.
reanudar is built on anudar 'to tie a knot' with re- 'again'. Anudar, older añudar (EtymDB), is built on nudo 'a knot', from Latin nōdus 'a knot' (Lewis & Short). To resume is to tie the thread again.

Lesson 2 of 6.
retrasar — to delay, to put back.

You'll want to know: retrasar.
retrasar — "to delay, to put back".
La nieve retrasó el vuelo. The snow delayed the flight.
Retrasarse is to be late: El tren se retrasó.

The word, taken apart.
retrasar is usually explained as built on tras 'behind', which comes from Latin trāns 'across, beyond' (Lewis & Short), with re- and the verb ending -ar.

Lesson 3 of 6.
aplazar — to postpone, to put off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to resume, then the Spanish for to delay.

You'll want to know: aplazar.
aplazar — "to postpone, to put off".
Aplazaron el partido por la lluvia. They postponed the match because of the rain.
It takes c before e: aplacé. Aplazar algo hasta el lunes — to put something off until Monday.

The word, taken apart.
aplazar is usually explained as built on plazo 'a time limit' with a- and -ar. Plazo comes from Latin placitum 'what has been agreed', from placēre 'to please' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
adelantar — to bring forward; to overtake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to delay, then the Spanish for to postpone.

You'll want to know: adelantar.
adelantar — "to bring forward; to overtake".
Adelantaron la reunión al lunes. They brought the meeting forward to Monday.
On the road it means to overtake. Adelantarse is to get ahead.

The word, taken apart.
adelantar is built on adelante 'forward' (EtymDB), which comes from delante, old Spanish denante, built on Latin dē, in and ante 'before'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
permanecer — to remain, to stay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to postpone, then the Spanish for to bring forward.

You'll want to know: permanecer.
permanecer — "to remain, to stay".
Permaneció en el hospital una semana. He remained in hospital for a week.
Formal; in speech quedarse. It takes a z: permanezco.

The word, taken apart.
permanecer comes from Latin permanēre 'to stay to the end, to last' (EtymDB), built on per and manēre 'to stay', with the Spanish ending -ecer.
