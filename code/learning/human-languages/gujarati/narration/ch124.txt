Gujarati, chapter 124: Butterfly, Squirrel, Hen, Cheek, Forehead.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પતંગિયું (patangiyũ) — a butterfly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a fly, then the Gujarati for a bee.

You'll want to know: પતંગિયું.
પતંગિયું (patangiyũ) — "a butterfly".

The word, taken apart.
પતંગિયું (patangiyũ) is built on Sanskrit pataṅga, "insect", a word used in the Veda for flying things (CDIAL 7721). CDIAL explains the Sanskrit word as a word for "wing" crossed with some non-Aryan word, and lists no Gujarati form there.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a squirrel, then the Gujarati for a hen.

You'll want to know: ગાલ.
ગાલ (gāl) — "a cheek".

The word, taken apart.
ગાલ (gāl) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit galla, "cheek" (CDIAL 4089). CDIAL compares gaṇḍa, "cheek", and reports a view that the word came from Dravidian.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a hen, then the Gujarati for a cheek.

You'll want to know: કપાળ.
કપાળ (kapāḷ) — "a forehead".

The word, taken apart.
કપાળ (kapāḷ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it under kappāla, a variant of Sanskrit kapāla, "skull, cranium" (CDIAL 2744). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word as Indo-European but thinks the variant forms suggest some Austroasiatic influence.
