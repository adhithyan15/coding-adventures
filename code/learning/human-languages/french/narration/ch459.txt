French, chapter 459: Les animaux des bois.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'écureuil — the squirrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reef, then the French for the seashell.

You'll want to know: l'écureuil.
l'écureuil — "the squirrel".
Un écureuil monte dans l'arbre avec une noisette. A squirrel runs up the tree with a hazelnut.
A masculine noun. The ending sounds like euil.

The word, taken apart.
écureuil comes from Middle French escureul (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin scūriolus, a small Latin sciūrus, from Greek skíouros 'shadow-tail'.

Les sangliers sortent du bois le soir. The wild boar come out of the wood in the evening.
La laie is the female.

The word, taken apart.
sanglier goes back to Latin singulāris 'solitary', as in singulāris porcus 'the pig that lives alone', from singulus 'single' (Lewis & Short).

Au lever du jour, nous avons vu un cerf dans la clairière. At daybreak we saw a stag in the clearing.
The f is silent: le cerf rhymes with serre.

cerf is inherited through Old French from Latin cervus 'deer' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la biche — the doe, the hind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the wild boar, then the French for the stag.

You'll want to know: la biche.
la biche — "the doe, the hind".
La biche et son petit boivent à l'étang. The doe and her fawn are drinking at the pond.
Affectionate: ma biche — my darling.

The word, taken apart.
biche comes from Middle French biche (EtymDB), which is usually traced through a northern dialect to Latin bestia 'animal'; the details are not settled.
