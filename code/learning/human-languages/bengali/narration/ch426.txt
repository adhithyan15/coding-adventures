Bengali, chapter 426: Cold, Frost and Thaw.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ঠান্ডা (ṭhānḍā) — cold; chilly; cold weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a lightning strike, then the Bengali for to spread over.

You'll want to know: ঠান্ডা.
ঠান্ডা — ṭhānḍā — "cold; chilly; cold weather".
ঠান্ডা জলে স্নান করে অরুণ কাঁপছিল। ṭhānḍā jôle snān kore ôrun kā̃pchhilô. After bathing in cold water Arun was shivering.
Also a cold you catch: ঠান্ডা (ṭhānḍā) লাগা. শীত is the cold season and the cold you feel; ঠান্ডা (ṭhānḍā) is cold things too.

The word, taken apart.
ঠান্ডা (ṭhānḍā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali ṭhāṇḍā, "cold", under Sanskrit stabdha, "stiffened, numb" (CDIAL 13676): cold is what makes you stiff.

Lesson 2 of 5.
তুষার (tushār) — snow; frost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to spread over, then the Bengali for cold (to the touch).

You'll want to know: তুষার.
তুষার — tushār — "snow; frost".
শীতে উঁচু পাহাড়ে তুষার পড়ে। shīte ũchu pāhāṛe tushār pôṛe. In winter snow falls on the high mountains.
বরফ is ice, and snow in everyday talk; তুষার (tushār) is the careful word for snow and frost.

The word, taken apart.
তুষার (tushār) is Sanskrit tuṣāra, "cold, frigid", and as a noun "frost, snow" (Monier-Williams), taken over whole.

Lesson 3 of 5.
জমে যাওয়া (jôme jāoyā) — to freeze; to set hard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for cold (to the touch), then the Bengali for snow.

You'll want to know: জমে যাওয়া.
জমে যাওয়া — jôme jāoyā — "to freeze; to set hard".
পাহাড়ে রাতে কলের জল জমে গেল। pāhāṛe rāte kôler jôl jôme gelô. In the hills the water in the tap froze overnight.
Past (he/she …): সেই জল জমে গেল, sei jôl jôme gelô.
Also of a crowd or a party: আড্ডা জমে গেল, the chat got going.

The word, taken apart.
জমে যাওয়া (jôme jāoyā) joins the -এ form of জমা, which CDIAL lists as Bengali jamā, "to collect, to coagulate, to freeze", under Sanskrit yamyátē (CDIAL 10428), to যাওয়া, to go: to go hard.

Lesson 4 of 5.
কনকনে (kônkône) — bitterly cold; biting (of cold).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for snow, then the Bengali for to freeze.

You'll want to know: কনকনে.
কনকনে — kônkône — "bitterly cold; biting (of cold)".
কনকনে ঠান্ডায় আমার হাত জমে গেল। kônkône ṭhānḍāy āmār hāt jôme gelô. In the biting cold my hands went numb.
Only of cold: কনকনে (kônkône) ঠান্ডা (ṭhānḍā), কনকনে (kônkône) হাওয়া.

The word, taken apart.
কনকনে (kônkône) is an expressive word whose sound carries the sting of the cold; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
গলে যাওয়া (gôle jāoyā) — to melt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to freeze, then the Bengali for bitterly cold.

You'll want to know: গলে যাওয়া.
গলে যাওয়া — gôle jāoyā — "to melt".
রোদে বরফ গলে গেল। rode bôrôph gôle gelô. The ice melted in the sun.
Past (he/she …): সেই বরফ গলে গেল, sei bôrôph gôle gelô.
Not গলা, the throat: the same letters, a different word.

The word, taken apart.
গলে যাওয়া (gôle jāoyā) joins the -এ form of গলা, which CDIAL lists as Bengali galā, "to melt, to escape through a hole", under Sanskrit galati, "drips, melts" (CDIAL 4074), to যাওয়া, to go.
