German, chapter 317: Weitererzählen.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
die Erzählung — the narrative, the short story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the chapter, then the German for the sequel.

You'll want to know: die Erzählung.
die Erzählung — "the narrative, the short story".
Die Erzählung spielt in einem kleinen Dorf. The narrative is set in a small village.
Plural: die Erzählungen. As a book, a short story.

The word, taken apart.
Erzählung is built on erzählen "to tell", from zählen "to count", Old High German zellen (EtymDB), with the ending -ung.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

nacherzählen — "to retell".
Die Schüler erzählten die Geschichte nach. The pupils retold the story.
Separable: sie erzählt nach, sie hat nacherzählt.

The word, taken apart.
nacherzählen joins nach "after" and erzählen "to tell", built on zählen "to count", Proto-Germanic taljaną (EtymDB): to tell after someone.

Lesson 3 of 6.
vorlesen — to read aloud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the narrative, then the German for to retell.

You'll want to know: vorlesen.
vorlesen — "to read aloud".
Der Vater las den Kindern eine Geschichte vor. The father read the children a story.
Separable, strong: liest vor, las vor, hat vorgelesen.

The word, taken apart.
vorlesen joins vor "before, in front of" and lesen "to read", from Old High German lesan, Proto-Germanic lesaną "to gather" (EtymDB): first gathering, then reading.

Lesson 4 of 6.
überliefern — to hand down, to pass on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to retell, then the German for to read aloud.

You'll want to know: überliefern.
überliefern — "to hand down, to pass on".
Die Lieder wurden von den Großeltern überliefert. The songs were handed down by the grandparents.
Often passive: Es ist überliefert — it has come down to us.

The word, taken apart.
überliefern is built on liefern "to deliver", Middle High German lieveren (EtymDB), which is usually traced through French livrer to Latin līberāre "to set free".

Lesson 5 of 6.

You'll want to know: das Schicksal.
das Schicksal — "the fate, destiny".
Sie konnte ihr Schicksal nicht ändern. She could not change her fate.
Plural: die Schicksale.

The word, taken apart.
Schicksal is built on schicken, which in Middle High German meant "to arrange, to order" (EtymDB), with the ending -sal: what has been arranged for you.
