Lesson 1 of 5.
नकुलः (nakulaḥ) — a mongoose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a cuckoo, then the Sanskrit for a sparrow.

You'll want to know: नकुलः.
नकुलः — nakulaḥ — "a mongoose".

The word, taken apart.
nakula "a mongoose" is old, in the Atharvaveda. Pāṇini's grammar splits it as na + kula, but MW thinks that probably wrong, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi neul and naulā "mongoose" (CDIAL 6908).

The word, taken apart.
hrasva "short, small" is inherited from Indo-Iranian (EtymDB), and MW sets it beside the root √hras "become short, lessen". In grammar it also names a short vowel (MW).

The word, taken apart.
mṛdu "soft, tender, mild" is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB): English mild and Latin mollis "soft" are its cousins. CDIAL 10292 sets it under the root √mṛd "press, crush".

Lesson 4 of 5.
शुद्धः (śuddhaḥ) — clean, pure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for short, then the Sanskrit for soft.

You'll want to know: शुद्धः.
शुद्धः — śuddhaḥ — "clean, pure".

The word, taken apart.
śuddha is the past participle of the root √śudh "purify, become pure" (MW; CDIAL 12520): "cleansed", so "clean, pure". It lives on as Hindi sūdhā "clean, pure, true" (CDIAL 12520).

Lesson 5 of 5.
मलिनः (malinaḥ) — dirty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for soft, then the Sanskrit for clean.

You'll want to know: मलिनः.
मलिनः — malinaḥ — "dirty".

The word, taken apart.
malina "dirty, dark" is built on mala "dirt, filth" (MW), formed like the colour words in -na (CDIAL 9905). MW compares mala with Greek melas "black", as in melancholy; Marathi kept it as maḷīṇ "dirty" (CDIAL 9905).
