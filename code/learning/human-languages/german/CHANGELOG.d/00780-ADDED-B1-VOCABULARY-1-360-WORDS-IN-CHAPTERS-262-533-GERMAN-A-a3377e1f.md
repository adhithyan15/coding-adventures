## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,360 words in chapters 262–533; German attains B1

- **272 new chapters (262–533) teach 1,360 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 485 of them are verbs. Each lesson gives:
  - the word with its article, and its English;
  - a short example with its translation; a separable verb is shown split, and a reflexive verb with *sich*;
  - a usage note: the plural where it is useful, the *sein* auxiliary, word order for the connectors, and in 35 lessons the Austrian or Swiss form;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 262–329, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (340 words): time and sequence (*zunächst*, *nachdem*, *sobald*, *inzwischen*), news and reports, crime and the courts, accidents and emergencies, life events, celebrations and customs, stories and history, and cooking step by step.
  - 330–397, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (340 words): opinion and argument, cause and consequence, work, society, conditions and plans, study and exams, rules and the law, money, and technology. The connectors (*obwohl*, *sodass*, *damit*, *sofern*, *es sei denn*, *deswegen*, *dennoch*, *zwar … aber* and others) each say where the verb goes.
  - 398–465, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (340 words): the road and the car, stations, airports and ferries, lodging and complaints, finding the way, the town, shops and services, nature, and weather.
  - 466–533, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (340 words): feelings and character, health and the body, family and relationships, hobbies, sport, music and the arts, reading and the screen, and life at home.
- **German now teaches 2,581 headwords at or below B1**, past the 2,500 the level gate asks for, and clears the 250-verb target. With the B1 core (chapters 252–261), German attains B1.
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1 (German entries with their Middle High German, Old High German and Proto-Germanic parents) and Lewis & Short. Where neither supports a step, the lesson hedges it or says the origin is not settled, and inherited words are told apart from borrowings. Where an EtymDB entry is plainly wrong (*Bremse* "brake" filed under "horsefly", *Raureif* under "ring"), the lesson says so. An independent review of each tranche fixed errors in 149 of the 1,360 words (11.0%), mostly origin claims the sources do not support.
- **Proto-Germanic nasal *ō*** is written *-ōn* (*gatwōn*, *sīdōn*), the older notation, because the book font has no *ǭ*.
- **No forward references.** Every candidate headword was screened against the text of every earlier lesson before drafting, and German headwords spelled like English words were avoided, since the matcher reads English text too. German's forward-reference count stays at its pin of 1.
