# الحمد لله (al-ḥamdu lillāh) — "praise be to God"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Ask an Arabic speaker how they are and this, more often than *bi-khayr*,
is what comes back. It also hides the best root story in the chapter.

## The letters

*(Skim if you read Arabic.)* **ا ل ح م د** and **ل ل ه** — **every letter is one
you have written**: *alif*, *lām*, *ḥāʾ*, *mīm*, *dāl*, *hāʾ*. Nothing new to
draw.

## The pieces

| piece | meaning | where from |
|---|---|---|
| **الـ** *al-* | "the" | Chapter 1 |
| **حمد** *ḥamd* | praise | **new**, root **ḥ–m–d** |
| **لـ** *li-* | "to, for" | a one-letter preposition, like *bi-* |
| **الله** *allāh* | God | *al-* + *ilāh*, "**the** god" |

→ **الحمد لله** = "**the praise** [is] **to God**." Zero copula once more.

Note **الله** itself: it is *al-* (the article you learned in Chapter 1) fused
onto *ilāh*, "a god." The word is literally "**the God**." Its Semitic cousins
are Hebrew ***El*** and ***Elohim*** — the same family pairing as *salām* /
*shalom* from Chapter 1.

## The root ḥ–m–d, and a name you know

**ḥ–m–d** means "**to praise**." Run it through Arabic's patterns:

| word | pattern meaning |
|---|---|
| **ḥamd** | praise (the plain noun) |
| **maḥmūd** | praised |
| **ḥamīd** | praiseworthy |
| **aḥmad** | "more praiseworthy" — the name **Ahmad** |
| **muḥammad** | "the **much-praised** one" — the name **Muhammad** |

So the most frequently used phrase in the language and the most common given name
on earth are built from **the same three consonants**. This is the root engine
doing exactly what Chapter 1 promised: three letters, and a whole family of
meanings drops out.

## Using it

- — **كيف حالك؟** *kayfa ḥāluka?*
- — **الحمد لله.** *al-ḥamdu lillāh.*

It works as "fine, thanks" — said whether things are going well or badly, which
is part of its point.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] What does **الحمد لله** literally say? ("**The praise [is] to God**.")
What root is *ḥamd* from? (**ḥ–m–d**, "to praise".) Which two names come out of
that root? (**Aḥmad** and **Muḥammad** — "the much-praised.") What is *allāh* made
of? (***al-*** + ***ilāh*** = "**the god**"; cousin of Hebrew *El*.) Next:
practice the whole exchange.
