You'll want to know: 小时.
小时 — xiǎoshí — "hour".
一个小时 yí ge xiǎoshí — an hour.
The next lesson puts the second character in your hand. It is the shí of shíhou and xiǎoshíhou.

The word, taken apart.
小时 (xiǎoshí) is built from 小 'small' and 时 (shí) 'time'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX and dzyi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), dzyi glossed 'a season; a time'.

What you've built.
一 before 个 rises: yí ge xiǎoshí, an hour. Before the other tones it falls: yì tiān, a day; yì nián, a year. Time words count with 一 as often as anything does.

Script — 日, then the right half of 对.
时 (shí).
Two pieces you own, side by side.
日, four strokes, narrow, on the left: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the middle bar, the bottom bar.
The right half of 对, three strokes: the bar, the vertical that hooks to the left at its foot, then the dot.
4 + 3 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 时 (shí), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 时 (shí)? (Seven.) Which character is on the left? (日.) Where have you written the right half? (At the end of 对.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
měitiān — every day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read the last lesson's character, then a day with 一 in front of it.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 两个小时, aloud — liǎng ge xiǎoshí, two hours]
[your turn — recall: say 一天 — yì tiān, a day, with 一 falling]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: měitiān.
měitiān — "every day".
wǒ měitiān hē chá — I drink tea every day.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
měitiān is built from měi 'each' and tiān 'day', the 天 you write. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, mwojX and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the Guangyun glosses mwojX 'frequently', and 'each' is assumed from that, not quoted.

What you've built.
A time word goes early, after the person and before the verb: 我, měitiān, hē chá. yì tiān is one day; měitiān is every day.

You'll want to know: yǐhòu.
yǐhòu — "after; afterwards".
xiàkè yǐhòu, wǒ qù chī fàn — After class I go and eat.
yí ge xiǎoshí yǐhòu — an hour later.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǐhòu is built from yǐ, the yǐ of kěyǐ, and hòu 'after', the hòu you know. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, yiX and huwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), huwX glossed 'after, in order'.

What you've built.
yǐhòu comes after the thing it follows: xiàkè yǐhòu, after class; yí ge xiǎoshí yǐhòu, an hour later.

Lesson 6 of 6.
My own line — the first Chinese with no model.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Everything you have written so far had something to reach for: a model to trace, a word somebody said, a sentence printed above the space.
[pause 1 second]
This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed: what this book has given you.
[your turn — recall: say 两个小时 — liǎng ge xiǎoshí, two hours]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Writing — controlled composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write one line of your own about your day. Three parts, in your own words: where you are, with 在; what you want, with 想要 or 要; and for how long, with 一个小时 or 两个小时. If you want a second thing, 也 brings it in, in front of its verb.
[pause 2 seconds]
Some words you will want have characters you have not written yet: měitiān, every day; yǐhòu, after. Write those in pinyin, inside the line, with their tone marks. This task allows that, and it allows nothing else: a character you can read but have not learned to write is not one you produce, so it goes in pinyin too.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three checks belong to the hand. Write each character in the stroke order you learned: 时 (shí) is 日 first, narrow, then the hooked vertical and the dot. Give every character the same size of square, two-stroke 了 as much as ten-stroke 都. And leave a space between clauses: no Chinese comma or full stop, as this book prints them.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the line. Then read it back, and check that every pinyin word has its tone marks before you check anything else.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which words did you write in pinyin? (Any whose characters you have not written: měitiān, yǐhòu.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which piece of 时 (shí) comes first? (日, narrow, on the left.)
[pause 2 seconds]
And what did you choose here that the earlier lessons chose for you? (The words.)
