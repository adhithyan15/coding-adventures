Persian, chapter 136: Still, A Question Word, At All, Surely Not, When.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
هنوز (hanuz) — still; (with a negative) not yet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to stick, then the Persian for to spin.

You'll want to know: هنوز.
هنوز — hanuz — "still; (with a negative) not yet". Persian negates a verb with ن (na-, ne-): من هنوز آماده نیستم — man hanuz âmâde nistam — "I'm not ready yet." نیستم is the negative of هستم.

The word, taken apart.
hanuz is a native Persian word, inherited rather than borrowed; it is usually traced to Middle Persian hanūz, "still, yet".

Lesson 2 of 5.
آیا (âyâ) — a word that opens a yes-or-no question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to spin, then the Persian for still.

You'll want to know: آیا.
آیا — âyâ — "a word that opens a yes-or-no question". It turns a statement into a question: آیا او اینجاست — âyâ u injâst — "Is he here?" In speech the rising voice alone often does the same.

The word, taken apart.
âyâ is a native Persian question word, but no source used here gives its older form, and its origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
اصلا (aslan) — at all; (with a negative) not at all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for still, then the Persian for a word that opens a yes-or-no question.

You'll want to know: اصلا.
اصلا — aslan — "at all; (with a negative) not at all". اصلا خسته نیستم — aslan khaste nistam — "I'm not tired at all."

The word, taken apart.
aslan is borrowed from Arabic aṣlan, "originally, at all". Its Arabic root is ʾ-ṣ-l (Lane). It is an adverb made from aṣl, "root, origin".

Lesson 4 of 5.
مگر (magar) — surely... not?; unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a word that opens a yes-or-no question, then the Persian for at all.

You'll want to know: مگر.
مگر — magar — "surely... not?; unless". It asks for a "yes": مگر خسته نیستی — magar khaste nisti — "Aren't you tired?"

The word, taken apart.
magar is a native Persian word, inherited rather than borrowed: it continues Middle Persian magar, "perhaps; lest" (EtymDB). EtymDB analyses it as ma, "not", with agar, "if".

Lesson 5 of 5.
چه وقت (che vaqt) — when, at what time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for at all, then the Persian for surely... not?.

You'll want to know: چه وقت.
چه وقت — che vaqt — "when, at what time". چه وقت آمدی — che vaqt âmadi — "When did you come?"

The word, taken apart.
che vaqt is "what time". che, "what", is native, from Middle Persian čē (EtymDB); vaqt, "time", is borrowed from Arabic waqt (root w-q-t, Lane).
