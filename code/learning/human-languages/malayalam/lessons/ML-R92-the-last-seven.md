# ചേച്ചി · ഹോട്ടൽ · ബിൽ — the last seven, run cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C82-ADDRESS-02, ML-LEX-C92-BILL-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The word you call an older woman you do not know. Then the word you
ask for at the end of a meal.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C92-FALSE-FRIEND-01, ML-LEX-C92-BILL-01, ML-LEX-C91-BUY-01] -->

**ഹോട്ടൽ** is the trap in this chapter, and it is worth a second look precisely
because it looks like a word you already know. In Malayalam it is an **eating
place**. Walking into one expecting a bed is the kind of mistake a borrowed word
sets up for you, and no amount of English helps.

**ബിൽ** is borrowed too and means what you expect — so the pair together is the
lesson: a loanword may keep its sense or take a new one, and the shape tells you
nothing about which.

The address word is not a title you attach to a name — it stands on its own as
the way you call to someone, and picking the wrong one is heard immediately.
