Lesson 1 of 5.
дождь (dozhd) — rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a star, then the Russian for a cloud.

You'll want to know: дождь.
дождь (dozhd) — "rain".

The word, taken apart.
дождь (dozhd) from Proto-Slavic dŭždžĭ — inherited. Vasmer thinks it probably goes with Norwegian dusk-regn and Swedish regn-dusk, "drizzle"; he also reports a view that starts from dus-dius, "bad weather", so the origin is debated.

You'll want to know: снег.
снег (sneg) — "snow".

The word, taken apart.
снег (sneg) from Proto-Slavic sněgŭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian sniẽgas, Gothic snaiws, Greek nípha and Latin nix, all "snow": English snow is a cousin, from PIE sneygʷʰ-, "to snow".

You'll want to know: ветер.
ветер (véter) — "the wind".

The word, taken apart.
ветер (véter) from Proto-Slavic větrŭ — inherited, from the root of веять, "to blow" (Vasmer). Vasmer lists Lithuanian vėjas, "wind", and Sanskrit vāta-, "wind"; English wind is probably a cousin, from the same PIE root h₂weh₁-, "to blow".

Lesson 4 of 5.
погода (pogóda) — the weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for snow, then the Russian for the wind.

You'll want to know: погода.
погода (pogóda) — "the weather".

The word, taken apart.
погода (pogóda) is по- with год, which in Old Russian meant "time, a fitting moment" (Vasmer); the same root is in годиться, "to be fit". Weather was first "fair, fitting time", as in непогода, "bad weather".

Lesson 5 of 5.
мир (mir) — the world; peace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the wind, then the Russian for the weather.

You'll want to know: мир.
мир (mir) — "the world; peace". Both meanings in one word.

The word, taken apart.
мир (mir) from Proto-Slavic mirŭ, "peace" — inherited, related to Old Lithuanian mieras, "peace", and Latvian miêrs (Vasmer). The sense "village community" grew from "peaceful community", and from it "the world".
