Latin, chapter 402: Learning by Heart.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ēdiscō, ēdiscere — to learn by heart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an elementary teacher of letters, then the Latin for a schoolfellow.

You'll want to know: ēdiscō, ēdiscere.
ēdiscō, ēdiscere — "to learn by heart".
Puerī carmina poētae ēdiscunt. The boys learn the poet's poems by heart.
ēdiscō, ēdiscere, ēdidicī: ē + discō, to learn out to the end.

The word, taken apart.
ēdiscō is built from ē "out, to the end" and discō "to learn" (Lewis & Short): to learn right through.

Lesson 3 of 5.
studeō, studēre — to be eager (for); to study.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to learn by heart, then the Latin for to learn thoroughly.

You'll want to know: studeō, studēre.
studeō, studēre — "to be eager (for); to study".
Litterīs studet. He is devoted to literature.
studeō, studēre, studuī + dative: litterīs studēre, to give oneself to literature. Studium is its noun. English student and study.

The word, taken apart.
studeō is usually traced to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to push, to strike" (EtymDB): to press on towards a thing. English student and study come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
meditor, meditārī — to think over; to rehearse, to practise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to learn thoroughly, then the Latin for to be eager.

You'll want to know: meditor, meditārī.
meditor, meditārī — "to think over; to rehearse, to practise".
Ōrātiōnem tōtam noctem meditābātur. He rehearsed his speech all night.
Deponent: meditor, meditārī, meditātus sum. To think over, and to practise, like a speaker rehearsing. English meditate.

The word, taken apart.
meditor is usually traced to the same root as medeor "to heal" (Lewis & Short compare medeor and remedium), a root meaning "to take fitting measures". English meditate comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
recordor, recordārī — to call to mind, to remember.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be eager, then the Latin for to think over.

You'll want to know: recordor, recordārī.
recordor, recordārī — "to call to mind, to remember".
Diem illum saepe recordor. I often recall that day.
Deponent: recordor, recordārī, recordātus sum, built on cor, heart: to bring back to the heart. English record comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
recordor is built from re- "back" and cor, cordis "heart" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the seat of memory for the Romans. English record comes from it through French.
