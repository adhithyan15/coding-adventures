Hindi, chapter 305: The Water Rose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
उफनना (uphannā) — to swell up; to boil over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for torrential (of rain), then the Hindi for destruction.

You'll want to know: उफनना.
उफनना — uphannā — "to swell up; to boil over".
भारी बारिश के बाद नदी उफन गई। bhārī bāriś ke bād nadī uphan gaī. After the heavy rain the river swelled over its banks.
Past (it …): वह नदी उफनी, vah nadī uphnī.

The word, taken apart.
उफनना (uphannā) is inherited from utphaṇati, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 1836). Milk on the fire उफनता है, and so does a river in flood.

Masculine.

Lesson 3 of 5.
क्रमशः (kramśaḥ) — little by little; by degrees.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to swell up, then the Hindi for a dam.

You'll want to know: क्रमशः.
क्रमशः — kramśaḥ — "little by little; by degrees".
नदी का पानी क्रमशः ऊपर आ रहा था। nadī kā pānī kramśaḥ ūpar ā rahā thā. The river water was rising little by little.
Formal and written; speech says धीरे-धीरे.

The word, taken apart.
क्रमशः (kramśaḥ) is Sanskrit kramaśas, "step by step, by degrees" (Monier-Williams), from krama, a step. It ends in the sign ः. At the end of a newspaper story printed in parts it means "to be continued".

Lesson 4 of 5.
सैलाब (sailāb) — a deluge; a flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a dam, then the Hindi for little by little.

You'll want to know: सैलाब.
सैलाब — sailāb — "a deluge; a flood".
इस साल का सैलाब पिछले साल से ज़्यादा बड़ा था। is sāl kā sailāb pichle sāl se zyādā baṛā thā. This year's flood was bigger than last year's.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
सैलाब (sailāb) is usually traced to Persian sailāb, "a flood", which joins Arabic sayl, a torrent, and Persian āb, water. Beside बाढ़, it is the word for a great rush of water.

भँवर में एक नाव डूब गई। bhãvar meṁ ek nāv ḍūb gaī. A boat went down in the whirlpool.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
is listed by CDIAL under Sanskrit bhramara, "moving unsteadily, revolving" (CDIAL 9650), beside bhramaraka, a spinning top or a whirlpool, of the family of bhram, to whirl about. Water that turns round and round is a भँवर (bhãvar).
