# ю — a syllable in one letter, again

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-YA-01, RU-SCRIPT-U-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write я, then у. One is a whole syllable; the other is the *oo* that
looks like a Latin y.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-SCRIPT-YU-01]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-YA-01] -->

> ю

A **new shape**, but a familiar idea. Two parts, joined: a **vertical stroke**
on the left, and a **closed loop** to its right, tied to the vertical by a short
horizontal at mid-height.

It says **yu** — the *you* of English, or the *u* of *use*. One letter, two
sounds, exactly the deal you already accepted with **я** (*ya*).

That is the pattern worth taking away: Russian has a small set of letters that
each write a **y-glide plus a vowel** in a single shape. You have met two of
them now. They are the reason a Russian word can look shorter than its
pronunciation, and the reason counting letters is a poor guide to counting
syllables.

Do not confuse its sound with **у**, the *oo* letter that looks like a Latin
y. ю is that
sound with a *y* welded to its front.
