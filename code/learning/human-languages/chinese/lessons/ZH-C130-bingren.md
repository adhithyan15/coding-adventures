## You'll want to know: 病人
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C130-ILL130-01]; assesses=[] -->

**病人** — *bìngrén* — "patient; sick person".

> **病人想喝水** *bìngrén xiǎng hē shuǐ* — The patient would like some water.

The new character, in pieces: 病 — outside, the sickbed piece of five strokes: a dot, a bar, a long fall down the left, then a dot and a rising stroke on the left side; inside it, five strokes: a bar, a box with a hooked right side, and a small 人 in the box. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**病人** (*bìngrén*) is built from 病 'illness' and 人 'person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *bjaengH* and *nyin* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'grief' and 'suffering', and quotes an older book: an illness grown worse.
