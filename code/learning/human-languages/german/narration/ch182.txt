Lesson 1 of 7.
kämpfen — to fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to greet, then the German for to inform.

You'll want to know: kämpfen.
kämpfen — "to fight".

The word, taken apart.
kämpfen from Old High German kempfan, the verb of the word for "fight", Old High German kampf from Latin campus, "field, the field of battle" (EtymDB 2.1) — an early loan.

You'll want to know: klettern.

The word, taken apart.
klettern is of uncertain origin. One account ties it to the word for "a burr", Old High German kletta (EtymDB 2.1), as if climbing were clinging; EtymDB has no source for klettern itself.

You'll want to know: kümmern.
kümmern — "to take care (sich kümmern um)".

The word, taken apart.
kümmern from Middle High German kumbern, "to burden, to trouble" (EtymDB 2.1), from the word for "grief, burden", which EtymDB lists beside English encumbrance. To take care is to take the burden on.

Lesson 5 of 7.
lächeln — to smile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to take care, then the German for to land.

You'll want to know: lächeln.
lächeln — "to smile".

The word, taken apart.
lächeln is lachen, "to laugh", with the ending -eln, which makes an action small: a little laugh. lachen from Proto-Germanic hlahjaną, English laugh. (EtymDB 2.1).
