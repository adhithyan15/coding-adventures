## You'll want to know: সে
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C23-SHE-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C03-AMI-01, BN-CONCEPT-C03-BHALO-01] -->

> **সে ভালো** — *she bhālo* — **he is well**, or **she is well.**

Thirty-three chapters, and every sentence this book can build has been about
**আমি** or **তুমি**. Somebody is either speaking or being spoken to. Nobody has
ever been **talked about**.

**সে** is the word that changes that, and it does two things at once that
English needs two words for. It is *he* and it is *she*, and Bengali will not
tell you which. Sex is not marked on a Bengali pronoun at all — not here, not
anywhere.

## Grammar Lens: the third row of a table you already half know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C23-SHE-01, BN-CONCEPT-C02-TUMIAPNI-01, BN-LEX-C21-EI-01] -->

| person | plain | careful |
|---|---|---|
| I | **আমি** | — |
| you | **তুমি** | **আপনি** |
| he, she | **সে** | *next lesson* |

Chapter two taught you that Bengali grades **you** for respect. It grades the
third person the same way, on the same ladder, and the careful form arrives in
the next lesson. What the table shows is that **সে** is the *plain* one: it is
what you use of a child, a friend, a younger sibling — and what you must not use
of somebody's grandmother.
