Telugu, chapter 483: Water, Fire and Light.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఆవిరవు (āviravu) — to evaporate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a breeze, then the Telugu for to cool down.

You'll want to know: ఆవిరవు.
ఆవిరవు — āviravu — "to evaporate".
ఎండకు నేల మీద నీరు ఆవిరైంది. eṇḍaku nēla mīda nīru āviraindi. In the sun the water on the ground evaporated.
Past (it …): ఆవిరయింది, āvirayindi.
ఆవిరి, steam, + అవు, to become. A hope that vanishes also ఆవిరవుతుంది. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: ఆవిరయింది, it evaporated.

The word, taken apart.
ఆవిరవు (āviravu) is native Dravidian: ఆవిరి, "vapour, steam" (DEDR 393), with అవు, "to become".

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to cool down, then the Telugu for to evaporate.

You'll want to know: ఇంకిపోవు.
ఇంకిపోవు — iṅkipōvu — "to soak in; to sink in".
వర్షం నీరు నేలలో ఇంకిపోయింది. varṣaṁ nīru nēlalō iṅkipōyindi. The rainwater soaked into the ground.
Past (it …): ఇంకిపోయింది, iṅkipōyindi.
Said of water going into the earth, and of a pond drying up. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: ఇంకిపోయింది, it soaked in.

The word, taken apart.
ఇంకిపోవు (iṅkipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 430 lists Telugu iṅku, "to dry up, to sink in", here with పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

You'll want to know: మండు.
మండు — maṇḍu — "to blaze; to burn".
మధ్యాహ్నం ఎండ మండుతోంది. madhyāhnaṁ eṇḍa maṇḍutōndi. At midday the sun is blazing.
Past (it …): మండింది, maṇḍindi.
Said of fire, of strong sun and of a burning feeling: కళ్ళు మండుతున్నాయి, my eyes are burning. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: మండింది, it blazed.

The word, taken apart.
మండు (maṇḍu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 4680 lists Telugu maṇḍu, "to burn, to blaze, to flame", with Tamil maṇṭu, "to blaze up".

You'll want to know: ఆరిపోవు.
ఆరిపోవు — āripōvu — "to go out; to die down".
గాలికి దీపం ఆరిపోయింది. gāliki dīpaṁ āripōyindi. The lamp went out in the wind.
Past (it …): ఆరిపోయింది, āripōyindi.
ఆరు, to cool, to go out, + పోవు. A fire, a lamp or wet clothes drying: బట్టలు ఆరిపోయాయి. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: ఆరిపోయింది, it went out.

The word, taken apart.
ఆరిపోవు (āripōvu) is native Dravidian: ఆరు, "to cool, to be put out, to dry" (DEDR 404), with పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 5 of 5.
చిగురించు (ciguriñcu) — to put forth leaves; to sprout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to blaze, then the Telugu for to go out.

You'll want to know: చిగురించు.
చిగురించు — ciguriñcu — "to put forth leaves; to sprout".
వర్షాలు పడగానే చెట్లు చిగురించాయి. varṣālu paḍagānē ceṭlu ciguriñcāyi. As soon as the rains came the trees put out new leaves.
Past (it …): చిగురించింది, ciguriñcindi.
చిగురు, a tender shoot, + -ఇంచు. Hope also చిగురిస్తుంది. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: చిగురించింది, it put out leaves.

The word, taken apart.
చిగురించు (ciguriñcu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2489 lists Telugu iguru, "a bud, a sprout", and igirincu, "to bud"; చిగురు is its everyday form.
