# cāh kī tālikā — wanting, on one page

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01, HI-LEX-CHAHNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the two forms of the wanting verb, and say what each one wants
after it.

## Grammar Lens: one root, two forms
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-CHAHNA-01, HI-LEX-C95-NEED-01] -->

| form | what follows | example |
|---|---|---|
| चाहता / चाहती | a **verb** | मैं जाना चाहता हूँ |
| चाहिए | a **thing** | मुझे चाय चाहिए |

**Ask what comes next, not what it means.** Both translate into English as
*want*, so the meaning will not tell the two apart — the shape of what follows
will, every time.

## Grammar Lens: who sits where
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-NEED-02, HI-CONCEPT-C35-PASAND-01] -->

| sentence | the person is |
|---|---|
| मैं जाना चाहता हूँ | **मैं** — the subject |
| मुझे चाय चाहिए | **मुझे** — the dative |
| मुझे चाय पसंद है | **मुझे** — the dative |

Two of the three put the person on the receiving end, and the one that does not
is the one where the person is genuinely doing something — going. **Wanting a
thing and liking a thing happen to you; wanting to act is something you do**,
and Hindi draws that line with the case of the first word.

## Grammar Lens: the verb that was only ever denied
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C95-KNOW-01, HI-LEX-C95-KNOW-02] -->

| what you can now say | what you had before |
|---|---|
| मैं जानता हूँ | only मैं नहीं जानता |
| क्या आप जानते हैं? | nothing |

The affirmative needed no new grammar at all — it needed somebody to introduce
the word, which the negative lesson used and never taught.
