Russian, chapter 472: Irritation and Fury.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
раздражать (razdrazhát) — to irritate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for touchy, then the Russian for to forgive.

You'll want to know: раздражать.
раздражать (razdrazhát) — "to irritate".
Меня раздражает шум за стеной. Menyá razdrazháyet shum za stenóy. The noise behind the wall irritates me.
Imperfective; perfective раздражить. The person who is irritated goes in the accusative: меня раздражает.

The word, taken apart.
раздражать (razdrazhát) is built on раз- and a stem usually linked to the one in дразнить 'to tease'; EtymDB lists the verb, glossed 'to irritate', without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
раздражение (razdrazhéniye) — irritation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to forgive, then the Russian for to irritate.

You'll want to know: раздражение.
раздражение (razdrazhéniye) — "irritation".
Он ответил с раздражением. On otvétil s razdrazhéniyem. He answered irritably.
Neuter. С раздражением — irritably.

The word, taken apart.
раздражение (razdrazhéniye) is built on раздражать (razdrazhát) 'to irritate' with -ение; the verb's stem is usually linked to дразнить 'to tease', and EtymDB has no entry for the noun.

Lesson 3 of 5.
злиться (zlítsya) — to be angry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to irritate, then the Russian for irritation.

You'll want to know: злиться.
злиться (zlítsya) — "to be angry".
Папа злится, когда мы опаздываем. Pápa zlítsya, kogdá my opázdyvayem. Dad gets angry when we're late.
Imperfective; perfective разозлиться. Злиться на кого — to be angry with someone. A little more colloquial than сердиться.

The word, taken apart.
злиться (zlítsya) is built on злой 'angry, wicked', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic zъlъ 'bad, evil' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ярость (yárost) — fury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for irritation, then the Russian for to be angry.

You'll want to know: ярость.
ярость (yárost) — "fury".
В ярости он бросил телефон на пол. V yárosti on brósil telefón ná pol. In a rage he threw his phone on the floor.
Feminine. The adjective is яростный — furious.

The word, taken apart.
ярость (yárost) is inherited from Proto-Slavic jarostь 'fury', derived from jarъ 'furious' (EtymDB), the root also behind яркий 'bright'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
вспыльчивый (vspýlchivyy) — quick-tempered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to be angry, then the Russian for fury.

You'll want to know: вспыльчивый.
вспыльчивый (vspýlchivyy) — "quick-tempered".
Мой дядя добрый, но вспыльчивый. Moy dyádya dóbryy, no vspýlchivyy. My uncle is kind but quick-tempered.
Built on вспылить — to flare up.

The word, taken apart.
вспыльчивый (vspýlchivyy) is built on вспылить 'to flare up', from пыл 'heat, ardour'; EtymDB has no entry, and пыл is usually traced to the root of пылать 'to blaze'.
