## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C46-NIGHT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**lūx** — "**light**."

Proto-Indo-European ***\*lewk-*** is one of the best-attested roots there is, and
it reaches English from three directions at once.

**Inherited:** English **light** is the Germanic descendant. Same ancestor, no
borrowing.

**Borrowed from Latin:** something **lucid** is clear as light; something
**translucent** lets light through; to **elucidate** is to shed light on. And
**Lucifer** is *lūx* plus *ferre* — the **light-bringer**, which was the Romans'
name for the morning star long before it was anything else.

**Borrowed from Greek:** the same root gave Greek *leukos*, "white," which
English carries in **leukemia**, named for white blood cells.

Three roads, one root — and the everyday word is the inherited one, which is the
usual pattern in English rather than an accident here.
