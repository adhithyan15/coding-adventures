Gujarati, chapter 406: At the Border.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વિઝા (vijhā) — a visa.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a suitcase, then the Gujarati for a circle in the air.

You'll want to know: વિઝા.
વિઝા — vijhā — "a visa".
પાસપોર્ટ સાથે વિઝા પણ જોઈએ. pāsporṭ sāthe vijhā paṇ joīe. As well as a passport you need a visa.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વિઝા (vijhā) is borrowed from English visa, the stamp or paper that lets you into another country.

Lesson 2 of 5.

આ ગામ સીમાની નજીક છે. ā gām sīmānī najīk che. This village is near the border.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સીમા (sīmā) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sīmā, "a boundary, a border" (Monier-Williams): the line where one land ends and the next begins.

Lesson 3 of 5.
જકાત (jakāt) — a customs duty; a toll on goods.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a visa, then the Gujarati for a boundary.

You'll want to know: જકાત.
જકાત — jakāt — "a customs duty; a toll on goods".
અમારે સીમા પર જકાત ભરવી પડી. amāre sīmā par jakāt bharvī paḍī. We had to pay customs duty at the border.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
જકાત (jakāt) is usually traced to Arabic zakāt, "alms, a religious levy", which came through Persian; in Gujarati it became a duty on goods carried across a border or into a town.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઠાલવવું (ṭhālavvũ) — to empty out; to tip out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a customs duty, then the Gujarati for an official fee.

You'll want to know: ઠાલવવું.
ઠાલવવું — ṭhālavvũ — "to empty out; to tip out".
તેણે થેલી ટેબલ પર ઠાલવી. teṇe thelī ṭebal par ṭhālvī. He emptied the bag out onto the table.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે થેલી ઠાલવી, teṇe thelī ṭhālvī.

The word, taken apart.
ઠાલવવું (ṭhālavvũ) is built on ઠાલું, "empty", which CDIAL lists under ṭhalla, "empty", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5497): to make a bag or a pot empty.
