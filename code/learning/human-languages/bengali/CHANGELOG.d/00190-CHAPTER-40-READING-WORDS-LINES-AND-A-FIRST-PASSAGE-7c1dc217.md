## Chapter 40 — reading: words, lines, and a first passage

Bengali's reading rung. No task shapes are declared, so it moves no number.

Three lessons, 6 -> 7 -> 36 tokens, no new word in any.

- **Words**: the lesson is about the **মাত্রা**, the head-line. Bengali hangs
  its letters from a line rather than standing them on one, and that line runs
  unbroken across a word and stops at its edge -- so it is a word boundary a
  reader sees before reading anything. A Bengali word looks like one object
  first and a row of signs second, which is the opposite of how it was learned
  to write.
- **Lines**: **নমস্কার** is a bow written down (*namas* + *kara*, the making of
  a bow) -- the commonest word in the language is a gesture that stopped being
  made. And **দয়া করে** asks by describing a kindness the other person has
  already done.
- **Passage**: three joiners carry it, and only one is borrowed. **কিন্তু** came
  through Persian, which is why it does not sound like **আর** and **বা** -- a
  borrowing you can hear without being told. The verb is last in every line that
  has one.

Cut by the checks: মীরা and বই as unseen words, and চশমা because it needs **শ**,
which the script ladder has not taught. The practice name is **রাম**, which the
track already uses.
