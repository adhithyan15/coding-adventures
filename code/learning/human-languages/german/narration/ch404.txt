German, chapter 404: Ein platter Reifen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Reifen — the tyre (US: tire).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the tow truck, then the German for the mechanic.

You'll want to know: der Reifen.
der Reifen — "the tyre (US: tire)".
Im Herbst wechseln wir die Reifen. In autumn we change the tyres.
Same form in the plural. Die Winterreifen — winter tyres.

The word, taken apart.
Reifen goes back to Reif 'ring, hoop', inherited from Old High German reif 'band, ring' (EtymDB). EtymDB links it with English rope, from the same Germanic word for a band.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Platten — the flat tyre (colloquial).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the mechanic, then the German for the tyre.

You'll want to know: der Platten.
der Platten — "the flat tyre (colloquial)".
Auf dem Weg zur Arbeit hatte sie einen Platten. On the way to work she had a flat tyre.
Everyday speech. More formally: ein platter Reifen.

The word, taken apart.
Der Platten is a short everyday form of ein platter Reifen, built on platt 'flat'. EtymDB links platt with French plat and Vulgar Latin plattus 'flat'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
aufpumpen — to pump up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the tyre, then the German for the flat tyre.

You'll want to know: aufpumpen.
aufpumpen — "to pump up".
Er pumpt die Reifen von seinem Fahrrad auf. He pumps up the tyres on his bike.
Separable: ich pumpe auf, ich habe aufgepumpt. Die Luftpumpe — the pump.

The word, taken apart.
aufpumpen is built from auf 'up' and pumpen, from Pumpe 'pump', a seafarers' word that EtymDB traces to Middle Low German pumpe; its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Wagenheber — the jack (for a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the flat tyre, then the German for to pump up.

You'll want to know: der Wagenheber.
der Wagenheber — "the jack (for a car)".
Mit dem Wagenheber hebt sie das Auto ein bisschen hoch. With the jack she lifts the car up a little.
Same form in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
Wagenheber is built from Wagen 'car, vehicle' and Heber 'lifter', from heben 'to lift'. Both are inherited: Wagen from Proto-Germanic wagnaz and heben from habjaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Warndreieck — the warning triangle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to pump up, then the German for the jack.

You'll want to know: das Warndreieck.
das Warndreieck — "the warning triangle".
Nach der Panne stellt er das Warndreieck hinter das Auto. After the breakdown he puts the warning triangle behind the car.
Plural: die Warndreiecke. By law every car carries one.

The word, taken apart.
Warndreieck is built from warnen 'to warn', inherited from Middle High German warnen and a cousin of English warn (EtymDB), and Dreieck 'triangle', from drei 'three' and Eck 'corner'.
