Hindi, chapter 114: Sorry, No Problem, Congratulations.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
माफ़ी (māfī) — an apology, pardon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for that very one, then the Hindi for maybe.

You'll want to know: माफ़ी.
माफ़ी (māfī) — "an apology, pardon", the noun.
माफ़ी (māfī) चाहता हूँ — "I beg your pardon" (a woman says चाहती). It shares its root with माफ़ कीजिए.

What you've built.
The apology as a thing you ask for.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: māfī, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say māfī cāhtā hūṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for that very one, then the Hindi for maybe, then say māfī again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does माफ़ी (māfī) mean? ("An apology, pardon".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: कोई बात नहीं.
कोई बात नहीं (koī bāt nahīṁ) — "no problem", "never mind", literally "no matter".
It is the answer to माफ़ कीजिए, and to thanks for a small favour.

Lesson 4 of 5.
शुभकामनाएँ (śubhkāmnāẽ) — good wishes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for no problem, then the Hindi for congratulations.

You'll want to know: शुभकामनाएँ.
शुभकामनाएँ (śubhkāmnāẽ) — "good wishes".
It opens on the शुभ of शुभ रात्रि, "auspicious". आपको शुभकामनाएँ (śubhkāmnāẽ) — "good wishes to you".

What you've built.
Wishes for what is coming, beside congratulations for what is done.

Lesson 5 of 5.
निमंत्रण (nimantraṇ) — an invitation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for congratulations, then the Hindi for good wishes.

You'll want to know: निमंत्रण.
निमंत्रण (nimantraṇ) — "an invitation", the formal word.
निमंत्रण (nimantraṇ) के लिए धन्यवाद — "thank you for the invitation", with के लिए and धन्यवाद.
A written invitation has the three parts of the short message you learned to write: the line that names the reader, प्रिय मीरा,; the sentences under it; and the writer's name at the foot.

What you've built.
Sorry, no problem, congratulations, wishes, and thanks for an invitation.

[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for congratulations, then the Hindi for good wishes, then say nimantraṇ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 3 seconds]
