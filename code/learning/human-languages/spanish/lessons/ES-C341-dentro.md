## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C341-WHERE-10]; assesses=[] -->

**dentro** — "inside," "within."

It is Latin **de intrō**, *from within*, squeezed into one word. Behind *intrō*
stands **intra**, the Latin for *inside*.

You already own a piece of this. **La entrada** is the way in, and it is built
on **intrāre**, *to go inside* — the same *intra*. So *dentro* and *entrada*
are family: one names the inside, the other names the door to it.

English took *intra* and *inter* and never let go. Your **interior** is your
inside. Something **internal** happens within. An **intranet** stays inside one
building. And the **intro** to a song is the way in.

With this, the set is closed: *arriba*, *abajo*, *delante*, *detrás*, *dentro*
— and *cerca*, *lejos*, *la derecha*, *la izquierda*, *enfrente* before them.
Ten words, and almost nothing can hide from you now.
