German, chapter 276: Zahlen in den Nachrichten.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Umfrage — the survey, the poll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to suspect, then the German for presumably.

You'll want to know: die Umfrage.
die Umfrage — "the survey, the poll".
Bei einer Umfrage sagten viele Leute Nein. In a survey many people said no.
Plural: die Umfragen.

The word, taken apart.
Umfrage joins um "around" and Frage "question", both inherited Germanic words: a question asked all around. EtymDB lists no parent for the compound.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Statistik — the statistics; a statistic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for presumably, then the German for the survey.

You'll want to know: die Statistik.
die Statistik — "the statistics; a statistic".
Die Statistik zeigt mehr Unfälle im Winter. The statistics show more accidents in winter.
Plural: die Statistiken. Stress on the middle: Sta-TIS-tik.

The word, taken apart.
Statistik is usually traced to Neo-Latin statisticus "of the state", built on Latin status "state, condition" (Lewis & Short): first, facts about a state. EtymDB lists no parent.

Plural: die Anteile. Anteil nehmen — to sympathise.

Anteil is built on Teil "part", from Old High German teil, Proto-Germanic dailą "part, deal" (EtymDB), with an.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zunehmen — to increase; to put on weight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the statistics, then the German for the share.

You'll want to know: zunehmen.
zunehmen — "to increase; to put on weight".
Der Verkehr nahm am Abend zu. The traffic increased in the evening.
Separable: es nimmt zu. For a person, to put on weight: Er hat zugenommen.

The word, taken apart.
zunehmen is built on nehmen "to take", from Old High German neman, Proto-Germanic nemaną (EtymDB), with zu: to take on more.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sinken — to sink, to fall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the share, then the German for to increase.

You'll want to know: sinken.
sinken — "to sink, to fall".
Die Preise sanken im Sommer. Prices fell in the summer.
Strong verb with sein: sank, ist gesunken.

The word, taken apart.
sinken is an inherited Germanic verb, from Old High German sinkan (EtymDB), and EtymDB lists Low German sinken and English sink among its cousins.
