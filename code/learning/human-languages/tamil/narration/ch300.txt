Tamil, chapter 300: A State of Emergency.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
அவசரநிலை (avacaranilai) — an emergency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a firefighter, then the Tamil for to suffocate.

You'll want to know: அவசரநிலை.
அவசரநிலை — avacaranilai — "an emergency".
மழை நின்றால், அவசரநிலை முடிவுக்கு வரும். maḻai niṉṟāl, avacaranilai muṭivukku varum. If the rain stops, the emergency will come to an end.
From அவசரம், urgency, and நிலை, a state.

The word, taken apart.
அவசரநிலை (avacaranilai) joins அவசரம், "urgency", borrowed from Sanskrit avasara, "occasion" (CDIAL 858), with native நிலை, "state, condition" (DEDR 3675).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ஊரடங்கு (ūraṭaṅku) — a curfew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to suffocate, then the Tamil for an emergency.

You'll want to know: ஊரடங்கு.
ஊரடங்கு — ūraṭaṅku — "a curfew".
நகரத்தில் மூன்று நாள் ஊரடங்கு இருந்தது. nakarattil mūṉṟu nāḷ ūraṭaṅku iruntatu. There was a three-day curfew in the city.
ஊர், town + அடங்கு, to settle down: the town goes quiet.

The word, taken apart.
ஊரடங்கு (ūraṭaṅku) is a Tamil compound built from ஊர், "village, town" (DEDR 752), and அடங்கு, "to submit, settle down, cease" (DEDR 63).

Lesson 3 of 5.
முடங்கு (muṭaṅku) — to stall; to grind to a halt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for an emergency, then the Tamil for a curfew.

You'll want to know: முடங்கு.
முடங்கு — muṭaṅku — "to stall; to grind to a halt".
வெள்ளத்தால் நகரம் முழுவதும் முடங்கியது. veḷḷattāl nakaram muḻuvatum muṭaṅkiyatu. Because of the flood the whole city ground to a halt.
Past (it …): முடங்கியது, muṭaṅkiyatu.
Said of a town, traffic or work that stops; the cause takes -ஆல்.

The word, taken apart.
முடங்கு (muṭaṅku) is native Dravidian (DEDR 4919, 4933): to contract, to be held back, to stop moving.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a curfew, then the Tamil for to stall.

You'll want to know: நிலைகுலை.
நிலைகுலை — nilaikulai — "to fall apart".
அந்தச் செய்தியைக் கேட்டு நான் நிலைகுலைந்தேன். antac ceytiyaik kēṭṭu nāṉ nilaikulaintēṉ. I fell apart when I heard that news.
Past (I …): நிலைகுலைந்தேன், nilaikulaintēṉ.
From நிலை, a steady state, and குலை, to be upset.

The word, taken apart.
நிலைகுலை (nilaikulai) is a Tamil compound built from நிலை, "standing, a steady state" (DEDR 3675), and குலை, "to be upset, thrown into confusion" (DEDR 1806).

Lesson 5 of 5.
இடிந்துவிழு (iṭintuviḻu) — to collapse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to stall, then the Tamil for to fall apart.

You'll want to know: இடிந்துவிழு.
இடிந்துவிழு — iṭintuviḻu — "to collapse".
பழைய பாலம் திடீரென்று இடிந்துவிழுந்தது. paḻaiya pālam tiṭīreṉṟu iṭintuviḻuntatu. The old bridge suddenly collapsed.
Past (it …): இடிந்துவிழுந்தது, iṭintuviḻuntatu.
Often written as two words: இடிந்து விழு.

The word, taken apart.
இடிந்துவிழு (iṭintuviḻu) is a Tamil compound built from இடிந்து, "having crumbled", from இடி, "to crumble, fall to pieces" (DEDR 432), and விழு, "to fall" (DEDR 5430).
