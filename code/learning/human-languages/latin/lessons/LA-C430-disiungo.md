## You'll want to know: disiungō, disiungere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C430-TEMO-05]; assesses=[] -->

**disiungō, disiungere** — "to unyoke; to separate".

> Vesperī agricola bovēs disiungit et domum redit.
>
> *In the evening the farmer unyokes the oxen and goes home.*

*disiungō, disiungere, disiūnxī, disiūnctum*. English *disjoint* and *disjunction* come from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*disiungō* is built from *dis-* 'apart' and *iungō* 'to join, to yoke' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *disjoint* comes from it through French.
