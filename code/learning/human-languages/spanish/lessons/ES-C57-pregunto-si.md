# preguntó si

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-BACKSHIFT, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Report a statement. (*Dijo que trabajaba*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the past of *preguntar*, which is perfectly ordinary.
(*Preguntó*.)

## Grammar Lens: a question, reported
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-YESNO-SI]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

> *¿Trabaja?* — the question, as asked.
> ***Preguntó si trabajaba.*** — the question, reported.

Two things changed, and both are worth naming.

[PAUSE 2s] **The joint is *si*, not *que*.** A statement goes in on *que*; a
yes-or-no question goes in on *si*. The verb still steps back into the
imperfect, exactly as before — that part has not changed at all.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SCRIPT-INVERTED-QUESTION, ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-YESNO-SI] -->

**And the question marks are gone.** Not forgotten — *not allowed*. Look at what
the reported sentence actually does: it tells you something. It is a statement
about an act of asking, and nobody is being asked anything. The marks belong to
a question being put, and no question is being put here.

[PAUSE 2s] So *¿Preguntó si trabajaba?* is a real sentence, but it means
something else — it asks whether he asked.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the *si* itself. It is the same *si* that opens a condition, and
that is not a coincidence dressed up as one. Both uses hold something open that
has not been settled: *si tengo tiempo* leaves the time unsettled, *si trabajaba*
leaves the answer unsettled. English splits the job between *whether* and *if*.
Spanish never needed to.
