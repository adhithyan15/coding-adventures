# tímido — fear, and the verb for putting it into somebody

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *serio*. A serious person and a shy person look similar from the
outside and are nothing alike inside. This lesson names the second one.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [serio](./ES-C423-serio.md) — earnest, from Latin for *weighty*.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `accent-acute` — *TÍ-mi-do*: the accent marks the stress on the third
  syllable from the end, which Spanish always writes out. →
  [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)
- `t-dental` — tongue against the teeth, no puff of air. An English *t* here is
  the single clearest giveaway of an English speaker.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-05]; assesses=[] -->

**tímido** — "shy, timid." Latin **timidus**, from the verb **timere**, "to
fear."

*Timere* built a small family, and English took all of it:

| word | literally |
|---|---|
| **timid** | inclined to fear |
| **timorous** | full of fear |
| **intimidate** | to put fear **into** someone |

That last one is the useful one. *In-* means *into*. To **intimidate** somebody
is to place the fear inside them — the exact fear that *tímido* names. The shy
person and the bully are described with the same Latin root, from opposite
ends.

Spanish keeps the whole family too: *tímido*, *temer* (to fear), *intimidar*.

A note on what the word does **not** say. *Tímido* describes how a person meets
strangers, not whether they are frightened at this moment. For being afraid
right now, Spanish says *tener miedo*. This is settled character, which is the
whole point of the chapter.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-05, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-01] -->

Three **-o** adjectives now, all agreeing the same way:

| masculine | feminine |
|---|---|
| antipático | antipática |
| serio | seria |
| tímido | tímida |

Two of the three carry a written accent, and in both cases the accent stays
exactly where it was when the ending changes: *tímido* → *tímida*.
