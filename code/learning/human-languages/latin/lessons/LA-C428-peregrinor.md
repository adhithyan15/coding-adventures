## You'll want to know: peregrīnor, peregrīnārī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C428-PROFECTIO-04]; assesses=[] -->

**peregrīnor, peregrīnārī** — "to travel abroad, to live in foreign parts".

> Mercātor multōs annōs in terrīs longinquīs peregrīnātur.
>
> *The merchant travels abroad in distant lands for many years.*

Deponent: *peregrīnor, peregrīnārī, peregrīnātus sum*. English *pilgrim* comes from the related adjective *peregrīnus*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*peregrīnor* is built on *peregrīnus* 'foreign' (EtymDB), which goes back to *peregrē* 'abroad', from *per-* 'through, beyond' and *ager* 'land' (Lewis & Short). English *pilgrim* comes from *peregrīnus*.
