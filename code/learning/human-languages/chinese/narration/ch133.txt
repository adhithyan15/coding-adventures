Mandarin Chinese, chapter 133: Housework.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
打扫 (dǎsǎo) — to clean (a room); to sweep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for in all, then the Mandarin for to count.

You'll want to know: 打扫.
打扫 — dǎsǎo — "to clean (a room); to sweep".
今天我们一起打扫 jīntiān wǒmen yìqǐ dǎsǎo — Today we're cleaning up together.
The new character, in pieces: 扫 (sǎo) — the hand piece on the left, as in 打, and on the right three strokes: a bar that turns down, and two bars inside it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
Two third tones: dǎsǎo is said dásǎo, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
打扫 (dǎsǎo) is built from 打 'to strike', here 'to do', and sǎo 'to sweep', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and sawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The regular sound change from taengX would not give the everyday dǎ; why is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Script — the hand piece, then three bars.
扫 (sǎo).
Two pieces, left before right.
The hand piece, three strokes, as in 打: a horizontal, a vertical that hooks to the left at its foot, then a stroke that rises to the right across it.
The right piece, three strokes. A horizontal that turns down at its right end, a horizontal across the middle, then a horizontal along the foot, which runs out past the right side.
3 + 3 = six.

[pause 3 seconds]
Which piece of 扫 (sǎo) comes from 打? (The hand piece, on the left.) How many strokes on the right? (Three.) Which bar runs out past the side? (The bottom one.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
jiāwù — housework.

You'll want to know: jiāwù.
jiāwù — "housework".
wǒ bù xǐhuan zuò jiāwù — I don't like doing housework.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiāwù is built from 家 'home' and wù 'affairs', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and mjuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
shōushi — to tidy up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to clean (a room), then the Mandarin for housework.

You'll want to know: shōushi.
shōushi — "to tidy up".
jīntiān wǒ shōushi fángjiān — Today I'm tidying my room.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shōushi is built from shōu 'to gather' and shi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuw and dzyip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second with this very word, and with 'to pick up'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
dié — to fold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to tidy up, then the Mandarin for to wipe.

You'll want to know: dié.
dié — "to fold".
wǒ dié yīfu nǐ shōushi fángjiān — I'll fold the clothes; you tidy the room.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dié is inherited from Middle Chinese dep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'layered' and 'to pile up'; folding, a cloth laid in layers, is the likely step, though it is not given there.
