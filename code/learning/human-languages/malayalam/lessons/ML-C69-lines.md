# (reading) — six lines, two verbs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson gave you words. These put them somewhere.

[PAUSE 1s] Six lines, three things, and no word you have not met.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-SKILL-READ-LINES]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-03, ML-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-04] -->

> ഇത് വാതിൽ ആണ്.
> ഇവിടെ വാതിൽ ഉണ്ട്.
> ഇത് പൂവ് ആണ്.
> ഇവിടെ പൂവ് ഇല്ല.
> അത് വിളക്ക് ആണ്.
> അവിടെ വിളക്ക് ഉണ്ട്.

[PAUSE 3s] Read the six once, straight down.

[PAUSE 3s] Now read only the last word of each line.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C52-WELCOME-01, ML-LEX-C46-ASK-03, ML-LEX-ILLA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] English would translate **ആണ്** and **ഉണ്ട്** with the same word. It
is, it is. Malayalam keeps them apart, and the difference is worth having.

[PAUSE 2s] **ആണ്** NAMES. It puts an equals sign between two things: this thing,
that word. It says nothing at all about where either of them is.

[PAUSE 2s] **ഉണ്ട്** PLACES. It says a thing exists somewhere, and the somewhere
is usually the first word on the line.

[PAUSE 2s] That is why the last word of the line is worth reading first. It
tells you whether the line is about to name something or point at it, before you
have read the middle.

[PAUSE 2s] And the pair that does NOT match: **ഇല്ല** is the negative of
**ഉണ്ട്**, never of **ആണ്**. It is the one line here that takes a thing away.
