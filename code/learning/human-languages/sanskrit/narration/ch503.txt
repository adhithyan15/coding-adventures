Sanskrit, chapter 503: People at Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सहकारी (sahakārī) — a colleague.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a saw, then the Sanskrit for a chisel.

You'll want to know: सहकारी.
सहकारी — sahakārī — "a colleague".
मम सहकारी अपि अत्र कार्यं करोति। mama sahakārī api atra kāryaṃ karoti. My colleague works here too.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सहकारी (sahakārī) is built from saha, "together", and kārin, "doing", from the root √kṛ, "to do"; MW gives sahakārin as one who acts together with another, an assistant; "a colleague" is its use today (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
नियोक्ता (niyoktā) — an employer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for adroit, then the Sanskrit for hard work.

You'll want to know: नियोक्ता.
नियोक्ता — niyoktā — "an employer".
मम नियोक्ता उदारः अस्ति। mama niyoktā udāraḥ asti. My employer is generous.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नियोक्ता (niyoktā) is built on ni and the root √yuj, "to yoke, to appoint", with the agent ending -tṛ; MW gives "one who fastens" and "a master"; "an employer" is the modern sense (MW).

You'll want to know: नियुंक्ते.

नियोक्ता मम भ्रातरं नियुंक्ते। niyoktā mama bhrātaraṃ niyuṅkte. The employer takes on my brother.
Past (he …): सः मम भ्रातरं नियुक्तवान्, saḥ mama bhrātaraṃ niyuktavān.

The word, taken apart.
नियुंक्ते (niyuṅkte) is built on ni, "down, into", and the root √yuj, "to yoke"; MW gives niyuj the senses "to fasten to" and "to appoint, to employ"; its past participle is niyukta (MW).
