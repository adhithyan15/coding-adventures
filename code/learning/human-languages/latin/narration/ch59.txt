Latin, chapter 59: Reading — Objects, Places, and a Day.
5 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aqua -> aquam — when the action reaches the thing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You already know aqua: water. Read one new sentence.
Aquam bibō. — I drink water.
[pause 2 seconds]
Nothing new has happened to the water. Its ending changed because it is now the thing the action reaches.

You'll want to know: -a becomes -am.
[pause 2 seconds]
Latin marks the direct object with the accusative case. For the familiar nouns ending in -a, the first useful move is small:
aqua -> aquam — I drink aquam.
epistula -> epistulam — I write epistulam.
porta -> portam — I open portam.
[pause 2 seconds]
The -m is a reading signal. It tells you that this word receives the action even if Latin moves the word somewhere English would not.

Grammar Lens: actor, action, object.
[pause 2 seconds]
In epistulam scrībō, the verb scrībō already says "I write." The ending -am tells you what I write.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read from the endings rather than from position: portam aperiō and aperiō portam mean the same basic thing.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: aquam bibō — the water receives the action]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: epistulam scrībō — the letter receives the action]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: portam aperiō — the gate receives the action]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Complete the sentence meaning I drink water: ___ bibo.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What does -am tell you here? (This familiar -a noun is the direct object.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Change epistula for "I write a letter." (Epistulam scrībō.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
amīcus -> amīcum — a second road to the same object marker.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You saw -a -> -am. Now read amīcum videō — I see the friend.
[pause 2 seconds]
The noun changed more visibly, but the final signal is familiar: the word ends in -m because the seeing reaches it.

You'll want to know: -us and -er reach -um.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three known nouns give you the useful masculine pattern:
amīcus -> amīcum — amīcum adiuvō, I help the friend.
magister -> magistrum — magistrum audiō, I hear the teacher.
liber -> librum — librum legō, I read the book.
[pause 2 seconds]
Magister and liber drop their nominative -er before -um. The genitives you learned — magistrī, librī — already showed the shorter stems magistr- and libr-.

Grammar Lens: the ending frees the order.
[pause 2 seconds]
Librum legō does not need English order to tell you what is read. The -um has already done that work.
[pause 2 seconds]
Put the two lessons together: epistulam scrībō; librum legō. Two actions, two objects, two visible endings.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: amīcum adiuvō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: magistrum audiō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: librum legō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Complete the sentence meaning I read the book: ___ lego.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What is the object form of liber? (Librum.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What ending do amīcum, magistrum and librum share? (-um, ending in the accusative -m.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ad scholam, in scholā, domum — toward, at, and homeward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The form scholam is no longer new. Read it after a tiny word: ad scholam.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ad points toward an endpoint: ad scholam ambulō — I walk to or toward school.

You'll want to know: motion and rest do not look alike.
[pause 2 seconds]
These three short shapes carry a whole route:
ad scholam ambulō — I walk toward school.
in scholā sedeō — I sit in school.
domum eō — I go home.
[pause 2 seconds]
Motion toward takes ad plus the accusative scholam. Rest in a place takes in plus ablative scholā, whose long final -ā matters. Domum is the old compact exception: homeward needs no preposition.

Grammar Lens: follow the case, then the journey.
[pause 2 seconds]
A reader can now follow three stages without translating every word: movement toward, rest inside, movement home.
[pause 2 seconds]
Add the object from the last lesson: in scholā librum legō. The long -ā locates the action; -um shows what the reading reaches.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ad scholam ambulō — movement toward]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: in scholā sedeō — rest inside]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: domum eō — movement home]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which two Latin words mean toward school?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which form means "in school"? (In scholā, with long -ā.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which destination needs no preposition? (Domum — homeward.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
(reading) — a whole meeting, one turn at a time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The first passage was forty-two words. This exchange is seventy-three.
[pause 2 seconds]
Do not race. Follow who asks, who answers, and where the two people go. The endings will keep the objects visible.

Reading.
— Salvē! — Salvē! — Quid tibi nōmen est? — Mihi nōmen est Iūlia. Quid tibi nōmen est? — Mihi nōmen est Mārcus. — Quōmodo valēs? — Bene valeō. Et tū? — Bene valeō. — Ubi schola tua est? — Schola mea ibi est. Ubi schola tua est? — Schola mea hic est. — Māne ad scholam ambulō. In scholā sedeō. — Ego māne librum legō. Post merīdiem epistulam scrībō. — Librum iterum legō. — Cūr? — Quia liber meus bonus est. — Bene. — Crās tē vidēbō. — Ita. Valē! — Valē!
[pause 4 seconds]
Read once for the route: names, wellbeing, school, work, reason, farewell.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read again and find the two things handled: librum and epistulam.

Grammar Lens: a dialogue is a chain of answers.
[pause 2 seconds]
You do not need to translate every turn. Quid tibi nōmen est? predicts a name. Quōmodo valēs? predicts wellbeing. Ubi predicts a place. Cūr? predicts quia.
[pause 2 seconds]
That prediction is reading. It lets the shape of one turn prepare you for the next.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the exchange once, preserving each turn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
What does one speaker write after midday?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Where does the first speaker's school stand? (Ibi — there.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Why is the book read again? (Quia liber meus bonus est.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
(reading) — diēs meus, my day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The exchange gave you seventy-three words in turns. This account gives you one hundred forty words in one voice.
[pause 2 seconds]
First follow the day words. Then follow the objects. You already know every form.

Reading.
Nōmen meum est Mārcus. Ego sum discipulus. Domus mea bona est. Canis meus hic est, et fēlēs mea ibi est. Māne prīma hōra est. Ego stō, lac bibō, et mel et ōvum edō. Portam aperiō et portam claudō, et ad scholam ambulō. Schola mea bona est. In scholā sedeō. Magister meus bonus est. Magistrum videō et audiō. Prīmum librum accipiō et legō. Secundum librum nōn accipiō, quia secundus liber malus est. Epistulam scrībō. Cōgitō et intellegō. Amīcum meum videō et adiuvō. Amīcus meus discipulus est. Sexta hōra est. Lac bibō, et mel et ōvum edō. Vīnum nōn bibō. Aquam bibō. Mare et caelum videō. Post merīdiem domum eō. Hic sedeō, et librum iterum legō. Cūr iterum legō? Quia liber meus bonus est. Vesper est. Nox est. Bene valeō. Dormiō. Crās iterum ad scholam veniō. Crās magistrum et amīcum meum videō.
[pause 4 seconds]
Read once for time: morning, first hour, sixth hour, after midday, evening, night, tomorrow.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read again for the route: home, toward school, in school, homeward.

Grammar Lens: the account has two maps.
[pause 2 seconds]
The time words make one map. The place phrases make another. Inside both, the object endings tell you what each action reaches: portam, magistrum, librum, epistulam, amīcum, aquam.
[pause 2 seconds]
The account repeats librum for a reason. The first book is read; the second is refused; the good book is read again. That is a connected thread, not a list of unrelated sentences.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the account once, pausing only at full stops]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
Which book is not taken, and why?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Put the route in order. (Domus — ad scholam — in scholā — domum.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What happens tomorrow? (He goes to school again and sees his teacher and friend.)
