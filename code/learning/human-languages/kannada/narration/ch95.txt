Kannada, chapter 95: Maybe, and Not Possible.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಸಾಧ್ಯವಿಲ್ಲ (sādhyavilla) — it's not possible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a plate, then the Kannada for the price.

You'll want to know: ಸಾಧ್ಯವಿಲ್ಲ.
ಸಾಧ್ಯವಿಲ್ಲ (sādhyavilla) — "it's not possible", from ಸಾಧ್ಯ (sādhya), "possible", with ‑ಇಲ್ಲ, "not".

The word, taken apart.
ಸಾಧ್ಯವಿಲ್ಲ (sādhyavilla) is sādhya, "possible", from Sanskrit sādhya, "to be accomplished", with native illa, "is not" (DEDR 2559).

What you've built.
Possible, and the no that closes it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಹಾಗಲ್ಲ (hāgalla) — it's not like that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for the price, then the Kannada for not possible.

You'll want to know: ಹಾಗಲ್ಲ.
ಹಾಗಲ್ಲ (hāgalla) — "it's not like that", "no, that's not it". It is ಹಾಗೆ (hāge), "like that", with ಅಲ್ಲ (alla), "is not".

The word, taken apart.
ಹಾಗಲ್ಲ (hāgalla) is native Dravidian: hāge, "in that way" (DEDR 1), and alla, "is not" (DEDR 234).

What you've built.
Two known pieces, one correction.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಓಹೋ (ōhō) — oh, I see.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for not possible, then the Kannada for not like that.

You'll want to know: ಓಹೋ.
ಓಹೋ (ōhō) — "oh, I see!", the sound of understanding arriving.

The word, taken apart.
ಓಹೋ (ōhō) is an exclamation: the sound of surprise made into a word, as in many languages. It comes from the sound itself, with no older word behind it.

What you've built.
The happy opposite of ayyō.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಇರಬಹುದು (irabahudu) — maybe, it may be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for not like that, then the Kannada for oh, I see.

You'll want to know: ಇರಬಹುದು.
ಇರಬಹುದು (irabahudu) — "maybe, it could be", from ಇರು (iru), "to be", with ‑ಬಹುದು, "may".

The word, taken apart.
ಇರಬಹುದು (irabahudu) is native Dravidian: ira, from iru, "to be" (DEDR 480), and bahudu, "it may", usually explained as an old future form of baru, "to come" (DEDR 5270): "it may come to be".

What you've built.
A known verb, softened into a guess.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಖಚಿತವಾಗಿ (khacitavāgi) — certainly, for sure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for oh, I see, then the Kannada for maybe.

You'll want to know: ಖಚಿತವಾಗಿ.
ಖಚಿತವಾಗಿ (khacitavāgi) — "certainly, for sure", with the ‑ಆಗಿ ending of ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ. ಖಂಡಿತ (khaṇḍita) says the same with more force.

The word, taken apart.
ಖಚಿತವಾಗಿ (khacitavāgi) is khacita, "certain, fixed", from Sanskrit khacita, "inlaid, set", with the adverb ending -āgi, "in a … way". What is set in place is certain.

What you've built.
The sure answer after maybe.
