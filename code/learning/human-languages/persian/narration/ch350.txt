Persian, chapter 350: Kings and castles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پادشاه (pâdeshâh) — a king, a monarch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to kill, then the Persian for to be taken prisoner.

You'll want to know: پادشاه.
پادشاه (pâdeshâh) — "a king, a monarch".
پادشاه (pâdeshâh) سه پسر داشت. Pâdeshâh se pesar dâsht. The king had three sons.
Also shâh. Pâdeshâhi "a kingdom, a reign".

The word, taken apart.
pâdeshâh is a native Persian word that EtymDB glosses "king, monarch" without a parent; it is usually traced to Middle Persian pādixšāh, "ruler".

Plural kâkh-hâ; many old palaces are museums now.

kâkh is a native Persian word for a palace. EtymDB lists no entry, and its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
قلعه (qal'e) — a castle, a fortress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a queen, then the Persian for a palace.

You'll want to know: قلعه.
قلعه (qal'e) — "a castle, a fortress".
دشمن نتوانست وارد قلعه شود. Doshman natavânest vâred-e qal'e shavad. The enemy could not get into the castle.
Written with ع. Plural qal'e-hâ.

The word, taken apart.
qal'e is borrowed from Arabic qal'a, "fort, fortress" (EtymDB), and EtymDB in turn connects the Arabic word with Persian kalât "fortress".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a palace, then the Persian for a castle.

You'll want to know: تاج.

پادشاه (pâdeshâh) یک تاج طلا بر سر داشت. Pâdeshâh yek tâj-e talâ bar sar dâsht. The king wore a gold crown on his head.
Tâj-o takht "crown and throne", royal power.

The word, taken apart.
tâj is an Iranian word: EtymDB traces it to Parthian tāg, "crown", and lists Arabic tāj beside it.
