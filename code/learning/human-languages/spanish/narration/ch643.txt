Spanish, chapter 643: Al volante.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
derrapar — to skid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pothole, then the Spanish for the stretch.

You'll want to know: derrapar.
derrapar — "to skid".
Con la lluvia, el coche derrapó. The car skidded in the rain.
Un derrape is a skid.

The word, taken apart.
derrapar was borrowed from French déraper (EtymDB), first said of an anchor that comes loose from the sea bed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
arrancar — to start (an engine); to pull out, to tear out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the stretch, then the Spanish for to skid.

You'll want to know: arrancar.
arrancar — "to start (an engine); to pull out, to tear out".
El coche no arranca. The car will not start.
The older sense is pulling out by force: arrancar una hoja — to tear out a page.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of arrancar is not settled. One proposal links it to Latin ēruncāre "to pull up weeds", but the path is disputed.

Lesson 4 of 5.
acelerar — to speed up, to accelerate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to start, then the Spanish for to get stuck.

You'll want to know: acelerar.
acelerar — "to speed up, to accelerate".
No aceleres en la ciudad. Do not speed up in town.
The pedal is el acelerador.

The word, taken apart.
acelerar is a learned borrowing from Latin accelerāre "to hasten" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), built from ad and celer "swift".

Lesson 5 of 5.
circular — to circulate, to drive (of traffic).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get stuck, then the Spanish for to speed up.

You'll want to know: circular.
circular — "to circulate, to drive (of traffic)".
En España se circula por la derecha. In Spain you drive on the right.
A word for signs and rules: circule despacio — drive slowly. As an adjective it means round.

The word, taken apart.
circular is a learned borrowing from Late Latin circulāre "to make round" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on circulus "a small ring", from circus "a ring".
