Lesson 1 of 5.
रज्जुः (rajjuḥ) — a rope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a fan, then the Sanskrit for a box.

You'll want to know: रज्जुः.
रज्जुः — rajjuḥ — "a rope".

The word, taken apart.
rajju "rope" is inherited from PIE résgus (EtymDB), and MW compares Lithuanian rezgù "I plait"; Latin restis "rope" is a cousin. It lives on as Hindi lej "rope" (CDIAL 10582).

Lesson 2 of 5.
गोधूमः (godhūmaḥ) — wheat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a box, then the Sanskrit for a rope.

You'll want to know: गोधूमः.
गोधूमः — godhūmaḥ — "wheat".

The word, taken apart.
MW reads godhūma "wheat" as go "earth, cow" + dhūma "smoke", "earth-smoke", but the word has no sure origin. It is old, in the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā, and lives on as Hindi gehūṁ and Gujarati ghaũ "wheat" (CDIAL 4287).

Lesson 3 of 5.
तण्डुलः (taṇḍulaḥ) — rice (uncooked).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a rope, then the Sanskrit for wheat.

You'll want to know: तण्डुलः.
तण्डुलः — taṇḍulaḥ — "rice (uncooked)".

The word, taken apart.
taṇḍula "grain after threshing, rice" is old, in the Atharvaveda (MW), and its origin is uncertain: CDIAL 5637 thinks it perhaps non-Aryan, after Bloch from Dravidian or, less probably, after Chatterji from Austroasiatic. Pali kept it as taṇḍula (CDIAL 5637).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मुद्गः (mudgaḥ) — mung beans.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for wheat, then the Sanskrit for rice.

You'll want to know: मुद्गः.
मुद्गः — mudgaḥ — "mung beans".

The word, taken apart.
mudga "the mung bean" is old, in the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā; the grammarians quoted by MW derive it from √mud "be glad", a learned guess, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi mūg and Gujarati mag (CDIAL 10198).

Lesson 5 of 5.
सूपः (sūpaḥ) — dal, soup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for rice, then the Sanskrit for mung beans.

You'll want to know: सूपः.
सूपः — sūpaḥ — "dal, soup".

The word, taken apart.
MW calls sūpa "sauce, soup, broth" of doubtful derivation; the grammarians tie it to √su "distil", so its origin is uncertain. Pali kept it as sūpa "broth, curry" (CDIAL 13571).
