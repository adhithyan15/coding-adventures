Portuguese, chapter 402: Ao volante.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o travão — the brake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the junction, then the Portuguese for to speed up.

You'll want to know: o travão.
o travão — "the brake".
O travão do meu carro faz um barulho estranho. The brake on my car makes a strange noise.
In Brazil: o freio. The verb is travar, to brake.

The word, taken apart.
travão is built on travar 'to hold back, to brake', which is usually traced to trave 'beam'; trave is inherited from Latin trabs 'beam' (EtymDB): a bar that holds something still.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o pedal — the pedal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the brake, then the Portuguese for to skid.

You'll want to know: o pedal.
o pedal — "the pedal".
Carrego no pedal do travão devagar. I press the brake pedal gently.
Carregar no pedal — to press the pedal. A bicycle has two pedals too.

The word, taken apart.
pedal is a learned borrowing from Latin pedālis 'of the foot' (EtymDB), built on pēs 'foot' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a embraiagem — the clutch (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to skid, then the Portuguese for the pedal.

You'll want to know: a embraiagem.
a embraiagem — "the clutch (of a car)".
No carro do meu pai, a embraiagem é muito pesada. In my father's car, the clutch is very heavy.
In Brazil: a embreagem. A car with automatic gears has no clutch pedal.

The word, taken apart.
embraiagem was borrowed from French embrayage, built on embrayer 'to put into gear' with the ending -age (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
engatar — to put into gear; to hook up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the pedal, then the Portuguese for the clutch.

You'll want to know: engatar.
engatar — "to put into gear; to hook up".
Ele engata a primeira e o carro começa a andar. He puts it into first gear and the car starts to move.
Engatar a marcha atrás — to put it into reverse. You also engatar a trailer to a car.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of engatar is not settled. It is usually linked with gato, which also meant 'hook, clamp'; gato is inherited from Late Latin cattus (EtymDB).
