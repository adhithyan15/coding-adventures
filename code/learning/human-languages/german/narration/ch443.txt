German, chapter 443: Kleine Läden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Feinkostgeschäft — the delicatessen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the snowplough, then the German for to slip.

You'll want to know: das Feinkostgeschäft.
das Feinkostgeschäft — "the delicatessen".
Im Feinkostgeschäft kauft er Oliven und Schinken. At the delicatessen he buys olives and ham.
Plural: die Feinkostgeschäfte. Die Feinkost — fine foods.

The word, taken apart.
Feinkostgeschäft is built from fein 'fine', which came into Middle High German as fīn from Old French fin, Kost 'food, fare', and Geschäft 'business, shop', inherited from Old High German giscaft (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Konditorei — the cake shop, the patisserie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to slip, then the German for the delicatessen.

You'll want to know: die Konditorei.
die Konditorei — "the cake shop, the patisserie".
In der Konditorei gibt es den besten Kuchen der Stadt. The cake shop has the best cake in town.
Plural: die Konditoreien. Der Konditor — the pastry cook.

The word, taken apart.
Konditorei is built on Konditor 'confectioner' with the ending -ei (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin condītor 'a seasoner', from condīre 'to season, to preserve' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Gemüsehändler — the greengrocer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the delicatessen, then the German for the cake shop.

You'll want to know: der Gemüsehändler.
der Gemüsehändler — "the greengrocer".
Der Gemüsehändler hat heute frische Tomaten. The greengrocer has fresh tomatoes today.
Same form in the plural. A woman: die Gemüsehändlerin.

The word, taken apart.
Gemüsehändler is built from Gemüse 'vegetables', from Middle High German gemüese, built on Mus 'mash, porridge' (EtymDB), and Händler 'dealer', from handeln 'to trade'.

die Drogerie — "the chemist (for toiletries), the drugstore".
In der Drogerie kauft sie Shampoo und Zahnpasta. At the chemist she buys shampoo and toothpaste.
Plural: die Drogerien. Medicine is sold at die Apotheke.

The word, taken apart.
Drogerie was borrowed from French droguerie (EtymDB), built on drogue 'dried goods, drug'; the deeper origin of drogue is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
das Reformhaus — the health food shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the greengrocer, then the German for the chemist.

You'll want to know: das Reformhaus.
das Reformhaus — "the health food shop".
Das Brot aus dem Reformhaus ist teuer, aber gut. The bread from the health food shop is expensive but good.
Plural: die Reformhäuser.

The word, taken apart.
Reformhaus is built from Reform 'reform', borrowed from French réforme (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin reformāre 'to reshape', and Haus 'house', inherited from Proto-Germanic hūsą (EtymDB). The shops grew out of a movement for a simpler, healthier life.
