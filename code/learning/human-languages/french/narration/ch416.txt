French, chapter 416: À l'aérogare.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le vol — the flight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tram, then the French for the scooter.

You'll want to know: le vol.
le vol — "the flight".
Notre vol part à sept heures du matin. Our flight leaves at seven in the morning.
Le vol is also a theft; that sense comes from voler 'to steal'.

The word, taken apart.
vol is built on voler 'to fly' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin volāre.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'aérogare — the airport building (for passengers).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the scooter, then the French for the flight.

You'll want to know: l'aérogare.
l'aérogare — "the airport building (for passengers)".
La navette va de la gare jusqu'à l'aérogare. The shuttle runs from the railway station to the airport building.
A feminine noun: une aérogare.

The word, taken apart.
aérogare is built from aéro-, from Greek aḗr 'air', and gare, the building for trains: a gare for planes. EtymDB lists the word without a source line.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'enregistrement — the check-in; the recording.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: l'enregistrement.
l'enregistrement — "the check-in; the recording".
L'enregistrement ferme une heure avant le départ. Check-in closes an hour before departure.
A masculine noun. Also a recording of music.

The word, taken apart.
enregistrement is built on enregistrer with -ment (EtymDB). Enregistrer comes from registre 'register', from Medieval Latin registrum.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la soute — the hold (of a plane or ship).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the airport building, then the French for the check-in.

You'll want to know: la soute.
la soute — "the hold (of a plane or ship)".
Sa grande valise voyage dans la soute. Her big suitcase travels in the hold.
Un bagage en soute — a checked bag.

The word, taken apart.
soute was borrowed from Provençal sota (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin subtus 'underneath': the space below deck.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'embarquement — the boarding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the check-in, then the French for the hold.

You'll want to know: l'embarquement.
l'embarquement — "the boarding".
L'embarquement commence à la porte douze. Boarding begins at gate twelve.
A masculine noun. La carte d'embarquement — the boarding pass.

The word, taken apart.
embarquement is built on embarquer 'to board' (EtymDB lists it), from en- and barque 'boat', which goes back to Latin barca (Lewis & Short).
