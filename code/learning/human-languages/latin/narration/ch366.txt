Latin, chapter 366: Evidently, And Yet, So Then.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vidēlicet — evidently, clearly; namely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for surely, then the Latin for to be sure.

You'll want to know: vidēlicet.
vidēlicet — "evidently, clearly; namely".
Domum rediit, vidēlicet fessus. He went home, tired, evidently.
Vidēlicet = vidēre licet, one may see. English viz. is its abbreviation.

The word, taken apart.
vidēlicet is contracted from vidēre licet "one may see" (Lewis & Short). English videlicet and its short form viz. were borrowed from it (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
nīmīrum — without doubt, surely.

You'll want to know: nīmīrum.
nīmīrum — "without doubt, surely".
Nīmīrum vērum dīcis. No doubt you are telling the truth.
Nīmīrum = nī mīrum, no wonder. It can carry a smile of irony.

The word, taken apart.
nīmīrum is built from nī "not" and mīrum "a wonder" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): it is no wonder, so surely.

Lesson 3 of 5.
atquī — and yet, but (raising an objection).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for evidently, then the Latin for without doubt.

You'll want to know: atquī.
atquī — "and yet, but (raising an objection)".
Dīcis tē pauperem esse; atquī vīllam magnam habēs. You say you are poor; and yet you have a large country house.
Atquī brings in a fact that tells against what was said. It takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
atquī is built from at "but" and quī, an old ablative form meaning "in any way" (Lewis & Short): but in any case.

Lesson 4 of 5.
proinde — therefore, accordingly; so then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for without doubt, then the Latin for and yet.

You'll want to know: proinde.
proinde — "therefore, accordingly; so then".
Hostēs adsunt; proinde arma capiāmus. The enemy is here; so then let us take up arms.
Proinde draws a practical consequence and often comes with a subjunctive of exhortation (capiāmus, let us take) or with a command.

The word, taken apart.
proinde is built from prō "for, in proportion to" and inde "from there" (Lewis & Short): from that, accordingly.

Lesson 5 of 5.
idcircō — for that reason, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for and yet, then the Latin for therefore.

You'll want to know: idcircō.
idcircō — "for that reason, therefore".
Aeger erat; idcircō nōn vēnit. He was ill; for that reason he did not come.
Idcircō points ahead to a reason or an aim: idcircō … quod, for this reason, that; idcircō … ut, for this purpose, that.

The word, taken apart.
idcircō is built from id "that" and an old form of circā, circum "around, about" (Lewis & Short give id-circa, EtymDB id + circum): about that, for that reason.
