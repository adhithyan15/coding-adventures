Urdu, chapter 405: At the Front Desk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
رجسٹر (rajisṭar) — a register (book).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to drip, then the Urdu for a facility.

You'll want to know: رجسٹر.
رجسٹر — rajisṭar — "a register (book)".
ہوٹل والے نے رجسٹر میں میرا نام لکھا۔ hoṭal vāle ne rajisṭar meṅ merā nām likhā. The man at the hotel wrote my name in the register.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
رجسٹر (rajisṭar) is borrowed from English register.

دربان نے ہمارے لیے دروازہ کھولا۔ darbān ne hamāre liye darvāzā kholā. The doorman opened the door for us.

دربان (darbān) is built from Persian dar, "a door", and -bān, "a keeper": it is usually traced to Persian darbān, "a doorkeeper".

Lesson 4 of 5.
منگانا (mangānā) — to send for; to order (to be brought).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for identification, then the Urdu for a doorman.

You'll want to know: منگانا.
منگانا — mangānā — "to send for; to order (to be brought)".
ہم نے کمرے میں چائے منگائی۔ ham ne kamre meṅ chāe mangāī. We ordered tea to the room.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کھانا منگایا, us ne khānā mangāyā.

The word, taken apart.
منگانا (mangānā) is built on مانگنا, "to ask for", whose Hindi cousin māgnā CDIAL lists under Sanskrit mārgati, "seeks" (CDIAL 10074): it is to have something brought to you.
