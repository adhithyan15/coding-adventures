Persian, chapter 463: Souvenirs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سوغاتی (sowghâti) — a souvenir, a present brought back from a trip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to sell, then the Persian for a box.

You'll want to know: سوغاتی.
سوغاتی (sowghâti) — "a souvenir, a present brought back from a trip".
از سفر برای مادرم سوغاتی آوردم. Az safar barâ-ye mâdaram sowghâti âvardam. I brought my mother a present back from the trip.
Bringing sowghâti for family and friends is expected after a trip.

The word, taken apart.
sowghâti is built from sowghât 'a gift from a journey', usually traced to a Turkic word for a present, and the Persian ending -i. EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
صنایع دستی (sanâye'-e dasti) — handicrafts, crafts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a box, then the Persian for a souvenir.

You'll want to know: صنایع دستی.
صنایع دستی (sanâye'-e dasti) — "handicrafts, crafts".
در بازار صنایع دستی خریدیم. Dar bâzâr sanâye'-e dasti kharidim. We bought handicrafts in the bazaar.
sanâye' is an Arabic broken plural of san'at 'craft, industry'; dasti 'by hand'.

The word, taken apart.
sanâye'-e dasti is built from sanâye' 'crafts', the Arabic plural ṣanāʾiʿ that EtymDB lists, and native dasti 'by hand', from dast 'hand'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

گلیم (gelim) — "a kilim, a flat-woven rug".
روی زمین اتاق یک گلیم قرمز بود. Ru-ye zamin-e otâq yek gelim-e qermez bud. There was a red kilim on the floor of the room.
A gelim has no pile, unlike a farsh or qâli.

The word, taken apart.
gelim is a Persian word that EtymDB links with Greek kálymma 'a cover, a covering'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
جواهر (javâher) — jewels, jewellery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for handicrafts, then the Persian for a kilim.

You'll want to know: جواهر.
جواهر (javâher) — "jewels, jewellery".
در موزه جواهر قدیمی دیدیم. Dar muze javâher-e qadimi didim. We saw old jewels in the museum.
An Arabic broken plural of jowhar; a jeweller is a javâherforush.

The word, taken apart.
javâher came back into Persian from Arabic jawāhir 'jewels' (EtymDB), which itself was taken from Persian gowhar 'jewel', from Middle Persian and Old Persian gauthra 'substance, essence'.
