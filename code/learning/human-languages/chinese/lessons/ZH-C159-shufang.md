## You'll want to know: 书房
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C159-ROOM159-03]; assesses=[] -->

**书房** — *shūfáng* — "study; a room for books and work".

> **我的书都在书房里** *wǒ de shū dōu zài shūfáng lǐ* — All my books are in the study.

The new character, in pieces: 房 — eight strokes in two pieces: on top a door of four strokes (a dot, a horizontal that turns down, a horizontal closing it, a long stroke falling to the left), and under it the four strokes of the piece read fāng. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**书房** (*shūfáng*) is built from 书 'book' and 房 'room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *syo* and *bjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a room': the room for books.
