5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
temperar — to season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to shred, then the Portuguese for the bowl.

You'll want to know: temperar.
temperar — "to season".
A avó tempera a carne com alho e sal. Grandmother seasons the meat with garlic and salt.
Temperar a salada — to dress the salad.

The word, taken apart.
temperar comes from Latin temperāre "to mix in due proportion" (EtymDB), related to tempus "time, the right moment".

Lesson 3 of 5.
refogar — to sauté gently, to soften in oil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to season, then the Portuguese for the seasoning.

You'll want to know: refogar.
refogar — "to sauté gently, to soften in oil".
Primeiro, o cozinheiro refoga a cebola em azeite. First the cook softens the onion in olive oil.
The first step of countless Portuguese dishes.

The word, taken apart.
refogar is usually traced to re- and fogo "fire", from Latin focus "a hearth" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o refogado — the onion and olive oil base.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the seasoning, then the Portuguese for to sauté gently.

You'll want to know: o refogado.
o refogado — "the onion and olive oil base".
O refogado é a base de muitos pratos portugueses. The onion and olive oil base is the start of many Portuguese dishes.
Onion, garlic and olive oil, sometimes with tomato.

The word, taken apart.
refogado is the past participle of refogar used as a noun; the verb is usually traced to fogo "fire", from Latin focus (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
o lume brando — low heat, a low flame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sauté gently, then the Portuguese for the onion and olive oil base.

You'll want to know: o lume brando.
o lume brando — "low heat, a low flame".
A sopa ficou ao lume brando durante uma hora. The soup stayed on a low heat for an hour.
Em lume brando — on a low heat. In Brazil: fogo baixo.

The word, taken apart.
lume goes back to Latin lūmen "light", and brando to Latin blandus "gentle, soft" (EtymDB).
