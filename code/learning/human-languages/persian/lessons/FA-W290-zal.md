# ذ (zâl) — the letter inside گذشته

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C22-DAL-01, FA-SCRIPT-C22-ZE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write د and ز once each, and name them.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **گذشته** (*gozashte*), "last", as in *sâl-e gozashte*.
You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it
in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ذ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-SCRIPT-C290-ZAL-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C290-GOZASHTE-01] -->

**ذ** — *zâl*, the sound *z*.

It is the second letter of **گذشته**, straight after **گ**. It is **د** with
**one dot above**, and like **د** it takes the hand of the letter on its right
and offers nothing to the left: after **ذ** the pen lifts, and **شته** starts
fresh.

You now hold three ways to write *z*: **ز**, **ظ**, and **ذ**. Persian says
them the same. Most words with **ذ** came from Arabic; **گذشته** is one of a few
Persian words that kept it. The usual explanation is that early New Persian
softened a *d* after a vowel towards the *th* of English *this*, and scribes
wrote that sound with **ذ**.

## Writing: ذ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C290-ZAL-01] -->

Put your pen on ذ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start this letter or which way to
> travel**. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
