Lesson 1 of 6.
男 (nán) — male.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for elder sister, then the Mandarin for younger brother.

You'll want to know: 男.
男 — nán — "male".
我有一个男同学 wǒ yǒu yí ge nán tóngxué — One of my classmates is a boy.
The new character, in pieces: 男 (nán) — a field on top, built like 日 with a cross inside, and below it a two-stroke piece meaning strength. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
男 (nán) is inherited from Middle Chinese nom (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a man'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
男 (nán) — a field above, strength below.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a field above, strength below and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rì, kǒu]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a field above, strength below.
男 (nán).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
The top piece, a field, five strokes. A vertical down the left. A horizontal across the top that turns down the right side. The short horizontal across the middle. The short vertical down the middle. Then the horizontal that closes the bottom. It is 日 with a cross inside instead of a single bar.
The bottom piece, strength, two strokes. A horizontal that turns down and hooks to the left, then a long left-falling stroke through it.
5 + 2 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 男 (nán), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 男 (nán)? (Two — the field, then strength.) Which character you know does the field look like? (日, with a cross inside.) Which stroke closes the field? (The bottom horizontal, the fifth.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 男 (nán), pinned stroke paths and medians.

男人 (nánrén) is built from 男 (nán) 'male' and 人 'person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nom and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 6 of 6.
háizi — child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for woman, then the Mandarin for younger sister.

You'll want to know: háizi.
háizi — "child".
tāmen yǒu sān ge háizi — They have three children.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
háizi is built from hái 'child' and the noun ending zi. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese hoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a newborn child'; the ending is tsiX, 'child', the same piece as the one in 儿子.
