Spanish, chapter 712: Mi estado de ánimo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la sensación — the sensation, the feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for moving, then the Spanish for to move.

You'll want to know: la sensación.
la sensación — "the sensation, the feeling".
Tengo la sensación de que va a llover. I have a feeling it is going to rain.
Tengo la sensación de que... — I have the feeling that...

The word, taken apart.
sensación is usually described as a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin sēnsātiō, built on sēnsus "feeling", from sentīre "to feel".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: el ánimo.
el ánimo — "mood, spirits; courage".
¡Ánimo! Tú puedes. Come on! You can do it.
¡Ánimo! is what you shout to encourage someone. Estado de ánimo — mood.

The word, taken apart.
ánimo is a learned borrowing from Latin animus "spirit, mind" (EtymDB); English unanimous, of one mind, is built on it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
desanimado — discouraged, down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sensation, then the Spanish for mood.

You'll want to know: desanimado.
desanimado — "discouraged, down".
Está desanimado porque no encuentra trabajo. He is down because he cannot find work.
Used with estar. The opposite is animado.

The word, taken apart.
desanimado is built on animado with the prefix des-; animar comes from Latin animāre "to give life", from anima "breath, soul" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
animar — to cheer up, to encourage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for mood, then the Spanish for discouraged.

You'll want to know: animar.
animar — "to cheer up, to encourage".
Mis amigos me animan a seguir. My friends encourage me to keep going.
Animar a alguien a + infinitive. Animarse — to cheer up, to decide to do something.

The word, taken apart.
animar is inherited from Latin animāre "to fill with breath, to give life" (EtymDB), from anima "breath, soul".
