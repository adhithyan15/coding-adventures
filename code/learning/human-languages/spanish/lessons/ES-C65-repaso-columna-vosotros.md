# Review — the column that belongs to *vosotros*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VOSOTROS, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-VERB-PRESENT-2PL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The pronoun for "you all," in Spain. (*Vosotros*.) "You all go."
(*Vais*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-IR-VERB-PRESENT-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-VUESTRO] -->

These arrived one tense at a time, chapters apart. Collected, they are one
column.

[PAUSE 2s] **Present.** *Vais.*
[PAUSE 2s] **Preterite, ordinary stem.** *Comisteis.*
[PAUSE 2s] **Preterite, strong stem.** *Tuvisteis.*
[PAUSE 2s] **And what belongs to you all.** *Vuestro.*

Four forms, four different chapters, one addressee.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-VUESTRO, ES-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-COMPLETE, ES-LEX-VOSOTROS] -->

The pairing is what makes this cheap to hold.

> *nosotros* → *nuestro*.  ·  *vosotros* → *vuestro*.

Same shape, same ending, same job. Once you see the first pair the second costs
nothing — and with *vuestro* in place, the possessives have no gaps left.

[PAUSE 2s] Worth saying plainly: most Spanish speakers, across the Americas, do
not use this column at all. They say *ustedes* and reach for the *they* forms.
You are learning it because you will **read** and **hear** it constantly, not
because you will be required to produce it.
