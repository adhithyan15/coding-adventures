Latin, chapter 347: Mōnstra et hērōēs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mōnstrum, mōnstrī — a monster; a portent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for mortal, then the Latin for immortal.

You'll want to know: mōnstrum, mōnstrī.
mōnstrum, mōnstrī — "a monster; a portent".
Mōnstrum ingēns in spēluncā habitābat. A huge monster lived in the cave.
Neuter, from moneō, 'to warn': first a sign sent by the gods, then a monster. English monster.

The word, taken apart.
mōnstrum is built on moneō, "to warn" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short): a warning sign, a portent, and then a monster. English monster comes from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
hērōs, hērōis — a hero, a demigod.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a monster, then the Latin for a giant.

You'll want to know: hērōs, hērōis.
hērōs, hērōis — "a hero, a demigod".
Hērōs mōnstrum gladiō interfēcit. The hero killed the monster with a sword.
Masculine, a Greek loan; accusative hērōa. A man of the old stories, often the son of a god.

The word, taken apart.
hērōs was borrowed from Greek hērōs, "demigod, hero" (EtymDB). English hero comes from it.

Feminine, a Greek loan; also 'a bride, a young woman'. Spirits of springs, trees and mountains.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vellus, velleris — a fleece.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hero, then the Latin for a nymph.

You'll want to know: vellus, velleris.
vellus, velleris — "a fleece".
Hērōēs vellus aureum per tōtum mare quaesīvērunt. The heroes searched the whole sea for the golden fleece.
Neuter, 3rd declension. A sheep's shorn wool; the golden one was guarded by a serpent.

The word, taken apart.
vellus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "wool, hair" (EtymDB); English wool is a cousin.
