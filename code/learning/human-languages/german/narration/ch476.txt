German, chapter 476: Stur und launisch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
stur — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for modest, then the German for vain.

You'll want to know: stur.
stur — "stubborn".
Mein Opa ist stur und ändert nie seine Meinung. My grandpa is stubborn and never changes his mind.
Informal: stur wie ein Esel — stubborn as a mule.

The word, taken apart.
stur is usually traced to Low German stūr "stiff, unyielding", which spread into standard German; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
hartnäckig — persistent, dogged.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for vain, then the German for stubborn.

You'll want to know: hartnäckig.
hartnäckig — "persistent, dogged".
Der Husten ist hartnäckig und geht nicht weg. The cough is persistent and won't go away.
Of people too: Er fragte hartnäckig weiter.

The word, taken apart.
hartnäckig is built on hart "hard" and the word for the back of the neck: someone with a stiff neck who will not bow. Hart is inherited from Old High German hart, from Proto-Germanic harduz (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
launisch — moody.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for stubborn, then the German for persistent.

You'll want to know: launisch.
launisch — "moody".
Am Morgen ist meine Schwester oft launisch. My sister is often moody in the morning.
Built on die Laune — mood: gute Laune, schlechte Laune.

The word, taken apart.
launisch is built on Laune "mood", which goes back to Middle High German lūne and is usually traced to Latin lūna "moon" (Lewis & Short): moods were thought to change with the moon.

Lesson 4 of 5.
gereizt — irritable, irritated.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for persistent, then the German for moody.

You'll want to know: gereizt.
gereizt — "irritable, irritated".
Nach der langen Fahrt waren alle müde und gereizt. After the long drive everyone was tired and irritable.
The participle of reizen — to irritate, to provoke.

The word, taken apart.
gereizt is the participle of reizen "to provoke, to irritate", a native verb usually traced to Old High German reizzen; EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
empfindlich — sensitive, touchy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for moody, then the German for irritable.

You'll want to know: empfindlich.
empfindlich — "sensitive, touchy".
Im Sommer ist meine Haut sehr empfindlich. In summer my skin is very sensitive.
Of people: easily hurt, touchy.

The word, taken apart.
empfindlich is built on empfinden "to feel, to sense" with -lich; empfinden is finden "to find" with the prefix ent- (EtymDB lists both German words).
