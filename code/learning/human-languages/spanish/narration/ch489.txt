Spanish, chapter 489: Por la ciudad.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aparcar — to park.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to say goodbye, then the Spanish for to greet.

You'll want to know: aparcar.
aparcar — "to park".
No se puede aparcar aquí. You cannot park here.
The verb behind el aparcamiento, the car park. In Latin America people say estacionar.

The word, taken apart.
aparcar is built on parque, "a park", in the sense of a place where vehicles are kept; parque came from French parc.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el atasco — the traffic jam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to greet, then the Spanish for to park.

You'll want to know: el atasco.
el atasco — "the traffic jam".
Hay un atasco a la entrada de la ciudad. There is a traffic jam at the entrance to the city.
In much of Latin America people say el embotellamiento or el tráfico.

The word, taken apart.
atasco is the noun of atascar, "to block, to clog", usually explained as a- and tasco, the coarse fibre left from beating flax or hemp, once used to plug gaps; where tasco comes from is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la gasolina — the petrol, the gas (for a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to park, then the Spanish for the traffic jam.

You'll want to know: la gasolina.
la gasolina — "the petrol, the gas (for a car)".
Tengo que echar gasolina. I have to put petrol in.
La gasolinera is the petrol station.

The word, taken apart.
gasolina from English gasoline (EtymDB 2.1), built on gas, a word coined in the 1600s by the Flemish chemist Van Helmont from Greek khaos.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el ayuntamiento — the town hall, the city council.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the traffic jam, then the Spanish for the petrol.

You'll want to know: el ayuntamiento.
el ayuntamiento — "the town hall, the city council".
El ayuntamiento abre a las nueve. The town hall opens at nine.

The word, taken apart.
ayuntamiento first meant a joining, a meeting: it is built on the old verb ayuntar, "to join", from Latin adiunctus, "joined to", a relative of junto, "together". The council is the meeting.
