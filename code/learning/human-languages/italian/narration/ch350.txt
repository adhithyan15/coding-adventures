Italian, chapter 350: Siccome e poiché.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
siccome — since, as (at the start of a sentence).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the factor, then the Italian for the reason.

You'll want to know: siccome.
siccome — "since, as (at the start of a sentence)".
Siccome piove, prendiamo la macchina. Since it is raining, we will take the car.
Siccome opens the sentence; perché comes after the main clause.

The word, taken apart.
siccome is built from sì "so", inherited from Latin sīc "thus", and come "as", inherited from Latin quōmodo "in what way" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
poiché — given that, since (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the reason, then the Italian for since.

You'll want to know: poiché.
poiché — "given that, since (formal)".
Il museo è chiuso, poiché oggi è lunedì. The museum is closed, since today is Monday.
Written or formal; in speech perché or siccome are usual.

The word, taken apart.
poiché is built from poi "then, after", inherited from Latin post "after" (Lewis & Short), and che "that": first "after that", then "since".

Lesson 3 of 5.
perciò — so, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for since, then the Italian for given that.

You'll want to know: perciò.
perciò — "so, therefore".
Ho perso il treno, perciò sono arrivato tardi. I missed the train, so I arrived late.
A little more formal than quindi; very frequent in writing and speech.

The word, taken apart.
perciò is built from per "for" and ciò "this", which comes from Latin ecce hoc "look, this" (Lewis & Short): "for this", and so "therefore".

Lesson 4 of 5.
pertanto — therefore, consequently (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for given that, then the Italian for so.

You'll want to know: pertanto.
pertanto — "therefore, consequently (formal)".
Il negozio è chiuso ad agosto; pertanto non possiamo cambiare il regalo. The shop is closed in August; consequently we cannot exchange the present.
Formal and written. Careful: in some regions and older texts it can mean however.

The word, taken apart.
pertanto is built from per "for" and tanto "so much", inherited from Latin tantum "so much" (Lewis & Short): "for so much", so "therefore".

Lesson 5 of 5.
dovuto — due (to), caused (by); owed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for so, then the Italian for therefore.

You'll want to know: dovuto.
dovuto — "due (to), caused (by); owed".
Il ritardo è dovuto al traffico. The delay is due to the traffic.
Essere dovuto a — to be due to. It is also the past participle of dovere: ho dovuto aspettare.

The word, taken apart.
dovuto is the past participle of dovere, inherited from Latin dēbēre "to owe, to be bound to" (Lewis & Short): what is owed to a cause.
