Latin, chapter 331: Lūctus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lūctus, lūctūs — grief, mourning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a funeral pyre, then the Latin for a funeral procession.

You'll want to know: lūctus, lūctūs.
lūctus, lūctūs — "grief, mourning".
Tōta domus in lūctū erat. The whole household was in mourning.
Masculine, 4th declension, from lūgeō, 'to mourn' (taught). Mourners wore dark clothes.

The word, taken apart.
lūctus is built on lūgeō, "to mourn" (EtymDB): sorrow shown for the dead.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nēnia, nēniae — a dirge, a funeral song.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a funeral procession, then the Latin for grief.

You'll want to know: nēnia, nēniae.
nēnia, nēniae — "a dirge, a funeral song".
Fēminae nēniam trīstem canēbant. The women were singing a sad dirge.
Feminine, also spelled naenia. Hired women sang it in praise of the dead; later also a lullaby or a silly song.

The word, taken apart.
nēnia is a word whose origin is not settled (Lewis & Short).

Before the new one: say the Latin for grief, then the Latin for a dirge.

laudātiō, laudātiōnis — "a speech of praise, a eulogy".
Fīlius in forō laudātiōnem patris habuit. The son gave a speech in praise of his father in the forum.
Feminine, from laudō, 'to praise' (taught). Laudātiō fūnebris: the funeral speech.

The word, taken apart.
laudātiō is built on laudō, "to praise" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tiō.

Lesson 4 of 5.
monumentum, monumentī — a memorial, a monument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a dirge, then the Latin for a speech of praise.

You'll want to know: monumentum, monumentī.
monumentum, monumentī — "a memorial, a monument".
Fīliī patrī monumentum ad viam posuērunt. The sons set up a memorial to their father by the road.
Neuter, from moneō, 'to remind' (taught): anything that keeps a memory alive, a tomb, a statue or a book. English monument.

The word, taken apart.
monumentum is built on moneō, "to remind", with the ending -mentum (EtymDB): a reminder. English monument comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
īnferiae, īnferiārum — offerings to the dead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a speech of praise, then the Latin for a memorial.

You'll want to know: īnferiae, īnferiārum.
īnferiae, īnferiārum — "offerings to the dead".
Fīliī īnferiās ad sepulcrum patris tulērunt. The sons brought offerings to their father's tomb.
Feminine, plural only. Wine, milk and flowers were poured or laid at the grave.

The word, taken apart.
īnferiae is built on īnferī, "those below, the dead", from īnferus, "lower" (Lewis & Short): gifts for those below.
