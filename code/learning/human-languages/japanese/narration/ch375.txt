Japanese, chapter 375: The First Train and the Last.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しはつ (shihatsu) — the first train of the day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for departure (of a train or bus), then the Japanese for to go near.

You'll want to know: しはつ.
しはつ — shihatsu — "the first train of the day".
しはつは ごじに でます。 — shihatsu wa goji ni demasu — "The first train leaves at five."

The word, taken apart.
しはつ (shihatsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "begin" (Middle Chinese syiX) and hatsu, "departure" (pjot) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
のりこす (norikosu) — to ride past one's stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the last train of the day, then the Japanese for the terminus.

You'll want to know: のりこす.
のりこす — norikosu — "to ride past one's stop".
でんしゃで しんぶんを よんで、えきを のりこしました。 — densha de shinbun o yonde, eki o norikoshimashita — "I read the newspaper on the train and rode past my station."
Polite のりこします, norikoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが えきを のりこしました。, ani ga eki o norikoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
のりこす (norikosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from nori, "riding" (from noru) and kosu, "to cross over"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
