# solicitar — the word the form uses

## You'll want to know: solicitar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C460-PEDIR-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02] -->

**solicitar** — to apply for, to request, formally.

> Para **solicitar** una plaza, entregue la ficha antes del día 20.

*Solicitar una beca* — to apply for a grant. *Solicitar cita previa* — to book
an appointment in advance, the phrase on Spanish health-centre and town-hall
pages; much of Latin America says *sacar turno* or *agendar una cita* for the
same act.

**La solicitud** is the application itself, and the piece of paper it is
written on.

## Grammar Lens: solicitar is not pedir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C460-PEDIR-02, ES-LEX-C460-PEDIR-01] -->

| what you are doing | the verb |
|---|---|
| asking a person for a thing | **pedir** |
| asking an institution, on a form | **solicitar** |

*Le pido un café al camarero.* *Solicito una beca al ministerio.* Using
*solicitar* with a friend would sound as though you had filled in a form to
borrow their drill.

That is the whole difference and it is register, not meaning: *solicitar* is
the bookish arrival, worn nowhere down, and it belongs to writing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C460-PEDIR-02, ES-LEX-C356-CLOCK-22] -->

Latin **sollicitare**: **sollus**, whole, plus **citare**, to set in motion —
so to stir something *entirely*, to shake it up.

The oldest sense is agitation, and Spanish keeps that branch too: *estar
solícito* is to be attentive, and English **solicitous** means anxiously
caring, while **solicitude** is worry. To apply for something is to set the
machinery in motion on your behalf.

That **citare** is the one inside **la cita** — an appointment is a *summoning*
— and English **cite**, **excite**, **recite** and **incite** are all the same
setting-in-motion.
