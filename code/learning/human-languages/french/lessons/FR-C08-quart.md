# et quart — quarter past

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Whole hours are not enough to make an appointment. Three small
additions finish the clock, and the first is already half-known to you from
counting.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-QUART-09]; assesses=[] -->

- **quart** = *kar*. The **qu-** is a plain *k*, and the final **t** is silent.
- The **r** is the throat-**r**, as in **quatre**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**quart** is "a fourth part," and it comes from the same Latin **quattuor**
that gave you **quatre**. English took the identical word and made **quarter**
— a fourth of an hour, a fourth of a gallon, a fourth of a town.

Once you see *quatre* inside *quart*, it stops being a new word and becomes a
number you already have, doing a different job.

## Grammar Lens: adding to the hour
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

French adds the quarter with **et** ("and"), after the hour:

> **Il est deux heures et quart.** — "It is a quarter past two."

Read literally: *"it is two hours **and a quarter**."* French does not say
"past"; it adds.
