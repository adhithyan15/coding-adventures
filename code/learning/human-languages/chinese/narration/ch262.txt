Mandarin Chinese, chapter 262: Win or Lose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wǎngqiú — tennis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for overseas, then the Mandarin for to experience for yourself.

You'll want to know: wǎngqiú.
wǎngqiú — "tennis".
wǒ xǐhuan wǎngqiú — I like tennis.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wǎngqiú is built from 网 'a net' and qiú 'a ball', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjangX and gjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a net' and the second 'a fine jade'; under another spelling of the second it glosses a ball of fur that is struck.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pīngpāngqiú — table tennis; ping-pong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to experience for yourself, then the Mandarin for tennis.

You'll want to know: pīngpāngqiú.
pīngpāngqiú — "table tennis; ping-pong".
tā shì pīngpāngqiú gāoshǒu — He's a table tennis ace.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pīngpāngqiú is built from pīngpāng, which imitates the sound of the ball, and qiú 'a ball', inherited from Middle Chinese gjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). No dictionary we use records an older form of the first part, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
对手 (duìshǒu) — opponent; rival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for tennis, then the Mandarin for table tennis.

You'll want to know: 对手.
对手 — duìshǒu — "opponent; rival".
他是我的对手 tā shì wǒ de duìshǒu — He's my opponent.

The word, taken apart.
对手 (duìshǒu) is built from 对 and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, twojH and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to answer' and 'to match': the hand that matches yours.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dǎbài — to defeat; to beat (an opponent).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for table tennis, then the Mandarin for an opponent.

You'll want to know: dǎbài.
dǎbài — "to defeat; to beat (an opponent)".
wǒmen dǎbàile tāmen — We beat them.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎbài is built from 打 'to strike' and bài, both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and baejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'to fall to ruin by itself'. Middle Chinese taengX would regularly give dǎng; dǎ is irregular.

Lesson 5 of 5.
jiāyóu — to cheer someone on; come on!

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an opponent, then the Mandarin for to defeat.

You'll want to know: jiāyóu.
jiāyóu — "to cheer someone on; come on!".
jiāyóu wǒmen néng dǎbài tāmen — Come on, we can beat them!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiāyóu is built from jiā 'to add' and yóu 'oil', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and yuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to increase' and the second 'oil'. How adding oil came to mean cheering someone on is not settled.
