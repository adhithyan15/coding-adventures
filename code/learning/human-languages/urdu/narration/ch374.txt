Urdu, chapter 374: Leaning on Others.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سہارا (sahārā) — a support; someone to lean on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to set (of the sun), then the Urdu for unfinished.

You'll want to know: سہارا.
سہارا — sahārā — "a support; someone to lean on".
اگر آپ کا سہارا نہ ہوتا تو میں گر جاتا۔ agar āp kā sahārā na hotā to maiṅ gir jātā. If you had not held me up, I would have fallen.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
سہارا (sahārā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin sahārā, "help", under Sanskrit sahakāra, "assistance" (CDIAL 13299).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ضد (zid) — stubbornness; insistence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for unfinished, then the Urdu for someone to lean on.

You'll want to know: ضد.
ضد — zid — "stubbornness; insistence".
اگر تم نے ضد کی تو کچھ نہیں ملے گا۔ agar tum ne zid kī to kuchh nahīṅ mile gā. If you are stubborn, you will get nothing.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ضد (zid) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḍidd, "the opposite"; Urdu uses it for stubborn insistence, a sense our sources do not trace.

Lesson 4 of 5.
انحصار (inhisār) — reliance; dependence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for stubbornness, then the Urdu for eligible.

You'll want to know: انحصار.
انحصار — inhisār — "reliance; dependence".
اگر انحصار صرف بارش پر ہے تو مشکل ہو گی۔ agar inhisār sirf bārish par hai to mushkil ho gī. If everything depends on the rain alone, things will be hard.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
انحصار (inhisār) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic inḥiṣār, "being confined, being limited", of the same family as منحصر, "depending".

Lesson 5 of 5.
اصرار کرنا (isrār karnā) — to insist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for eligible, then the Urdu for reliance.

You'll want to know: اصرار کرنا.
اصرار کرنا — isrār karnā — "to insist".
اگر آپ اصرار کرتے ہیں تو میں آؤں گا۔ agar āp isrār karte haiṅ to maiṅ āūṅ gā. If you insist, I will come.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے آنے پر اصرار کیا, us ne āne par isrār kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
اصرار کرنا (isrār karnā) is built from isrār, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic iṣrār, "persisting", and کرنا, "to do".
