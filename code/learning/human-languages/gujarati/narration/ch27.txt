8 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
ગામ (gām) — hear village first.

Warm-up.
Recall shahar, city. Now listen twice: gām. It means village.

The word, taken apart.
ગામ (gām) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from grāma, "troop, village" (CDIAL 4368).

Guided Practice.
Choose city or village as you hear shahar or gām, then say gām once.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Cambridge: village.

Lesson 4 of 8.
દુકાન (dukān) — hear shop first.

Warm-up.
Listen twice: dukān. It means shop. Hear the short u before the long final ā.

The word, taken apart.
દુકાન (dukān) is a loan from Arabic dukkān, "shop", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is d-k-n (Lane).

Guided Practice.
Picture a shop when you hear dukān, then say dukān from the meaning.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Cambridge: shop.
