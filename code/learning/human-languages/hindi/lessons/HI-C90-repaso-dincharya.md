# dincaryā kī tālikā — a day, in the order it happens

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the verb a day starts with and the noun you travel to, before
you look at the table.

## Grammar Lens: the six
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06] -->

| word | what it adds to a day |
|---|---|
| उठना | the action it starts with |
| काम | the place it goes to |
| बस | what carries you there |
| से | from, and by |
| पहले | which of two came first |
| के बाद | which came second |

Four of the six are not vocabulary in the ordinary sense. They are **joints**:
things that let two sentences be put in a relation instead of standing side by
side.

## Grammar Lens: the three small words, side by side
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06] -->

| small word | what it marks |
|---|---|
| पर | at a point |
| से | from it, or by way of it |
| के बाद | later than it |

All three trail their noun. That is now the whole postposition habit, and the
only thing left to learn about any new one is which meaning it carries.

## Grammar Lens: a day, assembled
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-04, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-05, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-02] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| पहले मैं सुबह उठता हूँ। | First I get up in the morning. |
| मैं बस से काम पर जाता हूँ। | I go to work by bus. |
| काम के बाद मैं घर आता हूँ। | After work I come home. |

Three sentences, in order, every word of which is yours.
