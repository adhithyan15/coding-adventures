Russian, chapter 28: The Small Ya.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
я (ya) — the word for I, written small.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: я and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write й and ю once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say я (ya), "I". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: я.
я — small ya.
я (ya) is also a whole word: "I". You wrote the capital Я in the letter chapters, but Russian writes "I" with the small letter: inside a sentence it is never capitalised, unlike English "I". So this is the shape you will write most.

Writing: я.
1. start here: climb the right stem from the baseline to the top.
2. without lifting, curve counterclockwise around the upper bowl.
3. without lifting, sweep left through the bowl's lower join.
4. without lifting, descend the diagonal leg to the lower-left tip — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: RussianIrina, ‘Learning Russian - Alphabet letters, handwriting’, lowercase я (ya) demonstration, 12:13–12:21, YouTube, 5 February 2013.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at я (ya) and point at я (ya) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: я three times, saying ya as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ю, then я (ya), from memory]
[your turn — read: я (ya) aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — я? (ya, ya.) Which word did it come from? (я, ya, "I".)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(one letter) (odna bukva) — From the letter to the word — a first pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say я (ya), then write the letter it gave you.

Guided Practice.
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: я. name: ya. the word it came from: я ya.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter in the table from memory, then check it]
[your turn — say: the word each one came from]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: each word in the table aloud, and point at its letter]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word did each letter come from? (я (ya) from я (ya).)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(one letter) (odna bukva) — From the word back to the letter — a second pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write я (ya) without looking, then say the word it came from.

Guided Practice.
[a table of 1 row, read aloud]
letter: я. name: ya. the word it came from: я ya.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: each word in the table, then write the letter it gave you without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter once more, slowly, larger than it is printed]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say each word in the table once more, and name the letter you took from it.
