Arabic, chapter 94: Clothes, Shirt, Dress, Trousers, Sock.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ملابس (malābis) — clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a city, then the Arabic for a restaurant.

You'll want to know: ملابس.
ملابس — malābis — "clothes".

The word, taken apart.
ملابس (malābis) is built on the native Arabic root l-b-s, "to put on, to wear" (Lane). Malābis is the plural of malbas, "a thing worn".

Lesson 2 of 5.
قميص (qamīṣ) — a shirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a restaurant, then the Arabic for clothes.

You'll want to know: قميص.
قميص — qamīṣ — "a shirt".

The word, taken apart.
قميص (qamīṣ) is listed by Lane under q-m-ṣ as a well-known sewn garment with sleeves. It is usually traced to Late Latin camisia, "shirt" (EtymDB), probably through Greek or Aramaic.

Lesson 3 of 5.
فستان (fustān) — a dress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for clothes, then the Arabic for a shirt.

You'll want to know: فستان.
فستان — fustān — "a dress".

The word, taken apart.
فستان (fustān) is a later loan, usually traced through Turkish fistan to Greek phoustani, "skirt"; the chain is not fully settled. It has no Arabic root.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سروال (sirwāl) — trousers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a shirt, then the Arabic for a dress.

You'll want to know: سروال.
سروال — sirwāl — "trousers".

The word, taken apart.
سروال (sirwāl) is a loan: Lane records it as a Persian word, originally shalwār, "drawers, trousers", arabicized, and quotes Sībawayh calling it foreign.
