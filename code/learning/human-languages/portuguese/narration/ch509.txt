Portuguese, chapter 509: Na baliza.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a baliza — goal (the posts and net).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for stand, then the Portuguese for substitute.

You'll want to know: a baliza.
a baliza — "goal (the posts and net)".
A bola bateu no poste e não entrou na baliza. The ball hit the post and did not go into the goal.
In Brazil: o gol. A scored goal is um golo in Portugal.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of baliza, once a marker post or a buoy, is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
o penálti — penalty (kick).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for substitute, then the Portuguese for goal.

You'll want to know: o penálti.
o penálti — "penalty (kick)".
O árbitro marcou um penálti no último minuto. The referee gave a penalty in the last minute.
In Brazil: o pênalti. A more formal name is a grande penalidade.

The word, taken apart.
penálti was borrowed from English penalty, which goes back through French and Medieval Latin to Latin poena "a punishment" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o avançado — forward, striker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for goal, then the Portuguese for penalty.

You'll want to know: o avançado.
o avançado — "forward, striker".
O avançado marcou dois golos. The striker scored two goals.
In Brazil: o atacante.

The word, taken apart.
avançado is built on avançar "to move forward", which goes back to Vulgar Latin abanteāre (EtymDB), built on ab ante "from in front".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rematar — to shoot (at goal); to finish off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for penalty, then the Portuguese for forward.

You'll want to know: rematar.
rematar — "to shoot (at goal); to finish off".
O avançado rematou com o pé esquerdo. The striker shot with his left foot.
A shot is um remate.

The word, taken apart.
rematar is built from re- and matar in its old sense "to finish, to end"; matar is usually traced to Latin mactāre "to slaughter" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: cabecear.
cabecear — "to head (the ball)".
O defesa cabeceou a bola para fora. The defender headed the ball out.
Built on a cabeça.

The word, taken apart.
cabecear is built on cabeça "head", which goes back to a Vulgar Latin form of Latin caput "head" (Lewis & Short).
