Mandarin Chinese, chapter 242: Farm Animals and Bugs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zhū — pig.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to leave hospital, then the Mandarin for to recover.

You'll want to know: zhū.
zhū — "pig".
tā jiā yǒu sān zhī zhū — His family keeps three pigs.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhū is inherited from Middle Chinese trjo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it, after an older dictionary, as the young of the pig.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
shānyáng — goat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to recover, then the Mandarin for a pig.

You'll want to know: shānyáng.
shānyáng — "goat".
zhè zhī shānyáng hěn xǐhuan chī cǎo — This goat loves eating grass.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shānyáng is built from 山 'hill' and yáng 'sheep'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese srean (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second, yang there, with the pair 'oxen and sheep'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
chóngzi — insect; bug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a goat, then the Mandarin for a snake.

You'll want to know: chóngzi.
chóngzi — "insect; bug".
wǒ nǚ'ér bú pà chóngzi — My daughter isn't afraid of bugs.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chóngzi is built from chóng 'insect' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjuwng and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which says, after an older dictionary, that creatures with feet are called by the first.

What you've built.

wénzi is built from wén 'mosquito' and the noun ending 子. The noun ending is inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the first, mjun there, beside another form of the character.
