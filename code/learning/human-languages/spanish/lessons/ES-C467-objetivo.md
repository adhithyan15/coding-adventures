# el objetivo — the target

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C345-REF-05, ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el objeto** and **rotativo**. One is the word underneath the
next; the other is its ending.

## You'll want to know: el objetivo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03] -->

**el objetivo** — the target, the aim, the objective.

> **Objetivo** del mes: alimentar a cincuenta familias.

*Cumplir el objetivo* — to meet the target. *Nuestro objetivo es…* — our aim
is… As an adjective it means **objective**: *un informe objetivo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04, ES-LEX-C345-REF-05] -->

You have the whole of this already. *El objeto* was **ob-**, before or
against, plus **iacere**, to throw — a thing *thrown before* you, put in your
way. That lesson listed the family: *object*, *project*, *inject*, *eject*,
*subject*, all of them throws.

*Objetivo* is that same thrown thing wearing **-ivo**: *to do with the thing
placed in front*. The throw does not turn round — the grammatical object is
already what an action is thrown at, as *el objeto*'s own chapter said. What
narrowed is **why** the thing is there.

| word | the thing placed before you |
|---|---|
| el objeto | whatever is there |
| el objetivo | the one put there to be reached |

English kept both jobs on the same word: an *objective* is an aim, and an
**objective lens** is the one that faces the object.

## Grammar Lens: -ivo makes an adjective that can stand alone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04, ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03] -->

*Rotativo* gave you **-ivo**: an ending that names a habit or a capacity — of
the kind that does this.

What *objetivo* adds is that such an adjective can harden into a noun and keep
both jobs:

| use | example |
|---|---|
| adjective | *un informe objetivo* |
| noun | *el objetivo del mes* |

Spanish does this often and English follows it here, which makes the pair easy
to trust: *an objective report*, *the month's objective*.
