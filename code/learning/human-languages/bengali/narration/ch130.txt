মানিব্যাগ (mānibyāg) — a wallet.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: মানিব্যাগ.
মানিব্যাগ — mānibyāg — "a wallet".

The word, taken apart.
মানিব্যাগ (mānibyāg) is a loan from English money-bag. It is English money + bag.

You'll want to know: পয়সা.
পয়সা — pôysā — "a coin".

The word, taken apart.
পয়সা (pôysā) is the coin CDIAL traces to padāṁśa, "quarter part", a form it reconstructs. It gives no Bengali form, and marks Oriya paisā, "quarter anna", as taken from Hindi (CDIAL 7761).

Lesson 3 of 5.
খাম (khām) — an envelope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a wallet, then the Bengali for a coin.

You'll want to know: খাম.
খাম — khām — "an envelope".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of খাম (khām) is not settled. CDIAL has no entry for it in the sense "envelope"; its Bengali khām is a different word, "pillar, post", from Sanskrit skambha (CDIAL 13639).

The word, taken apart.
ডাকটিকিট (ḍākṭikiṭ) is ḍāk, "post", which CDIAL lists under ḍakka, "relay, post", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5519), + ṭikiṭ, from English ticket.

Lesson 5 of 5.
পুতুল (putul) — a doll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an envelope, then the Bengali for a stamp.

You'll want to know: পুতুল.
পুতুল — putul — "a doll".

The word, taken apart.
পুতুল (putul) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from putrala, "puppet, pupil of eye", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 8269). Late Sanskrit has puttalikā, "puppet".
