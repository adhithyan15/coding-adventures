# je dormais / j'ai dormi — the same sleep, told two ways

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-IL-FAISAIT-01, FR-LEX-IL-Y-AVAIT-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Paint a cold morning in two short sentences. (*Il faisait froid.
Il n'y avait pas de soleil.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Nothing has happened in it yet. This lesson is about what
happens.

## Grammar Lens: the scene and the events
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-DORMIR-04, FR-GRAM-PASSE-COMPOSE-02, FR-LEX-MERE-03] -->

Take one fact, that you slept, and let the tense decide what kind of fact it
is:

> **Je dormais.** — I was sleeping. *(no edge: what was going on)*
> **J'ai dormi.** — I slept. *(an edge: a thing that happened and ended)*

Neither is more correct. A told story needs both, in a division of labour:

> The **imparfait** paints the **scene**: what was going on, what things
> were like.
> The **passé composé** drops the **events** into it: what happened.

*Il faisait froid. Je dormais. Ma mère a téléphoné.* — It was cold. I was
asleep. My mother phoned.

English has no separate ending for this. It leans on *was ‑ing* and *used
to* and lets the context carry the rest. French settles it in the verb.

## Grammar Lens: what used to happen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C140-PAS140-04, FR-GRAM-IMPARFAIT-01] -->

A habit is scene too. It went on, again and again, with no single edge:

> *Autrefois, je dormais beaucoup.* — In the old days, I used to sleep a lot.

So the imparfait answers two questions: what was going on at that moment,
and what used to happen. The passé composé answers a third: what happened,
once, and was done.

## The word, taken apart: why "imparfait"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-IL-FAISAIT-01] -->

**imparfait** is inherited from Latin *imperfectus*, "unfinished,
incomplete" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short): *in-*, "not", plus *perfectus*,
"carried all the way through", which is itself *per-*, "through", plus
*facere*, "to make". That *facere* is the one inside last lesson's
*faisait*.

So the name describes the job. The imparfait is the **not-carried-through**
past: unfinished in time, with no edge on it.
