Spanish, chapter 512: Lo que pasó.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
producirse — to occur, to take place (of an event).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to seem, then the Spanish for to keep.

You'll want to know: producirse.
producirse — "to occur, to take place (of an event)".
El accidente se produjo a las seis de la tarde. The accident occurred at six in the evening.
Common in the news. It changes like conducir: se produjo, se produjeron.

The word, taken apart.
producirse is built on producir 'to produce', a learned borrowing from Latin prōdūcere 'to lead forth, to bring out' (EtymDB), from prō 'forward' and dūcere 'to lead'. An event that occurs is one brought out into the open.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el suceso — the happening, the incident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to keep, then the Spanish for to occur.

You'll want to know: el suceso.
el suceso — "the happening, the incident".
El suceso ocurrió el lunes. The incident took place on Monday.
In a newspaper, la sección de sucesos is the page of accidents and crimes. It is a false friend: success is el éxito.

The word, taken apart.
suceso comes from Latin successus 'an advance, an outcome' (EtymDB), built on succēdere 'to come up after', from sub and cēdere 'to go' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: el acontecimiento.
el acontecimiento — "the event, the happening".
La boda de la princesa fue un gran acontecimiento. The princess's wedding was a big event.
Used for important or public events.

The word, taken apart.
acontecimiento is built on acontecer 'to come about' with the ending -miento (EtymDB). Acontecer goes back to Latin contingere 'to touch, to befall' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
acontecer — to come about; to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the happening, then the Spanish for the event.

You'll want to know: acontecer.
acontecer — "to come about; to happen".
Nadie sabe qué va a acontecer. Nobody knows what is going to come about.
A formal word, mostly in the third person: aconteció. It takes a z in acontezca.

The word, taken apart.
acontecer is built on old Spanish contecer with the prefix a- (EtymDB). Contecer comes from spoken Latin contingescere (EtymDB), from contingere 'to touch, to befall' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
tener lugar — to take place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the event, then the Spanish for to come about.

You'll want to know: tener lugar.
tener lugar — "to take place".
La reunión tuvo lugar en el ayuntamiento. The meeting took place at the town hall.
Used for planned events: La boda tendrá lugar en mayo.

The word, taken apart.
tener lugar is built on tener, inherited from Latin tenēre 'to hold' (EtymDB), and lugar, which is usually traced to Latin locālis 'of a place', from locus 'a place' (Lewis & Short). To take place is, word for word, to hold a place.
