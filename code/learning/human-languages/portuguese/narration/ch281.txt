Portuguese, chapter 281: Os factos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o facto — the fact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the edition, then the Portuguese for the article.

You'll want to know: o facto.
o facto — "the fact".
É um facto: ninguém viu nada. It is a fact: nobody saw anything.
In Brazil: o fato, which in Portugal is a suit. De facto — in fact.

The word, taken apart.
facto is a learned borrowing from Latin factum "a deed, a thing done" (EtymDB), the past participle of facere "to do, to make"; the inherited form of the same word is feito.

You'll want to know: relatar.
relatar — "to report, to give an account of".
O jornalista relatou tudo o que viu. The journalist reported everything he saw.
To tell in order what happened.

relatar is built on relato, from Latin relātus "a telling" (EtymDB), the past participle of referre "to bring back, to report" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o relatório — the written report.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to report, then the Portuguese for the account.

You'll want to know: o relatório.
o relatório — "the written report".
O chefe pediu um relatório até sexta-feira. The boss asked for a report by Friday.
A formal document at work; a reportagem belongs to the news.

The word, taken apart.
relatório is built on relato "an account" with the ending -ório (EtymDB), and goes back to Latin relātus "a telling".
