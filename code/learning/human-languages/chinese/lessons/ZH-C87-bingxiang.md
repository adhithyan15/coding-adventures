## You'll want to know: bīngxiāng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C87-BINGXIANG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*bīngxiāng* — "fridge; refrigerator".

> *bīngxiāng lǐ yǒu niúnǎi* — There's milk in the fridge.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*bīngxiāng* is built from bīng 'ice' and xiāng 'a box', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *ping* and *sjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'frozen water' and 'a box; a chest'. The pair names a machine the Guangyun never saw: the compound is modern.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-YOU-EXISTENCE-01, ZH-LEX-C81-CITY81-01] -->

The place first, then 有, then the thing: *bīngxiāng lǐ yǒu niúnǎi*. The 里 after *bīngxiāng* is the one you write, and it means inside.
