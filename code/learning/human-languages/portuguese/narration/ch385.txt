Portuguese, chapter 385: A burocracia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a burocracia — the bureaucracy, the red tape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to renew, then the Portuguese for the certificate.

You'll want to know: a burocracia.
a burocracia — "the bureaucracy, the red tape".
Para abrir uma empresa há muita burocracia. There is a lot of red tape to open a company.
Burocrático — bureaucratic.

The word, taken apart.
burocracia is usually traced to French bureaucratie, which EtymDB builds from bureau "office, desk" and -cratie "rule of" (EtymDB); -cratie goes back to Greek krátos "power".

Lesson 2 of 5.
o processo — the process; the case, the lawsuit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the certificate, then the Portuguese for the bureaucracy.

You'll want to know: o processo.
o processo — "the process; the case, the lawsuit".
O processo demorou três meses. The process took three months.
In law, um processo is a case: pôr um processo a alguém — to sue someone.

The word, taken apart.
processo is a learned borrowing from Latin prōcessus "a going forward, an advance" (EtymDB), from prōcēdere, built on cēdere "to go" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o pedido — the request, the application; the order (in a restaurant).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bureaucracy, then the Portuguese for the process.

You'll want to know: o pedido.
o pedido — "the request, the application; the order (in a restaurant)".
Fiz um pedido à câmara para abrir uma esplanada. I made an application to the council to open a terrace café.
Fazer um pedido — to make a request; a pedido de — at the request of.

The word, taken apart.
pedido is the participle of pedir used as a noun, from Latin petītus "sought" (EtymDB); pedir is inherited from Latin petere "to seek, to ask" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
legal — legal, lawful; cool (informal, in Brazil).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the process, then the Portuguese for the request.

You'll want to know: legal.
legal — "legal, lawful; cool (informal, in Brazil)".
Fazer isso é legal, mas não é bonito. Doing that is legal, but it is not nice.
In Brazil, informal legal! means cool, great; in Portugal it is mostly lawful.

The word, taken apart.
legal is a learned borrowing from Latin lēgālis "legal" (EtymDB), built on lēx, lēgis "law" (Lewis & Short); the same Latin word gave the inherited leal "loyal".

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: ilegal.
ilegal — "illegal".
Estacionar em cima do passeio é ilegal. Parking on the pavement is illegal.
The opposite of legal.

The word, taken apart.
ilegal is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin illēgālis (EtymDB), built from in- "not" and lēgālis "legal", on lēx "law" (Lewis & Short).
