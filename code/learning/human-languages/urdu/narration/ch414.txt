Urdu, chapter 414: Guards and Street Dogs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
گارڈ (gārḍ) — a guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a thatched roof, then the Urdu for to found (a town).

You'll want to know: گارڈ.
گارڈ — gārḍ — "a guard".
عمارت کے دروازے پر ایک گارڈ ہے۔ imārat ke darvāze par ek gārḍ hai. There's a guard at the door of the building.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
گارڈ (gārḍ) is borrowed from English guard.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بھونکنا (bhauṅknā) — to bark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a guard, then the Urdu for stray.

You'll want to know: بھونکنا.
بھونکنا — bhauṅknā — "to bark".
رات کو کتے بھونکتے ہیں۔ rāt ko kutte bhauṅkte haiṅ. At night the dogs bark.
Past (it …): وہ کتا دیر تک بھونکا, voh kuttā der tak bhauṅkā.

The word, taken apart.
بھونکنا (bhauṅknā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousins bhaũknā and bhū̃knā, "to bark", under Sanskrit búkkati, "barks" (CDIAL 9265).

Lesson 4 of 5.
غرانا (ghurrānā) — to growl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for stray, then the Urdu for to bark.

You'll want to know: غرانا.
غرانا — ghurrānā — "to growl".
اجنبی کو دیکھ کر کتا غرایا۔ ajnabī ko dekh kar kuttā ghurrāyā. The dog growled when it saw the stranger.
Past (it …): وہ کتا غرایا, voh kuttā ghurrāyā.

The word, taken apart.
غرانا (ghurrānā) is imitative: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin gurrānā, "to growl", under guragura, a form it reconstructs from the sound (CDIAL 4207); Urdu writes it with غ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بھگانا (bhagānā) — to chase off; to drive away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to bark, then the Urdu for to growl.

You'll want to know: بھگانا.
بھگانا — bhagānā — "to chase off; to drive away".
گارڈ نے کتوں کو بھگایا۔ gārḍ ne kuttoṅ ko bhagāyā. The guard chased the dogs away.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بلی کو بھگایا, us ne billī ko bhagāyā.

The word, taken apart.
بھگانا (bhagānā) is built on بھاگنا, "to run away", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit bhajyate, "is broken", and its past form bhagna, "routed" (CDIAL 9361): this is the form for putting someone to flight.
