Japanese, chapter 247: Praise and Scolding.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ほめる (homeru) — to praise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to forgive, then the Japanese for a pity.

You'll want to know: ほめる.
ほめる — homeru — "to praise".
せんせいは よく がくせいを ほめますか。 — sensei wa yoku gakusei o homemasu ka — "Does the teacher often praise the students?"
Polite ほめます, homemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ほめる (homeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ゆうき (yūki) — courage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for embarrassed, then the Japanese for to be in trouble.

You'll want to know: ゆうき.
ゆうき — yūki — "courage".
かれは ゆうきが ありますか。 — kare wa yūki ga arimasu ka — "Does he have courage?"

The word, taken apart.
ゆうき (yūki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yū, "brave" (Middle Chinese yowngX) and ki, "spirit, air" (khj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
