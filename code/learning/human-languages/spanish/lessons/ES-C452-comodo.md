# cómodo — comfortable, and convenient

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C331-WAY-18, ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el modo** and **duro**. A hard mattress and a comfortable one
are about to be the same question.

## You'll want to know: cómodo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-03] -->

**cómodo** — comfortable, and also convenient.

*Un sillón muy cómodo* — a very comfortable armchair. *¿Estás cómodo?* — are
you comfortable? And the other half: *una ruta más cómoda* — an easier route.
*A la hora que te sea más cómoda* — whenever suits you best.

So it covers both the cushion and the convenience, where English needs two
words. **La comodidad** is the noun, and **incómodo** the opposite, for a
chair and for a silence alike.

## Grammar Lens: the false friend runs one way only
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-04, ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-03] -->

English **commodious** means roomy, and that is not what *cómodo* means. The
trap is worth naming because it is asymmetric:

| you want to say | Spanish |
|---|---|
| a comfortable chair | *una silla c**ó**moda* |
| a convenient time | *una hora c**ó**moda* |
| a roomy flat | *un piso **amplio*** |

Going from Spanish to English, *cómodo* is safe — "comfortable" is nearly
always right. Going the other way, "commodious" will not get you there and
*amplio* will.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-04, ES-LEX-C331-WAY-18] -->

Latin **commodus**: **com-**, with, plus **modus**, a measure — the same
*modus* inside **el modo**.

Something *commodus* was "with measure": the right size, fitted to its
purpose, neither too much nor too little. From there the road to "comfortable"
is short, because a thing that fits is a thing that does not chafe.

The measuring sense survives in English **accommodate**, to make something
fit, and in **commodity**, which began as a thing that suited you before it
meant a sack of wheat. Spanish keeps the
plainest version: a chair that fits you is *cómodo*, and so is a time that
fits your day.
