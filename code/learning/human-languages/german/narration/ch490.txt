German, chapter 490: Schwiegervater und Stiefvater.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Schwiegervater — father-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for niece, then the German for brother-in-law.

You'll want to know: der Schwiegervater.
der Schwiegervater — "father-in-law".
Mein Schwiegervater kocht am Sonntag für alle. My father-in-law cooks for everyone on Sunday.
Plural: die Schwiegerväter. The same first part gives die Schwiegermutter (mother-in-law) and die Schwiegereltern (parents-in-law).

The word, taken apart.
Schwiegervater is built on Schwieger, inherited from Old High German swigar "mother-in-law", from Proto-Germanic swegrō (EtymDB), and Vater "father".

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Stiefvater — stepfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for brother-in-law, then the German for father-in-law.

You'll want to know: der Stiefvater.
der Stiefvater — "stepfather".
Sie versteht sich gut mit ihrem Stiefvater. She gets on well with her stepfather.
Plural: die Stiefväter. Likewise die Stiefmutter, das Stiefkind.

The word, taken apart.
Stiefvater is a Germanic compound: EtymDB sets it beside English stepfather, from Proto-Germanic steupafadēr. The first part is usually said to have meant "bereaved".

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

der Urgroßvater — "great-grandfather".
Mein Urgroßvater war Bauer. My great-grandfather was a farmer.
Plural: die Urgroßväter. Ur- adds one more generation: die Urgroßmutter, der Urenkel.

The word, taken apart.
Urgroßvater is built on ur- "original" and Großvater; groß is inherited from Old High German grōz, from Proto-Germanic grautaz (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for stepfather, then the German for great-grandfather.

You'll want to know: der Zwilling.
der Zwilling — "twin".
Meine Schwester hat Zwillinge bekommen. My sister has had twins.
Plural: die Zwillinge. Eineiige Zwillinge — identical twins.

The word, taken apart.
Zwilling is inherited from Middle High German zwillinc (EtymDB), built on the old stem zwi- "two"; English twin is related.
