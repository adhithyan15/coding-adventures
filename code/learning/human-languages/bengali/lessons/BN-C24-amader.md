# আমাদের (āmāder) — our

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C02-AMAR-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C23-TAR-01, BN-LEX-C24-AMRA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **my**, **your** and **his**. Then say **we**.

## You'll want to know: আমাদের
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-GRAMMAR-C24-AMADER-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C02-AMAR-01, BN-LEX-C11-PORIBAR-01] -->

> **আমাদের পরিবার** — *āmāder pôribār* — **our family.**

Here is the piece that finishes the possessive column. Watch what happens to the
plural ending:

**আমরা** → **আমাদের**, not *āmrār*.

The **-রা** does not survive. In its place comes **-দের**, which is the plural
possessive ending and is used across the board:

| | mine | ours |
|---|---|---|
| I / we | **আমার** | **আমাদের** |
| you, plainly | **তোমার** | **তোমাদের** |
| he, she / they | **তার** | **তাদের** |

## Grammar Lens: one suffix at a time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-GRAMMAR-C24-AMADER-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C23-TAR-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C22-PLURAL-GULO-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C20-CLASSIFIER-01] -->

This is the first sight of a rule that governs the whole back end of a Bengali
word: **the slot holds one ending, and only one.** Plural and possessive cannot
both be there, so a single ending is used that carries both meanings at once.

You have already seen the same economy without being told: **গুলো** does plural
*and* definite, so it occupies the place **টা** would otherwise have taken.
Bengali merges endings where English would stack them.

(**গুলো** — plural and definite.)
