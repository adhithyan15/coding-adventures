# habitual — the way it usually is

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HABER, ES-LEX-C316-ANSW-13] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **he hablado** and **siempre**. The *he* in the first is about
to turn up in an adjective.

## You'll want to know: habitual
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-02] -->

**habitual** — usual, regular, customary.

*El horario habitual* — the usual hours. *Nuestro proveedor habitual* — our
regular supplier. *Lo habitual es avisar* — the usual thing is to let people
know.

**Habitualmente** is the adverb, and **el cliente habitual** is a regular. The
opposite is *poco habitual* — unusual — rather than any single word.

## Grammar Lens: habitual, normal, siempre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-03, ES-LEX-C316-ANSW-13] -->

| what you mean | the word |
|---|---|
| this is the pattern, by habit | **habitual** |
| this is ordinary, not strange | **normal** |
| without exception | **siempre** |

*El horario habitual* is the one they usually keep — and this week they may
not. *Es normal* means nobody should be surprised. *Siempre abren a las nueve*
allows no exceptions at all.

So *habitual* sits between the other two: stronger than "not strange", weaker
than "always". A notice using it is telling you what to expect while leaving
itself room.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-03, ES-LEX-HABER] -->

Latin **habēre**, **to have, to hold** — by way of *habitus*, "the way one
holds oneself".

You own that verb already: *habēre* is the **haber** of *he hablado*, worn
down by centuries of use into an auxiliary so small it barely looks like a
word. Set the two side by side and the two roads are visible again:

| worn down by speech | lifted from the page |
|---|---|
| **he** hablado | **habit**ual |

A habit is something you *hold*, which is why English says you **have** a
habit rather than doing one. And *el hábito* in Spanish is both a habit and a
monk's robe — the clothing you hold yourself in, from the same *habitus*.

The **h** is silent, as it is anywhere in Spanish that it stands on its own.
