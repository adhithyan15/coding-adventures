Hindi, chapter 501: Getting Along.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नाता (nātā) — a bond; a tie of kinship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for the groom's wedding procession, then the Hindi for a farewell.

You'll want to know: नाता.
नाता — nātā — "a bond; a tie of kinship".
मीरा का अपने गाँव से गहरा नाता है। Mīrā kā apne gā̃v se gahrā nātā hai. Mira has a deep bond with her village.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
नाता (nātā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi nātā, "kinship, alliance", under jñātra, a form it reconstructs, close to Sanskrit jñāti, "a kinsman" (CDIAL 5279).

You'll want to know: निभाना.
निभाना — nibhānā — "to keep up; to stand by".
अरुण ने अपना वादा निभाया। Aruṇ ne apnā vādā nibhāyā. Arun kept his promise.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने वादा निभाया, usne vādā nibhāyā.

The word, taken apart.
निभाना (nibhānā) is the transitive beside nibhnā, "to last, succeed": CDIAL lists nibhnā and nibāhnā, "to carry on", under Sanskrit nírvahati, "carries through" (CDIAL 7397). One keeps up a promise, a friendship or a duty.
