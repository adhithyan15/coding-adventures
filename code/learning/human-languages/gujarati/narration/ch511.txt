Gujarati, chapter 511: People at Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
મજૂર (majūr) — a labourer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to cast, then the Gujarati for to have (something) made.

You'll want to know: મજૂર.
મજૂર — majūr — "a labourer".
મજૂરો સવારથી સાંજ સુધી કામ કરે છે. majūro savārthī sānj sudhī kām kare che. The labourers work from morning till evening.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
મજૂર (majūr) is usually traced to Persian mazdūr, "one who works for a wage".

You'll want to know: મુકાદમ.
મુકાદમ — mukādam — "a foreman".
મુકાદમે મજૂરોને કામ સમજાવ્યું. mukādme majūrone kām samjāvyũ. The foreman explained the work to the labourers.

The word, taken apart.
મુકાદમ (mukādam) is usually traced to Arabic muqaddam, "one placed in front, a headman", which came to Gujarati through Persian.

Lesson 3 of 5.
કડિયો (kaḍiyo) — a mason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a labourer, then the Gujarati for a foreman.

You'll want to know: કડિયો.
કડિયો — kaḍiyo — "a mason".
કડિયાએ ઈંટોથી નવી દીવાલ બનાવી. kaḍiyāe ī̃ṭothī navī dīvāl banāvī. The mason built a new wall of bricks.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કડિયો (kaḍiyo) builds with brick, stone and mortar. As the doer of a past action it becomes કડિયાએ. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
મિસ્ત્રી (mistrī) — a mechanic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a foreman, then the Gujarati for a mason.

You'll want to know: મિસ્ત્રી.
મિસ્ત્રી — mistrī — "a mechanic".
ગાડી બગડી ગઈ એટલે અમે મિસ્ત્રીને બોલાવ્યો. gāḍī bagḍī gaī eṭle ame mistrīne bolāvyo. The car broke down, so we called a mechanic.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મિસ્ત્રી (mistrī) is usually traced to Portuguese mestre, "a master of a trade". In Gujarati it names a skilled hand: a mechanic, or a master carpenter or mason.
