Lesson 1 of 7.
la panne — the breakdown, the failure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the link, then the French for to post.

You'll want to know: la panne.
la panne — "the breakdown, the failure".
Ma voiture est en panne. My car has broken down.
Tomber en panne — to break down. Une panne de courant — a power cut.

The word, taken apart.
panne was first a sailing word, mettre en panne, for stopping a ship by turning its sails. The origin is not settled.

planter — "to crash (of a computer, informal); to plant".
Mon ordinateur a planté pendant la réunion. My computer crashed during the meeting.
Informal in this sense. First, to plant: planter un arbre.

The word, taken apart.
planter is inherited from Latin plantāre "to plant" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from planta "a sprout, a cutting".

Lesson 3 of 7.
bloquer — to block; to freeze (reflexive, se bloquer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the breakdown, then the French for to crash.

You'll want to know: bloquer.
bloquer — "to block; to freeze (reflexive, se bloquer)".
L'écran s'est bloqué: rien ne bouge. The screen froze: nothing moves.
In Belgium, bloquer also means to cram for exams.

The word, taken apart.
bloquer is built on bloc "a block", which came through Old French from Middle Dutch blok "a tree trunk, a log" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 7.
redémarrer — to restart, to reboot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to crash, then the French for to block.

You'll want to know: redémarrer.
redémarrer — "to restart, to reboot".
Si ça ne marche pas, il faut redémarrer l'ordinateur. If it does not work, you have to restart the computer.
Re- means again: to start again.

The word, taken apart.
redémarrer is built from re- and démarrer "to start", first a sailing word for casting off a moored boat, from dé- and amarrer "to moor", a word borrowed from Dutch (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 7.
le virus — the virus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to block, then the French for to restart.

You'll want to know: le virus.
le virus — "the virus".
Si tu ouvres ce fichier, tu risques d'attraper un virus. If you open that file, you risk getting a virus.
For computers and for illness. The s is said.

The word, taken apart.
virus was taken whole from Latin vīrus "slime, poison" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short); the computer sense came from English.
