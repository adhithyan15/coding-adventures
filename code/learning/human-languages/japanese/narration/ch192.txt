Japanese, chapter 192: Asking a Police Officer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こうばん (kōban) — police box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for pharmacy, then the Japanese for to find.

You'll want to know: こうばん.
こうばん — kōban — "police box".
えきの まえに こうばんが あります。 — eki no mae ni kōban ga arimasu — "There is a police box in front of the station."

The word, taken apart.
こうばん (kōban) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "mingle, coming and going" (Middle Chinese kaew), and ban, "turn, number in a series" (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おまわりさん (omawarisan) — police officer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: おまわりさん.
おまわりさん — omawarisan — "police officer".
こうばんに おまわりさんが います。 — kōban ni omawarisan ga imasu — "There is a police officer at the police box."

The word, taken apart.
おまわりさん (omawarisan) is built from polite o-, native mawari, "going round, a patrol", from mawaru, "to go round", and the polite -san (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
たずねる (tazuneru) — to ask; to inquire, to look for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for police officer, then the Japanese for police.

You'll want to know: たずねる.
たずねる — tazuneru — "to ask; to inquire, to look for".
おまわりさんに みちを たずねます。 — omawarisan ni michi o tazunemasu — "I ask a police officer the way."
Polite たずねます, tazunemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
たずねる (tazuneru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
