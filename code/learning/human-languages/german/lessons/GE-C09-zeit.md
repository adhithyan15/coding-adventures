# die Zeit — "time"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] You have a clock and you have an hour. This is the thing they measure.

## You'll want to know: die Zeit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ZEIT-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-JAHR-01] -->

> **die Zeit** — "time."

Germanic, through and through, and the twin of an English word you would not
guess: **tide**.

*Tide* once meant a stretch of time, and it still does in the leftovers —
*Yuletide*, *eventide*, *in good time*. Then the sea took the word, and English
had to borrow *time*'s job out to a different root. German never had that
problem and kept *Zeit* for the whole idea.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-EI-AS-EYE-01, GE-LEX-ZEIT-01] -->

- `z-ts` — German *z* is **ts**, as it was at the front of *Dezember*.
- `diphthong-ei` — and *ei* is the vowel of English **eye**.

Put them together: *tsite*, rhyming with English *sight*.
