Mandarin Chinese, chapter 257: Music and Shows.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yuèqì — musical instrument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for it may be that, then the Mandarin for different.

You'll want to know: yuèqì.
yuèqì — "musical instrument".
nǐ huì tán shénme yuèqì — What instrument can you play?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuèqì is built from 乐 'music' and qì 'a vessel', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngaewk and khijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'music' and the second 'a dish, a vessel'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dǎgǔ — to play the drums.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for different, then the Mandarin for a musical instrument.

You'll want to know: dǎgǔ.
dǎgǔ — "to play the drums".
wǒ dìdi huì dǎgǔ — My little brother can play the drums.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before gǔ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dǎgǔ is built from 打 'to strike' and gǔ 'a drum', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and kuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older dictionary for the second, 'to beat a drum'. Middle Chinese taengX would regularly give dǎng; dǎ is irregular.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yǎnchū — to perform on stage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a musical instrument, then the Mandarin for to play the drums.

You'll want to know: yǎnchū.
yǎnchū — "to perform on stage".
jīntiān wǎnshang tāmen yǎnchū — They're performing tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǎnchū is built from yǎn and 出 'to come out', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yenX and tsyhwit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'wide' and 'water flowing far'. The sense 'to perform' is not given there, so how it came is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
gēshǒu — singer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to play the drums, then the Mandarin for to perform on stage.

You'll want to know: gēshǒu.
gēshǒu — "singer".
tā shì wǒ zuì xǐhuan de gēshǒu — He's my favourite singer.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gēshǒu is built from gē 'a song' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ka and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older book for the first: the voice of people is called song. A hand here is someone skilled at a thing; that sense is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 5.
gǔzhǎng — to clap; to applaud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to perform on stage, then the Mandarin for a singer.

You'll want to know: gǔzhǎng.
gǔzhǎng — "to clap; to applaud".
dàjiā dōu gǔzhǎng le — Everyone clapped.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: gǔ is said with a rising tone before zhǎng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
gǔzhǎng is built from gǔ 'a drum' and zhǎng 'the palm of the hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and tsyangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which quotes an older dictionary for the first, 'to beat a drum', and glosses the second 'the palm'.
