# perdi o comboio — when it goes wrong

## You'll want to know: perdi o comboio
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-PERDI-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C81-VERBOS7-04, PT-GRAMMAR-PERFEITO-I-02, PT-LEX-C54-COISAS3-03] -->

**perdi o comboio** — "I've missed the train".

You have **perder**, "to lose". Portuguese uses the same verb for a train you
missed: you **lost** it. Its event past is the *-i* of *dormi*: **perdi**, and
for "he" or "she", **perdeu**.

> **Perdi o comboio.** — I've missed the train.
> **Perdi o autocarro.** — I've missed the bus.
> **Perdi a mala.** — I've lost my suitcase.

Portuguese says *a* mala where English says *my* suitcase: whose it is goes
without saying. One warning about the present: "I lose" is irregular, **perco**.

And listen to the English. "I've missed" sounds like *tenho perdido*, but
chapter fifteen showed what that form means in Portuguese: something that keeps
happening lately. *Tenho perdido o comboio* is "I keep missing the train". For
one missed train, the plain **perdi**.

## Grammar Lens: reporting a problem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-TENHO-UM-PROBLEMA-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-DESCULPE-01, PT-LEX-C113-ESCOLA113-05, PT-LEX-C92-VIAGEM92-05, PT-GRAMMAR-TENHO-FALADO-03] -->

A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:

> **Desculpe, tenho um problema.** — Excuse me, I have a problem.
> **Perdi o comboio.** — What went wrong.
> **Há outro comboio hoje? A que horas?** — What you need.

Or the fault is not yours: *O comboio não chegou*, "the train didn't come".
Say what happened, in the perfeito, then ask the question that gets you moving
again.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**perder** is inherited from Latin *perdere* (EtymDB 2.1), built on *per-*,
"through", and *dare*, "to give": to give something away for good, to
squander, to lose (Lewis & Short, under *perdo*). The *dare* inside it is the
one that gave **dar**, "to give".
