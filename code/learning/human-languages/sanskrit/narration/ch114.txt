Lesson 1 of 5.
मेषः (meṣaḥ) — a sheep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a bull, then the Sanskrit for a buffalo.

You'll want to know: मेषः.
मेषः — meṣaḥ — "a sheep".

The word, taken apart.
meṣa "a ram, a sheep" is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB): Avestan maēša and Persian mīš "ewe" are cousins. In astronomy it names Aries, the first sign of the zodiac (MW). Hindi kept it as mes "ram" (CDIAL 10334).

Lesson 2 of 5.
कपोतः (kapotaḥ) — a pigeon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a buffalo, then the Sanskrit for a sheep.

You'll want to know: कपोतः.
कपोतः — kapotaḥ — "a pigeon".

The word, taken apart.
kapota "a dove, a pigeon" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-Iranian (EtymDB). Its cousin is Persian kabūtar "pigeon", which Hindi and Urdu took from Persian, so the Hindi pigeon is a Persian cousin of this Sanskrit word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
उलूकः (ulūkaḥ) — an owl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a sheep, then the Sanskrit for a pigeon.

You'll want to know: उलूकः.
उलूकः — ulūkaḥ — "an owl".

The word, taken apart.
ulūka "an owl" is old, in the Rigveda; MW cites only a grammarians' root √val for it, so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi ullū "owl", and Punjabi and Gujarati ullū also means "fool" (CDIAL 2359).

Lesson 4 of 5.
बकः (bakaḥ) — a heron.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pigeon, then the Sanskrit for an owl.

You'll want to know: बकः.
बकः — bakaḥ — "a heron".

The word, taken apart.
baka "a heron, a crane" is probably imitative in origin, as CDIAL 9115 explains after EWA, which is why its k survives into the modern forms. It lives on as Hindi bagulā and Bengali bak (CDIAL 9115).

Lesson 5 of 5.
भ्रमरः (bhramaraḥ) — a bee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an owl, then the Sanskrit for a heron.

You'll want to know: भ्रमरः.
भ्रमरः — bhramaraḥ — "a bee".

The word, taken apart.
bhramara "a large black bee" is filed by MW under the root √bhram "wander, whirl", which MW compares with Greek bremō and Latin fremere "roar, hum" (EtymDB gives an Indo-European root "make noise"). It lives on as Hindi bhaũrā "black bee" (CDIAL 9651).
