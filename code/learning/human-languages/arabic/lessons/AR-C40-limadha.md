## You'll want to know: لماذا
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C40-LIMADHA-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C02-MAA-ISMUKA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C39-MA-01] -->

> **لماذا؟** — *limādhā?* — **why?**

It is a compound and every piece is recoverable: **لـ**, the preposition *for*,
plus **ماذا**, *what* — which is itself **ما** plus **ذا**, *this*. Arabic's *why*
is transparently **for-what-this**.

And the **ما** inside it is the same particle the last chapter taught as the
negator of the past tense, and the same one chapter two taught as *what* in
**ما اسمك**. Three jobs, one word, told apart by where it stands.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C40-LIMADHA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C39-HAL-01] -->

**لماذا** and **هل** are the two ends of Arabic questioning. **هل** asks for a
yes or a no and adds no information; **لماذا** asks for a whole clause back, and
the next lesson supplies the word that clause begins with.

The letter **ذال** in the middle of this word has no lesson in this track's
script ladder yet, so the word is printed whole here, in the lesson that owns it,
and named in romanization wherever else it appears.
