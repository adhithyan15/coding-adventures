# म्हैं रोटी खाई — I ate

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-GRAMMAR-C155-VSTEM-MORE-01, MW-SCRIPT-ROTI-01, MW-SCRIPT-PAANI-01, MW-GRAMMAR-C159-GAYO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I eat roti* and *I drink water*. Then *mother went home*.

## Grammar Lens: म्हैं
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-C161-MHAI-AGENT-01]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C50-FAMILY50-05, MW-SCRIPT-ROTI-01, MW-SCRIPT-HOON-01] -->

Eating, drinking and seeing are done *to* something. In their past, *I* is
**म्हैं**, not **हूं**:

> **म्हैं रोटी खाई।**
>
> *mhaĩ roṭī khāī.* — I ate a roti.

Old Marwari already had *mẽ*, "I", beside *hũ* (CDIAL 9691, 992).

## Grammar Lens: the thing decides
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-C161-OBJ-AGREE-01]; assesses=[MW-LEX-C51-VERBS51-01, MW-SCRIPT-PAANI-01, MW-SCRIPT-ROTI-01] -->

The ending follows the thing, not you. **रोटी** is feminine, so **खाई**; **पाणी** is
masculine, so **पीयो**:

> **म्हैं पाणी पीयो।**
>
> *mhaĩ pāṇī pīyo.* — I drank water.

A man and a woman say the same: the roti decides, and so does the water.
