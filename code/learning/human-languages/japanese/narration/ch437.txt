Lesson 1 of 6.
きびしい (kibishii) — strict; severe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for shy with strangers, then the Japanese for to behave.

You'll want to know: きびしい.
きびしい — kibishii — "strict; severe".
じょうしは しごとに きびしいです。 — jōshi wa shigoto ni kibishii desu — "My boss is strict about work."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
きびしい (kibishii) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 6.
れいせい (reisei) — composed; cool-headed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to behave, then the Japanese for strict.

You'll want to know: れいせい.
れいせい — reisei — "composed; cool-headed".
かれは いつも れいせいです。 — kare wa itsumo reisei desu — "He keeps a cool head at all times."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
れいせい (reisei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are rei, "cool" (Middle Chinese lengX) and sei, "quiet" (dzjengX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
いじわる (ijiwaru) — spiteful; mean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for sincere, then the Japanese for modest.

You'll want to know: いじわる.
いじわる — ijiwaru — "spiteful; mean".
いもうとに いじわるを しないで ください。 — imōto ni ijiwaru o shinaide kudasai — "Please do not be mean to your younger sister."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
いじわる (ijiwaru) is a mixed word: Sino-Japanese iji, "disposition", from i, "mind" (Middle Chinese 'iH), and ji, "ground" (dijH), borrowed from Chinese, joined to native waru, "bad", the stem of warui (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
