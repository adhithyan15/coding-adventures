Marathi, chapter 74: Sad, Father, Mother, Son, Daughter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दुःखी (duḥkhī) — sad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for hunger, then the Marathi for happy.

You'll want to know: दुःखी.
दुःखी — duḥkhī — "sad".

The word, taken apart.
दुःखी (duḥkhī) is Sanskrit duḥkhin, "in pain, unhappy", a tatsama built on duḥkha, "difficulty, pain" (CDIAL 6375).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वडील (vaḍīl) — a father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for happy, then the Marathi for sad.

You'll want to know: वडील.
वडील — vaḍīl — "a father".

The word, taken apart.
वडील (vaḍīl) is inherited. CDIAL lists Marathi vaḍīl, "an elder", under Sanskrit vaḍra, "big", a word known from the old word-lists, and doubts a link with vṛddha, "grown" (CDIAL 11225).

Lesson 3 of 5.
आई (āī) — a mother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for sad, then the Marathi for a father.

You'll want to know: आई.
आई — āī — "a mother".

The word, taken apart.
आई (āī) goes back to āī, "mother, aunt", which CDIAL calls probably a nursery word (CDIAL 997). DEDR compares it with Dravidian words for "mother" such as Tamil āy and Kannada āyi (DEDR 364).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: मुलगा.
मुलगा — mulgā — "a son; a boy".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of मुलगा (mulgā) is not settled. Neither CDIAL nor DEDR, searched by form and by meaning, has an entry for it.
