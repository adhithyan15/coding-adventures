# el inventario — what we found

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VENIR, ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **venir** and **el almacén**. A very old verb is about to turn
up in a very dull word.

## You'll want to know: el inventario
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-01] -->

**el inventario** — the inventory, and the stocktake itself.

> Cerrado por **inventario**.

*Hacer inventario* — to do a stocktake. *Está en el inventario* — it's on the
list.

That sign goes up on a shop door for a morning once or twice a year, and it is
the reason this word is worth having before you need it.

## Grammar Lens: cerrado por — the cause on a door
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-02, ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-01] -->

You have met *por* plus a bare noun giving a cause on a notice. Shop doors are
where it lives:

| sign | why they are shut |
|---|---|
| *cerrado por inventario* | stocktaking |
| *cerrado por obras* | building work |
| *cerrado por vacaciones* | holidays |

No article, no verb, no explanation. The frame is *cerrado por* + the reason,
and a Spanish reader takes the whole thing in at a glance from across a street
— which is what a sign is for.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C459-STOCK-02, ES-LEX-VENIR] -->

Latin **invenire**: **in-** plus **venire**, **to come** — so *to come upon*,
and from there **to find**.

An *inventario* is therefore a **list of what was found**, which is exactly
what a stocktake produces: somebody walks the shelves and writes down what is
actually there, as opposed to what the records claim.

The same *invenire* gave **inventar**, to invent — and that is the same idea
seen from the other side. To invent something is to *come upon* it. Spanish
and English both treat discovery and invention as one act, and the word for a
warehouse list is the proof.

*Venir* is underneath all of it, and you have had it since the early lessons.
