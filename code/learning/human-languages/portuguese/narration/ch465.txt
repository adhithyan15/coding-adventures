Portuguese, chapter 465: Vento e tempestade.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a rajada — the gust (of wind).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the freezer, then the Portuguese for to melt.

You'll want to know: a rajada.
a rajada — "the gust (of wind)".
Uma rajada de vento levou o meu chapéu. A gust of wind took my hat.
Forecasts give rajadas in kilometres per hour.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of rajada is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
soprar — to blow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to melt, then the Portuguese for the gust.

You'll want to know: soprar.
soprar — "to blow".
O vento sopra forte do mar. The wind is blowing hard off the sea.
People sopram the candles on a cake too.

The word, taken apart.
soprar is inherited from Latin sufflāre 'to blow on' (EtymDB), built on flāre 'to blow'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o temporal — the storm (wind and rain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the gust, then the Portuguese for to blow.

You'll want to know: o temporal.
o temporal — "the storm (wind and rain)".
O temporal deixou muitas árvores no chão. The storm left many trees on the ground.
A long spell of bad weather, often in winter.

The word, taken apart.
temporal 'storm' is built on tempo 'weather' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin tempus 'time, season'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o furacão — the hurricane.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to blow, then the Portuguese for the storm.

You'll want to know: o furacão.
o furacão — "the hurricane".
O furacão passou longe da costa. The hurricane passed far from the coast.
Figurative: a very lively person.

The word, taken apart.
furacão was borrowed from Spanish huracán, which comes from Taíno hurakán, a word of the Caribbean islands (EtymDB).
