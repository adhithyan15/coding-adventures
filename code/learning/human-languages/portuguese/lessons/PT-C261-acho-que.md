# acho que — I find that, which is to say, I think

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-PENSAR-02, PT-GRAMMAR-HISTORIA-EM-ORDEM-01] -->

## You'll want to know: acho que
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-ACHO-QUE-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C75-CIDADE3-02, PT-LEX-C123-QUALIDADES123-03, PT-LEX-C66-ADJ3-05] -->

**acho que** — "I think that". It is the everyday opinion opener, in Portugal
as much as in Brazil, from **achar**, a regular *-ar* verb meaning "to find".

> **Acho que** o museu é interessante. — I think the museum is interesting.
> **Acho que** o comboio é caro. — I think the train is expensive.
> **Acho que sim.** — I think so. · **Acho que não.** — I don't think so.

To hand the opinion back, use the same verb: to a friend, **E tu, o que achas?** — "and you, what do you think?" — and to *o senhor* or *a senhora*, **O que acha?**

*Penso que* says the same thing, a little more carefully. After either one
the verb stays plain: *acho que é verdade*. Only a denial, *não acho que…*,
makes careful Portuguese bend the verb, which is a later chapter's work.

## Grammar Lens: para mim
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-PARA-MIM-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C92-VIAGEM92-05, PT-LEX-MAS-02] -->

**para mim** — "for me", and so "to my mind":

> **Para mim**, o comboio é caro, mas o museu é interessante.
> — To my mind, the train is expensive, but the museum is interesting.

*Mim* is the form *eu* takes after *para*, and *tu* becomes *ti*, so **para ti?**, "what about you?", hands the view back in two words. *Para mim* puts your own standpoint first; *acho que* puts the opinion first.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**achar** is inherited from Latin *afflāre*, "to breathe upon" (EtymDB 2.1;
Lewis & Short, under *afflo*). The usual account, which neither source spells
out, is a hound catching a scent: to find a thing was to pick up its breath.
So *acho que* is, at root, "I find that", as in English "I find it expensive".
