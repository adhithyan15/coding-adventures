Sanskrit, chapter 330: Old Customs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
परम्परा (paramparā) — a tradition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the waving of lamps, then the Sanskrit for prays for.

You'll want to know: परम्परा.
परम्परा — paramparā — "a tradition".
अस्माकं ग्रामे प्राचीना परम्परा अस्ति। asmākaṃ grāme prācīnā paramparā asti. Our village has an old tradition.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
परम्परा (paramparā) is built from para, "next, other", doubled with an m between: one after another; MW gives paramparā as an uninterrupted series, succession, and tradition (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for prays for, then the Sanskrit for a tradition.

You'll want to know: रीतिः.
रीतिः — rītiḥ — "a custom; a way of doing things".
पितामहस्य रीतिः अद्य अपि जीवति। pitāmahasya rītiḥ adya api jīvati. Grandfather's custom still lives on today.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
रीतिः (rītiḥ) MW's first senses are going, motion, a stream, and it also gives rīti as a general way, usage, custom, method; MW gives no root in the entry, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

आचरति — ācarati — "observes; practises".
वयम् अद्य अपि प्राचीनां रीतिम् आचरामः। vayam adya api prācīnāṃ rītim ācarāmaḥ. We still observe the old custom today.
Past (he …): सः प्राचीनां रीतिम् आचरितवान्, saḥ prācīnāṃ rītim ācaritavān.

The word, taken apart.
आचरति (ācarati) is built from ā and the root √car, "to move", as in carati; MW gives ā-car as to approach, to behave, and to act, practise, perform (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पर्व (parva) — a festival day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a custom, then the Sanskrit for observes a custom.

You'll want to know: पर्व.
पर्व — parva — "a festival day".
अद्य पर्व अस्ति, जनाः नूतनानि वस्त्राणि धारयन्ति। adya parva asti, janāḥ nūtanāni vastrāṇi dhārayanti. Today is a festival day, and people wear new clothes.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
पर्व (parva) is the nominative of parvan, which MW gives first as a knot or joint, then a break, a section, and also a festival or holiday; MW gives no root, so its origin is left uncertain here (MW).

A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तिलकः (tilakaḥ) MW gives tilaka as a mark on the forehead, of coloured earth, sandal or unguents, and as a freckle, without a derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
