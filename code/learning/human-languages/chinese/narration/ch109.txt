Lesson 1 of 6.
上网 (shàngwǎng) — to go online.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for size, then the Mandarin for a good fit.

You'll want to know: 上网.
上网 — shàngwǎng — "to go online".
nǐ měitiān shàngwǎng ma — Do you go online every day?
The new character, in pieces: 网 (wǎng) — a frame open at the foot, like the outside of 两, with two small crosses inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
上网 (shàngwǎng) is built from 上 'to go up, to get on' and wǎng 'net', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangX and mjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary describes the net as knotted cord for hunting and fishing. Getting on to the net is a modern sense.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 2 of 6.
网 (wǎng) — the frame of 两, and two crosses.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the frame of 两, and two crosses.
网 (wǎng).
The frame, two strokes, as in 两: a vertical down the left, then a stroke across the top that turns down the right side and hooks.
Two small crosses inside, two strokes each, left before right: a left-falling stroke, then a dot across it.
2 + 2 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 网 (wǎng), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 网 (wǎng)? (Six.) Where have you written its frame? (In 两.) What is inside? (Two small crosses, left one first.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 网 (wǎng), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: diànshì.
diànshì — "television".
wǒ wǎnshang kàn diànshì — I watch TV in the evening.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
diànshì is built from 电 'electricity' and shì 'to look', both inherited from Middle Chinese, denH and dzyijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as lightning. A modern word.
