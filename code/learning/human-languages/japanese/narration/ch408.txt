Japanese, chapter 408: Incense and Candles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
せんこう (senkō) — an incense stick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the main hall of a temple, then the Japanese for to pray with joined hands.

You'll want to know: せんこう.
せんこう — senkō — "an incense stick".
ほんどうの まえで せんこうを たてました。 — hondō no mae de senkō o tatemashita — "We stood incense sticks in front of the main hall."

The word, taken apart.
せんこう (senkō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sen, "line" (Middle Chinese sjenH) and kō, "incense" (xjang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
まつる (matsuru) — to enshrine; to worship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a household altar, then the Japanese for religion.

You'll want to know: まつる.
まつる — matsuru — "to enshrine; to worship".
この じんじゃは やまを まつっています。 — kono jinja wa yama o matsutte imasu — "This shrine worships the mountain."
Polite まつります, matsurimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): まちの みんなが やまを まつりました。, machi no minna ga yama o matsurimashita.

The word, taken apart.
まつる (matsuru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the verb behind matsuri, "a festival" (JMdict).
