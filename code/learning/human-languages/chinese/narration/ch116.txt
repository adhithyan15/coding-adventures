Mandarin Chinese, chapter 116: Things I Carry.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
qiánbāo — wallet; purse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay attention, then the Mandarin for dangerous.

You'll want to know: qiánbāo.
qiánbāo — "wallet; purse".
wǒ de qiánbāo zài bāo lǐ — My wallet's in my bag.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qiánbāo is built from qián 'money' and bāo 'to wrap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjen and paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

hùzhào is built from hù 'to protect' and zhào 'bright', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huH and tsyewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The passport is a modern sense of the pair, and how it came to it is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
shǒubiǎo — wristwatch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for wallet, then the Mandarin for passport.

You'll want to know: shǒubiǎo.
shǒubiǎo — "wristwatch".
nǐ de shǒubiǎo hěn hǎokàn — Your watch is lovely.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: shǒu rises before biǎo, and is still written shǒu.

The word, taken apart.
shǒubiǎo is built from 手 'hand' and biǎo, both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuwX and pjewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'to make clear' and 'an outer garment'. The watch is a modern sense, and how biǎo came to it is not settled.

You'll want to know: diū.
diū — "to lose (a thing)".
wǒ de yàoshi diū le — I've lost my keys.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
diū is the everyday verb for losing a thing. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.
