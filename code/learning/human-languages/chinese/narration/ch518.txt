Mandarin Chinese, chapter 518: Sunshine.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
qínglǎng — clear and cloudless (of the sky).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for starlight, then the Mandarin for twilight.

You'll want to know: qínglǎng.
qínglǎng — "clear and cloudless (of the sky)".
míngtiān tiānqì qínglǎng wǒmen qù gōngyuán — It'll be clear tomorrow, so we're going to the park.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qínglǎng is built from qíng and lǎng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjeng and langX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as the sky clearing and the second 'bright'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
阳光 (yángguāng) — sunshine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for twilight, then the Mandarin for clear and cloudless.

You'll want to know: 阳光.
阳光 — yángguāng — "sunshine".
这个季节的阳光最好 zhège jìjié de yángguāng zuì hǎo — The sunshine is at its best at this time of year.
The new character, in pieces: 阳 (yáng) — six strokes in two pieces: on the left the ear piece, two strokes, as on the right of 那; on the right 日, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
阳光 (yángguāng) is built from 阳 (yáng) and 光 'light', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yang and kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the yang of yin and yang and, after an older book, 'high and bright'; the light of the yang is the sun's.

Script — the ear piece on the left, then 日.
阳 (yáng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the ear, two strokes, as on the right of 那 and 都: one stroke that runs right, folds down twice like an ear and hooks, then a long vertical down its left edge.
The right piece, 日, four strokes, as you wrote it: a vertical, a bar that turns down the right side, a bar across the middle, then a bar closing the foot.
2 + 4 = six.

Lesson 4 of 6.
太阳能 (tàiyángnéng) — solar energy; solar power.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for clear and cloudless, then the Mandarin for sunshine.

You'll want to know: 太阳能.
太阳能 — tàiyángnéng — "solar energy; solar power".
我们家用了五年太阳能 wǒmen jiā yòng le wǔ nián tàiyángnéng — We've used solar power at home for five years.

The word, taken apart.
太阳能 (tàiyángnéng) is built from 太阳 'the sun' and 能 'energy', all inherited from Middle Chinese, thajH, yang and nong (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the last 'skilled' and 'able'; the sense 'energy' is modern, and is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dǎsǎn — to put up an umbrella.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sunshine, then the Mandarin for solar power.

You'll want to know: dǎsǎn.
dǎsǎn — "to put up an umbrella".
wàipó chūmén de shíhou zǒngshì dǎsǎn — Grandma always puts up an umbrella when she goes out.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: dǎ is said with a rising tone before sǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dǎsǎn is built from 打 and sǎn 'an umbrella', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and sanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'a canopy'. The vowel of dǎ is not the regular outcome of taengX.

Lesson 6 of 6.
dàwù — thick fog.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for solar power, then the Mandarin for to put up an umbrella.

You'll want to know: dàwù.
dàwù — "thick fog".
zǎoshang yǒu dàwù wǒmen kàn bu dào shān — There was thick fog this morning, and we couldn't make out the hills.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dàwù is built from 大 'big' and wù 'fog', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dajH and mjuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the second, after an older book, as what comes when yin and yang are in disorder, and after another, as the earth's breath that the sky does not answer.
