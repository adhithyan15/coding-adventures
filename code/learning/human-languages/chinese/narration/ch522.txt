Mandarin Chinese, chapter 522: Something Needs Fixing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
lòushuǐ — to leak water.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for neat and orderly, then the Mandarin for hygiene.

You'll want to know: lòushuǐ.
lòushuǐ — "to leak water".
chúfáng lòushuǐ yǐjīng sān tiān le — The kitchen has been leaking for three days.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lòushuǐ is built from lòu and 水 'water', both inherited from Middle Chinese, luwH and sywijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the water clock, a vessel that lets water drip away in marked steps; the sense 'to leak' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
上门 (shàngmén) — to call at the house (to do a job).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hygiene, then the Mandarin for to leak water.

You'll want to know: 上门.
上门 — shàngmén — "to call at the house (to do a job)".
xiū kōngtiáo de shīfu míngtiān shàngmén — The man who fixes air conditioners is coming round tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
上门 (shàngmén) is built from 上 'to go up' and 门 'a door', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangX and mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to climb, to rise': to come up to someone's door.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dìbǎn — the floor of a room; floorboards.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the water bill, then the Mandarin for a doorbell.

You'll want to know: dìbǎn.
dìbǎn — "the floor of a room; floorboards".
wǒ shuā le bàntiān dìbǎn — I spent ages scrubbing the floor.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dìbǎn is built from 地 'the ground' and bǎn 'a board', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dijH and paenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which gives the second as the same word as another spelling glossed, after an older dictionary, 'split wood'.
