# ഊണ് · വേണോ · അല്ലേ — asking, offering, and checking

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C40-OON-02, ML-LEX-C76-VENO-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The word for a meal. Say it, then offer one — the asking form of
*want*.

## Guided Practice: three moves that need each other
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C76-VENO-01, ML-LEX-C77-ALLE-01, ML-CONCEPT-C40-OON-03] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: offer a meal — *ūṇ vēṇō?*]
- [YOU SAY: check that you have understood — *allē?*]
- [YOU SAY: the meal word again, and say which verb it is built from]

**വേണോ** is not a separate word for *do you want*: it is the wanting word
wearing the question ending the book taught long before. **അല്ലേ** does the
opposite job — it does not ask a question so much as invite the other person to
agree with one you have already answered.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C80-GENTILIC-01, ML-LEX-C80-ORIGIN-04, ML-SCRIPT-RECOG-132] -->

**മലയാളി** is the language word with a person ending on it, and that is worth
holding because English does not build words this way reliably. The pattern is
productive: a place or a tongue plus the ending gives you the people of it.
