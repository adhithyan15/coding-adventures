Lesson 1 of 5.
das Eis — ice; ice cream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a pond, then the German for fire.

You'll want to know: das Eis.
das Eis — "ice; ice cream".

The word, taken apart.
Eis from Old High German īs from Proto-Germanic īsą — inherited, the same word as English ice. (EtymDB 2.1) "Ice cream" is a later sense.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Hügel — a hill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for fire, then the German for ice; ice cream.

You'll want to know: der Hügel.
der Hügel — "a hill".

The word, taken apart.
Hügel spread into standard German from central German dialects. It is a diminutive of Middle High German houc, "hill", related to the word for "high"; compare Old Norse haugr, "mound".

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: das Pferd.

The word, taken apart.
Pferd from Old High German pfarifrit from Late Latin paraverēdus, "spare post horse" (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed and worn down. English palfrey comes from the same Latin word through French.
