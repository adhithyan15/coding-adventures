# کمرے (kamre) — more than one

## You'll want to know: the plural
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-PLURAL]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KAMRA, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE] -->

> **کمرہ** — *kamrā* — a room.

> **کمرے** — *kamre* — **rooms.**

Twenty-four chapters, and no noun in this book has ever been more than one.

Look hard at the plural, because you have seen it before. **کمرے** is exactly
the oblique singular from chapter twenty-three — same three letters, same sound.
Urdu spells *rooms* and *of the room* identically and lets the rest of the
sentence tell them apart.

## Grammar Lens: the table, and what the postposition settles
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-PLURAL, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE, UR-GRAMMAR-ADJECTIVE-AGREEMENT, UR-LEX-KALA, UR-LEX-MAAN] -->

| | one | more than one |
|---|---|---|
| direct | **کمرہ** | **کمرے** |
| oblique | **کمرے** | *next lesson* |

Three of the four cells are filled and two of them look the same. A postposition
settles it: **کمرے میں** can only be *in the room*, because a plural before a
postposition takes a different shape again — which is the next lesson.

Nouns not ending in *-ā* do not change at all. **ماں** is one mother or several
and is spelled **ماں** either way, so number, like the oblique, is often
invisible in Urdu and has to be read off the sentence.

And the adjective agrees: **کالا کمرہ** → **کالے کمرے**.
