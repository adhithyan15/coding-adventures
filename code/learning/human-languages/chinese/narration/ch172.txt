Mandarin Chinese, chapter 172: Sweet and Sour.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
wèidao — taste; flavour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to press a button, then the Mandarin for to touch.

You'll want to know: wèidao.
wèidao — "taste; flavour".
zhège cài de wèidao hěn hǎo — This dish tastes good.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wèidao is built from wèi 'taste' and dao, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mj+jH and dawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the five tastes, sour, salty, sweet, bitter and hot, and the second 'a road'. In the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: wèidao]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wèidao, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhège cài de wèidao hěn hǎo — This dish tastes good.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to press a button, then the Mandarin for to touch, then say wèidao again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does wèidao mean? ("Taste; flavour".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
kǔ — bitter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a flavour, then the Mandarin for sour.

You'll want to know: kǔ.
kǔ — "bitter".
zhège kāfēi tài kǔ le — This coffee is too bitter.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kǔ is inherited from Middle Chinese khuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'coarse', 'toil' and 'suffering'; the taste itself is not given there in so many words.
