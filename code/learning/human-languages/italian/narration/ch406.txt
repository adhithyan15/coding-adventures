5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
accelerare — to speed up, to accelerate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the junction, then the Italian for to overtake.

You'll want to know: accelerare.
accelerare — "to speed up, to accelerate".
Non accelerare, c'è la nebbia. Do not speed up, it is foggy.
L'acceleratore is the accelerator pedal.

The word, taken apart.
accelerare is a learned borrowing from Latin accelerāre 'to hasten' (EtymDB), built on celer 'swift'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
il freno — the brake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to overtake, then the Italian for to speed up.

You'll want to know: il freno.
il freno — "the brake".
Il freno della bici non funziona. The brake on the bike does not work.
Il freno a mano is the handbrake. Figuratively, senza freni means without restraint.

The word, taken apart.
freno is inherited from Latin frēnum 'bridle, bit' (EtymDB): first the thing that held back a horse.

la frenata — "the braking; a sudden stop".
Ho fatto una frenata e il gatto è scappato. I braked hard and the cat ran off.
Frenare is to brake; una frenata brusca is slamming on the brakes.

The word, taken apart.
frenata is built on frenare 'to brake', from freno 'brake', which is inherited from Latin frēnum 'bridle' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
sterzare — to steer, to swerve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the brake, then the Italian for the braking.

You'll want to know: sterzare.
sterzare — "to steer, to swerve".
Ho sterzato per evitare un cane. I swerved to avoid a dog.
Lo sterzo is the steering; sterzare a sinistra — to turn the wheel left.

The word, taken apart.
sterzare is built on sterzo 'steering', which was borrowed from Lombardic sterz 'the handle of a plough' (EtymDB): the farmer guided the plough by it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sbandare — to skid, to swerve off course.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the braking, then the Italian for to steer.

You'll want to know: sbandare.
sbandare — "to skid, to swerve off course".
Con il ghiaccio la macchina ha sbandato. On the ice the car skidded.
Also figurative: un ragazzo sbandato is a young man who has lost his way.

The word, taken apart.
sbandare is built from s- and banda 'side': going off to one side. The origin of banda in this sense is not settled; it is usually linked to a Germanic word that reached Italian through Old Provençal.
