# તેઓ (teo) — they

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-E-01, GU-PRON-TE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say *she speaks Gujarati*. Then the pronoun on its
own, **તે**, the word for anyone who is not here.

## You'll want to know: તેઓ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-C145-NEG145-01]; assesses=[] -->

**તેઓ** (*teo*) — "they".

It is **તે** with **-ઓ** on the end. **તે** names one person who is not
here; **તેઓ** names more than one.

> **તેઓ ગુજરાતી બોલે છે.** — *teo gujarātī bole che.* — They speak Gujarati.

The verb does not move: **બોલે છે** serves **તેઓ** exactly as it serves **તે**.
In the present, *he*, *she*, familiar *you* and *they* all meet on **-ે છે**.

**તેઓ** does a second job. Said of one person, it is a respectful *he* or *she*:
a teacher, a grandparent, a guest. So **તેઓ અહીં છે** is *they are here*, or
*he is here*, said with respect.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**તેઓ** (*teo*) is **તે** plus **-ઓ**, the ending Gujarati nouns take for "more
than one". **તે** continues the old Sanskrit demonstrative stem *ta-*, whose
plural *te* already meant "they" (CDIAL 5612).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more pronoun, and the present now has four people on one ending.
