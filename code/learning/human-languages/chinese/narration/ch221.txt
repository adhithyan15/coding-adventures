Mandarin Chinese, chapter 221: Snow and Ice.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
雪人 (xuěrén) — snowman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to skip with a rope, then the Mandarin for to do a fitness routine.

You'll want to know: 雪人.
雪人 — xuěrén — "snowman".
门口有一个雪人 ménkǒu yǒu yí ge xuěrén — There's a snowman by the door.
The new character, in pieces: 雪 (xuě) — eleven strokes in two pieces: 雨 pressed flat on top, eight strokes, and under it three bars joined on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
雪人 (xuěrén) is built from 雪 (xuě) 'snow' and 人 'person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjwet and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'frozen rain'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
雪 (xuě) — 雨 on top, three bars below.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — 雨 on top, three bars below.
雪 (xuě).
Two pieces, top before bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
On top, 雨, eight strokes, pressed flat: a short bar; a short stroke down on the left; a bar that runs right and turns down with a hook; a vertical through the middle; then four dots, two on the left and two on the right.
Under it, three strokes. A bar that runs right and turns down, a bar across the middle, and a bar along the foot, all three meeting the stroke on the right.
8 + 3 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 雪 (xuě), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 雪 (xuě)? (Eleven.) What is on top? (雨, pressed flat.) How many bars at the foot? (Three.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
huáxuě — to ski.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do a fitness routine, then the Mandarin for a snowman.

You'll want to know: huáxuě.
huáxuě — "to ski".
nǐ huì huáxuě ma — Can you ski?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huáxuě is built from huá 'slippery' and 雪 (xuě) 'snow', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hweat and sjwet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary's gloss for the first is 'keen' or 'smooth', not 'slippery', so how it came to that sense is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: língxià.
língxià — "below zero".
jīntiān língxià wàibian hěn lěng — It's below zero today; it's freezing outside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
língxià is built from líng 'zero' and 下 'below', both inherited from Middle Chinese, leng and haeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to fall' and 'a gentle rain', so the sense zero came later, and how is not settled.
