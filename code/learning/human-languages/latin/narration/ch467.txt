Latin, chapter 467: Cobbler's shop, Barber's shop, Awl, Tanner, Furrier.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sūtrīna, sūtrīnae — a cobbler's shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to value, then the Latin for to be sold.

You'll want to know: sūtrīna, sūtrīnae.
sūtrīna, sūtrīnae — "a cobbler's shop".
Sūtor in sūtrīnā calceōs novōs facit. The cobbler makes new shoes in his shop.
First declension, feminine: sūtrīna, sūtrīnae f., from sūtor 'shoemaker'.

The word, taken apart.
sūtrīna is built on sūtor 'shoemaker' (Lewis & Short), which comes from suō 'to sew'. English suture comes from the same verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sūbula, sūbulae — an awl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cobbler's shop, then the Latin for a barber's shop.

You'll want to know: sūbula, sūbulae.
sūbula, sūbulae — "an awl".
Sūtor sūbulā et fīlō calceōs facit. The cobbler makes shoes with an awl and thread.
First declension, feminine: sūbula, sūbulae f. A pointed tool for piercing leather before sewing.

The word, taken apart.
sūbula is inherited from PIE syuh₁-dʰleh₂ (EtymDB), built on the root of suō 'to sew': a tool for sewing leather.

Lesson 4 of 5.
coriārius, coriāriī — a tanner, a currier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a barber's shop, then the Latin for an awl.

You'll want to know: coriārius, coriāriī.
coriārius, coriāriī — "a tanner, a currier".
Coriārius coria bovum in aquā lavat. The tanner washes the hides of the oxen in water.
Second declension, masculine: coriārius, coriāriī m., from corium 'hide, leather'. Tanneries stank and stood at the edge of town. English currier comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
coriārius is built on corium 'hide, leather' with the suffix -ārius (Lewis & Short). English currier, a leather dresser, comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pelliō, pelliōnis — a furrier, a worker in skins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an awl, then the Latin for a tanner.

You'll want to know: pelliō, pelliōnis.
pelliō, pelliōnis — "a furrier, a worker in skins".
Pelliō vestem calidam facit. The furrier makes a warm garment.
Third declension, masculine: pelliō, pelliōnis m. He made and sold warm clothes of skins.

The word, taken apart.
pelliō is built on pellis 'skin, hide' with the suffix -iō for a person who works with something (Lewis & Short). English pelt is often linked with pellis, but the link is not settled.
