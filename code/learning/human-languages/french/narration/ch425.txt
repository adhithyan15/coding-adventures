French, chapter 425: Au camping.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le camping — the campsite; camping.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to lodge, then the French for to stay for a while.

You'll want to know: le camping.
le camping — "the campsite; camping".
Le camping est à cinq minutes de la plage. The campsite is five minutes from the beach.
In French, le camping is also the place.

The word, taken apart.
camping was borrowed from English camping (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le sac de couchage — the sleeping bag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the campsite, then the French for the tent.

You'll want to know: le sac de couchage.
le sac de couchage — "the sleeping bag".
Il fait froid la nuit, alors je dors dans mon sac de couchage. It is cold at night, so I sleep in my sleeping bag.
In speech, often le duvet.

The word, taken apart.
sac is inherited from Latin saccus, from Greek sákkos 'bag' (EtymDB); couchage is built on coucher 'to lie down'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bivouaquer — to bivouac, to sleep out in the open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the sleeping bag, then the French for to camp.

You'll want to know: bivouaquer.
bivouaquer — "to bivouac, to sleep out in the open".
En montagne, ils ont bivouaqué sous les étoiles. In the mountains they slept out under the stars.
Un bivouac is one night in the open, often in the mountains.

The word, taken apart.
bivouaquer is built on bivouac, which is usually traced to Swiss or Alsatian German Biwacht 'extra night watch'. EtymDB lists the verb without a source.
