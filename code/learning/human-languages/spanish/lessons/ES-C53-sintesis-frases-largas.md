# Synthesis — sentences worth saying

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-RELATIVE-QUE, ES-LEX-LIBRO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Until this rung, every sentence you could build had one verb in it.
Count the verbs in the next one.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-RELATIVE-QUE, ES-GRAMMAR-RELATIVE-QUE-OBLIGATORY, ES-GRAMMAR-LO-QUE, ES-LEX-LIBRO, ES-LEX-COMPRAR-07, ES-LEX-CASA, ES-LEX-COMIDA, ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-PRESENT-SINGULAR] -->

> — *¿Tienes el libro **que** compré?*
> — *Sí. **Lo que** no tengo es la comida **que** haces.*

Read the second line again. It has three verbs in it, and you can build every
piece of it:

- *lo que no tengo* — the thing I do not have *(a noun-shaped hole)*
- *es* — is
- *la comida que haces* — the meal you make *(a noun with a sentence hung off it)*

Nothing in that sentence is new. What is new is that the pieces are allowed to
sit **inside** each other.

## Grammar Lens: length is not difficulty
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-RELATIVE-QUE, ES-GRAMMAR-LO-QUE, ES-GRAMMAR-DIRECT-OBJECT-LO] -->

Long sentences look like an increase in difficulty and are not.

A relative clause does not add grammar. It adds a **slot** where a whole
sentence can go, and you fill it with the same Spanish you were already making.
Two short sentences become one long one, and the only new thing is the joint.

That is why this is the last structural rung a beginner's course owes you. Not
because nothing is left, but because from here the growth is **more of what you
have**, arranged more deeply. What remains genuinely hard is vocabulary and
hearing speed, and both are time.
