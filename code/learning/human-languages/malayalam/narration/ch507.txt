Malayalam, chapter 507: The Monsoon.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
മൺസൂൺ (maṇsūṇ) — the monsoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the atmosphere, then the Malayalam for rain clouds.

You'll want to know: മൺസൂൺ.
മൺസൂൺ — maṇsūṇ — "the monsoon".
ജൂണിൽ മൺസൂൺ തുടങ്ങും. jūṇil maṇsūṇ tuṭaṅṅuṁ. The monsoon will begin in June.
English uses it for the season of the rains; Malayalam also says മഴക്കാലം.

The word, taken apart.
മൺസൂൺ (maṇsūṇ) was borrowed from English monsoon, the seasonal wind and the rains it brings.

Lesson 2 of 5.
കാലവർഷം (kālavarṣaṁ) — the southwest monsoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for rain clouds, then the Malayalam for the monsoon.

You'll want to know: കാലവർഷം.
കാലവർഷം — kālavarṣaṁ — "the southwest monsoon".
കാലവർഷം ആ വർഷം നേരത്തെ എത്തി. kālavarṣaṁ ā varṣaṁ nēratte etti. The southwest monsoon came early that year.
The big rains of June and July, the first of the two monsoons.

The word, taken apart.
കാലവർഷം (kālavarṣaṁ) is a Malayalam compound built from two Sanskrit words, kāla, "time, season", and varṣa, "rain" (Monier-Williams): the rain of the season.

Lesson 3 of 5.
തുലാവർഷം (tulāvarṣaṁ) — the northeast monsoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the monsoon, then the Malayalam for the southwest monsoon.

You'll want to know: തുലാവർഷം.
തുലാവർഷം — tulāvarṣaṁ — "the northeast monsoon".
തുലാവർഷത്തിൽ വൈകുന്നേരം ഇടിമിന്നലോടെ മഴ പെയ്യും. tulāvarṣattil vaikunnēraṁ iṭiminnalōṭe maḻa peyyuṁ. In the northeast monsoon it rains in the evenings with thunder.
It comes in the month of തുലാം, October to November.

The word, taken apart.
തുലാവർഷം (tulāvarṣaṁ) is a Malayalam compound built from തുലാം, the month named after Sanskrit tulā, the scales, and varṣa, rain (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
തോരുക (tōruka) — to stop raining; to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the southwest monsoon, then the Malayalam for the northeast monsoon.

You'll want to know: തോരുക.
തോരുക — tōruka — "to stop raining; to dry".
ഉച്ചയോടെ മഴ തോർന്നു. uccayōṭe maḻa tōrnnu. By noon the rain had stopped.
Past: തോർന്നു, tōrnnu.
For rain, tears and wet things: കണ്ണീർ തോർന്നു, the tears dried.

The word, taken apart.
തോരുക (tōruka) is native Dravidian, from a root that means to dry or to wipe off moisture (DEDR 3351). DEDR also lists under it a word for fair weather when the monsoon rain stops.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ചാറ്റൽമഴ (cāṟṟalmaḻa) — a light drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the northeast monsoon, then the Malayalam for to stop raining.

You'll want to know: ചാറ്റൽമഴ.
ചാറ്റൽമഴ — cāṟṟalmaḻa — "a light drizzle".
രാവിലെ മുതൽ ചാറ്റൽമഴ ഉണ്ട്. rāvile mutal cāṟṟalmaḻa uṇṭŭ. There has been a light drizzle since morning.
ചാറ്റൽ, drizzling, plus മഴ, rain.

The word, taken apart.
ചാറ്റൽമഴ (cāṟṟalmaḻa) is a native Dravidian compound of ചാറ്റൽ, drizzling (DEDR 2482), and മഴ, rain (DEDR 4753).
