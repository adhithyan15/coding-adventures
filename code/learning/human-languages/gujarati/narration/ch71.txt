Gujarati, chapter 71: White, Brown, Pink, and What Looks Good.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સફેદ (saphed) — white.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for blue, then the Gujarati for black.

You'll want to know: સફેદ.
સફેદ (saphed) — "white".

The word, taken apart.
સફેદ (saphed) is a loan, usually traced to Persian safēd, "white".

What you've built.
One more word for white, brown, pink, and what looks good.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ભૂરો (bhūro) — brown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for black, then the Gujarati for white.

You'll want to know: ભૂરો.
ભૂરો (bhūro) — "brown".

The word, taken apart.
ભૂરો (bhūro) is a tadbhava. It comes from bhrūra, "brown", a form CDIAL reconstructs and sets beside Indo-European bhrūro- and bhrūno- (CDIAL 9690). The second is the source of English brown.

Lesson 4 of 5.
સુંદર (sundar) — beautiful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for brown, then the Gujarati for pink.

You'll want to know: સુંદર.
સુંદર (sundar) — "beautiful".

The word, taken apart.
સુંદર (sundar) is Sanskrit sundara, "beautiful", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 13474). CDIAL asks whether the Sanskrit word is itself a Middle Indo-Aryan form of sūnara, "excellent".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ખરાબ (kharāb) — bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for pink, then the Gujarati for beautiful.

You'll want to know: ખરાબ.
ખરાબ (kharāb) — "bad". The opposite of સારું.

The word, taken apart.
ખરાબ (kharāb) is a loan from Arabic kharāb, "ruin, desolation", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is kh-r-b (Lane).
