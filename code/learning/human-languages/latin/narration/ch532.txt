Latin, chapter 532: Freedom.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
manūmissiō, manūmissiōnis — the freeing of a slave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a litter-bearer, then the Latin for a farm housekeeper.

You'll want to know: manūmissiō, manūmissiōnis.
manūmissiō, manūmissiōnis — "the freeing of a slave".
Post manūmissiōnem servus lībertus est. After being set free, the slave is a freedman.
Feminine, third declension: manus 'hand' + mittō 'to send' — to let go from the hand. English manumission.

The word, taken apart.
manūmissiō is built on manūmittō "to set free", from manus "hand, power" and mittō "to let go" (Lewis & Short). English manumission comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lībertīnus, lībertīnī — a freedman (by rank).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a farm housekeeper, then the Latin for the freeing of a slave.

You'll want to know: lībertīnus, lībertīnī.
lībertīnus, lībertīnī — "a freedman (by rank)".
Multī lībertīnī in urbe tabernās habent. Many freedmen keep shops in the city.
Masculine. Lībertīnus names a freed slave's rank in society, lībertus his tie to his former master.

The word, taken apart.
lībertīnus is built on lībertus "freedman" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from līber "free".

Lesson 3 of 5.
vindicta, vindictae — the rod of freedom (in freeing a slave); vengeance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the freeing of a slave, then the Latin for a freedman.

You'll want to know: vindicta, vindictae.
vindicta, vindictae — "the rod of freedom (in freeing a slave); vengeance".
Magistrātus servum vindictā tangit, et servus līber fit. The magistrate touches the slave with the rod, and the slave becomes free.
Feminine, first declension. In one way of freeing a slave, he was touched with this rod before a magistrate. Later it also means vengeance; English vindictive.

The word, taken apart.
vindicta is built on vindicō "to lay claim to, set free" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), from vindex "a defender". English vindictive comes from its later sense, vengeance.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ingenuus, ingenua, ingenuum — free-born; frank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a freedman, then the Latin for the rod of freedom.

You'll want to know: ingenuus, ingenua, ingenuum.
ingenuus, ingenua, ingenuum — "free-born; frank".
Hic puer ingenuus est, nōn servus. This boy is free-born, not a slave.
Born free, as against lībertīnus; then honest, frank. English ingenuous.

The word, taken apart.
ingenuus is built on in- and the root of gignō "to beget" (Lewis & Short): born in the place, then born free. English ingenuous comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pilleus, pilleī — a felt cap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the rod of freedom, then the Latin for free-born.

You'll want to know: pilleus, pilleī.
pilleus, pilleī — "a felt cap".
Servus līberātus pilleum gerit. The freed slave wears the felt cap.
Masculine, second declension. A newly freed slave put on the pilleus, a sign of liberty.

The word, taken apart.
pilleus is usually linked with Greek pilos "felt, felt cap" (Lewis & Short), but whether it is a loan or a cousin is not settled.
