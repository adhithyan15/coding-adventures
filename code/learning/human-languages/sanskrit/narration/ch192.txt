Lesson 1 of 5.
हारः (hāraḥ) — a necklace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pestle, then the Sanskrit for a sieve.

You'll want to know: हारः.
हारः — hāraḥ — "a necklace".

The word, taken apart.
MW lists "necklace, string of pearls" among the senses of hāra, a noun of the root √hṛ, "to carry, take". CDIAL 14059 treats the necklace word as a separate hāra, so the link is not settled. It lives on as Punjabi and Gujarati hār, "necklace".

The word, taken apart.
mukuṭa was borrowed from Dravidian, CDIAL 10144 says, comparing Tamil mukaṭu, "top, highest part" (DEDR 4888). It lives on as Hindi mauṛ, the crown a bridegroom wears.

Lesson 3 of 5.
अधोवस्त्रम् (adhovastram) — a lower garment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a necklace, then the Sanskrit for a crown.

You'll want to know: अधोवस्त्रम्.
अधोवस्त्रम् — adhovastram — "a lower garment".

The word, taken apart.
adhovastra is a compound of adhas, "below", and vastra, "garment", built on the root √vas, "to wear" (MW). MW compares √vas with Latin vestis, "garment", and English wear; MW does not list this compound itself.

Lesson 4 of 5.
परिधानम् (paridhānam) — clothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a crown, then the Sanskrit for a lower garment.

You'll want to know: परिधानम्.
परिधानम् — paridhānam — "clothing".

The word, taken apart.
paridhāna is built on pari, "around", and the root √dhā, "to put, place" (MW): clothing is what is put round you. MW compares √dhā with English do, and CDIAL 7838 traces Hindi pahirnā, "clothes", to paridhāna.

Lesson 5 of 5.
वसनम् (vasanam) — a garment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lower garment, then the Sanskrit for clothing.

You'll want to know: वसनम्.
वसनम् — vasanam — "a garment".

The word, taken apart.
vasana is built on the root √vas, "to wear, put on" (MW; CDIAL 11436), the root of vastra. MW compares Latin vestis, "garment", and English wear, so it is an inherited Indo-European root.
