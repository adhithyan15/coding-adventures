## Added — Sanskrit B1 vocabulary, 1,350 words in chapters 286–555

- Added 270 chapters of five words each (1,350 headwords, 394 of them
  verbs), one word per lesson, with eight reviews in each of five tranches:
  narrating (286–339), reasons and conditions (340–393), travel (394–447),
  experience (448–501), and crafts, the arts, plans and choices (502–555).
- Every verb shows its -tavat past agreeing with the doer (सः गतवान्, सा
  गतवती), checked against Monier-Williams or the Kṛdantarūpamālā; every noun's
  form follows its Monier-Williams gender. Examples use only the words and
  grammar taught before them: no passive, perfect or aorist, no case form the
  track has not shown, fear with the ablative, and every condition from 367
  on in यदि … तर्हि or the conditional.
- The first words of chapters 286–293 practise the core's last grammar
  points again; घ ढ औ ई each come back twice in chapters 294–316.
- Every word cites Monier-Williams; descendants cite CDIAL; Western cousins
  appear only where Monier-Williams compares them; modern senses are marked
  as modern. No etymology is invented. Root slugs reuse the corpus spelling
  of each etymon.
- Plain derivations of taught words, near-synonym clusters beyond two,
  homographs of inflected forms and rare classical words a B1 learner does
  not need were replaced during review.
