## You'll want to know: língqián
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C188-MONEY188-03]; assesses=[] -->

*língqián* — "small change; loose coins".

> *nǐ yǒu língqián ma* — Have you got any change?

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*língqián* is built from *líng* 'zero' and 钱 'money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *leng* and *dzjen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to fall' and 'a light rain'; the sense of small odd amounts is not given there, so how the pair came to mean change is not settled.
