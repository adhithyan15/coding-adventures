German, chapter 446: In der Reinigung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Reinigung — the dry cleaner; the cleaning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the locksmith, then the German for to unlock.

You'll want to know: die Reinigung.
die Reinigung — "the dry cleaner; the cleaning".
Den Anzug bringt er in die Reinigung. He takes the suit to the dry cleaner.
Plural: die Reinigungen. Also the cleaning of a room.

The word, taken apart.
Reinigung is built on reinigen 'to clean', from rein 'clean, pure', inherited from Proto-Germanic hrainiz (EtymDB lists Reinigung).

Lesson 3 of 5.
entfernen — to remove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the dry cleaner, then the German for the launderette.

You'll want to know: entfernen.
entfernen — "to remove".
Die Reinigung entfernt den Fleck vom Kleid. The dry cleaner removes the stain from the dress.
Formal. Entfernt also means distant: zwei Kilometer entfernt.

The word, taken apart.
entfernen is inherited from Middle High German entverren (EtymDB), built on fern 'far': to put something far away.

Lesson 4 of 5.
flicken — to patch, to mend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the launderette, then the German for to remove.

You'll want to know: flicken.
flicken — "to patch, to mend".
Die Schneiderin flickt das Loch in der Jacke. The dressmaker patches the hole in the jacket.
Der Flicken — the patch. Einen Fahrradschlauch flicken — to mend a bike tube.

The word, taken apart.
flicken is inherited from Middle High German vlicken 'to put patches on' (EtymDB), built on vlëc 'patch, spot', from Old High German flec (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
trocknen — to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to remove, then the German for to patch.

You'll want to know: trocknen.
trocknen — "to dry".
Im Sommer trocknet die Wäsche im Garten. In summer the washing dries in the garden.
Perfekt with sein when something dries by itself: die Wäsche ist getrocknet; with haben when someone dries it.

The word, taken apart.
trocknen is built on trocken 'dry', inherited from Middle High German trucken (EtymDB).
