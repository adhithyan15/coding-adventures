Kannada, chapter 318: It Depends, and What Would Have Been.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಅವಲಂಬಿಸು (avalambisu) — to depend, to rely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "on top, on" (mēle) and "according to" (prakāra). Then "If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home." (nāḷe maḷe bandare nānu maneyallē iruttēne.)

You'll want to know: ಅವಲಂಬಿಸು.
ಅವಲಂಬಿಸು — avalambisu — "to depend (on), to rely (on)".
ನಮ್ಮ ಊರು ಮಳೆಯ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ. namma ūru maḷeya mēle avalambiside. — "Our town depends on the rain."
What you depend on takes ಮೇಲೆ, "on": ಮಳೆಯ ಮೇಲೆ (maḷeya mēle, "on the rain"). ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ is ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿ, the -ಇ form, with ಇದೆ: "it is, having come to rest on", so "it depends". Written the same, ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ is also "I depended"; the sentence tells you which.
For "it depended", ಇದೆ becomes ಇತ್ತು:
ಆ ಹಳ್ಳಿ ಒಂದು ಕೆರೆಯ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿತ್ತು. ā haḷḷi ondu kereya mēle avalambisittu. — "That village depended on one tank."

The word, taken apart.
ಅವಲಂಬಿಸು (avalambisu) is Sanskrit avalamba made into a Kannada verb with -ಇಸು, as ಯೋಚಿಸು is. CDIAL 826 gives avalamba as "hanging down" and, as a noun, "stay, support"; Monier-Williams adds "depending, resting upon". To depend on something is to hang from it, which is also where English depend comes from: Latin dēpendēre, "to hang down".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ಅವಲಂಬಿಸು (avalambisu) mean? (To depend, to rely.) What does the thing you depend on take? (ಮೇಲೆ, "on".) "It depends"? (ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ.) "It depended"? (ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿತ್ತು.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ (mēle avalambiside) — it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to depend" (avalambisu) and "Our town depends on the rain." (namma ūru maḷeya mēle avalambiside.)

You'll want to know: ಅದು … ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ.
ನೀವು ನಾಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತೀರಾ? nīvu nāḷe baruttīrā? — "Will you come tomorrow?"
ಅದು ಹವಾಮಾನದ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ. ಮಳೆ ಬಂದರೆ ನಾನು ಬರುವುದಿಲ್ಲ. adu havāmānada mēle avalambiside. maḷe bandare nānu baruvudilla. — "It depends on the weather. If it rains, I won't come."
ಬರುತ್ತೀರಾ (baruttīrā, "will you come?"), said with respect; ಬರುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (baruvudilla, "(I) won't come"). ಅದು … ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ (mēle avalambiside) is the reply when the honest answer is "maybe": name what it hangs on, then give the condition with -ಅರೆ.
Shorter, and very common:
ಅದು ನಿಮ್ಮ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ. adu nimma mēle avalambiside. — "It's up to you."
Word for word, "it depends on you". Beside it sits ಪ್ರಕಾರ, "according to", for what a plan or a rule says: ವೇಳಾಪಟ್ಟಿಯ ಪ್ರಕಾರ ಬಸ್ ಆರು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಹೊರಡುತ್ತದೆ (vēḷāpaṭṭiya prakāra bas āru gaṇṭege horaḍuttade, "according to the timetable, the bus leaves at six"). Where ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ says "it hangs on", ಪ್ರಕಾರ says "it follows".

[your turn — say: "It depends on the weather. If it rains, I won't come." — adu havāmānada mēle avalambiside. maḷe bandare nānu baruvudilla.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It's up to you" — adu nimma mēle avalambiside]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one plan of yours that depends on something, and the condition]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on the weather"? (ಅದು ಹವಾಮಾನದ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ (mēle avalambiside).) "It's up to you"? (ಅದು ನಿಮ್ಮ ಮೇಲೆ ಅವಲಂಬಿಸಿದೆ (mēle avalambiside).) What comes after "it depends"? (The condition, with -ಅರೆ.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ಇದ್ದಿದ್ದರೆ … ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ (iddiddare … hōguttidde) — what would have been.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home." (nāḷe maḷe bandare nānu maneyallē iruttēne.) Then "I was reading". (ōduttidde.)
[pause 1 second]
The first is a real "if". Today's "if" is one that did not happen, and the second form comes back to say what would have followed.

You'll want to know: an if that did not happen.
ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ದುಡ್ಡು ಇದ್ದಿದ್ದರೆ ನಾನು ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ. nanna hattira duḍḍu iddiddare nānu hōguttidde. — "If I had had money, I would have gone."
ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ, "near me", is how Kannada says what you have on you: ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ದುಡ್ಡು ಇದೆ, "I have money (on me)".
For an "if" that did not happen, Kannada puts ಇದ್ದರೆ after the -ಇ or -ಉ form: ಬಂದು + ಇದ್ದರೆ becomes ಬಂದಿದ್ದರೆ (bandiddare, "if (you) had come"); ಮಾಡಿ + ಇದ್ದರೆ becomes ಮಾಡಿದ್ದರೆ (māḍiddare, "if (you) had done"); ಇದ್ದು + ಇದ್ದರೆ becomes ಇದ್ದಿದ್ದರೆ (iddiddare, "if (there) had been").

You'll want to know: would have.
The result takes -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ, the form of "I was doing" and "I used to do", here "I would have done": ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ, "I would have gone". For "it", -ಉತ್ತಿತ್ತು:
ನೀನು ಬಂದಿದ್ದರೆ ನನಗೆ ಖುಷಿ ಆಗುತ್ತಿತ್ತು. nīnu bandiddare nanage khuṣi āguttittu. — "If you had come, I would have been glad."
The if-clause tells you which meaning you have. With -ಇದ್ದರೆ in front of it, ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ is "would have gone", not "was going".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If I had had money, I would have gone" — nanna hattira duḍḍu iddiddare nānu hōguttidde]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you had come, I would have been glad" — nīnu bandiddare nanage khuṣi āguttittu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If the bus had come, I would have come early" — bas bandiddare nānu bēga baruttidde]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If (you) had come"? (ಬಂದಿದ್ದರೆ.) "If there had been"? (ಇದ್ದಿದ್ದರೆ.) "I would have gone"? (ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ.) "It would have been"? (-ಉತ್ತಿತ್ತು: ಆಗುತ್ತಿತ್ತು.) What tells you it means "would"? (The -ಇದ್ದರೆ clause in front of it.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip in the past, with a feeling, a reason and an if.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last connected message you wrote was about a familiar day. This one tells a story: six lines about a trip, to your home town or anywhere else.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list of events. It has a scene, with -ಉವಾಗ or -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ. It has events in the plain past, kept in order with ಮೊದಲು, ಅದರ ನಂತರ, the -ಇ / -ಉ form and ಕೊನೆಗೆ. It has at least one reason, with ಹಾಗಾಗಿ or ಯಾಕೆಂದರೆ, and a feeling, with ನನಗೆ … ಆಯಿತು.
[pause 2 seconds]
A good one ends by looking at what might have been: … -ಇದ್ದರೆ, … -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, in Kannada script, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, and what you were doing as it began, with -ಉವಾಗ;
the first thing you did, with ಮೊದಲು: what you cleaned or packed, and a ticket or a seat;
what happened next, with ಅದರ ನಂತರ or an -ಇ / -ಉ form, and one thing that went wrong: a missed bus, a lost bag, or a fever and a medicine;
why, or what followed, with ಯಾಕೆಂದರೆ or ಹಾಗಾಗಿ;
how it ended and how you felt, with ಕೊನೆಗೆ and ನನಗೆ … ಆಯಿತು;
one line about what might have been, with -ಇದ್ದರೆ … -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model on this page, and no word bank. Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing the right past for each line, and joining the lines.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines aloud. Mark every verb s for scene and e for event. Line one should be mostly s; lines two and three mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then two spelling checks. If you wrote ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ, is the ಛ small, under ಚ? If a word opens with au, as ಔಷಧ does, it opens with the letter ಔ; inside a word, au is the sign ◌ೌ.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. Does the if-clause end in -ಇದ್ದರೆ, and the main verb in -ಉತ್ತಿದ್ದೆ?
