Lesson 1 of 5.
اونٹ (ūṅṭ) — a camel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an ant, then the Urdu for a donkey.

You'll want to know: اونٹ.
اونٹ — ūṅṭ — "a camel".

The word, taken apart.
اونٹ (ūṅṭ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from uṣṭra, "buffalo; camel" (CDIAL 2387).

Lesson 2 of 5.
لومڑی (lomṛī) — a fox.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a donkey, then the Urdu for a camel.

You'll want to know: لومڑی.
لومڑی — lomṛī — "a fox".

The word, taken apart.
لومڑی (lomṛī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lompaṭa, "fox", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 11153). It is a variant CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit lomaṭaka, "fox"; CDIAL suggests these forms may blend a word for "fox" with words for "hair" and "tail".

خرگوش — khargosh — "a rabbit".

The word, taken apart.
خرگوش (khargosh) is a loan, usually traced to Persian khargōsh, "hare". It is built from khar, "donkey", and gōsh, "ear".
