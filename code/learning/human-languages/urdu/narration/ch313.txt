Urdu, chapter 313: A Movement for Freedom.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
انگریز (angrez) — an English person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a rebellion, then the Urdu for to challenge.

You'll want to know: انگریز.
انگریز — angrez — "an English person".
انگریز سو سال یہاں رہے۔ angrez sau sāl yahāṅ rahe. The English stayed here for a hundred years.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
انگریز (angrez) is usually traced to Portuguese inglês, "English", which reached Urdu through Persian angrez. انگریزی is the language.

ہر شہر میں آزادی کی تحریک تھی۔ har shahr meṅ āzādī kī tahrīk thī. There was a freedom movement in every city.

The word, taken apart.
تحریک (tahrīk) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic taḥrīk, "a setting in motion": people moved to act together.

The word, taken apart.
جدوجہد (jaddojahd) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic jidd, "effort", and jahd, "striving", joined by o, "and". It is feminine.

Lesson 5 of 5.
سونپنا (saunpnā) — to entrust; to hand over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a struggle, then the Urdu for a sacrifice.

You'll want to know: سونپنا.
سونپنا — saunpnā — "to entrust; to hand over".
بادشاہ نے بیٹے کو تخت سونپا۔ bādshāh ne beṭe ko takht saunpā. The king handed the throne to his son.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے چابی سونپی, us ne chābī saunpī.

The word, taken apart.
سونپنا (saunpnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin saũpnā, "hands over", under Sanskrit sámarpayati (CDIAL 13192). The one who receives takes کو, and the verb follows the thing: تخت سونپا.
