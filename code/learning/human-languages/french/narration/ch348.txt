French, chapter 348: Grâce à toi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
à cause de — because of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for consequently, then the French for that is why.

You'll want to know: à cause de.
à cause de — "because of".
Nous sommes restés à la maison à cause de la neige. We stayed at home because of the snow.
Followed by a noun; with a verb, use parce que. À cause du, à cause des.

The word, taken apart.
à cause de is built on cause, which comes from Latin causa "cause, reason, lawsuit" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
grâce à — thanks to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for that is why, then the French for because of.

You'll want to know: grâce à.
grâce à — "thanks to".
Grâce à toi, j'ai trouvé un travail. Thanks to you, I found a job.
For good results; for bad ones, à cause de.

The word, taken apart.
grâce comes through Old French grace from Latin grātia "favour, thanks" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on grātus "pleasing".

Lesson 3 of 5.
en effet — indeed; and that is because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for because of, then the French for thanks to.

You'll want to know: en effet.
en effet — "indeed; and that is because".
Il est fatigué; en effet, il a travaillé toute la nuit. He is tired; indeed, he worked all night.
It confirms or explains what came before; it does not mean in effect.

The word, taken apart.
effet comes through Old French effet from Latin effectus "an effect, a result" (EtymDB), from efficere "to bring about", built on facere "to do, to make" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
dès lors — from then on; consequently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for thanks to, then the French for indeed.

You'll want to know: dès lors.
dès lors — "from then on; consequently".
Il a perdu son travail; dès lors, tout a changé. He lost his job; from then on, everything changed.
Two senses: from that moment on, and so. Dès lors que means given that.

The word, taken apart.
lors goes back through Old French to Vulgar Latin illā hōrā "at that hour" (EtymDB). dès "from" is usually traced to Latin dē ex "from out of".

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'origine — the origin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for indeed, then the French for from then on.

You'll want to know: l'origine.
l'origine — "the origin".
Quelle est l'origine de ce problème? What is the origin of this problem?
À l'origine — originally. Feminine: une origine.

The word, taken apart.
origine is a learned borrowing from Latin orīgō "beginning, source, origin" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from orīrī "to rise".
