Portuguese, chapter 495: Educar os filhos.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
educar — to bring up, to educate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to crawl, then the Portuguese for to rock.

You'll want to know: educar.
educar — "to bring up, to educate".
Os meus pais educaram-nos com muito carinho. My parents brought us up with a lot of love.
Bem-educado — polite, well brought up.

The word, taken apart.
educar comes from Latin ēducāre "to bring up, to rear" (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
comportar — to behave (comportar-se); to hold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to rock, then the Portuguese for to bring up.

You'll want to know: comportar.
comportar — "to behave (comportar-se); to hold".
As crianças comportaram-se bem no restaurante. The children behaved well in the restaurant.
Reflexive in practice: comportar-se bem or mal. The noun is o comportamento.

The word, taken apart.
comportar comes from Latin comportāre "to carry together" (EtymDB), built on portāre "to carry".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: castigar.
castigar — "to punish".
Os pais castigaram o filho: uma semana sem televisão. The parents punished their son: a week without television.
Ficar de castigo — to be grounded.

The word, taken apart.
castigar is inherited, through Old Portuguese, from Latin castīgāre "to correct, to punish" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
a mesada — (monthly) pocket money, allowance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to behave, then the Portuguese for to punish.

You'll want to know: a mesada.
a mesada — "(monthly) pocket money, allowance".
Recebo uma mesada de vinte euros. I get twenty euros pocket money a month.
Built on o mês: money given each month. Weekly money is a semanada.

The word, taken apart.
mesada is built on mês "month", which is inherited from Latin mēnsis "month" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
mimado — spoiled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to punish, then the Portuguese for (monthly) pocket money.

You'll want to know: mimado.
mimado — "spoiled".
É um menino mimado que quer tudo. He is a spoiled little boy who wants everything.
Of a child who gets too much: uma criança mimada.

The word, taken apart.
mimado is the participle of mimar "to pamper", built on mimo "a treat, a caress"; the origin of mimo is not settled.
