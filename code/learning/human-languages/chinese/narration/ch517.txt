Lesson 1 of 6.
tiānliàng — to get light at dawn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the woods, then the Mandarin for to ripen.

You'll want to know: tiānliàng.
tiānliàng — "to get light at dawn".
wàipó shuō dōngtiān qī diǎn cái tiānliàng — Grandma says it doesn't get light until seven in winter.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiānliàng is built from 天 'the sky' and liàng 'bright', both inherited from Middle Chinese, then and ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'bright' and 'clear'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
光 (guāng) — light from the sun or a lamp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to ripen, then the Mandarin for to get light at dawn.

You'll want to know: 光.
光 — guāng — "light from the sun or a lamp".
停电以后房间里没有光了 tíngdiàn yǐhòu fángjiān lǐ méiyǒu guāng le — After the power went off, there was no light in the room.
The new character, in pieces: 光 (guāng) — six strokes in three pieces: on top a short vertical with a dot on each side, three strokes; a long bar across the middle; and 儿 at the foot, two strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
光 (guāng) is inherited from Middle Chinese kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'bright'; the dictionary also lists a departing-tone reading, kwangH, for adding colour. The noun 'light' is not glossed there.

Script — three short strokes, a bar, then 儿.
光 (guāng).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, three strokes: a short vertical down the middle, a dot on its left, then a short stroke on its right falling to the left.
One long bar across the middle, under them.
The bottom piece, 儿, two strokes, as you wrote it: a stroke falling to the left, then a vertical that bends right along the foot and hooks up.
3 + 1 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
发光 (fāguāng) — to give off light; to shine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get light at dawn, then the Mandarin for light from the sun.

You'll want to know: 发光.
发光 — fāguāng — "to give off light; to shine".
星星为什么会发光 xīngxing wèishénme huì fāguāng — Why do the stars shine?

The word, taken apart.
发光 (fāguāng) is built from 发 'to send out' and 光 (guāng) 'light', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjot and kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to start' and 'to open out' and the second 'bright'.

Warm-up.

huánghūn — "twilight; the fading light of evening".
wǒ zuì xǐhuan huánghūn de shíhou qù sànbù — Twilight is my favourite time for a walk.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huánghūn is built from huáng 'yellow' and hūn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwang and xwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as the colour of the centre and, after an older dictionary, the second as the sun gone dark.
