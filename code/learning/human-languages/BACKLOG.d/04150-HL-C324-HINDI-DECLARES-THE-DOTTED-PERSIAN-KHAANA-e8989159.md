## HL-C324 — Hindi declares the dotted Persian ख़ाना

After the explicit 11–20 inventory repair, **ख़ाना** is the next independent
modern Hindi form in the ranked glossed-but-never-taught queue. Chapter 37
already contrasts Sanskrit-derived **खाना** (“food; to eat”) with the
Persian-derived near-homograph **ख़ाना** (“house, compartment”), explains the
nuqta that separates them in careful writing, and notes that ordinary print can
omit that dot.

The lesson asks learners to produce both forms in guided practice and assesses
the meaning and origin of **ख़ाना** again in wrap-up recall. Its old headword
declared only **खाना**, leaving the complete assessed contrast invisible to
reports and downstream cards.

Declare **खाना / ख़ाना** as the lesson headword, align its gloss and
romanization with both forms, and regenerate the derived Hindi artifacts. This
is a metadata repair for existing teaching, not a new vocabulary claim.
