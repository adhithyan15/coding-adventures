5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おんせん (onsen) — a hot spring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a day trip, then the Japanese for to look after.

You'll want to know: おんせん.
おんせん — onsen — "a hot spring".
ふゆは おんせんが いちばん いいです。 — fuyu wa onsen ga ichiban ii desu — "In winter a hot spring is the best thing."

The word, taken apart.
おんせん (onsen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are on, "warm" (Middle Chinese 'won) and sen, "spring" (dzjwen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ゆげ (yuge) — rising steam; steam from hot water.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to look after, then the Japanese for a hot spring.

You'll want to know: ゆげ.
ゆげ — yuge — "rising steam; steam from hot water".
おんせんの ゆげが みえます。 — onsen no yuge ga miemasu — "You can see the steam from the hot spring."

The word, taken apart.
ゆげ (yuge) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the native Japanese (wago) yu, "hot water", inherited rather than borrowed, and the Sino-Japanese ke, "air" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese, joined as yuge (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
のぼせる (noboseru) — to feel dizzy (from the heat of a bath).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a hot spring, then the Japanese for rising steam.

You'll want to know: のぼせる.
のぼせる — noboseru — "to feel dizzy (from the heat of a bath)".
ながく おふろに はいって、のぼせました。 — nagaku ofuro ni haitte, nobosemashita — "I stayed in the bath too long and felt dizzy."
Polite のぼせます, nobosemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あにが おふろで のぼせました。, ani ga ofuro de nobosemashita.

The word, taken apart.
のぼせる (noboseru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese (JMdict).

つかる — tsukaru — "to soak (in water)".
