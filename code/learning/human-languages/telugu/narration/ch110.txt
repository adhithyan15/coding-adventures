Telugu, chapter 110: Lock, Key, Rope, Nail, Scissors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
తాళం (tāḷaṁ) — a lock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a blanket, then the Telugu for a curtain, a screen.

You'll want to know: తాళం.
తాళం — tāḷaṁ — "a lock".

The word, taken apart.
తాళం (tāḷaṁ) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil tāḻ and Kannada tāḻ, "bolt, bar" (DEDR 3179), which lists tāḷamu, "lock, key". DEDR compares Sanskrit tāla and tāḍaka, "latch, bolt" (CDIAL 5749).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: తాళంచెవి.

The word, taken apart.
తాళంచెవి (tāḷaṁcevi) is native Dravidian: tāḷaṁ, "lock" (DEDR 3179), and cevi, "ear", one word with Tamil cevi (DEDR 1977): the lock's ear. DEDR compares Sanskrit tāḍaka, "latch", with the first word (CDIAL 5749).

కత్తెర — katterā — "scissors".

The word, taken apart.
కత్తెర (katterā) comes from Sanskrit kartarī, "scissors, knife", reshaped to Telugu sounds: a tadbhava (CDIAL 2858).
