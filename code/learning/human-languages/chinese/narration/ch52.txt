Lesson 1 of 7.
手 (shǒu) — hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hot, then the Mandarin for to cry.

You'll want to know: 手.
手 — shǒu — "hand".
我的手很小 wǒ de shǒu hěn xiǎo — My hands are small.
The new character, in pieces: 手 (shǒu) — a short fall, two bars, and a vertical that hooks at the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
手 (shǒu) is inherited from Middle Chinese syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed with the pair 'hand and foot'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how you are.

Lesson 2 of 7.
手 (shǒu) — a fall, two bars, a hook.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a fall, two bars, a hook and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.

The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say shǒu — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
看 小.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kàn, xiǎo]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a fall, two bars, a hook.
手 (shǒu).
One piece, four strokes, top to bottom.
A short left-falling stroke across the top, from right to left.
Two horizontals, the lower one longer.
Last, a vertical down through both bars, hooking to the left at the foot.
The top of 看 is this hand, squeezed flat over the eye.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 手 (shǒu), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 手 (shǒu)? (Four.) Which way does the first stroke fall? (To the left.) Which character you know has a hand on top? (看.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 手 (shǒu), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Lesson 6 of 7.
ěrduo — ear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for foot, then the Mandarin for eye.

You'll want to know: ěrduo.
ěrduo — "ear".
háizi de ěrduo hěn xiǎo — The child's ears are tiny.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ěrduo is built from ěr 'ear' and duo, both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyiX and twaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as what hears and the second as something hanging from a tree; the second probably names the soft lobe that hangs, though that is not settled.

One more word for saying how you are.
