French, chapter 515: Sur scène.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le trac — stage fright, nerves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for costume, then the French for interval.

You'll want to know: le trac.
le trac — "stage fright, nerves".
Avant chaque concert, elle a le trac. Before every concert she gets stage fright.
Avoir le trac — to have nerves, also before an exam.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of le trac is not settled; it appeared in French in the nineteenth century.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la réplique — line (in a play); reply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for interval, then the French for stage fright.

You'll want to know: la réplique.
la réplique — "line (in a play); reply".
L'acteur a oublié sa réplique. The actor forgot his line.
Also a copy: une réplique de la statue.

The word, taken apart.
la réplique is built on répliquer "to reply", from Latin replicāre "to fold back, to reply", built on plicāre "to fold" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
interpréter — to perform, to play (a role); to interpret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for stage fright, then the French for line.

You'll want to know: interpréter.
interpréter — "to perform, to play (a role); to interpret".
Elle interprète le rôle de la mère. She plays the role of the mother.
Also to interpret a dream or a text. An interpreter between languages is un interprète.

The word, taken apart.
interpréter is a learned borrowing from Latin interpretārī "to explain, to translate" (EtymDB), from interpres "a go-between, an interpreter" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le comédien — actor (especially on stage).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for line, then the French for to perform.

You'll want to know: le comédien.
le comédien — "actor (especially on stage)".
Ce comédien joue au théâtre et au cinéma. This actor works in the theatre and in film.
A woman is la comédienne. Not only comic actors: any actor.

The word, taken apart.
le comédien is built on comédie (EtymDB), from Latin cōmoedia, a borrowing from Greek kōmōidía "comedy".

Lesson 5 of 5.
les applaudissements — applause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to perform, then the French for actor.

You'll want to know: les applaudissements.
les applaudissements — "applause".
À la fin, il y a eu de longs applaudissements. At the end there was long applause.
Usually plural. The verb is applaudir "to clap".

The word, taken apart.
les applaudissements is built on applaudir "to clap", a learned borrowing from Latin applaudere "to clap at", built on plaudere "to clap" (Lewis & Short).
