# صباح الخير (ṣabāḥ al-khayr) — "morning of goodness"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Three pieces you have — *ṣabāḥ*, *al-*, *khayr* — snap into the
standard "good morning."

## You'll want to know first

- [صباح (ṣabāḥ)](./AR-C01-sabah.md) · [خير (khayr)](./AR-C01-khayr.md) ·
  [ال (al-)](./AR-C01-al.md)

## The word, taken apart — by assembling it

**صباح الخير** = **ṣabāḥ** ("morning") + **al-khayr** ("the goodness") =
literally **"morning of the goodness."** *khayr* begins with خ, a **moon
letter**, so *al-* stays *al-* (no assimilation) — a clean contrast with
*as-salām* from the peace greeting.

This noun-next-to-noun construction ("morning [of] the-goodness") is how
Arabic builds "X of Y" — no separate word for "of." You'll see it everywhere.

## Why it's said this way

The reply is a small delight: **صباح النور** (*ṣabāḥ an-nūr*) — "morning of
**the light**." You wish goodness; they wish you light back. (*an-nūr*: *al-* +
*nūr* "light," with *n* a sun letter, so it assimilates to *an-nūr* — the same
rule as *as-salām*.)
