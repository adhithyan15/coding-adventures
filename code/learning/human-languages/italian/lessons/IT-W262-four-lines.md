# Four lines, and the full stop that answers

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-ESSERE-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson asked for writing **finished on time**. This one asks
for writing that **holds together**.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-MA-02, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can be
connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a
sentence stops standing on its own.

[PAUSE 2s] You hold three joiners. **e** adds. **ma** sets against. **perché**
gives the reason. After **perché** the words keep the order they would have on
their own: *perché non mi piace*. Nothing moves.

[PAUSE 2s] And **perché** is one word for two jobs: *why* and *because*. Spoken,
the voice tells them apart. On paper only the end of the line does: a
**question mark** asks, a **full stop** answers.

[PAUSE 2s] Its accent leans the other way from the one on **è**: **é** rises as
it goes right, **è** falls as it goes right. One line can hold both, so write
each one on purpose.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-ESSERE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-ESSERE-03, IT-LEX-MA-02, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the person you picked, on paper.

At least one must use **e** or **ma**. At least one must give a reason with
**perché**, and end with a full stop.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary; the
whole exercise is the joining.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PERCHE-02, IT-GRAMMAR-ESSERE-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Cover your first line and read the second alone. Does it still make
sense without the first? If it does, you wrote a **list**, which is where you
were one lesson ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Then find your **perché** line. A full stop at its end, not a
question mark? An accent that rises? If either is wrong, you wrote a question,
or a different mark, and the reader will meet it before they meet your reason.
