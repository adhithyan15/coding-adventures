Latin, chapter 547: Images and art.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
imāgō, imāginis — an image, likeness, portrait.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a prologue, then the Latin for a mime dancer.

You'll want to know: imāgō, imāginis.
imāgō, imāginis — "an image, likeness, portrait".
In ātriō imāginēs maiōrum sunt. In the hall there are portraits of the ancestors.
Feminine, third declension. Also the wax masks of ancestors kept in the hall. English image.

The word, taken apart.
imāgō is usually linked with imitor "to copy" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English image and imagine come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
effigiēs, effigiēī — a likeness, effigy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a mime dancer, then the Latin for an image.

You'll want to know: effigiēs, effigiēī.
effigiēs, effigiēī — "a likeness, effigy".
Effigiēs avī in ātriō stat. A likeness of grandfather stands in the hall.
Feminine, fifth declension, from effingō — to shape, portray. English effigy.

The word, taken apart.
effigiēs is built on effingō "to form, portray", from ex- and fingō "to shape" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English effigy comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
figūra, figūrae — a shape, form, figure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an image, then the Latin for a likeness.

You'll want to know: figūra, figūrae.
figūra, figūrae — "a shape, form, figure".
Pictor figūram equī in mūrō pingit. The painter paints the shape of a horse on the wall.
Feminine, first declension, from fingō. English figure.

The word, taken apart.
figūra is built on fingō "to shape, mould" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB traces fingō to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to knead, shape". English figure comes from it.

Feminine, a diminutive of tessera — a cube, a die. English tessellated.

tessella is a diminutive of tessera "a cube, die" (Lewis & Short). English tessellated comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
caelō, caelāre — to engrave, carve in relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a shape, then the Latin for a small cube.

You'll want to know: caelō, caelāre.
caelō, caelāre — "to engrave, carve in relief".
Faber pōculum argenteum caelat. The smith engraves a silver cup.
caelō, caelāre, caelāvī, caelātum: raised work on silver, gold or ivory. Not to be confused with cēlō — to hide.

The word, taken apart.
caelō is built on caelum "an engraver’s chisel", a different word from caelum "sky" (Lewis & Short).
