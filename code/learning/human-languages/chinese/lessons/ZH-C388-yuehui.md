## You'll want to know: 约会
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C388-DATE388-02]; assesses=[] -->

**约会** — *yuēhuì* — "a romantic date; an appointment (to meet)".

> **今天晚上我有一个约会** *jīntiān wǎnshang wǒ yǒu yí ge yuēhuì* — I've got a date this evening.

The new character, in pieces: 约 — six strokes in two pieces: the silk piece on the left of 给, three strokes; on the right a short fall, a bar that turns down into a hook, and a dot inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**约会** (*yuēhuì*) is built from 约 'to bind' and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *'jak* and *hwajH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tie up' and the second 'to come together': a meeting agreed on beforehand.

## What you've built
