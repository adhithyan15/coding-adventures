Japanese, chapter 476: Only Children and Twins.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
いとこ (itoko) — a cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a small plate, then the Japanese for flavouring.

You'll want to know: いとこ.
いとこ — itoko — "a cousin".
なつやすみに いとこと あそびました。 — natsuyasumi ni itoko to asobimashita — "I played with my cousin in the summer holidays."

The word, taken apart.
いとこ (itoko) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; JMdict writes it in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: ふたご.
ふたご — futago — "twins; a twin".
となりの こどもは ふたごです。 — tonari no kodomo wa futago desu — "The children next door are twins."

The word, taken apart.
ふたご (futago) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: futa, "two" and go, "child (ko)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ちょうなん (chōnan) — the eldest son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for twins, then the Japanese for an only child.

You'll want to know: ちょうなん.
ちょうなん — chōnan — "the eldest son".
ちょうなんは らいねん けっこんします。 — chōnan wa rainen kekkon shimasu — "Our eldest son is getting married next year."

The word, taken apart.
ちょうなん (chōnan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are chō, "senior" (Middle Chinese trjangX) and nan, "male" (nom) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
