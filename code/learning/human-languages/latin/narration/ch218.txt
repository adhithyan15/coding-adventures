Latin, chapter 218: Pace, Witness, Defendant, Punishment, Charge.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
passus, passūs — a pace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a vehicle, then the Latin for a chariot.

You'll want to know: passus, passūs.
passus, passūs — "a pace".

The word, taken apart.
passus is built on pandō, "to spread out, to stretch" (EtymDB 2.1): a pace as the stretch of the legs. A Roman mile was mīlle passūs, "a thousand paces".

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a chariot, then the Latin for a pace.

You'll want to know: testis, testis.
testis, testis — "a witness".

The word, taken apart.
testis is usually explained as an old compound, tri-stis, "the third who stands by", from the root of trēs, "three", and stō, "to stand": a witness as the third party.

Lesson 4 of 5.
poena, poenae — a punishment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a witness, then the Latin for a defendant.

You'll want to know: poena, poenae.
poena, poenae — "a punishment".

The word, taken apart.
poena was borrowed from Greek poinē, "a fine, blood-money" (Lewis & Short), which is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kwoyneh2, "penalty" (EtymDB 2.1). English pain comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
crīmen, crīminis — a charge, a crime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a defendant, then the Latin for a punishment.

You'll want to know: crīmen, crīminis.
crīmen, crīminis — "a charge, a crime".

The word, taken apart.
crīmen belongs with cernō, "to sift, to decide" (Lewis & Short), and is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European kreymn, "a verdict, a sifting" (EtymDB 2.1): first a judgement, then a charge.
