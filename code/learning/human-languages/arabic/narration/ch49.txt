Arabic, chapter 49: Letters From Food, Noon and Going.
5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ط (ṭāʾ) — the letter that opens طعام.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ط and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ز and غ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say طعام (ṭaʿām), "food". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ط.
ط — ṭāʾ, a heavy t.
It opens طعام (ṭaʿām), "food". A closed oval on the line, and a tall upright drawn last, from the top down.

Writing: ط.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the closed oval.
2. finish left along the baseline without lifting.
3. lift once, then draw the tall upright top-to-bottom — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet ط (ṭāʾ) ظ (ẓāʾ), independent emphatic Taa at 00:01.2–00:03.0 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-23).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at طعام and point at ط (ṭāʾ) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ط (ṭāʾ) three times, saying ṭ as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: غ, then ط (ṭāʾ), from memory]
[your turn — read: طعام aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

It is inside الظهر (aẓ-ẓuhr), "noon". It is ط (ṭāʾ), from the last lesson, with one dot above.

Writing: ظ.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the closed oval.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ذ (dhāl) — the letter that opens ذهب.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ذ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ط (ṭāʾ) and ظ (ẓāʾ) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ذهب (dhahaba), "he went". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ذ.
ذ — dhāl, the th of English "this".
It opens ذهب (dhahaba), "he went", the first verb in this book. It is د (dāl) with one dot above, and like د it never joins to the letter after it.

Writing: ذ.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend diagonally down and right through the curved shoulder.
2. turn left along the baseline without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet: د ذ (dhāl) ر, independent Dhaal body followed by its upper dot (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-21).
