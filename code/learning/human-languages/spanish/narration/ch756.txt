Spanish, chapter 756: El partido.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el delantero — the forward, the striker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get injured, then the Spanish for the muscle.

You'll want to know: el delantero.
el delantero — "the forward, the striker".
Nuestro delantero es muy rápido. Our striker is very fast.
As an adjective: la parte delantera — the front.

The word, taken apart.
delantero is built on delante "in front", which comes from Old Spanish denante (EtymDB), from Latin dē, in and ante "before".

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
marcar — to score; to mark; to dial.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the muscle, then the Spanish for the forward.

You'll want to know: marcar.
marcar — "to score; to mark; to dial".
Marcó dos goles en la final. He scored two goals in the final.
Also to dial a number: marcar el número. Spelling: marqué.

The word, taken apart.
marcar comes from Italian marcare "to mark" (EtymDB), which is built on Germanic marka "boundary, sign".

el marcador — "the scoreboard, the score".
El marcador final fue dos a uno. The final score was two-one.
In Latin America un marcador is also a marker pen.

The word, taken apart.
marcador is built on marcar with the ending -dor; marcar comes from Italian marcare, built on Germanic marka "boundary, sign".

Lesson 5 of 5.
empatar — to draw, to tie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the scoreboard, then the Spanish for the referee.

You'll want to know: empatar.
empatar — "to draw, to tie".
Empatamos uno a uno. We drew one all.
El empate — the draw.

The word, taken apart.
empatar was borrowed from Italian impattare "to draw", built on patta "a draw, a tie" (EtymDB). Where patta comes from is not settled: EtymDB lists both a Lombardic word and Latin pactum "agreement".
