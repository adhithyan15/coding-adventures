Mandarin Chinese, chapter 29: Reading — Characters, Words, and a First Passage.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) (zhōngguórén, wǒ de shū, kàn shū de rén, yī gè, shíbā suì, xǐhuan) (wǒ shì zhōngguórén. wǒ yǒu yī gè míngzi.) (rén, shū, kàn, yǒu, hé, shí) — six characters, nothing said first.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have written every one of these. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody is going to say them first.

Reading.
人 书 看 有 和 十.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once. Do not count strokes — look at the whole character and let it arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to take apart. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
In an alphabet a single letter is almost never a word. Here every one of these six is.
[pause 2 seconds]
That changes what reading is. There is no sounding out: a character either lands whole or it does not land, and the only thing that moves it from the second to the first is having met it before.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six is enough for one sitting, because six is already six words.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six characters down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which character is a book?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of them are whole words? (All six.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) (zhōngguórén, wǒ de shū, kàn shū de rén, yī gè, shíbā suì, xǐhuan) (wǒ shì zhōngguórén. wǒ yǒu yī gè míngzi.) (rén, shū, kàn, yǒu, hé, shí) — six words, not six characters.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you six characters that were each a word. These are six words that are not each a character.
[pause 1 second]
Every character in them is still one you have written.

Reading.
中国人 我的书 看书的人 一个 十八岁 喜欢.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time notice there is nothing between the characters. No space tells you where one word ends.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
看书的人 is four characters and one idea: the person who reads. You know all four, and knowing them is not the same as seeing where the join falls.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the work this lesson does. Mandarin writes no spaces, so a reader has to decide where a word ends, and the only tool for it is having met the word before.
[pause 2 seconds]
喜欢 is the clearest case: two characters that mean nothing apart, and to like together.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which is the person who reads?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many characters are in 看书的人? (Four.) How many words? (One.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What tells you where a word ends? (Nothing on the page — only having met it.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) (zhōngguórén, wǒ de shū, kàn shū de rén, yī gè, shíbā suì, xǐhuan) (wǒ shì zhōngguórén. wǒ yǒu yī gè míngzi.) (rén, shū, kàn, yǒu, hé, shí) — the first passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six characters, then six words. Now ten lines.
[pause 1 second]
Thirty-four characters, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
我是中国人 我有一个名字 我看书 我喜欢看书 我十八岁 你呢 对不对 你好 谢谢 再见.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it through once without stopping. Do not translate as you go.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and this time notice that the passage turns at line six. The first five are about the writer. The last five are addressed to you.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
你呢 is two characters and does the work of a whole English sentence: and what about you? It only means that because of the five lines before it, which is the first time this book has asked a line to depend on what came earlier.
[pause 2 seconds]
我十八岁 has no verb in it at all. Mandarin states an age by putting the number and 岁 straight after the person, and a reader who waits for a verb waits forever.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because every character was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
How old is the writer?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which line depends on the five before it? (你呢).
[pause 2 seconds]
Which line has no verb? (我十八岁 — and it needs none.)
