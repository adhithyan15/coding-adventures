Japanese, chapter 496: Soft and Salty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
やわらかい (yawarakai) — soft; tender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fresh, then the Japanese for light (of food).

You'll want to know: やわらかい.
やわらかい — yawarakai — "soft; tender".
この にくは やわらかいです。 — kono niku wa yawarakai desu — "This meat is tender."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
やわらかい (yawarakai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

しおからい — shiokarai — "salty".

しおからい (shiokarai) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from shio, "salt" and karai, "hot, spicy" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
えんぶん (enbun) — salt content.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for salty, then the Japanese for raw.

You'll want to know: えんぶん.
えんぶん — enbun — "salt content".
この みそしるは えんぶんが おおいです。 — kono misoshiru wa enbun ga ōi desu — "This miso soup has a lot of salt in it."

The word, taken apart.
えんぶん (enbun) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are en, "salt" (Middle Chinese yem) and bun, "part" (bjunH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
