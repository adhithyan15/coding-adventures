## Added — A2 vocabulary: 520 words in chapters 146–249; Japanese attains A2

- **104 new chapters (146–249) teach 520 new headwords, one word per lesson.** 210 of them are verbs, which clears the verb target the track was 47 short of. Each lesson gives:
  - the word in hiragana with its Hepburn romanization (macrons for long vowels, as chapters 133 onward write them);
  - a short spaced-kana example that uses only grammar and words the book has taught, its romanization, and its translation;
  - a note on the verb class, its polite form or its transitive partner;
  - a "The word, taken apart" origin;
  - one "type the Japanese" activity.

  Each word is practised again in the next two lessons, and a review lesson closes every six chapters. The six-chapter span keeps each review inside the lesson time budget. Every chapter sits on one of the A2 spine nodes chapters 138–142 realized:
  - 146–171 (130 words): daily routine and personal care, clothes, the home and housework, and time expressions (days, dates, how often).
  - 172–197 (130 words): food, cooking and eating out; shopping, money, the bank and the post office; places in town and directions.
  - 198–223 (130 words): trains, roads and planes; weather, seasons and nature; health, the body and the doctor's.
  - 224–249 (130 words): school, study and work; leisure, sport and hobbies; people, relationships and feelings.
- **Japanese now teaches 1,214 headwords at or below A2**, past the level gate's target of 1,200, and attains A2.
- **Script.** Headwords, examples and notes use only kana the writing lessons have taught, so letter-anchoring and script-closure stay at zero. There are no katakana loanwords, and no word with づ or ぢ, which have no writing lesson. Kanji appear only in the etymology hook, as the track already does.
- **Sources.** Native words (wago), Sino-Japanese words (kango) and mixed words are told apart.
  - Kanji spellings and meanings come from JMdict and KANJIDIC2.
  - Middle Chinese readings follow the Guangyun entry for the sense, in Baxter's transcription (tshet-uinh).
  - Older origins the sources do not give are marked not settled.
  - An independent review of each tranche fixed errors in 77 of the 520 words (14.8%), mostly kanji meanings KANJIDIC2 does not give and Middle Chinese entries for the wrong sense, and some unnatural example sentences.
- **No forward references and no hidden duplicates.** Every candidate headword was screened against every earlier lesson's text. The forward-reference census is empty, and Japanese's ceiling of 0 holds. Dictionary forms of taught -masu verbs (おきる for おきます) were ruled out.
