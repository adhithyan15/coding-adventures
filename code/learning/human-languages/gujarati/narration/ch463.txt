Gujarati, chapter 463: Chatty and Quiet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સરળ (saraḷ) — straightforward (of a person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for fun-loving, then the Gujarati for to go astray.

You'll want to know: સરળ.
સરળ — saraḷ — "straightforward (of a person)".
મારી બહેનપણી સરળ સ્વભાવની છે. mārī bahenpaṇī saraḷ svabhāvnī che. My friend has a straightforward nature.

The word, taken apart.
સરળ (saraḷ) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sarala, "straight, not crooked", as Monier-Williams records it.

અરુણ ઓછાબોલો છે, પણ ઘણું સમજે છે. Aruṇ ochābolo che, paṇ ghaṇũ samje che. Arun is a man of few words, but he understands a lot.

The word, taken apart.
ઓછાબોલું (ochābolũ) is built from ઓછું (ochũ), "less", and the stem of બોલવું (bolvũ), "to speak": someone who speaks little. CDIAL lists Gujarati ochũ under ōccha- (CDIAL 2540) and bolvũ under bōll- (CDIAL 9321).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for of few words, then the Gujarati for talkative.

You'll want to know: બકવું.
બકવું — bakvũ — "to babble; to talk nonsense".
તાવમાં દર્દી કંઈક બકતો હતો. tāvmā̃ dardī kaīk bakto hato. In his fever the patient was babbling something.
Past (he …): તે ઘણું બક્યો, te ghaṇũ bakyo.

The word, taken apart.
બકવું (bakvũ) goes back to a reconstructed bakk-, "chatter", an imitative word; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to prattle" (CDIAL 9117).
