Spanish, chapter 518: Un robo en el barrio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
robar — to steal, to rob.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to notice, then the Spanish for the detail.

You'll want to know: robar.
robar — "to steal, to rob".
Me robaron la cartera en el metro. Someone stole my wallet on the metro.
Robar algo a alguien — to steal something from someone.

The word, taken apart.
robar comes from a Germanic verb raubōn 'to rob, to plunder', through Medieval Latin raubāre (EtymDB). English rob goes back to the same Germanic verb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el sospechoso — the suspect; suspicious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to steal, then the Spanish for the robbery.

You'll want to know: el sospechoso.
el sospechoso — "the suspect; suspicious".
La policía busca a dos sospechosos. The police are looking for two suspects.
As an adjective: un coche sospechoso — a suspicious car.

The word, taken apart.
sospechoso is built on sospecha 'suspicion' with the ending -oso. Sospecha is built on sospechar, which is inherited from Latin suspectāre 'to look up at, to suspect' (EtymDB), from suspicere.

Lesson 4 of 5.
atracar — to hold up, to mug; to moor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the robbery, then the Spanish for the suspect.

You'll want to know: atracar.
atracar — "to hold up, to mug; to moor".
Dos hombres atracaron un banco en el centro. Two men held up a bank in the centre.
Un atraco is a hold-up. For a boat, atracar means to moor.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of atracar is not settled. The sense 'to hold up, to mug' is often linked to the sailors' verb atracar 'to bring a boat alongside'. EtymDB traces a Spanish atracar to an Arabic verb, without saying which sense it means.

Lesson 5 of 5.
huir — to flee, to run away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the suspect, then the Spanish for to hold up.

You'll want to know: huir.
huir — "to flee, to run away".
El ladrón huyó en un coche rojo. The thief fled in a red car.
It takes a y in many forms: huyo, huyó, huyeron.

The word, taken apart.
huir is inherited from Latin fugere 'to flee' (Lewis & Short), through old Spanish fuir (EtymDB). Latin f- became Spanish h-. English fugitive is from the same verb.
