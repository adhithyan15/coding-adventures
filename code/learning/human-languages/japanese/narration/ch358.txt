Japanese, chapter 358: Renting a Room.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
やちん (yachin) — rent (for a home).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for leeway, then the Japanese for to decide officially.

You'll want to know: やちん.
やちん — yachin — "rent (for a home)".
やちんが あがったら、ひっこします。 — yachin ga agattara, hikkoshimasu — "If the rent goes up, I will move."

The word, taken apart.
やちん (yachin) is built from the native ya, "house", a kun'yomi, and the Sino-Japanese chin, "fee", an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese nrimH) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おおや (ōya) — a landlord; a landlady.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to decide officially, then the Japanese for rent.

You'll want to know: おおや.
おおや — ōya — "a landlord; a landlady".
へやが こわれたら、おおやに れんらくして ください。 — heya ga kowaretara, ōya ni renraku shite kudasai — "If something in the flat breaks, contact the landlord."

The word, taken apart.
おおや (ōya) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from ō, "big" and ya, "house", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
こうざ (kōza) — a bank account.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for rent, then the Japanese for a landlord.

You'll want to know: こうざ.
こうざ — kōza — "a bank account".
こうざが あったら、おかねを あずけて ください。 — kōza ga attara, okane o azukete kudasai — "If you have a bank account, please put the money in it."

The word, taken apart.
こうざ (kōza) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "mouth" (Middle Chinese khuwX) and za, "seat" (dzwaH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ふりこむ (furikomu) — to pay into an account.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a landlord, then the Japanese for a bank account.

You'll want to know: ふりこむ.
ふりこむ — furikomu — "to pay into an account".
らいげつに なったら、やちんを ふりこんで ください。 — raigetsu ni nattara, yachin o furikonde kudasai — "When next month comes, please pay the rent into the account."
Polite ふりこみます, furikomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが やちんを ふりこみました。, chichi ga yachin o furikomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ふりこむ (furikomu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from furi, "swinging (furu)" and komu, "to go into", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ざんだか (zandaka) — a bank balance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bank account, then the Japanese for to pay into an account.

You'll want to know: ざんだか.
ざんだか — zandaka — "a bank balance".
ざんだかが すくなく なったら、おしえて ください。 — zandaka ga sukunaku nattara, oshiete kudasai — "If the balance gets low, please let me know."

The word, taken apart.
ざんだか (zandaka) is built from the Sino-Japanese zan, "remainder", an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese dzan), and the native taka, "amount", voiced to daka (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
