# దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) — grief

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C85-FEEL-05, TE-LEX-C93-FINE-03, TE-LEX-C312-PANI312-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C321-MISSED-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C321-LOST-REPORT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "sorrow, distress" (*bādha*), "comfort, ease" (*sukhaṁ*) and "a story"
(*katha*). Then "my bag is lost". (*nā sañci pōyindi*.)

## You'll want to know: దుఃఖం
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C322-DUHKHAM-01]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C312-PANI312-01, TE-LEX-C85-FEEL-05, TE-LEX-C93-FINE-03, TE-GRAMMAR-C317-CONJUNCTIVE-01] -->

**దుఃఖం** — *duḥkhaṁ* — "grief".

> **ఆ కథ చదివి నాకు దుఃఖం వచ్చింది.**
>
> *ā katha cadivi nāku duḥkhaṁ vaccindi.* — "Reading that story, I was moved to grief."

**బాధ** is the everyday hurt and sadness of a bad day. **దుఃఖం** is heavier: grief, the sorrow that brings tears.
Like a fever, it "comes" to you: **నాకు దుఃఖం వచ్చింది**.

The mark after **దు** is a breath, *ḥ*. Many speakers let it run into the next sound, close to *dukkhaṁ*. The next
lesson puts the mark in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**దుఃఖం** (*duḥkhaṁ*) is borrowed from Sanskrit *duḥkha*, "difficulty, pain" (CDIAL 6375), with Telugu's noun
ending **-ం**. CDIAL notes it was formed after *sukha*, the word you know as **సుఖం**: the two are a pair, ease and
its opposite.
