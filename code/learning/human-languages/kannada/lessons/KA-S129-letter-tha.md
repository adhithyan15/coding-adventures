# ಥ — one character, and the gap it fills

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: ಝ — which plain letter is it built on, and what

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time, and it closes the set you started three
chapters ago.

## Script you'll notice: ಥ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-129]; assesses=[] -->

**ಥ** — *tha*.

Same step as last time, on a letter you have read since the beginning: **ತ**
(*ta*) with breath behind it. **ತ** in **ಹತ್ತು** *hattu* leaves the air still;
**ಥ** pushes it into your palm. That completes the run:

- **ಕ** *ka* · **ಖ** *kha*
- **ಗ** *ga* · **ಘ** *gha*
- **ಜ** *ja* · **ಝ** *jha*
- **ಡ** *ḍa* · **ಢ** *ḍha*
- **ದ** *da* · **ಧ** *dha*
- **ಪ** *pa* · **ಫ** *pha*
- **ಬ** *ba* · **ಭ** *bha*
- **ತ** *ta* · **ಥ** *tha*

Eight pairs, one step between each, drawn the same way every time. The eighth
cost you almost nothing, because you learned the pattern at the first.

**A warning worth having.** Do not read **ಥ** as the English *th* of *think*.
There is no such sound here. It is a **ತ** with breath after it, nothing more.

## Writing: ಥ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-129] -->

- **1.** start at the upper left and go down the left side into the left lobe, rising into the middle point
- **2.** without lifting, drop from the point around the right lobe and climb the right side
- **3.** without lifting, close the bowl leftward along the top
- **4.** lift, then draw the top bar from left to right
- **5.** without lifting, curl up into the hook
- **6.** lift, then draw the tail downward
- **7.** lift, then set the dot in the middle

**Pen lifts: 3.** The body is drawn as in ದ; the bar, the tail and the dot
each start fresh.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna

> 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0.

> Filed as 'thha': that series spells ತ as 'tha'.
