Mandarin Chinese, chapter 383: Saving Energy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
diànfèi — electricity bill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to guarantee, then the Mandarin for to patrol.

You'll want to know: diànfèi.
diànfèi — "electricity bill".
xiàtiān kāi kōngtiáo diànfèi hěn guì — In summer, with the air conditioning on, the electricity bill is high.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
diànfèi is built from 电 'electricity' and fèi 'a cost', both inherited from Middle Chinese, denH and phj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'lightning' and the second 'to use up'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
néngyuán — energy; sources of energy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the electricity bill, then the Mandarin for to waste.

You'll want to know: néngyuán.
néngyuán — "energy; sources of energy".
yīnwèi néngyuán hěn guì suǒyǐ diànfèi yě hěn guì — Energy is expensive, so the electricity bill is high too.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
néngyuán is built from 能 'able' and yuán 'a source', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nong and ngjwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'skilled' and the second 'where water rises'. Energy as power for machines is a modern sense.

Lesson 4 of 5.
jiǎnshǎo — to reduce; to cut down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to waste, then the Mandarin for sources of energy.

You'll want to know: jiǎnshǎo.
jiǎnshǎo — "to reduce; to cut down".
zuò dìtiě kěyǐ jiǎnshǎo dǔchē — Taking the underground can cut down on traffic jams.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: jiǎn is said with a rising tone before shǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎnshǎo is built from jiǎn 'to lessen' and 少 'few', both inherited from Middle Chinese, keamX and syewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to diminish' and the second 'not many'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
yícìxìng — disposable; used once.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sources of energy, then the Mandarin for to cut down on.

You'll want to know: yícìxìng.
yícìxìng — "disposable; used once".
wǒmen zuìhǎo bú yòng yícìxìng kuàizi — We'd better not use disposable chopsticks.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 一 is said yí here, rising, because cì after it is a falling tone.

The word, taken apart.
yícìxìng is built from 一 'one', cì 'a time' and xìng 'nature', all inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit, tshijH and sjengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a stopping place' and 'an order'. The word is modern.
