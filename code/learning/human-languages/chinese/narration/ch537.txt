Lesson 1 of 6.
起因 (qǐyīn) — what set it off; the starting cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to see in a dream, then the Mandarin for something to look forward to.

You'll want to know: 起因.
起因 — qǐyīn — "what set it off; the starting cause".
rúguǒ zhīdào le qǐyīn jiù néng jiějué wèntí — If we know what set it off, we can solve the problem.

The word, taken apart.
起因 (qǐyīn) is built from 起 'to rise, to start' and 因 'a cause', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khiX and 'jin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and 'to start' and the second 'to rest on' and 'to follow from'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
原来 (yuánlái) — so (it turns out); as it turns out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for something to look forward to, then the Mandarin for what set it off.

You'll want to know: 原来.
原来 — yuánlái — "so (it turns out); as it turns out".
原来你也在这儿 yuánlái nǐ yě zài zhèr — So you're here too!
The new character, in pieces: 原 (yuán) — ten strokes: the cliff, a bar and a long stroke falling to the left, as at the top of 愿; under it 白, five strokes, and 小, three strokes: 愿 without its heart. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
原来 (yuánlái) is built from 原 (yuán) and 来 'to come', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwon and loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as wide flat land, a plain; the sense 'origin' is not given there, and how the pair came to mean 'as it turns out' is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Script — the cliff over 白 and 小.
原 (yuán).
Three pieces: the cliff, then 白, then 小 under it. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The cliff, two strokes: a bar, then a long stroke falling to the left down from its left end.
白, five strokes, under the cliff's arm: a short stroke falling to the left, a vertical, a bar that turns down, two short bars.
小, three strokes: a vertical that hooks to the left, a short stroke on its left, a dot on its right.
2 + 5 + 3 = ten, the first ten strokes of 愿.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Lesson 4 of 6.
dǎozhì — to result in something bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for what set it off, then the Mandarin for as it turns out.

You'll want to know: dǎozhì.
dǎozhì — "to result in something bad".
rúguǒ bú zhùyì jiù huì dǎozhì shìgù — If you're careless, it will lead to an accident.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎozhì is built from dǎo 'to lead' and zhì 'to bring to', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dawH and trijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to lead' and the second 'to arrive' and 'to send to'. The dipping tone of dǎo is not the regular outcome of dawH.
