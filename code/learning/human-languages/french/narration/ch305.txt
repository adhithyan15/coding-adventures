French, chapter 305: Les rescapés.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le naufrage — the shipwreck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the buoy, then the French for the lifeguard.

You'll want to know: le naufrage.
le naufrage — "the shipwreck".
Le naufrage a eu lieu près de la côte. The shipwreck happened near the coast.
Faire naufrage — to be shipwrecked.

The word, taken apart.
naufrage is a learned borrowing from Latin naufragium "a shipwreck" (EtymDB), from nāvis "ship" and frangere "to break".

Lesson 2 of 5.
écraser — to crash (as s'écraser); to crush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the lifeguard, then the French for the shipwreck.

You'll want to know: écraser.
écraser — "to crash (as s'écraser); to crush".
Un petit avion s'est écrasé dans la forêt. A small plane crashed in the forest.
Reflexive for a plane that crashes: s'écraser. Without se it is common too, to crush or to mash: écraser des pommes de terre.

The word, taken apart.
écraser comes from Middle French escraser "to squash" (EtymDB), usually traced to a Scandinavian word, Old Norse krasa "to shatter".

Lesson 3 of 5.
le rescapé — the survivor (of a disaster).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the shipwreck, then the French for to crash.

You'll want to know: le rescapé.
le rescapé — "the survivor (of a disaster)".
Les rescapés ont raconté leur nuit en mer. The survivors told the story of their night at sea.
Someone saved from a disaster: les rescapés du naufrage.

The word, taken apart.
rescapé is usually explained as a northern dialect form of réchappé "escaped", built on échapper "to escape", which comes from Old French eschaper (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: survivre.
survivre — "to survive".
Tous les passagers ont survécu. All the passengers survived.
Survivre à — to survive something: survivre à un accident.

The word, taken apart.
survivre is inherited from Late Latin supervīvere "to outlive" (EtymDB), from super "over" and vīvere "to live".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la catastrophe — the catastrophe, the disaster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the survivor, then the French for to survive.

You'll want to know: la catastrophe.
la catastrophe — "the catastrophe, the disaster".
Le tremblement de terre a été une catastrophe. The earthquake was a catastrophe.
Informal: c'est la catastrophe! — it is a disaster!

The word, taken apart.
catastrophe was borrowed from Latin catastropha, from Greek katastrophḗ "an overturning, a sudden end" (EtymDB).
