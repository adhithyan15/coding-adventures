Latin, chapter 161: Nothing, Nobody, No, Which of Two, Neither … Nor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nihil — nothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to arrive, then the Latin for to live (somewhere).

You'll want to know: nihil.
nihil — "nothing". Nihil videō. — "I see nothing." Latin needs no second negative: nihil already means "not anything". English keeps it in nil and annihilate.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nēmō, nēminis — nobody, no one.

You'll want to know: nēmō, nēminis.
nēmō, nēminis — "nobody, no one". Nēmō venit. — "Nobody is coming." It is ne-, "not", with an old form of homō, "a person": "not a person".

Lesson 3 of 5.
nūllus, nūlla, nūllum — no, not any.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for nothing, then the Latin for nobody.

You'll want to know: nūllus, nūlla, nūllum.
nūllus, nūlla, nūllum — "no, not any". It agrees with its noun: nūlla epistula — "no letter at all". English null and annul come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
uter, utra, utrum — which (of two).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for nobody, then the Latin for no.

You'll want to know: uter, utra, utrum.
uter, utra, utrum — "which (of two)". A question that picks one of two: Uter liber? — "Which of the two books?" neuter, "neither of the two", gave English neuter and neutral.

Lesson 5 of 5.
neque — and not, nor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for no, then the Latin for which.

You'll want to know: neque.
neque — "and not, nor". neque... neque is "neither... nor": Neque aquam neque vīnum bibō. — "I drink neither water nor wine." It is often shortened to nec.
