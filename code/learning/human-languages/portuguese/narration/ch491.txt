Portuguese, chapter 491: Namorar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
namorar — to date, to go out with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sweat, then the Portuguese for massage.

You'll want to know: namorar.
namorar — "to date, to go out with".
A minha prima namora com um colega da faculdade. My cousin is going out with a classmate from university.
In Portugal namorar com alguém; in Brazil namorar alguém.

The word, taken apart.
namorar is usually explained as a shortening of Old Portuguese enamorar "to make fall in love", built on amor "love", from Latin amor (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
a paixão — passion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to date, then the Portuguese for relationship.

You'll want to know: a paixão.
a paixão — "passion".
A música é a grande paixão do meu pai. Music is my father's great passion.
Also strong love: uma paixão de verão — a summer romance.

The word, taken apart.
paixão is inherited, through Old Portuguese paixon, from Late Latin passiō "suffering" (EtymDB), built on patī "to suffer".

Lesson 4 of 5.
apaixonar — to fall in love (apaixonar-se por).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for relationship, then the Portuguese for passion.

You'll want to know: apaixonar.
apaixonar — "to fall in love (apaixonar-se por)".
O meu irmão apaixonou-se pela vizinha do lado. My brother fell in love with the girl next door.
Reflexive in practice: apaixonar-se por alguém. Estar apaixonado — to be in love.

The word, taken apart.
apaixonar is built from a- and paixão "passion", which goes back to Late Latin passiō "suffering" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
abraçar — to hug, to embrace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for passion, then the Portuguese for to fall in love.

You'll want to know: abraçar.
abraçar — "to hug, to embrace".
A avó abraçou os netos à chegada. Grandma hugged her grandchildren when they arrived.
A hug is um abraço; letters often end um abraço.

The word, taken apart.
abraçar is inherited from Old Portuguese abraçar (EtymDB), built on braço "arm", from Latin bracchium "arm".
