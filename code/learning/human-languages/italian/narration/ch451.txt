Italian, chapter 451: Due passi in città.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il lungomare — the seafront, the promenade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the patron saint, then the Italian for the stall.

You'll want to know: il lungomare.
il lungomare — "the seafront, the promenade".
La sera passeggiamo sul lungomare. In the evening we stroll on the seafront.
By a lake it is il lungolago.

The word, taken apart.
lungomare is built from lungo 'along', inherited from Latin longus 'long', and mare 'sea', inherited from Latin mare (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: gironzolare.
gironzolare — "to wander around, to mooch about".
Ho gironzolato per i negozi senza comprare niente. I mooched around the shops without buying anything.
Informal; built on girare.

The word, taken apart.
gironzolare is built on girare 'to turn', which is inherited from Latin gyrāre, from Greek gŷros 'circle' (Lewis & Short): going round in circles.

The word, taken apart.
curiosare is built on curioso, a learned borrowing from Latin cūriōsus 'careful, inquisitive' (EtymDB), from cūra 'care'.
