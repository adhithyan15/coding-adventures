Latin, chapter 394: Getting Ready.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
apparō, apparāre — to prepare, to get ready.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an undertaking, then the Latin for to try.

You'll want to know: apparō, apparāre.
apparō, apparāre — "to prepare, to get ready".
Convīvium magnum apparāvit. He prepared a great banquet.
apparō, apparāre, apparāvī, apparātum, ad + parō. Apparātus, equipment, splendour; English apparatus.

The word, taken apart.
apparō is built from ad "towards" and parō "to make ready" (Lewis & Short). English apparatus comes from its noun.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dispōnō, dispōnere — to set out, to arrange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to try, then the Latin for to prepare.

You'll want to know: dispōnō, dispōnere.
dispōnō, dispōnere — "to set out, to arrange".
Mīlitēs in mūrīs dispositī sunt. The soldiers were posted along the walls.
dispōnō, dispōnere, disposuī, dispositum: dis- + pōnō, to place here and there. English dispose.

The word, taken apart.
dispōnō is built from dis- "apart" and pōnō "to put" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to put each thing in its own place. English dispose comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mōlior, mōlīrī — to set about (with effort), to undertake; to plot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to set out, then the Latin for to set in order.

You'll want to know: mōlior, mōlīrī.
mōlior, mōlīrī — "to set about (with effort), to undertake; to plot".
Magnam rem mōlītur. He is setting about a great undertaking.
Deponent: mōlior, mōlīrī, mōlītus sum, built on mōlēs, a mass: to heave a heavy thing. English demolish comes from dēmōlior, to pull down.

The word, taken apart.
mōlior is built on mōlēs "a mass, a heavy weight" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to move a great weight. English demolish comes from its compound dēmōlior.
