Gujarati, chapter 262: A day out with friends.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ગણગણવું (gaṇgaṇvũ) — to hum, to murmur.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for garba, then the Gujarati for firecracker.

You'll want to know: ગણગણવું.
ગણગણવું — gaṇgaṇvũ — "to hum, to murmur".
તેણે ધીમે એક ગીત ગણગણ્યું. — teṇe dhīme ek gīt gaṇgaṇyũ — "She quietly hummed a song."

The word, taken apart.
ગણગણવું (gaṇgaṇvũ) is inherited. It comes from ganagana, "murmur", a reconstructed form that imitates the sound; CDIAL lists Gujarati gaṇgaṇvũ, "murmur" (CDIAL 4013).

Lesson 3 of 5.
રમતગમત (ramatgamat) — sports, games.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to hum, then the Gujarati for to call out.

You'll want to know: રમતગમત.
રમતગમત — ramatgamat — "sports, games".
શાળામાં રમતગમતનો દિવસ હતો. — śāḷāmā̃ ramatgamatno divas hato — "There was a sports day at school."
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
રમતગમત (ramatgamat) is built on ramat, "game", from the taught ramvũ, "to play" (Sanskrit rámatē, CDIAL 10628), doubled by the rhyming gamat, "fun", whose own origin is not settled.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to call out, then the Gujarati for sports.

You'll want to know: ઉજાણી.
ઉજાણી — ujāṇī — "picnic, outing".
અમે નદી કિનારે ઉજાણી કરી. — ame nadī kināre ujāṇī karī — "We had a picnic on the river bank."

The word, taken apart.
ઉજાણી (ujāṇī) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit udyā́na, "going out" and then "park, garden"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "feast" (CDIAL 2052).

અમે તળાવમાં ડૂબકી મારી. — ame taḷāvmā̃ ḍūbkī mārī — "We took a dip in the lake."

The word, taken apart.
ડૂબકી મારવી (ḍūbkī mārvī) is built from ḍūbkī, "a dip", and mārvī, "to strike". Ḍūbkī is built on ḍūbvũ, "to sink", which CDIAL derives from ḍubb, "sink", a reconstructed form (CDIAL 5561).
