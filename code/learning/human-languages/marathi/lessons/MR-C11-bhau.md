# भाऊ (bhāū) — brother, and this time the resemblance is real

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-KUTUMB, MR-LEX-MITRA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **मित्र** and **कुटुंब** were both taken up whole from Sanskrit.
This next word was not — it was worn down, letter by letter, over centuries.

## You'll want to know: भाऊ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-BHAU]; assesses=[] -->

> **भाऊ** — *bhāū* — **brother** (masculine)

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One new letter: **ऊ**, the **independent long *ū***, used here because a
Marathi word cannot stack one vowel mark on top of another — after **आ**'s
**ा**, the second vowel needs its own full letter. **भ** you have from
*bheṭū*. *Bhā-ū*.

## The word, taken apart — worn down, but the family resemblance survives
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-ETYMON-BHAU-BROTHER]; assesses=[] -->

**भाऊ** is a **tadbhava** — not borrowed whole like **मित्र** or **कुटुंब**,
but the ordinary Prakrit-worn descendant of Sanskrit **भ्रातृ** *bhrātṛ*, three
syllables ground down to two over a thousand years of speech. That root is
Indo-European ***\*bʰréh₂tēr***, and here the family resemblance to English is
not a false friend — it is the real thing: **brother**, Latin **frāter**, and
Sanskrit's own **bhrātṛ** are all the same word, worn down four different ways
by four different languages.
