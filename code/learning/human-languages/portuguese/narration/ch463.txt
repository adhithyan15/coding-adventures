Portuguese, chapter 463: A trovoada.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a trovoada — the thunderstorm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for sunstroke, then the Portuguese for to sweat.

You'll want to know: a trovoada.
a trovoada — "the thunderstorm".
Ontem à noite houve uma trovoada forte. Last night there was a heavy thunderstorm.
Built on o trovão.

The word, taken apart.
trovoada is built on trovão 'thunder'. Trovão is usually traced to Latin tonitrus 'thunder' (Lewis & Short), with its sounds rearranged, but the details are not settled; EtymDB lists Galician trebón as a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o granizo — hail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to thunder, then the Portuguese for to flash with lightning.

You'll want to know: o granizo.
o granizo — "hail".
O granizo partiu o vidro da estufa. The hail broke the greenhouse glass.
Cair granizo — to hail.

The word, taken apart.
granizo was borrowed from Spanish granizo (EtymDB), built on grano 'grain', from Latin grānum: hail falls like grains.

Lesson 5 of 5.
o aguaceiro — the downpour, the heavy shower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to flash with lightning, then the Portuguese for hail.

You'll want to know: o aguaceiro.
o aguaceiro — "the downpour, the heavy shower".
Ao meio-dia caiu um aguaceiro. At midday there was a downpour.
Weather forecasts say aguaceiros for showers.

The word, taken apart.
aguaceiro is built on aguaça 'lots of water' with -eiro (EtymDB); água is inherited from Latin aqua.
