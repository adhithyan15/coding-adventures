# ayer — the word that opens the past

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Everything you have said so far happens now or later. This lesson
gives you the one word that points the other way — backwards — and warns you
what the verbs in this chapter are about to do.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [voy a + infinitive](./ES-C10-ir-a-futuro.md) — you can already point
  forwards; this points back.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `vowel-a`, `vowel-e` — two syllables, stress on the last: *a-YÉR*
  (*ah-YEHR*). → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-AYER, ES-ETYMON-AD-HERI]; assesses=[] -->

**ayer** — "yesterday." It is two Latin words that fused:

```
ad    +   heri        →   ayer
(to)      (yesterday)      (yesterday)
```

Latin *heri* is the same ancient word as English **yester**day and German
*gestern*. The *h-* fell silent and then fell away, the *-ri* softened to a
*-y-* sound, and *ad heri* — "to yesterday" — packed down into one short word.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-SEMANTIC-PAST-TIME-FRAME]; assesses=[] -->

*Ayer* is a **time frame**: a word that tells the listener *when*, before the
verb ever gets a chance to. That matters more than it looks, because Spanish
will not let a past time frame sit beside a present verb. Say *ayer* and the
verb must follow you backwards.

That is what the rest of this chapter is for. Spanish has **two** past tenses,
and they split the past between them:

| | what it says | met in |
|---|---|---|
| **preterite** | it happened, and it finished | this chapter |
| **imperfect** | it was going on, or it used to happen | a later chapter |

English blurs this — "I spoke" covers both. Spanish makes you choose, every
time. You do not have to choose yet; you only need to know that the choice is
coming, so the next few lessons do not look like arbitrary endings.
