Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 136: Radish, Garlic, Ginger, Turmeric, Cumin.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मूळो (mūḷo) — a radish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for an aubergine, then the Marwadi for spinach.

You'll want to know: मूळो.
मूळो — mūḷo — "a radish".

The word, taken apart.
मूळो (mūḷo) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mūla, "root; radish". CDIAL lists Old Marwari mūlī under it (CDIAL 10250). CDIAL notes a likely non-Aryan origin for the word; Gujarati has mūḷo, "radish".

Lesson 3 of 5.
अदरक (adrak) — ginger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a radish, then the Marwadi for garlic.

You'll want to know: अदरक.
अदरक — adrak — "ginger".

The word, taken apart.
अदरक (adrak) goes back to Sanskrit ārdraka, "fresh ginger" (CDIAL 1341). CDIAL lists the fully inherited forms as Hindi ādā and Gujarati ādũ; अदरक (adrak) keeps the r and k those forms lost, and CDIAL does not list it.
