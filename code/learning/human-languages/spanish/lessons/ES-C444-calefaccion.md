# la calefacción — heat, made

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C334-BODY-34, ES-LEX-C334-BODY-33] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el calor** and **el frío**. It is January and the flat is cold.
Name the thing that is not working.

## You'll want to know: la calefacción
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C334-BODY-34] -->

**la calefacción** — the heating.

*No funciona la calefacción* — the heating is not working, which is the sentence
a tenant needs first. *Calefacción central* — central heating. *Poner la
calefacción* — to put the heating on.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C334-BODY-34] -->

Two pieces, and you own both.

| piece | where you have it |
|---|---|
| **cale-** | *calor*, heat |
| **-facción** | *facere*, to make |

**Heat-making.** The second half is the same *facere* that sits under *hacer*
and under **la factura**, which you met as *a making*. A *factura* is a making
of accounts; a *calefacción* is a making of heat.

English has the pieces too, in **calorie** — a unit of heat — and in the
**-faction** of *satisfaction* and *liquefaction*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C444-CASA-01, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04] -->

Notice that Spanish names the *process*, not the *device*.

English **heating** can mean the radiators, the boiler, or the service. Spanish
**calefacción** is the making-of-heat, and the hardware has its own words —
*el radiador*, *la caldera*. That is why the bill says *calefacción* and the
plumber talks about *la caldera*: the two are not the same noun.

So *la factura de la calefacción* is the bill for heat-made, not the bill for a
machine. Both words on that line come from *facere*, one making heat and one
making the account of it.
