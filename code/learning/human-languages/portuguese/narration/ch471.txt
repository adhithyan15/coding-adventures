Portuguese, chapter 471: Sinceridade e mentiras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a sinceridade — sincerity, honesty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for modesty, then the Portuguese for to praise.

You'll want to know: a sinceridade.
a sinceridade — "sincerity, honesty".
Agradeço a tua sinceridade. I appreciate your honesty.
The adjective is sincero.

The word, taken apart.
sinceridade is a learned borrowing from Latin sinceritās "purity, soundness" (Lewis & Short), built on sincērus "pure, whole".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a franqueza — frankness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to praise, then the Portuguese for sincerity.

You'll want to know: a franqueza.
a franqueza — "frankness".
Com toda a franqueza, não gostei do filme. In all frankness, I did not like the film.
Com franqueza — frankly. The adjective is franco.

The word, taken apart.
franqueza is built on franco "frank", which came from French franc (EtymDB), the name of the Franks, Latin Franci (Lewis & Short); the sense "free, open" is usually explained by the Franks being the free men of their realm.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mentiroso — liar; lying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for frankness, then the Portuguese for to deceive.

You'll want to know: mentiroso.
mentiroso — "liar; lying".
Ninguém acredita nele, porque é um mentiroso. Nobody believes him, because he is a liar.
Built on mentir "to lie"; also an adjective: uma resposta mentirosa.

The word, taken apart.
mentiroso is inherited from Old Portuguese mentiroso, built on mentir "to lie" (EtymDB), from Latin mentīrī.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fingir — to pretend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to deceive, then the Portuguese for liar.

You'll want to know: fingir.
fingir — "to pretend".
O gato fingiu que estava a dormir. The cat pretended it was asleep.
Fingir que + verb — to pretend that. It has nothing to do with fingers.

The word, taken apart.
fingir is a learned borrowing from Latin fingere "to form, to shape, to devise, to feign" (EtymDB).
