Sanskrit, chapter 354: Citizens and Society.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नागरिकः (nāgarikaḥ) — a citizen (of a country).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a governor, then the Sanskrit for an official order.

You'll want to know: नागरिकः.
नागरिकः — nāgarikaḥ — "a citizen (of a country)".
नागरिकः नियमान् पालयेत्। nāgarikaḥ niyamān pālayet. A citizen should keep the laws.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नागरिकः (nāgarikaḥ) is built on nagara, "a town"; MW gives nāgarika "born or living in a town, civic", and "a citizen" is how modern Sanskrit uses it (MW).

You'll want to know: जनता.
जनता — janatā — "the public".
जनता नूतनां नीतिं न इच्छति। janatā nūtanāṃ nītiṃ na icchati. The public does not want the new policy.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
जनता (janatā) is built on jana, "people", with the collective ending -tā; MW gives jana-tā "a number of men, an assemblage of people, a community, subjects" (MW).

समाजे सर्वे जनाः समाः सन्ति। samāje sarve janāḥ samāḥ santi. In society all people are equal.

Lesson 5 of 5.
संघटयति (saṅghaṭayati) — organizes; brings together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for society, then the Sanskrit for independent.

You'll want to know: संघटयति.
संघटयति — saṅghaṭayati — "organizes; brings together".
मीरा ग्रामस्य जनान् संघटयति। mīrā grāmasya janān saṅghaṭayati. Mira brings the village people together.
Past (she …): सा सभां संघटितवती, sā sabhāṃ saṅghaṭitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
संघटयति (saṅghaṭayati) is built on sam, "together", and the root √ghaṭ, "to join", in its causative form; MW gives saṃ-√ghaṭ "to cause to assemble, to collect, to join together", and "organizes" is its modern sense (MW).
