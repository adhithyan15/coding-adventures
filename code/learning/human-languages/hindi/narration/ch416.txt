Hindi, chapter 416: Camping Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ख़ेमा (khemā) — a tent (for camping).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a place to stay, then the Hindi for to send for.

You'll want to know: ख़ेमा.
ख़ेमा — khemā — "a tent (for camping)".
नदी के किनारे हमारा ख़ेमा था। nadī ke kināre hamārā khemā thā. Our tent was on the river bank.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ख़ेमा (khemā) is usually traced to Arabic khayma, "a tent", which came through Persian; the diphthong has become a plain e.

Lesson 2 of 5.
सुलाना (sulānā) — to put to sleep; to lull (someone) to sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to send for, then the Hindi for a tent (for camping).

You'll want to know: सुलाना.
सुलाना — sulānā — "to put to sleep; to lull (someone) to sleep".
माँ ने बच्चे को ख़ेमे में सुलाया। māṁ ne bacce ko kheme meṁ sulāyā. The mother put the child to sleep in the tent.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्ची को सुलाया, usne baccī ko sulāyā.

The word, taken apart.
सुलाना (sulānā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit svāpayati, "lulls to sleep" (CDIAL 13928), which lists Hindi sulānā: the causative partner of सोना, to sleep.

Lesson 3 of 5.
बसेरा (baserā) — a roost; a night's shelter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a tent (for camping), then the Hindi for to put to sleep.

You'll want to know: बसेरा.
बसेरा — baserā — "a roost; a night's shelter".
शाम को चिड़ियाँ अपने बसेरे में लौटती हैं। śām ko ciṛiyā̃ apne basere meṁ lauṭtī haiṁ. In the evening the birds go back to their roost.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
बसेरा (baserā) goes back to vāsakara, "making a stay", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 11594), which lists Hindi baserā, "a place of staying": a bird's roost, or a traveller's bed for one night.

Lesson 4 of 5.
गुज़ारना (guzārnā) — to spend (time); to get through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to put to sleep, then the Hindi for a roost.

You'll want to know: गुज़ारना.
गुज़ारना — guzārnā — "to spend (time); to get through".
हमने जंगल में दो रातें गुज़ारीं। hamne jaṅgal meṁ do rāteṁ guzārīṁ. We spent two nights in the forest.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने वहाँ एक साल गुज़ारा, usne vahā̃ ek sāl guzārā.

The word, taken apart.
गुज़ारना (guzārnā) is the causative partner of गुज़रना, "to pass by", usually traced to Persian guzār, "passing": time passes, and you make it pass.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बिताना (bitānā) — to pass (time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a roost, then the Hindi for to spend (time).

You'll want to know: बिताना.
बिताना — bitānā — "to pass (time)".
हमने छुट्टियाँ गाँव में बिताईं। hamne chuṭṭiyā̃ gā̃v meṁ bitāīṁ. We spent the holidays in the village.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बचपन गाँव में बिताया, usne bacpan gā̃v meṁ bitāyā.

The word, taken apart.
बिताना (bitānā) is the causative partner of बीतना, "to pass (of time)", from Sanskrit vṛtta, "turned; passed" (CDIAL 12069). गुज़ारना (guzārnā) and बिताना say the same thing; बिताना is the plainer, warmer word.
