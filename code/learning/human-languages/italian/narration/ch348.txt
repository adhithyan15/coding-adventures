Italian, chapter 348: Pro e contro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il vantaggio — the advantage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for objective, then the Italian for subjective.

You'll want to know: il vantaggio.
il vantaggio — "the advantage".
Abitare in centro ha molti vantaggi. Living in the centre has many advantages.
Essere in vantaggio — to be ahead, in a match too.

The word, taken apart.
vantaggio is usually traced to Old French avantage, built on avant "before, ahead", from Late Latin abante, made from ab and ante "before" (Lewis & Short): being a step ahead.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lo svantaggio — the disadvantage.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: lo svantaggio.
lo svantaggio — "the disadvantage".
Lo svantaggio è che la casa è lontana dalla stazione. The disadvantage is that the house is far from the station.
S- in front reverses the meaning, as in comodo and scomodo.

The word, taken apart.
svantaggio is built from s- "un-" and vantaggio, which is usually traced to Old French avantage, from Late Latin abante "ahead", made from ab and ante (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'aspetto — the aspect, the side (of a question); the look, the appearance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the advantage, then the Italian for the disadvantage.

You'll want to know: l'aspetto.
l'aspetto — "the aspect, the side (of a question); the look, the appearance".
C'è un aspetto del problema che non abbiamo considerato. There is one side of the problem that we have not considered.
Also how something looks: ha un bell'aspetto. Do not mix it up with aspetto "I wait".

The word, taken apart.
aspetto is a learned borrowing from Latin aspectus "a look, an appearance" (Lewis & Short), from aspicere "to behold", built on specere "to look". The verb aspettare "to wait" comes from the same Latin family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la critica — the criticism; the critics.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the disadvantage, then the Italian for the aspect.

You'll want to know: la critica.
la critica — "the criticism; the critics".
Accetto la critica, ma il lavoro è finito. I accept the criticism, but the work is finished.
Fare una critica — to make a criticism. La critica can also mean the critics as a group.

The word, taken apart.
critica is built on critico, a learned borrowing from Latin criticus "able to judge" (Lewis & Short), from Greek kritikós, from krínein "to separate, to decide".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ribadire — to reiterate, to restate firmly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the aspect, then the Italian for the criticism.

You'll want to know: ribadire.
ribadire — "to reiterate, to restate firmly".
Ribadisco quello che ho detto: io non vengo. I repeat what I said: I am not coming.
Conjugated with -isc-: ribadisco. The first sense is to clinch a nail by bending its point.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of ribadire is not settled; EtymDB records the word with no source (EtymDB). Its first sense was "to clinch a nail by hammering its point flat", and from there it came to mean to hammer a point home with words.
