Mandarin Chinese, chapter 106: Stops and Stations.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
车站 (chēzhàn) — station; stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for soap, then the Mandarin for mirror.

You'll want to know: 车站.
车站 — chēzhàn — "station; stop".
车站在哪儿 chēzhàn zài nǎr — Where's the station?
The new character, in pieces: 站 (zhàn) — on the left a five-stroke piece read lì, 'to stand'; on the right a vertical with a short bar to its right, over 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
车站 (chēzhàn) is built from 车 'vehicle' and 站 (zhàn) 'to stand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhae and treamH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as an everyday word for standing alone. A place where vehicles stand is the usual explanation, but the step is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
站 (zhàn) — the standing piece, then a mark over 口.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the standing piece, then a mark over 口.
站 (zhàn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The standing piece, lì, five strokes: a dot at the top, a horizontal, a dot on the left and a short left-falling stroke on the right, and a stroke along the bottom that rises to the right.
The right piece, five strokes: a vertical with a short horizontal to its right; then 口 below, as you write it alone: the left side, the top that turns down, the bottom.
5 + 5 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 站 (zhàn), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 站 (zhàn)? (Two — the standing piece, then the mark over 口.) What is the standing piece read? (lì.) Which piece you know ends 站 (zhàn)? (口.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
dìtiě — underground; metro.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for mirror, then the Mandarin for station.

You'll want to know: dìtiě.
dìtiě — "underground; metro".
wǒmen zuò dìtiě qù — We'll take the metro.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dìtiě is built from dì 'ground' and tiě 'iron', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dijH and thet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as black metal. The railway under the ground is a modern sense.
