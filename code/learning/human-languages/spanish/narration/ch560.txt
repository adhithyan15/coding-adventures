Spanish, chapter 560: El reencuentro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
congregarse — to gather, to assemble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the float, then the Spanish for the crowd.

You'll want to know: congregarse.
congregarse — "to gather, to assemble".
Cientos de personas se congregaron en la plaza. Hundreds of people gathered in the square.
It takes gu before e: se congreguen. Common in the news.

The word, taken apart.
congregarse is a learned borrowing from Latin congregāre 'to gather into a flock, to assemble' (Lewis & Short), built on grex, gregis 'a flock'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
juntarse — to get together; to join.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the crowd, then the Spanish for to gather.

You'll want to know: juntarse.
juntarse — "to get together; to join".
Los amigos se juntan los viernes. The friends get together on Fridays.
Common in Latin America for meeting friends. Juntarse con alguien — to hang out with someone.

The word, taken apart.
juntarse is built on junto 'together', which comes from Latin iūnctus 'joined', the participle of iungere 'to join, to yoke' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la tertulia — the get-together (for conversation).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get together, then the Spanish for to call.

You'll want to know: la tertulia.
la tertulia — "the get-together (for conversation)".
Los viernes hay tertulia en el café. On Fridays there is a get-together at the café.
A regular group that meets to talk; on radio and TV, una tertulia is a panel discussion.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of tertulia is not settled. A common account links it to the Latin writer Tertullian, read and argued over in old gatherings, but this is not proved.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el reencuentro — the reunion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to call, then the Spanish for the get-together.

You'll want to know: el reencuentro.
el reencuentro — "the reunion".
El reencuentro de los hermanos fue muy bonito. The brothers' reunion was lovely.

The word, taken apart.
reencuentro is built on encuentro with re- 'again'. Encuentro comes from encontrar, which EtymDB traces to Latin in contrā 'facing'.
