# अतः (ataḥ) — therefore, in formal writing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C247-THG247-03, HI-GRAMMAR-C281-CHAHIYE-SHOULD-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "so, therefore" the everyday way. (**इसलिए**, *isliye*.)

## You'll want to know: अतः
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C281-ATAH-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C247-THG247-03, HI-LEX-C122-LATE-01, HI-LEX-C153-THINGS153-04, HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-02, HI-GRAMMAR-C277-FUTURE-PERSON-01] -->

**अतः** — *ataḥ* — "therefore". It is formal. You will read it in notices, letters and the news; in speech, Hindi says **इसलिए**.

> **कल छुट्टी है, अतः दफ़्तर बंद रहेगा।**
>
> *kal chuṭṭī hai, ataḥ daftar band rahegā.* — "Tomorrow is a holiday; the office will therefore stay closed."

Its last sign, the two dots **ः**, is new, and it has the next lesson to itself. It is said as a light breath of *h* after the vowel: *ataḥ*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**अतः** (*ataḥ*) is a tatsama, a Sanskrit word Hindi uses unchanged. Monier-Williams gives its sense as "from this", and so "hence, for this reason": the result comes from what was said before. CDIAL has no row for it.
