French, chapter 403: À la pompe.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la pompe — the pump.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to honk, then the French for to mist up.

You'll want to know: la pompe.
la pompe — "the pump".
La pompe numéro trois ne marche pas. Pump number three is not working.
La pompe à essence is the petrol pump; une pompe à vélo is a bicycle pump.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of pompe is not settled. It is usually linked to Middle Low German pumpe 'pump' (EtymDB) and may imitate the sound of the pump.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le carburant — the fuel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the pump, then the French for the diesel.

You'll want to know: le carburant.
le carburant — "the fuel".
Le prix du carburant augmente encore. The price of fuel is going up again.
The general word: petrol and diesel are both carburant.

The word, taken apart.
carburant is built on carburer 'to mix fuel with air', from carbure 'carbide', a learned formation on Latin carbō 'charcoal' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
démarrer — to start up (a car, an engine); to get going.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the fuel, then the French for the tank.

You'll want to know: démarrer.
démarrer — "to start up (a car, an engine); to get going".
Ce matin, la vieille voiture ne démarre pas. This morning the old car will not start.
Also for projects: le travail démarre lundi.

The word, taken apart.
démarrer is built from dé- and amarrer 'to moor', which is usually traced to Middle Dutch aenmarren 'to tie up': to start was first to untie a boat. EtymDB lists the verb without a source.
