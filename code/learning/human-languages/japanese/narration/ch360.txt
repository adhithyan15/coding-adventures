Japanese, chapter 360: Income and Spending.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しゅうにゅう (shūnyū) — income.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a refund, then the Japanese for a sum of money.

You'll want to know: しゅうにゅう.
しゅうにゅう — shūnyū — "income".
しゅうにゅうが ふえたら、ひっこしたいです。 — shūnyū ga fuetara, hikkoshitai desu — "If my income goes up, I want to move house."

The word, taken apart.
しゅうにゅう (shūnyū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shū, "income" (Middle Chinese syuw) and nyū, "enter" (nyip) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
たまる (tamaru) — to be saved up; to build up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for expenditure, then the Japanese for living costs.

You'll want to know: たまる.
たまる — tamaru — "to be saved up; to build up".
おかねが たまったら、りょこうに いきたいです。 — okane ga tamattara, ryokō ni ikitai desu — "When I have saved enough money, I want to go travelling."
Polite たまります, tamarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ごみが たまりました。, gomi ga tamarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
たまる (tamaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
おさえる (osaeru) — to keep down (costs); to hold down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for living costs, then the Japanese for to be saved up.

You'll want to know: おさえる.
おさえる — osaeru — "to keep down (costs); to hold down".
ねだんが あがったら、ししゅつを おさえた ほうが いいです。 — nedan ga agattara, shishutsu o osaeta hō ga ii desu — "If prices go up, we had better keep our spending down."
Polite おさえます, osaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ははが ししゅつを おさえました。, haha ga shishutsu o osaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
おさえる (osaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
