# Your own line — the first writing with no model

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Everything you have written so far had something to reach for.

[PAUSE 1s] This does not. You choose the words, from a supply that is closed:
only what this book has taught you.

## Writing — controlled composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04, LA-LEX-C48-DENY-04] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: controlled-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **one line** in Latin about yourself.

[PAUSE 2s] And here is what makes this step harder in Latin than it is in most
languages. Choosing the words is not enough. You must also choose **which ending
each word wears**.

[PAUSE 2s] In English or French the shape of a word is given and its job comes
from where you put it. In Latin the job comes from the ending, so a word you
have only ever met in one form is a word you cannot yet place — you know it, and
you still have to decide what it is doing.

[PAUSE 3s] Write the line. Use only forms the book has actually shown you, not
forms you have guessed at from a pattern.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C48-DENY-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] In Latin, what tells a reader what a word is doing in the sentence?
(**Its ending.**)

[PAUSE 2s] So what did you have to choose here that a French writer does not?
(**The form of each word**, not only which word.)
