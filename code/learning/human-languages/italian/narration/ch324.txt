Italian, chapter 324: Miti ed eroi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'eroe — the hero.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to narrate, then the Italian for the narrator.

You'll want to know: l'eroe.
l'eroe — "the hero".
L'eroe della storia è un ragazzo povero. The hero of the story is a poor boy.
A woman is l'eroina.

The word, taken apart.
eroe is a learned borrowing from Latin hērōs, from Greek hḗrōs "a hero, a demigod" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la leggenda — the legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the narrator, then the Italian for the hero.

You'll want to know: la leggenda.
la leggenda — "the legend".
Secondo la leggenda, la città è nata in una notte. According to the legend, the city was born in a single night.
Una leggenda vivente — a living legend.

The word, taken apart.
leggenda comes from Medieval Latin legenda "things to be read", from legere "to read" (Lewis & Short): first, the lives of saints read aloud.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il finale — the ending (of a story or film).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the legend, then the Italian for the myth.

You'll want to know: il finale.
il finale — "the ending (of a story or film)".
Il finale del film mi ha fatto piangere. The ending of the film made me cry.
Il lieto fine is a happy ending.

The word, taken apart.
finale is inherited from Late Latin fīnālis (EtymDB), built on fīnis "end".

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: la morale.
la morale — "the moral (of a story)".
La morale della storia è semplice. The moral of the story is simple.
Also morals in general. Masculine il morale is your spirits.

The word, taken apart.
morale is a learned borrowing from Latin mōrālis "of manners" (EtymDB), built on mōs, mōris "custom".
