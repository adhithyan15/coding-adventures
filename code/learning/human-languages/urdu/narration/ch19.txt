Urdu, chapter 19: The Word That Was Deferred.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
میں نہیں سمجھتا (maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā) — the sentence chapter one promised.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter one taught نہیں and said, in as many words, that its second job — not, inside a sentence — would matter once the book had verbs. Fourteen verbs later, here it is.
[your turn — recall: the wellbeing reply — maiṅ ṭhīk hūṅ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: نہیں in front of the verb.
میں نہیں سمجھتا — maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā — I do not understand (man speaking).
میں نہیں سمجھتی — maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtī — the same, a woman speaking.
Take the affirmative frame chapter seven built, maiṅ samajhtā hūṅ, and put نہیں in front of the verb. Nothing else moves, and one thing is allowed to disappear: with نہیں in the sentence, the closing ہوں is usually dropped in speech. Both maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā and maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā hūṅ are correct; the shorter one is what people say.

Grammar Lens: where the negator stands.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
maiṅ samajhtā hūṅ means I understand.
maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā means I do not understand.
maiṅ nahīṅ jāntā means I do not know.
نہیں goes before the verb, not after it. That is the opposite of Bengali and the same as English, which puts not before understand as well. The agreement stays on the verb and the negator never changes shape.
The three letters of نہیں are all yours: ن, ہ, ی, and the dotless ں that hums at the end. The verb beside it is not — three of its letters are still ahead of you in the script ladder — so it is printed here in the word this lesson owns and left in romanization everywhere else.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the affirmative first — maiṅ samajhtā hūṅ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṅ nahīṅ jāntā — I do not know]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṅ nahīṅ bōltā — I do not speak]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does نہیں stand in relation to the verb? (Before it.) What happens to ہوں when the negator arrives? (It is usually dropped, and keeping it is not wrong.) And what still marks who is speaking? (The verb ending — -tā or -tī.)

Lesson 2 of 4.
کیا آپ ٹھیک ہیں؟ (kyā āp ṭhīk haiṅ?) — the question that could not be asked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
jī hāṅ and نہیں were the first two words this book taught, and they answer a question. Eighteen chapters have gone by without the book being able to ask it.
[your turn — recall: the item before this one — maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: کیا at the front.
کیا آپ ٹھیک ہیں؟ — kyā āp ṭhīk haiṅ? — are you well?
کیا یہ پانی ہے؟ — kyā yih pānī hai? — is this water?
Chapter three taught کیا as what. Put it at the front of a statement and it stops meaning what altogether: it marks the whole sentence as a yes-or-no question, and nothing else in the sentence moves.

Grammar Lens: one word, two jobs, told apart by position.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
in the middle means what.
at the front means (yes-or-no).
Position is the whole difference. English cannot do this: it has to move the verb in front of the subject, you are becoming are you. Urdu adds one word at the edge and leaves the sentence alone. In speech the fronted کیا is often left out and the rise of the voice carries it by itself.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kyā āp ṭhīk haiṅ? — then answer it jī hāṅ or nahīṅ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kyā yih pānī hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same question without the word, on the voice alone]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What tells you whether کیا means what or marks a yes-or-no question? (Where it stands — front, or inside.) And which two words answer it? (jī hāṅ and نہیں, from chapter one.)

Lesson 3 of 4.
نہ … نہ … (na … na …) — neither, nor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
نہیں stands in front of a verb. Urdu has a shorter form of the same word that stands in front of a noun, and it is almost always doubled.
[your turn — recall: the two items before this one — maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā, kyā āp ṭhīk haiṅ?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: نہ … نہ …
نہ پانی، نہ ہوا — na pānī, na hawā — neither water nor air.
One نہ in front of each thing refused. Strip the -یں off نہیں and what is left is نہ, the older and shorter negator: the two are the same word at different stages of wear, which is why the doubled refusal sounds like the plain one cut short.

Grammar Lens: three negators, told apart by what follows them.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
form: نہیں. stands before: a verb. example: maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā.
form: نہیں. stands before: alone. example: the complete answer no.
form: نہ … نہ. stands before: each refused noun. example: na pānī, na hawā.
Urdu has a third, mat, which belongs in front of a command — and this book has no command yet, so it waits. Its second letter has no lesson in the script ladder either, which is a second reason to leave it where it is.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: na pānī, na hawā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: na chāy, na dūdh — neither tea nor milk]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: na garmī, na sardī — neither heat nor cold]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the refusal as a question — kyā pānī hai? … nahīṅ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which negator stands in front of a verb, and which in front of a noun? (نہیں before a verb; نہ before each refused noun.) How many نہ does neither … nor need? (Two, one for each thing.) And what is نہ to نہیں? (The same word, shorter and older.)
Source: Unicode Arabic chart.
