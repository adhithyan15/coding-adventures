6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
hánlěng — bitterly cold (of a climate).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be frozen stiff, then the Mandarin for to melt.

You'll want to know: hánlěng.
hánlěng — "bitterly cold (of a climate)".
zhèr de dōngtiān bǐ Běijīng hánlěng — Winters here are colder than in Beijing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hánlěng is built from hán and 冷 'cold', both inherited from Middle Chinese, han and lengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'cold, as against heat'; it lists the second three times, leng, laengX and lengX, the last two glossed 'cold'. Two words for cold, put together, make it bitter.

Lesson 2 of 6.
四季 (sìjì) — the four seasons.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to melt, then the Mandarin for bitterly cold.

You'll want to know: 四季.
四季 — sìjì — "the four seasons".
这儿四季都有花 zhèr sìjì dōu yǒu huā — There are flowers here all year round.
The new character, in pieces: 季 (jì) — eight strokes in two pieces: on top the grain piece, five strokes, as on the left of 秋, but spread wide; and 子 at the foot, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
四季 (sìjì) is built from 四 'four' and 季 (jì), both inherited from Middle Chinese, sijH and kjwijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as the youngest of brothers and 'young'; how it came to mean a season, as in 季节, is not settled.

Script — the grain piece, then 子 under it.
季 (jì).
Two pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, the grain piece, five strokes, as on the left of 秋, but spread out wide: a short stroke falling to the left at the top, a bar, a short vertical down through the bar, a stroke falling to the left from the crossing, then a long stroke falling to the right.
The bottom piece, 子, three strokes, as you wrote it: a bar that turns down to the left, a vertical that curves down and hooks to the left, then a long bar across.
5 + 3 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
雨季 (yǔjì) — rainy season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bitterly cold, then the Mandarin for the four seasons.

You'll want to know: 雨季.
雨季 — yǔjì — "rainy season".
我最不喜欢雨季 wǒ zuì bù xǐhuan yǔjì — I like the rainy season least of all.

The word, taken apart.
雨季 (yǔjì) is built from 雨 'rain' and 季 (jì) 'a season', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuX and kjwijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as the youngest of brothers, so its sense 'season' is not given there.

Lesson 6 of 6.
shōuhuò — to bring in the harvest; to reap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the rainy season, then the Mandarin for to escape the summer heat.

You'll want to know: shōuhuò.
shōuhuò — "to bring in the harvest; to reap".
qiūtiān shì shōuhuò de jìjié — Autumn is the season for bringing in the harvest.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shōuhuò is built from shōu 'to gather in' and huò 'to reap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuw and hwak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to collect' and the second, written with the grain piece, 'to cut down the crop'. The falling tone of huò is not the regular outcome of hwak, which would give huó.
