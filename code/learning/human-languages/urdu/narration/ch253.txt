Lesson 1 of 5.
چھپکلی (chhipkalī) — a lizard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a coconut, then the Urdu for a cashew.

You'll want to know: چھپکلی.
چھپکلی — chhipkalī — "a lizard".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Hindi chipkalī, "lizard", under Sanskrit śepyā, for which it reconstructs a sense "tail" (CDIAL 12607). How the word came to name the lizard is not explained.

کچھوا — kachhuā — "a tortoise".

The word, taken apart.
کچھوا (kachhuā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kacchapa, "turtle, tortoise" (CDIAL 2619).

Lesson 3 of 5.
مگرمچھ (magarmachh) — a crocodile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a lizard, then the Urdu for a tortoise.

You'll want to know: مگرمچھ.
مگرمچھ — magarmachh — "a crocodile".

The word, taken apart.
مگرمچھ (magarmachh) is "crocodile-fish". magar goes back to Sanskrit makara, "crocodile", a word CDIAL records without a Hindi form (CDIAL 9692), and machh, "fish", to Sanskrit matsya (CDIAL 9758).

Lesson 4 of 5.
زرافہ (zarāfā) — a giraffe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a tortoise, then the Urdu for a crocodile.

You'll want to know: زرافہ.
زرافہ — zarāfā — "a giraffe".

The word, taken apart.
زرافہ (zarāfā) is a loan from Arabic zarāfa, "giraffe", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is z-r-f (Lane). English giraffe comes from the same Arabic word.

The word, taken apart.
مکڑی (makṛī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from markaṭa, "spider" (CDIAL 9883). The Sanskrit word is known only from the old word-lists.
