## You'll want to know: The word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-QUINTO-02]; assesses=[] -->

> **quinto** (masculine) · **quinta** (feminine) — **fifth**.
> **o quinto andar** — the fifth floor. **a quinta vez** — the fifth time.

Thursday is ***quinta*-feira**, the feminine again unchanged. Note the vowel:
*cinco* is five, but the ordinal keeps the older *qu-* of Latin *quīntus*, so the
two do not rhyme.

## Why it's said this way: the fifth of the harvest
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-QUINTO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**A quinta** is a country estate or farm, and it is on the label of half the port
in Portugal — *Quinta do Noval*, *Quinta da Boeira*. It is this ordinal.

A tenant once paid the landowner a share of what the land produced, and in
Portugal that share was the ***quinta parte***, the **fifth part**. The land was
named for its rent, the rent stopped being a fifth centuries ago, and the name
stayed. So a Portuguese farm is a number, and the number is five.
