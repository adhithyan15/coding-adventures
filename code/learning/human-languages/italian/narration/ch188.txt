Italian, chapter 188: On the contrary, In fact, That is, Although, Despite.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
anzi — on the contrary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for while, then the Italian for a decade.

You'll want to know: anzi.
anzi — "on the contrary".

The word, taken apart.
anzi from Latin antea, "before" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1) "Before that" came to mean "rather, on the contrary".

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for in fact, then the Italian for that is.

You'll want to know: sebbene.
sebbene — "although".

The word, taken apart.
sebbene is se, "if", plus bene, "well": "even if, well and good". EtymDB 2.1 has no row for it; the parts are plain to see.

Lesson 5 of 5.
nonostante — despite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for that is, then the Italian for although.

You'll want to know: nonostante.
nonostante — "despite".

The word, taken apart.
nonostante is non, "not", plus ostante, "standing in the way", from Latin obstare: nothing standing in the way. EtymDB 2.1 lists French nonobstant and Spanish no obstante as its cognates.
