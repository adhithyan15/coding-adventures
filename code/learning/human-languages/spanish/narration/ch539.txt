Spanish, chapter 539: Semestres y quincenas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el semestre — the semester, the half-year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for recently, then the Spanish for lately.

You'll want to know: el semestre.
el semestre — "the semester, the half-year".
El primer semestre del año fue muy difícil. The first half of the year was very hard.
At university it is a term of six months.

The word, taken apart.
semestre comes from Latin sēmestris 'of six months' (EtymDB), built on sex 'six' and mēnsis 'month'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la quincena — the fortnight, two weeks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for lately, then the Spanish for the semester.

You'll want to know: la quincena.
la quincena — "the fortnight, two weeks".
Nos fuimos de vacaciones la primera quincena de agosto. We went on holiday in the first two weeks of August.
Some workers are paid por quincenas, every two weeks.

The word, taken apart.
quincena is built on quince 'fifteen' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin quīndecim 'fifteen', from quīnque 'five' and decem 'ten' (Lewis & Short). A fortnight is counted as fifteen days.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el lustro — the five-year period.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the semester, then the Spanish for the fortnight.

You'll want to know: el lustro.
el lustro — "the five-year period".
En el último lustro la ciudad cambió mucho. In the last five years the city changed a lot.
A formal word, common in the press.

The word, taken apart.
lustro is a learned borrowing from Latin lūstrum (EtymDB), the cleansing sacrifice that Rome held every five years after the census.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el período — the period.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the five-year period, then the Spanish for the millennium.

You'll want to know: el período.
el período — "the period".
Fue un período difícil para la familia. It was a difficult period for the family.
Periodo without the accent is also correct.

The word, taken apart.
período is a learned borrowing from Latin periodus, which comes from Greek períodos 'a going round, a cycle' (EtymDB), built on perí 'around' and hodós 'way'.
