Persian, chapter 367: Linking ideas.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
از طرف دیگر (az taraf-e digar) — on the other hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an objection, then the Persian for to give an example.

You'll want to know: از طرف دیگر.
از طرف دیگر (az taraf-e digar) — "on the other hand".
این خانه بزرگ است. از طرف دیگر، خیلی گران است. in khâne bozorg ast. az taraf-e digar, kheyli gerân ast. This house is big. On the other hand, it is very expensive.
Often paired with az yek taraf "on the one hand". A whole clause follows it.

The word, taken apart.
از طرف دیگر (az taraf-e digar) is literally "from the other side": az "from" and digar "other" are inherited Persian (EtymDB traces digar to PIE dwóh₁ "two"), and taraf was borrowed from Arabic ṭaraf "side" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 6.
به عبارت دیگر (be 'ebârat-e digar) — in other words.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to give an example, then the Persian for on the other hand.

You'll want to know: به عبارت دیگر.
به عبارت دیگر (be 'ebârat-e digar) — "in other words".
او کار را تمام نکرد. به عبارت دیگر، فردا باید دوباره بیاید. u kâr râ tamâm nakard. be 'ebârat-e digar, fardâ bâyad dobâre biyâyad. He did not finish the job. In other words, he has to come again tomorrow.
Be "with" + 'ebârat "an expression" + digar "other". It restates the same idea in plainer words.

The word, taken apart.
به عبارت دیگر (be 'ebârat-e digar) joins 'ebârat, from Arabic 'ibāra "an expression" (EtymDB), to the inherited Persian words be and digar "other": in another expression.

Lesson 3 of 6.
اما (ammâ) — but, however.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for on the other hand, then the Persian for in other words.

You'll want to know: اما.
اما (ammâ) — "but, however".
هوا سرد بود، اما ما بیرون رفتیم. havâ sard bud, ammâ mâ birun raftim. It was cold, but we went out.
A little more formal than vali; both join two clauses, and ammâ can also open a sentence.

The word, taken apart.
اما (ammâ) comes from Arabic ammā "as for, as regards" (EtymDB); Persian uses it to mean "but".

Lesson 4 of 6.
با این وجود (bâ in vojud) — nevertheless, even so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for in other words, then the Persian for but.

You'll want to know: با این وجود.
با این وجود (bâ in vojud) — "nevertheless, even so".
باران میآمد. با این وجود، مسابقه ادامه پیدا کرد. bârân miâmad. bâ in vojud, mosâbeqe edâme peydâ kard. It was raining. Nevertheless, the match went on.
Like bâ in hâl; a whole clause follows it. Compare bâ vojud-e + a noun, "despite".

The word, taken apart.
با این وجود (bâ in vojud) is literally "with this existing": bâ "with" and in "this" are inherited Persian, and vojud comes from Arabic wujūd "existence" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
هرچند (harchand) — although, even though.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for but, then the Persian for nevertheless.

You'll want to know: هرچند.
هرچند (harchand) — "although, even though".
هرچند خسته بودم، تا آخر فیلم را دیدم. harchand khaste budam, tâ âkhar film râ didam. Although I was tired, I watched the film to the end.
Takes an indicative clause, like agarche; the main clause follows without a linking word.

The word, taken apart.
هرچند (harchand) is a native Persian compound built from har "every" and chand "how many, however much": however much that is so, the main clause still holds.
