# ಕಾಫಿ (kāphi) — "coffee," the longest chain of loans in this book

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C39-CHAHA-01, KA-ETYMON-C39-CHAHA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Cahā* crossed two languages to reach Kannada. This drink crossed
four.

## You'll want to know: ಕಾಫಿ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C39-KAAPI-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ಕಾಫಿ** — *kāphi* — **coffee**

> **ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ಕಾಫಿ.** — *dayaviṭṭu kāphi.* — "Coffee, please."

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C20-HANNONDU-IPPATTU-01] -->

*(Skip this if you already read Kannada.)* **ಫ** (*pha*) is new: **ಪ**
(*pa*) with an aspirated puff of breath added, the same relationship
**ಕ**/**ಖ** and **ತ**/**ಥ** already carry. You met plain **ಪ** back in
Chapter 20's **ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು** (*ippattu*, "twenty") — one of the rare places a
native word still opens with *p*, since most word-initial *p*'s became *h*.
**ಫ** never went through that law at all: it shows up almost only in
borrowed words like this one, holding the foreign sound steady.

## The word, taken apart: five languages deep
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C39-KAAPI-02]; assesses=[] -->

**ಕಾಫಿ** traces to Arabic **قهوة** (*qahwa*), into Ottoman Turkish
**kahve**, into Dutch **koffie**, into English **coffee**, into Kannada
**kāphi** — five languages deep, the longest chain of loans in this book,
longer even than *cahā*'s three-language road through China and Persia.
South India's filter-coffee tradition is comparatively recent, arriving
through colonial-era sea trade — the newer drink, on the older-sounding
name.
