Portuguese, chapter 304: Catástrofes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a catástrofe — the catastrophe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the alert, then the Portuguese for to warn.

You'll want to know: a catástrofe.
a catástrofe — "the catastrophe".
O sismo foi uma catástrofe para a região. The quake was a catastrophe for the region.
Uma catástrofe natural — a natural disaster.

The word, taken apart.
catástrofe comes from Greek katastrophḗ "an overturning, a sudden end" (EtymDB), from katastréphein "to turn down", built on stréphein "to turn".

Lesson 2 of 5.
o desastre — the disaster; a crash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to warn, then the Portuguese for the catastrophe.

You'll want to know: o desastre.
o desastre — "the disaster; a crash".
O jogo foi um desastre. The match was a disaster.
Also a road crash: um desastre de carro.

The word, taken apart.
desastre came through Middle French from Italian disastro "an ill-starred event" (EtymDB), built on astro "a star", from Latin astrum.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the catastrophe, then the Portuguese for the disaster.

You'll want to know: a tragédia.
a tragédia — "the tragedy".
A morte do jovem foi uma tragédia. The young man’s death was a tragedy.
Also the kind of play.

The word, taken apart.
tragédia was borrowed from Latin tragoedia (EtymDB), from Greek tragōidía, built on trágos "a goat" and ōidḗ "a song".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o deslizamento — the landslide; a slip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the disaster, then the Portuguese for the tragedy.

You'll want to know: o deslizamento.
o deslizamento — "the landslide; a slip".
Um deslizamento de terra cortou a estrada. A landslide cut the road.
Deslizamento de terras — landslide.

The word, taken apart.
deslizamento is built on deslizar "to slide" with the ending -mento; the origin of the verb is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: soterrar.
soterrar — "to bury under earth or rubble".
A lama soterrou duas casas. The mud buried two houses.
Mostly of disasters: mud, snow or rubble.

The word, taken apart.
soterrar is built on terra "earth" (EtymDB), from Latin terra, with so- "under".
