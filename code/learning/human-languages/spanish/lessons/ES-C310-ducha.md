## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C310-ASK-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la ducha** — "the shower."

Spanish borrowed this one from Italian *doccia*, a pipe or a spout, and Italian
had it from Latin **ducere**: **to lead**.

That is the whole idea. A shower is not named for water and not named for
washing. It is named for the fact that something **leads** the water to where you
are standing.

*Ducere* is one of the busiest roots English owns. A **duct** leads air. An
**aqueduct** leads water — *aqua* plus *ducere*, and the Romans built them for
five hundred years. To **conduct** is to lead together; to **introduce** is to
lead somebody in; to **produce** is to lead a thing forward into the world. And a
**duke** is a *dux*, the one who leads.

A duke and a shower head, doing the same job.
