# Unit 22: *Hay* — "There is / There are"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say *es la una* and *son las dos* from Unit 11. Today's word,
*hay*, is a third, different way of using "to be" — for existence itself.

## New Material

**hay** — there is / there are
> ← Latin *habet* ("[it] has"), the same root behind *haber* (an auxiliary
> "to have" you'll see later, related to English *have* through French).
> *hay* is invariant — it never changes for singular or plural.

## Grammar Lens

*Hay* answers a different question than *ser* or *estar*: not "what is
this" (*ser*) or "what state is it in" (*estar*), but simply "does this
exist, here?" — *"Hay un libro"* (there is a book), *"Hay dos libros"*
(there are two books). Notice *hay* itself doesn't change between those
two sentences, even though English "there IS a book" / "there ARE two
books" does change. Spanish puts all the number information on the noun
(*libro* vs. *libros*) and leaves the existence-word alone — a third
"to be"-shaped concept, after *ser* and *estar*, that English also
collapses into a single (in this case number-agreeing) verb.
