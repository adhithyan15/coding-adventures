# (three vowels, five signs, one dot) — the same vowel, standing or hanging

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-08] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **அ**. Then look at **்** and say what it does to the letter it
sits on.

## Script: standing, or hanging
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-119, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-120, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-125, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-114, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-121] -->

| vowel | on its own | hanging on a consonant |
|---|---|---|
| long *ā* | **ஆ** | **ா** |
| *u* | **உ** | **ு** |
| short *a* | **அ** | nothing — it is already inside |

That table is the whole system. A Tamil vowel gets a **full-size letter** when
nothing comes before it, and a **hanging sign** when a consonant does. Short *a*
is the exception that proves it: every bare consonant already contains one, so it
needs no sign at all.

## Script: the three signs with no standing partner here
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-09, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-123, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-102] -->

**ி** *i* · **ே** *ē* · **ோ** *ō* — met one per chapter, and each one replaces
the built-in *a* of whatever letter it lands on.

Read them as a set and the shapes stop being arbitrary: **ே** and **ோ** share
the same upward stroke, because **ோ** is that stroke with a tail. Learning one
buys most of the other.

## Script: the dot that removes a vowel
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-DRIZZLE-08, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-118, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-122, TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-124] -->

Three consonants were never gathered with the rest: **ட** *ṭa* · **த** *ta* ·
**ய** *ya*. Each carries its built-in *a*, and **்** takes it away.

So one mark and one letter give you two readings — **த** is *ta*, **த்** is a
bare *t*. Everything the signs above do is a swap; this is the only one that
subtracts.
