## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C46-NIGHT-05]; assesses=[] -->

**somnus** — "**sleep**."

The last word of this chapter, and the right one to end a day on.

The root is Proto-Indo-European ***\*swep-***, "to sleep," and it reaches a long
way: Sanskrit **svapna**, "sleep" or "dream," is the same word, and Old English
had *sweven* for a dream — a word that died out but is still in Chaucer.

**Insomnia** is *in-*, "not," plus *somnus*: **no sleep**. Someone **somnolent**
is sleepy, and a **somnambulist** walks (*ambulāre* — you have that verb) in
their sleep.

The Roman god of sleep is **Somnus**, and his Greek counterpart **Hypnos** gave
English **hypnosis**. Two languages, two gods, one job.

Now put the chapter together. **Lūx** goes, **umbra** comes, the **stēllae** and
the **lūna** appear, and then **somnus**. That is a Latin evening in five
words.
