Portuguese, chapter 500: Agulhas e linhas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a costura — sewing; seam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for faithful, then the Portuguese for faithfulness.

You'll want to know: a costura.
a costura — "sewing; seam".
A minha avó aprendeu costura com a mãe. My grandmother learned sewing from her mother.
Also a seam: a costura rasgou.

The word, taken apart.
costura is usually traced to Vulgar Latin cōnsūtūra, built on Latin cōnsuere "to sew together" (Lewis & Short), from suere "to sew".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o tricô — knitting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for sewing, then the Portuguese for to sew.

You'll want to know: o tricô.
o tricô — "knitting".
O tricô acalma-me depois do trabalho. Knitting calms me down after work.
Fazer tricô — to knit. In Portugal fazer malha is also common.

The word, taken apart.
tricô was borrowed from French tricot "knitting" (EtymDB), from tricoter.

Lesson 4 of 5.
tricotar — to knit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sew, then the Portuguese for knitting.

You'll want to know: tricotar.
tricotar — "to knit".
No inverno, a tia tricota camisolas para todos. In winter, our aunt knits jumpers for everyone.
Also fazer tricô.

The word, taken apart.
tricotar was borrowed from French tricoter "to knit" (EtymDB), which in Old French meant "to beat with sticks".

bordar is linked in EtymDB with French broder "to embroider", which goes back to a Germanic word for an edge or border.
