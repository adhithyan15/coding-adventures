Lesson 1 of 5.
प्रजा (prajā) — the subjects (of a king).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a martyr, then the Hindi for a treaty.

You'll want to know: प्रजा.
प्रजा — prajā — "the subjects (of a king)".
राजा की प्रजा बहुत ग़रीब थी। rājā kī prajā bahut ġarīb thī. The king's subjects were very poor.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
प्रजा (prajā) is Sanskrit prajā, "offspring, people, subjects" (Monier-Williams): the people a king rules. It is singular and feminine: प्रजा ग़रीब थी.

हुक्म (hukm) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḥukm, "a judgement, a ruling". हुक्म देना is to give an order.

Lesson 4 of 5.
विद्रोह (vidroh) — a rebellion; a revolt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an order, then the Hindi for a slave.

You'll want to know: विद्रोह.
विद्रोह — vidroh — "a rebellion; a revolt".
भूख की वजह से किसानों ने विद्रोह किया। bhūkh kī vajah se kisānoṁ ne vidroh kiyā. The farmers rose up because they were going hungry.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
विद्रोह (vidroh) is built from vi- and Sanskrit droha, "injury, treachery"; Monier-Williams gives droha and the verb vi-druh, "to injure, to do wrong", but not vidroha itself, and the sense of a revolt is probably the later language's own. Hindi uses it for a revolt: विद्रोह करना is to rise up against a ruler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a slave, then the Hindi for a rebellion.

You'll want to know: ललकारना.
ललकारना — lalkārnā — "to challenge; to call out (an enemy)".
एक किसान ने राजा के सिपाहियों को ललकारा। ek kisān ne rājā ke sipāhiyoṁ ko lalkārā. A farmer challenged the king's soldiers.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दुश्मन को ललकारा, usne duśman ko lalkārā.

The word, taken apart.
ललकारना (lalkārnā) is the verb of lalkār, a challenge, which CDIAL 10973 lists under lallakka, a noise made to threaten: to shout at an enemy to come and fight.
