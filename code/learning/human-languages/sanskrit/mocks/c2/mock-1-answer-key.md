# Sanskrit C2 mock 1 — answer key and task notes

Use this key with [`rubric.md`](rubric.md) and the confidential
[`mock-1-examiner-script.md`](mock-1-examiner-script.md). Reading, Listening,
Writing, and Speaking are independent 100-point papers; each must reach
60/100. Play every listening part at a natural 165–200 words per minute
**once after one unscored orienting preview**. Collect the Reading and
Listening paper before Writing begins.

Part 1 tests the argument that high-resolution evidence remains governed by
site choice, embodied risk, purpose, privacy, uncertainty, and distributive
rules. Part 2 distinguishes the grain-store debate's competing voices and the
repeated door, authenticity, life, and empty-fund figures. Part 3 requires
cross-text comparison: Devadatta begins from access and adds safeguards;
Maitreyi begins from relational control and opens each use separately. Part 4
requires layered reading of map, river, Meghaduta allusion, dream, secrecy,
and the difference between an incomplete representation and a dishonest one.
Credit the keyed meaning, not a nearby attractive statement.

Across Reading, A occurs fourteen times, B fourteen times, and C twelve
times. The four part-level sequences are deliberately offset.

## Writing

Task 1 requires an independently produced **220–280 word three-source
synthesis and reframing** for the municipal public-health committee. A strong
response integrates the scientist's distinction between daytime averages and
night-time recovery, sensor coverage, mature shade and maintenance; the street
vendors' evidence about displacement, unpaid care, consultation timing, and
mobile shade; and the girl's cultural account of shade as invitation and
relationship rather than temperature alone. It gives a decision rather than
three summaries, distinguishes immediate canopies from long-term trees, names
paid maintenance and livelihood safeguards, and sets measurable indicators
and a review point. Source copying does not count as independent synthesis.

Task 2 requires an independently developed **420–520 word contrasting public
speech** to the city archive's annual meeting. It should address incomplete
public memory through explicit evidence categories, ownership and renewable
consent, oral testimony, state records, uncertainty, privacy, artistic
reconstruction, visible disagreement, correction, and future revision. It
must represent a serious counter-position fairly and answer it. Reward a
speech suited to a mixed public audience, not a disguised specialist report,
and require the proposed title, evidence labels, disagreement record, and
revision route.

For both tasks apply the shared rubric's Devanagari, external sandhi,
word-final visarga or anusvara, continuous shirorekha, word-boundary,
development, synthesis, precision, and register criteria. Documented alternatives used consistently receive full credit. Do not reward copied
prompt language as independent production. A dictionary, grammar reference,
spell-checker, translator, generative assistant, romanization, or a
non-Devanagari response is not permitted.

Across both production papers, documented alternatives used consistently receive full credit.

## Speaking

Part 1 must last **330–360 seconds**. It requires genuine mediation of all eight
observatory sources: fabric conservation, dignified local access, living
science, light pollution, privacy, mixed finance, limited data rights, and
reversible six-month governance. The candidate should distinguish sourced
facts, stakeholder positions, uncertainty, and their own recommendation; give
measurable stop, change, and expand conditions; and answer a serious objection
without caricature.

Part 2 must last **360–450 seconds**. Score the candidate's contribution to a
real three-party negotiation, including accurate reformulation of another
party, challenge, repair, concession, explicit residual disagreement, and a
joint six-month decision. The conservation committee, village youth group,
and university constraints must all materially shape the resolution. Do not
treat interlocutor-supplied language as candidate evidence.

Ask all six Part 3 prompts in order. Each examiner stimulus lasts **20–90
seconds**; that band applies to the examiner's expanded prompt, not to a single
candidate answer. The candidate's six responses together must last **390–480
seconds**. Prompts 1–4 test defence, prioritisation, data-governance precision,
and decision under mixed evidence. Prompt 5 requires reformulation for
twelve-year-old village children; Prompt 6 requires a concise, measurable
reformulation for a district finance officer. Keyword paper notes are allowed;
a written answer script and electronic notes are forbidden.

Score source synthesis and mediation, conceptual precision, organisation,
stakeholder interaction and repair, audience and register control, Sanskrit
vocabulary and grammar, and pronunciation and fluency in the candidate's
declared documented tradition. Documented alternatives used consistently receive full credit.

## Recording and provenance checks

Before operational use, verify the complete one-play delivery **once after one
unscored orienting preview**, the natural 165–200 words-per-minute pace, and
the confidential separation of the spoken masters from the learner paper.
Part 1 must use a documented Kāśī/Varanasi scholarly-and-spoken Sanskrit
pronunciation tradition and Part 4 a distinct documented Kerala
scholarly-and-spoken Sanskrit pronunciation tradition. The production log must
retain actual tradition names, speaker provenance, consent date, recording
date, tradition evidence, and qualified reviewer confirmation. These source
masters do not claim qualified recordings, external certification,
calibration, standard setting, or readiness.

Record and retain the complete Speaking paper for independent scoring. Log
candidate and interlocutor identifiers, the declared documented pronunciation
tradition, each part's timing, aggregate candidate time across the six Part 3
responses, every examiner-stimulus time, interruptions, accessibility changes,
and technical incidents.
