German, chapter 503: Im Garten.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gärtnern — to garden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for lens, then the German for to snap.

You'll want to know: gärtnern.
gärtnern — "to garden".
In ihrer Freizeit gärtnert meine Mutter gern. In her free time my mother likes gardening.
Built on der Gärtner — gardener.

The word, taken apart.
gärtnern is built on Gärtner "gardener", from Garten, which is inherited from Old High German garto "garden, enclosure", from Proto-Germanic gardô (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Unkraut — weeds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to snap, then the German for to garden.

You'll want to know: das Unkraut.
das Unkraut — "weeds".
Zwischen den Tomaten wächst viel Unkraut. Lots of weeds are growing between the tomatoes.
Mostly singular for all the weeds in a bed. Plural: die Unkräuter.

The word, taken apart.
Unkraut is built on un- "not, bad" and Kraut "herb, plant", inherited from Old High German krūt, from Proto-Germanic krūdą (EtymDB).

Am Samstag jäten wir das Unkraut im Garten. On Saturday we pull up the weeds in the garden.

The word, taken apart.
jäten is inherited from Middle High German jeten and Old High German jetan, from Proto-Germanic jedaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Gießkanne — watering can.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for weeds, then the German for to weed.

You'll want to know: die Gießkanne.
die Gießkanne — "watering can".
Mit der Gießkanne gießt das Kind die Blumen. The child waters the flowers with the watering can.
Plural: die Gießkannen. Gießen — to water, to pour.

The word, taken apart.
Gießkanne is built on gießen "to pour", inherited from Old High German giozan, from Proto-Germanic geutaną (EtymDB), and Kanne "jug".

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Kompost — compost.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to weed, then the German for watering can.

You'll want to know: der Kompost.
der Kompost — "compost".
Die Schalen von Obst und Gemüse kommen auf den Kompost. Fruit and vegetable peel goes on the compost.
Plural, rare: die Komposte.

The word, taken apart.
Kompost was borrowed from French compost (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin compositum "something put together", from compōnere.
