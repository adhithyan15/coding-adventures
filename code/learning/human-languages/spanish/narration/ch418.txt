Spanish, chapter 418: Writing --- Four Lines That Hold Together.
1 lesson. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
Four lines to one person — where the second needs the first.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last writing lesson asked for lines that were correct. This one asks for lines that hold together, which is a different thing and a harder one.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one person who will read this. A real one. You will need them in a moment.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Connected writing is not longer writing. Four short lines can be connected, and one long line can fail to be.
[pause 2 seconds]
What makes writing connected is that a sentence stops standing on its own — line two is about line one, and a reader who skipped line one would not follow it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Spanish gives you exactly two words for that at this point, and they do opposite jobs. y adds one thing to another. porque puts one thing under another and says it is the reason.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the whole difference between a list and a paragraph. A list can be read in any order. A paragraph cannot.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four lines about yesterday, addressed to the person you picked. Greet them, tell them two things you did, and close.
At least one line must use y, and at least one must use porque.
A worked example, from words you already hold:
Hola. Ayer fui a la tienda y comí pan. Hablé con mi amiga porque tuve tiempo. Hasta luego.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now write your own four. Do not copy the example — it is there to show the shape, and its second and third lines are the only ones doing the work.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Cover your first line and read the second on its own. Does it still make sense without the first?
[pause 2 seconds]
If it does, you wrote a list. That is not a failure — it is the stage you were at one lesson ago — but go back and make one line depend on another before you stop.
[pause 2 seconds]
And read it as the person you named would. Writing for a reader you can picture is the part that makes this a real task rather than an exercise, and it is the part the exam will keep asking for at every level above this one.
