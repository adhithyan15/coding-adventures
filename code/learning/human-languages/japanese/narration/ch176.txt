Japanese, chapter 176: Frying, Steaming and Seasoning.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょうゆ (shōyu) — soy sauce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for rice bowl, then the Japanese for oil.

You'll want to know: しょうゆ.
しょうゆ — shōyu — "soy sauce".
しょうゆを すこし つかいます。 — shōyu o sukoshi tsukaimasu — "I use a little soy sauce."

The word, taken apart.
しょうゆ (shōyu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shō, "fermented paste" (Middle Chinese tsjangH) and yu, "oil" (yuw) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
みそ (miso) — soybean paste; miso.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for oil, then the Japanese for soy sauce.

You'll want to know: みそ.
みそ — miso — "soybean paste; miso".
みそと とうふを かいます。 — miso to tōfu o kaimasu — "I buy miso and tofu."

The word, taken apart.
みそ (miso) is written with two characters read mi, "taste" (Middle Chinese mj+jH), and so; JMdict marks at least one of its senses as usually written in kana, and the word's older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
わる (waru) — to crack; to split, to divide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for soy sauce, then the Japanese for soybean paste.

You'll want to know: わる.
わる — waru — "to crack; to split, to divide".
たまごを ふたつ わります。 — tamago o futatsu warimasu — "I crack two eggs."
Polite わります, warimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
わる (waru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
