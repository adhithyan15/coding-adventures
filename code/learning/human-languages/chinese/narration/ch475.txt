Mandarin Chinese, chapter 475: Head to Knees.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
手心 (shǒuxīn) — the palm of the hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to put one's strength into it, then the Mandarin for a finger.

You'll want to know: 手心.
手心 — shǒuxīn — "the palm of the hand".
我手心很热是不是体温高了 wǒ shǒuxīn hěn rè shì bu shì tǐwēn gāo le — My palms are hot. Is my temperature up?

The word, taken apart.
手心 (shǒuxīn) is built from 手 'hand' and 心 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuwX and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the heart, or middle, of the hand.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 2 of 6.
xīgài — knee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a finger, then the Mandarin for the palm of the hand.

You'll want to know: xīgài.
xīgài — "knee".
wǒ zǒu le yì tiān lù xīgài hěn téng — I walked all day, and my knees really hurt.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xīgài is built from xī 'the knee' and gài 'a cover', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sit and kajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as the joint of the leg and glosses the second 'to cover'.

身高 (shēngāo) is built from 身 'body' and 高 'high', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syin and kaw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'oneself' and the second 'high'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
wān — to bend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the back of the body, then the Mandarin for a person's height.

You'll want to know: wān.
wān — "to bend".
wǒ de gēbo bù néng wān — I can't bend my arm.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wān is inherited from Middle Chinese 'waen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), explained after an older book as drawing a bow with the arrow set: the bow bends.
