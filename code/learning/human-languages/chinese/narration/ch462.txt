Mandarin Chinese, chapter 462: Surprises.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jīngxǐ — pleasant surprise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for treasured, then the Mandarin for to look forward to.

You'll want to know: jīngxǐ.
jīngxǐ — "pleasant surprise".
zhè shì wǒ jīnnián zuì dà de jīngxǐ — This is the biggest surprise I've had all year.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīngxǐ is built from jīng 'to startle' and 喜 'joy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaeng and xiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'fear' and, after an older book, a startled horse, and the second 'joy'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 2 of 5.
意想不到 (yìxiǎngbúdào) — unexpected; beyond what anyone imagined.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to look forward to, then the Mandarin for a pleasant surprise.

You'll want to know: 意想不到.
意想不到 — yìxiǎngbúdào — "unexpected; beyond what anyone imagined".
这真是一个意想不到的好方法 zhè zhēn shì yí ge yìxiǎngbúdào de hǎo fāngfǎ — That really is an unexpectedly good way of doing it.
不 is said with a rising tone before the falling tone of dào: bú dào.

The word, taken apart.
意想不到 (yìxiǎngbúdào) is built from 意 'the mind', 想 'to think', 不 'not' and 到 'to reach', all inherited from Middle Chinese, 'iH, sjangX, pjut and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): what the mind's thinking does not reach.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 4 of 5.
死心 (sǐxīn) — to give up hope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for beyond what anyone imagined, then the Mandarin for to hate deeply.

You'll want to know: 死心.
死心 — sǐxīn — "to give up hope".
wǒ děng le tā sān nián xiànzài sǐxīn le — I waited three years for him, and now I've given up.

The word, taken apart.
死心 (sǐxīn) is built from 死 'to die' and 心 'heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sijX and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as coming to an end: a heart that has stopped hoping.
