Latin, chapter 401: At School.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
paedagōgus, paedagōgī — a slave who takes a boy to school; a tutor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to equip, then the Latin for to instruct.

You'll want to know: paedagōgus, paedagōgī.
paedagōgus, paedagōgī — "a slave who takes a boy to school; a tutor".
Paedagōgus puerum ad scholam dūcit. The slave-tutor takes the boy to school.
paedagōgus, paedagōgī m., borrowed from Greek paidagōgos, child-leader. English pedagogue.

The word, taken apart.
paedagōgus was borrowed from Greek paidagōgos "child-leader" (EtymDB), built from pais "a child" and agōgos "leading". English pedagogue comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to instruct, then the Latin for a slave who takes a boy to school.

You'll want to know: praeceptor, praeceptōris.
praeceptor, praeceptōris — "a teacher, an instructor".
Praeceptor discipulōs suōs laudāvit. The teacher praised his pupils.
praeceptor, praeceptōris m., built on praecipiō: the one who lays down rules. English preceptor.

The word, taken apart.
praeceptor is built on praecipiō "to instruct" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tor of the one who does it.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a slave who takes a boy to school, then the Latin for a teacher.

You'll want to know: grammaticus, grammaticī.
grammaticus, grammaticī — "a teacher of literature and grammar".
Apud grammaticum poētās legēbāmus. With the grammar master we read the poets.
grammaticus, grammaticī m., borrowed from Greek grammatikos. After the first school, a boy went to the grammaticus to read the poets.

The word, taken apart.
grammaticus was borrowed from Greek grammatikos "skilled in letters" (EtymDB), built on gramma "a letter". English grammar comes from the same Greek family.
