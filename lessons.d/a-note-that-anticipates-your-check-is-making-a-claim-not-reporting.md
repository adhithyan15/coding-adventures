# A note that anticipates your check is making a claim, not reporting a result

Five consecutive Malayalam tranches each scoped from an exam-inventory note, and
**all five notes were wrong**. Four failed in ways already recorded here: the
wrong construction, an implied-empty domain, a plainly false statement about what
is taught. The fifth is the one worth a file of its own, because the note looked
like it had already done the work.

`ML-A1-TIME-09` read:

> **munpu** and **shesham** are not taught. **pinne** ('then') is the nearest and
> it sequences **turns** rather than **events**.

The first sentence is a claim about *absence* and it was true. The second is a
claim about what an existing lesson **teaches**, and it reads exactly like the
output of the domain check — "I looked at what else is here, and here is what it
does." So I did not run the check. I quoted the sentence as a finding, closed the
point on a contrast built from it, and wrote *"a note CAN be trustworthy, and
this is what one looks like"* into five artifacts including two test files.

`ML-C68-at-first.md:48` says the opposite in as many words: *"**ആദ്യം** puts an
**action** first in **time**, and it pairs with **പിന്നെ**."* Its summary table is
headed **"an action in time"**. Its worked example is *lunch first, then tea* —
which orders the world, not the telling of it.

**The general shape.** A note is written by somebody who was looking at something
else at the time. Its statements about *absence* are cheap to verify and usually
hold. Its statements about *what existing material means* are the author's
reading, carry no evidence, and are exactly the ones that decide how you frame
the new work. A note that pre-empts your question is the strongest possible
invitation to skip the check, and therefore the one to check hardest.

**What to do.** For every clause in a note that characterises an existing lesson,
**open that lesson and read what it says it teaches** — its own headings, its own
table headers, its own worked example. Do not accept the note's paraphrase, and
do not accept your own memory of the lesson either. The cost is one file read per
clause.

**And do not grade the note in the same pass that scopes from it.** Praising a
note ("accurate", "trustworthy", "survived the checks") is a claim of the same
kind as the note's own, and it propagates: this one reached the chapter note, the
exam inventory, the changelog and both pinned test files before it was caught.
Record what you verified, clause by clause, and let that stand instead.
