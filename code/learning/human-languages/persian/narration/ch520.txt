Persian, chapter 520: Musicians.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نوازنده (navâzande) — a musician (who plays).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for rhythm, then the Persian for a musical note.

You'll want to know: نوازنده.
نوازنده (navâzande) — "a musician (who plays)".
نوازنده جوان در کنسرت ویولن زد. navâzande-ye javân dar konsert viyolon zad. The young musician played the violin at the concert.
navâz- (stem of navâkhtan) + -ande 'one who'. A singer is khânande.

The word, taken apart.
navâzande is built on native Persian navâz-, the present stem of navâkhtan, 'to play music', with the ending -ande, 'one who'. EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
آهنگساز (âhangsâz) — a composer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a musical note, then the Persian for a musician.

You'll want to know: آهنگساز.
آهنگساز (âhangsâz) — "a composer".
این آهنگساز موسیقی فیلم میسازد. in âhangsâz musiqi-ye film misâzad. This composer writes film music.
âhang 'tune' + sâz 'maker' (stem of sâkhtan).

The word, taken apart.
âhangsâz is built on native Persian âhang, which EtymDB traces to a Middle Persian word for 'pull, draught; melody' (EtymDB), and sâz, the present stem of sâkhtan, 'to make'.

You'll want to know: ارکستر.
ارکستر (orkestr) — "an orchestra".
ارکستر شهر هر ماه یک کنسرت دارد. orkestr-e shahr har mâh yek konsert dârad. The city orchestra gives a concert every month.
rahbar-e orkestr — a conductor.

Lesson 4 of 5.
گروه کر (goruh-e kor) — a choir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a composer, then the Persian for an orchestra.

You'll want to know: گروه کر.
گروه کر (goruh-e kor) — "a choir".
دخترم در گروه کر مدرسه آواز میخواند. dokhtaram dar goruh-e kor-e madrese âvâz mikhânad. My daughter sings in the school choir.
goruh 'group' + kor 'choir'. Not kar 'deaf', spelled the same.

The word, taken apart.
goruh-e kor joins inherited goruh, 'group', which EtymDB traces to a Middle Persian word for 'group, crowd' (EtymDB), and kor, a modern loan usually traced to French chœur, 'choir'. EtymDB's entry for this spelling is the other word, kar, 'deaf'.
