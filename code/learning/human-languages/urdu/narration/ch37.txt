Lesson 1 of 7.
ف (fe) — the letter inside حافظ.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ف and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ھ and گ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say حافظ (hāfiz), "guardian". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ف.
ف — fe, the sound f.
It is inside حافظ (hāfiz). A small round head on a shallow tail, with one dot above.

Writing: ف.
1. start here: loop clockwise around the rounded head above the main line.
2. continue left through the shallow curved tail without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Fe, qāf, ṭe, ḍāl, and ṛe, independent ف (fe) calligraphic and handwriting animations and Fe instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Now say قمیض (qamīz), "shirt". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ق.
ق — qāf, a k made far back in the throat.
It opens قمیض (qamīz), "shirt". Its head is the head of ف (fe), from the last lesson, but it carries two dots, and its bowl dips below the line.

Writing: ق.
1. start here: loop clockwise around the rounded head above the main line.
2. continue down and left through the deep bowl without lifting.
3. after one lift, place the upper-right dot.
4. after another lift, place the upper-left dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Fe, qāf, ṭe, ḍāl, and ṛe, independent ق calligraphic and handwriting animations and Qāf instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say قمیض (qamīz), "shirt". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ض.
ض — zuād, the sound z.
It ends قمیض (qamīz). An oval with a short tooth, a bowl, and one dot above. Urdu writes z with more than one letter; ض (zuād) is the one that came with Arabic words like this.

Writing: ض.
1. start here: close the elongated upper oval clockwise and finish its short tooth.
2. after one lift, restart below the oval and sweep through the lower bowl from right to left.
3. after another lift, place the dot above last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Khe, ze, zal, swad, and zwad, independent ض (zuād) calligraphic and handwriting animations and Zwad instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Script you'll notice: ظ.
ظ — zoe, the sound z again.
It ends حافظ (hāfiz). A closed oval on the line, a tall upright, and one dot. It sounds exactly like ض (zuād) from the last lesson: two spellings, one sound, both kept from the Arabic words they came with.

Writing: ظ.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the closed oval.
2. finish left along the baseline without lifting.
3. after one lift, draw the tall upright.
4. after another lift, place the single dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, To’e, zo’e, se, zhe, and ġhain, independent ظ (zoe) handwriting animation and Zo’e instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Now say ہوئی (huī), "happened (in pleased to meet you)". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ئ.
ئ — hamza on its seat.
It is inside ہوئی (huī), the last word of آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی. Say hu-ī: two vowels side by side. The small hamza sits on a short tooth, its seat, and marks the place where one vowel ends and the next begins.

Writing: ئ.
Put your pen on ئ (hamza) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
