# પાંચમું (pā̃chmũ) — fifth, and the ending you can see

## You'll want to know: પાંચ, with something behind it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-PANCHMU]; assesses=[GU-Q-KETLA-01, GU-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE, GU-GRAMMAR-NEUTER-U-NASAL-TELL, GU-LEX-MAHINO-01] -->

**કેટલા** counts things, and **એક, બે, ત્રણ, ચાર, પાંચ** answer it. A number can
also answer *which one in the line*, and **કેટલા** does not reach that. **પાંચ
ચા** is five teas; picking the last cup out of a row is a different job.

> **પાંચમું** — *pā̃chmũ* — **fifth**

Look at it before anything is said about it. **પાંચ** is still there, whole, and
**-મું** sits behind it.

That **-ું** is the neuter you met on **મારું**, so this word agrees like any
other:

| gender | fifth |
|---|---|
| masculine | **પાંચમો** — *pā̃chmo* |
| feminine | **પાંચમી** — *pā̃chmī* |
| neuter | **પાંચમું** — *pā̃chmũ* |

**મહિનો** is masculine, so the fifth month is **પાંચમો મહિનો**.

## What you've built: an ending that keeps working
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-MU]; assesses=[GU-LEX-PANCHMU, GU-JOIN-KEMKE-01, GU-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE] -->

**-મું** is not a one-off. Wiktionary's entry for the suffix builds the ordinal
of every Gujarati number with it except five: **એક**, **બે**, **ત્રણ**, **ચાર**,
and the word for six, which the next chapter reaches.

That list lands well for you. Your count stops at **પાંચ**, so of the five
ordinals you can reach at all, **four stand on the exception list and one does
not**. This chapter is that exception list, plus the single word showing what
the exceptions are exceptions to.

Which is **કેમકે** the chapter starts at the far end rather than at *first*: a
broken shape stays unreadable until the shape has been seen.
