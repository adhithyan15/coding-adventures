आदत (ādat) — a habit.

You'll want to know: आदत.
आदत — ādat — "a habit".

The word, taken apart.
आदत (ādat) is a loan from Arabic ʿāda, "custom, habit", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ʿ-w-d (Lane).

Lesson 3 of 5.
सपना (sapnā) — a dream.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a habit, then the Hindi for a hobby.

You'll want to know: सपना.
सपना — sapnā — "a dream".

The word, taken apart.
सपना (sapnā) goes back to Sanskrit svapna, "sleep, dream" (CDIAL 13904), though CDIAL does not list the Hindi form. The dream-words of the sister languages, such as Gujarati samṇũ, sit under a separate form supna, "dream" (CDIAL 13481), and Hindi is not among them either.

Lesson 4 of 5.
खेलकूद (khelkūd) — sport, games.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a hobby, then the Hindi for a dream.

You'll want to know: खेलकूद.
खेलकूद — khelkūd — "sport, games".

The word, taken apart.
खेलकूद (khelkūd) is "play-and-jump": khel, from a reconstructed khel-, "play" (CDIAL 3918), and kūd, from Sanskrit kūrdati, "leaps", which CDIAL takes, probably, from Dravidian (CDIAL 3412).

Lesson 5 of 5.
गेंद (gẽd) — a ball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a dream, then the Hindi for sport.

You'll want to know: गेंद.
गेंद — gẽd — "a ball".

The word, taken apart.
गेंद (gẽd) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from genduka, "ball for play" (CDIAL 4248). CDIAL notes that the related kanduka has been traced to Dravidian.
