# గుండె (guṇḍe) — "heart," the everyday native word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C37-NORU-01, TE-ETYMON-C37-NORU-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Four face words done, one of them an honest gap. This chapter
gives you a fifth body word — and then, unusually, a second word for the
very same thing.

## You'll want to know: గుండె
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C38-GUNDE-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **గుండె** — *guṇḍe* — **heart**

> **నా గుండె.** — *nā guṇḍe.* — "My heart."

## The word, taken apart - native, and matched in Kannada
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-ETYMON-C38-GUNDE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**గుండె** is inherited from Proto-Dravidian **\*kuṇṭV**, and it is cognate
with Kannada's own native heart-word, **ಗುಂಡಿಗೆ** (*guṇḍige*) — plainly the
same root, wearing a different Telugu and Kannada shape. *Guṇḍe* is the
plain, physical, everyday word: the organ beating in your chest. The next
lesson gives Telugu's *other* word for heart — not a synonym so much as a
different register entirely.
