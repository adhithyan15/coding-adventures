Latin, chapter 442: Open sea, High sea, Level sea, Of the sea, Coastal.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
salum, salī — the open sea, the swell (of the sea).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lighthouse, then the Latin for a headland.

You'll want to know: salum, salī.
salum, salī — "the open sea, the swell (of the sea)".
Nāvēs extrā portum in salō ad ancorās stant. The ships lie at anchor on the open sea outside the harbour.
Second declension, neuter: salum, salī n. The rolling sea off the coast, where ships ride at anchor.

The word, taken apart.
salum matches Greek salos 'the rolling swell of the sea' (Lewis & Short), but EtymDB gives no parent, so whether it was borrowed is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pelagus, pelagī — the high sea, the open sea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a headland, then the Latin for the open sea.

You'll want to know: pelagus, pelagī.
pelagus, pelagī — "the high sea, the open sea".
Nāvis parva in pelagō sōla est. The small ship is alone on the high sea.
Second declension but neuter: pelagus, pelagī n. A poetic and Greek word. English pelagic 'of the open sea' is related.

The word, taken apart.
pelagus was borrowed from Greek pelagos 'the sea, the open sea' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which explains why it keeps the Greek neuter gender in -us.

Lesson 3 of 5.
aequor, aequoris — the level sea, the sea's surface; a level plain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the open sea, then the Latin for the high sea.

You'll want to know: aequor, aequoris.
aequor, aequoris — "the level sea, the sea's surface; a level plain".
Ventus nūllus est, et aequor quiētum est. There is no wind, and the surface of the sea is still.
Third declension, neuter: aequor, aequoris n. First any flat surface, then above all the calm, level sea (a word of poetry).

The word, taken apart.
aequor is built on aequus 'level, even' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a level surface, and so the sea when it is calm.

Lesson 4 of 5.
marīnus, marīna, marīnum — of the sea, marine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the high sea, then the Latin for the level sea.

You'll want to know: marīnus, marīna, marīnum.
marīnus, marīna, marīnum — "of the sea, marine".
Aqua marīna salsa est, nōn dulcis. Sea water is salty, not sweet.
An adjective of three endings, from mare 'sea'. English marine and marina come from it.

The word, taken apart.
marīnus is built on mare 'sea', inherited from Proto-Italic mari, PIE móri (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English marine and marina come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
maritimus, maritima, maritimum — coastal, by the sea; of the sea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the level sea, then the Latin for of the sea.

You'll want to know: maritimus, maritima, maritimum.
maritimus, maritima, maritimum — "coastal, by the sea; of the sea".
Oppida maritima multōs mercātōrēs habent. Towns by the sea have many merchants.
An adjective of three endings, from mare 'sea'. Ōra maritima, the sea coast. English maritime comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
maritimus is built on mare 'sea' with an old suffix also seen in fīnitimus 'neighbouring' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English maritime comes from it.
