German, chapter 402: An der Tankstelle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tanken — to fill up (with fuel), to get petrol.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to buckle up, then the German for the front-seat passenger.

You'll want to know: tanken.
tanken — "to fill up (with fuel), to get petrol".
Auf dem Weg ans Meer tanken wir einmal. On the way to the sea we fill up once.
Er tankt, er hat getankt. Die Tankstelle is the petrol station.

The word, taken apart.
tanken is built on Tank 'tank', which was borrowed from English tank (EtymDB). EtymDB traces the English word further back to Portuguese tanque 'water tank'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
volltanken — to fill the tank right up.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: volltanken.
volltanken — "to fill the tank right up".
Vor der langen Reise tankt er das Auto voll. Before the long trip he fills the car right up.
Separable: ich tanke voll, ich habe vollgetankt.

The word, taken apart.
volltanken is built from voll 'full', an inherited Germanic word, and tanken, from Tank, which was borrowed from English tank (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
das Benzin — petrol (US: gas).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to fill up, then the German for to fill the tank right up.

You'll want to know: das Benzin.
das Benzin — "petrol (US: gas)".
In den Ferien ist das Benzin wieder teurer. In the holidays petrol is more expensive again.
No plural in everyday use. Bleifrei — unleaded.

The word, taken apart.
Benzin is built on Benzoe 'benzoin', a sweet-smelling resin, with the chemist's ending -in (EtymDB). The name of the resin is usually traced to Arabic.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Zapfsäule — the petrol pump (at a filling station).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to fill the tank right up, then the German for petrol.

You'll want to know: die Zapfsäule.
die Zapfsäule — "the petrol pump (at a filling station)".
Wir halten an der Zapfsäule neben dem kleinen Laden. We stop at the pump next to the little shop.
Plural: die Zapfsäulen. Zapfen means to draw off a liquid, like beer from a barrel.

The word, taken apart.
Zapfsäule is built from zapfen 'to draw off a liquid', from Zapfen 'plug, bung', and Säule 'pillar', an inherited Germanic word (EtymDB lists Säule).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Ölwechsel — the oil change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for petrol, then the German for the petrol pump.

You'll want to know: der Ölwechsel.
der Ölwechsel — "the oil change".
Nach dem Ölwechsel fährt das Auto wieder ruhig. After the oil change the car runs smoothly again.
Same form in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
Ölwechsel is built from Öl 'oil', an early borrowing through Old High German oli from Latin oleum (EtymDB), and Wechsel 'change', inherited from Old High German wehsal (EtymDB).
