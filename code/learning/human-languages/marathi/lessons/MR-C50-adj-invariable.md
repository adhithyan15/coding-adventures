# The adjectives that never change

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-LAHAAN, MR-LEX-MOTHA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two adjectives, two behaviours. That is not an accident about those
two words.

## Grammar Lens: only the -आ adjectives agree
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-ADJ-INVARIABLE]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-ADJ-AGREEMENT, MR-LEX-LAHAAN, MR-LEX-MOTHA, MR-LEX-KHOLI] -->

Marathi's adjectives fall into two classes, and you can tell which class a word
is in by looking at its last letter.

An adjective ending in **-आ** agrees: **मोठा · मोठी · मोठं**. An adjective
ending in anything else does not agree at all, in any gender, ever: **लहान**
is **लहान** in front of every noun in the language.

That second class is large, and most of it is words Marathi borrowed —
from Sanskrit, from Persian, from Arabic. A borrowed word arrived without a
Marathi ending to change, and it never grew one.

This is the mistake to watch for in yourself. Having met **मोठा** first, the
temptation is to say *लहानी खोली* by analogy. It is wrong, and it is wrong in
a way a listener notices immediately, because no Marathi speaker has ever heard
that form. **Check the last letter before you reach for an ending.**
