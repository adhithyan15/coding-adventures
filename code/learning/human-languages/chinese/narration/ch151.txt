You'll want to know: 看到.
看到 — kàndào — "to see; to catch sight of".
我看到他了 wǒ kàndào tā le — I saw him.
我没看到 wǒ méi kàndào — I didn't spot it.
The next lesson puts the second character in your hand. It is the dào you already say for arriving.

The word, taken apart.
看到 (kàndào) is built from 看 'to look' and 到 (dào) 'to arrive; to reach'. 看 is inherited from Middle Chinese khanH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the Guangyun lists beside a level-tone reading glossed 'to look'; 到 (dào) is inherited from tawH, glossed 'to arrive'.

What you've built.
看 is the looking; 到 (dào) says the looking reached something.
看见, which you learned long ago as one word, is made the same way: 看, then 见.

Script — 至 on the left, then the blade of 前.
到 (dào).
Two pieces, and both have parts you write.
至, six strokes, on the left: a bar across the top; a stroke that falls to the left and turns right; a dot at its end; then 土, as at the foot of 在: a short bar, a vertical through it, and the bottom bar, rising a little to the right.
刂, two strokes, on the right, as on the right of 前: a short vertical, then a long vertical that hooks to the left at its foot.
6 + 2 = eight.

Lesson 3 of 8.
看到 — 没看到 (kàndào — méi kàndào): a verb and its result.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two verbs in a row, and the second one says how it turned out.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 看到, aloud — kàndào]
[your turn — recall: say 看见 — kànjiàn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 我没去 — wǒ méi qù, I didn't go]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: a verb, then what came of it.
A second word straight after a verb can say how it turned out. 看 is looking; 看到 (kàndào) is looking that reached something.
我看了 没看到 wǒ kàn le, méi kàndào — I looked, but I didn't spot it.
The negative is 没, because the result did not come about:
我没看到他 wǒ méi kàndào tā — I didn't see him. 你看到了吗 nǐ kàndào le ma — Did you see it?
看见 is made the same way, 看 and its result 见. The next four lessons add four more pairs like these, each a verb and how it ended.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 看到了 — kàndào le — Yes, I saw it.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: 我没看到他 — wǒ méi kàndào tā — I didn't see him.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

You'll want to know: 学会.
学会 — xuéhuì — "to learn (and now be able to)".
我学会开车了 wǒ xuéhuì kāichē le — I've learned to drive.

The word, taken apart.
学会 (xuéhuì) is built from 学 'to study' and 会 'can'. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, haewk and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to awaken to; to learn' and 'to come together'. 会 as 'can' is a later sense than that gloss, assumed here, not quoted.

What you've built.
学 is the studying; 会 is the result: now you can. With 了 at the end it is news: 我学会开车了.
Without the result, 我学开车 wǒ xué kāichē says only that you are studying it.

You'll want to know: tīngdǒng.
tīngdǒng — "to understand (on hearing)".
wǒ tīngdǒng le — I understood what was said.
nǐ tīngdǒng le ma — Did you catch that?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tīngdǒng is built from tīng 'to listen' and dǒng 'to understand', the two verbs you know. The character for tīng is inherited from Middle Chinese theng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to hear'. The character for dǒng has no reading in the Guangyun: no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
tīng is the listening; dǒng is the result. 没 says it did not come: wǒ méi tīngdǒng, I didn't catch it.
tīng alone is only listening; tīngdǒng is listening that worked.

Lesson 7 of 8.
zuòwán — to finish (doing).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: zuòwán.
zuòwán — "to finish (doing)".
wǒ zuòwán le — I've finished.
nǐ zuòwán le ma — Have you finished?
Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zuòwán is built from 做 'to do', the 做 you write, and wán 'to finish'. 做 has no reading in the Guangyun, so its history is not settled. wán is inherited from Middle Chinese hwan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'whole; complete'; the regular sound change from hwan would give huán, so why it is said wán is not settled.

What you've built.
做 is the doing; wán is the result: it is done. 了 at the end makes it news: wǒ zuòwán le.
Not finished yet takes 没: wǒ méi zuòwán.

Lesson 8 of 8.
我看到他了 (wǒ kàndào tā le) — two speakers check what the other has seen, understood, learned and finished.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The chapter closes here, with two people checking on each other.
[your turn — recall: say zuòwán, to finish — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say kàndǒng, to understand what you read — R1]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 学会 — xuéhuì — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange.
A: 你看到他了吗 nǐ kàndào tā le ma — Did you see him? B: 看到了 他在那儿 kàndào le, tā zài nàr — I did. He's over there. A: 他说什么 tā shuō shénme — What's he saying? A: nǐ tīngdǒng le ma — Did you catch it? B: méi tīngdǒng, tā shuō de tài kuài le — No. He talks too fast. A: nǐ zuòwán le ma — Have you finished? B: 没有 我在看书 méiyǒu, wǒ zài kàn shū — No, I'm reading. A: zhè běn shū nǐ kàndǒng le ma — Do you follow this book? B: kàndǒng le — Yes. A: 你学会开车了吗 nǐ xuéhuì kāichē le ma — Have you learned to drive? B: 学会了 xuéhuì le — I have.
Each yes repeats the verb and its result, with 了: 看到了, kàndǒng le, 学会了. Each no puts 没 in front: méi tīngdǒng.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Did B catch what he was saying?

What you've built — five verbs that finish.
A verb, then its result; 没 when the result did not come:
我看到他了 wǒ kàndào tā le — I saw him.
我学会开车了 wǒ xuéhuì kāichē le — I've learned to drive.
wǒ méi tīngdǒng — I didn't catch it.
wǒ zuòwán le — I've finished.
Listen. Nǐ kàndào wǒ de shǒujī le ma?
