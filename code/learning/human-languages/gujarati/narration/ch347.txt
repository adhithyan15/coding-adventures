Gujarati, chapter 347: The Law.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કાયદો (kāydo) — a law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for different, then the Gujarati for to compare.

You'll want to know: કાયદો.
કાયદો — kāydo — "a law".
નવો કાયદો ગરીબ માણસો માટે સારો છે. navo kāydo garīb māṇso māṭe sāro che. The new law is good for poor people.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કાયદો (kāydo) is usually traced to Arabic qāʿida, "a base, a rule", through Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉલ્લંઘન (ullanghan) — a violation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to compare, then the Gujarati for a law.

You'll want to know: ઉલ્લંઘન.
ઉલ્લંઘન — ullanghan — "a violation".
લાલ સિગ્નલ ઓળંગવું એ કાયદાનું ઉલ્લંઘન છે. lāl signal oḷangvũ e kāydānũ ullanghan che. Going through a red light is a breach of the law.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઉલ્લંઘન (ullanghan) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit ullaṅghana, "leaping over, passing beyond" (Monier-Williams): stepping over a line one should not cross.

Lesson 4 of 5.
લાગુ (lāgu) — in force; applicable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a violation, then the Gujarati for observance.

You'll want to know: લાગુ.
લાગુ — lāgu — "in force; applicable".
નવો કાયદો આવતા મહિનાથી લાગુ થશે. navo kāydo āvtā mahināthī lāgu thaśe. The new law comes into force next month.

The word, taken apart.
લાગુ (lāgu) CDIAL lists Gujarati lāgu, "near", under Sanskrit lagna, "sticking to, fixed on" (CDIAL 10893); "in force, applicable" is its sense in law and in offices.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઉથાપવું (uthāpvũ) — to defy (an order or a word).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for observance, then the Gujarati for in force.

You'll want to know: ઉથાપવું.
ઉથાપવું — uthāpvũ — "to defy (an order or a word)".
તેણે પિતાની વાત ઉથાપી. teṇe pitānī vāt uthāpī. He went against his father's word.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે નિયમ ઉથાપ્યો, teṇe niyam uthāpyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઉથાપવું (uthāpvũ) is inherited from a form CDIAL reconstructs as ut-sthāpyatē, "is raised"; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to take down, to remove, to transgress" (CDIAL 1904).
