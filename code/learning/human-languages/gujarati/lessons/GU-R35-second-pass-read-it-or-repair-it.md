# Read it, or repair it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-MAAF-01, GU-PERFORMANCE-REPAIR-FIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] You did not follow what was said. Two words, from memory, that buy
you a second attempt.

## You'll want to know: four words you can now read unaided
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-NAMASTE-READ-01, GU-SCRIPT-KAAGAL-01, GU-SCRIPT-MAAF-01, GU-LEX-NAAK] -->

| word | | |
|---|---|---|
| **નમસ્તે** | *namaste* | the greeting |
| **કાગળ** | *kāgaḷ* | paper |
| **માફ કરો** | *māf karo* | forgive me |
| **નાક** | *nāk* | nose |

Not one of them needed a new letter when it was read. Every piece was already
yours, taught pages or chapters earlier and waiting — which is the whole reason
the script came in a drizzle rather than a block.

## Script: the letters that stand alone, and the two with breath
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-INDEPENDENT-E-01, GU-SCRIPT-INDEPENDENT-U-01, GU-SCRIPT-INDEPENDENT-II-01, GU-SCRIPT-KHA-01, GU-SCRIPT-DDHA-01] -->

> એ  ઉ  ઈ   and   ખ  ઢ

Two groups, and the difference is worth naming. **એ**, **ઉ** and **ઈ** are
vowels standing on their own, needed only where no consonant is carrying them.
**ખ** and **ઢ** are consonants with the breath let out after them — the same
step that separates a plain letter from its aspirated partner all the way down
the row.

Standing alone is a question of **position**. Aspiration is a question of
**sound**. Neither tells you anything about the other.

## What you've built — courtesy, farewells, and the kit
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-SAARUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C01-PRACTICE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C04-PRACTICE-01, GU-PERFORMANCE-REPAIR-FIVE-01] -->

**સારું** — good, okay, fine — is the small word that keeps a conversation
moving when you have nothing else to add. Beside it sit the courtesy exchange
and the farewells, each practised once in its own chapter and not since.

And behind all of them, the repair kit this chapter finished: the way to say you
did not understand, and the way to ask forgiveness for it. **A beginner uses the
repair kit more than any single word in the book**, which is the argument for
running it cold rather than only where it was taught.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] When does a Gujarati vowel need a letter of its own? (**When no consonant is carrying it.**) What separates **ખ** from its plain partner? (**The breath after it.**) Which small word keeps a conversation moving? (**સારું**.) And what do you say when you did not follow? (**માફ કરો**, with the words for not understanding.)
