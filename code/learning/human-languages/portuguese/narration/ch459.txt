Portuguese, chapter 459: Nas rochas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o rochedo — the big rock, the crag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the gorge, then the Portuguese for to climb.

You'll want to know: o rochedo.
o rochedo — "the big rock, the crag".
Sentámo-nos num rochedo a ver o mar. We sat on a big rock watching the sea.
Built on a rocha.

The word, taken apart.
rochedo is built on rocha 'rock' with -edo, a group of things. Rocha came from French roche (EtymDB), whose earlier origin is not settled.

Com a maré baixa, podemos andar até à ilha. At low tide, we can walk to the island.
Maré alta — high tide; maré baixa — low tide.

The word, taken apart.
maré came from French marée 'tide', built on mer 'sea', from Latin mare (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
a concha — the seashell; the ladle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the big rock, then the Portuguese for the tide.

You'll want to know: a concha.
a concha — "the seashell; the ladle".
A minha filha apanhou conchas na praia. My daughter picked up shells on the beach.
In the kitchen, a concha is the soup ladle.

The word, taken apart.
concha is inherited from Vulgar Latin concla, from Latin conchula, a small form of concha 'shell' (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: bronzear.
bronzear — "to tan, to get a tan".
Passámos a tarde a bronzear-nos na areia. We spent the afternoon tanning on the sand.
Bronzear-se; estar bronzeado — to be tanned.

The word, taken apart.
bronzear is built on bronze, which was borrowed from French bronze, from Italian bronzo (EtymDB): skin the colour of bronze.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mergulhar — to dive (in), to plunge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the seashell, then the Portuguese for to tan.

You'll want to know: mergulhar.
mergulhar — "to dive (in), to plunge".
Ele mergulhou do rochedo para a água. He dived off the rock into the water.
Also to dip something: mergulhar o pão no café.

The word, taken apart.
mergulhar is inherited from Vulgar Latin merguliāre (EtymDB), from mergulus 'little diver (bird)', on mergere 'to dip'.
