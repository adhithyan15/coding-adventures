Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 278: Stories and Riddles.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
कहाणी (kahāṇī) — a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to sway, then the Marwadi for to be delighted.

You'll want to know: कहाणी.
कहाणी — kahāṇī — "a story".
दादी रोज कहाणी सुणावै है। dādī roj kahāṇī suṇāvai hai. Grandmother tells a story every day.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कहाणी (kahāṇī) is inherited from Sanskrit kathānaka, "a little tale"; CDIAL lists Marwari kā̃ṇi, "tale, proverb", and Gujarati kahāṇī (CDIAL 2705).

Lesson 3 of 6.
मीचणो (mīchṇo) — to shut one's eyes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a story, then the Marwadi for a riddle.

You'll want to know: मीचणो.
मीचणो — mīchṇo — "to shut one's eyes".
छोरो आंख मीचै है अर गिणै है। chhoro ā̃kh mīchai hai ar giṇai hai. The boy shuts his eyes and counts.
Past (I …): म्हैं आंख मीची, mhaĩ ā̃kh mīchī.

The word, taken apart.
मीचणो (mīchṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed micc, "close the eyes, blink"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi mīcnā and Gujarati micvũ, both "to close the eyes" (CDIAL 10118). In a hiding game the seeker shuts both eyes and counts.

म्हारी पुतळी नुवी है। mhārī putḷī nuvī hai. My doll is new.

लुकणो — lukṇo — "to hide (oneself)".
छोरो खाट हेठै लुकै है। chhoro khāṭ heṭhai lukai hai. The boy hides under the bed.

लुकणो (lukṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit lupyate, "is lost, is removed"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari lukaï, "hides" (CDIAL 11083).
