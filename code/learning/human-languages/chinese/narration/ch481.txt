Mandarin Chinese, chapter 481: Is It Serious?.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
要紧 (yàojǐn) — urgent; a cause for concern.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be allergic, then the Mandarin for to lie awake at night.

You'll want to know: 要紧.
要紧 — yàojǐn — "urgent; a cause for concern".
有一点儿血不要紧 yǒu yìdiǎnr xiě bú yàojǐn — There's a little blood, but it's nothing serious.
The new character, in pieces: 紧 (jǐn) — ten strokes in three pieces: at the top two short verticals and 又 yòu; under them two short zigzag strokes and a dot; and at the foot 小 xiǎo, three strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
要紧 (yàojǐn) is built from 要 'to need' and 紧 (jǐn) 'tight', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jiewH and kjinX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to bind' and the second 'tightly bound'.

Script — two verticals and 又, over a silk piece ending in 小.
紧 (jǐn).
Three pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top, four strokes. Two short verticals side by side, then 又: a bar that turns into a stroke falling to the left, and a long stroke falling to the right.
The middle, three strokes. A short stroke down that turns right, a second one like it to its lower right, then a dot.
The foot, 小, three strokes. A vertical that hooks to the left, a dot on the left, then a dot on the right.
4 + 3 + 3 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 紧 (jǐn) have? (Three: four strokes, three and three.) Which written characters are inside it? (又 at the top and 小 at the foot.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lie awake at night, then the Mandarin for serious or urgent.

You'll want to know: 病情.
病情 — bìngqíng — "a patient's condition; how an illness is going".
他的病情不要紧你不要伤心 tā de bìngqíng bú yàojǐn nǐ bú yào shāngxīn — His condition isn't serious, so don't be upset.

The word, taken apart.
病情 (bìngqíng) is built from 病 'illness' and 情 'the state of things', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaengH and dzjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as an illness growing worse.

Lesson 5 of 6.
gǎnrǎn — to become infected.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for how an illness is going, then the Mandarin for the cause of an illness.

You'll want to know: gǎnrǎn.
gǎnrǎn — "to become infected".
wǒ gǎnjué shāngkǒu gǎnrǎn le — I think the wound has become infected.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: gǎn is said with a rising tone before rǎn, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnrǎn is built from 感 'to move' and rǎn 'to dye', both inherited from Middle Chinese, komX and nyemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to move' and the second 'to dye'. The medical sense is modern.

The word, taken apart.
bìngdú is built from 病 'illness' and dú 'poison', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaengH and dowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'harm' and, after an older book, as a plant that harms people. The sense 'virus' is modern.
