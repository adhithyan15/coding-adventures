# подруга — "friend" (female), built on the word you just learned

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-DRUG] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Russian doesn't reach for a new word to make *друг* feminine — it
builds one, in front of your eyes, out of pieces you already have.

## You'll want to know first — подруга
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-PODRUGA]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-NOUN-GENDER-ENDING] -->

> **подруга** — *podrúga* — **friend** (a woman)

Ends in **-а**, so feminine — the rule holding for the second time in this
chapter, this time on a longer word.

## Grammar Lens: watch the word get built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-FEMININE-SUFFIX-A]; assesses=[RU-LEX-DRUG, RU-ETYMON-DRUG-ACCOMPANY] -->

Take *друг* apart and put it back together:

> **по-** (a prefix here worn down to little independent meaning) + **друг**
> (friend) + **-а** (the feminine ending) → **подруга**

This is Russian's ordinary machinery for turning a masculine person-word
feminine: keep the root, change the ending, sometimes add a light prefix
along the way. It is the same instinct English uses in *host* → *host-ess* or
*actor* → *actr-ess* — a suffix doing the work — except Russian reaches for
this pattern constantly, on ordinary words, not just a formal handful.

Say the pair together and the family resemblance is obvious:

> **друг · подруга** — *drug · podrúga*

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-PODRUGA, RU-GRAMMAR-FEMININE-SUFFIX-A, RU-LEX-DRUG, RU-ETYMON-DRUG-ACCOMPANY] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say "friend" for a woman. (**Подруга**.) What three pieces build
it, and what does each contribute? (**По-** + **друг** + **-а**, the
feminine ending.) What English pattern does the same job, on different
words? (**-ess**, as in *host/hostess*.) And what does *друг* itself trace
back to? (**PIE *dʰrewgʰ-*, "to accompany, be loyal."**)
