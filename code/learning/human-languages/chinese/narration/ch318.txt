Mandarin Chinese, chapter 318: Letters from Far Away.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bǐyǒu — pen pal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for household, then the Mandarin for beforehand.

You'll want to know: bǐyǒu.
bǐyǒu — "pen pal".
wǒ xiǎoshíhou yǒu yí ge bǐyǒu — When I was little I had a pen pal.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: bǐ is said with a rising tone before yǒu, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
bǐyǒu is built from bǐ 'a pen' and yǒu 'a friend', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pit and hjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which names the first as the writing brush and glosses the second as those who share one mind.

Lesson 2 of 5.
xìnfēng — envelope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for beforehand, then the Mandarin for a pen pal.

You'll want to know: xìnfēng.
xìnfēng — "envelope".
tā de xìnfēng lǐ yǒu yì zhāng zhàopiàn — There was a photo in his envelope.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xìnfēng is built from xìn 'a letter' and fēng 'to seal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sinH and pjowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'loyal and true' and the second 'great', 'a domain' and 'a rank'; the senses 'a letter' and 'to seal' are not given there.

míngxìnpiàn — "postcard".
tā cóng Zhōngguó gěi wǒ jì le yì zhāng míngxìnpiàn — He sent me a postcard from China.

míngxìnpiàn is built from 明 'bright, open', xìn 'a letter' and piàn 'a flat piece', all three inherited from Middle Chinese, mjaeng, sinH and phenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'light' and the last 'half' and 'a split piece of wood'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
回音 (huíyīn) — reply; echo.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an envelope, then the Mandarin for a postcard.

You'll want to know: 回音.
回音 — huíyīn — "reply; echo".
wǒ gěi tā xiě le xìn tā réngrán méiyǒu huíyīn — I wrote to him, and there's still no reply.

The word, taken apart.
回音 (huíyīn) is built from 回 'to turn back' and 音 'a sound', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwoj and 'im (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to turn' and the second 'a sound'. A sound that comes back is an echo; the sense 'a reply' is not given there.

zhuǎngào — "to pass on a message".
qǐng nǐ zhuǎngào tā míngtiān wǒ bú qù le — Please tell him for me that I'm not going tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
zhuǎngào is built from zhuǎn 'to turn' and gào 'to tell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjwenX and kawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to move' and the second 'to report'.
