You'll want to know: かぞく.
かぞく — kazoku — "a family", the people you live with or grew up with. Said of your own, it means my family.
Three beats: ka–zo–ku. か and く you already write. The middle sign is そ with the two short strokes of the voicing mark at its upper right, so so becomes zo. The next lesson writes it.

Lesson 2 of 9.
ぞ — そ, voiced to zo.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the change until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

[your turn — recall: say a family — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say well, as in healthy — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a place — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say relieved — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script — the change.
そ so + ゛ becomes ぞ zo.
Write そ in full: one stroke that zigzags twice before it drops into an open curve. Then add the two short strokes of the voicing mark at the upper right, the left one first. The base shape does not change.
The mark voices the opening consonant: s becomes z, and the vowel stays o. It is the change you made on さ for ざ, in ございます.
With か in front and く after it, you can now write the word from the last lesson: かぞく, kazoku, a family.

Guided Practice.
Write そ, then ぞ, and say so, zo.
Write さ ざ and そ ぞ (zo) in pairs, and say each one.
Write かぞく from memory and say kazoku.

Stop after one accurate form.

You'll want to know: かぞえる.
かぞえる — kazoeru — "to count": one, two, three, out loud or on your fingers. This is the dictionary form, the one a word list gives. Said politely, it is kazoemasu.
The new sign is spent at once. か, ぞ, え and る: four beats, ka–zo–e–ru. It ends in る, like the verb たべる. You write every sign in it now.
