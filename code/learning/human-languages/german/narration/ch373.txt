German, chapter 373: Mit allem rechnen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
unvorhergesehen — unforeseen, unexpected.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the schedule, then the German for to change your plans.

You'll want to know: unvorhergesehen.
unvorhergesehen — "unforeseen, unexpected".
Wegen eines unvorhergesehenen Problems kommt der Techniker erst morgen. Because of an unforeseen problem the technician is not coming until tomorrow.
Built from un-, vorher — beforehand — and gesehen — seen.

The word, taken apart.
unvorhergesehen is built from un- "not", vorher "beforehand" and gesehen, the participle of sehen "to see", Old High German sehan (EtymDB): not seen in advance.

Lesson 2 of 5.
spontan — spontaneous, on the spur of the moment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to change your plans, then the German for unforeseen.

You'll want to know: spontan.
spontan — "spontaneous, on the spur of the moment".
Wir sind ganz spontan ans Meer gefahren. We drove to the seaside on the spur of the moment.
Very common for plans made at the last minute: Ich bin da ganz spontan.

The word, taken apart.
spontan goes back to Latin spontāneus "of one's own accord" (EtymDB), from sponte "of one's free will" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
berücksichtigen — to take into account.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for unforeseen, then the German for spontaneous.

You'll want to know: berücksichtigen.
berücksichtigen — "to take into account".
Bei der Reise müssen wir auch das Wetter berücksichtigen. For the trip we also have to take the weather into account.
Inseparable: er berücksichtigt, er hat berücksichtigt.

The word, taken apart.
berücksichtigen is built on Rücksicht "consideration", literally a looking back, from Rücken "back" and Sicht "sight", of the family of sehen (EtymDB). Rücksicht is usually explained as a rendering of Latin respectus "a looking back" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Prognose — the forecast, the prognosis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for spontaneous, then the German for to take into account.

You'll want to know: die Prognose.
die Prognose — "the forecast, the prognosis".
Die Prognose für das Wochenende ist gut: viel Sonne. The forecast for the weekend is good: lots of sun.
Plural: die Prognosen. Eine Prognose stellen — to make a forecast; a doctor's prognosis too.

The word, taken apart.
Prognose is a learned borrowing from Late Latin prognōsis, from Greek prógnōsis "foreknowledge", built from pro "before" and gnôsis "knowing". EtymDB has no entry for the German word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
einplanen — to allow for, to plan in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to take into account, then the German for the forecast.

You'll want to know: einplanen.
einplanen — "to allow for, to plan in".
Für die Fahrt planen wir eine Stunde mehr ein. We are allowing an extra hour for the drive.
Separable: wir planen ein, wir haben eingeplant.

The word, taken apart.
einplanen is built from ein "in" and planen "to plan", from Plan, borrowed from French plan and going back to Latin plānus "flat" (EtymDB).
