# cobrar — the other end of paying

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PAGAR, ES-LEX-DINERO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *pagar*. Say *el dinero*. You have had both a long time. Now say
what the **shop** does, from its own side of the counter.

## You'll want to know: cobrar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PAGAR] -->

**cobrar** — to charge somebody, to collect money, to get paid.

One verb for all three, and which one it means depends on who is speaking.

## Grammar Lens: who is on each end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-05, ES-LEX-PAGAR] -->

**pagar** and **cobrar** are the same transaction from opposite sides:

| who | verb |
|---|---|
| the customer | paga |
| the shop | cobra |

So *me cobraron treinta euros* — they charged me thirty. And *cobro el viernes*
— I get paid on Friday, said by somebody with a job. English needs three
different verbs (*charge*, *collect*, *get paid*) where Spanish holds one, and
the one holds because the money is always moving **toward** the speaker.

That is also why **no se cobra nada** on a shop notice means nothing will be
charged to you.
