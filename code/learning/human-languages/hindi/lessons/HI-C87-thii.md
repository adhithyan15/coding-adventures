# थी — the ending you have bent a dozen times, arriving somewhere new

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] था ends in **-आ**. Before reading on: what has every other word
ending in **-आ** done when a woman was the subject?

## You'll want to know: थी
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-02]; assesses=[] -->

**थी** (*thī*) — "was," spoken by or about a female subject.

| speaker | earlier |
|---|---|
| a man | मैं घर पर था। |
| a woman | मैं घर पर थी। |

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-02, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

This is not a new pattern. It is the oldest one you have:

| masculine | feminine |
|---|---|
| काला | काली |
| बोलता | बोलती |
| था | थी |

A word ending in **-आ** swaps that ending for **-ई**. You learned it on
adjectives, met it again on the habitual verb, and here it is a third time. The
past copula behaves like an adjective because, historically, it once was one —
a participle meaning *situated*, agreeing with whoever was situated.

## Grammar Lens: what each tense listens to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-02] -->

| tense | listens to the person | listens to gender |
|---|---|---|
| present | yes (हूँ, है, हैं) | no |
| past | no | yes (था, थी) |

The two tenses divide the work differently, and neither one asks you to track
both at once. A woman says थी whether she is talking about herself or about
another woman.
