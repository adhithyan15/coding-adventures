Italian, chapter 302: Al fuoco.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'incendio — the fire, the blaze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the stretcher, then the Italian for the siren.

You'll want to know: l'incendio.
l'incendio — "the fire, the blaze".
L'incendio è cominciato in cucina. The fire started in the kitchen.
A destructive fire; a fire in the fireplace is il fuoco.

The word, taken apart.
incendio is a learned borrowing from Latin incendium "a fire" (EtymDB), from incendere "to set alight".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
divampare — to flare up, to break out (of a fire).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the siren, then the Italian for the fire.

You'll want to know: divampare.
divampare — "to flare up, to break out (of a fire)".
Il fuoco è divampato in pochi minuti. The fire flared up in a few minutes.
With essere: è divampato.

The word, taken apart.
divampare is built on vampa "a blaze, a flame" with di- (EtymDB); vampa is usually traced to Latin vapor "steam, heat".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'estintore — the fire extinguisher.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: l'estintore.
l'estintore — "the fire extinguisher".
C'è un estintore su ogni piano. There is an extinguisher on every floor.
Built on Latin exstinguere, ‘to put out’.

The word, taken apart.
estintore is built on Latin exstinctor "one who puts out", from exstinguere "to quench" (Lewis & Short).

A doctor's word for a burn on the skin.
