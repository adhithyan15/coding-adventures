## You'll want to know: ಔಷಧ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C314-AUSADHA-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C50-LEAVE-03, KA-LEX-C48-ROLE-03, KA-LEX-GIVE, KA-LEX-C34-TEGEDUKO-01, KA-ETYMON-C23-DINA-01, KA-LEX-C07-ERADU-01, KA-LEX-C71-MORE-03] -->

**ಔಷಧ** — *auṣadha* — "a medicine; a remedy".

> **ಪ್ರಯಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ ನನಗೆ ಜ್ವರ ಬಂತು. ವೈದ್ಯರು ಔಷಧ ಕೊಟ್ಟರು.**
>
> *prayāṇadalli nanage jvara bantu. vaidyaru auṣadha koṭṭaru.* — "On the journey I got a fever. The doctor gave (me) medicine."

**ಪ್ರಯಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ** (*prayāṇadalli*, "on the journey"). A fever "came" to me: **ನನಗೆ ಜ್ವರ ಬಂತು**.

> **ಈ ಔಷಧ ದಿನಕ್ಕೆ ಎರಡು ಸಲ ತೆಗೆದುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ.**
>
> *ī auṣadha dinakke eraḍu sala tegedukoḷḷi.* — "Take this medicine twice a day."

**ದಿನಕ್ಕೆ** (*dinakke*, "a day, per day"); **ಎರಡು ಸಲ**, "two times", with the **ಸಲ** of **ಇನ್ನೊಂದು ಸಲ ಹೇಳಿ**; **ತೆಗೆದುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ** is the polite "take".

In talk you will often hear **ಔಷಧಿ**, with a final **-ಇ**: the same word. **ಮಾತ್ರೆ** is one tablet; **ಔಷಧ** is any medicine.

Its first letter is one you have not written: the vowel *au*, standing at the start of a word. The next lesson takes it out.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ಔಷಧ** (*auṣadha*) is Sanskrit *auṣadha*. CDIAL 2571 gives it as "consisting of herbs" and, as a noun, "herbs in general, a herb", built on *ōṣadhi*, "a herb, a plant". Pali already has *ōsadha* for "medicine". A word for herbs became the word for the remedy made from them.
