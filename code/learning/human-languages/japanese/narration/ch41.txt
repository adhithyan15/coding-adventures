Lesson 1 of 5.
てがみ (tegami) — a letter (post).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a problem, then the Japanese for an answer.

You'll want to know: てがみ.
てがみ — tegami — "a letter (post)".

The word, taken apart.
てがみ (tegami) is a native compound: te, "hand", and kami, "paper", voiced to gami, both kun'yomi of its kanji (JMdict, KANJIDIC2). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
じかん (jikan) — time; an hour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an answer, then the Japanese for a letter.

You'll want to know: じかん.
じかん — jikan — "time; an hour".

The word, taken apart.
じかん (jikan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "time" (Middle Chinese dzyi) and kan, "interval" (kean) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 4 of 5.
おかね (okane) — money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for time; an hour, then the Japanese for a name.

You'll want to know: おかね.
おかね — okane — "money".

The word, taken apart.
おかね (okane) is the polite prefix o- with native kane, "metal; money", read as the kun'yomi of the character for gold and metal. It is inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
やま (yama) — a mountain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a name, then the Japanese for money.

You'll want to know: やま.
やま — yama — "a mountain".

The word, taken apart.
やま (yama) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
