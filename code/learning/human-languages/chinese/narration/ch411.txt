Lesson 1 of 6.
mǎtou — pier; wharf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to launch a new transport line, then the Mandarin for to hit the road.

You'll want to know: mǎtou.
mǎtou — "pier; wharf".
qù mǎtou zěnme zǒu — How do I get to the pier?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mǎtou is built from mǎ and the noun ending tou, both inherited from Middle Chinese, maeX and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first only in the name of agate, a stone like jade, and the second as 'the head'; how the pair came to mean a pier is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
船长 (chuánzhǎng) — ship's captain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to hit the road, then the Mandarin for a pier for boats.

You'll want to know: 船长.
船长 — chuánzhǎng — "ship's captain".
我想当船长 wǒ xiǎng dāng chuánzhǎng — I want to be a ship's captain.
The new character, in pieces: 船 (chuán) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the boat piece, six strokes, a short stroke falling to the left, a long one down the left, a bar that turns down and hooks, a dot, a bar rising across and a last dot; on the right a short stroke falling to the left and a bar that turns down and bends right, then 口 under them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
船长 (chuánzhǎng) is built from 船 (chuán) 'a boat' and 长 'the head', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zywen and trjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'big'.

Script — the boat piece, then 口 under a small frame.
船 (chuán).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the boat piece, six strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. A long stroke down the left side, falling to the left at the foot. A bar that turns down the right side and hooks to the left. A dot inside, a bar rising to the right across the middle, and a last dot under it.
The right piece, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left, and a bar that turns down and bends to the right at the foot: a small frame. Then 口 under it, three strokes.
6 + 5 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 船 (chuán), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 船 (chuán) have? (Two.) What is the left piece? (The boat piece, six strokes.) What sits at the foot of the right piece? (口.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
开船 (kāichuán) — to set sail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a pier for boats, then the Mandarin for a ship's captain.

You'll want to know: 开船.
开船 — kāichuán — "to set sail".
船长说十点开船 chuánzhǎng shuō shí diǎn kāichuán — The captain says we sail at ten.

The word, taken apart.
开船 (kāichuán) is built from 开 'to start off' and 船 (chuán) 'a boat', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khoj and zywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to open'.
