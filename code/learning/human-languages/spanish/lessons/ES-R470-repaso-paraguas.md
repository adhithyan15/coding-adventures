# (el paraguas, el mango, encontrar, avisar) — from cold

## Warm-up

you would describe, what you cannot do, and what you want the finder to do.

## Grammar Lens: one English verb, two Spanish ones
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-03, ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-04] -->

English says *look for it and tell me*. Spanish makes you choose twice, and
the choices are not the ones you would guess.

| the English | what Spanish splits it into |
|---|---|
| look / find | *buscar* is the effort, **encontrar** the result |
| tell | **avisar** shows; *decir* says |

*Avísame si lo encuentras* holds both splits in five words: let me know — not
order me, not explain to me — if the search actually lands.

That sentence is the whole chapter, and you could not have built it a lesson
ago.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-01, ES-LEX-C470-PARAGUAS-02] -->

Three of the four are older than they look, and each keeps a picture inside.

| word | the picture |
|---|---|
| el paraguas | made **ready** against the waters |
| el mango | the part built for the **hand** |
| encontrar | to come up **against** a thing |

*Avisar* is the fourth, and its picture is **seeing** rather than telling —
which is what made it the gentle verb in the lens above.
