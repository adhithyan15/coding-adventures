Lesson 1 of 5.
температура (temperatúra) — a temperature, a fever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a doctor, then the Russian for a hospital.

You'll want to know: температура.
температура (temperatúra) — "a temperature, a fever".

The word, taken apart.
температура (temperatúra) from Latin temperātūra, "due measure, mixing", from temperāre, "to mix in proportion" — borrowed in modern times, probably through German Temperatur.

Lesson 3 of 5.
отдых (ótdykh) — rest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a temperature, then the Russian for a cough.

You'll want to know: отдых.
отдых (ótdykh) — "rest".

The word, taken apart.
отдых (ótdykh) is от-, "back, away", with дых, "breath", from the verb дышать, "to breathe": rest is getting your breath back. For the root, Vasmer compares Lithuanian dūsėti, "to pant".

Lesson 4 of 5.
голод (gólod) — hunger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cough, then the Russian for rest.

You'll want to know: голод.
голод (gólod) — "hunger".

The word, taken apart.
голод (gólod) from Proto-Slavic goldŭ — inherited. Vasmer links it with Church Slavonic žĭlděti, "to crave", and Sanskrit gṛdhyati, "is greedy". The Church Slavonic shape is глад, "famine", with -ла- for Russian -оло-.

Lesson 5 of 5.
жажда (zházhda) — thirst.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for rest, then the Russian for hunger.

You'll want to know: жажда.
жажда (zházhda) — "thirst". вода answers it.

The word, taken apart.
жажда (zházhda) — taken from Church Slavonic, says Vasmer: -жд- is the Church Slavonic sound where East Slavic has -ж-, and Old Russian had the native жажа. Both go back to Proto-Slavic žędja.
