Mandarin Chinese, chapter 520: Around the House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
家电 (jiādiàn) — household appliance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a snowy landscape, then the Mandarin for to sit in the cool.

You'll want to know: 家电.
家电 — jiādiàn — "household appliance".
我们去年买了一些新家电 wǒmen qùnián mǎi le yìxiē xīn jiādiàn — We bought some new household appliances last year.

The word, taken apart.
家电 (jiādiàn) is built from 家 'home' and 电 'electricity', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kae and denH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the second as the flash when yin and yang clash, lightning; the sense 'electricity' is modern.

Lesson 2 of 5.
开关 (kāiguān) — a light switch; a switch on a machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sit in the cool, then the Mandarin for a household appliance.

You'll want to know: 开关.
开关 — kāiguān — "a light switch; a switch on a machine".
wǒ zhǎo bu dào dēng de kāiguān — I can't find the light switch.

The word, taken apart.
开关 (kāiguān) is built from 开 'to open' and 关 'to close', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khoj and kwaen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second, after an older dictionary, as the bar laid across a door to hold it shut: the thing that opens and closes.

rèshuǐqì — "water heater".
wǒmen de rèshuǐqì yòng le shí nián le — Our water heater has been going for ten years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rèshuǐqì is built from 热 'hot', 水 'water' and qì 'a vessel', all inherited from Middle Chinese, nyet, sywijX and khijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'vessels and dishes'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
nuǎnqì — central heating.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a light switch, then the Mandarin for a water heater.

You'll want to know: nuǎnqì.
nuǎnqì — "central heating".
yǒu nuǎnqì de fángjiān bǐ wàibian nuǎnhuo — A room with heating is warmer than outside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nuǎnqì is built from nuǎn 'warm' and 气 'air', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nwanX and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary gives the first as the same word as another spelling glossed 'the warmth of a fire', and the second 'breath'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
shěngdiàn — to use little electricity; to save electricity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a water heater, then the Mandarin for central heating.

You'll want to know: shěngdiàn.
shěngdiàn — "to use little electricity; to save electricity".
xīn jiādiàn bǐ jiù de shěngdiàn — New appliances use less electricity than old ones.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shěngdiàn is built from shěng 'to spare' and 电 'electricity', both inherited from Middle Chinese, srjaengX and denH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as a government office and, under another reading, 'to examine'; the sense 'to spare, to save' is not given there.
