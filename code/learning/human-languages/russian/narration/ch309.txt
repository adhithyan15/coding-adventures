Russian, chapter 309: Death and mourning.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
похороны (pokhoróny) — a funeral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a degree, then the Russian for a school-leaving certificate.

You'll want to know: похороны.
похороны (pokhoróny) — "a funeral".
На похороны пришло много людей. Na pokhoróny prishló mnógo lyudéy. A lot of people came to the funeral.
Plural only; genitive похорон: на похоронах — at the funeral.

The word, taken apart.
похороны (pokhoróny) is built on хоронить (khoronít) "to bury", an inherited Slavic verb whose older sense was "to keep, to hide away"; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: хоронить.
хоронить (khoronít) — "to bury".
Его похоронили рядом с женой. Yevó pokhoroníli ryádom s zhenóy. He was buried next to his wife.
Imperfective; perfective похоронить (pokhoronít).

The word, taken apart.
хоронить (khoronít) is an inherited Slavic verb whose older sense was "to keep safe, to hide away"; EtymDB lists no parent, and it is usually traced to Proto-Slavic xorniti.

Lesson 3 of 5.
смерть (smert) — death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a funeral, then the Russian for to bury.

You'll want to know: смерть.
смерть (smert) — "death".
После смерти отца он стал хозяином фермы. Pósle smérti ottsá on stal khozyáinom férmy. After his father's death he became the owner of the farm.
Feminine; genitive смерти.

The word, taken apart.
смерть (smert) is inherited through Old East Slavic sŭmĭrtĭ from Proto-Slavic sŭmĭrtĭ (EtymDB), usually explained as sŭ- and the root of умереть "to die", PIE mer-.

Lesson 4 of 5.
вдова (vdová) — a widow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to bury, then the Russian for death.

You'll want to know: вдова.
вдова (vdová) — "a widow".
Вдова жила одна в большом доме. Vdová zhilá odná v bolshóm dóme. The widow lived alone in a big house.
A widower is вдовец.

The word, taken apart.
вдова (vdová) is inherited from Proto-Slavic vĭdova, from PIE h₁widʰéwh₂ "widow" (EtymDB); English widow and Latin vidua are cousins.

The word, taken apart.
наследник (naslédnik) is built on наследовать "to inherit", from на- and след "a track" (EtymDB), with the ending -ник for a person.
