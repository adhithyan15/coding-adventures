Hindi, chapter 322: Swords and Shields.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तलवार (talvār) — a sword.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to clash, then the Hindi for to fight hard.

You'll want to know: तलवार.
तलवार — talvār — "a sword".
राजा ने तलवार उठाई। rājā ne talvār uṭhāī. The king raised his sword.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
तलवार (talvār) goes back to Sanskrit taravāri, "a sword" (CDIAL 5706), which CDIAL gives with a Hindi talwār. It is feminine: तलवार उठाई.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ढाल (ḍhāl) — a shield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to fight hard, then the Hindi for a sword.

You'll want to know: ढाल.
ढाल — ḍhāl — "a shield".
सिपाही के एक हाथ में तलवार और दूसरे में ढाल थी। sipāhī ke ek hāth meṁ talvār aur dūsre meṁ ḍhāl thī. The soldier had a sword in one hand and a shield in the other.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ढाल (ḍhāl) goes back to Sanskrit ḍhāla, "a shield" (CDIAL 5583). CDIAL marks the Hindi word masculine; standard Hindi today treats it as feminine.

Lesson 4 of 5.
तीर (tīr) — an arrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a shield, then the Hindi for a spear.

You'll want to know: तीर.
तीर — tīr — "an arrow".
उसने एक ही तीर से फल गिराया। usne ek hī tīr se phal girāyā. He brought down the fruit with a single arrow.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
तीर (tīr) is usually traced to Persian tīr, "an arrow". The bow is धनुष; तीर चलाना is to shoot an arrow.

Lesson 5 of 5.
प्रायः (prāyaḥ) — for the most part; usually.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a spear, then the Hindi for an arrow.

You'll want to know: प्रायः.
प्रायः — prāyaḥ — "for the most part; usually".
उस समय युद्ध प्रायः तलवार और भाले से होते थे। us samay yuddh prāyaḥ talvār aur bhāle se hote the. In those days wars were mostly fought with swords and spears.
Formal and written; speech says अक्सर.

The word, taken apart.
प्रायः (prāyaḥ) is Sanskrit prāyas, "for the most part, mostly" (Monier-Williams), and keeps the final sign ः. It belongs to writing and to formal talk.
