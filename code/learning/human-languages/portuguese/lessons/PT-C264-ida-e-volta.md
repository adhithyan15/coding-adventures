# ida e volta — the going and the coming back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C54-COISAS3-04, PT-LEX-C83-QUERER83-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "a ticket". (*Um bilhete.*) Say "I would like". (*Gostaria.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Now you are standing at a ticket window.

## You'll want to know: ida e volta, só de ida
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-IDA-E-VOLTA-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-03, PT-LEX-IR-02] -->

**ida e volta** — "there and back": a return ticket.
**só de ida** — "one way", the going only.

> *Um bilhete de ida e volta.* — A return ticket.
> *Só de ida.* — One way only.

**a ida** is "the going", from **ir**; **a volta** is "the coming back",
from your **voltar**. **só** means "only". Brazil says *passagem* where Portugal
says *bilhete*, and *trem* for *comboio*; *ida e volta* is the same on both
sides of the ocean.

## Grammar Lens: queria um bilhete para…
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-QUERIA-UM-BILHETE-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C83-QUERER83-01, PT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFEITO-01, PT-LEX-C172-VERBOS172-04, PT-GRAMMAR-HORA-04] -->

The whole request takes one frame:

> **Queria um bilhete para** Lisboa, **de ida e volta**.
> — I'd like a return ticket to Lisbon.

**queria** is the imperfeito of **querer**, and at a
counter in Portugal it is the everyday polite request: "I was wanting" softens
into "I'd like". *Gostaria* is more formal. Most cities take no article, *para Lisboa*, but a
few keep one: *para o Porto*.

And what comes back at you:

> — *Ida e volta?* — *Sim, ida e volta.* / *Não, só de ida.*
> — *A que horas parte o comboio?* — *Às dez.*

*Às* is *a* plus *as*, "at the", before the hour.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ir** is inherited from Latin *īre*, "to go" (EtymDB 2.1), and **ida** is
built on its participle *ido*, made a feminine noun, "the going"; EtymDB 2.1
has no row for the noun itself. **volta** comes from **voltar**, inherited
from spoken Latin *voltāre*, "to turn round" (EtymDB 2.1), from Latin
*volūtāre*, "to roll about" (Lewis & Short, under *voluto*). A return is a
turning round. **só** is inherited from Latin *sōlus*, "alone" (EtymDB 2.1).
