German, chapter 347: Zufall oder nicht?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Zufall — the chance, the coincidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for on purpose, then the German for the occasion.

You'll want to know: der Zufall.
der Zufall — "the chance, the coincidence".
Was für ein Zufall! Wir sind im selben Hotel. What a coincidence! We are in the same hotel.
Plural: die Zufälle. Durch Zufall — by chance.

The word, taken apart.
Zufall is built from zu "to" and Fall "fall, case", from Old High German val (EtymDB): what falls to you. It is usually explained as a rendering of Latin accidēns, from accidere "to fall upon, to happen" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
verhindern — to prevent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the chance, then the German for by chance.

You'll want to know: verhindern.
verhindern — "to prevent".
Der Regen hat das Spiel nicht verhindert. The rain did not prevent the game.
Inseparable: er verhindert, er hat verhindert. Verhindert sein — to be unable to come.

The word, taken apart.
verhindern is built with ver- on hindern "to hinder", a verb from the family of hinter "behind": you hold a thing back. EtymDB has no history for the verbs; Proto-Germanic hinder "behind" is the usual source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
weshalb — why, for what reason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to prevent, then the German for to avoid.

You'll want to know: weshalb.
weshalb — "why, for what reason".
Niemand weiß, weshalb der Laden heute geschlossen ist. Nobody knows why the shop is closed today.
A question word like warum. In an indirect question it sends the verb to the end: …, weshalb er geschlossen ist.

The word, taken apart.
weshalb joins wes, the old genitive of was "what", to halb in its old sense "on the side of, because of": "because of what". EtymDB has no entry, so this follows the usual account.
