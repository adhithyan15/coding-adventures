Lesson 1 of 2.
భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ) — "a meal," a word this book named before it ever taught it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Tea, then milk. This chapter's last word is not a drink — it is the meal you would sit down to with both.

You'll want to know: భోజనం.
భోజనం — bhōjanaṁ — a meal.
భోజనం చేద్దాం. — bhōjanaṁ cēddāṁ. — "Let's eat" (literally, "let's do a meal").
The tinu lesson already used this exact word, in passing, as the polite counterpart of native తిండి — without ever teaching it properly. This lesson finally does.

The word, taken apart - closing a loop left open earlier.
భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ) is Sanskrit भोजन, built on भुज् (bhuj), "to enjoy, to partake of, to eat." It sits above native తిండి the same way దయచేసి sits above the everyday -అండి ending: a formal Sanskrit layer over a plain Dravidian floor. కన్ను, "eye," was a word this book had already used once, unglossed, inside an etymology note — this lesson does the very same thing for భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ), which was named earlier without ever being taught.
If a meal runs late, Telugu reaches for the apology word taught earlier: భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ) ఆలస్యం అయింది, క్షమించండి — "the meal is late, sorry" — the same respectful -అండి ending that first met you on దయచేసి's own everyday alternative.

Lesson 2 of 2.
The table words, second pass — and how to ask for them.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Three words, from memory and without the page: tea, milk, and the word for a meal.

You'll want to know: three words, three different journeys.
The three sit together on a table and came from three different places:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
word: టీ ṭī. where it came from: borrowed, and by sea rather than by the overland road.
word: పాలు pālu. where it came from: the family's own — the whole Dravidian group shares it.
word: భోజనం bhōjanaṁ. where it came from: Sanskrit, and formal in tone because of it.
భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ) is the one to weigh. It is not the everyday word for food; it is the polite noun for a meal, the sit-down kind, and its tone is the reason it belongs beside please rather than beside a shopping list.

Grammar Lens: two ways to put "please" on a request.
Asking for any of the three uses what దయచేసి and the ‑ండి ending settled:
The ‑ండి ending on the verb is where ordinary Telugu keeps please. A request with it is already polite.
దయచేసి is the extra warmth on top — formal, or emphatic, and not the everyday word English speakers expect it to be.
So దయచేసి టీ is a warm, slightly formal ask, and the same request made with a ‑ండి verb and no దయచేసి is the ordinary polite one. Neither is rude.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of the three words is the family's own? (పాలు.) Which arrived from Sanskrit, and what does that do to its tone? (భోజనం (bhōjanaṁ) — it makes it formal.) Where does everyday Telugu keep please? (In the ‑ండి ending on the verb.) And what is దయచేసి for? (Extra warmth, or formality, on top of an ending that is already polite.)
