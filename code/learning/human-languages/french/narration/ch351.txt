French, chapter 351: Comment réagir.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
réagir — to react.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to arouse, then the French for the motive.

You'll want to know: réagir.
réagir — "to react".
Comment as-tu réagi quand tu as appris la nouvelle? How did you react when you heard the news?
Réagir à quelque chose. A -ir verb like finir.

The word, taken apart.
réagir is built from ré- "back" and agir "to act", which comes from Latin agere "to do, to drive" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

You'll want to know: la réaction.
la réaction — "the reaction".

The word, taken apart.
réaction comes from Medieval Latin reactiō (EtymDB), built from re- "back" and āctiō "a doing", from agere "to do" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: le prétexte.
le prétexte — "the pretext".
Il a trouvé un prétexte pour partir plus tôt. He found a pretext to leave earlier.
Sous prétexte que — on the pretext that.

The word, taken apart.
prétexte is a learned borrowing from Latin praetextus "an outward show, a pretext" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from praetexere "to weave in front, to cover", built on texere "to weave".

Lesson 4 of 5.
justifier — to justify.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reaction, then the French for the pretext.

You'll want to know: justifier.
justifier — "to justify".
Elle a justifié sa décision devant l'équipe. She justified her decision in front of the team.
Se justifier — to explain yourself.

The word, taken apart.
justifier comes from Late Latin iūstificāre "to justify" (EtymDB), built on iūstus "right, fair" (Lewis & Short) and -ficāre "to make".
