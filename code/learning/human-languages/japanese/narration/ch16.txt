Last chapter bought two signs for a count of ten. This chapter buys five, for a count of ten that Japanese runs beside the one you already have.

Script — the shape.
ひ — hi.
One stroke, and the KanjiVG stroke data for this sign holds exactly one path: start high on the left, run a short way to the right, then sweep down and far to the left in a long shallow curve and finish by turning up. The pen does not lift once.
Compare ろ, which you wrote five lessons ago: also one stroke, but ろ closes its loop and ひ never closes at all.

Guided Practice.
Trace ひ (hi) once and copy it once, without lifting.
Hide it; write it after ten seconds.
Write ろ and ひ (hi) side by side: one closes, one does not.

Wrap-up Recall.

You'll Want to Know — hear and say.
ひとつ (hitotsu) — hi-to-tsu — one thing.
Three beats, three signs: ひ (hi) from last lesson, と and つ long since yours.
You already have a word for one. It is いち, and it is borrowed. ひとつ (hitotsu) is the word Japanese had before it borrowed anything, and the two are not interchangeable:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: いち. what it is for: the number itself — one, counting aloud, a house number.
word: ひとつ (hitotsu). what it is for: one thing — a thing you are pointing at.
Wiktionary's usage note for ひとつ (hitotsu) is one sentence and it is the sentence this chapter turns on: it is used when the items counted have no specific counter category. Hold that. It will be the whole of the next chapter.

Guided Practice — read, copy, recall.
Write ひとつ. Say ichi, then hitotsu, and hear that they share nothing — not a beat, not a sound.

Wrap-up Recall.

いちど is worth stopping on. Its own lesson said do counts an occurrence — which is to say いちど is a number with a counter behind it, and you have been saying it since chapter nine without anyone calling it that. Two chapters from now that sentence is the whole point.

Script — the shape.
ふ — fu.
Four separate strokes, in this order, as KanjiVG records them: a short tick at the top; then the body, starting below it and curving down and to the left; then a small mark low on the left; then a small mark low on the right.
Everything this book has taught you so far has been one, two or three strokes. This is the first sign with four, and the last two are easy to run together by accident. They are separate.

You'll Want to Know — hear and say.
ふたつ (futatsu) — fu-ta-tsu — two things.
Three signs, and the only new one arrived last lesson.
Now hear the two words together: ひとつ (hitotsu), ふたつ (futatsu). Both end in つ, and the part in front changes — hito-, futa-. That つ is not decoration. It is the piece doing the counting, and every word in this chapter but the last one carries it.
に is two. ふたつ (futatsu) is two things. Same relation as いち and ひとつ (hitotsu), and again the two words share nothing at all.

みっつ (mittsu) — mi-t-tsu — three things.
Every sign is already yours: み, the small っ, and つ.
The small っ is the sign you met in ゆっくり: it holds the mouth shut for one beat. みっつ (mittsu) is three beats, and the middle one is silence.
さん is three. みっつ (mittsu) is three things.

You'll Want to Know — hear and say.
よっつ (yottsu) — yo-t-tsu — four things.
No new sign. よ, the small っ, つ.

Grammar Lens — the seam, from the other side.
Chapter fourteen taught よん and said something that was left hanging: that split is coming back; it is the same seam the counters run along. Here it is.
よん is a native word standing inside the borrowed count. よっつ (yottsu) is that same native word in the count it belongs to. They begin with the same beat, and that is not an accident — it is one word appearing twice:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
number: one. borrowed: いち. native: ひと-.
number: two. borrowed: に. native: ふた-.
number: three. borrowed: さん. native: み-.
number: four. borrowed: shi. native: よ-.
Every other line of the borrowed column is genuinely borrowed. At four, the native word came across into the borrowed count and stayed — which is why the number you actually say for four is よん and not shi. That is what chapter fourteen was pointing at, and you can now see both halves of it at once.

Guided Practice — read, copy, recall.
Write よっつ. Say yon, then yottsu, and hear the shared beat. Then say shi and hear that it has none.

Wrap-up Recall.

Sources: Wiktionary: yottsu.

いつつ (itsutsu) — i-tsu-tsu — five things.
Three signs, and two of them are the same one. い, つ, つ.
A warning worth one sentence: いつつ (itsutsu) opens on the same beat as いち, and the two have nothing to do with each other. ご is five; いつつ (itsutsu) is five things; いち is one. The ear will try to pair the wrong two.
