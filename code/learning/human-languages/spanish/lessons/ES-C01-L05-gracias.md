# gracias — thanks, grace, and a whole family you already know

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] If one word shows off the entire method of this book, it's this
one. *Gracias* is the tip of an enormous English family — by the end of this
you'll see it in a dozen words you already use.

## Sounds you'll need

- `soft-c` — the **c** before *i* is soft: *GRA-syas* in Latin America, a
  soft *th* (*GRA-thyas*) in much of Spain. Either is correct.
- Stress on the first syllable: *GRA-cias* (ends in *s*, second-to-last
  syllable — and it's only two, so: first).

## The word, taken apart

**gracias** — thanks. Literally it's the plural of *gracia*, "grace/favor" —
so you're saying "graces," good things, favors. (Same plural-as-warmth
instinct as *buenos días*.)

Root: Latin **gratia** ("favor, kindness, thanks, grace"), from **gratus**
("pleasing, grateful"). Now watch the family light up — every one of these is
a cousin of *gracias*:

- **grace** — the direct English inheritance of *gratia*.
- **grat**itude, **grate**ful — the feeling of thanks.
- **grat**uity — a tip, a thanks-gift; and **grat**is / **grat**uitous —
  "for free," given as a favor.
- in**grat**e — *in-* ("not") + *grat*, someone *not* grateful. (There's your
  first prefix in action: stick *in-* on the front to negate it.)
- con**grat**ulate — *con-* ("with") + *gratulari* ("show joy"), to share in
  someone's good fortune.
- even **agree** — through French *à gré*, "to (one's) pleasure," from the
  same *gratum*.

So *gracias* isn't a foreign word to memorize. It's the Spanish member of a
family whose English members — *grace, gratitude, gratuity, ingrate,
congratulate* — you've known your whole life.

## Why it's said this way

The plural again: *gracias*, "graces," many good favors — warmer than a flat
singular. There's also a fuller, more formal version worth knowing: *muchas
gracias* ("many thanks"), and the very formal *se lo agradezco* ("I thank you
for it") — but *gracias* alone is the everyday workhorse, usable with anyone,
in any register. Unlike the greetings, it carries no formal/informal split;
gratitude is gratitude.
