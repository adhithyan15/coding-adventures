Portuguese, chapter 440: A morada.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
a morada — the address (where someone lives).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the south-west, then the Portuguese for the landmark.

You'll want to know: a morada.
a morada — "the address (where someone lives)".
Escrevi a morada no envelope. I wrote the address on the envelope.
In Brazil: o endereço, which Portugal uses for e-mail: o endereço eletrónico.

The word, taken apart.
morada is built on morar 'to live, to dwell', which is inherited from Latin morārī 'to linger, to stay' (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.

You'll want to know: a periferia.
a periferia — "the outskirts, the edge".
Muitas famílias vivem na periferia da cidade. Many families live on the outskirts of the city.
Also the edge of a circle.

The word, taken apart.
periferia is a learned borrowing, through Late Latin, from Greek periphéreia 'circumference', from perí 'around' and phérein 'to carry' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
a freguesia — the parish (local district).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the address, then the Portuguese for the outskirts.

You'll want to know: a freguesia.
a freguesia — "the parish (local district)".
A minha avó mora numa freguesia pequena. My grandmother lives in a small parish.
The smallest unit of local government in Portugal, run by a junta de freguesia.

The word, taken apart.
freguesia is built on freguês 'parishioner, customer' with -ia (EtymDB); freguês goes back to Latin fīlius ecclēsiae 'son of the church'.

You'll want to know: o intercomunicador.
o intercomunicador — "the entry phone, the intercom".
Toquei no intercomunicador, mas ninguém respondeu. I rang the entry phone, but nobody answered.
Many people simply say a campainha.

The word, taken apart.
intercomunicador is built from inter- 'between' and comunicar, a learned borrowing from Latin commūnicāre 'to share' (Lewis & Short).
