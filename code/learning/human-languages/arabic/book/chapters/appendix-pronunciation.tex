\chapter*{Pronunciation \& Script --- a reference}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Pronunciation \& script (reference)}
\markboth{Pronunciation}{Pronunciation}

Not to be read before the lessons --- they teach reading through real words,
each letter introduced as a word needs it. This page just gathers the system.
Arabic appears both in script (\ar{مرحبا}) and in transliteration
(\emph{mar\d{h}aban}), a dot marking the heavy/throaty consonants.

\section*{The script, in one breath}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Right to left}, and letters \textbf{connect} --- each has up
    to four shapes (isolated/initial/medial/final). Met inside real words, not
    drilled.
  \item Short vowels (\emph{a i u}) are usually left unwritten; long vowels
    (\emph{\=a \=\i\ \=u}) are letters (\ar{ا و ي}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Consonants with no English equivalent}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \ar{ح} (\d{h}): a heavy \emph{h} from deep in the throat (vs.\ light
    \ar{ه}).
  \item \ar{خ} (kh): a throat-scrape = Scottish \emph{loch}, \textbf{Spanish
    \emph{j}}.
  \item \ar{ع} (\emph{\`{}}, ``\emph{ayn}''): a voiced throat-tightening; no
    English match.
  \item \ar{ء} (\emph{\'{}}, ``hamza''): a glottal stop --- the catch in
    ``uh-oh.''
  \item \ar{ق} (q): a \emph{k} made far back; \ar{غ} (gh): a gargled \emph{g}.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Emphatic (``heavy'') consonants}

\ar{ص ض ط ظ} (\d{s}, \d{d}, \d{t}, \d{z}): heavier, deeper versions of
\emph{s, d, t, dh}. They change the word: \ar{ص} \d{s} $\neq$ \ar{س} \emph{s}.

\section*{The article and sun/moon letters}

\ar{ال} (\emph{al-}, ``the'') attaches to its noun. Before a \textbf{sun
letter} the \emph{l} assimilates and the letter doubles: \emph{al-sal\=am}
$\to$ \emph{as-sal\=am}. Before a \textbf{moon letter} it stays:
\emph{al-khayr}. The sun rule is fossilized in Spanish \emph{az\'{u}car} (from
\emph{as-sukkar}).

\section*{The root system}

Most words are built on a \textbf{three-consonant root} carrying a core
meaning, poured into vowel \textbf{patterns}: \emph{k-t-b} (writing) $\to$
\emph{kit\=ab} (book), \emph{k\=atib} (writer), \emph{maktab} (office). See the
first lesson.
