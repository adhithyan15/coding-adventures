## You'll want to know: dǎjià
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C312-PRISON312-03]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎjià* — "to fight; to get into a fistfight".

> *liǎng ge rén zhèngzài ménkǒu dǎjià* — Two people were fighting at the door.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎjià* is built from 打 'to hit' and *jià* 'a frame', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *kaeH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'a roof frame'; how the pair came to mean a fight is not settled, and dǎ is an irregular outcome of *taengX*.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for telling what happened.
