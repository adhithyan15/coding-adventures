## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C301-ASK-01]; assesses=[] -->

**la cuerda** — "the rope," "the cord," "the string."

Greek **khordē** meant a gut — an intestine — and then the string made out of one,
because that is what strings were made of. Latin borrowed it as **chorda**, and
the stressed *o* did what it always does under stress in Spanish: it cracked open
into *ue*. So *chorda* became *cuerda*, by the same road as *somnus* to *sueño*.

English kept the gut in a whole shelf of words. A **cord** is the plain rope. A
**chord** is what several strings sound together. A **cordon** is a rope drawn
round a place to keep people out. And a **harpsichord** is, quite literally, a
harp made of gut strings.

So this is not a rough word for a rough object. It is a musical instrument's word
that ended up in the shed.
