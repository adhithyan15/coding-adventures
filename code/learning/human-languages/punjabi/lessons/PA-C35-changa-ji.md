# ਚੰਗਾ ਜੀ — a goodbye made of nothing new

## You'll want to know first — one phrase
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-CHANGA-JI]; assesses=[] -->

> **ਚੰਗਾ ਜੀ** — *changā jī* — **right then; bye**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-CHANGA-01, PA-SCRIPT-JA-01] -->

**ਚੰਗਾ** was assembled two chapters ago; **ਜੀ** has been writable since this
run of chapters opened. Nothing here is a new shape: **ਚੰਗਾ ਜੀ**.

## The word, taken apart — agreement doing the work of parting
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-CHANGA-JI, PA-LEX-CHANGA, PA-LEX-JI, PA-ETYMON-CHANGA-UNSETTLED] -->

This phrase adds no vocabulary. Its whole content is what happens when two known
words are set in that order at the end of a conversation.

*Changā* says good — the word whose trail stops at *caṅga* and is argued no
further. **ਜੀ** adds the person. Together, at the door, they mean *right
then* — the exchange is agreed, so it can end.

The two farewells you learned first both promise. *Phir milāṁge* promises another meeting;
*rabb rākhā* asks God to keep the other person. *Changā jī* promises nothing.
It closes by agreeing, which is why it fits a shopkeeper, a neighbour and a
phone call, and why it is the parting a learner will hear most.

English does the same with *all right then* and *right you are*. A word for
agreement, spoken at the end, becomes a way out.

## Writing: assemble ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-SCRIPT-JI-AAIAN-NU-01]; assesses=[PA-SCRIPT-INDEPENDENT-AA-01, PA-SCRIPT-DULAINKAR-01, PA-SCRIPT-SIHARI-01, PA-SCRIPT-NA-01, PA-SCRIPT-TIPPI-WRITING-01, PA-LEX-JI-AAIAN-NU, PA-SCRIPT-SALAAM-01, PA-SCRIPT-THIK-THAK-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

The standalone long *ā* and the hook that hangs below a letter have both been
traced, so the welcome from the last chapter can be written out.

Three words. **ਜੀ**. Then **ਆਇਆਂ**: the standalone **ਆ**, the standalone
**ਇ**, **ਆ** again, and the dot **ਂ** above. Then **ਨੂੰ**: **ਨ**, the hook
below, the tippi above.

Copy the whole line once with the model visible. Cover it, write it again, and
compare only the second word. Then add **ਸਲਾਮ** and **ਠੀਕ-ਠਾਕ** underneath from
memory, so neither goes cold.
