Spanish, chapter 408: Doctor, Pills and Tiredness.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
el doctor / la doctora — the teacher-word at the clinic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall el médico, the physician. Spanish has another word you will hear when making an appointment: el doctor or la doctora.

The word, taken apart.
Medieval Latin doctor meant “teacher,” from docēre, “to teach.” English kept the same academic word. Spanish also uses doctor for a physician and as a form of address. That does not erase the older academic meaning: context tells you whether the doctor holds a doctorate or is treating a patient.

Grammar Lens: name the person.
The ending follows the person: el doctor, la doctora. To ask for an appointment, use the frame pedir cita con el doctor. Médico remains the more specific profession-word; doctor is the word the mock message uses.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el doctor]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: la doctora]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: una cita con el doctor]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Doctor? (El doctor; la doctora.)
Source: DLE: doctor.


Lesson 2 of 4.
la pastilla — a small formed portion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have an appointment with el doctor. The next message names what you take: la pastilla.

The word, taken apart.
Pastilla belongs transparently with pasta and the small-form ending -illa: a little formed portion. The older physical idea survives in a bar of soap or a mint. In the clinic, una pastilla is a small medicinal tablet.
Do not overtranslate the shape. In this health frame, pastilla means the pill or tablet, not pasta to eat.
