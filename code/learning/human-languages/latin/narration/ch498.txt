Latin, chapter 498: Hope, despair and trust.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dēspērō, dēspērāre — to despair (of).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for annoyance, then the Latin for to torment.

You'll want to know: dēspērō, dēspērāre.
dēspērō, dēspērāre — "to despair (of)".
Medicus dē salūte aegrī dēspērat. The doctor despairs of the patient's recovery.
dēspērō, dēspērāre, dēspērāvī, dēspērātum: dē- 'away from' + spērō 'to hope'. With dē and the ablative, or the accusative. English despair, desperate.

The word, taken apart.
dēspērō is built on dē- "away from" and spērō "to hope", from spēs "hope" (Lewis & Short). English despair and desperate come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
cōnfīdō, cōnfīdere — to trust, rely on; to be confident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to despair, then the Latin for despair.

You'll want to know: cōnfīdō, cōnfīdere.
cōnfīdō, cōnfīdere — "to trust, rely on; to be confident".
Amīcō meō omnīnō cōnfīdō. I trust my friend completely.
Semi-deponent: cōnfīdō, cōnfīdere, cōnfīsus sum, with the dative of a person. English confide, confident.

The word, taken apart.
cōnfīdō is built on con- and fīdō "to trust" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB traces fīdō through Proto-Italic to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to trust". English confide and confident come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
diffīdō, diffīdere — to distrust; to lose hope.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for despair, then the Latin for to trust.

You'll want to know: diffīdō, diffīdere.
diffīdō, diffīdere — "to distrust; to lose hope".
Mercātor nautīs diffīdit. The merchant distrusts the sailors.
Semi-deponent: diffīdō, diffīdere, diffīsus sum, with the dative. The opposite of cōnfīdō. English diffident.

The word, taken apart.
diffīdō is built on dis- "apart, not" and fīdō "to trust" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English diffident comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
crēdulus, crēdula, crēdulum — trusting, credulous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to trust, then the Latin for to distrust.

You'll want to know: crēdulus, crēdula, crēdulum.
crēdulus, crēdula, crēdulum — "trusting, credulous".
Puer crēdulus omnia crēdit, quae audit. The trusting boy believes everything he hears.
Built on crēdō — to believe. English credulous.

The word, taken apart.
crēdulus is built on crēdō "to believe" with -ulus (Lewis & Short; EtymDB), and EtymDB traces crēdō to a Proto-Indo-European phrase meaning "to put one’s heart". English credulous comes from it.
