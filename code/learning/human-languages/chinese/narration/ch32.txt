Mandarin Chinese, chapter 32: Mum and Dad.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
妈妈 (māma) — mum; mother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for we, then the Mandarin for you all.

You'll want to know: 妈妈.
妈妈 — māma — "mum; mother".
我妈妈很好 wǒ māma hěn hǎo — My mum is well.
The new character, in pieces: 妈 (mā) — 女, the woman piece, on the left, and on the right a three-stroke piece read mǎ, the word for horse, which gives the sound as it does in 吗. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
妈妈 (māma) is written with a character the Guangyun records as muX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'mother'. The modern sound is not what muX would regularly become, so whether mā is inherited from it, or is a nursery word written with this character, is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
妈 (mā) — the woman piece, then a horse.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the woman piece, then a horse.
妈 (mā).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 女, three strokes, as you wrote it alone, but narrow: the fold that drops and turns, the long left-falling stroke, and last the horizontal, which here rises to the right so it does not cross into the next piece.
The right piece, the horse, three strokes. A horizontal that turns down. A vertical that folds right, folds down again and hooks at the foot. Then a rising stroke across the bottom.
3 + 3 = six. The same horse stands on the right of 吗, which is why the two sound alike.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 妈 (mā)? (Two — 女, then the horse.) What does the last stroke of 女 do here? (It rises to the right.) Which character you know has the same right piece? (吗.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
bàba — dad; father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for you all, then the Mandarin for mum.

You'll want to know: bàba.
bàba — "dad; father".
wǒ bàba xǐhuan kàn shū — My dad likes reading.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàba is inherited from Middle Chinese baX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where the character is glossed 'father'. Spoken, it is doubled, like the word for mum.

Lesson 4 of 6.

You'll want to know: gēge.
gēge — "elder brother".
wǒ yǒu yí ge gēge — I've got an older brother.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gēge is inherited from Middle Chinese ka (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary notes that the character was once written for 'song', and that in its own day people used it to call an elder brother.

The word, taken apart.
jiějie is inherited from Middle Chinese tsjaeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where it is glossed as the word one people used for mother. How it came to mean elder sister is not settled.
