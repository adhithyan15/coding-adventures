Lesson 1 of 6.
o escândalo — the scandal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the denial, then the Portuguese for the spokesperson.

You'll want to know: o escândalo.
o escândalo — "the scandal".
O escândalo saiu em todos os jornais. The scandal was in all the papers.
Que escândalo! — how shocking!

The word, taken apart.
escândalo is a learned borrowing from Latin scandalum (EtymDB), from Greek skándalon "a trap, a stumbling block".

a crónica — "the newspaper piece, a short personal essay; the chronicle".
Leio a crónica dela todos os domingos. I read her piece in the paper every Sunday.

crónica is a learned borrowing from Latin chronica "a chronicle" (EtymDB), from Greek khronikós "of time", built on khrónos "time".

Lesson 5 of 6.
o cronista — the columnist; the chronicler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the editorial, then the Portuguese for the newspaper piece.

You'll want to know: o cronista.
o cronista — "the columnist; the chronicler".
O cronista escreve sobre a vida na cidade. The columnist writes about city life.
The same form for a woman: a cronista. In old times, the writer of a kingdom’s history.

The word, taken apart.
cronista is built on crónica "a chronicle, a newspaper piece" with -ista for the person; crónica is a learned borrowing from Latin chronica, from Greek khronikós "of time" (EtymDB).
