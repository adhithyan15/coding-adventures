5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fallō, fallere — to deceive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to suspect, then the Latin for to press.

You'll want to know: fallō, fallere.
fallō, fallere — "to deceive".

The word, taken apart.
fallō has no sure origin; Greek sphallō, "to trip up" (Lewis & Short), is often compared, and EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed root ghwel-, "to deceive".

Lesson 3 of 5.
condō, condere — to found; to hide away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to deceive, then the Latin for to endure.

You'll want to know: condō, condere.
condō, condere — "to found; to hide away".

The word, taken apart.
condō is con-, "together", with -dō, "to put" (Lewis & Short): to put together, to found, or to put away. This -dō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dheh1-, "to put, to place" (EtymDB 2.1), and merged in form with dō, "to give".

Lesson 4 of 5.
creō, creāre — to create, to elect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to endure, then the Latin for to found; to hide away.

You'll want to know: creō, creāre.
creō, creāre — "to create, to elect".

The word, taken apart.
creō is inherited from Proto-Italic krēāō, "to make grow", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European ker-, "to grow" (EtymDB 2.1); crēscō, "to grow", is its partner. English create comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
crēdō, crēdere — to believe, to trust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to found; to hide away, then the Latin for to create.

You'll want to know: crēdō, crēdere.
crēdō, crēdere — "to believe, to trust".

The word, taken apart.
crēdō is inherited, usually traced to a Proto-Indo-European phrase kred dheh1-, "to put one's heart" (EtymDB 2.1), from kerd-, "heart", and dheh1-, "to put".
