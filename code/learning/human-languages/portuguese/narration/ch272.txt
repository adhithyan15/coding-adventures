Portuguese, chapter 272: Quanto tempo durou.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
durar — to last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the event, then the Portuguese for the incident.

You'll want to know: durar.
durar — "to last".
A viagem durou cinco horas. The journey lasted five hours.
Durar muito — to last a long time; durar pouco — not to last long.

The word, taken apart.
durar is inherited from Latin dūrāre "to harden, to hold out, to last" (Lewis & Short), built on dūrus "hard".

Lesson 3 of 5.
o instante — the instant, the moment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to last, then the Portuguese for to prolong.

You'll want to know: o instante.
o instante — "the instant, the moment".
Num instante, o céu ficou escuro. In an instant the sky went dark.
Um instante! — one moment!

The word, taken apart.
instante is a learned borrowing from Latin īnstāns "pressing, present" (EtymDB), from īnstāre "to stand upon, to press on", built on stāre "to stand".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o período — the period, the stretch of time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to prolong, then the Portuguese for the instant.

You'll want to know: o período.
o período — "the period, the stretch of time".
Vivi numa aldeia durante um período curto. I lived in a village for a short period.
At school, a term can also be um período.

The word, taken apart.
período is a learned borrowing from Latin periodus, from Greek períodos "a going round, a cycle" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
imediatamente — immediately, at once.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the instant, then the Portuguese for the period.

You'll want to know: imediatamente.
imediatamente — "immediately, at once".
O médico chegou imediatamente. The doctor arrived immediately.
Já is the short, everyday way to say it.

The word, taken apart.
imediatamente is built on imediato, a learned word from Late Latin immediātus "with nothing in between", from in- "not" and medius "middle" (Lewis & Short).
