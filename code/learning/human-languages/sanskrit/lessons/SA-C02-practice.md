# Chapter 2 — the whole exchange

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NAMA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] No new words. Two people meet, and every word in the exchange is one
you already have.

## The exchange

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-MY-NAME-IS, SA-PATTERN-WHATS-YOUR-NAME, SA-LEX-ANANDAH] -->

[PAUSE 1s each]
- [YOU SAY: *mama nāma mīrā asti.* — "My name is Mira."]
- [YOU SAY: *tava nāma kim?* — "What's your name?"]
- [YOU SAY: *mama nāma aruṇaḥ asti.* — "My name is Arun."]
- [YOU SAY: *bhavantaṁ dṛṣṭvā ānandaḥ.* — "Pleased to meet you."]

## How to answer: the atoms

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NAMA, SA-LEX-MAMA, SA-LEX-ASTI, SA-LEX-KIM, SA-GRAMMAR-COPULA-IS-KEPT] -->

[PAUSE 1s each]
- [YOU SAY: name, my, is, what (*nāma*, *mama*, *asti*, *kim*)]
- [YOU SAY: where the verb sits in the sentence (last)]
- [YOU SAY: whether Sanskrit drops the "is" (no — it keeps an explicit copula)]

## Guided Practice — the two "you"s and the roots

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-BHAVAN, SA-GRAMMAR-POLITENESS-BY-PERSON, SA-LEX-TVAM, SA-ETYMON-TVAM-THOU, SA-GRAMMAR-RESPECTFUL-QUESTION, SA-ETYMON-NAMA-NAME, SA-ETYMON-MAMA-GENITIVE, SA-ETYMON-AS-TO-BE, SA-ETYMON-KIM-INTERROGATIVE, SA-ETYMON-ANANDA-JOY] -->

[PAUSE 1s each]
- [YOU SAY: both words for "you" (*bhavān*, respectful, "your honour"; *tvam*, familiar, cousin of **thou**)]
- [YOU SAY: why *bhavān* takes a third-person verb (it speaks *about* the person)]
- [YOU SAY: "what's your name?" to a respected stranger (*bhavataḥ nāma kim?*)]
- [YOU SAY: the PIE roots of *nāma*, *asti* and *kim* (*\*h₃nomn-*; *\*h₁es-*; *\*kʷo-*)]
- [YOU SAY: what *mama* is the genitive of (*aham*, "I")]
- [YOU SAY: what *ānandaḥ* is built from (*ā-* "fully" + *nand* "to rejoice")]

## Script — the chapter's four shapes

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-201, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-04, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-202, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-109] -->

> भ · ◌ि · आ · ◌ः

- [YOU POINT: *ā*, then *bha*, then the *i*-sign, then *ḥ* — out of their printed order]
- [YOU SAY: what each does (**आ**, *ā* opening a word; **भ**, *bha*, its *a* built in; **◌ि**, puts *i* on a consonant and takes its *a* off; **◌ः**, a soft breathed *ḥ* closing a syllable)]

## What you've built: every atom is a source

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-HISTORY-CH2-SOURCE-WORDS]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word in that exchange is an *ancestor*, not a borrowing:

- *nāma* → English **name**, Latin *nōmen*
- *mama* → English **my**, **me**
- *asti* → English **is**, Latin *est*, Hindi *hai*
- *kim* → English **what**, Latin *quid*

And Sanskrit keeps the copula that its Dravidian neighbours drop. Next chapter:
*bhavān katham asti?* — "how are you?"

## Wrap-up Recall

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-HISTORY-CH2-SOURCE-WORDS, SA-PATTERN-WHATS-YOUR-NAME] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Give your name in Sanskrit, ask for someone else's, and name two
English words this chapter's vocabulary is the ancestor of. (*Mama nāma … asti;
tava nāma kim?*; any two of **name**, **my**, **is**, **what**.)
