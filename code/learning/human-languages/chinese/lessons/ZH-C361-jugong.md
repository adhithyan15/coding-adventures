## You'll want to know: jūgōng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C361-GUEST361-03]; assesses=[] -->

*jūgōng* — "to bow".

> *yǎnchū yǐhòu yǎnyuán yìqǐ jūgōng* — After the show, the actors bow together.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jūgōng* is built from *jū* and *gōng*, both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kjuwk* and *kjuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to examine closely' and 'to rear', and the second 'the body'. How the pair came to mean bowing is not settled.
