6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
さそう (sasou) — to invite; to ask out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for disliked, then the Japanese for to get tired of.

You'll want to know: さそう.
さそう — sasou — "to invite; to ask out".
せんしゅう ともだちを えいがに さそいました。 — senshū tomodachi o eiga ni sasoimashita — "Last week I invited a friend to a film."
Polite さそいます, sasoimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
さそう (sasou) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 6.
しばい (shibai) — a stage play; theatre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to get tired of, then the Japanese for to invite.

You'll want to know: しばい.
しばい — shibai — "a stage play; theatre".
せんげつ ははと しばいを みました。 — sengetsu haha to shibai o mimashita — "Last month I saw a play with my mother."

The word, taken apart.
しばい (shibai) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from shiba, "turf, lawn", and i, from iru, "to be, to sit" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ゆかい (yukai) — pleasant; delightful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to invite, then the Japanese for a stage play.

You'll want to know: ゆかい.
ゆかい — yukai — "pleasant; delightful".
きのうの しばいは ゆかいでした。 — kinō no shibai wa yukai deshita — "Yesterday's play was delightful."

The word, taken apart.
ゆかい (yukai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yu, "joy" (Middle Chinese yu) and kai, "cheerful" (khwaejH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: もうしこむ.
もうしこむ — mōshikomu — "to sign up; to apply".
せんげつ あには じゅうどうの しあいに もうしこみました。 — sengetsu ani wa jūdō no shiai ni mōshikomimashita — "Last month my elder brother signed up for a judo match."
Polite もうしこみます, mōshikomimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
もうしこむ (mōshikomu) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from mōshi, from mōsu, "to say", and komu, "to go in, to crowd in" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
やる (yaru) — to do; to play (a game).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for pleasant, then the Japanese for to sign up.

You'll want to know: やる.
やる — yaru — "to do; to play (a game)".
きのう ともだちと しょうぎを やりました。 — kinō tomodachi to shōgi o yarimashita — "Yesterday I played shogi with a friend."
Polite やります, yarimasu; a godan verb, more casual than します.

The word, taken apart.
やる (yaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and the word is usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
