Sanskrit, chapter 408: Beasts of Burden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
वाहः (vāhaḥ) — a draught animal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a goad, then the Sanskrit for harnesses.

You'll want to know: वाहः.
वाहः — vāhaḥ — "a draught animal".
ग्रामे वृषभः वाहः अस्ति। grāme vṛṣabhaḥ vāhaḥ asti. In the village the bull is the draught animal.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वाहः (vāhaḥ) comes from the root √vah, "to carry, to pull", which also gives vahati, "carries"; MW gives vāha as a draught animal: a horse, a bull or an ass (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अंकुशः (aṅkuśaḥ) — an elephant goad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a porter, then the Sanskrit for a mahout.

You'll want to know: अंकुशः.
अंकुशः — aṅkuśaḥ — "an elephant goad".
आधोरणः अंकुशेन गजं नयति। ādhoraṇaḥ aṅkuśena gajaṃ nayati. The mahout guides the elephant with the goad.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अंकुशः (aṅkuśaḥ) is the word MW gives for a hook, especially an elephant driver's hook; MW compares Greek ankistron, "a fish-hook", and German Angel, but gives no derivation within Sanskrit, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
