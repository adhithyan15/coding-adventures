French, chapter 530: Faire la vaisselle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la vaisselle — dishes, crockery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for ladle, then the French for lid.

You'll want to know: la vaisselle.
la vaisselle — "dishes, crockery".
Ce soir, c'est mon frère qui fait la vaisselle. Tonight it is my brother who does the dishes.
Faire la vaisselle — to do the washing-up. In Switzerland also relaver.

The word, taken apart.
la vaisselle is inherited, through Old French vaissele, from Vulgar Latin vascella (EtymDB), from Latin vāsculum "a small vessel", built on vās "a vessel" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
rincer — to rinse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for dishes, then the French for (kitchen) sink.

You'll want to know: rincer.
rincer — "to rinse".
Je rince les verres à l'eau chaude. I rinse the glasses in hot water.
Spelling: nous rinçons.

The word, taken apart.
rincer goes back to Old French reïncier. The origin is not settled: a Vulgar Latin recentiāre "to refresh", from recēns "fresh" (Lewis & Short), is often proposed, and EtymDB compares Old Norse hreinsa "to cleanse".

Il essuie les assiettes après le repas. He dries the plates after the meal.
J'essuie, nous essuyons. S'essuyer les mains — to dry one's hands.

Lesson 5 of 5.
le torchon — tea towel, dishcloth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to rinse, then the French for to wipe.

You'll want to know: le torchon.
le torchon — "tea towel, dishcloth".
Le torchon est accroché près de l'évier. The tea towel is hanging by the sink.
In Belgium l'essuie. Le torchon brûle — there is trouble between them.

The word, taken apart.
le torchon is built on torche "a twist of straw", which goes back to Vulgar Latin torca, from Latin torquēre "to twist" (Lewis & Short).
