Bengali, chapter 418: Profit, Taxes and Moneylenders.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
মুনাফা (munāphā) — profit (in trade).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to transfer (money), then the Bengali for a contribution (to a fund).

You'll want to know: মুনাফা.
মুনাফা — munāphā — "profit (in trade)".
এই বছর ব্যবসায় ভালো মুনাফা হল। ei bôchhôr bæbsāy bhālo munāphā hôlô. The business made a good profit this year.
লাভ, gain, is the wider word; মুনাফা (munāphā) is the trader's profit.

The word, taken apart.
মুনাফা (munāphā) is usually traced to Arabic manfaʿa, "use, benefit, profit", which reached Bengali through Persian.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: শুল্ক.
শুল্ক — shulkô — "a duty; a tax on goods".
বিদেশি জিনিসের ওপর শুল্ক দিতে হয়। bideshi jiniser opôr shulkô dite hôy. You have to pay duty on foreign goods.

The word, taken apart.
শুল্ক (shulkô) is Sanskrit śulka, "a toll, a tax, a duty, especially at ferries and roads" (Monier-Williams), taken over whole.

Lesson 4 of 5.
মহাজন (môhājôn) — a moneylender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a duty (tax), then the Bengali for land tax.

You'll want to know: মহাজন.
মহাজন — môhājôn — "a moneylender".
গরিব লোকটা মহাজনের কাছে ধার নিল। gôrib lokṭā môhājôner kāchhe dhār nilô. The poor man borrowed from the moneylender.

The word, taken apart.
মহাজন (môhājôn) is Sanskrit mahā-jana, "a great or eminent man", also "the head of a trade" and, with a query, "a merchant" (Monier-Williams); Bengali narrowed it to the one who lends money.

Lesson 5 of 5.
বাকি (bāki) — the rest; what is left; still owing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for land tax, then the Bengali for a moneylender.

You'll want to know: বাকি.
বাকি — bāki — "the rest; what is left; still owing".
বিলের বাকি টাকা কাল দেব। biler bāki ṭākā kāl debô. I will pay the rest of the bill tomorrow.
Also on credit: বাকিতে কেনা, to buy on credit.

The word, taken apart.
বাকি (bāki) is usually traced to Arabic bāqī, "remaining, left over", which reached Bengali through Persian.
