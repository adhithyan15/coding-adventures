## Guided Practice — the evening word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-ETYMON-MAALAI-01, TA-ETYMON-MAALAI-02] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "evening" in Tamil]

(*Mālai*.)

- Is the garland *mālai* the same word with a second meaning? (No: two separate
  etymologies that happen to be spelled alike.)
- Which way did the garland word likely travel? (From Dravidian into Sanskrit
  *mālā*: a loan in the reverse of the usual direction.)
- Is it certain that evening *mālai* comes from *māl*'s "darkness" sense? (No:
  it is plausible, but the source only says "compare *māl*".)

## Script — ங, ள and ◌ீ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-NGA-LLA-01, TA-SCRIPT-II-SIGN-01, TA-SCRIPT-READ-NIINGAL-03] -->

- Which four pieces build **நீங்கள்**? (**நீ** · **ங்** · **க** · **ள்**.)
- Why does the **க** in the middle sound like *g*? (**ங்**, a nasal, stands in
  front of it.)
- Which of the three *l*-letters is **ள**? (The retroflex one, tongue curled
  back.)
- How does **◌ீ** differ from **◌ி**? (It is the long *ī*: **நீ** is *nī*, where
  **றி** is *ṟi*.)

[YOU READ: **நீங்கள்** aloud, then find **நீ** inside it]
