# नहीं था — saying where you were not

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C07-NAHIN-01, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Where does **नहीं** stand in a present-tense sentence? Put it there
in a past-tense one and check yourself against what follows.

## You'll want to know: नहीं था
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-04]; assesses=[] -->

**नहीं था** (*nahīṃ thā*) — "was not."

नहीं keeps the seat it has always had: directly in front of the verb, at the end
of the sentence.

| present | past |
|---|---|
| मैं घर पर नहीं हूँ। | मैं घर पर नहीं था। |
| वह स्टेशन पर नहीं है। | वह स्टेशन पर नहीं थी। |

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-04, HI-CONCEPT-C07-NAHIN-01] -->

English rebuilds the sentence to negate it: *I am at home* becomes *I am **not**
at home*, and *I was* becomes *I was not* — but *I go* has to become *I do not
go*, with a helper verb appearing out of nowhere.

Hindi asks for none of that. One word, one seat, every tense. Learning the past
costs you nothing extra here, because there was nothing extra to learn.

## Grammar Lens: the seat in front of the verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-PAST-04, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-02, HI-LEX-C87-PAST-03, HI-LEX-C87-POST-01] -->

| sentence | who is speaking |
|---|---|
| मैं घर पर नहीं था। | a man |
| मैं घर पर नहीं थी। | a woman |
| हम घर पर नहीं थे। | a group |

नहीं never changes shape. Everything that bends in these three lines bends
after it, not because of it.
