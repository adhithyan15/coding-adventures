Lesson 1 of 6.
fàng gēzi — to stand someone up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for round the clock, then the Mandarin for even if.

You'll want to know: fàng gēzi.
fàng gēzi — "to stand someone up".
rúguǒ tā yòu fàng gēzi wǒ jiù bù děng tā le — If he stands me up again, I won't wait for him any more.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàng gēzi is built from fàng 'to let go' and gēzi 'a pigeon', all inherited from Middle Chinese, pjangH, kop and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'the name of a bird'. Letting the pigeon fly is a modern saying, and how it came to mean standing someone up is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
难道 (nándào) — surely not (in a question).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for even if, then the Mandarin for to stand someone up.

You'll want to know: 难道.
难道 — nándào — "surely not (in a question)".
难道你不想去吗 nándào nǐ bù xiǎng qù ma — Surely you want to go?
The new character, in pieces: 难 (nán) — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left 又 made narrow, with a dot for its last stroke; on the right the bird piece, eight strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
难道 (nándào) is built from 难 (nán) 'hard' and dào 'to say', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nan and dawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'difficult' and the second 'a way'. The sense 'to say' is not given there, and how the pair came to ask whether something can really be so is not settled.

Script — a narrow 又, then the bird piece.
难 (nán).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 又 made narrow, two strokes: a bar that turns and falls to the lower left, then a dot instead of the long stroke.
The right piece, the bird piece, eight strokes. A stroke falling to the left. A long vertical down from it. A dot at the top right. Three short bars, one under another. A second vertical down through them. Then a long bar across the foot.
2 + 8 = ten.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 难 (nán)? (Ten.) What ends the left piece? (A dot.) How many short bars in the bird piece before the long one? (Three.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
难得 (nándé) — rare; hard to come by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to stand someone up, then the Mandarin for surely not (in a question).

You'll want to know: 难得.
难得 — nándé — "rare; hard to come by".
我们难得有时间一起吃饭 wǒmen nándé yǒu shíjiān yìqǐ chī fàn — We seldom get time to eat together.

The word, taken apart.
难得 (nándé) is built from 难 (nán) 'hard' and 得 'to get', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nan and tok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'difficult'.
