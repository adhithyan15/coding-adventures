# tirar — the door that says pull

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C366-REF-31, ES-LEX-PUERTA, ES-LEX-C439-VIA-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la caja** and **la puerta**. The street was **cortada**, you
carried an empty box round the long way, and now you want rid of it. Name what
you do with the box.

## You'll want to know: tirar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C366-REF-31] -->

**tirar** — to throw, and above all **to throw away**.

*Tira la caja* — throw the box out. *No tires eso* — do not throw that away.
*Tirar la basura* is the everyday phrase for taking the rubbish out, and the bin
itself is where things **se tiran**.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-05, ES-LEX-PUERTA] -->

Now the sign, and it surprises every English speaker exactly once.

> **TIRAR** on a door means **PULL**.

The opposite side says **EMPUJAR**, push. A reader who knows *tirar* as *throw*
reads the door as an instruction to hurl something, and gets it backwards.

The two senses are one idea seen from each end. **Tirar** is about applying force
along a line away from where the thing sits: a box goes out of your hand, and a
door comes out of its frame toward you. Spanish did not split the word; English
happens to use two.

| the sign | what to do |
|---|---|
| TIRAR | pull |
| EMPUJAR | push |

Read the door before you lean on it. The mistake is harmless and universal, and
knowing it in advance saves you making it in a doorway with people behind you.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-05] -->

It does not come apart, and like **el andén** at the start of this chapter its
history is unsettled. A Germanic verb meaning to tear, reaching Spanish through
the Goths, is the usual suggestion; an origin in the sound of a snapping cord
has also been argued. Neither is agreed.

Two words in one chapter with no recoverable history is not unusual. The very
common, very short, very old verbs are the ones whose trail goes cold soonest,
because they were worn down long before anyone was writing Spanish at all.
