# 年じゅう (nenjū) — all year round

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C253-TOKI-01, JA-LEX-C53-ADJ53-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new word: say "when I was a child". (*kodomo no toki*.) Then say "hot". (*atsui*.)

## You'll want to know: 年じゅう
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C253-NENJUU-01]; assesses=[JA-LEX-KOKO, JA-LEX-C53-ADJ53-03] -->

**年じゅう** — *nenjū* — "all year round", the whole year through.

> **ここは 年じゅう あついです。**
>
> *koko wa nenjū atsui desu.* — "It is hot here all year round."

Four beats: *ne–n–ju–u*, the long *ū* held for two. The first sign is new, read **ねん** (*nen*): the *nen* of **らいねん** and **きょねん**. The next lesson writes it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**年じゅう** (*nenjū*) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are *nen*, "year" (Middle Chinese *nen*) and *jū*, "in, inside, middle" (*trjuwng*) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). KANJIDIC2 gives that second kanji the on'yomi *chū*; JMdict lists *nenchū* as another reading of this same word, so *jū* is *chū* voiced. JMdict spells the word with two kanji, or with the second part in kana, as here, and glosses it "all year round; throughout the year" (JMdict).
