Japanese, chapter 202: Cars, Drivers and Car Parks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じどうしゃ (jidōsha) — an automobile; a motor car.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to rush out, then the Japanese for to bump into.

You'll want to know: じどうしゃ.
じどうしゃ — jidōsha — "an automobile; a motor car".
ちちの じどうしゃは くろいです。 — chichi no jidōsha wa kuroi desu — "My father's car is black."

The word, taken apart.
じどうしゃ (jidōsha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "self" (Middle Chinese dzijH), dō, "to move" (duwngX) and sha, "cart" (tsyhae) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
とめる (tomeru) — to halt (a car); to park.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for driving, then the Japanese for a driver.

You'll want to know: とめる.
とめる — tomeru — "to halt (a car); to park".
くるまを ここに とめます。 — kuruma o koko ni tomemasu — "I park the car here."
Polite とめます, tomemasu; an ichidan verb. Its partner is とまる, tomaru, "to come to a stop".

The word, taken apart.
とめる (tomeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it pairs with tomaru, "to come to a stop" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
