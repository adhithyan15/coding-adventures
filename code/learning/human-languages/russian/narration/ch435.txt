Russian, chapter 435: Service and tips.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
обслуживание (obslúzhivaniye) — service.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for dissatisfied, then the Russian for out of order.

You'll want to know: обслуживание.
обслуживание (obslúzhivaniye) — "service".
В этой гостинице прекрасное обслуживание. V étoy gostínitse prekrásnoye obslúzhivaniye. This hotel has excellent service.
Neuter. Обслуживание номеров — room service.

The word, taken apart.
обслуживание (obslúzhivaniye) is built on обслуживать (obslúzhivat) 'to serve', from об- and служить 'to serve'; EtymDB lists both without a deeper source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
персонал (personál) — staff, personnel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for service, then the Russian for to serve.

You'll want to know: персонал.
персонал (personál) — "staff, personnel".
Персонал гостиницы был очень вежливый. Personál gostínitsy byl óchen vézhlivyy. The hotel staff were very polite.
Masculine and singular in Russian: персонал (personál) был.

The word, taken apart.
персонал (personál) is usually traced to German Personal 'staff', from Medieval Latin persōnālis 'of a person', on persōna 'a mask, a person' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
чаевые (chayevýye) — a tip, tips (for service).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to serve, then the Russian for staff.

You'll want to know: чаевые.
чаевые (chayevýye) — "a tip, tips (for service)".
В ресторане мы оставили чаевые. V restoráne my ostávili chayevýye. We left a tip in the restaurant.
Plural only; an adjective used as a noun: money на чай, for tea.

The word, taken apart.
чаевые (chayevýye) is built on чай 'tea' (EtymDB lists чай): money once given на чай, for a glass of tea.

Lesson 5 of 5.
хамить (khamít) — to be rude (to someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for staff, then the Russian for a tip.

You'll want to know: хамить.
хамить (khamít) — "to be rude (to someone)".
Официант хамил всем посетителям. Ofitsiánt khamíl vsem posetítelyam. The waiter was rude to all the customers.
Imperfective; perfective нахамить. Takes the dative. Present хамлю, хамишь. Colloquial.

The word, taken apart.
хамить (khamít) is built on хам 'a boor'. Хам is usually traced to a name from the Bible, a son who mocked his father; EtymDB lists it without a source, so the line is hedged.
