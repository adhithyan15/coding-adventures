You'll want to know: 如果.
如果 — rúguǒ — "if; in case".
如果你有时间 就来我家 rúguǒ nǐ yǒu shíjiān, jiù lái wǒ jiā — If you have time, come round.
如果 (rúguǒ) means what 要是 means, and it fits anywhere, in speech or in writing; 要是 sounds more casual. The next lesson puts the second character in your hand; the first is the 如 of 比如, and the second is the guǒ of jiéguǒ.

The word, taken apart.
如果 (rúguǒ) is built from 如 and 果 (guǒ), both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyo and kwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first with several words, among them 'to resemble', and the second 'resolute' and 'the fruit of a tree'. Whether 'if' was already among the first's senses the gloss does not make clear; 'if' for the pair is the modern word's sense.

What you've built.
如果 (rúguǒ) opens the condition as 要是 does, and 就 opens the result.
要是 belongs to easy talk; 如果 (rúguǒ) fits a chat, a note or a talk alike.

Script — 日, with 木 running through it.
果 (guǒ).
Two pieces, and you write both already, one woven through the other.
日, four strokes, on top: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, the middle bar, the bottom bar. It is closed before anything else.
木, four strokes, as in 本: the long horizontal under 日; then the vertical, which starts at the top of 日 and runs down through it to the foot; then the left-falling stroke and the right-falling stroke.
4 + 4 = eight.

Grammar Lens: 如果 … 就 …, if and then.
如果 (rúguǒ) opens the condition and 就 opens the result, as 要是 … 就 … does, in speech or in writing.
如果明天不下雨 我们就去山上 rúguǒ míngtiān bú xiàyǔ, wǒmen jiù qù shān shàng — If it doesn't rain tomorrow, we'll go up the mountain. 如果你有时间的话 就来我家 rúguǒ nǐ yǒu shíjiān dehuà, jiù lái wǒ jiā — If you have the time, come round.
就 sits where it sat after 要是: after the subject, before the verb. 的话 can close the condition here too.

Lesson 4 of 7.
如果…就会… (rúguǒ … jiù huì …) — what would happen.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: 如果 … 就会 …, a result you picture.
会 after 就 says what would happen: 如果 (rúguǒ) … 就会 … for a result you picture.
如果我有钱 我就会去中国 rúguǒ wǒ yǒu qián, wǒ jiù huì qù Zhōngguó — If I had the money, I would go to China. 如果我有时间 我就会学开车 rúguǒ wǒ yǒu shíjiān, wǒ jiù huì xué kāichē — If I had the time, I would learn to drive.
There is no new form here. It is the 会 of 明天会下雨, a forecast, now forecasting a result you picture.
Nothing in the verb says whether the condition is real: 如果我有钱 can be if I have the money, or if I had it. The rest of the conversation tells you.

Lesson 5 of 7.
不然 (bùrán) — otherwise.

Before the new one: last lesson's picture, and a word for hurrying.
[once you have stopped driving — read: 如果我有钱 我就会去中国, aloud — rúguǒ wǒ yǒu qián, wǒ jiù huì qù Zhōngguó]
[your turn — recall: say 快 — kuài, quick — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 不然.
不然 — bùrán — "otherwise; or else".
快点儿 不然我们就晚了 kuài diǎnr, bùrán wǒmen jiù wǎn le — Hurry up, or we'll be late.
你得早点儿走 不然就晚了 nǐ děi zǎo diǎnr zǒu, bùrán jiù wǎn le — You have to leave early, or you'll be late.
不 before the rising rán stays bù. Every character in it is one you write.

The word, taken apart.
不然 (bùrán) is built from 不 'not' and 然 'so', both inherited from Middle Chinese. The Guangyun lists 不 under three readings, pjuw, pjuwX and pjut, all glossed 'not'. bù comes from pjut irregularly: other syllables of its kind changed their p to f, while 不 kept the plain lip sound, now written b. 然 is nyen, glossed 'so, like this' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'Not so', and so 'otherwise', is the plain sum of the two.

What you've built.
不然 (bùrán) comes after the advice and says what happens if it is not taken. 就 often opens that result: 不然我们就晚了.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six connected lines are not six sentences in a row: each leans on the one before, and the reader does not have to ask what happened next.
[pause 2 seconds]
You own what does the leaning. Order words: 先, 然后, 突然, 最后. The time at the front: 那天, 第二天, 的时候. The subject said once: 我 in the first line, then the order word or the time. 因为 … 所以 … gives the reason; 如果 (rúguǒ) … 就 … or 应该 turns the story into advice.
[pause 2 seconds]
Your reader is the friend. Your purpose is to save them the same trouble.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write six lines to your friend. Here is a frame for each; the words are yours, and there is no model to copy.
Line 1: when and where, and what you did first, with 先 and 了.
Line 2: what came next, with 然后.
Line 3: what went wrong, with 突然, or with 的时候 and 正在.
Line 4: how it ended, with 最后, or what you had never done before, with 过.
Line 5: the reason it went wrong, with 因为 … 所以 ….
Line 6: your advice, with 如果 (rúguǒ) … 就 … or 应该.
[pause 2 seconds]
A word whose characters you have not written goes in pinyin, inside the line, with its tone marks. Every character comes from the 227 you have learned, each in the order you learned it, each in the same size of square.
[pause 2 seconds]
No Chinese commas or full stops: leave a space between clauses, as this book prints them.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write the six lines. Then read them once as your friend would, top to bottom: would they know what to do?
