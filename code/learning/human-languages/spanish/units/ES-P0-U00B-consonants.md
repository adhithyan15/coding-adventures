# Unit 0B: Consonants That Differ From English

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say the five vowels: *a, e, i, o, u.* Good. Most Spanish
consonants sound close enough to English that they don't need special
attention. A handful genuinely don't — those are today's unit.

## New Material

**ñ** — not in the English alphabet at all. Sounds like the "ny" in
*canyon*. *año* (year) is pronounced *AH-nyo*, not *AH-no* — mixing these
up changes the word entirely (*ano* without the tilde is a different,
much less polite word).

**ll** — in most of Latin America, sounds like the "y" in *yes*. (In Spain
and parts of Argentina it varies further — *y*-like or a soft "j"-like
sound — but "y" is the safe default to start with.) *llamar* (to call) is
*ya-MAR*, not *la-MAR* — you already met this word in Unit 1's *me llamo*.

**rr** — a rolled/trilled "r," genuinely a different sound from English "r,"
made by vibrating the tongue against the ridge behind your top teeth. A
single *r* between vowels is a quick tap, not rolled — *pero* (but) vs.
*perro* (dog) are a real minimal pair; mixing them up is a classic,
embarrassing mistake.

**j** — sounds like the "h" in *hot*, but with more friction at the back of
the throat — closer to the "ch" in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach* than to
any standard English sound. *jueves* (Thursday, from Unit 7) starts with
this sound, not an English "j" sound.

**h** — always silent. Never pronounced, ever. *hola* is *OH-la*, not
*HOH-la* — the letter is there but contributes nothing to the sound.

## Guided Practice

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "año" — with the *ñ* sound]
- [YOU SAY: "llamar" — with the *y* sound]
- [YOU SAY: "perro" — with a rolled *rr*]
- [YOU SAY: "hola" — silent *h*]

[REPEAT x2] All five sounds in their example words: *año, llamar, perro,
jueves, hola.*

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] What sound does a silent letter make in Spanish? ... No sound —
*h* is always silent, no exceptions.
