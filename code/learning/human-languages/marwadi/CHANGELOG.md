# Changelog

## The teens, and a word for no

HL-C353 filed two named gaps at the end of the numerals tranche and said which
kind each one was. Both are closed here, in three chapters (37-39, 29 lessons),
and the classification it made turned out to be exactly right in both cases.

**ELEVEN TO NINETEEN WAS A SCRIPT GAP, PRICED AT FOUR SIGNS, AND THE PRICE WAS
THE PRICE.** The count ran one to ten and then jumped to twenty. Filling it cost
the hand exactly four characters — the standing vowels **इ** (for *igyārā*),
**अ** (for *aṭhārā*) and **उ** (for *ughaṇīs*), and the retroflex consonant
**ळ** (for *soḷā*). Every other sign in all nine words was already in the track,
which a census of its 52 distinct Devanagari characters — headwords **and**
worked examples — confirmed before anything was designed. *bārā* is **ब** from
*bīs* beside **र** from the first greeting; *satarā* is **सात** with its long
mark taken off.

**TWENTY WAS ALREADY TAUGHT, AND THAT IS WHY NOTHING HAD TO BE REORDERED.**
Marwari counts DOWN to a round number: nineteen is *ughaṇīs*, and the **-णीस**
in it is *bīs*, twenty, worn down — so nineteen is *one short of twenty*, built
on the number **above** it rather than on nine. HL-C350's tranche taught *bīs*
before the teens for exactly this reason, and the lesson now says so on the
page. Turner records the same habit further up the line at *ekuṇcālīs*, one short
of forty.

`MW-A1-Q-01` was **already covered** before the teens landed, so **the teens move
no coverage number at all.** They are recorded in the point's probe anyway,
because a probe should name what is taught rather than the smallest set that
scored the point — otherwise this file would keep reporting a count that stops
at ten while the corpus reaches twenty.

**THE MISSING WORD FOR NO WAS A FUNCTIONAL HOLE, NOT A COVERAGE ONE, AND IT COST
NOTHING TO FIX.** Chapter 3 taught **हां सा** and gave it a four-skill lesson.
Nothing in the 312 lessons after it could decline. Chapters 26-31 are a market —
*how much is this?*, *make it a little cheaper*, *what final price will you
give?* — with an offer, a counter-offer, and no refusal.

**कोनी** (*konī*) is the word, sourced to a Swadesh word list collected for
Marwari, which gives it for "not" where Hindi has *nahī̃*. It is **को** + **नी**:
four signs the track has had since chapter 5. The gap was never a writing
problem, and saying that plainly is the point — a coverage column that reads
"absent" invites the next reader to price a script tranche that was never needed.

The refusal is taught as a two-line move rather than a word: **कोनी।** then
**पाछे मिलसू।** — the goodbye chapter 7 taught as a farewell, which in a bazaar
is a *walk-away* that leaves the bargain open instead of ending it. That closes
`MW-A1-F4-09`, which this file had called the single most consequential missing
move in the track.

**WHAT MOVED, AND WHAT DELIBERATELY DID NOT.** Coverage **76/197 -> 79/197**,
and all three points came from the one word:

- `MW-A1-ADV-05` polarity — closed; the adverb column moves from flat zero to one.
- `MW-A1-F1-05` confirm or deny — closed; it was exactly half a point before.
- `MW-A1-F4-09` decline an offer — closed.
- `MW-A1-OS-02` the negative declarative — **still open, for a narrower reason**.
  *konī* is taught as a one-word answer, not inside a sentence, and
  *ye sasto konī hai* is modelled nowhere. It is now blocked on a **frame**
  rather than on a word, and it is not claimed.
- `MW-A1-CJ-02` negative coordination and `MW-A1-F4-06` refuse permission — both
  still open, and both notes rewritten, because the reason each gave (no negator)
  is no longer the true one.
- `MW-A1-Q-02` **ordinals — RE-CHECKED and still unsourced.** The HL-C354 sweep
  searched again for a citable Marwari-specific ordinal series and found none:
  the Rajasthani numeral list this track cites for its cardinals prints
  cardinals only. **No series was invented.** What changed around it is that the
  sourcing block is now the *only* reason — the teens taught the four signs the
  numbers needed, and *pahlo, dūjo, tījo, chautho* need none of them.

**MARWADI IS ONE OF TWO TRACKS IN THE CORPUS WITH AN ALL-ZERO STRICT REPORT, AND
IT STILL IS, AT 341 LESSONS.** Every reinforcement window, forward reference,
order defect, glyph spike and script-closure violation stays at zero.

    reinforcementWindowMisses         0 -> 0   (R1, R2, R3 and R4 each 0)
    forwardReferences                 0 -> 0
    atomsNeverRevisited               0 -> 0
    atomsTaught                     272 -> 297

That was not free, and the three things it cost are worth recording:

1. **Growing 312 -> 341 makes 55 (lesson, window) slots judgeable for the first
   time** — debt the length EXPOSES, not creates. Every one is answered by the
   lesson that opens it: each lesson retrieves the previous lesson's word (R1),
   the word five lessons back (R2), the atom exactly twenty positions back (R3)
   and the atom exactly eighty positions back (R4). Verified atom by atom, not
   by watching the total; the first pass had **eight** defects and the verifier
   named every one of them.
2. **`atomsNeverRevisited` needed a 29th lesson.** An atom introduced by a
   track's final lesson can never be revisited, and chapter 39's payoff lesson
   introduced one. `MW-R39-script-close` — the closing retrieval every other
   chapter in this track already has — takes the tail slot and introduces
   nothing.
3. **`forwardReferences` broke and was fixed the way HL-C353 fixed it before.**
   The *hear* lesson printed **कोनी** in Devanagari one lesson before the
   writing lesson teaches it. The track's own rule is that a *hear* lesson is
   voice-first; the spelling is now named as the next lesson's, and the count is
   back to zero.

**A GENERATED LEDGER IS NOT A LESSON, and this tranche nearly shipped the
difference.** Seating the R-window payments in the frontmatter made the
measurement green while fifteen lessons assessed atoms the learner was never
told to retrieve. Every one of those now carries a visible
`[YOU RECALL: …]` line naming the word and its window, and the audit that found
them compared each block's assessed atoms against the prose that surrounds it.
