Mandarin Chinese, chapter 260: What I Can Do.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nénglì — ability.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a journalist, then the Mandarin for an electrician.

You'll want to know: nénglì.
nénglì — "ability".
tā hěn yǒu nénglì — He's very capable.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nénglì is built from 能 'able' and lì 'strength', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nong and lik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'skilled' and the second 'sinew'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
本事 (běnshi) — skill; what someone is capable of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an electrician, then the Mandarin for ability.

You'll want to know: 本事.
本事 — běnshi — "skill; what someone is capable of".
这个人很有本事 zhège rén hěn yǒu běnshi — This man can turn his hand to anything.

The word, taken apart.
本事 (běnshi) is built from 本 'the root' and 事 'affairs', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pwonX and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the root' and 'the beginning'. In the word the second loses its tone.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for ability, then the Mandarin for what someone is capable of.

高手 — gāoshǒu — "expert; master (at something)".
做饭他是高手 zuòfàn tā shì gāoshǒu — When it comes to cooking, he's an expert.

The word, taken apart.
高手 (gāoshǒu) is built from 高 'high' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a hand of a high level.

Lesson 4 of 5.
jìshù — skill; technique.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for what someone is capable of, then the Mandarin for an expert.

You'll want to know: jìshù.
jìshù — "skill; technique".
tā de jìshù hěn hǎo — He's very skilled.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìshù is built from jì 'a craft' and shù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjeX and zywit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'skill' and the second with this very word. Middle Chinese zywit would regularly give a rising tone, shú; the falling tone of shù is irregular.
