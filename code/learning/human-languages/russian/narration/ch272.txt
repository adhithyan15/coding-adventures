Russian, chapter 272: Beforehand and soon after.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
заранее (zaráneye) — in advance, beforehand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for immediately, then the Russian for an instant.

You'll want to know: заранее.
заранее (zaráneye) — "in advance, beforehand".
Мы купили билеты заранее. My kupíli biléty zaráneye. We bought the tickets in advance.
Заранее спасибо — thanks in advance.

The word, taken apart.
заранее (zaráneye) is built on за- and ранний "early", the adjective of рано, which is inherited from Proto-Slavic ranŭ "early" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
накануне (nakanúne) — the day before, on the eve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an instant, then the Russian for in advance.

You'll want to know: накануне.
накануне (nakanúne) — "the day before, on the eve".
Накануне свадьбы она не спала. Nakanúne svádby oná ne spalá. On the eve of the wedding she did not sleep.
With the genitive: накануне (nakanúne) праздника — on the eve of the holiday. Alone it means "the day before".

The word, taken apart.
накануне (nakanúne) is built on на "on" and канун "the eve of a feast", which is usually traced to Greek kanṓn "a rule", the name of a church service sung on the eve; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
прежде (prézhde) — before, formerly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for in advance, then the Russian for the day before.

You'll want to know: прежде.
прежде (prézhde) — "before, formerly".
Прежде здесь был маленький парк. Prézhde zdes byl málenkiy park. Formerly there was a small park here.
Прежде чем + infinitive — before doing: прежде (prézhde) чем уйти. Прежде всего — first of all.

The word, taken apart.
прежде (prézhde) is a Church Slavonic word, prěžde "before" (EtymDB), with the -ре- of the church language; the native Russian shape of the same root is перед "in front of".

Lesson 4 of 5.
вскоре (vskóre) — soon after, shortly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the day before, then the Russian for before.

You'll want to know: вскоре.
вскоре (vskóre) — "soon after, shortly".
Вскоре пошёл снег. Vskóre poshyól sneg. Soon after, it began to snow.
It looks back from a point in the story: soon after that. Вскоре после обеда — shortly after lunch.

The word, taken apart.
вскоре (vskóre) is built on в and скоро "quickly", from скорый "fast", which is inherited through Old East Slavic skorŭ from Proto-Slavic skorŭ "fast" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
спустя (spustyá) — later, after (a stretch of time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for before, then the Russian for soon after.

You'll want to know: спустя.
спустя (spustyá) — "later, after (a stretch of time)".
Спустя два года они снова встретились. Spustyá dva góda oní snóva vstrétilis. Two years later they met again.
With the accusative of time, before or after it: спустя (spustyá) неделю or неделю спустя (spustyá) — a week later.

The word, taken apart.
спустя (spustyá) is an old adverbial participle of спустить "to let down", built on пустить "to let go", inherited from Proto-Slavic pustiti (EtymDB): time let go by.
