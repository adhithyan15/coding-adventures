Russian, chapter 71: Sunday and the Four Seasons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
воскресенье (voskresénye) — Sunday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for Friday, then the Russian for Saturday.

You'll want to know: воскресенье.
воскресенье (voskresénye) — "Sunday".

The word, taken apart.
воскресенье (voskresénye) — Church Slavonic, says Vasmer, as its вос- shows: it meant "resurrection", then "the day of the Resurrection", then Sunday. Earlier, неделя named Sunday.

The word, taken apart.
зима (zimá) from Proto-Slavic zima — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian žiemà, Sanskrit himá-, Greek cheîma and Latin hiems, all "winter", from PIE ǵʰéyōm, "winter".

Lesson 4 of 5.
лето (léto) — summer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for winter, then the Russian for spring.

You'll want to know: лето.
лето (léto) — "summer".

The word, taken apart.
лето (léto) from Proto-Slavic lěto, "summer; year" — inherited. Its deeper origin is uncertain: Vasmer reports a possible link with Old Irish laithe, "day", and Old Gutnish laþigs, "in spring", and others with лить, "to pour". It also counts years: пять лет, "five years".

Lesson 5 of 5.
осень (ósen) — autumn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for spring, then the Russian for summer.

You'll want to know: осень.
осень (ósen) — "autumn".

The word, taken apart.
осень (ósen) from Proto-Slavic esenĭ — inherited; East Slavic turned je- into о-. Vasmer relates it to Old Prussian assanis, "autumn", Gothic asans, "harvest", and Old High German aran, "harvest": autumn as the reaping season.
