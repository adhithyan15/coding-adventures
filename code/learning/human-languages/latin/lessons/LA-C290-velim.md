# velim — I would like

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C287-CUPIO-INF-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I want to see Rome". (*Rōmam vidēre volō.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Volō* states a fact about you: I want. Said to a stranger it
can sound blunt. Latin softens it by changing the verb's mood.

## Grammar Lens: velim, the softer want
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C290-SUBIUNCTIVUS-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-01, LA-LEX-C111-WANT111-01] -->

> **Velim tē vidēre.** — I would like to see you.

[PAUSE 2s] **velim** is the **subjunctive** of *volō*: not "I want", but "I
would want, I would like". All six persons:

> **velim**, **velīs**, **velit**, **velīmus**, **velītis**, **velint**.

[PAUSE 2s] The other wanting and able verbs do the same, with the same
endings: **possim**, "I could, I might be able"; **mālim**, "I would
rather".

> **Hoc facere possim.** — I could do this.
> **Mālim manēre.** — I would rather stay.

[PAUSE 1s] Cicero writes *velim scire*, "I should like to know", in one of
his dialogues, and *velim* again in a letter telling his friend Atticus how
much he would like to see him (Lewis & Short quote both).

## Why it's said this way: a mood, not a tense
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-SUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Velim* is not past or future. It is a different way of holding
the same present: the **indicative**, *volō*, says what is; the
**subjunctive**, *velim*, says what would be, or could be. *Sum* has one
too, *sim*, "I may be", and the next lesson puts *sum*'s other subjunctive
to work.

## The word, taken apart: the -i- of velim
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-C290-OPTATIVUS-02]; assesses=[] -->

The **-i-** of *velim*, *possim* and *sim* is usually explained as the mark
of an old wishing mood, which Greek kept as a mood of its own and Latin
folded into the subjunctive. Lewis & Short do record an older *siem*,
*siet* for *sim*, *sit* in Plautus; the link to the Greek mood is the account
histories of Latin give, offered as one.
