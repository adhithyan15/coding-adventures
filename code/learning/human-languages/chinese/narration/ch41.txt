Mandarin Chinese, chapter 41: Paying.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
多少 (duōshao) — how much; how many.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bread, then the Mandarin for egg.

You'll want to know: 多少.
多少 — duōshao — "how much; how many".
你买了多少 nǐ mǎi le duōshao — How many did you buy?
The new character, in pieces: 多 (duō) — one three-stroke piece twice, one above the other; 少 (shǎo) — 小, the small piece, without its hook, and one long sweep below it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
多少 (duōshao) is built from 多 (duō) 'many' and 少 (shǎo) 'few', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ta and syewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): many-few, asked as a question.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

Script — the same three strokes, twice.
多 (duō).
One shape, twice: top, then bottom.
The shape, three strokes. A short left-falling stroke. A horizontal that turns and falls to the lower left. A dot inside the turn.
Make it again below, a little larger and a little to the right, so the two lean together.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xiǎo, mǎi]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 小 without its hook, and a sweep.
少 (shǎo).
One piece, built on 小.
Three strokes like 小. A vertical down the middle, but this time with no hook at the foot. A short stroke falling to the left. A dot on the right.
One more stroke. A long left-falling sweep from the right, under the vertical.
3 + 1 = four.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which character you know is inside 少 (shǎo)? (小.) What does the middle vertical lose? (Its hook.) Which stroke is last? (The long sweep to the left.)

Lesson 5 of 7.
kuài — yuan; lump.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for how much, then the Mandarin for money.

You'll want to know: kuài.
kuài — "yuan; lump".
nàge shí kuài qián — That one is ten yuan.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
kuài is the spoken word for the yuan: a price is a number, then kuài, then qián, which can be left off.

The word, taken apart.
kuài is inherited from Middle Chinese khwojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a clod of earth'. Its use for counting money is not given there, so how it took that job is not settled.

What you've built.
