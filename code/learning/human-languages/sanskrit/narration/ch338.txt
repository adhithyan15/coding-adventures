Sanskrit, chapter 338: Heaven and Hell.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पुण्यम् (puṇyam) — merit; a good deed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for promises, then the Sanskrit for hunting.

You'll want to know: पुण्यम्.
पुण्यम् — puṇyam — "merit; a good deed".
दानेन पुण्यं वर्धते। dānena puṇyaṃ vardhate. Merit grows through giving.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
पुण्यम् (puṇyam) MW suggests, with a perhaps, the root √puṣ, "to thrive", and gives puṇya as good, virtuous and, as a noun, virtue, a good work, religious merit, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
स्वर्गः (svargaḥ) — heaven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for merit, then the Sanskrit for a sin.

You'll want to know: स्वर्गः.
स्वर्गः — svargaḥ — "heaven".
पुण्येन जनः स्वर्गं गच्छति। puṇyena janaḥ svargaṃ gacchati. Through merit a person goes to heaven.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्वर्गः (svargaḥ) is built from svar, "light, the sky", and ga, "going", from the root √gam; MW gives svarga as leading to heaven and, as a noun, heaven (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for heaven, then the Sanskrit for hell.

You'll want to know: भुंक्ते.
भुंक्ते — bhuṅkte — "reaps; enjoys".
जनः पुण्यस्य फलं भुंक्ते। janaḥ puṇyasya phalaṃ bhuṅkte. A person reaps the fruit of merit.
Past (he …): सः फलं भुक्तवान्, saḥ phalaṃ bhuktavān.

The word, taken apart.
भुंक्ते (bhuṅkte) comes from the root √bhuj, "to enjoy, use, eat", the root of bhojana, "a meal"; MW gives it as to enjoy, use, possess, consume (MW).
