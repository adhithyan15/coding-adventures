Lesson 1 of 6.
国家 (guójiā) — country; nation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for for generations on end, then the Mandarin for to miss someone dearly.

You'll want to know: 国家.
国家 — guójiā — "country; nation".
我去过三个国家 wǒ qù guo sān ge guójiā — I've been to three countries.

The word, taken apart.
国家 (guójiā) is built from 国 'a state' and 家 'a home', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwok and kae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a realm' and the second 'a dwelling'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
世界 (shìjiè) — world.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to miss someone dearly, then the Mandarin for a nation with its own government.

You'll want to know: 世界.
世界 — shìjiè — "world".
我想去看世界 wǒ xiǎng qù kàn shìjiè — I want to go and see the world.
The new character, in pieces: 界 (jiè) — nine strokes: on top a field, a box of five strokes cut into four; under it 人 opened wide, and under that a short stroke falling to the left and a vertical. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
世界 (shìjiè) is built from 世 'a generation' and jiè 'a boundary', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syejH and keajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a generation' and the second 'a border' and 'an edge'. The sense 'the world' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 6.
界 (jiè) — a field over 人.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rén, shì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a field over 人.
界 (jiè).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The top piece, a field, five strokes. A short vertical on the left. A bar that runs right and turns down. A bar across the middle, then a vertical down the middle. Then the bar that closes the foot.
The bottom piece, four strokes. First 人, opened wide: a long stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right. Under them, a short stroke that falls to the left, and a vertical on the right.
5 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 界 (jiè), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 界 (jiè)? (Nine.) What is on top? (A field, five strokes.) What opens the bottom piece? (人.)

You'll want to know: 外界.
外界 — wàijiè — "the outside world".
外界都很关心他的病 wàijiè dōu hěn guānxīn tā de bìng — People everywhere are concerned about his illness.

The word, taken apart.
外界 (wàijiè) is built from 外 'outside' and 界 (jiè) 'a boundary', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwajH and keajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'outside' and 'far' and the second 'a border'.

jiànlì — to found; to set up.

You'll want to know: jiànlì.
jiànlì — "to found; to set up".
tāmen yìbǎi nián qián jiànlì le zhège xuéxiào — They founded this school a hundred years ago.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiànlì is built from jiàn and lì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjonH and lip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to set up' and 'to plant', and the second 'to stand' and 'to complete'.
