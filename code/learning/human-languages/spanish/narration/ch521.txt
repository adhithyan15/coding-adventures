Spanish, chapter 521: Siguiendo la pista.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
investigar — to investigate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the victim, then the Spanish for the investigation.

You'll want to know: investigar.
investigar — "to investigate".
La policía investiga el caso. The police are investigating the case.
It takes gu before e: investigué.

The word, taken apart.
investigar is a learned borrowing from Latin investīgāre 'to track, to search after' (EtymDB), built on vestīgium 'a footprint'. To investigate is to follow the footprints.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la pista — the clue, the lead; the track.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the investigation, then the Spanish for to investigate.

You'll want to know: la pista.
la pista — "the clue, the lead; the track".
La policía no tiene ninguna pista. The police do not have any leads.
It also means a track or court: una pista de tenis, la pista de baile.

The word, taken apart.
pista was borrowed from Italian pista 'a track, a beaten trail' (EtymDB). A clue is the trail you follow.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la prueba — the proof, the evidence; the test.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to investigate, then the Spanish for the clue.

You'll want to know: la prueba.
la prueba — "the proof, the evidence; the test".
No hay pruebas contra él. There is no evidence against him.
Una prueba is also a test: una prueba de inglés.

The word, taken apart.
prueba is built on probar 'to test, to try', which is inherited from Latin probāre 'to test, to prove' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
averiguar — to find out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the clue, then the Spanish for the proof.

You'll want to know: averiguar.
averiguar — "to find out".
Quiero averiguar qué pasó. I want to find out what happened.
It takes ü before e: averigüé.

The word, taken apart.
averiguar is usually traced to Late Latin verificāre 'to show to be true', built on vērus 'true'. To find out is to get at the truth.
