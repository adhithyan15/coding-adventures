Sanskrit, chapter 527: Aim and Shoot.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
धनुर्धरः (dhanurdharaḥ) — an archer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for leaps over, then the Sanskrit for a team.

You'll want to know: धनुर्धरः.
धनुर्धरः — dhanurdharaḥ — "an archer".
धनुर्धरः धनुः गृह्णाति। dhanurdharaḥ dhanuḥ gṛhṇāti. The archer takes up his bow.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
धनुर्धरः (dhanurdharaḥ) is built from dhanus, "a bow", and dhara, "holding", from the root √dhṛ, "to hold"; MW gives dhanurdhara as a bow-holder, an archer (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an archer, then the Sanskrit for focused.

You'll want to know: प्रहिणोति.
प्रहिणोति — prahiṇoti — "shoots; sends forth".
धनुर्धरः लक्ष्यं प्रति बाणं प्रहिणोति। dhanurdharaḥ lakṣyaṃ prati bāṇaṃ prahiṇoti. The archer shoots an arrow at the target.
Past (he …): सः बाणं प्रहितवान्, saḥ bāṇaṃ prahitavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रहिणोति (prahiṇoti) is built on pra, "forth", and the root √hi, "to send, to impel"; MW gives prahi as to urge on, to send, to hurl; its past participle is prahita (MW).

उत्साहयति — utsāhayati — "cheers on; encourages".
गुरुः शिष्यम् उत्साहयति। guruḥ śiṣyam utsāhayati. The teacher encourages the pupil.
Past (he …): सः शिष्यम् उत्साहितवान्, saḥ śiṣyam utsāhitavān.

The word, taken apart.
उत्साहयति (utsāhayati) is built on ud, "up", and the causative of the root √sah, "to bear, to be able"; MW gives utsāhayati as to animate, to encourage (MW).
