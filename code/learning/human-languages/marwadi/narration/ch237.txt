Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 237: Small Creatures of the Field.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तीतर (tītar) — a partridge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a horn, then the Marwadi for a hoof.

You'll want to know: तीतर.
तीतर — tītar — "a partridge".
खेत मांय तीतर है। khet mā̃y tītar hai. There's a partridge in the field.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तीतर (tītar) is inherited from Sanskrit tittira, "a partridge" (CDIAL 5809, which lists Marwari tītar).

Lesson 3 of 5.
चुगणो (chugṇo) — to peck; to pick up (grain or berries).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a partridge, then the Marwadi for a wing.

You'll want to know: चुगणो.
चुगणो — chugṇo — "to peck; to pick up (grain or berries)".
कबूतर बाजरो चुगै है। kabūtar bājro chugai hai. The pigeon pecks at the millet.
Past (I …): म्हैं केर चुग्या, mhaĩ ker chugyā.

The word, taken apart.
चुगणो (chugṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed cugyati, "pecks"; CDIAL lists Marwari cugṇo, "to peck, to eat" (CDIAL 4852).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a wing, then the Marwadi for to peck.

You'll want to know: इंडो.
इंडो — iṇḍo — "an egg".
चिड़ी इंडो देवै है। chiṛī iṇḍo devai hai. The sparrow lays an egg.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
इंडो (iṇḍo) is inherited from Sanskrit aṇḍa, "an egg"; CDIAL lists Gujarati ĩḍũ, "an egg". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 1111).
