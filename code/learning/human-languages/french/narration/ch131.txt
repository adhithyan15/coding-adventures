French, chapter 131: Busy, Free, Open, Closed, Calm.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
occupé — busy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for brown, then the French for pretty.

You'll want to know: occupé.
occupé — "busy".

The word, taken apart.
occupé is the past participle of the verb for "to occupy", a learned borrowing of Latin occupare, "to seize", from ob and capere, "to take" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ouvert — open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for busy, then the French for free.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ouvert belongs to ouvrir, to open, which takes the endings of a verb in -er: j'ouvre. Say j'ouvre, then its opposite, je ferme.

You'll want to know: ouvert.
ouvert — "open".

The word, taken apart.
ouvert is the past participle of ouvrir, "to open": opened. It goes back to Vulgar Latin opertus, from Latin apertus, "open". (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
fermé — closed.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for free, then the French for open.

Fermé is the closed form of fermer, to close. Say fermer, then fermé.

You'll want to know: fermé.
fermé — "closed".

The word, taken apart.
fermé is the past participle of fermer, "to close": closed. The verb from Latin firmare, "to make firm, to fasten" (Lewis & Short).

The word, taken apart.
calme from Italian calma from Late Latin cauma, "the heat of midday", from Greek kauma, "burning heat" (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed: the still hour when the sun was hottest.
