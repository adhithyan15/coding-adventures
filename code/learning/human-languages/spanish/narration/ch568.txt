Spanish, chapter 568: Reyes y revoluciones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
histórico — historic; historical.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the soldier, then the Spanish for the army.

You'll want to know: histórico.
histórico — "historic; historical".
Fue un día histórico para el país. It was a historic day for the country.
El centro histórico — the old town.

The word, taken apart.
histórico is a learned borrowing from Latin historicus, which comes from Greek historikós 'of history' (EtymDB), built on historía 'an inquiry'.

Masculine although it ends in -a. La monarquía is the monarchy.

The word, taken apart.
monarca is a learned borrowing from Late Latin monarcha, which comes from Greek monárchēs 'one who rules alone' (EtymDB), built on mónos 'alone' and árchein 'to rule'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
conquistar — to conquer; to win over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the monarch, then the Spanish for the queen.

You'll want to know: conquistar.
conquistar — "to conquer; to win over".
Los romanos conquistaron la península. The Romans conquered the peninsula.
It also means to win someone's heart: La conquistó con su humor.

The word, taken apart.
conquistar is usually traced to spoken Latin conquīstāre, built on conquīsītus, the participle of conquīrere 'to seek out, to bring together' (Lewis & Short), from quaerere 'to seek'.
