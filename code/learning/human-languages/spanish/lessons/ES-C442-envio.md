# el envío — put on the road

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ENVIAR, ES-LEX-C312-ASK-15, ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **enviar** and **la carta**. Say **la administración**. You have
the verb for sending. Name the thing that is on its way.

## You'll want to know: el envío
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ENVIAR] -->

**el envío** — the shipment, the dispatch, the consignment.

*Gastos de envío* — shipping costs, the line on every order page. *Su envío está
en camino* — your parcel is on its way. *El envío se ha retrasado* uses the
**retraso** family you already have.

The accent sits on the **í** and breaks it away from the **o**: *en-VÍ-o*, three
syllables.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-02] -->

**enviar** is Latin **inviāre** — **in-** onto, plus **via**, a road.

To send something was **to put it on the road**, and the road is still sitting
inside the word. *El envío* is what has been put on it.

| word | the road in it |
|---|---|
| enviar | put onto the way |
| el envío | the thing on the way |

English has *via* unchanged as a preposition, and the same *via* is inside
**obvious** — *ob viam*, lying in the way, so plain you trip over it.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-02, ES-LEX-C442-GESTION-01] -->

**Envío** is the second word in two chapters built by putting a plain ending on
a verb you already own: *prestar* gave **préstamo**, *enviar* gives **envío**.

That is a pattern worth using deliberately. When you know a Spanish verb and
need the noun for its result, the noun very often exists and is short — and you
can usually recognise it even when you could not have produced it.

The administración will use the noun where a friend would use the verb. A friend
says *te lo envío*; the tracking page says *su envío*. Same event, and the
choice of word tells you who is writing.
