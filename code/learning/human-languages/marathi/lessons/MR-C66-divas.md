# दिवस (divas) — day

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-RATRA, MR-GRAMMAR-TIME-II] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Four parts, four at-that-time words. Say them. Then ask yourself
what the four of them together are called.

## You'll want to know: दिवस
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-DIVAS]; assesses=[] -->

> **दिवस** — *divas* — **day**

Not a fifth part. **The whole that holds the other four** — the morning, the
afternoon, the evening and the night are stretches of a दिवस.

## Grammar Lens: the one that will not take -ी
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-DIVAS, MR-GRAMMAR-TIME-II, MR-GRAMMAR-THREE-GENDERS-ONE-CHAPTER] -->

Four words took **-ी** without complaint. This one does not:

| | at that time |
|---|---|
| **रात्र** | **रात्री** |
| **दिवस** | **दिवसा** — *divasā*, by day |

**-आ**, not **-ी**. And the reason is one you already own.

**सकाळ, दुपार, संध्याकाळ** and **रात्र** are all **feminine**. **दिवस** is
**masculine** — and gender is the thing that has decided endings for you since
the chapter that gave you three of them.

So this is not the pattern breaking. It is the pattern meeting a noun of a
different gender and doing what it has always done. **An exception you can
predict is not an exception.**
