Arabic, chapter 285: What I Think, And So.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
رأي (raʾy) — an opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Tell a two-step story: "first I read, then I wrote". (Awwalan qaraʾtu, thumma katabtu.) Then "I told the story when he came". (Ḥakaytu al-qiṣṣa ʿindamā jāʾa.)
[pause 1 second]
A story says what happened. The next node asks what you think about it, and why.

You'll want to know: رأي.
رأي — raʾy — "an opinion, a view".
ما رأيك في الفيلم؟ — mā raʾyuka fī al-fīlm? — What do you think of the film?
في رأيي، الفيلم مفيد. — fī raʾyī, al-fīlm mufīd — In my opinion, the film is useful.
Raʾyī is "my opinion", with the -ī of "my". Raʾyuka is "your opinion" to a man; to a woman it is raʾyuki. Fī, "in", says what the opinion is about.

The word, taken apart.
رأي (raʾy) is built on the native Arabic root r-a-y, "to see" (Lane): the root of raʾā, "he saw", from chapter twenty-nine. Lane gives raʾā fī al-amr raʾyan for forming a view of a matter. An opinion, in Arabic, is what you see in something.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: raʾy, with the catch in the middle — raʾ-y]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What do you think of the film?" — mā raʾyuka fī al-fīlm?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In my opinion, the book is useful" — fī raʾyī, al-kitāb mufīd]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same question to a woman, about a sentence — mā raʾyuki fī al-jumla?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does رأي (raʾy) mean? ("An opinion".) "In my opinion"? (Fī raʾyī.) Which verb shares its root? (Raʾā, he saw.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
أعتقد أن (aʿtaqidu anna) — I think that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "in my opinion". (Fī raʾyī.) Then say aktub anna Sāmī jārī, "I write that Sami is my neighbour".
[pause 1 second]
Anna hangs a whole sentence on a verb. Hang it on a verb of thinking, and you can give an opinion.

Grammar Lens: aʿtaqidu anna, aẓunnu anna.
Iʿtaqada, to believe, from chapter one hundred and sixty-four, has the present aʿtaqidu, I believe, I think. Put anna after it:
أعتقد أن الامتحان صعب. — aʿtaqidu anna al-imtiḥāna ṣaʿb — I think the exam is hard.
The noun after anna takes -a: al-imtiḥāna. With "it" the pronoun joins anna itself: aʿtaqidu annahu ṣaʿb, I think it's hard.
أظن أن — aẓunnu anna — is "I suppose", a little less sure: aẓunnu anna al-qiṭāra sarīʿ, I suppose the train is fast.

Grammar Lens: agreeing and not.
For "I don't think so", put lā in front and dhālika, "that", after: لا أعتقد ذلك — lā aʿtaqidu dhālika.
— mā raʾyuka fī al-fīlm? — aʿtaqidu annahu mumtāz. — lā aʿtaqidu dhālika!
Three ways to start an opinion, then: fī raʾyī, aʿtaqidu anna, aẓunnu anna. English drops its "that"; Arabic keeps anna.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I think the exam is hard" — aʿtaqidu anna al-imtiḥāna ṣaʿb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think it's excellent" — aʿtaqidu annahu mumtāz]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I suppose the train is fast" — aẓunnu anna al-qiṭāra sarīʿ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I don't think so" — lā aʿtaqidu dhālika]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think the lawyer is here" — aʿtaqidu anna al-muḥāmiya hunā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I think that"? (Aʿtaqidu anna.) The less sure one? (Aẓunnu anna.) What happens to the noun after anna? (It takes -a: al-imtiḥāna.) "I don't think so"? (Lā aʿtaqidu dhālika.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
سؤال (suʾāl) — a question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think that", then "he asked". (Aʿtaqidu anna, saʾala.)
[pause 1 second]
Before an opinion there is often a question. Here is the noun for it.

You'll want to know: سؤال.
سؤال — suʾāl — "a question".
عندي سؤال. — ʿindī suʾāl — I have a question.
ʿIndī, "at me", is how Arabic says "I have". The answer to a question is jawāb, from chapter seventy-four, and the two make a pair: suʾāl wa-jawāb, question and answer.
Say it su-ʾāl, with the catch before the long ā: the same catch as in the middle of saʾala.

The word, taken apart.
سؤال (suʾāl) is built on the native Arabic root s-a-l, "to ask" (Lane), the root of saʾala, "he asked". Lane gives suʾāl as "a question", the counterpart of jawāb. The catch in the middle is the root's own middle letter, a hamza. In saʾala it sits on ا. Here the vowel before it is u, and it is written on a different seat, which the next lesson puts in your hand.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: suʾāl, slowly, with the catch — su-ʾāl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I have a question" — ʿindī suʾāl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "question and answer" — suʾāl wa-jawāb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think the question is easy" — aʿtaqidu anna as-suʾāla sahl]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does سؤال (suʾāl) mean? ("A question".) Which verb shares its root? (Saʾala, he asked.) What is its partner? (Jawāb, an answer.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
ؤ (hamza on waw) — the catch inside سؤال (suʾāl).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ؤ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ء and و once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say سؤال (suʾāl), "a question". You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes its catch out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ؤ.
ؤ (hamza on waw) — hamza on a wāw seat, the glottal catch written ʾ in this book's romanization.
It is the second letter of سؤال (suʾāl), between س and ا. It is two pieces: a و below, the seat, and the small hamza mark riding on top of it. Like a plain و, it takes the hand of س on its right and does not join to the letter after it, so a gap opens before ال.
The seat does not change the sound. أ, ء and ؤ (hamza on waw) all say the same catch; the seat follows the vowels around it, and here the u before it calls for و.

Writing: ؤ.
Put your pen on ؤ (hamza on waw) and follow its line. Write the و first, then add the hamza above it. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this character or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — find: the seat and the mark of ؤ (hamza on waw) inside سؤال (suʾāl)]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ؤ (hamza on waw) three times, saying the catch as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: سؤ, joined, then the gap, then ال]
[once you have stopped driving — write: سؤال (suʾāl) whole, then سأل beside it: one catch, two seats]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is this — ؤ (hamza on waw)? (Hamza on a wāw seat, a catch.) Which word did it come from? (سؤال, suʾāl, "a question".) Does the seat change the sound? (No: only the spelling.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
لذلك، فـ (li-dhālika, fa-) — so.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I have a question". (ʿIndī suʾāl.) Then "because". (Li-anna.)
[pause 1 second]
Li-anna puts the reason after the claim. A story often wants the opposite order: first the reason, then what came of it.

Grammar Lens: li-dhālika, after the reason.
نمت مبكرا لأنني كنت متعبا. — nimtu mubakkiran li-annanī kuntu mutʿaban — I went to bed early because I was tired.
كنت متعبا، لذلك نمت مبكرا. — kuntu mutʿaban, li-dhālika nimtu mubakkiran — I was tired, so I went to bed early.
The same two facts. With li-anna the claim comes first; with li-dhālika, "therefore, so", the reason comes first and the result follows. The -an on mutʿaban after kuntu is kāna's doing; chapter two hundred and eighty-nine comes back to it.

Grammar Lens: fa-, the short "so".
فـ — fa- — does the same job in one letter. Like wa-, it is written onto the next word, with no space:
تأخر القطار، فأخذنا الحافلة. — taʾakhkhara al-qiṭār, fa-akhadhnā al-ḥāfila — The train was late, so we took the bus.
Wa- adds the next thing; fa- says the next thing came of the first. Li-dhālika is the heavier of the two, and it suits a reason that needs weight: kāna al-imtiḥān ṣaʿban, li-dhālika darastu kathīran, the exam was hard, so I studied a lot.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nimtu mubakkiran li-annanī kuntu mutʿaban]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same facts the other way — kuntu mutʿaban, li-dhālika nimtu mubakkiran]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The train was late, so we took the bus" — taʾakhkhara al-qiṭār, fa-akhadhnā al-ḥāfila]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: an opinion and its result — aʿtaqidu anna al-imtiḥāna ṣaʿb, li-dhālika adrusu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
After li-anna, what comes? (The reason.) After li-dhālika? (The result.) The one-letter "so"? (فـ, fa-, written onto the next word.) What do li-anna and li-dhālika share? (Li-, for.)


Lesson 6 of 6.
Review — an opinion, its reason and what came of it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retell the trip to the sea in three steps, with awwalan, thumma and fī an-nihāya. Then say how it ended, with ʿindamā: ʿindamā waṣaltu, ḥakaytu al-qiṣṣa. And one step with baʿda an: baʿda an akalnā, sabaḥnā.

What you've built.
Four lessons, and an opinion has its parts. A question to open it: عندي سؤال, ʿindī suʾāl. Three ways to start it: fī raʾyī, aʿtaqidu anna, aẓunnu anna. A reason after the claim, with li-anna; a result after the reason, with li-dhālika or fa-.
[pause 2 seconds]
All of it in one breath:
في رأيي القصة جميلة، لذلك أحكيها كثيرا. — fī raʾyī al-qiṣṣa jamīla, li-dhālika aḥkīhā kathīran — In my opinion the story is lovely, so I tell it often.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "What do you think of the story?" — mā raʾyuka fī al-qiṣṣa?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think the number is right" — aʿtaqidu anna ar-raqma ṣaḥīḥ]

[your turn — say: "The information was useful, so I wrote it" — kānat al-maʿlūmāt mufīdatan, fa-katabtuhā]

[your turn — say: an opinion of your own, its reason with li-anna, and a result with li-dhālika]

Wrap-up Recall.

"In my opinion"? (Fī raʾyī.) "I have a question"? (ʿIndī suʾāl.) Which seat carries the hamza in سؤال? (و.) The one-letter "so"? (Fa-.)
