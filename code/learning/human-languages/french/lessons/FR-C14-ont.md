# ils ont, elles ont — they have

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The sixth and last form, and the one that does not resemble the other
five.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-VERB-AVOIR-ONT-07]; assesses=[] -->

- **ils ont** = *eel-ZO(N)*. The **s** of *ils* buzzes across into the vowel.
- `nasal-on` — **ont** is one nasal vowel; the **n** and **t** are both silent.
- **elles ont** = *el-ZO(N)*, identical but for the first vowel.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ont** is Latin **habent**, and it is the odd one out: every other form of
*avoir* has an **a** in it, and this one does not.

There is a trap here worth meeting before you fall into it. **ils ont** is "they
have." **ils sont** is "they **are**." One consonant apart, and both extremely
common. The way to keep them apart is the liaison: *ils **ont*** buzzes — *eel-**z**-o(n)* — while *ils **sont*** hisses, *eel **s**o(n)*. The buzz means
*have*.

That is the whole paradigm. Six forms, and you now have all of them:

**j'ai · tu as · il a · nous avons · vous avez · ils ont**
