Mandarin Chinese, chapter 312: Locked Up.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiānyù — prison.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a warning siren, then the Mandarin for a traffic police officer.

You'll want to know: jiānyù.
jiānyù — "prison".
tā zài jiānyù lǐ zhù le sān nián — He spent three years in prison.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiānyù is built from jiān 'to watch over' and yù 'a jail', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaem and ngjowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to oversee' and explains the shape of the second as two dogs on guard.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

Lesson 3 of 6.
dǎjià — to fight; to get into a fistfight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: dǎjià.
dǎjià — "to fight; to get into a fistfight".
liǎng ge rén zhèngzài ménkǒu dǎjià — Two people were fighting at the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎjià is built from 打 'to hit' and jià 'a frame', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'a roof frame'; how the pair came to mean a fight is not settled, and dǎ is an irregular outcome of taengX.

Lesson 4 of 6.
活该 (huógāi) — it serves them right; serves you right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to impose a fine, then the Mandarin for to get into a fistfight.

You'll want to know: 活该.
活该 — huógāi — "it serves them right; serves you right".
活该他经常偷东西 huógāi, tā jīngcháng tōu dōngxi — Serves him right, he steals all the time.

The word, taken apart.
活该 (huógāi) is built from 活 'alive' and 该 'ought', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwat and koj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'complete' and 'all'; how the pair came to mean 'it serves you right' is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
死活 (sǐhuó) — no matter what; whatever anyone says.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get into a fistfight, then the Mandarin for it serves them right.

You'll want to know: 死活.
死活 — sǐhuó — "no matter what; whatever anyone says".
他死活不去报警 tā sǐhuó bú qù bàojǐng — Whatever anyone said, he wouldn't go to the police.

The word, taken apart.
死活 (sǐhuó) is built from 死 'to die' and 活 'to live', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sijX and hwat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): dead or alive, whatever happens.
