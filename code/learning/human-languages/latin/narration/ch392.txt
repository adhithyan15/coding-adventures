Latin, chapter 392: Taking Care.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
caveō, cavēre — to beware, to be on one's guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for differently, then the Latin for or if.

You'll want to know: caveō, cavēre.
caveō, cavēre — "to beware, to be on one's guard".
In mūrō scrīptum erat: CAVĒ CANEM. On the wall was written: BEWARE OF THE DOG.
caveō, cavēre, cāvī, cautum. Cavēre + accusative, to beware of; cavē nē + subjunctive, take care not to. English caution and caveat, let him beware.

The word, taken apart.
caveō is inherited from Proto-Italic kawēō (EtymDB), from a root meaning "to watch, to heed". English caution and caveat come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cautiō, cautiōnis — caution; a guarantee, a bond.

Warm-up.

cautiō, cautiōnis — "caution; a guarantee, a bond".
Sine cautiōne pecūniam nōn crēdit. He does not lend money without a guarantee.
cautiō, cautiōnis f., from caveō: carefulness, and in law a written security. English caution.

The word, taken apart.
cautiō is the noun built on caveō "to beware, to take care" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English caution comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
necessitās, necessitātis — necessity, need; compulsion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to beware, then the Latin for caution.

You'll want to know: necessitās, necessitātis.
necessitās, necessitātis — "necessity, need; compulsion".
Necessitās mē coēgit. Necessity forced me.
necessitās, necessitātis f., the noun of necesse, needful. Necessitās nōn habet lēgem, necessity knows no law. English necessity.

The word, taken apart.
necessitās is built on necesse "needful" (Lewis & Short), which EtymDB takes as ne- "not" with cessus, from cēdō "to give way": what does not give way. English necessity comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
opportūnus, opportūna, opportūnum — convenient, timely, opportune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for caution, then the Latin for necessity.

You'll want to know: opportūnus, opportūna, opportūnum.
opportūnus, opportūna, opportūnum — "convenient, timely, opportune".
Tempus opportūnum exspectāmus. We are waiting for a convenient time.
opportūnus, opportūna, opportūnum: said first of a wind blowing towards the harbour, then of anything well timed. English opportune.

The word, taken apart.
opportūnus is built from ob "towards" and portus "a harbour" (Lewis & Short): of a wind that drives you into port. English opportune and opportunity come from it.
