Lesson 1 of 5.
ಹೊಡೆದಾಟ (hoḍedāṭa) — a fistfight; a brawl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a preface, then the Kannada for the gist.

You'll want to know: ಹೊಡೆದಾಟ.
ಹೊಡೆದಾಟ — hoḍedāṭa — "a fistfight; a brawl".
ಪಂದ್ಯದ ನಂತರ ಎರಡು ಗುಂಪುಗಳ ನಡುವೆ ಹೊಡೆದಾಟ ಆಯಿತು. pandyada nantara eraḍu gumpugaḷa naḍuve hoḍedāṭa āyitu. After the match there was a fight between two groups.
ಹೊಡೆದು, having hit, and ಆಟ, play: hitting back and forth. The verb is ಹೊಡೆದಾಡು, to come to blows.

The word, taken apart.
ಹೊಡೆದಾಟ (hoḍedāṭa) is a native Dravidian compound: ಹೊಡೆದು, "having struck", from hoḍe, "to strike, to beat" (DEDR 4252), and ಆಟ, "play", from āḍu (DEDR 347).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ಹಿಂಸೆ.
ಹಿಂಸೆ — hiṃse — "violence; cruelty".
ಗಲಭೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ತುಂಬಾ ಹಿಂಸೆ ನಡೆಯಿತು. galabheyalli tumbā hiṃse naḍeyitu. There was a lot of violence in the riot.
ಹಿಂಸೆ (hiṃse) ಕೊಡು is to torment; ಅಹಿಂಸೆ is non-violence.

The word, taken apart.
ಹಿಂಸೆ (hiṃse) is borrowed from Sanskrit hiṃsā, "injury, harm, hurt" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಕೈದಿ (kaidi) — a prisoner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a fistfight, then the Kannada for violence.

You'll want to know: ಕೈದಿ.
ಕೈದಿ — kaidi — "a prisoner".
ರಾತ್ರಿ ಒಬ್ಬ ಕೈದಿ ಸೆರೆಮನೆಯಿಂದ ತಪ್ಪಿಸಿಕೊಂಡನು. rātri obba kaidi seremaneyinda tappisikoṇḍanu. At night a prisoner escaped from the prison.
Plural: ಕೈದಿಗಳು, kaidigaḷu.
A prisoner is held in a ಸೆರೆಮನೆ, a prison; ಕೈದಿ (kaidi) ಸಂಖ್ಯೆ is his number.

The word, taken apart.
ಕೈದಿ (kaidi) is a loan from Arabic qaidī, "a captive, a prisoner", which came through Persian and Hindustani.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ನೂಕು (nūku) — to shove; to push.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for violence, then the Kannada for a prisoner.

You'll want to know: ನೂಕು.
ನೂಕು — nūku — "to shove; to push".
ಜನಸಂದಣಿಯಲ್ಲಿ ಯಾರೋ ನನ್ನನ್ನು ನೂಕಿದರು. janasandaṇiyalli yārō nannannu nūkidaru. Someone shoved me in the crowd.
Past (I …): ನೂಕಿದೆ, nūkide.
Who is pushed takes -ಅನ್ನು. ನೂಕುನುಗ್ಗಲು is a crush of people pushing.

The word, taken apart.
ನೂಕು (nūku) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3722 lists Kannada nūku, "to shove, to push, to thrust aside", beside Telugu nūku.

ರಾತ್ರಿ ಪೊಲೀಸರು ಬೀದಿಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಗಸ್ತು ತಿರುಗುತ್ತಾರೆ. rātri polīsaru bīdigaḷalli gastu tiruguttāre. At night the police patrol the streets.
Police on their round are ಗಸ್ತಿನಲ್ಲಿ, on patrol.

The word, taken apart.
ಗಸ್ತು (gastu) is a loan from Persian gašt, "a walk, a round, a patrol", which came through Hindustani.
