Lesson 1 of 5.
उचितः (ucitaḥ) — proper, fitting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for happy, then the Sanskrit for unhappy.

You'll want to know: उचितः.
उचितः — ucitaḥ — "proper, fitting".

The word, taken apart.
ucita comes from the root √uc as its past participle; MW glosses the root "to take pleasure in, be accustomed, be suitable". What one is accustomed to is customary, and what is customary is proper.

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for unhappy, then the Sanskrit for proper.

You'll want to know: अनुचितः.
अनुचितः — anucitaḥ — "improper".

The word, taken apart.
anucita is an-, "not", with ucita, "proper, fitting", from the root √uc (MW): "improper, wrong". MW keeps it apart from a different anucita, "set in rows", from anu- and √ci.

Lesson 3 of 5.
आवश्यकः (āvaśyakaḥ) — necessary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for proper, then the Sanskrit for improper.

You'll want to know: आवश्यकः.
आवश्यकः — āvaśyakaḥ — "necessary".

The word, taken apart.
āvaśyaka is built on avaśya, "inevitable", the stem of avaśyam, "certainly", which you have already met, with the suffix -ka and a lengthened first vowel (MW). What no will can avoid is necessary.

Lesson 5 of 5.
एकाकी (ekākī) — alone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for necessary, then the Sanskrit for other.

You'll want to know: एकाकी.
एकाकी — ekākī — "alone".

The word, taken apart.
ekākī is the nominative of ekākin, built on eka, "one" (MW): "being one", alone. MW compares eka with Greek oios, "alone", and Gothic ains, "one", and eka lives on as Hindi ek (CDIAL 2462).
