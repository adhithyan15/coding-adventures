Lesson 1 of 7.
做饭 (zuòfàn) — to cook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for flower, then the Mandarin for the sun.

You'll want to know: 做饭.
做饭 — zuòfàn — "to cook".
我妈妈喜欢做饭 wǒ māma xǐhuan zuòfàn — My mum loves cooking.
The new character, in pieces: 做 (zuò) — 亻 on the left, 十 over 口 in the middle, and on the right a four-stroke piece like a hand striking. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
做饭 (zuòfàn) is built from 做 (zuò) 'to do, to make' and 饭 'meal'. 饭 is inherited from Middle Chinese bjonH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); 做 (zuò) has no reading in any dictionary we use, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Script — the person, gǔ, and a striking hand.
做 (zuò).
Three pieces, left to right. Count three, not eleven.
亻, two strokes, as in 你 and 他.
The middle piece, five strokes, read gǔ: 十, a horizontal and a vertical, standing on a small 口.
The right piece, four strokes: a short left-falling stroke, a short horizontal, a long left-falling stroke, and a long stroke falling to the right.
2 + 5 + 4 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 做 (zuò), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 做 (zuò)? (Three.) What sits in the middle? (十 on 口.) How does it end? (With a stroke falling to the right.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 做 (zuò), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Lesson 6 of 7.
好吃 (hǎochī) — tasty; good to eat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for lunch, then the Mandarin for dinner.

You'll want to know: 好吃.
好吃 — hǎochī — "tasty; good to eat".
我妈妈做饭很好吃 wǒ māma zuòfàn hěn hǎochī — My mum's cooking is delicious.

The word, taken apart.
好吃 (hǎochī) is built from 好 'good' and 吃 'to eat': good to eat. 好 is inherited from Middle Chinese xawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); 吃 is probably the khek the dictionary writes with another character and glosses 'to eat', though chī is not the sound khek would regularly give.
