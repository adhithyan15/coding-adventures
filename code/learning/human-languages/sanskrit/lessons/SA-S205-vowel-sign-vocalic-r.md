# ◌ृ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-110, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-113, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-204] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one, the ones you have already met: र · प · य. Say what each of them does.

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. One only — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ृ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-205]; assesses=[] -->

**◌ृ** — *ṛ*.

It is a **vowel sign**. It is not a letter and never stands alone: it attaches to a consonant and **replaces** the *a* built into it. Replaces, not adds — the *a* is gone.

Where it sits: a small hook below (the vocalic r, common in Sanskrit).

Worked through: **न** + **◌ृ** = **नृ** — *nṛ*.

You already say this word, and ◌ृ is one of the shapes inside it — the rest of its shapes are still ahead of you, so here the character stands on its own:

- *ahaṁ saṁskṛtaṁ vadāmi* — I speak Sanskrit

## Writing: ◌ृ — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-205] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ◌ृ yourself —
slowly, and larger than it is printed.

> The strip shows **where to start the character and which way to travel**, in
> one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school,
> so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the
> only one.
