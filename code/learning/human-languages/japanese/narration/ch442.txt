Japanese, chapter 442: Inside the Body.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
けっかん (kekkan) — a blood vessel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a lazy person, then the Japanese for to fool around.

You'll want to know: けっかん.
けっかん — kekkan — "a blood vessel".
いしゃは あしの けっかんを しらべました。 — isha wa ashi no kekkan o shirabemashita — "The doctor examined the blood vessels in my leg."

The word, taken apart.
けっかん (kekkan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ke, "blood" (Middle Chinese xwet) and kan, "tube" (kwanX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

The word, taken apart.
せぼね (sebone) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: se, "back" and bone, "bone (hone)" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
