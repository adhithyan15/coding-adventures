Urdu, chapter 32: Which One: the Ordinals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
پہلا (pehlā) — first.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read ۱, ۵ and ۱۰, then say ek.

You'll want to know first — one word.
پہلا — pehlā — first.
Counting says how many. Ordering says which one — and a floor number, a date on a notice, a queue, a chapter heading and "the first bus" all need the second. Twelve numbers do not give you one of them.

The word, taken apart — not built from one.
پہلا (pehlā) does NOT come from ایک. It descends through Prakrit from Sanskrit prathama, "first, foremost," a separate word.
English does the same thing: first is not built from one, and second is not built from two, while third, fourth and fifth fall into line behind their numbers. Urdu breaks in the same place, for the same reason — the words people say most often wear away from their families first.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: ek counts, pehlā orders]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — compare: English one/first breaks in the same place]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 5.
دوسرا and تیسرا — second, third, and the ending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say pehlā and why it is not built from ek. Then say do and tīn.

You'll want to know first — two words and an ending.
دوسرا — dūsrā — second تیسرا — tīsrā — third.
Look at what came back. دو is inside dūsrā; تین is inside tīsrā. The number returns, and an ending -rā is added to it.
So the series breaks at first and mends at second — one word out of line, then a pattern. English breaks one word later and mends at third; the shape is the same and only the seam moved.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — split: do + -srā, tīn + -srā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: pehlā, dūsrā, tīsrā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 3 of 5.
چوتھا (chauthā) — fourth, and the ending that runs all the way up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say dūsrā and tīsrā, and the number inside each.

You'll want to know first — one word, then the rule above it.
چوتھا — chauthā — fourth.
چار is still audible in it, bent at the vowel.
Above four the language settles. From five upward Urdu adds -vāṅ to the plain number and stops bending: pānchvāṅ, "fifth"; dasvāṅ, "tenth"; bīsvāṅ, "twentieth".
So the whole series is three irregular forms at the bottom — pehlā, dūsrā, tīsrā, with chauthā half a step out — and one ending for everything above them. Twelve numbers and one ending is twelve ordinals.

Guided Practice.
[your turn — say: chauthā — fourth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: pānch + -vāṅ becomes pānchvāṅ, fifth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: das + -vāṅ becomes dasvāṅ, tenth]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Wiktionary: چوتھا (chauthā); Omniglot, Numbers in Urdu.

Lesson 4 of 5.
Payoff — which one.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count aloud to ten, read ۷, ۲۰ and ۱۰۰, and say which way the digits ran.

Guided Practice.
Say the first four in order, then out of order.
Build, do not recall: fifth, seventh, tenth, twentieth, hundredth.
Say which of the series is not built from its number at all, and say what English does in the same place.
Use one: name the floor a room is on, and the day of a month a notice gives.
Say what the ordinals still cannot reach, and why — the same nine irregular teens that the cardinals could not reach either.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Omniglot, Numbers in Urdu.
[question — say your answer, then pause 16 seconds]
Say first, second, third, fourth in Urdu.


Lesson 5 of 5.
Close the numeral slice — the whole numeral slice, retrieved once more and closed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the first four ordinals, then count one to five.

Guided Practice.
Everything this slice added, with nothing in front of you.
Count aloud one to ten, then say bīs and sau.
Write the ten figures shuffled, checking ۴, ۵ and ۶ against the models rather than against memory.
Say the first four ordinals, then build fifth, tenth and twentieth.
Say the two things this slice deliberately did NOT give you, and why: the nine irregular teens, and the ordinals built on them.
Then say what the whole slice cost the hand: no new letter at all. Every sign in every one of the twelve number words was already on the page; only the ten figures were new. And say which road all twelve came by.
