Gujarati, chapter 471: How People Look.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વાન (vān) — complexion; skin colour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lock of hair, then the Gujarati for the parting of the hair.

You'll want to know: વાન.
વાન — vān — "complexion; skin colour".
દાદા કહે છે કે મારો વાન મારી માતાનો છે. dādā kahe che ke māro vān mārī mātāno che. Grandfather says that I have my mother's complexion.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વાન (vān) is inherited from Sanskrit varṇa, "appearance, colour"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "colour" (CDIAL 11338).

Lesson 3 of 5.
દેહ (deh) — the body (in careful speech); build.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for complexion, then the Gujarati for height.

You'll want to know: દેહ.
દેહ — deh — "the body (in careful speech); build".
કસરતને લીધે અરુણનો દેહ મજબૂત છે. kasratne līdhe Aruṇno deh majbūt che. Thanks to exercise, Arun has a strong build.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
દેહ (deh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit deha, "the body", as Monier-Williams records it, from a root meaning "to mould, to fashion"; everyday Gujarati says શરીર (śarīr).

Lesson 4 of 5.
વળિયાં (vaḷiyā̃) — wrinkles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for height, then the Gujarati for the body.

You'll want to know: વળિયાં.
વળિયાં — vaḷiyā̃ — "wrinkles".
દાદીના ચહેરા પર વળિયાં છે. dādīnā caherā par vaḷiyā̃ che. There are wrinkles on my grandmother's face.
A neuter noun, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
વળિયાં (vaḷiyā̃) is inherited from Sanskrit vali, "a fold of skin"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word as "wrinkles in a garment" (CDIAL 11412), and Gujarati uses it for the folds of the skin too.
