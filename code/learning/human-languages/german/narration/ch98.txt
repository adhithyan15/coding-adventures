Lesson 1 of 5.
die Bibliothek — a library.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a village, then the German for the police.

You'll want to know: die Bibliothek.
die Bibliothek — "a library".

The word, taken apart.
Bibliothek from Latin bibliothēca from Greek bibliothḗkē, built from biblíon, "book", and thḗkē, "store" (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the police, then the German for a library.

You'll want to know: das Fahrrad.
das Fahrrad — "a bicycle".

The word, taken apart.
Fahrrad is fahren, "to travel", + the word for "wheel", a cousin of Latin rota, "wheel" (EtymDB 2.1 lists it beside English rotary): a wheel you ride.

The word, taken apart.
Haltestelle is the verb "to hold, to stop" (Old High German haltan from Proto-Germanic haldaną, English hold, EtymDB 2.1) + the word for "place": the stopping place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Kleidung — clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a bicycle, then the German for a bus or tram stop.

You'll want to know: die Kleidung.
die Kleidung — "clothes".

The word, taken apart.
Kleidung from Middle High German kleidunge, from the verb "to clothe" + -ung (EtymDB 2.1). Its root is the word for "dress", Proto-Germanic klaiþą, "cloth", English cloth.

Hemd from Middle High German hemde from Old High German hemidi from Proto-Germanic hamiþiją, "clothing, shirt" — inherited; Dutch hemd is the same word. (EtymDB 2.1).
