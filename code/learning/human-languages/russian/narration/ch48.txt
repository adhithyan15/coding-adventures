Russian, chapter 48: House, Room, Kitchen, Window, Door.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
дом (dom) — a house, home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a mushroom, then the Russian for a glass.

You'll want to know: дом.
дом (dom) — "a house, home".

The word, taken apart.
дом (dom) from Proto-Slavic domŭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Sanskrit dámas, Greek dómos and Latin domus, all "house", from PIE dem-, "to build".

Lesson 2 of 5.
комната (kómnata) — a room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a glass, then the Russian for a house.

You'll want to know: комната.
комната (kómnata) — "a room".

The word, taken apart.
комната (kómnata) from Latin (camera) camīnāta, "a room with a stove", from camīnus, "furnace". Vasmer takes the initial stress as a sign it came through Old High German chemināta; Jakobson derived it from Czech.

Lesson 3 of 5.
кухня (kúkhnya) — a kitchen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a house, then the Russian for a room.

You'll want to know: кухня.
кухня (kúkhnya) — "a kitchen".

The word, taken apart.
кухня (kúkhnya) came, from 1717, through Polish kuchnia and Czech kuchyně from Old High German kuchina, from Latin coquīna, "kitchen"; before that Russians said поварня (Vasmer).

Lesson 4 of 5.
окно (oknó) — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a room, then the Russian for a kitchen.

You'll want to know: окно.
окно (oknó) — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
окно (oknó) from Proto-Slavic okŭno, built on око, "eye" (Vasmer): a window is the eye of the house, as English window is Old Norse vindauga, "wind-eye".

You'll want to know: дверь.
дверь (dver) — "a door".

The word, taken apart.
дверь (dver) from Proto-Slavic dvĭrĭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian dùrys, Gothic daúr, Greek thýra and Latin forēs: English door is a cousin, from PIE dʰwer-, "door".
