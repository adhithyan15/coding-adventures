Telugu, chapter 547: Housework.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
శుభ్రపరచు (śubhraparacu) — to cleanse; to clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a small water pot, then the Telugu for a cockroach.

You'll want to know: శుభ్రపరచు.
శుభ్రపరచు — śubhraparacu — "to cleanse; to clean".
పండుగకు ముందు ఇల్లంతా శుభ్రపరిచాము. paṇḍugaku mundu illantā śubhraparicāmu. Before the festival we cleaned the whole house.
Past (I …): శుభ్రపరిచాను, śubhraparicānu.
Formal; శుభ్రం చేయు in speech.

The word, taken apart.
శుభ్రపరచు (śubhraparacu) is built from శుభ్రం, "clean", from Sanskrit śubhra, "bright, white" (CDIAL 12539) and paracu, "to cause, to make", as in ఆశ్చర్యపరచు.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ఉతుకు (utuku) — to wash clothes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a cockroach, then the Telugu for to cleanse.

You'll want to know: ఉతుకు.
ఉతుకు — utuku — "to wash clothes".
ఆదివారం నేను బట్టలు ఉతుకుతాను. ādivāraṁ nēnu baṭṭalu utukutānu. On Sundays I wash the clothes.
Past (I …): ఉతికాను, utikānu.
For clothes; కడుగు is for dishes, hands and floors.

The word, taken apart.
ఉతుకు (utuku) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2667 lists Telugu utuku, "to wash, as clothes", with Kolami sutk-.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ఆరవేయు (āravēyu) — to hang to dry; to put out to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to cleanse, then the Telugu for to wash clothes.

You'll want to know: ఆరవేయు.
ఆరవేయు — āravēyu — "to hang to dry; to put out to dry".
ఉతికిన బట్టలను ఎండలో ఆరవేశాను. utikina baṭṭalanu eṇḍalō āravēśānu. I hung the washed clothes out in the sun to dry.
Past (I …): ఆరవేశాను, āravēśānu.
ఆరు, to dry, + వేయు, to put.

The word, taken apart.
ఆరవేయు (āravēyu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 404 lists Telugu āṟu, "to cool; to dry, to become dry"; vēyu, "to put", makes it something you do to the clothes.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to wash clothes, then the Telugu for to hang to dry.

You'll want to know: దులుపు.
దులుపు — dulupu — "to shake out; to dust".
అమ్మ దుప్పట్లు దులిపి ఎండలో వేసింది. amma duppaṭlu dulipi eṇḍalō vēsindi. Mum shook out the blankets and put them in the sun.
Past (I …): దులిపాను, dulipānu.
Shaking the dust off cloth or a mat.

The word, taken apart.
దులుపు (dulupu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3359 lists Telugu dulupu, "to shake so as to remove dust, to shake off", in the article of Tamil tuḷaṅku, "to move, to shake".

Lesson 5 of 5.
తగిలించు (tagiliñcu) — to hang up; to hook on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to hang to dry, then the Telugu for to shake out.

You'll want to know: తగిలించు.
తగిలించు — tagiliñcu — "to hang up; to hook on".
అతను చొక్కాను మేకుకు తగిలించాడు. atanu cokkānu mēkuku tagiliñcāḍu. He hung his shirt on a nail.
Past (I …): తగిలించాను, tagiliñcānu.
-కి తగిలించు (tagiliñcu), to hang on a peg or a nail.

The word, taken apart.
తగిలించు (tagiliñcu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3004 lists Telugu tagulu, "to touch, to catch", and tagilincu, "to cause to touch, to catch or to stick".
