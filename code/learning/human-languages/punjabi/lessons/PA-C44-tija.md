## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-LEX-TIJA-01]; assesses=[PA-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE, PA-LEX-DUJA-01] -->

> **ਤੀਜਾ** — *tījā* — **third**

Put the two you now have beside the numbers under them:

| how many | which one in the line |
|---|---|
| **ਦੋ** (*do*) | **ਦੂਜਾ** (*dūjā*) |
| **ਤਿੰਨ** (*tinn*) | **ਤੀਜਾ** (*tījā*) |

The first letter survives in both, and the same **-ਜਾ** sits on the end of each.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-TIJA-01, PA-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-SET-01] -->

That shared **-ਜਾ** is not a Punjabi invention and it is not a rule you can use.
Wiktionary traces **ਦੂਜਾ** to Sanskrit *dvitīya* and **ਤੀਜਾ** to Sanskrit
*tṛtīya* — one Sanskrit ending, **-tīya**, worn down to **-ਜਾ** in two words
that travelled together.

Two words is a resemblance, not a pattern, and this book will not call it one.
Watch what the next number does with it.
