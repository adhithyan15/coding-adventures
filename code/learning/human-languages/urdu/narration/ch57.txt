Lesson 1 of 5.
نارنگی (nārangī) — an orange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for honey, then the Urdu for grapes.

You'll want to know: نارنگی.
نارنگی — nārangī — "an orange".

The word, taken apart.
نارنگی (nārangī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nāraṅga, "orange tree" (CDIAL 7073).

Lesson 2 of 5.
لیموں (lemū̃) — a lemon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for grapes, then the Urdu for an orange.

You'll want to know: لیموں.
لیموں — lemū̃ — "a lemon".

The word, taken apart.
لیموں (lemū̃) belongs with Hindi nīmbū, "lime", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit nimbū, "the lime", a word it takes from an Austroasiatic language (CDIAL 7247). CDIAL lists lemū, "lemon", there too, and puts its e down to a different source of the loan.

ناشتہ — nāshtā — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
ناشتہ (nāshtā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian nāshtā, "not having eaten". The meal is named for the fast it ends.

Lesson 4 of 5.
کافی (kāfī) — coffee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a lemon, then the Urdu for breakfast.

You'll want to know: کافی.
کافی — kāfī — "coffee".

The word, taken apart.
کافی (kāfī) is a loan from English coffee. English took the word, through other European languages and Turkish, from Arabic qahwa. It only looks like kāfī, "enough", which is a different Arabic word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
گھر (ghar) — a house, home.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for breakfast, then the Urdu for coffee.

You'll want to know: گھر.
گھر — ghar — "a house, home".

The word, taken apart.
گھر (ghar) is inherited from ghara, "house". It is usually taken as a popular form of Sanskrit gṛha, "house", but CDIAL calls that link phonetically difficult (CDIAL 4428).
