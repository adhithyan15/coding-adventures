Malayalam, chapter 349: A Witness to the War.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
സാക്ഷി (sākṣi) — a witness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a principal, then the Malayalam for tuition.

You'll want to know: സാക്ഷി.
സാക്ഷി — sākṣi — "a witness".
അപകടം കണ്ട സാക്ഷി പോലീസിനോട് സംസാരിച്ചു. apakaṭaṁ kaṇṭa sākṣi pōlīsinōṭŭ saṁsāriccu. The witness who saw the accident spoke to the police.
Plural: സാക്ഷികൾ. To give evidence is സാക്ഷി (sākṣi) പറയുക, literally to say as a witness.

The word, taken apart.
സാക്ഷി (sākṣi) is borrowed from Sanskrit sākṣin, "a witness" (CDIAL 13321). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word apart as sa- "with" and akṣi "eye": the witness is the one who was there with his own eyes.

Lesson 2 of 5.
യുദ്ധം (yuddhaṁ) — a war; a battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for tuition, then the Malayalam for a witness.

You'll want to know: യുദ്ധം.
യുദ്ധം — yuddhaṁ — "a war; a battle".
ആ യുദ്ധം വളരെ കാലം മുൻപ് നടന്നു. ā yuddhaṁ vaḷare kālaṁ munpŭ naṭannu. That war took place a very long time ago.
With ചെയ്യുക it makes a verb: യുദ്ധം (yuddhaṁ) ചെയ്യുക, to fight a war.

The word, taken apart.
യുദ്ധം (yuddhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit yuddha, "fought", and as a noun "a battle", the past participle of the root yudh, "to fight" (CDIAL 10499). Like many Sanskrit nouns in -a, it ends in -ം in Malayalam.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ലേഖകൻ (lēkhakan) — a correspondent; a writer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a witness, then the Malayalam for a war.

You'll want to know: ലേഖകൻ.
ലേഖകൻ — lēkhakan — "a correspondent; a writer".
പത്രത്തിന്റെ ലേഖകൻ ഗ്രാമത്തിൽ എത്തി. patrattinṟe lēkhakan grāmattil etti. The newspaper's correspondent reached the village.
A woman writer or correspondent is ലേഖിക. A correspondent writes about events: ലേഖകൻ (lēkhakan) ആഘോഷം കണ്ടു, the correspondent saw the celebration.

The word, taken apart.
ലേഖകൻ (lēkhakan) is borrowed from Sanskrit lekhaka, "a writer, a scribe, a clerk" (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam masculine ending -ൻ. It belongs to the same family as ലേഖനം (lēkhanaṁ), "an article".

Lesson 4 of 5.
സംഭവിക്കുക (sambhavikkuka) — to happen; to occur.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a war, then the Malayalam for a correspondent.

You'll want to know: സംഭവിക്കുക.
സംഭവിക്കുക — sambhavikkuka — "to happen; to occur".
ഇന്നലെ രാത്രി എന്താണ് സംഭവിച്ചത്? innale rātri entāṇŭ sambhaviccatŭ? What happened last night?
Past: സംഭവിച്ചു, sambhaviccu.
The person something happens to takes -ക്ക് or -ന്: അവന് എന്ത് സംഭവിച്ചു? What happened to him?

The word, taken apart.
സംഭവിക്കുക (sambhavikkuka) is built from Sanskrit saṁbhava, "origin, coming into being" (CDIAL 12963), with the Malayalam verb ending -ിക്കുക. സംഭവം (sambhavaṁ), "an event", is the noun of the same word: what happens is a സംഭവം.

Lesson 5 of 5.
പ്രഖ്യാപിക്കുക (prakhyāpikkuka) — to announce; to declare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a correspondent, then the Malayalam for to happen.

You'll want to know: പ്രഖ്യാപിക്കുക.
പ്രഖ്യാപിക്കുക — prakhyāpikkuka — "to announce; to declare".
പ്രിൻസിപ്പൽ നാളെ അവധി പ്രഖ്യാപിച്ചു. prinsippal nāḷe avadhi prakhyāpiccu. The principal announced a holiday for tomorrow.
Past: പ്രഖ്യാപിച്ചു, prakhyāpiccu.
A formal word, common in the news. The noun is പ്രഖ്യാപനം, an announcement. ആഘോഷത്തിന് അവധി പ്രഖ്യാപിച്ചു: a holiday was declared for the celebration.

The word, taken apart.
പ്രഖ്യാപിക്കുക (prakhyāpikkuka) is built from Sanskrit prakhyāpana, "making known, a report" (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam verb ending -ിക്കുക. The Sanskrit noun comes from the verb pra-khyā, "to announce, to proclaim".
