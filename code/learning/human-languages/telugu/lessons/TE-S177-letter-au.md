# ఔ (au) — the letter that opens ఔషధం

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-165, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-133, TE-LEX-C321-AUSADHAM-01, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-175] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: write **ఒ** and **ఓ** once each. Then the sign **ౌ**, which you know from
**గౌరవం**, "respect".

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ఔషధం** (*auṣadhaṁ*). This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

## Script you'll notice: ఔ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-177]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C321-AUSADHAM-01] -->

**ఔ** — *au*, at the start of a word: one vowel that glides from *a* toward *u*, the way the middle of English
*now* does.

After a consonant, *au* is the sign **ౌ**: **గౌరవం**. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own,
**ఔ**. Few words open with it, and most of them come from Sanskrit.

In print **ఔ** is built on the body of **ఒ**, with a long stroke laid along its top that runs out to the right and
ends in a small loop.

## Writing: ఔ — copy the printed shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-177] -->

Put your pen beside **ఔ** and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Write the body of **ఒ** the way you
learned it, then copy the long top stroke and its loop.

> This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.
