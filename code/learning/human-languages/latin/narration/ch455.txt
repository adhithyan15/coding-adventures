Latin, chapter 455: To catch sight of, To come near, To turn back, To delay, To stop for a while.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cōnspicor, cōnspicārī — to catch sight of, to see.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to go round, then the Latin for a detour.

You'll want to know: cōnspicor, cōnspicārī.
cōnspicor, cōnspicārī — "to catch sight of, to see".
Tandem viātōrēs mūrōs urbis cōnspicantur. At last the travellers catch sight of the city walls.
Deponent: cōnspicor, cōnspicārī, cōnspicātus sum, with an accusative. A related active verb is cōnspiciō.

The word, taken apart.
cōnspicor is built from con- and the root of speciō 'to look' (Lewis & Short), the root seen in spectō 'to watch'. EtymDB lists it but gives no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
accēdō, accēdere — to come near, to approach; to be added.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a detour, then the Latin for to catch sight of.

You'll want to know: accēdō, accēdere.
accēdō, accēdere — "to come near, to approach; to be added".
Nāvis ad portum accēdit. The ship comes near the harbour.
accēdō, accēdere, accessī, accessum, with ad and the accusative. English access and accede come from it.

The word, taken apart.
accēdō is built from ad- 'towards' and cēdō 'to go' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English access and accede come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
revertor, revertī — to turn back, to return.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to catch sight of, then the Latin for to come near.

You'll want to know: revertor, revertī.
revertor, revertī — "to turn back, to return".
Via clausa est; itaque viātōrēs domum revertuntur. The road is closed; so the travellers turn back home.
Deponent in the present: revertor, revertī, but the perfect is active: revertī, reversus as a participle. English revert comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
revertor is built from re- 'back' and vertō 'to turn' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short, under revertō). English revert comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
moror, morārī — to delay, to linger; to hold up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to come near, then the Latin for to turn back.

You'll want to know: moror, morārī.
moror, morārī — "to delay, to linger; to hold up".
Viātor in caupōnā nōn morātur, quod nox venit. The traveller does not linger at the inn, because night is coming.
Deponent: moror, morārī, morātus sum, from mora 'delay'. Nōlī morārī, don't hang about. English moratorium comes from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
moror is built on mora 'delay' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), a regular first-conjugation deponent made from the noun.
