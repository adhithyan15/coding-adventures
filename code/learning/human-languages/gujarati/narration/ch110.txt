5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
પેટી (peṭī) — a box.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for rubbish, then the Gujarati for a basket.

You'll want to know: પેટી.
પેટી (peṭī) — "a box".

The word, taken apart.
પેટી (peṭī) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it under peṭṭa, one of several forms beside Sanskrit piṭaka, "basket, box", which it calls non-Aryan (CDIAL 8164).

Lesson 3 of 5.
મીણબત્તી (mīṇbattī) — a candle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a box, then the Gujarati for a jar.

You'll want to know: મીણબત્તી.
મીણબત્તી (mīṇbattī) — "a candle".

The word, taken apart.
મીણબત્તી (mīṇbattī) is "wax-wick": mīṇ, "beeswax", from the lexical Sanskrit madana, "beeswax" (CDIAL 9778), and battī, "wick", the Hindi form of Sanskrit varti, "wick" (CDIAL 11359).

Lesson 4 of 5.
તકિયો (takiyo) — a pillow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a jar, then the Gujarati for a candle.

You'll want to know: તકિયો.
તકિયો (takiyo) — "a pillow".

The word, taken apart.
તકિયો (takiyo) is a loan, usually traced to Persian takya, "pillow, support", a word Persian took from Arabic. Its Arabic root is w-k-ʾ, "to lean" (Lane).
