Lesson 1 of 5.
el divorcio — the divorce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the husband, then the Spanish for to toast.

You'll want to know: el divorcio.
el divorcio — "the divorce".
El divorcio fue difícil para los niños. The divorce was hard for the children.

The word, taken apart.
divorcio is a learned borrowing from Latin dīvortium 'a separation, a divorce' (EtymDB), built on dīvertere 'to turn aside'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
separarse — to separate, to split up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to toast, then the Spanish for the divorce.

You'll want to know: separarse.
separarse — "to separate, to split up".
Se separaron después de diez años. They split up after ten years.
Without se: separar is to separate two things.

The word, taken apart.
separarse is a learned borrowing from Latin sēparāre 'to set apart' (Lewis & Short), built on sē- 'apart' and parāre 'to make ready'.
