Lesson 1 of 5.
नीचः (nīcaḥ) — low.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for full, then the Sanskrit for empty.

You'll want to know: नीचः.
नीचः — nīcaḥ — "low".

The word, taken apart.
nīca is built from ni "down" and the root √añc "bend, turn" (MW; CDIAL 7540): "turned downward", so "low". It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi nīcā "low" (CDIAL 7540).

Lesson 2 of 5.
उन्नतः (unnataḥ) — high, tall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for empty, then the Sanskrit for low.

You'll want to know: उन्नतः.
उन्नतः — unnataḥ — "high, tall".

The word, taken apart.
unnata is the past participle of ud-√nam "bend upward, rise", the root √nam "bend, bow" with ud "up" (MW; CDIAL 2099): "raised, high, tall". It is the same root as namas in namaste, the greeting you already know.

The word, taken apart.
uḍḍayate comes from ud-√ḍī "fly up, soar", the root √ḍī "fly" with ud "up" (MW; CDIAL 1697). It lives on as Hindi uṛnā and Marathi uḍṇe "to fly" (CDIAL 1697).

Lesson 4 of 5.
तरति (tarati) — swims, crosses.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for high, then the Sanskrit for flies.

You'll want to know: तरति.
तरति — tarati — "swims, crosses".

The word, taken apart.
tarati comes from the root √tṝ "cross over", and then "float, swim" (CDIAL 5702). The root is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB), and MW sets it beside Latin trans "across" and terminus "boundary". It lives on as Hindi tarnā "to cross" and Punjabi tarnā "to swim" (CDIAL 5702).

Lesson 5 of 5.
कूर्दति (kūrdati) — jumps.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for flies, then the Sanskrit for swims.

You'll want to know: कूर्दति.
कूर्दति — kūrdati — "jumps".

The word, taken apart.
kūrdati "leaps, jumps" is probably borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 3412, after Burrow, compares a Dravidian verb for springing. It lives on as Hindi kūdnā "to jump" (CDIAL 3412).
