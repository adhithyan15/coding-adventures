Latin, chapter 164: A Notice, an Inscription, a Price, For Sale, Goods.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
titulus, titulī — a notice, a label; a title.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to decide, then the Latin for to foresee.

You'll want to know: titulus, titulī.
titulus, titulī — "a notice, a label; a title". A shop or house could carry one by its door. The famous Pompeian doorway warning is a mosaic, but it reads like one: Cavē canem. — "Beware of the dog." English title comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 7.
īnscrīptiō, īnscrīptiōnis — an inscription.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Latin for to foresee, then the Latin for a notice.

You'll want to know: īnscrīptiō, īnscrīptiōnis.
īnscrīptiō, īnscrīptiōnis — "an inscription". Read the stone: SPQR is Senātus Populusque Rōmānus, "the Senate and People of Rome". It is in + scrībō: writing cut into something.

Lesson 5 of 7.
merx, mercis — goods, merchandise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a price, then the Latin for for sale.

You'll want to know: merx, mercis.
merx, mercis — "goods, merchandise". What a shop sells: mercēs in the plural. That is not the same word as mercēs, mercēdis, "wages". English merchant and commerce come from it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Nothing? (nihil.) Nobody? (nēmō, nēminis.) No? (nūllus, nūlla, nūllum.) Which? (uter, utra, utrum.) And not? (neque.) Recently? (nūper.) The day before yesterday? (nudiustertius.) Once? (ōlim.) Before? (anteā.) To happen? (accidō, accidere.)
