# raccontare — to tell a story, which is to count it out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-POI-01, IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three pieces are in your hands: the imperfetto to hold the scene
still, *prima*, *poi* and *alla fine* to order the events, and *perché* to
explain one of them. This lesson adds no fourth piece. It puts the three
together, under the verb that names the act.

## Grammar Lens: the backbone of a told story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-STORIA-IN-ORDINE-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C136-VERBI136-01, IT-LEX-ERA-01, IT-LEX-FACEVA-01, IT-GRAMMAR-ALLA-FINE-02] -->

**raccontare**, "to tell (a story)", is a plain *-are* verb you have had
since chapter one hundred and thirty-six. Here is what it tells, once it has
both pasts to work with:

> **Era** sabato. **Faceva** caldo e **c'era** il sole. **Prima** **sono
> andato** al mercato. **Poi** **ho incontrato** un amico, e **abbiamo
> parlato** molto. **Alla fine** **sono tornato** a casa, **perché ero**
> stanco.
>
> *It was Saturday. It was hot and the sun was out. First I went to the market.
> Then I met a friend, and we talked a lot. In the end I went home, because I
> was tired.*

Follow the two pasts as they divide the work. *Era*, *faceva*, *c'era*:
imperfetto, the scene, held still. *Sono andato*, *ho incontrato*, *abbiamo
parlato*, *sono tornato*: passato prossimo, the events dropped in. *Prima*,
*poi* and *alla fine* put the events in order; *perché* explains the last one,
and its reason, *ero stanco*, is scene again. Every word of it is one this book
has taught you.

## The word, taken apart: counting it out
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-RACCONTARE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**raccontare** is built on **contare**, "to count", which you met in chapter
two hundred and six. *Contare* is inherited from Latin *computāre*, "to reckon
up" (EtymDB 2.1), and *computāre* is *com-*, "together", plus *putāre*, "to
settle a reckoning" (Lewis & Short). The *ra-* in front is Italian's *re-* plus
*a-*: to count something out for someone. EtymDB 2.1 has no row for
*raccontare* itself, so that last step is the usual reading of its parts.

English has the same picture. To **recount** a story is to count it out
(neither source traces that English word), and **tell** once meant "to count"
(EtymDB 2.1) — a bank **teller** still does.
