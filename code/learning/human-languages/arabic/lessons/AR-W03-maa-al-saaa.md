# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-WA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You wrote your own line with as long as you liked.

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-WA-01, AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What the target publishes.** The writing section runs **20 to 25 minutes** and
holds **three** prompts. Those numbers come out of `task-shapes/a1.json`.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the part that makes this different from every other timed paper,
and it is worth knowing before the clock starts rather than during it: **the
test is adaptive**. It chooses your next prompt from how you answered the last
one. Two candidates sitting the same test do not see the same questions.

[PAUSE 2s] Three things follow, and they change how to practise rather than how
to write:

[PAUSE 2s] **One — there is no paper to rehearse.** Memorising an answer buys
nothing, because the question it answered may never be asked of you.

[PAUSE 2s] **Two — a weak answer makes the next question easier**, and an easier
question caps the level you can be reported at. Guessing short to save time is
not neutral here; it lowers the ceiling of everything after it.

[PAUSE 2s] **Three — the result is not a pass mark.** This test reports a
separate benchmark level per skill and publishes no pass or fail. The A1 claim
in this book is the project's own mapping, stated in the contract, not
something the test awards.

[PAUSE 3s] Write for **twenty minutes** to one prompt of your choosing, at the
top of what you can actually do. Stop when the time is up.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-LAKIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Did you write at your ceiling, or somewhere comfortably below it?

[PAUSE 2s] On a fixed paper the difference costs you a few marks. On an adaptive
one it costs you the questions you never got to see.
