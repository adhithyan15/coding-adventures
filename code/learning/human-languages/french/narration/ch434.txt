French, chapter 434: Trouver son chemin.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
orienter — to get one’s bearings (mostly reflexive: s’orienter); to guide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the compensation, then the French for to pay damages to.

You'll want to know: orienter.
orienter — "to get one’s bearings (mostly reflexive: s’orienter); to guide".
Dans la vieille ville, je m'oriente mal. In the old town I find it hard to get my bearings.
Mostly reflexive: s'orienter. Le sens de l'orientation — a sense of direction.

The word, taken apart.
orienter is built on orient 'east', from Latin oriēns 'rising', said of the sun, from orīrī 'to rise' (Lewis & Short): first to turn towards the east.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la boussole — the compass.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: la boussole.
la boussole — "the compass".
En forêt, il garde toujours sa boussole sur lui. In the woods he keeps his compass on him the whole time.
Perdre la boussole — to lose one's head.

The word, taken apart.
boussole was borrowed from Italian bussola 'compass' (EtymDB), from Late Latin buxula 'small box', built on buxus 'box tree'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le repère — the landmark, the reference point.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the compass, then the French for to spot.

You'll want to know: le repère.
le repère — "the landmark, the reference point".
L'église est un bon repère pour retrouver l'hôtel. The church is a good landmark for finding the hotel again.
Un point de repère — a landmark.

The word, taken apart.
repère is usually explained as a spelling of repaire 'den, resting place' reshaped after Latin reperīre 'to find' (Lewis & Short); the details are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
égarer — to mislay; to get lost (s’égarer).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to spot, then the French for the landmark.

You'll want to know: égarer.
égarer — "to mislay; to get lost (s’égarer)".
Sans carte, ils se sont égarés dans les petites rues. Without a map they got lost in the little streets.
S'égarer — to lose your way; égarer ses clés — to mislay your keys.

The word, taken apart.
égarer comes from Old French esgarer (EtymDB), which is usually traced to a Frankish verb meaning 'to watch over', with es- 'away'.
