Latin, chapter 298: Quid ēvēnit.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ēveniō, ēvenīre — to happen, to turn out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to complete, then the Latin for to break off.

You'll want to know: ēveniō, ēvenīre.
ēveniō, ēvenīre — "to happen, to turn out".
Mihi rēs mīra herī ēvēnit. A strange thing happened to me yesterday.
ēveniō, ēvenīre, ēvēnī, ēventum, often with the dative: mihi ēvēnit. Its noun ēventus is taught.

The word, taken apart.
ēveniō is built on veniō, "to come", with ex-, "out" (EtymDB): to come out, to turn out. English event comes from its participle.

Lesson 2 of 6.
fīō, fierī — to become; to happen, to be done.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to break off, then the Latin for to happen.

You'll want to know: fīō, fierī.
fīō, fierī — "to become; to happen, to be done".
Quid hīc fit? Cūr omnēs currunt? What is going on here? Why is everyone running?
fīō, fierī, factus sum. It serves as the passive of faciō: fit 'it is done, it happens'.

The word, taken apart.
fīō is an inherited verb that serves as the passive of faciō (Lewis & Short). It is usually traced to the Proto-Indo-European root bʰuH-, "to become, to be", which also gives the Latin perfect fuī and English be.

Lesson 3 of 6.
orior, orīrī — to rise; to arise, to begin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to happen, then the Latin for to become.

You'll want to know: orior, orīrī.
orior, orīrī — "to rise; to arise, to begin".
Sōle oriente ē portā discessimus. As the sun was rising, we left by the gate.
orior, orīrī, ortus sum, a deponent. The sun rises, and so do quarrels and wars. The noun ortus, 'sunrise', is taught. English orient.

The word, taken apart.
orior is an inherited verb (EtymDB). English orient, the land of the rising sun, comes from its participle, and origin from its noun orīgō.

Lesson 5 of 6.
posterus, postera, posterum — next, following.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to rise, then the Latin for to come along.

You'll want to know: posterus, postera, posterum.
posterus, postera, posterum — "next, following".
Posterō diē nūntius ad urbem rediit. On the next day the messenger returned to the city.
Posterō diē: the next day, a set phrase in Caesar. The plural noun posterī means descendants.

The word, taken apart.
posterus is built on post, "after, behind" (EtymDB): coming after. English posterity comes from it.
