Italian, chapter 313: L'ultimo saluto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la morte — death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the lineage, then the Italian for the family tree.

You'll want to know: la morte.
la morte — "death".
La morte del nonno ci ha cambiato la vita. Grandfather's death changed our lives.
Annoiarsi a morte — to be bored to death.

The word, taken apart.
morte is inherited from Latin mors, mortis "death" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
il defunto — the deceased.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the family tree, then the Italian for death.

You'll want to know: il defunto.
il defunto — "the deceased".
La famiglia del defunto ringrazia tutti. The family of the deceased thanks everyone.
Also an adjective: il defunto marito.

The word, taken apart.
defunto is a learned borrowing from Latin dēfūnctus "one who has finished (life)" (EtymDB), from dēfungī "to discharge, to finish".

Lesson 3 of 5.
il funerale — the funeral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for death, then the Italian for the deceased.

You'll want to know: il funerale.
il funerale — "the funeral".
Al funerale c'era tutto il paese. The whole village was at the funeral.
Often plural: i funerali.

The word, taken apart.
funerale is inherited from Late Latin fūnerālis (EtymDB), built on fūnus "a burial".
