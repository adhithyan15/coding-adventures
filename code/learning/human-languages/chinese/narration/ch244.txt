Mandarin Chinese, chapter 244: A Storm Coming.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
táifēng — typhoon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to freeze over, then the Mandarin for temperature.

You'll want to know: táifēng.
táifēng — "typhoon".
hòutiān huì yǒu táifēng — There'll be a typhoon the day after tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
táifēng is built from tái and 风 'wind'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese pjuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the first in this sense, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about plans.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhènyǔ — a rain shower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for temperature, then the Mandarin for a typhoon.

You'll want to know: zhènyǔ.
zhènyǔ — "a rain shower".
míngtiān xiàwǔ yǒu zhènyǔ — There'll be showers tomorrow afternoon.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhènyǔ is built from zhèn 'a spell' and 雨 'rain', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drinH and hjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the first only as the everyday form of another character, and the sense 'a spell' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 5.
shuōbudìng — perhaps; who knows.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a typhoon, then the Mandarin for a rain shower.

You'll want to know: shuōbudìng.
shuōbudìng — "perhaps; who knows".
shuōbudìng míngtiān huì xiàyǔ — Who knows, it might rain tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 不 is light here, bu, with no tone of its own.

The word, taken apart.
shuōbudìng is built from 说 'to say', 不 'not' and dìng 'fixed', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sywet, pjut and dengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the last 'settled'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
wànyī — in case; if by any chance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a rain shower, then the Mandarin for who knows.

You'll want to know: wànyī.
wànyī — "in case; if by any chance".
wànyī xiàyǔ zěnme bàn — What if it rains?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 一 keeps its first tone here, yī, because it ends the word.

The word, taken apart.
wànyī is built from wàn 'ten thousand' and 一 'one', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjonH and 'jit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); it is usually explained as one chance in ten thousand.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
jìxù — to carry on; to continue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for who knows, then the Mandarin for in case.

You'll want to know: jìxù.
jìxù — "to carry on; to continue".
yǔ tíng le wǒmen jìxù zǒu — The rain's stopped; let's carry on.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìxù is built from jì 'to continue' and xù 'to join on'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese zjowk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to continue' and 'to join'; the dictionary glosses the first, kejH there, 'to carry on'.
