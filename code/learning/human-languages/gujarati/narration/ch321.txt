Gujarati, chapter 321: Swords and Shields.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
તલવાર (talvār) — a sword.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an enemy, then the Gujarati for an invasion.

You'll want to know: તલવાર.
તલવાર — talvār — "a sword".
સૈનિકના હાથમાં તલવાર હતી. sainiknā hāthmā̃ talvār hatī. The soldier had a sword in his hand.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
તલવાર (talvār) goes back to Sanskrit taravāri, "a sword", and CDIAL lists Gujarati tarvār, "a one-edged sword" (CDIAL 5706); the r has become l in the spoken word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
હથિયાર (hathiyār) — a weapon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a sword, then the Gujarati for a shield.

You'll want to know: હથિયાર.
હથિયાર — hathiyār — "a weapon".
દુશ્મને હથિયાર નીચે મૂક્યાં. duśmane hathiyār nīce mūkyā̃. The enemy laid down their weapons.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
હથિયાર (hathiyār) comes from hastakāra, a form CDIAL reconstructs on hásta, the hand, where it lists Gujarati hathiyār, "a tool" (CDIAL 14027): what the hand works with. In war it is a weapon.

Lesson 4 of 5.
તોપ (top) — a cannon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a shield, then the Gujarati for a weapon.

You'll want to know: તોપ.
તોપ — top — "a cannon".
કિલ્લા પર બે જૂની તોપ છે. killā par be jūnī top che. There are two old cannons on the fort.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
તોપ (top) is usually traced to Turkish top, "a ball", and then "a cannon", a word that came with the armies of the Mughal age. It is feminine: જૂની તોપ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
વીંધવું (vī̃dhvũ) — to pierce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a weapon, then the Gujarati for a cannon.

You'll want to know: વીંધવું.
વીંધવું — vī̃dhvũ — "to pierce".
સૈનિકે તલવારથી ઢાલ વીંધી. sainike talvārthī ḍhāl vī̃dhī. The soldier pierced the shield with his sword.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ઢાલ વીંધી, teṇe ḍhāl vī̃dhī.

The word, taken apart.
વીંધવું (vī̃dhvũ) comes from vindhati, a form CDIAL reconstructs, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to pierce" (CDIAL 11784). It agrees with what is pierced: ઢાલ (ḍhāl) વીંધી.
