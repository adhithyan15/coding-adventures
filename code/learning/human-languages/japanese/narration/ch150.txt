Japanese, chapter 150: Jackets, Gloves and Rings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
うわぎ (uwagi) — a jacket; a coat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for Western clothes, then the Japanese for underwear.

You'll want to know: うわぎ.
うわぎ — uwagi — "a jacket; a coat".
ふゆは うわぎを きます。 — fuyu wa uwagi o kimasu — "In winter I wear a jacket."

The word, taken apart.
うわぎ (uwagi) is native Japanese, built from uwa-, "upper", the joining form of ue, and ki, "wearing", voiced to gi; both parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
はずす (hazusu) — to remove; to take off (glasses, a ring).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a jacket, then the Japanese for gloves.

You'll want to know: はずす.
はずす — hazusu — "to remove; to take off (glasses, a ring)".
うちで めがねを はずします。 — uchi de megane o hazushimasu — "At home I take off my glasses."
Polite はずします, hazushimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
はずす (hazusu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
