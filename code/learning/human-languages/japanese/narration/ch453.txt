Japanese, chapter 453: Handouts and Deadlines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しりょう (shiryō) — handouts; materials.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a company employee, then the Japanese for a business card.

You'll want to know: しりょう.
しりょう — shiryō — "handouts; materials".
かいぎの しりょうを みんなに わたしました。 — kaigi no shiryō o minna ni watashimashita — "I gave everyone the handouts for the meeting."

The word, taken apart.
しりょう (shiryō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "resources" (Middle Chinese tsij) and ryō, "materials" (lew) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ていしゅつする (teishutsu suru) — to hand in; to submit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a briefing, then the Japanese for a cut-off date.

You'll want to know: ていしゅつする.
ていしゅつする — teishutsu suru — "to hand in; to submit".
しめきりの まえに ていしゅつして ください。 — shimekiri no mae ni teishutsu shite kudasai — "Please hand it in before the cut-off date."
Polite ていしゅつします, teishutsushimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): がくせいが しゅくだいを ていしゅつしました。, gakusei ga shukudai o teishutsu shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ていしゅつする (teishutsu suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango) teishutsu with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tei, "carry in hand" (Middle Chinese dej) and shutsu, "go out" (tsyhwit) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
