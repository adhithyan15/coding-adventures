## You'll want to know: けいやくする
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-LEX-C357-KEIYAKU357-01]; assesses=[] -->

**けいやくする** — *keiyaku suru* — "to sign a contract".

> **じょうけんに さんせいしたら、けいやくします。** — *jōken ni sansei shitara, keiyaku shimasu* — "If I agree to the terms, I will sign the contract."

Polite **けいやくします**, *keiyakushimasu*; a する verb. Past (polite): **ちちが かいしゃと けいやくしました。**, *chichi ga kaisha to keiyaku shimashita*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**けいやくする** (*keiyaku suru*) is built from a Sino-Japanese noun and the native verb *suru*, "to do". The noun, *keiyaku*, is two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese: *kei*, "pledge" (Middle Chinese *khejH*) and *yaku*, "promise" (*'jak*) (KANJIDIC2; JMdict; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
