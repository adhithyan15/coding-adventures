Mandarin Chinese, chapter 294: How It Turned Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
qíngkuàng — situation; how things stand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the front-page story, then the Mandarin for to follow a story closely.

You'll want to know: qíngkuàng.
qíngkuàng — "situation; how things stand".
nǐ děi gàosu wǒ qíngkuàng, bùrán wǒ huì dānxīn — You have to tell me how things are, or I'll worry.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qíngkuàng is built from qíng 'feeling' and kuàng 'to compare', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzjeng and xjwangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'what a person desires' and the second 'to compare'; how the pair came to mean 'a situation' is not settled. xjwangH would regularly give huàng; the k of kuàng is irregular.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

Lesson 2 of 5.
yǐnqǐ — to give rise to; to bring about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to follow a story closely, then the Mandarin for how things stand.

You'll want to know: yǐnqǐ.
yǐnqǐ — "to give rise to; to bring about".
zhège xīnwén yǐnqǐ le dàjiā de zhùyì — The story caught everyone's attention.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: yǐn is said with a rising tone before qǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
yǐnqǐ is built from yǐn 'to draw (a bow)' and 起 'to rise', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yinX and khiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'long' and 'to draw a bow' and the second 'to rise' and 'to start'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yǐngxiǎng — to affect; influence; to have an effect on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for how things stand, then the Mandarin for to give rise to something.

You'll want to know: yǐngxiǎng.
yǐngxiǎng — "to affect; influence; to have an effect on".
xiàyǔ yǐngxiǎng le bǐsài — The rain affected the match.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: yǐng is said with a rising tone before xiǎng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
yǐngxiǎng is built from yǐng 'a shadow' and xiǎng 'a sound', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jaengX and xjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a shape's shadow' and the second 'a sound'. It is usually explained as what follows a thing the way a shadow follows a shape and an echo a sound.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zhōngyú — at long last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give rise to something, then the Mandarin for to have an effect on.

You'll want to know: zhōngyú.
zhōngyú — "at long last".
děng le liǎng ge xiǎoshí, chē zhōngyú lái le — After a two-hour wait, the bus came at last.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhōngyú is built from zhōng 'the end' and yú 'at', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyuwng and 'jo (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the end' and 'to use up' and the second 'to dwell' and calls it a particle; its sense 'at' is not given there. The rising tone of yú is irregular for 'jo.

Lesson 5 of 5.
yúshì — so; and then (as a result).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have an effect on, then the Mandarin for at long last.

You'll want to know: yúshì.
yúshì — "so; and then (as a result)".
yǔ hěn dà, yúshì wǒmen huí jiā le — It was raining hard, so we went home.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yúshì is built from yú 'at' and 是, both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jo and dzyeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which calls the first a particle, without the sense 'at', and glosses the second 'right, not wrong'; how the pair came to mean 'and so' is not settled. The rising tone of yú is irregular for 'jo.
