Mandarin Chinese, chapter 447: On Foot.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
bùxíng — to get there on foot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the wall round a city, then the Mandarin for to move to the city.

You'll want to know: bùxíng.
bùxíng — "to get there on foot".
cóng jiǔdiàn bùxíng qù gǔchéng yào shí fēnzhōng — It's a ten-minute walk from the hotel to the old town.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bùxíng is built from bù 'a step' and xíng 'to go', both inherited from Middle Chinese, buH and haeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a walking step and the second 'to walk'.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

长城 — Chángchéng — "the Great Wall".
我们坐车去长城 wǒmen zuò chē qù Chángchéng — We're going to the great wall by bus.

The word, taken apart.
长城 (Chángchéng) is built from 长 'long' and 城 'a wall round a city', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjang and dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'long' and 'far'.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ràolù.
ràolù — "to take a detour; to take the long way round".
qiánbian zài xiū lù wǒmen děi ràolù — There are roadworks ahead, so we'll have to go the long way round.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ràolù is built from rào 'to wind round' and 路 'a road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyewX and luH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wind round'; today's falling tone is irregular beside it.
