# बुरा — bad, and the phrase you will hear it in first

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You have had *acchā* for a long time and have never had its
opposite. Say it, then notice that you cannot yet say the other thing.

## You'll want to know: बुरा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-01]; assesses=[] -->

**बुरा** (*burā*) — "bad."

It ends in **-आ**, so by now you know without being told: *burā*, *burī*,
*bure*. The shape rule you learned about size words works on character words
too, because it was never about meaning.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**बुरा** is the word inside one of the most-heard sentences in everyday Hindi:

> **बुरा मत मानो** — *burā mat māno* — "don't take it badly."

Literally: *do not consider it bad*. It is what a speaker adds after saying
something blunt, and it is closer to English **"no offence"** than to any
apology. You will hear it long before you need to produce it, so recognising it
is worth more right now than being able to build it.

A second everyday one: **बुरा लगना** — *burā lagnā*, "to feel hurt," literally
*to strike one as bad*. Hindi describes being offended as something the remark
does, not something you do.

## Grammar Lens: a pair that behaves identically
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-01, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-03, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| adjective | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| *acchā* | *acchā* | *acchī* |
| बुरा | बुरा | बुरी |

Both inherited, both ending in **-आ**, both bending. The pair is the easiest one
in the chapter, and the rest of the chapter is where it stops being easy: four
of the six words ahead do **not** bend.
