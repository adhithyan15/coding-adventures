Italian, chapter 481: Dalla coscia al tallone.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il fianco — hip, side (of the body).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to regret, then the Italian for to let off steam.

You'll want to know: il fianco.
il fianco — "hip, side (of the body)".
Mi fa male il fianco destro. My right side hurts.
Di fianco a — beside: di fianco alla porta.

The word, taken apart.
fianco comes from Frankish hlanca "side, hip" (EtymDB), a Germanic word, probably through Old French flanc.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la coscia — thigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to let off steam, then the Italian for hip.

You'll want to know: la coscia.
la coscia — "thigh".
Dopo la corsa mi fanno male le cosce. After the run my thighs hurt.
The plural is le cosce. In the kitchen: una coscia di pollo — a chicken leg.

The word, taken apart.
coscia is inherited from Latin coxa "hip" (Lewis & Short); in Italian the word moved down to the thigh.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il tallone — heel (of the foot).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for thigh, then the Italian for calf.

You'll want to know: il tallone.
il tallone — "heel (of the foot)".
Queste scarpe nuove mi fanno male al tallone. These new shoes hurt my heel.
The heel of a shoe is il tacco.

The word, taken apart.
tallone is usually traced to Vulgar Latin tālōnem, from Latin tālus "ankle, heel bone" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Ho sbattuto l'alluce contro il letto. I stubbed my big toe on the bed.
The other toes are le dita dei piedi.

The word, taken apart.
alluce is usually traced to Latin hallux, a later form of allex "the big toe" (Lewis & Short, which also lists hallus).
