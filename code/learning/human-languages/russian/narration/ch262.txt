Russian, chapter 262: Then, and At Last: A Story Told in Order.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
сначала, потом, затем, наконец (snachála, potóm, zatém, nakonéts) — the steps of a story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "at first", "later, then" and "at last". (Snachála. Potóm. Nakonéts.) Three step words you have had for a while. Now they get a story to carry.

You'll want to know: затем.
затем — zatém — "after that, then". It means much the same as потом, and sounds a little more careful: it is common in writing, in instructions and in a story told with some care. Between them, a story need not say потом twice in a row.

Grammar Lens: one finished step, then the next.
Сначала я позавтракал, потом прочитал газету. — snachála ya pozávtrakal, potóm prochitál gazétu — First I had breakfast, then I read the paper. Затем я позвонил маме. Наконец я пошёл на работу. — zatém ya pozvoníl máme. nakonéts ya poshól na rabótu — After that I rang Mum. At last I set off for work.
Every verb is perfective, so every step is done before the next one starts. Позавтракал is завтракать with по-. Пошёл, "set off", is the past of пойти, the perfective partner of идти.

Why it's said this way: the same steps, as a habit.
Обычно я сначала завтракал, потом читал газету. — obýchno ya snachála závtrakal, potóm chitál gazétu — Usually I had breakfast first, then read the paper.
Imperfective verbs and обычно: not one morning, but every morning.

The word, taken apart.
затем is за, "behind, after", with тем, a form of тот, "that": "after that". EtymDB 2.1 has no entry for it, so this is the plain reading of its parts. Тот sits on the Proto-Indo-European pointing stem to-, the same one behind English that and the.

Another word for потом, a little more careful? (Затем.) One morning, step by step: which aspect? (Perfective.) Every morning, as a habit? (Imperfective, often with обычно.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
рассказать (rasskazát) — to tell a story, once and to the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to tell (a story)". (Rasskázyvat.) Then three steps of a morning, with snachála, potóm and zatém.

You'll want to know: рассказать.
рассказать — rasskazát — "to tell", once, the whole story. It is the perfective partner of рассказывать.
Бабушка часто рассказывала сказки. — bábushka chásto rasskázyvala skázki — Grandma often told fairy tales. Вчера бабушка рассказала новую сказку. — vcherá bábushka rasskazála nóvuyu skázku — Yesterday Grandma told a new fairy tale.
The habit takes the imperfective; the one tale, told to its end, the perfective. To ask for a story: Расскажи! — rasskazhí! — Tell me! To a stranger, or to more than one: Расскажите! — rasskazhítye!

Grammar Lens: scene, steps, end.
A told story has three parts, and aspect marks each one.
The scene, in the imperfective: Это было в субботу. Шёл дождь. — éto býlo v subbótu. shol dozhd — It was on a Saturday. It was raining. Russian rain "goes": идёт дождь, and in the past шёл дождь.
The steps, in the perfective and in order, with сначала, потом, затем. Then the end, with наконец.

The word, taken apart.
рассказать (rasskazát) is рас-, "out, apart", with сказать, "to say". By the usual account сказать is built on an older казать, "to show", which EtymDB 2.1 traces to Proto-Slavic kazati; so telling is showing in words. EtymDB lists рассказать (rasskazát) itself without taking it apart.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Grandma told a fairy tale", once? (Бабушка рассказала сказку.) Every evening? (Рассказывала.) Which aspect sets the scene? (The imperfective.) And the steps? (The perfective.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
(reading) — a rainy Saturday, told in order.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The book's first passage was a few lines in the present. This one is a story, and it happened last summer and last Saturday.
[pause 1 second]
Every word in it has come up before. Three verbs appear in a new shape, and they are explained after the passage.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
Это было в субботу. Шёл дождь. Я сидел в кафе, пил кофе и читал газету. Когда я прочитал газету, пришла моя подруга Анна. Сначала мы пили чай и говорили. Потом Анна рассказала историю. Летом она была в Москве и жила в гостинице в центре. Утром она часто гуляла в парке. Но в понедельник, пока она гуляла, она потеряла паспорт! Сначала она плакала. Потом она позвонила в гостиницу. Затем она вернулась в парк. Наконец она нашла паспорт. Он был в сумке! Мы смеялись. Вечером мы ужинали в ресторане, и Анна рассказала ещё одну историю.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then read it again and listen for the step words.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
The scene is imperfective: сидел, пил, читал, шёл дождь. The steps are perfective, in order: прочитал, пришла, рассказала, потеряла, позвонила, вернулась, нашла.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three of them are new shapes of verbs you own. Пришла is the perfective partner of приходить, "she came". Вернулась is the partner of возвращаться, "she went back". Нашла is the partner of находить, "she found".
[pause 2 seconds]
Часто гуляла is a habit, every morning. Пока она гуляла is one walk going on; потеряла is the moment it went wrong. Плакала and смеялись are imperfective: crying and laughing that went on for a while, with no finish to report.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Where was Anna's passport in the end?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many step words keep Anna's day in order? (Four: сначала, потом, затем, наконец (snachála, potóm, zatém, nakonéts).)
[pause 2 seconds]
Пока она гуляла, она потеряла паспорт: which verb was going on? (Гуляла.) Which one happened once? (Потеряла.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — a day, retold in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the four step words in order. (Snachála, potóm, zatém, nakonéts.)

What you've built.
Three lessons, and a whole story fits in your mouth. A scene in the imperfective: было, шёл дождь, сидел. Steps in the perfective, one finished before the next, with сначала, потом, затем and наконец. And рассказать (rasskazát) for telling it once, to the end.
[pause 2 seconds]
Anna's day, in four steps:
Сначала она плакала. Потом она позвонила в гостиницу. Затем она вернулась в парк. Наконец она нашла паспорт.
The crying went on, so it is imperfective. The call, the walk back and the finding were each done once.

The exchange.
— Расскажи, что было в субботу! — rasskazhí, shto býlo v subbótu! — Tell me what happened on Saturday! — Мы были в кафе. Сначала мы пили чай, потом … — my býli v kafé. snachála my píli chay, potóm …
