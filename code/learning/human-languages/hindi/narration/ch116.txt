Hindi, chapter 116: Grandparents, and Which Side.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दादा (dādā) — grandfather, the father's father.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for by all means, then the Hindi for a polite no.

You'll want to know: दादा.
दादा (dādā) — "grandfather", your FATHER's father.
English has one word for both grandfathers; Hindi has two, and says which side of the family you mean. मेरे दादा (dādā) — "my grandfather", with the respectful plural मेरे.

What you've built.
A grandfather, on your father's side.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dādā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: dādā, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say mere dādā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for by all means, then the Hindi for a polite no, then say dādā again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does दादा (dādā) mean? ("Grandfather, the father's father".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for the father's father, then the Hindi for the father's mother.

You'll want to know: नाना.
नाना (nānā) — "grandfather", your MOTHER's father.
So दादा (dādā) and नाना (nānā) are both "grandfather" in English, and never the same man in Hindi.

What you've built.
A grandfather, on your mother's side.

Lesson 4 of 5.
नानी (nānī) — grandmother, the mother's mother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for the father's mother, then the Hindi for the mother's father.

You'll want to know: नानी.
नानी (nānī) — "grandmother", your mother's mother.
नानी (nānī) का घर — "grandmother's house", the mother's family home, where many summer holidays are spent.

What you've built.
All four grandparents, each with a name of their own.
