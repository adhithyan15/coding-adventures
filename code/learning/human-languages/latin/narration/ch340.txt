Latin, chapter 340: Arma.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
arma, armōrum — weapons, arms.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a raid, then the Latin for to fight it out.

You'll want to know: arma, armōrum.
arma, armōrum — "weapons, arms".
Mīlitēs arma capere iussī sunt. The soldiers were ordered to take up arms.
Neuter, plural only. Mostly armour and shields, the things fitted to the body. A famous epic opens with arma virumque canō.

The word, taken apart.
arma is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to fit together" (EtymDB): fitted gear. English arms and army come from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
eques, equitis — a horseman, a cavalryman; a knight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to fight it out, then the Latin for weapons.

You'll want to know: eques, equitis.
eques, equitis — "a horseman, a cavalryman; a knight".
Equitēs hostium ex silvā ērūpērunt. The enemy's cavalry burst out of the forest.
Masculine, 3rd declension, from equus (taught). The equitēs were also a rich class below the senators. English equestrian.

The word, taken apart.
eques is built on equus, "horse" (EtymDB). English equestrian comes from its family.

Lesson 3 of 5.
pedes, peditis — a foot soldier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for weapons, then the Latin for a horseman.

You'll want to know: pedes, peditis.
pedes, peditis — "a foot soldier".
Peditēs lentē post equitēs ībant. The foot soldiers were marching slowly behind the cavalry.
Masculine, 3rd declension, from pēs (taught). Mind the forms: pedes 'foot soldier', pedēs 'feet'.

The word, taken apart.
pedes is built on pēs, "foot" (Lewis & Short): one who goes on foot. English pedestrian comes from its family.

lōrīca, lōrīcae — "a breastplate, a cuirass".
Lōrīca mīlitem ā sagittā servāvit. His breastplate saved the soldier from the arrow.
Feminine. The first ones were made of leather straps.

The word, taken apart.
lōrīca is built on lōrum, "a leather strap" (Lewis & Short): a corselet of leather thongs.

Lesson 5 of 5.
clipeus, clipeī — a round shield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a foot soldier, then the Latin for a breastplate.

You'll want to know: clipeus, clipeī.
clipeus, clipeī — "a round shield".
Clipeus aureus in templō pendēbat. A golden round shield hung in the temple.
Masculine (also neuter clipeum). A round bronze shield; the long shield of the legions is the scūtum (taught).

The word, taken apart.
clipeus is linked by Lewis & Short with clepō, "to steal, to hide": the cover. The link is not certain.
