Arabic, chapter 289: How It Felt, What I Hope.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
كنت سعيدا (kuntu saʿīdan) — I was happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I missed the train", then "who is in charge here?". (Fātanī al-qiṭār, man al-masʾūl hunā?)
[pause 1 second]
A story of a trip is not finished until you say how it felt.

Grammar Lens: kāna and the -an.
Today needs no verb: anā saʿīd, I'm happy. In the past kāna comes in, and the word that describes takes -an, the accusative:
كنت سعيدا. — kuntu saʿīdan — I was happy.
كانت حزينة. — kānat ḥazīna — She was sad.
On most words the -an is written with a final ا: سعيدا, متعبا, غاضبا. On a word ending in ة it is not written, and at the end of a sentence it is not said: kānat ḥazīna. You met it already in kuntu mutʿaban and kāna aṭ-ṭaqs jamīlan.

Grammar Lens: I felt.
To name the feeling as a noun, say shaʿartu bi-, "I felt", with the feeling after bi-:
شعرت بالخوف عندما طارت الطائرة. — shaʿartu bi-l-khawf ʿindamā ṭārat aṭ-ṭāʾira — I felt afraid when the plane took off.
Word for word, "I felt fear". Aḥsastu bi-, from aḥassa, to feel, says the same: aḥsastu bi-l-khawf.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We were tired" — kunnā mutʿabīn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I felt afraid when the plane took off" — shaʿartu bi-l-khawf ʿindamā ṭārat aṭ-ṭāʾira]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I was happy"? (Kuntu saʿīdan, with -an.) Why the -an? (Kāna gives it to the word that describes.) "I felt afraid"? (Shaʿartu bi-l-khawf.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
تجربة (tajriba) — an experience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I was happy", then "I felt afraid". (Kuntu saʿīdan, shaʿartu bi-l-khawf.)
[pause 1 second]
You have a verb for trying on a coat, jarraba. Its noun covers much more than coats.

You'll want to know: تجربة.
تجربة — tajriba — "an experience"; also "a try, an experiment".
كانت الرحلة تجربة (tajriba) جديدة. — kānat ar-riḥla tajribatan jadīda — The trip was a new experience.
It is kāna's -an again: tajribatan, though the ة hides it on the page. Tajriba mumtiʿa is an enjoyable experience; tajriba mumilla, a boring one.

The word, taken apart.
تجربة (tajriba) is built on the native Arabic root j-r-b, "to try, to test" (Lane). Lane gives jarraba as making trial of a thing, time after time, and tajriba as its noun: a trial. An experience, in Arabic, is something you have tried.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tajriba, then jarraba]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The trip was a new experience" — kānat ar-riḥla tajribatan jadīda]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "an enjoyable experience", then "a boring one" — tajriba mumtiʿa, tajriba mumilla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In my opinion it was a good experience" — fī raʾyī kānat tajribatan jayyida]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does تجربة (tajriba) mean? ("An experience; a try".) Which verb shares its root? (Jarraba, to try.) "A new experience"? (Tajriba jadīda.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
أتمنى أن (atamannā an) — I hope to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the trip was a new experience". (Kānat ar-riḥla tajribatan jadīda.) Then "I felt afraid". (Shaʿartu bi-l-khawf.)
[pause 1 second]
That was the past. Now turn ahead: what you hope for, what you dream of, what you want to become.

Grammar Lens: atamannā an, aḥlumu bi-.
Tamannā, to wish, from chapter one hundred and fourteen, has the present atamannā, I hope, I wish. An and the subjunctive follow it, as after urīdu:
أتمنى أن أجد وظيفة جديدة. — atamannā an ajida waẓīfa jadīda — I hope to find a new job.
Ajida is the subjunctive of ajidu, I find, from wajada; the w has dropped again.
For a dream, use the verb of ḥulm: aḥlumu bi-, I dream of, with a noun after bi-: aḥlumu bi-riḥla ṭawīla, I dream of a long journey.

Grammar Lens: what you want to become.
Aṣbaḥa, to become, behaves like kāna: what it becomes takes -an.
أريد أن أصبح مهندسا. — urīdu an uṣbiḥa muhandisan — I want to become an engineer.
Uṣbiḥa is its subjunctive, after an. In the past: aṣbaḥtu muhandisan, I became an engineer.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I hope to find a new job" — atamannā an ajida waẓīfa jadīda]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I dream of a long journey" — aḥlumu bi-riḥla ṭawīla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I want to become an engineer" — urīdu an uṣbiḥa muhandisan]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She wants to become a doctor" — turīdu an tuṣbiḥa ṭabība]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: your own: atamannā an and one thing, then urīdu an uṣbiḥa and one job]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I hope to"? (Atamannā an.) "I dream of"? (Aḥlumu bi-.) "To become", after an? (Uṣbiḥa.) What does aṣbaḥa give the word after it? (The -an, as kāna does.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — how it felt, and what comes next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Buy a return ticket and ask the price. (Tadhkirat dhahāb wa-iyāb, min faḍlik. Bi-kam at-tadhkira?) Then report a problem and ask for the person in charge. (Fātanī al-qiṭār. Urīdu an atakallama maʿa al-masʾūl.)

What you've built.
Three lessons, and an experience can be told from both ends. Looking back: kuntu saʿīdan, kānat ḥazīna, shaʿartu bi-l-khawf, kānat tajribatan jadīda. Looking ahead: atamannā an, aḥlumu bi-, urīdu an uṣbiḥa.
[pause 2 seconds]
Both ends in one breath:
كانت تجربة (tajriba) صعبة، لكن أتمنى أن أسافر قريبا. — kānat tajribatan ṣaʿba, lākin atamannā an usāfira qarīban — It was a hard experience, but I hope to travel again soon.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I was tired"? (Kuntu mutʿaban.) "I felt afraid"? (Shaʿartu bi-l-khawf.) "I hope to travel"? (Atamannā an usāfira.) "An experience"? (Tajriba.)
