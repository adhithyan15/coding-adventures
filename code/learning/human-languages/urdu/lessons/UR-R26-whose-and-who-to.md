# Whose, and who to

## Guided Practice: the distant band — four chapters of shapes, run cold
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE, UR-GRAMMAR-PLURAL, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE-PLURAL, UR-GRAMMAR-KO, UR-GRAMMAR-MEIN-POST, UR-GRAMMAR-PAR, UR-GRAMMAR-IS-OBLIQUE, UR-GRAMMAR-UN-OBLIQUE, UR-GRAMMAR-IS-KA, UR-GRAMMAR-APNA, UR-GRAMMAR-HAMARA, UR-GRAMMAR-AAP-KA, UR-LEX-MERA-NAAM-HAI, UR-GRAMMAR-ADJECTIVE-AGREEMENT, UR-LEX-KAMRA, UR-LEX-MAAN, UR-LEX-HUM, UR-LEX-YAHAN, UR-LEX-VAHAN, UR-LEX-NILA, UR-LEX-CHAI, UR-LEX-GARMI, UR-LEX-SALAM, UR-FUNC-VOCATIVE] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Four chapters ago no Urdu noun in this book changed shape, nothing
was anywhere, nobody was more than one, and nobody but you and the person in
front of you could own anything. Run the whole distance, cold.

- [YOU SAY: the four shapes — *kamrā*, *kamre*, *kamre meṅ*, *kamroṅ meṅ*]
- [YOU SAY: and the fifth, for calling — *kamro*]
- [YOU SAY: the demonstratives bending — *is kamre meṅ*, *un kamroṅ meṅ*]
- [YOU SAY: the possessives agreeing — *is kā kamrā*, *is kī māṅ*, *is ke kamre meṅ*]
- [YOU SAY: one's own — *maiṅ apne kamre meṅ hūṅ*, *ham apne kamre meṅ haiṅ*]
- [YOU SAY: placed, with a colour — *nīle kamre meṅ*]
- [YOU SAY: the pantry and the weather — *chāy yahāṅ hai*, *garmī hai, hai nā?*]
- [YOU SAY: calling, then greeting, then checking — *māṅ! salām. āp yahāṅ haiṅ, hai nā?*]
- [YOU SAY: to whom — *is ko*, *un ko*, *māṅ ko*]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-IS-KA, UR-GRAMMAR-APNA, UR-FUNC-HAI-NA, UR-FUNC-VOCATIVE, UR-GRAMMAR-OBLIQUE] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Does Urdu have a word for *his*? (**No** — the oblique plus **کا**.)
When is **اپنا** compulsory? (**When the owner is the subject**.) What does the
tag do that **کیا** does not? (**Invite agreement**.) And how do you call
somebody? (**By a kin term**, with the plural ending **-و**.)
