# नमस्कारः (namaskāraḥ) — the fuller form

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Same bow, different grammar — and your first case-ending.

## The letters in this word

New: **का** kā (**क** + the ā-sign **ा**), **र** ra, and the **visarga** **ः** —
a soft breathed "ḥ" at a word's end, written as two dots. **नमस्कारः** =
*namas-kā-raḥ*.

## The word, taken apart

**नमस्कारः** = *namas* ("a bow") + *kāra* ("the making, the doing," from the root
√kṛ "to do/make"). So *namaskāra* is "the **making** of a bow" — an act, where
*namaste* is an address. That root √kṛ "to do" is among Sanskrit's most
productive (it gives *karma*, "a thing done"); it is PIE *kʷer-*. The final
**visarga** *-aḥ* marks the masculine singular — your first case-ending.

## Grammar Lens

*Namaste* and *namaskāra* share the same first half, *namas*, but end differently
because they do different grammatical jobs — one takes a pronoun (*te* "to you"),
the other builds a noun (*-kāra* "the making of"). Sanskrit words are
transparently assembled from roots and endings; once you see the seams, long
words stop being frightening.
