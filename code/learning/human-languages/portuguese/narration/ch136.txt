Portuguese, chapter 136: The Future, in a Week, a Plan, To Plan, To Organise.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o futuro — the future.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the past, then the Portuguese for to happen.

You'll want to know: o futuro.
o futuro — "the future". Portuguese has two futures. The everyday one is ir with an infinitive: Vou falar. — "I am going to speak." The one-word future adds endings to the infinitive: falarei, falarás, falará. It is the one you will read.

Lesson 2 of 5.
daqui a — in (so much time from now).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to happen, then the Portuguese for the future.

You'll want to know: daqui a.
daqui a — "in (so much time from now)". Count forward from now: daqui a dois dias — "in two days", Daqui a uma semana, vou viajar. — "In a week I am going to travel."

Lesson 4 of 5.
planear — to plan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for in, then the Portuguese for a plan.

You'll want to know: planear.
planear — "to plan". Portugal says planear; Brazil says planejar. Like other verbs in -ear, it takes an i where the stress falls: eu planeio. Vou planear a viagem. — "I am going to plan the trip."

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a plan, then the Portuguese for to plan.

You'll want to know: organizar.
organizar — "to organise". A regular -ar verb, so its one-word future follows falar: Organizarei tudo. — "I will organise everything." In everyday speech: Vou organizar tudo.
