# What a text tells you before you read it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MAS-LITERARY, ES-LEX-ASONANCIA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three details that look like noise until you know what they are — and
each one dates or places a text before you have understood a sentence of it.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MAS-LITERARY, ES-SEMANTIC-LITERARY-REGISTER, ES-LEX-ASONANCIA, ES-SEMANTIC-VERSE-CONVENTION] -->

[PAUSE 1s] For each, say what the detail tells you.

- [YOU SAY: you meet *mas* with no accent — what do you now know about the text?
  (**Literary**, and almost certainly not transcribed speech.)]
- [YOU SAY: you meet *vuestra merced* — what do you now know?
  (**Old**, and you were told long ago what it became: *usted*.)]
- [YOU SAY: the lines end *luna … musa … cuna* — is this rhymed?
  (**Yes** — assonance, and an English-trained ear will say no.)]

[PAUSE 2s]
- [YOU SAY: which of the three explains something still in use today
  (*vuestra merced* — it is why *usted* takes the he/she verb)]
- [YOU SAY: what all three have in common (each carries information that has
  nothing to do with what the sentence means)]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-SEMANTIC-LITERARY-REGISTER, ES-SEMANTIC-VERSE-CONVENTION] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name what each signals: register, date, form. Which one is a fossil
that still governs living grammar? (*Vuestra merced*, behind *usted*.) Which is
a rhyme your ear may not hear as one? (Assonance.)
