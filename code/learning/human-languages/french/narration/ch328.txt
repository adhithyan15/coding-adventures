French, chapter 328: Couper les légumes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la planche à découper — the chopping board.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the spoonful, then the French for to preheat.

You'll want to know: la planche à découper.
la planche à découper — "the chopping board".
Les légumes sont sur la planche à découper. The vegetables are on the chopping board.
Une planche alone is a plank or a board.

The word, taken apart.
planche is inherited from Late Latin planca "a board" (EtymDB); découper is built on couper "to cut", from coup "a blow".

Lesson 2 of 5.
éplucher — to peel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to preheat, then the French for the chopping board.

You'll want to know: éplucher.
éplucher — "to peel".
Mon père épluche les pommes de terre. My father is peeling the potatoes.
Figurative: éplucher un texte — to go through it in detail.

The word, taken apart.
éplucher is built on Old French peluchier "to pick at", from Vulgar Latin piluccāre (EtymDB), a verb usually traced to pilus "a hair" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
émincer — to slice thinly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to peel, then the French for to chop.

You'll want to know: émincer.
émincer — "to slice thinly".
Le cuisinier émince les poivrons. The cook slices the peppers thinly.
A cooking word; couper en fines tranches says the same.

The word, taken apart.
émincer is built on mince "thin", from Old French mincier "to cut fine", usually traced to Vulgar Latin minūtiāre, built on minūtus "small" (Lewis & Short).
