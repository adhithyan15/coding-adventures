# ¿Cómo es? — the question these eight words exist to answer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-02, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *inteligente* and *trabajador*. Two words a reference letter
cannot do without, and you have had neither until now.

## Grammar Lens: es for character, está for the moment
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-01, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-04, ES-LEX-C380-COUNT-37] -->

Spanish has two verbs where English has one, and character is the clean half of
the split:

| verb | what it reports | example |
|---|---|---|
| **es** | settled character | *Es tímido.* — He is a shy person. |
| **está** | how someone is right now | *Está alegre.* — He is cheerful today. |

All eight of these adjectives are about settled character, so all eight take
**es**. That is what makes them exam adjectives rather than mood adjectives: a
question asking *¿Cómo es tu hermano?* wants the lasting answer.

*Alegre* is the one that can honestly go either way — *es alegre* is a cheerful
person, *está alegre* is a person having a good day.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-01, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-02, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-03, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-05, ES-LEX-C380-COUNT-36] -->

Read aloud. Every word has been taught:

> — ¿Cómo es tu hermana?
> — Es simpática e inteligente. Es un poco tímida, pero es muy trabajadora.
> — ¿Y tu hermano?
> — No es antipático, pero es muy serio.

Two things to catch. *Y* becomes **e** before a word starting with the *i*
sound — *simpática **e** inteligente* — because *y inteligente* would blur into
one vowel. And every adjective describing *hermana* has taken its **-a**, while
the ones that cannot change have stayed put.
