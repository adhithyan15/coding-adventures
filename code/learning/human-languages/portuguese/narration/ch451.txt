Portuguese, chapter 451: Arranjar e remendar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o sapateiro — the cobbler, the shoemaker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the wine shop, then the Portuguese for to slice.

You'll want to know: o sapateiro.
o sapateiro — "the cobbler, the shoemaker".
O sapateiro mudou as solas das minhas botas. The cobbler replaced the soles of my boots.
Built on o sapato.

The word, taken apart.
sapateiro is built on sapato 'shoe' with -eiro. Sapato comes from Old Portuguese çapato (EtymDB); its earlier origin is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a costureira — the seamstress, the dressmaker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to slice, then the Portuguese for the cobbler.

You'll want to know: a costureira.
a costureira — "the seamstress, the dressmaker".
A costureira arranjou o meu vestido. The seamstress altered my dress.
Masculine: o costureiro, often a fashion designer.

The word, taken apart.
costureira is built on costura 'sewing' with -eira, the person who does it; costura goes back to Vulgar Latin consūtūra, from Latin consuere 'to sew together', on suere 'to sew' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
a bainha — the hem; the sheath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the cobbler, then the Portuguese for the seamstress.

You'll want to know: a bainha.
a bainha — "the hem; the sheath".
A costureira subiu a bainha das calças. The seamstress took up the hem of the trousers.
Subir a bainha — to take up a hem.

The word, taken apart.
bainha is inherited from Latin vāgīna 'sheath, scabbard' (EtymDB): the folded edge holds the cloth as a sheath holds a sword.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o serralheiro — the locksmith; the metalworker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: o serralheiro.
o serralheiro — "the locksmith; the metalworker".
Perdi a chave e chamei o serralheiro. I lost my key and called the locksmith.
He makes gates and railings too.

The word, taken apart.
serralheiro is usually traced through Old Portuguese serralha 'lock' to Latin sera 'bar, bolt' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
remendar — to patch, to mend.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: remendar.
remendar — "to patch, to mend".
A minha avó remenda as meias à noite. My grandmother patches socks in the evening.
Um remendo — a patch.

The word, taken apart.
remendar is built from re- and emendar 'to correct' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin ēmendāre 'to free from faults'.
