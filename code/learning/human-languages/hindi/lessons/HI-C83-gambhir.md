# गंभीर — deep, which is how three separate languages say serious

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You learned that **मोटा** describes a *deep* voice. Hold on to that
idea of depth — this lesson takes it somewhere else entirely.

## You'll want to know: गंभीर
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06]; assesses=[] -->

**गंभीर** (*gambhīr*) — "serious, grave, solemn."

It ends in **र**, a consonant, so it does not bend.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Sanskrit **गम्भीर**, and its first meaning was **deep** — of water, of a well,
of a voice.

What is striking is how many unrelated languages reached for *depth* or *weight*
when they needed a word for gravity of manner:

| language | word | first meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Hindi | गंभीर | deep |
| Latin | gravis | heavy |
| English | profound | deep |

A serious person, in all three, is one you cannot see the bottom of. Hindi still
uses the literal sense freely: a *gambhīr* river is a deep one, and a *gambhīr*
illness is a grave one.

One useful distinction, and it is the same one English draws. A *gambhīr* person
is solemn and weighty. A *gambhīr* situation is dangerous. The word does not
decide which; the noun does.

## Grammar Lens: all six, sorted by ending
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-06, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-05, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| ending | the words | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **-आ** | बुरा, शर्मीला | बुरी, शर्मीली |
| consonant | होशियार, मिलनसार, गंभीर | unchanged |
| **-ई** | मेहनती | unchanged |

Two bend and four do not, and you can sort them without knowing what a single
one means.
