Latin, chapter 257: To deserve, To correct, To offer, To watch, To stroll.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mereor, merērī — to deserve, to earn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to lessen, then the Latin for to warn.

You'll want to know: mereor, merērī.
mereor, merērī — "to deserve, to earn".

The word, taken apart.
mereor is the deponent twin of mereō, "to earn, to deserve" (Lewis & Short). It is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European smer-, "to get a share"; Greek meiromai, "to receive one's share", is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ēmendō, ēmendāre — to correct (a fault).

Before the new one: say the Latin for to warn, then the Latin for to deserve.

You'll want to know: ēmendō, ēmendāre.
ēmendō, ēmendāre — "to correct (a fault)".

The word, taken apart.
ēmendō is built on mendum, "a fault, a blemish" (Lewis & Short), with ex, "out": to take the faults out. English amend and mend come from it.
