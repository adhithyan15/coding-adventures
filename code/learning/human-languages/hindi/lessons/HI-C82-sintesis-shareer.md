# वह लंबी है — the sentence that makes you choose the ending

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **लंबा** and **पतला**. In a list they sit still. In a sentence
about a real person they will not.

## Grammar Lens: the ending is chosen by who you are talking about
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| about | sentence | the ending |
|---|---|---|
| a man | वह लंबा है | **-आ** |
| a woman | वह लंबी है | **-ई** |

**वह** does not change. **है** does not change. The adjective carries the whole
load, which is the opposite of English, where *he* and *she* do the work and
*tall* never moves.

That is worth saying plainly: in English the pronoun tells you the gender and
the adjective is inert. In Hindi the pronoun is silent about it and the
adjective tells you. A listener who misses the ending has missed the
information.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-02, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-03, HI-LEX-C82-BODY-04] -->

Read aloud. Every word has been taught:

> वह लंबी है। वह पतली है।
> वह लंबा है। वह मोटा है। वह नाटा है।

Five sentences, two people, and the only thing telling you which is which is
the last letter of the adjective.
