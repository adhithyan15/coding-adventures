A 26-year-old African American female with asthma history presented to the Emergency Department with a productive cough featuring blood-tinged sputum, shortness of breath, and new-onset facial swelling. Neck CT showed prevertebral soft tissue swelling. She was started on Augmentin and azithromycin and discharged.

Two days later, persistent neck and facial swelling led her primary care provider to refer her to dental services, where a tooth extraction was performed. Swelling persisted. Three weeks after initial symptoms she returned to the Emergency Department with continued facial swelling, worsening cough with blood-tinged sputum, and new-onset night sweats. She denied fever, sick contacts, travel, or known tuberculosis exposure. She reported weight loss that she attributed to intentional efforts. There was no smoking, alcohol, or intravenous drug use history. Her dentist confirmed no active dental infection.

Laboratory findings: white blood cell count 44,000 per microliter, with absolute neutrophilia, myelocyte precursors, and monocytosis; hemoglobin 9.1 grams per deciliter; platelet count 585,000 per microliter. Urinalysis was negative. Chest CT showed a right upper lobe mass, mediastinal involvement, regional lymphadenopathy, and a right-sided pleural effusion. She was admitted.

During admission, white blood cell count rose to 55,000 per microliter. She was given ceftriaxone, then piperacillin-tazobactam and vancomycin. Sputum culture grew Actinomyces. Symptoms did not improve.
