# schließlich — the step that closes the story

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DANN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-DANN-VERB-ZWEI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "then I went". (*Dann ging ich.*) Now "first I bought a
coffee". (*Zuerst kaufte ich einen Kaffee.*)

## You'll want to know: schließlich
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-SCHLIESSLICH-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C170-TIME170-04] -->

**schließlich** — "in the end, finally": *schlees-likh*.

> *Schließlich kam der Zug.* — In the end the train came.

You already have **endlich**, "at last". The two are close, and the
difference is the waiting. *Endlich* sighs: at last, after too long.
*Schließlich* counts: the last step, in order.

*Schließlich* has a second life outside stories, "after all": *Er ist
schließlich mein Bruder*, he is my brother, after all.

## Grammar Lens: the frame of a told story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ERZAEHL-GERUEST-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C171-TIME171-02, GE-LEX-C171-TIME171-03, GE-GRAMMAR-ERZAEHL-PRAETERITUM-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-WAR-01] -->

Four step words, and every one takes the first place, so the verb follows
it at once:

> **Zuerst** *wartete ich.*
> **Dann** *kam der Zug.*
> **Danach** *fuhr ich zwei Stunden.*
> **Schließlich** *kam ich in Berlin an.*

Around those steps sits the scene: *Es war Samstag. Es regnete.* A told
story is the scene, the steps in order, and the end. You do not need all
four step words every time: *zuerst … dann … schließlich* is already a
story.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SCHLIESSEN-10] -->

**schließlich** is **schließen**, "to close", + *-lich*. *Schließen* goes
back through Middle High German *sliezen* to Old High German *sliozan*,
"to close, to conclude" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB records *schließlich* without
taking it apart, so the split into verb and ending is given here as the
plain reading of the word: the closing one.
