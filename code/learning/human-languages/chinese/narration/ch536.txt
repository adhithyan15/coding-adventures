Mandarin Chinese, chapter 536: When Wishes Come True.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
成真 (chéngzhēn) — to become reality (of a wish).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for of one's own free will, then the Mandarin for let us hope that.

You'll want to know: 成真.
成真 — chéngzhēn — "to become reality (of a wish)".
希望我的愿望能成真 xīwàng wǒ de yuànwàng néng chéngzhēn — I hope my wish comes true.

The word, taken apart.
成真 (chéngzhēn) is built from 成 'to become' and 真 'true', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and tsyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the true against the false.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.

yuánmèng — "to fulfil a dream".
rúguǒ míngnián néng qù Zhōngguó wǒ jiù yuánmèng le — If I can go to China next year, my dream will have come true.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuánmèng is built from yuán 'round, whole' and mèng 'a dream', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjwen and mjuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second, after an older dictionary, as waking within sleep: a dream made whole.

Lesson 3 of 6.
měihǎo — fine and happy (of a life or a future).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to become reality, then the Mandarin for to fulfil a dream.

You'll want to know: měihǎo.
měihǎo — "fine and happy (of a life or a future)".
zhǐyào dàjiā nǔlì shēnghuó jiù huì gèng měihǎo — As long as everyone works hard, life will get better.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: měi is said with a rising tone before hǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
měihǎo is built from měi 'beautiful' and 好 'good', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mijX and xawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'good-looking' and, after an older dictionary, 'sweet', and the second 'good, beautiful'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
pàntou — something to look forward to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fine and happy, then the Mandarin for to see in a dream.

You'll want to know: pàntou.
pàntou — "something to look forward to".
yǒu le gōngzuò shēnghuó jiù yǒu pàntou le — With a job, life has something to look forward to.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pàntou is built from pàn 'to long for' and 头, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pheanH and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as beautiful eyes; the sense 'to long for' is not given there. The second, 'the head', is said light here, as at the end of other nouns.
