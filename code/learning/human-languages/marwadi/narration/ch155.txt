Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 155: Eat, Drink, Go, Come.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हूं जाऊं हूं — a stem that ends in a vowel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say to go, जाणो. Then I listen to songs and he listens to songs. And the market, बाजार.

Grammar Lens: जा.
जाणो leaves जा, a stem that ends in a vowel. For I, its ending is the full letter ऊं:
हूं रोज बाजार जाऊं हूं। hū̃ roj bājār jāū̃ hū̃. — I go to the market every day.
For वो, a व comes in before -ै:
वो रोज बाजार जावै है। vo roj bājār jāvai hai. — He goes to the market every day.
After आप, the व stays: आप जावो हो, the ending of the request लावो. Old Marwari had jāvaï, "goes" (CDIAL 10452).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: हूं रोज बाजार जाऊं हूं। — hū̃ roj bājār jāū̃ hū̃. — I go to the market every day.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: मां रोज बाजार जावै है। — mā̃ roj bājār jāvai hai. — Mother goes to the market every day.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: आप बाजार जावो हो। — āp bājār jāvo ho. — You go to the market.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
I go? (जाऊं हूं.) He goes? (जावै है.) You go, to आप? (जावो हो.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Say that he goes to the market every day.


Lesson 2 of 5.
खा, पी, आ — three more vowel stems.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say I go to the market every day. Then to eat, to drink, to come: खाणो, पीणो, आवणो.

Grammar Lens: the same three endings.
खा, पी and आ end in vowels, and go exactly like जा:
हूं रोज दिनूगै चाय पीऊं हूं। hū̃ roj dinūgai chāy pīū̃ hū̃. — I drink tea early every morning.
राम रोटी खावै है। rām roṭī khāvai hai. — Ram eats roti.
आप रोज आवो हो। āp roj āvo ho. — You come every day.
आवणो leaves आव, but its present follows जा: आऊं, आवै, आवो. Old Marwari had āvaï, "comes" (CDIAL 1200).

Guided Practice.
Every cell of the present, now that each one is taught:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
who: हूं. बोल-: बोलूं हूं. जा-: जाऊं हूं.
who: वो. बोल-: बोलै है. जा-: जावै है.
who: आप. बोल-: बोलो हो. जा-: जावो हो.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: हूं रोटी खाऊं हूं। — hū̃ roṭī khāū̃ hū̃. — I eat roti.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: वो चाय पीवै है। — vo chāy pīvai hai. — He drinks tea.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: वो रोज आवै है। — vo roj āvai hai. — He comes every day.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
He eats? (खावै है.) I come? (आऊं हूं.) You drink, to आप? (पीवो हो.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Say that you drink tea early every morning.


Lesson 3 of 5.
दूध (dūdh) — milk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say tea, water and yoghurt. (चाय, पाणी, दही.)

You'll want to know: दूध.
दूध — dūdh — "milk".
हूं रोज दूध पीऊं हूं। hū̃ roj dūdh pīū̃ hū̃. — I drink milk every day.
It ends on a letter you have not written yet: dh, a द with a breath after it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
दूध (dūdh) is inherited from Sanskrit dugdha, "milk", a word built on the verb for milking. CDIAL lists Marwari dūdh, masculine (CDIAL 6391).

What you've built.
दूध is masculine: दूध गरम है। (dūdh garam hai.), the milk is hot.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dūdh]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: वो दूध पीवै है। — vo dūdh pīvai hai. — He drinks milk.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say tea, then dūdh again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does दूध (dūdh) mean? ("Milk".) Say it once more.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Marwadi for 'milk'.


Lesson 4 of 5.
ध (dha) — the letter that ends दूध (dūdh).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ध, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write द once, and name it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say दूध (dūdh), "milk". It opens on द and ends on a द with a breath after it: dh. This lesson puts that letter in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ध.
ध — dha, the breathed d.
It ends दूध (dūdh). It sits beside द as घ sits beside ग: the same place in the mouth, with a breath. On the page it is an upper spiral that ends in a shoulder, a lower bowl, and a right stem under the headline.

Writing: ध.
1. start at the upper spiral's inner crossing, curl around the small opening, widen left and down around the outer loop, then sweep right through the shoulder.
2. without lifting, turn back left along the shoulder to the left waist.
3. sweep down and around the lower bowl to its right junction.
4. climb up the right stem to the headline.
5. descend the right stem top-to-bottom.
6. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 1.
The source shows four movements; native writers draw ध (dha) as two pen-down strokes (81% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 64 of 79). This ductus follows the four movements in order and direction, lifting only before the headline: after the shoulder the pen turns back along it into the lower bowl, then climbs the right stem before descending it.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ध (dha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.

Lesson 5 of 5.
Eat, drink, go, come — a review.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say I go to the market every day.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: I eat roti; he eats roti; you eat roti, to आप]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I come every day; he comes every day]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 3 seconds]
I eat? (खाऊं हूं.) He drinks? (पीवै है.) You come? (आवो हो.) Milk? (दूध (dūdh).)
