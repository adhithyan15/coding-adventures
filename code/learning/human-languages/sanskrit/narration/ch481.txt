Sanskrit, chapter 481: Blind, Deaf and Lame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अन्धः (andhaḥ) — blind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a broken bone, then the Sanskrit for oozes.

You'll want to know: अन्धः.
अन्धः — andhaḥ — "blind".
अन्धः याचकः मार्गे गायति। andhaḥ yācakaḥ mārge gāyati. A blind beggar sings by the road.

The word, taken apart.
अन्धः (andhaḥ) MW gives "blind" and "dark"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
मूकः (mūkaḥ) — mute.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for blind, then the Sanskrit for deaf.

You'll want to know: मूकः.
मूकः — mūkaḥ — "mute".
मूकः बालः न वदति, किन्तु सर्वं जानाति। mūkaḥ bālaḥ na vadati, kintu sarvaṃ jānāti. The mute boy does not speak, but he understands everything.

The word, taken apart.
मूकः (mūkaḥ) MW explains it as "tied or bound", that is, tongue-tied, and gives "dumb, speechless, mute"; it names no root, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पंगुः (paṅguḥ) — lame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for deaf, then the Sanskrit for mute.

You'll want to know: पंगुः.
पंगुः — paṅguḥ — "lame".
पंगुः वृद्धः शनैः चलति। paṅguḥ vṛddhaḥ śanaiḥ calati. The lame old man walks slowly.

The word, taken apart.
पंगुः (paṅguḥ) MW asks, with a question mark, whether it comes from a root √paj, so its origin is uncertain; it gives "lame, halt, crippled in the legs" (MW).
