German, chapter 261: Would — würde, hätte, wäre, and Six Lines.
5 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ich hätte gern — I'd like, and that would be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I had" and "I was". (Ich hatte. Ich war.) Change the a of each to ä, add an e to the second, and the past turns into "would".

You'll want to know: ich hätte gern.
ich hätte gern — "I'd like", literally "I would gladly have": ikh HET-tuh gairn.
Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee. — I'd like a coffee. Ich hätte gern eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin. — I'd like a ticket to Berlin.
It sits beside ich möchte, and both are polite. At a counter or in a café, ich hätte gern is a common way to order.
The forms: ich hätte, du hättest, er hätte, wir hätten, Sie hätten.

Grammar Lens: das wäre.
wäre is "would be", made from war:
Das wäre schön. — That would be lovely. Das wäre nett. — That would be kind.
The forms: ich wäre, du wärst, er wäre, wir wären, Sie wären.
This is the Konjunktiv II, the mood of what is not real, or not yet, or said gently. You have used one form already: du solltest, which is spelled like the Präteritum. Hätte and wäre add an umlaut to theirs.

The word, taken apart.
hätte is hatte and wäre is war, each with an umlaut. Neither form is in EtymDB 2.1, so this is the usual account in histories of German: the old subjunctive endings had an i in them, as in Old High German wārī, and that i pulled the vowel forward, a to ä. The i is gone; the umlaut it left is the mark of the mood. Haben itself EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic habjaną.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ich würde — would.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'd like a coffee" and "that would be lovely". (Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee. Das wäre schön.)
[pause 1 second]
Haben and sein have "would" forms of their own. Most verbs borrow one.

Grammar Lens: würde, and the infinitive last.
For "would" with an ordinary verb, German takes würde and puts the verb's infinitive at the end, the same bracket a modal builds:
Ich würde gern nach Italien reisen. — I would like to travel to Italy. Was würdest du machen? — What would you do?
The forms: ich würde, du würdest, er würde, wir würden, ihr würdet, Sie würden.
After dass or weil, würde goes to the very end, after the infinitive:
Ich glaube, dass er gern kommen würde.
In everyday German the split is roughly this: haben, sein and the modals keep their one-word forms, hätte, wäre, sollte; most other verbs take würde.

The word, taken apart.
würde is wurde, the Präteritum of werden, "to become", with an umlaut. That is the same move as hatte to hätte and war to wäre: an umlaut on the past marks the Konjunktiv II. Not every verb takes one: sollte, wollte and the regular -te verbs are identical to their past, and the sentence tells you which is meant.
Werden from Old High German werdan from Proto-Germanic werþaną, "to turn" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB does not list würde; the umlaut history is the usual account.

Lesson 3 of 5.
wenn ich Zeit hätte — if things were different.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if I have time, I'll come". (Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme ich.) That condition may well come true. Now one that is not true at all.

Grammar Lens: the Konjunktiv II in both halves.
When the condition is not real, both halves go into the Konjunktiv II:
Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich mehr lesen. — If I had time, I would read more.
I do not have time; that is the point. Set the two side by side:
Wenn ich Zeit habe, lese ich. — real: it may happen. Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich lesen. — not real: it is not happening.
The word order has not changed: the verb at the end of the wenn clause, then verb, comma, verb: hätte, würde.
One short version is worth having on its own: Wenn ich du wäre …, if I were you.

Why it's said this way: ich könnte.
ich könnte is "I could", in the sense of "I would be able to". It is konnte with an umlaut, and like würde it puts the infinitive last:
Wenn ich Geld hätte, könnte ich nach Japan reisen. — If I had money, I could travel to Japan.
It also makes a suggestion soft: Wir könnten morgen fahren, we could go tomorrow.
Listen for the vowel. Ich konnte is "I was able to", over and done. Ich könnte is "I could", if things were different.

The word, taken apart.
könnte is konnte with the umlaut of the Konjunktiv II. EtymDB 2.1 traces können through Old High German kunnan to Proto-Germanic kunnaną; you met its link to English could, and its old sense "to know how", with konnte in chapter twenty-five. Zeit goes back to Proto-Germanic tīdiz, "time, period" (EtymDB 2.1), the same word as English tide.

Lesson 4 of 5.
Review — what would happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Order a coffee, politely. (Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee.) And say "that would be lovely". (Das wäre schön.)

What you've built.
Three lessons, and German can now say what is not so. Four pasts with an umlaut: hätte, wäre, würde, könnte. Würde with the infinitive last for most verbs. And two kinds of wenn: the present for what may well happen, the Konjunktiv II for what is not true now.
[pause 2 seconds]
The two side by side, about the same weekend:
Wenn ich Zeit habe, fahre ich nach Berlin. Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich nach Berlin fahren.
The first is a plan. The second is a wish.

[your turn — say: Das wäre schön, aber ich habe keine Zeit.]

Lesson 5 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines to one reader about a trip — a scene and events in the past, a reason, a feeling, and a last line with wenn and the Konjunktiv II.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter fifty-five asked for four lines that held together, with weil. This one asks for six, and they tell a story.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one person who will read it: ein Freund, eine Freundin, someone in your family.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is more than a list of events. It has a scene: es war, es regnete. It has events in order, with the verb straight after each step word: zuerst, dann, danach, schließlich. It has at least one reason, pointing back with weil or forward with darum. And a good one ends by looking forward.
[pause 2 seconds]
For a message to a friend, either past is right. The Präteritum sounds like a story being told; the Perfekt sounds like you talking: ich bin nach Berlin gefahren. Keep war and hatte in the Präteritum either way.
[pause 2 seconds]
The Goethe-Zertifikat B1 writing paper asks for personal and formal messages and a short opinion post. Six lines to a friend is a first step towards the personal message.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, with vor (vor einem Monat), and the weather;
the first thing that happened, with zuerst;
the next thing, with dann, and a reason, with weil or darum;
how it ended, with schließlich;
how you felt, with ich war froh, dass …, or what you thought of it, with meiner Meinung nach and a comparison: schöner als, so schön wie;
one line forward, with wenn ich … hätte, würde ich … or wenn ich … hätte, könnte ich …, or das wäre …, or ich hoffe, … zu ….
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. The exercise is the joining, and the place of each verb.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now go through your lines and find every verb. In lines two, three and four, the verb should come straight after the step word: zuerst ging ich, not zuerst ich ging. That is the slip to check first.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then every weil, dass and wenn. Is its verb at the end of the clause? If a line starts with the wenn clause, does the main verb come straight after the comma: hätte, würde?
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, line six on its own. If it has hätte, würde or könnte, is it about something that is not true now? Then it looks forward, and the story is finished.
