Urdu, chapter 185: Pill, Prescription, Injection, Wound, Injury.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
گولی (golī) — a pill; a bullet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to get wet, then the Urdu for to travel.

You'll want to know: گولی.
گولی — golī — "a pill; a bullet".

The word, taken apart.
گولی (golī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from golikā, "little ball" (CDIAL 4321). It is built on gola, "ball".

Lesson 2 of 5.
نسخہ (nuskhā) — a prescription.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to travel, then the Urdu for a pill; a bullet.

You'll want to know: نسخہ.
نسخہ — nuskhā — "a prescription".

The word, taken apart.
نسخہ (nuskhā) is a loan from Arabic nuskha, "a copy, a written text", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is n-s-kh (Lane). Urdu uses it for a doctor's written prescription.

Lesson 5 of 5.
چوٹ (choṭ) — an injury, a knock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an injection, then the Urdu for a wound.

You'll want to know: چوٹ.
چوٹ — choṭ — "an injury, a knock".

The word, taken apart.
چوٹ (choṭ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. CDIAL lists Hindi coṭ, "wound", under Sanskrit cuṭati, "cuts off, strikes", in its form coṭṭ-, and calls the whole group non-Aryan in origin (CDIAL 4857).
