Mandarin Chinese, chapter 34: What You Are Called.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
叫 (jiào) — to be called; to call.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for younger sister, then the Mandarin for child.

You'll want to know: 叫.
叫 — jiào — "to be called; to call".
你叫什么名字 nǐ jiào shénme míngzi — What's your name?
The new character, in pieces: 叫 (jiào) — 口, the mouth piece, small on the left, and on the right two strokes: one that flicks up, and a long vertical. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
叫 (jiào) is inherited from Middle Chinese kewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to call out'. The sense 'to be named' is not given there; from calling a person to what a person is called is a likely step, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word about people.

Lesson 2 of 6.
叫 (jiào) — a small mouth, then two strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — a small mouth, then two strokes.
叫 (jiào).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 口, three strokes, as in 吗, small and set a little high: the left side, the top that turns down the right, the bottom that closes it.
The right piece, two strokes. A vertical that flicks up to the right at the foot. Then a long vertical, the tallest stroke in the character.
3 + 2 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 叫 (jiào), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 叫 (jiào)? (Two — 口, then two strokes.) Which character you know has the same small 口 on the left? (吗.) Which stroke is last? (The long vertical.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
nín — you (polite).

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for child, then the Mandarin for to be called.

You'll want to know: nín.
nín — "you (polite)".
nín jiào shénme míngzi — May I ask your name?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nín is the polite form of 你, for older people and strangers. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
rènshi is built from two old words for knowing, both inherited from Middle Chinese: nyinH, which the Guangyun glosses with the second, and syik, 'to know' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

péngyou — "friend".
tā shì wǒ de péngyou — He's a friend of mine.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
péngyou is built from two words for companions, both inherited from Middle Chinese: bong 'a band of companions' and hjuwX 'friend' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains as people of one mind.
