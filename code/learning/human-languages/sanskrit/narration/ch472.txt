Sanskrit, chapter 472: The Mouth.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
तालु (tālu) — the palate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an eyelash, then the Sanskrit for shaves.

You'll want to know: तालु.
तालु — tālu — "the palate".
जिह्वा तालु स्पृशति। jihvā tālu spṛśati. The tongue touches the palate.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
तालु (tālu) MW gives "the palate", from the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā on; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

The word, taken apart.
चर्वयति (carvayati) comes from the root √carv, "to grind with the teeth, to chew" (MW).

You'll want to know: निष्ठीवति.
निष्ठीवति — niṣṭhīvati — "spits".
मार्गे न निष्ठीवेत्। mārge na niṣṭhīvet. One should not spit on the road.
Past (he …): सः मार्गे निष्ठ्यूतवान्, saḥ mārge niṣṭhyūtavān.

The word, taken apart.
निष्ठीवति (niṣṭhīvati) is built from ni, "down", and the root √ṣṭhiv, "to spit"; MW's ppp niṣṭhyūta is "spit out" (MW).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for chews, then the Sanskrit for spits.

You'll want to know: रसः.
रसः — rasaḥ — "a taste; a flavour".
आम्रस्य रसः मधुरः अस्ति। āmrasya rasaḥ madhuraḥ asti. The mango tastes sweet.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
रसः (rasaḥ) MW's first sense is "the sap or juice of plants", the word in फलरसः, and "taste, flavour" comes later; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
