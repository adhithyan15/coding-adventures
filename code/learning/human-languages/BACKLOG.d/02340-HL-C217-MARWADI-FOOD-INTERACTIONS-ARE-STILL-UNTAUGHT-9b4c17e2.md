## HL-C217 — Marwadi has seven food nouns and no way to use them

Chapters 23--25 bring Marwadi's food vocabulary to seven source-attested words
(**दाल, घी, सबजी, रोटी, बाटी, चूरमा, चाय**) in four separately scored skills,
and take the track from 46 to 53 distinct taught headwords against a 300-word
pre-A1 target. Nothing in that slice teaches a learner to *do* anything with
the words.

What remains untaught, and was deliberately left out rather than gestured at:

- offering food or drink, and accepting or refusing one politely;
- asking for more, or saying that something is enough;
- a hunger or thirst statement;
- naming a meal, or saying when it is eaten;
- the price question that Chapters 18--20's shopping nouns also still lack.

The same shape holds for the shopping and transport nouns already secured: the
track now carries nineteen everyday nouns across three domains and not one
transaction. The next dependency-unblocking slice for Marwadi should be one
function, taught once, reused across all three noun sets, rather than a fourth
noun domain. Marwari Pathshala Lessons 6, 9 and 10 all print full sentences
around the words this track has taken in isolation; those sentences are the
source material for that slice.

Two notes for whoever writes it. First, the untaught Devanagari inventory is
now the binding constraint on sentence material rather than the vocabulary:
**ख, झ, ञ, ढ, ध, फ, ष, ळ**, the independent vowels other than **आ** and **ई**,
and **ृ** have no lesson, so any candidate sentence has to be checked against
the taught set before it is scheduled. Second, Marwadi is the corpus's only
track with zero script-closure violations, zero glyphs shown but never taught
and zero headwords lacking romanization; a sentence slice is exactly the kind
of change that breaks all three at once if a single connective word is printed
in native script before its signs are isolated.
