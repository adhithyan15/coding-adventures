Latin, chapter 203: To doubt, To look for, To use up, To annoy, To forget.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dubitō, dubitāre — to doubt, to hesitate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to do, then the Latin for to go forward.

You'll want to know: dubitō, dubitāre.
dubitō, dubitāre — "to doubt, to hesitate".

The word, taken apart.
dubitō is built on dubius, "doubtful" (Lewis & Short), whose first part is duo, "two": to be of two minds.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: quaerō, quaerere.
quaerō, quaerere — "to look for, to ask".

The word, taken apart.
quaerō has no sure origin. Old Latin wrote it quairō, and quaesō, "I beg", is an older form of the same verb (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
vexō, vexāre — to annoy, to harass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to look for, then the Latin for to use up.

You'll want to know: vexō, vexāre.
vexō, vexāre — "to annoy, to harass".

The word, taken apart.
vexō is usually taken as a frequentative of vehō, "to carry" (Lewis & Short): to keep carrying about, to toss, so to harass.

Lesson 5 of 5.
oblīvīscor, oblīvīscī — to forget.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to use up, then the Latin for to annoy.

You'll want to know: oblīvīscor, oblīvīscī.
oblīvīscor, oblīvīscī — "to forget".

The word, taken apart.
oblīvīscor is usually explained as ob, "over", with a root for smearing or smoothing: the mind wiped over. Lewis & Short tie it instead to līvor, "a dark colour".
