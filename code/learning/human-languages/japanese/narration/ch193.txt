Japanese, chapter 193: Shrines, Temples and Cinemas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じんじゃ (jinja) — shrine; Shinto shrine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to ask, then the Japanese for guidance.

You'll want to know: じんじゃ.
じんじゃ — jinja — "shrine; Shinto shrine".
まいとし じんじゃに いきます。 — maitoshi jinja ni ikimasu — "I go to the shrine every year."

The word, taken apart.
じんじゃ (jinja) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jin, "god, spirit" (Middle Chinese zyin) and ja, "shrine, society" (dzyaeX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
てら (tera) — temple; Buddhist temple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for guidance, then the Japanese for shrine.

You'll want to know: てら.
てら — tera — "temple; Buddhist temple".
じんじゃの となりに てらが あります。 — jinja no tonari ni tera ga arimasu — "There is a temple next to the shrine."

The word, taken apart.
てら (tera) is the native reading (kun'yomi) given to the character for "Buddhist temple"; its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
みえる (mieru) — to be visible; to be seen, to seem.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for cinema, then the Japanese for art museum.

You'll want to know: みえる.
みえる — mieru — "to be visible; to be seen, to seem".
あの たかい たてものが みえます。 — ano takai tatemono ga miemasu — "That tall building can be seen."
Polite みえます, miemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
みえる (mieru) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed, built on mi, "seeing"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
