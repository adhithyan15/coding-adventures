# पश्यति (paśyati) — "sees," a verb with two roots

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-GRAMMAR-DHATU-GANA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] So far one root has built each verb. Here two roots share the work,
and you have already met the quieter one.

## You'll want to know: पश्यति
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-PASHYATI-SEE]; assesses=[] -->

| Sanskrit | sound | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| पश्यति | *paśyati* | he, she, or it **sees** |
| दर्शन | *darśana* | a **beholding**, a viewing |

The present tense comes from the root **पश्** (*paś*). Every other tense of this
verb comes from a different root, **दृश्** (*dṛś*) — and *dṛś* is where
**दर्शन** was built, the word inside Chapter 4's farewell **पुनर्दर्शनाय**,
"until a seeing again."

Two roots, one verb, divided by tense. English does the same with *go* and
*went*, which were never the same verb either.

## Sounds you'll need: the letters in पश्यति
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**प** is *pa*. Then **श्य**, a stack of two letters: **श** *śa*, a *sh* made
with the tongue high and forward, plus **य** *ya*. Say them glued: *pash-ya-ti*.
Sanskrit has three different *s* letters, and this is the one that leans toward
*sh*.

## The word, taken apart — pashyati and spectacle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-SPEK-DESCENDANTS]; assesses=[] -->

**पश्** descends from PIE \**spek-*, "to observe closely." The *s* and the *p*
swapped places on the way into Indo-Iranian, which is why the Sanskrit form
begins *paś-* rather than *spaś-*.

Latin kept it straight as *specere*, and English is full of the results:
**spectacle**, **inspect**, **respect**, **species**, **suspect**, **spy**.

Greek swapped the letters too, in its own direction, giving *skop-*: **telescope**,
**microscope**, **skeptic**, **bishop** (from *episkopos*, an overseer).
