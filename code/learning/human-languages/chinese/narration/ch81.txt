Mandarin Chinese, chapter 81: The City.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
里 (lǐ) — inside; in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for doctor, then the Mandarin for company.

You'll want to know: 里.
里 — lǐ — "inside; in".
山里有马 shān lǐ yǒu mǎ — There are horses up in the hills.
The new character, in pieces: 里 (lǐ) — 日 with a vertical running down through it, standing on two bars. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
里 (lǐ) is inherited from Middle Chinese liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'inside' and, through an older book, 'the inside of a garment'.

What you've built.
One more word for meeting people.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: lǐ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: lǐ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: shān lǐ yǒu mǎ — There are horses up in the hills.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for doctor, then the Mandarin for company, then say lǐ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 里 (lǐ) mean? ("Inside; in".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 6.
里 (lǐ) — 日, a line through it, two bars.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 日, a line through it, two bars and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say lǐ — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
日 家.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rì, jiā]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 日, a line through it, two bars.
里 (lǐ).
One piece, built from the top down.
日, four strokes: the left side, the top that turns down, the middle bar, the bottom bar.
A vertical down the middle, through 日 and on below it.
Two horizontals, a short one across the vertical, then a long one along the bottom.
4 + 1 + 2 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 里 (lǐ), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 里 (lǐ)? (Seven.) Which character you know is at the top? (日.) Which bar is longest? (The last, along the bottom.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 里 (lǐ), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 6.

You'll want to know: chéngshì.
chéngshì — "city".
Běijīng shì yí ge dà chéngshì — Beijing is a big city.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chéngshì is built from chéng 'city walls' and shì 'market', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and dzyiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
