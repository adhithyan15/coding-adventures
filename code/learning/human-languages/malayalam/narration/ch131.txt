Malayalam, chapter 131: What Is Cooking.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
മീൻ (mīn) — fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a feast, then the Malayalam for payasam.

You'll want to know: മീൻ.
മീൻ (mīn) — "fish", straight from the boats every morning.

The word, taken apart.
മീൻ (mīn) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil mīṉ and Kannada mīn (DEDR 4885). Sanskrit mīna, "fish", is thought to come from this Dravidian word (CDIAL 10140a). DEDR 4885 compares Sanskrit mīna.

What you've built.
The coast's favourite food.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 4 of 5.
പച്ചക്കറി (paccakkaṟi) — vegetables.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for meat, then the Malayalam for an egg.

You'll want to know: പച്ചക്കറി.
പച്ചക്കറി (paccakkaṟi) — "vegetables". It begins with പച്ച, "green".

The word, taken apart.
പച്ചക്കറി (paccakkaṟi) is native Dravidian: pacca, "green, fresh" (DEDR 3821, with Tamil paca, "to be green"), with the word for "vegetables, curry" (DEDR 1391, with Tamil kaṟi).

What you've built.
The green part of the meal.
