Hindi, chapter 80: The Eating House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
होटल (hoṭal) — an eating house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two words back: what does mahãgā mean, and what is ख़रीदना? The next five words are for the place you buy a meal.

You'll want to know: होटल.
होटल (hoṭal) — "an eating house".
English hotel, and behind it Latin hospitāle, a house for guests. The same Latin word gave English hospital and hospitality: one root for the place that takes a stranger in, whether to feed, to lodge or to heal.
Here is the part a dictionary will not tell you loudly enough. In everyday Hindi a होटल (hoṭal) is most often somewhere you eat, not somewhere you sleep. A small roadside place with six tables and no rooms at all calls itself a होटल (hoṭal), and चाय होटल (hoṭal) is a tea shop.
So take the word as "eating house" first and "hotel" second, and you will read far more signboards correctly.
The ो is the o matra, a single stroke above the headline with a tail.

What you've built.
The first of five words for a meal you did not cook.

[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: say mahãgā, then read ख़रीदना and say what it means]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — hear: hoṭal]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hoṭal]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chāy hoṭal]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does होटल (hoṭal) usually mean on an Indian signboard? (A place to eat.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
नाश्ता (nāshtā) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say hoṭal, then write होटल: ह with ो above it, then ट, then ल.

You'll want to know: नाश्ता.
नाश्ता (nāshtā) — "breakfast".
Persian ناشتا (nāshtā) means "not having eaten, on an empty stomach". The meal took its name from the emptiness it ends.
English did the identical thing and nobody notices any more: breakfast is the breaking of the fast. Two languages, no contact, the same picture — the night is a fast and the morning meal is what ends it.
The middle of the word stacks श on त with the vowel-killer ्, the same stack you have written in नमस्ते and read in स्टेशन.
नाश्ता (nāshtā) is also a doing word's companion: नाश्ता (nāshtā) करना is "to have breakfast", built the way आराम करना was.

What you've built.
A place to eat, and the first meal of the day to eat there.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — recall: read ख़रीदना, then say it without looking]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nāshtā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nāshtā karnā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: होटल (hoṭal) once more, then नाश्ता (nāshtā) beside the printed model]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does नाश्ता (nāshtā) mean? ("breakfast".) What does the Persian word say about your stomach? (That it is empty.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
बिल (bil) — the bill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which of your two words is the place, and which is the meal?

You'll want to know: बिल.
बिल (bil) — "the bill".
English bill, borrowed for the slip of paper and used for nothing else. Three letters: ब, the ि matra written to its left, and ल.
Now a warning your eye needs. You already hold बिलकुल, "absolutely", and you have met बिल्ली, a cat. All three open on the same three letters and none of them is related to the others — बिलकुल is Arabic, बिल्ली is Sanskrit, बिल (bil) is English. Reading the first syllable and guessing will send you to the wrong word.
बिल (bil) दीजिए asks for it, in the same polite frame that got you a ticket and a cup of tea.

What you've built.
Three: होटल (hoṭal), नाश्ता (nāshtā), बिल (bil). A place, a meal, and what arrives afterwards.

Lesson 4 of 5.
वेटर (veṭar) — a waiter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write बिल (bil) from memory, remembering that the ि goes down before the ल it is heard after.

You'll want to know: वेटर.
वेटर (veṭar) — "a waiter".
English waiter, and the spelling shows two habits you have now seen three times. The English w is written व, because Hindi has one letter where English splits v and w. The English t is written ट, retroflex, as it was in टिकट and स्टेशन.
This is the word on the sign and on the pay slip, so it is the one to be able to read. Across a table it is used far less than you would expect: people catch an eye rather than call a title, and a raised hand does the work.
So hold वेटर (veṭar) for reading, and expect the room itself to be quieter than the word.

What you've built.
Four, and the person you ask for the fourth thing.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: veṭar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hoṭal, then veṭar, then bil]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: वेटर (veṭar), with व for the English w and ट for the English t]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which Hindi letter carries an English w? (व.) And an English t? (ट.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
पीना (pīnā) — to drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Name the four words of this chapter so far.

You'll want to know: पीना.
पीना (pīnā) — "to drink".
Sanskrit पिबति (pibati), "drinks", from a root so old that Latin bibere, "to drink", is the same word wearing different clothes. English potion and potable come down the Latin side of that family.
Now the pair. खाना you already own as "food" — and खाना is also "to eat", exactly as गाना was both the song and the singing. Put them together and Hindi has one of its most-used pairs:
खाना — food, and to eat. पीना (pīnā) — to drink.
मैं चाय पीता हूँ is "I drink tea", in the frame you have used all along. And मैं खाना खाता हूँ puts the word into one breath twice — once for the food, once for the eating — with nothing between them but the ending.

What you've built.
Five: होटल (hoṭal), नाश्ता (nāshtā), बिल, वेटर (veṭar), पीना (pīnā). A place, a meal, a bill, the person who brings it, and the two doing words that made you come in.
