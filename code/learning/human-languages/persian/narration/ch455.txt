Persian, chapter 455: Out and about.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شهربازی (shahrbâzi) — an amusement park, a funfair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a hairdresser's, then the Persian for a watchmaker.

You'll want to know: شهربازی.
شهربازی (shahrbâzi) — "an amusement park, a funfair".
بچه ها روز جمعه به شهربازی رفتند. Bachche-hâ ruz-e jom'e be shahrbâzi raftand. The children went to the funfair on Friday.
Literally 'play-town'.

The word, taken apart.
shahrbâzi is a native compound of shahr 'town', from Middle Persian šahr (EtymDB), and bâzi 'play', usually traced to Middle Persian wāzīg.

Lesson 2 of 5.
پارک جنگلی (pârk-e jangali) — a forest park, a wooded park.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a watchmaker, then the Persian for an amusement park.

You'll want to know: پارک جنگلی.
پارک جنگلی (pârk-e jangali) — "a forest park, a wooded park".
جمعه ها مردم در پارک جنگلی کباب درست میکنند. Jom'e-hâ mardom dar pârk-e jangali kabâb dorost mikonand. On Fridays people make kebabs in the forest park.
Many Iranian towns have a pârk-e jangali on their edge for picnics.

The word, taken apart.
pârk-e jangali is built from pârk, a loan usually traced to French parc, and jangali 'wooded', from jangal 'forest', usually traced to Sanskrit jaṅgala.

هفته پیش به نمایشگاه کتاب رفتیم. Hafte-ye pish be namâyeshgâh-e ketâb raftim. Last week we went to the book fair.

Lesson 5 of 5.
تعطیل شدن (ta'til shodan) — to close (for the day or a holiday), to shut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an exhibition, then the Persian for a cultural centre.

You'll want to know: تعطیل شدن.
تعطیل شدن (ta'til shodan) — "to close (for the day or a holiday), to shut".
بازار ساعت هشت شب تعطیل شد. Bâzâr sâ'at-e hasht-e shab ta'til shod. The bazaar closed at eight in the evening.
ta'til 'closed, a holiday' + shodan. Ruz-e ta'til — a day off.

The word, taken apart.
ta'til shodan is built from ta'til 'closure, a holiday', borrowed from Arabic taʿṭīl 'suspension, a break' (EtymDB), and the native verb shodan 'to become'.
