Lesson 1 of 7.
anrichten — to arrange on the plate; to cause (damage).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to taste for seasoning, then the German for to season.

You'll want to know: anrichten.
anrichten — "to arrange on the plate; to cause (damage)".
Sie richtete das Essen auf großen Tellern an. She arranged the food on large plates.
Separable. Also to cause damage: Schaden anrichten.

The word, taken apart.
anrichten is built on richten "to put right, to prepare", Old High German rihten, Proto-Germanic rihtijaną (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 7.
garnieren — to garnish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to season, then the German for to arrange on the plate.

You'll want to know: garnieren.
garnieren — "to garnish".
Er garnierte den Fisch mit Zitrone. He garnished the fish with lemon.
Regular, without ge-: hat garniert.

The word, taken apart.
garnieren was borrowed from French garnir "to furnish, to equip", which is usually traced to a Frankish word; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 4 of 7.
abkühlen — to cool down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to garnish, then the German for to serve.

You'll want to know: abkühlen.
abkühlen — "to cool down".
Der Kuchen kühlte am Fenster ab. The cake cooled down by the window.
Separable. Perfekt with sein when the thing itself cools: Der Kuchen ist abgekühlt.

The word, taken apart.
abkühlen joins ab and kühlen "to cool" (EtymDB), an inherited Germanic verb and a cousin of English cool.

Lesson 5 of 7.
knusprig — crispy, crunchy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to serve, then the German for to cool down.

You'll want to know: knusprig.
knusprig — "crispy, crunchy".
Das Brot war außen knusprig und innen weich. The bread was crispy outside and soft inside.
Built on knuspern, to nibble something crunchy.

The word, taken apart.
knusprig is built on knuspern "to nibble crunchily", a word that imitates the sound; EtymDB lists no parent.
