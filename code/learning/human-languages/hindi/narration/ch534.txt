5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मैच (maic) — a match (a game).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to pit against, then the Hindi for a stance.

You'll want to know: मैच.
मैच — maic — "a match (a game)".
कल शाम को क्रिकेट का मैच है। Kal śām ko krikeṭ kā maic hai. There is a cricket match tomorrow evening.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
मैच (maic) is a loan from English match.

Lesson 3 of 5.
जिताना (jitānā) — to bring victory to; to win (a match) for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a match (a game), then the Hindi for a contest.

You'll want to know: जिताना.
जिताना — jitānā — "to bring victory to; to win (a match) for".
अरुण ने आख़िर में दौड़कर टीम को जिताया। Aruṇ ne ākhir meṁ dauṛkar ṭīm ko jitāyā. In the end Arun ran it in and won the match for the team.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने अपनी टीम को जिताया, usne apnī ṭīm ko jitāyā.

The word, taken apart.
जिताना (jitānā) is the causative of jītnā, "to win", which comes from Sanskrit jita, "won, conquered" (CDIAL 5224): to make someone win.

Lesson 4 of 5.
विजेता (vijetā) — a winner; a champion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a contest, then the Hindi for to bring victory to.

You'll want to know: विजेता.
विजेता — vijetā — "a winner; a champion".
विजेता (vijetā) टीम ख़ुशी से नाच रही थी। Vijetā ṭīm khuśī se nāc rahī thī. The winning team was dancing for joy.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
विजेता (vijetā) is a tatsama, taken whole from Sanskrit vijetṛ, "a vanquisher, a conqueror" (Monier-Williams). Hindi takes the Sanskrit nominative form vijetā.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

The word, taken apart.
पुरस्कार (puraskār) is a tatsama, taken whole from Sanskrit puraskāra, "placing in front, honouring" (Monier-Williams). Hindi uses it for a prize or an award.
