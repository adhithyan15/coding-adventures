# നന്ദി · ശരി · അതെ — the first four chapters, run cold

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-PHRASE-C02-WHATS-YOUR-NAME-01, ML-PHRASE-C02-MY-NAME-IS-01, ML-LEX-SANTOSHAM-01, ML-PHRASE-C03-ARE-YOU-WELL-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s] A whole first meeting, in the order it happens.

- [YOU SAY: ask a name — *ninṟe pēru entāṇu?*]
- [YOU SAY: answer with your own — *enṟe pēru … āṇu*]
- [YOU SAY: that meeting them is a pleasure — *santōṣaṁ*]
- [YOU SAY: ask after them — *sukhamāṇō?*]
- [YOU SAY: leave, promising to come back — *pōyi varāṁ*]
- [YOU SAY: leave, promising to meet — *vīṇḍuṁ kāṇāṁ*]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C03-QUESTION-PARTICLE-O-01, ML-PHRASE-C04-POYI-VARAAM-01, ML-PHRASE-C04-VEENDUM-KAANAAM-01] -->

**A question in Malayalam can be made by an ending rather than a word order.**
*Sukhamāṇō* is the statement *you are well* with an **-ō** on the end, and the
whole difference between telling and asking sits in that one syllable.

The two goodbyes make a distinction English collapses. **പോയി വരാം** is *I will
go and come back* — said by the person leaving, about themselves. **വീണ്ടും
കാണാം** is *we will meet again* — said about both of you. Neither is a word for
*goodbye*, because neither treats the parting as final.

**ശരി** is the one to hold up. It is written four different ways across the
languages that share it, and the same spoken word carries across all of them —
a reminder that a script boundary is not a language boundary.
