Lesson 1 of 6.
おこなう (okonau) — to carry out; to hold (an event).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to compete, then the Japanese for bad.

You'll want to know: おこなう.
おこなう — okonau — "to carry out; to hold (an event)".
らいしゅう まちで まつりを おこないます。 — raishū machi de matsuri o okonaimasu — "The town holds a festival next week."
Polite おこないます, okonaimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): がっこうが しけんを おこないました。, gakkō ga shiken o okonaimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おこなう (okonau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 6.
きねん (kinen) — a commemoration; a memento.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hold (an event), then the Japanese for to join (in).

You'll want to know: きねん.
きねん — kinen — "a commemoration; a memento".
きねんに しゃしんを とりました。 — kinen ni shashin o torimashita — "We took a photo to remember the day."

The word, taken apart.
きねん (kinen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ki, "account" (Middle Chinese kiH) and nen, "thought" (nemH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
