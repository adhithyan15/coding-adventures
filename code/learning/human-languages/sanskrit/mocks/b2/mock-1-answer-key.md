## Writing

Task 1 must address the mobile-library coordinator, identify one access problem
and one specifically affected group, propose a practicable two-month trial,
name a credible risk, request a measurable result, and ask for a reply in
100–130 independently produced Sanskrit words. Task 2 must sustain a developed
220–280-word argument about expert knowledge and local memory in conservation,
consider both sides, develop two relevant examples, answer a counterargument,
state conditions for responsible decisions, and reach a reasoned conclusion.
Apply the shared rubric’s Devanagari, external sandhi, word-final visarga or
anusvara, continuous śirorekhā, and word-boundary criteria. Do not reward
copied prompt wording as independent production.

## Speaking

The prepared presentation must last 210–240 seconds and include affected
groups, a genuine comparison of two options, an answered objection, and two
usable success measures. The collaborative task must last 240–300 seconds and
show comparison, challenge, repair or revision, and a qualified joint
recommendation rather than parallel monologues. Ask all five defended-discussion
prompts. Score only the candidate’s own contribution for development,
interaction, organisation, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar, and pronunciation
and fluency in the declared documented tradition. Keyword notes are allowed;
language supplied by an interlocutor is not candidate evidence.

## Recording and provenance checks

Before operational use, verify the listening pace and the one-play delivery
once after one unscored orienting preview. Confirm that the production log names
the actual traditions represented by placeholders A and B, gives speaker provenance and reviewer confirmation, and records a qualified reviewer’s check
that educated regional pronunciation is natural rather than caricatured.
Retain the complete speaking paper for independent scoring.
