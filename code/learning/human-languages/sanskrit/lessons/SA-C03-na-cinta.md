# न चिन्ता (na cintā) — "no worry"

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-NA-NO, SA-LEX-DHANYAVADA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] When someone thanks you, this gentle reply says, simply, "don't give
it a thought."

## Sounds you'll need

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PATTERN-NA-CINTA]; assesses=[] -->

*na cin · tā*. The *c* is the *ch* of *church*, never a *k*; the final
*ā* is long.

## The word, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-CINTA-THOUGHT]; assesses=[] -->

*Na cintā* literally means "**[there is] no worry / no anxiety**" — used for
"never mind / no trouble / you're welcome." Two atoms: *na* ("not"), the
ancient negative from Chapter 1 — PIE **\*ne**, behind English **no**, **not**,
Latin *nōn*, and every Indo-Aryan "no." And *cintā* ("anxious
thought"), from the root *cint* ("to think, ponder") — the same
*cintā* borrowed into Hindi and Urdu (*cintā mat karo*, "don't worry"). So the
reply pairs the family's oldest negative with a verb of thinking: "think nothing
of it."
