Lesson 1 of 5.
фасоль (fasól) — beans.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bun, then the Russian for kefir.

You'll want to know: фасоль.
фасоль (fasól) — "beans".

The word, taken apart.
фасоль (fasól) came through Polish fasola from Middle High German fasôl, from Latin phaseolus, Greek phásēlos, "a bean" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
апельсин (apelsín) — an orange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for kefir, then the Russian for beans.

You'll want to know: апельсин.
апельсин (apelsín) — "an orange".

The word, taken apart.
апельсин (apelsín) comes from Dutch appelsien, "apple of China", like German Apfelsine, after French pomme de Sine; the Portuguese brought the fruit from China (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for beans, then the Russian for an orange.

You'll want to know: лимон.
лимон (limón) — "a lemon".

The word, taken apart.
лимон (limón) — recorded from the 1500s; it came through Modern Greek limóni or straight from Italian limone (Vasmer). The source is Arabic and Persian līmūn, a word from India (Vasmer).

Lesson 4 of 5.
банан (banán) — a banana.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an orange, then the Russian for a lemon.

You'll want to know: банан.
банан (banán) — "a banana".

The word, taken apart.
банан (banán) probably came from French banane, from a word of Kikongo, a language of the Congo (Vasmer); German Banane would have given a feminine noun.

Lesson 5 of 5.
виноград (vinográd) — grapes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a lemon, then the Russian for a banana.

You'll want to know: виноград.
виноград (vinográd) — "grapes".

The word, taken apart.
виноград (vinográd) — taken from Church Slavonic, says Vasmer, where it meant "a vine, a vineyard": вино, "wine", with град, "an enclosure". Some see a model in Gothic weinagards, "vineyard".
