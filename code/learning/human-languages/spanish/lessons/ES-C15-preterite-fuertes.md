# estuve — a third known verb joins the strong pattern

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ESTAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-ESTA-ESTAS, ES-LEX-BIEN, ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-SINGULAR-PATTERN, ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You know present **estoy, estás, está** and the strong past pairs
**tuve, tuviste, tuvo** and **hice, hiciste, hizo**. Now use the same strong
endings with **estuv-**: **estuve, estuviste, estuvo**.

## Grammar Lens: estar in the completed past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-ESTAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-ESTA-ESTAS, ES-LEX-BIEN, ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-SINGULAR-PATTERN] -->

| person | completed past |
|---|---|
| yo | **estuve** |
| tú | **estuviste** |
| él / ella / usted | **estuvo** |

Say **estuve · estuviste · estuvo**. The strong stem **estuv-** takes **-e,
-iste, -o**. These forms carry no written accents. Reuse known **bien**:

- **Estuve bien.** — “I was well.”
- **¿Estuviste bien?** — “Were you well?”
- **Estuvo bien.** — “He, she, or you-formal was well.”

## Grammar Lens: three strong singular rows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-SINGULAR-PATTERN, ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

- yo: **tuve · hice · estuve**.
- tú: **tuviste · hiciste · estuviste**.
- él / ella / usted: **tuvo · hizo · estuvo**.

All three rows use the learned strong endings. Only **hacer** changes its stem
shape from **hic-** to **hiz-** before third-person **-o**. Plural forms and
other irregular verbs still wait.

## The -uv- shape, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ETYMON-ESTUVE-ANALOGY]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-STARE, ES-GRAMMAR-ESTAR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-TENER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR] -->

**Estar** grew from a Romance verb related to Latin **stāre**, “stand.” But
modern **estuve** is not a direct, tidy wearing-down of Latin **stetī**.
Historical analogy helped reshape its past beside other strong forms, including
the **-uv-** pattern visible in **tuve**. This explains the family resemblance
without pretending that each modern letter came straight from one Latin form.
