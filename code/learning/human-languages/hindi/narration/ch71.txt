Hindi, chapter 71: Why, and Because.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
क्यों (kyoṁ) — why.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Retrieve the two items before this one by name: सकना and चाहना. Then run the k- words the deixis lesson gave you — क्या, कहाँ, and kaun. There is one more in that family, and it is the one a listener most often wants.

You'll want to know: क्यों.
क्यों — kyoṁ — why.
आप क्यों नहीं आ सकते? — āp kyoṁ nahīṁ ā sakte? — Why can't you come?
क्यों (kyoṁ) goes where any Hindi question word goes: in the sentence's ordinary slot, in front of the verb — not dragged to the front the way English drags why. The rest of the sentence keeps the order it always had, verb last.
On its own, क्यों (kyoṁ)? is a complete turn. It is how you ask somebody to explain what they have just said, and it costs one syllable.
The deixis lesson generalised the य- / व- / क- system and listed the k- words. This is one of the ones it listed and did not teach. Its क- is the same ancient stem as क्या and kaun, and it is Latin's qu- in quis and English's wh- in who — one Indo-European kʷ-, three different spellings of the same sound.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kyoṁ? on its own, as a whole turn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: āp kyoṁ nahīṁ ā sakte?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the four k- words together — kyā, kaun, kahāṁ, kyoṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Ask why the listener cannot come.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does क्यों (kyoṁ) stand in the sentence? (In its ordinary slot, in front of the verb — not fronted.) Which family does it belong to? (The k- question words.)

Lesson 2 of 3.
क्योंकि (kyoṁki) — because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask क्यों (kyoṁ)? out loud. Then notice that you have no way at all to answer it. Now say कि — the word from the last chapter that hangs a clause on a verb. The answer is those two words stuck together.

Grammar Lens: क्यों + कि.
मैं कल नहीं आ सकता क्योंकि मैं काम करता हूँ. — maiṁ kal nahīṁ ā saktā kyoṁki maiṁ kām kartā hūṁ. — I can't come tomorrow because I work.
Read the word: क्योंकि (kyoṁki) is क्यों + कि, why-that. Nothing is hidden in it. Hindi answers why by saying why-that and then a whole ordinary sentence, verb last, exactly as after plain कि.
The order is fixed and it is the useful way round: the claim first, the reason second. English can lead with because; the ordinary Hindi sentence does not.
That example is worth memorising whole, because it is the shape of an entire exam task: a written message that says you cannot meet and why. It needs three things this book has now given you — the negation, सकना, and क्योंकि (kyoṁki) — and it needed all three.
Look at what the word is made of one more time. क्यों (kyoṁ) comes down from Sanskrit; कि was borrowed from Persian. Hindi welded them into a single conjunction and nobody notices the seam. That is this language in one word.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the two pieces separately — kyoṁ, ki — then welded, kyoṁki]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṁ kal nahīṁ ā saktā kyoṁki maiṁ kām kartā hūṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which half of that sentence comes first — the claim]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Ask why the listener cannot come, then answer it: you cannot come tomorrow because you work.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What two words is क्योंकि (kyoṁki) made of? (क्यों + कि.) Which half of a क्योंकि (kyoṁki) sentence comes first? (The claim; the reason follows.)

Lesson 3 of 3.
Two turns: why, and because — asking for a reason and giving one, in two turns.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
क्योंकि (kyoṁki) is two words you already had. Say them apart, then together, then say where the verb of each half sits.
Reach back three chapters while you are here: या offers a choice and लेकिन contradicts. Neither can give a reason, which is what this chapter added.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kyoṁ? — then kyoṁki …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṁ kal nahīṁ ā saktā kyoṁki maiṁ kām kartā hūṁ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: maiṁ ānā chāhtā hūṁ lekin maiṁ nahīṁ ā saktā — the joiner and the two modal verbs in one sentence]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Take both turns: ask why the listener cannot come, then answer with a reason.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word asks for a reason, and which gives one? (क्यों asks, क्योंकि (kyoṁki) gives.) What is the second one made of? (The first one, plus कि.) Then stop.
