German, chapter 343: Die Konsequenzen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Konsequenz — the consequence; consistency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to trigger, then the German for the trigger.

You'll want to know: die Konsequenz.
die Konsequenz — "the consequence; consistency".
Wer zu spät kommt, muss die Konsequenzen tragen. Anyone who comes late has to bear the consequences.
Plural: die Konsequenzen. Konsequenzen ziehen — to draw conclusions and act on them.

The word, taken apart.
Konsequenz came through French conséquence from Latin cōnsequentia "a consequence, a natural succession" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from cōnsequī "to follow after".

Lesson 2 of 5.
das Resultat — the result.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: das Resultat.
das Resultat — "the result".
Das Resultat des Tests war sehr gut. The result of the test was very good.
Plural: die Resultate. A little more formal than das Ergebnis.

The word, taken apart.
Resultat was borrowed from French résultat (EtymDB), from Medieval Latin resultātum, built on resultāre "to spring back, to rebound" (Lewis & Short): what springs back from an action.

Lesson 3 of 5.
folglich — consequently, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the consequence, then the German for the result.

You'll want to know: folglich.
folglich — "consequently, therefore".
Es gab keinen Strom. Folglich konnten wir nicht kochen. There was no electricity. Consequently, we could not cook.
The verb comes right after it: folglich konnten wir …. Rather formal; in speech people often say deshalb.

The word, taken apart.
folglich is built with -lich on the family of folgen "to follow", inherited from Old High German folgēn and Proto-Germanic fulgijaną (EtymDB): what follows from something.

Lesson 4 of 5.
sodass — so that (as a result).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the result, then the German for consequently.

You'll want to know: sodass.
sodass — "so that (as a result)".
Es hat stark geschneit, sodass die Schule geschlossen war. It snowed heavily, so that the school was closed.
Sodass sends the verb to the end: …, sodass die Schule geschlossen war. Also written so dass. It names a result, not an aim.

The word, taken apart.
sodass is the joined spelling of so dass, from so and dass "that"; both spellings are correct today. EtymDB lists the word without a history, so this is its plain make-up.

Lesson 5 of 5.
daher — therefore, that is why; from there.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for consequently, then the German for so that.

You'll want to know: daher.
daher — "therefore, that is why; from there".
Ich hatte kein Geld dabei, daher habe ich nichts gekauft. I had no money on me, so I did not buy anything.
The verb comes right after it: daher habe ich …. Daher kommt das! — so that is where it comes from.

The word, taken apart.
daher joins da "there" and her "hither, from", and first meant "from there"; the sense "for that reason" grew out of it. EtymDB has the word but no history for it.
