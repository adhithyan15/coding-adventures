German, chapter 399: Am Steuer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Lenkrad — the steering wheel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the roundabout, then the German for to turn off.

You'll want to know: das Lenkrad.
das Lenkrad — "the steering wheel".
Mein Vater hält das Lenkrad mit beiden Händen. My father holds the steering wheel with both hands.
Plural: die Lenkräder. Also das Steuer: am Steuer sitzen — to be at the wheel.

The word, taken apart.
Lenkrad is a compound of lenken 'to steer' and Rad 'wheel' (EtymDB lists it under Rad). Both parts are inherited Germanic words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lenken — to steer; to direct.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to turn off, then the German for the steering wheel.

You'll want to know: lenken.
lenken — "to steer; to direct".
Auf dem Eis kann er das Auto kaum lenken. On the ice he can hardly steer the car.
Also figurative: das Gespräch auf ein Thema lenken — to steer the talk towards a topic.

The word, taken apart.
lenken is an inherited Germanic verb that first meant 'to bend, to turn' (EtymDB gives 'to turn'). EtymDB links it with English lank 'limp, thin'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
bremsen — to brake; to slow down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the steering wheel, then the German for to steer.

You'll want to know: bremsen.
bremsen — "to brake; to slow down".
Der Fahrer bremst, weil ein Hund über die Straße läuft. The driver brakes because a dog runs across the road.
Er bremst, er hat gebremst. Figurative: jemanden bremsen — to hold someone back.

The word, taken apart.
bremsen is built on the noun Bremse 'brake', which is usually traced to Middle Low German premse 'a clamp'. EtymDB files the verb under its look-alike Bremse 'horsefly', so the line is hedged.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Bremse — the brake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to steer, then the German for to brake.

You'll want to know: die Bremse.
die Bremse — "the brake".
Die Bremsen an meinem Fahrrad sind kaputt. The brakes on my bike are broken.
Plural: die Bremsen. Die Bremse is also a horsefly, a different word that only looks the same.

The word, taken apart.
Die Bremse 'brake' is usually traced to Middle Low German premse 'a clamp', first one put on a restless horse's nose. EtymDB only lists the look-alike Bremse 'horsefly', inherited from Middle Low and Middle High German, so the line for the brake is hedged.

Lesson 5 of 5.
schalten — to change gear; to switch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to brake, then the German for the brake.

You'll want to know: schalten.
schalten — "to change gear; to switch".
Am Berg schaltet sie in den zweiten Gang. On the hill she changes into second gear.
Schalten also means to switch: das Licht einschalten — to switch the light on.

The word, taken apart.
schalten is inherited through Middle High German schalten from Old High German scaltan (EtymDB), usually glossed 'to push, to shove'; it is said to have first meant pushing a boat along with a pole, later a lever pushed into place.
