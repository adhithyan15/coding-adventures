Arabic, chapter 399: If and unless.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
إلا إذا (illā idhā) — unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for literacy, then the Arabic for attendance.

You'll want to know: إلا إذا.
إلا إذا — illā idhā — "unless".
لن نخرج إلا إذا توقف المطر. lan nakhruja illā idhā tawaqqafa al-maṭar. We will not go out unless the rain stops.
After إذا the verb is usually in the past, even for the future.

The word, taken apart.
إلا إذا (illā idhā) joins the native Arabic particles illā, "except", and idhā, "if": except if, unless. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
في حال (fī ḥāl) — in case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for attendance, then the Arabic for unless.

You'll want to know: في حال.
في حال — fī ḥāl — "in case".
في حال تأخر القطار، سأتصل بك. fī ḥāl taʾakhkhara al-qiṭār, sa-attaṣilu bik. In case the train is late, I will call you.
Also في حالة, fī ḥālat: in the event of.

The word, taken apart.
في حال (fī ḥāl) joins fī, "in", with ḥāl, "a state, a condition", built on the native Arabic root ḥ-w-l, "to change, to turn" (Lane). Literally, in the state that.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بشرط أن (bi-sharṭi an) — provided that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for unless, then the Arabic for in case.

You'll want to know: بشرط أن.
بشرط أن — bi-sharṭi an — "provided that".
سأساعدك بشرط أن تساعدني. sa-usāʿiduka bi-sharṭi an tusāʿidanī. I will help you provided that you help me.
The verb after أن takes the subjunctive: أن تساعدني.

The word, taken apart.
بشرط أن (bi-sharṭi an) joins bi-, "with", sharṭ, "a condition", built on the native Arabic root sh-r-ṭ, "to set a mark, to stipulate" (Lane), and an, "that": on condition that.

Lesson 4 of 5.
طالما (ṭālamā) — as long as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for in case, then the Arabic for provided that.

You'll want to know: طالما.
طالما — ṭālamā — "as long as".
سأبقى هنا طالما أنت هنا. sa-abqā hunā ṭālamā anta hunā. I will stay here as long as you are here.
With the past it also means often: طالما (ṭālamā) حلمت بذلك, I often dreamed of that.

The word, taken apart.
طالما (ṭālamā) joins ṭāla, "it was long", built on the native Arabic root ṭ-w-l, "to be long" (Lane), with mā: for as long as.

Lesson 5 of 5.
وإلا (wa-illā) — otherwise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for provided that, then the Arabic for as long as.

You'll want to know: وإلا.
وإلا — wa-illā — "otherwise".
يجب أن يدرس، وإلا سيرسب. yajibu an yadrusa, wa-illā sa-yarsub. He has to study, otherwise he will fail.

The word, taken apart.
وإلا (wa-illā) joins the native Arabic wa-, "and", with illā, which here is in, "if", fused with lā, "not": and if not, otherwise. The deeper origin of such particles is not settled.
