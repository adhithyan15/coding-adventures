French, chapter 426: À la réception.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la réception — the reception desk; the reception (party).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to camp, then the French for to bivouac.

You'll want to know: la réception.
la réception — "the reception desk; the reception (party)".
Nous laissons la clé à la réception. We leave the key at reception.
Une réception is also a formal party.

The word, taken apart.
réception is a learned borrowing from Latin receptiō 'a receiving', from recipere 'to receive', built on capere 'to take' (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'étage — the storey, the floor (of a building).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reception desk, then the French for the receptionist.

You'll want to know: l'étage.
l'étage — "the storey, the floor (of a building)".
Notre chambre est au troisième étage. Our room is on the third floor.
A masculine noun. Le rez-de-chaussée is the ground floor; the premier étage is one floor up.

The word, taken apart.
étage comes from Old French estage 'dwelling, level', which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin word built on stāre 'to stand'. EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: le séjour.
le séjour — "the stay; the living room".
Le patron de l'hôtel nous a souhaité un bon séjour. The hotel owner wished us a pleasant stay.
La salle de séjour or le séjour — the living room.

The word, taken apart.
séjour is built on séjourner 'to stay', which goes back to Vulgar Latin subdiurnāre, built on diēs 'day' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
déballer — to unpack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the storey, then the French for the stay.

You'll want to know: déballer.
déballer — "to unpack".
Il a déballé ses affaires et rangé ses chemises. He unpacked his things and put his shirts away.
Figurative: déballer tout — to come out with everything.

The word, taken apart.
déballer is built from dé- and balle 'bale, bundle', which is usually traced to a Frankish word meaning 'ball'. EtymDB does not list the verb.
