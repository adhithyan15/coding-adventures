Arabic, chapter 340: Rulers and tribes.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
حاكم (ḥākim) — a governor; a ruler.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to lead, then the Arabic for to withdraw.

You'll want to know: حاكم.
حاكم — ḥākim — "a governor; a ruler".
كان الحاكم عادلا مع الناس. kāna al-ḥākim ʿādilan maʿa an-nās. The ruler was fair to the people.
Plural: حكام, ḥukkām.

The word, taken apart.
حاكم (ḥākim) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-m (Lane), "to judge, to rule". Ḥākim is the active participle, "one who rules".

Lesson 2 of 6.
سلطان (sulṭān) — a sultan; power.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to withdraw, then the Arabic for a governor.

You'll want to know: سلطان.
سلطان — sulṭān — "a sultan; power".
سكن السلطان في قصر على النهر. sakana as-sulṭān fī qaṣr ʿalā an-nahr. The sultan lived in a palace on the river.
Plural: سلاطين, salāṭīn.

The word, taken apart.
سلطان (sulṭān) is built on the native Arabic root s-l-ṭ (Lane), "to have power over". Lane gives sulṭān first as power and proof, then the one who holds power; Aramaic shulṭānā, power, is usually cited beside it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
بلاط (balāṭ) — a royal court; paving stones.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a sultan, then the Arabic for a tribe.

You'll want to know: بلاط.
بلاط — balāṭ — "a royal court; paving stones".
كان الشاعر يعمل في بلاط الملك. kāna ash-shāʿir yaʿmalu fī balāṭ al-malik. The poet worked at the king’s court.
At home, بلاط (balāṭ) is floor tiles.

The word, taken apart.
بلاط (balāṭ) is given by Lane under b-l-ṭ as stones laid for paving. It was borrowed from Aramaic pallāṭīn (EtymDB); the sense of a royal court is usually traced further to Latin palatium, a palace.

Lesson 5 of 6.
تولى (tawallā) — to take office; to take charge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a tribe, then the Arabic for a royal court.

You'll want to know: تولى.
تولى — tawallā — "to take office; to take charge".
تولى الأمير الحكم بعد أبيه. tawallā al-amīr al-ḥukm baʿda abīhi. The prince took power after his father.
Present: يتولى, yatawallā.
تولى (tawallā) الأمر: he took charge of the matter.

The word, taken apart.
تولى (tawallā) is built on the native Arabic root w-l-y (Lane), "to be near; to have charge of", the root of walī, a guardian. It is Form V, "to take charge".
