# eram, erat — the past that stays open

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-LEX-SUM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I am" and "they are". (*Sum. Sunt.*) Now "I was", in the
perfect. (*Fuī.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Fuī* closes a door: I was, and that is over. Latin has a second
"was" that holds it open.

## Grammar Lens: six forms, and the endings you know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C162-PAS162-03, LA-LEX-C68-AEGER-02] -->

> **Ōlim Rōma parva erat.** — Long ago Rome was small.

**erat** is "was", the **imperfect** of *sum*. All six persons:

> **eram** — I was. **erās** — you were. **erat** — he, she, it was.
> **erāmus** — we were. **erātis** — you all were. **erant** — they were.

[PAUSE 2s] The endings **-m**, **-s**, **-t**, **-mus**, **-tis**, **-nt**
are those of *sum*, *es*, *est*, *sumus*, *estis*, *sunt*, on the stem
**era-**. Before *-m*, *-t* and *-nt* the *a* is short.

[PAUSE 1s] *Erat* is a state that went on, with no edge in view.

> **Fessus eram.** — I was tired.
> **Caelum obscūrum erat.** — The sky was dark.

## Why it's said this way: fuit or erat?
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01, LA-LEX-C69-AFFECTUS-04, LA-LEX-C45-WORLD-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Both are "was"; the choice is the frame. *Laetus eram* paints a
mood that lasted through the story. *Laetus fuī* sums it up from outside:
I was happy, and that time is over. A story sets its scene with *erat*.

## The word, taken apart: eram
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-C281-ERAM-02]; assesses=[] -->

**eram** is usually explained as an older *\*esam*, built on the same root
as *est*: between two vowels an old Latin *s* turned into *r*, and *es-a-m*
came out as *eram*. Neither EtymDB nor Lewis & Short records a form that
old, so this is the account histories of Latin give, offered here as one.
The *s* survives where no vowel followed it: *es*, *est*, *estis*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01, LA-ETYMON-C281-ERAM-02, LA-LEX-C69-AFFECTUS-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "They were"? (***Erant.***) "We were"? (***Erāmus.***) Whose
endings does *eram* borrow? (The present ***sum, es, est***.) Which "was"
paints a scene, *fuit* or *erat*? (***Erat.***) And what did the *r* of
*eram* used to be? (An ***s***, between two vowels.)
