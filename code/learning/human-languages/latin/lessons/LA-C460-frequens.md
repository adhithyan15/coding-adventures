## You'll want to know: frequēns, frequentis
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C460-TURBA-02]; assesses=[] -->

**frequēns, frequentis** — "crowded, full of people; frequent".

> Forum hodiē frequēns est, quod mercātus est.
>
> *The forum is crowded today, because it is market day.*

An adjective of one ending: *frequēns*, genitive *frequentis*. First 'packed close', then 'often'. English *frequent* comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*frequēns* is usually traced to PIE *bʰrek-* 'to cram together' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short compare *farciō* 'to stuff'). English *frequent* comes from it.
