Latin, chapter 552: Rooms and furniture.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
domicilium, domiciliī — a home, dwelling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for writing tablets, then the Latin for to write back.

You'll want to know: domicilium, domiciliī.
domicilium, domiciliī — "a home, dwelling".
Haec vīlla domicilium familiae nostrae est. This villa is our family's home.
Neuter, second declension, from domus. English domicile.

The word, taken apart.
domicilium is built on domus "house"; Lewis & Short sees in its second part the root of cēlō "to hide". English domicile comes from it.

conclāve, conclāvis — "a room".
Hoc conclāve parvum sed lūcidum est. This room is small but bright.
Neuter, third declension: a room that can be locked with one key, clāvis. English conclave.

The word, taken apart.
conclāve is built on con- and clāvis "key" (Lewis & Short): a room shut with one key. English conclave comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
supellex, supellectilis — household furniture, goods.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a home, then the Latin for a room.

You'll want to know: supellex, supellectilis.
supellex, supellectilis — "household furniture, goods".
Supellex nostra simplex est: lectī, mēnsa, sellae. Our furniture is simple: beds, a table, chairs.
Feminine, third declension, singular only: all the movable things of a house. Ablative supellectile.

The word, taken apart.
supellex is usually traced, as in Lewis & Short, to super and legō "to gather", but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
scōpae, scōpārum — a broom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for household furniture, then the Latin for to sweep.

You'll want to know: scōpae, scōpārum.
scōpae, scōpārum — "a broom".
Servus scōpīs culīnam pūrgat. The slave cleans the kitchen with a broom.
Feminine, first declension, plural: a bundle of twigs. Italian scopa — broom — comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
scōpae first meant "twigs" (Lewis & Short), and a bundle of twigs made a broom. Lewis & Short connects it with a root meaning "to prop", but the origin is not settled.
