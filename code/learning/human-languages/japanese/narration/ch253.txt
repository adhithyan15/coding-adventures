Japanese, chapter 253: What Was Going On.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ふっています / ふっていました (futte imasu / futte imashita) — what is going on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "It will probably rain tomorrow". (ashita ame ga furu deshō.) Then the -て form of ふる. (futte.)

You'll want to know: -ています.
あめが ふっています。 ame ga futte imasu. — "It is raining."
The -て form with います (imasu), "is there", says what is going on: the rain has started and has not stopped.
いま あめが ふっています。 ima ame ga futte imasu. — "It is raining now."
ともだちを まっています。 tomodachi o matte imasu. — "I am waiting for a friend."

You'll want to know: -ていました.
With いました (imashita), it was going on:
きのう あめが ふっていました。 kinō ame ga futte imashita. — "It was raining yesterday."
ははは しんぶんを よんでいました。 haha wa shinbun o yonde imashita. — "My mother was reading the newspaper."
A story needs both kinds of past. -ていました is the scene, going on; -ました is the event, once, inside it:
あめが ふっていました。 ame ga futte imashita. — "It was raining."
でんしゃが とまりました。 densha ga tomarimashita. — "The train stopped."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "My mother was reading the newspaper" — haha wa shinbun o yonde imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My father was reading the newspaper" — chichi wa shinbun o yonde imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My elder sister was reading the newspaper" — ane wa shinbun o yonde imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that it was raining yesterday.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It is raining"? (あめが ふっています.) "It was raining"? (ふっていました.) "I am waiting"? (まっています.) Which is the scene, ふっていました or とまりました? (ふっていました.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
とき (toki) — when; the time when.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "My mother was asleep". (haha wa nete imashita.) Then say "I got up", to a friend. (okita.)

You'll want to know: とき.
とき — toki — "when", "the time when". The plain form goes before it, and 、 closes the time:
かえった とき、ははは ねていました。 kaetta toki, haha wa nete imashita. — "When I got home, my mother was asleep."
おきた とき、あめが ふっていました。 okita toki, ame ga futte imashita. — "When I got up, it was raining."
After a noun it takes の:
こどもの とき、うみに いきました。 kodomo no toki, umi ni ikimashita. — "When I was a child, I went to the sea."

The word, taken apart.
とき (toki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict writes it with a kanji glossed "time; hour; moment", and KANJIDIC2 gives とき as that kanji's native reading; it is the とき inside ときどき, "sometimes" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: toki]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When I got home, my mother was asleep" — kaetta toki, haha wa nete imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When I got home, my father was asleep" — kaetta toki, chichi wa nete imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say that when you got up, it was raining.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"When I was a child"? (こどもの とき (toki).) "When I got home"? (かえった とき (toki).)


Lesson 3 of 6.
年じゅう (nenjū) — all year round.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: You'll want to know: 年じゅう.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "when I was a child". (kodomo no toki.) Then say "hot". (atsui.)

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
You'll want to know: 年じゅう.
年じゅう — nenjū — "all year round", the whole year through.
ここは 年じゅう あついです。 koko wa nenjū atsui desu. — "It is hot here all year round."
Three beats: nen–jū, with the long ū. The first sign is new, read ねん (nen): the nen of らいねん and きょねん. The next lesson writes it.

The word, taken apart.
年じゅう (nenjū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nen, "year" (Middle Chinese nen) and jū, "in, inside, middle" (trjuwng), voiced from chū (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict spells the word with two kanji, or with the second part in kana, as here, and glosses it "all year round; throughout the year" (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: nenjū]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It is hot here all year round" — koko wa nenjū atsui desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It is cold here all year round" — koko wa nenjū samui desu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'all year round'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"All year round"? (年じゅう, nenjū.) Its first sign is read? (ねん, nen, as in らいねん.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
年 (nen) — the year, six strokes.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script — the shape and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say nenjū, all year round. Then call up the four strokes of 月: left side, top and right side, two bars.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the shape.
年 — nen.
Its parts:
a short falling stroke at the top.
three bars.
a short stem on the left.
a long centre stem.
Six strokes, five lifts:
draw the short falling stroke at the top, down to the left.
lift, draw the top bar from left to right.
lift, draw the second bar from left to right.
lift, draw the short stem on the left, down from the second bar.
lift, draw the long third bar from left to right.
lift, draw the long centre stem from the top bar straight down through the others.
The long stem comes last and runs through the bars below the top one. Here it is read ねん (nen), as in 年じゅう (nenjū); this lesson asks only for that reading.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
Find 年 (nen) in 年じゅう and say nenjū.
Copy 年 three times, saying nen at each one.
Find it once more, in ここは 年じゅう あついです (koko wa nenjū atsui desu), and say the line.

Wrap-up Recall.
How many strokes in all? (Six.)

[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which stroke of the kanji read nen comes last?


Lesson 5 of 6.
(reading) — a day at the river.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Thirteen short lines, a summer day told in order. Every word in them is one you know.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
なつやすみに ちちと かわに いきました。 あさ はやく おきて、でかけました。 でんしゃに のって、えきで おりました。 それから、すこし あるきました。 ついた とき (toki)、あめが ふっていました。 あめは ひるに やみました。 ちちと かわで およぎました。 みずは きれいでした。 ひるごはんを たべてから、さかなを みました。 ちちは しんぶんを よんでいました。 そして、ゆうがた うちに かえりました。 かわの みずは 年じゅう (nenjū) つめたいです。 月日は はやいです。
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the thirteen lines once, without stopping]
Line by line:
なつやすみに ちちと かわに いきました。 (natsuyasumi ni chichi to kawa ni ikimashita.) — "In the summer holidays I went to the river with my father."
あさ はやく おきて、でかけました。 (asa hayaku okite, dekakemashita.) — "I got up early in the morning and set off."
でんしゃに のって、えきで おりました。 (densha ni notte, eki de orimashita.) — "We took the train and got off at the station."
それから、すこし あるきました。 (sorekara, sukoshi arukimashita.) — "After that, we walked a little."
ついた とき、あめが ふっていました。 (tsuita toki, ame ga futte imashita.) — "When we arrived, it was raining."
あめは ひるに やみました。 (ame wa hiru ni yamimashita.) — "The rain stopped at noon."
ちちと かわで およぎました。 (chichi to kawa de oyogimashita.) — "I swam in the river with my father."
みずは きれいでした。 (mizu wa kirei deshita.) — "The water was clean."
ひるごはんを たべてから、さかなを みました。 (hirugohan o tabete kara, sakana o mimashita.) — "After eating lunch, we watched the fish."
ちちは しんぶんを よんでいました。 (chichi wa shinbun o yonde imashita.) — "My father was reading the newspaper."
そして、ゆうがた うちに かえりました。 (soshite, yūgata uchi ni kaerimashita.) — "And in the evening we went home."
かわの みずは 年じゅう つめたいです。 (kawa no mizu wa nenjū tsumetai desu.) — "The river water is cold all year round."
月日は はやいです。 (tsukihi wa hayai desu.) — "Time flies."
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the thirteen lines again, aloud, without the glosses]

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: who went to the river — chichi to]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what the father was doing after lunch — shinbun o yonde imashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: when they went home — yūgata]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
When they arrived at the river, what was the weather doing?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which lines set a scene, going on? (Five and ten: ふっていました, よんでいました.) Which word puts lunch first? (たべてから.)
