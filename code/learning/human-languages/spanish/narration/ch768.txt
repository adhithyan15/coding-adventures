Spanish, chapter 768: Lo que cociné.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hervir — to boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to seem, then the Spanish for to keep.

You'll want to know: hervir.
hervir — "to boil".
Herví el agua para la pasta. I boiled the water for the pasta.
Stem change: hiervo, hirvió. El agua está hirviendo — the water is boiling.

The word, taken apart.
hervir is inherited from Latin fervēre 'to boil, to seethe' (EtymDB), through old Spanish herver. Latin f- became Spanish h-. English fervent comes from the same verb.

You'll want to know: freír.
freír — "to fry".
Frió los huevos con un poco de aceite. He fried the eggs in a little oil.
Stem change: frío, frió. Participle: frito — patatas fritas.

The word, taken apart.
freír is inherited from Latin frīgere 'to roast, to fry' (EtymDB). The g between vowels was lost. It is not the frigidus 'cold' behind frío.

You'll want to know: pelar.
pelar — "to peel".
Pelé las patatas y las corté. I peeled the potatoes and cut them.
Pelarse is to peel after sunburn. Hace un frío que pela — it is bitterly cold.

The word, taken apart.
pelar is inherited from Latin pilāre 'to strip of hair' (EtymDB), built on pilus 'a hair', the word behind pelo. Peeling a fruit is stripping its skin.

You'll want to know: batir.
batir — "to beat, to whisk".
Batí tres huevos para la tortilla. I beat three eggs for the omelette.
Batir un récord — to break a record.

The word, taken apart.
batir comes from Late Latin battere 'to beat' (EtymDB), a form of Latin battuere. Batido 'milkshake' is its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to peel, then the Spanish for to beat.

You'll want to know: el ingrediente.
el ingrediente — "the ingredient".
Usé solo cuatro ingredientes para la salsa. I used only four ingredients for the sauce.

The word, taken apart.
ingrediente is a learned borrowing from Latin ingrediēns 'going in' (EtymDB), from ingredī 'to enter', built on in and gradī 'to step'. An ingredient is what goes into the dish.
