French, chapter 437: Petites rues et grandes avenues.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'impasse — the dead end; the deadlock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to go round, then the French for to turn back.

You'll want to know: l'impasse.
l'impasse — "the dead end; the deadlock".
L'hôtel est au fond d'une impasse calme. The hotel is at the end of a quiet dead end.
A feminine noun. Figurative: être dans l'impasse — to be at a deadlock.

The word, taken apart.
impasse is built from in- 'not' and passe, from passer 'to pass' (EtymDB), which goes back to Vulgar Latin passāre, from passus 'a step'.

Nous nous promenons dans les ruelles du vieux quartier. We stroll through the alleys of the old quarter.
A small rue.

The word, taken apart.
ruelle is a small rue 'street', which is inherited from Latin rūga 'wrinkle, fold' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB lists it without a source.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'avenue — the avenue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the dead end, then the French for the alley.

You'll want to know: l'avenue.
l'avenue — "the avenue".
Les grands magasins sont sur l'avenue principale. The big shops are on the main avenue.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
avenue is the old feminine past participle of avenir 'to come to' (EtymDB), from Latin advenīre, built on venīre 'to come': the way that leads up to a place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
le boulevard — the boulevard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the alley, then the French for the avenue.

You'll want to know: le boulevard.
le boulevard — "the boulevard".
Le bus passe par le boulevard qui fait le tour du centre. The bus goes along the boulevard that runs round the centre.
Often lined with trees.

The word, taken apart.
boulevard was borrowed from Middle Dutch bolwerc 'bulwark' (EtymDB): first the top of a town wall, later the wide road built where the wall had been.
