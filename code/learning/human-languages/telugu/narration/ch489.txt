Telugu, chapter 489: Cheated at the Market.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
జేబుదొంగ (jēbudoṅga) — a pickpocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a shortage, then the Telugu for an auction.

You'll want to know: జేబుదొంగ.
జేబుదొంగ — jēbudoṅga — "a pickpocket".
రద్దీ బస్సులో జేబుదొంగ నా పర్సు కొట్టేశాడు. raddī bassulō jēbudoṅga nā parsu koṭṭēśāḍu. In the crowded bus a pickpocket took my purse.
జేబు, a pocket, + దొంగ, a thief.

The word, taken apart.
జేబుదొంగ (jēbudoṅga) is a Telugu compound: జేబు, "a pocket", a loan from Arabic jayb through Persian and Hindustani, and దొంగ, "a thief", native Dravidian (DEDR 2982).

Lesson 3 of 5.
మోసపోవు (mōsapōvu) — to be cheated.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a pickpocket, then the Telugu for adulteration.

You'll want to know: మోసపోవు.
మోసపోవు — mōsapōvu — "to be cheated".
నకిలీ బంగారం కొని అతను మోసపోయాడు. nakilī baṅgāraṁ koni atanu mōsapōyāḍu. He was cheated when he bought fake gold.
Past (I …): మోసపోయాను, mōsapōyānu.
మోసం, a fraud, + పోవు: to fall into a fraud. మోసం చేయు is to cheat someone.

The word, taken apart.
మోసపోవు (mōsapōvu) is a Telugu compound: మోసం, "deceit", usually traced to Sanskrit mōṣa, "theft" (CDIAL 10358), and the native పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572).

Lesson 4 of 5.
కుళ్ళు (kuḷḷu) — to rot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for adulteration, then the Telugu for to be cheated.

You'll want to know: కుళ్ళు.
కుళ్ళు — kuḷḷu — "to rot".
వేడికి పండ్లు రెండు రోజుల్లో కుళ్ళాయి. vēḍiki paṇḍlu reṇḍu rōjullō kuḷḷāyi. In the heat the fruit rotted in two days.
Past (it …): కుళ్ళింది, kuḷḷindi.
కుళ్ళిన, rotten: కుళ్ళిన టమాటాలు, rotten tomatoes. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: కుళ్ళింది, it rotted.

The word, taken apart.
కుళ్ళు (kuḷḷu) is a native-looking Telugu verb whose origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మిగులు (migulu) — to be left over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be cheated, then the Telugu for to rot.

You'll want to know: మిగులు.
మిగులు — migulu — "to be left over".
పెళ్ళి తరువాత చాలా భోజనం మిగిలింది. peḷḷi taruvāta cālā bhōjanaṁ migilindi. After the wedding a lot of food was left over.
Past (I …): మిగిలాను, migilānu.
What is left takes no ending; the one who keeps it takes -కి: నాకు వంద రూపాయలు మిగిలాయి.

The word, taken apart.
మిగులు (migulu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 4838 lists Telugu migulu, "to remain, to be left over, to be saved", with Tamil miku, "to exceed".
