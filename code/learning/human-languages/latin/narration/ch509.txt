Latin, chapter 509: Courage and good sense.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fortitūdō, fortitūdinis — courage, bravery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for vigorous, then the Latin for diligent.

You'll want to know: fortitūdō, fortitūdinis.
fortitūdō, fortitūdinis — "courage, bravery".
Mīlitēs magnā fortitūdine pugnant. The soldiers fight with great courage.
Feminine, third declension, from fortis. One of the four great virtues of ancient ethics. English fortitude.

The word, taken apart.
fortitūdō is built on fortis "strong, brave" with -tūdō (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English fortitude comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
audācia, audāciae — boldness, daring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for diligent, then the Latin for courage.

You'll want to know: audācia, audāciae.
audācia, audāciae — "boldness, daring".
Audācia nautae omnēs servat. The sailor's daring saves everyone.
Feminine, first declension, from audāx. Often in a bad sense: recklessness, impudence. English audacity.

The word, taken apart.
audācia is built on audāx "bold", from audeō "to dare" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English audacity comes from the same adjective.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cōnstāns, cōnstantis — steadfast, consistent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for boldness, then the Latin for rash.

You'll want to know: cōnstāns, cōnstantis.
cōnstāns, cōnstantis — "steadfast, consistent".
Amīcus cōnstāns in perīculīs nōn fugit. A steadfast friend does not run away in danger.
One ending. The present participle of cōnstō — to stand firm. English constant.

The word, taken apart.
cōnstāns is the present participle of cōnstō "to stand firm", built on con- and stō "to stand" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English constant comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
prūdentia, prūdentiae — good sense, prudence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rash, then the Latin for steadfast.

You'll want to know: prūdentia, prūdentiae.
prūdentia, prūdentiae — "good sense, prudence".
Prūdentiā patris familia servātur. The family is saved by the father's good sense.
Feminine, first declension, from prūdēns, a short form of prōvidēns — foreseeing. English prudence.

The word, taken apart.
prūdentia is built on prūdēns "wise", a shortened prōvidēns "foreseeing" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB links it with prōvidentia). English prudence comes from it.
