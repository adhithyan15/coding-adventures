# Practice — four words, one law

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Four words, learned one at a time. Run them as a set now, and then
cash in what each one buys you in English.

## Guided Practice: the set, with articles
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PERE-01, FR-LEX-MERE-03, FR-LEX-FRERE-06, FR-LEX-SOEUR-08] -->

The article is half the word, and a family word said bare is a word half
learned.

- [YOU SAY: "le père, la mère" — the parents, masculine then feminine]
- [YOU SAY: "le frère, la sœur" — the siblings, same order]
- [YOU SAY: all four straight through, then in reverse]

[REPEAT x2] "le père, la mère, le frère, la sœur"

## Guided Practice: the three that rhyme, and the one that does not
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PERE-01, FR-LEX-MERE-03, FR-LEX-FRERE-06, FR-LEX-SOEUR-08, FR-ETYMON-SOROR-SORORITE-09] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "père, mère, frère" — one vowel, three times: the open **è**]
- [YOU SAY: "sœur" after them, and hear the break]
- [YOU SAY: why it broke — "soror ground down two syllables into one"]

## Guided Practice: cash in the English
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-ETYMON-PATER-PATERNEL-02, FR-ETYMON-MATER-MATERNEL-04, FR-ETYMON-FRATER-FRATERNEL-07, FR-ETYMON-SOROR-SORORITE-09] -->

Each French word hands you an English one you already knew and never thought
about, and the French is the key rather than the lock.

- [YOU SAY: "père → paternal, patron"]
- [YOU SAY: "mère → maternal, matter"]
- [YOU SAY: "frère → fraternal, friar"]
- [YOU SAY: "sœur → sorority"]

## What you've built this chapter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-ETYMON-GRIMM-PERE-FATHER-05, FR-SCRIPT-OE-LIGATURE-10] -->

- **four family words**, each with its article.
- **one sound-law** — Grimm's law, *p → f* and *t → th*. It is why *père* is
  *father*, *mère* is *mother* and *frāter* is *brother*, and it turned three
  separate memorisations into one fact.
- **a letter** — **œ**, one character, waiting in *cœur*, *œuf* and *œil*.
- **a habit worth more than any of them**: when a French word looks like
  nothing, reach for the long formal English word rather than the short common
  one. *Père* is opaque next to *father* and obvious next to *paternal*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PERE-01, FR-LEX-MERE-03, FR-LEX-FRERE-06, FR-LEX-SOEUR-08, FR-ETYMON-GRIMM-PERE-FATHER-05, FR-SCRIPT-OE-LIGATURE-10] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Give all four family words with their articles. (**le père, la mère,
le frère, la sœur**.) Which two are feminine? (**la mère, la sœur**.) State
Grimm's law in the form you need. (**p → f**, **t → th**.) Use it: what English
word is *frāter*? (**brother**.) How many letters is **œ**, and name a word
besides *sœur* that has it. (**One**; *cœur*, *œuf* or *œil*.) Which English
word for physical stuff traces back to *mère*? (**matter**.)
