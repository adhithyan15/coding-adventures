# (el correo del jefe) — read it, then do it yourself

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el jefe** and **reducir**. The message below is from one
about the other.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03] -->

An internal message, and one reply. Read both, then answer.

**1 — de la jefa**

> Buenos días. Tenemos que **reducir** el gasto de material este trimestre.
>
> Antes del viernes, decidme qué se puede dejar de comprar en vuestra sección.

**2 — la respuesta**

> Lo miro hoy. Todavía no **me decido** entre quitar el papel de color o bajar
> los pedidos de tinta, así que te digo mañana.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: what has to be cut, and by when]
- [YOU SAY: the two things the second person is choosing between]
- [YOU SAY: when the answer is coming]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01] -->

**No me decido entre** is the phrase to keep whole. With **entre** you name the
options you are stuck between; with **por** you name the one you finally take.
*No me decido entre A y B*, then *me decidí por A*.

**Decidme** is *vosotros*, plural and informal — a boss writing to her own
team in Spain. A Latin American writer would say *díganme*, and a formal
Spanish notice to the public would too. The verb ending places the writer,
the reader and the register in one word.

And **tenemos que reducir**: *nosotros*, although the decision is hers. Spanish
management writing does this constantly, and it is doing the same softening as
the *se* on a shop notice — spreading the action out so nobody is singled out
as its author.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-02, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines: as a boss,
say spending has to be cut and ask for suggestions by Friday; then as the
reply, say you are torn between two options and will answer tomorrow.

Then check yourself: did you use *entre* for the options and *por* for the
choice?
