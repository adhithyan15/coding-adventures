Gujarati, chapter 167: Okra, peas and eggs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ભીંડો (bhī̃ḍo) — okra, lady's finger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for fresh coriander, then the Gujarati for to grind.

You'll want to know: ભીંડો.
ભીંડો — bhī̃ḍo — "okra, lady's finger".
મને ભીંડાનું શાક ગમે છે. — mane bhī̃ḍānũ śāk game che — "I like okra curry."

The word, taken apart.
ભીંડો (bhī̃ḍo) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit bhiṇḍā, the vegetable Abelmoschus esculentus, and CDIAL lists the Gujarati bhĩḍo (CDIAL 9492).

Lesson 4 of 5.
બાફવું (bāphvũ) — to steam, to boil soft.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for pea, then the Gujarati for egg.

You'll want to know: બાફવું.
બાફવું — bāphvũ — "to steam, to boil soft".
તે શાક બાફે છે. — te śāk bāphe che — "She steams the vegetables."

The word, taken apart.
બાફવું (bāphvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit bāṣpāyate, "emits vapour", built on bāṣpa, "steam, tears", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to boil" (CDIAL 9225).

Lesson 5 of 5.
તળવું (taḷvũ) — to fry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for egg, then the Gujarati for to steam.

You'll want to know: તળવું.
તળવું — taḷvũ — "to fry".
માતા તેલમાં ભજિયાં તળે છે. — mātā telmā̃ bhajiyā̃ taḷe che — "Mother fries bhajiyas in oil."

The word, taken apart.
તળવું (taḷvũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from talati, "fries", a reconstructed form beside Sanskrit talita, "fried", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to fry, cook in ghee" (CDIAL 5736).
