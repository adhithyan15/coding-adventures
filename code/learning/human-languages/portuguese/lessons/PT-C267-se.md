# se — if

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-SABER-CONHECER-02, PT-LEX-HA-QUE-DURACAO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I don't know". (*Não sei.*) Then "I have been studying
Portuguese for three years". (*Há três anos que estudo português.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Every sentence so far has said what is, or what was. This chapter
says what follows **if** something else is true, and it begins with two
letters.

## Grammar Lens: se opens a condition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-SE-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C35-SAUDE2-02, PT-LEX-C43-VERBOS4-01, PT-LEX-C48-CASA1-03, PT-LEX-C43-VERBOS4-03] -->

**se** — "if".

> **Se** tens fome, come. — If you're hungry, eat.
> **Se** tens frio, fecha a janela. — If you're cold, shut the window.

Whatever follows *se* is the **condition**; the rest of the sentence is what
follows from it. The condition can come first, with a comma, or second,
without one: *Fecha a janela **se** tens frio.* Notice that Portuguese **has**
hunger and cold, *tens fome*, *tens frio*, where English **is** hungry and cold.

**se** also means "whether", after a verb of knowing or asking, and here your
*não sei* comes in useful:

> **Não sei se** é verdade. — I don't know if it's true.

## The word, taken apart: one spelling, two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-SE-SI-02]; assesses=[PT-ETYMON-COMO-SE-CHAMA-02, PT-LEX-C132-VERBOS132-02] -->

You have met **se** before, in *como se chama* and *levantar-se*. That one
means "oneself", and it is a different word that happens to be spelled the
same way. EtymDB 2.1 keeps the two apart: **se**, "if", is inherited from
Latin *sī*, "if" (Lewis & Short, under *si*), whose vowel opened to *e*; and
**se**, "oneself", is inherited from Latin *sē*, "himself, herself, itself".

The ear tells them apart by their place. The "oneself" *se* clings to a verb:
*chama-se*, *levanta-se*, *como se chama*. The "if" *se* opens a whole clause.
