# inteligente — picking between things, which is all intelligence ever was

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C380-COUNT-37] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *alegre*. Notice it ends in **-e** and never changes for a man
or a woman. The word in this lesson does the same thing.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- [alegre](./ES-C380-alegre.md) — cheerful, and the first **-e** adjective you
  met that refuses to agree.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `g-soft` — the **g** in *-gente* comes before **e**, so it is the
  throaty sound. *In-te-li-JEN-te*, not *gen* as in English. →
  [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)
- `stress-penultimate` — stress on *JEN*, the second syllable from the end, and
  the word carries no written accent because that is already the default.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-02]; assesses=[] -->

**inteligente** — "intelligent." Latin **intellegere**, which is two pieces:

- **inter** — between
- **legere** — to gather, to pick out, and later to read

To be intelligent is to **pick between**. Not to know a great deal; to choose
well among the things in front of you. The Romans named the ability after the
sorting, not after the store of facts.

*Legere* is one of the most productive verbs Latin ever handed over, and every
one of these is the same act of picking:

| word | what is picked |
|---|---|
| **select** | chosen out (*se-* apart + *legere*) |
| **collect** | picked together |
| **elect** | picked out of a group |
| **legible** | able to be picked out by eye |
| **lecture** | a reading aloud |

Spanish has them too: *seleccionar*, *colección*, *elegir*, *legible*, *lectura*.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-02, ES-LEX-C423-CHAR-01] -->

Two shapes of adjective, side by side:

| ending | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **-o** | antipátic**o** | antipátic**a** |
| **-e** | inteligente | inteligente |

An **-o** adjective has two forms. An **-e** adjective has one. That is the
whole distinction, and it saves you from ever inventing *inteligenta*.
