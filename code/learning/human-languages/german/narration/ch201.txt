5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Dose — a tin, a can.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a waitress, then the German for cutlery.

You'll want to know: die Dose.
die Dose — "a tin, a can".

The word, taken apart.
Dose was borrowed into standard German from Low German dose, "box, case". Its origin is uncertain; EtymDB 2.1 has the word but no source for it.

The word, taken apart.
Topf from Middle High German topf (EtymDB 2.1), a central German word of uncertain origin; Dutch dop, "little cup", may be related.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Pfanne — a frying pan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a tin, then the German for a pot.

You'll want to know: die Pfanne.
die Pfanne — "a frying pan".

The word, taken apart.
Pfanne from Middle High German pfanne from Old High German phanna (EtymDB 2.1), from Latin patina, "pan, dish" — an early loan whose p shifted to pf. English pan is the same word.

Spülmaschine is spülen, "to rinse", + the word for "machine" from French machine from Latin māchina (EtymDB 2.1).
