Japanese, chapter 264: A Bad Fall.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おちる (ochiru) — to fall; to drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to panic, then the Japanese for at once.

You'll want to know: おちる.
おちる — ochiru — "to fall; to drop".
こどもが かいだんから おちました。 — kodomo ga kaidan kara ochimashita — "A child fell down the stairs."
Polite おちます, ochimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): こどもが おちました。, kodomo ga ochimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おちる (ochiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ぶつける.
ぶつける — butsukeru — "to bump (something against); to hit".
こどもが つくえに あたまを ぶつけました。 — kodomo ga tsukue ni atama o butsukemashita — "A child banged his head on a desk."
Polite ぶつけます, butsukemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あにが あたまを ぶつけました。, ani ga atama o butsukemashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぶつける (butsukeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict). It is the transitive partner of butsukaru, "to bump into".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ほうたい (hōtai) — a bandage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to fall, then the Japanese for to bump (something against).

You'll want to know: ほうたい.
ほうたい — hōtai — "a bandage".
ほうたいは けがの ばしょに よります。 — hōtai wa kega no basho ni yorimasu — "The kind of bandage depends on where the wound is."

The word, taken apart.
ほうたい (hōtai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hō, "wrap" (Middle Chinese paew) and tai, "belt" (tajH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
