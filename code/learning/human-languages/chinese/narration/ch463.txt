Lesson 1 of 7.
dǎnxiǎo — timid; easily scared.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give up hope, then the Mandarin for to fall out of love.

You'll want to know: dǎnxiǎo.
dǎnxiǎo — "timid; easily scared".
wǒ dìdi bǐ wǒ dǎnxiǎo — My little brother scares more easily than I do.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: dǎn is said with a rising tone before xiǎo, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dǎnxiǎo is built from dǎn 'the gall' and 小 'small', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tamX and sjewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first in the pair liver and gall. Courage was thought to sit in the gall, so a small gall is a timid heart; the dictionary does not say so itself.

Lesson 2 of 7.
可怕 (kěpà) — frightening; awful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to fall out of love, then the Mandarin for easily scared.

You'll want to know: 可怕.
可怕 — kěpà — "frightening; awful".
zhège diànyǐng hěn kěpà — This film is really frightening.
The new character, in pieces: 怕 (pà) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left the heart piece, a dot, a second dot and a long vertical; on the right 白 bái, five strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
可怕 (kěpà) is built from 可 'can' and 怕 (pà) 'to fear', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khaX and phaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to permit' and the second 'to fear'. The dictionary lists 怕 (pà) under a second reading, phaek, glossed 'still and quiet', which is not this word.

Script — the heart piece, then 白.
怕 (pà).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, three strokes: the heart piece, as in 情. A short dot on the left, a short dot on the right, then a long vertical down between them.
The right piece, 白, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left at the top, a vertical down the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, a short bar inside, then the bar across the foot.
3 + 5 = eight.

[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 怕 (pà) have? (Two: three strokes and five.) Which written character is on the right? (白.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
xiàrén — scary; giving people a fright.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for easily scared, then the Mandarin for frightening.

You'll want to know: xiàrén.
xiàrén — "scary; giving people a fright".
tiānsè hēi le wàibian yǒudiǎnr xiàrén — It's getting dark, and it's a bit scary outside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàrén is built from xià and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xaeH and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under two readings and glosses this one as the sound of laughing; the sense 'to frighten' is not given there.

Lesson 6 of 7.
èmèng — nightmare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for giving people a fright, then the Mandarin for to tremble.

You'll want to know: èmèng.
èmèng — "nightmare".
wǒ zuò guo yí ge hěn kěpà de èmèng — I once had a terrifying nightmare.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
èmèng is built from è and mèng 'a dream', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngak and mjuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary gives the first only in the name of a year, and explains the second, after an older book, as the spirit wandering in sleep; the sense 'dreadful' for the first is not given there.
