Mandarin Chinese, chapter 137: Holidays.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
节日 (jiérì) — festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to thunder, then the Mandarin for pleasantly cool.

You'll want to know: 节日.
节日 — jiérì — "festival".
今天是什么节日 jīntiān shì shénme jiérì — What festival is it today?
The new character, in pieces: 节 (jié) — the grass piece on top, a bar crossed by two short verticals, and under it a bar that turns down into a hook, with a long vertical through it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
节日 (jiérì) is built from 节 (jié) 'a joint' and 日 'day', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tset and nyit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary quotes an older book for the first: the joint of a bamboo. A festival as a joint in the year is the likely image, though it is not given there.

What you've built.

Script — the grass piece over a hook.
节 (jié).
Two pieces, top to bottom.
The grass piece, three strokes, as on top of 菜: a horizontal across, then a short vertical on the left and a short vertical on the right, both crossing it.
The lower piece, two strokes. A horizontal that turns down and ends in a hook to the left, then a long vertical down through it to the foot.
3 + 2 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 节 (jié), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 节 (jié)? (Two.) How many strokes in the grass piece? (Three.) Which stroke is the last? (The long vertical.)

You'll want to know: 春节.
春节 — Chūnjié — "the Spring Festival; Chinese New Year".
春节前大家都打扫 Chūnjié qián dàjiā dōu dǎsǎo — Before the festival, everybody cleans the house.

The word, taken apart.
春节 (Chūnjié) is built from 春 'spring' and 节 (jié) 'festival', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwin and tset (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the festival at the head of spring.

Lesson 4 of 6.
guònián — to celebrate the New Year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for festival, then the Mandarin for Chinese New Year.

You'll want to know: guònián.
guònián — "to celebrate the New Year".
guònián wǒmen chī jiǎozi — We eat dumplings to see in the new year.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guònián is built from guò 'to pass' and 年 'year', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaH and nen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The pair is not given there; seeing the year through is the plain picture, but how it came to mean celebrating is not settled.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàngjià is built from fàng 'to let go' and jià 'leave', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjangH and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to borrow' and 'leave from duty'.
