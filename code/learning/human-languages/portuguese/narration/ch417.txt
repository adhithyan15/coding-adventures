Portuguese, chapter 417: No porto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o molhe — the breakwater, the pier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to cancel, then the Portuguese for the change.

You'll want to know: o molhe.
o molhe — "the breakwater, the pier".
Ao fim da tarde, há pescadores no molhe. In the late afternoon, there are fishermen on the breakwater.
A long stone wall that protects a harbour from the waves.

The word, taken apart.
molhe came through Catalan moll 'mole, jetty' (EtymDB) from Latin mōlēs 'mass, great stone structure' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o pontão — the pontoon, the jetty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the change, then the Portuguese for the breakwater.

You'll want to know: o pontão.
o pontão — "the pontoon, the jetty".
O barco pequeno está preso ao pontão. The small boat is tied to the pontoon.
A floating platform where small boats stop.

The word, taken apart.
pontão is inherited through Old Portuguese ponton from Latin pontō 'ferryboat, pontoon', built on pōns, pontis 'bridge' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). EtymDB also reads it as ponte with -ão.

You'll want to know: embarcar.
embarcar — "to board, to embark".
Os passageiros embarcam às nove. The passengers board at nine.
Also for planes and trains: embarcar no avião.

The word, taken apart.
embarcar is built from em- 'into' and barca 'boat', inherited from Late Latin barca (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
zarpar — to set sail, to weigh anchor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to board, then the Portuguese for to go ashore.

You'll want to know: zarpar.
zarpar — "to set sail, to weigh anchor".
O navio zarpou ao nascer do sol. The ship set sail at sunrise.
Informal: to leave in a hurry. Ele zarpou logo — he was off at once.

The word, taken apart.
zarpar was borrowed from Spanish zarpar, which came from Old Italian sarpare 'to weigh anchor' (EtymDB).
