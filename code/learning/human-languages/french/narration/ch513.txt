French, chapter 513: L'orchestre.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'orchestre — orchestra; stalls (in a theatre).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for chorus, then the French for melody.

You'll want to know: l'orchestre.
l'orchestre — "orchestra; stalls (in a theatre)".
L'orchestre joue ce soir au théâtre. The orchestra is playing at the theatre tonight.
Masculine. In a theatre, l'orchestre is also the stalls.

The word, taken apart.
l'orchestre comes from Latin orchēstra, a borrowing from Greek orkhḗstra "the space where the chorus danced" (EtymDB), built on orkheîsthai "to dance".

Le chef d'orchestre lève sa baguette. The conductor raises his baton.
A woman is often la cheffe d'orchestre or la chef d'orchestre.

le chef d'orchestre is "the head of the orchestra": chef is inherited from Latin caput "head" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 6.
la partition — (musical) score, sheet music.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for orchestra, then the French for conductor.

You'll want to know: la partition.
la partition — "(musical) score, sheet music".
Le pianiste a oublié sa partition à la maison. The pianist left his sheet music at home.
Also a division, as in English partition.

The word, taken apart.
la partition is a learned borrowing from Latin partītiō "a division" (EtymDB), from partīrī "to divide", built on pars "part" (Lewis & Short).

la répétition — rehearsal; repetition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for conductor, then the French for (musical) score.

You'll want to know: la répétition.
la répétition — "rehearsal; repetition".
La répétition commence à vingt heures. The rehearsal starts at eight o'clock.
Une répétition générale — a dress rehearsal.

The word, taken apart.
la répétition is a learned borrowing from Latin repetītiō "a repeating" (EtymDB), from repetere "to go back to", built on petere "to seek" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
improviser — to improvise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for (musical) score, then the French for rehearsal.

You'll want to know: improviser.
improviser — "to improvise".
Les musiciens de jazz improvisent beaucoup. Jazz musicians improvise a lot.
Also outside music: on a improvisé un repas.

The word, taken apart.
improviser was borrowed from Italian improvvisare (EtymDB), from improvviso, from Latin imprōvīsus "unforeseen", built on vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).
