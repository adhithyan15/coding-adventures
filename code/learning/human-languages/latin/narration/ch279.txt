cerasum, cerasī — "a cherry".

The word, taken apart.
cerasum was borrowed from Greek kerasos, "cherry tree", itself probably from a language of Asia Minor. English cherry comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
porrum is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European prso-, "leek" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek prason, "leek", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
puls, pultis — porridge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for cabbage, then the Latin for a leek.

You'll want to know: puls, pultis.
puls, pultis — "porridge".

The word, taken apart.
puls is usually linked with Greek poltos, "porridge" (Lewis & Short), and may have been borrowed from it; both go back to a root for meal, pel-, "flour, dust" (EtymDB 2.1). English pulse, "beans and lentils", comes from it.
