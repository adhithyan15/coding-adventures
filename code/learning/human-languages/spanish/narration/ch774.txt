el sótano — the basement, the cellar.

You'll want to know: el sótano.
el sótano — "the basement, the cellar".
El sótano siempre estaba frío y oscuro. The basement was always cold and dark.

The word, taken apart.
sótano is usually traced to spoken Latin subtulum 'a place below', built on subtus 'beneath' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for to top up, then the Spanish for the basement.

You'll want to know: el desván.
el desván — "the attic, the loft".
En el desván había cajas de fotos viejas. In the attic there were boxes of old photos.
In Spain el ático is usually a top-floor flat, not a loft.

The word, taken apart.
desván is usually traced to des- and vano 'an empty space', from Latin vānus 'empty, void' (Lewis & Short); the details are not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el cajón — the drawer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the basement, then the Spanish for the attic.

You'll want to know: el cajón.
el cajón — "the drawer".
Mi abuela guardaba las cartas en un cajón. My grandmother kept the letters in a drawer.
De cajón — obvious, of course.

The word, taken apart.
cajón is built on caja 'box' with the ending -ón. Caja comes from Latin capsa 'a box, a case for books' (Lewis & Short), most likely through Catalan caixa.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la persiana — the blind, the shutter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the drawer, then the Spanish for the mirror.

You'll want to know: la persiana.
la persiana — "the blind, the shutter".
Mi abuela siempre bajaba la persiana por la tarde. My grandmother always lowered the blind in the afternoon.
Subir and bajar la persiana — to raise and lower the blind.

The word, taken apart.
persiana comes from French persienne (EtymDB), a word built on the name of Persia; the slatted blind was thought to come from there.
