Latin, chapter 476: To thresh, To winnow, Chaff, Stalk, Granary.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
trītūrō, trītūrāre — to thresh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an ear of grain, then the Latin for wheat.

You'll want to know: trītūrō, trītūrāre.
trītūrō, trītūrāre — "to thresh".
In āreā servī frūmentum trītūrant. On the threshing floor the slaves thresh the grain.
trītūrō, trītūrāre, trītūrāvī, trītūrātum, from trītūra 'threshing'. Oxen often trod the grain out. A late word; earlier writers say frūmentum terere.

The word, taken apart.
trītūrō is built on trītūra 'threshing' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which comes from terō 'to rub'. English triturate 'to grind to powder' comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ventilō, ventilāre — to winnow; to fan, to toss in the air.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wheat, then the Latin for to thresh.

You'll want to know: ventilō, ventilāre.
ventilō, ventilāre — "to winnow; to fan, to toss in the air".
Agricolae frūmentum in āreā ventilant. The farmers winnow the grain on the threshing floor.
ventilō, ventilāre, ventilāvī, ventilātum. English ventilate comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
ventilō is built on ventulus 'a slight wind' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), a diminutive of ventus 'wind'. English ventilate comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
palea, paleae — chaff; straw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to thresh, then the Latin for to winnow.

You'll want to know: palea, paleae.
palea, paleae — "chaff; straw".
Palea levis est et ā ventō portātur. Chaff is light and is carried by the wind.
First declension, feminine: palea, paleae f. The husks left after threshing, used for fodder and bedding. English keeps palea as a term in botany.

The word, taken apart.
palea is inherited from PIE pel- 'flour, dust' (EtymDB), the root also seen in pulvis 'dust' and pollen. French paille 'straw' comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
stipula, stipulae — a stalk, a straw; stubble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to winnow, then the Latin for chaff.

You'll want to know: stipula, stipulae.
stipula, stipulae — "a stalk, a straw; stubble".
Post messem in agrō sōla stipula manet. After the harvest only stubble is left in the field.
First declension, feminine: stipula, stipulae f., a diminutive linked with stīpes 'stake'.

The word, taken apart.
stipula is inherited from Proto-Italic stipelā 'straw' (EtymDB), and Lewis & Short connect it with stīpes 'stake, trunk'. English stubble comes, through French, from a related Late Latin form.

Lesson 5 of 5.
horreum, horreī — a granary, a barn, a storehouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for chaff, then the Latin for a stalk.

You'll want to know: horreum, horreī.
horreum, horreī — "a granary, a barn, a storehouse".
Frūmentum in horreō servātur. The grain is kept in the granary.
Second declension, neuter: horreum, horreī n. Big public horrea by the river stored the city's grain.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of horreum is not settled. EtymDB traces it to Greek hōreion 'storehouse, granary', while Lewis & Short compare farreum, from far 'spelt'.
