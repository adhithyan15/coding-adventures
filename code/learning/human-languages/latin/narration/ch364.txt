Latin, chapter 364: Drawing a Conclusion.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
cōnclūdō, cōnclūdere — to shut in; to conclude, to infer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to throw in the way, then the Latin for to insist.

You'll want to know: cōnclūdō, cōnclūdere.
cōnclūdō, cōnclūdere — "to shut in; to conclude, to infer".
Ex hīs rēbus cōnclūdō eum mentīrī. From these facts I conclude that he is lying.
cōnclūdō, cōnclūdere, cōnclūsī, cōnclūsum. Built on claudō, to shut: to shut an argument up tight. English conclude.

The word, taken apart.
cōnclūdō is built from con- and claudō "to shut" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the vowel changed inside the compound. English conclude comes from it.

cōnclūsiō, cōnclūsiōnis — "a conclusion; the close of a speech".
Cōnclūsiō tua falsa est. Your conclusion is false.
cōnclūsiō, cōnclūsiōnis f. The last step of an argument, and the last part of a speech. English conclusion.

cōnclūsiō is the noun built on cōnclūdō "to shut up, to infer" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English conclusion comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 6.
ratiōcinor, ratiōcinārī — to reason, to calculate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to shut in, then the Latin for a conclusion.

You'll want to know: ratiōcinor, ratiōcinārī.
ratiōcinor, ratiōcinārī — "to reason, to calculate".
Bene ratiōcināris, sed falsum est initium. You reason well, but the starting point is false.
Deponent: ratiōcinor, ratiōcinārī, ratiōcinātus sum. Built on ratiō, reckoning: to work a thing out step by step. English ratiocination.

The word, taken apart.
ratiōcinor is built on ratiō "a reckoning, reason" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to keep accounts, then to reason. English ratiocination comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
significō, significāre — to indicate, to mean; to show.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a conclusion, then the Latin for to reason.

You'll want to know: significō, significāre.
significō, significāre — "to indicate, to mean; to show".
Quid hoc verbum significat? What does this word mean?
significō, significāre, significāvī, significātum. Built on signum, a sign: to make a sign. English signify and significant.

The word, taken apart.
significō is built on signum "a sign" (EtymDB) and the root of faciō "to make" (Lewis & Short): to make a sign. English signify comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
suspīciō, suspīciōnis — a suspicion, a mistrust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to reason, then the Latin for to indicate.

You'll want to know: suspīciō, suspīciōnis.
suspīciō, suspīciōnis — "a suspicion, a mistrust".
In eum suspīciō cecidit. Suspicion fell on him.
suspīciō, suspīciōnis f., with a long ī; suspicor, to suspect, is its verb. English suspicion.

The word, taken apart.
suspīciō comes from suspiciō "to look up at; to suspect" (Lewis & Short), built from sub "up from under" and speciō "to look": a look from below. English suspicion comes from it.
