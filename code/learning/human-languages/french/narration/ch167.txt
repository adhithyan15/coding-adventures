French, chapter 167: Mother-in-law, Son-in-law, Daughter-in-law, Fiancé, Fiancée.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la belle-mère — a mother-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a dentist, then the French for a pharmacist.

You'll want to know: la belle-mère.
la belle-mère — "a mother-in-law".

The word, taken apart.
belle-mère — the feminine of the word for "beautiful", from Latin bella, used as a courtesy, and mère, "mother".

Lesson 4 of 5.
le fiancé — a fiancé.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a son-in-law, then the French for a daughter-in-law.

You'll want to know: le fiancé.
le fiancé — "a fiancé".

The word, taken apart.
fiancé is the past participle of the verb for "to betroth", built on Old French fiance, "a promise", from Vulgar Latin fidare, "to trust", from Latin fides, "faith" (Lewis & Short).
