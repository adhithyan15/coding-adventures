Bengali, chapter 210: To sign, To phone, To meet, To marry, To survive.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
সই করা (sôi kôrā) — to sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to spend, then the Bengali for to weigh.

You'll want to know: সই করা.
সই করা — sôi kôrā — "to sign".

The phrase, taken apart.
সই করা (sôi kôrā) is sôi, "a signature", usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ṣaḥīḥ, "sound, correct" (Lane), + kôrā, "to do", from Sanskrit karoti, "does" (CDIAL 2814).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to meet, then the Bengali for to marry.

You'll want to know: বাঁচা.
বাঁচা — bā̃chā — "to survive, to live".

The word, taken apart.
বাঁচা (bā̃chā) (bāṁchā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vañcati, "moves; escapes from" (CDIAL 11208). CDIAL lists Bengali bāṁcā, "to escape".
