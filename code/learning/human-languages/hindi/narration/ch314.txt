Hindi, chapter 314: A Rumour Spreads.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अफ़वाह (afvāh) — a rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an editor, then the Hindi for a detailed account.

You'll want to know: अफ़वाह.
अफ़वाह — afvāh — "a rumour".
बाज़ार में एक अफ़वाह फैली। bāzār meṁ ek afvāh phailī. A rumour spread through the market.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
अफ़वाह (afvāh) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic afwāh, "mouths": what goes from mouth to mouth. It is feminine: अफ़वाह फैली.

पूरे शहर में उस अफ़वाह की चर्चा थी। pūre śahar meṁ us afvāh kī carcā thī. The whole town was talking about that rumour.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
चर्चा (carcā) is Sanskrit carcā (Monier-Williams), a repeating of words and a thinking over; in Hindi it is what everyone is talking about.

दहशत (dahśat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic dahsha, "bewilderment, alarm". It is a fear that spreads through a whole place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
भड़काना (bhaṛkānā) — to stir up; to incite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for talk, then the Hindi for terror.

You'll want to know: भड़काना.
भड़काना — bhaṛkānā — "to stir up; to incite".
कुछ लोगों ने अफ़वाह से भीड़ को भड़काया। kuch logoṁ ne afvāh se bhīṛ ko bhaṛkāyā. A few people used the rumour to stir up the crowd.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने लोगों को भड़काया, usne logoṁ ko bhaṛkāyā.

The word, taken apart.
भड़काना (bhaṛkānā) is the causative of भड़कना, to flare up, from bhaṭ (CDIAL 9365): to make a fire, or a crowd, blaze.

Lesson 5 of 5.
सनसनी (sansanī) — a sensation; a thrill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for terror, then the Hindi for to stir up.

You'll want to know: सनसनी.
सनसनी — sansanī — "a sensation; a thrill".
उस ख़बर से पूरे देश में सनसनी फैल गई। us khabar se pūre deś meṁ sansanī phail gaī. That news caused a sensation across the whole country.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
सनसनी (sansanī) belongs with sansanānā, to hum or tingle, which CDIAL 13901 sets under Sanskrit svana, "a noise", noting that many of these words are imitative: a stir that runs through people like a buzz.
