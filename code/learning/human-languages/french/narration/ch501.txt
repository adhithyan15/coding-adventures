French, chapter 501: Collectionneurs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
collectionner — to collect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for bet, then the French for chance.

You'll want to know: collectionner.
collectionner — "to collect".
Mon voisin collectionne les vieilles cartes postales. My neighbour collects old postcards.
As a hobby. To pick up someone is aller chercher.

The word, taken apart.
collectionner is built on collection (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin collēctiō "a gathering", from colligere "to gather", built on legere "to gather, to read" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: la maquette.
la maquette — "scale model; mock-up".
Il a construit une maquette de bateau. He built a model boat.
Also a designer's mock-up of a page or a building.

The word, taken apart.
la maquette was borrowed from Italian macchietta "a little sketch" (EtymDB), from macchia "a spot", inherited from Latin macula "a spot" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le modélisme — model-making.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for collector, then the French for scale model.

You'll want to know: le modélisme.
le modélisme — "model-making".
Mon père fait du modélisme: il construit des petits trains. My father does model-making: he builds little trains.
Un modéliste — a model-maker.

The word, taken apart.
le modélisme is built on modèle "model" with -isme; modèle was borrowed from Italian modello, which goes back to Latin modulus "a small measure", from modus "measure" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
assembler — to put together, to assemble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for scale model, then the French for model-making.

You'll want to know: assembler.
assembler — "to put together, to assemble".
Il a assemblé l'armoire en deux heures. He put the wardrobe together in two hours.
S'assembler — to gather.

The word, taken apart.
assembler is inherited from Latin assimulāre (EtymDB), which in Classical Latin meant "to make like"; the sense "to bring together" is usually traced to simul "together" (Lewis & Short).
