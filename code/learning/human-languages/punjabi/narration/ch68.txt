Punjabi, chapter 68: Table, Wall, Roof, Cupboard, Floor.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਮੇਜ਼ (mez) — a table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a window, then the Punjabi for a chair.

You'll want to know: ਮੇਜ਼.
ਮੇਜ਼ — mez — "a table".

The word, taken apart.
ਮੇਜ਼ (mez) is a loan, usually traced to Persian mēz, "table". Many take the Persian word from Portuguese mesa, "table".

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ਕੰਧ.
ਕੰਧ — kandh — "a wall".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists ਕੰਧ (kandh), "wall", under Sanskrit kanthā, "wall, town", which it takes from Iranian (CDIAL 2720). It adds that the word coincides with kaṇṭha, "throat; border", under which it lists Punjabi kaṇḍh, "wall", as perhaps belonging there (CDIAL 2680).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਛੱਤ (chhatt) — a roof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a table, then the Punjabi for a wall.

You'll want to know: ਛੱਤ.
ਛੱਤ — chhatt — "a roof".

The word, taken apart.
ਛੱਤ (chhatt) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from chatti, "covering", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4971). It is built on the Sanskrit root chad, "to cover".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਫ਼ਰਸ਼ (farsh) — the floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a roof, then the Punjabi for a cupboard.

You'll want to know: ਫ਼ਰਸ਼.
ਫ਼ਰਸ਼ — farsh — "the floor".

The word, taken apart.
ਫ਼ਰਸ਼ (farsh) is a loan from Arabic farsh, "anything spread, a carpet", which reached Punjabi through Persian. Its Arabic root is f-r-sh (Lane).
