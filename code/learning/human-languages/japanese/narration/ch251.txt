Japanese, chapter 251: Tomorrow, and What I Will Not Do.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
あした いく (ashita iku) — the future, with a time word.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "tomorrow" and "next week". (ashita, raishū.) Then say "I go", politely. (ikimasu.)

You'll want to know: no future form.
あした いきます。 ashita ikimasu. — "I will go tomorrow."
Japanese has no separate form for the future. いきます (ikimasu) is "I go" and also "I will go"; the time word says which.
あした やまに いきます。 ashita yama ni ikimasu. — "I will go to the mountains tomorrow."
らいしゅう はたらきます。 raishū hatarakimasu. — "I will work next week."
With まいにち (mainichi), "every day", the same verb is what you do as a habit:
まいにち はたらきます。 mainichi hatarakimasu. — "I work every day."

You'll want to know: the plain future.
To a friend, the dictionary form does the same job:
えいがを みる。 eiga o miru. — "I will see a film."
あした えいがを みる。 ashita eiga o miru. — "I will see a film tomorrow."
That plain form is the one you already put before つもり (tsumori), "a plan", and it goes before でしょう (deshō), "probably", too:
らいねん うみに いく つもりです。 rainen umi ni iku tsumori desu. — "I plan to go to the sea next year."
あした あめが ふるでしょう。 ashita ame ga furu deshō. — "It will probably rain tomorrow."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It will probably rain tomorrow" — ashita ame ga furu deshō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It will probably snow tomorrow" — ashita yuki ga furu deshō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It will probably rain next week" — raishū ame ga furu deshō]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, politely, that you will go to the sea tomorrow.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I will go tomorrow", politely? (あした いきます.) To a friend? (あした いく (ashita iku).) What tells the listener it is the future? (The time word, あした.) "It will probably rain"? (あめが ふるでしょう.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
いかない / たべない (ikanai / tabenai) — the plain "not".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say, to a friend, "I will go tomorrow". (ashita iku.) Then say "I will not go", politely. (ikimasen.) This lesson gives the plain "not".

You'll want to know: -ない.
A friend asks whether you are going tomorrow. The plain answer:
いかない。 ikanai. — "I won't go."
あした いかない。 ashita ikanai. — "I won't go tomorrow."
いかない (ikanai) is いきません (ikimasen) without the politeness. A verb that drops る for た drops it for ない too: たべる (taberu) has たべない (tabenai).
にくは たべない。 niku wa tabenai. — "I do not eat meat."
The other verbs take the sound of their last kana in the a row: いく has いかない, のむ (nomu) has のまない (nomanai), まつ (matsu) has またない (matanai). A verb in う takes わ: かう (kau) has かわない (kawanai).
コーヒーは のまない。 kōhī wa nomanai. — "I do not drink coffee."
Three are short: する has しない (shinai), くる has こない (konai), and ある (aru), "there is", has plain ない (nai).
じかんが ない。 jikan ga nai. — "I have no time."

You'll want to know: -なかった.
For "did not", the last い of ない becomes かった: いかなかった (ikanakatta), たべなかった (tabenakatta). It is ませんでした without the politeness.
きのう いかなかった。 kinō ikanakatta. — "I did not go yesterday."
あさごはんを たべなかった。 asagohan o tabenakatta. — "I did not eat breakfast."
Like the plain past, the plain "not" has a second job that later lessons give it: it goes in front of the word that keeps a sentence polite.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I do not drink coffee" — kōhī wa nomanai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I do not drink tea" — ocha wa nomanai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I do not drink water" — mizu wa nomanai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
Say, as you would to a friend, that you did not eat breakfast.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I won't go"? (いかない, ikanai.) "I won't buy it"? (かわない, kawanai.) "I won't do it"? (しない, shinai.) "He won't come"? (こない, konai.) "I did not go"? (いかなかった, ikanakatta.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
なる (naru) — to become; to get.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new word: say "I will not go", to a friend. (ikanai.) Then say "cold" and "a teacher". (samui, sensei.)

You'll want to know: なる.
なる — naru — "to become", "to get". Polite なります, narimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あには せんせいに なりました, ani wa sensei ni narimashita.
After a noun it takes に:
あには せんせいに なりました。 ani wa sensei ni narimashita. — "My elder brother became a teacher."
After an i-adjective it takes the -く form you know from はやく: さむい becomes さむく.
さむく なりました。 samuku narimashita. — "It got cold."
そらが くらく なりました。 sora ga kuraku narimashita. — "The sky got dark."

The word, taken apart.
なる (naru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana, as here, and gives "to become" and "to get" as its first senses (JMdict).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "It got cold" — samuku narimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It got hot" — atsuku narimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It got dark" — kuraku narimashita]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Japanese for 'to become'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"To become"? (なる, naru.) After a noun, which particle? (に: せんせいに なりました.) After さむい? (さむく なりました.)
