Lesson 1 of 5.
ほたて (hotate) — a scallop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a Pacific saury, then the Japanese for a short-neck clam.

You'll want to know: ほたて.
ほたて — hotate — "a scallop".

The word, taken apart.
ほたて (hotate) is native: ho, "sail", and tate, "raising", from tatsu: the shell lifted like a sail. JMdict marks it as a shortened word. It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
のみ (nomi) — a flea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a short-neck clam, then the Japanese for a scallop.

You'll want to know: のみ.
のみ — nomi — "a flea".

The word, taken apart.
のみ (nomi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

You'll want to know: かもしか.
かもしか — kamoshika — "a Japanese serow".

The word, taken apart.
かもしか (kamoshika) is native and ends in shika, "deer". JMdict writes it with characters for "felt" and "deer", read as a whole. Where the first part comes from is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
すいとう (suitou) — a water flask.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bat, then the Japanese for a Japanese serow.

You'll want to know: すいとう.
すいとう — suitou — "a water flask".

The word, taken apart.
すいとう (suitou) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "water" (Middle Chinese sywijX) and tō, "tube, pipe" (duwng) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
