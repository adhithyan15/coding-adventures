# Review — the mask, the share, and the thing thrown down

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C345-REF-04, ES-LEX-C345-REF-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two nouns from this chapter. The person. (*La persona*.) The object.
(*El objeto*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C345-REF-03, ES-LEX-C345-REF-04, ES-LEX-C345-REF-05] -->

Three chants, and one thing they share.

[PAUSE 2s] **First.** *La parte.* — Latin *partem*: a piece cut off a whole.

[PAUSE 2s] **Second.** *La persona.* — Latin *persōna*: a carved **mask**, held
up in front of an actor's face.

[PAUSE 2s] **Third.** *El objeto.* — Latin *objectum*: something **thrown down
in front of** you.

Every one of those started as a thing you could pick up. A cut piece. A mask. A
thrown thing. And every one of them now names something with no shape at all —
a share, a human being, a topic.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C345-REF-04, ES-LEX-C345-REF-05] -->

Here is the part worth keeping, and it is not about vocabulary.

[PAUSE 2s] You did not meet *persona* and *objeto* this chapter. You have been
using both for three hundred chapters, as **grammar labels**.

> *primera* ***persona*** — first person: *hablo*.
> ***objeto*** *directo* — direct object: *lo tengo*.

[PAUSE 2s] The first, second and third **person** are three masks a speaker can
wear: *I*, *you*, and the one being talked about. And the **object** of a verb
is exactly what the word says — the thing the action is thrown at.

So the grammar words were never jargon. They were ordinary Spanish nouns the
whole time, doing the same job in a classroom that they do in a kitchen.
