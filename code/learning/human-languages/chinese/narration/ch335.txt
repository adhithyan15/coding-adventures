Mandarin Chinese, chapter 335: Lanterns and Riddles.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
Yuánxiāo — the Lantern Festival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the Spring Festival couplets, then the Mandarin for flourishing in business.

You'll want to know: Yuánxiāo.
Yuánxiāo — "the Lantern Festival".
Yuánxiāo de wǎnshang wǒmen chūqù wán — On the night of the lantern festival we go out and have fun.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
Yuánxiāo is built from 元 'first' and xiāo 'night', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwon and sjew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'great' and 'the beginning' and the second 'night': the first full-moon night of the year.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

dēnglóng — lantern.

dēnglóng — "lantern".
ménkǒu yǒu liǎng ge hóng dēnglóng — There are two red lanterns at the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dēnglóng is built from dēng 'a lamp' and lóng 'a basket', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tong and luwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a lamp' and quotes an older book for the second, a basket for a fire.

Lesson 3 of 6.
cāi — to guess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the Lantern Festival, then the Mandarin for a paper lantern.

You'll want to know: cāi.
cāi — "to guess".
wǒ cāi le sān cì dōu bú duì — I guessed three times and got it wrong each time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cāi is inherited from Middle Chinese tshoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'to suspect' and 'to resent'. The sense 'to guess' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 6.
biānpào — firecrackers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to guess an answer, then the Mandarin for a riddle.

You'll want to know: biānpào.
biānpào — "firecrackers".
Chúxī wǎnshang shí'èr diǎn wàibian de rén dōu zài fàng biānpào — At midnight on the last night of the year everyone outside is letting off firecrackers.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biānpào is built from biān 'a whip' and pào, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjien and phaewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a horse whip' and the second 'the look of something scorched'.
