Bengali, chapter 37: Here, There, and Where.
5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
থ (thô) — the breathy dental.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write ত, খ and ধ. Say what খ has that ক has not.
[your turn — recall: two chapters back, the ending that marks the person a thing is done to — -ke]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Script.
থ (thô).
Six chapters ago you filled in the fourth corner of a square. This is a different square, and it has been three-quarters full for even longer.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
voice off. plain: ত tô. with breath: থ thô.
voice on. plain: দ dô. with breath: ধ dhô.
All four are made in the same place: the tongue flat on the back of the top front teeth. What separates them is the two switches — the voice, and the puff of breath you learned to hear when খ arrived beside ক.
থ (thô) is the puff on the voiceless one. Hold a hand in front of your mouth: for ত the air barely moves, for থ (thô) it pushes.
And it is not the th of English this or thin. That sound does not exist in Bengali. থ (thô) is a t with breath after it, nothing more.

[your turn — say: ত then থ (thô) — same place, breath added]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the whole square — ত থ (thô) দ ধ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: থা]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ট থ (thô) — the two letters this book waited longest for]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where is থ (thô) made? (On the teeth, like ত.) What is added? (Breath.) Is it the English th? (No — that sound is not in Bengali.) And which letter is its voiced partner? (ধ.)

Lesson 2 of 5.
এখানে (ekhāne) — here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write থ (thô). Then say this shirt.

You'll want to know: এখানে.
চা এখানে — chā ekhāne — the tea is here.
সে এখানে — she ekhāne — he is here.
Thirty-six chapters, and nothing in this book has had a place. Things could be named, pointed at, counted, owned and acted upon, and not one of them could be said to be anywhere.
এখানে (ekhāne) takes the near head এ- you have owned since chapter thirty-two and puts it on a frame that means place. And as with এটা জল, there is no verb in the middle: the thing, then where it is, and stop.

Grammar Lens: the grid, now in three dimensions.
[a table of 9 rows, read aloud]
head: এ- near. frame: thing. word: এটা.
head: এ- near. frame: person. word: এ.
head: এ- near. frame: place. word: এখানে (ekhāne).
head: ও- far. frame: thing. word: ওটা.
head: ও- far. frame: person. word: ও.
head: ও- far. frame: place. word: next lesson.
head: ক- asking. frame: thing. word: কী.
head: ক- asking. frame: person. word: কে.
head: ক- asking. frame: place. word: the lesson after.
Nine cells, three heads, three frames. You have met seven of the nine, and the last two arrive in the next two lessons — which is the whole reason the pointing chapter was worth its five lessons.
And the denial is already yours: চা এখানে (ekhāne) নেই — the tea is not here — using the নেই of chapter twenty-seven, which denies existence rather than identity.

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ওখানে (okhāne) — there.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the tea is here. Then say that shirt. Now guess this lesson's word before you read it.

You'll want to know: ওখানে.
সে ওখানে — she okhāne — he is there.
If you guessed it, that is the lesson. Swap the head and cross the room, exactly as এই became ওই five chapters ago. The frame does not change; only the first letter does.
এখানে (ekhāne) — here, where I am.
ওখানে (okhāne) — there, where I am not.
Two places, as there were two distances. Bengali does not add a third.

Grammar Lens: eight of nine.
[a table of 7 rows, read aloud]
head: এ- near. frame: place. word: এখানে (ekhāne).
head: ও- far. frame: place. word: ওখানে (okhāne).
head: এ- near. frame: thing. word: এটা.
head: ও- far. frame: thing. word: ওটা.
head: ক- asking. frame: thing. word: কী.
head: ক- asking. frame: person. word: কে.
head: ক- asking. frame: place. word: one cell left.
Eight cells filled. The ninth is the next lesson, and it is the question an A1 examiner asks in the first minute of a speaking test.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is the only difference between এখানে (ekhāne) and ওখানে (okhāne)? (The head — near against far.) How many places does Bengali distinguish? (Two.) And which cell of the grid is still empty? (The asking one, for place.)

Lesson 4 of 5.
কোথায় (kothāy) — where?

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say here and there. Then ask who?

You'll want to know: কোথায়.
তুমি কোথায়? — tumi kothāy? — where are you?
জামাটা কোথায়? — jāmāṭā kothāy? — where is the shirt?
The ninth cell, and the grid is closed:
[a table of 9 rows, read aloud]
head: এ- near. frame: thing. word: এটা.
head: এ- near. frame: person. word: এ.
head: এ- near. frame: place. word: এখানে (ekhāne).
head: ও- far. frame: thing. word: ওটা.
head: ও- far. frame: person. word: ও.
head: ও- far. frame: place. word: ওখানে (okhāne).
head: ক- asking. frame: thing. word: কী.
head: ক- asking. frame: person. word: কে.
head: ক- asking. frame: place. word: কোথায় (kothāy).
Three heads, three frames, nine words — and you were shown the first head six chapters ago and have been filling the table in ever since.

Grammar Lens: the question and its answer are the same shape.
Ask down the bottom row and answer up the two rows above it:
তুমি কোথায় (kothāy)? — আমি এখানে (ekhāne).
এটা কী? — এটা চা.
The same ক- opens কখন, which chapter thirty showed you inside a table of three heads without ever making it a word you could use. The grid was there before the words were.
Note the spelling: কোথায় (kothāy) ends in য়, the dotted য, and it is a glide, not a j.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tumi kothāy? — and answer — āmi ekhāne]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: jāmāṭā kothāy? — and answer — jāmāṭā okhāne]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three questions of the bottom row — eṭā ki?, ke?, kothāy?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which head opens কোথায় (kothāy)? (ক-, the asking one.) Which two words answer it? (এখানে (ekhāne) and ওখানে (okhāne).) And what letter does it end in? (য়, the dotted one — a glide.)
