Latin, chapter 69: Joy, Anger, Fear.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gaudium, gaudiī — joy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for thirst, then the Latin for care.

You'll want to know: gaudium, gaudiī.
gaudium, gaudiī — "joy".

The word, taken apart.
gaudium is built on gaudeō, "to rejoice" (EtymDB 2.1). English joy comes from its plural through French.

You'll want to know: īra, īrae.
īra, īrae — "anger". English ire and irate.

The word, taken apart.
īra is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root h1eysh2-, "to fall upon, to act sharply" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
timor, timōris — fear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for joy, then the Latin for anger.

You'll want to know: timor, timōris.
timor, timōris — "fear". English timid.

The word, taken apart.
timor is built on timeō, "to fear" (EtymDB 2.1), with the ending -or, "a state". Where timeō comes from is uncertain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
laetus, laeta, laetum — happy, glad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for anger, then the Latin for fear.

You'll want to know: laetus, laeta, laetum.
laetus, laeta, laetum — "happy, glad".

The word, taken apart.
laetus has no sure origin; no proposal is generally accepted. Lewis & Short give it the sense "rich, fertile" for crops and fields.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fear, then the Latin for happy.

You'll want to know: trīstis, trīste.
trīstis, trīste — "sad".

The word, taken apart.
trīstis has no sure origin. Lewis & Short call it doubtful; EtymDB 2.1 records a reconstructed Proto-Indo-European treystis, "in a bad mood", but the link is not settled.
