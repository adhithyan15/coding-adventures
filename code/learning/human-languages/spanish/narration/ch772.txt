Spanish, chapter 772: Cuesta arriba.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la brisa — the breeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for raw, then the Spanish for to smell.

You'll want to know: la brisa.
la brisa — "the breeze".
Arriba sopla una brisa fresca. Up there a cool breeze blows.
La brisa del mar — the sea breeze.

The word, taken apart.
brisa comes from old Spanish briza (EtymDB), which named the northeast wind. Where briza came from is not settled; French brise and English breeze are related words.

Lesson 2 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: soplar.
soplar — "to blow".
Si sopla mucho viento, volvemos. If the wind blows hard, we'll turn back.
Soplar las velas — to blow out the candles.

The word, taken apart.
soplar is inherited from Latin sufflāre 'to blow from below, to puff out' (Lewis & Short), built on sub and flāre 'to blow'.

resbalar is built on old Spanish esbarar 'to slide' with re- (EtymDB). Where esbarar came from is not settled.

Sin mapa, usamos la brújula. With no map, we used the compass.

The word, taken apart.
brújula comes from Italian bussola 'a compass' (EtymDB), from Late Latin buxula 'a little box', built on buxus 'boxwood' (Lewis & Short). The needle sat in a small wooden box.
