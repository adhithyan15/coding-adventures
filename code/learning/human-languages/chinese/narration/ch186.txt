Mandarin Chinese, chapter 186: Odds and Ends.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiǎndāo — scissors.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for morning and evening, then the Mandarin for more often than not.

You'll want to know: jiǎndāo.
jiǎndāo — "scissors".
jiǎndāo zài nǎr — Where are the scissors?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎndāo is built from two parts inherited from Middle Chinese (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): tsjenX, glossed 'to cut off' and 'to make even', and taw, the word for a knife: a cutting knife.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lājītǒng — rubbish bin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for more often than not, then the Mandarin for scissors.

You'll want to know: lājītǒng.
lājītǒng — "rubbish bin".
lājītǒng mǎn le — The bin is full.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lājītǒng is built from lājī 'rubbish' and tǒng 'tub'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese thuwngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a wooden tub'; for lājī no dictionary we use records an older form, so the history of that part is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sùliàodài — plastic bag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for scissors, then the Mandarin for a rubbish bin.

You'll want to know: sùliàodài.
sùliàodài — "plastic bag".
wǒ bú yào sùliàodài — I don't need a plastic bag.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sùliàodài is built from sùliào 'plastic' and dài 'bag'. Of its parts, liào 'material' and dài are inherited from Middle Chinese, lew and dojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the first is listed there as suH, glossed 'a modelled figure': a material that can be modelled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 4 of 5.
píngzi — bottle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a rubbish bin, then the Mandarin for a plastic bag.

You'll want to know: píngzi.
píngzi — "bottle".
zhège píngzi lǐ yǒu shuǐ — There's water in this bottle.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píngzi is built from píng and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, beng and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a vessel for drawing water. In the word the second loses its tone.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 5 of 5.
chōuti — drawer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a plastic bag, then the Mandarin for a bottle.

You'll want to know: chōuti.
chōuti — "drawer".
jiǎndāo zài chōuti lǐ — The scissors are in the drawer.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōuti is built from chōu 'to pull out' and ti 'a tray'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese trhjuw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to pull out'; for the second no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled. In the word it loses its tone.
