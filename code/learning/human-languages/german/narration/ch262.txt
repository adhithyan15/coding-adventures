German, chapter 262: Zunächst und anschließend.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zunächst — at first, initially; for now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for at least, then the German for during.

You'll want to know: zunächst.
zunächst — "at first, initially; for now".
Zunächst war alles ruhig. At first everything was quiet.
A little more formal than zuerst. It can also mean 'for now': zunächst bleibe ich zu Hause.

The word, taken apart.
zunächst is built on zu "to" and nächst "nearest", the superlative of nah "near", which is inherited from Old High German nāh "close, near" (EtymDB): the nearest step comes first.

Lesson 2 of 5.
anschließend — afterwards, subsequently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for during, then the German for at first.

You'll want to know: anschließend.
anschließend — "afterwards, subsequently".
Wir waren im Kino, und anschließend haben wir gegessen. We were at the cinema, and afterwards we ate.
At the start of a clause the verb comes second: Anschließend gingen wir nach Hause.

The word, taken apart.
anschließend is the present participle of anschließen "to join on", built on schließen "to close", which is inherited from Old High German sliozan "to close, to lock" (EtymDB): what is joined on next.

Lesson 3 of 5.
daraufhin — as a result, thereupon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for at first, then the German for afterwards.

You'll want to know: daraufhin.
daraufhin — "as a result, thereupon".
Der Bus kam nicht. Daraufhin nahmen wir ein Taxi. The bus did not come. As a result, we took a taxi.
It says that one thing led to the next. The verb comes second: Daraufhin rief sie an.

The word, taken apart.
daraufhin joins darauf "on that, after that" and hin "towards, away", all inherited Germanic pieces; EtymDB lists no parent for the whole, which is a modern compound.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zuletzt — last of all, finally; the last time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for afterwards, then the German for as a result.

You'll want to know: zuletzt.
zuletzt — "last of all, finally; the last time".
Zuletzt hat er die Tür zugemacht. Last of all, he shut the door.
Also 'the last time': Wann hast du sie zuletzt gesehen?

The word, taken apart.
zuletzt joins zu and letzt "last". Letzt is usually traced to Old High German lezzisto, the superlative of laz "slow, weary", a cousin of English late; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Reihenfolge — the order, the sequence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for as a result, then the German for last of all.

You'll want to know: die Reihenfolge.
die Reihenfolge — "the order, the sequence".
Die Reihenfolge der Bilder war falsch. The order of the pictures was wrong.
Plural: die Reihenfolgen. In der richtigen Reihenfolge — in the right order.

The word, taken apart.
Reihenfolge joins Reihe "row", from Middle High German rīhe "row, line", and Folge "what follows", from Middle High German volge (EtymDB). Both are inherited Germanic words.
