Urdu, chapter 90: All, Oneself, Which one?, One, Here.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
تمام (tamām) — all, entire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for something, then the Urdu for both.

You'll want to know: تمام.
تمام — tamām — "all, entire".

The word, taken apart.
تمام (tamām) is a loan from Arabic tamām, "completeness; complete, whole", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is t-m-m (Lane).

کونسا — kaunsā — "which one?".

The word, taken apart.
کونسا (kaunsā) is kaun, "who, which", from Sanskrit kaḥ punar, "who then" (CDIAL 2575), with the ending -sā, "-like".

Lesson 4 of 5.
والا (vālā) — the one (that).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for oneself, then the Urdu for which one?.

You'll want to know: والا.
والا — vālā — "the one (that)".

The word, taken apart.
والا (vālā) is usually traced to Sanskrit pālaka, "guardian, keeper", built on pāla, "protector". CDIAL has no entry for vālā itself, but it lists gharwālā, "householder", under a reconstructed gharapāla, "house guardian" (CDIAL 4435).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ادھر (idhar) — here, this way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for which one?, then the Urdu for the one.

You'll want to know: ادھر.
ادھر — idhar — "here, this way".

The word, taken apart.
ادھر (idhar) is inherited, but its history is not settled. It is built on the near stem i- that is also in is, "this", and CDIAL has no entry for it.
