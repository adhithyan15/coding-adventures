Latin, chapter 513: Pity and shame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
misericors, misericordis — compassionate, merciful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for praise, then the Latin for to blame.

You'll want to know: misericors, misericordis.
misericors, misericordis — "compassionate, merciful".
Fēmina misericors pauperibus cibum dat. The compassionate woman gives food to the poor.
One ending: miser 'wretched' + cor 'heart' — with a heart for the wretched.

The word, taken apart.
misericors is built on miser "wretched" and cor "heart" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
misereor, miserērī — to pity, feel sorry for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for compassionate, then the Latin for pity.

You'll want to know: misereor, miserērī.
misereor, miserērī — "to pity, feel sorry for".
Miserēmur puerōrum, quī nūllōs parentēs habent. We pity the children who have no parents.
Deponent: misereor, miserērī, miseritus sum, with the genitive: tuī misereor — I pity you.

The word, taken apart.
misereor is built on miser "wretched, pitiable" (Lewis & Short). English misery comes from miseria, from the same adjective.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pudet, pudēre — it shames, one is ashamed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for pity, then the Latin for to pity.

You'll want to know: pudet, pudēre.
pudet, pudēre — "it shames, one is ashamed".
Mē pudet errōris meī. I am ashamed of my mistake.
Impersonal: pudet, pudēre, puduit. The person in the accusative, the cause in the genitive. Pudor, already known, is shame.

The word, taken apart.
pudet is the impersonal use of pudeō "to be ashamed", and pudor "shame" is built on it (Lewis & Short). English impudent comes from the family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
verēcundia, verēcundiae — shyness, modesty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to pity, then the Latin for it shames.

You'll want to know: verēcundia, verēcundiae.
verēcundia, verēcundiae — "shyness, modesty".
Puella prae verēcundiā tacet. The girl stays silent out of shyness.
Feminine, first declension, from verēcundus — shy, modest. A gentle sense of shame.

The word, taken apart.
verēcundia is built on verēcundus "modest, shy", from vereor "to respect, fear" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).
