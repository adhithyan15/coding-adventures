Persian, chapter 521: Poetry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شعر (she'r) — a poem; poetry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a choir, then the Persian for an album.

You'll want to know: شعر.
شعر (she'r) — "a poem; poetry".
او هر شب یک شعر میخواند. u har shab yek she'r mikhânad. He reads a poem every night.
Plural she'rhâ, or the Arabic ash'âr. she'r goftan — to write poetry.

The word, taken apart.
she'r comes from Arabic šiʿr, 'poem, poetry' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
غزل (ghazal) — a ghazal (a love lyric).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a poem, then the Persian for a poet.

You'll want to know: غزل.
غزل (ghazal) — "a ghazal (a love lyric)".
مادربزرگم غزلهای زیادی را از حفظ میداند. mâdarbozorgam ghazalhâ-ye ziyâdi râ az hefz midânad. My grandmother knows many ghazals by heart.
A short lyric poem in couplets, usually about love. Plural ghazalhâ.

The word, taken apart.
ghazal was borrowed from Arabic ġazal, which EtymDB links with a verb meaning to speak words of love to the beloved (EtymDB). English ghazal is usually traced to Persian and Urdu.

Lesson 4 of 5.
رباعی (robâ'i) — a quatrain (four-line poem).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a poet, then the Persian for a ghazal.

You'll want to know: رباعی.
رباعی (robâ'i) — "a quatrain (four-line poem)".
او یک رباعی کوتاه برای مادرش نوشت. u yek robâ'i-ye kutâh barâ-ye mâdarash nevesht. He wrote a short quatrain for his mother.
Four lines, the third free of the rhyme. Plural robâ'iyât.

The word, taken apart.
robâ'i is a loan, usually traced to Arabic rubāʿī, 'fourfold', from the Arabic word for four. EtymDB has no entry for it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بیت (beyt) — a couplet, a line of verse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a ghazal, then the Persian for a quatrain.

You'll want to know: بیت.
بیت (beyt) — "a couplet, a line of verse".
او یک بیت از این غزل را خواند. u yek beyt az in ghazal râ khând. He recited a couplet from this ghazal.
Plural abyât (Arabic). A beyt is two half-lines.

The word, taken apart.
beyt was borrowed from Arabic bayt, 'house' (EtymDB), which EtymDB traces on to Proto-Semitic bayt-. In poetry the couplet is a little house of words.
