# لاہوری (Lāhaurī) — from Lahore

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-LAHORE, UR-LEX-GARMI, UR-LEX-SARDI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **this is Lahore**. Then say **heat** and **cold**, and listen to
how both of them end.

## You'll want to know: the ending that makes an origin
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-NISBA]; assesses=[UR-LEX-LAHORE, UR-LEX-MAIN] -->

> **میں لاہوری ہوں** — *maiṅ Lāhaurī hūṅ* — **I am from Lahore.**

Add **-ی** to a place name and you have an adjective meaning *belonging to that
place*. That is the whole rule, and this book has already taught you the ending
twice without saying so: *garmī* and *sardī* are *garm* and *sard* — *hot* and
*cold* — wearing the same **-ی**. (Those four stay in romanization: *garm* needs
gāf and *sard* needs dāl, and the script ladder has reached neither.)

## Grammar Lens: the ending that became a surname
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-NISBA, UR-LEX-GARMI, UR-LEX-SARDI, UR-LEX-SHAHR, UR-GRAMMAR-ADJECTIVE-AGREEMENT] -->

This is the Arabic **nisba**, borrowed into Persian and then into Urdu, and once
you can see it you will see it everywhere:

| name | means |
|---|---|
| **Bukhārī** | the man from Bukhara |
| **Ghazālī** | the man from Ghazala |
| **Lāhaurī** | the one from Lahore |

For most of Islamic history a person's last name **was** their place. The nisba
is a naming system, not merely a suffix, and Urdu inherited it whole.

One warning: unlike **کالا** and **ہمارا**, a nisba adjective ends in **-ی**
already and does not change for gender. A man and a woman are both **لاہوری**.
