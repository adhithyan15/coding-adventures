# ich war — "I was"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You know the simple past is mostly a written tense in German, and
that a handful of very common verbs keep it in speech. This is the most common
of them.

## You'll want to know: ich war
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01, GE-LEX-SEIN-01] -->

> **ich war** — "I was."

Nobody says *ich bin gewesen* in ordinary speech when *ich war* will do. This
is the past-tense form Germans actually use, in every region, out loud.

And hear the shape of it. *Bin* has a *b*. *Ist* has an *s* and a *t*.
*War* has a *w* and an *r*, and no sound in common with either. That is now
three completely unalike stems inside one verb.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01] -->

- `w-as-v` — German **w** is English **v**, so *war* begins like *var*.
- `vowel-a-long` — the **a** is long and open, the *a* of English *father*.
- `r-final` — the final **r** softens towards a vowel rather than curling.

The result sounds close to English *var*, and nothing like English *war*.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "ich war" — *var*, long open *a*]
- [YOU SAY: "ich bin … ich war" — now and then]
- [YOU SAY: "Ich war müde."]

[REPEAT x2] "ich bin, ich war."

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say "I was." (**Ich war**.) How does the *w* sound? (Like English
**v**.) Is this form used in speech or only in writing? (**In speech,
everywhere** — it is one of the survivors.) How many unlike stems does this verb
now have? (**Three**.) Next: the same form for *du*.
