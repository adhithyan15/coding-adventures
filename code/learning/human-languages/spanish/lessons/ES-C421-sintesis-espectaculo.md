# ¿Quién está en la película? — the question the two new words were for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el actor* and *la actriz* once each. You are about to use both
inside a question rather than on their own.

## Grammar Lens: naming a person answers a different question
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-PELICULA] -->

Two question words you already have, pointed at the same film:

| question | what it asks for | answer from this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| ¿Qué es? | a thing | *Es una película.* |
| ¿Quién está en la película? | a person | *El actor.* / *La actriz.* |

*Qué* wants the thing word. *Quién* wants the person word. Until this chapter
you could answer only the first of the two about anything on a screen or a
stage, because the corpus had named the film and never named anyone in it.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-PELICULA, ES-LEX-C354-WHERE-28] -->

> — ¿Vamos al cine?
> — Sí. ¿Qué película es?
> — Es la película de la actriz.
> — ¿Y el actor?
> — El actor está en el teatro.

Note the inverted marks opening each question, and that *de la actriz* is how
Spanish says *the actress's*.

## Guided Practice

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-01, ES-LEX-C421-ARTS-02, ES-LEX-PELICULA] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which question word asks for a person rather than a thing?
(*Quién*.) What happens to *de el* in *la película del actor*? (It contracts to
*del*.) Why does *de la actriz* not contract? (The contraction exists only for
*de + el*.)
