Urdu, chapter 539: Free to Choose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
زبردستی (zabardastī) — force (making someone do something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to totter, then the Urdu for unshakable.

You'll want to know: زبردستی.
زبردستی — zabardastī — "force (making someone do something)".
اگر وہ نہیں چاہتا تو زبردستی ٹھیک نہیں۔ agar voh nahīṅ chāhtā to zabardastī ṭhīk nahīṅ. If he doesn't want to, forcing him isn't right.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
زبردستی (zabardastī) is built on zabardast, "having the upper hand", usually traced to Persian zabardast, from zabar, "above", and dast, "a hand", with the noun ending -ī.

You'll want to know: ناگزیر.

Lesson 4 of 5.
صلاح (salāh) — advice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for free to decide, then the Urdu for unavoidable.

You'll want to know: صلاح.
صلاح — salāh — "advice".
اگر آپ میری صلاح سنتے تو یہ مشکل نہ ہوتی۔ agar āp merī salāh sunte to yih mushkil na hotī. If you had listened to my advice, this trouble wouldn't have happened.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
صلاح (salāh) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ṣalāḥ, "soundness, what is right"; the sense "advice" came later, and the sources we use do not show how it arose.

Lesson 5 of 5.
جھکانا (jhukānā) — to bow (one's head); to lower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for unavoidable, then the Urdu for a piece of advice.

You'll want to know: جھکانا.
جھکانا — jhukānā — "to bow (one's head); to lower".
اگر وہ شرمندہ ہوتا تو سر جھکاتا۔ agar voh sharmindā hotā to sar jhukātā. If he were ashamed, he would hang his head.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے سر جھکایا, us ne sar jhukāyā.

The word, taken apart.
جھکانا (jhukānā) is the causative of jhuknā, "to bend", which CDIAL lists under jhukkati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 5399).
