5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
грубый (grúbyy) — rude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for that means, then the Russian for for example.

You'll want to know: грубый.
грубый (grúbyy) — "rude".

The word, taken apart.
грубый (grúbyy) from Proto-Slavic grǫbŭ — inherited. Vasmer thinks it possibly related to Lithuanian grubùs, "rough, uneven", and grùpti, "to grow rough".

Lesson 2 of 5.
честный (chéstnyy) — honest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for for example, then the Russian for rude.

You'll want to know: честный.
честный (chéstnyy) — "honest".

The word, taken apart.
честный (chéstnyy) is честь, "honour", with -ный. Vasmer puts честь with чтить, "to honour", and relates it to Sanskrit cítti-, "thought, understanding".

Lesson 4 of 5.
щедрый (shchédryy) — generous.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for honest, then the Russian for brave.

You'll want to know: щедрый.
щедрый (shchédryy) — "generous".

The word, taken apart.
щедрый (shchédryy) — its е shows it came through Church Slavonic, Vasmer says; Old Russian had щедрый (shchédryy). He thinks it apparently related to Latvian šķedērns, "a chip", and Greek skedánnymi, "I scatter": the generous man scatters his goods.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: жадный.
жадный (zhádnyy) — "greedy".

The word, taken apart.
жадный (zhádnyy) from Old Russian žadĭnŭ, "thirsting"; Vasmer links it with жажда, "thirst". The greedy one thirsts for more.
