Hindi, chapter 499: Growing Up.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
किशोर (kiśor) — an adolescent; a teenager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to feed, then the Hindi for to be brought up.

You'll want to know: किशोर.
किशोर — kiśor — "an adolescent; a teenager".
किशोर उम्र में बच्चे बहुत जल्दी बदलते हैं। kiśor umr meṁ bacce bahut jaldī badalte haiṁ. In their teens, children change very quickly.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
किशोर (kiśor) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit kiśora, first "a colt" and then "a youth, a lad" (Monier-Williams). The feminine is kiśorī.

अठारह साल का होकर अरुण बालिग़ हो गया। aṭhārah sāl kā hokar Aruṇ bāliġ ho gayā. At eighteen Arun came of age.

The word, taken apart.
बालिग़ (bāliġ) is usually traced to Arabic bāliġ, "having reached maturity", which reached Hindi through Persian; it is the word of the law.

यौवन — yauvan — "the prime of youth".
दादी कहती हैं कि यौवन जल्दी बीत जाता है। dādī kahtī haiṁ ki yauvan jaldī bīt jātā hai. Grandmother says that youth passes quickly.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
यौवन (yauvan) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit yauvana, "youth, youthfulness", from yuvan, "young" (Monier-Williams); everyday speech says javānī.

You'll want to know: बिसूरना.
बिसूरना — bisūrnā — "to whimper; to sulk tearfully".
छोटा भाई मिठाई के लिए बिसूर रहा था। choṭā bhāī miṭhāī ke liye bisūr rahā thā. My little brother was whimpering for a sweet.
Past (he …): वह दिन भर बिसूरा, vah din bhar bisūrā.

The word, taken apart.
बिसूरना (bisūrnā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi bisūrnā, "to put on a crying face", under viśūratē, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 11941).
