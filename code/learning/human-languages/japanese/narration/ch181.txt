Japanese, chapter 181: Prices and Selling.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かいもの (kaimono) — shopping; purchases.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to grip, then the Japanese for rice ball.

You'll want to know: かいもの.
かいもの — kaimono — "shopping; purchases".
まちで かいものを します。 — machi de kaimono o shimasu — "I go shopping in town."

The word, taken apart.
かいもの (kaimono) is native Japanese, built from kai, the stem of kau, "to buy", and mono, "thing" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

The word, taken apart.
ねだん (nedan) is built from native ne, "price, value", and Sino-Japanese dan, "grade, step", a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese dwanH) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
いくら (ikura) — how much.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for shopping, then the Japanese for price.

You'll want to know: いくら.
いくら — ikura — "how much".
この ほんは いくらですか。 — kono hon wa ikura desu ka — "How much is this book?"

The word, taken apart.
いくら (ikura) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi; JMdict marks it as usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
うれる (ureru) — to sell well; to be popular.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for how much, then the Japanese for to sell.

You'll want to know: うれる.
うれる — ureru — "to sell well; to be popular".
この ほんは よく うれます。 — kono hon wa yoku uremasu — "This book sells well."
Polite うれます, uremasu; an ichidan verb. Its transitive partner is うる (uru), "to sell".

The word, taken apart.
うれる (ureru) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed, built on the same stem as uru, "to sell"; its kanji was attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
