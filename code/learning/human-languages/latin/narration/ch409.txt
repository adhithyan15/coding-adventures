Latin, chapter 409: Edicts and Rules.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ēdictum, ēdictī — an edict, a proclamation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lash, then the Latin for to be beaten.

You'll want to know: ēdictum, ēdictī.
ēdictum, ēdictī — "an edict, a proclamation".
Praetor ēdictum prōposuit. The praetor published his edict.
ēdictum, ēdictī n., from ēdīcō, to speak out. Each praetor posted an edict saying how he would run the courts. English edict.

The word, taken apart.
ēdictum is the participle of ēdīcō "to speak out, to proclaim" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from ē and dīcō "to say". English edict comes from it.

licitus, licita, licitum, the participle of licet, it is allowed. Its opposite is illicitus. English licit and illicit.
