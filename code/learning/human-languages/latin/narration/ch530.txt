Latin, chapter 530: Masters and slaves.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
erus, erī — a master (of slaves).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a little daughter, then the Latin for brotherly.

You'll want to know: erus, erī.
erus, erī — "a master (of slaves)".
Servus erō suō pāret. The slave obeys his master.
Masculine, second declension. The word of comedy for the master of slaves; era is the mistress. Dominus is the everyday word.

The word, taken apart.
erus is inherited through Proto-Italic from a Proto-Indo-European word for "master", and Hittite ishas "lord" is a cousin (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
verna, vernae — a home-born slave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for brotherly, then the Latin for a master.

You'll want to know: verna, vernae.
verna, vernae — "a home-born slave".
Verna in domō dominī nātus est. A home-born slave was born in his master's house.
Masculine or feminine, first declension. Such slaves were often trusted. English vernacular — the speech of home — comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
verna is a native Latin word whose origin is not settled; Lewis & Short links it with a root meaning "to dwell". English vernacular comes from vernāculus, built on it.

ancillula, ancillulae — "a young maidservant".
Ancillula dominae speculum dat. The little maid gives her mistress a mirror.
Feminine, a diminutive of ancilla, already known, which is itself a diminutive.

The word, taken apart.
ancillula is a diminutive of ancilla "maidservant" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

servīlis, servīle — "of a slave, slavish".
Labor servīlis dūrus est. A slave's work is hard.
Two endings, from servus. English servile.

The word, taken apart.
servīlis is built on servus "slave" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English servile comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pedisequus, pedisequī — a footman, attendant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a young maidservant, then the Latin for of a slave.

You'll want to know: pedisequus, pedisequī.
pedisequus, pedisequī — "a footman, attendant".
Pedisequus dominum in forum sequitur. The footman follows his master into the forum.
Masculine; pedisequa — a female attendant. pēs 'foot' + sequor 'to follow'.

The word, taken apart.
pedisequus is built on pēs "foot" and sequor "to follow" (Lewis & Short): one who follows on foot.
