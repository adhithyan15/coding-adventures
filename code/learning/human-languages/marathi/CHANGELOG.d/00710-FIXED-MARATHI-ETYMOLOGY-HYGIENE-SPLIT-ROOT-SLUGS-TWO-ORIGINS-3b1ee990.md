## Fixed — Marathi etymology hygiene: split root slugs, two origins corrected, short hooks

- **Root slugs that named two etyma.** MR-C236-parpatra moves from `sanskrit-para` (*para* "other", CDIAL 7793) to `sanskrit-para-bank` (*pāra* "far bank", the slug Bengali and Urdu use). MR-C146-chati moves from `indo-aryan-chatti` (the roof word) to `indo-aryan-chatti-chest` (*\*chātti* "chest", CDIAL 5014), as in Gujarati and Hindi. MR-C180-vastuvisarad moves from `sanskrit-vastu` (*vastu* "thing") to a new `sanskrit-vastu-site` (*vāstu* "site", CDIAL 11606). MR-C230-darroj moves from `persian-dar` to a new `persian-dar-in`, since its *dar* is the preposition "in", not *-dār* "holder" or *dar* "door".
- **ठेवणे (MR-C22-thevne).** It said the verb came through Prakrit *ṭhavei* from *sthāpayati*. CDIAL files Marathi *ṭhevṇẽ* under *sthēya* "to be stationed" (CDIAL 13777), as a causative formed on *ṭheṇẽ* "to stand still", and lists no Marathi form under *sthāpayati* (13756). The block and hook now say so; the root slug `prakrit-thavai-place` becomes `sanskrit-stheya`. The *sthā-* / *\*steh₂-* root and its English cousins stand.
- **मिरची (MR-C87-mirchi).** It traced *mirchī* to *marīca* like *mirī*. CDIAL's only Marathi rows under 9875 are *mirī* and *mirẽ* "a peppercorn", under plain *marīca*; *mirchī*'s *-ch-* matches the variant *\*maricca*, where CDIAL puts Hindi *mirc* and Gujarati *marcī*. The block and hook now say that, in line with the Gujarati and Marwadi lessons.
- **Hooks cut to 120 characters.** The 45 long hooks in chapters 6-36 and those of MR-C60-chautha and MR-C61-navva were cut, each keeping its origin claim; MR-C07-khane's and MR-C36-lyaavar's now say where the word comes from instead of describing grammar. Five were checked against CDIAL and now say what it says: *asṇe* (CDIAL files it under *āsatē*, 1480, not *asti*), *pāhṇe* (under *\*pracāh*, 8492, not *prapaśyati*), *sāṅgṇe* (under *saṁkhyāti*, 12842, not *śaṃsati*), *sāṭhī* (under *saṁsthá*, 13049, rather than *sārtha*) and *bhākrī* (*bhākar* under *\*bhakkha* "lump", 9330). The bodies of MR-C07-asne, MR-C07-pahne and MR-C24-sangne still teach the older derivations and need a separate rework.
