Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 270: Saying Sorry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
माफी (māphī) — pardon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for stubbornness, then the Marwadi for to interrupt.

You'll want to know: माफी.
माफी — māphī — "pardon".
हूं माफी मांगूं हूं। hū̃ māphī māṅgū̃ hū̃. I'm sorry: I ask your pardon.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
माफी (māphī) is usually traced, through Persian muʿāfī, "pardon", to Arabic muʿāf, "excused".

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
पछताणो (pachhtāṇo) — to regret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for pardon, then the Marwadi for a wrongdoing.

You'll want to know: पछताणो.
पछताणो — pachhtāṇo — "to regret".
हूं घणो पछताऊं हूं। hū̃ ghaṇo pachhtāū̃ hū̃. I am very sorry for it.
Past (he …): वो पछतायो, vo pachhtāyo.

The word, taken apart.
पछताणो (pachhtāṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed paścottāpa, "repentance"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari pichatāi and pachitāi under it (CDIAL 8010).

Lesson 4 of 5.
फोड़णो (phoṛṇo) — to smash; to break open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a wrongdoing, then the Marwadi for to regret.

You'll want to know: फोड़णो.
फोड़णो — phoṛṇo — "to smash; to break open".
मटको मत फोड़ो। maṭko mat phoṛo. Don't smash the pot.
Past (I …): म्हैं मटको फोड़्यो, mhaĩ maṭko phoṛyo.

The word, taken apart.
फोड़णो (phoṛṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit sphoṭayati, "bursts, splits, breaks"; CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi phoṛnā, Gujarati phoṛvũ and Sindhi phoṛaṇu, all "to break" (CDIAL 13857). It is the transitive partner of फूटणो, to crack open: मटको फूट्यो, but म्हैं मटको फोड़्यो.

Lesson 5 of 5.
खीजणो (khījṇo) — to get cross.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to regret, then the Marwadi for to smash.

You'll want to know: खीजणो.
खीजणो — khījṇo — "to get cross".
मत खीजो सा। mat khījo sā. Please don't be cross.
Past (he …): वो खीज्यो, vo khījyo.

The word, taken apart.
खीजणो (khījṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit kṣīyate, "is worn away, is injured"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari khijaï, "to be irritated", and Gujarati khījvũ, "to take offence" (CDIAL 3695).
