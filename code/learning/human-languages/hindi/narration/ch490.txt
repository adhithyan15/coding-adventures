Hindi, chapter 490: Cuts and Scrapes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
छाला (chālā) — a blister from rubbing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an ointment, then the Hindi for a herbal brew.

You'll want to know: छाला.
छाला — chālā — "a blister from rubbing".
नए जूतों से मेरे पैर में छाला पड़ गया। nae jūtoṁ se mere pair meṁ chālā paṛ gayā. My new shoes gave me a blister on my foot.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
छाला (chālā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi chālā, with the sense "skin, hide", under Sanskrit challi, "bark" (CDIAL 5005), beside chālī, "a small blister". The "blister" sense of chālā itself, skin lifted from the flesh, is not given there.

घुटने के घाव पर पपड़ी जम गई है। ghuṭne ke ghāv par papṛī jam gaī hai. A scab has formed on the wound on my knee.

The word, taken apart.
पपड़ी (papṛī) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi papṛī, "thin crust, scab", under Sanskrit párpaṭa (CDIAL 7934). The thin, crisp papṛī of the snack seller is the same word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कुरेदना (kurednā) — to pick at; to scrape at.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a stitch, then the Hindi for a scab.

You'll want to know: कुरेदना.
कुरेदना — kurednā — "to pick at; to scrape at".
डाक्टर ने कहा कि पपड़ी को मत कुरेदो। ḍākṭar ne kahā ki papṛī ko mat kuredo. The doctor said not to pick at the scab.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने पपड़ी कुरेदी, usne papṛī kuredī.

The word, taken apart.
कुरेदना (kurednā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi kurednā, "to scrape", under kura, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 3319). One can also kurednā an old memory: stir it up again.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a scab, then the Hindi for to pick at.

You'll want to know: दाग़.
दाग़ — dāġ — "a scar; a stain".
बचपन की चोट का दाग़ अरुण के माथे पर आज भी है। bacpan kī coṭ kā dāġ Aruṇ ke māthe par āj bhī hai. Arun still has the scar of a childhood injury on his forehead.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
दाग़ (dāġ) is usually traced to Persian dāġ, "a brand, a mark": a stain on cloth or a scar on skin.
