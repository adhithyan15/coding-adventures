French, chapter 278: La presse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'actualité — the news, current events.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the instant, then the French for at the end of.

You'll want to know: l'actualité.
l'actualité — "the news, current events".
Mon père suit l'actualité tous les matins. My father follows the news every morning.
Les actualités — the news on TV or radio, a little old-fashioned. D'actualité — topical.

The word, taken apart.
actualité is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin āctuālitās (EtymDB), built on āctuālis "active, real", from agere "to do" (Lewis & Short): what is happening now.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la presse — the press, the newspapers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for at the end of, then the French for the news.

You'll want to know: la presse.
la presse — "the press, the newspapers".
La presse a beaucoup parlé de cette histoire. The press talked a lot about this story.
La presse écrite — the print press. It also names a machine that presses.

The word, taken apart.
presse is built on presser "to press", which is inherited from Latin pressāre, built on premere "to press" (Lewis & Short): first the printing press, then the papers it printed.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le quotidien — the daily paper; daily life.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the news, then the French for the press.

You'll want to know: le quotidien.
le quotidien — "the daily paper; daily life".
Il achète un quotidien tous les matins. He buys a daily paper every morning.
As an adjective, quotidien means daily: la vie quotidienne.

The word, taken apart.
quotidien is a learned borrowing from Latin quotīdiānus, cottīdiānus "daily" (EtymDB), from cottīdiē "every day", built on diēs "day" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'hebdomadaire — the weekly (paper or magazine).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the press, then the French for the daily paper.

You'll want to know: l'hebdomadaire.
l'hebdomadaire — "the weekly (paper or magazine)".
Ma grand-mère lit un hebdomadaire sur la cuisine. My grandmother reads a weekly about cooking.
Often un hebdo in speech. As an adjective: une réunion hebdomadaire.

The word, taken apart.
hebdomadaire is a learned borrowing from Late Latin hebdomadārius (EtymDB), built on Greek hebdomás "a group of seven, a week".

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'abonné — the subscriber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the daily paper, then the French for the weekly.

You'll want to know: l'abonné.
l'abonné — "the subscriber".
Les abonnés reçoivent le journal à la maison. Subscribers get the paper at home.
Être abonné à un journal — to take a paper. Feminine: l'abonnée. Online: les abonnés are followers.

The word, taken apart.
abonné is the past participle of abonner "to subscribe" (EtymDB), built on Old French bonne, borne "a boundary", which is usually traced to a Gaulish word: a price fixed within limits.
