French, chapter 299: Une mauvaise chute.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
la chute — the fall; the drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the airbag, then the French for to dent.

You'll want to know: la chute.
la chute — "the fall; the drop".
Ma grand-mère a fait une chute dans l'escalier. My grandmother had a fall on the stairs.
Les chutes de neige — snowfalls; une chute d'eau — a waterfall.

The word, taken apart.
chute is built on chu, the old past participle of choir "to fall" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Latin cadere "to fall" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
fouler — to sprain (as se fouler); to tread on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to dent, then the French for the fall.

You'll want to know: fouler.
fouler — "to sprain (as se fouler); to tread on".
Il s'est foulé la cheville en courant. He sprained his ankle while running.
In this sense mostly reflexive, with a body part: se fouler le poignet. Without se, it means to tread on or to press: fouler le sol.

The word, taken apart.
fouler is inherited through Old French foler "to crush, to trample" from a verb built on Latin fullō "a fuller", who trod cloth (EtymDB).
