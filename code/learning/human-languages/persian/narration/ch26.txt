Persian, chapter 26: Letters From Thinking and Coming.
5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ف (fe) — the letter that opens فکر.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ف and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ط and ظ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say فکر کردن (fekr kardan), "to think". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ف.
ف — fe, the sound f.
It opens فکر (fekr), "thought", in فکر کردن. A small closed head on a broad bowl, with one dot above.

Writing: ف.
1. start here: loop clockwise around the small closed head.
2. continue left through the broad bowl without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ف (fe) demonstration at 02:09–02:13 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — look: at فکر کردن and point at ف (fe) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ف (fe) three times, saying f as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ظ, then ف (fe), from memory]
[your turn — read: فکر کردن aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ف? (fe, f.) Which word did it come from? (فکر کردن, fekr kardan, "to think".)

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 2 seconds]
Now say خوشوقتم (khoshvaghtam), "pleased to meet you". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ق.
ق — ghâf, a gh made far back in the throat.
It is inside خوشوقتم (khoshvaghtam), "pleased to meet you". Its head is the head of ف (fe), from the last lesson, but it carries two dots, and standing alone its bowl dips below the line.

Writing: ق.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the small closed head.
2. continue down and left through the deep bowl without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper-right dot.
4. lift again, then place the upper-left dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ق (ghâf) demonstration at 02:14–02:18 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Lesson 3 of 5.
آ (alef-e madd) — the alef that opens آمدن.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: آ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ف (fe) and ق (ghâf) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say آمدن (âmadan), "to come". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: آ.
آ (alef-e madd) — alef with a madd, the long â at the start of a word.
It opens آمدن (âmadan), "to come". It is ا, which you already write, wearing a small wavy hat. A plain ا at the start of a word says a short a; the hat makes it a long â.

Writing: آ.
Put your pen on آ (alef-e madd) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
