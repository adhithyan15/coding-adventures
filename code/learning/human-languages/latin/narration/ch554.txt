Latin, chapter 554: Household chores.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
subsellium, subselliī — a bench, low seat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hinge, then the Latin for provisions.

You'll want to know: subsellium, subselliī.
subsellium, subselliī — "a bench, low seat".
Discipulī in subselliīs sedent. The pupils sit on benches.
Neuter, second declension: sub + sella — lower than a chair. Also the benches of a law court.

The word, taken apart.
subsellium is built on sub "below" and sella "seat, chair" (Lewis & Short).

Ancilla aquam urceō portat. The maid carries water in a jug.
Masculine, second declension.

The word, taken apart.
urceus is a word of uncertain origin; Lewis & Short compares Greek hurche "a jar", and EtymDB links it with orca "a cask", but nothing is settled.

Lesson 3 of 7.
repōnō, repōnere — to put back; to store away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bench, then the Latin for a jug.

You'll want to know: repōnō, repōnere.
repōnō, repōnere — "to put back; to store away".
Post cēnam ancilla vāsa in armāriō repōnit. After dinner the maid puts the dishes back in the cupboard.
repōnō, repōnere, reposuī, repositum. English repository.

The word, taken apart.
repōnō is built on re- "back" and pōnō "to put" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English repository comes from it.

sarciō, sarcīre, sarsī, sartum. Of clothes, roofs and friendships.

Lesson 5 of 7.
pectō, pectere — to comb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to put back, then the Latin for to mend.

You'll want to know: pectō, pectere.
pectō, pectere — "to comb".
Ancilla crīnēs dominae pectit. The maid combs her mistress's hair.
pectō, pectere, pexī, pexum. Pecten, already known, is the comb.

The word, taken apart.
pectō is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to comb, shear", and Greek peko is a cousin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). pecten "comb" is built on it.
