Latin, chapter 206: Breath, Memory, Wisdom, Talent, Habit.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
anima, animae — breath, soul.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a drug, a medicine, then the Latin for an ointment.

You'll want to know: anima, animae.
anima, animae — "breath, soul".

The word, taken apart.
anima is the partner of animus, "mind, spirit" (EtymDB 2.1), and both are usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2enh1-, "to breathe" (EtymDB 2.1): the soul as breath.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ingenium, ingeniī — talent, character.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for memory, then the Latin for wisdom.

You'll want to know: ingenium, ingeniī.
ingenium, ingeniī — "talent, character".

The word, taken apart.
ingenium is in, "in", with the root of gignō, "to beget, to give birth to" (Lewis & Short): what is born in a person, one's nature. English engine and ingenious come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cōnsuētūdō, cōnsuētūdinis — a habit, a custom.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: cōnsuētūdō, cōnsuētūdinis.
cōnsuētūdō, cōnsuētūdinis — "a habit, a custom".

The word, taken apart.
cōnsuētūdō is built on cōnsuēscō, "to get used to" (Lewis & Short), with -tūdō, an ending for a state. English custom comes from it through French.
