## You'll want to know: chuáng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C87-CHUANG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*chuáng* — "bed".

> *fángjiān lǐ yǒu chuáng ma* — Is there a bed in the room?

Its character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*chuáng* is inherited from Middle Chinese *dzrjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a bed of slats'. It is the chuáng of *qǐchuáng*: to rise from the bed.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-GRAMMAR-YOU-EXISTENCE-01, ZH-LEX-C87-MEIYOU-01] -->

*fángjiān lǐ yǒu chuáng ma* is 有 in its 'there is' job: the place first, then 有, then the thing. The answer is 有 or 没有.
