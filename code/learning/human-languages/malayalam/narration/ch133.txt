Malayalam, chapter 133: Sweet and Sour.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
മാങ്ങ (māṅṅa) — a mango.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for puttu, then the Malayalam for a curry.

You'll want to know: മാങ്ങ.
മാങ്ങ (māṅṅa) — "a mango", a പഴം from the tree in the മുറ്റം.

The word, taken apart.
മാങ്ങ (māṅṅa) is native Dravidian, the mango tree's name mā with the word for "fruit" fused on; DEDR 4782 lists Malayalam māṅṅa itself, beside Tamil mā, Kannada mā and Telugu māvi. DEDR 4782 compares Sanskrit mākanda.

What you've built.
The summer fruit.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ചക്ക (cakka) — a jackfruit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a curry, then the Malayalam for a mango.

You'll want to know: ചക്ക.
ചക്ക (cakka) — "a jackfruit", the huge fruit that grows straight off the trunk.

The word, taken apart.
ചക്ക (cakka) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil cakkai and Kannada jaka (DEDR 2275). English jack in jackfruit comes from this word, through Portuguese jaca.

What you've built.
Kerala's own fruit.

Lesson 3 of 5.
നാരങ്ങ (nāraṅṅa) — a lemon, a lime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a mango, then the Malayalam for a jackfruit.

You'll want to know: നാരങ്ങ.
നാരങ്ങ (nāraṅṅa) — "a lemon", or a lime. നാരങ്ങ വെള്ളം — lemon water.

The word, taken apart.
നാരങ്ങ (nāraṅṅa) from Sanskrit nāraṅga, "orange tree" (CDIAL 7073), reshaped as a tadbhava: ṅg became ṅṅ. DEDR 3653 compares it with Tamil nāram, "orange". The same word travelled west through Persian and Arabic and became English orange.

What you've built.
Sour, and cool on a hot day.

ഇഞ്ചി (iñci) — "ginger", good in ചായ and for a ചുമ.

The word, taken apart.
ഇഞ്ചി (iñci) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil iñci (DEDR 429). English ginger is usually traced to the same word, through Sanskrit, Greek and Latin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
പഞ്ചസാര (pañcasāra) — sugar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a lemon, then the Malayalam for ginger.

You'll want to know: പഞ്ചസാര.
പഞ്ചസാര (pañcasāra) — "sugar". പഞ്ചസാര വേണോ? — "do you want sugar?"

The word, taken apart.
പഞ്ചസാര (pañcasāra) is of uncertain origin. It is often explained as Sanskrit pañca, "five", + sāra, "essence", but that may be a story told after the fact.

What you've built.
Asked with every cup of tea.
