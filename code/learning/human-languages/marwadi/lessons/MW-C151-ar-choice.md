# चाय अर पाणी? — offer two familiar choices

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-AR-01, MW-GRAMMAR-COORDINATE-AND-01, MW-LEX-CHAAY-01, MW-LEX-PAANI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **चाय** (*chāy*, tea), then **पाणी** (*pāṇī*, water). Join
them once with **अर** as “tea and water”.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-ALTERNATIVE-CHOICE-01]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-AR-01, MW-LEX-CHAAY-01, MW-LEX-PAANI-01] -->

The official survey gives **अर** both “and” and “or” value. When somebody
offers two alternatives with a question contour, the same small word carries
the choice:

> **चाय अर पाणी?** — *chāy ar pāṇī?* — Tea or water?

The setting and the rising question do the separating. Do not import a Hindi
connector to make the choice look more familiar.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-AR-01] -->

This lesson adds a new use, not a new connector. The official survey attests
**अर** with “and / or” value but does not derive it; its deeper origin remains
unknown in that source.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-FUNCTION-ASK-PREFERENCE-ALTERNATIVES-01]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-AR-01, MW-GRAMMAR-ALTERNATIVE-CHOICE-01, MW-LEX-CHAAY-01, MW-LEX-PAANI-01] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU ASK: **चाय अर पाणी?**]
- [YOU ANSWER: **पाणी।**]
- [YOU ASK: reverse the alternatives — **पाणी अर चाय?**]
- [YOU ANSWER: **चाय।**]

One short item is enough for the answer. This lesson teaches how to hand over a
real choice; it does not yet teach “I prefer”.
