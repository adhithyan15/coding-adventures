Sanskrit, chapter 251: Earth, Universe, World, Nature, Mud.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
पृथिवी (pṛthivī) — the earth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a building, then the Sanskrit for lodging.

You'll want to know: पृथिवी.
पृथिवी — pṛthivī — "the earth".

The word, taken apart.
pṛthivī is the feminine of pṛthu, "broad, wide" (MW): the earth is "the broad, extended one". MW compares pṛthu with Greek platys, "broad", and English plate, so the word is inherited from Indo-European.

Lesson 2 of 5.
जगत् (jagat) — the universe, the world.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for lodging, then the Sanskrit for the earth.

You'll want to know: जगत्.
जगत् — jagat — "the universe, the world".

The word, taken apart.
jagat is a reduplicated form of the root √gam, "to go" (MW): "moving, living". In the Rigveda it means "men and animals", and in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa "the world" (CDIAL 5078): the world as everything that moves.

Lesson 3 of 5.
लोकः (lokaḥ) — the world, people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the earth, then the Sanskrit for the universe.

You'll want to know: लोकः.
लोकः — lokaḥ — "the world, people".

The word, taken apart.
loka is a Rigvedic word for "free space, the world", and later "people" (CDIAL 11119). MW compares Latin lūcus, first "a clearing in a forest". Hindi loī, "people", comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
प्रकृतिः (prakṛtiḥ) — nature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the universe, then the Sanskrit for the world.

You'll want to know: प्रकृतिः.
प्रकृतिः — prakṛtiḥ — "nature".

The word, taken apart.
prakṛti is built on pra, "before, forth", and the root √kṛ, "to make" (MW): "making or placing first", and so the original or natural form of anything. From there it came to mean "nature" (CDIAL 8446).

Lesson 5 of 5.
पंकः (paṅkaḥ) — mud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the world, then the Sanskrit for nature.

You'll want to know: पंकः.
पंकः — paṅkaḥ — "mud".

The word, taken apart.
paṅka is mud or mire (MW), and MW reports that it is said to come from a root √pac, "to spread", so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi paṅkā, "mud", and the lotus is paṅkaja, "mud-born" (CDIAL 7645).
