Mandarin Chinese, chapter 477: Inside the Body.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
xīnzàng — the heart as an organ.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shake one's head, then the Mandarin for bodily pain.

You'll want to know: xīnzàng.
xīnzàng — "the heart as an organ".
wǒ yéye de xīnzàng bú tài hǎo — My grandfather has a weak heart.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xīnzàng is built from 心 'heart' and zàng 'an inner organ'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the organ of fire; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second under this spelling, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
gǔtou — bone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bodily pain, then the Mandarin for the heart as an organ.

You'll want to know: gǔtou.
gǔtou — "bone".
gǒu zuì xǐhuan chī gǔtou — Dogs love chewing bones more than anything.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǔtou is built from gǔ 'bone' and 头, a noun ending here, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwot and duw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as the core of the flesh; the second is said light.

Lesson 4 of 5.
chángwèi — stomach and gut; digestion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bone, then the Mandarin for the brain.

You'll want to know: chángwèi.
chángwèi — "stomach and gut; digestion".
wǒ chángwèi bù shūfu le liǎng tiān — My stomach has been upset for two days.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chángwèi is built from cháng 'the gut' and wèi 'the stomach', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjang and hjw+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first in this very pair and the second, after an older book, as the storehouse of grain.
