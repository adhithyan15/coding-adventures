# expliquer pourquoi — to unfold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-JE-PENSE-QUE-01, FR-GRAM-DEUX-FLECHES-01, FR-LEX-JE-DEVRAIS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Four pieces are in your hands: *je pense que* for a view, *parce
que* and *donc* for the two arrows, *je devrais* for advice. This lesson
puts them in a row, under the verb that names the act.

## Grammar Lens: explaining something to someone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-EXPLIQUER-A-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C144-VERBES144-01, FR-LEX-C225-CHOSES225-05, FR-LEX-POUVOIR-02] -->

You have **expliquer**, "to explain", a regular *-er* verb. The thing
explained comes straight after it; the person who hears it takes **à**, or
stands in front as a small pronoun:

> *J'explique le problème **à** ma mère.* — I explain the problem to my
> mother.
> *Je **t'**explique.* — Let me explain. *(to a friend)*
> *Vous pouvez **m'**expliquer ?* — Could you explain it to me?

And the explanation itself often opens with **la raison**, "the reason":
*La raison, c'est que…* — "The reason is that…"

## Grammar Lens: the shape of an explanation
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-EXPLICATION-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PARCEQUE-01, FR-LEX-C54-SANTE6-05, FR-LEX-IL-FAISAIT-01, FR-LEX-C98-TEMPS50-01] -->

Here is a whole explanation, every word of it met before:

> — Pourquoi tu ne sors pas ?
> — **Je pense qu'**il va pleuvoir. Et je suis malade, **parce qu'**hier il
> faisait froid. **Donc** je reste à la maison. **Je devrais** dormir.

Four moves, and a word for each: a **view** (*je pense que*), a **cause**
pointing back (*parce que*), a **consequence** pointing forward (*donc*),
and **advice** (*je devrais*). With both arrows you can build it cause
first or claim first, and the listener can follow either.

## The word, taken apart: folded, and unfolded
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C148-VERBES148-04] -->

**expliquer** is a learned borrowing from Latin *explicāre*, "to unfold, to
open out flat" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short, under *explico*), which is
*ex-*, "out", plus *plicāre*, "to fold". **To explain is to unfold**:
something was folded shut, and you open it out.

The same *plicāre* came down into French the slow way, by mouth, and gave
**plier**, "to fold", which you have (EtymDB 2.1). English kept the Latin in
**explicit**, and **complicated** is the opposite: folded together.
