# Review — the scene, and what cut into it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFEITO-01, PT-LEX-TINHA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Build "I was reading" from *ler*. (*Lia*, or *estava a ler*.) Then
say "I had a dog". (*Tinha um cão.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFEITO-01, PT-LEX-HAVIA-02, PT-LEX-FAZIA-FRIO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-ESTAVA-A-01] -->

Five lessons, and every piece of a scene is in your hands. The recipe, **-ava**
and **-ia**, on almost any verb: *falava*, *comia*, *partia*. The four that
refuse it, led by *era* and *tinha*. The scene-setter *havia*. The weather, made
rather than being: *fazia frio*. The European *estava a*, for a moment caught
in progress. And the division of labour: the imperfeito paints, the perfeito
drops the events in.

[PAUSE 2s] The joint between the two is **quando**.

> *Estava a ler quando a minha mãe telefonou.*
> *Estava à espera do autocarro quando começou a chover.*

The second one is worth hearing twice. The rain is an event here, because it
**began**: *começou*. Once it is falling, it is scene again: *chovia*. And
*estar à espera*, "to be waiting", is the European way to say it, with
*espera* from your *esperar*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01, PT-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-03, PT-ETYMON-IMPERFEITO-BA-02, PT-GRAMMAR-QUANDO-CENA-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] After *quando*, in these sentences, which past? (The **perfeito** —
the event.) Why is *começou a chover* in the perfeito? (The rain **began**:
that is an event.) What does *imperfeito* mean, taken apart? (**Not carried
through.**) And where did the *-v-* of *falava* come from? (The ***b*** of
Latin *amābat*.)
