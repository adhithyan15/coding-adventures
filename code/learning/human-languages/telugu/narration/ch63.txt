Telugu, chapter 63: Hunger, Sleep and Health.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఆకలి (ākali) — hunger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did addaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: ఆకలి.
ఆకలి (ākali) — "hunger".
ఆకలి (ākali) is hunger. Telugu does not say "I am hungry" — it says నాకు ఆకలి (ākali), "to me, hunger", the same shape as నాకు తెలుసు and నాకు తెలుగు వచ్చు. Hunger is something that happens to you.
The word is inherited, and it is among the first a child in a Telugu house learns to say — usually loudly, and usually before bhōjanaṁ is anywhere near ready.
ఆకలిగా ఉందా? is a real greeting in a house you are close to. It gets asked before మీరు ఎలా ఉన్నారు? has a chance.

What you've built.
The first thing to say about yourself.

Lesson 2 of 5.
నిద్ర (nidra) — sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did ākali mean?

You'll want to know: నిద్ర.
నిద్ర (nidra) — "sleep".
నిద్ర (nidra) is sleep, and it takes the same shape as ఆకలి (ākali): నాకు నిద్ర (nidra) వస్తోంది, "sleep is coming to me". You do not do it; it arrives.
The word came from Sanskrit nidrā and settled without changing. Beside it Telugu keeps నిదుర, the softer everyday form, and the two are one word wearing different clothes.
నిద్ర (nidra) also makes a polite reason for leaving late in the evening, and it works the way గాలి worked earlier. Nobody argues with it.

What you've built.
Two, and both of them come to you.

Lesson 3 of 5.
దగ్గు (daggu) — a cough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nidra mean?

You'll want to know: దగ్గు.
దగ్గు (daggu) — "a cough".
దగ్గు (daggu) is a cough. It is inherited, and it is a sound turned into a word, which is how a good many of these words start out.
Like ఆకలి (ākali) and నిద్ర (nidra) it comes to you rather than from you: నాకు దగ్గు (daggu), and nobody needs telling any more than that.
Telugu keeps దగ్గు (daggu) for the whole thing at once — the single cough, the fit of coughing, and the illness sitting behind both — where English reaches for three phrases. A మంచు morning in the hills fills a house with it.

What you've built.
Three.

Lesson 4 of 5.
రోగం (rōgaṁ) — a disease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did daggu mean?

You'll want to know: రోగం.
రోగం (rōgaṁ) — "a disease".
రోగం (rōgaṁ) is illness in general: not the దగ్గు (daggu) you have this week, but the thing a వైద్యుడు puts a name to.
It is Sanskrit, from a root about breaking — an illness is something breaking in you — and Telugu took the word whole, the way it took hṛdayaṁ.
రోగి is the person who has one. A వైద్యుడు's whole working day is spent between those two words, and so is a good deal of ordinary conversation about relatives.

What you've built.
Four.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ) — health.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did rōgaṁ mean?

You'll want to know: ఆరోగ్యం.
ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ) — "health".
ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ) is health, and it is the word before it with a small piece stuck on the front. The piece means "not", and రోగ is the illness: health here is what is left when the illness is gone.
That is a real difference in outlook and worth a moment. English health is something to be built up. ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ) is a subtraction.
మీ ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ) ఎలా ఉంది? is what a house asks an atithi who has been ill. It is more particular than మీరు ఎలా ఉన్నారు?, and it expects a real answer.

What you've built.
Five: ఆకలి (ākali), నిద్ర (nidra), దగ్గు (daggu), రోగం (rōgaṁ), ఆరోగ్యం (ārōgyaṁ). Enough to say what is wrong and to ask after somebody else.
