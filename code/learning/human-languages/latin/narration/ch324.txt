Latin, chapter 324: Puer et pueritia.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cūnae, cūnārum — a cradle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to suckle, then the Latin for a nurse.

You'll want to know: cūnae, cūnārum.
cūnae, cūnārum — "a cradle".
Īnfāns in cūnīs dormiēbat. The baby was sleeping in the cradle.
Feminine, plural. In Plautus a grown man is asked if he still sleeps in cūnīs.

The word, taken apart.
cūnae is inherited from Proto-Italic koinā, "cradle" (EtymDB), from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to lie down".

Lesson 2 of 5.
puer, puerī — a boy; a child; a slave boy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a nurse, then the Latin for a cradle.

You'll want to know: puer, puerī.
puer, puerī — "a boy; a child; a slave boy".
Puer in hortō lūdēbat. The boy was playing in the garden.
Masculine, 2nd declension; the e stays in every form. Also a young slave of any age; the plural puerī can mean 'children'.

The word, taken apart.
puer is inherited from Proto-Indo-European ph₂weros (EtymDB), from a root meaning "few, small". English puerile, "childish", comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
īnfantia, īnfantiae — infancy, early childhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cradle, then the Latin for a boy.

You'll want to know: īnfantia, īnfantiae.
īnfantia, īnfantiae — "infancy, early childhood".
Ab īnfantiā in vīllā avī vīxit. From early childhood he lived at his grandfather's villa.
Feminine, from īnfāns (taught), 'not yet speaking'. English infancy.

The word, taken apart.
īnfantia is built on īnfāns, "unable to speak" (EtymDB), from in-, "not", and fārī, "to speak". English infancy comes from it.

pueritia, pueritiae — "childhood, boyhood".
In pueritiā multās fābulās audiēbam. In my childhood I used to hear many stories.
Feminine, from puer; up to about the seventeenth year.

The word, taken apart.
pueritia is built on puer, "boy, child", with the ending -itia for a state (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ēducō, ēducāre — to bring up, to rear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for infancy, then the Latin for childhood.

You'll want to know: ēducō, ēducāre.
ēducō, ēducāre — "to bring up, to rear".
Avia eum ēducāvit, quod parentēs aberant. His grandmother brought him up, because his parents were away.
ēducō, ēducāre, ēducāvī, ēducātum, first conjugation, short u. Do not confuse ēdūcō, ēdūcere, 'to lead out'. English educate.

The word, taken apart.
ēducō is built on ēdūcō, "to lead out, to draw out" (Lewis & Short), and so on dūcō, "to lead". English educate comes from it.
