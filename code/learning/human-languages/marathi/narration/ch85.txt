Lesson 1 of 5.
मासा (māsā) — a fish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to fall, then the Marathi for meat.

You'll want to know: मासा.
मासा — māsā — "a fish".

The word, taken apart.
मासा (māsā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from matsya, "fish" (CDIAL 9758).

You'll want to know: भात.
भात — bhāt — "cooked rice".

The word, taken apart.
भात (bhāt) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bhakta, "meal, food; boiled rice" (CDIAL 9331). It is the past participle of bhaj, "to share out": a portion.

The word, taken apart.
डाळ (ḍāḷ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit dāla, "a kind of grain, split peas" (CDIAL 6309). CDIAL asks whether it is the same word as dāla, "tearing, splitting".

The word, taken apart.
फळ (phaḷ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit phala, "fruit" (CDIAL 9051). CDIAL compares Tamil paṛam, "ripe fruit", and Telugu paṇḍu, "to ripen".

सफरचंद — safarchand — "an apple".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of सफरचंद (safarchand) is not settled. It is often linked with Persian safarjal, "quince", from Arabic, but CDIAL has no entry for it and no source here confirms the link.
