# Bengali pre-A1 mock rubric

Score either form against [task-shapes/pre-a1.json](../../task-shapes/pre-a1.json).
Reading, listening, writing, and speaking are four separate 100-point papers.
The candidate must earn at least 60/100 on every paper in the same timed
administration; a 240/400 total cannot compensate for a skill below 60. Every
required skill must be attempted. This is a project-defined equivalent, not an
external Bengali qualification.
Administer each skill on a separate sheet. Collect Reading and Listening before
issuing Writing; otherwise their learner-facing Bengali text can reveal the
spelling of delayed-recall and dictation targets. Keep the examiner script,
model cards, and answer keys out of learner view throughout.

## Reading — 100 points

- Signs and words: 40 points across six keyed selections.
- Phrases and notices: 30 points across four keyed selections.
- Personal details: 30 points across four keyed selections.

Within each part, multiply the number correct by the part's points divided by
its item count; sum the three parts and round the paper score to one decimal.
Only the selected meaning or situation earns reading credit. Pre-A1 support-
language directions may explain how to mark a choice, but never translate an
answer-bearing Bengali prompt.

## Listening — 100 points

- Sounds and words: 40 points across six keyed selections.
- Greeting responses: 30 points across four keyed selections.
- Personal details: 30 points across four keyed selections.

Use the same part-weight formula as reading. Read or play each examiner-only
stimulus twice at 70–90 words per minute, leaving an eight-second answer pause.
Do not show a transcript, answer-bearing written label, romanization, dictionary,
or translation while the learner listens.

## Writing — 100 points

- Delayed recall: 20 points, ten for each of two words. Show each model for ten
  seconds, remove it completely, then score recall without a visible model.
- Dictation: 40 points, ten for each of four words. Read each twice without a
  visible spelling or transcription.
- Bounded independent production: 40 points, twenty for each of two responses.
  Give only the nonverbal cue; no copyable answer model is present.

For each response, score four equally weighted dimensions from 0 to 5: task
fulfilment, comprehensibility and word organisation, control of the relevant
Bengali word or memorized chunk, and Bengali orthographic control. Scale the
20-point dimension sum to the item's ten- or twenty-point maximum. For
orthographic control, assess the matra line, recognizable letter order, the
vowel-sign side of its base letter, hasanta placement where the chosen word
requires it, and legibility. Do not require an ornamental typeface shape. A
romanized, traced, or visibly copied response is not independent Bengali writing
evidence. Accept a chosen identity rather than forcing a model name.

## Speaking — 100 points

- Greeting and identity: 30 points across two prompts.
- Three familiar questions: 40 points across three prompts.
- Short request: 30 points for one prompt.

For each part, score four equally weighted dimensions from 0 to 5: task
fulfilment and relevant content, comprehensibility and interaction, control of
known Bengali words or chunks, and pronunciation and fluency. Scale the 20-point
dimension sum to the part maximum. A memorized chunk is valid at pre-A1 when
it completes the communicative task. Allow one slow repetition without penalty;
do not supply a written answer script or coach the response.

## Readiness boundary

The forms, keys, and rubric make this target administrable; they do not prove
that the book has taught delayed recall or dictation, nor that a book-only learner
can pass. No book-only readiness claim follows from passing artifact tests.
Readiness requires a clean timed administration and qualified human review of
the paper, recordings, writing, and scoring decisions, followed by the planned
book-only validation study.
