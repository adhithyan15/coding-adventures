Gujarati, chapter 220: When the river rises.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વહેવું (vahevũ) — to flow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for hailstones, then the Gujarati for unseasonal rain.

You'll want to know: વહેવું.
વહેવું — vahevũ — "to flow".
વરસાદ પછી નદી વહેશે. — varsād pachī nadī vaheśe — "After the rain the river will flow."

The word, taken apart.
વહેવું (vahevũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from Sanskrit vahati, "carries, bears along (of rivers)", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to flow" (CDIAL 11453).

You'll want to know: છલકાવું.
છલકાવું — chalkāvũ — "to overflow, to brim over".
કાલે તળાવ છલકાશે. — kāle taḷāv chalkāśe — "The lake will overflow tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
છલકાવું (chalkāvũ) comes from chala, "splash", an imitative reconstructed form; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, glossed "to be spilt" (CDIAL 5002).

Lesson 3 of 5.
પૂર (pūr) — flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to flow, then the Gujarati for to overflow.

You'll want to know: પૂર.
પૂર — pūr — "flood".
નદીમાં પૂર આવશે. — nadīmā̃ pūr āvśe — "The river will flood."
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
પૂર (pūr) is inherited from Sanskrit pūra, "filling", which already meant "flood" in the medical texts (CDIAL 8330).
