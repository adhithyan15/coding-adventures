## Grammar Lens: what this chapter actually cost you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01, ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-04] -->

Three of the four are assembled out of parts you already held.

| word | where the parts came from |
|---|---|
| producir | *reducir*'s verb class, *ducere*'s prefix trick |
| objetivo | *el objeto*'s throw, *rotativo*'s *-ivo* |

Neither needed a new rule — only a new arrangement of rules you had.

*Alimentar* borrowed *-mento* from *el medicamento*. Only *suficiente* asked
for something genuinely new, and even that was a distinction rather than a
word: you already had *bastante*.

That is what the earlier chapters bought. New words stop being new machinery
and start being new arrangements of it, and each one costs less than the last.

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-02, ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-03] -->

Two of the four hide a physical action.

| word | what is underneath |
|---|---|
| suficiente | *sub-* + *facere* — **putting enough underneath** to hold it up |
| alimentar | *alere* — **growing** a thing, with food as the means |

*Producir* leads forward; *objetivo* is the thing placed in front to be
reached. Four words off a garden shed, four Latin verbs of handling and
growing underneath them — the pattern this book keeps finding.
