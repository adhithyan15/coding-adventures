Arabic, chapter 95: Pocket, Ring, Hat, Shoe, Coat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جيب (jayb) — a pocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for trousers, then the Arabic for a sock.

You'll want to know: جيب.
جيب — jayb — "a pocket".

The word, taken apart.
جيب (jayb) is built on the native Arabic root j-y-b, which Lane treats under j-w-b, "to cut through, to hollow out". Lane's jayb is the opening at the neck and bosom of a shirt; "pocket" came later. The usual account is that medieval Latin translators read the Arabic word for a sine, a loan from Sanskrit jyā, as this word and rendered it sinus, the source of English sine.

The word, taken apart.
قبعة (qubbaʿa) is usually traced to Syriac qubbaʿā, "hood" (EtymDB); Lane has no entry for it, though a root q-b-ʿ exists in his lexicon.

The word, taken apart.
حذاء (ḥidhāʾ) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-dh-w, "to cut a sandal to a pattern" (Lane). Lane's verb ḥadhā is to measure and cut a sandal by a pattern.
