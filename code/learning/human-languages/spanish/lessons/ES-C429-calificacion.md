# la calificación — the word the paper uses when the person would say *nota*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *nota*. Now imagine it printed at the top of an official column
on a school document, and expect a longer word.

## You'll want to know: la calificación
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-03]; assesses=[] -->

**la calificación** — the grade, in its formal dress.

Spoken between students it is *nota*. Printed on a report, a transcript or a
form, it is **calificación**, and the two name the same thing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-03] -->

Medieval Latin **qualificare**, built from **qualis** ("of what kind") plus
**facere** ("to make"). To *calificar* is **to say what kind a thing is**.

That is a startlingly honest description of what a grade does. A number out of
ten is a claim about the **kind** of work in front of the reader, which is why
the formal word for it is built from the question *of what kind?*

English took the same compound as **qualify**, and kept the other half of the
sense: to *qualify* a statement is to say what kind of statement it is.

## Grammar Lens: where the accent sits, and why
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-03, ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02] -->

**Calificación** ends in **-n**, which by default pulls the stress to the
second-last syllable — *ca-li-fi-CA-cion*. The stress is on the last one, so an
accent has to be written: **-ción**.

Every Spanish word ending in **-ción** works this way, and you have met dozens:

| word | stressed syllable |
|---|---|
| calificación | -ción |
| educación | -ción |
| información | -ción |

In the plural the accent disappears — *calificaciones* — because adding a
syllable puts the stress where the default would have put it anyway.
