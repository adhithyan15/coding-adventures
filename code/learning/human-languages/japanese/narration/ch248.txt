Japanese, chapter 248: Promises, Lies and Secrets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しんじる (shinjiru) — to believe; to trust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be in trouble, then the Japanese for courage.

You'll want to know: しんじる.
しんじる — shinjiru — "to believe; to trust".
かれの こたえを しんじますか。 — kare no kotae o shinjimasu ka — "Do you believe his answer?"
Polite しんじます, shinjimasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
しんじる (shinjiru) is a mixed word, built on Sino-Japanese shin, "trust" (Middle Chinese sinH), borrowed from Chinese, with a native verb ending -jiru (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
うそ (uso) — a lie; a fib.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for courage, then the Japanese for to believe.

You'll want to know: うそ.
うそ — uso — "a lie; a fib".
かれは うそを いいません。 — kare wa uso o iimasen — "He does not tell lies."

The word, taken apart.
うそ (uso) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to believe, then the Japanese for a lie.

You'll want to know: やくそく.
やくそく — yakusoku — "a promise; an appointment".
あしたの やくそくは ごごですか。 — ashita no yakusoku wa gogo desu ka — "Is tomorrow's appointment in the afternoon?"

The word, taken apart.
やくそく (yakusoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yaku, "promise" (Middle Chinese 'jak) and soku, "bundle, tie" (syowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
