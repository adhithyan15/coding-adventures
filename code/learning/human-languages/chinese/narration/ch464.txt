Mandarin Chinese, chapter 464: Losing My Temper.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
发火 (fāhuǒ) — to lose one's temper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to tremble, then the Mandarin for a nightmare.

You'll want to know: 发火.
发火 — fāhuǒ — "to lose one's temper".
他发火的时候我很怕他 tā fāhuǒ de shíhou wǒ hěn pà tā — I'm really scared of him when he loses his temper.

The word, taken apart.
发火 (fāhuǒ) is built from 发 'to send out' and 火 'fire', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjot and xwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to start' and 'to raise'. Anger as a fire that breaks out is the usual explanation, not one the dictionary gives.

Lesson 2 of 5.
火气 (huǒqì) — temper; anger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a nightmare, then the Mandarin for to lose one's temper.

You'll want to know: 火气.
火气 — huǒqì — "temper; anger".
他的火气比我大 tā de huǒqì bǐ wǒ dà — He has a worse temper than I do.

The word, taken apart.
火气 (huǒqì) is built from 火 'fire' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwaX and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'breath'. The sense 'anger' is not given there.

You'll want to know: 气人.
气人 — qìrén — "to be infuriating; to make people angry".
这个人真气人 zhège rén zhēn qìrén — That man is really infuriating.

The word, taken apart.
气人 (qìrén) is built from 气 'to anger' and 人 'a person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khj+jH and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'breath'; the sense 'to anger' is not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
出气 (chūqì) — to vent one's anger; to take it out on someone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a hot temper, then the Mandarin for to be infuriating.

You'll want to know: 出气.
出气 — chūqì — "to vent one's anger; to take it out on someone".
nǐ shēngqì de shíhou bié ná wǒ chūqì — When you get angry, don't take it out on me.

The word, taken apart.
出气 (chūqì) is built from 出 'out' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'breath'; letting angry breath out is the usual explanation, not one the dictionary gives.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fènnù — furious; indignant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be infuriating, then the Mandarin for to take it out on someone.

You'll want to know: fènnù.
fènnù — "furious; indignant".
tīng le tā de huà wǒ gǎnjué hěn fènnù — When I heard what he said, I was furious.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fènnù is built from fèn and nù, both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjunX and nuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'vexed' and the second 'anger'.
