Bengali, chapter 152: To dry, To soak, To launder, To comb, To bite.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
শুকানো (shukāno) — to dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to hang, then the Bengali for to tie.

You'll want to know: শুকানো.
শুকানো — shukāno — "to dry".

The word, taken apart.
শুকানো (shukāno) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from śuṣkati, "becomes dry", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 12552). CDIAL lists the causing form, Bengali sukāna.

The word, taken apart.
ভেজানো (bhejāno) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bhiyajyate, "is smeared, gets wet", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9502). Bhejāno is the causing form of bhijā, "to get wet", which CDIAL lists.

You'll want to know: কাচা.
কাচা — kāchā — "to launder, to wash (clothes)".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of কাচা (kāchā) is not settled: CDIAL has no Bengali verb of this shape meaning "to wash", and a search by form and by the glosses "wash" and "clean" finds nothing close.

The word, taken apart.
আঁচড়ানো (ā̃chṛāno) (āṁchṛāno), "to comb, to scratch", appears to belong with Assamese āṁsor, "scratch", which CDIAL lists under añca, "curve", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 165); CDIAL gives no Bengali form there.
