Japanese, chapter 328: Out in Public.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
こうきょう (kōkyō) — public (places, services).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a nuisance, then the Japanese for a bow.

You'll want to know: こうきょう.
こうきょう — kōkyō — "public (places, services)".
こうきょうの ばしょは みんなが つかうから、ごみを すてないで ください。 — kōkyō no basho wa minna ga tsukau kara, gomi o sutenaide kudasai — "Everyone uses public places, so please do not drop rubbish there."

The word, taken apart.
こうきょう (kōkyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "public" (Middle Chinese kuwng) and kyō, "together" (gjowngH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.
うるさい (urusai) — annoying; noisy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for public, then the Japanese for in the way.

You'll want to know: うるさい.
うるさい — urusai — "annoying; noisy".
そとが うるさいから、まどは あけないで ください。 — soto ga urusai kara, mado wa akenaide kudasai — "It is noisy outside, so please do not open the window."
An i-adjective.

The word, taken apart.
うるさい (urusai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 6.
ひかえる (hikaeru) — to refrain from; to cut down on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for in the way, then the Japanese for annoying.

You'll want to know: ひかえる.
ひかえる — hikaeru — "to refrain from; to cut down on".
ねつが あるから、おふろは ひかえて ください。 — netsu ga aru kara, ofuro wa hikaete kudasai — "You have a fever, so please skip the bath."
Polite ひかえます, hikaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ちちは おさけを ひかえました。, chichi wa osake o hikaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
ひかえる (hikaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 6.
わりこむ (warikomu) — to cut in line; to push into a queue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for annoying, then the Japanese for to refrain from.

You'll want to know: わりこむ.
わりこむ — warikomu — "to cut in line; to push into a queue".
れつが ながいから、わりこまないで ください。 — retsu ga nagai kara, warikomanaide kudasai — "The queue is long, so please do not push in."
Polite わりこみます, warikomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おとこが わりこみました。, otoko ga warikomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
わりこむ (warikomu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built from waru, "to split" and komu, "to crowd in", and its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
