Mandarin Chinese, chapter 119: Bags and Boxes.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
书包 (shūbāo) — schoolbag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for text message, then the Mandarin for to receive.

You'll want to know: 书包.
书包 — shūbāo — "schoolbag".
我的书包里有两本书 wǒ de shūbāo lǐ yǒu liǎng běn shū — There are two books in my schoolbag.
The new character, in pieces: 包 (bāo) — a wrapping stroke that bends round from the top, and inside it three strokes: a turn, a short bar, and a vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
书包 (shūbāo) is built from 书 'book' and 包 (bāo) 'to wrap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syo and paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a wrapper for books.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

包 (bāo) — a wrapping stroke, and three strokes inside.

Script — a wrapping stroke, and three strokes inside.
包 (bāo).
The wrapping piece, two strokes: a short left-falling stroke, then a stroke across that turns down the right side and hooks.
The inside, three strokes: a stroke across that turns down, a short horizontal under it, and a vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up, out past the wrapping, as the last stroke of 也 does.
2 + 3 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 包 (bāo), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 包 (bāo)? (Five.) Which piece comes first, the wrapping or the inside? (The wrapping.) Which character you know ends with the same stroke? (也.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
xiāngzi — box; suitcase; trunk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to receive, then the Mandarin for schoolbag.

You'll want to know: xiāngzi.
xiāngzi — "box; suitcase; trunk".
zhège xiāngzi tài dà le — This suitcase is too big.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiāngzi is built from xiāng and the noun ending zi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, sjang and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as a box or a hamper.

xíngli — "luggage".
nǐ de xíngli zài nǎr — Where's your luggage?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xíngli is built from xíng 'to go' and lǐ 'plum', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeng and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the pair itself under the second, but how 'plum' came into a word for luggage is not settled.
