Tamil, chapter 98: Come, Eat, Drink.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) — congratulations, good wishes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for that's how it is, then the Tamil for why not.

You'll want to know: வாழ்த்துகள்.
வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) — "congratulations", "good wishes".
It is built on வாழ், "to live": to wish someone well is to wish them life.

The word, taken apart.
வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) is native Dravidian. DEDR 5372 lists Malayalam vāḻttuka, "to bless". It is vāḻttu, "a good wish", with the plural -kaḷ.

What you've built.
Wishes for someone's good news.

Lesson 2 of 5.
அழைப்பு (aḻaippu) — an invitation, a call.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for why not, then the Tamil for congratulations.

You'll want to know: அழைப்பு.
அழைப்பு (aḻaippu) — "an invitation", and "a call".
அழைப்புக்கு நன்றி — "thank you for the invitation", with the நன்றி you know.

The word, taken apart.
அழைப்பு (aḻaippu) is native Dravidian (DEDR 282). The entry lists aḻai, "to call", beside aḻu, "to cry": an invitation is "a calling".

What you've built.
An invitation, and thanks for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
பரிசு (paricu) — a gift, a prize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for congratulations, then the Tamil for an invitation.

You'll want to know: பரிசு.
பரிசு (paricu) — "a gift", and "a prize".
உங்களுக்கு ஒரு பரிசு (paricu) — "a gift for you".

The word, taken apart.
பரிசு (paricu) belongs with paricil, "gift, present", in DEDR 3970, beside Malayalam pariyam, "a token given to the bride". The entry notes a proposal to derive the family from Sanskrit sparśa, "touch"; that is not settled.

What you've built.
Something to bring.

Lesson 4 of 5.
வாருங்கள் (vāruṅkaḷ) — please come (in).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for an invitation, then the Tamil for a gift.

You'll want to know: வாருங்கள்.
வாருங்கள் (vāruṅkaḷ) — "please come", "come in".
It is the verb வா, "come", with the respectful -ங்கள். At the door, it is the welcome.

The word, taken apart.
வாருங்கள் (vāruṅkaḷ) is native Dravidian. DEDR 5270 lists Malayalam varuka. It is vā, "come", with the polite ending -uṅkaḷ.

What you've built.
A welcome at the door.

Lesson 5 of 5.
சாப்பிடுங்கள் (cāppiṭuṅkaḷ) — please eat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a gift, then the Tamil for please come in.

You'll want to know: சாப்பிடுங்கள்.
சாப்பிடுங்கள் (cāppiṭuṅkaḷ) — "please eat".
A host says it, and says it again. சாப்பிடு is Tamil's everyday verb for eating.

The word, taken apart.
சாப்பிடுங்கள் (cāppiṭuṅkaḷ) is sāppiḍu, "to eat", with the polite ending. As an earlier lesson showed, sāppiḍu is sāppu, "food", with iḍu, "to put" (DEDR 442).

What you've built.
Congratulations, an invitation, a gift, come in, please eat.
