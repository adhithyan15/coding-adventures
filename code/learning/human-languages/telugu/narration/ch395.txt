Telugu, chapter 395: Giving Reasons.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
కనుక (kanuka) — so; hence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to justify, then the Telugu for exact.

You'll want to know: కనుక.
కనుక — kanuka — "so; hence".
వర్షం పడింది, కనుక మేము బయటకు వెళ్ళలేదు. varṣaṁ paḍindi, kanuka mēmu bayaṭaku veḷḷalēdu. It rained, so we did not go out.
It opens the result, after the reason, as అందుకే does.

The word, taken apart.
కనుక (kanuka) is a Telugu connective of reason whose origin is uncertain; DEDR gives it no article of its own.

Lesson 2 of 5.
అందుచేత (anducēta) — for that reason; therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for exact, then the Telugu for so.

You'll want to know: అందుచేత.
అందుచేత — anducēta — "for that reason; therefore".
అతనికి జ్వరం వచ్చింది, అందుచేత పనికి రాలేదు. ataniki jvaraṁ vaccindi, anducēta paniki rālēdu. He had a fever, and for that reason he did not come to work.
అందు, in that, + చేత, by: by that. A cousin of అందువల్ల, which you know.

The word, taken apart.
అందుచేత (anducēta) is native Telugu: andu, "in that, there", built on the far-pointing base a- (DEDR 1), and cēta, "by", the ending that marks the means.

Lesson 3 of 5.
దృష్ట్యా (dṛṣṭyā) — in view of; considering.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for so, then the Telugu for for that reason.

You'll want to know: దృష్ట్యా.
దృష్ట్యా — dṛṣṭyā — "in view of; considering".
వర్షం దృష్ట్యా పాఠశాలకు సెలవు ఇచ్చారు. varṣaṁ dṛṣṭyā pāṭhaśālaku selavu iccāru. In view of the rain, they gave the school a holiday.
Formal; it follows a noun: వర్షం దృష్ట్యా (dṛṣṭyā), in view of the rain.

The word, taken apart.
దృష్ట్యా (dṛṣṭyā) is borrowed whole from Sanskrit dṛṣṭyā, "by the sight (of)", the instrumental case of dṛṣṭi, "seeing, sight" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
అనగా (anagā) — that is to say; namely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for for that reason, then the Telugu for in view of.

You'll want to know: అనగా.
అనగా — anagā — "that is to say; namely".
రేపు అనగా శుక్రవారం మాకు సెలవు. rēpu anagā śukravāraṁ māku selavu. Tomorrow, that is Friday, is a holiday for us.
A bookish cousin of అంటే; it explains the word before it.

The word, taken apart.
అనగా (anagā) is native Dravidian, a form of anu, "to say" (DEDR 868), the verb behind అని and అంటే.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ప్రాతిపదిక (prātipadika) — a footing; a basis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for in view of, then the Telugu for that is to say.

You'll want to know: ప్రాతిపదిక.
ప్రాతిపదిక — prātipadika — "a footing; a basis".
మార్కుల ప్రాతిపదికన బహుమతులు ఇస్తారు. mārkula prātipadikana bahumatulu istāru. Prizes are given on the basis of marks.
ప్రాతిపదికన, on the basis of, follows a noun in the genitive.

The word, taken apart.
ప్రాతిపదిక (prātipadika) is borrowed from Sanskrit prātipadika, a grammar word for "the crude form or base of a noun" (Monier-Williams); Telugu widened it to any basis or footing.
