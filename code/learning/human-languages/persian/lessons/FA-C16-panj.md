# پنج — five, and the word you have already met on a map

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-CHAHAR, FA-ETYMON-CHAHAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **chahâr**, and what three languages did with its first sound.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-PANJ]; assesses=[] -->

> **پنج** — *panj* — **five**

**پ** is Persian's own letter, one of the four the alphabet added for sounds
Arabic does not have.

## The word, taken apart — five rivers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-ETYMON-PANJ]; assesses=[FA-LEX-PANJ] -->

**پنج** continues Middle Persian **panj**, from Indo-European \**pénkʷe*,
"five." Greek **pénte**, Latin **quīnque** and English **five** are the same
word.

This one an English speaker has probably already met without knowing it.
**Punjab** is *panj* + *âb*, "five waters" — the land of the five rivers. So is
the fruit drink **punch**, named for its five ingredients. The number was in the
language before the lesson was.
