French, chapter 273: La veille et le lendemain.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la veille — the day before, the eve.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for until then, then the French for since then.

You'll want to know: la veille.
la veille — "the day before, the eve".
La veille de son examen, elle n'a pas dormi. The day before her exam, she did not sleep.
La veille au soir — the evening before. The verb veiller means to stay up.

The word, taken apart.
veille is inherited from Latin vigilia "wakefulness, a night watch" (EtymDB), built on vigil "awake": the night of waiting before a feast.

Lesson 2 of 5.
le lendemain — the next day, the day after.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for since then, then the French for the day before.

You'll want to know: le lendemain.
le lendemain — "the next day, the day after".
Le lendemain, il a téléphoné pour s'excuser. The next day, he phoned to apologise.
It looks forward from a day in the past; from today, say demain.

The word, taken apart.
lendemain grew out of l'endemain, built on demain, which is inherited from Latin dē māne "from the morning" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le surlendemain — two days later, the day after next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the day before, then the French for the next day.

You'll want to know: le surlendemain.
le surlendemain — "two days later, the day after next".
Il est arrivé le lundi et il est reparti le surlendemain. He arrived on the Monday and left again two days later.
Sur + lendemain: one day beyond the next day.

The word, taken apart.
surlendemain is built on lendemain with sur "over, beyond"; demain is inherited from Latin dē māne "from the morning" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
dernièrement — lately, recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the next day, then the French for two days later.

You'll want to know: dernièrement.
dernièrement — "lately, recently".
Dernièrement, je dors mal. Lately, I have been sleeping badly.
Close to récemment; it goes with the present or the passé composé.

The word, taken apart.
dernièrement is built on dernier "last", from Old French derrenier, usually traced to Latin dē retrō "from behind" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: prochainement.
prochainement — "soon, shortly".
Le magasin va ouvrir prochainement. The shop is going to open soon.
A word of announcements and posters: prochainement au cinéma.

The word, taken apart.
prochainement is built on prochain "next", inherited from Vulgar Latin propeānus, built on Latin prope "near" (Lewis & Short).
