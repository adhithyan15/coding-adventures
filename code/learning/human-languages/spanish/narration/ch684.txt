Spanish, chapter 684: Ciudad o campo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el forastero — the outsider, the stranger (from out of town).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the hamlet, then the Spanish for the mayor’s office.

You'll want to know: el forastero.
el forastero — "the outsider, the stranger (from out of town)".
En el pueblo todos saben quién es forastero. In the village everyone knows who is from out of town.
As an adjective: gente forastera. It is not quite extranjero, a foreigner.

The word, taken apart.
forastero was borrowed from Catalan foraster (EtymDB), built on fora "outside", from Latin forās "out of doors".

Lesson 4 of 5.

residir — "to reside, to live".
Residimos en la ciudad desde hace años. We have lived in the city for years.
A formal word for vivir, common on forms.

The word, taken apart.
residir is a learned borrowing from Latin residēre "to remain, to settle" (EtymDB), built on sedēre "to sit".

Lesson 5 of 5.
el vecindario — the neighbourhood; the residents.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for rural, then the Spanish for to reside.

You'll want to know: el vecindario.
el vecindario — "the neighbourhood; the residents".
El vecindario es muy tranquilo. The neighbourhood is very quiet.
Common in Latin America; Spain often says el barrio.

The word, taken apart.
vecindario is built on vecino "neighbour", inherited from Latin vīcīnus (EtymDB), from vīcus "a village, a street".
