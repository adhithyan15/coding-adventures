Mandarin Chinese, chapter 71: Tastes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
好喝 (hǎohē) — nice to drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dumpling, then the Mandarin for soup.

You'll want to know: 好喝.
好喝 — hǎohē — "nice to drink".
zhèr de chá hěn hǎohē — The tea here is lovely.

The word, taken apart.
好喝 (hǎohē) is built from 好 'good' and 喝 'to drink', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawX and xat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as 'to shout at', and the drinking sense is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 5 of 5.
diǎncài — to order food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for spicy, then the Mandarin for salty.

You'll want to know: diǎncài.
diǎncài — "to order food".
wǒ xiǎng diǎncài — I'd like to order.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
diǎncài is built from 点 'a dot, to mark' and cài 'dish', both inherited from Middle Chinese, temX and tshojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as any plant fit to eat. Marking the dishes you want is the likely picture, but the step is not settled.

What you've built.
