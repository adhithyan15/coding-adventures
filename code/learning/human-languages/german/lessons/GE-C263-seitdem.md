## You'll want to know: seitdem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-C263-NACHDEM-05]; assesses=[] -->

**seitdem** — "since then; since (joining two clauses)".

> Seitdem sie ein Auto hat, fährt sie nicht mehr mit dem Bus.
>
> *Since she has had a car, she no longer goes by bus.*

Two uses: an adverb, with the verb second (*Seitdem ist er glücklich*), and a conjunction, with the verb at the end. The present covers something still true.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*seitdem* joins *seit* "since" and *dem* "that". *Seit* comes from Middle High German *sīt* (EtymDB), usually traced to Old High German *sīd* "later, since", a cousin of English *since*.
