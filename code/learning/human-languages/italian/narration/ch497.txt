Italian, chapter 497: Crescere i figli.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
educare — to bring up, to educate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to rock, then the Italian for to crawl.

You'll want to know: educare.
educare — "to bring up, to educate".
I miei genitori mi hanno educato al rispetto. My parents brought me up to be respectful.
Mostly about manners and values; for school learning, say istruire.

The word, taken apart.
educare is a learned borrowing from Latin ēducāre "to bring up, to rear" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: viziato.
viziato — "spoilt".
È un bambino viziato: vuole tutto subito. He is a spoilt child: he wants everything at once.
The feminine is viziata.

The word, taken apart.
viziato is the past participle of viziare "to spoil", from Latin vitiāre (EtymDB), built on vitium "a fault".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rimproverare — to scold, to tell off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to spoil, then the Italian for spoilt.

You'll want to know: rimproverare.
rimproverare — "to scold, to tell off".
La maestra mi ha rimproverato perché parlavo in classe. The teacher told me off because I was talking in class.
Rimproverare qualcuno per qualcosa. The noun is il rimprovero.

The word, taken apart.
rimproverare is usually traced to Vulgar Latin reimproperāre, built on Late Latin improperāre "to taunt" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il castigo — punishment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for spoilt, then the Italian for to scold.

You'll want to know: il castigo.
il castigo — "punishment".
Per castigo stasera niente televisione. As a punishment, no television tonight.
Mettere in castigo — to punish (a child).

The word, taken apart.
castigo comes from castigare "to punish", a learned borrowing from Latin castīgāre "to correct, to chastise" (Lewis & Short), from castus "pure".
