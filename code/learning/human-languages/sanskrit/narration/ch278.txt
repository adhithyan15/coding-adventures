Sanskrit, chapter 278: What I Think, and Why.
6 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
मम मतेन (mama matena) — in my opinion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "an opinion". (matam.) And "my". (mama.) This chapter gives your opinion, and a reason for it.

You'll want to know: मम मतेन.
मम मतेन एतत् सुन्दरम्। mama matena etat sundaram. — "In my opinion this is beautiful."
मतेन (matena) is मतम् (matam), "an opinion", with -ena, "by": "by my opinion", that is, "in my opinion".
मम मतेन सः सत्यं वदति। mama matena saḥ satyaṃ vadati. — "In my opinion he is telling the truth."
तव मतेन किम्? tava matena kim? — "What is your opinion?"

You'll want to know: अहं मन्ये.
मन्ये (manye) is "I think". It ends in -e, the "I" ending of verbs like रोचते (rocate). यत् (yat), "that", opens what you think:
अहं मन्ये यत् वर्षा भविष्यति। ahaṃ manye yat varṣā bhaviṣyati. — "I think that it will rain."
Or say the thought first and close it with इति (iti), as you close what someone said:
वर्षा भविष्यति इति मन्ये। varṣā bhaviṣyati iti manye. — "It will rain, I think."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I think that he will go" — ahaṃ manye yat saḥ gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think that she will go" — ahaṃ manye yat sā gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think that they will go" — ahaṃ manye yat te gamiṣyanti]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that in your opinion this is beautiful, then that you think it will rain.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"In my opinion"? (मम मतेन (mama matena).) "I think that it will rain"? (अहं मन्ये यत् वर्षा भविष्यति.) Where does इति stand? (After the thought.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
यतः, कारणात् (yataḥ, kāraṇāt) — because, and because of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I think that it will rain". (ahaṃ manye yat varṣā bhaviṣyati.) Now give the reason for a choice.

You'll want to know: यतः.
यतः (yataḥ), "because", opens the reason, and the reason can follow what you did:
अहं न आगमिष्यामि, ahaṃ na āgamiṣyāmi, यतः अहं रुग्णः अस्मि। yataḥ ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi. — "I will not come, because I am ill."
सः न पठति, saḥ na paṭhati, यतः सः श्रान्तः अस्ति। yataḥ saḥ śrāntaḥ asti. — "He is not reading, because he is tired."
आगमिष्यामि (āgamiṣyāmi) is गमिष्यामि with the prefix आ- of आगच्छति: "I will come".

You'll want to know: कारणात्, हेतुः.
कारणात् (kāraṇāt) is कारणम् (kāraṇam), "a reason", with -āt, "from": "because of". The cause goes in front of it, joined to it as one word:
वर्षाकारणात् सः न आगतवान्। varṣākāraṇāt saḥ na āgatavān. — "Because of the rain he did not come."
हेतुः (hetuḥ), "a cause", names the reason as a thing:
हेतुः किम्? hetuḥ kim? — "What is the cause?"
हेतुः वर्षा अस्ति। hetuḥ varṣā asti. — "The cause is the rain."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I will not come, because I am tired" — ahaṃ na āgamiṣyāmi, yataḥ ahaṃ śrāntaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I will not come, because I am ill" — ahaṃ na āgamiṣyāmi, yataḥ ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I will not come, because I am afraid" — ahaṃ na āgamiṣyāmi, yataḥ ahaṃ bhītaḥ asmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Say that you will not come because you are ill, then that he did not come because of the rain.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Because" before a reason? (यतः.) "Because of the rain"? (वर्षाकारणात्.) "What is the cause?" (हेतुः किम्?)


Lesson 3 of 6.
मन्यते (manyate) — thinks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I think that it will rain". (ahaṃ manye yat varṣā bhaviṣyati.) You have said this verb's "I" form.

You'll want to know: मन्यते.
मन्यते — manyate — "thinks". मन्ये (manye) is its "I".
सः किं मन्यते? saḥ kiṃ manyate? — "What does he think?"
मीरा मन्यते यत् एतत् सत्यम्। mīrā manyate yat etat satyam. — "Mira thinks that this is true."

The word, taken apart.
मन्यते (manyate) is built on the root √man, "to think", which Monier-Williams lists in the fourth class with the middle ending -te. मतम् (matam), "an opinion", is its -ta form: what is thought. It lives on as Hindi mānnā, "to respect, obey" (CDIAL 9857).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: manyate]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What does Mira think?" — mīrā kiṃ manyate?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Arun thinks that this is true" — aruṇaḥ manyate yat etat satyam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does मन्यते (manyate) mean? ("Thinks".) Its "I" form? (मन्ये.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ) — firm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "what does he think?" (saḥ kiṃ manyate?)

You'll want to know: दृढः.
दृढः — dṛḍhaḥ — "firm". It agrees with its noun: दृढः for a masculine noun, दृढा (dṛḍhā) for a feminine one, दृढम् (dṛḍham) for a neuter one.
मम दृढः विश्वासः अस्ति। mama dṛḍhaḥ viśvāsaḥ asti. — "I have a firm belief."
गृहं दृढम् अस्ति। gṛhaṃ dṛḍham asti. — "The house is solid."
मम निर्णयः दृढः। mama nirṇayaḥ dṛḍhaḥ. — "My decision is firm."

The word, taken apart.
दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ) comes from दृढ (dṛḍha), "fixed, firm, strong", in Monier-Williams; CDIAL 6508 sets it under the root √dṛṃh and lists Hindi diṛh, "firm", among its descendants.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dṛḍhaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My decision is firm" — mama nirṇayaḥ dṛḍhaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "In my opinion the house is solid" — mama matena gṛhaṃ dṛḍham]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ) mean? ("Firm".) "A solid house"? (दृढं गृहम्.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
ढ (ḍha) — one character, met first in दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ढ and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: say दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ), "firm". Then say the sounds of ड and घ. (ḍa, gha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
One character this time: the one in the middle of दृढः (dṛḍhaḥ).

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ढ.
ढ — ḍha.
It is a consonant: ड (ḍa) with a breath after it, as घ is ग with a breath.
What it is made of:
a descending stem joined to a broad outer bowl and closed inner loop.
the top shirorekhā.
You have met it here:
दृढः dṛḍhaḥ — firm.

Writing: ढ.
1. start at the stem's headline junction, descend, turn left through the upper shoulder, sweep counterclockwise around the broad outer bowl, and continue counterclockwise around the closed inner loop without lifting.
2. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 1. The pen comes up 1 time and no more.
Verified two-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ढ (ḍha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
