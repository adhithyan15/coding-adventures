Arabic, chapter 323: Growing up and growing older.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نشأ (nashaʾa) — to be reared, to grow up; to arise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to besiege, then the Arabic for to defend.

You'll want to know: نشأ.
نشأ — nashaʾa — "to be reared, to grow up; to arise".
نشأت أمي في قرية صغيرة. nashaʾat ummī fī qarya ṣaghīra. My mother grew up in a small village.
Present: ينشأ, yanshaʾ.
Of a problem or an idea: to arise.

The word, taken apart.
نشأ (nashaʾa) is built on the native Arabic root n-sh-ʾ (Lane), "to come into being; to grow up"; nashʾa is upbringing.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ترعرع (taraʿraʿa) — to be brought up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to defend, then the Arabic for to be reared.

You'll want to know: ترعرع.
ترعرع — taraʿraʿa — "to be brought up".
ترعرع أبي قرب البحر. taraʿraʿa abī qurba al-baḥr. My father was brought up near the sea.
Present: يترعرع, yataraʿraʿ.
A literary word beside نشأ (nashaʾa).

The word, taken apart.
ترعرع (taraʿraʿa) is built on the native Arabic root r-ʿ-ʿ (Lane), whose doubled verb raʿraʿa is "to make a child grow". Taraʿraʿa, its reflexive, is to grow up.

Lesson 3 of 5.
مسقط رأس (masqaṭ raʾs) — a birthplace, a home town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be reared, then the Arabic for to be brought up.

You'll want to know: مسقط رأس.
مسقط رأس — masqaṭ raʾs — "a birthplace, a home town".
زرت مسقط رأس أبي في الصيف. zurtu masqaṭ raʾs abī fī aṣ-ṣayf. I visited my father’s home town in the summer.
Literally the place where the head fell: where one was born.

The word, taken apart.
مسقط رأس (masqaṭ raʾs) is "the place where the head fell": masqaṭ is built on the native Arabic root s-q-ṭ, "to fall" (Lane), and raʾs, a head, is an inherited Semitic word, beside Hebrew rōsh and Aramaic rēshā (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
صبا (ṣibā) — boyhood, early youth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be brought up, then the Arabic for a birthplace.

You'll want to know: صبا.
صبا — ṣibā — "boyhood, early youth".
قضى جدي صباه في الريف. qaḍā jaddī ṣibāhu fī ar-rīf. My grandfather spent his boyhood in the countryside.
A literary word; the east wind is also صبا (ṣibā), ṣabā.

The word, taken apart.
صبا (ṣibā) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-b-w (Lane), "to incline, to be youthful", the root of ṣabiyy, a boy. Ṣibā is the time of being one.

Lesson 5 of 5.
كهولة (kuhūla) — middle age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a birthplace, then the Arabic for boyhood.

You'll want to know: كهولة.
كهولة — kuhūla — "middle age".
بدأ أبي الرسم في كهولته. badaʾa abī ar-rasm fī kuhūlatihi. My father took up painting in middle age.
A man in middle age is كهل.

The word, taken apart.
كهولة (kuhūla) is built on the native Arabic root k-h-l (Lane), where kahl is a man past youth whose hair is turning grey. Kuhūla is that time of life.
