# The letters that wrote my name

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-03, AR-CONCEPT-W09-KHAYR-BIKHAYR-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Write **اسمي** and **خير** by hand, right to left, without looking
below. Both were drawn stroke by stroke in the writing set and neither has been
asked for since.

## Script: one bowl, four letters
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-01, AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-02, AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-03] -->

| letter | dots | where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| **ب** *bāʾ* | one below | inside **بخير** |
| **ن** *nūn* | one above | inside **أنت** |
| **ت** *tāʾ* | two above | inside **أنت**, **تشرفنا** |
| **ث** *thāʾ* | three above | — |

One shallow boat-bowl, drawn the same way every time. Only the dots change, and
the dots **are** the letter. Two of these four build the word for *you*: an
*alif* with a hamza seat, then *nūn*, then *tāʾ*.

## Script: the letter with three jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-01, AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-02, AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-03] -->

**ي** *yāʾ* is the same bowl body with **two dots below** — a tooth when joined.
It does three separate jobs, and you have used all three:

- consonant **y**, as in *yawm*, a day
- the long vowel **ī**, a seat for the *ee* sound
- the suffix **ـي**, glued on to mean **my**

That third job is what turns **اسم** into **اسمي**. Four letters, right to left:
*alif · sīn · mīm · yāʾ*.

## Script: the marks everyday text leaves off
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W06-HARAKAT-01, AR-CONCEPT-W06-HARAKAT-02, AR-CONCEPT-W06-HAMZA-01, AR-CONCEPT-W06-HAMZA-02] -->

The **ḥarakāt** are three small marks written around a consonant, not letters of
their own: *fatḥa* above for *a*, *kasra* below for *i*, *ḍamma* above for *u*.
Arabic is an abjad, so ordinary text leaves them out — and one of them carries
the whole difference between **أنتَ** and **أنتِ**.

**Hamza** is not one of them. It writes a real consonant: the glottal catch in
the middle of English *uh-oh*. Written alone it is **ء**; riding an *alif* seat
it is **أ**, which is how the word for *you* begins.

## Script: خير, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W07-HOOK-FAMILY-HA-KHA-02, AR-CONCEPT-W09-KHAYR-BIKHAYR-01, AR-CONCEPT-W09-KHAYR-BIKHAYR-02] -->

**خ** *khāʾ* belongs to the hook-and-tail family — a rounded hook flowing into a
tail that drops below the line, with **one dot above**. Its plain cousin **ح** has
no dot and **ج** takes one below.

- [YOU WRITE: **خير** right to left — *khāʾ* with its dot, into *yāʾ*, then *rā*, and lift]
- [YOU WRITE: glue **بـ** on the front — **بخير**, its one dot below, joining straight into the *khāʾ*]
- [YOU WRITE: **اسمي** — *alif · sīn · mīm · yāʾ*]

Nothing joins after **ر**, which is why the pen lifts there.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W04-DOTS-FAMILY-01, AR-CONCEPT-W05-YA-02, AR-CONCEPT-W06-HAMZA-01, AR-CONCEPT-W09-KHAYR-BIKHAYR-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Name the one feature that separates **ب ن ت ث**. (**The dots** — one
below, one above, two above, three above.) Give *yāʾ*'s three jobs. (**Consonant
y, long vowel ī, the suffix meaning my**.) Say whether hamza is a vowel mark.
(**No** — a glottal stop.) And say what **بـ** adds to **خير**. (***In*** — in
goodness.)
