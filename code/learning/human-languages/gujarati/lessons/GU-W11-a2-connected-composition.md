# A connected message about familiar life

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUNNAAMCHHE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-HUNGUJARATIBOLUNCHHUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-RAHEVUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A connected message is not a pile of vocabulary and not one long
sentence. It is a short route for one reader. Before writing, draw eight empty
boxes and give them these jobs:

1. reader and greeting;
2. reason for writing;
3. your name and where you live;
4. a language you speak and work or activity you do;
5. one more familiar detail;
6. one preference and one reason;
7. one direct question for the reader;
8. a close.

Write only one or two English cue words in each box. Do not draft Gujarati yet.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C02-MAARUNNAAMCHHE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-HUNGUJARATIBOLUNCHHUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-RAHEVUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-KAAMKARVUN-01, GU-LEX-GAMVU, GU-LEX-PAANI, GU-Q-KYAAN-01, GU-JOIN-KEMKE-01, GU-JOIN-PAN-ALSO-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

There is no model, sentence bank, or copyable answer on this page.

**Reader:** your friend Kiran. **Purpose:** help Kiran understand your familiar
life and choose an activity you could do together.

Write **70–90 words in Gujarati using Gujarati script**. Use every planning
box. Include your name, where you live, a language, at least two familiar
actions or activities, one preference, one reason, and one direct question for
Kiran. Make every detail help with the shared activity. The instructions give
content, not sentences: choose only words and forms this book has taught, and
connect them yourself. If you cannot form a line honestly, make it simpler;
do not borrow a Hindi form to fill the gap.

This first connected attempt is untimed. Put away romanization, dictionaries,
translators, generative assistants, and spell-checkers. Do not copy from an
earlier lesson. Ordinary legible Gujarati handwriting is enough.

When the whole message exists, count its words once. If it is short, add one
useful taught detail to a planning box. If it is long, remove a detail that does
not help Kiran. Do not replace the message with a memorised model.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-GAMVU, GU-Q-KYAAN-01, GU-JOIN-KEMKE-01, GU-JOIN-PAN-ALSO-01] -->

Review the same attempt in four separate passes:

1. **Task fulfilment and relevant content** — Kiran, the familiar-life purpose,
   shared activity, and every requested content point are present.
2. **Comprehensibility and organisation** — the eight jobs form a route rather
   than a list of unrelated words or lines.
3. **Gujarati vocabulary and grammar** — every form comes from the taught
   Gujarati track; a familiar shorter line is better than an invented form.
4. **Orthographic control** — Gujarati letters, vowel signs, word boundaries,
   and punctuation remain readable.

[PAUSE 2s] Mark one strength and one next step. Repair only the next step; keep
the original attempt so the next 70–90 word message can show real growth.
