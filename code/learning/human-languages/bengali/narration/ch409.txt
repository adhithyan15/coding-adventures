Bengali, chapter 409: Areas and Localities.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
এলাকা (elākā) — an area; a locality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a security deposit, then the Bengali for to reside.

You'll want to know: এলাকা.
এলাকা — elākā — "an area; a locality".
এই এলাকায় অনেক দোকান আছে। ei elākāy ônek dokān āchhe. There are many shops in this area.

The word, taken apart.
এলাকা (elākā) is usually traced to Arabic ʿalāqa, "a connection, a holding", which in Persian came to mean a district.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to reside, then the Bengali for an area.

You'll want to know: বস্তি.
বস্তি — bôsti — "a slum; a poor settlement".
স্টেশনের পাশে একটা বস্তি আছে। sṭeshôner pāshe ekṭā bôsti āchhe. There is a slum beside the station.

The word, taken apart.
বস্তি (bôsti) probably goes back to Sanskrit vasati, "a dwelling, a staying" (Monier-Williams); in Bengali the word has narrowed to a crowded settlement of the poor.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an area, then the Bengali for a slum.

You'll want to know: কলোনি.
কলোনি — kôloni — "a housing colony; an estate".
মীরার মামা রেল কলোনিতে থাকেন। mīrār māmā rel kôlonite thāken. Mira's uncle lives in the railway colony.

The word, taken apart.
কলোনি (kôloni) is borrowed from English colony, used in South Asia for a planned group of houses, often for one employer's staff.

Lesson 5 of 5.
আড্ডা দেওয়া (āḍḍā deoyā) — to chat in a group; to hang out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a housing colony, then the Bengali for a resident.

You'll want to know: আড্ডা দেওয়া.
আড্ডা দেওয়া — āḍḍā deoyā — "to chat in a group; to hang out".
সন্ধ্যায় পাড়ার ছেলেরা চায়ের দোকানে আড্ডা দেয়। sôndhyāy pāṛār chhelerā chāyer dokāne āḍḍā dey. In the evenings the local young men hang out at the tea stall.
Past (he/she …): তিনি বন্ধুদের সঙ্গে আড্ডা দিলেন, tini bôndhuder sôngge āḍḍā dilen.
আড্ডা alone is the gathering itself, a well-loved Bengali habit: long, easy talk over tea about everything and nothing.

The word, taken apart.
আড্ডা দেওয়া (āḍḍā deoyā) joins আড্ডা, "a gathering place, a long chat", to দেওয়া, to give; the origin of আড্ডা is not settled.
