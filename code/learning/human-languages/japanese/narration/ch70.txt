You'll want to know: もち.
もち — mochi — "a rice cake".

The word, taken apart.
もち (mochi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. It is usually written in kana; its kanji, when used, is a kun'yomi attached later (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
なっとう (nattou) — natto, fermented soybeans.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the summer holidays, then the Japanese for a rice cake.

You'll want to know: なっとう.
なっとう — nattou — "natto, fermented soybeans".

The word, taken apart.
なっとう (nattou) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nō, "receive, store" (Middle Chinese nop) and tō, "bean" (duwH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart). Nō is shortened to nat before tō.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ちくわ (chikuwa) — a fish-paste tube.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a rice cake, then the Japanese for natto.

You'll want to know: ちくわ.
ちくわ — chikuwa — "a fish-paste tube".

The word, taken apart.
ちくわ (chikuwa) mixes the two layers: chiku, "bamboo", the on'yomi of its first character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese trjuwk), and native wa, "ring", read as a kun'yomi: a bamboo ring, from its shape (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
