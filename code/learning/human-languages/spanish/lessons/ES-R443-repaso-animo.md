## Grammar Lens: five new words, no new grammar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-01, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03] -->

This chapter taught nothing you did not already have. It handed five words to
two machines you owned.

| the machine | where you got it | the new words |
|---|---|---|
| **the thing is the subject** | *gustar* | doler, interesar |
| **the reflexive** | *equivocarse* | acercarse, concentrarse, arrepentirse |

*Me duele la cabeza* — the head hurts, and the verb agrees with the head. *Me
interesan las clases* — plural thing, plural verb. If you can catch yourself
about to say *yo duelo*, the machine is working.

That is the whole point of building on what came before, and it is worth
noticing when it happens: the fifth verb on a pattern costs almost nothing,
where the first one cost a lesson.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-02, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-05, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-04] -->

**You can now say what is going on inside you** — what hurts, what you care
about, what you are struggling to do and what you wish you had not done. A
reading paper asks that constantly, because most short texts about people are
about exactly this.

You also have the sharpest case yet of a word that misleads. **Arrepentirse**
looks like it is made of *sentir* and behaves like it, and is not related to it
at all. A resemblance is not evidence, and the check is whether the piece you
think you see is a word in its own right that leaves something standing behind it.
