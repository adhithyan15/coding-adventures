Urdu, chapter 99: The Letters Ze and Toe.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ز (ze) — the letter that ends روز.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ز and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ظ and ئ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say روز (roz), "every day". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ز.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
ز — ze, the sound z.
It ends روز (roz), "every day". You already write ر, the short curve that drops below the line. ز (ze) is the same curve with one dot above it. Like ر, it never joins the letter after it.

Writing: ز.
1. start here: draw the independent ze downward.
2. continue curving to the left without lifting.
3. after one lift, place the dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Khe, ze, zāl, swād, and zwād, independent ز calligraphic and handwriting animations and Ze instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at روز and point at ز (ze) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ز (ze) three times, saying z as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ئ, then ز (ze), from memory]
[once you have stopped driving — read: روز aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ز? (ze, z.) Which word did it come from? (روز, roz, "every day".)


Lesson 2 of 4.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ئ and ز (ze) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say طرف (taraf), "a side, a direction". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ط.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
ط — toe, a plain t sound, the same as ت.
It opens طرف (taraf), "a side, a direction". You already write ظ: a flat loop on the line with a tall upright stroke. ط (toe) is that shape with no dot. Urdu keeps it for words that came from Arabic.

Writing: ط.
1. start here: draw the independent to'e-series loop.
2. continue through its leftward finish without lifting.
3. after one lift, draw the tall upright — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, To’e, zo’e, se, zhe, and ġhain, independent ط (toe) handwriting animation and To’e instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

[once you have stopped driving — look: at طرف and point at ط (toe) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ط (toe) three times, saying t as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ز (ze), then ط (toe), from memory]
[once you have stopped driving — read: طرف aloud once more]

Lesson 3 of 4.
(two letters) (do harf) — From the letter to the word — a first pass.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[pause 3 seconds]
Say روز (roz), then write the letter it gave you.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
letter: ز. name: ze. the word it came from: روز roz.
letter: ط. name: toe. the word it came from: طرف taraf.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[once you have stopped driving — write: each letter in the table from memory, then check it]
[your turn — say: the word each one came from]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — read: each word in the table aloud, and point at its letter]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word did each letter come from? (ز (ze) from روز; ط (toe) from طرف.)
