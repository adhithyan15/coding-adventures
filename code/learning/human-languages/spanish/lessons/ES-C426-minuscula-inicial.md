# la minúscula inicial — the capital that depends on what stands beside it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the principle from the last lesson: a name takes a capital, a category
does not. This lesson gives you a case where the same word takes a capital in
one sentence and not in another.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C426-02]; assesses=[] -->

Many Spanish surnames contain a small word: **de**, **del**, **la**, **las**.
Whether it is capitalised depends on **what stands in front of it**:

| written | form |
|---|---|
| with the first name | Pedro **de** la Cruz |
| without it | el señor **De** la Cruz |

With the forename present, the small word is lower-case. Standing alone at the
head of the name, it takes the capital.

## Grammar Lens: why the rule has that shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-02, ES-ORTH-C426-01] -->

It is not arbitrary. **De** originally meant *from* — *Pedro de la Cruz* was
Pedro **from** the Cross, a description of where he came from rather than a
name. A description does not take a capital, which is the rule from the last
lesson exactly.

Once the forename is gone, *De la Cruz* has become the whole name, and a name
takes a capital.

This appears on forms. A field asking for *apellidos* alone is the second row;
a field with *nombre y apellidos* together is the first.
