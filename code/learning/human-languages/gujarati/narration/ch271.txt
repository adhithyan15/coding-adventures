Gujarati, chapter 271: Polite or selfish.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
નમ્ર (namra) — polite, humble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for funny, then the Gujarati for arrogant.

You'll want to know: નમ્ર.
નમ્ર — namra — "polite, humble".
શું તમારો દીકરો નમ્ર છે? — śũ tamāro dīkro namra che? — "Is your son polite?"

The word, taken apart.
નમ્ર (namra) is Sanskrit namrá, "bent, submissive", taken over whole as a tatsama (CDIAL 6964).

મારો દોસ્ત સ્વાર્થી નથી. — māro dost svārthī nathī — "My pal is not selfish."

સ્વાર્થી (svārthī) is built on Sanskrit svārtha, "one's own aim", from sva, "own", and ártha, "aim" (CDIAL 638), with -ī; a learned word, a tatsama in shape.

Lesson 5 of 5.
જિદ્દી (jiddī) — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for selfish, then the Gujarati for foolish.

You'll want to know: જિદ્દી.
જિદ્દી — jiddī — "stubborn".
મારો ભત્રીજો જિદ્દી છે, તે સાંભળતો નથી. — māro bhatrījo jiddī che, te sā̃bhaḷto nathī — "My nephew is stubborn; he does not listen."

The word, taken apart.
જિદ્દી (jiddī) is built on jid, "obstinacy", with -ī. Jid is usually traced to Arabic ḍidd, "an opposite", through Persian; Lane gives the root ḍ-d-d, "to overcome in contention" (Lane).
