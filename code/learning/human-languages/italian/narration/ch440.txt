Italian, chapter 440: Chiedere la strada.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il navigatore — the satnav; the navigator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the street plan, then the Italian for the compass.

You'll want to know: il navigatore.
il navigatore — "the satnav; the navigator".
Il navigatore dice di girare a sinistra. The satnav says to turn left.
In full il navigatore satellitare; also a seafarer.

The word, taken apart.
navigatore is a learned borrowing from Latin nāvigātor 'sailor', from nāvigāre 'to sail', built on nāvis 'ship' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
svoltare — to turn (into a street).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the satnav, then the Italian for the direction.

You'll want to know: svoltare.
svoltare — "to turn (into a street)".
Svolta a destra dopo la farmacia. Turn right after the chemist's.
La svolta — the turn, and a turning point.

The word, taken apart.
svoltare is built from s- and voltare 'to turn', which is inherited from Vulgar Latin volvitāre, built on volvere 'to roll' (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: proseguire.
proseguire — "to carry on, to continue".
Prosegua dritto per cento metri. Carry on straight for a hundred metres.
Buon proseguimento! — enjoy the rest of your trip.

The word, taken apart.
proseguire is inherited from Latin prōsequī 'to follow on' (EtymDB), built on sequī 'to follow', the verb behind seguire.

Lesson 5 of 5.
imboccare — to take (a road), to turn into.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to turn, then the Italian for to carry on.

You'll want to know: imboccare.
imboccare — "to take (a road), to turn into".
Imbocchiamo la prima strada a sinistra. We take the first road on the left.
Literally to put into the mouth: it also means to spoon-feed a child.

The word, taken apart.
imboccare is built from in- and bocca 'mouth', which is inherited from Latin bucca 'cheek' (EtymDB): going into the mouth of a street.
