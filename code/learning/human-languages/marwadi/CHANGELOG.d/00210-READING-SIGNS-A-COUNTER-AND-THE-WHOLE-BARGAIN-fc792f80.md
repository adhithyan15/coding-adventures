## Reading — signs, a counter, and the whole bargain

Chapter 40 is Marwadi's reading rung, and the first connected Devanagari the
track has asked anyone to read rather than to write.

Three lessons, ramping 6 -> 16 -> 23 tokens: single-word signboards
(**चाय**, **रोटी**, **कपड़ा**, **रिक्शा**, **पैसा**, **भाव**), then the six
counter lines the track has practised aloud since chapter 13, then those same
lines read in order as one bargain.

- **No new word in any of the three.** Every Devanagari token was checked to
  occur in a lesson with a lower sequence number, so the claim that nothing is
  new is verified rather than asserted.
- The track had already *written* all six signs. What it had never done was ask
  anyone to read one off a board -- a different skill, because writing gives you
  the letters in order and all the time you want, and a signboard gives you one
  look.
- The passage is deliberately the market exchange rather than an invented
  paragraph. Marwadi is still mid-script, and a 78-word narrative like Hindi's
  would have run past the glyphs the track has taught. The bargain is what this
  learner can actually read, and the price moving बीस -> दस -> पंदरा carries the
  whole negotiation on three words learned as counting.
- Three lessons rather than one, because an atom introduced once and never
  revisited revokes the track's level on the reinforcement criterion.
