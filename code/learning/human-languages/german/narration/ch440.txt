German, chapter 440: Auf der Straße.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Bürgersteig — the pavement (US: sidewalk).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the police station, then the German for the barracks.

You'll want to know: der Bürgersteig.
der Bürgersteig — "the pavement (US: sidewalk)".
Die Kinder fahren mit dem Roller auf dem Bürgersteig. The children ride their scooters on the pavement.
Plural: die Bürgersteige. Also der Gehweg; in Switzerland das Trottoir.

The word, taken apart.
Bürgersteig is built from Bürger 'townsman, citizen', from Burg 'castle, town', and Steig 'path', inherited from Old High German stīg (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Parkbank — the park bench.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the barracks, then the German for the pavement.

You'll want to know: die Parkbank.
die Parkbank — "the park bench".
Ein alter Mann füttert auf der Parkbank die Tauben. An old man feeds the pigeons on the park bench.
Plural: die Parkbänke. Die Bank is a bench (plural Bänke) or a bank (plural Banken).

The word, taken apart.
Parkbank is built from Park, which EtymDB traces to Old French parc 'enclosure, pen', and Bank 'bench', inherited from Old High German bank (EtymDB). The money Bank came back into German from Italian banca, a money-changer's counter.

die Laterne — "the street lamp; the lantern".
Die Laternen gehen um sechs Uhr an. The street lamps come on at six o'clock.
Plural: die Laternen. Also a paper lantern.

The word, taken apart.
Laterne was borrowed from Latin lanterna 'lamp, lantern', also spelled lāterna (Lewis & Short). EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Kopfsteinpflaster — the cobbles, the cobblestone paving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the park bench, then the German for the street lamp.

You'll want to know: das Kopfsteinpflaster.
das Kopfsteinpflaster — "the cobbles, the cobblestone paving".
Auf dem Kopfsteinpflaster ist das Fahrrad sehr laut. The bike is very noisy on the cobbles.
No plural. Der Kopfstein is one stone, round like a head.

The word, taken apart.
Kopfsteinpflaster is built from Kopf 'head', Stein 'stone', inherited from Proto-Germanic stainaz (EtymDB), and Pflaster 'paving', which goes back to Latin emplastrum 'plaster' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Brunnen — the fountain; the well.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the street lamp, then the German for the cobbles.

You'll want to know: der Brunnen.
der Brunnen — "the fountain; the well".
Auf dem Marktplatz steht ein alter Brunnen. There is an old fountain in the market square.
Same form in the plural. Der Springbrunnen — a fountain that sprays.

The word, taken apart.
Brunnen is inherited through Middle High German brunne from Old High German brunno 'spring, well' and Proto-Germanic brunnô (EtymDB). Scots burn 'stream' is a cousin.
