Mandarin Chinese, chapter 219: Cloudy Tomorrow.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
云 (yún) — cloud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dry air, then the Mandarin for damp.

You'll want to know: 云.
云 — yún — "cloud".
明天下午会有云 míngtiān xiàwǔ huì yǒu yún — There'll be some cloud tomorrow afternoon.
The new character, in pieces: 云 (yún) — four strokes: two bars, then a stroke that falls to the left and turns right along the foot, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
云 (yún) is inherited from Middle Chinese hjun (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains it, after an older book, as the vapour of mountains and rivers. The dictionary writes the word for cloud with 雨 on top, and lists 云 as the older way of writing it.

Script — two bars, then a turn and a dot.
云 (yún).
Two pieces, top before bottom.
On top, 二, two strokes: a short bar, then a longer one under it.
Under it, two strokes. A stroke falling to the left that turns and runs right along the foot. Then a dot at the right, above the end of that stroke, falling to the right.
2 + 2 = four.

Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 云 (yún)? (Four.) What is on top? (二, two bars.) What does the third stroke do? (It falls to the left and turns right.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
多云 (duōyún) — cloudy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for damp, then the Mandarin for a cloud.

You'll want to know: 多云.
多云 — duōyún — "cloudy".
明天多云风不大 míngtiān duōyún fēng bú dà — Tomorrow will be cloudy, without much wind.

The word, taken apart.
多云 (duōyún) is built from 多 'many' and 云 (yún) 'cloud', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ta and hjun (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
jiàngwēn — to turn colder; to cool down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a cloud, then the Mandarin for cloudy.

You'll want to know: jiàngwēn.
jiàngwēn — "to turn colder; to cool down".
hòutiān huì jiàngwēn — It's going to turn colder the day after tomorrow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiàngwēn is built from jiàng 'to come down' and wēn 'warm', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaewngH and 'won (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'down' and 'to fall', and the second, among other senses, 'mild' and 'warm'.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fàngqíng is built from fàng 'to let go' and qíng 'clear'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese pjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to drive out'; the dictionary glosses the second, dzjeng there, 'a clear sky'.
