Urdu, chapter 438: Beaks and Nests.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
چونچ (choṅch) — a beak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for moss, then the Urdu for to come up (of plants).

You'll want to know: چونچ.
چونچ — choṅch — "a beak".
پرندے کی چونچ میں ایک تنکا ہے۔ parinde kī choṅch meṅ ek tinkā hai. The bird has a straw in its beak.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
چونچ (choṅch) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin cõc, "a beak", under Sanskrit cañcu (CDIAL 4569).

Lesson 2 of 5.
پنکھ (pankh) — a wing; a feather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to come up (of plants), then the Urdu for a beak.

You'll want to know: پنکھ.
پنکھ — pankh — "a wing; a feather".
طوطے کے پنکھ ہرے ہیں۔ tote ke pankh hare haiṅ. The parrot's feathers are green.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
پنکھ (pankh) is inherited from Sanskrit pakṣá, "a wing, a feather", under which CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin pā̃kh, "a wing" (CDIAL 7627); پنکھا, "a fan", comes from the same word.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a beak, then the Urdu for a wing.

چڑیا نے درخت پر آشیانہ بنایا۔ chiṛyā ne darakht par āshiyānā banāyā. The sparrow built a nest in the tree.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
آشیانہ (āshiyānā) is usually traced to Persian āshiyāna, "a nest"; poets also use it for a home.

You'll want to know: چگنا.
چگنا — chugnā — "to peck (at); to pick up (grain)".
کبوتر زمین سے چاول چگتے ہیں۔ kabūtar zamīn se chāval chugte haiṅ. The pigeons peck rice off the ground.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے چاول چگے, us ne chāval chuge.

The word, taken apart.
چگنا (chugnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin cugnā, "to peck, to pick up", under cugyati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 4852).

Lesson 5 of 5.
شکار (shikār) — prey; hunting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a nest, then the Urdu for to peck (at).

You'll want to know: شکار.
شکار — shikār — "prey; hunting".
بلی کا شکار ایک چوہا تھا۔ billī kā shikār ek chūhā thā. The cat's prey was a mouse.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
شکار (shikār) is usually traced to Persian shikār, "hunting, the chase"; in Urdu it is both the hunt and the animal hunted.
