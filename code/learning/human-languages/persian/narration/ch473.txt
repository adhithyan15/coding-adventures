Persian, chapter 473: The forecast.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
آب و هوا (âb-o havâ) — the climate; the weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a volcano, then the Persian for a habitat.

You'll want to know: آب و هوا.
آب و هوا (âb-o havâ) — "the climate; the weather".
آب و هوای شمال مرطوب است. Âb-o havâ-ye shomâl martub ast. The climate in the north is humid.
Literally 'water and air'.

The word, taken apart.
âb-o havâ is listed in EtymDB as 'water and air': native âb 'water', from Middle Persian āb, joined with havâ 'air', borrowed from Arabic hawāʾ.

هواشناسی (havâshenâsi) برای فردا باران اعلام کرد. Havâshenâsi barâ-ye fardâ bârân e'lâm kard. The weather service forecast rain for tomorrow.
havâ 'air' + shenâsi 'knowing, the study of'.

The word, taken apart.
havâshenâsi is built from havâ 'air', borrowed from Arabic hawāʾ (EtymDB), and native shenâsi 'knowing, the study of', from shenâkhtan 'to know'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
تغییر کردن (taghyir kardan) — to change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for the weather service, then the Persian for a degree.

You'll want to know: تغییر کردن.
تغییر کردن (taghyir kardan) — "to change".
هوا ناگهان تغییر کرد. Havâ nâgahân taghyir kard. The weather suddenly changed.
Intransitive: the thing changes. Taghyir dâdan is to change something.

The word, taken apart.
taghyir kardan is built from taghyir 'change', borrowed from Arabic taġyīr (EtymDB), and kardan 'to do', inherited from Middle Persian kardan (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
زیر صفر (zir-e sefr) — below zero (of temperature).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a degree, then the Persian for to change.

You'll want to know: زیر صفر.
زیر صفر (zir-e sefr) — "below zero (of temperature)".
دیشب دما ده درجه زیر صفر بود. Dishab damâ dah daraje zir-e sefr bud. Last night it was ten degrees below zero.
zir 'under' + sefr 'zero'.

The word, taken apart.
zir-e sefr is built from native zir 'under' and sefr 'zero', borrowed from Arabic ṣifr 'nothing, cipher' (EtymDB). EtymDB traces English zero to the same Arabic word.
