# ആശുപത്രി (āśupatri) — a hospital

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C48-ROLE-03, ML-LEX-C107-MEDICINE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *a doctor*. Say *medicine*. You have had the person and the
substance for some time. You have never had the building they are both in.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C112-HOSPITAL-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C48-ROLE-03, ML-LEX-C107-MEDICINE-01] -->

**ആശുപത്രി** (*āśupatri*) — **a hospital**.

The doctor, the medicine, the building. The set is complete now.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C112-HOSPITAL-01, ML-LEX-C112-POLICE-01] -->

Sound this one out and notice what does **not** happen.

The last two words handed themselves to you. You met **ബാങ്ക്**, heard English
*bank*, and were done. **പോലീസ്** did the same. Your English ear did the work.

**ആശുപത്രി** will not do that for you. Whatever its history, the word that
reaches you today has Malayalam's own shape and gives your English ear nothing
to catch. This lesson is not going to tell you where it came from, because that
question has more than one answer in circulation and a guess dressed up as a
fact would be worth less to you than the honest gap.

What matters for reading is the part that is certain: some words you can decode
from English and some you cannot, and you will not know which until you try. So
try first, and learn the word properly when the ear comes back empty.
