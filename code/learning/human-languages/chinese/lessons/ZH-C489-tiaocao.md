## You'll want to know: tiàocáo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C489-STAFF489-01]; assesses=[] -->

*tiàocáo* — "to move to another employer; to change jobs".

> *gōngzuò yālì tài dà suǒyǐ tā tiàocáo le* — The pressure at work was too much, so he moved to another firm.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*tiàocáo* is built from *tiào* 'to jump' and *cáo* 'a trough', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *dew* and *dzaw* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to leap' and the second 'a horse's trough'. A horse leaving one trough for the next is the picture usually given, but the dictionary does not give the sense.
