# no ... nada — the double negative — where Spanish stacks what English forbids

## Grammar lens: the double negative — where Spanish stacks what English forbids
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-C267-NONADA-01]; assesses=[] -->

English teachers spend years stamping out *I don't see nothing*. Spanish
requires it.

*No veo* **nada** — literally *I don't see nothing*, and it means **I see
nothing**.

The rule: when a negative word comes AFTER the verb, *no* still has to sit in
front of it. Both halves stay.

> *No* veo **nada**.  ·  *No* hablo con **nadie**.  ·  *No* voy **nunca**.

Move the negative word to the front and the *no* disappears, because the sentence
already has its negation:

> **Nada** veo.  ·  **Nadie** habla.  ·  **Nunca** voy.

Both are correct. The first is what people say.
