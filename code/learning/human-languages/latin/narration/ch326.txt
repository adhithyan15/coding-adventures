5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
adulēscentia, adulēscentiae — young adulthood, youth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an extra family name, then the Latin for a guardian.

You'll want to know: adulēscentia, adulēscentiae.
adulēscentia, adulēscentiae — "young adulthood, youth".
In adulēscentiā multa et stulta fēcī. In my youth I did many foolish things.
Feminine, from adulēscēns (taught): roughly from fifteen to thirty. English adolescence.

The word, taken apart.
adulēscentia is built on adulēscēns, the participle of adulēscō, "to grow up" (Lewis & Short). English adolescence comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
iuventūs, iuventūtis — youth; young people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a guardian, then the Latin for young adulthood.

You'll want to know: iuventūs, iuventūtis.
iuventūs, iuventūtis — "youth; young people".
Tōta iuventūs urbis in campō lūdēbat. All the young people of the city were playing on the field.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from iuvenis, 'young'. Both the time of youth and the young people themselves.

The word, taken apart.
iuventūs is built on iuvenis, "young" (EtymDB), which is inherited and a cousin of English young. English juvenile comes from iuvenis.

Lesson 3 of 5.
senectūs, senectūtis — old age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for young adulthood, then the Latin for youth.

You'll want to know: senectūs, senectūtis.
senectūs, senectūtis — "old age".
Avus meus in senectūte in vīllā habitābat. In his old age my grandfather lived at the villa.
Feminine, 3rd declension, from senex (taught). Cicero wrote a whole dialogue in its praise.

The word, taken apart.
senectūs is built on senex, "old man" (Lewis & Short). English senior and senile come from the same family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
orbus, orba, orbum — orphaned; childless, bereft.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for youth, then the Latin for old age.

You'll want to know: orbus, orba, orbum.
orbus, orba, orbum — "orphaned; childless, bereft".
Puella orba ab avunculō ēducāta est. The orphaned girl was brought up by her uncle.
Bereft of parents or of children; with the ablative, bereft of anything.

The word, taken apart.
orbus is an inherited word that Lewis & Short compare with Greek orphanos, "orphan", the source of English orphan.

Lesson 5 of 5.
pūpillus, pūpillī — a ward, an orphan boy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for old age, then the Latin for orphaned.

You'll want to know: pūpillus, pūpillī.
pūpillus, pūpillī — "a ward, an orphan boy".
Tūtor pecūniam pūpillī fidēliter servābat. The guardian faithfully kept the ward's money safe.
Masculine. The girl is a pūpilla, also 'the pupil of the eye'. English pupil.

The word, taken apart.
pūpillus is a diminutive of pūpulus, "little child" (Lewis & Short): a little child in the care of another. English pupil comes from it through French.
