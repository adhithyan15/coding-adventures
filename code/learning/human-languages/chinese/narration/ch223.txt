6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
dòngwù — animal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sneeze, then the Mandarin for miserable.

You'll want to know: dòngwù.
dòngwù — "animal".
nǐ xǐhuan shénme dòngwù — What animals do you like?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dòngwù is built from dòng 'to move' and wù 'thing', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwngX and mjut (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to shake' and the second 'the ten thousand things'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
xióngmāo — a panda; giant panda.

You'll want to know: xióngmāo.
xióngmāo — "a panda; giant panda".
xióngmāo hěn kě'ài — Pandas are so cute.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xióngmāo is built from xióng 'bear' and māo 'cat', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwng and mjew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which lists the first as a beast like a pig and the second as the animal that catches mice; the level tone of māo is not the regular outcome of that form.

Lesson 3 of 6.
lǎohǔ — tiger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an animal, then the Mandarin for a panda.

You'll want to know: lǎohǔ.
lǎohǔ — "tiger".
wǒ mèimei pà lǎohǔ — My little sister is scared of tigers.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: lǎo and hǔ are both third tones, so the first rises and it sounds like láohǔ.

The word, taken apart.
lǎohǔ is built from lǎo 'old' and hǔ 'tiger', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lawX and xuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which calls the second, after an older book, the lord of the mountain beasts. Here the first is a prefix with no sense of age; how it came to sit there is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hóuzi is built from hóu 'monkey' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, huw and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first with a word for the macaque.

Lesson 5 of 6.
tùzi — rabbit; hare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a tiger, then the Mandarin for a monkey.

You'll want to know: tùzi.
tùzi — "rabbit; hare".
wǒ jiā yǒu liǎng zhī tùzi — We've got two rabbits at home.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tùzi is built from a word for the hare and the noun ending 子. The noun ending is inherited from Middle Chinese tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), and the dictionary lists the first as the name of a beast.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.
