# த — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. Just one — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: த
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-122]; assesses=[] -->

**த** — *ta*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *t*, it is **ta**.

You already say these, and every one of them has த somewhere inside it:

- **மீண்டும் சந்திப்போம்** *mīṇḍum sandippōm* — we'll meet again
- **தமிழ் எழுத்து** *tamiḻ eḻuttu* — why Tamil writing is so round
- **நான் தமிழ் பேசுகிறேன்** *nāṉ tamiḻ pēsugiṟēṉ* — I speak Tamil
- **எனக்குத் தமிழ் தெரியும்** *enakkut tamiḻ teriyum* — 'I know Tamil'

## Writing: த — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-RECOG-122] -->

Put your pen on த and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
