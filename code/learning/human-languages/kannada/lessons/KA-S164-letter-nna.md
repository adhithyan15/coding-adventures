# ಣ — the n with the tongue curled back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-163] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the word for an eye. There are two n-sounds in Kannada and that word has the one you have never been shown.

## Script you'll notice: ಣ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-164]; assesses=[] -->

**ಣ** — *ṇa*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *ṇ*, it is **ṇa**.

Kannada keeps **two different n letters** apart on the page, and English
marks neither.

| letter | romanized | where the tongue goes |
|---|---|---|
| ನ | *na* | tip behind the teeth |
| ಣ | *ṇa* | curled back along the roof |

You have read **ನ** since the first chapter. **ಣ** is the same sound made
further back, with the tongue curled — the position you already use for **ಟ**
and **ಡ**.

You already say these:

- **ಕಣ್ಣು** *kaṇṇu* — an eye
- **ಸಿಗೋಣ** *sigōṇa* — let us meet, inside the farewell
- **ಖಂಡಿತ** *khaṇḍita* — certainly

## Writing: ಣ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-164] -->

- **1.** start at the foot of the lower curl and round it up to the short tick at the waist
- **2.** without lifting, come back and round the upper curl into the middle point
- **3.** without lifting, arch over to the right and come down the right side
- **4.** without lifting, round the base and spiral in to the centre

**Pen lifts: 0.** The whole letter is one run, from the foot of the lower
curl over the arch to the end of the spiral. There is no curled bar on top.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna
