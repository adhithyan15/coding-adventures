# el cargador — the one that loads

## You'll want to know: el cargador
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-04] -->

**el cargador** — the charger.

**cargar** — to load, to charge. **cargar el móvil** is to charge the phone.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C434-MAQ-05, ES-LEX-C431-PEDIDO-04] -->

Fourth time for **-dor**, and by now it should arrive before the lesson does:

| verb | the doer |
|---|---|
| proveer | el proveedor |
| lavar | la lavadora |
| contar | el contador |
| cargar | el cargador |

**cargar** is worth more than the charger. It means *to load*, and it reaches
much further than electricity: *cargar el coche* is to load the car, *la carga*
is the load itself, and *no se cobra* from earlier meets it in *hacerse cargo
de* — to take charge of something.

The same verb also turns up wherever a shop charges you, which is why **cobrar**
and **cargar** feel adjacent without being the same word.
