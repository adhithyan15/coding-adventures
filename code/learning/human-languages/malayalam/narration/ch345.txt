Malayalam, chapter 345: Feelings and Hopes.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ദുഃഖം (duḥkhaṁ) — grief, sorrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "sadness", then "well-being", then "news". (saṅkaṭaṁ, sukhaṁ, vārtta.)

You'll want to know: ദുഃഖം.
ദുഃഖം — duḥkhaṁ — "grief, sorrow".
ആ വാർത്ത കേട്ടപ്പോൾ എനിക്ക് വലിയ ദുഃഖമുണ്ടായി. ā vārtta kēṭṭappōḷ enikkŭ valiya duḥkhamuṇṭāyi. — "When I heard that news, I felt deep sorrow."
സങ്കടം is the everyday sadness of a bad day. ദുഃഖം (duḥkhaṁ) is heavier: grief, or sorrow you speak of with care.
അവരുടെ ദുഃഖം എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലാകുന്നു. avaruṭe duḥkhaṁ enikkŭ manassilākunnu. — "I understand their grief."
The mark after ദു is a breath, ḥ. Many speakers let it turn into a held k, close to dukkhaṁ. The next lesson puts the mark in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ദുഃഖം (duḥkhaṁ) from Sanskrit duḥkha, "difficulty, pain", a tatsama with Malayalam's noun ending (CDIAL 6375). CDIAL notes it was formed after sukha, the word you know as സുഖം: the two are a pair, ease and its opposite.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ഃ.
ഃ — the visarga, ḥ: a short breath after a vowel. It is two small rings, one above the other, written after its syllable: ദു + ഃ becomes ദുഃ, duḥ.
Do not confuse it with ം, one ring, which is ṁ. The visarga lives in words taken from Sanskrit, and most of them are learned words.

Writing: ഃ.
One recorded hand writes ഃ (ḥ) in two strokes: the upper ring first, then, after a lift, the lower one. Each ring starts at its top and goes round anticlockwise, to the left first.
Write ഃ (ḥ) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed: two rings the same size, one above the other. Copy the printed shape and keep the order above.

Lesson 3 of 5.
എനിക്ക് സന്തോഷം തോന്നി (enikkŭ santōṣaṁ tōnni) — I felt happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "joy", "sadness", "fear", and "tiredness". (santōṣaṁ, saṅkaṭaṁ, pēṭi, kṣīṇaṁ.)

Grammar Lens: the feeling comes to you.
English says "I felt happy", with "I" in charge. Malayalam puts you in the dative and lets the feeling arrive: "to me, joy seemed".
വാർത്ത കേട്ടപ്പോൾ എനിക്ക് സന്തോഷം തോന്നി. vārtta kēṭṭappōḷ enikkŭ santōṣaṁ tōnni. — "When I heard the news, I felt happy."
എനിക്ക് ക്ഷീണം തോന്നുന്നു. enikkŭ kṣīṇaṁ tōnnunnu. — "I feel tired."
The noun names the feeling; തോന്നി, തോന്നുന്നു carry the time. അവന്, അവൾക്ക്, ഞങ്ങൾക്ക് take the same place for "he", "she", "we".

Grammar Lens: a feeling as scene, or as event.
A feeling that lasted is scene, in ആയിരുന്നു. A feeling that struck is an event, and Malayalam says it came:
ഇന്നലെ എനിക്ക് വലിയ സങ്കടമായിരുന്നു. innale enikkŭ valiya saṅkaṭamāyirunnu. — "Yesterday I was very sad."
അത് കേട്ടപ്പോൾ അവന് ദേഷ്യം വന്നു. atŭ kēṭṭappōḷ avanŭ dēṣyaṁ vannu. — "When he heard that, he got angry."
To describe a person, add -ഉള്ള: സന്തോഷമുള്ള കുട്ടി (santōṣamuḷḷa kuṭṭi), "a happy child".

Lesson 4 of 5.
എനിക്ക് കേരളം കാണാൻ ആഗ്രഹമുണ്ട് (enikkŭ kēraḷaṁ kāṇān āgrahamuṇṭŭ) — I would like to see Kerala.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a wish", "a dream", and "to read" in the -ആൻ form. (āgrahaṁ, svapnaṁ, vāyikkān.)

Grammar Lens: -ആൻ ആഗ്രഹമുണ്ട്.
The -ആൻ form of chapter 74 says "to do it". Put it before ആഗ്രഹം, "a wish", and the wish belongs to someone in the dative, with ഉണ്ട്:
എനിക്ക് കേരളം കാണാൻ ആഗ്രഹമുണ്ട്. enikkŭ kēraḷaṁ kāṇān āgrahamuṇṭŭ. — "I would like to see Kerala."
The verb ആഗ്രഹിക്കുക, "to wish, to long", says the same with ഞാൻ in front:
ഞാൻ ഒരു അധ്യാപകൻ ആകാൻ ആഗ്രഹിക്കുന്നു. ñān oru adhyāpakan ākān āgrahikkunnu. — "I want to become a teacher."
ആകുക is "to become"; ആകാൻ, "to become".

Grammar Lens: … എന്നാണ് എന്റെ ആഗ്രഹം.
For a bigger hope, say the hope first, close it with എന്നാണ്, "is that …", and name it last:
ഒരു ചെറിയ കട തുടങ്ങണം എന്നാണ് എന്റെ ആഗ്രഹം. oru ceṟiya kaṭa tuṭaṅṅaṇaṁ ennāṇŭ enṟe āgrahaṁ. — "My wish is to start a small shop."
ആഗ്രഹം can give way to സ്വപ്നം, "dream", or ലക്ഷ്യം, "aim", in the same frame.
