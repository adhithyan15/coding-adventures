Italian, chapter 518: Intonati e stonati.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
intonato — in tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to tune, then the Italian for to compose.

You'll want to know: intonato.
intonato — "in tune".
Hai una voce molto intonata. You sing very much in tune.
Also matching: una borsa intonata alle scarpe.

The word, taken apart.
intonato comes from intonare "to tune, to strike up", built on tono "tone", from Latin tonus, from Greek tónos "a stretching, a pitch" (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

You'll want to know: stonare.
stonare — "to sing or play out of tune".
Quando canto stono sempre. When I sing I am always out of tune.
Stonato — out of tune; also out of place.

The word, taken apart.
stonare is built from s- and tono "tone", from Latin tonus, from Greek tónos "a pitch" (EtymDB): to go off the note.

Lesson 3 of 6.
il ritornello — chorus, refrain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for in tune, then the Italian for to sing or play out of tune.

You'll want to know: il ritornello.
il ritornello — "chorus, refrain".
Tutti cantano il ritornello insieme. Everyone sings the chorus together.
Also a phrase someone repeats: sempre lo stesso ritornello.

The word, taken apart.
ritornello is built on ritorno "return", from ritornare, from Latin tornāre "to turn on a lathe" (Lewis & Short): the part that comes back.

Lesson 4 of 6.
la strofa — verse, stanza.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to sing or play out of tune, then the Italian for chorus.

You'll want to know: la strofa.
la strofa — "verse, stanza".
Non ricordo la seconda strofa della canzone. I do not remember the second verse of the song.
Also la strofe.

The word, taken apart.
strofa is a learned borrowing, through Latin stropha, from Greek strophḗ "a turning" (EtymDB, under English strophe), the turn of the chorus as it danced.

Lesson 5 of 6.
canticchiare — to hum, to sing softly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for chorus, then the Italian for verse.

You'll want to know: canticchiare.
canticchiare — "to hum, to sing softly".
Canticchio mentre cucino. I hum while I cook.
Built on cantare with -icchiare, for a light, repeated action.

The word, taken apart.
canticchiare is built on cantare "to sing" (EtymDB), inherited from Latin cantāre.
