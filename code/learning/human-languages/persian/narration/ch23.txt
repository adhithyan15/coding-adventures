Lesson 1 of 4.
ح (he-ye jimi) — the letter that opens حال.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ح and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ز and د once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say حال (hâl), "state, how you are". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ح.
ح — he-ye jimi, the sound h.
It opens حال (hâl), the "how you are" in حالت چطور است؟. Persian already has an h, ه, which you write; this second one came in with Arabic words and sounds the same. Its name, he-ye jimi, means "the h shaped like jim": a short head and a deep bowl, with no dots.

Writing: ح.
1. start here: draw the short upper head from left to right.
2. continue down and around the deep bowl without lifting — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ح (he-ye jimi) demonstration at 00:42–00:46 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Script you'll notice: ج.
ج — jim, the sound j.
It ends پنج (panj), "five". It is ح (he-ye jimi), from the last lesson, with one dot below the head.

Writing: ج.
Put your pen on ج (jim) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Now say خدا (khodâ), "God". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: خ.
خ — khe, a kh scraped at the back of the throat.
It opens خدا (khodâ). The same head and bowl as ح (he-ye jimi), with one dot above. One skeleton, and the dot decides the sound.

Writing: خ.
1. start here: draw the short upper head from left to right.
2. without lifting, continue down and around the deep bowl.
3. lift once, then place the dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, خ (khe) demonstration at 00:49–00:54 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Now say چیست (chist), "what is?". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: چ.
چ — che, the sound ch.
It opens چیست (chist), "what is?", one of your first questions. It is one of the four letters Persian added to the Arabic alphabet: the same skeleton as ح (he-ye jimi), with three dots below.

Writing: چ.
1. start here: draw the short upper head from left to right.
2. continue down and around the deep bowl without lifting.
3. lift, then place the lower-left dot.
4. lift again and place the lower-right dot.
5. lift again and place the lower-center dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, چ demonstration at 00:35–00:41 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).
