Lesson 1 of 5.
चप्पल (cappal) — a sandal, a slipper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a mirror, then the Hindi for a ring.

You'll want to know: चप्पल.
चप्पल — cappal — "a sandal, a slipper".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of चप्पल (cappal) is uncertain, and CDIAL has no entry for it. DEDR, under Dravidian words for "sandal" such as Telugu ceppu, adds "Cf. H. cappal" (DEDR 1963): a comparison, not a settled source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
जूता (jūtā) — a shoe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a ring, then the Hindi for a sandal.

You'll want to know: जूता.
जूता — jūtā — "a shoe".

The word, taken apart.
जूता (jūtā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from yukta, "joined, yoked" (CDIAL 10479). CDIAL lists jūtā, "shoe", here: the shoe as the thing fastened on.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कमीज़ (kamīz) — a shirt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a shoe, then the Hindi for a sock.

You'll want to know: कमीज़.
कमीज़ — kamīz — "a shirt".

The word, taken apart.
कमीज़ (kamīz) is a loan from Arabic qamīṣ, "shirt", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its Arabic root is q-m-ṣ (Lane).
