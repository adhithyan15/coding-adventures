Spanish, chapter 650: Autocar y tranvía.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el autocar — the coach (long-distance bus).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to pedal, then the Spanish for the cyclist.

You'll want to know: el autocar.
el autocar — "the coach (long-distance bus)".
El autocar sale a las ocho. The coach leaves at eight.
A Spain word; Latin America says el autobús, el bus or el micro.

The word, taken apart.
autocar was borrowed from French autocar (EtymDB), made from auto- and English car.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
el tranvía — the tram, the streetcar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the cyclist, then the Spanish for the coach.

You'll want to know: el tranvía.
el tranvía — "the tram, the streetcar".
El tranvía pasa cada diez minutos. The tram comes every ten minutes.
It ends in -a but is masculine: el tranvía.

The word, taken apart.
tranvía was borrowed from English tramway, with the second half remade as Spanish vía "track, way", from Latin via.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el bonobús — the bus pass (for several trips).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the coach, then the Spanish for the tram.

You'll want to know: el bonobús.
el bonobús — "the bus pass (for several trips)".
Con el bonobús el viaje sale más barato. With a bus pass the trip works out cheaper.
A Spain word; it holds ten trips, or a month of travel.

The word, taken apart.
bonobús is built from bono "voucher", from Latin bonus "good", and bus, short for autobús, from Latin omnibus "for all".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el transbordo — the change, the transfer (between trains or buses).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tram, then the Spanish for the bus pass.

You'll want to know: el transbordo.
el transbordo — "the change, the transfer (between trains or buses)".
Hay que hacer transbordo en la siguiente estación. You have to change at the next station.
Hacer transbordo — to change trains or lines.

The word, taken apart.
transbordo is built on Latin trans "across" and bordo "side of a ship", which comes from borde "edge" (EtymDB). It first meant moving goods from one ship to another.

Lesson 5 of 5.
validar — to validate (a ticket).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the bus pass, then the Spanish for the change.

You'll want to know: validar.
validar — "to validate (a ticket)".
Valide el billete al subir. Validate your ticket when you get on.
Buses and trams have a small machine for this.

The word, taken apart.
validar is built on válido "valid", a learned borrowing from Latin validus "strong, able" (Lewis & Short), from valēre "to be strong".
