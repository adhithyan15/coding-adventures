Russian, chapter 264: Advice, and Why.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
тебе стоит, лучше, ты должен — advice at three strengths.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "to cost" and "to rest". (Stóit. Otdykhát.) Then "it seems to me". (Mne kázhetsya.) Мне was "to me"; the same shape for "you" is тебе, tebé.

Grammar Lens: тебе стоит, it is worth your while.
Тебе стоит отдохнуть. — tebé stóit otdokhnút — You should rest.
Стоит, "it costs", here means "it is worth it": rest would pay you back. The infinitive follows, and the person advised takes the "to you" form: тебе, or polite вам. Отдохнуть is the perfective partner of отдыхать: one rest, taken.

Grammar Lens: лучше, it is better.
— Уже поздно, а метро далеко. — Тебе лучше взять такси. — uzhé pózdno, a metró dalekó. tebé lúchshe vzyat taksí — It's late already and the metro is far. You'd better take a taxi.
Лучше — lúchshe — "better": one choice against another. Взять is the perfective partner of брать, the pair with two different roots.

Grammar Lens: ты должен, you must.
Ты должен позвонить маме. — ty dólzhen pozvonít máme — You must ring Mum.
Должен is a duty, and it agrees like the past: должен to a man, должна (dolzhná) to a woman, должны (dolzhný) to more than one or to вы. It can sound strong between friends; стоит and лучше are gentler.

The word, taken apart.
должен comes from долг, "a debt, a duty", which EtymDB 2.1 traces to Proto-Slavic dŭlgŭ. A duty is a debt: Я должен тебе сто рублей still means "I owe you a hundred roubles".

Lesson 2 of 3.
причина в том, что … (prichína v tom, shto …) — giving the reason at length.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "you must ring Mum". (Ty dólzhen pozvonít máme.) Then "a reason". (Prichína.) Advice asks for a reason back.

Grammar Lens: причина в том, что …
— Почему ты не позвонил? — Причина в том, что я потерял телефон. — pochemú ty ne pozvoníl? prichína v tom, shto ya poteryál telefón — Why didn't you ring? The reason is that I lost my phone.
Word for word, "the reason is in that, that …": в том points ahead, and что brings in the explanation, with its comma. It is weightier than потому что.

Grammar Lens: an explanation in three steps.
What happened, then поэтому, then what followed:
Я потерял телефон, поэтому не мог позвонить. — ya poteryál telefón, poétomu ne mog pozvonít — I lost my phone, so I couldn't ring.
Мог is the past of мочь, one of the few without an л: мог, могла, могли.

Why it's said this way: the aspect of an explanation.
Учитель говорил быстро, и я не понимал правило. Потом он повторил медленно, и я понял. — uchítel govoríl býstro, i ya ne ponimál právilo. potóm on povtoríl médlenno, i ya pónyal — The teacher was talking fast and I didn't understand the rule. Then he said it again slowly, and I understood.
A process with no result, then two deeds done: повторил, from the partner of повторять, and понял, from понять, stressed on the front: pónyal.

The word, taken apart.
причина is при- with чин, by the usual account "order, rank". EtymDB 2.1 lists причина itself without a parent, and takes чин through Old East Slavic činŭ to Proto-Slavic činŭ. A reason, on this reading, is what set things in motion.

Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — advice, and the reason for it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Three pieces of advice: "you should rest", "you'd better take a taxi", "you must ring Mum". (Tebé stóit otdokhnút. Tebé lúchshe vzyat taksí. Ty dólzhen pozvonít máme.)

What you've built.
Two lessons, and Russian can now advise and explain. Advice at three strengths, each with an infinitive: тебе стоит, лучше, ты должен. And причина в том, что … (prichína v tom, shto …), with the explanation built from what happened and поэтому.

The exchange.
— Я не понимаю правило. — По-моему, тебе стоит спросить учителя. Он поможет.
Ya ne ponimáyu právilo — "I don't understand the rule." Po-móyemu, tebé stóit sprosít uchítelya. On pomózhet — "I think you should ask the teacher. He'll help." Спросить is the perfective partner of спрашивать.
