# parlavo — the past that is still running

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-PASSATO-PROSSIMO-02, IT-GRAMMAR-PARLARE-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I spoke" the way chapter fifteen built it. (*Ho parlato.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That past has an edge: it happened, and it finished. Italian has a
second past with no edge at all, and every story you tell from here on needs
both of them.

## Grammar Lens: one ending, three families
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-AVERE-02, IT-LEX-C48-VERBI2-05, IT-GRAMMAR-LAVORARE-04] -->

Take the infinitive, drop its last two letters, **-re**, and add **-vo** for "I":

> parla-re, **parlavo** — I was speaking, I used to speak
> ave-re, **avevo** — I had, I used to have
> dormi-re, **dormivo** — I was sleeping

The vowel in front of the **-v-** is the family's own: *-a-*, *-e-*, *-i-*.
Nothing else moves. The other persons keep that **-v-** and change only what
comes after it: **parlavo**, **parlavi**, **parlava**, **parlavamo**,
**parlavate**, **parlavano** — I, you, he or she, we, you all, they.

This is the **imperfetto**, and it is the most regular tense Italian owns: one
recipe for almost every verb. The few that refuse it begin in the next lesson.

What it means is "was ‑ing", or "used to". *Parlavo* is speaking with no start
and no finish in view, which is exactly what a story needs for its background.

## The word, taken apart: why a v
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-IMPERFETTO-BA-02]; assesses=[IT-ETYMON-AVERE-04] -->

Latin already had this past, and it marked it with a **b**: Latin *amābam*, "I
was loving", and *habēbat*, "he was having". Between two vowels that *b*
softened to a *v*, the same softening that turned Latin *habēre* into
**avere**. So *habēbat* comes down to **aveva** sound by sound.

One piece is younger. Old Italian said *io parlava*, with the same *-a* as
*lui parlava*. The **-o** of *parlavo* was borrowed later from the present,
*parlo*, so that "I" had an ending of its own. Neither EtymDB nor Lewis & Short
records endings this late; that step is the usual explanation in histories of
Italian, and it is given here as one.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01, IT-ETYMON-IMPERFETTO-BA-02, IT-LEX-AVERE-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] How do you build "I was sleeping"? (Drop the *-re* of *dormire* and
add *-vo*: ***dormivo***.) Which Latin letter became the *-v-*? (The ***b*** of
*amābam*, softened as in *habēre* and **avere**.) Which past has an edge, and
which has none? (***Ho parlato*** has one; ***parlavo*** has none.)
