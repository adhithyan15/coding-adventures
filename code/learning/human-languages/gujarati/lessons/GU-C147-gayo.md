# હું ગયો, હું ગઈ — the past of moving

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-E-TIME-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-GHARE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-KAALE-TENSE-01, GU-LEX-JAVU, GU-LEX-AAVVU] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *yesterday I was at home*, and say which day **કાલે** means in **કાલે મળીશું**. Then the two verbs of movement: **જવું**,
*to go*, and **આવવું**, *to come*.

## Grammar Lens: one word does the whole job
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-GAYO-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-JAVU, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-HATO-01, GU-LEX-BAJAR-01, GU-LEX-C79-WEEK2-04] -->

> **ગઈકાલે હું બજાર ગયો.** — *gaīkāle hũ bajār gayo.* — Yesterday I went to the market.

**ગયો** is a past of **જવું** all on its own: no **છું**, no **હતો** beside it.
It ends the way **હતો** ends, and it agrees the same way:

- **ગયો** — a man; **ગઈ** — a woman;
- **ગયું** — something neuter; **ગયા** — more than one, or respect.

**ગઈ** is not new to you. It is the first half of **ગઈકાલ**, *yesterday*: the
gone day. **ગયો** goes back through Old Gujarati *gayaü* to Sanskrit *gata*,
"gone" (CDIAL 4008).

## You'll want to know: આવ્યો
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-AAVYO-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-AAVVU, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-GHARE-01] -->

**આવવું** builds its past on **આવ્ય-**:

> **તે ગઈકાલે ઘરે આવ્યો.** — *te gaīkāle ghare āvyo.* — He came home yesterday.
> **તે આવી.** — *te āvī.* — She came.

The same four endings again: **આવ્યો**, **આવી**, **આવ્યું**, **આવ્યા**. The
feminine sheds the **્ય**.
