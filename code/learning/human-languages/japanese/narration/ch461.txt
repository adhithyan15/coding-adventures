Lesson 1 of 6.
たっきゅう (takkyū) — table tennis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to take on a challenge, then the Japanese for to change jobs.

You'll want to know: たっきゅう.
たっきゅう — takkyū — "table tennis".
そふは たっきゅうが すきです。 — sofu wa takkyū ga suki desu — "My grandfather loves table tennis."

The word, taken apart.
たっきゅう (takkyū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ta, "table" (Middle Chinese traewk) and kyū, "ball" (gjuw) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for table tennis, then the Japanese for kendo.

You'll want to know: からて.
からて — karate — "karate".
からての しあいで かちました。 — karate no shiai de kachimashita — "I won a karate match."

The word, taken apart.
からて (karate) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: kara and te, "hand". JMdict gives two spellings, one writing kara with the kanji for "empty" and one with the kanji for "China" (KANJIDIC2), so which kara it holds is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
