Urdu, chapter 302: A Rumour Spreads.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
افواہ (afvāh) — a rumour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a detail, then the Urdu for to have written down.

You'll want to know: افواہ.
افواہ — afvāh — "a rumour".
شہر میں ایک افواہ تھی۔ shahr meṅ ek afvāh thī. There was a rumour going round the city.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
افواہ (afvāh) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic afwāh, "mouths": what passes from mouth to mouth. It is feminine.

Lesson 2 of 5.
چرچا (charchā) — talk of the town; a much-discussed matter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to have written down, then the Urdu for a rumour.

You'll want to know: چرچا.
چرچا — charchā — "talk of the town; a much-discussed matter".
ہر جگہ اس بات کا چرچا تھا۔ har jagah is bāt kā charchā thā. Everyone everywhere was talking about it.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
چرچا (charchā) is usually traced to Sanskrit carcā, "repetition, a going over", in Monier-Williams; in Urdu it is the talk that goes round. It is masculine: چرچا تھا.

Lesson 3 of 5.
دہشت (dahshat) — terror; panic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a rumour, then the Urdu for talk of the town.

You'll want to know: دہشت.
دہشت — dahshat — "terror; panic".
لوگوں میں دہشت تھی۔ logoṅ meṅ dahshat thī. People were terrified.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
دہشت (dahshat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic dahsha, "bewilderment, alarm". It is a fear that grips a whole crowd.

Lesson 4 of 5.
بھڑکانا (bhaṛkānā) — to stir up; to incite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for talk of the town, then the Urdu for terror.

You'll want to know: بھڑکانا.
بھڑکانا — bhaṛkānā — "to stir up; to incite".
افواہ نے لوگوں کو بھڑکایا۔ afvāh ne logoṅ ko bhaṛkāyā. The rumour stirred people up.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے لوگوں کو بھڑکایا, us ne logoṅ ko bhaṛkāyā.

The word, taken apart.
بھڑکانا (bhaṛkānā) is the causing partner of بھڑکنا, to flare up, which CDIAL lists for Hindi under bhaṭ (CDIAL 9365): to make a fire, or a crowd, flare.

Lesson 5 of 5.
عوام (avām) — the public; the common people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for terror, then the Urdu for to stir up.

You'll want to know: عوام.
عوام — avām — "the public; the common people".
عوام پریشان تھے۔ avām pareshān the. The public were worried.
Masculine, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
عوام (avām) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ʿawāmm, "the common people". It takes a plural verb: عوام پریشان تھے.
