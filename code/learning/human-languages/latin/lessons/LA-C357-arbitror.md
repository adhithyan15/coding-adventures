## You'll want to know: arbitror, arbitrārī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C357-SENTENTIA-01]; assesses=[] -->

**arbitror, arbitrārī** — "to be of the opinion, to judge".

> Arbitror eum vērum dīcere.
>
> *I believe he is telling the truth.*

Deponent: *arbitror, arbitrārī, arbitrātus sum*, passive in form and active in meaning. Like *putō* it takes the accusative and infinitive. English *arbitrate* comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*arbitror* is built on *arbiter* "an eyewitness, an umpire" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): to judge a thing as one who saw it. English *arbiter*, *arbitrate* and *arbitrary* come from the same family.
