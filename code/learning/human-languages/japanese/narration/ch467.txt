Japanese, chapter 467: Sewing and Knitting.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ぬう (nuu) — to sew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for paper folding, then the Japanese for the arts.

You'll want to know: ぬう.
ぬう — nuu — "to sew".
ははは ゆかたを ぬっています。 — haha wa yukata o nutte imasu — "My mother is sewing a cotton kimono."
Polite ぬいます, nuimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): そぼが きものを ぬいました。, sobo ga kimono o nuimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぬう (nuu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 6.
ししゅう (shishū) — embroidery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for knitting, then the Japanese for to dye.

You'll want to know: ししゅう.
ししゅう — shishū — "embroidery".
ははの ししゅうは きれいです。 — haha no shishū wa kirei desu — "My mother's embroidery is beautiful."

The word, taken apart.
ししゅう (shishū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "prick" (Middle Chinese tshjeH) and shū, "sew" (sjuwH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
