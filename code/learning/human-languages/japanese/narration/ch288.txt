Japanese, chapter 288: Starting School.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
にゅうがく (nyūgaku) — entering school.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a relative, then the Japanese for to grow up.

You'll want to know: にゅうがく.
にゅうがく — nyūgaku — "entering school".
おとうとは らいねん にゅうがくします。 — otōto wa rainen nyūgaku shimasu — "My little brother starts school next year."

The word, taken apart.
にゅうがく (nyūgaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nyū, "enter" (Middle Chinese nyip) and gaku, "learning" (haewk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しゅっせきする (shusseki suru) — to attend; to be present.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for entering school, then the Japanese for a ceremony.

You'll want to know: しゅっせきする.
しゅっせきする — shusseki suru — "to attend; to be present".
ははも しきに しゅっせきします。 — haha mo shiki ni shusseki shimasu — "My mother is going to the ceremony too."
Polite しゅっせきします, shussekishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): ちちが しきに しゅっせきしました。, chichi ga shiki ni shusseki shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しゅっせきする (shusseki suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shutsu, "exit" (Middle Chinese tsyhwit) and seki, "seat" (zjek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The first is shortened from shutsu to shus- before seki. The native verb suru, "to do", makes it a verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
にぎやか (nigiyaka) — lively; bustling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a ceremony, then the Japanese for to attend.

You'll want to know: にぎやか.
にぎやか — nigiyaka — "lively; bustling".
この まちは 年じゅう にぎやかです。 — kono machi wa nenjū nigiyaka desu — "This town is lively all year round."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
にぎやか (nigiyaka) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
おいわい (oiwai) — a celebration; a present for a happy occasion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to attend, then the Japanese for lively.

You'll want to know: おいわい.
おいわい — oiwai — "a celebration; a present for a happy occasion".
おいわいに みんなで しょくじを しました。 — oiwai ni minna de shokuji o shimashita — "We all had a meal to celebrate."

The word, taken apart.
おいわい (oiwai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from o-, "the polite prefix", and iwai, "celebrating" (from iwau) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
