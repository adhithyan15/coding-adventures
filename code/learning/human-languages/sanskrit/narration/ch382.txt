Sanskrit, chapter 382: For Our Good.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हितम् (hitam) — benefit; welfare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for repeatedly, then the Sanskrit for sums up.

You'll want to know: हितम्.
हितम् — hitam — "benefit; welfare".
यदि एतत् मम हितम्, तर्हि अहम् अवश्यं करिष्यामि। yadi etat mama hitam, tarhi aham avaśyaṃ kariṣyāmi. If this is for my good, I will do it by all means.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
हितम् (hitam) comes from the root √dhā, "to put", whose participle MW gives as hita, "put, placed"; MW gives hita the senses "beneficial, advantageous, salutary" (MW).

You'll want to know: विश्राम्यति.
विश्राम्यति — viśrāmyati — "rests".
यदि भवान् श्रान्तः, तर्हि क्षणं विश्राम्यतु। yadi bhavān śrāntaḥ, tarhi kṣaṇaṃ viśrāmyatu. If you are tired, rest for a moment.
Past (he …): सः वृक्षस्य छायायां विश्रान्तवान्, saḥ vṛkṣasya chāyāyāṃ viśrāntavān.

The word, taken apart.
विश्राम्यति (viśrāmyati) is built on vi and the root √śram, "to be weary"; MW gives vi-√śram "to rest, to repose" and vi-śrānta "rested" (MW).

सत्करोति (satkaroti) is built from sat, "good", and the root √kṛ, "to do"; MW gives sat-√kṛ "to treat well or with respect, to honour, to receive hospitably" (MW).
