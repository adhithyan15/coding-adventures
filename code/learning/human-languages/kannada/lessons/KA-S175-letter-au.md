# ಔ (au) — the letter that opens ಔಷಧ

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-158, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-125, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-163, KA-LEX-C314-AUSADHA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: which letters are **ಒ** and **ಓ**? (*o* and *ō*.) Which sign says *au* after a consonant? (**◌ೌ**, the mark in **ಹೌದು**.)

[PAUSE 2s] Now say **ಔಷಧ** (*auṣadha*), "a medicine". You met it a moment ago. This lesson takes its first letter out.

## Script you'll notice: ಔ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-175]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C314-AUSADHA-01, KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-163] -->

**ಔ** — *au*.

It is an **independent vowel**: the shape *au* takes at the start of a word. It is one vowel that glides from *a* toward *u*, the way the middle of English *now* does.

After a consonant, *au* is the sign **◌ೌ**: **ಹೌದು**, *haudu*, "yes"; **ಗೌರವ**, *gaurava*, "respect". At the start of a word there is no consonant to carry a sign, so the vowel stands as a letter of its own. With it, every vowel this book uses has its letter for the front of a word, and all but **ಅ** have a sign for inside one.

In print the lower part of **ಔ** is the pair of round bowls you know from the bottom of **ಒ**. Above them a flat bar runs along the top; on the left it drops into the body, and on the right it ends in a small curl that turns inward. Few words open with **ಔ**, and most of them come from Sanskrit.

## Writing: ಔ — copy the printed shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-175] -->

Put your pen beside **ಔ** and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Write the two bowls first, then the bar along the top, and close it with the small inward curl on the right.

> This book does not yet tell you where to start this character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.
