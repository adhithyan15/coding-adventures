French, chapter 270: Tout à coup.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tout à coup — all of a sudden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for at once, then the French for while.

You'll want to know: tout à coup.
tout à coup — "all of a sudden".
Tout à coup, il a commencé à pleuvoir. All of a sudden, it started to rain.
Close to soudain; storytellers love it. Tout d'un coup also means 'all at once'.

The word, taken apart.
coup is inherited from Late Latin colpus "a blow", from Latin colaphus "a punch" (EtymDB), itself borrowed from Greek.

Built on subit, 'sudden': une mort subite.

subitement is built on subit "sudden", a learned borrowing from Latin subitus "sudden", from subīre "to come up, to steal upon" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: brusquement.
brusquement — "abruptly, sharply".
La voiture s'est arrêtée brusquement. The car stopped abruptly.
Brusque also describes a manner: un ton brusque, a curt tone.

The word, taken apart.
brusquement is built on brusque, which was borrowed from Italian brusco "rough, sharp, sour" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
dans la foulée — straight after, in the same stride.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for abruptly, then the French for at the time.

You'll want to know: dans la foulée.
dans la foulée — "straight after, in the same stride".
Il a quitté l'école et il a trouvé un travail dans la foulée. He left school and found a job straight after.
Neutral and frequent in the press. La foulée is a runner's stride.

The word, taken apart.
foulée is built on fouler "to tread, to press", which is inherited through Old French foler from a verb built on Latin fullō "a fuller, a cloth-treader" (EtymDB).
