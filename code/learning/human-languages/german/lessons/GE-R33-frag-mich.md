## Guided Practice: the distant band — flip every statement you own
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-JA-NEIN-FRAGE-01, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-SECOND-02, GE-LEX-WOHNEN-01, GE-ETYMON-WOHNEN-WONT-02, GE-LEX-ICH-WOHNE-IN-02, GE-LEX-LERNEN-01, GE-ETYMON-LERNEN-LORE-02, GE-LEX-HABEN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-HABEN-DU-HAST-01, GE-LEX-KAFFEE-02, GE-ETYMON-KAFFEE-04, GE-LEX-TEE-02, GE-ETYMON-TEE-04, GE-LEX-MILCH-02, GE-ETYMON-MILCH-04, GE-LEX-BROT-01, GE-LEX-WASSER-01, GE-LEX-WEIN-01, GE-LEX-HUND-02, GE-LEX-KATZE-04, GE-LEX-KEINEN-01, GE-LEX-KEINE-01, GE-LEX-KEIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Every statement in this book can now be a question. Take the verb and
move it left.

- [YOU SAY: "Du wohnst in Berlin." → "Wohnst du in Berlin?"; "Du lernst
  Deutsch." → "Lernst du Deutsch?"]
- [YOU SAY: the six things to eat and drink, asked for — "Hast du Kaffee, Tee,
  Milch, Brot, Wasser, Wein?"]
- [YOU SAY: the two animals, then last chapter's answer — "Hast du einen Hund?"
  "Nein, ich habe keinen Hund." "Hast du eine Katze?" "Nein, ich habe keine
  Katze."]
- [YOU SAY: which of those words German bought and which it always had —
  *Kaffee* and *Tee* came from far away, *Milch* and *Brot* did not]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-JA-NEIN-FRAGE-01, GE-GRAMMAR-IMPERATIV-DU-01, GE-LEX-WER-01, GE-LEX-WANN-01, GE-LEX-WARUM-01, GE-ETYMON-WARUM-WAR-UM-02, GE-ETYMON-WER-WHO-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] What turns a statement into a yes-or-no question? (**The verb moves
to first place**.) What does a command leave out? (**The pronoun**.) Which sound
law lines German *w-* up with English *wh-*? (**Grimm's**.) And which of the six
W-words is two words welded together? (***Warum*** — an old *was* plus *um*.)
