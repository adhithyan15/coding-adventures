Japanese, chapter 342: Causes and Effects.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
げんいん (gen'in) — a cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for as much as possible, then the Japanese for for example.

You'll want to know: げんいん.
げんいん — gen'in — "a cause".
じこの げんいんが わからないので、しらべています。 — jiko no gen'in ga wakaranai node, shirabete imasu — "Nobody knows the cause of the accident, so it is being investigated."

The word, taken apart.
げんいん (gen'in) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gen, "original" and in, "cause" (Middle Chinese 'jin) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
なぜなら (nazenara) — because (the reason is).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an influence, then the Japanese for a result.

You'll want to know: なぜなら.
なぜなら — nazenara — "because (the reason is)".
なぜなら、あした しけんが あるからです。 — nazenara, ashita shiken ga aru kara desu — "The reason is that I have an exam tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
なぜなら (nazenara) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built on naze, "why", and JMdict lists it as a conjunction, usually written in kana (JMdict).
