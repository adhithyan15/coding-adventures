Spanish, chapter 753: Música y canciones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el músico — the musician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for magic, then the Spanish for to bet.

You'll want to know: el músico.
el músico — "the musician".
Mi vecino es músico de jazz. My neighbour is a jazz musician.
La música is music and also a woman musician.

The word, taken apart.
músico comes from Latin mūsicus "musician", from Greek mousikós "of the Muses" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
tararear — to hum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to bet, then the Spanish for the musician.

You'll want to know: tararear.
tararear — "to hum".
Siempre tarareo en la ducha. I always hum in the shower.
To sing a tune without the words.

The word, taken apart.
tararear comes from the syllables ta-ra-ra people sing when they do not know the words; it is an imitative word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el ritmo — rhythm; pace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to hum, then the Spanish for the song.

You'll want to know: el ritmo.
el ritmo — "rhythm; pace".
Esta canción tiene buen ritmo. This song has a good beat.
Also pace: el ritmo de vida — the pace of life.

The word, taken apart.
ritmo comes from Latin rhythmus, borrowed from Greek rhythmós "measured flow", from rheîn "to flow" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
componer — to compose, to write (music).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the song, then the Spanish for rhythm.

You'll want to know: componer.
componer — "to compose, to write (music)".
Compone canciones para su grupo. He writes songs for his band.
Conjugates like poner: compongo, compuse.

The word, taken apart.
componer is inherited from Latin compōnere "to put together, to arrange" (EtymDB), built on pōnere "to put".
