French, chapter 468: Sincères et menteurs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sincère — sincere, honest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for clumsy, then the French for scatterbrained.

You'll want to know: sincère.
sincère — "sincere, honest".
Elle est toujours sincère avec ses amis. She is always honest with her friends.
The noun is la sincérité.

The word, taken apart.
sincère is a learned borrowing from Latin sincērus "pure, clean, whole" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le menteur — liar; lying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for scatterbrained, then the French for sincere.

You'll want to know: le menteur.
le menteur — "liar; lying".
Ne le crois pas, c'est un menteur. Do not believe him, he is a liar.
A woman is la menteuse. A lie is un mensonge.

The word, taken apart.
le menteur is inherited from Old French menteor (EtymDB), built on mentir "to lie", from Latin mentīrī "to lie" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
naïf — naive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sincere, then the French for liar.

You'll want to know: naïf.
naïf — "naive".
J'étais si naïf que je croyais tout. I was so naive that I believed everything.
Feminine naïve. The noun is la naïveté.

The word, taken apart.
naïf is inherited from Latin nātīvus "inborn, natural" (EtymDB), from nāscī "to be born" (Lewis & Short); the sense moved from "natural" to "unspoiled" and then to "naive".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rusé — cunning, crafty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for liar, then the French for naive.

You'll want to know: rusé.
rusé — "cunning, crafty".
Le renard est rusé: il trouve toujours une sortie. The fox is cunning: it always finds a way out.
The noun une ruse is a trick.

The word, taken apart.
rusé comes from ruser, first said of a hunted animal that doubles back. Ruser is inherited from Latin recūsāre "to refuse, to draw back" (Lewis & Short), itself built on causa.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mentir — to lie, to tell lies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for naive, then the French for cunning.

You'll want to know: mentir.
mentir — "to lie, to tell lies".
Il a menti à ses parents sur ses notes. He lied to his parents about his marks.
Present: je mens, il ment, nous mentons. Past: j'ai menti.

The word, taken apart.
mentir is inherited, through Old French, from Vulgar Latin mentīre, a form of Latin mentīrī "to lie" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
