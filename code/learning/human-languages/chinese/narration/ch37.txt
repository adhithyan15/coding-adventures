Mandarin Chinese, chapter 37: This One and That One.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
这 (zhè) — this.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for Beijing, then the Mandarin for which.

You'll want to know: 这.
这 — zhè — "this".
这是我的书 zhè shì wǒ de shū — This is my book.
The new character, in pieces: 这 (zhè) — 文, from 中文, and a walking piece that runs under it from the left. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
这 (zhè) is written with an old character that the Guangyun records as ngjenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to meet'. That is a different word: ngjenH would not become zhè. How the character came to write the word for this is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: zhè]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhè, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhè shì wǒ de shū — This is my book.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for Beijing, then the Mandarin for which, then say zhè again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 这 (zhè) mean? ("This".) Say it once more.

文 书.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — wén, shū]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 文, then the walking piece.
这 (zhè).
Two pieces, and the one underneath goes last. The walking piece wraps the bottom-left, but it is written after what it carries.
The top-right piece, 文, four strokes, as in 中文: a dot, a horizontal, a left-falling stroke and a right-falling stroke crossing it.
The walking piece, three strokes. A dot. A stroke that runs across, folds down and folds again, ending in a short fall. Then a long flat sweep along the bottom, under 文, out to the right.
4 + 3 = seven.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 这 (zhè), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 这 (zhè)? (Two — 文, then the walking piece.) Which goes first? (文, even though the walking piece is on the left.) Which stroke is last? (The long sweep along the bottom.)

Lesson 4 of 7.
那 (nà) — that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for this, then the Mandarin for this one.

You'll want to know: 那.
那 — nà — "that".
那是我的家 nà shì wǒ de jiā — That's my home.
The new character, in pieces: 那 (nà) — a four-stroke piece on the left, and on the right a two-stroke piece shaped like an ear. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
那 (nà) is an old character: the Guangyun records it as na (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'how, what'. The sense 'that' is not given there, so how it came to mean that is not settled.

这 (zhè) 二.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — zhè, èr]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — four strokes, then an ear.
那 (nà).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, four strokes. A horizontal that turns down and hooks. Two short horizontals inside it. Then a left-falling stroke from the top, down through them all.
The ear, two strokes. One stroke that runs right, loops down twice like a folded ear and hooks. Then a long vertical down its left edge.
4 + 2 = six.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 那 (nà)? (Two — four strokes, then the ear.) Which stroke of the left piece is last? (The left-falling stroke through it all.) Which is the last stroke of the character? (The long vertical.)
