Sanskrit, chapter 373: Permission First.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अनुमतिः (anumatiḥ) — permission.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for holds up, then the Sanskrit for removes.

You'll want to know: अनुमतिः.
अनुमतिः — anumatiḥ — "permission".
यदि पिता अनुमतिं ददाति, तर्हि अहं गमिष्यामि। yadi pitā anumatiṃ dadāti, tarhi ahaṃ gamiṣyāmi. If father gives permission, I will go.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अनुमतिः (anumatiḥ) is built on anu, "after", and the root √man, "to think"; MW gives anu-mati "assent, permission, approbation" (MW).

निषेधति — niṣedhati — "forbids".
यदि वैद्यः निषेधति, तर्हि सः मिष्टान्नं न खादति। yadi vaidyaḥ niṣedhati, tarhi saḥ miṣṭānnaṃ na khādati. If the doctor forbids it, he does not eat sweets.
Past (he …): सः मिष्टान्नं निषिद्धवान्, saḥ miṣṭānnaṃ niṣiddhavān.

The word, taken apart.
निषेधति (niṣedhati) is built on ni and the root √sidh, "to keep off"; MW gives ni-√ṣidh "to keep off, to prevent, to prohibit" and ni-ṣiddha "forbidden" (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for permission, then the Sanskrit for forbids.

You'll want to know: प्रतिबन्धः.
प्रतिबन्धः — pratibandhaḥ — "a restriction; an impediment".
यदि प्रतिबन्धः अस्ति, तर्हि कः अपि न प्रविशतु। yadi pratibandhaḥ asti, tarhi kaḥ api na praviśatu. If there is a restriction, let no one go in.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
प्रतिबन्धः (pratibandhaḥ) is built on prati and the root √bandh, "to bind"; MW gives prati-bandha "an obstacle, a hindrance, an impediment", and "a restriction" is how modern Sanskrit uses it (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अपवादः (apavādaḥ) — an exception.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for forbids, then the Sanskrit for a restriction.

You'll want to know: अपवादः.
अपवादः — apavādaḥ — "an exception".
यदि नियमः अस्ति, तर्हि अपवादः अपि भवति। yadi niyamaḥ asti, tarhi apavādaḥ api bhavati. If there is a rule, there is also an exception.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
अपवादः (apavādaḥ) is built on apa, "away", and the root √vad, "to speak"; MW gives apa-vāda "a special rule setting aside a general one, an exception" (MW).
