Urdu, chapter 366: What Must Happen First.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
صرف (sirf) — only.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a change, then the Urdu for to land in trouble.

You'll want to know: صرف.
صرف — sirf — "only".
اگر صرف ایک ٹکٹ ہے تو آپ جائیے۔ agar sirf ek ṭikaṭ hai to āp jāiye. If there is only one ticket, you go.

The word, taken apart.
صرف (sirf) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ṣirf, "pure, unmixed": that and nothing else.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
منع کرنا (manā karnā) — to forbid; to tell (someone) not to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for permission, then the Urdu for on no account.

You'll want to know: منع کرنا.
منع کرنا — manā karnā — "to forbid; to tell (someone) not to".
اگر استاد نے منع کیا تو ہم نہیں کھیلیں گے۔ agar ustād ne manā kiyā to ham nahīṅ kheleṅ ge. If the teacher says no, we will not play.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے مجھے منع کیا, us ne mujhe manā kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
منع کرنا (manā karnā) is built from manā, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic manʿ, "holding back, prevention", and کرنا, "to do".
