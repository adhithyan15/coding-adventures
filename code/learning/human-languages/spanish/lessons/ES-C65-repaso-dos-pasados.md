# Review — two pasts, both finished

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-IMPERFECT-PLURAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "You all ate." (*Comisteis*.) "We used to eat." (*Comíamos*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PRETERITE-COMPLETE, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-STRONG-PRETERITE-2PL, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-COMPLETE, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-IRREGULAR-PLURAL] -->

Two whole tenses closed, and each closed on one small thing.

[PAUSE 2s] **The preterite** ran out of gaps when a single ending, **-isteis**,
turned out to cover every verb that is not an *-ar* verb — and the strong stems
with it. *Comisteis. Tuvisteis.* One syllable, and the last hole was filled.

[PAUSE 2s] **The imperfect** closed on *comíamos*, and on the only three verbs
that refuse the pattern: *éramos*, *íbamos*, *veíamos*.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTHOGRAPHY-IMPERFECT-ACCENT, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-IMPERFECT-PLURAL, ES-GRAMMAR-DAR-PRETERITE, ES-LEX-DAR] -->

Set the two imperfect sets side by side and one of them is covered in accents.

> *hablábamos* — one accent, in one form.
> *comíamos* — an accent on **every** form of the set.

That is not a spelling habit. In *comíamos* the *í* and the *a* are two separate
syllables, and Spanish would otherwise run them together into one. The accent is
what keeps them apart — take it off and the word stops being a past tense.

[PAUSE 2s] And one verb sits across the line. ***Dar*** is an *-ar* verb, yet in
the preterite it took the *-er* endings and went as bare as *vi*: **di**. I gave.
