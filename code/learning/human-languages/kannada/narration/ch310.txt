Kannada, chapter 310: Telling a Story in Order.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha) — clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "to sweep" and "to wash". (guḍisu, toḷe.) Then "There was a small shop in our town." (namma ūrinalli ondu cikka aṅgaḍi ittu.)

You'll want to know: ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ.
ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ — svaccha — "clean".
ನಿನ್ನೆ ನಾನು ನನ್ನ ಕೋಣೆ ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ ಮಾಡಿದೆ. ninne nānu nanna kōṇe svaccha māḍide. — "Yesterday I cleaned my room."
ಈಗ ಕೋಣೆ ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾಗಿದೆ. īga kōṇe svacchavāgide. — "Now the room is clean."
ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha) ಮಾಡು, "to make clean", is how Kannada says "to clean", the way ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡು says "to work". To say something is clean, add ಆಗಿದೆ, "has become, is", joined by a -ವ-: ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾಗಿದೆ. In front of a noun it takes -ವಾದ: ಸ್ವಚ್ಛವಾದ ನೀರು (svacchavāda nīru, "clean water").
Say the middle slowly: svac-cha. Hold the c, then let a puff of breath out with the second one. That second consonant is a letter you have not met yet, written small under ಚ. The next lesson takes it out.

The word, taken apart.
ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha) is Sanskrit, kept whole as a tatsama. Monier-Williams gives svaccha as "very transparent or clear", and also "pure", and writes it sv-accha: su-, "well, very", and accha, "clear". The Kannada sense, "clean", sits close to that "clear, pure": nothing in the way, nothing on it.

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 6.
ಛ (cha) — the breath partner of ಚ, taken from ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ಛ and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: which letter is ಚ? (ca.) And ಧ? (ದ with the breath let out, dha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha), "clean". You met it a moment ago. Its second c carries the letter of this lesson.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ಛ.
ಛ — cha.
It is ಚ (ca) with a puff of breath after it. Hold a hand in front of your mouth: on ca the air stays put, on cha it comes out. The ch of the romanization is that breath. It is not the English ch of church; that sound is already ಚ.
It is the breath pair you were missing: you can read the others, from ಕ ಖ to ಬ ಭ, and now ಚ ಛ (cha).
Its shape does not grow out of ಚ the way ಧ grows out of ದ. In print ಛ (cha) has a small closed loop on the left, a round, cup-shaped body, a right side that rises and bends over to the right at the top, and a short straight tail hanging from the bottom of the cup. That tail is the breath mark you know from ಧ and ಢ.
In ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha) you met it small: ಚ್ಛ is ಚ with a reduced ಛ (cha), without its top hook, written under it. That is how this script writes two consonants with no vowel between them, as ವ sits small under ಸ in ಸ್ವ. Most words that hold ಛ (cha) came from Sanskrit.

Writing: ಛ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ಛ (cha) and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large. Check the copy against the print: the loop on the left, the cup, the hook at the top right, the short tail.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this character or which way to travel. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 6.
ಅದರ ನಂತರ (adara nantara) — first, after that, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "afterwards" (āmēle) and "later" (nantara). Then the word for clean. (svaccha.) Which letter sits small inside it? (ಛ, cha.)

You'll want to know: ಮೊದಲು … ಅದರ ನಂತರ … ಕೊನೆಗೆ.
ಮೊದಲು ನಾನು ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದೆ. ಅದರ ನಂತರ ಶಾಲೆಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. modalu nānu kāphi kuḍide. adara nantara śālege hōde. — "First I drank coffee. After that I went to school."
ಮೊದಲು (modalu, "first, at first") opens a story. ಅದರ ನಂತರ (adara nantara) is ನಂತರ, "later", with ಅದರ (adara, "of that") in front: "after that". ಆಮೇಲೆ does the same job, and you will hear both.
ಕೊನೆಗೆ ರಾತ್ರಿ ಹತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬಂದೆ. konege rātri hattu gaṇṭege manege bande. — "In the end I came home at ten at night."
A told story often runs on three posts: ಮೊದಲು … ಅದರ ನಂತರ (adara nantara) (or ಆಮೇಲೆ) … ಕೊನೆಗೆ.

You'll want to know: before and after a thing.
ಊಟದ ನಂತರ ನಾವು ಸಿನಿಮಾಗೆ ಹೋದೆವು. ūṭada nantara nāvu sinimāge hōdevu. — "After the meal we went to the cinema."
ಊಟದ is ಊಟ with -ದ, "of": "after (of) the meal". ಮೊದಲು works for "before", but it takes the "to" ending instead: ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಮೊದಲು (ūṭakke modalu, "before the meal"). Each word keeps its own ending.
With a number, ನಂತರ counts time:
ಎರಡು ದಿನಗಳ ನಂತರ ಅವನು ಬಂದನು. eraḍu dinagaḷa nantara avanu bandanu. — "Two days later he came."
ದಿನಗಳ (dinagaḷa, "of days") is ದಿನ with the plural -ಗಳ.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ಬಂದು (bandu) — having come, and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "First I drank coffee. After that I went to school." (modalu nānu kāphi kuḍide. adara nantara śālege hōde.)
[pause 1 second]
Two sentences, two full verbs. Kannada can fold them into one.

You'll want to know: the -ಇ and -ಉ form.
ನಾನು ಮನೆಗೆ ಬಂದು ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದೆ. nānu manege bandu ūṭa māḍide. — "I came home and had a meal."
The first verb carries no person and no time: ಬಂದು (bandu), "having come". Only the last verb says who and when. So a chain of these runs on to one full verb at the end:
ಅವನು ಅಂಗಡಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಿ ಒಂದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಖರೀದಿಸಿ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬಂದನು. avanu aṅgaḍige hōgi ondu pustaka kharīdisi manege bandanu. — "He went to the shop, bought a book and came home."
Build it from the past without its ending. Where the -ಇ- came in front of the bead (ಮಾಡಿದ-, ಖರೀದಿಸಿದ-), keep the -ಇ and drop the bead: ಮಾಡಿ, ಖರೀದಿಸಿ, ನೋಡಿ. Everywhere else, the last -ಅ becomes -ಉ: ಬಂದ- becomes ಬಂದು (bandu), ಕುಡಿದ- becomes ಕುಡಿದು, ಕೊಟ್ಟ- becomes ಕೊಟ್ಟು, ಇದ್ದ- becomes ಇದ್ದು. Two common verbs go their own way: ಹೋಗು becomes ಹೋಗಿ, ಆಗು becomes ಆಗಿ.
You have met this form inside words already: ಕಳೆದುಹೋಗು, "to get lost", starts with ಕಳೆದು, the -ಉ form of ಕಳೆ, "to lose, to leave behind", and ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು, "please", is "having placed compassion".

You'll want to know: -ಇದ ನಂತರ, after doing.
ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದ ನಂತರ ನಾನು ಮಲಗಿದೆ. ūṭa māḍida nantara nānu malagide. — "After having the meal, I lay down."
ಮಾಡಿದ is the past without its ending, "that was done", the same piece as the ಬಂದ inside ಬಂದಾಗ. Before ನಂತರ it gives "after doing": ಬಂದ ನಂತರ, "after coming"; ನೋಡಿದ ನಂತರ, "after seeing". Where -ಇ and -ಉ tie two actions tightly, -ಇದ ನಂತರ marks a clear step in time.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Having come"? (ಬಂದು (bandu).) "Having done"? (ಮಾಡಿ.) "Having gone"? (ಹೋಗಿ.) Which verb in the chain says who and when? (Only the last.) "After seeing"? (ನೋಡಿದ ನಂತರ.)


Lesson 5 of 6.
(reading) — the day the guests came.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
About ninety words, nearly all met before; the few new forms are glossed after it. The writer is a young woman, telling a friend about last Sunday.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
ಕಳೆದ ಭಾನುವಾರ ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಗೆ ಅತಿಥಿಗಳು ಬಂದರು. ಮೊದಲು ನಾನು ಮತ್ತು ನನ್ನ ತಂಗಿ ಮನೆ ಸ್ವಚ್ಛ (svaccha) ಮಾಡಿದೆವು. ನಾನು ಕೋಣೆ ಗುಡಿಸಿದೆ. ತಂಗಿ ಪಾತ್ರೆ ತೊಳೆದಳು. ಅಮ್ಮ ಅಡುಗೆ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು. ನಾವು ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡುವಾಗ ಫೋನ್ ಬಂತು. ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಮತ್ತು ಅಜ್ಜ ಬಸ್ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದರು. ಅಪ್ಪ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣಕ್ಕೆ ಹೋಗಿ ಅವರನ್ನು ಕರೆದುಕೊಂಡು ಬಂದರು. ನಾವು ಒಟ್ಟಿಗೆ ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದೆವು. ಊಟದ ನಂತರ ಅಜ್ಜ ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದು ಪತ್ರಿಕೆ ಓದಿದರು. ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಒಂದು ಕಥೆ ಹೇಳಿದರು. ಅವರ ಹಳ್ಳಿಯಲ್ಲಿ ಒಂದು ಚಿಕ್ಕ ಶಾಲೆ ಇತ್ತು. ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ ನಡೆದು ಶಾಲೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು. ಈಗ ಅಲ್ಲಿ ಒಂದು ದೊಡ್ಡ ಶಾಲೆ ಇದೆ. ಕೊನೆಗೆ ರಾತ್ರಿ ಹತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಅತಿಥಿಗಳು ಹೊರಟರು. ಆಮೇಲೆ ನಾನು ಮಲಗಿದೆ. ಅದು ಒಳ್ಳೆಯ ದಿನ.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping, then once more, sorting every verb: event, scene, or habit.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
New forms: ಅತಿಥಿಗಳು (atithigaḷu, "guests"), ಅತಿಥಿ with the plural -ಗಳು; ನಿಲ್ದಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ (nildāṇadalli, "at the station"); ಅವರನ್ನು (avarannu, "them"); ಅವರ (avara, "her"), said with respect; ಕರೆದುಕೊಂಡು ಬಂದರು (karedukoṇḍu bandaru, "brought (them)"), literally "having called (them) along, came"; ಹೊರಟರು (horaṭaru, "set off, left"), the past of ಹೊರಡು.
[pause 2 seconds]
The scene is ಮಾಡುವಾಗ and ಅಡುಗೆ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು; the habit is ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು, with ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ; the background is ಇತ್ತು, and ಇದ್ದರು says where the grandparents were. The ಇದೆ of "now" is the present. Every other verb is an event. ಹೋಗಿ, ಕುಡಿದು and ನಡೆದು carry no time of their own: the verb after them does.
