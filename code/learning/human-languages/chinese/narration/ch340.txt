Mandarin Chinese, chapter 340: Old Ways.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
chuántǒng — tradition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for full of festive joy, then the Mandarin for fireworks lighting up the sky.

You'll want to know: chuántǒng.
chuántǒng — "tradition".
guònián chī jiǎozi shì yí ge lǎo chuántǒng — Eating dumplings at the new year is an old tradition.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chuántǒng is built from 传 'to pass on' and tǒng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjwen and thowngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to gather together' and 'the end of a thread'.

guīju — "rules of conduct; manners".
chī niányèfàn yǒu shénme guīju — What are the rules at the reunion dinner?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guīju is built from guī 'a compass' and jǔ 'a set square', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjwie and kjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'round', quoting an older book that uses this very pair, and the second 'a rule'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
节气 (jiéqì) — solar term.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be particular about details, then the Mandarin for the rules of conduct.

You'll want to know: 节气.
节气 — jiéqì — "solar term".
每年有二十四个节气 měinián yǒu èrshísì ge jiéqì — Every year has twenty-four solar terms.

The word, taken apart.
节气 (jiéqì) is built from 节 'a joint' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tset and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a joint of bamboo' and the second 'breath'. The calendar sense is not given there.
