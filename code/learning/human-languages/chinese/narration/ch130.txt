Mandarin Chinese, chapter 130: Feeling Ill.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
病人 (bìngrén) — patient; sick person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for convenient, then the Mandarin for interesting.

You'll want to know: 病人.
病人 — bìngrén — "patient; sick person".
病人想喝水 bìngrén xiǎng hē shuǐ — The patient would like some water.
The new character, in pieces: 病 (bìng) — outside, the sickbed piece of five strokes: a dot, a bar, a long fall down the left, then a dot and a rising stroke on the left side; inside it, five strokes: a bar, a box with a hooked right side, and a small 人 in the box. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
病人 (bìngrén) is built from 病 (bìng) 'illness' and 人 'person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjaengH and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'grief' and 'suffering', and quotes an older book: an illness grown worse.

What you've built.

Script — the sickbed piece around a box.
病 (bìng).
Two pieces: the sickbed piece outside, a boxed piece inside.
The sickbed piece, five strokes. A dot at the top, a horizontal under it, a long left-falling stroke down the left side, then on the left a short dot and a short stroke rising to the right.
The inside piece, five strokes. A horizontal across the top; a short vertical, the left side of the box; a horizontal that turns down the right side and hooks; then a small 人 from the top bar into the box: its left-falling stroke, then its dot.
5 + 5 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 病 (bìng), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 病 (bìng)? (Two — the sickbed piece and the boxed piece.) What sits on the left of the sickbed piece? (A dot and a rising stroke.) What is inside the box? (A small 人.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
gǎnmào — to catch a cold; a cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for interesting, then the Mandarin for a sick person.

You'll want to know: gǎnmào.
gǎnmào — "to catch a cold; a cold".
wǒ gǎnmào le — I've caught a cold.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnmào is built from gǎn and mào, both inherited from Middle Chinese, komX and mawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to move' and the second 'to cover' and 'to wade'; how the pair came to mean a cold is not settled.

看病 — kànbìng — "to see a doctor".
我病了我去看病 wǒ bìng le wǒ qù kànbìng — I'm ill, so I'm off to the doctor.

The word, taken apart.
看病 (kànbìng) is built from 看 'to look at' and 病 (bìng) 'illness', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khanH and bjaengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to have an illness looked at.
