Telugu, chapter 316: Was Doing, Did, Used To.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
వెళ్ళాను (veḷḷānu) — the plain past: what happened.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I go", or "I will go". (nēnu veḷtānu.) Then the last two people you met: the watchman and the milkman. (kāpalādāru, pālavāḍu.)
[pause 1 second]
వెళ్తాను says what happens, or what will. A story has already happened. It needs the past.

You'll want to know: the plain past.
నిన్న నేను పట్టణానికి వెళ్ళాను. ninna nēnu paṭṭaṇāniki veḷḷānu. — "Yesterday I went to the town."
వెళ్ళాను (veḷḷānu) is "I went". Where the form you know has -తా- (వెళ్తాను), the past has -ఆ-: వెళ్ళ + ఆను. The person ending at the end works the way it does in వెళ్తాను:
నేను వెళ్ళాను (nēnu veḷḷānu) — I went.
నువ్వు వెళ్ళావు (nuvvu veḷḷāvu) — you went.
అతను వెళ్ళాడు (atanu veḷḷāḍu) — he went.
ఆమె వెళ్ళింది (āme veḷḷindi) — she went, and "it went".
మేము వెళ్ళాము (mēmu veḷḷāmu) — we went.
వాళ్ళు వెళ్ళారు (vāḷḷu veḷḷāru) — they went; with మీరు, "you went", said with respect.
Only "she" and "it" step out of line: వెళ్ళింది, with -ఇంది.

You'll want to know: the stems that change.
Many verbs take the past on their own stem. A -ఉ- before the last consonant turns to -ఇ-: చదువు becomes చదివాను (cadivānu), "I read"; నడుచు becomes నడిచాను (naḍicānu), "I walked".
A few of the commonest verbs change more, and they are worth learning as pairs:
వచ్చు becomes వచ్చాను (vaccānu), "I came".
చూడు becomes చూశాను (cūśānu), "I saw"; she saw: చూసింది (cūsindi).
చేయు becomes చేశాను (cēśānu), "I did"; she did: చేసింది (cēsindi).
తిను becomes తిన్నాను (tinnānu), "I ate".
ఇవ్వు becomes ఇచ్చాను (iccānu), "I gave".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She came in the evening" — āme sāyantraṁ vaccindi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "We drank tea" — mēmu ṭī tāgāmu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I read a book. After that I had a meal." — nēnu oka pustakaṁ cadivānu. ā taruvāta bhōjanaṁ cēśānu.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I went"? (వెళ్ళాను (veḷḷānu).) "He came"? (వచ్చాడు.) "She saw"? (చూసింది.) "I ate"? (తిన్నాను.) Which person takes -ఇంది instead of -ఆ-? (She, and it.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
చేస్తున్నప్పుడు (cēstunnappuḍu) — the scene and the event.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I am eating now". (nēnu ippuḍu tiṇṭunnānu.) Then "I ate". (tinnānu.)
[pause 1 second]
The first is going on. The second is done. A told story needs both, one inside the other.

You'll want to know: -తున్నప్పుడు, while it was going on.
నేను భోజనం చేస్తున్నప్పుడు ఫోను వచ్చింది. nēnu bhōjanaṁ cēstunnappuḍu phōnu vaccindi. — "While I was having a meal, a call came."
Take చేస్తున్నాను, "I am doing", drop the person ending, and add అప్పుడు, "then, at that time": చేస్తున్న + అప్పుడు becomes చేస్తున్నప్పుడు (cēstunnappuḍu), "at the time (I) was doing".
That clause is the scene. The thing that cut into it is the event, in the plain past: వచ్చింది. Telugu says a call "came", where English says the phone rang.
మేము నడుస్తున్నప్పుడు అకస్మాత్తుగా వర్షం పడింది. mēmu naḍustunnappuḍu akasmāttugā varṣaṁ paḍindi. — "We were walking when suddenly it rained."
వర్షం (varṣaṁ, "rain") with పడింది (paḍindi, "fell"): rain "falls" in Telugu.

Grammar Lens: 'was doing' on its own.
Telugu has no separate tense for "was doing". The same form that says "I am reading" says "I was reading" once a past time sets the scene:
నిన్న రాత్రి తొమ్మిది గంటలకు నేను పుస్తకం చదువుతున్నాను. ninna rātri tommidi gaṇṭalaku nēnu pustakaṁ caduvutunnānu. — "At nine last night I was reading a book."
The past is carried by నిన్న రాత్రి, not by the verb. ఉన్నాను, the "am" inside చదువుతున్నాను, works the same way: > నిన్న నేను ఇంట్లో ఉన్నాను.
ninna nēnu iṇṭlō unnānu. — "Yesterday I was at home."
ఇంట్లో (iṇṭlō, "at home") is the ఇంటి- of ఇంటికి with -లో, "in".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which form paints the scene? (-తున్నప్పుడు, or -తున్నాను with a past time.) Which form drops in the event? (The plain past: వచ్చింది, పడింది.) "While I was eating"? (నేను తింటున్నప్పుడు, nēnu tiṇṭunnappuḍu.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
చదివేవాడిని (cadivēvāḍini) — what used to happen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "While I was having a meal, a call came." (nēnu bhōjanaṁ cēstunnappuḍu phōnu vaccindi.)
[pause 1 second]
That happened once. Some things happened many times: what you did every day as a child.

You'll want to know: -ఏవాడిని, 'used to'.
బాల్యంలో నేను ప్రతి రోజు ఒక కథ చదివేవాడిని. bālyaṁlō nēnu prati rōju oka katha cadivēvāḍini. — "In my childhood I used to read a story every day."
చదివేవాడిని (cadivēvāḍini) is "I used to read", said by a man. A woman says చదివేదాన్ని (cadivēdānni). Taken apart, it is the -ఏ form of the verb (చదివే, "who reads") with వాడు, "that man", or దాని, "that one", and the -ని of "I": roughly "I was one who reads".
నేను చదివేవాడిని (nēnu cadivēvāḍini) / చదివేదాన్ని (cadivēdānni) — I used to read.
అతను చదివేవాడు (atanu cadivēvāḍu) — he used to read.
ఆమె చదివేది (āme cadivēdi) — she used to read.
మేము చదివేవాళ్ళం (mēmu cadivēvāḷḷaṁ) — we used to read.
వాళ్ళు చదివేవాళ్ళు (vāḷḷu cadivēvāḷḷu) — they used to read.
The same -ఏ sits on any verb: వెళ్ళేవాడిని, "I used to go"; తినేవాడిని, "I used to eat".

You'll want to know: ఉండేది, 'there used to be'.
మా గ్రామంలో ఒక చిన్న అంగడి ఉండేది. mā grāmaṁlō oka cinna aṅgaḍi uṇḍēdi. — "There used to be a small shop in our village."
ఉండేది is the same form of ఉండు, "to be": "it used to be there". Set it against the present:
ఇప్పుడు అక్కడ ఒక పెద్ద హోటలు ఉంది. ippuḍu akkaḍa oka pedda hōṭalu undi. — "Now there is a big restaurant there."
A story about the old days leans on ఉండేది for the background.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "In my childhood I used to read a story every day" — bālyaṁlō nēnu prati rōju oka katha cadivēvāḍini]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "A woman says: I used to go to school every day" — nēnu prati rōju baḍiki veḷḷēdānni]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My grandmother used to cook every evening" — mā ammamma prati sāyantraṁ vaṇḍēdi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I used to go", said by a man? (వెళ్ళేవాడిని.) By a woman? (వెళ్ళేదాన్ని.) "There used to be"? (ఉండేది.) Which vowel marks the habit? (-ఏ, as in చదివే.)
