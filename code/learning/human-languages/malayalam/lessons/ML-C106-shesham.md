# ശേഷം — after

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01, ML-CONCEPT-C40-OON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *before two o'clock*. Then say the word for the meal.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-BEFORE-01] -->

**ശേഷം** (*śēṣaṁ*) — **after**.

It stands where **മുൻപ്** stands, on the same kind of ending:

> **ഊണിന് ശേഷം** — *ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ* — "**after** the meal."

## Grammar Lens: the same case, wearing its other shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C102-HOUR-DATIVE-01] -->

**ഊണിന്** is the dative again — and it is the **other** of the two shapes you
were given. The hour took **-ക്ക്**; **ഊണ്** takes **-ിന്**, because the ending is
chosen by the sound the word finishes on.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **രണ്ട് മണിക്ക് മുൻപ്** | *raṇṭŭ maṇikkŭ munpŭ* | before two o'clock |
| **ഊണിന് ശേഷം** | *ūṇinŭ śēṣaṁ* | after the meal |

**Same case, two shapes, and both of these words lean on it.** You are not
learning a new piece of machinery — you are hanging two more words on a piece
you have had since your first case ending.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C106-AFTER-01] -->

**ശേഷം** is Sanskrit, and its plain meaning is **the remainder** — what is left
over. *After the meal* is, in the word's own terms, **what is left of the day
once the meal is done**.
