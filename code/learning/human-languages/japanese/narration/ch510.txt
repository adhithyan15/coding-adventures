Japanese, chapter 510: By Chance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぐうぜん (gūzen) — by chance; a coincidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for probability, then the Japanese for to hit (the mark).

You'll want to know: ぐうぜん.
ぐうぜん — gūzen — "by chance; a coincidence".
もし ぐうぜん かれに あったら、てがみを わたして ください。 — moshi gūzen kare ni attara, tegami o watashite kudasai — "If you happen to meet him, please give him the letter."

The word, taken apart.
ぐうぜん (gūzen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are gū, "accidentally" (Middle Chinese nguwH) and zen, "sort of thing" (nyen) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hit (the mark), then the Japanese for by chance.

You'll want to know: きっかけ.
きっかけ — kikkake — "a chance; a start".
もし きっかけが あったら、かれと はなします。 — moshi kikkake ga attara, kare to hanashimasu — "If I get the chance, I will talk to him."

The word, taken apart.
きっかけ (kikkake) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and usually written in kana; its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ふと (futo) — out of the blue; suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for by chance, then the Japanese for a chance.

You'll want to know: ふと.
ふと — futo — "out of the blue; suddenly".
もし ふと めが さめたら、みずを のみます。 — moshi futo me ga sametara, mizu o nomimasu — "If I happen to wake up, I will drink some water."

The word, taken apart.
ふと (futo) is usually counted a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: JMdict marks it as usually written in kana and its rare kanji spellings as ateji, chosen for their sound, and no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.
いかす (ikasu) — to make good use of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a chance, then the Japanese for out of the blue.

You'll want to know: いかす.
いかす — ikasu — "to make good use of".
もし ちえを いかしたら、いい しごとが みつかるでしょう。 — moshi chie o ikashitara, ii shigoto ga mitsukaru deshō — "If you put that know-how to good use, you will probably find a good job."
Polite いかします, ikashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かれが ちえを いかしました。, kare ga chie o ikashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
いかす (ikasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
