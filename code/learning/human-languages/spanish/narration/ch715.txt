Spanish, chapter 715: Amar y detestar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
amar — to love.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for tender, then the Spanish for to adore.

You'll want to know: amar.
amar — "to love".
Te amo con todo mi corazón. I love you with all my heart.
Deep, romantic love; with friends and family people usually say te quiero.

The word, taken apart.
amar is inherited from Latin amāre "to love" (EtymDB); English amateur, one who does something for love, comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
detestar — to detest, to loathe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to adore, then the Spanish for to love.

You'll want to know: detestar.
detestar — "to detest, to loathe".
Detesto las mentiras. I loathe lies.
Close to odiar, and a little more formal. It takes a direct object: detesto el frío.

The word, taken apart.
detestar is a learned borrowing from Latin dētestārī "to curse, to call the gods to witness against", built on testis "witness".

Lesson 4 of 5.
besar — to kiss.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to detest, then the Spanish for in love.

You'll want to know: besar.
besar — "to kiss".
Me besó en la cara. She kissed me on the face.
Un beso is a kiss; besos closes many messages.

The word, taken apart.
besar is usually traced to Latin bāsiāre "to kiss" (Lewis & Short), built on bāsium "a kiss"; the Latin -si- became -s-.
