# Unit 1: Greetings & Introductions

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] No prior unit to recall from — this is session 1. Take a breath.
Today you learn how to say hello and introduce yourself in Spanish.

## New Material

**hola** — hello
> Etymology: uncertain / contested. Often traced to an Old Spanish hailing
> call *¡ola!*, possibly nautical in origin. A popular claim links it to
> English "hello/holler" — that link is **not** established; treat it as a
> fun coincidence, not a shared root.

**buenos días** — good morning (literally "good days")
> Etymology: *bueno* ← Latin *bonus* ("good" — cognate with English *bonus*,
> *bounty*, *bona fide*). *día* ← Latin *dies* ("day" — cognate with English
> *diurnal*, and via French, *journal*/*journey*, both originally "a day's
> [amount of something]").

**buenas tardes** — good afternoon
> Etymology: *tarde* ← Latin *tardus* ("slow, late" — cognate with English
> *tardy*). The shift from "late" to "afternoon" makes sense: the afternoon
> is the "late" part of the day.

**buenas noches** — good evening / good night
> Etymology: *noche* ← Latin *nox, noctis* ("night" — cognate with English
> *nocturnal*, *equinox* ["equal night"]).

**¿cómo estás?** — how are you?
> Etymology: *cómo* ← Latin *quomodo* ("in what way"). *estás* is from the
> verb *estar* ← Latin *stare* ("to stand" — cognate with English *stand*,
> *state*, *status*). You'll get the full *estar* story in Unit 5 — for now,
> just learn the phrase whole.

**me llamo...** — my name is... (literally "I call myself...")
> Etymology: *llamar* ← Latin *clamare* ("to shout, call out" — cognate with
> English *claim*, *exclaim*, *acclaim*, *clamor*). Spanish uses the
> reflexive: you're literally saying "I call myself ___."

## Grammar Lens

A quick preview, not a full lesson yet: *me llamo* is built from a
**reflexive verb** — one where the action bounces back onto the person
doing it. Literally, *me llamo* is "I call myself," not just "I am named."
English *can* do this ("I call myself Sam"), but usually doesn't bother —
we just say "I'm Sam." Spanish, here, grammatically requires the
bounce-back. You'll get the full mechanics of reflexive verbs in a later
chapter (daily-routine verbs like "I get myself dressed" work the same
way) — for now, just notice that *me* is doing real grammatical work, not
decoration.
