# Four lines, and the word that has a twin

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson asked for writing **finished on time**. This one asks
for writing that **holds together**.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C48-DENY-04, LA-LEX-C52-ASK-03, LA-LEX-C48-DENY-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can be
connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a
sentence stops standing on its own.

[PAUSE 2s] You hold four joiners. **et** adds. **sed** sets against. **aut**
offers. **quia** gives the reason.

[PAUSE 2s] Use **quia** for your reason, and use it deliberately, because Latin
has a second word that also means *because* — **quod** — and that one is doing
two jobs at once. **quod** is also the ordinary word for *which*.

[PAUSE 2s] So a sentence built on **quod** hands the reader a small decision the
writer may not have noticed making: is this a reason, or is it a relative
clause? **quia** hands them nothing to decide. At this level, write the word that
can only mean one thing.

[PAUSE 2s] That is a real habit and not a beginner's crutch. Choosing the
unambiguous word where one exists is most of what clear writing is, in any
language.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C52-ASK-03, LA-LEX-C49-FRAME-04] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the person you picked.

At least one must use **et**, **sed** or **aut**. At least one must use
**quia**.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words and forms the book has shown you. This lesson adds no
vocabulary; the whole exercise is the joining.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C52-ASK-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Cover your first line and read the second alone. Does it still make
sense without the first? If it does, you wrote a **list** — which is where you
were one lesson ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Which of Latin's two words for *because* can also mean *which*?
(**quod.**) And which one did you write? (**quia** — the one that cannot be
mistaken.)
