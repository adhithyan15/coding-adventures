Lesson 1 of 5.
बरोबर (barobar) — with.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can say where a thing is. You cannot yet say who is there with you — the book has taught मित्र since chapter eighteen with no way to attach him to anything.
[your turn — recall: say ḍāvā, then ujvā, then lāmb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: बरोबर.
बरोबर — barobar — with, together with.
मी मित्राबरोबर जातो. — mī mitrābarobar jāto. — I go with my friend.
Another postposition, and the same oblique stem you have been bending for four chapters: मित्र becomes मित्रा-, then बरोबर (barobar). Nothing new about the noun.
The word has a second life you should meet now, because you will hear it far more often than the first. Said alone, बरोबर (barobar) means that's right — agreement, confirmation, correct. Look at the base it shares with बरं, the fine, good of chapter four: बरोबर (barobar) is good-alongside, and a thing that lines up alongside another is both with it and right.
बरोबर! — barobar! — that's right!
So one word gives you a way to travel with somebody and a way to agree with them.

Lesson 2 of 5.
माझ्याबरोबर (mājhyābarobar) — a pronoun bends too.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
With my friend works. Try with me and you will find you cannot say it: मी बरोबर (barobar) is not Marathi.

Grammar Lens: the pronoun goes through its possessive first.
A noun takes an oblique stem before a postposition. A pronoun does too, and its oblique stem is built on the possessive you already know rather than on the subject form:
मी becomes माझ्या- becomes माझ्याबरोबर (mājhyābarobar) — with me तू becomes तुझ्या- becomes तुझ्याबरोबर — with you तुम्ही becomes तुमच्या- becomes तुमच्याबरोबर — with you (respectful).
Read the middle column. Each one is माझं / तुझं / तुमचं with its ending changed to -या, which is exactly what a noun in -ए did back in chapter forty-one. The machinery is the same; only the starting form is different, and the starting form is the possessive.
That is the rule worth holding, because it saves you from guessing every time: to put a pronoun in front of a postposition, take its possessive and bend that.

[your turn — say: mājhyābarobar — with me]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tumchyābarobar — with you, respectfully]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which form each one started from — माझं, तुमचं]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Say 'with me' and 'with you' politely.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which form of a pronoun does the oblique start from? (The possessive.) So what is with you, said to a stranger? (तुमच्याबरोबर.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
-शी (-śī) — with, to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
बरोबर (barobar) puts two people side by side. It does not work when the second person is the one you are TALKING to.

You'll want to know: -शी.
-शी — -śī — with, to.
मी मित्राशी बोलतो. — mī mitrāśī bolto. — I speak to my friend.
-शी (-śī) is glued straight onto the oblique stem with no space, which is the first difference from बरोबर (barobar). The second is the job. बरोबर (barobar) is alongside — two people going somewhere together. -शी (-śī) is engaged with — the partner a verb reaches toward.
That is why बोलणे takes it. English says speak TO; Marathi says मित्राशी, and the choice is not free: मित्राबरोबर बोलतो would mean you and your friend were both speaking, side by side, to somebody else.
Keep the pair as a contrast rather than as two words:
मित्राबरोबर — alongside my friend. मित्राशी — engaged with my friend.

Lesson 4 of 5.
-ने (-ne) — by, with (an instrument).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two ways of being with somebody, and still no way of saying what you did it with.

You'll want to know: -ने.
-ने — -ne — by, with (an instrument).
मी डोळ्याने पाहतो. — mī ḍoḷyāne pāhto. — I see with my eye.
English overloads one word: with my friend, with my eye. Marathi refuses to. A companion takes बरोबर (barobar) or -शी (-śī); a tool takes -ने (-ne), and the two sets never swap. Ask yourself which one the thing is — a person you are with, or a thing you are using — and the ending picks itself.
-ने (-ne) glues to the oblique stem like everything else in this chapter, and a pronoun in front of it bends the same way: माझ्याने, by me.
One thing to file away rather than learn now. This same -ने (-ne) has a second and much larger job in Marathi: it also marks the SUBJECT of a past transitive verb, which is a rule no European language has. That is a later chapter. For now, note only that the ending you have just met is one you will meet again wearing a different hat.
