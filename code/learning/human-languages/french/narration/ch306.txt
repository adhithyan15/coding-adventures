French, chapter 306: Petits accidents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
étouffer — to choke (as s'étouffer); to suffocate, to stifle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to survive, then the French for the catastrophe.

You'll want to know: étouffer.
étouffer — "to choke (as s'étouffer); to suffocate, to stifle".
Il s'est étouffé avec un bonbon. He choked on a sweet.
For choking on food, the reflexive s'étouffer. Without se, to smother or to stifle: on étouffe ici! — it is stifling in here.

The word, taken apart.
étouffer comes from Old French estouffer (EtymDB); the origin of the root is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'intoxication — the poisoning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the catastrophe, then the French for to choke.

You'll want to know: l'intoxication.
l'intoxication — "the poisoning".
Toute la famille a eu une intoxication alimentaire. The whole family had food poisoning.
Une intoxication alimentaire — food poisoning. It does not mean being drunk.

The word, taken apart.
intoxication is built on intoxiquer, from Medieval Latin intoxicāre (EtymDB), built on toxicum "poison", a borrowing from Greek toxikón "poison for arrows".

Lesson 3 of 5.
piquer — to sting, to bite (of insects); to prick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to choke, then the French for the poisoning.

You'll want to know: piquer.
piquer — "to sting, to bite (of insects); to prick".
Un insecte l'a piqué au bras. An insect stung him on the arm.
Informal: piquer also means to pinch, to steal.

The word, taken apart.
piquer is inherited from Vulgar Latin piccāre "to prick, to sting" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
la morsure — the bite (a wound).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to sting, then the French for to bite.

You'll want to know: la morsure.
la morsure — "the bite (a wound)".
La morsure du chien n'était pas grave. The dog bite was not serious.
For insects, French uses the verb piquer.

The word, taken apart.
morsure is built on mors, the old past participle of mordre "to bite", from Latin morsus "a bite" (Lewis & Short).
