# संध्याकाळ (sandhyākāḷ) — evening

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-SAKAL, MR-LEX-DUPAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two parts of the day, and their at-that-time forms. Say all four
words before you turn to the new one.

## You'll want to know: संध्याकाळ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-SANDHYAKAL]; assesses=[] -->

> **संध्याकाळ** — *sandhyākāḷ* — **evening**

It is the longest word in this chapter, and **you have already read half of
it.** Look at its end:

| | |
|---|---|
| स**काळ** | *sa-**kāḷ*** |
| संध्या**काळ** | *sandhyā-**kāḷ*** |

The morning and the evening **end in the same piece — काळ**. Once you see
that, संध्याकाळ stops being a long new word and becomes a short new piece —
**संध्या** — in front of one you can already read.

That is worth doing to any long word in this language before deciding it is
hard: look for the part of it you have met.

## Grammar Lens: third time, same ending
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-TIME-II, MR-LEX-SANDHYAKAL] -->

> **मी संध्याकाळी येतो.**
> — *mī sandhyākāḷī yeto.* — **I come in the evening.**

| the part of the day | at that time |
|---|---|
| **सकाळ** | **सकाळी** |
| **दुपार** | **दुपारी** |
| **संध्याकाळ** | **संध्याकाळी** |

Three for three. The ending has not cared how long the word was, where the
stress fell, or what letter came before it.
