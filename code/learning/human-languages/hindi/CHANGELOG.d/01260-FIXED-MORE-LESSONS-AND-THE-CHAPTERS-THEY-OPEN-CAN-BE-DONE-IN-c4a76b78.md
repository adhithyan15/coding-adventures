## Fixed — more lessons, and the chapters they open, can be done in the car

A chapter whose first lesson a driver cannot do is introduced in the narration with "The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped." These lessons needed eyes or hands for only one sentence, or for a phrase that was never about the page. The step is now deferred as an authored cue, said for the ear, or moved into a section the car sets aside. The book keeps every step (human-language-data: `chaptersWithoutDrivablePrefixByContent`).

- **4 anchor-word lessons** (HI-C14-ghee, HI-C15-dhol, HI-C22-os, HI-C22-uun): the anchor word's "Look at its shape as you say it: …" is now `[YOU LOOK: at its shape as you say it.]`, so the narration says it after "once you have stopped driving" and the rest of the lesson, which teaches the word by ear, is drivable at the core.
- **HI-C73-kar**: the gloss of the model sentence is quoted (**"I look at the book and come."**), so it is no longer read as an instruction to look at the page.
- **HI-C80-aasaan-mushkil** and **HI-C82-lambaa** said "Look at the ending" and "look at the last letter" to tell bending adjectives from fixed ones. The -ā is just as audible, so they now say "Listen for the ending" and "listen for the last sound".
