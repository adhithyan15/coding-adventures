# સારું (sārũ) — "good," "okay"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The all-purpose "good / fine / okay / alright" — and your first taste
of Gujarati's three genders.

## The letters in this word

**સા** (*sā*) + **રું** (*rũ*, with the nasal *anusvāra* on the *u*) → **સારું**
(*sārũ*).

## The word, taken apart

**સારું** (*sārũ*, "good, fine, okay") is from Sanskrit **સાર** (*sāra*,
"essence, substance, the best part") — the same *sāra* behind Bengali's *sāramilla*
("no matter"). As "okay/agreed" it oils every Gujarati conversation, the way
English uses "alright."

## Grammar Lens: three genders, on the adjective

Look at that ending **-ું** (*-ũ*). Gujarati, like Marathi, keeps **three**
genders from Sanskrit — masculine, feminine, **and neuter** — where Hindi and
Punjabi kept only two. An adjective agrees with its noun in all three:

- **સારો** (*sāro*) — masculine
- **સારી** (*sārī*) — feminine
- **સારું** (*sārũ*) — **neuter**

Standing alone as "okay," it takes the neuter *sārũ*. You will meet this
three-way agreement on every adjective and possessive.
