# la vía — the track

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-01, ES-LEX-C322-WHEN-15] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el andén** and **todavía**. One of those two has a road
inside it, and it is not the one at the station.

## You'll want to know: la vía
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C439-VIA-01] -->

**la vía** — the track, the way, the route.

> **Vía** 4. Tren con destino Valencia.

*No cruce la vía* — do not cross the track. *La vía pública* — the public
highway, the wording on every municipal notice. *Por vía aérea* — by air.

The **andén** is where you stand; the **vía** is the rails in front of it. A
station announcement gives you both, and mixing them up will put you on the
platform edge.

## Grammar Lens: the accent that breaks a diphthong
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02, ES-LEX-C322-WHEN-15] -->

**vía** is two syllables: **VÍ-a**. Without the accent, *via* would be one
syllable, because **i** and **a** together normally fuse.

| written | syllables |
|---|---|
| v**ía** | VÍ-a |
| d**ía** | DÍ-a |
| fam**ilia** | fa-MI-lia |

The mark on the **i** is not about stress position at all here. It is there
to say: **this vowel refuses to join the next one**. Spanish has no other way
to write that, so the accent does double duty across the language — sometimes
marking stress, sometimes prising two vowels apart.

*Todavía* has it for the same reason, and you have been reading it correctly
for chapters without being told why.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-01] -->

Latin **via**, road — the word on every Roman milestone.

And here is the payoff. **Todavía** is *toda vía*: **all the way**. It meant
"by every route, in any case", and from there slid to "still" — the sense of
something that has gone the whole distance and is *still* going.

The family is everywhere once you see it: *el viaje*, a journey; *el
viaducto*, a way carried over; English **via**, **obvious** (*ob-viam*, lying
in the way), **trivial** (*tri-vium*, a place where three roads meet, so
common it is worth nothing), and **voyage**.
