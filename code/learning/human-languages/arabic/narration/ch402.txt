Arabic, chapter 402: How sure.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حتما (ḥatman) — inevitably.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to consider likely, then the Arabic for to rule out.

You'll want to know: حتما.
حتما — ḥatman — "inevitably".
سينجح حتما. sa-yanjaḥu ḥatman. He is bound to succeed.

The word, taken apart.
حتما (ḥatman) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-t-m, "to make firm, to decree" (Lane). Ḥatman is the verbal noun ḥatm, decreeing, with the adverb ending -an: as decreed, inevitably.

Lesson 2 of 5.
بالضرورة (bi-ḍ-ḍarūra) — necessarily.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to rule out, then the Arabic for inevitably.

You'll want to know: بالضرورة.
بالضرورة — bi-ḍ-ḍarūra — "necessarily".
الغالي ليس بالضرورة أفضل. al-ghālī laysa bi-ḍ-ḍarūra afḍal. Expensive is not necessarily better.

The word, taken apart.
بالضرورة (bi-ḍ-ḍarūra) is built on the native Arabic root ḍ-r-r, "to harm; to compel" (Lane). Ḍarūra is necessity, what one is compelled to; bi-ḍ-ḍarūra is by necessity. ضروري, necessary, is from the same root.

Lesson 4 of 5.
إطلاقا (iṭlāqan) — not at all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for necessarily, then the Arabic for relatively.

You'll want to know: إطلاقا.
إطلاقا — iṭlāqan — "not at all".
لا أحب القهوة إطلاقا. lā uḥibbu al-qahwa iṭlāqan. I do not like coffee at all.
With a negative: لا … إطلاقا (iṭlāqan), not … at all.

The word, taken apart.
إطلاقا (iṭlāqan) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-l-q, "to be free, let loose" (Lane). Iṭlāqan is the Form IV verbal noun, setting loose, with the adverb ending -an: without limit, and with a negative, not at all.

You'll want to know: يقين.
يقين — yaqīn — "certainty".
عندي يقين بأنه صادق. ʿindī yaqīn bi-annahu ṣādiq. I am certain that he is honest.

The word, taken apart.
يقين (yaqīn) is built on the native Arabic root y-q-n, usually traced to the sense "to be sure". Yaqīn is certainty, knowledge without doubt.
