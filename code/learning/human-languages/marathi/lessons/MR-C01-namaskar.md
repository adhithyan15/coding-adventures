# Namaskar — first hear the greeting

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The first Marathi word — and the everyday greeting of Maharashtra.
First, only listen: *namaskār*.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-NAMASKAR-01]; assesses=[] -->

*Namaskār* is how you greet someone, and how you take your leave. Hear its
beats slowly: *na-ma-skār*. Answer with the same greeting once.

The whole Devanagari spelling is deliberately absent from this first pass. The
lessons right after this one give your eye and hand one shape at a time, and
the whole written greeting arrives only when every piece of it is already
yours.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-NAMASKAR-01] -->

*Namaskār* = Sanskrit *namaḥ* ("a bow") + *kāra* ("the making of") —
literally "the making of a bow." The same word Kannada and Telugu borrowed,
and close kin to Hindi's *namaskār*. But note: where Hindi leans on *namaste*,
Marathi's default, all-purpose greeting is **namaskār** — one of many small
ways the two Devanagari languages differ.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-NAMASKAR-01] -->

Said with palms pressed together, respectful and neutral — hello and goodbye,
any time, to almost anyone. Warmer and more casual: *kāy mhaṇtā?* ("what do you
say?" ≈ "how's it going?") among friends.

## Writing — let your finger meet one shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-NAMASKAR-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: observe-trace -->

Here is the first printed shape of the greeting's written form: **न**. Follow
that shape once with your finger while you say *namaskār*.

Do not write it yet, and do not try to read it. The shape gets its own short
lesson later in this chapter; tracing a typeface here does not claim a
handwriting stroke order.
