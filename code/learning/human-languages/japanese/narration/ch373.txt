Japanese, chapter 373: Through the Ticket Barrier.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かいさつ (kaisatsu) — ticket inspection; the ticket barrier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a waiting room, then the Japanese for a vending machine.

You'll want to know: かいさつ.
かいさつ — kaisatsu — "ticket inspection; the ticket barrier".
かいさつの まえで あにを まちました。 — kaisatsu no mae de ani o machimashita — "I waited for my brother in front of the ticket barrier."

The word, taken apart.
かいさつ (kaisatsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "examine" (Middle Chinese kojX) and satsu, "tag" (tsreat) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
とおりすぎる (tōrisugiru) — to pass by; to go past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a book of tickets, then the Japanese for fare adjustment.

You'll want to know: とおりすぎる.
とおりすぎる — tōrisugiru — "to pass by; to go past".
でんしゃは ちいさい えきを とおりすぎました。 — densha wa chiisai eki o tōrisugimashita — "The train went past the small station."
Polite とおりすぎます, tōrisugimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): とっきゅうが えきを とおりすぎました。, tokkyū ga eki o tōrisugimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とおりすぎる (tōrisugiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tōri, "passing" (from tōru) and sugiru, "to go beyond"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
