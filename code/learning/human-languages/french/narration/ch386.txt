French, chapter 386: Les accusations.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
accuser — to accuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the certificate, then the French for the official statement.

You'll want to know: accuser.
accuser — "to accuse".
Ils l'accusent d'avoir volé un vélo. They accuse him of stealing a bike.
Accuser quelqu'un de quelque chose.

The word, taken apart.
accuser comes through Old French acuser (EtymDB) from Latin accūsāre "to call to account, to accuse", built from ad and causa "cause, lawsuit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le voleur — the thief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to accuse, then the French for the accusation.

You'll want to know: le voleur.
le voleur — "the thief".
Le voleur est entré par la fenêtre. The thief came in through the window.
Feminine voleuse. Au voleur! — stop thief!

The word, taken apart.
voleur is built from voler "to steal" and -eur. voler "to steal" is the same word as voler "to fly", from Latin volāre "to fly" (Lewis & Short): the falcon that flies off with its prey.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la plainte — the complaint (to the police); the moan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the accusation, then the French for the thief.

You'll want to know: la plainte.
la plainte — "the complaint (to the police); the moan".
Elle a porté plainte à la police. She filed a complaint with the police.
Porter plainte — to file a complaint. From se plaindre.

The word, taken apart.
plainte comes from Old French plainte "lament, complaint" (EtymDB), formed on plaindre, from Latin plangere "to beat the breast, to lament" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: le délit.
le délit — "the misdemeanour, the offence".
Voler dans un magasin est un délit. Shoplifting is an offence.
Less serious than un crime.

The word, taken apart.
délit comes through Old French delit (EtymDB) from Latin dēlictum "a fault, an offence", from dēlinquere "to fail, to do wrong" (Lewis & Short).
