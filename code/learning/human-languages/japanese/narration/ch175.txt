Japanese, chapter 175: Cooking at Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
りょうり (ryōri) — cooking; a dish, cuisine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for plate, then the Japanese for to dish up.

You'll want to know: りょうり.
りょうり — ryōri — "cooking; a dish, cuisine".
あには りょうりが うまいです。 — ani wa ryōri ga umai desu — "My older brother is good at cooking."

The word, taken apart.
りょうり (ryōri) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ryō, "measure, materials" (Middle Chinese lew) and ri, "arrange, reason" (liX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ゆでる (yuderu) — to boil food; to cook in hot water.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to dish up, then the Japanese for cooking.

You'll want to know: ゆでる.
ゆでる — yuderu — "to boil food; to cook in hot water".
あさ たまごを ゆでます。 — asa tamago o yudemasu — "I boil eggs in the morning."
Polite ゆでます, yudemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ゆでる (yuderu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed; JMdict also lists a spelling with the kanji for yu, "hot water", which suggests a link to that word, though the sources here do not settle it (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
