Mandarin Chinese, chapter 299: Help Arrives.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
得救 (déjiù) — to be rescued; to be saved from danger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shout loudly, then the Mandarin for the cry for help.

You'll want to know: 得救.
得救 — déjiù — "to be rescued; to be saved from danger".
事故发生以后大家都得救了 shìgù fāshēng yǐhòu, dàjiā dōu déjiù le — After the accident, everyone was rescued.

The word, taken apart.
得救 (déjiù) is built from 得 'to get' and 救 'to save', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tok and kjuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to gain': to get saved.

You'll want to know: jíshí.
jíshí — "in time; promptly; before it was too late".
yīshēng lái de hěn jíshí — The doctor came in good time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jíshí is built from jí 'to reach' and 时 'time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gip and dzyi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to arrive' and 'to catch up with': to catch the time.

Lesson 4 of 5.
yūndǎo — to faint; to pass out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for before it was too late, then the Mandarin for first aid.

You'll want to know: yūndǎo.
yūndǎo — "to faint; to pass out".
tā tūrán yūndǎo le — She suddenly fainted.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yūndǎo is built from yūn 'dizzy' and dǎo 'to fall over', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjunH and tawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a halo round the sun or moon' and the second 'to fall flat'. The sense 'dizzy' is not given there, and the level tone of yūn is irregular for hjunH.

Lesson 5 of 5.
qiǎngjiù — to fight to save a patient; to give emergency treatment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for first aid, then the Mandarin for to pass out.

You'll want to know: qiǎngjiù.
qiǎngjiù — "to fight to save a patient; to give emergency treatment".
yīshēng zhèngzài qiǎngjiù tā — The doctors are fighting to save him.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qiǎngjiù is built from qiǎng 'to snatch' and 救 'to save', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjangX and kjuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The Guangyun gives the first no gloss in this reading and glosses it 'to resist' and 'to dash' in the level tone; the sense 'to snatch' is not given there.
