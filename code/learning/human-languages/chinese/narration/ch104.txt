Lesson 1 of 7.
本子 (běnzi) — notebook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to repair, then the Mandarin for broken.

You'll want to know: 本子.
本子 — běnzi — "notebook".
我能用你的本子吗 wǒ néng yòng nǐ de běnzi ma — Could I use your notebook?
The new character, in pieces: 本 (běn) — the tree piece of 机, standing alone, with a short bar across its foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
本子 (běnzi) is built from 本 (běn) and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, pwonX and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'root' and explains its shape as a tree with a mark at its foot; how it came to mean a book is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Script — the tree piece, and a bar at its foot.
本 (běn).
The tree piece, four strokes, as on the left of 机 but full width: a horizontal, a vertical down through it, a left-falling stroke and a long right-falling one.
A short horizontal across the vertical, low down: the mark at the root.
4 + 1 = five.

[pause 3 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: qiānbǐ.
qiānbǐ — "pencil".
wǒ yòng qiānbǐ xiě zì — I write with a pencil.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qiānbǐ is built from qiān 'lead' and bǐ 'pen', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ywen and pit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as a grey-blue metal. The regular change from ywen gives yuán, not qiān; how the word came to its sound is not settled.

What you've built.

The word, taken apart.
zhǐ is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where the dictionary says paper is as smooth as a whetstone, and that in the Later Han it was made from old fishing nets and tree bark.
