# ஈரம் (īram) — dampness, moisture

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-GRAMMAR-C284-ONGOING-PAST-01, TA-GRAMMAR-C284-THERE-WAS-01, TA-LEX-C67-WEAR-01, TA-LEX-C44-ASK-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "It was raining." (*maḻai peytukoṇṭiruntatu*.) Then the words for a shirt and a cloth. (*caṭṭai*, *tuṇi*.)

## You'll want to know: ஈரம்
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C285-IRAM-01]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C67-WEAR-01, TA-LEX-C44-ASK-02, TA-LEX-C91-WEATHER-04] -->

**ஈரம்** — *īram* — "dampness, moisture".

> **மழை பெய்தது. என் சட்டை ஈரமாக இருக்கிறது.**
>
> *maḻai peytatu. eṉ caṭṭai īramāka irukkiṟatu.* — "It rained. My shirt is wet."

**ஈரம்** names the wetness itself. To say a thing **is** wet, add the **-ஆக** you know from **நன்றாக** and **மெதுவாக**, then **இருக்கிறது**: **ஈரமாக இருக்கிறது**, "it is damp". In front of a noun it takes **-ஆன**: **ஈரமான துணி** (*īramāṉa tuṇi*, "a wet cloth").

Say the first sound long: *ī-ram*. It is the long *ī* you know as the sign ீ on **நீ**, here standing at the very front of the word. Standing alone, it is a letter of its own, one you have not met as a letter yet. The next lesson takes it out.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ஈரம்** (*īram*) is native Dravidian. DEDR 3690 lists Tamil *īram*, "wet, moisture", in the same entry as *nīr*, "water", the **நீர்** at the end of **தேநீர்**, and sets Malayalam *īram* and Kannada *īra*, "moisture", beside it.
