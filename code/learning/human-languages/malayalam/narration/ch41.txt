Malayalam, chapter 41: Pointing, and Asking.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ഇത് (itŭ) — this one — the thing near me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you POINT at them.

You'll want to know: ഇത്.
ഇത് — itŭ — this one — the thing near me.
Say it, and point while you say it. That is the whole word: it does not mean anything on its own, it means whatever your finger is on.
Its partner is അത് atŭ, which you will meet in a moment. The two of them differ by one sound at the front — i- for near — and that is not a coincidence. It is the whole system, and the last lesson of this chapter says so.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "ഇത് (itŭ)" three times, pointing at something different each time]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: it once with a word you already know after it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is itŭ? (this one — the thing near me.) What sound does it start with, and does that sound mean near, far, or a question? (i- — near.)

Lesson 7 of 7.
i- / a- / e- — six words, one machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the six words from this chapter out loud, in order. Listen to how they begin.

Grammar lens: one slot changes.
You did not learn six words. You learned three beginnings and reused them.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
meaning: near. word: ഇവിടെ iviṭe.
meaning: far. word: അവിടെ aviṭe.
meaning: question. word: എവിടെ eviṭe.
Malayalam spells the pattern out longest — ഇവിടെ (iviṭe), അവിടെ (aviṭe), എവിടെ (eviṭe) — which makes it the easiest to hear.
Change the front, and the meaning walks from near to far to a question. Nothing else in the word moves.
The same three beginnings run the thing-words too: ഇത് itŭ and അത് atŭ start exactly like the first two rows. This chapter did not teach you the matching question word for things — that one arrives later, and when it does you will already know what it has to start with.
This is worth more than the six words are. When you meet a new word in this family later, you will not have to be taught all three of it — you will be taught one and work out the others.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the near word, then the far word, then the question word]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: them again for place, not for things]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — notice: the part that did NOT change]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which beginning means near? (i-) Far? (a-) And which one turns the word into a question? (e-).
