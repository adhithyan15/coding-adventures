## You'll want to know: 树
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C516-TREES516-02]; assesses=[] -->

**树** — *shù* — "tree".

> **秋天的树最好看** *qiūtiān de shù zuì hǎokàn* — Trees look their best in autumn.

The new character, in pieces: 树 — nine strokes in three pieces: on the left the tree piece, four strokes, as in 机; in the middle 又, two strokes, the second shrunk to a short stroke; and on the right a bar, a vertical that hooks to the left, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**树** (*shù*) is inherited from Middle Chinese *dzyuH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the general name for trees and 'to set up'; the dictionary also lists *dzyuX*, glossed 'to prop up'. The falling tone of shù comes from *dzyuH*.
