Japanese, chapter 210: Trees and Growing Things.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
き (ki) — a tree; wood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a cherry tree, then the Japanese for to scatter.

You'll want to know: き.
き — ki — "a tree; wood".
やまには きが おおいです。 — yama ni wa ki ga ōi desu — "There are many trees on the mountain."

The word, taken apart.
き (ki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and in compounds it can take the shape ko (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しょくぶつ (shokubutsu) — a plant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a tree, then the Japanese for to plant.

You'll want to know: しょくぶつ.
しょくぶつ — shokubutsu — "a plant".
この しょくぶつは みずが ひつようです。 — kono shokubutsu wa mizu ga hitsuyō desu — "This plant needs water."

The word, taken apart.
しょくぶつ (shokubutsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shoku, "to plant" (Middle Chinese dzyik) and butsu, "thing" (mjut) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
