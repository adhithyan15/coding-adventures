Lesson 1 of 5.
मसालो (masālo) — spice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for turmeric, then the Marwadi for cumin.

You'll want to know: मसालो.
मसालो — masālo — "spice".

The word, taken apart.
मसालो (masālo) is a loan from Arabic maṣāliḥ, "things that are needful", which reached Marwadi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ṣ-l-ḥ (Lane).

पपीतो — papīto — "a papaya".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of पपीतो (papīto) is not settled. It is usually linked with papaya, a Caribbean word that Spanish and Portuguese sailors spread; CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
खजूर (khajūr) — dates.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a papaya, then the Marwadi for a pomegranate.

You'll want to know: खजूर.
खजूर — khajūr — "dates".

The word, taken apart.
खजूर (khajūr) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kharjūra, "the date-palm". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 3828); the match is through Hindi khajūr and Gujarati khajur.

Lesson 5 of 5.
नारेळ (nāreḷ) — a coconut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a pomegranate, then the Marwadi for dates.

You'll want to know: नारेळ.
नारेळ — nāreḷ — "a coconut".

The word, taken apart.
नारेळ (nāreḷ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nārikela, "coconut palm and fruit". CDIAL lists Old Marwari nārela under it (CDIAL 7075). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word from Dravidian.
