## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C342-CLOCK-02]; assesses=[] -->

**el cuarto** — "the quarter," "a fourth."

You have counted **cuatro** for a long time. This is that number turned into an
order: Latin **quattuor**, *four*, gives **quārtus**, *fourth*, and Spanish
wears that down to **cuarto**.

So *cuatro* and *cuarto* are one letter apart on the page and one step apart in
meaning: the count, and the position in the count. Hearing the difference is
worth practising, because the two are easy to swap.

English counts the same way. A **quarter** is a fourth. A **quart** is a fourth
of a gallon. A **quartet** is four players. And **quarantine** is *quaranta
giorni*, forty days — forty being four tens, the period ships once waited out
before landing.

On a clock, **y cuarto** is *quarter past*: **la una y cuarto**. Spanish also
uses *cuarto* for a room, which is the same idea — a quarter of a house
divided into four.
