Mandarin Chinese, chapter 538: One Thing Leads to Another.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
miǎnde — so as not to; lest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to cause damage, then the Mandarin for how well it works.

You'll want to know: miǎnde.
miǎnde — "so as not to; lest".
nǐ zuìhǎo dài yǔsǎn miǎnde línyǔ — You'd better take an umbrella, so you don't get caught in the rain.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
miǎnde is built from miǎn 'to escape' and 得, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjenX and tok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stop' and 'to get free of'; the second is said light.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

Lesson 3 of 5.
本身 (běnshēn) — itself (in itself).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for so as not to, then the Mandarin for and thereby.

You'll want to know: 本身.
本身 — běnshēn — "itself (in itself)".
这个问题本身不大 zhège wèntí běnshēn bú dà — The problem itself isn't a big one.

The word, taken apart.
本身 (běnshēn) is built from 本 'the root' and 身 'the body', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pwonX and syin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which says, after an older dictionary, that the base of a tree is pwonX.
