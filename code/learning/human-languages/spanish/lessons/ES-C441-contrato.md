# el contrato — drawn together

## You'll want to know: el contrato
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-TRABAJO] -->

**el contrato** — the contract.

*Un contrato de trabajo* is an employment contract; *un contrato de alquiler* is
a tenancy agreement. The verb is **contratar**, to hire or to contract: *me han
contratado* — they have hired me.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-01] -->

**con-** together, plus **-trat-** from Latin **trahere**, to pull or draw.

A contract is **a drawing-together** — two parties pulled into the same
agreement. English says the same thing and has stopped hearing it: *contract*,
and also *contraction*, which is a muscle drawing itself in.

*Trahere* is busy in Spanish too. **traer** is to bring, to draw something to
you; **atraer** is to attract; **contraer** is to contract a debt or an illness,
the same word as the noun above with its verb ending still on.
