Latin, chapter 396: Making a Promise.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
prōmissum, prōmissī — a promise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a putting off, then the Latin for expectation.

You'll want to know: prōmissum, prōmissī.
prōmissum, prōmissī — "a promise".
Prōmissum suum servāvit. He kept his promise.
prōmissum, prōmissī n., the participle of prōmittō: a thing sent forward. Prōmissum servāre, to keep a promise. English promise.

The word, taken apart.
prōmissum is the participle of prōmittō "to send forward, to promise" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from prō and mittō "to send". English promise comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 6.
polliceor, pollicērī — to promise, to offer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for expectation, then the Latin for a promise.

You'll want to know: polliceor, pollicērī.
polliceor, pollicērī — "to promise, to offer".
Pater mihi equum pollicitus est. Father promised me a horse.
Deponent: polliceor, pollicērī, pollicitus sum. Often with the accusative and future infinitive: pollicētur sē ventūrum esse, he promises that he will come.

The word, taken apart.
polliceor is built from an old prefix por- "forth" and liceor "to bid, to offer a price" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to hold out an offer.

fīdūcia, fīdūciae — "trust, confidence; a thing held in trust".
Magnam fīdūciam in amīcīs habēbat. He had great trust in his friends.
fīdūcia, fīdūciae f., built on fīdō, to trust. In law it is a thing handed over in trust. English fiduciary.

The word, taken apart.
fīdūcia is built on fīdō "to trust" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the verb behind fīdus "faithful". English fiduciary comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 6.
spōnsiō, spōnsiōnis — a solemn promise, a pledge; a wager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to promise, then the Latin for trust.

You'll want to know: spōnsiō, spōnsiōnis.
spōnsiō, spōnsiōnis — "a solemn promise, a pledge; a wager".
Spōnsiōnem inter sē fēcērunt. They made a formal wager with each other.
spōnsiō, spōnsiōnis f., built on spondeō, to pledge: the old spoken contract, spondēsne? — spondeō. Also a bet. English sponsor is from the same verb.

The word, taken apart.
spōnsiō is the noun built on spondeō "to pledge solemnly" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English sponsor comes from the same verb.

Lesson 5 of 6.
obstringō, obstringere — to bind, to put under an obligation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for trust, then the Latin for a solemn promise.

You'll want to know: obstringō, obstringere.
obstringō, obstringere — "to bind, to put under an obligation".
Beneficiō mē obstrīnxistī. You have bound me by your kindness.
obstringō, obstringere, obstrīnxī, obstrictum: to tie fast, then to bind by a promise or a favour.

The word, taken apart.
obstringō is built from ob and stringō "to draw tight, to bind" (Lewis & Short): to tie someone fast to a duty.
