Gujarati, chapter 165: Adding, pouring and stirring.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઉમેરવું (umervũ) — to add.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for mustard seed, then the Gujarati for cardamom.

You'll want to know: ઉમેરવું.
ઉમેરવું — umervũ — "to add".
તે ચામાં ખાંડ ઉમેરે છે. — te cāmā̃ khā̃ḍ umere che — "He adds sugar to his tea."

The word, taken apart.
ઉમેરવું (umervũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from ummēr, "join", a reconstructed form, and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to add, augment" (CDIAL 2347).

Lesson 2 of 5.
રેડવું (reḍvũ) — to pour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for cardamom, then the Gujarati for to add.

You'll want to know: રેડવું.
રેડવું — reḍvũ — "to pour".
હું પ્યાલામાં ચા રેડું છું. — hũ pyālāmā̃ cā reḍũ chũ — "I pour tea into the cup."

The word, taken apart.
રેડવું (reḍvũ) is inherited, but its source is not settled. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to pour", under Sanskrit raya, "stream", and again under recayati, "empties", and says it may come from either (CDIAL 10638, 10811).

Lesson 3 of 5.
હલાવવું (halāvvũ) — to stir, to shake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to add, then the Gujarati for to pour.

You'll want to know: હલાવવું.
હલાવવું — halāvvũ — "to stir, to shake".
તે ચમચીથી દાળ હલાવે છે. — te camcīthī dāḷ halāve che — "She stirs the dal with a spoon."

The word, taken apart.
હલાવવું (halāvvũ) is a tadbhava, a causative. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to shake", under hallati, "moves, shakes", a reconstructed form (CDIAL 14018).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઉકાળવું (ukāḷvũ) — to boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to stir, then the Gujarati for to mix.

You'll want to know: ઉકાળવું.
ઉકાળવું — ukāḷvũ — "to boil".
માતા સવારે દૂધ ઉકાળે છે. — mātā savāre dūdh ukāḷe che — "Mother boils the milk in the morning."

The word, taken apart.
ઉકાળવું (ukāḷvũ) is a tadbhava. CDIAL lists it, glossed "to cause to boil", under Sanskrit utkālayati, "drives out", in the entry for utkalati, "goes out or up", a reconstructed form that gives the intransitive ukaḷvũ, "to boil" (CDIAL 1716).
