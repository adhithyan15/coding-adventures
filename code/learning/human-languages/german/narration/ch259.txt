German, chapter 259: Older Than, Since When — älter als, seit, vor.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
älter als — older than.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "old" and "fast". (Alt. Schnell.)
[pause 1 second]
And remember als, from chapter two hundred and fifty-two: "when", for one time in the past. It has a second job.

Grammar Lens: -er, then als.
To say "more", German adds -er to the adjective, as English does with "faster". "Than" is als:
Der Zug ist schneller als das Auto. — The train is faster than the car. Mein Bruder ist älter als ich. — My brother is older than me.
Many short adjectives with a, o or u take an umlaut as well: alt, älter; groß, größer, bigger. Others do not: klein, kleiner; billig, billiger.
A few are irregular, as in English: gut, besser; viel, mehr; and gern, lieber, which you already have.
After als, a pronoun keeps its subject form: älter als ich, als du. In some regions and in casual speech you may hear älter wie ich; in standard German it is als.

Why it's said this way: so … wie for the same.
For "as … as", German says so … wie, with the plain adjective in the middle:
Sie ist so alt wie ich. — She is as old as me. Das Auto ist nicht so schnell wie der Zug. — The car is not as fast as the train.
Wie is for the same, and als is for more. So alt wie, älter als.

The word, taken apart.
alt from Old High German alt from Proto-Germanic aldaz (EtymDB 2.1), the parent of English old. EtymDB does not list the comparative, so this part is the usual account: the old comparative ending had an i in it, and that i pulled the vowel forward, a to ä. English went through the same change and kept its trace in elder, beside the newer older.

Lesson 2 of 3.
seit and vor — since when, and how long ago.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I am learning German". (Ich lerne Deutsch.) Now we add how long.

Grammar Lens: seit, with the present.
English says "I have been learning German for two years". German keeps the present, because the learning is still going on:
Ich lerne seit zwei Jahren Deutsch.
seit means "since, for", and it takes the dative: seit einem Jahr, seit einer Stunde, and in the plural an extra -n: seit zwei Jahren, seit drei Monaten.
The question is seit wann?, since when: Seit wann lernst du Deutsch?
The trap for English speakers is the tense. Ich habe seit zwei Jahren Deutsch gelernt forces the English shape onto German, and for something still going on it sounds wrong. If it is still true, keep the present.

Why it's said this way: vor for ago.
For something over and done, German says vor, also with the dative, and it stands before the time, where English "ago" comes after:
Vor zwei Jahren war ich in Berlin. — Two years ago I was in Berlin.
The time phrase takes the first place, so the verb comes next: vor zwei Jahren war ich.
And for a stretch of time that is finished, no little word is needed at all: Ich war drei Monate in Berlin, I was in Berlin for three months.
So: still going on, seit and the present. Over, vor for when, or the bare time for how long.

The word, taken apart.
seit from Middle High German sīt (EtymDB 2.1). Further back it is usually traced to an Old High German word meaning "later", and linked with the first part of English since; EtymDB does not record those steps. vor is usually traced to Old High German fora, a cousin of English fore in before; EtymDB's entry for vor does not settle it, so that too is given as the usual account.

Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — then and now.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "my brother is older than me". (Mein Bruder ist älter als ich.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, and an experience can be told from the inside: how it felt, what you hope, what it compares with, and when. Älter als, so alt wie. Seit with the present for what is still true; vor for what is over.
[pause 2 seconds]
A short piece with vor, als and seit in it:
Vor zwei Jahren war ich in Berlin. Der Zug war schneller als das Auto, und ich war froh. Seit einem Jahr lerne ich Deutsch. Ich hoffe, bald wieder nach Berlin zu fahren.
Listen for the verb after the time phrases: vor zwei Jahren war ich, seit einem Jahr lerne ich.
