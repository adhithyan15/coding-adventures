# un formulario completo — where all of it actually turns up

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-05, ES-ORTH-C426-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say what **DNI** stands for, and say whether it takes points.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C426-03, ES-ORTH-C426-05, ES-ORTH-C426-06, ES-ORTH-C426-02, ES-ORTH-C426-04] -->

Read this form. Every short form in it has been taught:

> **Datos personales**
> Nombre y apellidos: Pedro de la Cruz
> DNI: 12345678-A
> Dirección: c/ Mayor, n.º 5, 1.º
> Teléfono: 600 123 456
> Correo electrónico:
> Nacionalidad: español

Six things to notice, and every one is a rule rather than a detail: *de la Cruz*
is lower-case because the forename is present; **DNI** carries no points;
**c/** is the street and **n.º** the number; **1.º** is the floor, with the
point before the raised letter; and **español** is lower-case because a
nationality is a category.

## Grammar Lens: the same data spoken aloud
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C425-01, ES-ORTH-C426-03] -->

A form prints the short forms; a person says them in full:

| on the form | said aloud |
|---|---|
| c/ Mayor | calle Mayor |
| n.º 5 | número cinco |
| 1.º | primero |

A reading paper gives you the left column and a listening paper the right, which
is why both have to be learned.
