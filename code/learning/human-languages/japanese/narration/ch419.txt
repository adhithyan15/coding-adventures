6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
こくせき (kokuseki) — nationality.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a box on a form, then the Japanese for a date.

You'll want to know: こくせき.
こくせき — kokuseki — "nationality".
ようしに こくせきを きにゅうしました。 — yōshi ni kokuseki o kinyū shimashita — "I filled in my nationality on the form."

The word, taken apart.
こくせき (kokuseki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are koku, "country" (Middle Chinese kwok) and seki, "domiciliary register" (dzjek) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
うけつける (uketsukeru) — to accept applications; to take bookings.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for sex (on a form), then the Japanese for one's full name.

You'll want to know: うけつける.
うけつける — uketsukeru — "to accept applications; to take bookings".
まどぐちは ごじまで よやくを うけつけます。 — madoguchi wa goji made yoyaku o uketsukemasu — "The counter takes bookings until five."
Polite うけつけます, uketsukemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): まどぐちが ようしを うけつけました。, madoguchi ga yōshi o uketsukemashita.

The word, taken apart.
うけつける (uketsukeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from uke, "receiving" (from ukeru) and tsukeru, "to attach"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
