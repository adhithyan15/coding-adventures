Lesson 1 of 6.
まいご (maigo) — a lost child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for at last, then the Japanese for unharmed.

You'll want to know: まいご.
まいご — maigo — "a lost child".
もし まいごに なったら、こうばんに いきます。 — moshi maigo ni nattara, kōban ni ikimasu — "If I get lost, I go to the police box."

The word, taken apart.
まいご (maigo) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mai, "losing the way" (from mayou), and go, "child" (from ko) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

よびだす — yobidasu — "to call (someone) out; to page".
えきいんが ははを よびだしました。 — ekiin ga haha o yobidashimashita — "A station attendant paged my mother."
Polite よびだします, yobidashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): せんせいが ははを よびだしました。, sensei ga haha o yobidashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
よびだす (yobidasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from yobi, "calling" (from yobu), and dasu, "to put out" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ほうそう (hōsō) — an announcement; a broadcast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a lost child, then the Japanese for to call (someone) out.

You'll want to know: ほうそう.
ほうそう — hōsō — "an announcement; a broadcast".
えきで まいごの ほうそうが ありました。 — eki de maigo no hōsō ga arimashita — "At the station they announced a lost child."

The word, taken apart.
ほうそう (hōsō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hō, "release" (Middle Chinese pjangH) and sō, "send" (suwngH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: ほっと.
ほっと — hotto — "with relief".
こどもが みつかって、ほっと しました。 — kodomo ga mitsukatte, hotto shimashita — "The child was found, and we breathed again."

The word, taken apart.
ほっと (hotto) is a native Japanese mimetic word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).
