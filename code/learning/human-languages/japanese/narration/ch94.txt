Lesson 1 of 5.
このむ (konomu) — to like, to prefer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a candidate, then the Japanese for a plan.

You'll want to know: このむ.
このむ — konomu — "to like, to prefer".

The word, taken apart.
このむ (konomu) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しゅみ (shumi) — a hobby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to like, then the Japanese for a liking.

You'll want to know: しゅみ.
しゅみ — shumi — "a hobby".

The word, taken apart.
しゅみ (shumi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shu, "interest, what attracts one" (Middle Chinese tshjuH) and mi, "taste" (mj+jH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).
