Punjabi, chapter 38: And, Or, and Neither.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਅਤੇ (ate) — and.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
From the chapter behind you, the two admissions. And from the food chapter far back, the word for milk.
[your turn — recall: mainū̃ samajh nahī̃ āī, mainū̃ nahī̃ patā — then dudh]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
Milk and bread were taught together, and read back together at a distant window. Until this lesson there has been no way to name them in one breath.

The word, taken apart.
ਦੁੱਧ ਅਤੇ ਰੋਟੀ — dudh ate roṭī — milk and bread.
ਅਤੇ (ate) stands between the two things it joins, exactly where English puts and.
It has a shorter twin. In ordinary speech Punjabi very often says just ਤੇ, and the two are the same word — ਅਤੇ (ate) is the fuller, more written form. That matters here for an odd reason: the short ਤੇ is also a syllable this book drills in its script lessons, so a search of the corpus for and kept finding the drill and never the conjunction. The word looked present and was absent.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: dudh alone, then the pair. Hear what the middle word buys.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: any two things you can name, joined.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Join two foods. What is the shorter everyday form of ਅਤੇ (ate), and why did it make the word hard to find in this book? (ਤੇ — it is also a script drill syllable.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
ਅਤੇ (ate), joining two sentences — and, joining two whole sentences — the first two-clause sentence in this book.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back, the joining word. Four lessons back, at the head of the last chapter, where ਨਹੀਂ stands.
[your turn — recall: ate, and nahī̃ before the verb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The pattern, taken apart.
The same ਅਤੇ (ate) joins two whole sentences, and changes nothing about either:
(first sentence) ਅਤੇ (ate) (second sentence).
No ending appears, no word moves. That is why and is the joining word to learn first: it is the only one that costs the sentences on either side of it nothing at all.
And it composes with the chapter behind you — the second half is free to be a negative:
… ਅਤੇ … (… ate …) ਨਹੀਂ …
That is a two-clause sentence, and it is the first one this book has been able to make. Until now the longest thing the track taught was a single clause of four slots.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — build: two short sentences you own, joined with ate.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: the same again, the second half negative.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the two said separately, then joined. Same words.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What changes about a sentence when ਅਤੇ (ate) joins it to another? (Nothing.) Make one whose second half is negative.


Lesson 3 of 5.
ਅਤੇ (ate) reaches the page — and, on three signs taught long ago.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — three pieces, none new until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
One chapter back you wrote ਨਹੀਂ. Write it once from memory, bindi and all, before anything is uncovered.

Script — three pieces, none new.
ਅ — the vowel carrier, ਤ — ta, ੇ — the laav stroke above.
All three were taught long before this chapter. Say it plainly: the word this book most needed was never a writing problem. Nobody had written it down.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Keep the model in view and copy ਅਤੇ (ate) once. Check the laav sits above ਤ, not above ਅ.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write ਅਤੇ (ate) from memory. How many of its three signs were new today? (None.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ਜਾਂ (jāṁ) — or.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back you wrote the joining word. Say it, then write it in the air.
[your turn — recall: ਅਤੇ (ate)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
ਅਤੇ (ate) offers you both. ਜਾਂ (jāṁ) offers you one of two:
ਦੁੱਧ ਜਾਂ ਰੋਟੀ — dudh jāṁ roṭī — milk or bread.
Same position, different job: it stands between the two things and asks the listener to choose. The bindi on it is not decoration — the vowel goes through the nose, and this book has been marking that since its earliest script lessons.
This is the word a shopkeeper uses on you, and the one you need to answer with.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: dudh jāṁ roṭī, then dudh ate roṭī. One asks, one gives.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the two joining words back to back, twice.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: offer someone a choice of two things you can name.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Offer a choice, then offer both. Which word did each need? (jāṁ to choose, ate to give both.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
ਨਾ … ਨਾ … (nā … nā …) — neither … nor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The two joining words of this chapter, and the rule from the chapter before it.
[your turn — recall: ate, jāṁ — and nahī̃ before the verb]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The pattern, taken apart.
English uses two different words — neither and nor. Punjabi repeats one:
ਨਾ (first thing) ਨਾ (second thing) — "neither … nor …"
ਨਾ is the short bare negative you have already met beside ਨਹੀਂ. Here it does the joining and the negating at once, standing in front of each item rather than between them.
That is worth noticing against ਅਤੇ (ate) and ਜਾਂ (jāṁ), which stand between. This is the first joining pattern in the book that goes in front of both halves.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: refuse two things at once with nā … nā ….]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: ate between, then nā … nā in front of each.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: offered a choice with jāṁ, refuse both.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Refuse two things in one sentence. Where does ਨਾ stand, and how is that different from ਅਤੇ? (In front of each item; ate stands between them.) Next chapter: but — and Punjabi has two words for it, one from each side of the language's history.
