# دوست — "friend," and the relationship تم was made for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-AAP-TUM-TU, UR-PRAGMATICS-YOU-REGISTER, UR-ETYMON-TUM-THOU] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Back in Chapter 3 you learned three Urdu words for "you" —
respectful **āp**, familiar **tum**, intimate **tū** — and were told to use
**tum** once a relationship supports familiarity. Here is the relationship
that phrase meant.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-DOST]; assesses=[] -->

> **دوست** — *dost* — **friend**

> **میرا دوست۔** — *merā dost.* — "[He's/She's] my friend."

## Grammar Lens: a noun that will not pick a gender
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-MERA-GENDER-AGREEMENT, UR-LEX-DOST] -->

**بھائی** was masculine and needed **merā**. **بہن** was feminine and
needed **merī**. **دوست** breaks the pattern on purpose: it takes **the
same form** for a male or a female friend, and **merā dost** works for
either. Native Urdu nouns almost always commit to a gender; this Persian
loan simply does not carry the machinery that would make it agree.

## The word, taken apart — a friend is someone chosen
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-ETYMON-DOST-CHOOSE]; assesses=[UR-LEX-DOST, UR-ETYMON-TUM-THOU] -->

**dost** is Persian, inherited from Old Persian **dauštā**, from
Proto-Iranian **\*jawštā́**, from Proto-Indo-European **\*ǵews-**, "to
taste, to choose, to enjoy." That root also gave English **choose** and
Latin *gustus* — behind English **gusto**. A friend, on this history, is
someone **tasted and chosen**, the same picture as *lenā*'s worn-down
"take."

Persian's own word for "ask," **porsīdan**, turned out to share one root
with Urdu's inherited **پوچھنا**. **دوست** is not that kind of cousin —
Urdu has no inherited word from *\*ǵews-* to compare it against — but it is
the same general shape: an old Indo-European root, arriving in Urdu by the
Persian road alone.

## Grammar Lens: where تم (*tum*) actually lives
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-AAP-TUM-TU, UR-SCRIPT-AAP-TUM-TU, UR-PRAGMATICS-YOU-REGISTER] -->

Chapter 3 taught **āp / tum / tū** and told you to move to **tum** once
familiarity is safe. A **دوست** is exactly that relationship: someone you
have moved off **āp** for. You do not need a new rule — just the word for
the person the old rule was describing.
