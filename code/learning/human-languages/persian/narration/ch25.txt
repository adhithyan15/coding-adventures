Persian, chapter 25: Letters That Share a Sound.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
ش (shin) — the letter that opens شما.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ش and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ک and گ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say شما (shomâ), "you (respectful)". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ش.
ش — shin, the sound sh.
It opens شما (shomâ), the respectful "you". It is س, which you already write, with three dots above.

Writing: ش.
1. start here: form the three teeth from right to left.
2. flow into the final bowl without lifting.
3. lift, then place the lower-left dot.
4. lift again and place the lower-right dot.
5. lift again and place the centered upper dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ش (shin) demonstration at 01:29–01:35 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Lesson 2 of 4.

[pause 2 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say صد (sad), "a hundred". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ص.
ص — sâd, the sound s.
It opens صد (sad), "a hundred". In Persian it sounds exactly like س: the two letters are spelled differently and said the same, because ص (sâd) came in with Arabic words and kept its Arabic spelling. The letter is a closed oval with a bowl after it.

Writing: ص.
Put your pen on ص (sâd) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 2 seconds]
Now say چطور (chetor), "how". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ط.
ط — tâ, the sound t.
It is inside چطور (chetor), "how". Like ص (sâd), it sounds exactly like a letter you know, here ت. A closed oval on the line and a tall upright drawn from the top down.

Writing: ط.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the closed oval.
2. finish left along the baseline without lifting.
3. lift once, then draw the tall upright top-to-bottom — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ط (tâ) demonstration at 01:54–01:56 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).
