Telugu, chapter 325: It Depends, and What Would Have Been.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఆధారపడు (ādhārapaḍu) — to depend, to rely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a support, a basis" (ādhāraṁ) and "to hurry" (tondarapaḍu). Then "if it rains tomorrow, I will not come". (rēpu varṣaṁ paḍitē nēnu rānu.)

You'll want to know: ఆధారపడు.
ఆధారపడు — ādhārapaḍu — "to depend (on), to rely (on)".
అమ్మమ్మ నాన్న మీద ఆధారపడుతుంది. ammamma nānna mīda ādhārapaḍutundi. — "Grandmother depends on Father."
మా ఊరు వర్షం మీద ఆధారపడుతుంది. mā ūru varṣaṁ mīda ādhārapaḍutundi. — "Our town depends on the rain."
What you depend on takes మీద (mīda, "on"). The past, "I depended", is ఆధారపడ్డాను (ādhārapaḍḍānu): -పడు makes its past పడ్డాను, as తొందరపడు makes తొందరపడ్డాను.

The word, taken apart.
ఆధారపడు (ādhārapaḍu) is two pieces. ఆధారం, "a support, a basis", which you know, is borrowed from Sanskrit ādhāra (CDIAL 1165). -పడు is the native verb that DEDR 3853 lists as "to feel, to suffer", and that makes verbs of nouns, as in తొందరపడు and సిగ్గుపడు. So: "to come to rest on a support".

Lesson 2 of 5.
మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది (mīda ādhārapaḍi uṇṭundi) — it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "our town depends on the rain" (mā ūru varṣaṁ mīda ādhārapaḍutundi). Then "according to". (prakāraṁ.)

You'll want to know: … మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది.
అది వాతావరణం మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది. adi vātāvaraṇaṁ mīda ādhārapaḍi uṇṭundi. — "That depends on the weather."
రేపటి ప్రయాణం వర్షం మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది. rēpaṭi prayāṇaṁ varṣaṁ mīda ādhārapaḍi uṇṭundi. — "Tomorrow's trip depends on the rain."
ఆధారపడి, the -ఇ form, with ఉంటుంది, "it stays": "it stays resting on". This is the answer to a question that has no single answer: > అది మీ మీద ఆధారపడి ఉంటుంది (mīda ādhārapaḍi uṇṭundi).
adi mī mīda ādhārapaḍi uṇṭundi. — "That is up to you."
రేపటి (rēpaṭi, "tomorrow's") is రేపు before a noun. When the answer comes from a rule or a list, use ప్రకారం, "according to":
కాలపట్టిక ప్రకారం రైలు ఆరు గంటలకు వస్తుంది. kālapaṭṭika prakāraṁ railu āru gaṇṭalaku vastundi. — "According to the timetable, the train comes at six."

Lesson 3 of 5.
ఉండి ఉంటే (uṇḍi uṇṭē) — what would have been.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I used to go", as a man and as a woman (veḷḷēvāḍini, veḷḷēdānni). Then "if there is" (uṇṭē) and "having come" (vacci).

You'll want to know: -ఇ ఉంటే, 'if … had'.
నా దగ్గర డబ్బు ఉండి ఉంటే, నేను వెళ్ళేవాడిని. nā daggara ḍabbu uṇḍi uṇṭē, nēnu veḷḷēvāḍini. — "If I had had money, I would have gone."
Two pieces from this core, joined: the -ఇ form and ఉంటే. ఉండి ఉంటే (uṇḍi uṇṭē), "if there had been"; వచ్చి ఉంటే, "if (you) had come"; చెప్పి ఉంటే, "if (you) had told". It looks at something that did not happen. నా దగ్గర, "near me", is how Telugu says "I have (it on me)".

You'll want to know: -ఏవాడిని, 'would have'.
The main verb is the form of the habit, from the first chapter of this core: -ఏవాడిని, -ఏదాన్ని. The same form says "used to" and "would have"; the -ఇ ఉంటే clause in front tells you which.
నువ్వు ముందే చెప్పి ఉంటే, నేను వచ్చేదాన్ని. nuvvu mundē ceppi uṇṭē, nēnu vaccēdānni. — "If you had told me earlier, I would have come."
బస్సు సమయానికి వచ్చి ఉంటే, మేము రైలు అందుకునేవాళ్ళం. bassu samayāniki vacci uṇṭē, mēmu railu andukunēvāḷḷaṁ. — "If the bus had come on time, we would have caught the train."
ముందే (mundē, "earlier, beforehand") is ముందు with -ఏ; సమయానికి (samayāniki, "on time") is సమయం with -కి.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If (you) had come"? (వచ్చి ఉంటే.) "I would have gone"? (వెళ్ళేవాడిని, వెళ్ళేదాన్ని.) What else does that form say? ("Used to".)


Lesson 4 of 5.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip in the past, with a feeling, a reason and an if.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last A2 writing task asked for a connected message about a familiar day. This one asks for six lines, and they tell a story: a trip, to your home town or anywhere else.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list of events. It has a scene, in -తున్నప్పుడు or ఉండేది. It has events in the plain past, kept in order with మొదట, ఆ తరువాత, -ఇ and చివరికి. It has at least one reason, with కాబట్టి or ఎందుకంటే, and a feeling, with నాకు … -గా అనిపించింది.
[pause 2 seconds]
A good one ends by looking at what might have been: … -ఇ ఉంటే, … -ఏవాడిని (or -ఏదాన్ని).

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, in Telugu script, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, and what you were doing as it began, with -తున్నప్పుడు;
the first thing that happened, with మొదట, and a ticket or a seat;
what happened next, with ఆ తరువాత or an -ఇ form, and one thing that went wrong;
why, with కాబట్టి or ఎందుకంటే; and if you like, what someone did ఈలోగా;
how it ended and how you felt, with చివరికి and నాకు … -గా అనిపించింది;
one line about what might have been, with -ఇ ఉంటే … -ఏవాడిని or -ఏదాన్ని.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model on this page. Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing the right past for each line, and joining them.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines as you read the village story earlier in this core. Mark every verb s for scene, e for event. Line one should be mostly s; lines two and three mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then five spelling checks. A word that starts with long ī starts with ఈ, not ఇ; one with long ō starts with ఓ, not ఒ; one with long ū starts with ఊ, not ఉ. The au of ఔషధం at the start of a word is the letter ఔ. దుఃఖం keeps its two rings, ః.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. Does the if-clause end in -ఇ ఉంటే, and the main verb in -ఏవాడిని or -ఏదాన్ని?
