Hindi, chapter 472: Lazy or Brisk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
फुर्तीला (phurtīlā) — nimble; brisk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for etiquette, then the Hindi for to tease.

You'll want to know: फुर्तीला.
फुर्तीला — phurtīlā — "nimble; brisk".
फुर्तीला खिलाड़ी गेंद को जल्दी पकड़ लेता है। phurtīlā khilāṛī gẽd ko jaldī pakaṛ letā hai. A nimble player catches the ball quickly.

The word, taken apart.
फुर्तीला (phurtīlā) is phurtī, "briskness", with the ending -īlā, "full of"; CDIAL lists Hindi phurtī, "activity", under Sanskrit sphuráti, "darts, quivers" (CDIAL 13849).

Lesson 3 of 5.
निकम्मा (nikammā) — good-for-nothing; idle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for nimble, then the Hindi for sluggish.

You'll want to know: निकम्मा.
निकम्मा — nikammā — "good-for-nothing; idle".
वह लड़का निकम्मा है, दिन भर कुछ नहीं करता। vah laṛkā nikammā hai, din bhar kuch nahīṃ kartā. That boy is good for nothing; he does nothing all day.

The word, taken apart.
निकम्मा (nikammā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi nikammā, "useless, unemployed", under Sanskrit niṣkarman (CDIAL 7475), from niḥ, "without", and karman, "work".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: कर्मठ.
कर्मठ — karmaṭh — "dedicated; diligent in work".
मीरा की माँ बहुत कर्मठ हैं, सुबह से रात तक काम करती हैं। Mīrā kī māṁ bahut karmaṭh haiṁ, subah se rāt tak kām kartī haiṁ. Mira's mother is very dedicated; she works from morning till night.

The word, taken apart.
कर्मठ (karmaṭh) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit karmaṭha, "capable of work; working diligently" (Monier-Williams), from karman, "work".

Lesson 5 of 5.
मुस्तैद (mustaid) — alert; ready for action.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for good-for-nothing, then the Hindi for dedicated.

You'll want to know: मुस्तैद.
मुस्तैद — mustaid — "alert; ready for action".
चुनाव के दिन पुलिस मुस्तैद थी। cunāv ke din pulis mustaid thī. On election day the police were on the alert.

The word, taken apart.
मुस्तैद (mustaid) is usually traced to Arabic mustaʿidd, "ready, prepared", which reached Hindi through Persian.
