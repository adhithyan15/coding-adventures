Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 209: Shopkeeper and Customer.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
बाणियो (bāṇiyo) — a trader; a shopkeeper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a court of law, then the Marwadi for to come open.

You'll want to know: बाणियो.
बाणियो — bāṇiyo — "a trader; a shopkeeper".
बाणियो दिनूगै दुकान खोलै है। bāṇiyo dinūgai dukān kholai hai. The trader opens the shop early in the morning.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
बाणियो (bāṇiyo) is inherited from Sanskrit vāṇija, "trader"; CDIAL lists Marwari bā̃ṇyõ, "trader" (CDIAL 11484).

गाहक — gāhak — "a customer".
गाहक भाव पूछै है। gāhak bhāv pūchhai hai. The customer asks the price.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
गाहक (gāhak) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit grāhaka, "one who takes; a purchaser" (Monier-Williams), with the r lost.

Lesson 3 of 6.

[pause 2 seconds]

सेठ — seṭh — "a merchant".
सेठ हवेली मांय रैवै है। seṭh havelī mā̃y raivai hai. The merchant lives in a mansion.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
सेठ (seṭh) is inherited from Sanskrit śreṣṭhin, "a distinguished man; the head of a guild". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry, but lists Hindi seṭh, "merchant, banker" (CDIAL 12726).

Lesson 5 of 6.
मंगावणो (maṅgāvṇo) — to order; to have brought.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a merchant, then the Marwadi for a deal.

You'll want to know: मंगावणो.
मंगावणो — maṅgāvṇo — "to order; to have brought".
हूं होटल सूं रोटी मंगाऊं हूं। hū̃ hoṭal sū̃ roṭī maṅgāū̃ hū̃. I order flatbread from the hotel.
Past (I …): म्हैं चाय मंगाई, mhaĩ chāy maṅgāī.

The word, taken apart.
मंगावणो (maṅgāvṇo) is the causative of मांगणो, "to ask for": to ask for a thing to be brought. Both are inherited from Sanskrit mārgati, "seeks, requests"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari māgaï, "asks for" (CDIAL 10074).
