Portuguese, chapter 477: Lágrimas e gargalhadas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o choro — crying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to console, then the Portuguese for to get something off your chest.

You'll want to know: o choro.
o choro — "crying".
Ouvia-se o choro de um bebé no comboio. You could hear a baby crying on the train.
The noun of chorar.

The word, taken apart.
choro comes from chorar "to cry", which is inherited from Latin plōrāre "to wail" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
soluçar — to sob; to hiccup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to get something off your chest, then the Portuguese for crying.

You'll want to know: soluçar.
soluçar — "to sob; to hiccup".
A criança soluçava e não conseguia falar. The child was sobbing and could not speak.
O soluço is a sob, and also the hiccups: tenho soluços.

The word, taken apart.
soluçar is built on soluço "a sob, a hiccup", which is inherited, through Old Portuguese solluço, from Vulgar Latin suggluttium (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
suspirar — to sigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for crying, then the Portuguese for to sob.

You'll want to know: suspirar.
suspirar — "to sigh".
O meu pai suspira sempre que vê a conta da luz. My father sighs every time he sees the electricity bill.
Suspirar por — to long for.

The word, taken apart.
suspirar is inherited, through Old Portuguese suspirar (EtymDB), from Latin suspīrāre "to draw a deep breath, to sigh" (Lewis & Short), built on spīrāre "to breathe".

Lesson 5 of 5.
a gargalhada — burst of laughter, guffaw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sigh, then the Portuguese for sigh.

You'll want to know: a gargalhada.
a gargalhada — "burst of laughter, guffaw".
O público deu uma gargalhada. The audience burst out laughing.
Dar uma gargalhada — to burst out laughing.

The word, taken apart.
gargalhada is usually taken to imitate the sound of loud laughter in the throat, like gargarejar "to gargle"; the exact origin is not settled.
