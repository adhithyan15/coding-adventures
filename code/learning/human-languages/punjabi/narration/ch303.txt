Punjabi, chapter 303: Water for the Fields.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਨਹਿਰ (nahir) — a canal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to sprout, then the Punjabi for mustard.

You'll want to know: ਨਹਿਰ.
ਨਹਿਰ — nahir — "a canal".
ਸਾਡੇ ਪਿੰਡ ਦੇ ਨੇੜੇ ਨਹਿਰ ਹੈ। sāḍe piṇḍ de neṛe nahir hai. There is a canal near our village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਨਹਿਰ (nahir) is a loan, usually traced through Persian to Arabic nahr, "river"; its sense here, "canal", is a shift from that.

ਖੂਹ ਦਾ ਪਾਣੀ ਠੰਢਾ ਹੈ। khūh dā pāṇī ṭhaṇḍhā hai. The water of the well is cold.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਸਿੰਜਣਾ (sinjṇā) — to irrigate; to water a field.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a canal, then the Punjabi for a well.

You'll want to know: ਸਿੰਜਣਾ.
ਸਿੰਜਣਾ — sinjṇā — "to irrigate; to water a field".
ਅਸੀਂ ਨਹਿਰ ਦੇ ਪਾਣੀ ਨਾਲ ਖੇਤ ਸਿੰਜਦੇ ਹਾਂ। asīṁ nahir de pāṇī nāl khet sinjde hāṁ. We irrigate the fields with canal water.
Past (I …): ਮੈਂ ਖੇਤ ਸਿੰਜਿਆ, maiṁ khet sinjiā.

The word, taken apart.
ਸਿੰਜਣਾ (sinjṇā) is inherited from Sanskrit siñcati, "pours out, sprinkles"; CDIAL lists Punjabi siñjṇā, "to water, irrigate", under it (CDIAL 13394).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਕਿਸਾਨ (kisān) — a farmer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a well, then the Punjabi for to irrigate.

You'll want to know: ਕਿਸਾਨ.
ਕਿਸਾਨ — kisān — "a farmer".
ਕਿਸਾਨ ਸਵੇਰੇ ਖੇਤ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ। kisān savere khet jāndā hai. The farmer goes to the field in the morning.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਕਿਸਾਨ (kisān) goes back to Sanskrit kṛṣāṇa, "ploughing"; CDIAL lists Punjabi kisāṇ, "farmer", under it and marks it as borrowed rather than inherited, as its -s- shows (CDIAL 3447).
