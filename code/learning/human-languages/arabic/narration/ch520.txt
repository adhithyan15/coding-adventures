Arabic, chapter 520: Hours and tasks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اعتاد (iʿtāda) — to get used to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a trainee, then the Arabic for to practise.

You'll want to know: اعتاد.
اعتاد — iʿtāda — "to get used to".
اعتادت الممرضة على العمل في الليل. iʿtādat al-mumarriḍa ʿalā al-ʿamal fī al-layl. The nurse got used to working at night.
Present: يعتاد, yaʿtād.
It takes على, or a direct object.

The word, taken apart.
اعتاد (iʿtāda) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-w-d, "to return" (Lane). ʿĀda, a habit, which you know, is from the same root: a thing that keeps coming back.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
إجازة (ijāza) — time off, leave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to get used to, then the Arabic for a shift.

You'll want to know: إجازة.
إجازة — ijāza — "time off, leave".
أخذت أمي إجازة من العمل. akhadhat ummī ijāza min al-ʿamal. My mother took time off work.
It also means a permit or a licence.

The word, taken apart.
إجازة (ijāza) is built on the native Arabic root j-w-z, "to pass, to go through" (Lane). Jawāz, a passport, which you know, is from the same root: both let you pass.

The word, taken apart.
نفذ (naffadha) is built on the native Arabic root n-f-dh, "to pass through, to go through" (Lane). Form II naffadha is to make a plan go through.
