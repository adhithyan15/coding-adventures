## You'll want to know: dúshēngzǐnǚ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C508-SIBLINGS508-03]; assesses=[] -->

*dúshēngzǐnǚ* — "only child".

> *wǒ shì dúshēngzǐnǚ xiǎoshíhou zuì xiǎng yào yí ge dìdi* — I'm an only child; when I was little, what I wanted most was a little brother.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: zǐ is said with a rising tone before nǚ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dúshēngzǐnǚ* is built from *dú* 'alone', 生 'to be born' and 子女 'sons and daughters', all inherited from Middle Chinese, *duwk*, *srjaeng*, *tsiX* and *nrjoX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). For *duwk* the dictionary quotes an older one that sets it against *qún* 'in flocks', the way sheep go.
