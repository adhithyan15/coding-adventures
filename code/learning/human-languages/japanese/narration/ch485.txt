Lesson 1 of 6.
おる (oru) — to weave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to come apart at the seams, then the Japanese for to tie up.

You'll want to know: おる.
おる — oru — "to weave".
そぼは きものの ぬのを おります。 — sobo wa kimono no nuno o orimasu — "My grandmother weaves cloth for kimono."
Polite おります, orimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): そぼが ぬのを おりました。, sobo ga nuno o orimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おる (oru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ぬのじ (nunoji) — fabric; dress material.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to weave, then the Japanese for silk.

You'll want to know: ぬのじ.
ぬのじ — nunoji — "fabric; dress material".
この ぬのじで ふくを つくります。 — kono nunoji de fuku o tsukurimasu — "I make clothes from this fabric."

The word, taken apart.
ぬのじ (nunoji) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: native nuno, "linen" and Sino-Japanese ji, "ground" (Middle Chinese dijH), borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 6.
じみ (jimi) — plain; quiet (in colour).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for silk, then the Japanese for fabric.

You'll want to know: じみ.
じみ — jimi — "plain; quiet (in colour)".
ちちの ふくは いつも じみです。 — chichi no fuku wa itsumo jimi desu — "My father's clothes are always plain."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
じみ (jimi) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ji, "ground" (Middle Chinese dijH) and mi, "flavor" (mj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: はで.
はで — hade — "showy; flashy".
あの ゆかたは はでですね。 — ano yukata wa hade desu ne — "That yukata is really flashy."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
はで (hade) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese ha, "faction" (Middle Chinese pheaH), borrowed from Chinese and native de, "hand" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
