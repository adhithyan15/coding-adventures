Italian, chapter 393: Pagare per gli errori.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il carcere — the prison, the jail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the conviction, then the Italian for the penalty.

You'll want to know: il carcere.
il carcere — "the prison, the jail".
Se ti prendono, vai in carcere. If they catch you, you go to prison.
Stress on the first syllable: càrcere. The plural is feminine: le carceri.

The word, taken apart.
carcere is inherited from Latin carcer, carceris "a prison, an enclosure" (EtymDB); it also named the starting gates of a racecourse.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: il detenuto.
il detenuto — "the prisoner, the inmate".
I detenuti possono studiare in carcere. The inmates can study in prison.
Past participle of detenere "to hold". A woman is la detenuta.

The word, taken apart.
detenuto is the past participle of detenere "to hold, to detain", from Latin dētinēre "to hold back" (Lewis & Short), built on tenēre "to hold".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la punizione — the punishment; the free kick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the prisoner, then the Italian for to punish.

You'll want to know: la punizione.
la punizione — "the punishment; the free kick".
Per punizione non guardi la televisione per una settimana. As a punishment you are not watching television for a week.
In football, un calcio di punizione is a free kick.

The word, taken apart.
punizione is a learned borrowing from Latin pūnītiō "a punishing" (Lewis & Short), from pūnīre "to punish", on poena "penalty".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la sanzione — the sanction, the penalty, the fine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to punish, then the Italian for the punishment.

You'll want to know: la sanzione.
la sanzione — "the sanction, the penalty, the fine".
Se paghi in ritardo, c'è una sanzione. If you pay late, there is a penalty.
In offices and on tax forms it often means a fine.

The word, taken apart.
sanzione is a learned borrowing from Latin sānctiō "a decree; the clause that sets a penalty" (Lewis & Short), from sancīre "to make sacred, to ratify".
