## You'll want to know: canīcula, canīculae
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C488-BRUMA-03]; assesses=[] -->

**canīcula, canīculae** — "the Dog Star; a little dog".

> Ubi canīcula ardet, agricolae in umbrā quiēscunt.
>
> *When the Dog Star blazes, the farmers rest in the shade.*

First declension, feminine: *canīcula, canīculae f.*, a diminutive of *canis* 'dog'. Its rising in high summer brought the hottest days, the dog days.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*canīcula* is a diminutive of *canis* 'dog' with *-cula* (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a little dog, and the star in the mouth of the Great Dog. English *canicular* comes from it.
