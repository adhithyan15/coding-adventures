Italian, chapter 442: Su e giù.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la salita — the climb, the uphill slope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the avenue, then the Italian for the one-way street.

You'll want to know: la salita.
la salita — "the climb, the uphill slope".
La salita fino al castello è dura. The climb up to the castle is hard.
In salita — uphill.

The word, taken apart.
salita is built on salire 'to go up', which is inherited from Latin salīre 'to jump' (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la discesa — the descent, the downhill slope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the one-way street, then the Italian for the climb.

You'll want to know: la discesa.
la discesa — "the descent, the downhill slope".
In discesa la bici va da sola. Downhill the bike goes by itself.
In discesa — downhill; on the slopes, la discesa is the downhill run.

The word, taken apart.
discesa is built on discendere 'to go down', which is inherited from Latin dēscendere (EtymDB), from dē 'down' and scandere 'to climb' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
sbucare — to come out, to pop out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the descent, then the Italian for to run along.

You'll want to know: sbucare.
sbucare — "to come out, to pop out".
Dopo il vicolo siamo sbucati in piazza. After the alley we came out in the square.
Sbucare all'improvviso — to pop up suddenly.

The word, taken apart.
sbucare is built from s- 'out of' and buca 'hole': coming out of a hole. The origin of buca is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
aggirare — to go round, to get round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to run along, then the Italian for to come out.

You'll want to know: aggirare.
aggirare — "to go round, to get round".
Aggiriamo il centro per evitare il traffico. We go round the centre to avoid the traffic.
Also to get round a problem or a rule.

The word, taken apart.
aggirare is built from a- and girare 'to turn', which is inherited from Latin gyrāre, from Greek gŷros 'circle' (Lewis & Short).
