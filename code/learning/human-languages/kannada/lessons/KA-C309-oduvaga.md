# ಓದುವಾಗ (ōduvāga) — the scene, the event, and what was there

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C72-WANT-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C72-WANT-02, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-ONGOING-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "when (somebody) comes". (*bandāga*.) Then "I was reading". (*ōduttidde*.)

[PAUSE 1s] A told story often has one thing going on and another that cuts into it.

## You'll want to know: -ಉವಾಗ, while it was going on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-WHILE-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C40-OOTA-01, KA-LEX-C106-SOON-04, KA-LEX-C152-ACT152-05, KA-LEX-C158-KRIYE158-03] -->

> **ನಾನು ಊಟ ಮಾಡುವಾಗ ಫೋನ್ ಬಂತು.**
>
> *nānu ūṭa māḍuvāga phōn bantu.* — "While I was having a meal, a call came."

**ಮಾಡುವಾಗ** is **ಮಾಡುವ**, "that does", with **ಆಗ**, "when", the **ಆಗ** you know from **ಬಂದಾಗ**. **ಬಂದಾಗ** is "when (it) came": done. **ಮಾಡುವಾಗ** is "while doing": still going on.

That clause is the **scene**. The thing that cut into it is the **event**, in the plain past: **ಬಂತು**. Kannada says a call "came", where English says the phone rang.

> **ನಾವು ನಡೆಯುವಾಗ ಮಳೆ ಬಂತು.**
>
> *nāvu naḍeyuvāga maḷe bantu.* — "While we were walking, it rained."

A stem that ends in **-ಎ** takes a **-ಯ-** first: **ನಡೆ** → **ನಡೆಯುವಾಗ**. The **-ಉವಾಗ** form carries no person: the rest of the sentence says who.

## You'll want to know: ಇತ್ತು, 'there was'
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-THERE-WAS-01]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C91-ABOUTYOU-04, KA-LEX-C42-ADJ-02, KA-LEX-C104-JOBS-05, KA-LEX-C50-LEAVE-01, KA-LEX-C42-ADJ-01, KA-LEX-C111-LOC111-01, KA-LEX-C41-DEIXIS-04] -->

> **ನಮ್ಮ ಊರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಒಂದು ಚಿಕ್ಕ ಅಂಗಡಿ ಇತ್ತು.**
>
> *namma ūrinalli ondu cikka aṅgaḍi ittu.* — "There was a small shop in our town."

**ನಮ್ಮ** (*namma*, "our"); **ಊರಿನಲ್ಲಿ** (*ūrinalli*, "in the town"). **ಇತ್ತು** is the "it" form of **ಇದ್ದೆ**: "there was", "it was". Set it against the present:

> **ಈಗ ಅಲ್ಲಿ ಒಂದು ದೊಡ್ಡ ಹೋಟೆಲ್ ಇದೆ.**
>
> *īga alli ondu doḍḍa hōṭel ide.* — "Now there is a big hotel there."

"There wasn't" is **ಇರಲಿಲ್ಲ** (*iralilla*), which ends in **ಇಲ್ಲ**, "not". The same ending gives any verb's "did not", and it stays the same for every person: **ಅವನು ಬರಲಿಲ್ಲ** (*avanu baralilla*, "he did not come"), **ನಾನು ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ** (*nānu hōgalilla*, "I did not go").
