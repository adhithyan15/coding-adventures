Mandarin Chinese, chapter 202: At the Till.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sǎomǎ — to scan a code (to pay).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to send electronically, then the Mandarin for to get something sent.

You'll want to know: sǎomǎ.
sǎomǎ — "to scan a code (to pay)".
kěyǐ sǎomǎ ma — Can I pay by scanning the code?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: sǎo is said with a rising tone before mǎ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
sǎomǎ is built from 扫 'to sweep' and mǎ 'a code', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sawX and maeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The pair is recent, and how sweeping came to mean scanning is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

Lesson 5 of 5.
shōuyíntái — checkout; till.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take goods back, then the Mandarin for customer.

You'll want to know: shōuyíntái.
shōuyíntái — "checkout; till".
shōuyíntái zài ménkǒu — The checkout is by the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shōuyíntái is built from shōu 'to take in', yín 'silver, money' and tái 'a counter', all inherited from Middle Chinese, syuw, ngin and doj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the last as a raised square platform of earth.
