Italian, chapter 470: Dopo la pioggia.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
piovigginare — to drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to blow, then the Italian for the cloudburst.

You'll want to know: piovigginare.
piovigginare — "to drizzle".
Pioviggina, ma non serve l'ombrello. It is drizzling, but there is no need for an umbrella.
La pioggerella — drizzle.

The word, taken apart.
piovigginare is built on piovere 'to rain' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Vulgar Latin plovere, from Latin pluere.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Sono arrivato a casa fradicio. I got home soaked.
Bagnato fradicio — soaking wet; also rotten: una mela fradicia.

The word, taken apart.
fradicio is usually traced to Latin fracidus 'soft, over-ripe' (Lewis & Short), but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 7.
l'impermeabile — the raincoat; waterproof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the puddle, then the Italian for soaked.

You'll want to know: l'impermeabile.
l'impermeabile — "the raincoat; waterproof".
Metti l'impermeabile, piove ancora. Put on your raincoat, it is still raining.
As an adjective: una giacca impermeabile.

The word, taken apart.
impermeabile is a learned borrowing from Late Latin impermeābilis 'that cannot be passed through', built on meāre 'to pass'.
