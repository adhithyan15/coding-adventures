## You'll want to know: 马路
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C194-ROAD194-01]; assesses=[] -->

**马路** — *mǎlù* — "road; a street for traffic".

> **我家在马路对面** *wǒ jiā zài mǎlù duìmiàn* — My home is across the road.

The new character, in pieces: 路 — thirteen strokes in two halves: on the left the foot piece, 口 over four short strokes; on the right a short fall, a bar that turns and falls to the left, a long stroke falling to the right, and 口 under them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**马路** (*mǎlù*) is built from 马 'horse' and 路 'road', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *maeX* and *luH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Why a road for traffic is named after horses is not settled.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for asking where things are.
