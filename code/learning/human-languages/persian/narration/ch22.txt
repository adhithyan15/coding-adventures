Persian, chapter 22: Letters That Never Join Forward.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ر (re) — the letter that ends چطور.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ر and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ه and و once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say چطور (chetor), "how". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ر.
ر — re, the sound r.
It ends چطور (chetor), "how", from "how are you?". ر is a non-connector, like ا and و, which you already write: it joins the letter before it but never the one after, so a small gap always follows it.

Writing: ر.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend through the short stroke.
2. without lifting, sweep left through the lower curve — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ر (re) demonstration at 01:10–01:12 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ر three times, saying r as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: و, then ر (re), from memory]
[your turn — read: چطور aloud once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say زبان (zabân), "language". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ز.
ز — ze, the sound z.
It opens زبان (zabân), "language", the thing you are learning. It is ر, from the last lesson, with one dot above, and like ر it never joins the letter after it.

Writing: ز.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend through the short stroke.
2. without lifting, sweep left through the lower curve.
3. lift once, then place the dot above — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ز (ze) demonstration at 01:13–01:16 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Lesson 3 of 3.
د (dâl) — the letter inside خدا.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: د and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ر and ز (ze) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say خدا (khodâ), "God". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: د.
د — dâl, the sound d.
It is inside خدا (khodâ), the first half of the farewell خدا حافظ. It is the third letter that never joins forward: after د (dâl), as after ر and ا, the pen lifts and the next letter starts fresh.

Writing: د.
1. start here: begin at the upper tip and descend through the folded shoulder.
2. turn left along the baseline without lifting — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, د (dâl) demonstration at 01:04–01:06 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).
