Lesson 2 of 5.
लापसी (lāpsī) — a sweet wheat dish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a papad, then the Marwadi for pickle.

You'll want to know: लापसी.
लापसी — lāpsī — "a sweet wheat dish".

The word, taken apart.
लापसी (lāpsī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lappasikā, "a sweetmeat or dish", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 10941); the match is through Hindi lāpsī and Gujarati lāpśī. CDIAL calls Sanskrit lapsikā a Sanskritized form of the Middle Indo-Aryan word.

घेवर (ghevar) — a honeycomb sweet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for pickle, then the Marwadi for a sweet wheat dish.

You'll want to know: घेवर.
घेवर — ghevar — "a honeycomb sweet".

The word, taken apart.
घेवर (ghevar) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghṛtapūra, "sweetmeat of flour, milk, coconut and ghee". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 4502); the match is through Gujarati ghebar. It is built on ghṛta, "ghee", and pūra, "cake".

Lesson 4 of 5.
मक्की (makkī) — maize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a sweet wheat dish, then the Marwadi for a honeycomb sweet.

You'll want to know: मक्की.
मक्की — makkī — "maize".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Hindi makkā and makāī, "maize", under Sanskrit markaka, the name of a heron-like bird (CDIAL 9879), without explaining the link. Maize reached India from the Americas, so how its name arose is not settled.
