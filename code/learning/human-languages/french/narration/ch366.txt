French, chapter 366: Donner un coup de main.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le bénévole — the volunteer; unpaid, voluntary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to discriminate, then the French for the immigration.

You'll want to know: le bénévole.
le bénévole — "the volunteer; unpaid, voluntary".
Les bénévoles servent des repas aux personnes âgées. The volunteers serve meals to elderly people.
The same form for a man or a woman. As an adjective: un travail bénévole.

The word, taken apart.
bénévole is a learned borrowing from Latin benevolus "well-wishing, kind" (Lewis & Short), built from bene "well" and velle "to want".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'association — the association, the club, the charity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the volunteer, then the French for the volunteering.

You'll want to know: l'association.
l'association — "the association, the club, the charity".
Je travaille dans une association qui aide les personnes âgées. I work for an association that helps elderly people.
In France, une association is a non-profit group of any kind. Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
association is built on associer (EtymDB), from Latin associāre "to join to", built on socius "companion" (Lewis & Short).

Après l'incendie, il y a eu beaucoup de solidarité dans le village. After the fire, there was a lot of solidarity in the village.

The word, taken apart.
solidarité is built on solidaire (EtymDB), from the Latin legal phrase in solidum "for the whole sum", from solidus "solid, whole" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: la communauté.
la communauté — "the community".
Toute la communauté a aidé cette famille. The whole community helped this family.
La vie en communauté — living together in a group.

The word, taken apart.
communauté is usually explained as built on commun "common", which is inherited from Latin commūnis "shared, common" (EtymDB), after Latin commūnitās "fellowship" (Lewis & Short).
