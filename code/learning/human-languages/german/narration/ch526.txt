German, chapter 526: Wischen und fegen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Staub — dust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to play video games, then the German for headphones.

You'll want to know: der Staub.
der Staub — "dust".
Auf dem alten Schrank liegt viel Staub. There's a lot of dust on the old cupboard.
No plural.

The word, taken apart.
Staub is inherited from Old High German stoup "dust" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for dust, then the German for to wipe.

You'll want to know: fegen.
fegen — "to sweep".
Mein Vater fegt jeden Morgen die Terrasse. My father sweeps the terrace every morning.
In southern Germany and Austria also kehren.

The word, taken apart.
fegen goes back to Middle High German vegen "to clean, to polish"; EtymDB lists the word, glossed "to cleanse, to sweep", without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
staubsaugen — to vacuum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to wipe, then the German for to sweep.

You'll want to know: staubsaugen.
staubsaugen — "to vacuum".
Jeden Freitag staubsaugt mein Mann die ganze Wohnung. Every Friday my husband vacuums the whole flat.
Both forms are correct: er staubsaugt and er saugt Staub.

The word, taken apart.
staubsaugen is built on Staub "dust" and saugen "to suck", inherited from Old High German sūgan, from Proto-Germanic sūganą (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Lappen — cloth, rag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to sweep, then the German for to vacuum.

You'll want to know: der Lappen.
der Lappen — "cloth, rag".
Sie putzt das Fenster mit einem feuchten Lappen. She cleans the window with a damp cloth.
Plural: die Lappen. Informal for a driving licence.

The word, taken apart.
Lappen is inherited from Middle High German lappe and Old High German lappo, from Proto-Germanic lappa- (EtymDB).
