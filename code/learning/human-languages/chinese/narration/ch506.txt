Mandarin Chinese, chapter 506: Music on Stage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yuèduì — band (of musicians).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a stage (in a theatre), then the Mandarin for to rehearse.

You'll want to know: yuèduì.
yuèduì — "band (of musicians)".
wǒ zài yuèduì tán le sān nián jítā — I played guitar in a band for three years.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuèduì is built from 乐 'music' and duì 'a troop', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngaewk and dwojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'music' and the second 'a band of people'.

xiǎotíqín — "violin".

The word, taken apart.
xiǎotíqín is built from 小 'small', tí 'to lift' and qín 'a zither', all inherited from Middle Chinese, sjewX, dej and gim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to carry by hand' and the third as a stringed instrument; the name for the violin is modern.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to perform on an instrument, then the Mandarin for a violin.

You'll want to know: Jīngjù.
Jīngjù — "Beijing opera".
nǐ kàn guo Jīngjù ma — Have you ever seen Beijing opera?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
Jīngjù is built from Jīng, short for the capital Beijing, and jù 'a play', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaeng and gjaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'great' and the second 'to increase' and 'hard'; the sense 'a play' is not given there.
