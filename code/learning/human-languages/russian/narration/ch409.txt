Russian, chapter 409: Breaking down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
поломка (polómka) — a breakdown, a fault.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a driving licence, then the Russian for to fine.

You'll want to know: поломка.
поломка (polómka) — "a breakdown, a fault".
Из-за поломки мы приехали домой поздно. Iz-za polómki my priyékhali domóy pózdno. Because of the breakdown we got home late.
Feminine. Built on поломать — to break.

The word, taken apart.
поломка (polómka) is built on поломать 'to break', from ломать 'to break'; EtymDB lists ломать without a deeper source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
эвакуатор (evakuátor) — a tow truck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: эвакуатор.
эвакуатор (evakuátor) — "a tow truck".
Эвакуатор приехал через час. Evakuátor priyékhal chérez chas. The tow truck came an hour later.
Masculine. Built on эвакуировать — to evacuate, to remove.

The word, taken apart.
эвакуатор (evakuátor) is built on эвакуировать 'to evacuate, to clear away', which EtymDB lists without a source; it is usually traced to Latin ēvacuāre 'to empty out', on vacuus 'empty' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
буксировать (buksírovat) — to tow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a breakdown, then the Russian for a tow truck.

You'll want to know: буксировать.
буксировать (buksírovat) — "to tow".
Сосед буксировал нашу машину до дома. Soséd buksíroval náshu mashínu do dóma. The neighbour towed our car home.
Imperfective; perfective отбуксировать. Present буксирую.

The word, taken apart.
буксировать (buksírovat) is built on буксир 'a tug, a towrope'. Буксир is usually traced to a Dutch sailors' word for towing; EtymDB lists it without a source, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 4 of 5.
домкрат (domkrát) — a jack (for a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tow truck, then the Russian for to tow.

You'll want to know: домкрат.
домкрат (domkrát) — "a jack (for a car)".
Без домкрата колесо не поменять. Bez domkráta kolesó ne pomenyát. You cannot change a wheel without a jack.
Masculine; the stress is on the last syllable.

The word, taken apart.
домкрат (domkrát) was borrowed from Dutch dommekracht (EtymDB), a word usually explained as dom 'dumb' and kracht 'strength': a tool that lifts by plain force.

Lesson 5 of 5.
автосервис (avtosérvis) — a car repair shop, a garage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to tow, then the Russian for a jack.

You'll want to know: автосервис.
автосервис (avtosérvis) — "a car repair shop, a garage".
Мы оставили машину в автосервисе на два дня. My ostávili mashínu v avtosérvise na dva dnya. We left the car at the garage for two days.
Masculine. In speech, often сервис.

The word, taken apart.
автосервис (avtosérvis) is built from авто-, short for автомобиль 'a car', and сервис 'service', which was borrowed from English service (EtymDB). EtymDB traces the English word through Old French to Latin servitium.
