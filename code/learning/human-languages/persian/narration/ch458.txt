Persian, chapter 458: Sellers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
دستفروش (dastforush) — a street vendor, a hawker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a covered hall in the bazaar, then the Persian for a caravanserai.

You'll want to know: دستفروش.
دستفروش (dastforush) — "a street vendor, a hawker".
در مترو یک دستفروش جوراب میفروخت. Dar metro yek dastforush jurâb miforukht. A hawker was selling socks in the metro.
Literally 'hand-seller': dast 'hand' + forush 'seller'.

The word, taken apart.
dastforush is a native compound of dast 'hand', which EtymDB traces to Old Persian dasta-, and forush, the present stem of forukhtan 'to sell', usually traced to Middle Persian frōxtan.

Lesson 2 of 5.
عطاری ('attâri) — a herbalist's shop, a herb and spice shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a caravanserai, then the Persian for a street vendor.

You'll want to know: عطاری.
عطاری ('attâri) — "a herbalist's shop, a herb and spice shop".
مادرم از عطاری دارچین خرید. Mâdaram az 'attâri dârchin kharid. My mother bought cinnamon at the herbalist's.
The seller is the 'attâr. These shops sell dried herbs, spices and old remedies.

The word, taken apart.
'attâri is built from 'attâr 'a seller of perfumes and herbs', usually traced to Arabic ʿaṭṭār, a word built on ʿiṭr 'perfume', which EtymDB lists, with the Persian ending -i.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a herbalist's shop, then the Persian for a draper.

You'll want to know: عمده فروش.
عمده فروش ('omdeforush) — "a wholesaler".
عمده فروش ('omdeforush) برنج را کیسه ای میفروشد. 'Omdeforush berenj râ kise-i miforushad. The wholesaler sells rice by the sack.
'omde 'in bulk' + forush 'seller'.

The word, taken apart.
'omdeforush is built from 'omde 'main part, in bulk', which EtymDB takes from Arabic ʿumda, and native forush, the present stem of forukhtan 'to sell'.
