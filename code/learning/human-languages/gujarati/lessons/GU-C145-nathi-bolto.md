# હું નથી બોલતો — saying *not* about what people do

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-IE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-PRESENT-O-01, GU-NEG-SAMAJTO-NATHI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *we speak Gujarati* and *you speak Gujarati*. Then the repair
sentence from long ago: **સમજતો નથી**, *I do not understand*.

That sentence already holds the whole negative. Here is how it is built.

## Grammar Lens: નથી takes the copula's place
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NEGATE-01]; assesses=[GU-NEG-NATHI-01, GU-NEG-SAMAJTO-NATHI-01] -->

> **હું ગુજરાતી નથી બોલતો.** — *hũ gujarātī nathī bolto.* — I don't speak Gujarati.

Two changes, and only two:

1. **છું** goes, and **નથી** comes in. **નથી** is *is not* worn into one word,
   and it is the same for every person: **હું નથી**, **તે નથી**, **અમે નથી**.
2. The verb drops its person ending and takes **-તો**, the shape **સમજતો** has had
   all along.

**-તો** is the man's form. A woman says **હું ગુજરાતી નથી બોલતી**, with **-તી**.
A group, or a respectful *you*, take **-તા**: **અમે નથી બોલતા**, **તમે નથી બોલતા**.

**નથી** may also stand after the verb, as it does in **સમજતો નથી**. Both orders
are everyday Gujarati: **હું ગુજરાતી બોલતો નથી** says the same thing.

## Grammar Lens: when there is no other verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-NEG-NATHI-01, GU-LEX-C101-DEF101-04] -->

With the copula alone, **નથી** replaces **છે** and nothing else moves: **તે અહીં
નથી**, *she is not here*. That was the first job **નથી** ever did for you.
