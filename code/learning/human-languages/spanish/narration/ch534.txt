Spanish, chapter 534: De pronto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
de pronto — suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for unexpected, then the Spanish for sudden.

You'll want to know: de pronto.
de pronto — "suddenly".
De pronto se apagaron las luces. Suddenly the lights went out.
The same as de repente.

The word, taken apart.
de pronto is built on pronto, which comes from Latin promptus 'brought forth, ready' (EtymDB). De pronto is the moment something is ready to happen and happens.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
súbitamente — all of a sudden, suddenly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for sudden, then the Spanish for suddenly.

You'll want to know: súbitamente.
súbitamente — "all of a sudden, suddenly".
Súbitamente se abrió la puerta. Suddenly the door opened.
A written word; in speech, de repente or de pronto.

The word, taken apart.
súbitamente is built on súbito 'sudden' and the ending -mente. Súbito is a learned borrowing from Latin subitus 'sudden' (EtymDB), from subīre 'to come up', built on sub and īre 'to go'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
a continuación — next, then; below.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for all of a sudden, then the Spanish for immediately.

You'll want to know: a continuación.
a continuación — "next, then; below".
A continuación, el ladrón salió por la ventana. Next, the thief went out through the window.
In a text it means below: Lea las instrucciones que aparecen a continuación.

The word, taken apart.
a continuación is built on continuación, a learned borrowing from Latin continuātiō 'an unbroken series, a following of one thing after another' (Lewis & Short), from continuus 'joined, unbroken'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bruscamente — abruptly, sharply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for immediately, then the Spanish for next.

You'll want to know: bruscamente.
bruscamente — "abruptly, sharply".
El autobús frenó bruscamente. The bus braked sharply.
Brusco also describes a rude manner: una respuesta brusca.

The word, taken apart.
bruscamente is built on brusco 'abrupt' and the ending -mente. Brusco was borrowed from Italian brusco 'sharp, sour' (EtymDB), whose own origin is less certain.
