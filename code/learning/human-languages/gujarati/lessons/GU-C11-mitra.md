# મિત્ર — a friend, and the word inside your own "please"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-ROTLI, GU-PHRASE-MEHERBANI-KARINE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Last chapter asked politely for **roṭlī** with *meherbānī karīne*.
That phrase is about to turn out to share its root with the very first word of
this new chapter.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-MITRA]; assesses=[] -->

> **મિત્ર** — *mitra* — **friend**

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**મ** *ma*, **િ** the short-*i* sign written **before** the consonant it
belongs to — the same leftward quirk you saw open *vichārvũ* — then a
conjunct: **ત્ર** — **ત** *ta* with the virama **્** stripping its vowel,
joined straight into **ર** *ra*. *Mi-tra*, two syllables, the second one a
cluster.

## The word, taken apart — the friend hiding inside "please"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-ETYMON-MITRA-BIND]; assesses=[GU-PHRASE-MEHERBANI-KARINE] -->

**mitra** is Sanskrit for "friend," from Proto-Indo-Iranian ***mitras**,
"(that which) causes binding" — a covenant word before it was a friendship
word. Its exact twin is Avestan **Mithra**, the god of contracts and given
word, whose name lived on in Middle Persian as **Mihr**.

That is not a dead end. **મહેરબાની**, the "kindness" you built *meherbānī
karīne* from last chapter, is Persian *mihrbānī* — built on that very same
**Mihr**. So **મિત્ર**, inherited straight down from Sanskrit, and
**મહેરબાની**, borrowed centuries later from Persian, are cousins by the same
Indo-Iranian root — one word for "friend," the other for "favor," both once
naming the same idea of a bond that holds.
