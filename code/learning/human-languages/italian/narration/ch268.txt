Italian, chapter 268: A Return Ticket --- And When It Goes Wrong.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
andata e ritorno — the going and the coming back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket". (Un biglietto.) Ask for a coffee the polite way. (Vorrei un caffè.)
[pause 1 second]
Put the two together and you are standing at a ticket window.

You'll want to know: andata e ritorno.
andata e ritorno — "there and back": a return ticket. solo andata — "one way", the going only.
Un biglietto di andata e ritorno. — A return ticket. Solo andata. — One way only.
ritorno you have, "the return". andata is new, and you can almost hear how it was made: it is andato, "gone", turned into a feminine noun, "the going". solo means "only", the same as soltanto.

Grammar Lens: vorrei un biglietto per…
The whole request takes one frame:
Vorrei un biglietto per Roma, andata e ritorno. — I'd like a return ticket to Rome.
per plus the place names where you are going, the same per as in per andare. No article before a city: per Roma, per Milano.
Then the questions that come back at you, and the answers you need:
— Andata e ritorno? — Sì, andata e ritorno. / No, solo andata. — Che binario? — Binario tre.
Che binario?, "which platform?", is the same che that asks che cosa?.

The phrase, taken apart.
andata is built on andare, whose own origin is not settled: EtymDB 2.1 gives it no Latin parent, and the candidates usually proposed, such as Latin ambulāre, "to walk", are disputed. ritorno is ri-, "back", plus tornare, "to turn", inherited from Latin tornāre, "to turn on a lathe" (Lewis & Short, under torno); EtymDB 2.1 derives the noun through French retour. Either way, a return is a turning round.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: andata e ritorno, then solo andata]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Vorrei un biglietto per Roma, andata e ritorno.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the one-way version — Vorrei un biglietto per Milano, solo andata.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask the platform — Scusi, che binario?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is a one-way ticket? (Solo andata.) Which word goes before the city, and does it take an article? (Per — and no article.) What is andata, taken apart? (Andato, "gone", made a noun: the going.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
ho perso il treno — when it goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask for a one-way ticket to Rome. (Vorrei un biglietto per Roma, solo andata.)
[pause 1 second]
Now the ticket is in your pocket, and the train has gone without you.

You'll want to know: ho perso il treno.
ho perso il treno — "I've missed the train".
You have perdere, "to lose". Italian uses the same verb for a train you missed: you lost it. Its past does not follow the -uto pattern of credere, creduto. The participle is short: perso.
Ho perso il treno. — I've missed the train. Ho perso la coincidenza. — I've missed my connection. Ho perso il biglietto. — I've lost my ticket.
Italian says il biglietto where English says my ticket: whose it is goes without saying.

Grammar Lens: reporting a problem.
A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:
Scusi, ho un problema. — Excuse me, I have a problem. Ho perso il treno. — What went wrong. C'è un altro treno oggi? A che ora? — What you need.
Or the fault is not yours: Il treno è in ritardo, "the train is late". Say what happened, in the passato prossimo, then ask the question that gets you moving again.

The phrase, taken apart.
perdere is inherited from Latin perdere (EtymDB 2.1), built on per-, "through", and dare, "to give": to give something away for good, to squander, to lose (Lewis & Short, under perdo). Its Latin participle was perditus (Lewis & Short), but Italian made new ones of its own: the short perso, and the longer perduto, which sounds older and more literary. Neither source says how perso was formed, so that step is left open here.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Ho perso il treno. — then Ho perso la coincidenza.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three parts — Scusi, ho un problema. Ho perso il treno. C'è un altro treno oggi?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the fault that is not yours — Il treno è in ritardo.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the worst one — Ho perso il biglietto.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is the past participle of perdere? (Perso.) How do you say you missed your connection? (Ho perso la coincidenza.) What are the three parts of a complaint? (A courtesy, what went wrong, what you need.) And what are the two Latin pieces of perdere? (Per-, through, and dare, to give.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — one morning at the station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I've missed the train". (Ho perso il treno.)

What you've built.
Two chapters of travel, and they make one morning. You find the station, you buy the ticket, something goes wrong, and you ask your way out of it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every step is a sentence you have said: Scusi, per andare alla stazione?, Vorrei un biglietto per Roma, andata e ritorno., Scusi, ho un problema: ho perso il treno., C'è un altro treno oggi?
[pause 2 seconds]
And when you tell someone about it afterwards, the morning becomes a story: Il treno era in ritardo, quindi ho perso la coincidenza. Scene, arrow, event.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: find it — Scusi, per andare alla stazione?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the answer, said back — Dritto, poi a sinistra?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: buy it — Vorrei un biglietto per Roma, andata e ritorno.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the problem — Scusi, ho un problema. Ho perso il treno.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tell it afterwards — Il treno era in ritardo, quindi ho perso la coincidenza.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is a return ticket? (Andata e ritorno.) What do you say before the problem itself? (Scusi, ho un problema.) In il treno era in ritardo, quindi ho perso la coincidenza, which verb is the scene? (Era.)
