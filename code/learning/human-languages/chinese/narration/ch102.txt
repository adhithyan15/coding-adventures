Mandarin Chinese, chapter 102: Something to Drink.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yǐnliào — drink; soft drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for plate, then the Mandarin for knife.

You'll want to know: yǐnliào.
yǐnliào — "drink; soft drink".
nǐ xiǎng hē shénme yǐnliào — What would you like to drink?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǐnliào is built from yǐn 'to drink' and liào, both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'imX and lew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'to measure' and lists it with a departing tone too, lewH, the one liào continues; how the pair came to mean a drink is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking for what I want.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: yǐnliào]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yǐnliào, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nǐ xiǎng hē shénme yǐnliào — What would you like to drink?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for plate, then the Mandarin for knife, then say yǐnliào again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does yǐnliào mean? ("Drink; soft drink".) Say it once more.

guǒzhī is built from guǒ 'fruit' and zhī 'juice', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaX and tsyip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the fruit of a tree' and 'liquid'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
kělè — cola.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for soft drink, then the Mandarin for juice.

You'll want to know: kělè.
kělè — "cola".
tāmen dōu hē kělè — They all drink cola.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kělè is borrowed from the name of a cola drink, usually traced to English cola; its two syllables are written with the characters for kě 'may' and lè 'joy', chosen for their sound.

Lesson 4 of 5.
píjiǔ — beer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for juice, then the Mandarin for cola.

You'll want to know: píjiǔ.
píjiǔ — "beer".
wǒ bù hē píjiǔ — I don't drink beer.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píjiǔ is built from pí and jiǔ 'alcoholic drink'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese tsjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the first is borrowed for its sound from a European word for beer, usually traced to English beer.

Lesson 5 of 5.
bǐnggān — biscuit; cracker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cola, then the Mandarin for beer.

You'll want to know: bǐnggān.
bǐnggān — "biscuit; cracker".
wǒ xiǎngyào yí kuài bǐnggān — I'd like a biscuit.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bǐnggān is built from bǐng, a flat cake, and gān 'dry', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjiengX and kan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); gān is the gān of gānjìng 'clean'.
