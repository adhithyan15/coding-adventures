Mandarin Chinese, chapter 227: A Day at the Sea.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hǎibiān — the seaside; the seashore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for grassland, then the Mandarin for scenery.

You'll want to know: hǎibiān.
hǎibiān — "the seaside; the seashore".
shàng ge yuè wǒmen qùle hǎibiān — Last month we went to the seaside.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hǎibiān is built from hǎi 'sea' and 边 'edge', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xojX and pen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as the pool of heaven that takes in a hundred rivers.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yùnchuán — to get seasick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the seaside, then the Mandarin for a boat.

You'll want to know: yùnchuán.
yùnchuán — "to get seasick".
wǒ yùnchuán le hěn nánshòu — I got seasick and felt awful.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yùnchuán is built from yùn 'dizzy' and chuán 'boat'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese zywen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first, hjunH there, as a halo round the sun or moon, so how it came to mean dizziness is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
yùdào — to run into; to come across.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get seasick, then the Mandarin for a sandy beach.

You'll want to know: yùdào.
yùdào — "to run into; to come across".
zuótiān wǒ yùdàole yí ge lǎo tóngxué — Yesterday I ran into an old classmate.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yùdào is built from yù 'to meet by chance' and 到 'to arrive', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjuH and tawH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to meet without arranging it'.
