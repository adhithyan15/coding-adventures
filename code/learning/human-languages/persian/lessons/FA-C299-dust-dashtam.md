# دوست داشتم (dust dâshtam) — I would like, and if only

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-DUST-DARAM-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-UNREAL-CONDITION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I'd like to go". (*Dust dâram beravam.*) Then "if I had
time, I would go". (*Agar vaqt dâshtam, miraftam.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Put those two together and you have the politest way to turn
something down.

## Grammar Lens: dust dâshtam, for a wish held back
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-DUST-DASHTAM-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-BIYAYAD-01, FA-LEX-C158-CHERA158-02, FA-GRAMMAR-SUBJUNCTIVE-BE-01] -->

*Dust dâram beravam* is open: I'd like to go, and I may. Move *dâshtan* into
the past and the wish steps back from what is happening:

> **دوست داشتم بیایم، ولی وقت ندارم.** — *dust dâshtam biyâyam, vali vaqt
> nadâram* — I'd love to come, but I don't have time.

The wished-for verb stays in the subjunctive, *biyâyam*, as it did after
*dust dâram*. Only *dâshtan* moves. Said about the past, it is "I would have
liked": *dust dâshtam ân film râ bebinam*, "I'd have liked to see that
film". *Bebinam* is *didan* in the subjunctive, on its stem *bin-*.

## Grammar Lens: kâsh, "if only"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-KASH-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-WOULD-MI-PAST-01, FA-GRAMMAR-AGAR-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-OMIDVARAM-KE-01] -->

**کاش**, *kâsh*, is a wish with no result attached. With the plain past it
wishes against what is true now:

> **کاش وقت داشتم.** — *kâsh vaqt dâshtam* — If only I had time.

With the subjunctive it wishes for something still open:

> **کاش فردا هوا خوب باشد.** — *kâsh fardâ havâ khub bâshad* — I do hope
> the weather is good tomorrow.

That second one sits close to *omidvâram ke*, with more longing in it.
