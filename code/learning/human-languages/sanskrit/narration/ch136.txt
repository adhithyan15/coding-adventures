Lesson 1 of 5.
प्राप्नोति (prāpnoti) — reaches, obtains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for climbs, then the Sanskrit for climbs down.

You'll want to know: प्राप्नोति.
प्राप्नोति — prāpnoti — "reaches, obtains".

The word, taken apart.
prāpnoti comes from pra "forward" and the root √āp "reach, obtain" (MW; CDIAL 8947): "reaches forward to, attains". MW compares √āp with Latin apiscor "reach" and aptus, the source of English apt; Gujarati kept it as pāmvũ "to obtain" (CDIAL 8947).

Lesson 2 of 5.
धारयति (dhārayati) — wears, holds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for climbs down, then the Sanskrit for reaches.

You'll want to know: धारयति.
धारयति — dhārayati — "wears, holds".

The word, taken apart.
dhārayati is the causative form of the root √dhṛ "hold, bear, support", and in practice the usual present (MW; CDIAL 6791). The root is inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB), and dharma, "what holds things up", comes from it too. Hindi kept it as dhārnā "to hold, owe" (CDIAL 6791).

Lesson 3 of 5.
गणयति (gaṇayati) — counts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for reaches, then the Sanskrit for wears.

You'll want to know: गणयति.
गणयति — gaṇayati — "counts".

The word, taken apart.
gaṇayati "counts, reckons" appears from the Mahābhārata on. CDIAL 3993, after J. C. Wright, suggests it grew from a Middle Indic form of gṛṇ- "address, praise", so its origin is uncertain. It lives on as Hindi ginnā "to count" (CDIAL 3993).

Lesson 4 of 5.
मार्जयति (mārjayati) — wipes, cleans.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for wears, then the Sanskrit for counts.

You'll want to know: मार्जयति.
मार्जयति — mārjayati — "wipes, cleans".

The word, taken apart.
mārjayati is the causative form of the root √mṛj "wipe, rub, clean" (MW). MW sets √mṛj beside Latin mulgere "to milk" and English milk. The plain verb mārjati lives on as Gujarati mājvũ "to scour" (CDIAL 10080).

Lesson 5 of 5.
बध्नाति (badhnāti) — ties.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for counts, then the Sanskrit for wipes.

You'll want to know: बध्नाति.
बध्नाति — badhnāti — "ties".

The word, taken apart.
badhnāti comes from the root √bandh "bind, tie", in its Rigvedic class-9 form (CDIAL 9133). MW sets √bandh beside Gothic bindan and English bind, and the other present bandhati lives on as Hindi bāṁdhnā "to tie" (CDIAL 9139).
