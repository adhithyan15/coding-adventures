Hindi, chapter 405: Speed and Bumps.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
गति (gati) — speed (formal); motion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to crawl, then the Hindi for to drive (animals).

You'll want to know: गति.
गति — gati — "speed (formal); motion".
इस सड़क पर गति सीमा चालीस है। is saṛak par gati sīmā cālīs hai. The speed limit on this road is forty.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
गति (gati) is a tatsama, Sanskrit gati, "going, moving, motion" (Monier-Williams), kept unchanged; road signs and the news use it: गति सीमा, the speed limit.

You'll want to know: गड्ढा.
गड्ढा — gaḍḍhā — "a pothole; a pit".
इस सड़क में बहुत गड्ढे हैं। is saṛak meṁ bahut gaḍḍhe haiṁ. This road has a lot of potholes.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
गड्ढा (gaḍḍhā) belongs with gaḍḍa, "hole, pit", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 3981); CDIAL gives Hindi gāṛā, "hole", there, but not this form with ḍḍh, so the link is probable rather than shown.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a pothole, then the Hindi for a conductor.

You'll want to know: उछलना.
उछलना — uchalnā — "to bounce; to jump up".
गड्ढे में बस उछली और सब लोग हँस पड़े। gaḍḍhe meṁ bas uchlī aur sab log hãs paṛe. The bus bounced in the pothole and everyone burst out laughing.
Past (he …): वह बच्चा ख़ुशी से उछला, vah baccā khuśī se uchlā.

The word, taken apart.
उछलना (uchalnā) goes back to ut-śalati, "springs up", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 1843), which lists Hindi uchalnā, "to leap".

Lesson 5 of 6.
कटना (kaṭnā) — to be cut; to branch off (of a road).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a conductor, then the Hindi for to bounce.

You'll want to know: कटना.
कटना — kaṭnā — "to be cut; to branch off (of a road)".
आगे से एक सड़क बाएँ कटती है। āge se ek saṛak bāẽ kaṭtī hai. Further on, a road branches off to the left.
Past (it …): वह पतंग कट गई, vah patang kaṭ gaī.
बिजली कट गई, the power went off.

The word, taken apart.
कटना (kaṭnā) is the intransitive partner of काटना, both from Sanskrit kartati, "cuts" (CDIAL 2854), which lists Hindi kaṭnā, "to be cut". A side road कटती where it cuts away from the main one.
