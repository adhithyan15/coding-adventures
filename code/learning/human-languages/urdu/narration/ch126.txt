Urdu, chapter 126: Crow, Peacock, Butterfly, Ant, Donkey.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
کوا (kavvā) — a crow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an island, then the Urdu for a sparrow.

You'll want to know: کوا.
کوا — kavvā — "a crow".

The word, taken apart.
کوا (kavvā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kāka, "crow" (CDIAL 2993). CDIAL notes that the later forms partly imitate the bird's call.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مور (mor) — a peacock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a sparrow, then the Urdu for a crow.

You'll want to know: مور.
مور — mor — "a peacock".

The word, taken apart.
مور (mor) is inherited. CDIAL lists it under mora, a form beside Sanskrit mayūra, "peacock" (CDIAL 9865). Some trace the Sanskrit word to Dravidian or to Austroasiatic; Bailey connects it with an Iranian colour word.
