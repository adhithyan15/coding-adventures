Mandarin Chinese, chapter 320: On the Phone.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guàduàn — to hang up the phone; to end a call.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to write to each other, then the Mandarin for phone charges.

You'll want to know: guàduàn.
guàduàn — "to hang up the phone; to end a call".
tā hái méi shuō wán jiù guàduàn le diànhuà — He hung up before he had finished.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guàduàn is built from guà 'to hang' and duàn 'to cut off', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kweaH and dwanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to suspend' and the second 'to sever'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bōdǎ — to dial a number.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for phone charges, then the Mandarin for to end a call.

You'll want to know: bōdǎ.
bōdǎ — "to dial a number".
yǒu shì qǐng bōdǎ zhège hàomǎ — If anything comes up, please call this number.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bōdǎ is built from bō and 打 'to strike', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pat and taengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to set in order' and 'to remove' and the second 'to hit'. The sense 'to dial' is modern. Middle Chinese taengX would not regularly give dǎ.

You'll want to know: dǎtōng.
dǎtōng — "to get through on the phone".
dì'èr tiān wǒ cái dǎtōng tā de diànhuà — I didn't get through to him until the next day.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
说起 (shuōqǐ) — to mention; to bring up a subject.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to dial a number, then the Mandarin for to get through on the phone.

You'll want to know: 说起.
说起 — shuōqǐ — "to mention; to bring up a subject".
他在信里说起了你 tā zài xìn lǐ shuōqǐ le nǐ — He mentioned you in his letter.

The word, taken apart.
说起 (shuōqǐ) is built from 说 'to say' and 起 'to rise', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sywet and khiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to tell' and the second 'to rise' and 'to stand up'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
语音 (yǔyīn) — voice message; speech sounds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get through on the phone, then the Mandarin for to bring up a subject.

You'll want to know: 语音.
语音 — yǔyīn — "voice message; speech sounds".
wǒ tīng le māma de yǔyīn — I listened to my mum's voice message.

The word, taken apart.
语音 (yǔyīn) is built from 语 'speech' and 音 'a sound', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjoX and 'im (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to discuss' and the second 'a sound'. A recorded message is a modern sense.
