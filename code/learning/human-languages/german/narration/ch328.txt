German, chapter 328: Abgießen und abschmecken.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Sieb — the sieve, the colander.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to brown quickly, then the German for the lid.

You'll want to know: das Sieb.
das Sieb — "the sieve, the colander".
Er goss die Nudeln in ein Sieb. He poured the pasta into a colander.
Plural: die Siebe.

The word, taken apart.
Sieb is inherited from Old High German sib, from Proto-Germanic sibi "sieve" (EtymDB), the same source as English sieve.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the German for the lid, then the German for the sieve.

You'll want to know: abgießen.
abgießen — "to drain (by pouring off)".
Sie goss die Kartoffeln ab. She drained the potatoes.
Separable, strong: goss ab, hat abgegossen.

The word, taken apart.
abgießen is built on gießen "to pour", Old High German giozan, Proto-Germanic geutaną (EtymDB), with ab "off".

Separable, often with lassen. Perfekt with sein.

Lesson 4 of 5.
abschmecken — to taste for seasoning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: abschmecken.
abschmecken — "to taste for seasoning".
Am Ende schmeckte sie die Suppe mit Salz ab. At the end she seasoned the soup to taste with salt.
Separable: mit Salz und Pfeffer abschmecken.

The word, taken apart.
abschmecken is built on schmecken "to taste", from Proto-Germanic smakkijaną "to taste" (EtymDB), with ab.
