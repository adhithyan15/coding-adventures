Hindi, chapter 443: Canals and Fields.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नहर (nahar) — a canal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a straw, then the Hindi for to roll (something).

You'll want to know: नहर.
नहर — nahar — "a canal".
इस नहर से खेतों को पानी मिलता है। is nahar se khetoṁ ko pānī miltā hai. The fields get their water from this canal.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
नहर (nahar) is usually traced to Arabic nahr, "a river, a stream", through Persian; Hindi breaks the final hr with a vowel and uses it for a channel dug by people.

Lesson 2 of 5.
जलाशय (jalāśay) — a reservoir.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to roll (something), then the Hindi for a canal.

You'll want to know: जलाशय.
जलाशय — jalāśay — "a reservoir".
गर्मी में जलाशय का पानी बहुत कम हो जाता है। garmī meṁ jalāśay kā pānī bahut kam ho jātā hai. In the summer the water in the reservoir gets very low.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
जलाशय (jalāśay) is a tatsama, Sanskrit jalāśaya, "a reservoir, a pond, a lake" (Monier-Williams), from jala, "water", and āśaya, "a resting place".

Lesson 4 of 5.
सींचना (sī̃cnā) — to water; to irrigate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a reservoir, then the Hindi for a current.

You'll want to know: सींचना.
सींचना — sī̃cnā — "to water; to irrigate".
किसान नहर के पानी से खेत सींचते हैं। kisān nahar ke pānī se khet sī̃cte haiṁ. The farmers water their fields from the canal.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बगीचा सींचा, usne bagīcā sī̃cā.

The word, taken apart.
सींचना (sī̃cnā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit siñcati, "pours out, sprinkles" (CDIAL 13394), which lists Hindi sī̃cnā, "to irrigate"; the nasal before c has become a nasal vowel.

Lesson 5 of 5.
निराना (nirānā) — to weed (a field).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a current, then the Hindi for to irrigate.

You'll want to know: निराना.
निराना — nirānā — "to weed (a field)".
किसान धूप में खेत निरा रहे थे। kisān dhūp meṁ khet nirā rahe the. The farmers were weeding the fields in the sun.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने खेत निराया, usne khet nirāyā.

The word, taken apart.
निराना (nirānā) goes back to nīḍāti, "cuts down, weeds", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 7542), which lists Hindi nirānā, "to reap, clear weeds".
