Latin, chapter 370: Weight and Standing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
auctōritās, auctōritātis — authority, influence; backing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to excuse, then the Latin for an excuse.

You'll want to know: auctōritās, auctōritātis.
auctōritās, auctōritātis — "authority, influence; backing".
Senex magnam auctōritātem in oppidō habet. The old man has great influence in the town.
auctōritās, auctōritātis f. A man's weight in public life, earned by age and deeds. English authority.

The word, taken apart.
auctōritās is built on auctor "a backer, an originator" (EtymDB), which comes from augeō "to increase" (Lewis & Short). English authority comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.

dignitās, dignitātis — "worth, rank, dignity".
Dignitātem suam semper servāvit. He always kept his dignity.
dignitās, dignitātis f., built on dignus, worthy. A Roman's dignitās is his rank and good name. English dignity.

The word, taken apart.
dignitās is built on dignus "worthy" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -tās that makes a quality. English dignity comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
gravitās, gravitātis — weight; seriousness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for authority, then the Latin for worth.

You'll want to know: gravitās, gravitātis.
gravitās, gravitātis — "weight; seriousness".
Cum gravitāte et sine īrā locūtus est. He spoke seriously and without anger.
gravitās, gravitātis f., from gravis, heavy. A Roman virtue: the seriousness of a man who weighs his words. English gravity.

The word, taken apart.
gravitās is built on gravis "heavy" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), whose cousins include Sanskrit guru "heavy". English gravity comes from it.
