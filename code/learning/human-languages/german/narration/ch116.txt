Lesson 1 of 5.
der Wecker — an alarm clock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for a washing machine, then the German for an armchair.
[pause 2 seconds]
An alarm clock is for the morning: say der Morgen, then der Tag.

You'll want to know: der Wecker.
der Wecker — "an alarm clock".

The word, taken apart.
Wecker is the verb "to wake someone" + -er: "the waker". The verb from Old High German wecchen from Proto-Germanic wakjaną, a cousin of English wake. (EtymDB 2.1).

The word, taken apart.
Kamera from New Latin camera obscura, "dark chamber" from Latin camera, "vaulted room" from Greek kamára (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed: the first cameras were dark rooms.

You'll want to know: die Brille.
die Brille — "glasses".

The word, taken apart.
Brille from Middle High German brille (EtymDB 2.1), from berille, "beryl", from Latin bēryllus: the first eyeglass lenses were ground from beryl. Borrowed.
