Spanish, chapter 635: Pactos y dudas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el pacto — the pact, the deal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to agree on, then the Spanish for to persuade.

You'll want to know: el pacto.
el pacto — "the pact, the deal".
Hicimos un pacto: yo cocino y tú limpias. We made a deal: I cook and you clean.
Pactar — to agree a deal.

The word, taken apart.
pacto is a learned borrowing from Latin pactum 'an agreement' (EtymDB), from pacisci 'to make a bargain'.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cuestionar — to question, to call into doubt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to persuade, then the Spanish for the pact.

You'll want to know: cuestionar.
cuestionar — "to question, to call into doubt".
Nadie cuestiona su palabra. Nobody questions his word.
It means to doubt something, not to ask a question: that is preguntar.

The word, taken apart.
cuestionar is built on cuestión, a learned borrowing from Latin quaestio 'inquiry, question', from quaerere 'to seek, to ask' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
avalar — to vouch for, to guarantee; to back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pact, then the Spanish for to question.

You'll want to know: avalar.
avalar — "to vouch for, to guarantee; to back".
Mis padres me avalan para el piso. My parents are my guarantors for the flat.
El aval — the guarantee; el avalista — the guarantor.

The word, taken apart.
avalar is built on aval 'guarantee', which was borrowed from French aval (EtymDB); where the French word comes from is not settled.

Sus ideas tienen coherencia. Her ideas are consistent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dispuesto — willing, ready.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: dispuesto.
dispuesto — "willing, ready".
Estoy dispuesto a ayudar. I am willing to help.
Estar dispuesto a + infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
dispuesto is the participle of disponer, a learned borrowing from Latin disponere 'to set in order', built from dis and ponere 'to put' (EtymDB).
