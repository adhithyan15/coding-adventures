Telugu, chapter 444: Fuel and Breakdowns.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఇంధనం (indhanaṁ) — fuel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a brake, then the Telugu for a horn.

You'll want to know: ఇంధనం.
ఇంధనం — indhanaṁ — "fuel".
దారి మధ్యలో కారులో ఇంధనం అయిపోయింది. dāri madhyalō kārulō indhanaṁ ayipōyindi. Halfway there the car ran out of fuel.
A word for every kind of fuel: firewood, petrol, gas. అయిపోవు, to run out, goes with it.

The word, taken apart.
ఇంధనం (indhanaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit indhana, "lighting, kindling, fuel", from the root indh, "to kindle" (CDIAL 1584).

Lesson 2 of 5.
చెడిపోవు (ceḍipōvu) — to break down; to go bad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a horn, then the Telugu for fuel.

You'll want to know: చెడిపోవు.
చెడిపోవు — ceḍipōvu — "to break down; to go bad".
దారిలో మా బస్సు చెడిపోయింది. dārilō mā bassu ceḍipōyindi. Our bus broke down on the way.
Past (it …): చెడిపోయింది, ceḍipōyindi.
చెడు, bad, + పోవు, to go: gone bad. A machine breaks down this way, and food goes off this way too. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: చెడిపోయింది, it broke down.

The word, taken apart.
చెడిపోవు (ceḍipōvu) is native Dravidian: చెడు, "to rot, to be spoiled" (DEDR 2760), with పోవు, "to go" (DEDR 4572), which marks a change that has happened.

Lesson 3 of 5.
మొరాయించు (morāyiñcu) — to refuse to start; to balk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for fuel, then the Telugu for to break down.

You'll want to know: మొరాయించు.
మొరాయించు — morāyiñcu — "to refuse to start; to balk".
చలికి మా కారు మొరాయించింది. caliki mā kāru morāyiñcindi. In the cold our car would not start.
Past (it …): మొరాయించింది, morāyiñcindi.
Said of a machine, or a stubborn animal, that will not do what it should. Its subject is a thing, so the past shown is the third person: మొరాయించింది, it would not start.

The word, taken apart.
మొరాయించు (morāyiñcu) is a Telugu verb in -ఇంచు whose first part has an origin that is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
పంక్చరు (paṅkcaru) — a puncture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to break down, then the Telugu for to refuse to start.

You'll want to know: పంక్చరు.
పంక్చరు — paṅkcaru — "a puncture".
సైకిలు టైరుకు పంక్చరు అయింది. saikilu ṭairuku paṅkcaru ayindi. The bicycle tyre got a puncture.
పంక్చరు (paṅkcaru) అయింది, it got a puncture; the shop that mends it is the పంక్చరు (paṅkcaru) shop.

The word, taken apart.
పంక్చరు (paṅkcaru) is a loan from English puncture, with the Telugu final -ఉ.

Lesson 5 of 5.
మరమ్మతు చేయు (marammatu cēyu) — to mend; to repair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to refuse to start, then the Telugu for a puncture.

You'll want to know: మరమ్మతు చేయు.
మరమ్మతు చేయు — marammatu cēyu — "to mend; to repair".
మెకానిక్కు గంటలో బస్సును మరమ్మతు చేశాడు. mekānikku gaṇṭalō bassunu marammatu cēśāḍu. The mechanic repaired the bus within an hour.
Past (I …): మరమ్మతు చేశాను, marammatu cēśānu.
మరమ్మతు, a repair, + చేయు, to do. The thing repaired takes -ని or -ను: బస్సును.

The word, taken apart.
మరమ్మతు చేయు (marammatu cēyu) pairs మరమ్మతు, a loan from Arabic marammat, "repair", which came through Persian and Hindustani, with the native చేయు, "to do" (DEDR 1957).
