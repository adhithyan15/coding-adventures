Arabic, chapter 542: On stage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مسرحية (masraḥiyya) — a stage play.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to carve, then the Arabic for a painter.

You'll want to know: مسرحية.
مسرحية — masraḥiyya — "a stage play".
شاهدنا مسرحية مضحكة في المسرح. shāhadnā masraḥiyya muḍḥika fī al-masraḥ. We watched a funny play at the theatre.

The word, taken apart.
مسرحية (masraḥiyya) is built on the native Arabic root s-r-ḥ, "to send out to pasture" (Lane). Masraḥ, a theatre, which you know, first named a pasture: a wide open place.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مشهد (mashhad) — a scene; a view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a painter, then the Arabic for a stage play.

You'll want to know: مشهد.
مشهد — mashhad — "a scene; a view".
بكت أختي في المشهد الأخير. bakat ukhtī fī al-mashhad al-akhīr. My sister cried at the last scene.
Plural: مشاهد, mashāhid.

The word, taken apart.
مشهد (mashhad) is built on the native Arabic root sh-h-d, "to be present, to witness" (Lane). Shāhada, to watch, which you know, is from the same root: a mashhad is what is witnessed.

تصفيق (taṣfīq) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-f-q, "to strike, to clap" (Lane). Taṣfīq is the verbal noun of Form II.

Lesson 4 of 5.
صفق (ṣaffaqa) — to applaud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a scene, then the Arabic for applause.

You'll want to know: صفق.
صفق — ṣaffaqa — "to applaud".
صفق الناس للممثلين. ṣaffaqa an-nās li-l-mumaththilīn. The people applauded the actors.
Present: يصفق, yuṣaffiq.
It takes لـ for the one applauded.

The word, taken apart.
صفق (ṣaffaqa) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-f-q, "to strike, to clap" (Lane). Form II ṣaffaqa is to clap again and again.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

تمثيل — tamthīl — "acting".
درست أختي التمثيل في الجامعة. darasat ukhtī at-tamthīl fī al-jāmiʿa. My sister studied acting at university.

The word, taken apart.
تمثيل (tamthīl) is built on the native Arabic root m-th-l, "to be like, to resemble" (Lane). Mumaththil, an actor, which you know, is from the same root: acting is making a likeness.
