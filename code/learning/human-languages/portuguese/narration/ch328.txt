Portuguese, chapter 328: Pelo mar fora.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o navegador — the navigator, the seafarer; the web browser.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the throne, then the Portuguese for the crown.

You'll want to know: o navegador.
o navegador — "the navigator, the seafarer; the web browser".
O navegador desenhou um mapa da costa. The navigator drew a map of the coast.
On a computer, o navegador is the web browser.

The word, taken apart.
navegador comes from Latin nāvigātor "a sailor" (EtymDB), from nāvigāre "to sail".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a caravela — the caravel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the crown, then the Portuguese for the navigator.

You'll want to know: a caravela.
a caravela — "the caravel".
A caravela era um barco leve e rápido. The caravel was a light, fast ship.
The small sailing ship of the age of discovery.

The word, taken apart.
caravela comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB); it is usually traced through Late Latin carabus to Greek kárabos "a light boat".

Lesson 3 of 5.
naufragar — to be shipwrecked, to sink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the navigator, then the Portuguese for the caravel.

You'll want to know: naufragar.
naufragar — "to be shipwrecked, to sink".
O barco naufragou perto da costa. The boat sank near the coast.
Also of plans: o projeto naufragou.

The word, taken apart.
naufragar is a learned borrowing from Latin naufragāre "to suffer shipwreck", built on nāvis "a ship" and frangere "to break" (Lewis & Short).

tesouro is inherited from Latin thēsaurus "a treasure" (EtymDB), from Greek thēsaurós "a storehouse, a treasure".
