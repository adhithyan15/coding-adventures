# (reading) — six signs

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word below, you have already been taught. Not one of them is
new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody is going to tell you which one is coming.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SKILL-CONNECTED-READING]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-01, HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-02] -->

> प्रवेश
> निकास
> खुला
> बंद
> दुकान
> स्टेशन

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once. Do not sound each letter out — look at the
whole word and let it arrive.

[PAUSE 3s] Now again, and notice which ones came instantly and which ones you
had to assemble. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-03, HI-LEX-C74-SIGNS-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **प्रवेश** and **निकास** are the two you will meet at every door in
India, and they are worth knowing by shape rather than by letter, because you
usually read them while walking.

[PAUSE 2s] A sign is the one kind of Hindi that gives you no sentence to lean
on — no verb at the end, no **है** to tell you what is going on. One word, and
it has to carry everything.

[PAUSE 2s] That is why this is the place reading starts. Six words is not much
to hold, and holding them without help is the whole exercise.
