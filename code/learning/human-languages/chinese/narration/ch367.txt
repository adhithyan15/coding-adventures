6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
wùhuì — to get the wrong idea; to misunderstand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give up, then the Mandarin for to be worth it.

You'll want to know: wùhuì.
wùhuì — "to get the wrong idea; to misunderstand".
nǐ wùhuì wǒ de yìsi le — You've misunderstood me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wùhuì is built from wù and 会 'to meet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nguH and hwajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'an error' and the second 'to come together'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
了解 (liǎojiě) — to know in depth; to find out about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be worth it, then the Mandarin for to get the wrong idea.

You'll want to know: 了解.
了解 — liǎojiě — "to know in depth; to find out about".
我很了解他 wǒ hěn liǎojiě tā — I know him well.
The new character, in pieces: 解 (jiě) — thirteen strokes in three pieces: on the left a horn piece, seven strokes; on the top right a knife piece, two strokes; on the bottom right a cow piece, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.
Two third tones meet in this word: liǎo is said with a rising tone before jiě, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
了解 (liǎojiě) is built from 了 and 解 (jiě), both inherited from Middle Chinese, lewX and keaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'clever' and 'finished', and the second 'to explain' and 'to loosen'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
解 (jiě) — a horn, a knife and a cow.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a horn, a knife and a cow and Guided Practice.

了 见.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — le, jiàn]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a horn, a knife and a cow.
解 (jiě).
Three pieces: the horn, then the knife, then the cow. Count three, not thirteen.
The horn piece, seven strokes, on the left: a short left-falling stroke, a horizontal that turns down to the left, a long left-falling stroke down the left side, a horizontal that turns down and hooks, two short horizontals inside, then a vertical down the middle.
The knife piece, two strokes, on the top right: a horizontal that turns down and hooks, then a left-falling stroke inside it.
The cow piece, four strokes, on the bottom right: a short left-falling stroke, a short horizontal, a long horizontal, then a vertical down through them.
7 + 2 + 4 = thirteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 解 (jiě), all thirteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 解 (jiě)? (Three: the horn, the knife, the cow.) Which piece comes first? (The horn, on the left.) How many strokes does the knife have? (Two.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
理解 (lǐjiě) — to understand someone's reasons; to sympathize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get the wrong idea, then the Mandarin for to know in depth.

You'll want to know: 理解.
理解 — lǐjiě — "to understand someone's reasons; to sympathize".
因为我也当过学生所以我很理解你 yīnwèi wǒ yě dāng guo xuésheng suǒyǐ wǒ hěn lǐjiě nǐ — I was a student once too, so I really do understand.
Two third tones meet in this word: lǐ is said with a rising tone before jiě, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
理解 (lǐjiě) is built from 理 'order' and 解 (jiě) 'to untie', both inherited from Middle Chinese, liX and keaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to put in order' and the second 'to explain'.
