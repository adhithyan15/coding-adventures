## You'll want to know: کیا کہا؟
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-FUNC-KYA-KAHA]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KYA, UR-LEX-KAHNA] -->

> **کیا کہا؟** — *kyā kahā?* — **what did you say?**

Two words. **کیا** is the *what* of chapter three, back in the middle of the
sentence where it means *what* rather than marking a yes-or-no question — the
distinction the nineteenth chapter drew. **کہا** is **کہنا** in the past, the
form you use for something that has already been said.

Say it with the voice rising and it is a request to repeat. Said flatly, with a
frown, it is a challenge — so the tune matters more here than in any sentence
this book has taught.

## Grammar Lens: the repair kit, in three sentences
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-FUNC-KYA-KAHA, UR-GRAMMAR-NAHIN-VERB, UR-LEX-SAMAJHNA, UR-GRAMMAR-KYA-POLAR] -->

| when | say |
|---|---|
| you did not follow at all | *maiṅ nahīṅ samajhtā* |
| you did not catch the words | *kyā kahā?* |
| you want it slower | the next lesson |

Not one of these needed a new word before this chapter. **کیا** has been in the
book since chapter three, **نہیں** since chapter one, and **کہنا** arrived one
lesson ago. What was missing was somebody putting them next to each other.
