Mandarin Chinese, chapter 363: Out in Public.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zūnzhòng — to show respect for; to respect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for impartial, then the Mandarin for a duty you owe.

You'll want to know: zūnzhòng.
zūnzhòng — "to show respect for; to respect".
wǒmen yīnggāi zūnzhòng biérén de xiǎngfǎ — We should respect other people's ideas.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zūnzhòng is built from zūn and 重 'heavy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tswon and drjowngX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'honoured, as against humble' and the second 'much' and 'careful'.

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
