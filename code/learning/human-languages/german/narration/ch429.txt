Lesson 1 of 6.
der Schimmel — the mould (US: mold); a white horse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for leaky, then the German for the plumber.

You'll want to know: der Schimmel.
der Schimmel — "the mould (US: mold); a white horse".
In der Ecke vom Bad ist Schimmel. There is mould in the corner of the bathroom.
No plural for mould. Ein Schimmel is also a white horse.

The word, taken apart.
Schimmel is inherited from Middle High German schimel (EtymDB). Dutch schimmel 'mould' is a cousin; the white horse is usually said to be named for its mould-white colour.

You'll want to know: flackern.
flackern — "to flicker".
Die Lampe über dem Bett flackert. The lamp above the bed flickers.
Es flackert, es hat geflackert.

The word, taken apart.
flackern is an old German verb for a light that flutters (EtymDB lists 'to flicker, to flutter'); its origin before that is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.
klemmen — to jam, to stick; to pinch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the mould, then the German for to flicker.

You'll want to know: klemmen.
klemmen — "to jam, to stick; to pinch".
Die Tür zum Balkon klemmt. The door to the balcony sticks.
Ich habe mir den Finger geklemmt — I caught my finger.

The word, taken apart.
klemmen is an inherited Germanic verb for squeezing and pinching (EtymDB lists 'to be stuck, to stick'). English clamp is usually counted among its cousins.

Lesson 5 of 6.
defekt — faulty, not working (of a device).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to jam, then the German for to squeak.

You'll want to know: defekt.
defekt — "faulty, not working (of a device)".
Der Fernseher im Zimmer ist defekt. The television in the room is not working.
A little formal; everyday speech says kaputt. Der Defekt — the fault.

The word, taken apart.
defekt is a learned borrowing from Latin dēfectus 'weakened, lacking', the participle of dēficere 'to fail, to fall short' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.
