Arabic, chapter 410: Allowed or required.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أباح (abāḥa) — to permit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to stipulate in writing, then the Arabic for to legislate.

You'll want to know: أباح.
أباح — abāḥa — "to permit".
أباح القانون العمل يوم السبت. abāḥa al-qānūn al-ʿamal yawm as-sabt. The law permitted work on Saturdays.
Present: يبيح, yubīḥ.

The word, taken apart.
أباح (abāḥa) is built on the native Arabic root b-w-ḥ, "to become known, to disclose" (Lane). It is Form IV, to lay a thing open to people: to permit it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
خالف (khālafa) — to break a rule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to permit, then the Arabic for to make obligatory.

You'll want to know: خالف.
خالف — khālafa — "to break a rule".
خالف الرجل قواعد المكان. khālafa ar-rajul qawāʿid al-makān. The man broke the rules of the place.
Present: يخالف, yukhālif.
Also to differ: خالفه الرأي, disagreed with him.

The word, taken apart.
خالف (khālafa) is built on the native Arabic root kh-l-f, "to come after, behind" (Lane). It is Form III, to go against the one behind or before you: to break a rule, to differ.

التزم — iltazama — "to comply".
التزم الجميع بالقواعد. iltazama al-jamīʿ bi-l-qawāʿid. Everyone complied with the rules.
Present: يلتزم, yaltazim.

The word, taken apart.
التزم (iltazama) is built on the native Arabic root l-z-m, "to cling, to stick to" (Lane). It is Form VIII, to bind oneself to a thing: to comply, to commit.

أعفى (aʿfā) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-f-w, "to efface; to pardon" (Lane). It is Form IV, to release someone from a thing: to exempt.
