Latin, chapter 98: Rich, Poor, Foolish, Enough, Too Much.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dīves, dīvitis — rich.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for brave, then the Latin for wise.

You'll want to know: dīves, dīvitis.
dīves, dīvitis — "rich".

The word, taken apart.
dīves is often derived from dīvus, "godlike, divine" (Lewis & Short): the rich as those the gods favour. That account is old and still uncertain.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pauper, pauperis — poor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wise, then the Latin for rich.

You'll want to know: pauper, pauperis.
pauper, pauperis — "poor". English pauper and poverty.

The word, taken apart.
pauper is usually explained as pau-, "little", as in paucus, "few", plus the root of pariō, "to produce" (Lewis & Short): one who gets or produces little.

Lesson 3 of 5.
stultus, stulta, stultum — foolish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rich, then the Latin for poor.

You'll want to know: stultus, stulta, stultum.
stultus, stulta, stultum — "foolish".

The word, taken apart.
stultus is probably related to stolidus, "dull, stupid" (Lewis & Short), and both are often traced to Proto-Indo-European ster-, "stiff" (EtymDB 2.1): a fool as one stiff of mind.

Lesson 4 of 5.
satis — enough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for poor, then the Latin for foolish.

You'll want to know: satis.
satis — "enough". English satisfy.

The word, taken apart.
satis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European sh2tis, "fullness", from seh2-, "to satisfy, to fill" (EtymDB 2.1). English sad is a cousin: it first meant "having had enough".

Lesson 5 of 5.
nimis — too much.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for foolish, then the Latin for enough.

You'll want to know: nimis.
nimis — "too much".

The word, taken apart.
nimis probably begins with the negative ne-, "not", but the rest is disputed: some link it to the stem of minus, "less", while Lewis & Short tie it to a root for measuring.
