# منذ ثلاث سنوات (mundhu thalāth sanawāt) — for three years

## Grammar Lens: mundhu and the present
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C290-MUNDHU-SPAN-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C233-WAQT233-01, AR-LEX-C64-VERBS64-02, AR-LEX-C64-VERBS64-03, AR-LEX-C93-TOWN93-04, AR-LEX-C86-ADJ86-04] -->

For something that began in the past and is still going on, Arabic keeps the
present and adds *mundhu* with the span:

> **أعمل هنا منذ ثلاث سنوات.** — *aʿmalu hunā mundhu thalāth sanawāt* — I have
> been working here for three years.

English says "have been working"; Arabic says "I work, since three years".
With a point in time instead of a span, *mundhu* is "since": *anā hunā
mundhu aṣ-ṣabāḥ*, I've been here since the morning.

Counting takes care. *Sana*, a year, ends in **ة**, and three to ten take the
opposite ending to their noun: *thalāth sanawāt*, three years, but *thalāthat
ashhur*, three months. For two, the noun takes *-ayn*: *sanatayn*, two years;
*usbūʿayn*, two weeks.

## Grammar Lens: ago
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C290-QABLA-AGO-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C87-TIME87-05, AR-LEX-C86-ADJ86-03, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-03, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01] -->

For a finished event, use the past and **قبل** — *qabla*, "before" — with
the span:

> **وصلت قبل أسبوع.** — *waṣaltu qabla usbūʿ* — I arrived a week ago.

*Mundhu* with a past verb can say "ago" too: *waṣaltu mundhu usbūʿ*. With the
present it can only mean "for".
