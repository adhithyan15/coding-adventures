Italian, chapter 408: Dal benzinaio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il benzinaio — the petrol station attendant; the petrol station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the horn, then the Italian for to mist up.

You'll want to know: il benzinaio.
il benzinaio — "the petrol station attendant; the petrol station".
Il benzinaio controlla l'olio. The attendant checks the oil.
In speech andare dal benzinaio means to go to the petrol station.

The word, taken apart.
benzinaio is built on benzina 'petrol', which is usually traced to German Benzin, named after benzoin, a resin; the word came into Italian as a borrowing.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: il gasolio.
il gasolio — "the diesel (fuel)".
La mia macchina va a gasolio. My car runs on diesel.
Also il diesel. Andare a gasolio — to run on diesel.

The word, taken apart.
gasolio is built from gas, a word coined in Dutch from Greek kháos 'empty space', and olio 'oil', which is inherited from Latin oleum (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il carburante — the fuel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the petrol station attendant, then the Italian for the diesel.

You'll want to know: il carburante.
il carburante — "the fuel".
Il carburante costa sempre di più. Fuel costs more and more.
The general word: la benzina and il gasolio are both carburante.

The word, taken apart.
carburante comes from carburare 'to mix fuel with air', which was borrowed from French carburer, built on Latin carbō 'charcoal' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
rifornire — to refuel; to supply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the fuel, then the Italian for the tank.

You'll want to know: rifornire.
rifornire — "to refuel; to supply".
Dobbiamo rifornire la macchina prima del viaggio. We need to refuel the car before the trip.
Il rifornimento is refuelling. Conjugated like finire: rifornisco.

The word, taken apart.
rifornire is built from ri- 'again' and fornire 'to supply', which is usually traced to a Germanic verb; it was borrowed into Italian through Old French fornir.
