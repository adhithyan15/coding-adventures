# j'étais content — how it felt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-SKILL-READ-NARRATIVE-01, FR-LEX-CETAIT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the last line of the journey you read: "I was pleased".
(*J'étais content.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That sentence chose its past for a reason. This lesson is about
why.

## Grammar Lens: a feeling is scene
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-SENTIMENT-ETAIS-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C56-SENTIR8-02, FR-LEX-C162-QUAL162-01, FR-LEX-C218-QUAL218-04, FR-GRAM-FEMININ-E-01] -->

You already own the feelings: **content**, **heureux**, **triste**,
**inquiet**, **nerveux**, **fier**. A feeling has no edge. It was going on,
like the weather, so in a story it is scene, and it takes the imparfait of
*être*:

> *D'abord, j'étais nerveux. Ensuite, j'étais content.*
> — At first I was nervous. Then I was pleased.

The adjective agrees with whoever felt it, and the ear hears some of that.
*Content* ends in a silent *t*; **contente** sounds it. *Inquiet* becomes
**inquiète**, *nerveux* becomes **nerveuse**, *heureux* becomes
**heureuse**. *Triste* has one form for a man or a woman.

> *Ma mère était inquiète.* — My mother was worried.
> *Nous étions contents.* — We were pleased.

The passé composé is possible too, and it changes the picture: it makes the
feeling a reaction, an event at one moment. *Quand j'ai vu mon ami, j'ai été
content.* — When I saw my friend, I was glad.

## Grammar Lens: content de
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-CONTENT-DE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C110-LIEU62-04, FR-LEX-C218-QUAL218-03, FR-LEX-DE-PREP-01] -->

To say **what** you were pleased with, add **de**:

> *J'étais content **du** voyage.* — I was pleased with the trip.
> *Je suis content **de** partir.* — I'm glad to be leaving.

*De* and *le* weld into **du**, and an infinitive can follow *de*
directly. **Fier** works the same way: *je suis
fier de toi*, I'm proud of you.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**content** comes from Latin *contentus*, "satisfied", the participle of
*continēre*, "to hold together" (Lewis & Short, under *contentus* and
*contineo*), itself *con-* plus *tenēre*, "to hold". EtymDB 2.1 follows it
back through Old French *content*. To be content is to hold yourself in,
wanting nothing further. **inquiet** is a borrowing from Latin *inquiētus*,
"restless" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis & Short): *in-*, "not", and *quiētus*,
"quiet".
