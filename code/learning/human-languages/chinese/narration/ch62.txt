Mandarin Chinese, chapter 62: Fast, Far and Near.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
快 (kuài) — fast; quick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for short in height, then the Mandarin for new.

You'll want to know: 快.
快 — kuài — "fast; quick".
我的手机很快 wǒ de shǒujī hěn kuài — My phone is fast.
The new character, in pieces: 快 (kuài) — on the left the heart piece from the foot of 想, standing upright, and on the right a four-stroke piece read guài. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
快 (kuài) is inherited from Middle Chinese khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to one's liking' and 'glad'. The sense 'fast' is not given there, so how it got it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 2 of 6.
快 (kuài) — the upright heart, then guài.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the upright heart, then guài.
快 (kuài).
Two pieces, left before right.
The upright heart piece, three strokes: a short dot on the left, a short dot on the right, then a long vertical down between them: the heart piece from the foot of 想, standing upright.
The right piece, four strokes, read guài: a horizontal that turns down at its right end, a long horizontal across, a long left-falling stroke from the top down through the bars, and a stroke falling to the right.
3 + 4 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 快 (kuài), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 快 (kuài)? (Two.) Which dot comes first? (The left one.) How many strokes on the right side? (Four.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 快 (kuài), pinned stroke paths and medians.

Lesson 6 of 6.
zhīdào — to know a fact; to be aware.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for far, then the Mandarin for near.

You'll want to know: zhīdào.
zhīdào — "to know a fact; to be aware".
wǒ zhīdào nǐ xiǎng shuō shénme — I know what you want to say.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhīdào is built from zhī 'to perceive' and dào 'the way', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trje and dawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean knowing a fact is not settled.
