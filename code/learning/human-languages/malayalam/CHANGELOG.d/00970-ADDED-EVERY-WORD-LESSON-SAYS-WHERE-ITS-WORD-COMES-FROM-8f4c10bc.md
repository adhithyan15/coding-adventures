## Added — every word lesson says where its word comes from

The 1384 word lessons that lacked an etymology hook, a visible origin, or both now have both: a `## The word, taken apart` block (or `phrase`) after the word's introduction and a matching `etymology_hook` of 120 characters or fewer.

- **Native words** cite the Dravidian Etymological Dictionary by entry number, as in "native Dravidian, one word with Tamil *maram*, Kannada *mara* and Telugu *m(r)ānu* (DEDR 4711)". Cognates are named only when the full DEDR article lists them under that language, in transliteration. Where DEDR compares a Sanskrit word or puts a query against the Malayalam one, the lesson says so. Words DEDR files in its appendix of Indo-Aryan loans (*vaṇṭi*, *āṇi*, *vāl*, *tūṇŭ*, *kōṭāli*) are not called native.
- **Sanskrit loans** say whether they were taken whole (tatsama) or reshaped (tadbhava) and cite Turner's CDIAL where it has the form. Where CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word itself from Dravidian (*māla*, *kala*, *mīna*, *kōṇa*), the lesson says that too.
- **Other loans** name the donor: English (*kampyūṭṭar*, *beñcŭ*), Portuguese (*kasēra*, *pēna*, *kōppa*), Arabic and Persian, often through Hindustani (*kaṭalās*, *vakkīl*, *jilla*, *kūja*).
- **Uncertain origins** are said plainly, with the usual account when there is one (*śari*, *mōśaṁ*, *cēcci*); words DEDR does not record are not called native.
- **Parts are explained in English** wherever a part would be a word a later lesson teaches.
- **Lessons near the time cap.** ML-C32-ariyuka, ML-C80-malayali and ML-C102-ethra-manikku state the origin in one sentence inside an existing section instead of a new block, since a new heading would have pushed them past 300 s.
