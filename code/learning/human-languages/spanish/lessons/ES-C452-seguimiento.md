# el seguimiento — where the parcel is

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SEGUIR, ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **seguir** and **el entrenamiento**. Put the ending of the
second onto the first.

## You'll want to know: el seguimiento
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SEGUIR] -->

**el seguimiento** — tracking, and in a clinic or an office, follow-up.

*El número de seguimiento* — the tracking number. *Hacer seguimiento de un
pedido* — to track an order. *Una consulta de seguimiento* — a follow-up
appointment.

Whatever is being followed, the word is the same: a parcel across a country,
a patient across six months, a complaint across three departments.

## Grammar Lens: you could have built this yourself
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-03, ES-LEX-C450-SUELO-05] -->

**Seguir** you have had for chapters. **-miento** you met on *entrenamiento*.
Put them together and the word comes out with its gender attached:

| verb | noun |
|---|---|
| entrenar | el entrena**miento** |
| seguir | el segui**miento** |
| sentir | el senti**miento** |

Masculine, all of them, as every **-miento** noun is. And note what happened
to the stem: *seguir* has an **i** that *sigo* turns into... nothing you need
here, because **-miento** attaches to the calm form of the verb, not the
shouting one.

This is the point at which Spanish vocabulary starts paying compound interest.
A verb plus a known ending is not a new word to learn; it is two things you
own, joined.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-03, ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-02] -->

Latin **sequi**, to follow. It is a root you have been walking past for a long
time.

**Enseguida** — *en seguida*, "in following" — is next-thing-along turned into
right-away. And the family in English is enormous: a **sequence** is things
following each other, a **consequence** is what follows from something, a
**sequel** follows a film, and a **second** is the one that follows the first.

*Perseguir* is to follow all the way through, and *conseguir* — to get hold of
something — is to follow it until you have it. Spanish named succeeding after
the chasing.
