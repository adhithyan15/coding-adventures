French, chapter 433: Être dédommagé.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'acompte — the down payment, the part payment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the cleaning, then the French for faulty.

You'll want to know: l'acompte.
l'acompte — "the down payment, the part payment".
Pour le gîte, nous avons versé un acompte de cent euros. For the cottage we paid a hundred euros up front.
A masculine noun. Verser un acompte — to pay a part in advance.

The word, taken apart.
acompte comes from à compte 'on account', from compte, built on compter 'to count', which goes back to Latin computāre (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
les arrhes — the deposit (that is not refunded).

You'll want to know: les arrhes.
les arrhes — "the deposit (that is not refunded)".
Si vous annulez, vous perdez les arrhes. If you cancel, you lose the deposit.
Feminine plural: des arrhes. The h is silent.

The word, taken apart.
arrhes is a learned borrowing from Latin arrha 'pledge' (EtymDB), which came from Greek arrhabṓn.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dédommager — to compensate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the down payment, then the French for the deposit.

You'll want to know: dédommager.
dédommager — "to compensate".
La compagnie a dédommagé les passagers du vol annulé. The airline compensated the passengers of the cancelled flight.
Dédommager quelqu'un de quelque chose.

The word, taken apart.
dédommager is built from dé- 'un-' and dommage 'harm', which goes back to Latin damnum 'loss' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists the verb.

Lesson 5 of 5.
indemniser — to pay damages to, to indemnify.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to compensate, then the French for the compensation.

You'll want to know: indemniser.
indemniser — "to pay damages to, to indemnify".
L'assurance va nous indemniser pour la valise perdue. The insurance is going to pay us for the lost suitcase.
Formal, used by insurers: l'indemnisation.

The word, taken apart.
indemniser is built on indemne 'unharmed', a learned borrowing from Latin indemnis, from in- 'not' and damnum 'loss' (Lewis & Short).
