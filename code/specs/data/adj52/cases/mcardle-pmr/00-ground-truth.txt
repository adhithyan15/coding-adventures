GROUND TRUTH (NOT given to ingester, deriver, or plain-Claude subagents)

Source: PMC11724029 — "Late Presentation of McArdle's Disease Mimicking
Polymyalgia Rheumatica: A Case Report and Review of the Literature" (2025,
open access).

Final diagnosis: Late-onset McArdle disease (Glycogen Storage Disease
Type V; myophosphorylase deficiency).

Confirmed by:
- Muscle biopsy: myophosphorylase staining entirely negative in myofibres;
  PAS staining strongly positive in fibers and vacuoles (glycogen).
- Enzymatic assay (Erasmus University Rotterdam): phosphorylase activity
  grossly reduced (3.9; ref 75-400 nmol/min/mg); muscle glycogen elevated
  (234.0; ref 30-180 ug/h/mg).
- Genetics: homozygous PYGM pathogenic variant c.148C>T p.(Arg50*).

Initial misdiagnosis: Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR).

Why PMR was wrong / the discriminators the framework SHOULD catch:
- Markedly elevated creatine kinase (811, then 3473 IU/L) is INCOMPATIBLE
  with PMR — PMR has a normal CK. Elevated CK points to a primary myopathy.
- Lifelong exercise intolerance (childhood inability to sprint, muscle
  spasms on long walks, episodic short-lived myalgia "normal for him") is
  the hallmark of a metabolic myopathy (the McArdle "second wind"), not an
  inflammatory rheumatologic disease of older age.
- Negative ANA/ENA/myositis panel argues against autoimmune myositis.
- The steroid response is a CONFOUNDER that made PMR look correct.

Correct disposition: pursue tissue/genetic diagnosis (muscle biopsy,
myophosphorylase stain, PYGM genetic testing) rather than committing to
PMR. Definitive management is conservative (carbohydrate-rich diet, oral
sucrose before exercise, paced activity), NOT chronic steroids.

What a naive reader / LLM is likely to produce: polymyalgia rheumatica
(common, fits age + proximal pattern + elevated inflammatory markers +
steroid response) and continue/taper steroids — missing the elevated CK
and the lifelong exercise-intolerance red flags.
