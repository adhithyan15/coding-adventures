Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 223: Off to the Fair.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
झूलणो (jhūlṇo) — to swing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to shiver, then the Marwadi for fog.

You'll want to know: झूलणो.
झूलणो — jhūlṇo — "to swing".
मेळो काल है अर हूं झूलसूं। meḷo kāl hai ar hū̃ jhūlsū̃. The fair is tomorrow, and I'll ride the swings.
Past (he …): वो झूल्यो, vo jhūlyo.

The word, taken apart.
झूलणो (jhūlṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed jhulyati, "swings"; CDIAL lists Marwari jhūlṇo, "to swing" (CDIAL 5406).

Lesson 2 of 5.
हिंडोळो (hiṇḍoḷo) — a big swing; a swing cradle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for fog, then the Marwadi for to swing.

You'll want to know: हिंडोळो.
हिंडोळो — hiṇḍoḷo — "a big swing; a swing cradle".
हिंडोळो घणो बड़ो है। hiṇḍoḷo ghaṇo baṛo hai. The swing is very big.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
हिंडोळो (hiṇḍoḷo) is inherited from Sanskrit hindola, "a swing, a swing cradle"; CDIAL lists an Old Marwari word for a revolving swing under it (CDIAL 14094).

Lesson 4 of 5.
झंडो (jhaṇḍo) — a flag (on a pole).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a big swing, then the Marwadi for a big drum.

You'll want to know: झंडो.
झंडो — jhaṇḍo — "a flag (on a pole)".
मंदिर माथै लाल झंडो है। mandir māthai lāl jhaṇḍo hai. There's a red flag on the temple.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
झंडो (jhaṇḍo) is inherited from a reconstructed dhvajadaṇḍa, "a flagstaff", built from Sanskrit dhvaja, "a flag", and daṇḍa, "a staff"; CDIAL lists Hindi jhaṇḍā, "a flag", and a Gujarati cousin. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 6898).

Lesson 5 of 5.
तमासो (tamāso) — a show; a spectacle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a big drum, then the Marwadi for a flag.

You'll want to know: तमासो.
तमासो — tamāso — "a show; a spectacle".
हूं काल तमासो देखसूं। hū̃ kāl tamāso dekhsū̃. Tomorrow I'll watch the show.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तमासो (tamāso) is usually traced to Persian tamāšā, "a spectacle", which Persian took from Arabic; Marwadi ends it in -o.
