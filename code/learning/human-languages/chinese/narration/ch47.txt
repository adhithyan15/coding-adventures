Mandarin Chinese, chapter 47: Months and Years.
8 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 8.
年 (nián) — year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for weekend, then the Mandarin for Sunday.

You'll want to know: 年.
年 — nián — "year".
一年有多少个星期 yì nián yǒu duōshao ge xīngqī — How many weeks are there in a year?
The new character, in pieces: 年 (nián) — six strokes: a short fall, two bars, a short vertical on the left, a long bar, and one long vertical through them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
年 (nián) is inherited from Middle Chinese nen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the grain is ripe'. One harvest a year is the usual explanation of the sense, though the step is not settled.

What you've built.

Script — three bars and a long vertical.
年 (nián).
One piece, six strokes.
A short left-falling stroke at the top left.
Two horizontals, the upper one short, the one below it longer.
A short vertical down from the left end of the second bar.
A long horizontal across the bottom.
Last, the long vertical down through the middle, past the bottom bar.

Lesson 3 of 8.

You'll want to know: yuè.
yuè — "month; moon".
zhège yuè wǒ hěn máng — I'm busy this month.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuè is inherited from Middle Chinese ngjwot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), where the dictionary quotes an old saying that the moon is the essence of water.

You'll want to know: 明年.
明年 — míngnián — "next year".
明年我买车 míngnián wǒ mǎi chē — I'm buying a car next year.
The new character, in pieces: 明 (míng) — 日 on the left and, on the right, the moon piece, the word yuè from the lesson before. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
明年 (míngnián) is built from 明 (míng) 'bright' and 年 (nián) 'year', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjaeng and nen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses mjaeng 'light'.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the sun beside the moon.
明 (míng).
Two pieces, left before right: the sun, then the moon.
日, four strokes, narrow and set a little high on the left.
The moon, four strokes, as on the right of 期: a left-falling stroke, a horizontal that turns down and hooks, and two short horizontals inside.
4 + 4 = eight.
