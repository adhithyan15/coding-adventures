French, chapter 296: Le braquage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le braquage — the armed robbery, the hold-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to acquit, then the French for to release.

You'll want to know: le braquage.
le braquage — "the armed robbery, the hold-up".
Le braquage de la banque a duré dix minutes. The bank robbery lasted ten minutes.
Informal but frequent in the news.

The word, taken apart.
braquage is built with -age on the verb meaning "to aim, to point (a gun)"; the origin of that verb is not settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'otage — the hostage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the armed robbery, then the French for the armed robber.

You'll want to know: l'otage.
l'otage — "the hostage".
Les braqueurs ont pris trois otages. The robbers took three hostages.
Masculine even when the hostage is a woman: un otage.

The word, taken apart.
otage comes from Old French ostage, usually traced to oste "guest, host", from Latin hospes (Lewis & Short): a guest held as a pledge. EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 5 of 5.
échapper — to escape; to slip from (one's hands).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the hostage, then the French for the ransom.

You'll want to know: échapper.
échapper — "to escape; to slip from (one's hands)".
Un des braqueurs s'est échappé par le toit. One of the robbers escaped over the roof.
Used both ways. With se for breaking out: s'échapper de prison. Without se, with à: échapper à un danger — to escape a danger.

The word, taken apart.
échapper comes from Old French eschaper (EtymDB), usually traced to Vulgar Latin excappāre "to slip out of one's cloak", built on cappa "a cloak".
