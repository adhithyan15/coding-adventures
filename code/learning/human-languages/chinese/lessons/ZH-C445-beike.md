## You'll want to know: bèiké
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C445-SEA445-04]; assesses=[] -->

*bèiké* — "seashell".

> *wǒ zhǎodào le hěn duō bèiké fàng zài bāo lǐ* — I found lots of shells and put them in my bag.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*bèiké* is built from *bèi* 'a shellfish' and *ké* 'a hard shell'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese *pajH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as a creature of the sea in a shell; the second is in the Guangyun only under an older spelling, read *khaewk* and glossed 'a hard skin', and the everyday *ké* does not follow from that reading, so its history is not settled.
