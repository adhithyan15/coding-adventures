# Six lines about a trip

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-SCENE-EVENT-01, AR-SKILL-C284-READ-NARRATIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter forty-five asked for four lines that held together. This
one asks for six, and they tell a story.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C284-STORY-STEPS-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C292-LAW-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C291-IDHA-PAST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A told story is not a list of events. It has a **scene**, in
*kāna* and *kāna* with the present. It has **events** in the plain past, put
in order with *awwalan*, *thumma*, *ʿindamā* and *fī an-nihāya*. It has at
least one **reason**, with *li-anna*, *bi-sabab* or *fa-*, and a **feeling**,
with *kuntu* and *-an*, or *shaʿartu bi-*.

[PAUSE 2s] A good one ends by looking forward or wishing: *atamannā an …*,
*idhā … fa-sa- …*, or *law … la- …*.

[PAUSE 2s] At B1 a connected text runs longer than six lines. Six lines is
the first step towards it, not the whole of it.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-KANA-IMPERFECT-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C284-FI-AN-NIHAYA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C284-INDAMA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C284-BADA-AN-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C285-FA-SO-01, AR-LEX-C40-LIANNA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-BI-SABAB-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C289-KANA-ACCUSATIVE-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C289-SHAARTU-BI-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C290-QABLA-AGO-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C290-MUNDHU-SPAN-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C291-FA-RESULT-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C292-LAYTA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C289-WISH-AN-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-AS-SABAB-HUWA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-SUBJUNCTIVE-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-YAJIBU-01, AR-LEX-C288-SAFAR288-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C288-COUNTER-QUESTIONS-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] On paper, right to left, write **six lines** to your reader about a
trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:

1. the scene: when it was, with *qabla* or *al-māḍī*, and the weather, with *kāna*;
2. where you went and who was with you, with *maʿa*, and the ticket: *tadhkirat dhahāb wa-iyāb* or *dhahāb faqaṭ*, and from which platform;
3. the first thing that happened, with *awwalan* or *ʿindamā*;
4. the next thing, with *thumma* or *baʿda an*, and a reason, with *li-anna*, *bi-sabab*, *as-sababu huwa anna* or *fa-*, or what you had to do, with *kāna yajibu an*;
5. how it ended and how you felt, with *fī an-nihāya* and *kuntu … -an* or *shaʿartu bi-*;
6. one line forward: *atamannā an*, a real *idhā*, or *law … la-*.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing
the right past for each line, and joining them.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C38-WA-01, AR-SCRIPT-C285-HAMZA-WAW-01, AR-SCRIPT-C288-HAMZA-YAA-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-SCENE-EVENT-01, AR-LEX-C285-RAY285-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now read your lines as you read the trip to the sea in chapter two
hundred and eighty-four. Mark every verb **S** for scene or **E** for event.
Line one should be mostly **S**; lines three and four mostly **E**. A line
about the weather marked **E** is the commonest slip: change *amṭarat* back
to *kāna aṭ-ṭaqs mumṭiran* if you meant the scene.

[PAUSE 2s] Then three spelling checks. Every *wa-* and *fa-* is joined to the
next word, with no space: **وكان**, **فذهبنا**. Every *-an* after *kāna* on a
word without **ة** has its **ا**: **سعيدا**. And each hamza sits on its seat:
**سؤال** with **ؤ**, **طائرة** with **ئ**.

[PAUSE 2s] Last, read line six alone. If it has *idhā*, is the verb after it
in the past? If it has *law*, does the result carry *la-*?
