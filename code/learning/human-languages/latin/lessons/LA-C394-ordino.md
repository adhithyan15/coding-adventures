## You'll want to know: ōrdinō, ōrdināre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C394-APPARO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**ōrdinō, ōrdināre** — "to set in order, to arrange".

> Librōs suōs ōrdināvit.
>
> *He put his books in order.*

*ōrdinō, ōrdināre, ōrdināvī, ōrdinātum*, built on *ōrdō*, a row. English *ordain* and *coordinate*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*ōrdinō* is built on *ōrdō, ōrdinis* "a row, an order" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English *ordain* comes from it through French.
