Mandarin Chinese, chapter 494: The Entrance Exam.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
高考 (gāokǎo) — college entrance exam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to tutor, then the Mandarin for to seek advice.

You'll want to know: 高考.
高考 — gāokǎo — "college entrance exam".
gāokǎo de shíhou wǒ gǎnjué hěn jǐnzhāng — I felt very nervous during the entrance exam.
The new character, in pieces: 考 (kǎo) — six strokes: the top of 老 lǎo, four strokes, then a short bar and a stroke that drops, runs right, turns down and hooks. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
高考 (gāokǎo) is built from 高 'high' and 考 (kǎo) 'to examine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and khawX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to check' and 'to complete'. It is short for a longer official name, the exam for the higher schools.

Script — the top of lǎo, then two strokes.
考 (kǎo).
The top of 老, four strokes, as in 老 and on the left of 教: a short bar, a vertical through it, a long bar, and a long stroke falling to the left.
Then two strokes under it: a short bar, and a stroke that starts under the bar's left end, drops a little, runs right, then turns down and hooks to the left.
4 + 2 = six.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 考 (kǎo) have? (Six.) What are the first four? (The top of 老.) How does the last stroke end? (It turns down and hooks to the left.)

Lesson 6 of 6.
luòbǎng — to fail an entrance exam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to enter for an exam, then the Mandarin for to pass the entrance exam for.

You'll want to know: luòbǎng.
luòbǎng — "to fail an entrance exam".
luòbǎng yǐhòu tā gǎnjué hěn nánguò — After he failed the entrance exam, he felt awful.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
luòbǎng is built from luò 'to fall' and bǎng 'a board', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lak and pangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as leaves falling and the second 'a wooden board'. Falling off the list of names posted on a board is the usual explanation, but the dictionary does not give that sense.
