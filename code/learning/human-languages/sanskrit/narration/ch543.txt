Sanskrit, chapter 543: Free to Choose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
स्वेच्छा (svecchā) — free will.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for pleasing, then the Sanskrit for neutral.

You'll want to know: स्वेच्छा.
स्वेच्छा — svecchā — "free will".
यदि सः स्वेच्छया आगच्छति, तर्हि वयं प्रसन्नाः स्मः। yadi saḥ svecchayā āgacchati, tarhi vayaṃ prasannāḥ smaḥ. If he comes of his own free will, we are glad.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्वेच्छा (svecchā) is built from sva, "own", and icchā, "a wish", from the root √iṣ, "to wish", which also gives icchati; MW gives svecchā as one's own wish, free will (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
हठः (haṭhaḥ) — obstinacy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for free will, then the Sanskrit for helpless.

You'll want to know: हठः.
हठः — haṭhaḥ — "obstinacy".
यदि बालः हठं करोति, तर्हि माता कुपिता भवति। yadi bālaḥ haṭhaṃ karoti, tarhi mātā kupitā bhavati. If the child is stubborn, my mother gets angry.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
हठः (haṭhaḥ) is the word MW gives for violence, force, and obstinacy, pertinacity; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
स्वयम् (svayam) — by oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: स्वयम्.
स्वयम् — svayam — "by oneself".

The word, taken apart.
स्वयम् (svayam) probably comes from an old nominative of sva, "own", as MW takes it; MW gives svayam as self, by oneself, of one's own accord (MW).
