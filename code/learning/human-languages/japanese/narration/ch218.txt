Japanese, chapter 218: A Stay in Hospital.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
にゅういん (nyūin) — going into hospital; a hospital stay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a dentist, then the Japanese for a cavity.

You'll want to know: にゅういん.
にゅういん — nyūin — "going into hospital; a hospital stay".
にゅういんは ながいですか。 — nyūin wa nagai desu ka — "Is the hospital stay long?"

The word, taken apart.
にゅういん (nyūin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nyū, "to enter" (Middle Chinese nyip) and in, "courtyard, institution" (hjwenH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

きず (kizu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
