Gujarati, chapter 480: Trouble with the Skin.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ખસ (khas) — an itchy rash; scabies.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a lump, then the Gujarati for bedridden.

You'll want to know: ખસ.
ખસ — khas — "an itchy rash; scabies".
ગંદા પાણીથી બાળકને ખસ થઈ. gandā pāṇīthī bāḷakne khas thaī. The child got an itchy rash from dirty water.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ખસ (khas) goes back to Sanskrit khasa, "itch, scab", which CDIAL thinks probably came from Dravidian; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 3854).

સડવું — saḍvũ — "to rot; to decay".
મીઠાઈથી દાંત સડે છે. mīṭhāīthī dā̃t saḍe che. Sweets make your teeth decay.
Past (it …): તે દાંત સડી ગયો, te dā̃t saḍī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
સડવું (saḍvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit śaṭati, "disintegrates"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to rot" (CDIAL 12268).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઊપસવું (ūpasvũ) — to puff up; to rise (of the skin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ઊપસવું.
ઊપસવું — ūpasvũ — "to puff up; to rise (of the skin)".
મચ્છર કરડ્યા પછી ચામડી ઊપસી આવી. macchar karaḍyā pachī cāmḍī ūpsī āvī. After the mosquito bite the skin puffed up.
Past (it …): તે ફોલ્લો ઊપસી આવ્યો, te phollo ūpsī āvyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઊપસવું (ūpasvũ) goes back to a reconstructed utpruṣati, "bubbles up"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to rise, of a blister or bubble" (CDIAL 1832).
