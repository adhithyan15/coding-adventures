## You'll want to know: 白菜
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C101-KITCHEN101-02]; assesses=[] -->

**白菜** — *báicài* — "Chinese cabbage".

> **我去洗白菜** *wǒ qù xǐ báicài* — I'll go and wash the cabbage.

The new character, in pieces: 菜 — the grass piece on top, three strokes; under it a hand reaching down, four strokes; and at the foot the tree piece of 机, standing alone. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**白菜** (*báicài*) is built from 白 'white' and 菜 'vegetable', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *baek* and *tshojH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary says every plant that can be eaten is called *tshojH*; the white one is probably named for its pale stalks.
