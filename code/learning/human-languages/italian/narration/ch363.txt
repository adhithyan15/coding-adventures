Italian, chapter 363: Cittadini.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il cittadino — the citizen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for efficient, then the Italian for the productivity.

You'll want to know: il cittadino.
il cittadino — "the citizen".
Ogni cittadino può votare a diciotto anni. Every citizen can vote at eighteen.
As an adjective it means of the city: il traffico cittadino.

The word, taken apart.
cittadino is built on città, older cittade, inherited from Latin cīvitās, cīvitātis "citizenship, the body of citizens, a city" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
il diritto — the right; law (as a subject).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the citizen, then the Italian for the citizenship.

You'll want to know: il diritto.
il diritto — "the right; law (as a subject)".
Tutti hanno diritto a un avvocato. Everyone has the right to a lawyer.
Avere diritto a — to be entitled to. Studiare diritto — to study law. As an adjective it means straight.

The word, taken apart.
diritto is inherited from Vulgar Latin dērēctus, a form of Latin dīrēctus "straight" (EtymDB), from dīrigere "to set straight", built on regere "to rule": what is straight is what is right.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la libertà — the freedom, the liberty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the citizenship, then the Italian for the right.

You'll want to know: la libertà.
la libertà — "the freedom, the liberty".
La libertà di parola è importante. Freedom of speech is important.
In libertà — free, at large. Stress on the last syllable.

The word, taken apart.
libertà is a learned borrowing from Latin lībertās, lībertātis "freedom" (Lewis & Short), from līber "free", a different word from liber "book".

The word, taken apart.
uguaglianza is built on uguale "equal", inherited from Latin aequālis "equal", from aequus "level, even" (Lewis & Short).
