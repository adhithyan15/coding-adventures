## You'll want to know: la responsabilidad
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-01] -->

**la responsabilidad** — responsibility.

*Es una responsabilidad grande* — it is a big responsibility. *Bajo mi
responsabilidad* — on my own responsibility. The adjective is **responsable**,
which is both *responsible* and, in an advert, *the person in charge*: *el
responsable de compras*.

Six syllables, stress on the last: *res-pon-sa-bi-li-DAD*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02, ES-LEX-C306-KIN-05] -->

**re-** back, plus **spondēre**, to promise, plus the ending **-bilidad** that
turns an adjective into a quality.

To be *responsable* is to be **able to promise back** — to answer for what you
did. That is the same *spondēre* you already have in a place you would never
look for it:

| word | the promise in it |
|---|---|
| **el esposo** | the one who was **promised** |
| responder | to promise **back**, to answer |
| la responsabilidad | being answerable |

A spouse and a responsibility are the same verb. Latin *spondēre* was the formal
word for pledging — a betrothal was a *sponsalia* — and English keeps it in
**sponsor**, **respond** and **despondent**, which is what you are when the
promise has gone out of you.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02] -->

The long ending is worth a moment, because it is a machine.

**-idad** (and **-bilidad** after *-ble*) turns an adjective into the abstract
noun of its quality, and it is completely regular:

| adjective | quality |
|---|---|
| responsable | responsabilidad |
| posible | posibilidad |
| fácil | facilidad |

English does the same with *-ity*, and the two endings are the same Latin
*-itātem*, which is why they line up so neatly. If you know a Spanish adjective
in **-ble**, you can usually build its noun without being taught it.

Note the stress: every one of these nouns ends stressed, on the **-dad**.
