Hindi, chapter 433: Queues and Counters.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
क़तार (qatār) — a queue; a row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a label, then the Hindi for to wrap.

You'll want to know: क़तार.
क़तार — qatār — "a queue; a row".
टिकट घर के सामने लंबी क़तार थी। ṭikaṭ ghar ke sāmne lambī qatār thī. There was a long queue in front of the ticket office.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
क़तार (qatār) is usually traced to Arabic qiṭār, "a line of camels tied one behind another", through Persian: people waiting in line, one behind another.

Lesson 4 of 5.
हाट (hāṭ) — a weekly market; a village market.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a token, then the Hindi for a counter.

You'll want to know: हाट.
हाट — hāṭ — "a weekly market; a village market".
हर मंगलवार को गाँव में हाट लगती है। har maṅgalvār ko gā̃v meṁ hāṭ lagtī hai. Every Tuesday there is a market in the village.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
हाट (hāṭ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit haṭṭa, "a market, a fair" (CDIAL 13944), which lists Hindi hāṭ, "market, shop"; हड़ताल, a strike, closing the market, is in the same entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
थमाना (thamānā) — to hand (to); to put into someone's hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a counter, then the Hindi for a weekly market.

You'll want to know: थमाना.
थमाना — thamānā — "to hand (to); to put into someone's hand".
दुकानदार ने मुझे थैला थमाया। dukāndār ne mujhe thailā thamāyā. The shopkeeper handed me the bag.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे चाबी थमाई, usne mujhe cābī thamāī.

The word, taken apart.
थमाना (thamānā) is the causative of थामना, "to hold", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit stambhate, "supports, arrests" (CDIAL 13683): to make someone hold a thing.
