Urdu, chapter 289: Putting the Fire Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سائرن (sāiran) — a siren.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for brightness, then the Urdu for scorching heat.

You'll want to know: سائرن.
سائرن — sāiran — "a siren".
سائرن سن کر سب باہر آئے۔ sāiran sun kar sab bāhar āe. Hearing the siren, everyone came outside.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
سائرن (sāiran) is borrowed from English siren. Fire engines, ambulances and the police all have one.

Lesson 2 of 5.
عملہ (amlā) — the staff; a crew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: عملہ.
عملہ — amlā — "the staff; a crew".
عملے نے آگ بجھائی۔ amle ne āg bujhāī. The crew put the fire out.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
عملہ (amlā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ʿamala, "workers": the people who do the work of an office, a hospital or a fire station.

بجھنا — bujhnā — "to go out (of a fire); to go dark".

Past (it …): وہ چراغ بجھ گیا, voh chirāgh bujh gayā.
