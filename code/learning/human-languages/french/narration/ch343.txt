French, chapter 343: Le pour et le contre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'avantage — the advantage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for subjective, then the French for impartial.

You'll want to know: l'avantage.
l'avantage — "the advantage".
Le grand avantage de ce quartier, c'est le calme. The big advantage of this neighbourhood is the quiet.
Masculine: un avantage.

The word, taken apart.
avantage is built from avant "before, ahead" and the suffix -age: being ahead. avant is usually traced to Late Latin abante "in front", from ab and ante "before" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'inconvénient — the drawback, the disadvantage.

You'll want to know: l'inconvénient.
l'inconvénient — "the drawback, the disadvantage".
Le seul inconvénient, c'est le prix. The only drawback is the price.
Je n'y vois pas d'inconvénient — I have no objection. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
inconvénient comes from Latin inconveniēns "unsuitable" (Lewis & Short), built from in- "not" and convenīre "to come together, to suit", on venīre "to come".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'aspect — the aspect, the side (of a question).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the advantage, then the French for the drawback.

You'll want to know: l'aspect.
l'aspect — "the aspect, the side (of a question)".
Il faut regarder tous les aspects du problème. We have to consider every aspect of the problem.
The final ct is usually silent. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
aspect is a learned borrowing from Latin aspectus "a seeing, a look, an appearance" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from aspicere "to behold", built on specere "to look".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la critique — the criticism; the review.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the drawback, then the French for the aspect.

You'll want to know: la critique.
la critique — "the criticism; the review".
Elle n'aime pas les critiques. She does not like criticism.
Une critique is a criticism or a review; un critique is a critic. The verb is critiquer.

The word, taken apart.
critique comes from Latin criticus, which came from Greek kritikós "able to judge" (EtymDB), built on krínein "to separate, to judge".

Lesson 5 of 5.
souligner — to stress, to point out; to underline.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the aspect, then the French for the criticism.

You'll want to know: souligner.
souligner — "to stress, to point out; to underline".
Il a souligné deux erreurs dans mon texte. He pointed out two mistakes in my text.
Literally to draw a line under: souligner un mot.

The word, taken apart.
souligner is built from sous "under" and ligne "line", which comes from Latin līnea "a linen thread, a line" (Lewis & Short).
