Mandarin Chinese, chapter 307: Marrying and Parting.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
fēnshǒu — to break up; to end a relationship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to hold a ceremony, then the Mandarin for wedding sweets for the guests.

You'll want to know: fēnshǒu.
fēnshǒu — "to break up; to end a relationship".
tāmen fēnshǒu le, wǒ hěn nánguò — They've split up, and I'm sad.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fēnshǒu is built from fēn 'to part' and 手 'a hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjun and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to give out' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'to separate': to part hands.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
