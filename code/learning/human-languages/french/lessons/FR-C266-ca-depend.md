# ça dépend — the condition you do not have to finish

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-SI-PRESENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if it rains, I stay at home". (*S'il pleut, je reste à la
maison.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That sentence names its condition. Sometimes you want the
condition without saying what it is.

## You'll want to know: ça dépend
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-CA-DEPEND-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-C179-VERBES179-01, FR-LEX-C107-VILLE59-02] -->

**ça dépend** — "it depends".

> — *Tu vas au cinéma demain ?* — ***Ça dépend.*** — Going to the cinema tomorrow? It depends.

You have **dépendre**, "to depend". *Ça dépend* is its "it" form, and on its
own it is a complete answer: it says a condition exists and declines to say
which.

*Ça* is the everyday "it, that", and ***tout dépend*** is "it all
depends".

## Grammar Lens: ça dépend de
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-DEPEND-DE-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-DE-PREP-01, FR-LEX-TEMPS-02, FR-LEX-TOI-02, FR-LEX-HEURE-01, FR-GRAM-SI-01] -->

Add **de** and you can name the condition after all:

> ***Ça dépend du** temps.* — It depends on the weather.
> ***Ça dépend de** toi.* — It depends on you.
> ***Ça dépend de l'**heure.* — It depends on the time.

*De* and *le* weld into **du**, as in *content du voyage*. English says
*depend **on***; French says *dépend **de***, "hangs **from**", which is the
older picture.

Or name it with a whole *si*: *Ça dépend. S'il pleut, je reste à la
maison.* The *ça dépend* announces a condition; the *si* sentence states it.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-ETYMON-PENSER-05, FR-GRAM-PENDANT-QUE-02] -->

**dépendre** comes from Latin *dēpendēre*, "to hang from, to hang down"
(Lewis & Short, under *dependeo*): *dē*, "down from", and *pendēre*, "to
hang". To depend on something is to **hang from** it, and the answer swings
until the condition settles. The same *pendēre* is inside *pendant*, which
you met with *pendant que*. Its close relative *pendere*, "to make hang, to
weigh on the scales" (Lewis & Short, under *pendo*), is the one behind
*penser*. English **pendant** and **pending** belong to the family too.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-CA-DEPEND-01, FR-GRAM-DEPEND-DE-02, FR-LEX-C179-VERBES179-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What does *ça dépend* say, and what does it not say? (That there
**is** a condition — not **which**.) What do you add to name it? (***De***:
*ça dépend de toi*.) What is hanging, in *dēpendēre*? (**The answer**, from
the condition.)
