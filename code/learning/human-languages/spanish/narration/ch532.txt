You'll want to know: el misterio.
el misterio — "the mystery".
Lo que pasó aquella noche sigue siendo un misterio. What happened that night is still a mystery.

The word, taken apart.
misterio comes from Latin mystērium, a learned word taken from Greek mystḗrion 'a secret rite' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ocultar — to conceal, to hide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the mystery, then the Spanish for the enigma.

You'll want to know: ocultar.
ocultar — "to conceal, to hide".
Ocultó la verdad durante años. She hid the truth for years.
A little formal; often for information or feelings: ocultar la verdad.

The word, taken apart.
ocultar is a learned borrowing from Latin occultāre 'to hide' (EtymDB), built on occultus 'hidden', from occulere 'to cover over' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
esconder — to hide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the enigma, then the Spanish for to conceal.

You'll want to know: esconder.
esconder — "to hide".
Escondió el dinero debajo de la cama. He hid the money under the bed.
Esconderse is to hide yourself.

The word, taken apart.
esconder is inherited from Latin abscondere 'to put away, to hide' (Lewis & Short), built on abs 'away' and condere 'to put, to store'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el secreto — the secret; secret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to conceal, then the Spanish for to hide.

You'll want to know: el secreto.
el secreto — "the secret; secret".
Es un secreto, no se lo digas a nadie. It is a secret, don't tell anyone.
As an adjective: una puerta secreta.

The word, taken apart.
secreto is a learned borrowing from Latin sēcrētus 'set apart' (EtymDB), the participle of sēcernere 'to separate', built on cernere 'to sift'.
