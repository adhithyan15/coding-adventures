Hindi, chapter 115: Come In, Here You Are.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
आइए (āie) — please come (in).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for good wishes, then the Hindi for an invitation.

You'll want to know: आइए.
आइए (āie) — "please come", "come in".
It is आना, "to come", with the respectful -इए. आइए (āie), आइए (āie)! — doubled, it is a warm welcome at the door.

What you've built.
A welcome at the door.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: āie]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: āie, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say āie, āie!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for good wishes, then the Hindi for an invitation, then say āie again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does आइए (āie) mean? ("Please come (in)".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 7.
पधारिए (padhārie) — please come (formal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an invitation, then the Hindi for please come in.

You'll want to know: पधारिए.
पधारिए (padhārie) — "please come", in its most formal, honouring form.
The everyday āie comes first in this chapter; पधारिए (padhārie) is what you say to an honoured guest.

What you've built.
A second welcome, for an honoured guest.

Lesson 3 of 7.
लीजिए (lījie) — please take; here you are.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for please come in, then the Hindi for please come, formally.

You'll want to know: लीजिए.
लीजिए (lījie) — "please take", "here you are", as you hand something over.
It is लेना, "to take", with the respectful ending of बोलिए. चाय लीजिए (lījie) — "have some tea".

What you've built.
An offer as you hand it over.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for please come in, then the Hindi for please come, formally, then say lījie again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does लीजिए (lījie) mean? ("Please take; here you are".) Say it once more.


Lesson 4 of 7.
शौक़ से (śauq se) — by all means, gladly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for please come, formally, then the Hindi for here you are.

You'll want to know: शौक़ से.
शौक़ से (śauq se) — "by all means", "gladly", literally "with pleasure".
The answer when someone asks if they may: क्या मैं बैठ सकता हूँ? — शौक़ से (śauq se)!

What you've built.
A generous yes.

Lesson 5 of 7.
जी नहीं (jī nahīṁ) — no, politely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for here you are, then the Hindi for by all means.

You'll want to know: जी नहीं.
जी नहीं (jī nahīṁ) — "no", said politely.
It pairs with the जी हाँ you know: जी makes either answer respectful.

What you've built.
Come in, please come, here you are, by all means, and a polite no.

Lesson 6 of 7.
A visit, start to finish — a second pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
A guest is at your door. Welcome them in, and hand them a cup of tea.

Grammar Lens: the respectful ending.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
word: āie. from: come.
word: लीजिए (lījie). from: take.
word: पधारिए (padhārie). from: arrive, formally.
One ending turns a verb into a polite request, as it did for बोलिए.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: you bumped into someone: beg their pardon; they answer "no problem"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: congratulate a friend; then send them good wishes]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: thank someone for an invitation]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a guest asks to sit: say "by all means"; offered more food, say a polite no]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
An apology? (माफ़ी.) No problem? (कोई बात नहीं.) Congratulations? (बधाई.) Good wishes? (शुभकामनाएँ.) An invitation? (निमंत्रण.) Come in? (āie.) Please come, formally? (पधारिए (padhārie).) Here you are? (लीजिए (lījie).) By all means? (शौक़ से (śauq se).) A polite no? (जी नहीं (jī nahīṁ).)


Lesson 7 of 7.
Reacting and answering — a second pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Someone gives an answer. Tell them it is right; then tell them it is wrong.

Grammar Lens: two words you already had, joined.
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
answer: कुछ नहीं. built from: something + not.
answer: अभी नहीं. built from: right now + not.
answer: सच में? built from: truth + in.
answer: वही. built from: that + exactly.
Most short answers are two small words you already own, put side by side.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: react to surprising news: oh, really?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask if someone is sure; answer "definitely"]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: asked what happened, say nothing; asked if you have finished, say not yet]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say that's enough; say "that's the one"; say maybe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: answer a request with "by all means", then with a polite no]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Right? (सही.) Wrong? (ग़लत.) Sure? (पक्का.) Really? (सच में?) Oh? (अरे.) Nothing? (कुछ नहीं.) Not yet? (अभी नहीं.) Enough? (काफ़ी.) That very one? (वही.) Maybe? (हो सकता है.)
