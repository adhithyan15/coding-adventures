Japanese, chapter 234: Winning and Losing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しあい (shiai) — a match; a game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to run, then the Japanese for exercise.

You'll want to know: しあい.
しあい — shiai — "a match; a game".
せんしゅう がっこうで しあいが ありました。 — senshū gakkō de shiai ga arimashita — "There was a match at school last week."

The word, taken apart.
しあい (shiai) is built on native ai, "meeting, matching", from au, inherited rather than borrowed. JMdict gives two spellings whose first characters, read shi, mean "try" and "serve"; which of them, if either, is the source of shi is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
かつ (katsu) — to win.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for exercise, then the Japanese for a match.

You'll want to know: かつ.
かつ — katsu — "to win".
きのうの しあいで あにが かちました。 — kinō no shiai de ani ga kachimashita — "My elder brother won yesterday's match."
Polite かちます, kachimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
かつ (katsu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
せんしゅ (senshu) — a player; an athlete.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to win, then the Japanese for to be beaten.

You'll want to know: せんしゅ.
せんしゅ — senshu — "a player; an athlete".
きょねん あには がっこうの せんしゅでした。 — kyonen ani wa gakkō no senshu deshita — "Last year my elder brother was one of the school's players."

The word, taken apart.
せんしゅ (senshu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sen, "choose" (Middle Chinese sjwenX) and shu, "hand" (syuwX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
