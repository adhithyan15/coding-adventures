Portuguese, chapter 320: Ritos e costumes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o costume — the custom, the habit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the sardine feast, then the Portuguese for the street dance.

You'll want to know: o costume.
o costume — "the custom, the habit".
É costume jantar tarde nesta região. It is the custom to eat late in this region.
Como de costume — as usual. Not a party outfit.

The word, taken apart.
costume comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB) and goes back to Latin cōnsuētūdō "habit, custom", from cōnsuēscere "to get used to" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o ritual — the ritual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the street dance, then the Portuguese for the custom.

You'll want to know: o ritual.
o ritual — "the ritual".
Tomar café depois do almoço é um ritual para ele. Having coffee after lunch is a ritual for him.
Also an adjective: uma dança ritual.

The word, taken apart.
ritual is a learned borrowing from Latin rītuālis "of rites", from rītus "a rite, a custom" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o peregrino — the pilgrim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the ritual, then the Portuguese for the ceremony.

You'll want to know: o peregrino.
o peregrino — "the pilgrim".
O peregrino caminhou duzentos quilómetros. The pilgrim walked two hundred kilometres.
A woman is a peregrina.

The word, taken apart.
peregrino comes from Latin peregrīnus "foreign, a foreigner" (EtymDB), built on per "through" and ager "a field": one who comes from beyond the fields.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the ceremony, then the Portuguese for the pilgrim.

You'll want to know: a peregrinação.
a peregrinação — "the pilgrimage".
Todos os anos, a minha tia faz uma peregrinação a pé. Every year my aunt goes on a pilgrimage on foot.
Built on peregrino, ‘pilgrim’.

The word, taken apart.
peregrinação is a learned borrowing from Latin peregrīnātiō "a travelling abroad", from peregrīnus "foreign", built on per and ager "a field" (Lewis & Short).
