Latin, chapter 369: Urging and Dissuading.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
hortor, hortārī — to urge, to encourage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to discuss, then the Latin for to speak out.

You'll want to know: hortor, hortārī.
hortor, hortārī — "to urge, to encourage".
Mīlitēs hortātus est ut fortiter pugnārent. He urged the soldiers to fight bravely.
Deponent: hortor, hortārī, hortātus sum + accusative and ut (or nē) with the subjunctive. English exhort.

The word, taken apart.
hortor comes from an old verb horior "to urge on" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), traced to a root meaning "to desire" that Greek khairō "to rejoice" shares. English exhort comes from the compound exhortor.

Lesson 2 of 5.
dissuādeō, dissuādēre — to advise against, to dissuade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to speak out, then the Latin for to urge.

You'll want to know: dissuādeō, dissuādēre.
dissuādeō, dissuādēre — "to advise against, to dissuade".
Amīcī mihi hoc iter dissuāsērunt. My friends advised me against this journey.
dissuādeō, dissuādēre, dissuāsī, dissuāsum, with the dative of the person: the opposite of suādeō. English dissuade.

The word, taken apart.
dissuādeō is built from dis- "apart, away" and suādeō "to advise" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English dissuade comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
persuāsiō, persuāsiōnis — persuasion; a conviction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to urge, then the Latin for to advise against.

You'll want to know: persuāsiō, persuāsiōnis.
persuāsiō, persuāsiōnis — "persuasion; a conviction".
Persuāsiōne, nōn vī, vincere mālō. I prefer to win by persuasion, not by force.
persuāsiō, persuāsiōnis f., the noun of persuādeō. English persuasion.

The word, taken apart.
persuāsiō is the noun built on persuādeō "to persuade" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), itself per- "thoroughly" with suādeō "to advise". English persuasion comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
excūsō, excūsāre — to excuse; to plead as an excuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to advise against, then the Latin for persuasion.

You'll want to know: excūsō, excūsāre.
excūsō, excūsāre — "to excuse; to plead as an excuse".
Morbum excūsāvit. He pleaded illness as his excuse.
excūsō, excūsāre, excūsāvī, excūsātum. Sē excūsāre, to excuse oneself. Built on causa: out of the case. English excuse.

The word, taken apart.
excūsō is built from ex "out of" and causa "a case, a charge" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to take someone out of the charge. English excuse comes from it.
