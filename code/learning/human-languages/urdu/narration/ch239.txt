Lesson 1 of 5.
نگلنا (nigalnā) — to swallow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a goldsmith, then the Urdu for a potter.

You'll want to know: نگلنا.
نگلنا — nigalnā — "to swallow".

The word, taken apart.
نگلنا (nigalnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nigalati, "swallows" (CDIAL 7163).

Lesson 4 of 5.
گھورنا (ghūrnā) — to stare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to chew, then the Urdu for to smell.

You'll want to know: گھورنا.
گھورنا — ghūrnā — "to stare".

The word, taken apart.
گھورنا (ghūrnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ghūra, "frown", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants; CDIAL glosses the Hindi verb "to frown at, stare at, ogle" (CDIAL 4496). A different verb ghūrnā, "to roll round", sits under ghurati, "revolves" (CDIAL 4488).

تلاش کرنا — talāsh karnā — "to search".

The phrase, taken apart.
تلاش کرنا (talāsh karnā) joins talāsh, "search", usually traced, through Persian, to a Turkic word, and karnā, "to do", from Sanskrit karoti, "does" (CDIAL 2814).
