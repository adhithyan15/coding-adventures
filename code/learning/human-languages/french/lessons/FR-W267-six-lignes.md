# Six lines about a journey

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-LEX-PARCEQUE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter forty-eight asked for four lines that held together, with
*parce que*. This one asks for six, and they tell a story.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it: *un ami*, *une amie*, someone
in your family.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-RECIT-SQUELETTE-02, FR-GRAM-DEUX-FLECHES-01, FR-GRAM-SI-PRESENT-01, FR-LEX-CE-SERAIT-01, FR-LEX-MAIS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] A told story is not a list of events. It has a **scene**, in the
imparfait: *c'était*, *il y avait*, *il faisait froid*, *j'étais content*.
It has **events**, in the passé composé, put in order with *d'abord*,
*ensuite* and *enfin*. It has at least one **reason**, pointing back with
*parce que* or forward with *donc*, and perhaps a turn with *mais*.

[PAUSE 2s] And a good one ends by looking forward: *j'espère …*, or *ce
serait …*, or a condition with *si*.

[PAUSE 2s] The DELF B1 writing paper asks for a connected text of at least
160 words. Six lines is the first step towards it, not the whole of it.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-IMPARFAIT-01, FR-LEX-CETAIT-01, FR-LEX-IL-Y-AVAIT-02, FR-LEX-IL-FAISAIT-01, FR-GRAM-PASSE-COMPOSE-02, FR-LEX-DABORD-01, FR-GRAM-RECIT-ORDRE-02, FR-GRAM-SENTIMENT-ETAIS-01, FR-GRAM-ESPERER-INFINITIF-01, FR-GRAM-CONDITIONNEL-01, FR-GRAM-SI-IMPARFAIT-02, FR-GRAM-PENDANT-DUREE-02, FR-GRAM-EXPLICATION-02, FR-LEX-RACONTER-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] On paper, write **six lines** to your reader about a journey,
real or invented, one line for each of these:

1. the scene: when it was, and the weather;
2. where you were, who was with you, and how long you stayed, with *pendant*;
3. the first thing that happened, with *d'abord*;
4. the next thing, with *ensuite*, and a reason, with *parce que* or *donc*;
5. how it ended and how you felt, with *enfin* and *j'étais*;
6. one line forward, with *j'espère*, *ce serait* or *si*.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. The exercise is choosing the
right past for each line.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-DECOR-EVENEMENT-01, FR-GRAM-RECIT-ORDRE-02, FR-SKILL-READ-NARRATIVE-01, FR-LEX-LIRE-06] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Now go through your lines as you went through the journey in
chapter two hundred and fifty-nine. Mark every verb **S** for scene or
**E** for event. Line one should be all **S**; two mostly **S**, though its
*pendant* verb is an **E**; three and four mostly **E**; five has one of each. A line about the weather marked **E**
is the commonest slip: change *il a plu* back to *il pleuvait*.

[PAUSE 2s] Then two spelling checks. The participle of an *-er* verb ends in
**-é**: *j'ai parlé*, *je suis arrivé*, and with *être* it agrees with a
woman writing about herself: *je suis arrivée*. And the imparfait's endings are silent but spelled: *-ais*,
*-ait*, *-aient*.

[PAUSE 2s] Last, read line six on its own. If it has a *si* about something
real, is the verb after it in the plain present? If it has *ce serait*, is
it your judgement on something that has not happened yet? Then it looks
forward, and the story is finished.
