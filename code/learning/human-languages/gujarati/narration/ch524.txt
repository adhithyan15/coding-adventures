Lesson 1 of 6.
સંગ્રહ (sangrah) — a collection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be to one's taste, then the Gujarati for to dunk.

You'll want to know: સંગ્રહ.
સંગ્રહ — sangrah — "a collection".
અરુણ પાસે જૂના સિક્કાઓનો મોટો સંગ્રહ છે. Aruṇ pāse jūnā sikkāono moṭo sangrah che. Arun has a big collection of old coins.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંગ્રહ (sangrah) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit saṃgraha, "holding together, gathering" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 6.
કોડી (koḍī) — a cowrie shell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a collection, then the Gujarati for a spinning top.

You'll want to know: કોડી.
કોડી — koḍī — "a cowrie shell".
દાદી કોડીઓથી એક જૂની રમત રમતી હતી. dādī koḍīothī ek jūnī ramat ramtī hatī. Grandmother used to play an old game with cowrie shells.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કોડી (koḍī) goes back to Sanskrit kaparda, "a small shell", which CDIAL notes may itself come from Dravidian; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word for a cowrie (CDIAL 2740). Cowries were once counters in games, and small change.

You'll want to know: સંતાકૂકડી.
સંતાકૂકડી — santākūkḍī — "hide-and-seek".
બાળકો બગીચામાં સંતાકૂકડી રમતાં હતાં. bāḷko bagīcāmā̃ santākūkḍī ramtā̃ hatā̃. The children were playing hide-and-seek in the garden.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંતાકૂકડી (santākūkḍī) starts from santāvũ, "to hide", which CDIAL files under Sanskrit sáṁtāyatē (CDIAL 12887); the second half is not explained in the dictionaries we use, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a cowrie, then the Gujarati for hide-and-seek.

You'll want to know: કોયડો.
કોયડો — koyḍo — "a puzzle".
અરુણે ગણિતનો એક અઘરો કોયડો ઉકેલ્યો. Aruṇe gaṇitno ek aghro koyḍo ukelyo. Arun solved a hard maths puzzle.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કોયડો (koyḍo) is something to be solved, ઉકેલવું. CDIAL files the Gujarati word, "a puzzle, a riddle", under Sanskrit kuha, "a cheat" (CDIAL 3385); how a trick became a puzzle is not explained there.
