Russian, chapter 45: Potatoes, Tomato, Onion, Cucumber, Carrot.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
картошка (kartóshka) — potatoes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cherry, then the Russian for a berry.

You'll want to know: картошка.
картошка (kartóshka) — "potatoes".

The word, taken apart.
картошка (kartóshka) is картофель, "potato", with the fond -ошка. картофель came from German Kartoffel, which comes from Italian tartufolo, "truffle": the tubers looked like truffles (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
лук (luk) — an onion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for potatoes, then the Russian for a tomato.

You'll want to know: лук.
лук (luk) — "an onion".

The word, taken apart.
лук (luk), "onion", was borrowed from early Germanic lauka- (Vasmer): Old Norse laukr, Old English lēac, English leek. The лук (luk) that means "a bow" is a separate, native word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
огурец (oguréts) — a cucumber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tomato, then the Russian for an onion.

You'll want to know: огурец.
огурец (oguréts) — "a cucumber".

The word, taken apart.
огурец (oguréts) from Byzantine Greek ágouros, "cucumber", from older Greek áōros, "unripe": cucumbers are eaten unripe, unlike melons (Vasmer).

Lesson 5 of 5.
морковь (morkóv) — a carrot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an onion, then the Russian for a cucumber.

You'll want to know: морковь.
морковь (morkóv) — "a carrot".

The word, taken apart.
морковь (morkóv) from Proto-Slavic mŭrky — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Old High German moraha, "carrot", and holds that a loan from Germanic cannot be proved.
