# spiegare — to unfold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-SECONDO-ME-01, IT-GRAMMAR-DUE-FRECCE-01, IT-LEX-DOVREI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three pieces are in your hands: *secondo me* for an opinion,
*perché* and *quindi* for the two arrows, *dovrei* for advice. This lesson puts
them in a row, under the verb that names the act.

## Grammar Lens: the shape of an explanation
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-SPIEGAZIONE-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C141-VERBI141-05, IT-LEX-PERCHE-02, IT-LEX-C185-SAL185-05] -->

You met **spiegare**, "to explain", in chapter one hundred and forty-one. It
is a regular *-are* verb with one spelling catch: **spiego**, **spieghi**,
**spiega**. The *h* in *spieghi* keeps the *g* hard before the *i*, the same
trick the *h* plays in *che*.

Here is a whole explanation, every word of it taught:

> — Perché resti a casa?
> — **Secondo me** piove. E ho il raffreddore, **perché** ieri faceva freddo.
> **Quindi** resto a casa. **Dovrei** dormire.
>
> *— Why are you staying at home?*
> *— If you ask me, it's going to rain. And I've got a cold, because it was
> cold yesterday. So I'm staying at home. I ought to sleep.*

Four moves, and a word for each: an **opinion** (*secondo me*), a **cause**
pointing back (*perché*), a **consequence** pointing forward (*quindi*), and
**advice** (*dovrei*). With both arrows, you can build it cause first or claim
first.

## The word, taken apart: folded, and unfolded
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-SPIEGARE-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C180-VERBI180-04] -->

**piegare**, "to fold", which you met in chapter one hundred and eighty, is
inherited from Latin *plicāre*, "to fold" (EtymDB 2.1). **spiegare** is the
same verb with Latin *ex-*, "out", worn down to an *s-*: Latin *explicāre*, "to
unfold, to spread out" (Lewis & Short, under *explico*). EtymDB 2.1 has no row
for *spiegare*, so that pairing is the usual reading rather than a cited one.

**To explain is to unfold**: something was folded shut, and you open it out.
English kept the Latin in **explicit**, and **complicated** is the
opposite, folded together. **explain** is another verb, Latin *explānāre*,
"to make plain" (EtymDB 2.1).
