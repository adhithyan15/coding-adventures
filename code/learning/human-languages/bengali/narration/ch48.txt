মহিলা (môhilā) is Sanskrit mahilā, "woman", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama.

Lesson 3 of 5.
শোনা (shonā) — to hear, to listen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a woman, then the Bengali for a neighbour.

You'll want to know: শোনা.
শোনা — shonā — "to hear, to listen".

The word, taken apart.
শোনা (shonā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from śṛṇoti, "hears" (CDIAL 12598). CDIAL writes the Bengali verb sunā.

You'll want to know: বসা.
বসা — bôsā — "to sit".

The word, taken apart.
বসা (bôsā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vasati, "stays, dwells" (CDIAL 11435). CDIAL also gives basā under upaviśati, "sits down", and asks whether it belongs there instead (CDIAL 2245).

The word, taken apart.
হাঁটা (hā̃ṭā) (hāṁṭā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from haṇṭ, "move", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 13943). (In CDIAL the entry is headed haṭṭ, "move", and the Bengali verb sits under its variant haṇṭ-.)
