Japanese, chapter 511: What Caused It.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ひきおこす (hikiokosu) — to cause; to bring about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to make good use of, then the Japanese for to be blessed (with).

You'll want to know: ひきおこす.
ひきおこす — hikiokosu — "to cause; to bring about".
もし かれが あぶない うんてんを したら、じこを ひきおこすでしょう。 — moshi kare ga abunai unten o shitara, jiko o hikiokosu deshō — "If he drives dangerously, he will probably cause an accident."
Polite ひきおこします, hikiokoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ゆきが じこを ひきおこしました。, yuki ga jiko o hikiokoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ひきおこす (hikiokosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from hiku, "to pull", and okosu, "to raise"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

What you've built.
One more word for saying what happens if.

Lesson 2 of 5.
せいか (seika) — an achievement; the fruits (of effort).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be blessed (with), then the Japanese for to cause.

You'll want to know: せいか.
せいか — seika — "an achievement; the fruits (of effort)".
もし れんしゅうの せいかが あったら、せんしゅは よろこぶでしょう。 — moshi renshū no seika ga attara, senshu wa yorokobu deshō — "If the practice brings results, the players will be glad."

The word, taken apart.
せいか (seika) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sei, "turn into" (Middle Chinese dzyeng) and ka, "fruit" (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
しょうじる (shōjiru) — to arise; to come about.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to cause, then the Japanese for an achievement.

You'll want to know: しょうじる.
しょうじる — shōjiru — "to arise; to come about".
もし もんだいが しょうじたら、でんわして ください。 — moshi mondai ga shōjitara, denwa shite kudasai — "If a problem arises, please call."
Polite しょうじます, shōjimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): もんだいが しょうじました。, mondai ga shōjimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しょうじる (shōjiru) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese shō, "life" (Middle Chinese srjaeng), borrowed from Chinese and the native ending jiru (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ききめ (kikime) — how well something works.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an achievement, then the Japanese for to arise.

You'll want to know: ききめ.
ききめ — kikime — "how well something works".
この くすりの ききめは びょうきに よります。 — kono kusuri no kikime wa byōki ni yorimasu — "How well this medicine works depends on the illness."

The word, taken apart.
ききめ (kikime) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from kiki, "working" and me, "eye" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
