Mandarin Chinese, chapter 315: Once Again.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiànjiàn — gradually; little by little.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a cemetery, then the Mandarin for to shed tears.

You'll want to know: jiànjiàn.
jiànjiàn — "gradually; little by little".
wǒ jiànjiàn xíguàn le zhèr de shēnghuó — Little by little I got used to life here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiànjiàn is the word jiàn said twice, inherited from Middle Chinese dzjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'step by step' and 'to advance'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
又 (yòu) — yet again; it happened again.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shed tears, then the Mandarin for little by little.

You'll want to know: 又.
又 — yòu — "yet again; it happened again".
你又来了 nǐ yòu lái le — Here you are again.
The new character, in pieces: 又 (yòu) — two strokes: a bar that turns into a long sweep to the lower left, then a long stroke falling to the right across it, the fork at the foot of 报 and 发. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
又 (yòu) is inherited from Middle Chinese hjuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'further' and 'once more'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
又 (yòu) — the fork at the foot of 报.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the fork at the foot of 报 and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the fork at the foot of 报.
又 (yòu).
One piece, two strokes.
A bar that turns into a sweep: across to the right, then a long sweep down to the lower left.
A long stroke falling to the right, from under the start of the bar, across the sweep to the foot.
You have made this fork in 报, 发 and 经.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 又 (yòu), all two, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 又 (yòu)? (Two.) Where have you made this shape? (At the foot of 报, and in 发 and 经.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
自在 (zìzai) — at ease; free and relaxed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for little by little, then the Mandarin for it happened again.

You'll want to know: 自在.
自在 — zìzai — "at ease; free and relaxed".
我来了一年才觉得自在 wǒ lái le yì nián cái juéde zìzai — It took me a year to feel at home.

The word, taken apart.
自在 (zìzai) is built from 自 'by oneself' and 在 'to be in place', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzijH and dzojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'from' and 'by way of'; the sense 'oneself' is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 6.
jìngrán — to my surprise; unexpectedly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for it happened again, then the Mandarin for at ease in a place.

You'll want to know: jìngrán.
jìngrán — "to my surprise; unexpectedly".
tā jìngrán wàng le wǒ de shēngrì — He actually forgot my birthday!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jìngrán is built from jìng 'to the end' and 然 'so', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kjaengH and nyen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to use up' and 'the end'; how the pair came to mean 'to one's surprise' is not settled.
