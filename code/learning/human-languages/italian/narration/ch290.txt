Italian, chapter 290: Delitti e vittime.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il crimine — the crime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the audience, then the Italian for the editorial.

You'll want to know: il crimine.
il crimine — "the crime".
È un crimine molto grave. It is a very serious crime.
La lotta al crimine — the fight against crime.

The word, taken apart.
crimine is inherited from Latin crīmen "a charge, a crime" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
il delitto — the serious crime; the murder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the editorial, then the Italian for the crime.

You'll want to know: il delitto.
il delitto — "the serious crime; the murder".
Il delitto è avvenuto in una villa. The crime took place in a villa.
In the papers un delitto is often a killing.

The word, taken apart.
delitto is a learned borrowing from Latin dēlictum "a fault, a misdeed" (EtymDB), from dēlinquere "to fall short, to do wrong".

Lesson 3 of 5.
il criminale — the criminal.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the crime, then the Italian for the serious crime.

You'll want to know: il criminale.
il criminale — "the criminal".
Il criminale è stato visto vicino alla stazione. The criminal was seen near the station.
Also an adjective: un'azione criminale.

The word, taken apart.
criminale is a learned borrowing from Late Latin crīminālis "of a crime", built on crīmen (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: la vittima.
la vittima — "the victim".
La vittima è un uomo di cinquant'anni. The victim is a fifty-year-old man.
Feminine in grammar, whoever the person is.

vittima is a learned borrowing from Latin victima "a beast for sacrifice" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
commettere — to commit (a crime, an error).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the criminal, then the Italian for the victim.

You'll want to know: commettere.
commettere — "to commit (a crime, an error)".
Ha commesso un errore grave. He made a serious mistake.
Like mettere: ha commesso.

The word, taken apart.
commettere is inherited from Latin committere "to bring together; to commit" (EtymDB), from con- and mittere "to send".
