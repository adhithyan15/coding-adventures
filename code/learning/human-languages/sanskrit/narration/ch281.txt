Sanskrit, chapter 281: At the Station, and When Things Go Wrong.
6 lessons. You can do the first 4 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
एकं यानपत्रं ददातु (ekaṃ yānapatraṃ dadātu) — a ticket, please.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "where is the station?" (sthānakaṃ kutra asti?) Then say "a ticket". (yānapatram.) You are at the counter now.

You'll want to know: tickets.
एकं यानपत्रं ददातु। ekaṃ yānapatraṃ dadātu. — "One ticket, please."
एकम् (ekam), "one", agrees with यानपत्रम्, a neuter noun. For two, the number is द्वे (dve) and the noun takes the dual, यानपत्रे (yānapatre):
कृपया द्वे यानपत्रे ददातु। kṛpayā dve yānapatre dadātu. — "Two tickets, please."
मूल्यं कियत्? mūlyaṃ kiyat? — "How much is it?"

You'll want to know: when does it leave?
The future asks when: गमिष्यति (gamiṣyati), "will go", is "will leave".
धूमयानं कदा गमिष्यति? dhūmayānaṃ kadā gamiṣyati? — "When will the train leave?"
सायं गमिष्यति। sāyaṃ gamiṣyati. — "It will leave in the evening."
लोकयानं कुत्र तिष्ठति? lokayānaṃ kutra tiṣṭhati? — "Where does the bus stop?"

Lesson 2 of 6.
अग्रिमः (agrimaḥ) — next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "when will the train leave?" (dhūmayānaṃ kadā gamiṣyati?) That one has gone. You want the next.

You'll want to know: अग्रिमः.
अग्रिमः — agrimaḥ — "next", the one coming up. It agrees with its noun: अग्रिमम् (agrimam) for a neuter noun such as धूमयानम्.
अग्रिमं धूमयानं कदा? agrimaṃ dhūmayānaṃ kadā? — "When is the next train?"
अग्रिमः सप्ताहः (agrimaḥ saptāhaḥ) is "next week".
अहम् अग्रिमे सप्ताहे गमिष्यामि। aham agrime saptāhe gamiṣyāmi. — "I will go next week."
अग्रिमे सप्ताहे (agrime saptāhe) is "in the next week": -e says "in", for a time as for a place.

The word, taken apart.
अग्रिमः (agrimaḥ) is built on अग्र (agra), "the foremost point, the front": Monier-Williams gives agrima as "being in front, preceding". The next train is the one in front. CDIAL 92 lists Punjabi aggiõ, "first".

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "When is the next bus?" — agrimaṃ lokayānaṃ kadā?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does अग्रिमः (agrimaḥ) mean? ("Next".) "The next train"? (अग्रिमं धूमयानम्.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
मम स्यूतः नष्टः (mama syūtaḥ naṣṭaḥ) — when things go wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the next train". (agrimaṃ dhūmayānam.) Then say "is lost". (naśyati.) Now something has gone wrong.

You'll want to know: lost.
मम स्यूतः नष्टः। mama syūtaḥ naṣṭaḥ. — "My bag is lost."
नष्टः (naṣṭaḥ), "lost", is the -ta form of नश्यति (naśyati), "is lost". It describes, so it agrees with the thing: स्यूतः is masculine, so नष्टः.
मम यानपत्रं नष्टम्। mama yānapatraṃ naṣṭam. — "My ticket is lost."
मम माला नष्टा। mama mālā naṣṭā. — "My garland is lost."

You'll want to know: missed, and ill.
धूमयानं गतम्। dhūmayānaṃ gatam. — "The train has gone."
गतम् (gatam) is गतः for a neuter noun: the train is gone.
अहं रुग्णः अस्मि। ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi. — "I am ill."
A woman says अहं रुग्णा अस्मि (ahaṃ rugṇā asmi). And to ask for help, the polite -तु on "does":
कृपया साहाय्यं करोतु। kṛpayā sāhāyyaṃ karotu. — "Please help."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "My bag is lost" — mama syūtaḥ naṣṭaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My ticket is lost" — mama yānapatraṃ naṣṭam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "My book is lost" — mama pustakaṃ naṣṭam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that your ticket is lost and the train has gone, then ask for help.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"My bag is lost"? (मम स्यूतः नष्टः (mama syūtaḥ naṣṭaḥ).) Why नष्टम् with यानपत्रम्? (It is neuter.) "The train has gone"? (धूमयानं गतम्.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
औषधम् (auṣadham) — a medicine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I am ill". (ahaṃ rugṇaḥ asmi.) Then "you should go to the doctor", and "the doctor will give advice". (bhavān vaidyaṃ gacchet; vaidyaḥ upadeśaṃ dāsyati.)

You'll want to know: औषधम्.
औषधम् — auṣadham — "a medicine". It is a neuter noun.
औषधं कुत्र अस्ति? auṣadhaṃ kutra asti? — "Where is the medicine?"
वैद्यः औषधं दास्यति। vaidyaḥ auṣadhaṃ dāsyati. — "The doctor will give medicine."
भवान् औषधं पिबेत्। bhavān auṣadhaṃ pibet. — "You should take the medicine."
पिबेत् (pibet) is पिबति (pibati), "drinks", with the -et of advice.

The word, taken apart.
औषधम् (auṣadham) is built on ओषधि (oṣadhi), "a herb": Monier-Williams gives auṣadha as "consisting of herbs", and then herbs used as medicine. It lives on as Oriya usuā, "medicine" (CDIAL 2571).

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "You should take the medicine" — bhavān auṣadhaṃ pibet]

Lesson 5 of 6.
औ (au) — the vowel of ौ, standing alone.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: औ and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: say औषधम् (auṣadham), "a medicine". Then name the sign ौ and the letter अ. (au, a.)
[pause 2 seconds]
The sign ौ has a standing form, and औषधम् (auṣadham) opens with it.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: औ.
औ — au.
It is an independent vowel: the full-size letter au wears when it opens a word. On a consonant the same vowel is the sign ौ, as in द्वौ (dvau).
What it is made of:
the joined upper and lower bowls of अ.
a middle shoulder running into the inner stem.
the inner stem.
the trailing stem.
two separate upper arcs.
the top shirorekhā.
You have met it here:
औषधम् auṣadham — a medicine.

Writing: औ.
1. curve right around the upper bowl and continue down and around the lower bowl without lifting.
2. lift and sweep the middle shoulder right.
3. without lifting, climb up the inner stem to the headline.
4. descend the inner stem.
5. lift and descend the trailing stem.
6. lift at the trailing stem's headline junction and sweep the lower upper arc upward and left.
7. lift at the same junction and sweep the taller upper arc upward and left.
8. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 5. The pen comes up 5 times and no more.
The source shows seven movements; native writers draw औ (au) as six pen-down strokes (60% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 49 of 82). This ductus follows the seven movements in order and direction, lifting five times: the middle shoulder runs on, climbing the inner stem and descending it, and every other movement starts with a lift.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari औ (au) stroke order.svg’, panels 1–7, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
