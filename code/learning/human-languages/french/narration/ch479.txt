French, chapter 479: Petits accidents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
blesser — to injure, to hurt; (se blesser) to hurt oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for nostril, then the French for temple.

You'll want to know: blesser.
blesser — "to injure, to hurt; (se blesser) to hurt oneself".
Il s'est blessé au pied en jouant au foot. He hurt his foot playing football.
Often reflexive: se blesser à la main. Also to hurt feelings: ses mots m'ont blessé.

The word, taken apart.
blesser comes from Old French blecier "to injure" (EtymDB), from Frankish blētjan "to bruise", a Germanic word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la coupure — cut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for temple, then the French for to injure.

You'll want to know: la coupure.
la coupure — "cut".
J'ai une petite coupure au doigt. I have a small cut on my finger.
Also a break in a supply: une coupure de courant — a power cut.

The word, taken apart.
la coupure is built on couper "to cut" with -ure (EtymDB); couper comes from coup "blow", from Latin colaphus "a punch".

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'égratignure — scratch, graze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to injure, then the French for cut.

You'll want to know: l'égratignure.
l'égratignure — "scratch, graze".
Le chat m'a fait une égratignure sur le bras. The cat gave me a scratch on my arm.
Feminine. Lighter than une coupure.

The word, taken apart.
l'égratignure is built on égratigner "to scratch", itself built on gratter "to scratch", which comes from a Germanic word, Frankish krattōn.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la brûlure — burn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for cut, then the French for scratch.

You'll want to know: la brûlure.
la brûlure — "burn".
Elle s'est fait une brûlure en sortant le gâteau du four. She burnt herself taking the cake out of the oven.
Built on brûler "to burn".

The word, taken apart.
la brûlure is built on brûler "to burn" with -ure. The origin of brûler is not settled: Latin ustulāre "to scorch" and a Germanic verb for burning have both been proposed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la bosse — bump, lump.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for scratch, then the French for burn.

You'll want to know: la bosse.
la bosse — "bump, lump".
Après sa chute, l'enfant a une bosse sur le front. After his fall, the child has a bump on his forehead.
Also a camel's hump: la bosse du chameau.

The word, taken apart.
la bosse goes back to Old French boce (EtymDB). Its earlier origin is not settled; a Vulgar Latin bottia "swelling" is sometimes proposed.
