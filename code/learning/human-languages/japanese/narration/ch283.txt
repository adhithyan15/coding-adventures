Japanese, chapter 283: The Announcement.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はっぴょうする (happyō suru) — to announce; to make public.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to fight, then the Japanese for the government.

You'll want to know: はっぴょうする.
はっぴょうする — happyō suru — "to announce; to make public".
せいふは あした はっぴょうする つもりです。 — seifu wa ashita happyō suru tsumori desu — "The government means to make an announcement tomorrow."
Polite はっぴょうします, happyōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せいふが はっぴょうしました。, seifu ga happyō shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
はっぴょうする (happyō suru) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hatsu, "publish" (Middle Chinese pjot) and hyō, "surface" (pjewX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). Together they are read happyō: hatsu shortens to hap- and hyō becomes pyō. The native verb suru, "to do", makes it a verb.

What you've built.
One more word for telling what happened.
