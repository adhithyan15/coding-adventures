Mandarin Chinese, chapter 508: Brothers and Sisters.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
jiěmèi — sisters.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a married couple, then the Mandarin for one's elders.

You'll want to know: jiěmèi.
jiěmèi — "sisters".
wǒmen jiěmèi sān ge wǒ zuì xiǎo — There are three of us sisters, and I'm the youngest.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiěmèi is built from jiě 'an elder sister' and mèi 'a younger sister', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjaeX and mwojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a word for mother among a people of the west and the second 'a younger sister'; the sense 'elder sister' is not given for the first there.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 3 of 6.
dúshēngzǐnǚ — only child.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sisters, then the Mandarin for elder brother and younger sister.

You'll want to know: dúshēngzǐnǚ.
dúshēngzǐnǚ — "only child".
wǒ shì dúshēngzǐnǚ xiǎoshíhou zuì xiǎng yào yí ge dìdi — I'm an only child; when I was little, what I wanted most was a little brother.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: zǐ is said with a rising tone before nǚ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
dúshēngzǐnǚ is built from dú 'alone', 生 'to be born' and 子女 'sons and daughters', all inherited from Middle Chinese, duwk, srjaeng, tsiX and nrjoX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). For duwk the dictionary quotes an older one that sets it against qún 'in flocks', the way sheep go.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 4 of 6.
qīfu — to bully; to pick on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for elder brother and younger sister, then the Mandarin for an only child.

You'll want to know: qīfu.
qīfu — "to bully; to pick on".
xiǎoshíhou gēge chángcháng qīfu wǒ — When I was little, my elder brother often picked on me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qīfu is built from qī 'to deceive' and fù 'to bear', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khi and bjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to deceive' and the second 'to carry' and 'to repay kindness with ingratitude'; in the word the second syllable is light.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dǒngshì — sensible; grown-up for one's age (of a child).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an only child, then the Mandarin for to pick on (someone weaker).

You'll want to know: dǒngshì.
dǒngshì — "sensible; grown-up for one's age (of a child)".
jiějie bǐ wǒ dǒngshì — My big sister is more grown-up than me.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǒngshì is built from dǒng 'to understand' and 事 'a matter'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to employ'; the Guangyun has no entry for the first, so its history is not settled.
