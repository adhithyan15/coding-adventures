Hindi, chapter 464: Bored or Curious.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ऊबना (ūbnā) — to get bored.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to eat one's fill, then the Hindi for consolation.

You'll want to know: ऊबना.
ऊबना — ūbnā — "to get bored".
रोज़ दाल खाकर अरुण ऊब गया। roz dāl khākar Aruṇ ūb gayā. Arun got bored of eating dal every day.
Past (he …): वह ऊब गया, vah ūb gayā.

The word, taken apart.
ऊबना (ūbnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi ūbnā, with the sense "to be agitated, be bewildered", under Sanskrit údvijatē, "is agitated" (CDIAL 2087). The modern sense, the restless weariness of boredom, is a later turn that CDIAL does not give.

Lesson 2 of 5.
दिलचस्पी (dilcaspī) — interest; a keen interest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for consolation, then the Hindi for to get bored.

You'll want to know: दिलचस्पी.
दिलचस्पी — dilcaspī — "interest; a keen interest".
मीरा को इतिहास में बहुत दिलचस्पी है। Mīrā ko itihās meṁ bahut dilcaspī hai. Mira is very interested in history.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
दिलचस्पी (dilcaspī) is usually traced to Persian dilchaspī, "interest", built on dil, "heart", and a word for "sticking": what the heart sticks to.

Lesson 3 of 5.
उत्सुक (utsuk) — eager; keen.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to get bored, then the Hindi for interest.

You'll want to know: उत्सुक.
उत्सुक — utsuk — "eager; keen".
बच्चे नया खिलौना देखने के लिए उत्सुक थे। bacce nayā khilaunā dekhne ke liye utsuk the. The children were eager to see the new toy.

The word, taken apart.
उत्सुक (utsuk) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit utsuka, "restless, uneasy" and so "eager" (Monier-Williams).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: रोचक.

दादा की कहानियाँ बहुत रोचक होती हैं। dādā kī kahāniyā̃ bahut rocak hotī haiṁ. Grandfather's stories are always very interesting.

The word, taken apart.
रोचक (rocak) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit rocaka, "giving an appetite" and "pleasing, agreeable" (Monier-Williams): what gives the mind an appetite.
