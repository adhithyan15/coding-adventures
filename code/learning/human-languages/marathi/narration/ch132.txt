Marathi, chapter 132: Hammer, Scissors, Needle, Thread, Comb.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हातोडा (hātoḍā) — a hammer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a rope, then the Marathi for a nail.

You'll want to know: हातोडा.
हातोडा — hātoḍā — "a hammer".

The word, taken apart.
हातोडा (hātoḍā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from hastakūṭa, "hand hammer", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 14028). It is hasta, "hand", and kūṭa, "hammer".

कंगवा — kaṅgvā — "a comb".

The word, taken apart.
कंगवा (kaṅgvā) goes back to Sanskrit kaṅkata, "comb" (CDIAL 2598). CDIAL lists Marathi kaṅkvā as the inherited form and kaṅgvā among the forms taken from Punjabi.
