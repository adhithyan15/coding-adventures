Gujarati, chapter 476: A Long Night's Sleep.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઘોરવું (ghorvũ) — to snore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for eyesight, then the Gujarati for to touch.

You'll want to know: ઘોરવું.
ઘોરવું — ghorvũ — "to snore".
દાદા આખી રાત ઘોરે છે. dādā ākhī rāt ghore che. Grandfather snores all night.
Past (he …): તે આખી રાત ઘોર્યો, te ākhī rāt ghoryo.

The word, taken apart.
ઘોરવું (ghorvũ) goes back to Sanskrit ghurati, "cries out frighteningly", probably an imitative word; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to snore" (CDIAL 4487).

You'll want to know: જાગરણ.
જાગરણ — jāgraṇ — "staying up all night".
દર્દીને સંભાળવા મેં આખી રાત જાગરણ કર્યું. dardīne sambhāḷvā mẽ ākhī rāt jāgraṇ karyũ. I stayed up all night to look after the patient.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
જાગરણ (jāgraṇ) is a tatsama, Sanskrit jāgaraṇa, "waking, keeping awake", as Monier-Williams records it.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for slumber, then the Gujarati for staying up all night.

You'll want to know: પોઢવું.
પોઢવું — poḍhvũ — "to lie down to sleep".
બાળક પલંગ પર પોઢી ગયું. bāḷak palaṅg par poḍhī gayũ. The baby fell asleep on the bed.
Past (it …): તે બાળક પોઢી ગયું, te bāḷak poḍhī gayũ.

The word, taken apart.
પોઢવું (poḍhvũ) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to sleep", under Sanskrit pravardhate, "grows up", whose sense "lie down" CDIAL explains as "stretched out" (CDIAL 8789).
