# ఆ — the long one, beside the short one that needs no sign

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-162, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-07] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Two from the first chapter: అ, and the sign ◌ా. Say what each of them
does.

[PAUSE 2s] Today's character is the missing corner of that square.

## Script you'll notice: ఆ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-166]; assesses=[] -->

**ఆ** — *ā*.

It is an **independent vowel** — a vowel that is a letter in its own right, not a mark hung on a consonant.

- **ఆరు** *āru* — six

With this one the *a* vowels are complete, and they make a square worth
drawing:

| | short | long |
|---|---|---|
| **carried by a consonant** | nothing at all | ◌ా |
| **starting a word** | అ | ఆ |

Three of those four cells you have had since chapter one. **The top-left cell is
the one that surprises people**: short *a* on a consonant is written with no
mark, which is why క is *ka* and not *k*. Every other cell gets a shape.

Look at the two letters side by side — అ and ఆ. The long one is the short one
with a stroke added on the right, and that stroke has the same shape as the sign
◌ా. Length shows up on the page as *more ink*, in both columns of the square.

## Writing: ఆ — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-166] -->

Follow the numbered strip: start where movement 1 begins, and let each panel
show you the next movement before your pen makes it. Then write ఆ yourself,
slowly and larger than it is printed, with అ beside it, and find the difference.

> The strip shows **where to start the character and which way to travel**, in
> one attested order. That is taught with real variation from school to school,
> so the strip names its source beneath it: treat it as a sound way in, not the
> only one.
