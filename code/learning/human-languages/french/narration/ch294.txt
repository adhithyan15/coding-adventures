French, chapter 294: L'arrestation.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
interpeller — to stop and question (police); to call out to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the alibi, then the French for the suspicion.

You'll want to know: interpeller.
interpeller — "to stop and question (police); to call out to".
La police a interpellé deux jeunes hommes. The police stopped two young men for questioning.
In the news, interpeller means the police took someone in. In the street, it means to call out to someone.

The word, taken apart.
interpeller is a learned borrowing from Latin interpellāre "to interrupt, to address" (EtymDB).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'arrestation — the arrest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the suspicion, then the French for to stop and question.

You'll want to know: l'arrestation.
l'arrestation — "the arrest".
L'arrestation a eu lieu tôt le matin. The arrest took place early in the morning.
The verb is arrêter: il a été arrêté.

The word, taken apart.
arrestation comes from Medieval Latin arrestātiō (EtymDB), built on arrestāre "to stop", from ad and restāre "to stay back" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
les menottes — the handcuffs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to stop and question, then the French for the arrest.

You'll want to know: les menottes.
les menottes — "the handcuffs".
Le policier lui a mis les menottes. The police officer put the handcuffs on him.
Literally 'little hands', from main.

The word, taken apart.
menottes is a diminutive of main "hand", which is inherited from Latin manus "hand" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la garde à vue — police custody.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the arrest, then the French for the handcuffs.

You'll want to know: la garde à vue.
la garde à vue — "police custody".
Le jeune homme a passé la nuit en garde à vue. The young man spent the night in police custody.
Être placé en garde à vue — to be held for questioning.

The word, taken apart.
garde comes from garder "to keep, to watch", from a Frankish verb wardōn (EtymDB); vue is built on voir, from Latin vidēre "to see": kept in sight.

Lesson 5 of 5.

interroger — "to question, to interrogate".
Les policiers l'ont interrogé pendant trois heures. The police questioned him for three hours.
At school, interroger un élève — to test a pupil orally.

The word, taken apart.
interroger is a learned borrowing from Latin interrogāre "to ask, to question" (EtymDB), built on rogāre "to ask".
