Latin, chapter 553: Doors and stores.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vestibulum, vestibulī — a forecourt, entrance court.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sweep, then the Latin for a broom.

You'll want to know: vestibulum, vestibulī.
vestibulum, vestibulī — "a forecourt, entrance court".
Hospitēs in vestibulō exspectant. The guests wait in the forecourt.
Neuter, second declension. The space between the street and the front door. English vestibule.

The word, taken apart.
vestibulum is of uncertain origin; Lewis & Short connects it with a root meaning "to dwell", but it is not settled. English vestibule comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: pessulus, pessulī.
pessulus, pessulī — "a door bolt".
Iānitor noctū pessulum iānuae obdit. At night the doorkeeper shoots the bolt of the door.
Masculine, second declension. Pessulum obdere — to shoot the bolt.

The word, taken apart.
pessulus was borrowed from Greek passalos "a peg" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
līmen, līminis — a threshold, doorstep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a forecourt, then the Latin for a door bolt.

You'll want to know: līmen, līminis.
līmen, līminis — "a threshold, doorstep".
Nova nūpta līmen nōn tangit. The new bride does not touch the threshold.
Neuter, third declension. A bride was carried over the threshold, so as not to stumble on it. English subliminal.

The word, taken apart.
līmen is a native Latin noun; Lewis & Short connects it with līmus "sidelong", a crossbeam, but the origin is not settled. English subliminal comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cardō, cardinis — a hinge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a door bolt, then the Latin for a threshold.

You'll want to know: cardō, cardinis.
cardō, cardinis — "a hinge".
Iānua vetus in cardine sonat. The old door creaks on its hinge.
Masculine, third declension. Also a turning point, and so English cardinal — that on which things turn.

The word, taken apart.
cardō is a native Latin noun of uncertain origin; Lewis & Short offers only guesses. English cardinal, "chief, pivotal", comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
penus, penūs — provisions, the store of food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a threshold, then the Latin for a hinge.

You'll want to know: penus, penūs.
penus, penūs — "provisions, the store of food".
Vīlica penum in cellā servat. The housekeeper keeps the provisions in the storeroom.
Masculine or feminine, fourth declension; also penum, neuter. The stored food of a household.

The word, taken apart.
penus is inherited from a Proto-Indo-European word for "food" (EtymDB).
