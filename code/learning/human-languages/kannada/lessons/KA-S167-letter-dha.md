# ಧ — the breath that opens the thank-you word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-166] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say thank you in Kannada. It begins on today's character, and it has done since the first chapter.

## Script you'll notice: ಧ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-167]; assesses=[] -->

**ಧ** — *dha*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *dh*, it is **dha**.

**ಧ** is **ದ** with a puff of air after it. Hold a hand in front of your mouth:
say ದ and the air stays put; say ಧ and it comes out.

**This is the last big idea left in this script, and it accounts for seven of
the characters you have never been shown.** Kannada gives the breathy partner of
a consonant its own shape rather than leaving you to guess:

| no breath | breath |
|---|---|
| ದ | ಧ |

The six that follow are the same move on six other letters.

You already say these:

- **ಧನ್ಯವಾದ** *dhanyavāda* — thank you
- **ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ** *madhyāhna* — noon
- **ಶುಭ ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ** *śubha madhyāhna* — good afternoon

## Writing: ಧ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-167] -->

- **1.** start at the upper left and go down the left side into the left lobe, rising into the middle point
- **2.** without lifting, drop from the point around the right lobe and climb the right side
- **3.** without lifting, close the bowl leftward along the top
- **4.** lift, then draw the top bar from left to right
- **5.** without lifting, curl up into the hook
- **6.** lift, then draw the tail downward

**Pen lifts: 2.** This is ದ with a tail: the body, then the curled bar,
then the tail below the point, each after a lift.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna

> 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0.

> The animation is filed as 'dhha': that series spells ದ as 'dha'.
