German, chapter 414: Jeden Tag mit dem Zug.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Anschluss — the connection (to another train).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to book, then the German for to travel on.

You'll want to know: der Anschluss.
der Anschluss — "the connection (to another train)".
Wegen der Verspätung verpassen wir den Anschluss. Because of the delay we miss our connection.
Plural: die Anschlüsse. Also a connection for water, gas or the internet.

The word, taken apart.
Anschluss is built on anschließen 'to connect, to join on' (EtymDB), from schließen 'to close, to lock', inherited from Old High German sliozan (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Nahverkehr — the local transport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to travel on, then the German for the connection.

You'll want to know: der Nahverkehr.
der Nahverkehr — "the local transport".
Mit dem Nahverkehr kommt man gut ins Zentrum. With local transport you can get to the centre easily.
No plural. Der öffentliche Nahverkehr — public transport in a town and its region.

The word, taken apart.
Nahverkehr is built from nah 'near', inherited from Old High German nāh and a cousin of English nigh (EtymDB), and Verkehr 'traffic', from kehren 'to turn'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
pendeln — to commute; to swing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the local transport, then the German for the long-distance transport.

You'll want to know: pendeln.
pendeln — "to commute; to swing".
Sie pendelt jeden Tag zwischen zwei Städten. She commutes between two towns every day.
Perfekt with sein for travel: er ist jahrelang gependelt.

The word, taken apart.
pendeln is built on Pendel 'pendulum' (EtymDB), a learned borrowing from Latin pendulus 'hanging', from pendēre 'to hang' (Lewis & Short): a commuter swings back and forth like a pendulum.
