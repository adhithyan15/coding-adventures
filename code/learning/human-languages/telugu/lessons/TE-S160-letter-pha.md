# ఫ — ప, with the breath let out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-155, TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-113] -->

[PAUSE 1s] A page ago you met ట and ఠ — one tongue position, with and without a
puff of air after it. Say both.

[PAUSE 2s] Now say ప. Today's character does to ప what ఠ did to ట.

## Script you'll notice: ఫ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160]; assesses=[] -->

**ఫ** — *pha*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *ph*, it is **pha**.

- **ఫాల్గుణం** *phālguṇam* — Phalguna, the twelfth month of the year

Hold a hand in front of your mouth. Say ప: the air stays put. Say ఫ: the air
comes out. That puff is the whole difference, and Telugu gives it a shape of
its own rather than leaving you to guess from the neighbours.

| no breath | breath |
|---|---|
| ట | ఠ |
| ప | ఫ |

**Read the romanization carefully here**: *ph* is one sound with air behind it,
not the *f* that the same two English letters make in *phone*. Two letters on
the romanized line, one character on the Telugu line, one puff in the mouth.

This character is rare on these pages. It earns a lesson because the last of
the twelve months needs it, and an unfamiliar shape inside a list you already
know by heart leaves a reader unable to tell whether the word or the printing
is at fault.

## Writing: ఫ — copy what you see
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-SCRIPT-RECOG-160] -->

Put your pen on ఫ and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly,
and larger than it is printed. Then write ప beside it and look at the two.

> This book does not yet tell you **where to start the character or which way to
> travel**. That is a real thing, taught with real variation from school to
> school, and it is not written down here until it can be written down with a
> source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the
> shape gets into your hand in the meantime.
