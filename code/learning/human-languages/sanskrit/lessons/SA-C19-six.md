# षट् (ṣaṭ) — six

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can count to five. The next five are where the family resemblance becomes impossible to miss.

## You'll want to know: षट्
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-C19-NUM-01]; assesses=[] -->

**षट्** (*ṣaṭ*) — "six."

Say it beside its relatives: Latin *sex*, Greek *hex*, English *six*. The
Sanskrit form looks least like the others on the page, because *ṣ* is a
retroflex sound English has no letter for — but it is the same word.

This chapter is the clearest evidence a beginner can be shown that Sanskrit,
Latin and English are relatives rather than neighbours. The numbers are the
proof, because numbers are borrowed less often than almost anything else.
