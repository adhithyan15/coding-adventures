## You'll want to know: The word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-TERCEIRO-02]; assesses=[] -->

> **terceiro** (masculine) · **terceira** (feminine) — **third**.
> **o terceiro dia** — the third day. **a terceira vez** — the third time.

Now look at Tuesday. It is ***terça*-feira**, not *terceira-feira*.

## Grammar Lens: two children of one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-TERCEIRO-04]; assesses=[] -->

Latin *tertius* left **two** descendants in Portuguese, and both survive:

| form | where it lives |
|---|---|
| **terça** | frozen in *terça-feira*, and in **um terço**, "a third part" |
| **terceiro** | the ordinary counting word — *o terceiro andar* |

*Terça* is the older, inherited shape, worn down in ordinary speech; *terceiro*
was rebuilt later on the pattern of *primeiro*. The week was numbered in the
sixth century and kept the shape that existed then, which is why Tuesday looks
older than the word for "third" — it is.

So *quarta* and *quinta* will match their ordinals exactly, and this one does
not. Watch for it: it is the only weekday that disagrees.
