## HL-C403 — ML-C83 says "every noun ending in -ം does it", and five taught words do not

Flagged in three consecutive chapter reviews (99, 100, 101) and filed here so it
stops being re-discovered.

### The claim

`ML-C83-keralathil` (sequence **2900**):

> **Every noun ending in -ം does it** … the moment **anything** is added, the
> word changes into its working shape first.

"It" is the replacement of **ം** by **-ത്ത-**, as in **കേരളം** →
**കേരളത്തിൽ**.

### What breaks it

Five taught words put something after a **ം** without any **-ത്ത-** appearing:

| word | joined | where |
|---|---|---|
| **മോശം** | **മോശമാണ്** | `ML-C89-mosham` |
| **എളുപ്പം** | **എളുപ്പമാണ്** | `ML-C96-eluppam` |
| **സുന്ദരം** | **സുന്ദരമാണ്** | `ML-C97-sundaram` |
| **നൃത്തം** | **നൃത്തമാണ്** | `ML-C99-nrttham` |
| **ഉയരം** | **ഉയരമുണ്ട്** | `ML-C101-uyaram` |

And a sixth goes a third way: **സുന്ദരം** → **സുന്ദരവും** before the *and*
ending, with a **വ**.

### Why this one is not a one-clause fix

`ML-C96-eluppam` **already states the correct version**, six hundred points
later:

> **Not every ending is this quiet.** A case ending takes the **ം** away and
> puts **ത്ത** in its place… The *and* ending puts **വും** there. The plural
> puts **ങ്ങൾ**… So what a word ending in **ം** does **depends on what attaches
> to it**.

So the corpus contains both the over-broad claim (2900) and its correction
(3410), and the learner meets them in that order. The question is not what is
true — `ML-C96` has that right — but whether `ML-C83` should be narrowed to the
case endings it is actually about, or should forward-reference the fuller
account. That is a judgement about teaching order, which is why it is filed
rather than patched.

### The narrow reading, for whoever takes it

`ML-C83` is a lesson about the **locative**, and "anything" plausibly meant "any
**case ending**" — under which the claim is true. But the word on the page is
"anything", and the learner has already said **മോശമാണ്** at 3130 by the time
they reach `ML-C96`'s correction.

Same family as `HL-C400`, `HL-C401` and `HL-C402`: an early lesson stating a
universal that later lessons break. Unlike those three, **this one's correction
already exists in the corpus** — it is only in the wrong place relative to the
claim.
