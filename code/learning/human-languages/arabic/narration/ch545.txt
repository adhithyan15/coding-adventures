Arabic, chapter 545: Poets and directors.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
شاعر (shāʿir) — a poet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a reed flute, then the Arabic for a band.

You'll want to know: شاعر.
شاعر — shāʿir — "a poet".
كتب الشاعر قصيدة عن البحر. kataba ash-shāʿir qaṣīda ʿan al-baḥr. The poet wrote a poem about the sea.
Plural: شعراء, shuʿarāʾ.
Shiʿr, poetry, has the same letters as shaʿr, hair.

The word, taken apart.
شاعر (shāʿir) is built on the native Arabic root sh-ʿ-r, "to know, to perceive" (Lane). Shuʿūr, a feeling, which you know, is from the same root: a poet is one who perceives.

Lesson 2 of 5.
قافية (qāfiya) — a rhyme.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a band, then the Arabic for a poet.

You'll want to know: قافية.
قافية — qāfiya — "a rhyme".
كل بيت في القصيدة له نفس القافية. kull bayt fī al-qaṣīda lahu nafs al-qāfiya. Every line in the poem has the same rhyme.
بيت is a line of verse as well as a house.

The word, taken apart.
قافية (qāfiya) is built on the native Arabic root q-f-w, "to follow" (Lane). The qāfiya is the end of the line that follows the same sound each time.

Lesson 3 of 5.
أنشد (anshada) — to recite (verse); to chant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a poet, then the Arabic for a rhyme.

You'll want to know: أنشد.
أنشد — anshada — "to recite (verse); to chant".
أنشد الطلاب نشيد المدرسة. anshada aṭ-ṭullāb nashīd al-madrasa. The students sang the school anthem.
Present: ينشد, yunshid.
The noun نشيد is an anthem or a chant.

The word, taken apart.
أنشد (anshada) is built on the native Arabic root n-sh-d, "to seek; to recite verse" (Lane). Form IV anshada is to recite verse aloud.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a rhyme, then the Arabic for to recite.

You'll want to know: أبدع.
أبدع — abdaʿa — "to create; to excel".
أبدعت الفرقة في الحفلة. abdaʿat al-firqa fī al-ḥafla. The band was brilliant at the party.
Present: يبدع, yubdiʿ.
أبدع (abdaʿa) في … is to do brilliantly at a thing.

The word, taken apart.
أبدع (abdaʿa) is built on the native Arabic root b-d-ʿ, "to originate, to make a thing new" (Lane). Mubdiʿ, creative, is its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
أخرج (akhraja) — to direct films; to bring out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to recite, then the Arabic for to create.

You'll want to know: أخرج.
أخرج — akhraja — "to direct films; to bring out".
أخرج صديقي فيلما قصيرا. akhraja ṣadīqī fīlman qaṣīran. My friend directed a short film.
Present: يخرج, yukhrij.
Its first sense is to take out; the director, مخرج, is spelled like makhraj, an exit.

The word, taken apart.
أخرج (akhraja) is built on the native Arabic root kh-r-j, "to go out" (Lane). Kharaja, to go out, which you know, is from the same root; Form IV is to bring out.
