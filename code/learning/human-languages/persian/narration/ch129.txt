Persian, chapter 129: Tired, Silent, Glad, Wise, Ignorant.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خسته (khaste) — tired.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for simple, then the Persian for calm.

You'll want to know: خسته.
خسته — khaste — "tired".

The word, taken apart.
khaste is the past participle of khastan, "to wound, to injure" (EtymDB): "wounded", then "worn out, tired". The verb is native Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
دانا (dânâ) — wise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for silent, then the Persian for glad.

You'll want to know: دانا.
دانا — dânâ — "wise".

The word, taken apart.
dânâ is dân-, the present stem of dânestan, "to know", with the ending -â, "-ing": "knowing". The verb is inherited, from Middle Persian dānistan.

Lesson 5 of 5.
نادان (nâdân) — ignorant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for glad, then the Persian for wise.

You'll want to know: نادان.
نادان — nâdân — "ignorant".

The word, taken apart.
nâdân is built from nâ-, "not", and dân, "knowing", from dânestan, "to know": "unknowing". dânestan is inherited, from Middle Persian dānistan, a cousin of English know.
