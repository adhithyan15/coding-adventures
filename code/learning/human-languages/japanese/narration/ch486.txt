Japanese, chapter 486: Parts and Fittings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ぶひん (buhin) — a part (of a machine).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for plain, then the Japanese for showy.

You'll want to know: ぶひん.
ぶひん — buhin — "a part (of a machine)".
ぶひんが こわれたら、とりかえます。 — buhin ga kowaretara, torikaemasu — "If a part breaks, I replace it."

The word, taken apart.
ぶひん (buhin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are bu, "section" (Middle Chinese buX) and hin, "goods" (phimX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
かなぐ (kanagu) — a metal fitting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for showy, then the Japanese for a part (of a machine).

You'll want to know: かなぐ.
かなぐ — kanagu — "a metal fitting".
かばんの かなぐが こわれました。 — kaban no kanagu ga kowaremashita — "The metal fitting on my bag broke."

The word, taken apart.
かなぐ (kanagu) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: native kana, "gold" and Sino-Japanese gu, "tool" (Middle Chinese gjuH), borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
さびる (sabiru) — to rust.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a part (of a machine), then the Japanese for a metal fitting.

You'll want to know: さびる.
さびる — sabiru — "to rust".
じてんしゃが あめで さびました。 — jitensha ga ame de sabimashita — "My bicycle rusted in the rain."
Polite さびます, sabimasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): じてんしゃが さびました。, jitensha ga sabimashita.

The word, taken apart.
さびる (sabiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
