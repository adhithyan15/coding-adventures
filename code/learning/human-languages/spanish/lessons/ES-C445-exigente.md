# exigente — driving it out of you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DIFICIL, ES-LEX-FACIL, ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **difícil** and **fácil**. Say **la responsabilidad**. A job can
be hard without anybody being hard on you. Name the word for a boss who is.

## You'll want to know: exigente
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DIFICIL] -->

**exigente** — demanding, exacting.

*Un jefe exigente* — a demanding boss. *Es muy exigente consigo mismo* — he is
very hard on himself. The verb is **exigir**, to demand: *exigen dos años de
experiencia* — they require two years of experience, which is the line you meet
in a job advert.

It does not change for gender: *un jefe exigente*, *una jefa exigente*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-03] -->

**ex-** out, plus **-igente** from Latin **agere**, to drive.

*Exigere* was **to drive something out** — originally to drive out a payment, to
collect what was owed. An *exigente* boss drives the work out of you, and the
picture is the same one the Roman tax collector used.

*Agere* is one of the great Latin roots, and the words it gave English all keep
some sense of driving something forward:

| word | what is driven |
|---|---|
| **agenda** | the things to be driven through |
| **actor** | the one who drives the action |
| **agile** | easily driven |

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C445-PUESTO-03, ES-LEX-FACIL] -->

**Difícil** and **exigente** are not the same complaint, and a job advert uses
them differently.

*Difícil* is a property of the task: the work is hard. *Exigente* is a property
of whoever is asking: the standard is high. A puzzle is *difícil*; a person is
*exigente*. You can have an easy job with a demanding manager, and Spanish
requires you to pick which you mean.

The ending **-ente** is doing that work. It comes from a Latin present
participle and marks **the one doing the verb** — *exigente* is the one
demanding, the way *estudiante* is the one studying and *cliente* is the one
leaning on you for service.

So when you meet an unfamiliar word in **-ante** or **-ente**, look for a verb
inside it and read the whole as *the one who does that*.
