Russian, chapter 424: On the ferry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
паром (paróm) — a ferry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to cancel, then the Russian for compensation.

You'll want to know: паром.
паром (paróm) — "a ferry".
На остров ходит паром. Na óstrov khódit paróm. A ferry goes to the island.
Masculine. На пароме — by ferry.

The word, taken apart.
паром (paróm) is inherited from Proto-Slavic pormŭ 'a ferry' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
причал (prichál) — a mooring, a berth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a deck, then the Russian for a cabin.

You'll want to know: причал.
причал (prichál) — "a mooring, a berth".
Лодки стоят у причала. Lódki stoyát u prichála. The boats are at the mooring.
Masculine. Built on причалить — to moor.

The word, taken apart.
причал (prichál) is built on причалить 'to moor', from при- and чалить 'to tie up (a boat)'. EtymDB lists чалить without a source, so its origin is left open.

Lesson 5 of 5.
пристань (prístan) — a landing stage, a jetty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cabin, then the Russian for a mooring.

You'll want to know: пристань.
пристань (prístan) — "a landing stage, a jetty".
Мы ждали паром на пристани. My zhdáli paróm na prístani. We waited for the ferry at the landing stage.
Feminine, though it ends in -ь: на пристани.

The word, taken apart.
пристань (prístan) is built on пристать 'to put in to shore', from при- and стать 'to stand, to come to a stop'. EtymDB lists пристать without a deeper source.
