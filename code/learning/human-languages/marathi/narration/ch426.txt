Marathi, chapter 426: Cash and Cheques.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
रोकड (rokaḍ) — cash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for fake, then the Marathi for a copy.

You'll want to know: रोकड.
रोकड — rokaḍ — "cash".
दुकानदाराला रोकड हवी होती. dukāndārālā rokaḍ havī hotī. The shopkeeper wanted cash.
Feminine.
रोख is the adjective: रोख पैसे, cash money.

The word, taken apart.
रोकड (rokaḍ) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi rokaḍ, "cash", under rōkka, "ready money" (CDIAL 10828).

Lesson 2 of 5.
धनादेश (dhanādeś) — a cheque.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a copy, then the Marathi for cash.

You'll want to know: धनादेश.
धनादेश — dhanādeś — "a cheque".
त्याने भाड्यासाठी धनादेश दिला. tyāne bhāḍyāsāṭhī dhanādeś dilā. He gave a cheque for the rent.
Masculine.
A formal word; in speech many people say चेक.

The word, taken apart.
धनादेश (dhanādeś) is a modern word built from Sanskrit dhana, "wealth", and ādeśa, "an order" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
चिल्लर (cillar) — small change.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for cash, then the Marathi for a cheque.

You'll want to know: चिल्लर.
चिल्लर — cillar — "small change".
माझ्याकडे चिल्लर नाही. mājhyākaḍe cillar nāhī. I have no small change.
Feminine.
Coins and small notes; it also means petty, trivial.

The word, taken apart.
चिल्लर (cillar) is a Marathi word for loose coins; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
रक्कम (rakkam) — a sum of money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a cheque, then the Marathi for small change.

You'll want to know: रक्कम.
रक्कम — rakkam — "a sum of money".
ही रक्कम खूप मोठी आहे. hī rakkam khūp moṭhī āhe. This sum is very large.
Feminine.
A feminine noun: मोठी रक्कम (rakkam).

The word, taken apart.
रक्कम (rakkam) is a loan through Persian, usually traced to Arabic raqam, "a written figure".

Lesson 5 of 5.
जमा करणे (jamā karṇe) — to deposit; to collect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for small change, then the Marathi for a sum of money.

You'll want to know: जमा करणे.
जमा करणे — jamā karṇe — "to deposit; to collect".
मी पगाराचे पैसे खात्यात जमा केले. mī pagārāce paise khātyāt jamā kele. I paid my salary into my account.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने जमा केलं, tyāne jamā kelaṁ.
The verb agrees with the money: पैसे जमा केले, रक्कम (rakkam) जमा केली.

The word, taken apart.
जमा करणे (jamā karṇe) joins जमा, "collected, deposited", a loan through Persian, usually traced to Arabic jamʿ, "gathering", to the light verb करणे.
