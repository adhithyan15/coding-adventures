## You'll want to know: 力气
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C474-STRENGTH474-02]; assesses=[] -->

**力气** — *lìqi* — "physical strength".

> **他的力气比我大** *tā de lìqi bǐ wǒ dà* — He's stronger than I am.

The new character, in pieces: 力 — two strokes: a stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks to the left, and a long stroke falling to the left through it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**力气** (*lìqi*) is built from 力 'strength' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *lik* and *khj+jH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'sinew' and the second 'breath'; the second is said light.
