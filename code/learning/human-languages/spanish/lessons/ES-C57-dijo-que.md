# dijo que

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-QUE-OBLIGATORY, ES-GRAMMAR-DECIR-PRETERITE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Report it in the present. (*Dice que está cansado*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Now move only the first verb into the past. (*Dijo*...)

## Grammar Lens: both verbs move
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-BACKSHIFT]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-IMPERFECT-SINGULAR] -->

> ***Dice*** *que* ***está*** *cansado.*
> ***Dijo*** *que* ***estaba*** *cansado.*

The inner sentence cannot stay in the present once the reporting has moved back.
It steps back too — into the **imperfect**.

[PAUSE 2s] Two more, so the shape is unmistakable:

| now | reported later |
|---|---|
| *Dice que trabaja.* | *Dijo que* ***trabajaba***. |
| *Dice que hace calor.* | *Dijo que* ***hacía*** *calor.* |

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-BACKSHIFT, ES-GRAMMAR-IMPERFECT-ONGOING-HABITUAL] -->

Of the two pasts you own, why this one?

[PAUSE 2s] Because of what the imperfect was always for: **what was going on**,
rather than what happened and finished. And that is precisely what a report
describes. He was tired *at the moment he spoke* — not for an instant, and with
no news about when it started or stopped.

The preterite would say something else entirely. It would package his tiredness
as one completed event, which is not what he told you.

[PAUSE 2s] So the backshift is not a rule about reporting at all. It is the
imperfect doing the job it has always done, in a place you had not needed it
yet.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-REPORTED-BACKSHIFT] -->

[PAUSE 2s] When *dice* becomes *dijo*, what happens to the verb after *que*?
(It moves into the **imperfect**.)

[PAUSE 2s] And why that past and not the other one? (Because the report is about
**what was going on** when he spoke.)
