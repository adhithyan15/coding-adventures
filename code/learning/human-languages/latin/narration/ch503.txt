Latin, chapter 503: Temperament.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nātūra, nātūrae — nature, character.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for funny, then the Latin for witty.

You'll want to know: nātūra, nātūrae.
nātūra, nātūrae — "nature, character".
Nātūra frātris meī placida est. My brother's nature is calm.
Feminine, first declension, from nāscor — to be born: what you are born with. English nature.

The word, taken apart.
nātūra is built on nātus, the participle of nāscor "to be born" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB). English nature comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
indolēs, indolis — natural disposition, talent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for witty, then the Latin for nature.

You'll want to know: indolēs, indolis.
indolēs, indolis — "natural disposition, talent".
Hic puer bonam indolem habet. This boy has a good disposition.
Feminine, third declension. Inborn character, often of a promising young person.

The word, taken apart.
indolēs is built on old indu "within" and the root of olēscō "to grow", a relative of alō "to nourish" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB): what grows from within.

Lesson 3 of 5.
alacer, alacris, alacre — lively, eager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for nature, then the Latin for natural disposition.

You'll want to know: alacer, alacris, alacre.
alacer, alacris, alacre — "lively, eager".
Mīlitēs alacrēs ad pugnam currunt. The soldiers run eagerly to the fight.
Three endings in the nominative, like ācer. English alacrity comes from alacritās.

The word, taken apart.
alacer is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short suggests a link with alō "to nourish", but the origin is not settled. English alacrity comes from alacritās, built on it.

Fābula lepida et brevis est. The story is charming and short.
A word of comedy and of friendly letters: neat, pleasant, nicely done.

lepidus is built on lepōs "charm, grace" (Lewis & Short); the origin of lepōs is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lascīvus, lascīva, lascīvum — playful, frisky.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for lively, then the Latin for charming.

You'll want to know: lascīvus, lascīva, lascīvum.
lascīvus, lascīva, lascīvum — "playful, frisky".
Catulī lascīvī in hortō lūdunt. The playful puppies play in the garden.
Of animals and children, frisky; of grown-ups, sometimes naughty. English lascivious is narrower.

The word, taken apart.
lascīvus is inherited and traced to a Proto-Indo-European root las- "eager, wanton" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English lascivious comes from it, with a narrower meaning.
