# el portal — the big door, and the space behind it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PUERTA, ES-LEX-C378-WHERE-60] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *la puerta*. Say *el edificio*. Between the street and the stairs
of a Spanish block there is a space you have no word for. Here it is.

## You'll want to know: el portal
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PUERTA] -->

**el portal** — the street door of a block of flats, and the hall behind it.

One word covers both the door and the space. *Te espero en el portal* means
downstairs, inside, out of the rain.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C433-EDIF-01, ES-LEX-PUERTA] -->

**puerta** plus **-al**, an ending that makes a bigger or more general thing of
the noun it lands on.

| the thing | the larger thing |
|---|---|
| la puerta | el portal |
| el árbol | la arboleda |
| la rama | el ramal |

English has no single word here, which is why translations reach for *doorway*,
*entrance*, *lobby* or *hallway* and none quite fits. When a Spanish word has no
clean English partner, learning the **situation** beats learning a gloss: the
portal is where you buzz, where the post boxes are, and where you wait.
