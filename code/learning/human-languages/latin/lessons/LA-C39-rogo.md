# rogō — asking as reaching straight out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-CAPIO-01, LA-LEX-DICO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Dīcō** tells someone something. **Capiō** takes. This verb is
what you do when you want to take and have to ask first.

## You'll want to know: rogō — "I ask"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-ROGO-01]; assesses=[] -->

**Rogō** means "**I ask**" — both asking a question and asking for a thing.
The dictionary form is **rogāre**. The *g* is hard: *ROH-goh*.

- **rogō** — I ask
- **rogās** — you ask, to one person
- **rogat** — he asks, she asks
- **rogāmus** — we ask
- **rogātis** — you ask, to several people
- **rogant** — they ask

Latin's harder-edged partner is **petō**, **petere**, "I seek, I aim at, I
ask for" — asking with an object in view.

## The word, taken apart: the reach and the flight
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-ETYMON-ROGO-02]; assesses=[] -->

*Rogāre* is generally traced to the Proto-Indo-European root ***\*reg-***,
"to move in a straight line, to direct" — asking pictured as reaching
straight out toward somebody. That last step is the standard account rather
than a certainty, so hold it loosely. The root itself is a giant: it gives
Latin *regō* ("I rule") and *rēx* ("king"), hence **regal**, **regent**,
**region**, **direct**, **correct**, and **erect**. In the Germanic branch
it gives English **right**; in Sanskrit it gives the **raja** of
**maharaja**.

From *rogāre* itself English has **interrogate** ("to ask thoroughly"),
**arrogant** and **arrogate** (*ad-rogāre*, "to claim for oneself"),
**abrogate** ("to ask away," to repeal), **derogatory**, and **surrogate**
(*sub-rogāre*, "to ask in someone's place").

*Petere* runs a second family: **petition**, **appetite**, **compete**,
**repeat**, **impetus**, **perpetual**, and **centripetal**. Its root meant
"to fly, to rush," and that is why Latin *penna*, "feather," belongs to it —
so **pen** is a cousin of *petō*, and Greek supplies **helicopter**, a
spiral wing.

Two flags. English **ask** is Germanic and unrelated. And **rogue** is often
guessed to come from *rogāre*; its origin is genuinely unknown.
