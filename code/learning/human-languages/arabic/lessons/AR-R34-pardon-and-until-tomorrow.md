# Pardon, and until tomorrow

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-01, AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Say *see you tomorrow* and *good night* in Arabic. Each was taught in a
single lesson and neither has come back. Then say what the second one literally
wishes you.

## You'll want to know: one word, several jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C23-AFWAN-02] -->

**عفوا** *ʿafwan* answers *shukran* as **you are welcome**. But the same word, on a
different tone, does duty as **excuse me** and as **pardon** — somebody stepping
past you in a corridor says it, and so does somebody who did not quite hear you.

One word, three jobs, and the root explains all three: **ʿ-f-w** is about
**erasing**. Whatever was owed or intruded upon is wiped off.

## You'll want to know: until tomorrow
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-01, AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-02] -->

**إلى الغد** *ilā l-ghad* is the farewell pattern you already own with a new word
in the slot: **إلى** (*until*) — the very same *ilā* as in *ilā l-liqāʾ* — plus
**الغد**, tomorrow.

The root **gh-d-w** means **to set out at dawn**. Arabic's *tomorrow* therefore
grows out of *early morning*: the next day is named after the hour you start it.

## Why it's said this way: a good night that means a good morning
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-01, AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-02] -->

**تصبح على خير** *tuṣbiḥ ʿalā khayr* is the everyday **good night** — and it
wishes you nothing whatever about the night.

Literally it is **may you wake up into goodness**. English, French, German and
Spanish all wish the sleeper a peaceful *night*; Arabic looks straight past it to
the morning on the other side. *Tuṣbiḥ* carries the same root as the morning
greeting, and **خير** is the goodness you already answer *how are you* with.

So the two halves of the phrase are borrowed from two things you met long before
bedtime came up at all.

## Script: the throat letter and the loop
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-W10-AYN-01, AR-CONCEPT-W10-AYN-02, AR-CONCEPT-W11-HA-AND-TA-MARBUTA-01] -->

- [YOU WRITE: **ع** — the open-mouth curve, then the bowl beneath it]
- [YOU WRITE: **عفوا**, then **على** — the same letter opening both]

**ع** *ʿayn* is a curve above a bowl, and it heads a **third** dots-family, with
**غ** *ghayn* as its dotted partner. It sits at the front of two of the three
phrases on this page.

**ه** *hāʾ* is the shape-shifter: one loop, no dots, four different coats by
position. Its dotted relative **ة** is the tied *tāʾ* that closes feminine words.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C23-AFWAN-02, AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-02, AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-02, AR-CONCEPT-W10-AYN-02] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Name two jobs **عفوا** does besides *you are welcome*. (**Excuse me**
and **pardon**.) Give the literal sense of the root behind *tomorrow*. (**To set
out at dawn**.) Say what **تصبح على خير** actually wishes. (**That you wake into
goodness** — the morning, not the night.) And name **ع**'s dotted partner.
(**غ**.)
