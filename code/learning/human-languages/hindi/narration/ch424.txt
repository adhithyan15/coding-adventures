Hindi, chapter 424: Street Food and Sellers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ढाबा (ḍhābā) — a roadside eatery, a dhaba.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to growl, then the Hindi for to bark.

You'll want to know: ढाबा.
ढाबा — ḍhābā — "a roadside eatery, a dhaba".
राजमार्ग पर हम एक ढाबे पर रुके। rājmārg par ham ek ḍhābe par ruke. On the highway we stopped at a dhaba.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ढाबा (ḍhābā) goes back to ḍhapp, "cover", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5579), which lists Hindi ḍhābā as "thatched roofing". The roadside eatery is likely named for its thatched shelter, though CDIAL does not give that sense.

Lesson 2 of 5.
हलवाई (halvāī) — a sweet-maker; a sweet shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to bark, then the Hindi for a roadside eatery.

You'll want to know: हलवाई.
हलवाई — halvāī — "a sweet-maker; a sweet shop".
हलवाई की दुकान से मैंने लड्डू ख़रीदे। halvāī kī dukān se maiṁne laḍḍū kharīde. I bought laddus from the sweet shop.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
हलवाई (halvāī) is built on हलवा, which is usually traced to Arabic ḥalwā, "a sweetmeat", through Persian; the ending -āī makes the person who makes it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
फेरीवाला (pherīvālā) — a hawker, a street seller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a sweet-maker, then the Hindi for a handcart.

You'll want to know: फेरीवाला.
फेरीवाला — pherīvālā — "a hawker, a street seller".
फेरीवाला हर सुबह गली में आता है। pherīvālā har subah galī meṁ ātā hai. The hawker comes to the lane every morning.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
फेरीवाला (pherīvālā) is pherī, "a round", with -वाला: the man who makes the rounds of the lanes. pherī belongs with phirati, "turns, wanders", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 9078), which lists Hindi pher, "a turn".
