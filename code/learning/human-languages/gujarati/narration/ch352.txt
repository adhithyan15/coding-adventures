Gujarati, chapter 352: Who Governs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સરકાર (sarkār) — a government.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for true to the facts, then the Gujarati for to be taken in.

You'll want to know: સરકાર.
સરકાર — sarkār — "a government".
સરકારે ગામમાં નવી શાળા બનાવી. sarkāre gāmmā̃ navī śāḷā banāvī. The government built a new school in the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સરકાર (sarkār) is usually traced to Persian sarkār, "head of affairs", built from sar, "head", and kār, "work".

Lesson 3 of 5.
સત્તા (sattā) — power to rule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a government, then the Gujarati for a state (of a country).

You'll want to know: સત્તા.
સત્તા — sattā — "power to rule".
નિયમ બદલવાની સત્તા ફક્ત સરકાર પાસે છે. niyam badalvānī sattā phakt sarkār pāse che. Only the government has the power to change the rules.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સત્તા (sattā) is borrowed from Sanskrit sattā, "existence, being" (Monier-Williams); "power, authority" is the sense it has taken in Gujarati.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઘડવું (ghaḍvũ) — to shape; to frame (a law or a plan).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for power to rule, then the Gujarati for the people of a state.

You'll want to know: ઘડવું.
ઘડવું — ghaḍvũ — "to shape; to frame (a law or a plan)".
સરકાર નવો કાયદો ઘડશે. sarkār navo kāydo ghaḍśe. The government will draw up a new law.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે નવો નિયમ ઘડ્યો, teṇe navo niyam ghaḍyo.

The word, taken apart.
ઘડવું (ghaḍvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit gháṭatē; CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "to shape, to hammer" (CDIAL 4407). A law is shaped as a smith shapes metal.
