Spanish, chapter 581: Por culpa de, gracias a.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
causar — to cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for seeing as, then the Spanish for given that.

You'll want to know: causar.
causar — "to cause".
La lluvia causó muchos problemas. The rain caused a lot of problems.
Regular -ar verb.

The word, taken apart.
causar is built on causa, a learned borrowing from Latin causa 'reason, cause' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
provocar — to bring about, to cause; to provoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for given that, then the Spanish for to cause.

You'll want to know: provocar.
provocar — "to bring about, to cause; to provoke".
El ruido provoca dolor de cabeza. Noise causes headaches.
Often for bad results: provocar un accidente. Spelling: provoqué.

The word, taken apart.
provocar is a learned borrowing from Latin provocare 'to call forth, to challenge', built from pro- and vocare 'to call' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
debido a — due to, owing to.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for to cause, then the Spanish for to bring about.

You'll want to know: debido a.
debido a — "due to, owing to".
El tren llega tarde debido a la nieve. The train arrives late due to the snow.
Followed by a noun: debido al frío.

The word, taken apart.
debido a is built on debido, the participle of deber, which is inherited from Latin debere 'to owe' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
gracias a — thanks to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for due to, then the Spanish for because of.

You'll want to know: gracias a.
gracias a — "thanks to".
Aprobé gracias a tu ayuda. I passed thanks to your help.
For a good cause; for a bad one people say por culpa de.

The word, taken apart.
gracias a is built on gracias, the plural of gracia, inherited from Latin gratia 'favour, thanks' (EtymDB).
