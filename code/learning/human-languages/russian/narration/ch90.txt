You'll want to know: стоянка.
стоянка (stoyánka) — "a car park".

The word, taken apart.
стоянка (stoyánka) is стоять, "to stand", with -нка: a place where something stands.

Lesson 3 of 5.
север (séver) — the north.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a car park, then the Russian for a lift.

You'll want to know: север.
север (séver) — "the north".

The word, taken apart.
север (séver) from Proto-Slavic sěverŭ — inherited; dialects still use сивер for "the north wind". Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian šiaurỹs, "north wind", and Latin caurus, "north-west wind".

Lesson 4 of 5.
юг (yug) — the south.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a lift, then the Russian for the north.

You'll want to know: юг.
юг (yug) — "the south".

The word, taken apart.
юг (yug) from Proto-Slavic jugŭ, "south" — inherited. Vasmer calls the proposed etymologies unreliable, among them a link with Latin augeō, "I increase". The same word gave ужин, "supper", first the midday meal.

Lesson 5 of 5.
восток (vostók) — the east.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for the north, then the Russian for the south.

You'll want to know: восток.
восток (vostók) — "the east".

The word, taken apart.
восток (vostók) — taken from Church Slavonic, says Vasmer; dialects kept native всток. Old Church Slavonic vŭstokŭ, "the rising (of the sun)", copies Greek anatolḗ, "rising, east".
