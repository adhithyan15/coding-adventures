Japanese, chapter 126: Happy, Drowsy, Relieved, Ordinary, Tasty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しあわせ (shiawase) — happy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for very young, then the Japanese for plentiful.

You'll want to know: しあわせ.
しあわせ — shiawase — "happy".

The word, taken apart.
しあわせ (shiawase) is native. JMdict's older spelling shows shi, from suru, "to do", and awase, "fitting together"; it is usually explained as first meaning how things fell out, good or bad. It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 3 of 5.
あんしん (anshin) — relieved.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for happy, then the Japanese for drowsy.

You'll want to know: あんしん.
あんしん — anshin — "relieved".

The word, taken apart.
あんしん (anshin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are an, "peaceful" (Middle Chinese 'an) and shin, "heart, mind" (sim) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

The word, taken apart.
うまい (umai) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed, usually written in kana; JMdict gives one kanji spelling for "skilful" and another for "tasty", both chosen for their meaning. Its older origin is not settled.
