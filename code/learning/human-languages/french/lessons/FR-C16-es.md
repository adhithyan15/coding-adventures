# tu es — you are

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] After *suis*, the shortest form in the verb — and one that is easy to
swallow entirely.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-VERB-ETRE-ES-02]; assesses=[] -->

- **tu es** = *tü ay*. Two letters, one vowel sound.
- The **s** is silent, as it was in **tu as**.
- Careful: **tu es** and **tu as** differ only in that vowel — *ay* against
  *ah*. Say them back to back until the difference is deliberate.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**es** is Latin **es**, unchanged. Latin said *es* for "you are" and French still
does, which for a form this heavily used is unusual — most such words are ground
down past recognition.

The pair to watch is not Latin, though. It is **tu es** against **tu as**: "you
are" and "you have," one vowel apart, both extremely common, both with a silent
**s**. English keeps them far apart; French does not.

That is the practical difficulty of this verb. Not the etymology — the fact that
its forms are short, unstressed, and easily confused with *avoir*'s.
