## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-SHADA-READ-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-SA-01, BN-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01, BN-SCRIPT-DA-01] -->

> **স** + **া** + **দ** + **া** → **সাদা**

Two consonants, each wearing the same mātrā. As plain a spelling as this book
has shown.

**shādā** — *white*. And notice the first sound: the letter is the *s*-letter,
and it is said **sh**. That was the news of the greeting back in the first script
chapter, and it has not changed. In Bengali this letter is *sh* far more often
than it is *s*, which is the second leg of the track's sound-fingerprint.

Three colours are now readable:

> **লাল** *lāl* — red
> **কালো** *kālo* — black
> **সাদা** *shādā* — white

White is the one of the three where Bengali and Hindi went different ways: Hindi
took a Persian loan for it and Bengali kept its own word, where for red both took
the same Persian gem-name. A page of Bengali colour words is a small map of who
borrowed what.
