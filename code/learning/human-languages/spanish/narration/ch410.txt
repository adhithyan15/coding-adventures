Spanish, chapter 410: Academy, French, Student and Exam.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la academia — from a place-name to a place of learning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall estudiante, a student. A student may learn at la academia, an academy or teaching centre.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish academia came through Latin academia from Greek Akadēmía, the place-name associated with Plato's school. English academy belongs to the same travelled family. In an A1 message, una academia de idiomas is a language school, not necessarily a university institution.

Grammar Lens: what kind of academy?
Use de to name the subject: una academia de idiomas. The noun is feminine: la academia, una academia.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: la academia]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: una academia de idiomas]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: soy estudiante en una academia]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Academy or teaching centre? (La academia.)
Source: DLE: academia.


Lesson 2 of 5.
francés / francesa — language and person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
At una academia de idiomas, one possible subject is francés, French.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish francés came from French français. The language name is the masculine singular form: hablo francés. For people and adjectives, the ending agrees: un estudiante francés, una estudiante francesa.

Grammar Lens: the accent comes and goes.
The final stress of francés needs its written accent. Adding -a moves the natural stress: francesa, with no accent mark. The plural follows the same pattern: franceses, francesas.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: francés]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: una academia de francés]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: una estudiante francesa]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
French? (Francés.) A French woman or feminine adjective? (Francesa.)
Source: DLE: francés.


Lesson 3 of 5.
el examen — a weighing of what you know.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A student of French may take un examen de francés, a French exam.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish examen is Latin exāmen, a weighing or examination. English exam and examination are learned relatives. The old weighing image is a useful memory hook: an exam weighs what has been learned.

Grammar Lens: singular and plural stress.
The singular is el examen, with no written accent because the stress falls naturally on -xa-. The plural is los exámenes; the accent appears to keep that stress when another syllable is added.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: el examen]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un examen de francés]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Exam? (El examen.) Exams? (Los exámenes.)
Source: DLE: examen.
