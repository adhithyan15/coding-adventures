Mandarin Chinese, chapter 65: Excuse Me.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
门 (mén) — door; gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to borrow, then the Mandarin for to give back.

You'll want to know: 门.
门 — mén — "door; gate".
这是我家的门 zhè shì wǒ jiā de mén — This is the door to my home.
The new character, in pieces: 门 (mén) — a dot, a post down the left, and a frame that turns down the right and hooks: the right-hand piece of 们. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
门 (mén) is inherited from Middle Chinese mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary says its old character is made of two door leaves.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

Lesson 3 of 7.
请问 (qǐngwèn) — excuse me, may I ask.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give back, then the Mandarin for door.

You'll want to know: 请问.
请问 — qǐngwèn — "excuse me, may I ask".
请问这是你的手机吗 qǐngwèn zhè shì nǐ de shǒujī ma — Excuse me, is this your phone?
The new character, in pieces: 问 (wèn) — 门 (mén) with 口 inside it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
请问 (qǐngwèn) is built from 请 'please' and 问 (wèn) 'to ask', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjengX and mjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary already glosses the first as 'to beg, to ask'.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 门 with 口 inside.
问 (wèn).
Two pieces: the door first, then the mouth inside it.
门 (mén), three strokes: the dot, the left post, the frame that turns down and hooks.
口, three strokes, small and inside: the left side, the top that turns down, the bottom that closes it.
3 + 3 = six, and both pieces are already yours.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 问 (wèn)? (Two — 门 (mén), then 口.) Which goes first? (门 (mén), the frame.) Where does 口 sit? (Inside the door.)

Lesson 5 of 7.
bù hǎoyìsi — excuse me; sorry to trouble you.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for door, then the Mandarin for may I ask.

You'll want to know: bù hǎoyìsi.
bù hǎoyìsi — "excuse me; sorry to trouble you".
bù hǎoyìsi wǒ xiǎng wèn nǐ — Excuse me, could I ask you something?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
bù keeps its own falling tone here, because hǎo after it is a third tone, not a fourth.

The word, taken apart.
bù hǎoyìsi is built from bù 'not', hǎo 'good' and yìsi 'meaning', all inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut, xawX, 'iH and si (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the last two as 'intention' and 'to think of'. 'Not a good feeling' is the literal sense; how it became an apology is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for asking politely.

Lesson 7 of 7.
guān — to shut; to close; to switch off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for excuse me, then the Mandarin for to open.

You'll want to know: guān.
guān — "to shut; to close; to switch off".
qǐng guān mén — Please shut the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guān is inherited from Middle Chinese kwaen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains, through an older book, as a wooden bar held across a door. Shutting is probably what the bar does, but the step is not given there.
