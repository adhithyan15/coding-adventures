Japanese, chapter 382: Down to the Coast.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かいがん (kaigan) — the coast; the seashore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to row, then the Japanese for to drift.

You'll want to know: かいがん.
かいがん — kaigan — "the coast; the seashore".
なつに かいがんを あるきました。 — natsu ni kaigan o arukimashita — "In summer I walked along the coast."

The word, taken apart.
かいがん (kaigan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "sea" (Middle Chinese xojX) and gan, "beach" (nganH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
すなはま (sunahama) — a sandy beach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to drift, then the Japanese for the coast.

You'll want to know: すなはま.
すなはま — sunahama — "a sandy beach".
こどもが すなはまで あそんでいます。 — kodomo ga sunahama de asonde imasu — "The children are playing on the sandy beach."

The word, taken apart.
すなはま (sunahama) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from suna, "sand" and hama, "beach"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
