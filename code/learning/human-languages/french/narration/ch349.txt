French, chapter 349: D'où ça vient.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
provenir — to come from, to originate in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for from then on, then the French for the origin.

You'll want to know: provenir.
provenir — "to come from, to originate in".
Ce bruit provient de la cuisine. This noise is coming from the kitchen.
Conjugated like venir, with être. En provenance de — arriving from.

The word, taken apart.
provenir comes from Latin prōvenīre "to come forth, to arise" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on venīre "to come".

Lesson 2 of 6.
résulter — to result (from).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for the origin, then the French for to come from.

You'll want to know: résulter.
résulter — "to result (from)".
Cet accident résulte d'une erreur humaine. This accident results from human error.
Il en résulte que — the result is that. Mostly in the third person.

The word, taken apart.
résulter is a learned borrowing from Latin resultāre "to spring back, to rebound" (Lewis & Short), built on saltāre "to leap".

Lesson 3 of 6.
l'influence — the influence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to come from, then the French for to result.

You'll want to know: l'influence.
l'influence — "the influence".
Ses amis ont une mauvaise influence sur lui. His friends are a bad influence on him.
Sous l'influence de — under the influence of. Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
influence is usually traced to Medieval Latin influentia "a flowing in", built on Latin influere "to flow into" (Lewis & Short), from fluere "to flow".

Lesson 5 of 6.
engendrer — to generate, to give rise to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the influence, then the French for to influence.

You'll want to know: engendrer.
engendrer — "to generate, to give rise to".
Le bruit engendre la fatigue. Noise brings on tiredness.
Formal. The older sense is to father a child.

The word, taken apart.
engendrer is inherited from Latin ingenerāre "to implant, to produce" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on generāre "to beget", from genus "birth, kind".
