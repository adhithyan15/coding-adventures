# ধীরে — the word you will need most

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C19-DHIRE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Someone is speaking faster than you can follow. One word, and it is
usually said twice.

## You'll want to know: said twice, and that is the idiom
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C19-DHIRE-01] -->

**ধীরে** — *slowly*. On its own it is an ordinary adverb. Doubled, **ধীরে
ধীরে**, it is the everyday way to ask for it: Bengali repeats a word to soften
and to spread it out, and a single **ধীরে** can sound like an instruction where
the doubled one sounds like a request.

It is half of the repair kit. The other half asks someone to say it again; this
half asks them to say it at a speed you can follow, and of the two it is the
one a beginner reaches for more often.

## Reading: the two that had no second look
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-PORA-READ-01, BN-SCRIPT-HRIDOY-READ-01] -->

> পড়া · হৃদয়

Both were taught once and read once. Say each aloud, then say what makes the
second one harder than the first: **হৃদয়** carries **ৃ**, a sign for a vowel
that used to be an *r*, and nothing else in the word warns you it is coming.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C19-DHIRE-01, BN-SCRIPT-PORA-READ-01, BN-SCRIPT-HRIDOY-READ-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] How do you ask someone to slow down? (**ধীরে ধীরে** — doubled.) What does the doubling do? (**Softens it** into a request.) Which of the two words above needs **ৃ**? (**হৃদয়**.) And what did that sign once write? (**An *r***.)
