# (el plazo, entregar, admitir) — from cold

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-01, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Close the lessons before this one. Say all three: *the deadline*,
*to hand in*, *to let in*.

## Grammar Lens: the three of them are one sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-01, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03] -->

Put them in the order they happen and you have the whole of official life:

> **Entregue** la solicitud dentro del **plazo**. Fuera de plazo no se
> **admiten**.

You hand it in, there is a window, and the office decides. Three words, and
every form you meet in Spanish will use at least two of them.

Note *dentro del plazo* against *fuera de plazo*: the first takes an article,
the second does not, because *fuera de* + bare noun is the fixed frame you met
in *fuera de servicio*.

## Grammar Lens: mittere, for the third time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-03, ES-LEX-C458-PLAZO-02] -->

| word | the prefix, and what it does |
|---|---|
| meter | none — put in |
| el permiso | *per-*, sent through |
| admitir | *ad-*, sent towards, let in |

One Latin verb, three Spanish words, and the prefix is the whole difference.
*Meter* came down through speech and looks least like the others; *permiso*
and *admitir* were lifted off the page and kept their shape — the same split
you saw in *rueda* against *rotativo*.

And **entregar** hides a different picture: *integrare*, to make whole, so
what you hand over should arrive entire.
