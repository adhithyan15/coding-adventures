German, chapter 278: Verhandlungen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
verhandeln — to negotiate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to demonstrate, then the German for the crisis.

You'll want to know: verhandeln.
verhandeln — "to negotiate".
Die beiden Firmen verhandelten eine ganze Woche. The two companies negotiated for a whole week.
Über etwas verhandeln — to negotiate about something.

The word, taken apart.
verhandeln is built on handeln "to deal", from Old High German hantalōn, which EtymDB derives from Proto-Germanic handuz "hand".

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 5.
unterzeichnen — to sign (formally).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the negotiation, then the German for the contract.

You'll want to know: unterzeichnen.
unterzeichnen — "to sign (formally)".
Beide Seiten unterzeichneten den Vertrag. Both sides signed the treaty.
More formal than unterschreiben. Not separable: hat unterzeichnet.

The word, taken apart.
unterzeichnen joins unter "under" and zeichnen "to mark", from Zeichen "sign", an inherited Germanic word; EtymDB lists no parent for the verb: to put one's mark under.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Konferenz — the conference.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the contract, then the German for to sign.

You'll want to know: die Konferenz.
die Konferenz — "the conference".
Die Konferenz war im Rathaus. The conference was in the town hall.
Plural: die Konferenzen. Stress at the end: Kon-fe-RENZ.

The word, taken apart.
Konferenz comes from Medieval Latin conferentia, built on conferre "to bring together", from con- "together" and ferre "to carry" (EtymDB).
