Arabic, chapter 429: By boat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
قبطان (qubṭān) — a captain (of a ship).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for the metro, then the Arabic for to unload.

You'll want to know: قبطان.
قبطان — qubṭān — "a captain (of a ship)".
تكلم القبطان مع الركاب. takallama al-qubṭān maʿa ar-rukkāb. The captain spoke with the passengers.

The word, taken apart.
قبطان (qubṭān) was borrowed from Latin capitaneus, "a chief" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
قارب (qārib) — a boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to unload, then the Arabic for a captain.

You'll want to know: قارب.
قارب — qārib — "a boat".
ركبنا قاربا صغيرا إلى الجزيرة. rakibnā qāriban ṣaghīran ilā al-jazīra. We took a small boat to the island.
Plural: قوارب, qawārib.

The word, taken apart.
قارب (qārib) is built on the native Arabic root q-r-b, "to be near" (Lane). Lane lists qārib as a separate sense of the root: a ship's small boat, a skiff the seamen use for their errands.

Lesson 3 of 5.
عبارة (ʿabbāra) — a ferry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a captain, then the Arabic for a boat.

You'll want to know: عبارة.
عبارة — ʿabbāra — "a ferry".
عبرنا النهر بالعبارة. ʿabarnā an-nahr bi-l-ʿabbāra. We crossed the river by ferry.
Unvocalized, the same letters spell ʿibāra, "a phrase".

The word, taken apart.
عبارة (ʿabbāra) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-b-r, "to cross" (Lane). The pattern faʿʿāla names a thing that does an action again and again: the crosser.

The word, taken apart.
أبحر (abḥara) is built on the native Arabic root b-ḥ-r, "the sea" (Lane). It is Form IV, "to go out to sea".

Lesson 5 of 5.
رسا (rasā) — to dock; to anchor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a ferry, then the Arabic for to sail.

You'll want to know: رسا.
رسا — rasā — "to dock; to anchor".
رست السفينة في الميناء بعد الظهر. rasat as-safīna fī al-mīnāʾ baʿda aẓ-ẓuhr. The ship docked in the port in the afternoon.
Present: يرسو, yarsū.

The word, taken apart.
رسا (rasā) is built on the native Arabic root r-s-w, "to be firm, fixed" (Lane). Lane gives it of a mountain standing firm and of a ship coming to anchor.
