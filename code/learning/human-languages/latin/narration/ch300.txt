Latin, chapter 300: Tabellārius et praecō.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tabellārius, tabellāriī — a letter-carrier, a courier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to report, then the Latin for I say.

You'll want to know: tabellārius, tabellāriī.
tabellārius, tabellāriī — "a letter-carrier, a courier".
Tabellārius epistulam patris ad nōs portāvit. The courier carried father's letter to us.
Masculine. Romans with money sent their own slaves as couriers; letters went on wax tabellae.

The word, taken apart.
tabellārius is built on tabella, "a small writing tablet, a letter" (Lewis & Short): the man who carries the tablets.

Lesson 2 of 5.
praecō, praecōnis — a herald, a crier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for I say, then the Latin for a letter-carrier.

You'll want to know: praecō, praecōnis.
praecō, praecōnis — "a herald, a crier".
Praecō in forō victōriam nūntiāvit. The herald announced the victory in the forum.
Masculine, 3rd declension. He called for silence at trials, announced the lots at auctions and proclaimed winners at the games.

The word, taken apart.
praecō is usually traced to prae, "before, in front", and vocō, "to call": one who calls out in front. Lewis & Short give the word without a settled origin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mandātum, mandātī — an instruction, an order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a letter-carrier, then the Latin for a herald.

You'll want to know: mandātum, mandātī.
mandātum, mandātī — "an instruction, an order".
Lēgātus mandāta imperātōris memoriā tenēbat. The envoy kept the general's instructions in his memory.
Neuter, from mandō (taught), 'to entrust'. The plural mandāta is the message an envoy carried. English mandate.

The word, taken apart.
mandātum is the neuter participle of mandō, "to entrust, to order" (Lewis & Short): what has been entrusted. English mandate comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lēgātiō, lēgātiōnis — an embassy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a herald, then the Latin for an instruction.

You'll want to know: lēgātiō, lēgātiōnis.
lēgātiō, lēgātiōnis — "an embassy".
Lēgātiō ab hostibus vēnit et pācem petīvit. An embassy came from the enemy and asked for peace.
Feminine, 3rd declension. The lēgātus (taught) led it. English legation.

The word, taken apart.
lēgātiō is built on lēgō, "to send with a commission" (EtymDB): the sending of envoys, and the envoys themselves. English legation comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
renūntiō, renūntiāre — to bring back word, to report.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an instruction, then the Latin for an embassy.

You'll want to know: renūntiō, renūntiāre.
renūntiō, renūntiāre — "to bring back word, to report".
Servus ad dominum rediit et omnia renūntiāvit. The slave went back to his master and reported everything.
renūntiō, renūntiāre, renūntiāvī, renūntiātum: re- 'back' + nūntiō (taught). Also 'to declare someone elected', and 'to give up, to renounce'.

The word, taken apart.
renūntiō is built on nūntiō, "to announce", with re-, "back" (Lewis & Short): to carry word back. English renounce comes from another sense, "to call off, to give up".
