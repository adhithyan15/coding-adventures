# নাম (nām) — "name," a word older than Europe

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The first word of the introduction — and one of the clearest bridges
between India and Europe.

## The letters in this word

**ন** (*nô*) + **া** (the long-*ā* sign) + **ম** (*mô*) → **নাম** (*nām*). Here
the long-*ā* sign overrides the inherent *ô*, so it is *nām*, not "nôm."

## The word, taken apart

**নাম** (*nām*, "name") ← Sanskrit **নামন্** (*nāman*), from Proto-Indo-European
**\*h₃nómn̥** — the *same* ancient root as English **name**, Latin *nōmen*
(English **noun**, **nom**inate), and Greek *onoma*. Bengali *nām* and English
*name* are cousins from before either language existed — one of the clearest
threads tying the Indian and European branches of one family.
