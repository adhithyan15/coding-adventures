Spanish, chapter 498: El deporte.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el campeonato — the championship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to look after, then the Spanish for friendship.

You'll want to know: el campeonato.
el campeonato — "the championship".
Ganamos el campeonato de baloncesto. We won the basketball championship.

The word, taken apart.
campeonato is campeón, "champion", with -ato. Campeón came from Italian campione (EtymDB 2.1), a fighter in the field, from Latin campus, "a field".

Lesson 2 of 5.
el deportista — the sportsperson, the athlete.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for friendship, then the Spanish for the championship.

You'll want to know: el deportista.
el deportista — "the sportsperson, the athlete".
Es una deportista muy conocida. She is a very well-known athlete.
The same form for a woman: la deportista.

The word, taken apart.
deportista is deporte, "sport", with -ista. Deporte first meant amusement: it comes from deportarse, "to enjoy oneself", Latin deportare, "to carry away".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the championship, then the Spanish for the sportsperson.

You'll want to know: competir.
competir — "to compete".
Compite en los cien metros. He competes in the hundred metres.
The e breaks into i: compito.

The word, taken apart.
competir from Latin competere, "to strive together, to meet" (EtymDB 2.1): con- and petere, "to seek" — a learned word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
montar — to ride; to set up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the sportsperson, then the Spanish for to compete.

You'll want to know: montar.
montar — "to ride; to set up".
Monto en bici los domingos. I ride my bike on Sundays.
Montar a caballo — to ride a horse. In much of Latin America people say andar en bici.

The word, taken apart.
montar is probably from French monter, "to climb", from spoken Latin montare, built on mons, montis, "a mountain" (EtymDB 2.1 has the spoken Latin verb).

Lesson 5 of 5.
dibujar — to draw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to compete, then the Spanish for to ride.

You'll want to know: dibujar.
dibujar — "to draw".
Mi hija dibuja muy bien. My daughter draws very well.
El dibujo is the drawing.

The word, taken apart.
dibujar is usually traced to Old French deboissier, "to carve in wood" (EtymDB 2.1), built on bois, "wood".
