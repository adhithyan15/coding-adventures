Arabic, chapter 102: Roof, Stairs, Ceiling, Behind, Between.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سطح (saṭḥ) — a roof.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a bathroom, then the Arabic for a balcony.

You'll want to know: سطح.
سطح — saṭḥ — "a roof".

The word, taken apart.
سطح (saṭḥ) is built on the native Arabic root s-ṭ-ḥ, "to spread out flat" (Lane). Saṭḥ is a flat roof, the flat top of a house.

You'll want to know: سقف.
سقف — saqf — "a ceiling".

The word, taken apart.
سقف (saqf), "a roof, a ceiling", is an old native noun, listed under the root s-q-f (Lane) beside a verb meaning "to roof a house".

You'll want to know: خلف.

The word, taken apart.
خلف (khalfa) is built on the native Arabic root kh-l-f, "to come after, to succeed" (Lane). Khalfa is "behind"; the same root gives khalīfa, "successor", the source of English caliph.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: بين.

The word, taken apart.
بين (bayna) is built on the native Arabic root b-y-n, "to be separate, to be distinct" (Lane). Bayna is "between", the space that parts two things; Hebrew bēn, "between", is its cousin.
