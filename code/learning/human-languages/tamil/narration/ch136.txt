Tamil, chapter 136: Bottle, Ball, Kite, Doll, Purse.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
குப்பி (kuppi) — a bottle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a nail, then the Tamil for a ladder.

You'll want to know: குப்பி.
குப்பி — kuppi — "a bottle".

The word, taken apart.
குப்பி (kuppi) is probably an Indo-Aryan word for a flask: CDIAL 3402 sets North Indian forms for an oil vessel under kūpa, "leather oil vessel", and its variant kuppa.

Lesson 3 of 5.
பட்டம் (paṭṭam) — a kite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a bottle, then the Tamil for a ball.

You'll want to know: பட்டம்.
பட்டம் — paṭṭam — "a kite".

The word, taken apart.
பட்டம் (paṭṭam) is probably the same word as paṭṭam, "flat piece", which is native (DEDR 3878). DEDR does not list the "kite" sense, so the link is likely rather than proven.

பணப்பை (paṇappai) is paṇam, "money", from Sanskrit paṇa, "a coin" (CDIAL 7715), and native pai, "bag" (DEDR 4450).
