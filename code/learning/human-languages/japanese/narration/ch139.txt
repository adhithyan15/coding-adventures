Japanese, chapter 139: Is It? No: Nothing, Nobody, Nowhere.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ですか (desu ka) — is it...? (the polite yes-or-no question).

Warm-up.
[pause 20 seconds]
Four recalls before the new word.
[your turn — recall: say I go to bed, politely — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say I get up, politely — R2, five lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say a wind, or a cold — R3, twenty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say tea — R4, eighty lessons back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: ですか.
ですか — desu ka — "is it...?". です, desu, says politely that something is so. Put か, ka, at the end, and the sentence becomes a question that can be answered yes or no. The order of the words does not change, and the voice rises a little on ka.
これは みずですか。 — kore wa mizu desu ka — "Is this water?" はい、みずです。 — hai, mizu desu — "Yes, it is water."
A verb takes か the same way: ます becomes ますか.
あした はたらきますか。 — ashita hatarakimasu ka — "Are you working tomorrow?"

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kore wa mizu desu ka — Is this water?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: turn I go home into a question: kaerimasu ka]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you ask "is it...?", politely? (ですか, desu ka.) Say it once more.

You'll want to know: ちがいます.
ちがいます — chigaimasu — "it is different", and so, as an answer, "no, that is not right". It is the polite form of the verb ちがう, chigau, "to differ". When someone asks whether a thing is so and it is not, this is the everyday answer.
これは おちゃですか。 — kore wa ocha desu ka — "Is this tea?" いいえ、ちがいます。みずです。 — iie, chigaimasu. mizu desu — "No, it isn't. It is water."

You'll want to know: じゃない.
じゃない — ja nai — "is not". It is the plain way, among friends, to say that something is not so. The polite way is じゃありません, ja arimasen.
みずじゃない。 — mizu ja nai — "It isn't water."
Verbs say not with their ending. In the polite form, ます becomes ません, masen:
はたらきます becomes はたらきません — hatarakimasen — "I don't work" ねます becomes ねません — nemasen — "I don't go to bed".

You'll want to know: なにも.
なにも — nani mo — "nothing", "not anything". It pairs with a negative verb: Japanese says "anything... not", where English says "nothing".
なにも のみません。 — nani mo nomimasen — "I don't drink anything."
のむ, nomu, "to drink", gives のみます nomimasu and then のみません nomimasen. With ます instead of ません the sentence does not work: なにも (nani mo) needs the not.

You'll want to know: だれも.
だれも — dare mo — "nobody", "not anyone". Like なにも (nani mo), it needs a negative verb.
ここには だれも いません。 — koko ni wa dare mo imasen — "There is nobody here."
いません, imasen, is the negative of います, imasu, "someone is there", the verb Japanese uses for people and animals.

You'll want to know: どこにも.
どこにも — doko ni mo — "nowhere", "not anywhere". It is どこ, "where", with に for the place you go to and も, and like the other two it needs a negative verb.
あしたは どこにも いきません。 — ashita wa doko ni mo ikimasen — "I'm not going anywhere tomorrow."
Now you can ask and answer:
あした はたらきますか。 — "Are you working tomorrow?" いいえ、はたらきません。どこにも (doko ni mo) いきません。 — "No, I'm not. I'm not going anywhere."
