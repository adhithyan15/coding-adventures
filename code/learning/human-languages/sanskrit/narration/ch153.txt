Lesson 1 of 5.
इतः (itaḥ) — from here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a calendar date, then the Sanskrit for a century.

You'll want to know: इतः.
इतः — itaḥ — "from here".

The word, taken apart.
itaḥ is built from the pronoun base i-, the base of idam "this", and the ending -tas "from": "from this place, hence" (MW). It is the same -tas as in yataḥ, ataḥ and tataḥ.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ततः (tataḥ) — from there, then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a century, then the Sanskrit for from here.

You'll want to know: ततः.
ततः — tataḥ — "from there, then".

The word, taken apart.
tataḥ is built from the pronoun base ta- "that" and the ending -tas "from" (MW; CDIAL 5639): "from there", and so "then, after that". It answers yataḥ "from where", and the base ta- is inherited from Indo-European, the same word as English that (EtymDB).

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for from here, then the Sanskrit for from there.

You'll want to know: उभयतः.
उभयतः — ubhayataḥ — "on both sides".

The word, taken apart.
ubhayataḥ is built from ubhaya "both, of both kinds" and the ending -tas "from, on the side of": "on both sides" (MW). MW sets ubha "both" beside Greek amphō, Latin ambo and Gothic bai, the cousin of English both.
