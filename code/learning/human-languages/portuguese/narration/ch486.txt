Portuguese, chapter 486: Vacinas e ligaduras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o termómetro — thermometer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to swell, then the Portuguese for swollen.

You'll want to know: o termómetro.
o termómetro — "thermometer".
O termómetro marcava trinta e nove graus. The thermometer read thirty-nine degrees.
In Brazil: termômetro.

The word, taken apart.
termómetro is usually traced to French thermomètre, a learned coinage from Greek thermós "hot" and métron "measure".

Lesson 2 of 5.
a vacina — vaccine, jab.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for swollen, then the Portuguese for thermometer.

You'll want to know: a vacina.
a vacina — "vaccine, jab".
As crianças levaram a vacina da gripe. The children had the flu jab.
Levar a vacina — to get vaccinated.

The word, taken apart.
vacina is a learned borrowing from Latin vaccīna (EtymDB), from vaccīnus "of cows", built on vacca "cow": the first vaccine came from cowpox.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: a entorse.
a entorse — "sprain".
Fiz uma entorse no tornozelo a jogar à bola. I sprained my ankle playing football.
Feminine in Portugal: uma entorse.

The word, taken apart.
entorse is usually traced to French entorse "a twist", built on a verb that goes back to Latin torquēre "to twist" (Lewis & Short).
