Mandarin Chinese, chapter 145: Late Again.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yuányīn — reason; cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for special, then the Mandarin for to find (that).

You'll want to know: yuányīn.
yuányīn — "reason; cause".
wǒ bù zhīdào shì shénme yuányīn — I don't know what the reason is.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuányīn is built from yuán and 因 'because', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwon and 'jin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a broad plain' and the second 'to follow from'; how the first came to mean origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
晚点 (wǎndiǎn) — to run behind schedule; to be late (of a train or plane).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to find (that), then the Mandarin for a reason.

You'll want to know: 晚点.
晚点 — wǎndiǎn — "to run behind schedule; to be late (of a train or plane)".
火车晚点了半个小时 huǒchē wǎndiǎn le bàn ge xiǎoshí — The train was half an hour late.
Two third tones: wǎndiǎn is said wándiǎn, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
晚点 (wǎndiǎn) is built from 晚 and 点, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjonX and temX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'dusk' and 'a dot'. Late against the hour is the sense of the pair, which the dictionary does not give.

Lesson 3 of 5.
nàozhōng — alarm clock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a reason, then the Mandarin for to run behind schedule.

You'll want to know: nàozhōng.
nàozhōng — "alarm clock".
wǒ méiyǒu nàozhōng suǒyǐ wǒ chídào le — I haven't got an alarm clock, so I was late.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nàozhōng is built from nào 'to stir up', the nao of rènao, and zhōng 'bell', the zhōng of fēnzhōng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, nraewH and tsyowng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a bell that stirs you up.

Lesson 4 of 5.
shuì lǎnjiào — to sleep in; to lie in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to run behind schedule, then the Mandarin for alarm clock.

You'll want to know: shuì lǎnjiào.
shuì lǎnjiào — "to sleep in; to lie in".
jīntiān shì xīngqītiān wǒ xiǎng shuì lǎnjiào — It's Sunday, so I want a lie-in.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shuì lǎnjiào is built from shuì 'to sleep', lǎn 'lazy' and jiào 'a sleep', all inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyweH, lanX and kaewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the last with the word shuìjiào itself.

Lesson 5 of 5.
gǎnkuài — quickly; hurry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for alarm clock, then the Mandarin for to lie in.

You'll want to know: gǎnkuài.
gǎnkuài — "quickly; hurry".
bā diǎn shàngkè wǒmen gǎnkuài zǒu — Class is at eight, so let's hurry.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gǎnkuài is built from gǎn 'to hurry' and 快 'fast'. 快 is inherited from Middle Chinese khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.
