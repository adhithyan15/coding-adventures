Lesson 3 of 5.
ٹانگ (ṭāng) — a leg.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the face, then the Urdu for blood.

You'll want to know: ٹانگ.
ٹانگ — ṭāng — "a leg".

The word, taken apart.
ٹانگ (ṭāng) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ṭaṅga, "leg" (CDIAL 5428). CDIAL lists it under ṭaṅka, which the old lexicons gloss both "rod, spike" and "leg", and asks whether the word first meant "stick".

The word, taken apart.
گھٹنا (ghuṭnā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. CDIAL lists it under ghuṭṭa, a variant it reconstructs beside Sanskrit ghuṭa, "ankle", and takes the Sanskrit word from Dravidian; it allows a second source in the variant ghuṇṭa (CDIAL 4479).

Lesson 5 of 5.
بخار (bukhār) — a fever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a leg, then the Urdu for a knee.

You'll want to know: بخار.
بخار — bukhār — "a fever".

The word, taken apart.
بخار (bukhār) is a loan from Arabic bukhār, "vapour, steam", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is b-kh-r (Lane). In Urdu it came to mean "fever".
