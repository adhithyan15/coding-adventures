# Donnerstag — "Thursday"

build.

## You'll want to know: Donnerstag
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-DONNERSTAG-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-TAG-02, GE-LEX-DONNER-01] -->

> **Donnerstag** — "Thursday."

*Donner* plus *-s-* plus *Tag*. The little *s* in the middle is a joining sound
German drops between the halves of a long compound; it carries no meaning of its
own.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-ETYMON-DONNERSTAG-THOR-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DONNERSTAG-01] -->

The thunder-god was **Donar** in the German-speaking south and **Thor** in the
Norse north — one god, two names, and *Donner* is his name still doing duty as
an ordinary weather word.

English named the day for the Norse form: **Thurs**day is *Thor's day*.

He stood in for **Jupiter**, who was the Roman sky-and-thunder king. So this one
slot has been filled three times over — Jupiter, Donar, Thor — and every version
of it is the god who throws the lightning.

Say *Donnerstag* and *Thursday* back to back and the shift you already own does
the rest: German *d* where English has *th*.
