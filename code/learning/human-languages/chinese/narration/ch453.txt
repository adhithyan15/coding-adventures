Mandarin Chinese, chapter 453: Bags and Cases.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bēibāo — backpack; rucksack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for how dark the sky is getting, then the Mandarin for a paper napkin.

You'll want to know: bēibāo.
bēibāo — "backpack; rucksack".
wǒ de bēibāo bú jiàn le — My backpack has gone missing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bēibāo is built from bēi 'to carry on the back' and 包 'a bag', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pwojH and paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as the back of the body and the second 'to wrap'; the level-toned bēi 'to carry on the back' is irregular beside it.

You'll want to know: xínglixiāng.
xínglixiāng — "luggage case; a travel case".
wǒ de xínglixiāng fàng zài fángjiān lǐ — My suitcase is in the room.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xínglixiāng is built from xíngli 'luggage' and xiāng 'a box', all inherited from Middle Chinese, haeng, liX and sjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses lǐ as a fruit tree and gives xíngli itself under it, and glosses xiāng as a box or a basket.

Lesson 3 of 5.
sāi — to stuff in; to squeeze in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a backpack, then the Mandarin for a travel case.

You'll want to know: sāi.
sāi — "to stuff in; to squeeze in".
yīfu dōu sāi zài xiāngzi lǐ le — The clothes are all stuffed into the case.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
sāi is inherited from Middle Chinese sok (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'full', 'stopped up' and 'to separate'; today's level-toned sāi is irregular beside it. The dictionary lists a second reading, sojH, glossed as a frontier pass.

Lesson 5 of 5.
biāoqiān — label; luggage tag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to stuff into a bag, then the Mandarin for to carry in the hand.

You'll want to know: biāoqiān.
biāoqiān — "label; luggage tag".
měi ge xínglixiāng dōu yǒu yí ge biāoqiān — Every suitcase has a tag.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biāoqiān is built from biāo 'a mark' and qiān 'a tally', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjiew and tshjem (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to raise' and the second, after an older dictionary, 'to check'.
