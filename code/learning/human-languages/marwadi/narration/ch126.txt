Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 126: Coal, Dung cake, Sack, Rope, Peg.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कोयलो (koylo) — coal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for matches, then the Marwadi for wood.

You'll want to know: कोयलो.
कोयलो — koylo — "coal".

The word, taken apart.
कोयलो (koylo) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kokila, "lighted coal, charcoal". CDIAL lists Marwari koilo under it (CDIAL 3484). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word from Middle Indo-Aryan kōila, itself from Proto-Munda.

The word, taken apart.
बोरो (boro) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bora, "sacking, sack", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 9320); the match is through Hindi borā. CDIAL thinks it first meant cloth of goat's hair, and asks whether it goes back to Sanskrit bakura, a goatskin bag.

The word, taken apart.
रस्सी (rassī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from raśmi, "rope, bridle, ray of light". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 10648); the match is through Hindi rassī and Gujarati rasī. CDIAL marks the doubled ss forms as taken from Punjabi.
