# নাক — nose, read

[PAUSE 2s] Read **চোখ**. Then write **ক**.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-NAK-READ-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-NA-01, BN-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01, BN-SCRIPT-KA-01] -->

> **ন** + **া** + **ক** → **নাক**

**nāk** — *nose.* Every piece of this word came out of the chapter that taught
you to read the greeting, several thousand words ago. Nothing has been added
since, and nothing needed to be.

Two things about the last letter. **ক** ends the word, so its inherent **ô** is
gone — the mouth closes and stops, with no vowel after it. And the closure is
**clean**: no breath escapes, which is the whole difference between this letter
and the **খ** that ended **চোখ** a moment ago. Read the two words one after the
other and the contrast is unmissable:

> **চোখ** — ends in air
> **নাক** — ends in silence

That pair is the best short demonstration of aspiration this book can offer,
because both words are things you can point at on your own face.

Say it and mean it. **নাক** is a plain, unglamorous, everyday word, and being
able to read one of those off the page without help is a better measure of
progress than being able to read a beautiful one.
