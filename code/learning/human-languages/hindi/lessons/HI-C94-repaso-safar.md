# se kī tālikā — five jobs, one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name two things **से** has done before this chapter, and the word
that has now joined it.

## Grammar Lens: everything से does
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-04] -->

| job | example |
|---|---|
| from a place | स्टेशन से |
| by a means | ट्रेन से |
| measured against | बच्चे से बड़ा |
| the start of a span | सोमवार से |
| one option in a correction | बस से नहीं |

Five jobs and no change of form. **से never bends**, whatever it is doing and
whatever stands in front of it — unlike **का**, which had to become **के** the
moment another word followed.

The reason is worth keeping: **का** is a possessive and possessives agree,
while **से** is a plain postposition and has nothing to agree with.

## Grammar Lens: the partner, and where it stands
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-01, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-02, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-03] -->

| span | start | stop |
|---|---|---|
| days | सोमवार से | शुक्रवार तक |
| parts of a day | सुबह से | शाम तक |
| clock hours | दस बजे से | पाँच बजे तक |

**तक** can stand alone — **पाँच बजे तक** needs no **से**. **से** can stand
alone too. The pair is a choice, not a requirement.

## Grammar Lens: the one place two से are compulsory
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-05, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-04] -->

**बस से नहीं, ट्रेन से।**

English keeps one *by* and lets it cover both halves. Hindi gives each option
its own **से**, because each option is a complete phrase in its own right and
**नहीं** sits between two complete phrases rather than inside one.

Count the **से** when you read a correction. Two means a swap is being offered;
one means something else is going on.
