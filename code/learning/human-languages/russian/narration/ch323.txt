Russian, chapter 323: War.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
война (voyná) — a war.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a shield, then the Russian for a throne.

You'll want to know: война.
война (voyná) — "a war".
Война продолжалась пять лет. Voyná prodolzhálas pyat let. The war went on for five years.
Feminine; plural войны. На войне — in the war.

The word, taken apart.
война (voyná) is inherited through Old East Slavic voina from Proto-Slavic vojĭna "war", built on vojĭ "a soldier" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
сражение (srazhéniye) — a pitched battle, a battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a war, then the Russian for a battle.

You'll want to know: сражение.
сражение (srazhéniye) — "a pitched battle, a battle".
После сражения солдаты отдыхали. Pósle srazhéniya soldáty otdykháli. After the battle the soldiers rested.
Neuter. A little more formal than битва (bítva).

The word, taken apart.
сражение (srazhéniye) is built on сразиться "to fight", from с- and разить "to strike" (EtymDB glosses разить "to strike"); the з turns to ж in сражать, as in возить, вожу.

Lesson 4 of 5.
сражаться (srazhátsya) — to fight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a battle, then the Russian for a pitched battle.

You'll want to know: сражаться.
сражаться (srazhátsya) — "to fight".
Солдаты сражались всю ночь. Soldáty srazhális vsyu noch. The soldiers fought all night.
Imperfective; perfective сразиться (srazítsya). Сражаться с + instrumental — against; за + accusative — for.

The word, taken apart.
сражаться (srazhátsya) is built on с- and разить "to strike" (EtymDB), with the reflexive -ся: to strike at each other.

Lesson 5 of 5.
враг (vrag) — an enemy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pitched battle, then the Russian for to fight.

You'll want to know: враг.
враг (vrag) — "an enemy".
Враг подошёл к городу. Vrag podoshyól k górodu. The enemy approached the town.
Genitive врага. The opposite of друг.

The word, taken apart.
враг (vrag) is a Church Slavonic word, vragŭ "foe" (EtymDB), from Proto-Slavic vorgŭ "enemy"; the native Russian shape is the old ворог.
