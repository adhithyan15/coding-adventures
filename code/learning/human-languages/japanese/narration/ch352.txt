Japanese, chapter 352: Little Accidents.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こぼれる (koboreru) — to be spilled; to overflow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for whether someone is safe, then the Japanese for to be on the safe side.

You'll want to know: こぼれる.
こぼれる — koboreru — "to be spilled; to overflow".
みずが こぼれたら、ゆかが ぬれます。 — mizu ga koboretara, yuka ga nuremasu — "If the water spills, the floor gets wet."
Polite こぼれます, koboremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): ぎゅうにゅうが こぼれました。, gyūnyū ga koboremashita.

The word, taken apart.
こぼれる (koboreru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ひっくりかえる (hikkurikaeru) — to tip over; to turn over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be spilled, then the Japanese for to come undone.

You'll want to know: ひっくりかえる.
ひっくりかえる — hikkurikaeru — "to tip over; to turn over".
かびんが ひっくりかえったら、みずが こぼれます。 — kabin ga hikkurikaettara, mizu ga koboremasu — "If the vase tips over, the water spills."
Polite ひっくりかえります, hikkurikaerimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かびんが ひっくりかえりました。, kabin ga hikkurikaerimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ひっくりかえる (hikkurikaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: it is built on kaeru, "to turn over", the intransitive partner of kaesu, "to give back", with a native first part, hikkuri, and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
どうぐ (dōgu) — a tool; an instrument.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to tip over, then the Japanese for to slip out of place.

You'll want to know: どうぐ.
どうぐ — dōgu — "a tool; an instrument".
どうぐが こわれたら、かいしゃに れんらくして ください。 — dōgu ga kowaretara, kaisha ni renraku shite kudasai — "If a tool breaks, please let the company know."

The word, taken apart.
どうぐ (dōgu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are dō, "road-way" (Middle Chinese dawX) and gu, "tool" (gjuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
