Bengali, chapter 423: Bags, Wrappers and Sizes.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ঠোঙা (ṭhongā) — a paper bag; a paper cone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a customer (formal), then the Bengali for to test (by trying).

You'll want to know: ঠোঙা.
ঠোঙা — ṭhongā — "a paper bag; a paper cone".
মুদি একটা ঠোঙায় মুড়ি দিলেন। mudi ekṭā ṭhongāy muṛi dilen. The grocer gave me puffed rice in a paper bag.
Folded and pasted from old newspaper, the ঠোঙা (ṭhongā) holds roasted nuts, puffed rice and sweets.

The word, taken apart.
ঠোঙা (ṭhongā) is the paper bag of every Bengal shop; its origin is not settled.

মোড়ক — moṛôk — "a wrapper; packaging".
আমি উপহারের মোড়ক খুললাম। āmi uphārer moṛôk khullām. I opened the wrapping of the present.

মোড়ক (moṛôk) is built on মোড়া, "to wrap", which CDIAL sets beside mōṭati, "twists" (CDIAL 10186), with the noun ending -ক: the thing that wraps.

ক্যারিব্যাগ — kæribyāg — "a carrier bag; a plastic bag".
দোকানদার একটা ক্যারিব্যাগে জিনিসগুলো দিলেন। dokāndār ekṭā kæribyāge jinisgulo dilen. The shopkeeper gave me the things in a carrier bag.

The word, taken apart.
ক্যারিব্যাগ (kæribyāg) is borrowed from English carry bag, the Indian English name for a shop's plastic bag, written as one word; ব্যাগ inside it is already taught.

Lesson 5 of 6.
গছানো (gôchhāno) — to palm off; to foist (something on someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a carrier bag, then the Bengali for a size (of clothes or shoes).

You'll want to know: গছানো.
গছানো — gôchhāno — "to palm off; to foist (something on someone)".
দোকানদার আমাকে একটা পচা আম গছাল। dokāndār āmāke ekṭā pôchā ām gôchhālô. The shopkeeper palmed off a rotten mango on me.
Past (he/she …): তিনি পুরনো মাল গছালেন, tini purôno māl gôchhālen.
The person who is landed with it takes -কে: আমাকে গছাল, foisted it on me.

The word, taken apart.
গছানো (gôchhāno) probably belongs with Sanskrit gácchati, "goes, falls to the share of" (CDIAL 3955), under which CDIAL lists Bengali gachāna, "to take a deposit"; to palm off is to make a thing fall to someone else's share.

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.
