# One reflexive relation, held as a whole

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C212-WEL212-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Recall the known आत्मा, “the self” or “the soul.” Today that
known word helps you hear who an object points back to inside one sentence.

## You'll want to know: one complete attested sentence

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PATTERN-C273-REFLEXIVE-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **अहमेव स्वात्मानं न वेद्मि।**
>
> *ahameva svātmānaṃ na vedmi.*
>
> “I myself do not know my own self.”

This complete attested sentence appears in Central Sanskrit University's
[*Ādhunika Saṃskṛta Sāhitya Pustakālaya*](https://www.sanskrit.nic.in/ASSP/Vikramcharitam/6frame7.htm).
The lesson preserves the source sentence rather than silently composing a new
Sanskrit line from the English meaning.

## Grammar Lens: notice one relation

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C273-REFLEXIVE-01] -->

In this one whole line, the speaker is “I.” The form **स्वात्मानम्** is the
object and points back to the first-person subject: the speaker does not know
the speaker's own self.

Keep the complete sentence together. This lesson does not teach every
reflexive form, the middle voice, or a productive method for building new case
forms or sandhi combinations.

## Guided Practice

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C273-REFLEXIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Read the sentence once slowly. Then say it as one admission: “I
myself do not know my own self.”

## Wrap-up Recall

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PATTERN-C273-REFLEXIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Say the complete line once more. Keep the object-to-speaker relation
inside this attested sentence.
