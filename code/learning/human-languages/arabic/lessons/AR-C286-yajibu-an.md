# يجب أن (yajibu an) — must, should

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C286-SUBJUNCTIVE-01, AR-LEX-C221-AMAL221-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I want to go". (*Urīdu an adhhaba*.) Then the Arabic for
homework, a duty. (*Wājib*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Wanting is one reason to act. Having to is another, and a piece
of advice is a third.

## Grammar Lens: yajibu an, it is necessary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C286-YAJIBU-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C87-TIME87-03, AR-LEX-C283-MADI283-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C283-KANA-IMPERFECT-01] -->

**يجب** — *yajibu* — "it is necessary". It does not change for the person.
The person is in the verb after *an*, in the subjunctive:

> **يجب أن أذهب الآن.** — *yajibu an adhhaba al-ān* — I must go now.

So *yajibu an tadhhaba*, you must go; *yajibu an nadhhaba*, we must go. Put
*kāna* in front for "had to": *kāna yajibu an adhhaba*, I had to go.

*Yajibu* comes from the root of *wājib*, "a duty". The root's *w* drops in
the present, as it does in *yaṣilu*, he arrives, from *waṣala*.

## Grammar Lens: yanbaghī an, you ought to
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C286-YANBAGHI-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C54-HEALTH54-04, AR-LEX-C255-ACT255-02, AR-LEX-C161-ACT161-04] -->

**ينبغي** — *yanbaghī* — "it is right, it is fitting". It is softer than
*yajibu*: advice, not an order.

> **ينبغي أن تزور الطبيب.** — *yanbaghī an tazūra aṭ-ṭabīb* — You ought to
> visit the doctor.

*Yanbaghī an tastarīḥa*, you should rest. The verb after it behaves exactly as
after *yajibu*.
