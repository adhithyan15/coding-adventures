5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نائی (nāī) — a barber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a bill, then the Urdu for an expense.

You'll want to know: نائی.
نائی — nāī — "a barber".

The word, taken apart.
نائی (nāī) is a tadbhava, inherited and reshaped by sound change. CDIAL lists it under Sanskrit nāpita, "barber", and lists nāū, "barber", beside it under snāpaka, "bath attendant" (CDIAL 13792).

Lesson 2 of 5.
دھوبی (dhobī) — a washerman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for an expense, then the Urdu for a barber.

You'll want to know: دھوبی.
دھوبی — dhobī — "a washerman".

The word, taken apart.
دھوبی (dhobī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from dhauvati, "washes", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6886). CDIAL notes that the forms with -b- spread from here into every language around.

The word, taken apart.
موچی (mochī) comes from mocika, "tanner and cobbler", built on moca, "shoe", which CDIAL takes from an Iranian word, Pahlavi mōč, "shoe" (CDIAL 10349).

Lesson 4 of 5.
لوہار (lohār) — a blacksmith.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a washerman, then the Urdu for a cobbler.

You'll want to know: لوہار.
لوہار — lohār — "a blacksmith".

The word, taken apart.
لوہار (lohār) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lohakāra, "iron-worker" (CDIAL 11159). It is built from loha, "iron", and kāra, "maker".
