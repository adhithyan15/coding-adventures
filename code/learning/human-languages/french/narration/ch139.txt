French, chapter 139: Nothing, None, Neither, Which One, Anything at All.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rien — nothing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to drive, then the French for to get dressed.

You'll want to know: rien.
rien — "nothing". With a verb it takes ne in front: Je ne comprends rien. — "I understand nothing." On its own it answers a question: Rien.

aucun / aucune — "none, not a single". It agrees with its noun and also takes ne: Il n'y a aucun train lundi. — "There is no train at all on Monday."

Before the new one: say the French for nothing, then the French for none.

You'll want to know: non plus.
non plus — "neither, not either". The answer to a negative: Je ne travaille pas samedi. — Moi non plus. — "Me neither." After a yes, French says moi aussi instead.

You'll want to know: lequel / laquelle.
lequel / laquelle — "which one". A question that picks from a set, and it agrees with what is picked: Lequel? for a train, Laquelle? for a chambre.
