Mandarin Chinese, chapter 239: Healthy Habits.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jiànkāng — healthy; health.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the open sea, then the Mandarin for a rainbow.

You'll want to know: jiànkāng.
jiànkāng — "healthy; health".
tā měitiān pǎobù hěn jiànkāng — He runs every day and he's very fit.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiànkāng is built from jiàn 'strong' and kāng 'at ease', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjonH and khang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'in harmony' and 'happy'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
jièyān — to give up smoking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a rainbow, then the Mandarin for healthy.

You'll want to know: jièyān.
jièyān — "to give up smoking".
wèile shēntǐ wǒ bàba jièyān le — My dad gave up smoking for his health.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jièyān is built from jiè 'to guard against' and yān 'smoke'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese 'en (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'the breath of fire'; the dictionary glosses the first, keajH there, 'to be careful' and 'to warn'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
hējiǔ — to drink alcohol.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for healthy, then the Mandarin for to give up smoking.

You'll want to know: hējiǔ.
hējiǔ — "to drink alcohol".
tā bù xīyān yě bù hējiǔ — He doesn't smoke or drink.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hējiǔ is built from 喝 'to drink' and jiǔ 'alcohol', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xat and tsjuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'to shout', so how it came to mean drinking is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
hūxī — to breathe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give up smoking, then the Mandarin for to drink alcohol.

You'll want to know: hūxī.
hūxī — "to breathe".
tā gǎnmào le bù néng yòng bízi hūxī — He's got a cold and can't breathe through his nose.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hūxī is built from hū 'to breathe out' and xī 'to breathe in', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xu and xip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to call' and, after an older book, 'the outward breath', and the second 'the inward breath'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tǐjiǎn — medical check-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to drink alcohol, then the Mandarin for to breathe.

You'll want to know: tǐjiǎn.
tǐjiǎn — "medical check-up".
wǒ měi nián zuò yí cì tǐjiǎn — I have a check-up once a year.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: tǐ and jiǎn are both third tones, so the first rises and it sounds like tíjiǎn.

The word, taken apart.
tǐjiǎn is built from 体 'body' and jiǎn 'to check', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thejX and kjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).
