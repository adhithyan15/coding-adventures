# Unit 24: Possessive Adjectives

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say *tengo una casa*. Today's words let you say *whose* it is
without a whole *tener* sentence.

## New Material

**mi** — my ← Latin *meus*.
**tu** — your (informal) ← Latin *tuus* (note: no accent — this is
different from *tú*, "you," which Unit 2 taught *with* an accent; same
letters, different words, same accent-marks-a-distinction pattern from
Unit 13's question words).
**su** — his/her/your (formal)/their ← Latin *suus* — one word covering
four different English possessives, disambiguated only by context.
**nuestro** — our ← Latin *noster*.

## Grammar Lens

Like adjectives (Unit 18), possessives **agree with the noun they
describe, not with the owner** — *nuestro libro* (masc.) but *nuestra
casa* (fem.), regardless of who "we" are. English possessives ("my,"
"your," "our") never change form based on the thing owned. And *su* is
worth sitting with: one word does the job of English "his," "her,"
"your" (formal), and "their" all at once — Spanish leans on context to
disambiguate, the same way *cómo* trusted an accent mark instead of a
whole different word.

## Guided Practice

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: "mi casa" then "mi libro"]
- [YOU SAY: "nuestro libro" then "nuestra casa" — same owner, different
  agreement]
- [YOU SAY: "su casa" — could mean his, her, your, or their]
