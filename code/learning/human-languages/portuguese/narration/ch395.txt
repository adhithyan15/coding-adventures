Portuguese, chapter 395: Ganhos e perdas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ganhar — to earn; to win; to gain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to refund, then the Portuguese for the credit.

You'll want to know: ganhar.
ganhar — "to earn; to win; to gain".
Ela ganha mil e quinhentos euros por mês. She earns one thousand five hundred euros a month.
Ganhar a vida — to earn a living; ganhar um jogo — to win a game.

The word, taken apart.
ganhar is usually traced to a Germanic verb meaning "to graze, to hunt for food", through Vulgar Latin wadaniāre (EtymDB); French gagner and Italian guadagnare are its cousins.

Lesson 4 of 5.
falir — to go bankrupt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the profit, then the Portuguese for the loss.

You'll want to know: falir.
falir — "to go bankrupt".
A loja do meu tio faliu. My uncle's shop went bankrupt.
Defective verb: forms like faliu and falimos are common. A falência — bankruptcy.

The word, taken apart.
falir is usually traced to Italian fallire "to go bankrupt", which EtymDB derives from Latin fallere (EtymDB), "to deceive, to disappoint" (Lewis & Short).

endividado — "in debt".
Muitas famílias estão endividadas. Many families are in debt.
Built on dívida — debt.

The word, taken apart.
endividado is built from en- and dívida "debt", from Latin dēbita "things owed", on dēbēre "to owe" (Lewis & Short).
