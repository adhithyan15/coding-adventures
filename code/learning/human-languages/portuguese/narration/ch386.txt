Portuguese, chapter 386: As acusações.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o ladrão — the thief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for legal, then the Portuguese for illegal.

You'll want to know: o ladrão.
o ladrão — "the thief".
O ladrão fugiu pela janela. The thief escaped through the window.
Plural os ladrões; feminine a ladra.

The word, taken apart.
ladrão is inherited from Latin latrō, latrōnis "a hired servant, a mercenary", later "a robber" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
acusar — to accuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for illegal, then the Portuguese for the thief.

You'll want to know: acusar.
acusar — "to accuse".
Acusaram o vizinho de roubar a bicicleta. They accused the neighbour of stealing the bicycle.
Acusar alguém de + noun or infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
acusar is inherited from Latin accūsāre "to call to account, to accuse" (EtymDB), built from ad and causa "cause, lawsuit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
a acusação — the accusation, the charge; the prosecution.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the thief, then the Portuguese for to accuse.

You'll want to know: a acusação.
a acusação — "the accusation, the charge; the prosecution".
Ele negou a acusação. He denied the accusation.
In court, a acusação is also the prosecution.

The word, taken apart.
acusação is a learned borrowing from Latin accūsātiō "accusation, indictment" (EtymDB), from accūsāre, on causa "lawsuit" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
lícito — lawful, permissible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to accuse, then the Portuguese for the accusation.

You'll want to know: lícito.
lícito — "lawful, permissible".
Não é lícito usar fotografias de outras pessoas sem autorização. It is not lawful to use other people's photographs without permission.
Formal.

The word, taken apart.
lícito is a learned borrowing from Latin licitus "lawful, allowed" (EtymDB), from licēre "to be allowed" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ilícito — unlawful, illicit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the accusation, then the Portuguese for lawful.

You'll want to know: ilícito.
ilícito — "unlawful, illicit".
A polícia descobriu um negócio ilícito. The police uncovered an illicit business.
The opposite of lícito.

The word, taken apart.
ilícito is a learned borrowing from Latin illicitus "not allowed, unlawful", built from in- "not" and licitus "allowed", on licēre (Lewis & Short).
