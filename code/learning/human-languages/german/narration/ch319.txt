German, chapter 319: Kaiser und Könige.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Kaiser — the emperor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to defeat, then the German for the defeat.

You'll want to know: der Kaiser.
der Kaiser — "the emperor".
Der Kaiser lebte in einem großen Palast. The emperor lived in a big palace.
Plural: die Kaiser. Feminine die Kaiserin.

The word, taken apart.
Kaiser comes from Old High German keisur, Proto-Germanic kaisaraz "emperor" (EtymDB), a very early loan from the Latin title Caesar.

Lesson 2 of 5.
herrschen — to rule, to reign; to prevail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the defeat, then the German for the emperor.

You'll want to know: herrschen.
herrschen — "to rule, to reign; to prevail".
Der König herrschte vierzig Jahre. The king ruled for forty years.
Also of a mood or the weather: Es herrschte Ruhe — quiet prevailed.

The word, taken apart.
herrschen is inherited from Middle High German hērschen, Old High German hērisōn (EtymDB), built on hēr "noble, venerable" (EtymDB), whose comparative gave Herr "lord".

Lesson 3 of 5.
regieren — to govern.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the emperor, then the German for to rule.

You'll want to know: regieren.
regieren — "to govern".
Die Partei regierte zwölf Jahre lang. The party governed for twelve years.
Die Regierung, already known, is built on it.

The word, taken apart.
regieren comes from Middle High German regieren (EtymDB), a borrowing usually traced through Old French to Latin regere "to guide, to rule" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
erobern — to conquer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to rule, then the German for to govern.

You'll want to know: erobern.
erobern — "to conquer".
Die Soldaten eroberten die Burg. The soldiers conquered the castle.
Die Eroberung — the conquest. Also of hearts: ein Herz erobern.

The word, taken apart.
erobern comes from Middle High German geoberen, Old High German gaobarōn (EtymDB), built on ober "upper": to get the upper hand.
