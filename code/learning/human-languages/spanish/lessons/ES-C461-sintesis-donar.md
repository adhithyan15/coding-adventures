# (la campaña de donación) — read it, then do it yourself

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say **donar** and **rechazar**. The notice below does the first and
warns about the second.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-01, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-03] -->

A poster in a stairwell. Read it, then answer.

> **RECOGIDA DE JUGUETES**

> Puede **donar** juguetes en buen estado hasta el día 30.

> Se **rechazan** los que estén rotos o sean **peligrosos** para menores de
> tres años.

> Entréguelos en portería. Gracias por colaborar.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: what is being collected, and until when]
- [YOU SAY: the two reasons something would be turned away]
- [YOU SAY: where to hand things in]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-03, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02] -->

**Se rechazan los que estén rotos o sean peligrosos** is the sentence to take
apart, and the subjunctive is doing the same job it did on the swimming-course
notice.

*Los que **estén** rotos*, not *están*. The poster is not saying that some of
the toys are broken; it is describing a **category that may or may not turn
up** — whichever ones happen to be broken. The indicative would point at
particular toys the writer had in mind, which on a poster addressed to a whole
building would be an odd thing to imply.

Note **sean** as well: *ser* for a lasting property, because a toy that is
dangerous for a toddler is dangerous by design, not by circumstance. And
*estén* for *rotos*, because being broken is a state a thing arrived at. One
sentence, both verbs, each right for its own reason.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-01, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-02, ES-LEX-C461-DONAR-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines for a
collection poster: say what can be donated and until when, say what will be
turned away and why, and say where to hand it in.

Then check yourself: did you use the subjunctive for the things that might turn
up, and *ser* against *estar* for the two reasons?
