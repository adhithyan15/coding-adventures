Lesson 1 of 6.
正月 (zhēngyuè) — first lunar month.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to keep the New Year vigil, then the Mandarin for to pay a New Year call.

You'll want to know: 正月.
正月 — zhēngyuè — "first lunar month".
正月里大家都很开心 zhēngyuè lǐ dàjiā dōu hěn kāixīn — In the first month everyone is cheerful.
正 is said zhēng here, with a level tone, not the zhèng of 正在.

The word, taken apart.
正月 (zhēngyuè) is built from 正 and 月 'a month', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyeng and ngjwot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary gives the first its level-tone reading tsyeng for the start of the calendar year, apart from tsyengH, 'upright': so zhēng here, and zhèng in 正在.

You'll want to know: 红包.
红包 — hóngbāo — "red envelope".
过年的时候外公给了我一个红包 guònián de shíhou wàigōng gěi le wǒ yí ge hóngbāo — At the new year my grandpa gave me a red envelope.
The new character, in pieces: 红 (hóng) — six strokes: on the left the silk piece of 给, three strokes; on the right 工. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
红包 (hóngbāo) is built from 红 (hóng) 'red' and 包 'a packet', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huwng and paew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a colour' and the second 'to wrap'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
红 (hóng) — the silk piece, then 工.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the silk piece, then 工 and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — gěi, gōng]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the silk piece, then 工.
红 (hóng).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, three strokes, as on the left of 给: a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, a second one like it below, then a short stroke rising to the right.
The right piece, 工, three strokes. A short bar, a vertical down from its middle, then a longer bar along the foot.
3 + 3 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 红 (hóng), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 红 (hóng)? (Six.) Where have you met the left piece? (On the left of 给.) What is the right piece? (工.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
tiē — to stick; to paste up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the first lunar month, then the Mandarin for a red envelope of gift money.

You'll want to know: tiē.
tiē — "to stick; to paste up".
wǒ zài ménkǒu tiē le yì zhāng hóng zhǐ — I stuck a sheet of red paper up by the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiē is inherited from Middle Chinese thep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it 'to pawn something for money'. The sense 'to stick on' is not given there, and how it came is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.
chūnlián — Spring Festival couplets.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a red envelope of gift money, then the Mandarin for to paste up on a door.

You'll want to know: chūnlián.
chūnlián — "Spring Festival couplets".
Chúxī de xiàwǔ wǒmen zài ménkǒu tiē le chūnlián — On the afternoon of the last day of the year we put up the couplets by the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chūnlián is built from 春 'spring' and lián 'to join'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhwin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), the first of the four seasons. The dictionary lists the second only under an older spelling of the character, ljen, which it glosses 'unbroken'.
