Mandarin Chinese, chapter 142: More Housework.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tuōdì — to mop the floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take a walk, then the Mandarin for to go fishing.

You'll want to know: tuōdì.
tuōdì — "to mop the floor".
wǒ měitiān tuōdì — I mop the floor every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuōdì is built from tuō 'to drag' and dì 'ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tha and dijH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to drag' under an older way of writing it, and notes that it is popularly written the modern way; tha is also the old form of the word for he, a different word.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Lesson 4 of 5.
zhěnglǐ — to sort out; to put in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for rubbish, then the Mandarin for to dry in the sun.

You'll want to know: zhěnglǐ.
zhěnglǐ — "to sort out; to put in order".
wǒ zhěnglǐ le wǒ de shū — I've sorted out my books.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones: zhěnglǐ is said zhénglǐ, the first rising.

The word, taken apart.
zhěnglǐ is built from zhěng 'straight, even' and lǐ 'to put in order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyengX and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary quotes an older book for the second: to work jade.
