Gujarati, chapter 158: Sweeping and wiping.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
સાફ કરવું (sāph karvũ) — to clean up, to clean.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for curtain, then the Gujarati for shelf.

You'll want to know: સાફ કરવું.
સાફ કરવું — sāph karvũ — "to clean up, to clean".
અમે રોજ ઘર સાફ કરીએ છીએ. — ame roj ghar sāph karīe chīe — "We clean the house every day."

The word, taken apart.
સાફ કરવું (sāph karvũ) is built from sāph, "clean", and the inherited karvũ, "to do". Sāph is a loan from Persian sāf, "clear, pure" (EtymDB), which comes from Arabic; its Arabic root is ṣ-f-w, "to be clear" (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 6.
વાળવું (vāḷvũ) — to sweep, to fold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for shelf, then the Gujarati for to clean up.

You'll want to know: વાળવું.
વાળવું — vāḷvũ — "to sweep, to fold".
તે સાવરણીથી કચરો વાળે છે. — te sāvarṇīthī kacro vāḷe che — "She sweeps the rubbish with a broom."
It also means to fold or to turn.

The word, taken apart.
વાળવું (vāḷvũ) is inherited, but the sweeping sense is not settled. CDIAL lists Gujarati vāḷvũ, "to fold, twist, turn", under Sanskrit valati, "turns", and does not list the sense of sweeping (CDIAL 11405).

Lesson 3 of 6.
લૂછવું (lūchvũ) — to wipe, to dry off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to clean up, then the Gujarati for to sweep.

You'll want to know: લૂછવું.
લૂછવું — lūchvũ — "to wipe, to dry off".
હું ટુવાલથી હાથ લૂછું છું. — hũ ṭuvālthī hāth lūchũ chũ — "I wipe my hands with a towel."

The word, taken apart.
The origin of લૂછવું (lūchvũ) is not settled. CDIAL lists no Gujarati verb of this form; it lists the similar pũchvũ, "to wipe", under Sanskrit proñchati, "wipes off", without linking the two (CDIAL 9011).

Lesson 4 of 6.
પોતું (potũ) — floor cloth, mop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to sweep, then the Gujarati for to wipe.

You'll want to know: પોતું.
પોતું — potũ — "floor cloth, mop".
તે સવારે પોતું કરે છે. — te savāre potũ kare che — "He mops the floor in the morning."
પોતું કરવું, potũ karvũ, to mop the floor.

The word, taken apart.
પોતું (potũ) is a tadbhava. It comes from pōtta, "cloth", a reconstructed by-form of Sanskrit pōta, "cloth", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati word "rag" (CDIAL 8400).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ડાઘ (ḍāgh) — stain, spot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to wipe, then the Gujarati for floor cloth.

You'll want to know: ડાઘ.
ડાઘ — ḍāgh — "stain, spot".
આ ડાઘ નથી જતો. — ā ḍāgh nathī jato — "This stain does not come out."

The word, taken apart.
ડાઘ (ḍāgh) is a loan, usually traced to Persian dāgh, "mark, brand, stain". CDIAL, which follows inherited words, lists no Gujarati form.
