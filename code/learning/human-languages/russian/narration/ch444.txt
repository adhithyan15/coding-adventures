Russian, chapter 444: The old town.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ратуша (rátusha) — a town hall (in an old town).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a suburb, then the Russian for a roadway.

You'll want to know: ратуша.
ратуша (rátusha) — "a town hall (in an old town)".
На площади стоит старая ратуша. Na plóshchadi stoít stáraya rátusha. The old town hall stands on the square.
Feminine. Said of old town halls in central Europe and the Baltic.

The word, taken apart.
ратуша (rátusha) came through Old East Slavic ratuša from Polish ratusz (EtymDB), a cousin of German Rathaus 'a council house'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
собор (sobór) — a cathedral; a council.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a roadway, then the Russian for a town hall.

You'll want to know: собор.
собор (sobór) — "a cathedral; a council".
Рядом с ратушей — большой собор. Ryádom s rátushey — bolshóy sobór. Next to the town hall there is a large cathedral.
Masculine. A big main church; also an assembly.

The word, taken apart.
собор (sobór) came from Church Slavonic sŭborŭ 'a meeting, a council' (EtymDB), which is inherited from Proto-Slavic sŭborŭ 'a gathering', a noun usually linked with собирать 'to gather': first the gathering of the faithful, then their great church.

Lesson 3 of 5.
колокольня (kolokólnya) — a bell tower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a town hall, then the Russian for a cathedral.

You'll want to know: колокольня.
колокольня (kolokólnya) — "a bell tower".
С колокольни виден весь город. S kolokólni víden ves górod. The whole town can be seen from the bell tower.
Feminine. Built on колокол — a bell.

The word, taken apart.
колокольня (kolokólnya) is built from колокол 'a bell' and -ня, an ending for places (EtymDB). Колокол is inherited from Proto-Slavic kolkolŭ 'a bell' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
крепость (krépost) — a fortress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cathedral, then the Russian for a bell tower.

You'll want to know: крепость.
крепость (krépost) — "a fortress".
На холме стоит старая крепость. Na kholmé stoít stáraya krépost. An old fortress stands on the hill.
Feminine. Also strength: крепость (krépost) чая — the strength of tea.

The word, taken apart.
крепость (krépost) is inherited through Old East Slavic krěpostĭ 'strength' from Proto-Slavic krěpostĭ (EtymDB), on the root of крепкий 'strong': first strength, then a strong place.

Lesson 5 of 5.
мэрия (mériya) — a city hall, a mayor's office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bell tower, then the Russian for a fortress.

You'll want to know: мэрия.
мэрия (mériya) — "a city hall, a mayor's office".
Документы можно получить в мэрии. Dokuménty mózhno poluchít v mérii. You can get the documents at the city hall.
Feminine. Мэр — a mayor.

The word, taken apart.
мэрия (mériya) was borrowed from French mairie 'a mayor's office' (EtymDB), built on maire 'a mayor'.
