French, chapter 355: Quitter son travail.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
licencier — to dismiss, to lay off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to apply, then the French for the hiring.

You'll want to know: licencier.
licencier — "to dismiss, to lay off".
L'usine a licencié cinquante ouvriers. The factory laid off fifty workers.
Formal; in speech people also say renvoyer.

The word, taken apart.
licencier is built on licence "leave, permission", from Latin licentia "freedom, licence" (Lewis & Short), from licēre "to be allowed": first, to give someone leave to go.

You'll want to know: la démission.
la démission — "the resignation".

The word, taken apart.
démission is a learned borrowing from Latin dēmissiō "a letting down, a lowering" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from dēmittere "to send down", built on mittere "to send".

Lesson 5 of 5.
le préavis — the notice (period).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the resignation, then the French for to resign.

You'll want to know: le préavis.
le préavis — "the notice (period)".
Je dois faire un mois de préavis avant de partir. I have to work a month of notice before leaving.
Sans préavis — without notice.

The word, taken apart.
préavis is built from pré- "before" and avis "opinion, notice". avis comes from Old French avis, built on vis "sight, view" (EtymDB), from Latin vīsus "a look, a sight", from vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).
