## You'll want to know: niánqīng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C94-NIANQING-01]; assesses=[] -->

*niánqīng* — "young".

> *wǒ de lǎoshī hěn niánqīng* — My teacher is very young.

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*niánqīng* is built from nián 'year', the 年 you write, and qīng 'light', not heavy: light in years. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *nen* and *khjieng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), *khjieng* glossed 'light in weight'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C35-WORK35-02] -->

*niánqīng* is said of grown people; a child is *xiǎo*. *lǎo*, old, is its opposite.
