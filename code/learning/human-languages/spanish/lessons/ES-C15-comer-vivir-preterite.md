# viví — reuse one past pattern with an -ir verb

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VIVIR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-ORTHOGRAPHY-ER-IR-PRETERITE-FINAL-STRESS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You know present **vivo, vives, vive**, and you just learned past
**comí, comiste, comió**. Keep **viv-** and reuse those three past endings:
**viví, viviste, vivió**.

## Grammar Lens: one -ir singular past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VIVIR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-LEX-EN] -->

| person | present | completed past |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **vivo** | **viví** |
| tú | **vives** | **viviste** |
| él / ella / usted | **vive** | **vivió** |

Say **viví · viviste · vivió**. Reuse known **en** for a small contrast:

- **Vivo en Madrid.** — “I live in Madrid.”
- **Viví en Madrid.** — “I lived in Madrid.”
- **¿Viviste en Madrid?** — “Did you live in Madrid?”
- **Vivió en Madrid.** — “He, she, or you-formal lived in Madrid.”

The written accents in **viví** and **vivió** show final stress. **Viviste**
already has ordinary next-to-last stress and needs no mark.

## Grammar Lens: -er and -ir meet in this past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-CONVERGENCE]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRETERITE-SINGULAR, ES-ORTHOGRAPHY-ER-IR-PRETERITE-FINAL-STRESS] -->

| person | **comer** | **vivir** |
|---|---|---|
| yo | com**í** | viv**í** |
| tú | com**iste** | viv**iste** |
| él / ella / usted | com**ió** | viv**ió** |

The shared endings are **-í** in the first row, **-iste** in the second, and
**-ió** in the third. The presents differ, but these two singular past rows
converge on one ending set. That is a fact about the forms you have learned,
not permission to guess plural forms; those wait.

## The shared family, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ER-IR-PRETERITE-CONVERGENCE, ES-HISTORY-ER-IR-PRETERITE-LATIN-PERFECT, ES-ETYMON-VIVERE] -->

Spanish **vivir** continues Latin **vīvere**. Its modern past endings belong to
the same Romance history as the **comer** endings: Latin perfect material was
reshaped by sound change and analogy. The shared **-iste** is a useful family
resemblance, not proof of one simple letter-by-letter erosion.
