Portuguese, chapter 285: Quem ouve e quem lê.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
entrevistar — to interview.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the newspaper piece, then the Portuguese for the columnist.

You'll want to know: entrevistar.
entrevistar — "to interview".
A jornalista entrevistou o novo treinador. The journalist interviewed the new coach.
Built on entrevista.

The word, taken apart.
entrevistar is built on entrevista "interview", which is usually said to follow French entrevue (EtymDB has no source for it); vista goes back to Latin vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
o ouvinte — the listener.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to interview, then the Portuguese for the interviewee.

You'll want to know: o ouvinte.
o ouvinte — "the listener".
Um ouvinte telefonou para a rádio. A listener phoned the radio station.
The same form for a woman: a ouvinte.

The word, taken apart.
ouvinte is inherited from Latin audiēns "hearing, a hearer" (EtymDB), from audīre "to hear".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o telespectador — the TV viewer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the interviewee, then the Portuguese for the listener.

You'll want to know: o telespectador.
o telespectador — "the TV viewer".
Cada telespectador pode votar por telefone. Each viewer can vote by phone.
Built on tele- and espectador, ‘onlooker’.

The word, taken apart.
telespectador joins tele-, from Greek tēle "far off", and espectador, from Latin spectātor "an onlooker", from spectāre "to watch" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the listener, then the Portuguese for the TV viewer.

You'll want to know: o leitor.
o leitor — "the reader".
Um leitor escreveu uma carta ao jornal. A reader wrote a letter to the paper.
A woman is a leitora. Also a device: um leitor de CD.

The word, taken apart.
leitor comes from Latin lēctor "a reader" (EtymDB), from legere "to read".
