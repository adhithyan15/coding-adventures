Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 254: At the Healer's.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
वैद (vaid) — a traditional healer; a physician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to throb, then the Marwadi for to ache.

You'll want to know: वैद.
वैद — vaid — "a traditional healer; a physician".
गांव मांय एक वैद है। gā̃v mā̃y ek vaid hai. There is a healer in the village.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
वैद (vaid) goes back to Sanskrit vaidya, "learned; a physician" (Monier-Williams), with its last syllable worn away in speech.

Lesson 2 of 5.
गोळी (goḷī) — a pill; a tablet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to ache, then the Marwadi for a traditional healer.

You'll want to know: गोळी.
गोळी — goḷī — "a pill; a tablet".
म्हैं दिनूगै गोळी खाई। mhaĩ dinūgai goḷī khāī. I took a pill early in the morning.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गोळी (goḷī) is inherited from Sanskrit gola, "a ball". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Gujarati goḷī, "pill" (CDIAL 4321).

Lesson 3 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: सूजणो.
सूजणो — sūjṇo — "to swell up".
म्हारो पग सूज्यो। mhāro pag sūjyo. My foot swelled up.
Past (it …): वो गोडो सूज्यो, vo goḍo sūjyo.

The word, taken apart.
सूजणो (sūjṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit śūyate, "is swollen". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi sūjnā (CDIAL 12568).
