Mandarin Chinese, chapter 531: A Lucky Escape.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tuōxiǎn — to get out of danger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a prize you win, then the Mandarin for the jackpot.

You'll want to know: tuōxiǎn.
tuōxiǎn — "to get out of danger".
zhǐyào zhǎo dào lù wǒmen jiù néng tuōxiǎn — As long as we find the way, we'll get out of danger.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuōxiǎn is built from tuō and xiǎn 'danger', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thwat and xjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as bone with the flesh taken off, and the second 'dangerous' and 'hard'; the sense 'to slip free' is not given for the first.

Lesson 2 of 6.
jīhū — very nearly; all but.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the jackpot, then the Mandarin for to get out of danger.

You'll want to know: jīhū.
jīhū — "very nearly; all but".
rúguǒ xiàyǔ gōngyuán lǐ jīhū méiyǒu rén — If it rains, there's hardly anyone in the park.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīhū is built from jī and hū, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kj+j and hu (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first four times: kj+j, which gives jī, in a phrase meaning 'very nearly', and gj+j, glossed 'near'. It glosses the second as a grammar word; the level tone of hū is not the regular outcome of hu, which would give hú.

Lesson 3 of 6.
jiǎoxìng — lucky by a fluke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get out of danger, then the Mandarin for all but.

You'll want to know: jiǎoxìng.
jiǎoxìng — "lucky by a fluke".
wǒmen zhè cì méi chūshì zhǐ shì jiǎoxìng — Nothing happened to us this time, but it was only a fluke.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiǎoxìng is built from jiǎo and xìng, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kewX and heangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary reads the first character as written today ngew, the name of a far country, and lists the word itself, in an older spelling, under its second syllable; the first syllable of that spelling, kewX, regularly gives jiǎo.

Lesson 4 of 6.
búxìng — misfortune; unfortunate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for all but, then the Mandarin for lucky by a fluke.

You'll want to know: búxìng.
búxìng — "misfortune; unfortunate".
jíshǐ yùdào búxìng yě búyào fàngqì — Even if misfortune comes your way, don't give up.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: 不 is said bú before the falling tone of xìng, and the spelling shows it.

The word, taken apart.
búxìng is built from 不 'not' and xìng 'fortunate', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjut and heangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as having good fortune and escaping harm.

Lesson 5 of 6.
shīshǒu — to lose one's grip; to do by a slip of the hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for lucky by a fluke, then the Mandarin for a misfortune.

You'll want to know: shīshǒu.
shīshǒu — "to lose one's grip; to do by a slip of the hand".
yàoshi nǐ shīshǒu dǎpò le bēizi jiù zài mǎi yí ge — If your hand slips and you break a cup, buy another one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shīshǒu is built from shī 'to lose' and 手 'the hand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syit and syuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a mistake' and 'to let go': the hand lets it go.
