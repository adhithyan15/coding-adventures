German, chapter 442: Schnee in der Stadt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Glatteis — the black ice, the icy road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the covered market, then the German for the pillar.

You'll want to know: das Glatteis.
das Glatteis — "the black ice, the icy road".
Wegen Glatteis fahren heute keine Busse. There are no buses today because of the black ice.
No plural. Jemanden aufs Glatteis führen — to trick someone.

The word, taken apart.
Glatteis is built from glatt 'smooth', inherited from Proto-Germanic gladaz, and Eis 'ice', from Proto-Germanic īsą (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
räumen — to clear (snow, a room).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the pillar, then the German for the black ice.

You'll want to know: räumen.
räumen — "to clear (snow, a room)".
Am Morgen räumt der Nachbar den Schnee vor dem Haus. In the morning the neighbour clears the snow in front of the house.
Aufräumen — to tidy up.

The word, taken apart.
räumen is inherited from Old High German rūmen 'to make room' and Proto-Germanic rūmijaną, built on rūmaz 'roomy' (EtymDB). German Raum and English room are cousins.

Lesson 3 of 5.
streuen — to grit (a road); to scatter, to sprinkle.

You'll want to know: streuen.
streuen — "to grit (a road); to scatter, to sprinkle".
Die Stadt streut Salz auf die Straßen. The town spreads salt on the roads.
Gestreut — gritted. Zucker auf den Kuchen streuen — to sprinkle sugar on the cake.

The word, taken apart.
streuen is inherited from Middle High German ströuwen (EtymDB), and English strew is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: der Schneepflug.
der Schneepflug — "the snowplough (US: snowplow)".
In der Nacht fährt der Schneepflug durch unsere Straße. In the night the snowplough goes along our street.
Plural: die Schneepflüge.

The word, taken apart.
Schneepflug is built from Schnee 'snow' and Pflug 'plough', both inherited: Pflug comes from Middle High German pfluoc, a cousin of English plough (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
rutschen — to slip, to slide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to grit, then the German for the snowplough.

You'll want to know: rutschen.
rutschen — "to slip, to slide".
Auf dem Eis rutscht sie und fällt hin. She slips on the ice and falls over.
Perfekt with sein: ich bin gerutscht. Die Rutsche — the slide in a playground.

The word, taken apart.
rutschen is an old expressive verb for sliding (EtymDB lists 'to slide'); its origin is not settled.
