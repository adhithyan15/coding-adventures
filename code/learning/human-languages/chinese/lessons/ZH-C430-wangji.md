## You'll want to know: wàngjì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C430-SIGHTS430-05]; assesses=[] -->

*wàngjì* — "peak season; busy season".

> *wàngjì de shíhou jiǔdiàn dōu kèmǎn le* — In the peak season the hotels are all fully booked.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*wàngjì* is built from *wàng* 'thriving' and *jì* 'a season', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *hjwangH* and *kjwijH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a fine light and the second as the youngest of brothers; the senses 'thriving' and 'a season' are not given there.
