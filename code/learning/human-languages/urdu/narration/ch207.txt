5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
لونگ (laung) — a clove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for moonlight, then the Urdu for a tempest, a storm.

You'll want to know: لونگ.
لونگ — laung — "a clove".

The word, taken apart.
لونگ (laung) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lavaṅga, "clove" (CDIAL 10977).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: الائچی.
الائچی — ilāichī — "cardamom".

The word, taken apart.
الائچی (ilāichī) is usually linked with Sanskrit elā, "cardamom", a word CDIAL takes from Dravidian (CDIAL 2522). CDIAL lists no Hindi form under it, and the shape of ilāichī is not explained.

Lesson 4 of 5.
کالی مرچ (kālī mirch) — black pepper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for cardamom, then the Urdu for cinnamon.

You'll want to know: کالی مرچ.
کالی مرچ — kālī mirch — "black pepper".

The phrase, taken apart.
کالی مرچ (kālī mirch) is "black pepper". kālī, "black", comes from Sanskrit kāla, "black, dark-blue", which CDIAL takes from Dravidian (CDIAL 3083). mirch comes from maricca, a variant CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit marīca, "peppercorn" (CDIAL 9875).
