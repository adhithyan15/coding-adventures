German, chapter 285: Der Betrug.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
betrügen — to cheat, to deceive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the investigation, then the German for to arrest.

You'll want to know: betrügen.
betrügen — "to cheat, to deceive".
Der Verkäufer betrog viele alte Leute. The salesman cheated many old people.
Strong verb: betrog, hat betrogen. Also to be unfaithful: Er betrügt seine Frau.

The word, taken apart.
betrügen is inherited from Old High German bitriogan, which EtymDB traces to Proto-Germanic bidreuganą "to betray, to deceive".

Betrüger is built on betrügen "to cheat", from Proto-Germanic bidreuganą "to deceive" (EtymDB), with the ending -er for a person.

Lesson 5 of 6.
erpressen — to blackmail, to extort.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the con man, then the German for to forge.

You'll want to know: erpressen.
erpressen — "to blackmail, to extort".
Ein Unbekannter erpresste die Firma. A stranger blackmailed the company.
Built on pressen 'to press': to squeeze money out of someone. Die Erpressung — blackmail.

The word, taken apart.
erpressen is built on pressen "to press", Old High German pressōn (EtymDB), from Latin pressāre, built on premere "to press" (Lewis & Short).
