## You'll want to know: lā dùzi
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C240-SICK240-05]; assesses=[] -->

*lā dùzi* — "to have diarrhoea; to have an upset stomach".

> *tā zuótiān lā dùzi le* — He had an upset stomach yesterday.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*lā dùzi* is built from *lā* 'to pull' and *dùzi* 'belly', all three syllables inherited from Middle Chinese, *lop*, *duX* and *tsiX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to break'; how the phrase came to mean an upset stomach is not settled.
