# estava contente — how it felt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the last line of the journey you read: "I was pleased, and so
was my friend". (*Eu estava contente, e o meu amigo também.*)

[PAUSE 1s] That sentence worked for a reason. This lesson is about why.

## Grammar Lens: a feeling is scene, and it takes estar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-SENTIMENTO-ESTAVA-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C36-SENTIR-04, PT-LEX-C215-QUAL215-03, PT-GRAMMAR-SER-VS-ESTAR-02, PT-LEX-C112-ESCOLA112-05] -->

You already own the feelings: **contente**, **feliz**, **triste**,
**nervoso**, **preocupado**. A feeling of the moment takes **estar**, and in a
story the past it wants is the imperfeito, **estava**:

> *Primeiro estava nervoso. Depois do exame, estava contente.*
> — At first I was nervous. After the exam, I was pleased.

A feeling has no edge. It was going on, like the weather, so in a story it is
scene. Set it against **ser**, which chapter sixteen kept for what something
is by nature: *ela é feliz* is a happy person; *ela estava feliz* is how she
was that day.

And the adjective agrees with whoever felt it. *Nervoso* has four endings:
**nervoso**, **nervosa**, **nervosos**, **nervosas**. *Contente*, *feliz* and
*triste* have one form for a man or a woman, and add *-s* or *-es* for more:
**contentes**, **felizes**, **tristes**.

> *Estávamos contentes.* — We were pleased.
> *A minha mãe estava preocupada.* — My mother was worried.

## Grammar Lens: contente com
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-CONTENTE-COM-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C128-QUALIDADES128-03, PT-LEX-C91-VIAGEM91-03, PT-LEX-C187-QUAL187-03] -->

To say **what** you were pleased with, add **com**, "with":

> *Estava contente **com** a viagem.* — I was pleased with the journey.
> *Estou contente **com** o meu português.* — I'm pleased with my Portuguese.

*Preocupado com* works the same way: *estava preocupado com o exame*, worried
about the exam. Portuguese says "with" where English says "about".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**contente** comes from Latin *contentus*, "satisfied", the participle of
*continēre*, "to hold together", itself *con-* plus *tenēre*, "to hold"
(Lewis & Short, under *contineo*); EtymDB 2.1 traces it only as far as Old
Portuguese *contente*. To be content is to hold yourself in, wanting nothing
further. That *tenēre* is the one inside **ter** and **tinha**, and English
kept both senses of the word: the **content** of a box, and a **content**
person.
