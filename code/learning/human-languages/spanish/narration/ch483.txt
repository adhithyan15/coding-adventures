Lesson 1 of 5.
la infancia — childhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for fast, then the Spanish for the variety.

You'll want to know: la infancia.
la infancia — "childhood".
Pasé la infancia en un pueblo. I spent my childhood in a village.

The word, taken apart.
infancia from Latin infantia, "inability to speak; early childhood" (EtymDB 2.1), from infans, "not speaking": in-, "not", and fari, "to speak" — a learned word. The young child was named as the one who cannot talk yet.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la costumbre — the custom, the habit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the variety, then the Spanish for childhood.

You'll want to know: la costumbre.
la costumbre — "the custom, the habit".
Es costumbre comer tarde. It is the custom to eat late.
Tener la costumbre de — to be in the habit of.

The word, taken apart.
costumbre from Old Spanish costumne (EtymDB 2.1), from Latin consuetudo, consuetudinis, "custom, habit" (Lewis & Short) — inherited and much worn: the shortened ending became -mbre, as hominem, "man", became hombre.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la finalidad — the purpose, the aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for childhood, then the Spanish for the custom.

You'll want to know: la finalidad.
la finalidad — "the purpose, the aim".
La finalidad del curso es hablar más. The aim of the class is to speak more.
A formal word, common on forms and notices.

The word, taken apart.
finalidad is built from final, "final", with -idad. Final is Latin finalis, from finis, "an end": a purpose is the end you aim at.

Lesson 5 of 5.
acudir — to go, to come (to a place or to someone for help).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the purpose, then the Spanish for the improvement.

You'll want to know: acudir.
acudir — "to go, to come (to a place or to someone for help)".
Acuda a la oficina con su pasaporte. Come to the office with your passport.
Notices use it where speech would use ir or venir.

The word, taken apart.
acudir is usually explained as Old Spanish recudir, "to come back, to turn to", with its prefix changed to a-; recudir is Latin recutere, "to strike back, to make rebound" (Lewis & Short).
