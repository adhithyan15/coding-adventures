# लंबा — tall and long are the same word, and this one bends

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C80-EASE-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last chapter gave you four adjectives and **not one of them
changed its ending**. Say **आसान** and remember why it stayed put. This chapter
gives you three that do the opposite, and you can see it coming from the
spelling.

## You'll want to know: लंबा
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01]; assesses=[] -->

**लंबा** (*lambā*) — "tall," and also "long."

Hindi does not split those two. A person is *lambā*, a road is *lambā*, a film
is *lambā*. English keeps three words for one measurement and chooses between
them by what the thing is doing: **tall** if it stands up, **long** if it lies
down, **lengthy** if it is made of time. Hindi measures the distance and stops
there.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**लंबा** is Sanskrit **लम्ब** (*lamba*), "hanging down, drooping." The idea
began as *what dangles* — and a thing that dangles is measured by how far down
it reaches.

That root travelled. Latin **limbus** is a hanging border or hem, and English
took it twice:

| word | the hanging thing |
|---|---|
| **limb** | what hangs off the trunk |
| **limbo** | the *edge*, the hem of things |

So **लंबा** and English **limb** are the same old idea about hanging, arrived at
from opposite ends of a continent.

## Grammar Lens: this one ends in -आ, so it bends
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C82-BODY-01, HI-CONCEPT-C11-KAALAA-SAFED-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

Nothing new here — the shape you already learned, meeting a new word:

| shape | masculine singular | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| ends in **-आ** | लंबा | लंबी |
| ends in a consonant | आसान | आसान |

**लंबा** ends in **-आ**, so it behaves like **काला**: *lambā* for a man,
*lambī* for a woman, *lambe* for more than one man.

This is the whole reason the easy-and-difficult lesson and this one belong next
to each other. That one gave you four adjectives that never move, and every one
of them ended in a consonant. These three all end in **-आ**, and every one of
them moves. Nothing has to be memorised — look at the last letter.
