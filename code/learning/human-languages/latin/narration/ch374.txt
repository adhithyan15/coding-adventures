Latin, chapter 374: Harm and Help.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
noceō, nocēre — to harm, to hurt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to turn out well, then the Latin for to overflow.

You'll want to know: noceō, nocēre.
noceō, nocēre — "to harm, to hurt".
Fūmus oculīs nocet. Smoke harms the eyes.
noceō, nocēre, nocuī, nocitum + dative: tibi nocet, it harms you. English innocent (not harming) and nuisance come from its family.

The word, taken apart.
noceō is inherited from Proto-Italic nokeō (EtymDB), a causative of the root of nex "violent death": to make perish, then to harm. English innocent and nuisance come from its family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
prōsum, prōdesse — to be useful, to benefit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to overflow, then the Latin for to harm.

You'll want to know: prōsum, prōdesse.
prōsum, prōdesse — "to be useful, to benefit".
Haec medicīna tibi prōderit. This medicine will do you good.
prōsum, prōdesse, prōfuī + dative. It goes like sum, with prōd- before a vowel: prōdest, prōderit.

The word, taken apart.
prōsum is built from prō "for, on behalf of" and sum "to be" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with an old -d- kept before a vowel: prōdest.

obsum, obesse — "to be a hindrance, to do harm".
Nimia pecūnia saepe obest. Too much money often does harm.
obsum, obesse, obfuī + dative: the opposite of prōsum. Nōn obest, it does no harm.

The word, taken apart.
obsum is built from ob "against, in the way" and sum "to be" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to be in someone's way.

Lesson 4 of 5.
impediō, impedīre — to hinder, to hamper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be useful, then the Latin for to be a hindrance.

You'll want to know: impediō, impedīre.
impediō, impedīre — "to hinder, to hamper".
Nix iter nostrum impedīvit. Snow hampered our journey.
impediō, impedīre, impedīvī, impedītum. Built on pēs, pedis: to shackle the feet. Impediō quōminus + subjunctive, to keep from. English impede and impediment.

The word, taken apart.
impediō is built from in and pēs, pedis "a foot" (Lewis & Short): to catch someone by the feet. English impede and impediment come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
perturbō, perturbāre — to throw into confusion, to upset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be a hindrance, then the Latin for to hinder.

You'll want to know: perturbō, perturbāre.
perturbō, perturbāre — "to throw into confusion, to upset".
Nūntius malus mē perturbāvit. The bad news upset me.
perturbō, perturbāre, perturbāvī, perturbātum. Per-, thoroughly, with a verb for stirring up. English perturb.

The word, taken apart.
perturbō is built from per "thoroughly" and turbō "to stir up, to disturb" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), which belongs with turba "an uproar". English perturb comes from it.
