# युष्मान् (yuṣmān) — you, several people, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C262-ASMAN-01, SA-LEX-C259-YUSMAKAM-02, SA-GRAMMAR-C259-PLURAL-POSSESSORS-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] “Us, several people” is **अस्मान्** (*asmān*). “Your” to several
people is **युष्माकम्** (*yuṣmākam*). Keep the **yuṣm-** opening and make the
several addressees the object.

## You'll want to know: युष्मान्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C262-YUSMAN-02]; assesses=[] -->

**युष्मान्** (*yuṣmān*) means “you” when several familiar addressees are the
object.

> **सः युष्मान् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ yuṣmān paśyati.*
>
> **He sees you all.**

Whitney §491 lists **युष्मान्** as the independent accusative plural. Keep it
apart from **युष्माकम्**, “your / of you all”: the first is an object and the
second is a possessor.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **युष्मान्** comes from the second-person pronoun
paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit
Grammar* §491 records it as the independent accusative plural. This lesson
makes no deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
