# qui — "who," the word Latin barely changed

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-QUAND-04, FR-LEX-COMMENT-05, FR-GRAMMAR-ESTCEQUE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Five question words are yours — *où, quand, comment, combien,
pourquoi*. Every one of them asks about a circumstance. None of them can ask
about a **person**.

- [YOU RECALL: two chapters back, the pronoun that was Latin for *man* — *on*]

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-QUI-01]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-CEST-01, FR-LEX-VOICI-01, FR-GRAMMAR-ESTCEQUE-02, FR-LEX-MERE-03] -->

> **qui** — who.

> **Qui est-ce ?** — Who is it? · **Qui est là ?** — Who's there?

Latin **quis**, and almost nothing has happened to it. That is unusual: most
French words are half the length of their Latin parents — *aqua* to *eau*,
*calidus* to *chaud* — and this one lost a single consonant in two thousand
years.

The **qu-** is the same opening that starts *quand*, *quel* and *combien*'s
cousins, and English carries it in the borrowed words **query**, **question**
and **quest**.

One thing to watch: **qui** never elides. *Qui est* stays two clean words where
*que* would have become *qu'*.
