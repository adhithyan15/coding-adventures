Portuguese, chapter 493: Bisavós, bisnetos e adoção.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o bisavô — great-grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for stepson, then the Portuguese for relative.

You'll want to know: o bisavô.
o bisavô — "great-grandfather".
O meu bisavô nasceu numa aldeia perto do mar. My great-grandfather was born in a village near the sea.
A bisavó — great-grandmother; os bisavós — great-grandparents.

The word, taken apart.
bisavô is built from bis- "twice" and avô "grandfather", which goes back to Latin avus "grandfather" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
o órfão — orphan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for great-grandfather, then the Portuguese for great-grandson.

You'll want to know: o órfão.
o órfão — "orphan".
O rapaz ficou órfão muito novo e foi viver com os avós. The boy was orphaned very young and went to live with his grandparents.
Also an adjective: ficar órfão — to lose your parents. A girl is a órfã; the plural is órfãos.

The word, taken apart.
órfão is inherited from Late Latin orphanus "an orphan" (Lewis & Short), which was taken from Greek orphanós "without parents" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
adotar — to adopt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for great-grandson, then the Portuguese for orphan.

You'll want to know: adotar.
adotar — "to adopt".
Eles adotaram uma menina de três anos. They adopted a three-year-old girl.
Before AO90 spelled adoptar in Portugal.

The word, taken apart.
adotar goes back, through Old Portuguese adoutar, to Latin adoptāre "to choose for oneself, to adopt" (EtymDB), built on optāre "to choose".
