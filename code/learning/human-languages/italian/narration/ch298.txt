Italian, chapter 298: La banda.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la banda — the gang; the brass band.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to capture, then the Italian for to flee.

You'll want to know: la banda.
la banda — "the gang; the brass band".
Una banda ha rapinato tre negozi. A gang robbed three shops.
At a town festival, la banda is the brass band.

The word, taken apart.
banda "a band of people" is usually traced to Provençal banda "a troop", which EtymDB derives from a Frankish word binda "a bond, a link".

Lesson 2 of 5.
il complice — the accomplice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to flee, then the Italian for the gang.

You'll want to know: il complice.
il complice — "the accomplice".
Il rapinatore aveva un complice in macchina. The robber had an accomplice in the car.
Also an adjective: uno sguardo complice, a knowing look.

The word, taken apart.
complice is a learned borrowing from Late Latin complex, complicis "closely joined, an accomplice", from complicāre "to fold together" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: aggredire.
aggredire — "to attack, to assault".
Due uomini lo hanno aggredito di notte. Two men attacked him at night.
Present like capire: aggredisce.

The word, taken apart.
aggredire is a learned borrowing from Latin aggredī "to go at, to attack" (EtymDB), from ad and gradī "to step".

Lesson 5 of 5.
minacciare — to threaten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to attack, then the Italian for the attack.

You'll want to know: minacciare.
minacciare — "to threaten".
Lo hanno minacciato con un coltello. They threatened him with a knife.
Minacciare di + infinitive: minaccia di piovere, it looks like rain.

The word, taken apart.
minacciare is built on minaccia "threat", from Latin mināciae "threats", built on mināx "threatening" (Lewis & Short).
