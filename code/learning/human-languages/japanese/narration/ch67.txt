うちわ (uchiwa) — a hand fan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a flute, then the Japanese for a drum.

You'll want to know: うちわ.
うちわ — uchiwa — "a hand fan".

The word, taken apart.
うちわ (uchiwa) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. JMdict writes it with characters for "circle" and "fan", read as a whole, and marks it as usually written in kana. Its older origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
せんす (sensu) — a folding fan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a drum, then the Japanese for a hand fan.

You'll want to know: せんす.
せんす — sensu — "a folding fan".

The word, taken apart.
せんす (sensu) is Sino-Japanese, borrowed from Chinese: sen, "fan" (Middle Chinese syenH), and su, a noun ending written with the character for "child"; su is a later Chinese reading of that character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a folding fan, then the Japanese for charcoal; a corner.

You'll want to know: いど.
いど — ido — "a well".

The word, taken apart.
いど (ido) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: i, "well", and to, "door, opening", voiced to do, both kun'yomi of its kanji (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

You'll want to know: はま.
はま — hama — "a beach".

The word, taken apart.
はま (hama) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
