Sanskrit, chapter 67: Tired, Hungry, Thirsty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
निद्रा (nidrā) — sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an illness, then the Sanskrit for health.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — read: a few lines aloud as a passage, not as a list — let each line lead into the next]

You'll want to know: निद्रा.
निद्रा (nidrā) — "sleep". It comes at रात्रिः.

The word, taken apart.
Nidrā is built from ni, "down", and the root √drā, "to sleep", which MW compares with Latin dormiō. It lives on as Hindi nīd, "sleep" (CDIAL 7200).

What you've built.
What the night is for.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: क्षुधा.
क्षुधा (kṣudhā) — "hunger". मम क्षुधा अस्ति — "I am hungry".

The word, taken apart.
Kṣudhā is the later form of Vedic kṣudh, "hunger", built on the root √kṣudh, "to be hungry" (MW, CDIAL 3716).

Lesson 3 of 5.
पिपासा (pipāsā) — thirst.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sleep, then the Sanskrit for hunger.

You'll want to know: पिपासा.
पिपासा (pipāsā) — "thirst". पानीयम् answers it.

The word, taken apart.
Pipāsā is built on the desire form of the root √pā, "to drink": thirst is "wanting to drink" (MW, CDIAL 8199). It lives on as Hindi pyās, "thirst".

What you've built.
The need that water answers.

Lesson 4 of 5.
विश्रामः (viśrāmaḥ) — rest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hunger, then the Sanskrit for thirst.

You'll want to know: विश्रामः.
विश्रामः (viśrāmaḥ) — "rest", a pause from work or from a यात्रा.

The word, taken apart.
Viśrāma is built from vi and the root √śram, "to toil, be weary": rest is the release from weariness (MW, CDIAL 11956).

What you've built.
What a tired traveller wants.

Lesson 5 of 5.
सुखम् (sukham) — ease, happiness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for thirst, then the Sanskrit for rest.

You'll want to know: सुखम्.
सुखम् (sukham) — "ease", "happiness". सुखम् अस्ति — "all is well".

The word, taken apart.
Sukha first described a chariot running easily. MW reports the old reading su.
kha, "having a good axle-hole", and also suggests a Prakrit form of su-stha, "standing well"; the question is not settled (CDIAL 13451).

What you've built.
How things are after rest.
