cognātus, cognātī — "a blood relative".

The word, taken apart.
cognātus is built on nātus, "born" (Lewis & Short), older gnātus, with con-, "together": born into the same family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
avunculus, avunculī — a mother's brother, an uncle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tenant farmer, then the Latin for a blood relative.

You'll want to know: avunculus, avunculī.
avunculus, avunculī — "a mother's brother, an uncle".

The word, taken apart.
avunculus is a diminutive built on avus, "a grandfather" (Lewis & Short): "little grandfather", an old name for the mother's brother. English uncle comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
vēritās, vēritātis — truth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a mother's brother, then the Latin for a mother's sister.

You'll want to know: vēritās, vēritātis.
vēritās, vēritātis — "truth".

The word, taken apart.
vēritās is built on vērus, "true" (Lewis & Short), with -tās, an ending for a quality. vērus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European weh1ros, "true" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 5 of 5.
mendācium, mendāciī — a lie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a mother's sister, then the Latin for truth.

You'll want to know: mendācium, mendāciī.
mendācium, mendāciī — "a lie".

The word, taken apart.
mendācium is built on mendāx, "lying, a liar" (Lewis & Short). Where mendāx comes from is uncertain; a link with mendum, "a fault", is often suggested.
