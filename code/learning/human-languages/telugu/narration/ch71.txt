Telugu, chapter 71: The Wider Family.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
మామ (māma) — an uncle; a father-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did sālīḍu mean?

You'll want to know: మామ.
మామ (māma) — "an uncle; a father-in-law".
మామ (māma) is a mother's brother, and it is also a father-in-law. Telugu keeps one word for both because for a very long time they were often the same man.
That is the whole of it: a man's sister's daughter was the expected match for his son, so the uncle a child grew up with became the father-in-law they married into.
Asking after a మామ (māma)'s ఆరోగ్యం is the first thing said after నమస్కారం in a house you have married into.

What you've built.
One. And the reason Telugu needs fewer family words than you expect.

Lesson 2 of 5.
అత్త (atta) — an aunt; a mother-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did māma mean?

You'll want to know: అత్త.
అత్త (atta) — "an aunt; a mother-in-law".
అత్త (atta) is the మామ (māma)'s counterpart: a father's sister, and a mother-in-law.
The same reasoning folds them together. She is the woman a child calls అత్త (atta) while growing up and the woman they answer to after marrying, and one word covers both lives.
It is inherited and it is among the oldest words in the family. The neighbouring languages all keep something very close to it.

What you've built.
Two: మామ (māma) and అత్త (atta), and they usually arrive as a pair.

Lesson 3 of 5.
బావ (bāva) — a brother-in-law; a male cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did atta mean?

You'll want to know: బావ.
బావ (bāva) — "a brother-in-law; a male cousin".
బావ (bāva) is a sister's husband, a wife's brother, and the మామ (māma)'s son — the cousin you might have married.
So the word covers a bond that is close, level and a little teasing. Two men who call each other బావ (bāva) can joke in a way an అన్న and a తమ్ముడు cannot.
City Telugu now uses it for a friend, roughly where English says mate. That irritates some people and has stopped nobody.

What you've built.
Three. The first one standing on level ground with you.

Lesson 4 of 5.
మరదలు (maradalu) — a sister-in-law; a female cousin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did bāva mean?

You'll want to know: మరదలు.
మరదలు (maradalu) — "a sister-in-law; a female cousin".
మరదలు (maradalu) is the బావ (bāva)'s counterpart on the other side: a wife's younger sister, a younger brother's wife, and the మామ (māma)'s daughter — the cousin you might have married.
So it carries the same easy, teasing register that బావ (bāva) does. A బావ (bāva) and a మరదలు (maradalu) can joke across a room in a way an అన్న and a చెల్లి cannot, and Telugu films have lived off that for eighty years.
It is inherited, and it belongs to the same reckoning as the rest of this chapter: the person a marriage hands you was very often somebody the family already had a word for.

What you've built.
Four. The one who matches బావ (bāva).

Lesson 5 of 5.
అల్లుడు (alluḍu) — a son-in-law.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did maradalu mean?

You'll want to know: అల్లుడు.
అల్లుడు (alluḍu) — "a son-in-law".
అల్లుడు (alluḍu) is a daughter's husband — the మామ (māma)'s word for the young man on the far side of the match.
He is treated as a guest for life. An అల్లుడు (alluḍu) on a visit is fed first, seated first and asked after first, and the household's own people wait their turn.
Which is what the chapter has been building to: మామ (māma), అత్త (atta), బావ (bāva), మరదలు (maradalu), అల్లుడు (alluḍu). Ask each of them how they are and you have done the whole visit properly.

What you've built.
Five: మామ (māma), అత్త (atta), బావ (bāva), మరదలు (maradalu), అల్లుడు (alluḍu). A marriage's worth of new people to ask after.
