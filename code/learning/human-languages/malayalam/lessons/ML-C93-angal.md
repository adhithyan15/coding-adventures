# പുസ്തകങ്ങൾ (pustakaṅṅaḷ) — books

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C93-PLURAL-01, ML-LEX-C46-ASK-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *a book*. Say what its last letter is. Then make *a child* into
*children*.

## Grammar Lens: a noun ending in ം trades it away
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C93-ANGAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

**പുസ്തകം** does not become *pustakaṁkaḷ*. The **ം** comes off and **ങ്ങൾ**
goes on in its place:

| one | more than one |
|---|---|
| **പുസ്തകം** *pustakaṁ* — a book | **പുസ്തകങ്ങൾ** *pustakaṅṅaḷ* — books |
| **മരം** *maraṁ* — a tree | **മരങ്ങൾ** *maraṅṅaḷ* — trees |

The money lesson said that a word ending in **ം** tells you in advance how it
will behave. **This is one of the things it was telling you.** You do not have
to remember which nouns take which ending — you can see it on the page, in the
last letter.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C93-ANGAL-01, ML-LEX-NII-NINGAL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C02-RESPECT-BY-PLURAL-01] -->

Now look at a word you have had since your first pages.

> **നിങ്ങൾ** *niṅṅaḷ* — you.

**Read the end of it.** That is the ending you learned a moment ago, sitting
where it has always sat. The polite *you* was introduced to you as the plural
*you* used for one person — and this is what made it plural.

Nothing you were taught then was wrong, and nothing has to be unlearned. **A
piece of the language you had been pronouncing for months turns out to have a
name and a job**, and you can now put it on nouns of your own.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C93-ANGAL-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C93-PLURAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What happens to the **ം** when the plural arrives? (**It is traded

ം**.) Where else have you been saying that ending? (**On the end of the
respectful *you***.)
