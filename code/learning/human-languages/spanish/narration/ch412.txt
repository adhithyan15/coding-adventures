Spanish, chapter 412: Gym and Sport.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
el gimnasio — the long form behind gym.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
English shortens gymnasium to gym. Spanish keeps more of the old word: el gimnasio.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish gimnasio came through Latin gymnasium from Greek gymnásion, a place for exercise and training. English gymnasium is the same travelled word, and gym is simply its clipped form. Spanish spelling turns the learned gy- into gi-, pronounced with the strong Spanish g sound heard before i.

Grammar Lens: naming the place.
The noun is masculine: el gimnasio, un gimnasio. Opening-hours signs often place the name first: Gimnasio Atlas — abierto de 7 a 23 h.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: un gimnasio]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: el gimnasio está abierto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Gym? (El gimnasio.)

Lesson 2 of 4.
el deporte — recreation that became sport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall the place for exercise: el gimnasio. What you may do there is deporte, sport.

The word, taken apart.
Deporte belongs to an older deportar family that once meant recreation or amusement. English sport hides a useful cousin: it is the shortened form of disport, which came through French from the same Latin deportāre family. The histories narrowed toward organised physical activity.

Grammar Lens: hacer deporte.
Spanish commonly says hacer deporte, literally “to do sport,” for taking exercise. No article is needed in that general expression. Compare el deporte es importante, where the noun is the subject and keeps el.
