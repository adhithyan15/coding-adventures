Japanese, chapter 498: A Day Off.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きゅうじつ (kyūjitsu) — a day off; a holiday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to roll into a ball, then the Japanese for a side dish (eaten with rice).

You'll want to know: きゅうじつ.
きゅうじつ — kyūjitsu — "a day off; a holiday".
きゅうじつは うちで やすみます。 — kyūjitsu wa uchi de yasumimasu — "On my days off I rest at home."

The word, taken apart.
きゅうじつ (kyūjitsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kyū, "rest" (Middle Chinese xjuw) and jitsu, "day" (nyit) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a side dish (eaten with rice), then the Japanese for a day off.

You'll want to know: ふゆやすみ.
ふゆやすみ — fuyuyasumi — "the winter holidays".
ふゆやすみに そふの うちへ いきます。 — fuyuyasumi ni sofu no uchi e ikimasu — "In the winter holidays I go to my grandfather's house."

The word, taken apart.
ふゆやすみ (fuyuyasumi) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built from fuyu, "winter" and yasumi, "rest" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ゆうきゅう (yūkyū) — paid leave; a paid holiday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a nap, then the Japanese for nodding off.

You'll want to know: ゆうきゅう.
ゆうきゅう — yūkyū — "paid leave; a paid holiday".
なつの ゆうきゅうに りょこうします。 — natsu no yūkyū ni ryokō shimasu — "I go travelling on my summer paid leave."

The word, taken apart.
ゆうきゅう (yūkyū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yū, "have" (Middle Chinese hjuwX) and kyū, "salary" (kip); JMdict gives "paid" and "paid leave" among its senses and marks it as a shortened word (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
