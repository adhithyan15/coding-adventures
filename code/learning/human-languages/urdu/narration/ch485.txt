Urdu, chapter 485: Childhood.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
بچپن (bachpan) — childhood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to feed, then the Urdu for to give (someone) a drink.

You'll want to know: بچپن.
بچپن — bachpan — "childhood".
بچپن میں میں روز پارک جاتا تھا۔ bachpan meṅ maiṅ roz pārk jātā thā. As a child I went to the park every day.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
بچپن (bachpan) is built on بچہ, "a child", usually traced to Persian bachcha, with the Indo-Aryan ending -pan, "-hood".

Lesson 2 of 5.
شرارت (sharārat) — mischief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to give (someone) a drink, then the Urdu for childhood.

You'll want to know: شرارت.
شرارت — sharārat — "mischief".
بچے کلاس میں شرارت کر رہے تھے۔ bachche klās meṅ sharārat kar rahe the. The children were up to mischief in class.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
شرارت (sharārat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic sharāra, "wickedness"; in Urdu it is mostly a child's mischief.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for mischief, then the Urdu for lisping.

You'll want to know: پلنا.
پلنا — palnā — "to be brought up".
وہ دادی کے گھر میں پلی۔ voh dādī ke ghar meṅ palī. She was brought up in her grandmother's house.
Past (he …): وہ نانی کے پاس پلا, voh nānī ke pās palā.

The word, taken apart.
پلنا (palnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin palnā, "to be reared", under Sanskrit pāláyati, "protects" (CDIAL 8129); پالنا, "to bring up", is its partner.

Lesson 5 of 5.
پہنانا (pahnānā) — to dress (someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for lisping, then the Urdu for to be brought up.

You'll want to know: پہنانا.
پہنانا — pahnānā — "to dress (someone)".
ماں نے بیٹی کو نیا کرتا پہنایا۔ māṅ ne beṭī ko nayā kurtā pahnāyā. The mother dressed her daughter in a new kurta.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بچے کو جوتا پہنایا, us ne bachche ko jūtā pahnāyā.

The word, taken apart.
پہنانا (pahnānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin pahnānā, "to clothe", under pinahati, "ties on" (CDIAL 8198); پہننا, "to wear", is its partner.
