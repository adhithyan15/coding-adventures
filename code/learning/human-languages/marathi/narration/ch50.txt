Marathi, chapter 50: Small, and the Words That Never Change.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
लहान (lahān) — small.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last chapter gave you मोठा and the two rules it obeys. This word is its opposite and it breaks one of them.
[your turn — recall: say moṭhaṁ ghar, then mājhaṁ ghar moṭhaṁ āhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: लहान.
लहान — lahān — small, little; young.
लहान घर, लहान खोली, लहान मित्र — lahān ghar, lahān khoolī, lahān mitra.
Three genders, one form. लहान (lahān) does not end in -आ, so it has nothing to change, and it stays exactly as it is in front of any noun at all. Compare it with मोठा, which moved three ways one lesson ago, and the contrast tells you which kind of adjective you are holding.
It also does the work English splits between small and young: लहान (lahān) भाऊ is a younger brother, not a short one. Marathi treats age and size as the same scale for people.
Its history is a good one. Sanskrit लघु (laghu) meant "light, slight, small" — the same ancient word as Latin levis and English light. The Marathi form wore लघु down to लहान (lahān) over two thousand years, and the meaning barely moved.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: lahān ghar, lahān khoolī, lahān mitra — nothing moved]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: moṭhaṁ ghar, moṭhī khoolī — and hear that something did]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say म्हणणे and report that your room is small]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]
Put lahān on all three genders, then moṭhā on all three, and name the difference.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many forms does लहान (lahān) have? (One.) What does लहान (lahān) भाऊ mean? (A younger brother.)

Lesson 2 of 4.
The adjectives that never change — the adjectives that never agree — the class a learner who has met only मोठा will over-apply endings to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two adjectives, two behaviours. That is not an accident about those two words.

Grammar Lens: only the -आ adjectives agree.
Marathi's adjectives fall into two classes, and you can tell which class a word is in by looking at its last letter.
An adjective ending in -आ agrees: मोठा, मोठी, मोठं. An adjective ending in anything else does not agree at all, in any gender, ever: लहान (lahān) is लहान (lahān) in front of every noun in the language.
That second class is large, and most of it is words Marathi borrowed — from Sanskrit, from Persian, from Arabic. A borrowed word arrived without a Marathi ending to change, and it never grew one.
This is the mistake to watch for in yourself. Having met मोठा first, the temptation is to say लहानी खोली by analogy. It is wrong, and it is wrong in a way a listener notices immediately, because no Marathi speaker has ever heard that form. Check the last letter before you reach for an ending.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: lahān khoolī — and add nothing]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: moṭhī khoolī — and hear where the ending was earned]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say शिकणे and that you are learning with a friend]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'a small room' and then say what you must NOT say.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What tells you whether an adjective will agree? (Its last letter — only -आ agrees.) Where do most of the non-agreeing ones come from? (Other languages — Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
सुंदर (sundar) — beautiful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have one word that never agrees. Here is a second, and knowing the class means you already know how it behaves before you meet it.

You'll want to know: सुंदर.
सुंदर — sundar — beautiful, lovely.
सुंदर घर, सुंदर खोली — sundar ghar, sundar khoolī — a beautiful house, a beautiful room.
No ending, in either gender, because सुंदर (sundar) does not end in -आ. You could predict that from the last letter one lesson before meeting the word, which is the whole return on having a class rather than a list.
The word came into Marathi from Sanskrit सुन्दर (sundara) unchanged, and that is exactly why it does not bend: it arrived already finished. Sanskrit built it from सु- (su-), "good, well" — the same prefix behind सुद्धा — so beautiful is, underneath, well-made.
It is a wide word in use. Marathi says सुंदर (sundar) of a person, a room, a song and a day, where English would reach for beautiful, lovely, nice and fine in turn.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: sundar khoolī, sundar ghar]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mājhyākaḍe sundar ghar āhe — I have a beautiful house]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say मिळणे and how it puts you in the dative]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say 'a beautiful room' and 'a small beautiful house'.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Why does सुंदर (sundar) not agree? (It does not end in -आ; it arrived from Sanskrit already finished.) What does the सु- in it mean? (Good, well — as in सुद्धा.)
