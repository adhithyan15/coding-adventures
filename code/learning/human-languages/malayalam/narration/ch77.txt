Malayalam, chapter 77: Not There, Not So.
3 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
അല്ല (alla) — "is not so".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two words you have had since the opening chapters: the one that says something is, and the one that says something is not there.

You'll want to know: അല്ല.
ഞാൻ അധ്യാപകൻ അല്ല. ñān adhyāpakan alla — "I am not a teacher."
അല്ല (alla) is the negative of ആണ്. Where ആണ് says X is Y, അല്ല (alla) says X is not Y — and it sits in the same place, at the end, where the sentence closes.

Grammar Lens: Malayalam negates twice, and English only once.
You now hold two negatives, and they are not interchangeable:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഇല്ല, it is not there — denies that something exists.
അല്ല (alla), it is not so — denies that something is what you called it.
English spends one word on both. There is no tea and this is not tea both use not, and Malayalam will not let you.
That is the thing to carry: before you reach for a negative in this language, ask which question you are answering. Is there any? takes ഇല്ല. Is it that? takes അല്ല (alla).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: alla — is not so]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ñān adhyāpakan alla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which negative answers is there any tea?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which negative answers is this tea?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How do you say I am not a teacher? (ഞാൻ അധ്യാപകൻ അല്ല (alla).) Which word does അല്ല (alla) negate? (ആണ്.) And which negative would you use to say something is not present? (ഇല്ല.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
അല്ലേ (allē) — "isn't it?"

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say is not so. Then say the letter Malayalam adds to turn a statement into a genuine question.

You'll want to know: അല്ലേ.
നിങ്ങൾ അധ്യാപകൻ ആണ്, അല്ലേ? niṅṅaḷ adhyāpakan āṇ, allē? — "You are a teacher, aren't you?"
Look at the front of it. അല്ലേ (allē) opens on അല്ല (alla), the word from the last lesson, and that containment is the whole of why it means what it means: it hangs "or is it not so?" on the end of a statement you have already made.

Grammar Lens: asking, and asking for agreement.
Malayalam has two ways to end a sentence with a question, and they want different things back:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
-ഓ, a genuine question — I do not know; tell me.
അല്ലേ (allē), a tag — I think I know; agree with me.
ചായ വേണോ? genuinely does not know whether you want tea. നിങ്ങൾ അധ്യാപകൻ ആണ്, അല്ലേ (allē)? is fairly sure you are one and is checking.
English hides this difference inside intonation and a repeated verb — you are, aren't you. Malayalam puts it in one word and does not change the sentence in front of it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: allē?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: niṅṅaḷ adhyāpakan āṇ, allē?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — look: at അല്ലേ (allē) and find അല്ല (alla) inside it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which ending you would use if you genuinely did not know]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does അല്ലേ (allē) invite — an answer, or agreement? (Agreement.) Which word is hiding at the front of it? (അല്ല (alla).) And which ending asks a question you truly do not know the answer to? (-ഓ.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(not there, not so) (illa alla ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say both negatives, and say which question each one answers.

Grammar Lens: one English word, two Malayalam ones.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
ഇല്ല. denies: that it is there. pairs with: is there any?
അല്ല (alla). denies: that it is so. pairs with: is it that?
English spends one word where Malayalam spends two, so the choice is a decision English never made you make. Make it by looking at the question, not at the word you would use at home.
അല്ല (alla) is the negative of ആണ്, which is why it closes a sentence in the same place ആണ് does.

What you've built.
The ability to say what something is not — and, from that same word, to check something you think you already know.
നിങ്ങൾ അധ്യാപകൻ ആണ്, അല്ലേ (allē)?
അല്ലേ (allē) carries അല്ല (alla) at its front, and hangs "or is it not so?" on a statement. It invites agreement where -ഓ asks for an answer.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ñān adhyāpakan alla]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the same idea as a tag question about somebody else]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which negative answers is there any?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which negative answers is it that?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the difference between what -ഓ wants and what അല്ലേ (allē) wants]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say I am not a teacher. (ഞാൻ അധ്യാപകൻ അല്ല (alla).) How many negatives does Malayalam have where English has one? (Two.) And what does അല്ലേ (allē) ask for? (Agreement.)
