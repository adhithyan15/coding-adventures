## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,320 words in chapters 273–536; Italian attains B1

- **264 new chapters (273–536) teach 1,320 new headwords, one word per lesson.** Each lesson gives the word, its English, one short example sentence with its translation, a usage note and a "The word, taken apart" origin. Each new word is practised again in the next two lessons. A review lesson closes every eight chapters, and each tranche's last chapter gets one more short review. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes:
  - 273–338, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (330 words): time and sequence, news and reports, crime, accidents and emergencies, life events, celebrations, stories and history, and cooking step by step.
  - 339–404, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (330 words): opinion and argument, cause and consequence, work, society and politics, hypotheses and conditions, study, rules and the law, money, and technology. It includes *siccome*, *poiché*, *pertanto*, *purché* and *qualora*, with the subjunctive where they need it.
  - 405–470, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (330 words): the road and the car, stations, ports and airports, lodging and complaints, finding the way, the town, shops and services, nature, and weather.
  - 471–536, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (330 words): feelings and character, health and the body, relationships and family, hobbies, sport, music and the arts, reading and the screen, and life at home.
- **Italian now teaches 2,577 headwords at or below B1**, past the 2,500 the level gate asks for. With the B1 core (chapters 263–272), Italian attains B1.
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1 and Lewis & Short. Where neither supports a step, the lesson hedges it ("usually traced to …") or says the origin is not settled. Learned borrowings (cultismi) are told apart from inherited words. Where an EtymDB row is wrong, the lesson says so: *fiera* "fair" comes from *fēriae*, not *fera*, and *impronta* is not the *improntare* that means "to borrow". An independent review of each tranche found and fixed errors in 202 of the 1,320 words (15.3%). Most were origin claims the sources do not support; the rest were examples, glosses, labels and root slugs.
- **No forward references.** Before drafting, every candidate headword was checked against the text of every earlier lesson, the same way the continuity measure checks it, and examples avoid words taught later. Italian's forward-reference count stays at 0.
- **Hands-free.** Examples describe actions instead of instructing them, so no new chapter opens on a lesson that needs the eyes or the hands.
