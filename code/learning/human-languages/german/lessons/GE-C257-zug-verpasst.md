# ich habe den Zug verpasst — when something goes wrong

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C190-VERB190-03, GE-GRAMMAR-PERFEKT-HABEN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to miss", a train or a chance. (*Verpassen.*) Then "I
said", in the Perfekt. (*Ich habe gesagt.*)

## You'll want to know: ich habe den Zug verpasst
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-ZUG-VERPASST-01]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PARTIZIP-FINAL-01, GE-GRAMMAR-PRAETERITUM-SCHRIFTSPRACHE-01] -->

**Ich habe den Zug verpasst.** — "I missed the train": *ikh HAH-buh dehn
tsook fer-PASST*.

Three things in it matter. It is the **Perfekt**, because a ticket
counter is a conversation, and conversation mostly runs in the Perfekt.
The participle is **verpasst**, with no *ge-*: verbs that begin with
*ver-* do without it. And *der Zug* becomes **den Zug**, because the train
is the thing missed.

## Grammar Lens: say what happened, then ask
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-PROBLEM-MELDEN-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C67-VERBEN4-01, GE-LEX-C217-QUAL217-01, GE-LEX-C111-FAHRT111-03, GE-LEX-C142-FUT142-02, GE-LEX-C111-FAHRT111-02] -->

Reporting a problem goes in two moves. First the problem, in the Perfekt
or the present:

> *Ich habe den Zug verpasst.*
> *Ich habe meine Fahrkarte verloren.* — I've lost my ticket.
> *Mein Koffer ist kaputt.* — My suitcase is broken.

Then the question that asks for help:

> *Wann fährt der nächste Zug?* — When is the next train?
> *Was kann ich machen?* — What can I do?

*Der nächste Zug* is the *nächste* of *nächste Woche*, next.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C190-VERB190-03] -->

**der Zug** ← Middle High German *zuc* (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB sets it beside
Old English *tyge*, which it traces to a Proto-Germanic word for "a tug, a
pull". By the usual account it belongs to the family of **ziehen**, "to pull": a train is the thing the
engine pulls.

**verpassen**, from chapter one hundred and ninety, is *ver-* + *passen*.
EtymDB 2.1 traces *passen* to Dutch, and further back to French *passer*,
"to pass": to let a thing pass you by.
