Telugu, chapter 549: Making a Home.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
అమర్చు (amarcu) — to set up; to arrange.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to sift, then the Telugu for tempering.

You'll want to know: అమర్చు.
అమర్చు — amarcu — "to set up; to arrange".
కొత్త ఇంట్లో మేము సామాను అమర్చాము. kotta iṇṭlō mēmu sāmānu amarcāmu. We set up our things in the new house.
Past (I …): అమర్చాను, amarcānu.
Putting things where they belong.

The word, taken apart.
అమర్చు (amarcu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 162 lists Telugu amaru, "to suit, to be fit", and amar(u)cu, "to fit, to arrange", with Tamil amar.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to spread out, then the Telugu for to kindle.

You'll want to know: చిలుకు.
చిలుకు — ciluku — "to churn".
అమ్మమ్మ పెరుగు చిలికి వెన్న తీసింది. ammamma perugu ciliki venna tīsindi. Grandma churned the curd and took out the butter.
Past (I …): చిలికాను, cilikānu.
Churning curd for butter or buttermilk.

The word, taken apart.
చిలుకు (ciluku) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2570 lists Telugu ciluku, "to churn", with Tamil ciluppu, "to churn, to stir".

Lesson 5 of 7.
అలసిపోవు (alasipōvu) — to get tired; to wear out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to kindle, then the Telugu for to churn.

You'll want to know: అలసిపోవు.
అలసిపోవు — alasipōvu — "to get tired; to wear out".
ఇల్లు సర్దిన తరువాత మేము బాగా అలసిపోయాము. illu sardina taruvāta mēmu bāgā alasipōyāmu. After tidying the house we were very tired.
Past (I …): అలసిపోయాను, alasipōyānu.
Its noun అలసట is tiredness.

The word, taken apart.
అలసిపోవు (alasipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 236 lists Telugu alayu, "to be tired", and alasaṭa, "fatigue"; the verb ends in pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572).
