Sanskrit, chapter 523: A Play in the Village.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नटः (naṭaḥ) — an actor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for imitates, then the Sanskrit for clapping.

You'll want to know: नटः.
नटः — naṭaḥ — "an actor".
मम मित्रं नाटके नटः अस्ति। mama mitraṃ nāṭake naṭaḥ asti. My friend is an actor in the play.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
नटः (naṭaḥ) comes from the root √naṭ, "to dance", as MW derives it; MW gives naṭa as an actor, a dancer, a mime (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
अभिनयति (abhinayati) — acts a part.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for clapping, then the Sanskrit for an actor.

You'll want to know: अभिनयति.
अभिनयति — abhinayati — "acts a part".
सः नाटके चौरम् अभिनयति। saḥ nāṭake cauram abhinayati. He plays a thief in the play.
Past (he …): सः चौरम् अभिनीतवान्, saḥ cauram abhinītavān.

The word, taken apart.
अभिनयति (abhinayati) is built on abhi, "towards", and the root √nī, "to lead", which also gives nayati; MW gives abhinī as to bring near, and to represent dramatically, to act (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
विदूषकः (vidūṣakaḥ) — a jester.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an actor, then the Sanskrit for acts a part.

You'll want to know: विदूषकः.
विदूषकः — vidūṣakaḥ — "a jester".
विदूषकं दृष्ट्वा सर्वे हसन्ति। vidūṣakaṃ dṛṣṭvā sarve hasanti. Everyone laughs at the sight of the jester.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
विदूषकः (vidūṣakaḥ) is built on vi and the causative of the root √duṣ, "to spoil, to defile", with the agent ending -aka; MW gives vidūṣaka first as defiling, then as a jester, above all the witty friend of the hero in a play (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
नेपथ्यम् (nepathyam) — backstage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for acts a part, then the Sanskrit for a jester.

You'll want to know: नेपथ्यम्.
नेपथ्यम् — nepathyam — "backstage".
नटः नेपथ्ये वस्त्रं परिवर्तयति। naṭaḥ nepathye vastraṃ parivartayati. The actor changes his clothes backstage.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
नेपथ्यम् (nepathyam) is the word MW gives for an ornament, a costume, and in drama the place behind the stage, the tiring-room; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

अभिनयः (abhinayaḥ) is built on abhi and the root √nī, "to lead"; MW gives abhinaya as acting, dramatic action, the showing of a feeling by look and gesture (MW).
