Latin, chapter 379: Earning a Living.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
quaestus, quaestūs — gain, profit; a way of earning a living.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sew, then the Latin for to carve.

You'll want to know: quaestus, quaestūs.
quaestus, quaestūs — "gain, profit; a way of earning a living".
Piscātor ex marī quaestum facit. The fisherman makes his living from the sea.
quaestus, quaestūs m., built on quaerō, to seek: what you go out to get. Quaestum facere, to make money.

The word, taken apart.
quaestus is the noun built on quaerō "to seek, to get" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): what a man goes out to win.

Lesson 2 of 5.
stīpendium, stīpendiī — pay, wages (especially a soldier's).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to carve, then the Latin for gain.

You'll want to know: stīpendium, stīpendiī.
stīpendium, stīpendiī — "pay, wages (especially a soldier's)".
Mīlitēs stīpendium accēpērunt. The soldiers received their pay.
stīpendium, stīpendiī n. A soldier's pay, then a year of service: vīgintī stīpendia, twenty campaigns. English stipend.

The word, taken apart.
stīpendium is contracted from stipipendium, built from stips "a small coin" and pendō "to weigh out, to pay" (Lewis & Short): coins weighed out. English stipend comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mercēnārius, mercēnāriī — a hired worker; a mercenary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for gain, then the Latin for pay.

You'll want to know: mercēnārius, mercēnāriī.
mercēnārius, mercēnāriī — "a hired worker; a mercenary".
Mercēnāriī prō mercēde labōrant. Hired men work for a wage.
mercēnārius, mercēnāriī m. (also spelled mercennārius), built on mercēs, a wage. As an adjective, hired. English mercenary.

The word, taken apart.
mercēnārius is built on mercēs, mercēdis "wages, hire" (Lewis & Short), which belongs with merx "goods". English mercenary comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
labōriōsus, labōriōsa, labōriōsum — toilsome; hard-working.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for pay, then the Latin for a hired worker.

You'll want to know: labōriōsus, labōriōsa, labōriōsum.
labōriōsus, labōriōsa, labōriōsum — "toilsome; hard-working".
Vīta agricolae labōriōsa est. A farmer's life is full of toil.
labōriōsus, labōriōsa, labōriōsum: of a task, toilsome; of a person, hard-working. English laborious.

The word, taken apart.
labōriōsus is built on labor, labōris "toil" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -ōsus "full of". English laborious comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dīligentia, dīligentiae — care, diligence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hired worker, then the Latin for toilsome.

You'll want to know: dīligentia, dīligentiae.
dīligentia, dīligentiae — "care, diligence".
Opus magnā dīligentiā perfēcit. He finished the work with great care.
dīligentia, dīligentiae f., the noun of dīligēns, careful, hard-working. English diligence.

The word, taken apart.
dīligentia is the noun built on dīligēns "careful" (Lewis & Short), the participle of a verb made from dis- and legō "to pick out". English diligence comes from it.
