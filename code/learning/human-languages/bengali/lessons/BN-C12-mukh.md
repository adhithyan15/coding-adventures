# মুখ (mukh) — "mouth, face," a word two families both claim

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C12-CHOKH-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] চোখ (*chokh*) was half old sound, half new. This word is a full tatsama again
— and its own history is a real, unresolved argument among linguists.

## You'll want to know: মুখ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C12-MUKH-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **মুখ** — *mukh* — **mouth, face**

**আমার মুখ** (*āmār mukh*) — my mouth, my face.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**মুখ** is **ম** with the vowel sign **ু** (*mu*) plus **খ** (*kh*), silent
at the end — one bare consonant again: *mukh*, not *mukho*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**মুখ** is a **tatsama**, taken whole from Sanskrit **মুখ** (*mukha*) — a
tadbhava twin, **মু** (*mu*), also exists, but only buried inside compound
words, never standing alone. What makes মুখ worth pausing on is where
*mukha* itself came from, and nobody fully agrees. One well-known account,
Pokorny's, derives it from **Dravidian**, a language family with no Sanskrit
or PIE connection at all. Another, Mayrhofer's, argues for an
**Indo-European** source instead, pointing to the word's presence in the
Rigveda and in Iranian languages as evidence it is old enough to be inherited
rather than borrowed. Both scholars are serious; neither has won. The honest
report is that মুখ's deepest root is still an open question.
