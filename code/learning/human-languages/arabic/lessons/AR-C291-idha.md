# إذا (idhā) — if

## Grammar Lens: idhā and the past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C291-IDHA-PAST-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C207-SABAB207-04, AR-LEX-C170-WAQT170-01, AR-CONCEPT-C33-QAHWA-01, AR-LEX-C159-ACT159-03] -->

After **إذا** — *idhā* — Arabic puts a **past** verb, and means the future:

> **إذا وصلت مبكرا، أشرب قهوة.** — *idhā waṣaltu mubakkiran, ashrabu qahwa*
> — If I arrive early, I'll have a coffee.

*Waṣaltu* looks like "I arrived", but after *idhā* it is "if I arrive". The
condition is something that may well happen: you are not sure, but you
expect it. The second half, the result, can be a plain present.

With *kāna*: *idhā kāna ʿindī waqt, adhhabu maʿaka*, if I have time, I'll go
with you.

## Grammar Lens: in, the other "if"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C291-IN-01]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PAST-PERSONS-01, AR-LEX-C283-MADI283-01, AR-LEX-C105-LOC105-05] -->

**إن** — *in* — is "if" as well, usually described as a little less sure
than *idhā*. It takes the past in the same way: *in waṣalta
mubakkiran*, if you arrive early. You hear it inside a phrase you may know
already: *in shāʾa allāh*, "if God wills".

*In*, if, with no doubled letter, is a different word from *anna*, that,
and *an*, to.
