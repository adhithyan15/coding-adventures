Mandarin Chinese, chapter 482: Treatment.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
jízhěn — emergency department; urgent treatment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to become infected, then the Mandarin for a virus.

You'll want to know: jízhěn.
jízhěn — "emergency department; urgent treatment".
wǒ xiǎng dāng jízhěn yīshēng — I want to be an emergency doctor.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jízhěn is built from jí 'urgent' and zhěn 'to examine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kip and tsyinX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'urgent' and the second as feeling the pulse.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhìliáo — to give medical treatment; to cure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a virus, then the Mandarin for the emergency department.

You'll want to know: zhìliáo.
zhìliáo — "to give medical treatment; to cure".
tā zhìliáo le bàn nián xiànzài yǐjīng hǎo le — He was treated for six months, and now he's well again.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhìliáo is built from zhì 'to put in order' and liáo 'to heal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dri and ljewH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under three readings and glosses them 'to put in order'; it glosses the second 'to treat an illness'. The falling tone of zhì follows the reading driH.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yàopiàn — tablet; pill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the emergency department, then the Mandarin for to give medical treatment.

You'll want to know: yàopiàn.
yàopiàn — "tablet; pill".
yīshēng shuō yàopiàn yì tiān chī sān cì — The doctor says to take the pills three times a day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yàopiàn is built from yào 'medicine' and piàn 'a slice', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yak and phenH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as a plant that cures illness, and glosses the second 'half' and 'a split piece of wood'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
护理 (hùlǐ) — to care for the sick; to tend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a pill, then the Mandarin for to disinfect.

You'll want to know: 护理.
护理 — hùlǐ — "to care for the sick; to tend".
护理病人的时候你不能怕血 hùlǐ bìngrén de shíhou nǐ bù néng pà xiě — When you look after patients, you can't be afraid of blood.

The word, taken apart.
护理 (hùlǐ) is built from 护 'to protect' and 理 'to put in order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huH and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rescue' and 'to help' and the second 'to manage'; the sense of caring for the sick is not given there.
