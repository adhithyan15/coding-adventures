# क्यों (*kyoṁ*) — why

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-C40-DEIXIS-SYSTEM, HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01, HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-06] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Retrieve the two items before this one by name: **सकना** and
**चाहना**. Then run the k- words the deixis lesson gave you — **क्या**,
**कहाँ**, and *kaun*. There is one more in that family, and it is the one a
listener most often wants.

## You'll want to know: क्यों
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-JOIN-KYON-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-SAKNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C40-DEIXIS-SYSTEM] -->

> **क्यों** — *kyoṁ* — **why**

> **आप क्यों नहीं आ सकते?** — *āp kyoṁ nahīṁ ā sakte?* —
> **Why can't you come?**

**क्यों** goes where any Hindi question word goes: in the sentence's ordinary
slot, in front of the verb — **not** dragged to the front the way English drags
*why*. The rest of the sentence keeps the order it always had, verb last.

On its own, **क्यों?** is a complete turn. It is how you ask somebody to explain
what they have just said, and it costs one syllable.

The deixis lesson generalised the **य- / व- / क-** system and listed the k-
words. This is one of the ones it listed and did not teach. Its **क-** is the
same ancient stem as **क्या** and *kaun*, and it is Latin's **qu-** in *quis*
and English's **wh-** in *who* — one Indo-European **kʷ-**, three different
spellings of the same sound.
