# la rueda — the wheel

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-20, ES-LEX-C363-WHERE-45] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **alrededor** and **la bicicleta**. There is a wheel inside one
of those words, and it is not the obvious one.

## You'll want to know: la rueda
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C363-WHERE-45] -->

**la rueda** — the wheel, and on a car the tyre as well.

*La rueda de atrás* — the back wheel. *Se me ha pinchado una rueda* — I've got
a puncture. *La rueda de repuesto* — the spare.

A bicycle has *dos ruedas*, and Spanish will name a vehicle by counting them:
*un vehículo de dos ruedas*.

## Grammar Lens: the o that breaks in two
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-02] -->

Latin *rota* has an **o**. Spanish has **ue**. That change is everywhere once
you look for it, and it happens under one condition — stress:

| unstressed o | stressed, so ue |
|---|---|
| r**o**dar — to roll | la r**ue**da |
| p**o**der — to be able | p**ue**do |
| v**o**lver — to return | v**ue**lvo |

Say *rueda* and *rodar* one after the other and you can hear the same word
twice. The vowel splits under the weight of the stress and closes up again
when the stress moves off.

This is why so many Spanish verbs look irregular and are not. They are one
vowel obeying one condition.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-20] -->

Latin **rota**, wheel. And here is the payoff on a word you already use daily.

**Alrededor** is *al* + *rededor*, and *rededor* comes from Latin **retrō**,
*backwards* — **pulled sideways by *rota***, the wheel. That is the careful
version and it is worth keeping careful: *rota* is not where the word started,
it is what bent it on the way. Both ideas ended up inside it, which is why
something *alrededor* is behind you as well as in front.

The wheel half is generous. A **rotunda** is a round building, a **rotation**
turns, Spanish keeps *rotonda* for the roundabout you drive round, and
*la rodilla* — the knee — is *rotella*, a little wheel. A knee and the space
around you, named by the same rolling.
