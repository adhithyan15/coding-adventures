> **نان** — *nân* — **bread**

> **نان، لطفاً.** — *nân, lotfan.* — **Bread, please.**

Three letters, all already yours: **ن ا ن** — *n*, long *â*, *n* again.

## The word, taken apart — a root this track cannot trace
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-ETYMON-NAN]; assesses=[] -->

**نان** continues Middle Persian **nān**, and there this word's trail runs
cold: no secure root beyond that form is agreed, and no English cousin can
honestly be claimed. Not every word in this book gets a family tree, and this
one is named plainly rather than forced into one.

The word travelled anyway, just not by inheritance. Persian **نان** passed
into Hindi-Urdu as **nān**, and from there into English as **naan** — the
bread on a restaurant menu is this exact word, arriving by borrowing three
languages after Persian, not by any shared ancestor.
