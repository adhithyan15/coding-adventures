Japanese, chapter 530: At Least, About, Almost.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かなり (kanari) — fairly; quite.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for would sooner, then the Japanese for as one would expect.

You'll want to know: かなり.
かなり — kanari — "fairly; quite".
もし あめが ふったら、みちは かなり こむでしょう。 — moshi ame ga futtara, michi wa kanari komu deshō — "If it rains, the roads will be fairly crowded."

The word, taken apart.
かなり (kanari) is usually counted a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, though that is not certain: JMdict marks it as usually written in kana and lists only rare kanji spellings, and no dictionary we use records its history, so where it comes from is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
すくなくとも (sukunakutomo) — at the very least; no fewer than.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for as one would expect, then the Japanese for fairly.

You'll want to know: すくなくとも.
すくなくとも — sukunakutomo — "at the very least; no fewer than".
もし くるまで いったら、すくなくとも いちじかん かかるでしょう。 — moshi kuruma de ittara, sukunakutomo ichijikan kakaru deshō — "If you go by car, it will take at least an hour."

The word, taken apart.
すくなくとも (sukunakutomo) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: built on sukunai, "few", with the ending tomo, "even"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
