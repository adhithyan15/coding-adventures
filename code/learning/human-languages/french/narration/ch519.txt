French, chapter 519: Un bon roman.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le romancier — novelist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for work, then the French for to put in a frame.

You'll want to know: le romancier.
le romancier — "novelist".
Ce romancier écrit un livre par an. This novelist writes a book a year.
A woman is la romancière.

The word, taken apart.
le romancier is built on roman "novel", from Old French romanz, a story told in the common Romance tongue rather than in Latin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'intrigue — plot (of a story); intrigue.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for to put in a frame, then the French for novelist.

You'll want to know: l'intrigue.
l'intrigue — "plot (of a story); intrigue".
L'intrigue du livre se passe dans un petit village. The plot of the book takes place in a small village.
Feminine. Also a secret scheme.

The word, taken apart.
l'intrigue was borrowed from Italian intrigo (EtymDB), from intrigare, from Latin intrīcāre "to entangle, to perplex" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le dénouement — ending, outcome (of a story).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for novelist, then the French for plot.

You'll want to know: le dénouement.
le dénouement — "ending, outcome (of a story)".
Je n'ai pas aimé le dénouement du film. I did not like the ending of the film.
Literally the untying of the knot.

The word, taken apart.
le dénouement is built on dénouer "to untie", from dé- and nouer "to tie", inherited from Latin nōdāre, from nōdus "a knot" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le rebondissement — twist, new development.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for plot, then the French for ending.

You'll want to know: le rebondissement.
le rebondissement — "twist, new development".
Le roman est plein de rebondissements. The novel is full of twists.
Built on rebondir "to bounce back".

The word, taken apart.
le rebondissement is built on rebondir "to bounce back", from bondir "to leap", which first meant "to resound" and goes back to Vulgar Latin bombitīre, from Latin bombus "a booming sound".

Lesson 5 of 5.
captivant — gripping, captivating.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for ending, then the French for twist.

You'll want to know: captivant.
captivant — "gripping, captivating".
J'ai lu un livre captivant pendant les vacances. I read a gripping book during the holidays.
Built on captiver "to captivate".

The word, taken apart.
captivant is the present participle of captiver "to captivate", from Late Latin captīvāre "to take prisoner", built on captīvus "a captive", from capere "to take" (Lewis & Short).
