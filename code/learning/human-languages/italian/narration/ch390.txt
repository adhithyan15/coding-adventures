Italian, chapter 390: Legale o no.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
legale — legal, lawful.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the request, then the Italian for the certificate.

You'll want to know: legale.
legale — "legal, lawful".
Se è legale, per me va bene. If it is legal, it is fine by me.
As a noun, il legale is a lawyer: parlane con il tuo legale.

The word, taken apart.
legale is a learned borrowing from Latin lēgālis "of the law" (Lewis & Short), from lēx, lēgis "law"; the everyday leale "loyal" is the same Latin word, inherited.

Lesson 2 of 5.
illegale — illegal.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Italian for the certificate, then the Italian for legal.

You'll want to know: illegale.
illegale — "illegal".
Scaricare film senza pagare è illegale. Downloading films without paying is illegal.
In- + legale: before l the n becomes l.

The word, taken apart.
illegale is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin illēgālis "unlawful", built from in- "not" and lēgālis, on lēx, lēgis "law" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: lecito.
lecito — "permissible, allowed".
È lecito chiedere, ma non insistere. It is all right to ask, but do not insist.
Stress on the first syllable: lècito. Moral as well as legal.

The word, taken apart.
lecito comes from Latin licitus "allowed, lawful" (Lewis & Short), past participle of licēre "to be allowed".

Lesson 4 of 5.
illecito — unlawful, illicit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for illegal, then the Italian for permissible.

You'll want to know: illecito.
illecito — "unlawful, illicit".
La polizia ha scoperto un traffico illecito di animali. The police uncovered an illicit trade in animals.
As a noun, un illecito is an offence: un illecito amministrativo.

The word, taken apart.
illecito comes from Latin illicitus "not allowed, unlawful" (Lewis & Short), built from in- "not" and licitus, on licēre "to be allowed".

Lesson 5 of 5.
abusivo — unauthorised, unlicensed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for permissible, then the Italian for unlawful.

You'll want to know: abusivo.
abusivo — "unauthorised, unlicensed".
Questa casa è abusiva: non ha i permessi. This house is unauthorised: it has no permits.
Un parcheggiatore abusivo — an unlicensed parking attendant.

The word, taken apart.
abusivo comes from Latin abūsīvus "misapplied, improper" (EtymDB), from abūsus "misuse", built on abūtī "to use up, to misuse" and ūtī "to use".
