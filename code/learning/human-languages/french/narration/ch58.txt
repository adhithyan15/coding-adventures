French, chapter 58: Neighbour, Guest, Baby, Grandson, Granddaughter.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le voisin — a neighbour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for an aunt, then the French for a cousin.

You'll want to know: le voisin.
le voisin — "a neighbour".

The word, taken apart.
voisin from Latin vicinus, "neighbour", from vicus, "a village" — inherited. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'invité — a guest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a cousin, then the French for a neighbour.

You'll want to know: l'invité.
l'invité — "a guest".

The word, taken apart.
invité is the past participle of the French verb for "to invite", used as a noun: one who is invited. The verb from Latin invitare — a learned borrowing. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
le petit-fils — a grandson.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for a guest, then the French for a baby.

You'll want to know: le petit-fils.
le petit-fils — "a grandson".

The word, taken apart.
petit-fils — built from the French words for "little" and "son": a grandson. The "little" word from Vulgar Latin pittitus, and the "son" word from Latin filius. (EtymDB 2.1).
