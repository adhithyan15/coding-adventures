Japanese, chapter 232: Talking Things Over.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はなしあう (hanashiau) — to discuss; to talk together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a response, then the Japanese for to pass on.

You'll want to know: はなしあう.
はなしあう — hanashiau — "to discuss; to talk together".
どうりょうと かいぎで はなしあいます。 — dōryō to kaigi de hanashiaimasu — "I discuss things with my colleagues at the meeting."
Polite はなしあいます, hanashiaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
はなしあう (hanashiau) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from hanashi, from hanasu, "to speak", and au, "to do together" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pass on, then the Japanese for to discuss.

You'll want to know: まとめる.
まとめる — matomeru — "to put together; to sum up".
せんぱいは かいぎで みんなの しつもんを まとめます。 — senpai wa kaigi de minna no shitsumon o matomemasu — "My senior sums up everyone's questions at the meeting."
Polite まとめます, matomemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
まとめる (matomeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and the word is usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
