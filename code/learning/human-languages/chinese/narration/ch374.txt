Mandarin Chinese, chapter 374: People and Government.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
人口 (rénkǒu) — population.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for naive, then the Mandarin for to take it seriously.

You'll want to know: 人口.
人口 — rénkǒu — "population".
zhège chéngshì de rénkǒu yǒu yìqiān wàn — This city has a population of ten million.

The word, taken apart.
人口 (rénkǒu) is built from 人 'a person' and 口 'a mouth', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyin and khuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'what a person speaks and eats with'. Counting the mouths of a place to mean its people is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhèngfǔ — government.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to take it seriously, then the Mandarin for the population (of a place).

You'll want to know: zhèngfǔ.
zhèngfǔ — "government".
zhèngfǔ yīnggāi guānxīn lǎo rén — The government should look after old people.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhèngfǔ is built from zhèng 'governing' and fǔ 'an office', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyengH and pjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to set right' and the second 'an office of state' and 'a store of documents'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
wénhuà — culture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the population (of a place), then the Mandarin for the government.

You'll want to know: wénhuà.
wénhuà — "culture".
wǒ hěn xǐhuan Zhōngguó wénhuà bǐrú hànzì — I really like Chinese culture, the characters for one.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wénhuà is built from 文 'writing' and huà 'to transform', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjun and xwaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to change' and 'to transform by example'. The sense 'culture' is not given there.

fāzhǎn — to develop; to expand.

The word, taken apart.
fāzhǎn is built from fā 'to set going' and zhǎn 'to open out', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjot and trjenX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses each with the same word, 'to unfold'.
