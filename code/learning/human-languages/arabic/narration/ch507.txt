Arabic, chapter 507: Falling ill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مرض (mariḍa) — to fall ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a chin, then the Arabic for a nerve.

You'll want to know: مرض.
مرض — mariḍa — "to fall ill".
مرض أخي في الشتاء الماضي. mariḍa akhī fī ash-shitāʾ al-māḍī. My brother fell ill last winter.
Present: يمرض, yamraḍ.
The noun مرض (mariḍa), read maraḍ, is an illness.

The word, taken apart.
مرض (mariḍa) is built on the native Arabic root m-r-ḍ, "to be sick" (Lane). Marīḍ, sick, which you know, is from the same root.

Lesson 2 of 5.
نزلة برد (nazlat bard) — a chill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a nerve, then the Arabic for to fall ill.

You'll want to know: نزلة برد.
نزلة برد — nazlat bard — "a chill".
عند أخي نزلة برد خفيفة. ʿinda akhī nazlat bard khafīfa. My brother has a slight chill.

The word, taken apart.
نزلة برد (nazlat bard) is built on two native Arabic roots: nazla, "a coming down", from n-z-l, the root of nazala, to go down (Lane), and bard, "cold": a cold that comes down on you.

شعرت بالغثيان في السفينة. shaʿartu bi-l-ghathayān fī as-safīna. I felt sick on the ship.

The word, taken apart.
غثيان (ghathayān) is built on the native Arabic root gh-th-y, "to be troubled, to heave (of the stomach)" (Lane). The pattern faʿalān often names a restless movement.

Lesson 5 of 5.
إسهال (ishāl) — diarrhoea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for nausea, then the Arabic for to vomit.

You'll want to know: إسهال.
إسهال — ishāl — "diarrhoea".
أعطى الطبيب الطفل دواء للإسهال. aʿṭā aṭ-ṭabīb aṭ-ṭifl dawāʾ li-l-ishāl. The doctor gave the child medicine for diarrhoea.

The word, taken apart.
إسهال (ishāl) is built on the native Arabic root s-h-l, "to be smooth, easy" (Lane). Lane gives Form IV ashala, of a medicine, as to loosen the bowels; sahl, easy, is the same root.
