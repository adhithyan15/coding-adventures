Sanskrit, chapter 198: Deer, Leopard, Rhinoceros, Nest, Egg.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हरिणः (hariṇaḥ) — a deer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dew, then the Sanskrit for moonlight.

You'll want to know: हरिणः.
हरिणः — hariṇaḥ — "a deer".

The word, taken apart.
hariṇa is built on hari, "tawny, yellowish", so the deer is "the fawn-coloured one" (MW). MW compares hari with English yellow. The word lives on as Hindi hiran (CDIAL 13982).

You'll want to know: चित्रकः.
चित्रकः — citrakaḥ — "a leopard".

The word, taken apart.
citraka is built on citra, "bright, variegated", from the root √cit (MW): the spotted one. It lives on as Hindi cītā, "leopard" (CDIAL 4804), and English cheetah was borrowed from Hindi.

The word, taken apart.
gaṇḍaka is probably not an Indo-Aryan word: CDIAL 4000 thinks it shares a non-Aryan origin with khaḍga, another rhinoceros word, perhaps from Munda. Hindi gaĩṛā comes from a related form.

Lesson 4 of 5.
नीडम् (nīḍam) — a nest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a leopard, then the Sanskrit for a rhinoceros.

You'll want to know: नीडम्.
नीडम् — nīḍam — "a nest".

The word, taken apart.
nīḍa is built on ni, "down", and the root √sad, "to sit" (MW): a nest is where a bird sits down. It is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Latin nīdus and English nest.
