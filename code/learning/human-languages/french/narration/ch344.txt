French, chapter 344: Raisonner.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
raisonner — to reason, to think logically.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the criticism, then the French for to stress.

You'll want to know: raisonner.
raisonner — "to reason, to think logically".
Il raisonne bien, mais il parle trop vite. He reasons well, but he speaks too fast.
From raison. Not to be mixed up with résonner, to resound.

The word, taken apart.
raisonner is built on raison "reason", which is inherited from Latin ratiō, ratiōnis "a reckoning, reason" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the French for to stress, then the French for to reason.

You'll want to know: le raisonnement.
le raisonnement — "the reasoning".
Ton raisonnement est juste. Your reasoning is sound.
Un raisonnement is one chain of thought. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
raisonnement is built from raisonner and the suffix -ment (EtymDB). The verb rests on raison, from Latin ratiō "a reckoning, reason" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
conclure — to conclude.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to reason, then the French for the reasoning.

You'll want to know: conclure.
conclure — "to conclude".
Après la réunion, nous avons conclu qu'il fallait attendre. After the meeting, we concluded that we had to wait.
Past participle conclu. Conclure un accord — to close a deal.

The word, taken apart.
conclure came into Old French as conclure, a borrowing from Latin conclūdere "to shut up, to bring to an end" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on claudere "to shut".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la nuance — the subtle difference, the shade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the reasoning, then the French for to conclude.

You'll want to know: la nuance.
la nuance — "the subtle difference, the shade".
Il y a une petite nuance entre ces deux mots. There is a slight difference between these two words.
Of colours too: une nuance de bleu.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of nuance is usually traced to Old French nuer "to shade, like clouds", from nue "cloud", inherited from Latin nūbēs "cloud" (Lewis & Short).
