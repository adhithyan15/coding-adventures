Portuguese, chapter 453: Na caixa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o talão — the till receipt, the slip; the stub.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the optician, then the Portuguese for the stain.

You'll want to know: o talão.
o talão — "the till receipt, the slip; the stub".
Guardei o talão para trocar a camisa. I kept the receipt so I could exchange the shirt.
O talão is the slip from the till; a fatura has your tax number.

The word, taken apart.
talão was borrowed from French talon 'heel; stub of a booklet' (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin tālō, on Latin tālus 'ankle, heel'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
a liquidação — the clearance sale; the settling (of a debt).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the till receipt, then the Portuguese for the bargain.

You'll want to know: a liquidação.
a liquidação — "the clearance sale; the settling (of a debt)".
A loja está em liquidação total. The shop is having a closing-down sale.
Os saldos are the seasonal sales.

The word, taken apart.
liquidação is built on liquidar 'to settle, to clear', a learned formation on Latin liquidus 'liquid, clear' (Lewis & Short): a debt made clear.

Lesson 4 of 5.
regatear — to haggle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bargain, then the Portuguese for the clearance sale.

You'll want to know: regatear.
regatear — "to haggle".
Na feira, as pessoas regateiam o preço. At the market, people haggle over the price.
In shops with fixed prices nobody regateia.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of regatear is not settled; it is linked with regatão 'street trader', and EtymDB has no entry for either word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
embrulhar — to wrap (up).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the clearance sale, then the Portuguese for to haggle.

You'll want to know: embrulhar.
embrulhar — "to wrap (up)".
A senhora embrulhou o presente em papel azul. The woman wrapped the present in blue paper.
É para oferta? — Is it a gift? Then they embrulham it. Um embrulho — a parcel.

The word, taken apart.
embrulhar comes from Old Portuguese envurullar (EtymDB); its earlier origin is not settled.
