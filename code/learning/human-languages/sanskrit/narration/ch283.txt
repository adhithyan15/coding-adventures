Sanskrit, chapter 283: Bigger, Better, and How Long.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
अपेक्षया (apekṣayā) — compared with.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "big". (mahat.) Then say "the city is big". (nagaraṃ mahat asti.) Now set it beside the village.

You'll want to know: Y-अपेक्षया.
नगरं ग्रामापेक्षया महत्। nagaraṃ grāmāpekṣayā mahat. — "The city is bigger than the village."
अपेक्षया (apekṣayā), "compared with", joins the end of the thing compared, as one word: grāma and apekṣayā meet as ग्रामापेक्षया (grāmāpekṣayā), two short as making one long ā. The describing word stays as it is: महत्, "big", says "bigger" here.
उद्यानं वनापेक्षया सुन्दरम्। udyānaṃ vanāpekṣayā sundaram. — "The garden is more beautiful than the forest."
धूमयानं लोकयानापेक्षया शीघ्रं गच्छति। dhūmayānaṃ lokayānāpekṣayā śīghraṃ gacchati. — "The train goes faster than the bus."

You'll want to know: -आत् with -तर, and the best.
The other way puts the thing compared in the -āt form of भारतात् (bhāratāt), "from India", and adds -तर (-tara), "more", to the describing word (Pāṇini 2.3.42):
नगरं ग्रामात् महत्तरम्। nagaraṃ grāmāt mahattaram. — "The city is bigger than the village."
उद्यानं वनात् सुन्दरतरम्। udyānaṃ vanāt sundarataram. — "The garden is more beautiful than the forest."
For the best of all, सर्वेषु (sarveṣu), "among all", with श्रेष्ठः (śreṣṭhaḥ), "best":
सर्वेषु फलेषु आम्रं श्रेष्ठम्। sarveṣu phaleṣu āmraṃ śreṣṭham. — "Of all fruits, the mango is the best."
सर्वेषु मीरा श्रेष्ठा। sarveṣu mīrā śreṣṭhā. — "Mira is the best of all."

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The garden is bigger than the shop" — udyānam āpaṇāpekṣayā mahat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The city is bigger than the shop" — nagaram āpaṇāpekṣayā mahat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 5.
आरभ्य, पर्यन्तम् (ārabhya, paryantam) — from, until, and how long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "morning" and "evening". (prātaḥ, sāyam.) Then say "the city is bigger than the village". (nagaraṃ grāmāpekṣayā mahat.)

You'll want to know: from … until.
प्रातः आरभ्य सायं पर्यन्तम्। prātaḥ ārabhya sāyaṃ paryantam. — "From morning until evening."
आरभ्य (ārabhya) is the -ya form of आरभते (ārabhate), "begins": "beginning from", so "from, since". पर्यन्तम् (paryantam) is "up to, until".
अहं सायं पर्यन्तं कार्यं करोमि। ahaṃ sāyaṃ paryantaṃ kāryaṃ karomi. — "I work until evening."
सः ह्यः आरभ्य रुग्णः अस्ति। saḥ hyaḥ ārabhya rugṇaḥ asti. — "He has been ill since yesterday."

You'll want to know: for how long.
How long a thing lasts needs no word for "for": the time takes the -am form, the form of the thing a verb acts on (Pāṇini 2.3.5). होरा (horā), "an hour", becomes होराम्:
अहम् एकां होरां पठामि। aham ekāṃ horāṃ paṭhāmi. — "I read for an hour."
सः सर्वं दिनं कार्यं करोति। saḥ sarvaṃ dinaṃ kāryaṃ karoti. — "He works the whole day."
For two, the dual: द्वौ मासौ (dvau māsau), "two months". स्थितवती (sthitavatī) is the past of तिष्ठति, "stays":
सा द्वौ मासौ ग्रामे स्थितवती। sā dvau māsau grāme sthitavatī. — "She stayed in the village for two months."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I read for an hour" — aham ekāṃ horāṃ paṭhāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I read the whole day" — ahaṃ sarvaṃ dinaṃ paṭhāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I read until evening" — ahaṃ sāyaṃ paryantaṃ paṭhāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 28 seconds]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"From morning"? (प्रातः आरभ्य.) "Until evening"? (सायं पर्यन्तम्.) "For an hour"? (एकां होराम्.) Which word means "for"? (None: the time says it by its form.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ईदृशः (īdṛśaḥ) — such, of this kind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "the mango is the best of all fruits". (sarveṣu phaleṣu āmraṃ śreṣṭham.)

You'll want to know: ईदृशः.
ईदृशः — īdṛśaḥ — "such, of this kind". It agrees with its noun: ईदृशम् (īdṛśam) for a neuter noun, ईदृशी (īdṛśī) for a feminine one.
ईदृशः अनुभवः मह्यं रोचते। īdṛśaḥ anubhavaḥ mahyaṃ rocate. — "I like an experience like this."
ईदृशं नगरम् अहं न दृष्टवान्। īdṛśaṃ nagaram ahaṃ na dṛṣṭavān. — "I have not seen a city like this."

The word, taken apart.
ईदृशः (īdṛśaḥ) is built from ī, "this", and the root √dṛś, "to see", the root of द्रक्ष्यति: "looking like this" (CDIAL 1611 gives the two parts). Monier-Williams compares Latin tālis, "such". It lives on as Hindi aisā, "such, like this" (CDIAL 1611).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: īdṛśaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Such an experience" — īdṛśaḥ anubhavaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Such a city" — īdṛśaṃ nagaram]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ईदृशः (īdṛśaḥ) mean? ("Such, of this kind".) "Such a city"? (ईदृशं नगरम्.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
ई — the long ī, standing alone.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ई and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: say ईदृशः (īdṛśaḥ), "such". Then name the sign ी and the letter इ. (ī, i.)
[pause 2 seconds]
The long sign ी has a standing form, and ईदृशः (īdṛśaḥ) opens with it.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ई.
ई — ī.
It is an independent vowel: इ (i) made long. On a consonant the same vowel is the sign ी, as in नदी (nadī).
What it is made of:
the continuous body of इ.
a separate upper curl.
the top shirorekhā.
You have met it here:
ईदृशः īdṛśaḥ — such.

Writing: ई.
1. descend the upright, turn through both bowls, and finish down-right through the tail without lifting.
2. lift at the headline junction and sweep the upper curl upward, left, and around to the right.
3. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 2. The pen comes up 2 times and no more.
Verified modern printed teaching form; everyday handwriting may simplify the curves.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari ई (ī) stroke order.svg’, panels 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
