French, chapter 467: Qualités et défauts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
têtu — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to sulk, then the French for to tease.

You'll want to know: têtu.
têtu — "stubborn".
Mon grand-père est têtu: il ne change jamais d'avis. My grandfather is stubborn: he never changes his mind.
Built on la tête: someone with a hard head. Feminine têtue.

The word, taken apart.
têtu is built on Old French teste "head" (EtymDB), now tête, which is inherited from Latin testa "an earthen pot, a shell" (Lewis & Short).

la paresse — "laziness".
Le dimanche, je me laisse aller à la paresse. On Sundays I give in to laziness.

The word, taken apart.
la paresse is inherited, through Old French peresce, from Latin pigritia "sluggishness" (EtymDB), built on piger "reluctant, slow" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: avare.
avare — "stingy, mean with money; miser".
Il n'est pas avare, mais il compte chaque euro. He is not stingy, but he counts every euro.
Also a noun: un avare — a miser.

The word, taken apart.
avare is a learned borrowing from Latin avārus "greedy", related to avēre "to long for, to crave" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
maladroit — clumsy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for laziness, then the French for stingy.

You'll want to know: maladroit.
maladroit — "clumsy".
Je suis maladroit: j'ai encore cassé un verre. I am clumsy: I broke another glass.
The opposite is adroit "skilful". Une maladresse is a clumsy act.

The word, taken apart.
maladroit is built from mal "badly" and adroit "skilful" (EtymDB). Adroit comes from à droit "rightly", and droit is inherited from Latin dīrēctus "straight".

Lesson 5 of 5.
étourdi — scatterbrained, absent-minded.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for stingy, then the French for clumsy.

You'll want to know: étourdi.
étourdi — "scatterbrained, absent-minded".
Ma sœur est un peu étourdie; elle oublie souvent ses clés. My sister is a bit scatterbrained; she often forgets her keys.
Also dazed, after a blow: il est encore étourdi.

The word, taken apart.
étourdi is the participle of étourdir "to daze". It is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin exturdīre, built on turdus "a thrush", a bird once thought to be easily dazed; the origin is not fully settled.
