Lesson 1 of 5.
कालः (kālaḥ) — a period, time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for jealousy, then the Sanskrit for pride.

You'll want to know: कालः.
कालः — kālaḥ — "a period, time".

The word, taken apart.
kāla "time" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and MW derives it from the root √kal "calculate, count". CDIAL 3084 keeps it apart from kāla "black" (CDIAL 3083), and it lives on as Hindi kāl "time, fate" (CDIAL 3084).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वारः (vāraḥ) — a turn, a day of the week.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for pride, then the Sanskrit for a period.

You'll want to know: वारः.
वारः — vāraḥ — "a turn, a day of the week".

The word, taken apart.
vāra comes from the root √vṛ "cover, enclose" (MW): "anything enclosed in space or time", so an appointed place, a person's turn, and the day of the week (CDIAL 11547). It lives on as Marathi and Gujarati vār "day of the week" (CDIAL 11547).

Lesson 3 of 5.
तिथिः (tithiḥ) — a lunar day, a date.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a period, then the Sanskrit for a turn.

You'll want to know: तिथिः.
तिथिः — tithiḥ — "a lunar day, a date".

The word, taken apart.
tithi "a lunar day", a thirtieth of the moon's month, is a word of the ritual manuals (CDIAL 5810). Neither MW nor CDIAL gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain.

Lesson 4 of 5.
दिनांकः (dināṅkaḥ) — a calendar date.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a turn, then the Sanskrit for a lunar day.

You'll want to know: दिनांकः.
दिनांकः — dināṅkaḥ — "a calendar date".

The word, taken apart.
dināṅka is built from dina "day", which MW compares with Latin nundinae "market day", and aṅka "mark, number" (MW): "the day's number". The pairing for a calendar date is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
शताब्दी (śatābdī) — a century.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lunar day, then the Sanskrit for a calendar date.

You'll want to know: शताब्दी.
शताब्दी — śatābdī — "a century".

The word, taken apart.
śatābdī is built from śata "hundred" and abda "year" (MW): "a period of a hundred years". MW sets śata beside Latin centum and English hundred; using it for a century is modern.
