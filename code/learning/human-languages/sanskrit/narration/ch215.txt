Lesson 1 of 5.
चलः (calaḥ) — moving, unsteady.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for without, then the Sanskrit for towards.

You'll want to know: चलः.
चलः — calaḥ — "moving, unsteady".

The word, taken apart.
cala is built on the root √cal, "to move, stir, tremble" (MW; CDIAL 4713). MW compares Latin celer, "swift". The verb calati lives on as Hindi calnā, "to move, go" (CDIAL 4715).

Lesson 3 of 5.
तीक्ष्णः (tīkṣṇaḥ) — sharp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for moving, then the Sanskrit for slow.

You'll want to know: तीक्ष्णः.
तीक्ष्णः — tīkṣṇaḥ — "sharp".

The word, taken apart.
tīkṣṇa is built on the root √tij, "to be or become sharp" (MW). In the Rigveda it means "sharp", later also "hot, pungent" (CDIAL 5839). It lives on as Hindi tīkhā, "sharp, pungent". MW compares √tij with Greek stizō, "I prick".

The word, taken apart.
kuṇṭha is of uncertain origin. CDIAL 3261 compares Persian kund, "blunt", but also counts it among a set of words for defects whose source is not settled. MW files it under a root √kuṇṭh.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मूल्यवान् (mūlyavān) — valuable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sharp, then the Sanskrit for blunt.

You'll want to know: मूल्यवान्.
मूल्यवान् — mūlyavān — "valuable".

The word, taken apart.
mūlyavān is the nominative of mūlyavat, built on mūlya, "original value, price", and the suffix -vat, "having" (MW). Mūlya comes from mūla, "root", so a price is the root value of a thing (CDIAL 10257). Gujarati mūl, "price", comes from it.
