Mandarin Chinese, chapter 523: Buying and Selling.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
pīfā — to sell wholesale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a doorbell, then the Mandarin for the floor of a room.

You'll want to know: pīfā.
pīfā — "to sell wholesale".
zhè jiā shāngdiàn pīfā shuǐguǒ — This shop sells fruit wholesale.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
pīfā is built from pī and 发 'to send out', both inherited from Middle Chinese, phej and pjot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to strike', 'to push' and 'to pass on'; how the pair came to mean selling in bulk is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
买卖 (mǎimai) — buying and selling for a living; a business deal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the floor of a room, then the Mandarin for to sell wholesale.

You'll want to know: 买卖.
买卖 — mǎimai — "buying and selling for a living; a business deal".
他做了二十年买卖 tā zuò le èrshí nián mǎimai — He has made his living buying and selling for twenty years.
The new character, in pieces: 卖 (mài) — eight strokes in two pieces: 十 on top, two strokes; and 买 under it, six strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
买卖 (mǎimai) is built from 买 'to buy' and 卖 (mài) 'to sell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, meaX and meaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, 'to trade' and the second 'to put goods out'. The two differ only in their tones, and in the word the second is said light.

Lesson 3 of 6.
卖 (mài) — 十 on top, then 买 under it.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 十 on top, then 买 under it and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 十 on top, then 买 under it.
卖 (mài).
Two pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The top piece, 十, two strokes: a short bar, then a short vertical down through it.
The bottom piece, 买, six strokes, as you wrote it: a bar that hooks down at its right end, two short strokes falling to the right under it, a long bar, a long stroke falling to the left and a long stroke falling to the right.
2 + 6 = eight: to sell is to buy with 十 on top.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 卖 (mài), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 卖 (mài) have? (Two: 十 and 买.) How does 卖 (mài) differ from 买? (It has 十 on top.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
卖家 (màijiā) — seller.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sell wholesale, then the Mandarin for buying and selling for a living.

You'll want to know: 卖家.
卖家 — màijiā — "seller".
我觉得这个卖家最好 wǒ juéde zhège màijiā zuì hǎo — I think this seller is the best.

The word, taken apart.
卖家 (màijiā) is built from 卖 (mài) 'to sell' and 家, both inherited from Middle Chinese, meaH and kae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'a dwelling'; its use for the one who does a trade is not given there.

Lesson 6 of 6.
língshòu — to sell retail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the seller (in a sale), then the Mandarin for the buyer (in a sale).

You'll want to know: língshòu.
língshòu — "to sell retail".
zhè jiā shāngdiàn zhǐ língshòu bù pīfā — This shop sells only retail, not wholesale.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
língshòu is built from líng and shòu 'to sell', both inherited from Middle Chinese, leng and dzyuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to fall' and, after an older dictionary, as a slow rain; the sense 'in small bits' is not given there. It glosses the second 'to sell goods'.
