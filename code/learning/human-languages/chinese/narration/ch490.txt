Mandarin Chinese, chapter 490: Pressure at Work.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guǎnlǐ — to manage; to be in charge of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a position in a firm, then the Mandarin for to get promoted.

You'll want to know: guǎnlǐ.
guǎnlǐ — "to manage; to be in charge of".
tā guǎnlǐ yì jiā fàndiàn — She manages a restaurant.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: guǎn is said with a rising tone before lǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
guǎnlǐ is built from guǎn 'to be in charge' and 理 'to put in order', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwanX and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a pipe (a wind instrument)' and 'to be in charge', and the second 'to work jade' and 'a pattern'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 3 of 5.
yèbān — night shift.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to manage, then the Mandarin for to work together.

You'll want to know: yèbān.
yèbān — "night shift".
zuò le yì nián yèbān yǐhòu tā chángcháng shīmián — After a year of night shifts, he often couldn't sleep.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yèbān is built from yè 'night' and bān 'a shift', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yaeH and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'dusk' and the second 'to divide jade tokens'; the sense 'a turn of work' is not given there.
