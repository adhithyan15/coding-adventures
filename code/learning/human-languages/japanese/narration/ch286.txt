Japanese, chapter 286: A Baby Is Born.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
あかちゃん (akachan) — a baby.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a commemoration, then the Japanese for a public holiday.

You'll want to know: あかちゃん.
あかちゃん — akachan — "a baby".
あかちゃんが ないています。 — akachan ga naite imasu — "The baby is crying."

The word, taken apart.
あかちゃん (akachan) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from aka, "red" (from akai), and -chan, "the affectionate suffix" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

うまれる — umareru — "to be born".
いもうとが うまれた とき、月が きれいでした。 — imōto ga umareta toki, tsuki ga kirei deshita — "When my little sister was born, the moon was beautiful."
Polite うまれます, umaremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): あかちゃんが うまれました。, akachan ga umaremashita.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 3 of 5.
しゅっさん (shussan) — childbirth; giving birth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a baby, then the Japanese for to be born.

You'll want to know: しゅっさん.
しゅっさん — shussan — "childbirth; giving birth".
あねは らいげつ しゅっさんの よていです。 — ane wa raigetsu shussan no yotei desu — "My elder sister is due to give birth next month."

The word, taken apart.
しゅっさん (shussan) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shutsu, "exit" (Middle Chinese tsyhwit) and san, "give birth" (sreanX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The first is shortened from shutsu to shus- before san.
