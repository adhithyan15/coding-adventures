## You'll want to know: énerver
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-C469-HUMEUR-04]; assesses=[] -->

**énerver** — "to annoy, to get on someone's nerves; (s'énerver) to get worked up".

> Je m'énerve quand le train est en retard.
>
> *I get worked up when the train is late.*

Often reflexive: *s'énerver*, past *je me suis énervé*. *Ça m'énerve* — that gets on my nerves.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*énerver* is a learned borrowing from Latin *ēnervāre* "to cut the sinews, to weaken" (EtymDB), built on *nervus* "a sinew" (Lewis & Short). French later gave it the sense "to set the nerves on edge".
