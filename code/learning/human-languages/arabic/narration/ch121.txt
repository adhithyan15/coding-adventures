Lesson 1 of 5.
ملفوف (malfūf) — a cabbage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for lettuce, then the Arabic for an aubergine.
[pause 1 second]
Say "dog" and "cat." (Kalb, like Hebrew kelev; qiṭṭ, compared with Latin cattus.)

You'll want to know: ملفوف.
ملفوف — malfūf — "a cabbage".

The word, taken apart.
ملفوف (malfūf) is built on the native Arabic root l-f-f, "to wrap, to roll up" (Lane). Malfūf is the passive participle, "wrapped", for the cabbage's folded leaves.

You'll want to know: نعناع.
نعناع — naʿnāʿ — "mint".

The word, taken apart.
نعناع (naʿnāʿ) is a reduplicated word with no entry in Lane. EtymDB sets it beside Syriac nanʿā, "mint", but which way the word travelled is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بقدونس (baqdūnis) — parsley.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: بقدونس.
بقدونس — baqdūnis — "parsley".

The word, taken apart.
بقدونس (baqdūnis) is usually traced to Greek makedonisi, "Macedonian (parsley)", the same word that gave Turkish maydanoz; the route into Arabic is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
زعتر (zaʿtar) — thyme.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for mint, then the Arabic for parsley.

You'll want to know: زعتر.
زعتر — zaʿtar — "thyme".

The word, taken apart.
زعتر (zaʿtar) is Lane's saʿtar or ṣaʿtar, a well-known herb, and he notes that in his day it was also written with z. An Aramaic source has been suggested, and its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
كمون (kammūn) — cumin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for parsley, then the Arabic for thyme.

You'll want to know: كمون.
كمون — kammūn — "cumin".

The word, taken apart.
كمون (kammūn) is an old Semitic plant name; Akkadian kamūnu is its relative, and English cumin came through Greek kyminon from a Semitic word of this kind.
