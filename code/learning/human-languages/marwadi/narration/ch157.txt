Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 157: Not Today.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कोनी (konī) — not.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say no, the way you turn down a price: कोनी (konī)। Then say I drink tea: हूं चाय पीऊं हूं।.

Grammar Lens: कोनी before the verb.
The word you refuse with also turns a sentence round. Put कोनी (konī) in front of the verb, and let the हूं or है at the end go:
हूं चाय कोनी पीऊं। hū̃ chāy konī pīū̃. — I don't drink tea.
वो मांस कोनी खावै। vo mā̃s konī khāvai. — He doesn't eat meat.

Grammar Lens: कोनी in place of है.
With no other verb, कोनी (konī) stands where है stood:
राम आज अठै कोनी। rām āj aṭhai konī. — Ram isn't here today.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: हूं मांस कोनी खाऊं। — hū̃ mā̃s konī khāū̃. — I don't eat meat.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: आप चाय कोनी पीवो। — āp chāy konī pīvo. — You don't drink tea.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: दूध गरम कोनी। — dūdh garam konī. — The milk isn't hot.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Where does कोनी (konī) go? (In front of the verb.) What goes from the end? (हूं or है.)
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
Say that you don't drink tea.


Lesson 2 of 5.
मत (mat) — don't.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask for tea: चाय लावो। Then say I don't drink tea.

Grammar Lens: मत before a request.
मत (mat) in front of a request makes it don't:
मत जावो। mat jāvo. — Don't go.
चाय मत लावो। chāy mat lāvo. — Don't bring tea.
कोनी (konī) says what is not so; मत (mat) asks someone not to. Old Marwari had mati in the same use (CDIAL 9981).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: अबार मत जावो। — abār mat jāvo. — Don't go now.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: मत भूलो। — mat bhūlo. — Don't forget.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: हूं चाय कोनी पीऊं। चाय मत लावो। — hū̃ chāy konī pīū̃. chāy mat lāvo. — I don't drink tea. Don't bring tea.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Don't go? (मत (mat) जावो।) I don't go? (हूं कोनी (konī) जाऊं।).
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Ask someone politely not to go.


Lesson 3 of 5.
समझणो (samajhṇo) — to understand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say to know, to learn and to think. (जाणणो, सीखणो, सोचणो.)

You'll want to know: समझणो.
समझणो — samajhṇo — "to understand".
हूं थोड़ु समझूं हूं। hū̃ thoṛu samajhū̃ hū̃. — I understand a little.
हूं कोनी समझूं। hū̃ konī samajhū̃. — I don't understand.
It holds a letter you have not written yet: jh, a ज with a breath after it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
समझणो (samajhṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit sambudhyate, "wakes up, understands". CDIAL lists Old Marwari samajhaï, "understands" (CDIAL 12959).

What you've built.
Its stem समझ ends in a consonant, so it goes like बोलणो: समझूं हूं, समझै है, समझो हो.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: samajhṇo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: हूं कोनी समझूं। — hū̃ konī samajhū̃. — I don't understand.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: वो थोड़ु समझै है। — vo thoṛu samajhai hai. — He understands a little.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does समझणो (samajhṇo) mean? ("To understand".) I don't understand? (हूं कोनी (konī) समझूं।).
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Say the Marwadi for 'to understand'.


Lesson 4 of 5.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: झ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

Script you'll notice: झ.
झ — jha, the breathed j.
It sits inside समझणो (samajhṇo). It pairs with ज as छ pairs with च: the same place in the mouth, with a breath. On the page it is one joined body, with a short upper stem, two loops and a diagonal tail, then a middle crossbar, a right stem and the headline.

Writing: झ.
1. descend the short upper stem, turn left and curve clockwise around the upper bowl, continue through the waist and clockwise around the lower loop, then finish down-right through the diagonal tail without lifting.
2. lift at the central junction and draw the middle crossbar left-to-right.
3. without lifting, climb up the right stem to the headline.
4. descend the right stem top-to-bottom.
5. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 2.
The source shows four movements; native writers draw झ (jha) as three pen-down strokes (53% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 44 of 83). This ductus follows the four movements in order and direction, lifting only before the crossbar and the headline: the crossbar ends on the right stem, so the pen climbs the stem before descending it.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-झ (jha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
