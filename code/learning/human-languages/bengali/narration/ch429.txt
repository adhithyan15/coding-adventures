Bengali, chapter 429: Storms and Hail.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
কালবৈশাখী (kālboishākhī) — a nor'wester; a spring thunderstorm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for damp (and dank), then the Bengali for to be covered over.

You'll want to know: কালবৈশাখী.
কালবৈশাখী — kālboishākhī — "a nor'wester; a spring thunderstorm".
বিকেলে কালবৈশাখী এসে অনেক গাছ ভেঙে দিল। bikele kālboishākhī ese ônek gāchh bhenge dilô. In the afternoon a nor'wester came and broke many trees.
The sudden violent storms of April and May, in the month of Baishakh.

The word, taken apart.
কালবৈশাখী (kālboishākhī) joins কাল, "black, deadly", to বৈশাখী, "of the month of Baishakh", from Sanskrit vaiśākha, the spring month (Monier-Williams): the black storm of Baishakh.

Lesson 3 of 5.
গর্জানো (gôrjāno) — to roar; to rumble (of thunder or the sea).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a nor'wester, then the Bengali for hail.

You'll want to know: গর্জানো.
গর্জানো — gôrjāno — "to roar; to rumble (of thunder or the sea)".
সারারাত মেঘ গর্জাল। sārārāt megh gôrjālô. The clouds rumbled all night.
Past (he/she …): সেই সমুদ্র গর্জাল, sei sômudrô gôrjālô.

The word, taken apart.
গর্জানো (gôrjāno) probably goes back to Sanskrit gárjati, "thunders, roars" (CDIAL 4046), with the Bengali verb ending -আনো; CDIAL lists no Bengali form there, so the link is hedged.

Lesson 4 of 5.
কাঁপানো (kā̃pāno) — to shake (something); to make tremble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for hail, then the Bengali for to roar.

You'll want to know: কাঁপানো.
কাঁপানো — kā̃pāno — "to shake (something); to make tremble".
ঝড় জানালাগুলো কাঁপাল। jhôṛ jānālāgulo kā̃pālô. The storm rattled the windows.
Past (he/she …): সেই ঝড় জানালা কাঁপাল, sei jhôṛ jānālā kā̃pālô.
The causing partner of কাঁপা, to shiver: you কাঁপো with cold, and the wind কাঁপায় the door.

The word, taken apart.
কাঁপানো (kā̃pāno) is the causing form of কাঁপা, "to shiver, to tremble", from Sanskrit kampate, "trembles" (CDIAL 2767).

Lesson 5 of 5.
নুয়ে পড়া (nuye pôṛā) — to bend low; to droop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to roar, then the Bengali for to shake (something).

You'll want to know: নুয়ে পড়া.
নুয়ে পড়া — nuye pôṛā — "to bend low; to droop".
ঝড়ে গাছগুলো নুয়ে পড়ল। jhôṛe gāchhgulo nuye pôṛlô. The trees bent low in the storm.
Past (he/she …): সেই গাছ নুয়ে পড়ল, sei gāchh nuye pôṛlô.
Also of a person bowed with age or a load: বুড়ো লোকটা নুয়ে হাঁটেন.

The word, taken apart.
নুয়ে পড়া (nuye pôṛā) joins নুয়ে, the -এ form of নোয়া, "to bow", which CDIAL lists as Bengali nuyā, "bows, stoops", under Sanskrit námati, "bends, bows" (CDIAL 6956), to পড়া, to fall.
