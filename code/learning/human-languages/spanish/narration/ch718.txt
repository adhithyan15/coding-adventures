Spanish, chapter 718: Guiños y bromas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guiñar — to wink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tear, then the Spanish for desperate.

You'll want to know: guiñar.
guiñar — "to wink".
Me guiñó un ojo y sonreí. He winked at me and I smiled.
Guiñar un ojo — to wink. Un guiño — a wink.

The word, taken apart.
For guiñar the origin is not settled; a Germanic source has been suggested, and some see an imitative word, but none is proved.

Lesson 2 of 5.
bromista — joker; fond of jokes.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for desperate, then the Spanish for to wink.

You'll want to know: bromista.
bromista — "joker; fond of jokes".
Mi tío es muy bromista. My uncle loves a joke.
Same form for men and women: es una bromista.

The word, taken apart.
bromista is built on broma "joke" with the ending -ista; broma comes from Greek brôma "food" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la broma — the joke, the prank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to wink, then the Spanish for joker.

You'll want to know: la broma.
la broma — "the joke, the prank".
Era una broma, no te enfades. It was a joke, do not be angry.
Gastar una broma — to play a joke. En broma — jokingly.

The word, taken apart.
broma comes from Greek brôma "food" (EtymDB), through Latin. It first named the shipworm that eats ship timber, then any nuisance, and at last a joke.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: burlarse.
burlarse — "to make fun of".
No te burles de tu hermano. Do not make fun of your brother.
Burlarse de alguien. Less kind than bromear.

The word, taken apart.
burlarse is built on burla "mockery". For burla the origin is not settled; one old guess links it with Latin burrae "trifles".
