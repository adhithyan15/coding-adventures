# mudarse — changing where you live

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CASA, ES-LEX-C355-WHERE-33] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la casa** and **el piso**. You are about to leave one of them.

## You'll want to know: mudarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CASA] -->

**mudarse** — to move house.

*Me mudo el sábado* — I move on Saturday. *Nos mudamos a un piso más grande* —
we are moving to a bigger flat. *¿Cuándo te mudas?* — when do you move?

Spanish does not reach for a verb of carrying here. It reaches for a verb of
**changing**, and that is the whole idea: you have not transported yourself,
you have changed which address is yours.

## Grammar Lens: what the -se is doing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-01, ES-LEX-C355-WHERE-33] -->

There is a plain verb **mudar** underneath, and it means to change something
else:

| without -se | with -se |
|---|---|
| *la serpiente muda la piel* — sheds its skin | *me mudo a Sevilla* — I move |
| *mudar de opinión* — change your mind | *se muda de ropa* — changes clothes |

On the left the verb reaches out to something: a skin, an opinion. On the
right it turns back and lands on the person. Once it lands on you, the thing
being changed is understood to be **where you live** — or, in a bedroom, what
you are wearing. Context decides between those two, and it decides easily,
because nobody packs a lorry to change their shirt.

Note that *mudarse* takes **a** for the destination — *me mudo a Madrid* —
the same **a** you already use for going anywhere.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-01] -->

Latin **mutare**, to change. It is a wide family and you can hear it in
English: a **mutation** is a change, to **mute** a sound is to alter it, and a
bird in **moult** is changing its feathers — the same picture as the snake.

Spanish keeps the plain sense in *mudar de opinión* and the specialised one in
*mudarse*. A word narrowing from "change anything" to "change address" is
ordinary: the commonest thing people announce they are changing is where they
sleep.
