## You'll want to know: ほうちょう

**ほうちょう** — *hōchō* — "kitchen knife".

> **ちちは ほうちょうを よく つかいます。** — *chichi wa hōchō o yoku tsukaimasu* — "My father often uses a kitchen knife."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ほうちょう** (*hōchō*) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. Today they are *hō*, "wrap", and *chō*, a character that also counts tools (Middle Chinese *teng*); JMdict also lists an older spelling whose first character the Guangyun glosses "kitchen" (*baew*), so the history of the pair is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
