Hindi, chapter 522: Milk and Curd.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
छाछ (chāch) — buttermilk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to temper (with hot spiced oil), then the Hindi for to roast.

You'll want to know: छाछ.
छाछ — chāch — "buttermilk".
गर्मी में हम दोपहर को ठंडी छाछ पीते हैं। Garmī meṁ ham dopahar ko ṭhaṇḍī chāch pīte haiṁ. In summer we drink cold buttermilk at midday.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
छाछ (chāch) is a tadbhava, inherited from chācchī, "buttermilk", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 5012).

Lesson 4 of 5.
खौलना (khaulnā) — to seethe; to boil hard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to churn, then the Hindi for a starter (for setting curd).

You'll want to know: खौलना.
खौलना — khaulnā — "to seethe; to boil hard".
कड़ाही में तेल खौल रहा है। Kaṛāhī meṁ tel khaul rahā hai. The oil in the wok is seething.
Past (it …): वह पानी खौल उठा, vah pānī khaul uṭhā.

The word, taken apart.
खौलना (khaulnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from kha-ul, "be heated", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 3760). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to be heated, boil".

Lesson 5 of 5.
निथारना (nithārnā) — to drain off (liquid); to let settle and pour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a starter (for setting curd), then the Hindi for to seethe.

You'll want to know: निथारना.
निथारना — nithārnā — "to drain off (liquid); to let settle and pour".
पनीर बनाकर माँ ने ऊपर का पानी निथारा। Panīr banākar māṁ ne ūpar kā pānī nithārā. After making the paneer my mother drained off the water on top.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दाल का पानी निथारा, usne dāl kā pānī nithārā.

The word, taken apart.
निथारना (nithārnā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit nistārayati, "delivers from" (CDIAL 7532). CDIAL gives the Hindi verb as "to draw off, purify".
