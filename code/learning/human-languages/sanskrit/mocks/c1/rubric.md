# Sanskrit C1 mock rubric

Score either C1 mock against
[`task-shapes/c1.json`](../../task-shapes/c1.json). Reading, listening, writing,
and speaking are independent 100-point papers. A learner passes only by earning
at least **60/100 on every paper** in one administration; a 240/400 total
cannot compensate for a failed skill, and every section must be attempted.

## Reading — 100 points

Dense public, professional, academic, and literary texts are worth 25 points
each. Divide each part evenly among its nine keyed items and retain one decimal
place. Score argument and discourse structure, rhetorical or professional
purpose, evidence and qualification, unstated implications, register effects,
narrative voice, figurative meaning, cultural inference, precise textual
support, and external-sandhi decoding. Credit the answer best supported by the
source even when a distractor is generally plausible.

## Listening — 100 points

Unscripted discussion, lecture, interview, and narrative parts are worth 25
points each. Divide each part evenly among its seven keyed items. Play every
part once at 160–185 words per minute with natural variation, after one unscored
orienting preview. Natural overlap, reformulation, implicit alignment, and
documented regional features may appear, but acoustic difficulty must not
replace language comprehension. No transcript, romanization, dictionary,
translator, or learner-controlled replay is allowed.

Each form must use two distinct actual documented regional Sanskrit
pronunciation traditions for the designated multi-speaker parts. The
confidential record must retain the actual tradition names, speaker provenance
and reviewer confirmation by a qualified Sanskrit reviewer before operational
use. A label alone is not evidence. Documented alternatives used consistently
receive full credit when they satisfy the task.

## Writing — 100 points

The 180–220 word source synthesis is worth 40 points: source accuracy 10,
selection and integration 8, organisation and cohesion 7, audience and register
5, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 7, and orthographic control 3. It must
distinguish source claims from the writer's additions. The 300–380 word extended
response is worth 60 points: task fulfilment and intellectual development 17,
organisation and cohesion 12, genre, audience, and register control 9, Sanskrit
vocabulary and grammar 16, and orthographic control 6. A response outside its
band loses only credit for evidence it fails to provide; there is no automatic
length penalty.

Orthographic control includes ordered and recognizable letter bodies, vowel
signs and conjuncts, external sandhi, continuous śirorekhā where the word
requires it, word-final visarga or anusvara, and clear word boundaries.
Romanized, traced, translated, copied, non-Devanagari, spell-checked, or
answer-model-dependent work earns no independent writing credit.

## Speaking — 100 points

The 270–300 second source-based presentation is worth 35 points: source
synthesis 9, argument 9, organisation 6, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 7,
and pronunciation and fluency 4. The 300–360 second collaborative synthesis is
worth 30 points: source integration 7, interaction and repair 7, negotiation
and conclusion 7, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 6, and pronunciation and
fluency 3. The 330–420 second challenge and defence is worth 35 points:
reasoned defence 9, qualification and reformulation 7, interaction and repair
6, register control 4, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 6, and pronunciation
and fluency 3.

Permit only paper keyword notes made during fifteen minutes of preparation,
never a written answer script or electronic notes. Accept a consistent
documented pronunciation tradition. Score paired or grouped learners only on
their own contributions; another learner's performance cannot compensate.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Classical Sanskrit is in scope; Vedic accent and morphology remain outside
this assessment. This project-defined approximation is not an external CEFR
qualification. These learner papers, confidential masters, keys, and rubric
are not qualified Sanskrit recordings, independent human review, calibrated
double-scoring, or standard setting. Book-only validation cannot establish a
readiness or validated-pass claim. Those artifacts remain separate
prerequisites, and `assessment.json` remains absent until they exist.
