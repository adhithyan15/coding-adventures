Japanese, chapter 228: Learning, Slacking and Graduating.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
まなぶ (manabu) — to learn; to study in depth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to make sure, then the Japanese for to compare.

You'll want to know: まなぶ.
まなぶ — manabu — "to learn; to study in depth".
あねは がっこうで にほんごを まなびます。 — ane wa gakkō de nihongo o manabimasu — "My elder sister studies Japanese at school."
Polite まなびます, manabimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
まなぶ (manabu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
やくす (yakusu) — to translate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to compare, then the Japanese for to learn.

You'll want to know: やくす.
やくす — yakusu — "to translate".
ちちは にほんごの てがみを やくします。 — chichi wa nihongo no tegami o yakushimasu — "My father translates letters written in Japanese."
Polite やくします, yakushimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
やくす (yakusu) is a mixed word, built on Sino-Japanese yaku, "translate" (Middle Chinese yek), borrowed from Chinese, with a native verb ending -su (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
そつぎょう (sotsugyō) — graduation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to slack off, then the Japanese for to quit.

You'll want to know: そつぎょう.
そつぎょう — sotsugyō — "graduation".
あねは らいねん そつぎょうです。 — ane wa rainen sotsugyō desu — "My elder sister graduates next year."

The word, taken apart.
そつぎょう (sotsugyō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sotsu, "graduate, finish" (Middle Chinese tswit) and gyō, "work, vocation" (ngjaep) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
