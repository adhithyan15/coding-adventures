Persian, chapter 464: Shops and deals.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مغازه (maghâze) — a shop (US: a store).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for jewels, then the Persian for a perfume.

You'll want to know: مغازه.
مغازه (maghâze) — "a shop (US: a store)".
این مغازه تا ساعت ده شب باز است. In maghâze tâ sâ'at-e dah-e shab bâz ast. This shop is open until ten at night.
The everyday word; forushgâh is a bigger store.

The word, taken apart.
maghâze was borrowed from Russian magazín 'shop' (EtymDB), which comes in the end from Arabic maḫāzin 'storehouses'. French magasin and English magazine go back to the same Arabic word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مغازه (maghâze) دار (maghâzedâr) — a shopkeeper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a perfume, then the Persian for a shop.

You'll want to know: مغازه دار.
مغازه (maghâze) دار (maghâzedâr) — "a shopkeeper".
مغازه (maghâze) دار (maghâzedâr) به ما تخفیف داد. Maghâzedâr be mâ takhfif dâd. The shopkeeper gave us a discount.
maghâze 'shop' + dâr 'keeper'.

The word, taken apart.
maghâzedâr is built from maghâze 'shop', which EtymDB takes from Russian magazín, and native dâr 'holder, keeper', from the stem of dâshtan 'to have'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
معامله کردن (mo'âmele kardan) — to do a deal, to trade.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a shop, then the Persian for a shopkeeper.

You'll want to know: معامله کردن.
معامله کردن (mo'âmele kardan) — "to do a deal, to trade".
پدرم سالها با این فروشنده معامله کرده است. Pedaram sâl-hâ bâ in forushande mo'âmele karde ast. My father has done business with this seller for years.
mo'âmele 'a deal' + kardan; the other side takes bâ.

The word, taken apart.
mo'âmele kardan is built from mo'âmele 'a deal', borrowed from Arabic muʿāmala 'conduct, dealing' (EtymDB), and kardan 'to do', inherited from Middle Persian kardan (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
بیعانه (bey'âne) — a down payment (to hold a purchase); a deposit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a shopkeeper, then the Persian for to do a deal.

You'll want to know: بیعانه.
بیعانه (bey'âne) — "a down payment (to hold a purchase); a deposit".
برای فرش بیعانه دادیم و فردا بقیه پول را میدهیم. Barâ-ye farsh bey'âne dâdim va fardâ baqiye-ye pul râ midahim. We paid a deposit on the carpet and we'll pay the rest tomorrow.
Paid when you agree to buy: a house, a car, a carpet.

The word, taken apart.
bey'âne is usually traced to Arabic bayʿ 'sale', with the Persian ending -âne. EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
گران فروختن (gerân forukhtan) — to overcharge, to sell at too high a price.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to do a deal, then the Persian for a down payment.

You'll want to know: گران فروختن.
گران فروختن (gerân forukhtan) — "to overcharge, to sell at too high a price".
این فروشنده به مسافرها گران میفروشد. In forushande be mosâfer-hâ gerân miforushad. This seller overcharges travellers.
gerân 'dear' + forukhtan 'to sell'. Present stem forush-: gerân miforushad.

The word, taken apart.
gerân forukhtan is built from gerân 'dear, expensive', which EtymDB traces through Middle Persian grān to PIE gʷréh₂us 'heavy', and forukhtan 'to sell', usually traced to Middle Persian frōxtan.
