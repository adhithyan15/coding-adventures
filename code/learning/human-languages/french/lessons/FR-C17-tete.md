# la tête — "the head"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The first body part, and one of the best etymologies in French. The
word for *head* is not the Latin word for head.

## The word

> **la tête** — the head. Feminine, so **la**.

## Latin had a perfectly good word, and French threw it away

Latin's word for "head" was ***caput***. You can see it surviving all over
Europe:

| | |
|---|---|
| Portuguese | *cabeça* |
| Spanish | *cabeza* |
| Italian | *capo* (now "chief, boss") |
| English | **cap**tain, **cap**ital, de**cap**itate |

French has *caput* too, and in two layers. **Inherited**, worn down by use:
**chef** ("chief," originally "head"), **chapitre**, **cap** (a headland),
**achever** ("to finish" — to bring to a head). **Re-borrowed** later, straight
from written Latin: **capital**, **capitaine**, **décapiter**.

What French does *not* use it for is the thing on your shoulders.

**Tête** comes from Latin ***testa***: an **earthenware pot**, a shell, a
potsherd.

## Why a pot?

Soldiers' slang. Roman troops called the skull a *testa* the way English speakers
say someone got hit on the **noggin** — a noggin being a small mug. It was a
joke, it spread, and eventually the joke *replaced the real word* in Gaul and
Italy.

So a French speaker saying *j'ai mal à la tête* is, historically, saying "my pot
hurts."

*Testa* is still visible in English **test** — which began as the shallow pot an
alchemist used to assay metals.

## The circumflex is a receipt

Look at the accent: **tê**te. Chapter W01 taught what a circumflex records — a
letter that used to be there, almost always an **s**.

> *testa* → Old French **teste** → modern **tête**

The *s* stopped being pronounced, and the accent was put in to mark the grave.
That is why English, which borrowed from Old French *before* the *s* fell,
still has it in **test**.

soldiers' **slang** for the skull, which replaced the proper word.) Where did
*caput* survive in French? (**Inherited** in *chef*, *chapitre*, *cap*,
*achever*; **re-borrowed** in *capital*, *capitaine*, *décapiter* — just never
for the head itself.) What does the circumflex on *tête* record? (**A
