# (reading) — six words off a counter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-CAFE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word below you have met. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[PT-LEX-LEITE-02, PT-LEX-AMIGO-02] -->

> água
> pão
> café
> leite
> livro
> amigo

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again — and this time say the article you would put in front of each
one. **A** água. **O** pão.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-FAMILIA-02, PT-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Portuguese hangs an article on nearly every noun, and the article is
the gender said out loud.

[PAUSE 2s] That is why a written list like this one is harder than a real
Portuguese page. On a page the article is already there, doing the remembering
for you. Stripped of it, each word makes you do the work yourself.

[PAUSE 2s] Six words, and the hardest thing about them is what is missing.
