Telugu, chapter 512: Falling Ill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
జబ్బు (jabbu) — a sickness; an illness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to go over again, then the Telugu for to remind.

You'll want to know: జబ్బు.
జబ్బు — jabbu — "a sickness; an illness".
ఆయనకు గుండె జబ్బు ఉంది. āyanaku guṇḍe jabbu undi. He has heart trouble.
An everyday word: గుండె జబ్బు (jabbu), heart disease. జబ్బుగా ఉంది, (he) is unwell.

The word, taken apart.
జబ్బు (jabbu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 2338 lists Telugu jabbu, "illness, sickness, weakness", in the article of Tamil cappai, "that which is weak".

Lesson 2 of 5.
జబ్బుపడు (jabbupaḍu) — to fall ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: జబ్బుపడు.
జబ్బుపడు — jabbupaḍu — "to fall ill".
వర్షంలో తడిసి తమ్ముడు జబ్బుపడ్డాడు. varṣaṁlō taḍisi tammuḍu jabbupaḍḍāḍu. My younger brother got wet in the rain and fell ill.
Past (I …): జబ్బుపడ్డాను, jabbupaḍḍānu.
జబ్బు (jabbu) + పడు: to fall into illness.

The word, taken apart.
జబ్బుపడు (jabbupaḍu) is native Dravidian: jabbu, "illness" (DEDR 2338), and the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853).

Lesson 3 of 5.
నలత (nalata) — a slight indisposition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a sickness, then the Telugu for to fall ill.

You'll want to know: నలత.
నలత — nalata — "a slight indisposition".
నిన్నటి నుంచి నాకు కొంచెం నలతగా ఉంది. ninnaṭi nuñci nāku koñceṁ nalatagā undi. Since yesterday I have been feeling a bit off.
Milder than జబ్బు (jabbu): నలతగా ఉంది, (I) feel a bit off.

The word, taken apart.
నలత (nalata) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3611 lists Telugu nalata, "disease", beside naliyu, "to be crushed, to be bruised", and Tamil nalaṅku, "to grow faint".

Lesson 4 of 5.
మంచం పట్టు (mañcaṁ paṭṭu) — to be bedridden; to take to one's bed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to fall ill, then the Telugu for a slight indisposition.

You'll want to know: మంచం పట్టు.
మంచం పట్టు — mañcaṁ paṭṭu — "to be bedridden; to take to one's bed".
జ్వరంతో తాతయ్య వారం రోజులు మంచం పట్టారు. jvarantō tātayya vāraṁ rōjulu mañcaṁ paṭṭāru. With fever, grandpa was in bed for a week.
Past (I …): మంచం పట్టాను, mañcaṁ paṭṭānu.
Literally to take hold of the bed: మంచం, bed, + పట్టు, to hold.

The word, taken apart.
మంచం పట్టు (mañcaṁ paṭṭu) is a Telugu phrase: మంచం, "a bed", from Sanskrit mañca, "a platform, a bed" (CDIAL 9715), and the native verb paṭṭu, "to hold, to seize" (DEDR 4034).

Lesson 5 of 5.
తేరుకో (tērukō) — to come round; to recover from a shock.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a slight indisposition, then the Telugu for to be bedridden.

You'll want to know: తేరుకో.
తేరుకో — tērukō — "to come round; to recover from a shock".
ఆ వార్త నుంచి అమ్మ తేరుకోవడానికి వారం పట్టింది. ā vārta nuñci amma tērukōvaḍāniki vāraṁ paṭṭindi. It took mum a week to get over that news.
Past (I …): తేరుకున్నాను, tērukunnānu.
-నుంచి తేరుకో (tērukō), to get over a shock, a fright or a faint.

The word, taken apart.
తేరుకో (tērukō) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3471 lists Telugu tēṟu with Tamil tēṟu, "to recover from swooning, to be comforted, to cheer up"; -కో is the reflexive ending.
