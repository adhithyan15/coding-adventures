Bengali, chapter 150: To doubt, To argue, To carry on, To sneeze, To cough.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
সন্দেহ করা (sôndeh kôrā) — to doubt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to hurry, then the Bengali for to delay.

You'll want to know: সন্দেহ করা.
সন্দেহ করা — sôndeh kôrā — "to doubt".

The phrase, taken apart.
সন্দেহ করা (sôndeh kôrā) is sôndeh, "doubt", Sanskrit saṁdeha taken over whole, + kôrā, "to do", from Sanskrit karoti, "does" (CDIAL 2814).

চালিয়ে যাওয়া (chāliye jāoyā) — to carry on.

The phrase, taken apart.
চালিয়ে যাওয়া (chāliye jāoyā) is chāliye, "keeping going", a form of chālāno, "to drive, run", which belongs with Bengali cālā, "to be moved", under calyati, "goes" (CDIAL 4721), + jāoyā, "to go", from Sanskrit yāti, "goes" (CDIAL 10452).

হাঁচা (hā̃chā) — to sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to argue, then the Bengali for to carry on.

You'll want to know: হাঁচা.
হাঁচা — hā̃chā — "to sneeze".

The word, taken apart.
হাঁচা (hā̃chā) (hāṁchā) goes back to Sanskrit hañji, "sneezing". CDIAL gives no Bengali form under that entry, but it lists Assamese hāṁci, "sneezing", the forms nearest to Bengali (CDIAL 13941a). CDIAL notes that the Sanskrit word is thought to be imitative.

The word, taken apart.
কাশা (kāshā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kāsate, "coughs" (CDIAL 3135).
