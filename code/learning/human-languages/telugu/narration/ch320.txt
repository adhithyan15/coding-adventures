Telugu, chapter 320: Asking the Way.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ఊరు (ūru) — a village or town; one's home place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a village" (grāmaṁ) and "a town" (paṭṭaṇaṁ). Then "I must go". (nēnu veḷḷāli.)

You'll want to know: ఊరు.
ఊరు — ūru — "a village or town; one's home place".
మీది ఏ ఊరు? mīdi ē ūru? — "Which town are you from?"
మా ఊరు చిన్నది, కానీ చాలా బాగుంటుంది. mā ūru cinnadi, kānī cālā bāguṇṭundi. — "My home town is small, but it is very nice."
మీది (mīdi, "yours") and ఏ (ē, "which"): "yours is which town?", the usual way to ask where someone comes from.
ఊరు (ūru) is wider than గ్రామం or పట్టణం: any place where people live, and most of all the one you come from. Before an ending its -ఉ turns to -ఇ: ఊరికి (ūriki, "to the town, home"), ఊరిలో (ūrilō, "in the town"). ఊరికి వెళ్తున్నాను on its own means "I am going home, to my own place". Many town names end in it, as నెల్లూరు does.

The word, taken apart.
ఊరు (ūru) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil, Malayalam and Kannada ūr and Tulu ūru, all "village, town" (DEDR 752). It is one of the words the whole family shares.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 2 of 5.
ఊ (ū) — the letter that opens ఊరు (ūru).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ఊ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ఉ and ూ once each, and name them. (Short u at the start of a word; long ū after a consonant, as in కూర.) Then write ఈ once.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ఊరు (ūru). This lesson takes its first letter out and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ఊ.
ఊ — ū, a long ū at the start of a word.
After a consonant, long ū is the sign ూ. At the start of a word it stands as a letter of its own, ఊ, and it pairs with the short ఉ you know.
In print ఊ (ū) is built on ఉ: the same round body with the bar across it, and a small curl added on its right, like the curl beside ఈ, with the bar running on into it. Some typefaces also double the short upright stroke on top.

Writing: ఊ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside ఊ (ū) and copy it, slowly, and larger than it is printed. Write the body of ఉ the way you learned it, then copy the small curl on the right and run the bar into it.
This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]

Lesson 3 of 5.
సూటిగా (sūṭigā) — straight on, directly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "slowly" (nemmadigā) and "quickly" (tvaragā). Then say "one's home place". (ūru.)

You'll want to know: సూటిగా.
సూటిగా — sūṭigā — "straight on, directly".
సూటిగా వెళ్ళండి. sūṭigā veḷḷaṇḍi. — "Go straight on."
అతను సూటిగా మాట్లాడతాడు. atanu sūṭigā māṭlāḍatāḍu. — "He speaks plainly, straight to the point."
Like నెమ్మదిగా and త్వరగా, it is a word with -గా on the end, and it answers "how?". On a road it is "straight on"; about speech it is "straight to the point".

The word, taken apart.
సూటిగా (sūṭigā) is native Dravidian: సూటి, sūṭi, "aim", which DEDR 2658 lists with Tamil cuṭṭu, "to point out, to aim at", and the adverb ending -గా. Going straight is going where you aim.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఎలా వెళ్ళాలి? (elā veḷḷāli?) — asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "where?" (ekkaḍa), "how?" (elā) and "I must go" (nēnu veḷḷāli). Then "go straight on". (sūṭigā veḷḷaṇḍi.)

You'll want to know: asking.
క్షమించండి, ఆసుపత్రి ఎక్కడ ఉంది? kṣamiñcaṇḍi, āsupatri ekkaḍa undi? — "Excuse me, where is the hospital?"
ఆసుపత్రికి ఎలా వెళ్ళాలి? āsupatriki elā veḷḷāli? — "How do I get to the hospital?"
The second is "how must (I) go to …", with the -ఆలి of "must".
ఇక్కడి నుంచి దూరమా? ikkaḍi nuñci dūramā? — "Is it far from here?"
-ఆ at the end makes a yes-or-no question: దూరం, "far" becomes దూరమా?, "far?". ఇక్కడి is the form ఇక్కడ takes before నుంచి.

You'll want to know: following the answer.
సూటిగా వెళ్ళండి. రెండవ మలుపులో ఎడమ వైపు తిరగండి. sūṭigā veḷḷaṇḍi. reṇḍava malupulō eḍama vaipu tiragaṇḍi. — "Go straight on. At the second turning, turn left."
ఆసుపత్రి కుడి వైపు ఉంది. దగ్గరే. āsupatri kuḍi vaipu undi. daggarē. — "The hospital is on the right. It is close."
వైపు (vaipu, "side"): ఎడమ వైపు, "on the left, to the left"; కుడి వైపు, "on the right". తిరగండి (tiragaṇḍi, "turn") is the polite command of a verb for turning, with the -అండి of క్షమించండి. దగ్గరే (daggarē, "close by") is దగ్గర with an -ఏ that leans on it.
