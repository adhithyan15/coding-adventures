Urdu, chapter 270: Asking the Way.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
کتنا (kitnā) — how much, how many.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a fare" and "far". (kirāyā, dūr.) A traveller asks about both.

You'll want to know: کتنا.
کتنا — kitnā — "how much; how many".
کرایہ کتنا ہے؟ kirāyā kitnā hai? — "How much is the fare?"
It follows its noun like اچھا (achchhā): کتنا (kitnā) for کرایہ, کتنے (kitne) for a plural.
کتنے مسافر تھے؟ kitne musāfir the? — "How many passengers were there?"
کتنی دور (kitnī dūr), "how far", goes as one pair:
اسٹیشن کتنی دور ہے؟ isṭeshan kitnī dūr hai? — "How far is the station?"

The word, taken apart.
کتنا (kitnā) is inherited. CDIAL lists its Hindi twin, with the sense "how great?", under a reconstructed kiyatta (CDIAL 3167).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kitnā, then kirāyā kitnā hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "How far is the market?" — bāzār kitnī dūr hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does کتنا (kitnā) mean? ("How much, how many".) "How far is the station?" (اسٹیشن کتنی دور ہے؟).


Lesson 2 of 5.
ذرا (zarā) — a little.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "how much is the fare?" (kirāyā kitnā hai?) Then "expensive". (mahãgā.)

You'll want to know: ذرا.
ذرا — zarā — "a little".
یہ ذرا مہنگا ہے۔ yih zarā mahãgā hai. — "This is a little expensive."
ذرا آرام سے۔ zarā ārām se. — "A little more slowly, please."
Before a request it softens it, as "please" does. The next lessons put it to work.

The word, taken apart.
ذرا (zarā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic dharra, "a speck, an atom". Its first letter is new to your hand; the next lesson takes it out.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: zarā, then zarā ārām se]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "This is a little difficult" — yih zarā mushkil hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ذرا (zarā) mean? ("A little".) What does it do before a request? (It softens it.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
ذ (zāl) — the letter that opens ذرا (zarā).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ذ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write غ once, and name it. (ghain.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ذرا (zarā), "a little". This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ذ.
ذ — zāl, the sound z.
It opens ذرا (zarā). You already write د: ذ (zāl) is that shape with one dot above it. Like د, it does not join the letter after it. It is one more way Urdu writes z, beside ز, ض and ظ, and it comes in words from Arabic.

Writing: ذ.
Put your pen on ذ (zāl) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at ذرا (zarā) and point at ذ (zāl) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: ذ (zāl) three times, saying z as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: ذرا (zarā) once, with the dot above its first letter]
[once you have stopped driving — read: ذرا (zarā) aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — ذ? (zāl, z.) What tells it from د? (The dot.) Which word did it come from? (ذرا, zarā, "a little".)


Lesson 4 of 5.
کیسے جاتے ہیں (kaise jāte haiṅ) — asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "where?" (kahāṅ?) Then "how far is the station?" (isṭeshan kitnī dūr hai?) Now ask the way there.

You'll want to know: how do you get there?
اسٹیشن کہاں ہے؟ isṭeshan kahāṅ hai? — "Where is the station?"
اسٹیشن کیسے جاتے ہیں؟ isṭeshan kaise jāte haiṅ? — "How do you get to the station?"
Word for word, "how do people go to the station?": the plural جاتے ہیں (jāte haiṅ) asks about anyone.
کیا راستہ لمبا ہے؟ kyā rāstā lambā hai? — "Is it a long way?"

You'll want to know: the polite request.
سیدھے جائیے۔ sīdhe jāiye. — "Go straight on."
جائیے (jāiye) is جانا asked of someone you call آپ. The stem takes -یے (-iye); after a vowel, -ئیے:
بائیں مڑیے۔ bāeṅ muṛiye. — "Turn left."
پل کے بعد دائیں مڑیے۔ pul ke bād dāeṅ muṛiye. — "After the bridge, turn right."
ذرا بتائیے، بازار کہاں ہے؟ zarā batāiye, bāzār kahāṅ hai? — "Could you tell me where the market is?"
سنیے (suniye), "listen", opens a question to a stranger: "excuse me". A few verbs bend the stem, among them دیجیے (dījiye) from دینا, لیجیے (lījiye) from لینا, کیجیے (kījiye) from کرنا.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Turn left" — bāeṅ muṛiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Turn right" — dāeṅ muṛiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Go straight on" — sīdhe jāiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Ask a stranger, politely, how to get to the station; then tell someone to turn left after the bridge.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"How do you get to the market?" (بازار کیسے جاتے ہیں (kaise jāte haiṅ)؟) "Turn right"? (دائیں مڑیے.) "Please tell me"? (ذرا (zarā) بتائیے.)
