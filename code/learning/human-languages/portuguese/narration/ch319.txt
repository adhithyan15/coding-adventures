Portuguese, chapter 319: Os santos populares.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o santo — the saint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the toast, then the Portuguese for to toast.

You'll want to know: o santo.
o santo — "the saint".
Cada aldeia tem o seu santo. Every village has its own saint.
Also an adjective, ‘holy’: a água santa. Before a name it shortens to São.

The word, taken apart.
santo is inherited from Latin sānctus "holy, sacred" (EtymDB), from sancīre "to make sacred".

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to toast, then the Portuguese for the saint.

You'll want to know: a marcha.
a marcha — "the march; a neighbourhood parade".
A marcha do nosso bairro foi a mais bonita. Our neighbourhood’s parade was the most beautiful.
In Lisbon in June, neighbourhoods parade in marchas populares, with songs and colourful clothes.

The word, taken apart.
marcha came from French marche, built on marcher "to walk", which is usually traced to Frankish markōn "to mark, to tread" (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: a sardinhada.
a sardinhada — "the sardine feast".
Fizemos uma sardinhada no quintal. We had a sardine barbecue in the back yard.
Grilled sardines on bread, outdoors, typical of summer festivals.

The word, taken apart.
sardinhada is built on sardinha, from Latin sardīna "a sardine" (Lewis & Short), with -ada for a meal of something.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: o bailarico.
o bailarico — "the street dance, a local dance party".
Depois da sardinhada, houve bailarico na praça. After the sardine feast there was a dance in the square.
An informal word, from bailar ‘to dance’ and baile.

The word, taken apart.
bailarico is built on bailar "to dance", which goes back to Late Latin ballāre "to dance" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), with the ending -ico.
