Lesson 1 of 5.
el margen — the margin; the room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for advisable, then the Spanish for to suit.

You'll want to know: el margen.
el margen — "the margin; the room".
No hay margen para errores. There is no room for mistakes.
Al margen — on the sidelines.

The word, taken apart.
margen is inherited from Latin margo, marginis 'edge, border', through Old Spanish margen (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: el control.
el control — "the control; the check".
Hay un control de seguridad. There is a security check.
El control de pasaportes — passport control.

The word, taken apart.
control was borrowed from French contrôle 'a check', from Old French contrerole, a second roll kept to check the first, from Medieval Latin contrarotulum, built from contra and rotulus 'roll' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: excepto.
excepto — "except".
Abrimos todos los días excepto el lunes. We are open every day except Monday.
Means the same as salvo and menos.

The word, taken apart.
excepto is a learned borrowing from Latin exceptus 'taken out', the participle of excipere, from capere 'to take' (EtymDB).
