Lesson 1 of 5.
મધરાત (madhrāt) — midnight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an apology, pardon, then the Gujarati for okay, fine.
[pause 1 second]
Build the sentence "I live in Ahmedabad." (Hũ Amdāvādmā rahũ chhũ.)

You'll want to know: મધરાત.
મધરાત (madhrāt) — "midnight".

The word, taken apart.
મધરાત (madhrāt) is "mid-night". Its first part goes back to Sanskrit madhya, "middle" (CDIAL 9804), and rāt is a tadbhava of rātrī, "night" (CDIAL 10702).

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists પરોઢ (paroḍh) under two Sanskrit words, prabhāta, "daybreak" (CDIAL 8707), and praroha, "sprout; ray" (CDIAL 8747). It thinks praroha explains the Gujarati vowel better.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: પરમદિવસ.
પરમદિવસ (paramdivas) — "the day after tomorrow".

The word, taken apart.
પરમદિવસ (paramdivas) is param, "the day after tomorrow", with divas, "day" (CDIAL 6333). CDIAL has no entry for Gujarati param, so its origin is not settled; the Sanskrit word for that day was paraśvas (CDIAL 7799j).
