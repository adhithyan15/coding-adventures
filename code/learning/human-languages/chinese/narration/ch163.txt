Mandarin Chinese, chapter 163: Free This Weekend.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
yǒukòng — to have free time; to be free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for now and then, then the Mandarin for normally.

You'll want to know: yǒukòng.
yǒukòng — "to have free time; to be free".
nǐ zhōumò yǒukòng ma — Are you free at the weekend?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yǒukòng is built from 有 'to have' and kòng 'a gap', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuwX and khuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a gap'. It is the falling-tone partner of kōng 'empty': to have a gap in the day.

yěcān is built from yě 'open country' and cān 'meal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yaeX and tshan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second 'to swallow'. A meal out in the fields.

Lesson 5 of 6.
yíkuàir — all together; together (in everyday speech).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a picnic, then the Mandarin for next time.

You'll want to know: yíkuàir.
yíkuàir — "all together; together (in everyday speech)".
wǒmen yíkuàir qù yěcān — Let's all go for the picnic together.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
The tone of 一 changes: yī is said yí before the fourth tone of kuài.

The word, taken apart.
yíkuàir is built from 一 'one', kuài 'a lump' and the ending 儿, all three inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit, khwojH and nye (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second 'a clod of earth': in one lump.

What you've built.
