6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
روایت (rivāyat) — a tradition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a prayer of asking, then the Urdu for charity.

You'll want to know: روایت.
روایت — rivāyat — "a tradition".
یہ ہمارے گاؤں کی پرانی روایت ہے۔ yih hamāre gāoṅ kī purānī rivāyat hai. This is an old tradition of our village.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
روایت (rivāyat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic riwāya, "a handing on of a report": what is passed down.

Lesson 3 of 6.
رسم (rasm) — a rite; a ceremony.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a tradition, then the Urdu for a custom.

You'll want to know: رسم.
رسم — rasm — "a rite; a ceremony".
شادی سے پہلے مہندی کی رسم ہوئی۔ shādī se pehle mehndī kī rasm huī. Before the wedding there was the henna ceremony.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رسم (rasm) is usually traced to Persian rasm, "a usage, a custom", a word Persian took from Arabic. It is feminine: رسم ہوئی.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ماننا (mānnā) — to accept; to agree to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a custom, then the Urdu for a rite.

You'll want to know: ماننا.
ماننا — mānnā — "to accept; to agree to".
آخر اس نے میری بات مانی۔ ākhir us ne merī bāt mānī. In the end he accepted what I said.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے سچ مانا, us ne sach mānā.

The word, taken apart.
ماننا (mānnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin mānnā, "to respect, to obey", under Sanskrit mányatē, "thinks" (CDIAL 9857). بات ماننا is to accept what someone says.

Lesson 5 of 6.
نبھانا (nibhānā) — to keep up; to carry through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a rite, then the Urdu for to accept.

You'll want to know: نبھانا.
نبھانا — nibhānā — "to keep up; to carry through".
ہم نے یہ روایت نبھائی۔ ham ne yih rivāyat nibhāī. We kept up this tradition.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے رسم نبھائی, us ne rasm nibhāī.

The word, taken apart.
نبھانا (nibhānā) is the causing form of nibhnā, "to last, to be carried through", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under Sanskrit nírvahati (CDIAL 7397): to make a custom or a promise last.
