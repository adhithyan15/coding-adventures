Italian, chapter 36: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Passage.
3 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six words off a counter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have met. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them first.

Reading.
caffè pane acqua libro amico casa.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice that on a real sign each of these would arrive with a word in front of it.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Italian almost never writes a noun bare. Il libro, l'acqua, un caffè — the article is part of how the word arrives.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is why a list like this one is slightly unusual to an Italian eye, and why a menu is easier to read than it looks: the article in front tells you the gender before you have to remember it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, and each one is a decision the page will normally make for you.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which word is water?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What would normally stand in front of each one? (An article — and it carries the gender.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines, three of them one word.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words. These are things you would say.
[pause 1 second]
You have said every one of them.

Reading.
Ciao! Grazie! Arrivederci! Non so. Che buono! Questo è Marco.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice the list gets longer as it goes, from one word to three.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Non so is two words and a complete sentence. Italian puts the person in the verb, so so is already I know, and non in front of it is the whole negative.
[pause 2 seconds]
Arrivederci is the longest line here and the easiest to recognise, because length and difficulty are not the same thing on a page. You meet that word as a shape long before you take it apart.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and the shortest one is the one that needs a verb.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which line says you do not know?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Where is the I in Non so? (Inside the verb.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first passage.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now twelve of them together.
[pause 1 second]
Forty-seven words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
Ciao! Il mio nome è Marco. Questo è il mio amico. Lui è molto buono. Oggi ho un caffè. Ho anche il pane. Leggo un libro, ma non capisco. Non so perché. Il libro è abbastanza buono. Qui c'è l'acqua. Domani leggo il libro. Grazie e arrivederci!
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and look at the word perché.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Perché is both why and because. Italian does not give you two words for those, so a reader decides which one from where it sits.
[pause 2 seconds]
Here it sits after Non so, so it is why. Had it opened a clause after a full sentence, it would have been because. You have done a piece of reading with no equivalent in English, and nothing on the page marked it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Notice also il mio nome. Italian keeps the article in front of the possessive, which Spanish does not, and it is the kind of small difference that only becomes visible when you read rather than translate.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
In this passage, does perché mean why or because?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that passage were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What tells you whether perché is asking or answering? (Where it sits — nothing else does.)
