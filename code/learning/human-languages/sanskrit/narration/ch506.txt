Sanskrit, chapter 506: Thread and Loom.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
वयति (vayati) — weaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a kiln, then the Sanskrit for a shape.

You'll want to know: वयति.
वयति — vayati — "weaves".
तन्तुवायः कार्पासस्य वस्त्रं वयति। tantuvāyaḥ kārpāsasya vastraṃ vayati. The weaver weaves cotton cloth.
Past (he …): सः वस्त्रम् उतवान्, saḥ vastram utavān.

The word, taken apart.
वयति (vayati) comes from the root √ve, "to weave", whose present is vayati; its past participle is uta, and KRM lists the past utavān (MW).

तन्तुः — tantuḥ — "a strand; a thread of the warp".
सा सूच्यां तन्तुं स्थापयति। sā sūcyāṃ tantuṃ sthāpayati. She threads the needle.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
तन्तुः (tantuḥ) comes from the root √tan, "to stretch, to spin out"; MW gives tantu as a thread, a cord, the warp of a web, a fibre (MW).

रञ्जकः (rañjakaḥ) comes from the root √rañj, "to be coloured, to redden", with the agent ending -aka; MW gives rañjaka as a colourist, a dyer, a painter (MW).
