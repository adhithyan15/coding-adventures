Hindi, chapter 415: At the Front Desk.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मैनेजर (mainejar) — a manager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for comfortable, then the Hindi for to wake (someone).

You'll want to know: मैनेजर.
मैनेजर — mainejar — "a manager".
होटल का मैनेजर बहुत अच्छा आदमी था। hoṭal kā mainejar bahut acchā ādmī thā. The hotel manager was a very nice man.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
मैनेजर (mainejar) is borrowed from English manager.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ठिकाना (ṭhikānā) — a place to stay; whereabouts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a register, then the Hindi for a booking.

You'll want to know: ठिकाना.
ठिकाना — ṭhikānā — "a place to stay; whereabouts".
रात के लिए हमें कोई ठिकाना नहीं मिला। rāt ke liye hamẽ koī ṭhikānā nahīṃ milā. We found nowhere to stay for the night.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
ठिकाना (ṭhikānā) goes back to ṭhīkka, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5503), the same entry as ठीक; it lists Hindi ṭhikānā, "a station". A ठिकाना is a fixed place where you can be found.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मँगाना (mãgānā) — to send for; to order (to be brought).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a booking, then the Hindi for a place to stay.

You'll want to know: मँगाना.
मँगाना — mãgānā — "to send for; to order (to be brought)".
हमने कमरे में चाय मँगाई। hamne kamre meṁ cāy mãgāī. We ordered tea to our room.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने खाना मँगाया, usne khānā mãgāyā.

The word, taken apart.
मँगाना (mãgānā) is the causative partner of माँगना, "to ask for", which goes back to Sanskrit mārgati, "seeks, requests" (CDIAL 10074): you ask for a thing to be brought to you.
