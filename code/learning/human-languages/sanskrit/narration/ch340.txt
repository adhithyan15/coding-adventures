Lesson 1 of 5.
निमित्तम् (nimittam) — an occasion; a cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for radio, then the Sanskrit for the internet.

You'll want to know: निमित्तम्.
निमित्तम् — nimittam — "an occasion; a cause".
विवाहः मम आगमनस्य निमित्तम् अस्ति। vivāhaḥ mama āgamanasya nimittam asti. A wedding is the occasion of my visit.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
निमित्तम् (nimittam) MW gives nimitta the senses "a mark", "a sign" and "a cause, a motive, a ground", and says it is possibly connected with ni and the root √mā, "to measure"; so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: उत्पद्यते.
उत्पद्यते — utpadyate — "comes about; arises".
क्रोधात् विवादः उत्पद्यते। krodhāt vivādaḥ utpadyate. Quarrels come about from anger.
Past (it …): विवादः क्रोधात् उत्पन्नवान्, vivādaḥ krodhāt utpannavān.

The word, taken apart.
उत्पद्यते (utpadyate) is built on ut, "up, out", and the root √pad, "to go, to fall"; MW gives ut-√pad the senses "to arise, originate, be born or produced" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
जनयति (janayati) — gives rise to; produces.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lack, then the Sanskrit for comes about.

You'll want to know: जनयति.
जनयति — janayati — "gives rise to; produces".
क्रोधः दुःखं जनयति। krodhaḥ duḥkhaṃ janayati. Anger gives rise to sorrow.
Past (he …): सः भयं जनितवान्, saḥ bhayaṃ janitavān.

The word, taken apart.
जनयति (janayati) comes from the root √jan, "to be born", in its causative form, "to cause to be born, to produce"; MW gives janita "born" (MW), and KRM gives the participle janita for the root.
