Persian, chapter 528: Books.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
کتابفروشی (ketâbforushi) — a bookshop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a dancer, then the Persian for to admire.

You'll want to know: کتابفروشی.
کتابفروشی (ketâbforushi) — "a bookshop".
نزدیک خانه ما یک کتابفروشی قدیمی هست. nazdik-e khâne-ye mâ yek ketâbforushi-ye qadimi hast. There's an old bookshop near our house.
ketâb 'book' + forush 'selling' (stem of forukhtan) + -i.

The word, taken apart.
ketâbforushi is built in Persian on ketâb, 'book', from Arabic kitāb (EtymDB), and forush, the stem of native forukhtan, 'to sell', with -i for the shop.

عنوان این کتاب خیلی جالب است. 'onvân-e in ketâb kheyli jâleb ast. The title of this book is very interesting.
Plural 'anâvin (Arabic). be 'onvân-e — as, in the role of.

The word, taken apart.
'onvân was borrowed from Arabic ʿunwān (EtymDB), a word for the title or address on a letter.

Lesson 3 of 5.
جلد (jeld) — a volume; a (book) cover.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a bookshop, then the Persian for a title.

You'll want to know: جلد.
جلد (jeld) — "a volume; a (book) cover".
این رمان دو جلد دارد. in român do jeld dârad. This novel is in two volumes.
Also the counter for books: se jeld ketâb 'three books'.

The word, taken apart.
jeld is a loan, usually traced to Arabic jild, which EtymDB glosses 'skin, hide' (EtymDB): the leather a book was bound in, then the book itself. Urdu jild is usually traced to the same word. EtymDB has no Persian entry for it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
مترجم (motarjem) — a translator.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a volume, then the Persian for a publisher.

You'll want to know: مترجم.
مترجم (motarjem) — "a translator".
مترجم این کتاب را از انگلیسی ترجمه کرد. motarjem in ketâb râ az engelisi tarjome kard. The translator translated this book from English.
Plural motarjemân. It shares its root with tarjome 'translation'.

The word, taken apart.
motarjem comes from Arabic mutarjim, 'translator' (EtymDB), from the same root as tarjome, 'translation'.
