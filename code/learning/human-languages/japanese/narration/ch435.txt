Japanese, chapter 435: Satisfied or Bored.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
まんぞく (manzoku) — satisfied; contented.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for miserable, then the Japanese for to encourage.

You'll want to know: まんぞく.
まんぞく — manzoku — "satisfied; contented".
この ばんごはんに まんぞくしました。 — kono bangohan ni manzoku shimashita — "I was satisfied with this dinner."
A na-adjective: な before a noun.

The word, taken apart.
まんぞく (manzoku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are man, "full" (Middle Chinese manX) and zoku, "be sufficient" (tsjowk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ほほえむ (hohoemu) — to smile gently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for discontented, then the Japanese for bored.

You'll want to know: ほほえむ.
ほほえむ — hohoemu — "to smile gently".
かのじょは すこし ほほえみました。 — kanojo wa sukoshi hohoemimashita — "She gave a little smile."
Polite ほほえみます, hohoemimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): そぼが ほほえみました。, sobo ga hohoemimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ほほえむ (hohoemu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).
