## You'll want to know: শেষ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C265-SHESH-01]; assesses=[BN-CONCEPT-C05-KAJKORA-01, BN-LEX-C128-THINGS128-04, BN-LEX-C78-TIME78-01] -->

**শেষ** — *shesh* — "the end; last".

> **কাল আমার কাজ শেষ হবে।**
>
> *kāl āmār kāj shesh hôbe.* — "My work will be finished tomorrow."

**শেষ হওয়া** is "to come to an end", and **শেষ করা** "to finish": **আমি বইটা শেষ করলাম** (*āmi bôiṭā shesh kôrlām*), "I finished the book". Before a noun, **শেষ** is "last":

> **শেষ ট্রেন রাতে ছাড়বে।**
>
> *shesh ṭren rāte chhāṛbe.* — "The last train will leave at night."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**শেষ** (*shesh*) is a tatsama: Sanskrit *śeṣa*, "what remains", which CDIAL (12611) and Monier-Williams both give. Monier-Williams also lists "end, conclusion" and "last" among its senses, so the Bengali uses were already Sanskrit's.
