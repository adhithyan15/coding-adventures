## You'll want to know: 开花
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C224-BLOOM224-01]; assesses=[] -->

**开花** — *kāihuā* — "to bloom; to flower".

> **这些花三月开花** *zhèxiē huā sānyuè kāihuā* — These flowers come out in March.

The new character, in pieces: 花 — seven strokes: the grass piece of 菜 on top, then the person piece, then two strokes like the right half of 比. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**开花** (*kāihuā*) is built from 开 'to open' and 花 'flower', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *khoj* and *xwae* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which notes the second as the everyday form of the word for blossom.
