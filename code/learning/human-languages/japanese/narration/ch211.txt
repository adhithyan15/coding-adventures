Japanese, chapter 211: Animals in the Hills.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
どうぶつ (dōbutsu) — an animal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to grow, then the Japanese for to wither.

You'll want to know: どうぶつ.
どうぶつ — dōbutsu — "an animal".
もりに どうぶつが います。 — mori ni dōbutsu ga imasu — "There are animals in the forest."

The word, taken apart.
どうぶつ (dōbutsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are dō, "to move" (Middle Chinese duwngX) and butsu, "thing" (mjut) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

You'll want to know: さる.

やまに さるが います。 — yama ni saru ga imasu — "There are monkeys on the mountain."

The word, taken apart.
さる (saru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

The word, taken apart.
ことり (kotori) is native Japanese (wago), built from ko, "small", and tori, "bird", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
