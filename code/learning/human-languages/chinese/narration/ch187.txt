Mandarin Chinese, chapter 187: What a Day.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
dǎpò — to break; to smash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a bottle, then the Mandarin for a drawer.

You'll want to know: dǎpò.
dǎpò — "to break; to smash".
zuótiān wǒ dǎpò le yí ge bēizi — Yesterday I broke a glass.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎpò is built from 打 'to strike' and pò 'to break', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and phaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), though dǎ is not the sound taengX would regularly give.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Lesson 3 of 7.
hòuhuǐ — to regret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to smash, then the Mandarin for to have a fall.

You'll want to know: hòuhuǐ.
hòuhuǐ — "to regret".
wǒ hěn hòuhuǐ zuótiān méi qù wǎnhuì — I really regret not going to the party yesterday.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hòuhuǐ is built from 后 'after' and huǐ 'to repent', both inherited from Middle Chinese, huwX and xwojH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to repent and change'; today's third tone goes with its other reading, xwojX. To repent afterwards.

Lesson 4 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a fall, then the Mandarin for to regret.

You'll want to know: dāngshí.
dāngshí — "at that time; then".
dāngshí wǒ bú zài jiā — I wasn't at home at the time.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dāngshí is built from dāng and 时 'time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tang and dzyi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to match' and 'to meet with': the time it met with.

Lesson 5 of 7.
jiéguǒ — in the end; as a result; a result.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to regret, then the Mandarin for at that time.

You'll want to know: jiéguǒ.
jiéguǒ — "in the end; as a result; a result".
wǒmen děng le yí ge xiǎoshí jiéguǒ tā méi lái — We waited an hour, and in the end he didn't come.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiéguǒ is built from jié 'to tie' and guǒ 'fruit', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ket and kwaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to set fruit, and so an outcome. The step to 'in the end' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.
