Arabic, chapter 446: Good service.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
بقشيش (baqshīsh) — a tip (for service).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to slow down, then the Arabic for to hamper.

You'll want to know: بقشيش.
بقشيش — baqshīsh — "a tip (for service)".
ترك أبي بقشيشا للنادل. taraka abī baqshīshan li-n-nādil. My father left a tip for the waiter.

The word, taken apart.
بقشيش (baqshīsh) was borrowed from Persian bakhshish, "a gift" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 6.
خدم (khadama) — to serve; to wait on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a tip, then the Arabic for a service.

You'll want to know: خدم.
خدم — khadama — "to serve; to wait on".
خدمنا نادل لطيف. khadamanā nādil laṭīf. A kind waiter served us.
Present: يخدم, yakhdum.
Lane gives the present both as yakhdumu and yakhdimu.

The word, taken apart.
خدم (khadama) is built on the native Arabic root kh-d-m, "to serve" (Lane). It is the plain verb.

Lesson 4 of 6.
رحب (raḥḥaba) — to greet warmly; to welcome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a service, then the Arabic for to serve.

You'll want to know: رحب.
رحب — raḥḥaba — "to greet warmly; to welcome".
رحب بنا موظف الاستقبال. raḥḥaba binā muwaẓẓaf al-istiqbāl. The receptionist greeted us warmly.
Present: يرحب, yuraḥḥib.
It takes bi-: raḥḥaba bi-, "to welcome someone".

The word, taken apart.
رحب (raḥḥaba) is built on the native Arabic root r-ḥ-b, "to be wide, spacious" (Lane). It is Form II, "to make room for a guest"; marḥaban, "welcome", shares the root.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: أوصى.
أوصى — awṣā — "to recommend".
أوصانا المرشد بمطعم صغير. awṣānā al-murshid bi-maṭʿam ṣaghīr. The guide recommended a small restaurant to us.
Present: يوصي, yūṣī.
It takes bi- before the thing recommended.

The word, taken apart.
أوصى (awṣā) is built on the native Arabic root w-ṣ-y, "to join; to enjoin" (Lane). It is Form IV.
