Portuguese, chapter 529: A roupa lavada.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o detergente — detergent, washing powder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to scrub, then the Portuguese for mop.

You'll want to know: o detergente.
o detergente — "detergent, washing powder".
Acabou o detergente da máquina. We have run out of washing powder.
Detergente da loiça — washing-up liquid.

The word, taken apart.
detergente is a learned word built on Latin dētergēns "wiping off", from dētergēre, from tergēre "to wipe" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
estender — to hang out (washing); to stretch out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for mop, then the Portuguese for detergent.

You'll want to know: estender.
estender — "to hang out (washing); to stretch out".
Estendo a roupa ao sol. I hang the washing out in the sun.
Estender a roupa — to hang out the washing.

The word, taken apart.
estender is inherited, through Old Portuguese, from Latin extendere "to stretch out" (EtymDB), built on tendere "to stretch".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: a mola.
a mola — "clothes peg; spring".
Prendo as meias com molas. I peg the socks up with clothes pegs.
Also a spring: a mola do colchão.

The word, taken apart.
mola was borrowed from Italian molla "a spring" (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin mollis "soft, pliant".

Lesson 5 of 5.
engomar — to iron; to starch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for clothes horse, then the Portuguese for clothes peg.

You'll want to know: engomar.
engomar — "to iron; to starch".
O meu pai engoma as camisas ao domingo. My father irons the shirts on Sundays.
Also passar a ferro.

The word, taken apart.
engomar is built on goma "starch, gum", which goes back to Latin gummi, from Greek kómmi; the Greek word is usually traced to Egyptian.
