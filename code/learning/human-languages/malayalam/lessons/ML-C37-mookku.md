# മൂക്ക് (mūkkŭ) — "nose," and the same softening you met on "son"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-CHEVI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two face words down. This one brings back a sound-change from
the last chapter, in a new place.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-MOOKKU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **മൂക്ക്** — *mūkkŭ* — **nose**

> **എന്റെ മൂക്ക്.** — *ente mūkkŭ.* — "My nose."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-MOOKKU-02]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C36-MAKAN-02] -->

**മൂക്ക്** is native Dravidian, close to Tamil **மூக்கு** (*mūkku*) and
Telugu **ముక్కు** (*mukku*). Kannada shows **ಮೂಗು** (*mūgu*) — the very
same *-k-* to *-g-* softening you already met between Malayalam's **മകൻ**
and Kannada's **ಮಗ**. It is a small, recurring signature: wherever Malayalam
and Tamil hold a *-k-* between vowels, Kannada is likely to have worn it
down to a *-g-*, whether the word is a family word or a body word.
