German, chapter 310: Karneval.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Karneval — the carnival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the balloon, then the German for the fireworks.

You'll want to know: der Karneval.
der Karneval — "the carnival".
Im Februar feiert die Stadt Karneval. In February the town celebrates carnival.
Plural: die Karnevale. The name differs by region; in the southwest it is die Fastnacht.

The word, taken apart.
Karneval comes from Italian carnevale, from Medieval Latin carnelevale, built on caro "flesh, meat" and levāre "to lighten, to raise", here "to take away" (EtymDB): the farewell to meat before Lent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Fasching — the Shrovetide carnival.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the fireworks, then the German for the carnival.

You'll want to know: der Fasching.
der Fasching — "the Shrovetide carnival".
Im Fasching gibt es in der Schule eine Party. At carnival time there is a party at school.
The word in southern Germany and Austria; it is the season before Lent.

The word, taken apart.
Fasching is usually traced to Middle High German vaschanc "the serving of drink before the fast"; EtymDB has the word but lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
das Kostüm — the costume; a skirt suit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the carnival, then the German for the Shrovetide carnival.

You'll want to know: das Kostüm.
das Kostüm — "the costume; a skirt suit".
Sie nähte ihr Kostüm selbst. She sewed her costume herself.
Plural: die Kostüme. Also a woman's skirt suit.

The word, taken apart.
Kostüm was borrowed from French costume, which came from Italian and goes back to Latin cōnsuētūdō "custom" (EtymDB): first the dress of a time and place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
verkleiden — to dress up (as); mostly reflexive: sich verkleiden.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the Shrovetide carnival, then the German for the costume.

You'll want to know: verkleiden.
verkleiden — "to dress up (as); mostly reflexive: sich verkleiden".
Mein Bruder verkleidete sich als Löwe. My brother dressed up as a lion.
Sich als etwas verkleiden. Die Verkleidung — the disguise.

The word, taken apart.
verkleiden is built on Kleid "dress", an inherited Germanic word with the Dutch cousin kleed (EtymDB), with ver-.
