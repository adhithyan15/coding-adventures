Arabic, chapter 470: Springs and rivers.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ينبوع (yanbūʿ) — a spring (of water).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for fertile, then the Arabic for to uproot.

You'll want to know: ينبوع.
ينبوع — yanbūʿ — "a spring (of water)".
شربنا من ينبوع في الجبل. sharibnā min yanbūʿ fī al-jabal. We drank from a spring in the mountain.
Plural: ينابيع, yanābīʿ.

The word, taken apart.
ينبوع (yanbūʿ) is built on the native Arabic root n-b-ʿ, "to well up, to gush" (Lane). EtymDB traces the word back to Proto-Semitic.

Lesson 3 of 6.
مصب (maṣabb) — a river mouth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a spring, then the Arabic for a river source.

You'll want to know: مصب.
مصب — maṣabb — "a river mouth".
عند المصب يلتقي النهر بالبحر. ʿinda al-maṣabb yaltaqī an-nahr bi-l-baḥr. At the mouth the river meets the sea.

The word, taken apart.
مصب (maṣabb) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-b-b, "to pour" (Lane). It is the place where a river pours out; ṣabba, "to pour", shares the root.

Lesson 5 of 6.
عذب (ʿadhb) — sweet (of water); fresh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a river mouth, then the Arabic for a riverbank.

You'll want to know: عذب.
عذب — ʿadhb — "sweet (of water); fresh".
ماء الينبوع عذب. māʾ al-yanbūʿ ʿadhb. The water of the spring is sweet.
Of water, sweet means fresh, not salty.

The word, taken apart.
عذب (ʿadhb) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-dh-b, "to be sweet (of water)" (Lane).
