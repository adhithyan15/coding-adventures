# مسؤول (masʾūl) — the person in charge

## You'll want to know: مسؤول
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C288-SAFAR288-03]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C65-VERBS65-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C286-SUBJUNCTIVE-01, AR-LEX-C251-AMAL251-03] -->

**مسؤول** — *masʾūl* — "the person in charge, an official"; as a
description, "responsible".

> **أريد أن أتكلم مع المسؤول.** — *urīdu an atakallama maʿa al-masʾūl* — I
> want to speak to the person in charge.

> **من المسؤول هنا؟** — *man al-masʾūl hunā?* — Who is in charge here?

As a description it takes *ʿan*, "for": *al-muwaẓẓaf masʾūl ʿan at-tadhākir*,
the employee is responsible for the tickets. It is written with the **ؤ** of
**سؤال**; some writers spell it **مسئول**, with the other seat.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C31-SAALA-01, AR-LEX-C285-RAY285-02] -->

**مسؤول** (*masʾūl*) is built on the native Arabic root *s-a-l*, "to ask"
(Lane), the root of *saʾala* and *suʾāl*. Its shape *mafʿūl* is the one that
means "done to", so *masʾūl* is "asked", and Lane lists it among the root's
words for a thing asked for. "The one asked to answer", and so "the one in
charge", is the usual way the modern sense is explained.
