Telugu, chapter 494: Worry and Unease.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
దిగులు (digulu) — gloom; low spirits; worry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a watchman, then the Telugu for a milkman.

You'll want to know: దిగులు.
దిగులు — digulu — "gloom; low spirits; worry".
అమ్మ దూరంగా ఉంటే నాకు దిగులుగా ఉంటుంది. amma dūraṅgā uṇṭē nāku digulugā uṇṭundi. When mum is far away, I feel low.
Said with the dative: నాకు దిగులుగా ఉంది, I feel low.

The word, taken apart.
దిగులు (digulu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3202 lists Telugu digulu, "fear, alarm", beside Tamil tikil, "fright, terror". In everyday Telugu the fear has softened into a heavy, anxious sadness.

Lesson 2 of 5.
చింతించు (cintiñcu) — to worry; to regret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a milkman, then the Telugu for gloom.

You'll want to know: చింతించు.
చింతించు — cintiñcu — "to worry; to regret".
నాన్న డబ్బు గురించి చాలా చింతించాడు. nānna ḍabbu guriñci cālā cintiñcāḍu. Dad worried a lot about money.
Past (I …): చింతించాను, cintiñcānu.
What you worry about takes గురించి. The noun is చింత, worry or care.

The word, taken apart.
చింతించు (cintiñcu) is borrowed from Sanskrit cintayati, "thinks; cares for" (CDIAL 4815), and takes the Telugu ending -ఇంచు, which turns a Sanskrit stem into a verb. Thinking too much about a thing becomes worrying about it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
కలవరపడు (kalavarapaḍu) — to be agitated; to be upset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for gloom, then the Telugu for to worry.

You'll want to know: కలవరపడు.
కలవరపడు — kalavarapaḍu — "to be agitated; to be upset".
ఆ వార్త విని అమ్మ కలవరపడింది. ā vārta vini amma kalavarapaḍindi. Hearing that news, mum was upset.
Past (I …): కలవరపడ్డాను, kalavarapaḍḍānu.
The noun is కలవరం, confusion of mind. A noun of feeling + -పడు means to feel it.

The word, taken apart.
కలవరపడు (kalavarapaḍu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 1306 lists Telugu kalavaramu, "confusion, anxiety", and kalavara-paḍu, "to be confused", beside Tamil kalavaram, "perturbation". The second part is the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853).

Lesson 4 of 5.
కంగారుపడు (kaṅgārupaḍu) — to panic; to get flustered.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to worry, then the Telugu for to be agitated.

You'll want to know: కంగారుపడు.
కంగారుపడు — kaṅgārupaḍu — "to panic; to get flustered".
రైలు రాకపోతే అతను కంగారుపడతాడు. railu rākapōtē atanu kaṅgārupaḍatāḍu. If the train does not come, he gets flustered.
Past (I …): కంగారుపడ్డాను, kaṅgārupaḍḍānu.
The noun కంగారు is fluster or panic: కంగారుగా, in a flurry.

The word, taken apart.
కంగారుపడు (kaṅgārupaḍu) is built from kaṅgāru, "fluster, panic", and the native verb paḍu, "to feel, to suffer" (DEDR 3853). The origin of kaṅgāru itself is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ఆత్రుత (ātruta) — eagerness; impatience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to be agitated, then the Telugu for to panic.

You'll want to know: ఆత్రుత.
ఆత్రుత — ātruta — "eagerness; impatience".
ఫలితాల కోసం విద్యార్థులు ఆత్రుతగా ఎదురుచూశారు. phalitāla kōsaṁ vidyārthulu ātrutagā edurucūśāru. The students waited eagerly for the results.
Mostly as an adverb: ఆత్రుతగా, eagerly, impatiently.

The word, taken apart.
ఆత్రుత (ātruta) is usually traced to Sanskrit ātura, "suffering, troubled in body or mind" (Monier-Williams), with an abstract ending. The restless feeling became eager impatience.
