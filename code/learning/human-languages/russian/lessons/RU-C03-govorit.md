# говорить — "to speak"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Your first new letter in three lessons, and an English word that
looks like a relative and is not one.

## You'll want to know first — говорить
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-GOVORIT, RU-SCRIPT-GE-GAMMA]; assesses=[] -->

> **говорить** — *govorít'* — **to speak, to talk**

One letter here is **new**: **г**. It is Greek **gamma** (Γ) with the corner
rounded off, and it says a hard **g**, as in *go* — never the *j* of *gem*.

Everything else you have met. And the two **о**s behave the way *спасибо*
taught you: an **о** away from the stress slides towards *a*. The stress falls
on the last syllable, so this is **ga-va-RIT'**. Russian spells the *o*; your
mouth says *a*.

## Grammar Lens: verbs come in two families
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-TWO-VERB-FAMILIES]; assesses=[] -->

> *ya gavaryú* — **I speak.**

The **I** ending is the same **-ю** you put on *znáyu*. Every Russian verb in the
present ends the same way for *I*. Then switch to *you*, and the two families
separate:

- *ty znáyesh* — you know
- *ty gavarísh* — you speak

An **-е-** against an **-и-**. That one vowel is the whole difference, and it is
why you learn the *you* form alongside the dictionary form: it names the family,
and the remaining endings follow from it.
