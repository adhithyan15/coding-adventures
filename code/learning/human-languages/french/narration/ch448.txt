French, chapter 448: Coiffeur et opticien.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le coiffeur — the hairdresser, the barber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to dye, then the French for to alter.

You'll want to know: le coiffeur.
le coiffeur — "the hairdresser, the barber".
Je vais chez le coiffeur une fois par mois. I go to the hairdresser's once a month.
Feminine la coiffeuse. Aller chez le coiffeur.

The word, taken apart.
coiffeur is built on coiffer 'to do someone's hair' (EtymDB), from coiffe 'cap', which is usually traced to Late Latin cofia, of Germanic origin.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
raser — to shave (often se raser); to raze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to alter, then the French for the hairdresser.

You'll want to know: raser.
raser — "to shave (often se raser); to raze".
Le coiffeur lui a rasé la barbe. The barber shaved off his beard.
Se raser — to shave; informal ça me rase — that bores me.

The word, taken apart.
raser is inherited from Vulgar Latin rāsāre, from Latin rāsus 'scraped', the past participle of rādere 'to scrape' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'opticien — the optician.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the hairdresser, then the French for to shave.

You'll want to know: l'opticien.
l'opticien — "the optician".
L'opticien a réparé mes lunettes tout de suite. The optician fixed my glasses straight away.
A masculine noun; feminine l'opticienne.

The word, taken apart.
opticien is built on optique 'optics', a learned word from Latin opticus, from Greek optikós 'of seeing' (EtymDB). EtymDB lists opticien itself without a source.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'horloger — the watchmaker, the clockmaker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to shave, then the French for the optician.

You'll want to know: l'horloger.
l'horloger — "the watchmaker, the clockmaker".
L'horloger a changé la pile de ma montre. The watchmaker changed the battery in my watch.
A masculine noun. L'horlogerie — the watchmaker's shop.

The word, taken apart.
horloger is built on horloge 'clock', which comes from Latin hōrologium, from Greek hōrológion 'thing that tells the hour' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.
