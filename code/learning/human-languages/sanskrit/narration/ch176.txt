Lesson 1 of 5.
परिवर्तयति (parivartayati) — changes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for endures, then the Sanskrit for tries.

You'll want to know: परिवर्तयति.
परिवर्तयति — parivartayati — "changes".

The word, taken apart.
parivartayati is built on pari "around" and the root √vṛt, "to turn" (MW), with the causative ending -aya-: it means "makes something turn round", and so "changes". MW compares √vṛt with Latin vertere, "to turn".

Lesson 2 of 5.
निवेदयति (nivedayati) — reports, submits.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tries, then the Sanskrit for changes.

You'll want to know: निवेदयति.
निवेदयति — nivedayati — "reports, submits".

The word, taken apart.
nivedayati is the causative of ni- with the root √vid, "to know" (MW): it means "makes known", and so "reports, presents". The root is inherited from Indo-European; MW compares Latin vidēre, "to see".

Lesson 3 of 5.
आमन्त्रयति (āmantrayati) — invites.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for changes, then the Sanskrit for reports.

You'll want to know: आमन्त्रयति.
आमन्त्रयति — āmantrayati — "invites".

The word, taken apart.
āmantrayati is ā- "towards" with mantrayate, "speaks, takes counsel", which MW calls a verb made from the noun mantra, built on the root √man "to think". CDIAL 1242 records "summons" for it in the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa and "invites" in the Mahābhārata; it lives on as Gujarati āmãtravũ, "invites".

Lesson 4 of 5.
शोधयति (śodhayati) — cleans, corrects.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for reports, then the Sanskrit for invites.

You'll want to know: शोधयति.
शोधयति — śodhayati — "cleans, corrects".

The word, taken apart.
śodhayati is the causative of the root √śudh, "to be purified, become pure" (MW). So it comes from "makes pure", which covers both cleaning a thing and correcting a text.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पूरयति (pūrayati) — fills.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for invites, then the Sanskrit for cleans.

You'll want to know: पूरयति.
पूरयति — pūrayati — "fills".

The word, taken apart.
pūrayati is built on the root √pṝ, "to fill" (MW), and is listed in CDIAL 8335. The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Latin plēnus and English full. CDIAL gives Punjabi pūrnā, "to fill", as one of its descendants.
