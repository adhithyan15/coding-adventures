# tu as — you have

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The second form of the verb, and the first sign that this paradigm is
easier to say than it looks to read.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-VERB-AVOIR-AS-02]; assesses=[] -->

- `silent-final` — the **s** of **as** is **not pronounced**.
- **tu as** = *tü ah*. The **u** of *tu* is the tight front-rounded vowel; the
  **as** is a bare open *ah*.
- Say **j'ai** and **tu as** back to back and notice how little work the verb is
  doing in either.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**as** is the **tu** form — the one you use with a friend, a child, or anyone
you would call by their first name. It is the same *tu* you met when you learned
which "you" to pick.

The written **s** is a signal to the eye and nothing to the ear. That is the
normal state of affairs in French verbs: the endings are alive on the page and
almost dead in the mouth. It is why the pronoun is not optional — *as* on its own
would tell a listener nothing about who has something.

Keep that in mind as the paradigm fills in. You are learning six written forms
and rather fewer sounds.
