## Grammar Lens: direct owner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-FORM-PAHILE-ORDINAL-193]; assesses=[PA-LEX-PAHILA-01, PA-LEX-C71-HOME71-01, PA-PLACE-KAMRE-ROOM-01, PA-POSTPOSITION-VICH-01] -->

> **ਪਹਿਲਾ ਕਮਰਾ** — *pahilā kamrā* — the first room

> **ਪਹਿਲੇ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ** — *pahile kamre vicc* — in the first room

The familiar direct form has the final *-aa* sound. In this masculine singular
phrase, the noun is oblique before **ਵਿੱਚ**, and the ordinal takes final *-e*.

## Grammar Lens: source boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-PAHILE-ORDINAL-193, PA-LEX-PAHILA-01, PA-LEX-DUJA-01] -->

Gill and Gleason's masculine adjective table contrasts the singular direct and
singular oblique patterns. Here that contrast is **ਪਹਿਲਾ** beside **ਪਹਿਲੇ**.
Their following numeral section lists first **ਪਹਿਲਾ** and second **ਦੂਜਾ**
among Punjabi ordinals. This chapter follows these two ordinals, not a claim
that every Punjabi adjective changes this way.
Source: Harjeet Singh Gill and Henry A. Gleason, Jr., *A Reference Grammar of
Punjabi* (1969), pp. 71–73, [adjectives and
numerals](https://apnaorg.com/books/english/a-reference-grammar-of-punjabi/a-reference-grammar-of-punjabi.pdf).
