# imprimir — to print

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-IMPRESO, ES-LEX-C333-QUIET-29] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el impreso** and **la prisa**. The first is this verb's
participle and the second is its distant cousin.

## You'll want to know: imprimir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-IMPRESO] -->

**imprimir** — to print.

> Voy a **imprimir** el documento.

*Imprimir a doble cara* — to print on both sides, where *cara* is the face of
the sheet: the same word you learned for a face, lent to paper.

You already have the participle. When you learned **el impreso**, it was given
to you as *the irregular past participle of imprimir* — so the verb has been
named in this book once, without ever being yours to use. Now it is.

## Grammar Lens: the participle you already hold
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01, ES-LEX-IMPRESO] -->

Most Spanish verbs build a participle by rule. This one does not, and you have
had the exception longer than the verb.

[The irregular participles](./ES-C49-participios-irregulares.md) gave you
*hecho*, *dicho*, *visto* and *puesto*; *cubierto* and *roto* joined the list
later. **Impreso** belongs on it too.

| verb | participle |
|---|---|
| imprimir | **impreso** |
| escribir | escrito |

*He impreso el documento* — I have printed the document. You will also meet
*imprimido*, which the Academy allows, but *impreso* is what a notice prints
and what a printer's menu says.

And *el impreso* as a **noun** — a form to fill in — is that same participle
hardened, which is the move *el pedido* taught you.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C469-OFICINA-01, ES-LEX-C333-QUIET-29] -->

Latin **imprimere**: **in-**, into, plus **premere**, to **press**. To print
is to **press something into** a surface, which is exactly what a press does
and exactly what the word has always meant.

*Premere* is the root under *la prisa* — a hurry is a pressing. Follow it
outward and English fills up: **press**, **pressure**, **print**, **imprint**,
**express** (pressed out), **compress**, **impression** — a mark pressed in,
whether into wax or into a person.

So a printer does not "produce" a page. Historically it **presses** one, and
the word kept the machine in it long after the machine changed.
