Gujarati, chapter 368: Luck and Fate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
નસીબ (nasīb) — luck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for an assumption, then the Gujarati for to assess.

You'll want to know: નસીબ.
નસીબ — nasīb — "luck".
જો નસીબ સારું હશે તો આપણે જીતીશું. jo nasīb sārũ haśe to āpaṇe jītīśũ. If our luck holds, we will win.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
નસીબ (nasīb) is usually traced to Arabic naṣīb, "a share, a portion", through Persian: the share life deals out.

Lesson 3 of 5.
સંયોગ (sanyog) — a coincidence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for luck, then the Gujarati for destiny.

You'll want to know: સંયોગ.
સંયોગ — sanyog — "a coincidence".
જો આ સંયોગ ન હોત તો અમે એક જ ટ્રેનમાં ન હોત. jo ā sanyog na hot to ame ek ja ṭrenmā̃ na hot. But for this coincidence, we would not have been on the same train.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સંયોગ (sanyog) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit saṃyoga, "a joining, a connection" (Monier-Williams). સંજોગ (sanjog), "a circumstance", is the same word in an older, inherited shape.

You'll want to know: ફળવું.
ફળવું — phaḷvũ — "to bear fruit".
જો તમે સતત મહેનત કરશો તો તે ફળશે. jo tame satat mahenat karśo to te phaḷśe. If you keep working hard, it will pay off.
Past (it …): તે કેરીનો છોડ ફળ્યો, te kerīno choḍ phaḷyo.

The word, taken apart.
ફળવું (phaḷvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit phálati, "bears fruit"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb (CDIAL 9057).

Lesson 5 of 5.
નીવડવું (nīvaḍvũ) — to turn out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a coincidence, then the Gujarati for to bear fruit.

You'll want to know: નીવડવું.
નીવડવું — nīvaḍvũ — "to turn out".
જો આ દવા સારી નીવડશે તો ઘણા દર્દીઓ સાજા થશે. jo ā davā sārī nīvaḍśe to ghaṇā dardīo sājā thaśe. If this medicine turns out well, many patients will get better.
Past (it …): તે વાત સાચી નીવડી, te vāt sācī nīvḍī.

The word, taken apart.
નીવડવું (nīvaḍvũ) CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to turn out, to come up", under a reconstructed nirvarati, "restrains" (CDIAL 7392); how "restrain" became "turn out" is not explained there.
