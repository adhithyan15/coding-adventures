Lesson 1 of 5.
പലഹാരം (palahāraṁ) — a snack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a purse, then the Malayalam for a pocket.

You'll want to know: പലഹാരം.
പലഹാരം — palahāraṁ — "a snack".

The word, taken apart.
പലഹാരം (palahāraṁ) from Sanskrit phalāhāra, "eating fruit", reshaped as a tadbhava, with Malayalam's noun ending -ṁ (CDIAL 9060). Its parts are phala, "fruit", and āhāra, "food"; Malayalam softened the ph and widened the sense to any light snack.

പഴംപൊരി (paḻampori) — a banana fritter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a pocket, then the Malayalam for a snack.

You'll want to know: പഴംപൊരി.
പഴംപൊരി — paḻampori — "a banana fritter".

The word, taken apart.
പഴംപൊരി (paḻampori) is native Dravidian, "fried fruit": paḻam, "ripe fruit" (DEDR 4004), and pori, from the verb "to fry, parch" (DEDR 4537, with Tamil pori). DEDR 4004 compares Sanskrit phala.
