Bengali, chapter 400: Addresses and Signs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ঠিকানা (ṭhikānā) — an address.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to warm oneself, then the Bengali for a guide (for visitors).

You'll want to know: ঠিকানা.
ঠিকানা — ṭhikānā — "an address".
আমি বন্ধুর নতুন ঠিকানা খুঁজে পেলাম। āmi bôndhur nôtun ṭhikānā khũje pelām. I found my friend's new address.
It shares its first syllable with ঠিক, right: an address is the place a thing is fixed.

The word, taken apart.
ঠিকানা (ṭhikānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali ṭhikānā, "a station, a fixed place", beside ṭhik, "correct", under the reconstructed ṭhīkka, "firm, right" (CDIAL 5503); the sense "a postal address" is the modern use.

Lesson 3 of 5.
উল্টো (ulṭo) — opposite; the other way; reversed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for an address, then the Bengali for a sign.

You'll want to know: উল্টো.
উল্টো — ulṭo — "opposite; the other way; reversed".
আমরা ভুল করে উল্টো দিকের বাসে উঠলাম। āmrā bhul kore ulṭo diker bāse uṭhlām. By mistake we got on the bus going the other way.
উল্টো (ulṭo) দিকে, in the opposite direction; উল্টো (ulṭo) দিকের দোকান, the shop across the road.

The word, taken apart.
উল্টো (ulṭo) is inherited: CDIAL lists Bengali ulṭo, "reversed", under the reconstructed ullaṭati, "turns over" (CDIAL 2368), the same root as উল্টানো, to overturn.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ইঙ্গিত করা (inggit kôrā) — to point (to); to hint at.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for opposite, then the Bengali for an instruction.

You'll want to know: ইঙ্গিত করা.
ইঙ্গিত করা — inggit kôrā — "to point (to); to hint at".
দোকানদার ডানদিকের গলির দিকে ইঙ্গিত করলেন। dokāndār ḍāndiker gôlir dike inggit kôrlen. The shopkeeper pointed towards the lane on the right.
Past (he/she …): তিনি নদীর দিকে ইঙ্গিত করলেন, tini nôdīr dike inggit kôrlen.
ইঙ্গিত alone is a sign or a hint: চোখের ইঙ্গিত, a sign with the eyes.

The word, taken apart.
ইঙ্গিত করা (inggit kôrā) joins Sanskrit iṅgita, "a motion of the body that shows what one means, a hint" (Monier-Williams), to করা, to do; ঙ্গ is said ngg.
