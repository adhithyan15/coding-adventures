Urdu, chapter 72: High, Low, Full, Empty, Sweet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
اونچا (ū̃chā) — high.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for expensive, then the Urdu for cheap.

You'll want to know: اونچا.
اونچا — ū̃chā — "high".

The word, taken apart.
اونچا (ū̃chā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ucca, "tall, lofty" (CDIAL 1634). CDIAL calls the influence of udañc, "turned upwards", on the nasal very doubtful.

Lesson 4 of 5.
خالی (khālī) — empty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for low, then the Urdu for full.

You'll want to know: خالی.
خالی — khālī — "empty".

The word, taken apart.
خالی (khālī) is a loan from Arabic khālī, "empty", which reached Urdu through Persian. Its Arabic root is kh-l-w (Lane).

The word, taken apart.
میٹھا (mīṭhā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from miṣṭa, "dainty, sweet" (CDIAL 10299). CDIAL lists it under mṛṣṭa, "pure; prepared, savoury", of which miṣṭa is an early variant.
