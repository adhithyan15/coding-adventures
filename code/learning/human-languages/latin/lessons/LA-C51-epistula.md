## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C51-CITY-05]; assesses=[] -->

**epistula** — "**a letter**."

The farewell lessons quoted **two** of Cicero's letters and never gave you the word for one.
Here it is, and it is Greek: *epistolē*, "a thing sent **to** somebody," from
*epi-* ("to, upon") plus *stellō* ("I send"). Rome borrowed the object and the word
together.

The same *stellō* is inside **apostle** — *apo-stolos*, "one sent **away**" — so an
epistle and an apostle are the same verb with opposite prefixes, which is why the
letters of the apostles are called epistles twice over.

**scrībō**, from the verbs of mind, is the verb this noun needed. *Epistulam scrībō*: "I am
writing a letter." The verbs of mind gave you the writing and the *propediem* lesson gave you the
reading, and this is the thing itself.
