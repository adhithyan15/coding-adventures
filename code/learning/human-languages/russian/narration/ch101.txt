Russian, chapter 101: People, Jumper, Tie, Gloves, Scarf.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
люди (lyúdi) — people.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a calendar, then the Russian for a rucksack.

You'll want to know: люди.
люди (lyúdi) — "people".

The word, taken apart.
люди (lyúdi) from Proto-Slavic ljudĭje, "people", the plural of old люд, "folk" — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian liáudis, "the people", Old High German liut, "people", and Greek eleútheros, "free", from a root "to grow".

Lesson 4 of 5.
перчатки (perchátki) — gloves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a jumper, then the Russian for a tie.

You'll want to know: перчатки.
перчатки (perchátki) — "gloves".

The word, taken apart.
перчатки (perchátki) comes from перст, an old word for "finger" (Vasmer): first pĭrstŭčatŭ, "fingered", as in Old Russian пьрстатица, "a glove". Vasmer relates перст to Lithuanian pirštas, "finger".

Lesson 5 of 5.
шарф (sharf) — a scarf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a tie, then the Russian for gloves.

You'll want to know: шарф.
шарф (sharf) — "a scarf".

The word, taken apart.
шарф (sharf) probably came through Polish szarfa, "a military sash", from German Schärpe, "a sash", which is traced to French écharpe, "an arm sling" (Vasmer).
