## You'll want to know: chūfā
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C155-CHUFA-01]; assesses=[] -->

*chūfā* — "to set off; to depart".

> *huǒchē bā diǎn chūfā* — The train leaves at eight.

> *wǒmen jǐ diǎn chūfā* — What time do we set off?

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*chūfā* is built from 出 'to go out', the 出 you write, and fā 'to send off; to start', the fā of *fāshāo*. Both are inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsyhwit* and *pjot* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to go forward; to appear' and 'to start up'.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C72-TRAVEL72-05, ZH-LEX-C72-TRAVEL72-01] -->

*chūfā* says when a journey starts; 到 says when it arrives: *bā diǎn chūfā, shí diǎn dào*.

On a timetable the start is often written 开, as a train or a bus 开 when it leaves.
