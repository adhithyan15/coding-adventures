# The three goodbyes again, now that you can name the day

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C04-VELLI-VASTAANU-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] From memory: the ordinary Telugu goodbye. Then say what it means
word for word, which is not "goodbye" at all.

## You'll want to know: none of the three says "goodbye"
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C04-VELLI-VASTAANU-01, TE-LEX-C04-REEPU-KALUDDAAM-01, TE-LEX-C04-MALLI-KALUDDAAM-01] -->

You have three ways to leave, and every one of them promises a return:

| what you say | what it says |
|---|---|
| **వెళ్ళి వస్తాను** *veḷḷi vastānu* | having gone, I come |
| **రేపు కలుద్దాం** *rēpu kaluddām* | tomorrow, let us meet |
| **మళ్ళీ కలుద్దాం** *maḷḷī kaluddām* | again, let us meet |

Telugu has no comfortable word for a plain, final parting. The polite thing is
to name the return, and the three above are three sizes of it — the
next hour, the next day, some day.

## Grammar Lens: the "having …ed" link
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-GRAMMAR-C04-VELLI-VASTAANU-03, TE-LEX-C04-VELLU-01] -->

**వెళ్ళి** is not a finished verb — it is *having gone*, waiting for a second
verb to complete it. Telugu chains actions this way: every verb but the last
takes the *having …ed* shape, and the last one carries the person and the time.

**వెళ్ళి వస్తాను** = *having gone* + *I come*. The **‑తాను** at the end is the
same "I" ending you met on **మాట్లాడతాను**.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C04-VELLU-01, TE-LEX-C04-VELLI-VASTAANU-01, TE-GRAMMAR-C04-VELLI-VASTAANU-03, TE-LEX-C04-REEPU-KALUDDAAM-01, TE-LEX-C04-MALLI-KALUDDAAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What do all three farewells have in common? (**Each one promises a
return.**) What does **వెళ్ళి వస్తాను** say literally? (**Having gone, I
come.**) Which part of it carries the "I"? (**The ‑తాను ending.**) And which of
the three would you use with someone you see tomorrow? (**రేపు కలుద్దాం.**)
