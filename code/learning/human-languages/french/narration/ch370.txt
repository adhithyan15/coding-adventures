French, chapter 370: Les imprévus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'imprévu — the hitch, the unexpected event.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the possibility, then the French for for lack of.

You'll want to know: l'imprévu.
l'imprévu — "the hitch, the unexpected event".
J'ai un imprévu, je ne peux pas venir ce soir. Something has come up, I cannot come tonight.
Also an adjective: une visite imprévue. Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
imprévu is built from in- "not" and prévu, the participle of prévoir "to foresee" (EtymDB), from Latin praevidēre "to foresee", built on vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: prévisible.
prévisible — "foreseeable, predictable".
Ce retard était prévisible avec cette neige. This delay was predictable with this snow.

The word, taken apart.

Lesson 4 of 5.
adapter — to adapt (mostly reflexive, s'adapter).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for foreseeable, then the French for unpredictable.

You'll want to know: adapter.
adapter — "to adapt (mostly reflexive, s'adapter)".
Les enfants s'adaptent vite à une nouvelle école. Children adapt quickly to a new school.
Mostly reflexive: s'adapter à — to adapt to. Adapter un livre — to adapt a book for the screen.

The word, taken apart.
adapter is a learned borrowing from Latin adaptāre "to fit to" (EtymDB), built from ad "to" and aptāre "to fit", from aptus "fitted" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
souple — supple; easygoing, adaptable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for unpredictable, then the French for to adapt.

You'll want to know: souple.
souple — "supple; easygoing, adaptable".
Mes horaires sont souples: je peux venir plus tard. My hours are adaptable: I can come later.
Of the body: supple. Of a person or a rule: easygoing.

The word, taken apart.
souple is inherited from Latin supplex "bending the knee, humble" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on plicāre "to fold".
