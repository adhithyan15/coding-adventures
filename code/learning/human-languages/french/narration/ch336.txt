French, chapter 336: Pas d'accord.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le désaccord — the disagreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the line of argument, then the French for to persuade.

You'll want to know: le désaccord.
le désaccord — "the disagreement".
Nous sommes en désaccord sur presque tout. We disagree about almost everything.
Être en désaccord — to disagree. The opposite is être d'accord.

The word, taken apart.
désaccord is built from dés- "not" and accord "agreement", which comes from the Old French verb acorder (EtymDB). That verb is usually traced to a Latin formation on cor, cordis "heart".

Lesson 2 of 5.
objecter — to object, to say in reply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to persuade, then the French for the disagreement.

You'll want to know: objecter.
objecter — "to object, to say in reply".
Elle a objecté que c'était trop cher. She objected that it was too expensive.
Objecter que… — to object that… Formal; in speech many people say je ne suis pas d'accord.

The word, taken apart.
objecter is a learned borrowing from Latin obiectāre "to throw against, to set before" (Lewis & Short), formed on obicere "to throw before", from ob and iacere "to throw".

l'objection — "the objection".
Pas d'objection? Alors nous commençons. No objection? Then we start.

objection is a learned borrowing from Late Latin obiectiō "a throwing against, an objection", from obicere "to throw before" (Lewis & Short), built on iacere "to throw".

Lesson 4 of 5.
rétorquer — to retort.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the French for to object, then the French for the objection.

You'll want to know: rétorquer.
rétorquer — "to retort".
Il a rétorqué que ce n'était pas vrai. He retorted that it was not true.
Literally to twist back: you turn the words back on the speaker.

The word, taken apart.
rétorquer is a learned borrowing from Latin retorquēre "to twist or bend back" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on torquēre "to twist".

Lesson 5 of 5.
contredire — to contradict.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the objection, then the French for to retort.

You'll want to know: contredire.
contredire — "to contradict".
Je n'aime pas quand tu me contredis devant tout le monde. I do not like it when you contradict me in front of everyone.
Like dire, but vous contredisez. Se contredire — to contradict yourself.

The word, taken apart.
contredire comes through Old French from Latin contrādīcere "to speak against" (EtymDB), built from contrā "against" and dīcere "to say" (Lewis & Short).
