# la estantería — a place full of shelves

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-02, ES-LEX-CASA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el contador*. That ending has now served you twice. Here is a
different one, and it does a different job.

## You'll want to know: la estantería
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-03]; assesses=[] -->

**la estantería** — the shelving, the bookcase, the shelf unit.

A single shelf is **el estante**. The **-ería** makes it the whole structure.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-03] -->

**-ería** does not make a doer. It makes a **place or collection** of the thing:

| the thing | the place or collection |
|---|---|
| el estante | la estantería |
| el pan | la panadería |
| el libro | la librería |

That is why so many shop names end this way: a *panadería* is where the bread
is, a *librería* where the books are. Meeting the ending once tells you what a
shop sells before you have read the sign properly.

Two endings, two different jobs: **-dor** gives you who or what does it,
**-ería** gives you where it is kept.
