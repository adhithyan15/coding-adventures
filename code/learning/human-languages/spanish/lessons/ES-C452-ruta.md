# la ruta — the way through

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-05, ES-LEX-C302-AWAY-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **roto** and **la calle**. One of those two is about to turn up
inside a word for a road.

## You'll want to know: la ruta
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-01] -->

**la ruta** — the route.

*La ruta del reparto* — the delivery round. *Cambiar de ruta* — to change
route. *La ruta más corta* — the shortest way. *En ruta* — on the way.

It is the word for a **planned** road, which is why a delivery driver, a bus
company and a hiking map all use it. The street itself stays *la calle*; the
line you trace across several of them is *la ruta*.

## Grammar Lens: ruta, camino, calle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-02, ES-LEX-C302-AWAY-01] -->

Three words an English speaker would cover with "road" or "way":

| what it is | the word |
|---|---|
| the street with the houses on it | **la calle** |
| the path underfoot | **el camino** |
| the plan of where to go | **la ruta** |

*Voy por la calle Mayor* — I'm going along a particular street. *El camino
está mojado* — the path is wet, something you can stand on. *La ruta pasa por
tres pueblos* — the route goes through three villages, which is a fact about
the plan and not about any surface.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C452-REPARTO-02, ES-LEX-C448-MUDA-05] -->

Latin **via rupta** — a **broken** way.

*Rupta* is the participle of *rumpere*, to burst or break, and it is the very
word you already have in **roto**. A *via rupta* was a road forced through
what was in the way: trees felled, rock broken, a line opened where there had
been none.

Latin then dropped the *via* and kept the adjective, which is how a word
meaning "broken" ended up meaning "route". English took it from French as
**route**, and also as **rout** — an army broken open — and **routine**, a way
so well worn that you no longer choose it.

So *roto* and *ruta* are one word. A broken plate and a road through a
mountain are the same idea applied to two different obstacles.
