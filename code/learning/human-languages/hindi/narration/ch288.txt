Hindi, chapter 288: A Theft in the Lane.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
चोरी (corī) — a theft.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: चोरी.
चोरी — corī — "a theft".
चोरी के बाद क्या होगा, यह पुलिस पर निर्भर करता है। corī ke bād kyā hogā, yah pulis par nirbhar kartā hai. What happens after the theft depends on the police.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
is inherited from Sanskrit caurikā, "theft" (CDIAL 4937). It sits beside चोर, a thief: चोर is the person, चोरी (corī) is what the person does, and चोरी (corī) होना is for a theft to take place.

Lesson 2 of 5.
चुराना (curānā) — to steal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a stretch (of the body), then the Hindi for a theft.

You'll want to know: चुराना.
चुराना — curānā — "to steal".
चोर ने मेरी साइकिल चुराई। cor ne merī sāikil curāī. The thief stole my bicycle.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बटुआ चुराया, usne baṭuā curāyā.

The word, taken apart.
चुराना (curānā) belongs with Sanskrit corayati, "steals" (CDIAL 4933), where CDIAL records an older Hindi verb cornā. With ने the verb agrees with the thing taken: साइकिल चुराई, but बटुआ चुराया.

Lesson 3 of 5.
चौकीदार (caukīdār) — a watchman.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a theft, then the Hindi for to steal.

You'll want to know: चौकीदार.
चौकीदार — caukīdār — "a watchman".
चौकीदार रात भर गली में घूम रहा था। caukīdār rāt bhar galī meṁ ghūm rahā thā. The watchman was walking up and down the lane all night.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
चौकीदार (caukīdār) joins caukī, a guard post, from Sanskrit catuṣka, "a set of four, a square" (CDIAL 4629), with -dār, usually traced to Persian dār, "holding": the one who keeps the post. दुकानदार is built with the same ending.

Lesson 4 of 5.
गवाह (gavāh) — a witness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to steal, then the Hindi for a watchman.

You'll want to know: गवाह.
गवाह — gavāh — "a witness".
गवाह ने पुलिस को सब कुछ बताया। gavāh ne pulis ko sab kuch batāyā. The witness told the police everything.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
गवाह (gavāh) is usually traced to Persian gavāh, "a witness", a word that reached Hindi through the courts. It is used of a man or a woman: वह गवाह है.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बाहर (bāhar) — outside.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a watchman, then the Hindi for a witness.

You'll want to know: बाहर.
बाहर — bāhar — "outside".
चोरी के बाद हम सब घर से बाहर आए। corī ke bād ham sab ghar se bāhar āe. After the theft we all came out of the house.

The word, taken apart.
बाहर (bāhar) is inherited from bāhira, "outer", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9226). It pairs with अंदर: घर से बाहर, out of the house, against घर के अंदर, inside it.
