Persian, chapter 356: Chop, mince and fry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ریز کردن (riz kardan) — to chop finely, to dice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to sift, then the Persian for to soak.

You'll want to know: ریز کردن.
ریز کردن (riz kardan) — "to chop finely, to dice".
سبزی ها را ریز کرد. Sabzi-hâ râ riz kard. She chopped the herbs finely.
Riz "small, fine". Present riz mikonad.

The word, taken apart.
riz "fine, small" is a native Persian word that EtymDB lists without a parent; kardan "to do" is inherited Persian.

Lesson 2 of 5.
چرخ کردن (charkh kardan) — to mince, to grind (meat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to soak, then the Persian for to chop finely.

You'll want to know: چرخ کردن.
چرخ کردن (charkh kardan) — "to mince, to grind (meat)".
قصاب گوشت را چرخ کرد. Qassâb gusht râ charkh kard. The butcher minced the meat.
Gusht-e charkh karde is minced meat. Charkh "wheel", for the mincer.

The word, taken apart.
charkh "wheel" is inherited: EtymDB traces it through Middle Persian to the Indo-European word for a wheel, kʷékʷlos. kardan "to do" is inherited too.

Lesson 4 of 5.
تفت دادن (taft dâdan) — to sauté, to fry lightly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to mince, then the Persian for to mash.

You'll want to know: تفت دادن.
تفت دادن (taft dâdan) — "to sauté, to fry lightly".
پیاز را در کره تفت داد. Piyâz râ dar kare taft dâd. She lightly fried the onion in butter.
Quick frying while stirring. Present taft midahad.

The word, taken apart.
taft "heat" is the past stem of the native verb taftan "to heat, to glow", and dâdan "to give" is inherited Persian: to give it heat.

Lesson 5 of 5.
پیاز داغ (piyâz dâgh) — fried onion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to mash, then the Persian for to sauté.

You'll want to know: پیاز داغ.
پیاز داغ (piyâz dâgh) — "fried onion".
روی آش پیاز داغ ریخت. Ru-ye âsh piyâz dâgh rikht. He spooned fried onion over the soup.
Thinly sliced onion fried until golden, a topping for many dishes. Dâgh "hot".

The word, taken apart.
piyâz "onion" is inherited from Middle Persian (EtymDB), and dâgh "hot" is native too; EtymDB takes it back to the Indo-European root dʰegʷʰ- "to burn".
