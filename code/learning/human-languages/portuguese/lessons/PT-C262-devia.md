# devia — what you ought to do about it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C83-QUERER83-02, PT-GRAMMAR-DUAS-SETAS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I would like". (*Gostaria.*) Then run a reason forward: "I've
got a cold, so I'm not going out." (*Estou constipado, por isso não saio.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An explanation often ends on a third move: what somebody ought to
do. That move has its own form.

## You'll want to know: devia
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-DEVIA-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C82-PODER82-02, PT-LEX-C203-VERBOS203-02, PT-LEX-C55-VERBOS5-04] -->

You have **dever**, "to have to, must": **devo estudar**, I must study. Put it
in the imperfeito and it softens into advice:

> **Devia** estudar. — I should study.
> **Devias** descansar. — You should rest. *(to a friend)*
> **Devia** descansar. — You should rest. *(to o senhor, a senhora or você)*

A bare infinitive follows, with nothing in between. *Devo estudar* has no way
out; *devia estudar* says I know I ought to, and have not promised. That
softness is the register advice usually wants.

This is the imperfeito doing a present job, and in Portugal it is the
everyday way to say "should". The same verb has a second soft form,
**deveria**, with the ending of your **gostaria**:

> **Deveria** descansar mais. — I ought to rest more.

*Deveria* is a little more careful, and Brazil uses it more than Portugal. Its
*-ia* belongs to the would-form of Portuguese, which the last chapter of this
run builds properly.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-DEBERE-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C162-COISAS162-01] -->

**dever** is inherited from Latin *dēbēre*, "to owe" (EtymDB 2.1), and
*dēbēre* is a compound: *dē*, "from", plus *habēre*, "to have" (Lewis & Short,
under *debeo*). To owe is to **have something from someone**: you are holding
what is theirs. **a dívida**, "a debt", which you have met, is the same family,
and English took it as **debt**, **due** and **duty**.
