Mandarin Chinese, chapter 507: Fathers and Sons.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
xiōngdì — brothers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for Beijing opera, then the Mandarin for a fan (of a singer).

You'll want to know: xiōngdì.
xiōngdì — "brothers".
xiōngdì liǎng ge gēge bǐ dìdi gāo — Of the two brothers, the elder is taller.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiōngdì is built from xiōng 'an elder brother' and dì 'a younger brother', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xjwaeng and dejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, 'a man born first' and the second 'a man born after'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
父子 (fùzǐ) — father and son.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a fan (of a singer), then the Mandarin for brothers.

You'll want to know: 父子.
父子 — fùzǐ — "father and son".
他们父子都是歌手 tāmen fùzǐ dōu shì gēshǒu — Father and son are both singers.
The new character, in pieces: 父 (fù) — four strokes: two short strokes on top, one falling left and a dot falling right, then two long strokes that cross, one falling left and one falling right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
父子 (fùzǐ) is built from 父 (fù) 'a father' and 子 'a son', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjuX and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older dictionary, as the head of a family who leads and teaches, and the second 'offspring'. The dictionary also lists 父 (fù) as pjuX, a title of respect for a man.

Lesson 3 of 6.
父 (fù) — two short strokes, then two long ones.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two short strokes, then two long ones.
父 (fù).
Two short strokes on top: on the left a stroke falling to the left, on the right a dot falling to the right.
Then two long strokes that cross: a long stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right, like the legs of 人 set wide.
2 + 2 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 父 (fù), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes does 父 (fù) have? (Four.) Which way does the first stroke fall? (To the left.) What do the last two strokes do? (They cross.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 父 (fù), pinned stroke paths and medians.
