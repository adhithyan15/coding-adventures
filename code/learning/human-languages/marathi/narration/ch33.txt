Marathi, chapter 33: Asking a Yes-or-No Question.
3 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
… का? (… kā?) — the yes-or-no question.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Retrieve the last two items by name: the tag ना, and the sentence-final नाही. Both go at the end. So does this one.

Grammar Lens: का at the end.
माहीत आहे का? — māhīt āhe kā? — Do you know?
Marathi leaves the person out when it is obvious, so this three-word question needs no pronoun at all.
Take a statement, put का on the end, and nothing else moves — no word order, no verb. Marathi does not turn a sentence around to ask; it appends.
ना says this is so, agree with me. का says I do not know, tell me.
चालेल का? — chālel kā? — Is that all right?
That is how you ask permission, and the answer comes back as the चालेल you already own.

Sounds you'll need: the question can ride on the voice alone.
Speakers often drop का and let pitch do the work: चहा? with a rising voice is a complete offer. Say चहा. flat, then चहा? rising. A listening paper gives you the rise without the particle, so the ear must hold both.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: māhīt āhe kā? and answer it — ho or nāhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chālel kā? and answer it — chālel]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chahā? with the rise, and chahā. flat]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Ask whether the listener knows, then ask whether that is all right.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does का on the end do, and what else moves? (Makes a yes-or-no question; nothing.) How does it differ from a final ना? (ना expects agreement; का does not know the answer.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
? and! — the marks that go only at the end.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script: one mark, and only at the end and Script: the exclamation mark until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can now ask a yes-or-no question out loud. On paper, the rising voice is gone. Something has to take its place.

Script: one mark, and only at the end.
चहा आहे का?
Marathi writes the question mark exactly where English does: one mark, at the end, and nowhere else. If you have met Spanish, this is worth naming — Spanish opens a question with an upside-down mark as well as closing it, and Marathi does not. There is nothing at the front of the sentence to warn you.
That is the practical consequence: in a printed Marathi sentence the only signal that you are reading a question may be the last character on the line, or the little का just before it. Read to the end before you decide.
And the rising voice you learned last lesson leaves no trace at all. Written down, चहा? has to carry the mark, because there is no pitch on paper.

Script: the exclamation mark.
नमस्कार!
Same rule, same place, one mark. It marks a greeting or a raised voice, and it never doubles at the front either.

Writing — copy and mark.
Write चहा आहे का? once. Then write नमस्कार! once. Check that each line carries exactly one mark and that it is the last thing on the line.
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Write the tea question and the greeting, each with its own end mark.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many question marks does a written Marathi question carry, and where? (One, at the end.) So what must you do before deciding whether a printed sentence is a question? (Read to the end of it.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Ask it, then answer it — a whole exchange out of a statement, a particle, and the two answers.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
A question in Marathi is a statement with का on the end and one mark on the page. Say one, then write it — and write नमस्कार! underneath, so the two end marks stand side by side.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: chahā āhe kā? — answer ho]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: māhīt āhe kā? — answer malā māhīt nāhī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chālel kā? — answer nako, dhanyavād]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 35 seconds]
Ask whether there is tea, answer yes; ask whether that is all right, decline.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Name the three answers a का question can get. (हो, नाही, नको — and the last only for an offer.) Then stop.
