Latin, chapter 316: Auxilium.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ēripiō, ēripere — to snatch away, to rescue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to overwhelm, then the Latin for a disaster.

You'll want to know: ēripiō, ēripere.
ēripiō, ēripere — "to snatch away, to rescue".
Pater fīlium ē flūmine ēripuit. The father snatched his son out of the river.
ēripiō, ēripere, ēripuī, ēreptum: ē + rapiō. Ēripere ex perīculō: to rescue from danger.

The word, taken apart.
ēripiō is built on rapiō, "to snatch", with ex-, "out" (EtymDB); the vowel changes inside the compound.

Lesson 2 of 5.
incolumis, incolume — unharmed, safe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a disaster, then the Latin for to snatch away.

You'll want to know: incolumis, incolume.
incolumis, incolume — "unharmed, safe".
Omnēs nautae incolumēs ad lītus pervēnērunt. All the sailors reached the shore unharmed.
Two endings. Caesar uses it of soldiers who came back safe and sound.

The word, taken apart.
incolumis joins in-, "not", and a second part that EtymDB traces to Proto-Italic kadami-, "damaged". Lewis & Short compare calamitās; the details are not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
subveniō, subvenīre — to come to help.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to snatch away, then the Latin for unharmed.

You'll want to know: subveniō, subvenīre.
subveniō, subvenīre — "to come to help".
Vīcīnī celeriter subvēnērunt. The neighbours quickly came to help.
subveniō, subvenīre, subvēnī, subventum, with the dative: mihi subvēnit. English subvention.

The word, taken apart.
subveniō is built on veniō, "to come", with sub- (EtymDB): to come up in support, as reserves do in battle. English subvention comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
auxilium, auxiliī — help, aid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to come to help, then the Latin for to run to help.

You'll want to know: auxilium, auxiliī.
auxilium, auxiliī — "help, aid".
Fēmina auxilium ā vīcīnīs petīvit. The woman asked the neighbours for help.
Neuter. The plural auxilia means auxiliary troops. English auxiliary.

The word, taken apart.
auxilium is built on augeō, "to increase" (EtymDB): what adds to your strength. English auxiliary comes from it.
