## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-ORDINAL-APOCOPE, ES-GRAMMAR-DISTRIBUTIVE-UNO-OTRO, ES-GRAMMAR-INFINITIVE-AS-SUBJECT, ES-GRAMMAR-CIRCUMSTANTIAL-MOVES] -->

Four chants.

[PAUSE 2s] **First.** *El primer libro*, *la primera casa* — ordinals go in
front, and two of them drop their *o* before a masculine noun.

[PAUSE 2s] **Second.** *Uno es grande, otro es bueno* — a pair split across the
sentence. *Otro libro*, with no article in front of it.

[PAUSE 2s] **Third.** *Comer es bueno* — the infinitive naming an activity,
where English would reach for *-ing* and Spanish will not.

[PAUSE 2s] **Fourth.** *Hoy como en casa* — the when and the where can move, and
the front is where the emphasis lives.

## Grammar Lens: the thread
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-APOCOPE-PATTERN, ES-GRAMMAR-WORD-ORDER-EMPHASIS, ES-GRAMMAR-INFINITIVE-NOT-GERUND] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Primer* is the **third** time you have watched Spanish bite the
ending off a word leaning on another — after *muy* and *mal*. Three sightings
make a habit, not a list of exceptions, and you will meet a fourth without being
surprised.

[PAUSE 2s] And the moving pieces close a thread that has run through this whole
level. Latin marked who did what with **endings**. Those endings fell away, and
Spanish built replacements out of small audible things: an article to say which
one, a preposition to mark the person on the receiving end, a comma to address
somebody, and a fixed front position to carry emphasis.

[PAUSE 2s] Every one of those was a chapter of its own. Together they are one
story, and you now have all of it.
