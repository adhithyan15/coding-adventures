Mandarin Chinese, chapter 238: Sea and Sky.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tiānkōng — the sky.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sprout, then the Mandarin for a watermelon.

You'll want to know: tiānkōng.
tiānkōng — "the sky".
jīntiān de tiānkōng hěn lán — The sky is so blue today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiānkōng is built from 天 'heaven' and kōng 'empty', both inherited from Middle Chinese, then and khuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'hollow'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

yuèliang — "the moon".
jīntiān wǎnshang yuèliang hěn dà — The moon is huge tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuèliang is built from 月 'moon' and liang 'bright', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwot and ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'bright'; in the word the second loses its tone.

Lesson 3 of 5.
wūyún — dark clouds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the sky, then the Mandarin for the moon.

You'll want to know: wūyún.
wūyún — "dark clouds".
wūyún lái le yào xiàyǔ le — Dark clouds are coming in; it's about to rain.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wūyún is built from wū 'black as a crow' and 云 'cloud'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese hjun (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary explains the first, 'u there, after an older book, as the crow that is all black.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cǎihóng — rainbow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dark clouds, then the Mandarin for the open sea.

You'll want to know: cǎihóng.
cǎihóng — "rainbow".
yǔ tíng le yǒu cǎihóng — The rain's stopped, and there's a rainbow.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cǎihóng is built from cǎi 'colour' and hóng 'rainbow'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese tshojX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'brilliance'; the dictionary glosses the second, huwng there, with an old two-character word for the rainbow.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.
