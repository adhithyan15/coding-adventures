Latin, chapter 153: Cloudy, Windy, To build, To climb, To go down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nūbilus, nūbila, nūbilum — cloudy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for icy, then the Latin for clear.

You'll want to know: nūbilus, nūbila, nūbilum.
nūbilus, nūbila, nūbilum — "cloudy".

The word, taken apart.
nūbilus is built on nūbēs, "a cloud" (Lewis & Short), which is probably traced to Proto-Indo-European (s)newdh-, "to cover" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ventōsus, ventōsa, ventōsum — windy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for clear, then the Latin for cloudy.

You'll want to know: ventōsus, ventōsa, ventōsum.
ventōsus, ventōsa, ventōsum — "windy".

The word, taken apart.
ventōsus is built on ventus, "wind" (Lewis & Short), with -ōsus, "full of". ventus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2weh1nts, "blowing, wind", from h2weh1-, "to blow" (EtymDB 2.1); English wind is a cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
aedificō, aedificāre — to build.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for cloudy, then the Latin for windy.

You'll want to know: aedificō, aedificāre.
aedificō, aedificāre — "to build".

The word, taken apart.
aedificō is built from aedēs, "a building, a temple", and faciō, "to make" (Lewis & Short), which takes the form -ficō in compounds: to make a building. English edifice comes from its family.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ascendō, ascendere — to climb, to go up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for windy, then the Latin for to build.

You'll want to know: ascendō, ascendere.
ascendō, ascendere — "to climb, to go up".

The word, taken apart.
ascendō is ad, "to, towards", with scandō, "to climb" (Lewis & Short); the a of the verb weakened to e in the compound.
