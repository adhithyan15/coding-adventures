# पण (paṇ) — set one statement against another

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MW-LEX-CHAAY-01, MW-LEX-PAANI-01, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-01, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-02, MW-LEX-HAI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **चाय गरम है।** Tea is hot. Then say **पाणी ठंडो है।** Water
is cold. Keep both short statements intact.

## You'll want to know: पण
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-CONJ-PAN-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **पण** — *paṇ* — **but, yet**

The Government of India's survey records Marwari **पण** as the particle for
“yet/but”. A Marwari dictionary independently prints the same Devanagari form
and labels it a conjunction meaning “but”.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

The two sources attest the Marwari form and meaning but do not give an
etymology. Its origin remains unknown in the sources used for this lesson.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MW-GRAMMAR-ADVERSATIVE-PAN-01]; assesses=[MW-CONJ-PAN-01, MW-LEX-CHAAY-01, MW-LEX-PAANI-01, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-01, MW-LEX-C74-ADJ74-02, MW-LEX-HAI-01] -->

Keep the two familiar statements and put **पण** at their turning point:

> **चाय गरम है, पण पाणी ठंडो है।**
>
> *chāy garam hai, paṇ pāṇī ṭhaṇḍo hai.*
>
> Tea is hot, but water is cold.

The first statement stays true. **पण** tells the listener that the second one
pulls in a different direction.
