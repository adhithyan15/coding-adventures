Japanese, chapter 155: Moving House and Shifting Furniture.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ひっこす (hikkosu) — to move house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to get by, then the Japanese for to get used to.

You'll want to know: ひっこす.
ひっこす — hikkosu — "to move house".
おじさんは よく ひっこします。 — ojisan wa yoku hikkoshimasu — "My uncle often moves house."
Polite ひっこします, hikkoshimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
ひっこす (hikkosu) is native Japanese, built from hiki, "pulling", the stem of hiku, and kosu, "to cross over", joined with a held k; both parts are inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
うごかす (ugokasu) — to shift; to move (something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to move house, then the Japanese for to carry.

You'll want to know: うごかす.
うごかす — ugokasu — "to shift; to move (something)".
つくえを うごかします。 — tsukue o ugokashimasu — "I shift the desk."
Polite うごかします, ugokashimasu; a godan verb. Its intransitive partner is うごく, ugoku.

The word, taken apart.
うごかす (ugokasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as ugoku, "to move"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かぐ (kagu) — furniture.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to carry, then the Japanese for to shift.

You'll want to know: かぐ.
かぐ — kagu — "furniture".
この へやは かぐが すくないです。 — kono heya wa kagu ga sukunai desu — "This room has little furniture."

The word, taken apart.
かぐ (kagu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ka, "house, home" (Middle Chinese kae) and gu, "tool, utensil" (gjuH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
