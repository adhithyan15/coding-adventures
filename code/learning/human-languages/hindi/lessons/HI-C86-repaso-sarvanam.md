# sarvanām kī tālikā — subject and oblique, side by side

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the five subject pronouns and as many obliques as you can,
before reading the table.

## Grammar Lens: the table, complete
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-01, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-02, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04] -->

| subject | oblique |
|---|---|
| मैं | *mujhe* |
| हम | हमें |
| तुम | तुम्हें |
| आप | आपको |
| वह | *use* |
| वे | *unhẽ* |

Two of the obliques are visibly **को** attached — *āpko* plainly, *use* after
wear. Three take the nasal ending. The set is small and none of it is arbitrary
once you know that a postposition is hiding in each one.

## Grammar Lens: the fourth appearance of one decision
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C86-PRON-03, HI-LEX-C86-PRON-04, HI-LEX-C84-POSS-03] -->

| system | at the tum level | at the āp level |
|---|---|---|
| possessive | तुम्हारा | आपका |
| copula | हो | हैं |
| command | बोलो | बोलिए |
| **oblique pronoun** | **तुम्हें** | **आपको** |

Four systems, one decision. Choosing how to address someone settles all four at
once, which is why the grid has been worth building across three chapters
rather than learning four unrelated lists.
