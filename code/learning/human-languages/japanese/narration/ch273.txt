Japanese, chapter 273: The Trial.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
さいばん (saiban) — a trial; a court case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to hide (something), then the Japanese for true.

You'll want to know: さいばん.
さいばん — saiban — "a trial; a court case".
あした さいばんが あります。 — ashita saiban ga arimasu — "The trial is tomorrow."

The word, taken apart.
さいばん (saiban) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sai, "judge" (Middle Chinese dzoj) and ban, "judgement" (phanH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The second is voiced from han to ban.

Lesson 4 of 6.
うったえる (uttaeru) — to sue; to complain (to an authority).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a fine, then the Japanese for a verdict.

You'll want to know: うったえる.
うったえる — uttaeru — "to sue; to complain (to an authority)".
その おんなは かいしゃを うったえました。 — sono onna wa kaisha o uttaemashita — "That woman took the company to court."
Polite うったえます, uttaemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): おとこが みせを うったえました。, otoko ga mise o uttaemashita.

The word, taken apart.
うったえる (uttaeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
