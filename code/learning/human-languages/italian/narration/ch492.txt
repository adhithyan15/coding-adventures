Italian, chapter 492: Abbracci e coccole.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'abbraccio — hug, embrace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for tenderness, then the Italian for kiss.

You'll want to know: l'abbraccio.
l'abbraccio — "hug, embrace".
Mia nonna mi saluta sempre con un abbraccio forte. My grandmother always greets me with a big hug.
The noun for abbracciare. At the end of a message: un abbraccio — a hug.

The word, taken apart.
abbraccio comes from abbracciare "to hug", built on braccio "arm", which is inherited from Latin bracchium (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
coccolare — to cuddle, to pamper.

Before the new one: say the Italian for kiss, then the Italian for hug.

You'll want to know: coccolare.
coccolare — "to cuddle, to pamper".
Il nonno coccola il nipotino tutto il giorno. Grandpa cuddles his little grandson all day.
Also to spoil with attention: coccolarsi — to treat oneself.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of coccolare is not settled; it may come from a word children use.

Lesson 3 of 5.
le coccole — cuddles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for hug, then the Italian for to cuddle.

You'll want to know: le coccole.
le coccole — "cuddles".
Il gatto vuole le coccole. The cat wants cuddles.
Usually plural: fare le coccole a qualcuno.

The word, taken apart.
coccole is the noun that goes with coccolare "to cuddle"; the origin of the word is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
affezionarsi — to grow fond of, to get attached to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to cuddle, then the Italian for cuddles.

You'll want to know: affezionarsi.
affezionarsi — "to grow fond of, to get attached to".
Mi sono affezionato al cane dei vicini. I have grown fond of the neighbours' dog.
Affezionarsi a + person or thing. Affezionato — fond, attached.

The word, taken apart.
affezionarsi is built on affezione "affection", a learned borrowing from Latin affectiō "a state of mind, a feeling" (Lewis & Short), from afficere "to affect".

Lesson 5 of 5.
affettuoso — affectionate, loving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for cuddles, then the Italian for to grow fond of.

You'll want to know: affettuoso.
affettuoso — "affectionate, loving".
Mia figlia è molto affettuosa con tutti. My daughter is very affectionate with everyone.
At the end of a letter: saluti affettuosi — warm wishes.

The word, taken apart.
affettuoso is a learned borrowing from Late Latin affectuōsus "full of affection" (Lewis & Short), from affectus "feeling".
