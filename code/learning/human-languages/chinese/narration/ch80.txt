Mandarin Chinese, chapter 80: Jobs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shàngbān — to go to work.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for homework, then the Mandarin for to take an exam.

You'll want to know: shàngbān.
shàngbān — "to go to work".
wǒ jiǔ diǎn shàngbān — I start work at nine.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shàngbān is built from 上 'to go up to' and bān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyangX and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the second, through an older book, as dividing up jade tallies; a shift of work is a likely step, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word about people.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: shàngbān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: shàngbān, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ jiǔ diǎn shàngbān — I start work at nine.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for homework, then the Mandarin for to take an exam, then say shàngbān again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does shàngbān mean? ("To go to work".) Say it once more.

sījī is built from sī 'to be in charge' and 机 'machine', both inherited from Middle Chinese, si and kj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the one in charge of the machine.
