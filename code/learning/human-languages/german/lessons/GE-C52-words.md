# (reading) — six nouns, and a capital letter doing real work

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERBKLAMMER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word below you have met. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERBKLAMMER-01] -->

> Brot
> Kaffee
> Wasser
> Buch
> Bruder
> Schwester

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and notice they all begin with a capital. Every one of them.

## Grammar Lens: the capital is information
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SKILL-READ-WORDS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] German capitalises every noun. Spoken, that rule is invisible and
costs nothing. Written, it is the single most useful thing on a German page for
a beginner.

[PAUSE 2s] It means you can find the nouns in a sentence before you understand
the sentence. In a language that puts its verb at the end and its cases on the
articles, knowing which words are the things is most of the way to a reading.

[PAUSE 2s] That is the trade: a writer capitalises a hundred nouns so a reader
can skip a hundred decisions.
