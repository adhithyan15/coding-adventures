Latin, chapter 313: Ruīna.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ruō, ruere — to rush; to fall down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for exile, then the Latin for a chain.

You'll want to know: ruō, ruere.
ruō, ruere — "to rush; to fall down".
Aqua dē monte ruēbat. Water was rushing down from the mountain.
ruō, ruere, ruī, rutum: any violent movement, forward or down.

The word, taken apart.
ruō is an inherited verb (EtymDB). English ruin comes from its noun ruīna.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ruīna, ruīnae — a collapse, a fall; ruins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a chain, then the Latin for to rush.

You'll want to know: ruīna, ruīnae.
ruīna, ruīnae — "a collapse, a fall; ruins".
Post ruīnam aedificiī nihil nisi saxa manēbat. After the collapse of the building nothing remained but stones.
Feminine, from ruō. The plural ruīnae: ruins. English ruin.

The word, taken apart.
ruīna is built on ruō, "to rush down, to collapse" (EtymDB): a falling down. English ruin comes from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
corruō, corruere — to collapse, to tumble down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to rush, then the Latin for a collapse.

You'll want to know: corruō, corruere.
corruō, corruere — "to collapse, to tumble down".
Tabernae mihi duae corruērunt. Two of my shops have tumbled down.
corruō, corruere, corruī: con- + ruō. A landlord in one of Cicero's letters complains in these very words.

The word, taken apart.
corruō is built on ruō, "to fall down", with con-, "together, wholly" (Lewis & Short): to fall in a heap.

Lesson 4 of 5.
collābor, collābī — to fall in, to collapse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a collapse, then the Latin for to collapse.

You'll want to know: collābor, collābī.
collābor, collābī — "to fall in, to collapse".
Pōns vetus subitō collāpsus est. The old bridge suddenly collapsed.
collābor, collābī, collāpsus sum, a deponent: con- + lābor (taught). English collapse comes from its participle.

The word, taken apart.
collābor is built on lābor, "to slip, to fall", with con- (Lewis & Short): to fall together in ruins. English collapse comes from its participle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēcidō, dēcidere — to fall down, to fall off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to collapse, then the Latin for to fall in.

You'll want to know: dēcidō, dēcidere.
dēcidō, dēcidere — "to fall down, to fall off".
Tēgula dē tēctō dēcidit et ante mē frācta est. A tile fell off the roof and broke in front of me.
dēcidō, dēcidere, dēcidī, short i: dē + cadō (taught). Do not confuse dēcīdō, 'to cut off'.

The word, taken apart.
dēcidō is built on cadō, "to fall", with dē, "down from" (Lewis & Short); the vowel changes inside the compound.
