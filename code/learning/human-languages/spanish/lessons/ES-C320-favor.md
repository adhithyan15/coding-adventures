## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C320-COURT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**el favor** — "a favour," a good turn done for somebody.

Latin **favēre** meant *to be well disposed toward* — to be on someone's side.
The noun **favor** was what that goodwill looked like when it did something.

You have been carrying it without opening it. ***Por favor*** is *por* plus
*favor*: **for a favour**. Every time you have said *please* in Spanish you have
been saying *as a good turn to me*. Now the second half stands on its own:
*un favor* is a thing you ask for, and *el favor* is the one being asked.

English has it three ways over: a **favour** is the good turn, your **favourite**
is what your goodwill lands on, and **favourable** weather is weather on your
side.
