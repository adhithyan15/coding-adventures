6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
gēbo — arm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a tooth pulled, then the Mandarin for to have a tooth filled.

You'll want to know: gēbo.
gēbo — "arm".
wǒ gōngzuò le shí ge xiǎoshí gēbo hěn téng — I worked for ten hours, and my arms really ache.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gēbo is built from gē and bo, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kak and phak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the armpit and the second as dried meat; how the pair came to mean the arm is not settled, and the first tone of gē is irregular.

Lesson 2 of 6.
力气 (lìqi) — physical strength.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a tooth filled, then the Mandarin for the arm.

You'll want to know: 力气.
力气 — lìqi — "physical strength".
他的力气比我大 tā de lìqi bǐ wǒ dà — He's stronger than I am.
The new character, in pieces: 力 (lì) — two strokes: a stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks to the left, and a long stroke falling to the left through it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
力气 (lìqi) is built from 力 (lì) 'strength' and 气 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lik and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'sinew' and the second 'breath'; the second is said light.

Script — a turn with a hook, then a falling stroke.
力 (lì).
One piece of two strokes.
The first stroke. It runs right, turns down, and at the foot hooks back to the left.
The second stroke. A long stroke falling to the left, from above the first stroke, down through it to the bottom left.
1 + 1 = two.

Lesson 6 of 6.
shǒuzhǐ — finger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for stamina, then the Mandarin for to put one's strength into it.

You'll want to know: shǒuzhǐ.
shǒuzhǐ — "finger".
wǒ gǎnjué shǒuzhǐ hěn lěng — My fingers feel really cold.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Two third tones meet in this word: shǒu is said with a rising tone before zhǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
shǒuzhǐ is built from 手 'hand' and zhǐ 'finger', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuwX and tsyijX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second with this very word.
