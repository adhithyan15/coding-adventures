# dēbēs — what you should do

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C109-CAN109-03, LA-LEX-C198-ACT198-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You own **dēbeō**, "I have to, I must; I owe", and **quiēscō**,
"to rest". Say "I have to work". (*Labōrāre dēbeō.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Turn the same verb on a friend, and it becomes advice.

## Grammar Lens: dēbēs + infinitive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C284-DEBERE-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C68-AEGER-01, LA-LEX-C75-VERBA1-01] -->

> **Aeger es; quiēscere dēbēs.** — You are ill; you should rest.
> **Nōn dēbēs tantum labōrāre.** — You shouldn't work so much.

[PAUSE 2s] *Dēbēs* is the second person, "you must", and Latin uses it for
what English softens into "you should". The infinitive carries the advice,
and *nōn* in front turns it round.

[PAUSE 1s] **oportet**, "it is right, one ought", says the same thing
without pointing at anyone, and it takes the accusative and infinitive you
met with *putō*:

> **Oportet tē quiēscere.** — You ought to rest.

## Grammar Lens: dēbēbās, you should have
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C284-DEBEBAS-02]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-IMPERFECTUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C283-ACC-INF-02] -->

English moves the past onto the infinitive: "you should **have come**".
Latin moves it onto *dēbeō* instead, and keeps the plain infinitive:

> **Herī venīre dēbēbās.** — You should have come yesterday.

[PAUSE 2s] *Dēbēbās* is the imperfect, "you were bound to", and the
sentence carries the regret: the coming was owed, and it did not happen.

## The word, taken apart: dēbeō
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C109-CAN109-03] -->

**dēbeō** is contracted from *dē*, "away from", and *habeō*, "to have"
(Lewis & Short), as its own lesson said: to have something that belongs to
someone else. A debt, *dēbitum*, is what you hold and must give back; advice
with *dēbēs* treats the right action as something owed.
