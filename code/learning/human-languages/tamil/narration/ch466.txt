Tamil, chapter 466: Courage and Cowardice.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
துணிச்சல் (tuṇiccal) — courage; daring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for infatuation, then the Tamil for to be moved.

You'll want to know: துணிச்சல்.
துணிச்சல் — tuṇiccal — "courage; daring".
இரவில் தனியாகப் போக அவளுக்குத் துணிச்சல் இருந்தது. iravil taṉiyākap pōka avaḷukkut tuṇiccal iruntatu. She had the courage to go alone at night.
From துணி "to dare", not துணி "cloth".

The word, taken apart.
துணிச்சல் (tuṇiccal) is native Dravidian, a noun from துணி tuṇi, "to dare" (DEDR 3306), which lists it.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
அஞ்சு (añcu) — to dread; to be afraid.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for courage, then the Tamil for a coward.

You'll want to know: அஞ்சு.
அஞ்சு — añcu — "to dread; to be afraid".
அவன் யாருக்கும் அஞ்சவில்லை. avaṉ yārukkum añcavillai. He was afraid of no one.
Past (I …): அஞ்சினேன், añciṉēṉ.
More literary than பயப்படு, the everyday verb. What is feared takes -க்கு.

The word, taken apart.
அஞ்சு (añcu) is native Dravidian (DEDR 55); the same entry gives அச்சம் accam, "fear".

Lesson 4 of 5.
பதுங்கு (patuṅku) — to cower; to crouch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a coward, then the Tamil for to dread.

You'll want to know: பதுங்கு.
பதுங்கு — patuṅku — "to cower; to crouch".
பூனை சத்தம் கேட்டுக் கட்டிலுக்குக் கீழே பதுங்கியது. pūṉai cattam kēṭṭuk kaṭṭilukkuk kīḻē patuṅkiyatu. Hearing the noise, the cat crouched under the bed.
Past (I …): பதுங்கினேன், patuṅkiṉēṉ.
To hide by crouching low, as an animal does.

The word, taken apart.
பதுங்கு (patuṅku) is native Dravidian (DEDR 3912); its strong partner பதுக்கு patukku means to hide something away.
