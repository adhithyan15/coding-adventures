## Added — Japanese B1 vocabulary, 1,360 words in chapters 262–533

- Added 272 chapters of five words each (1,360 headwords, 439 of them
  verbs), one word per lesson, with 52 reviews across five tranches:
  narrating (262–316: news and accidents, the police, disasters, life events,
  festivals, folk tales, letters and memory), reasons and then conditions
  (317–371: opinions, rules and manners, society and saving, then plans,
  appointments, risks and money), travel (372–425: stations, roads, hotels,
  sightseeing, regions and problems on the road), experience (426–479:
  feelings, character, the body and health, work, sport, the arts and family
  life), and experience then conditions (480–533: school, nature, the
  household and shopping, then chance, cause and wishes).
- Examples use only the words and grammar taught before them, in the core's
  polite frame: a plain clause only before から / ので, とき, こと and ほう; no
  plain -ている; でしょう only after the dictionary form; no na-adjective with
  attributive な or adverbial に; no untaught sense of a taught word (もう is
  only "again", よる is not "night", ひ is not "day").
- Verb classes follow the lesson's sense where one kana spelling has two
  (きる "wear" and かえる "change" are ichidan, かえる "return" is godan).
- The first words of chapters 262–269 practise the core's last grammar
  points again, and 月 年 出 人 中 今 each come back twice in chapters 270–296.
- Every word cites JMdict or KANJIDIC2: a native word is marked as
  inherited, and a Sino-Japanese word gives each kanji's reading with its
  Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription. No etymology is invented. Root slugs reuse the corpus spelling of
  each etymon.
