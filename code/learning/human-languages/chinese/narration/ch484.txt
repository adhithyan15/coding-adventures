Mandarin Chinese, chapter 484: Staying Well.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yùfáng — to prevent; to guard against.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for looking after one's health, then the Mandarin for chronic.

You'll want to know: yùfáng.
yùfáng — "to prevent; to guard against".
chángcháng xǐ shǒu kěyǐ yùfáng gǎnmào — Washing your hands often helps prevent colds.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yùfáng is built from yù 'beforehand' and fáng 'to ward off', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yoH and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'in advance' and the second 'to guard against'.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dǐkànglì — resistance to illness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get into a habit, then the Mandarin for worn out.

You'll want to know: dǐkànglì.
dǐkànglì — "resistance to illness".
chángcháng duànliàn de rén dǐkànglì bǐ biérén hǎo — People who exercise regularly fight off illness better than others.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǐkànglì is built from dǐ 'to push against', kàng 'to resist' and 力 'strength', all inherited from Middle Chinese, tejX, khangH and lik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to push' and lists the second under two readings, glossing khangH as lifting with the hand.
