Mandarin Chinese, chapter 59: Clothes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
chuān — to wear; to put on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for cup, then the Mandarin for clothes.

You'll want to know: chuān.
chuān — "to wear; to put on".
míngtiān nǐ chuān shénme — What are you wearing tomorrow?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chuān is inherited from Middle Chinese tsyhwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to go through' and 'a hole'. Wearing is not given there; putting arms and legs through clothes is the usual explanation, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday answers.

Lesson 3 of 5.
màozi — hat; cap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to wear, then the Mandarin for shoe.

You'll want to know: màozi.
màozi — "hat; cap".
wǒ de tóu hěn dà màozi tài xiǎo le — My head's big; the hat is too small.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
màozi is built from mào and the noun ending zi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, mawH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first as a covering for the head.

Lesson 4 of 5.
kùzi — trousers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for shoe, then the Mandarin for hat.

You'll want to know: kùzi.
kùzi — "trousers".
zhè shì wǒ gēge de kùzi — These are my brother's trousers.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kùzi is built from kù and the noun ending zi, both inherited from Middle Chinese, khuH and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under a variant form of the character and explains it, through an older book, as a garment for the shins.

Lesson 5 of 5.
piàoliang — pretty; good-looking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hat, then the Mandarin for trousers.

You'll want to know: piàoliang.
piàoliang — "pretty; good-looking".
tā de yǎnjing hěn piàoliang — She has beautiful eyes.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
piàoliang is built from piào and liang, both inherited from Middle Chinese, phjiewH and ljangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as beating silk floss in water and the second as 'bright'; how the pair came to mean pretty is not settled.
