# पर — the word that follows the place instead of leading it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C36-GHAR-01, HI-LEX-C69-GOING-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can name **घर** and **स्टेशन**. Try to say *at home* — and notice
that you have the place and no way to stand in it.

## You'll want to know: पर
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C87-POST-01]; assesses=[] -->

**पर** (*par*) — "at," pinning you to one point.

**घर पर** — at home. **स्टेशन पर** — at the station.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C36-GHAR-01, HI-LEX-C69-GOING-03] -->

English sets *at* in front of the place. Hindi sets पर behind it:

| English | Hindi |
|---|---|
| at home | घर पर |
| at the station | स्टेशन पर |

That ordering is why these words are called **post**positions. Every one of
them trails its noun, so once you have the noun you know exactly where the
small word goes.

What पर wants is a **point** rather than an interior — a house you are at, a
platform you are standing on. A place you are *inside* reaches for a different
small word, which arrives later.

## Grammar Lens: where the pieces land
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C87-POST-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-HAI-01] -->

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| मैं घर पर हूँ। | I am at home. |
| वह स्टेशन पर है। | He is at the station. |

Person, then place, then the small word, then the verb. The verb goes last and
stays last, which is the shape every Hindi sentence you have met so far has had.
