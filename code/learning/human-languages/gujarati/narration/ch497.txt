Gujarati, chapter 497: Wedding Plans.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કંકોતરી (kankotrī) — a wedding invitation card.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to welcome with rites, then the Gujarati for a baby's nappy.

You'll want to know: કંકોતરી.
કંકોતરી — kankotrī — "a wedding invitation card".
અમે બધા સંબંધીઓને કંકોતરી મોકલી. ame badhā sambandhīone kankotrī moklī. We sent wedding invitations to all our relatives.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
કંકોતરી (kankotrī) goes back to a reconstructed kuṅkumapattrikā-, "a saffron letter", from kuṅkuma, "saffron", and pattrikā, "letter"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, an invitation dyed red (CDIAL 3215).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a wedding invitation card, then the Gujarati for the turmeric rite.

You'll want to know: જાન.
જાન — jān — "the groom's wedding party".
સાંજે જાન ગામમાં આવી. sā̃je jān gāmmā̃ āvī. In the evening the groom's wedding party arrived in the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
જાન (jān) is inherited from Sanskrit janya, "belonging to a people" and "a friend of the bridegroom"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "bridegroom's procession" (CDIAL 5116).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the turmeric rite, then the Gujarati for the groom's wedding party.

You'll want to know: વરઘોડો.
વરઘોડો — varghoḍo — "the groom's procession".
વરઘોડામાં બધા નાચ્યા. varghoḍāmā̃ badhā nācyā. Everyone danced in the groom's procession.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
વરઘોડો (varghoḍo) is built from var, "bridegroom", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit vara, "suitor, bridegroom" (CDIAL 11309), and ઘોડો (ghoḍo), "a horse" (CDIAL 4516): the groom rides to the wedding.

You'll want to know: પરણાવવું.

તેઓ આવતા વરસે દીકરીને પરણાવશે. teo āvtā varse dīkrīne parṇāvśe. They will marry off their daughter next year.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે પોતાના સંતાનને પરણાવ્યું, teṇe potānā santānne parṇāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
પરણાવવું (parṇāvvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit pariṇāpayati, "causes a man to marry"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to give in marriage" (CDIAL 7821).
