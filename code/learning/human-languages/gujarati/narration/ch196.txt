Gujarati, chapter 196: Up to the top storey.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
મકાન (makān) — building, house.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for whereabouts, then the Gujarati for distance.

You'll want to know: મકાન.
મકાન — makān — "building, house".
આ મકાન જૂનું છે. — ā makān jūnũ che — "This building is old."

The word, taken apart.
મકાન (makān) is a loan from Arabic makān, "place", which reached Gujarati through Persian. Its Arabic root is k-w-n, "to be" (Lane).

Lesson 2 of 5.
માળ (māḷ) — storey, floor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for distance, then the Gujarati for building.

You'll want to know: માળ.
માળ — māḷ — "storey, floor".
અમારું ઘર ત્રીજા માળે છે. — amārũ ghar trījā māḷe che — "Our home is on the third floor."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
માળ (māḷ) is listed by CDIAL, glossed "loft, upper storey", under māḍa, "upper storey of a house", a reconstructed form that it takes from Dravidian, as in Tamil māṭam, "storeyed house" (CDIAL 9996).

Lesson 4 of 5.
પેસવું (pesvũ) — to go in, to enter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for storey, then the Gujarati for elevator.

You'll want to know: પેસવું.
પેસવું — pesvũ — "to go in, to enter".
તે મકાનમાં પેઠો. — te makānmā̃ peṭho — "He went into the building."
Past તે પેઠો, te peṭho.

The word, taken apart.
પેસવું (pesvũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from práviśati, "enters", and CDIAL glosses the Gujarati verb "enters" (CDIAL 8803).

Lesson 5 of 5.
પ્રવેશ (praveś) — entry, way in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for elevator, then the Gujarati for to go in.

You'll want to know: પ્રવેશ.
પ્રવેશ — praveś — "entry, way in".
પ્રવેશ પાછળથી છે. — praveś pāchaḷthī che — "The way in is at the back."
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
પ્રવેશ (praveś) is Sanskrit praveśa, "entering, entrance", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama. It shares its root with práviśati, "enters" (CDIAL 8803).
