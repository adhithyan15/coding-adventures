Malayalam, chapter 348: It Depends, and What Would Have Been.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
അത് കാലാവസ്ഥയെ ആശ്രയിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു (atŭ kālāvasthaye āśrayiccirikkunnu) — it depends on the weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the weather" and "according to". (kālāvastha, anusariccŭ.) Then "if it rains, I will not come". (maḻa peytāl ñān varilla.)

Grammar Lens: ആശ്രയിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു.
What something depends on takes the object ending -എ, as in അവനെ, and the verb is ആശ്രയിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു: "rests on, depends on".
നിങ്ങൾ നാളെ വരുമോ? — അത് കാലാവസ്ഥയെ ആശ്രയിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു. niṅṅaḷ nāḷe varumō? — atŭ kālāvasthaye āśrayiccirikkunnu. — "Will you come tomorrow? — It depends on the weather."
സമയം gives സമയത്തെ, and പണം gives പണത്തെ: "it depends on time", "on money". The verb is ആശ്രയിക്കുക, "to rely on", built on Sanskrit āśraya, "that on which anything rests" (Monier-Williams), and its form here is the -ഇച്ച് form, as in അനുസരിച്ച്, with ഇരിക്കുന്നു after it: it stays in a state of resting on.

Grammar Lens: then both outcomes.
After "it depends", give both sides:
മഴ പെയ്തില്ലെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ വരും. മഴ പെയ്താൽ വരില്ല. maḻa peytilleṅkil ñān varuṁ. maḻa peytāl varilla. — "If it does not rain, I will come. If it rains, I will not."
Everyday speech often reaches for the അനുസരിച്ച് you know: സമയം അനുസരിച്ച്, "depending on the time".

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: both outcomes: if it does not rain, if it rains]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "depending on the time" — samayaṁ anusariccŭ]

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 2 of 6.
ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) — a celebration.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "Onam", "a festival", and "a wedding". (ōṇaṁ, utsavaṁ, kalyāṇaṁ.)

You'll want to know: ആഘോഷം.
ആഘോഷം — āghōṣaṁ — "a celebration, festivities".
ഓണം കേരളത്തിലെ ഏറ്റവും വലിയ ആഘോഷമാണ്. ōṇaṁ kēraḷattile ēṟṟavuṁ valiya āghōṣamāṇŭ. — "Onam is the biggest celebration in Kerala."
കല്യാണത്തിന് വലിയ ആഘോഷം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നു. kalyāṇattinŭ valiya āghōṣaṁ uṇṭāyirunnu. — "There was a big celebration at the wedding."
ഉത്സവം is the festival itself, often at a temple; ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) is the celebrating, at a festival, a wedding or a birthday. The verb is ആഘോഷിക്കുക, "to celebrate": ഞങ്ങൾ ഓണം ആഘോഷിച്ചു.
Its first consonant is new. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) is a Sanskrit word, āghoṣa, which Monier-Williams glosses as "a calling out, a proclaiming". In Malayalam it has come to mean the noise and gathering of a celebration; how the sense moved is not recorded there.

What does ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) mean? (a celebration.) How is it different from ഉത്സവം? (ഉത്സവം is the festival; ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) is the celebrating.)

ഘ — gha: a g with a puff of breath after it. It is to ഗ what ഖ is to ക.
In ആഘോഷം (āghōṣaṁ) it wears the ō sign, ഘോ. Most words with ഘ (gha) come from Sanskrit.

Writing: ഘ.
Put your pen on ഘ (gha) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see, slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 4 of 6.
ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ (uṇṭāyirunneṅkil) — if there had been: the unreal condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I used to go". (ñān pōkumāyirunnu.) Then "there was". (uṇṭāyirunnu.) Then "if there is". (uṇṭeṅkil.)

Grammar Lens: -ഇരുന്നെങ്കിൽ, if only it had been so.
For something that is not so, or was not, put ഇരുന്നെങ്കിൽ after the past form: വന്നു becomes വന്നിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ, "if (he) had come"; അറിഞ്ഞു becomes അറിഞ്ഞിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ, "if (I) had known"; ഉണ്ടായി becomes ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ, "if there had been", "if (I) had".

Grammar Lens: -ഉമായിരുന്നു, would.
The other half is the -ഉമായിരുന്നു from the start of this core, now with its second job: "would", "would have".
പണം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ പോകുമായിരുന്നു. paṇaṁ uṇṭāyirunneṅkil ñān pōkumāyirunnu. — "If I had money, I would go."
The same sentence also says "if I had had money, I would have gone"; the context decides which.
നീ നേരത്തെ വന്നിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ നമുക്ക് ബസ് കിട്ടുമായിരുന്നു. nī nēratte vannirunneṅkil namukkŭ basŭ kiṭṭumāyirunnu. — "If you had come earlier, we would have caught the bus."
അറിഞ്ഞിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ വിളിക്കുമായിരുന്നു. aṟiññirunneṅkil ñān viḷikkumāyirunnu. — "If I had known, I would have called."
With -അണം it is regret: ഞാൻ പോകണമായിരുന്നു (ñān pōkaṇamāyirunnu), "I should have gone".

[your turn — say: "If I had known, I would have called."]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If he had come"? (vannirunneṅkil.) "Would have called"? (viḷikkumāyirunnu.) How does pōkumāyirunnu tell "used to go" from "would have gone"? (By the context: an if-clause in front makes it "would".)


Lesson 5 of 6.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip in the past, with a feeling, a reason and an if.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last A2 writing task asked for a connected message about a familiar day. This one asks for six lines, and they tell a story.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
A told story is not a list of events. It has a scene, in ആയിരുന്നു and the -ഉകയായിരുന്നു past. It has events in the plain past, kept in order with ആദ്യം, എന്നിട്ട്, -അപ്പോൾ, അതിനുശേഷം and ഒടുവിൽ. It has at least one reason, with -അതുകൊണ്ട് or കാരണം, and a feeling, with എനിക്ക് … തോന്നി.
[pause 2 seconds]
A good one ends by looking at what might have been: … -ഇരുന്നെങ്കിൽ … -ഉമായിരുന്നു.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, with ഈയിടെ or കഴിഞ്ഞ, and what it was like, with ആയിരുന്നു;
where you went and what you were doing on the way, in the -ഉകയായിരുന്നു past;
the first thing that happened, with ആദ്യം;
what happened next, with എന്നിട്ട് or -അപ്പോൾ, and one thing that went wrong;
how it ended and how you felt, with ഒടുവിൽ and എനിക്ക് … തോന്നി, and a reason;
one line about what might have been, with -ഇരുന്നെങ്കിൽ … -ഉമായിരുന്നു.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model on this page. Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing the right past for each line, and joining them.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines as you read the village story earlier in this core. Mark every verb s for scene or e for event. Line two should be mostly s; lines three and four mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then three spelling checks. A word that starts with long ī starts with ഈ, not ഇ. The au of സൗകര്യം is the tail ൗ after its consonant. ദുഃഖം keeps its two rings, ഃ.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. Does the if-clause end in -ഇരുന്നെങ്കിൽ, and the main verb in -ഉമായിരുന്നു?
