# ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು — "I know Kannada"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-STACKING-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter 5 gave you **ನಾನು ಕನ್ನಡ** — "**I** speak Kannada" — with the
verb **ಮಾತನಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ**. Now say "I **know** Kannada." Kannada will not let you
use *nānu*.

## You'll want to know: The sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು.**
> *nanage kannaḍa gottu.*
> literally: "**to-me** Kannada [is] **known**."

| piece | |
|---|---|
| **ನನಗೆ** *nanage* | "to me" — the dative from the last lesson |
| **ಕನ್ನಡ** *kannaḍa* | Kannada (Ch. 5) |
| **ಗೊತ್ತು** *gottu* | "known" |

**You are not the subject**: *nānu* has moved into the dative, and the sentence
is about *kannaḍa*. And *gottu* is not an action — it is "**[is] known**." Nothing
here is *doing* anything.

## Grammar Lens: Why: things that happen *to* you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-02]; assesses=[] -->

Kannada separates what you **do** from what **happens to** you. Speaking is an
action, so Ch. 5 used *nānu*. Knowing, liking, wanting **arrive** — so the person
goes in the **dative**.

| English frames it as | Kannada frames it as |
|---|---|
| I **know** Kannada | Kannada is known **to me** |
| I **like** it | it is pleasing **to me** |
| I **am** cold | cold is **to me** |

English keeps one fossil of it: "**methinks**" — dative *me*, "it seems **to
me**." Kannada made it a rule.

## Grammar Lens: All four sisters do it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C06-DATIVE-SUBJECT-03]; assesses=[] -->

| language | "I know [the language]" | the dative |
|---|---|---|
| Kannada | *nanage kannaḍa gottu* | **-ge** |
| Tamil | *enakku tamiḻ teriyum* | **-ukku** |
| Telugu | *nāku telugu vaccu* | **-ku** |
| Malayalam | *enikku malayāḷam aṟiyām* | **-ikku** |

One construction, four visibly related suffixes — Kannada's *g* the softened *k*
of the others: the Dravidian family showing its bones.

## Guided Practice — the ending underneath
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C06-DATIVE-GE-01, KA-ETYMON-C06-DATIVE-GE-02] -->

- Say "to the name", then "for work". (***hesarige***, ***kelasakke*** — one
  suffix, two shapes.)
- Why *g* where Tamil has *k*? (Kannada **voiced** the *k* between vowels;
  doubled ***-kke*** resisted and kept it.)
