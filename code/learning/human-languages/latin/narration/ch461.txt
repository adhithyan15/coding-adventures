Lesson 1 of 5.
thermopōlium, thermopōliī — a hot-drinks bar, a tavern selling warm drinks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to go back and forth, then the Latin for near the city.

You'll want to know: thermopōlium, thermopōliī.
thermopōlium, thermopōliī — "a hot-drinks bar, a tavern selling warm drinks".
Fabrī in thermopōliō vīnum calidum bibunt. The workmen drink warm wine in the hot-drinks bar.
Second declension, neuter: thermopōlium, thermopōliī n. A word of comedy; such bars, with jars set in the counter, line the streets of excavated towns.

The word, taken apart.
thermopōlium comes from Greek, built from thermos 'hot' and pōleō 'to sell' (Lewis & Short give the sense, a place where warm drinks were sold). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
calda, caldae — hot water (for mixing with wine).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for near the city, then the Latin for a hot-drinks bar.

You'll want to know: calda, caldae.
calda, caldae — "hot water (for mixing with wine)".
Hospes caldam et vīnum rogat. The guest asks for hot water and wine.
First declension, feminine: calda, caldae f., short for (aqua) calida. Romans liked wine mixed with hot water in winter.

The word, taken apart.
calda is a shortened form of (aqua) calida 'hot water', from calidus 'hot' (Lewis & Short), which is built on caleō 'to be warm'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
pōtō, pōtāre — to drink, to drink deep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a hot-drinks bar, then the Latin for hot water.

You'll want to know: pōtō, pōtāre.
pōtō, pōtāre — "to drink, to drink deep".
Pōtōrēs usque ad mediam noctem pōtant. The drinkers drink on until midnight.
pōtō, pōtāre, pōtāvī, pōtātum or pōtum. Stronger than bibō: drinking hard, drinking for pleasure. English potion and potable come from the same root.

The word, taken apart.
pōtō is inherited from PIE peh₃- 'to drink', with Greek pinō 'to drink' as a cousin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English potion and potable come from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fūmōsus, fūmōsa, fūmōsum — smoky, full of smoke.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for hot water, then the Latin for to drink.

You'll want to know: fūmōsus, fūmōsa, fūmōsum.
fūmōsus, fūmōsa, fūmōsum — "smoky, full of smoke".
Popīna fūmōsa est, et oculī hospitum dolent. The cookshop is smoky, and the customers' eyes hurt.
An adjective of three endings, from fūmus 'smoke' with -ōsus 'full of'. Poets mock the smoky tavern.

The word, taken apart.
fūmōsus is built on fūmus 'smoke' with the suffix -ōsus 'full of' (Lewis & Short). English fume comes from fūmus.

An adjective of three endings, from in- 'not' and mundus 'clean, neat'.
