Italian, chapter 418: Il biglietto.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la biglietteria — the ticket office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the long-distance bus, then the Italian for to pass through.

You'll want to know: la biglietteria.
la biglietteria — "the ticket office".
La biglietteria apre alle sei. The ticket office opens at six.
La biglietteria automatica is the ticket machine.

The word, taken apart.
biglietteria is built on biglietto 'ticket', which was borrowed from French billet 'note' (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
timbrare — to stamp, to validate (a ticket).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the ticket office, then the Italian for the season ticket.

You'll want to know: timbrare.
timbrare — "to stamp, to validate (a ticket)".
Ricordati di timbrare il biglietto prima di salire. Remember to stamp your ticket before you get on.
At work, timbrare il cartellino is to clock in.

The word, taken apart.
timbrare is built on timbro 'stamp', which was borrowed from French timbre, a word that goes back through Latin tympanum to Greek týmpanon 'drum'.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: il controllore.
il controllore — "the ticket inspector".
Il controllore passa tra i sedili. The inspector comes along the seats.
Il controllore di volo is an air traffic controller.

The word, taken apart.
controllore is built on controllo 'check', which was borrowed from French contrôle, from contre-rôle: a second list kept to check the first.

Lesson 5 of 5.
il supplemento — the supplement, the extra charge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to stamp, then the Italian for the ticket inspector.

You'll want to know: il supplemento.
il supplemento — "the supplement, the extra charge".
Per il treno veloce c'è un supplemento. For the fast train there is a supplement.
Also a newspaper supplement.

The word, taken apart.
supplemento is a learned borrowing from Latin supplēmentum 'a filling up' (EtymDB), from supplēre 'to fill up', built on plēnus 'full'.
