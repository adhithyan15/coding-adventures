# 上 — and a rule that does not apply

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-SCRIPT-ZAO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write 早. Sun first, then the two strokes below.

## Script — the shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-SCRIPT-SHANG]; assesses=[] -->

> 上

**Three strokes.** A vertical, a short horizontal to its right, and a long
horizontal underneath.

Now guess the order, and expect to be wrong:

1. the **central vertical**, from the top down
2. the **short horizontal**, left to right, starting at the vertical
3. the **long base horizontal**, left to right

Two pen lifts.

**The vertical comes first.** Every character so far has trained you toward *top
before bottom, left before right*, and here the top stroke is not the one you
start with. This is worth meeting on a three-stroke character, because it tells
you something true about the system: the habits are **strong tendencies with
named exceptions**, not laws. 上 is one of the exceptions, and it is common
enough that you will see it constantly.

The other thing to notice: **short horizontal before long horizontal.** The base
bar is the widest stroke and it goes last, exactly as the closing bar did in 再.
When two horizontals differ in length, the long one is usually the finisher.
