German, chapter 468: Wut und Zorn.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Wut — rage, fury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for embarrassment, then the German for to blush.

You'll want to know: die Wut.
die Wut — "rage, fury".
Vor Wut warf er sein Handy auf das Sofa. In a rage, he threw his phone onto the sofa.
No plural. Eine Wut haben auf + accusative — to be furious with.

The word, taken apart.
Wut is inherited from Middle High German wuot and Old High German wuot "mad, madness", from Proto-Germanic wōdaz (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ärgern — to annoy; to be annoyed (sich ärgern).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for rage, then the German for anger.

You'll want to know: ärgern.
ärgern — "to annoy; to be annoyed (sich ärgern)".
Ich ärgere mich über den Lärm der Nachbarn. I'm annoyed about the neighbours' noise.
Sich ärgern über + accusative — to be annoyed about. Without sich: Du ärgerst mich! — You're annoying me!

The word, taken apart.
ärgern is inherited from Old High German argerōn "to make worse" (EtymDB), built on arg "bad", from Proto-Germanic argaz.

Lesson 4 of 5.
aufregen — to get worked up (sich aufregen); to upset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for anger, then the German for to annoy.

You'll want to know: aufregen.
aufregen — "to get worked up (sich aufregen); to upset".
Mein Opa regt sich über jede Kleinigkeit auf. My grandpa gets worked up about every little thing.
Separable and mostly reflexive: sich über etwas aufregen. Das regt mich auf! — That makes me mad!

The word, taken apart.
aufregen is built on auf "up" and regen "to stir, to move", which goes back to Middle High German regen "to excite, to move" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
toben — to rage; to romp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to annoy, then the German for to get worked up.

You'll want to know: toben.
toben — "to rage; to romp".
Die Kinder toben im Garten. The children are romping in the garden.
Of people in anger, of storms, and of children playing wildly.

The word, taken apart.
The verb toben goes back to Old High German tobēn "to rage, to be mad"; its older origin is not settled, and EtymDB has no entry for it.
