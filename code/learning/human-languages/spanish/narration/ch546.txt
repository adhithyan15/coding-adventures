Spanish, chapter 546: El bautizo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el bautizo — the christening.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for adolescence, then the Spanish for the generation.

You'll want to know: el bautizo.
el bautizo — "the christening".
El bautizo fue en la iglesia del pueblo. The christening was in the village church.
Bautizar is to christen. El bautismo is the sacrament itself.

The word, taken apart.
bautizo is built on bautizar, which comes from Church Latin baptīzāre 'to baptize' (Lewis & Short), taken from Greek baptízein 'to dip in water'.

At a wedding, el padrino walks with the bride or the groom. Los padrinos — the godparents.

padrino is inherited from spoken Latin patrīnus 'a godfather' (EtymDB), built on pater 'father'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el nieto — the grandson, the grandchild.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the godfather, then the Spanish for the godmother.

You'll want to know: el nieto.
el nieto — "the grandson, the grandchild".
Mis abuelos tienen seis nietos. My grandparents have six grandchildren.
La nieta is the granddaughter; los nietos can mean all the grandchildren.

The word, taken apart.
nieto is usually traced to spoken Latin neptus, a masculine made from Latin neptis 'a granddaughter' (Lewis & Short), which belongs with nepōs 'grandson'.
