Spanish, chapter 405: Photography, Photos and Permission.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la fotografía — writing with light.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say to make or do and the room. (Hacer, la sala.)

Sounds you'll need.
fo-to-gra-FEE-a: the written accent keeps í and a in separate syllables.

The word, taken apart.
la fotografía means both “photography” and “a photograph.” Its international parts come from Greek phôs, phōtós, “light,” and graphía, “writing or description.” A photograph is, quite literally, writing with light.
Spanish commonly says hacer fotografías, “to take photographs.” The verb is the familiar hacer: Spanish pictures the activity as making photographs.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Sources: DLE: fotografía and DLE: foto-.


Lesson 2 of 5.
la foto — the short word keeps the long word's gender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the photograph and the passport. (La fotografía, el pasaporte.)

The word, taken apart.
la foto is the everyday short form of la fotografía. It ends in -o, but it remains feminine because the full word is feminine: la foto, never el foto for an ordinary photograph.
The two forms overlap, but the short one is especially natural for a physical photo or an ID picture: una foto y el pasaporte.

Lesson 3 of 5.
permitir — let something pass through.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say to take photographs in the room. (Hacer fotografías en la sala.)

Sounds you'll need.
per-mi-TEER: keep the two r sounds light and stress the final syllable.

The word, taken apart.
permitir means “to permit” or “to allow.” It continues Latin permittere, “to let through,” built from per-, “through,” and mittere, “to send or let go.” English permit belongs to the same family.
On a public sign, está permitido means “it is permitted,” while no está permitido means “it is not permitted.” Treat that whole sign formula as one useful chunk here.
