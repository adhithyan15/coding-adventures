Telugu, chapter 328: First, Then, At Last.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
తొలుత (toluta) — at first.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a kingdom, then the Telugu for to reign over.

You'll want to know: తొలుత.
తొలుత — toluta — "at first".
తొలుత నాకు భయం వేసింది. toluta nāku bhayaṁ vēsindi. At first I felt afraid.
A storytelling word, a little formal; in speech మొదట says the same.

The word, taken apart.
తొలుత (toluta) is native Dravidian, built on toli, "beginning; first, former", which DEDR 3516 lists with Tamil tol, "old, ancient".

Lesson 2 of 5.
అనంతరం (anantaraṁ) — afterwards; after that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to reign over, then the Telugu for at first.

You'll want to know: అనంతరం.
అనంతరం — anantaraṁ — "afterwards; after that".
భోజనం అనంతరం అందరూ ఇంటికి వెళ్ళారు. bhōjanaṁ anantaraṁ andarū iṇṭiki veḷḷāru. After the meal everyone went home.
It follows a noun as తరువాత does, but sounds more formal: news readers use it.

The word, taken apart.
అనంతరం (anantaraṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit anantaram, "immediately after", from an-antara, "having no interval" (CDIAL 287).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ఎట్టకేలకు (eṭṭakēlaku) — at long last.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for at first, then the Telugu for afterwards.

You'll want to know: ఎట్టకేలకు.
ఎట్టకేలకు — eṭṭakēlaku — "at long last".
ఎట్టకేలకు బస్సు వచ్చింది. eṭṭakēlaku bassu vaccindi. At long last the bus came.
It tells you the wait was long; చివరికి only says what came last.

The word, taken apart.
ఎట్టకేలకు (eṭṭakēlaku) is a Telugu adverb of time; its origin is not settled, and DEDR has no entry for it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
మరుసటి రోజు (marusaṭi rōju) — the next day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for afterwards, then the Telugu for at long last.

You'll want to know: మరుసటి రోజు.
మరుసటి రోజు — marusaṭi rōju — "the next day".
మరుసటి రోజు అతను మళ్ళీ వచ్చాడు. marusaṭi rōju atanu maḷḷī vaccāḍu. The next day he came again.
మరుసటి works with other time words too: మరుసటి వారం, the next week.

The word, taken apart.
మరుసటి రోజు (marusaṭi rōju) is a Telugu phrase: marusaṭi, "the following", is native Dravidian (DEDR 4766, beside Tamil maṟu, "other, next"), and rōju is the everyday word for a day.

Lesson 5 of 5.
గుర్తుచేసుకో (gurtucēsukō) — to recall; to remember.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for at long last, then the Telugu for the next day.

You'll want to know: గుర్తుచేసుకో.
గుర్తుచేసుకో — gurtucēsukō — "to recall; to remember".
ఆమె తన బాల్యం గుర్తుచేసుకుంది. āme tana bālyaṁ gurtucēsukundi. She recalled her childhood.
Past (I …): గుర్తుచేసుకున్నాను, gurtucēsukunnānu.
Built like గుర్తుపెట్టుకో: గుర్తు, a mark in the mind, with చేసుకో, to make for oneself.

The word, taken apart.
గుర్తుచేసుకో (gurtucēsukō) is a native Telugu compound: gurtu, "a sign, a mark in the memory" (DEDR 1847), and cēsukō, "to do for oneself", the -కో form of cēyu.
