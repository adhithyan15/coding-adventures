Lesson 1 of 5.
vectīgal, vectīgālis — a tax, a toll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for truth, then the Latin for a lie.

You'll want to know: vectīgal, vectīgālis.
vectīgal, vectīgālis — "a tax, a toll".

The word, taken apart.
vectīgal is built on vectus, "carried", the participle of vehō, "to carry" (Lewis & Short): first a toll on goods carried in, then any tax.

Lesson 2 of 5.
tribūtum, tribūtī — a tribute, a tax.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lie, then the Latin for a tax.

You'll want to know: tribūtum, tribūtī.
tribūtum, tribūtī — "a tribute, a tax".

The word, taken apart.
tribūtum is the past participle of tribuō, "to assign, to pay out" (Lewis & Short), used as a noun: what is paid. tribuō is built on tribus, "a tribe": first to share out among the tribes.

Lesson 3 of 5.
mercēs, mercēdis — wages, pay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tax, then the Latin for a tribute.

You'll want to know: mercēs, mercēdis.
mercēs, mercēdis — "wages, pay".
It is not the same word as merx, mercis, "goods", even though the plural of merx is spelled mercēs too. The second form tells them apart: mercēdis here, mercis there.

The word, taken apart.
mercēs is usually linked with mereō, "to earn" (Lewis & Short): what one has earned. It may share a root with merx, "goods" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
dēnārius, dēnāriī — a denarius (silver coin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tribute, then the Latin for wages.

You'll want to know: dēnārius, dēnāriī.
dēnārius, dēnāriī — "a denarius (silver coin)".

The word, taken apart.
dēnārius is built on dēnī, "ten each" (Lewis & Short): the coin was first worth ten bronze coins. dēnī comes from decem, "ten".

Lesson 5 of 5.
sēstertius, sēstertiī — a sesterce (coin).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wages, then the Latin for a denarius.

You'll want to know: sēstertius, sēstertiī.
sēstertius, sēstertiī — "a sesterce (coin)".

The word, taken apart.
sēstertius is contracted from sēmis "half" and tertius "third" (Lewis & Short): two whole units and half of the third, the coin's first value.
