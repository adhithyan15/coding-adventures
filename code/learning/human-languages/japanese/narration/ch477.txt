Japanese, chapter 477: Parents and Children.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おや (oya) — a parent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the eldest son, then the Japanese for the eldest daughter.

You'll want to know: おや.
おや — oya — "a parent".
おやは こどもを しんぱいします。 — oya wa kodomo o shinpai shimasu — "Parents worry about their children."

The word, taken apart.
おや (oya) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for the eldest daughter, then the Japanese for a parent.

おやこ — oyako — "parent and child".
こうえんで おやこが あそんでいます。 — kōen de oyako ga asonde imasu — "A parent and child are playing in the park."

The word, taken apart.
おやこ (oyako) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: oya, "parent" and ko, "child" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
めんどう (mendō) — a bother; looking after someone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for parent and child, then the Japanese for the youngest child.

You'll want to know: めんどう.
めんどう — mendō — "a bother; looking after someone".
おとうとの めんどうを みました。 — otōto no mendō o mimashita — "I looked after my younger brother."

The word, taken apart.
めんどう (mendō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are men, "face" (Middle Chinese mjienH) and dō, "collapse" (tawX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
