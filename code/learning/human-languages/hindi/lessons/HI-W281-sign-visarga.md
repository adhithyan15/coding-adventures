# ◌ः — two dots and a breath

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C281-ATAH-01, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-148] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Say **अतः**, "therefore". Then find the dot of **◌ं** in **मैं**: one dot, above the head-line.

[PAUSE 2s] Today's sign has two dots, and they stand beside the line, not above it.

## Script you'll notice: ◌ः
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-C281-VISARGA-01]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-148] -->

**◌ः** — the **visarga**, *ḥ*: a light breath of *h* after the vowel before it.

Where it sits: **to the right of its syllable**, two dots one above the other. In print they hang under a short piece of head-line.

What it is made of:

- an upper dot
- a lower dot
- a short piece of headline above them

Worked through: **त** + **◌ः** = **तः**, the end of **अतः**.

It is rare in everyday Hindi. You will meet it in formal words taken straight from Sanskrit, such as **अतः**.

## Writing: ◌ः — follow the numbered strip
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-C281-VISARGA-01] -->

- **1.** start at the top of the upper dot and curve down its left side
- **2.** without lifting, round the bottom and up the right side to close the loop
- **3.** lift, then start at the top of the lower dot and curve down its left side
- **4.** without lifting, round the bottom and up the right side to close the loop — and only now lift

**Pen lifts: 1.** The upper dot first, then the lower.

The short piece of head-line stays grey in the strip: in a word it is part of the head-line, which you draw last, once, across the whole word.

> Two strokes — four movements, one pen lift; the upper dot, then the lower, each a small loop drawn anticlockwise from its top, as ं is; the short piece of headline the printed sign carries is left undrawn, because in a word it is part of the one headline drawn last across the whole word; cited to native writers' pen traces in HP Labs India's LipiTk Devanagari recognizer, class 60.

> This is one attested order, and handwriting varies by writer. Source: HP Labs India, Lipi Toolkit, Lipi Indic Character Recognizers 4.0, Devanagari recognizer, class 60 (ः, visarga): stored online-handwriting prototypes from native writers (MIT licence, 2012).
