French, chapter 475: Jalousie et rancune.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la jalousie — jealousy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for funny, then the French for to tickle.

You'll want to know: la jalousie.
la jalousie — "jealousy".
La jalousie a gâché leur amitié. Jealousy spoilt their friendship.
Built on jaloux. Also a slatted blind on a window.

The word, taken apart.
la jalousie is built on jaloux (EtymDB), which goes back to Late Latin zēlōsus "full of zeal", from zēlus, a borrowing from Greek zêlos "zeal, rivalry".

Lesson 2 of 5.
envier — to envy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to tickle, then the French for jealousy.

You'll want to know: envier.
envier — "to envy".
J'envie mon frère: il voyage beaucoup. I envy my brother: he travels a lot.
Envier quelque chose à quelqu'un — to envy someone something.

The word, taken apart.
envier is built on envie (EtymDB), inherited from Latin invidia "envy", from invidēre "to look askance at", built on vidēre "to see" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la rancune — grudge, resentment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for jealousy, then the French for to envy.

You'll want to know: la rancune.
la rancune — "grudge, resentment".
Je ne garde pas de rancune contre lui. I do not bear him a grudge.
Sans rancune! — no hard feelings!

The word, taken apart.
la rancune comes from Old French rancure, which goes back to Latin rancor "a rancid smell, a grudge" (Lewis & Short); its ending was later reshaped.

Ma voisine est rancunière: elle n'oublie rien. My neighbour is resentful: she forgets nothing.

Lesson 5 of 5.
réconcilier — to reconcile; (se réconcilier) to make up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for grudge, then the French for resentful.

You'll want to know: réconcilier.
réconcilier — "to reconcile; (se réconcilier) to make up".
Après des mois de silence, ils se sont réconciliés. After months of silence, they made up.
Mostly reflexive: se réconcilier avec quelqu'un.

The word, taken apart.
réconcilier is a learned borrowing from Latin reconciliāre "to bring together again", built on concilium "a gathering" (Lewis & Short).
