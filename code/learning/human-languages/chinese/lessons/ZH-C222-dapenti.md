## You'll want to know: dǎ pēntì
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C222-CHILL222-04]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎ pēntì* — "to sneeze".

> *gǎnmào de shíhou wǒ chángcháng dǎ pēntì* — When I've got a cold I sneeze a lot.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*dǎ pēntì* is built from 打 'to strike, to do' and *pēntì* 'a sneeze', all inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX*, *phwonH* and *tejH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to breathe out' and the third 'breath from the nose'. Neither dǎ nor the level tone of pēn is the regular outcome of the form listed.
