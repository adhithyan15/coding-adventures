Tamil, chapter 98: Come, Eat, Drink.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) — congratulations, good wishes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for that's how it is, then the Tamil for why not.

You'll want to know: வாழ்த்துகள்.
வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) — "congratulations", "good wishes".
It is built on வாழ், "to live": to wish someone well is to wish them life.

What you've built.
Wishes for someone's good news.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: vāḻttukaḷ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: vāḻttukaḷ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say vāḻttukaḷ!]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Tamil for that's how it is, then the Tamil for why not, then say vāḻttukaḷ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does வாழ்த்துகள் (vāḻttukaḷ) mean? ("Congratulations, good wishes".) Say it once more.

Lesson 4 of 5.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for an invitation, then the Tamil for a gift.

You'll want to know: வாருங்கள்.
வாருங்கள் (vāruṅkaḷ) — "please come", "come in".
It is the verb வா, "come", with the respectful -ங்கள். At the door, it is the welcome.

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
சாப்பிடுங்கள் (cāppiṭuṅkaḷ) — please eat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a gift, then the Tamil for please come in.

You'll want to know: சாப்பிடுங்கள்.
சாப்பிடுங்கள் (cāppiṭuṅkaḷ) — "please eat".
A host says it, and says it again. சாப்பிடு is Tamil's everyday verb for eating.

What you've built.
Congratulations, an invitation, a gift, come in, please eat.
