Malayalam, chapter 237: Daytime, Someday, Day before, Following day, Day before yesterday.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പകൽ (pakal) — daytime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for dusk, then the Malayalam for a moment.

You'll want to know: പകൽ.
പകൽ — pakal — "daytime".

The word, taken apart.
പകൽ (pakal) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil pakal, Kannada pagal and Telugu pagalu (DEDR 3805).

Lesson 2 of 5.
എന്നെങ്കിലും (enneṅkiluṁ) — someday, ever.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a moment, then the Malayalam for daytime.

You'll want to know: എന്നെങ്കിലും.
എന്നെങ്കിലും — enneṅkiluṁ — "someday, ever".

The word, taken apart.
എന്നെങ്കിലും (enneṅkiluṁ) is native Dravidian: ennu, "what day", from the question stem (DEDR 5151), then a conditional form of the verb "to say" meaning "if" (DEDR 868), and -uṁ: "on whatever day it may be".

Lesson 3 of 5.
തലേന്ന് (talēnnŭ) — the day before (an event).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for daytime, then the Malayalam for someday.

You'll want to know: തലേന്ന്.
തലേന്ന് — talēnnŭ — "the day before (an event)".

The word, taken apart.
തലേന്ന് (talēnnŭ) is native Dravidian, built on tala, "head, the front end" (DEDR 3103), with a word for "day": the day at the head of an event.

Lesson 4 of 5.
പിറ്റേന്ന് (piṟṟēnnŭ) — the following day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for someday, then the Malayalam for the day before.

You'll want to know: പിറ്റേന്ന്.
പിറ്റേന്ന് — piṟṟēnnŭ — "the following day".

The word, taken apart.
പിറ്റേന്ന് (piṟṟēnnŭ) is native Dravidian, "the day after": its first part belongs with pin, "after" (DEDR 4205, with Tamil piṉ), and its end is a word for "day".
