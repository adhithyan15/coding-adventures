Portuguese, chapter 120: Ankle, Wrist, Cheek, Lips, Chest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o tornozelo — an ankle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a dolphin, then the Portuguese for an elbow.

You'll want to know: o tornozelo.
o tornozelo — "an ankle".

The word, taken apart.
tornozelo is built on the word for a turn, with a small-making ending: the joint the foot turns on. EtymDB 2.1 links it to the verb "to turn", which from Latin tornare, "to turn on a lathe".

Lesson 2 of 5.
o pulso — a wrist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for an elbow, then the Portuguese for an ankle.

You'll want to know: o pulso.
o pulso — "a wrist".

The word, taken apart.
pulso from Latin pulsus, "a beat" (EtymDB 2.1), from pellere, "to drive" — a learned borrowing from Latin. The wrist is where the pulse beats.
