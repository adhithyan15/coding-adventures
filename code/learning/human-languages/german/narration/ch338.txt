German, chapter 338: Vermutungen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Vermutung — the guess, the supposition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to have doubts, then the German for to doubt.

You'll want to know: die Vermutung.
die Vermutung — "the guess, the supposition".
Meine Vermutung war richtig: Er hatte den Schlüssel im Auto vergessen. My guess was right: he had left the key in the car.
Plural: die Vermutungen. The noun of vermuten.

The word, taken apart.
Vermutung is the noun of vermuten "to suppose", which is usually traced to muten, from Mut in its old sense "mind, feeling". EtymDB has no entry for the verb, so the account is hedged.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Annahme — the assumption; the acceptance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to doubt, then the German for the guess.

You'll want to know: die Annahme.
die Annahme — "the assumption; the acceptance".
Wir sind von der falschen Annahme ausgegangen, dass das Museum montags offen ist. We started from the wrong assumption that the museum is open on Mondays.
Plural: die Annahmen. The noun of annehmen: die Annahme eines Pakets — accepting a parcel.

The word, taken apart.
Annahme is the noun of annehmen, built on nehmen "to take", inherited from Old High German neman and Proto-Germanic nemaną (EtymDB): something you take as given.

Lesson 3 of 5.
anscheinend — apparently, it seems.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the guess, then the German for the assumption.

You'll want to know: anscheinend.
anscheinend — "apparently, it seems".
Anscheinend ist niemand zu Hause: Alle Fenster sind dunkel. Apparently nobody is at home: all the windows are dark.
It says how things look, and they probably are so. Scheinbar says they only look so.

The word, taken apart.
anscheinend is the present participle of anscheinen "to appear to", built on scheinen "to shine, to seem", inherited from Old High German skīnan and Proto-Germanic skīnaną (EtymDB), a cousin of English shine.

Lesson 4 of 5.
offensichtlich — obviously, evident.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the assumption, then the German for apparently.

You'll want to know: offensichtlich.
offensichtlich — "obviously, evident".
Offensichtlich hat er den Brief nicht gelesen. Obviously he has not read the letter.
An adjective and an adverb: ein offensichtlicher Fehler — an obvious mistake.

The word, taken apart.
offensichtlich joins offen "open" to Sicht "sight", from the family of sehen "to see", Old High German sehan (EtymDB), with -lich: open to sight.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ahnen — to suspect, to have a feeling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for apparently, then the German for obviously.

You'll want to know: ahnen.
ahnen — "to suspect, to have a feeling".
Ich habe es schon geahnt: Der Zug hat Verspätung. I already had a feeling: the train is late.
Keine Ahnung — no idea — belongs to the same family.

The word, taken apart.
ahnen is inherited from Middle High German anen (EtymDB). It is usually explained as a verb made from an "on": a feeling comes upon you.
