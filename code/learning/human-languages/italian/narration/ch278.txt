Italian, chapter 278: Dal principio alla fine.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il principio — the beginning; the principle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the moment, then the Italian for the phase.

You'll want to know: il principio.
il principio — "the beginning; the principle".
Raccontami tutto dal principio. Tell me everything from the beginning.
In principio — in the beginning. The plural i principi means principles.

The word, taken apart.
principio is a learned borrowing from Latin prīncipium "a beginning, a foundation" (EtymDB), built on prīnceps "first".

Lesson 2 of 5.
terminare — to finish, to end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the phase, then the Italian for the beginning.

You'll want to know: terminare.
terminare — "to finish, to end".
La lezione termina alle sei. The lesson ends at six.
A formal finire; with or without an object: ho terminato il lavoro, lo spettacolo è terminato.

The word, taken apart.
terminare is a learned borrowing from Latin termināre "to mark the bounds; to end" (EtymDB), built on terminus "boundary, limit".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: concludere.
concludere — "to conclude, to wind up".
Per concludere, vi ringrazio tutti. To conclude, I thank you all.
Past participle concluso: abbiamo concluso l'accordo.

The word, taken apart.
concludere is a learned borrowing from Latin conclūdere "to shut in; to close, to end" (EtymDB), from con- and claudere "to shut".

Lesson 5 of 5.
riprendere — to resume, to start again; to take back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: riprendere.
riprendere — "to resume, to start again; to take back".
Dopo la pausa, abbiamo ripreso a lavorare. After the break, we started working again.
Past participle ripreso. Riprendere a + infinitive: to start doing again.

The word, taken apart.
riprendere is inherited from Latin reprehendere "to hold back, to seize again" (EtymDB), built on prehendere "to take hold of".
