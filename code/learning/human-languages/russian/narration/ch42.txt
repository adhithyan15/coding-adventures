Russian, chapter 42: Open, Close, Drink, Question, Answer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
открывать (otkryvát) — to open.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to wait, then the Russian for to give.

You'll want to know: открывать.
открывать (otkryvát) — "to open".

The word, taken apart.
открывать (otkryvát) is от-, "off, away", with -крывать, from крыть, "to cover": to open is to take the cover off. Vasmer relates крыть to Lithuanian kráuti, "to pile up", and Greek krýptō, "I hide", the source of English crypt.

Lesson 3 of 5.
пить (pit) — to drink.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to open, then the Russian for to close.

You'll want to know: пить.
пить (pit) — "to drink". What you do with вода and чай.

The word, taken apart.
пить (pit) from Proto-Slavic piti — inherited from PIE peh₃(i)-, "to drink". Vasmer lists Sanskrit píbati, "drinks", Greek pínō, "I drink", and Latin bibō, "I drink", from older pibō.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ответ (otvét) — an answer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to drink, then the Russian for a question.

You'll want to know: ответ.
ответ (otvét) — "an answer". The noun behind отвечать.

The word, taken apart.
ответ (otvét) is от-, "back", with вет, an old word: Old Russian větŭ meant "counsel, agreement" (Vasmer). An answer is a word given back. The same root is in привет, "hi", and совет, "advice". Vasmer compares Old Prussian waitiāt, "to speak".
