Lesson 1 of 6.
descolar — to take off (of a plane); to come unstuck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the detector, then the Portuguese for to stamp.

You'll want to know: descolar.
descolar — "to take off (of a plane); to come unstuck".
O avião descolou com meia hora de atraso. The plane took off half an hour late.
In Brazil: decolar. A sticker that comes off also descola.

The word, taken apart.
descolar is built from des- 'un-' and colar 'to stick', on cola 'glue' (EtymDB): the plane comes unstuck from the ground.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: aterrar.
aterrar — "to land (of a plane)".
Aterrámos às onze da noite. We landed at eleven at night.
In Brazil: aterrissar. The noun is a aterragem.

The word, taken apart.
aterrar 'to land' is built from a-, terra 'ground' and -ar; terra is inherited from Latin terra 'earth' (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: a turbulência.
a turbulência — "turbulence".
Durante a turbulência, as pessoas ficaram nervosas. During the turbulence, people got nervous.
The adjective is turbulento.

The word, taken apart.
turbulência is a learned borrowing from Latin turbulentia (EtymDB), from turbulentus 'stormy', built on turba 'uproar, crowd'.

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to land, then the Portuguese for turbulence.

You'll want to know: a hospedeira.
a hospedeira — "the flight attendant".
A hospedeira trouxe água e bolachas. The flight attendant brought water and biscuits.
Hospedeira de bordo in full; airlines now say assistente de bordo. In Brazil: a aeromoça.

The word, taken apart.
hospedeira is built on hóspede 'guest', which goes back to Latin hospes 'host, guest' (Lewis & Short): the woman who looks after the guests.

Lesson 5 of 6.
a tripulação — the crew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for turbulence, then the Portuguese for the flight attendant.

You'll want to know: a tripulação.
a tripulação — "the crew".
A tripulação deseja-nos uma boa viagem. The crew wishes us a good trip.
The verb tripular means to crew a ship or plane.

The word, taken apart.
tripulação is built on tripular 'to crew'. The origin of tripular is not settled; one view links it with Latin interpolāre 'to refurbish', and EtymDB has no entry for it.
