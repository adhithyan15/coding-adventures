Kannada, chapter 85: Slowly, Once More.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ (nidhānavāgi) — slowly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for hospital, then the Kannada for a tablet.

You'll want to know: ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ.
ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ (nidhānavāgi) — "slowly". The ending ‑ಆಗಿ (-āgi) turns a word into a manner, "in a slow way", like ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ (cennāgi), "well".
ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಮಾತಾಡಿ (nidhānavāgi mātāḍi) — "please speak slowly".

The word, taken apart.
ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ (nidhānavāgi) is nidhāna, "steadiness, slowness", with the adverb ending -āgi, "in a … way". Nidhāna is Sanskrit, where it meant "a place for depositing, a store" (CDIAL 7205); in Kannada it has come to mean steadiness and slowness.

What you've built.
The most useful request a learner can make.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ಮತ್ತೊಮ್ಮೆ (mattomme) — once more.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a tablet, then the Kannada for slowly.

You'll want to know: ಮತ್ತೊಮ್ಮೆ.
ಮತ್ತೊಮ್ಮೆ (mattomme) — "once more, one more time".
ಮತ್ತೊಮ್ಮೆ ಹೇಳಿ (mattomme hēḷi) — "please say it once more", with ಹೇಳಿ, "please tell", which you already know.

The word, taken apart.
ಮತ್ತೊಮ್ಮೆ (mattomme) is native Dravidian: matte, "again" (DEDR 4766), and omme, "once", which DEDR 990 lists on the root for "one".

What you've built.
The request that comes after slowly.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ಜೋರಾಗಿ (jōrāgi) — loudly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for slowly, then the Kannada for once more.

You'll want to know: ಜೋರಾಗಿ.
ಜೋರಾಗಿ (jōrāgi) — "loudly". It has the same ‑ಆಗಿ ending as ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ (nidhānavāgi).
ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಜೋರಾಗಿ (svalpa jōrāgi) — "a little louder", with ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ, "a little".

The word, taken apart.
ಜೋರಾಗಿ (jōrāgi) is jōr, "force, loudness", with the adverb ending -āgi, "in a … way". Jōr is a loan from Persian zōr, "strength, force", which came through Hindustani.

What you've built.
A second way of saying how.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಅರ್ಥ (artha) — meaning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for once more, then the Kannada for loudly.

You'll want to know: ಅರ್ಥ.
ಅರ್ಥ (artha) — "meaning". You have seen it inside ಅರ್ಥಮಾಡಿಕೊ, "to understand", which is "to make the meaning one's own".
ಇದರ ಅರ್ಥ ಏನು? (idara artha ēnu?) — "what is the meaning of this?"

The word, taken apart.
ಅರ್ಥ (artha) is Sanskrit artha, "aim, cause, thing", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama (CDIAL 638). In Kannada the "aim" of a word is its meaning.

What you've built.
The piece hidden inside the verb to understand.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ಬರೆದು ಕೊಡಿ (baredu koḍi) — please write it down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for loudly, then the Kannada for meaning.

You'll want to know: ಬರೆದು ಕೊಡಿ.
ಬರೆದು ಕೊಡಿ (baredu koḍi) — "please write it down (for me)", literally "having written, give". Both verbs are ones you know: ಬರೆ, "write", and ಕೊಡು, "give".
When a word will not stay in the ear, ask for it on paper.

The word, taken apart.
ಬರೆದು ಕೊಡಿ (baredu koḍi) is native Dravidian: baredu, "having written", from bare, "to write" (DEDR 5263), and koḍi, the polite form of koḍu, "to give" (DEDR 2053). You ask someone to "write and give". DEDR 5263 compares, with a query, Prakrit rajju.

What you've built.
Two known verbs make one request.
