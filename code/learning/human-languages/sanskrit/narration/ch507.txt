Sanskrit, chapter 507: Metal and Gold.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
भस्त्रा (bhastrā) — a bellows.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dyes, then the Sanskrit for a tailor.

You'll want to know: भस्त्रा.
भस्त्रा — bhastrā — "a bellows".
लोहकारः भस्त्रां चालयति। lohakāraḥ bhastrāṃ cālayati. The blacksmith works the bellows.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
भस्त्रा (bhastrā) is the word MW records for a leather bottle or bag, and so a bellows; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
विलापयति (vilāpayati) — melts down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a bellows, then the Sanskrit for tongs.

You'll want to know: विलापयति.
विलापयति — vilāpayati — "melts down".
स्वर्णकारः अग्नौ सुवर्णं विलापयति। svarṇakāraḥ agnau suvarṇaṃ vilāpayati. The goldsmith melts the gold in the fire.
Past (he …): सः सुवर्णं विलापितवान्, saḥ suvarṇaṃ vilāpitavān.

The word, taken apart.
विलापयति (vilāpayati) is built on vi, "apart", and the causative of the root √lī, "to cling, to dissolve"; MW gives vilī the sense "to melt", and its causative vilāpayati makes a thing melt (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
धमति (dhamati) — blows on a fire.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tongs, then the Sanskrit for melts down.

You'll want to know: धमति.
धमति — dhamati — "blows on a fire".
लोहकारः भस्त्रया अग्निं धमति। lohakāraḥ bhastrayā agniṃ dhamati. The blacksmith blows on the fire with the bellows.
Past (he …): सः शंखं ध्मातवान्, saḥ śaṅkhaṃ dhmātavān.

The word, taken apart.
धमति (dhamati) comes from the root √dham (or dhmā), "to blow", as one blows a fire, a conch or a bellows; its past participle is dhmāta (MW).
