Lesson 1 of 6.
méimao — eyebrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for short-sighted, then the Mandarin for to wink.

You'll want to know: méimao.
méimao — "eyebrow".
tā de méimao bǐ wǒ de hēi — His eyebrows are darker than mine.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
méimao is built from méi 'the brow' and máo 'hair', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mij and maw (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older book, as the hair above the eye; the second is said light.

Lesson 2 of 6.
脸色 (liǎnsè) — complexion; the colour of one's face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to wink, then the Mandarin for an eyebrow.

You'll want to know: 脸色.
脸色 — liǎnsè — "complexion; the colour of one's face".
你脸色不好是不是病了 nǐ liǎnsè bù hǎo shì bu shì bìng le — You don't look well. Are you ill?
The new character, in pieces: 脸 (liǎn) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left 月 yuè, four strokes; on the right seven, as on the right of 检 jiǎn: a long stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right, a short bar, two dots, a short stroke falling to the left and a long bar. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
脸色 (liǎnsè) is built from 脸 (liǎn) 'face' and 色 'colour', both inherited from Middle Chinese, leamX and srik (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists the first under two readings, tshjem and leamX, and glosses leamX as a kind of stew; neither gloss is the face, so how it came to mean face is not settled. It glosses the second 'colour of the face'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Script — 月, then the right piece of 检.
脸 (liǎn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 月, four strokes, narrow. The stroke down the left that curves to the left at the foot, the stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks, then two short bars inside.
The right piece, seven strokes, as on the right of 检. A long stroke falling to the left and a long one falling to the right, like a roof. A short bar under them. Two short dots and a short stroke falling to the left. Then a long bar across the foot.
4 + 7 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 脸 (liǎn), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 脸 (liǎn) have? (Two: four strokes and seven.) Where have you written the right piece before? (On the right of 检.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
变脸 (biànliǎn) — to turn hostile suddenly; to change one's attitude abruptly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an eyebrow, then the Mandarin for the colour of one's face.

You'll want to know: 变脸.
变脸 — biànliǎn — "to turn hostile suddenly; to change one's attitude abruptly".
我没说什么他怎么突然变脸了 wǒ méi shuō shénme tā zěnme tūrán biànliǎn le — I didn't say anything, so why has he suddenly turned nasty?

The word, taken apart.
变脸 (biànliǎn) is built from 变 'to change' and 脸 (liǎn) 'face', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjenH and leamX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to change'. The sense of a sudden change of attitude is not given there.

Lesson 5 of 6.
biǎoqíng — facial expression; the look on one's face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the colour of one's face, then the Mandarin for to turn hostile suddenly.

You'll want to know: biǎoqíng.
biǎoqíng — "facial expression; the look on one's face".
kàn tā de biǎoqíng wǒ gǎnjué tā bú tài gāoxìng — From the look on his face, I get the feeling he's not very happy.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biǎoqíng is built from biǎo 'the outside' and 情 'feeling', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjewX and dzjeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'to make clear' and, after an older book, as an outer garment: feeling shown on the outside.
