Japanese, chapter 180: Cafes, Canteens and Rice Balls.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
きっさてん (kissaten) — cafe; coffee shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to spill, then the Japanese for to warm up.

You'll want to know: きっさてん.
きっさてん — kissaten — "cafe; coffee shop".
ともだちと きっさてんで コーヒーを のみます。 — tomodachi to kissaten de kōhī o nomimasu — "I drink coffee at a cafe with a friend."

The word, taken apart.
きっさてん (kissaten) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kis, "consume, drink" (Middle Chinese khek), sa, "tea" (drae) and ten, "shop" (temH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: みそしる.
みそしる — misoshiru — "miso soup".
あさごはんに みそしるを のみます。 — asagohan ni misoshiru o nomimasu — "I have miso soup for breakfast."

The word, taken apart.
みそしる (misoshiru) is built from miso, "soybean paste", and native shiru, "soup, juice", inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
にぎる (nigiru) — to grip; to grasp, to shape (rice).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for cafeteria, then the Japanese for miso soup.

You'll want to know: にぎる.
にぎる — nigiru — "to grip; to grasp, to shape (rice)".
こどもが ははの てを にぎります。 — kodomo ga haha no te o nigirimasu — "The child holds his mother's hand."
Polite にぎります, nigirimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
にぎる (nigiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
おにぎり (onigiri) — rice ball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for miso soup, then the Japanese for to grip.

You'll want to know: おにぎり.
おにぎり — onigiri — "rice ball".
ははは まいあさ おにぎりを にぎります。 — haha wa maiasa onigiri o nigirimasu — "My mother makes rice balls every morning."

The word, taken apart.
おにぎり (onigiri) is built from polite o- and native nigiri, "a grip, something shaped in the hand", which comes from nigiru, "to grip" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
