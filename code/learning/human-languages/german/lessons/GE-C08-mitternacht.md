## You'll want to know: die Mitternacht
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-MITTERNACHT-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MITTE-01, GE-LEX-NACHT-02] -->

> **die Mitternacht** — "midnight," literally "**mid-night**."

*Mitter-* is *Mitte* with the linking form it takes inside a compound, and
*-nacht* is the night. English **midnight** is the same two words again.

To say it: *Es ist Mitternacht.* Like *Mittag*, no number and no *Uhr*.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-NACHT-CH-01, GE-ETYMON-ACHT-EIGHT-02] -->

- `ch-ach` — the raspy back-of-the-throat sound from *Nacht* itself, unchanged
  inside the longer word.

And that *-cht* is the correspondence you were given with the numbers: German
*-cht-* answers English *-ght-* every time.

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *acht* | eight |
| *Nacht* | night |
| *Licht* | light |

You met the first pair counting to ten. Here it is again on a word you now need,
which is what a sound correspondence is for — you learn it once and it keeps
paying.
