## You'll want to know: 应该
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C282-YINGGAI-01]; assesses=[] -->

**应该** — *yīnggāi* — "should; ought to".

> **你应该去看医生** *nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng* — You should see a doctor.

The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. The second is the 该 you wrote last.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**应该** (*yīnggāi*) is built from 应 and 该, both inherited from Middle Chinese. The Guangyun lists 应 under two readings: *'ing*, glossed with a word that can mean 'ought to', and *'ingH*, glossed 'things answering each other'; yīng is the first. 该 is *koj*, glossed 'complete' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C282-GAI-01, ZH-LEX-C80-JOB80-04] -->

该 says the moment has come; 应该 gives advice, or says what is right.

Both sit before the verb: **你应该去看医生** *nǐ yīnggāi qù kàn yīshēng*. 看医生 is to see a doctor.
