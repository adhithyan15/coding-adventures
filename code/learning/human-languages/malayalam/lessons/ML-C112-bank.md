# ബാങ്ക് (bāṅkŭ) — a bank

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C91-MONEY-02, ML-LEX-C110-PHONE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *money*. Say *a telephone*. One of those two you learned as an
English word wearing Malayalam letters. Hold on to that feeling — this chapter
hands you two more of them.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C112-BANK-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C91-MONEY-02] -->

**ബാങ്ക്** (*bāṅkŭ*) — **a bank**.

You have been able to say **പണം** for a while. Now you can say where it lives.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C112-BANK-01, ML-LEX-C110-PHONE-01] -->

Read it aloud and you can hear that nothing was done to it. English *bank* came
into Malayalam whole, and the letters spell out the English sounds one for one.

That is the second time you have seen this. The word for a telephone arrived the
same way, and for the same reason: the thing itself arrived from outside, and
the name came along in the box with it. A language that meets a new object often
takes the object's name rather than building a fresh one.

Which means you can read **ബാങ്ക്** before anybody teaches it to you — and that
is a real skill, not a trick. There are a great many words like these two.
