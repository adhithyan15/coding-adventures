Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 252: Bones and Breath.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हाड (hāḍ) — a bone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for an elbow, then the Marwadi for the waist.

You'll want to know: हाड.
हाड — hāḍ — "a bone".
म्हारो हाड कोनी टूट्यो। mhāro hāḍ konī ṭūṭyo. My bone did not break.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
हाड (hāḍ) is inherited from Sanskrit haḍḍa, "bone"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari hāḍī under it (CDIAL 13952).

Lesson 3 of 5.
हांफणो (hā̃phṇo) — to pant; to be out of breath.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a bone, then the Marwadi for breath.

You'll want to know: हांफणो.
हांफणो — hā̃phṇo — "to pant; to be out of breath".
डूंगर माथै दादा हांफै है। ḍū̃gar māthai dādā hā̃phai hai. On the hill, grandfather is out of breath.
Past (he …): वो छोरो हांफ्यो, vo chhoro hā̃phyo.

The word, taken apart.
हांफणो (hā̃phṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed hamph, "pant". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi hā̃phnā, "to pant" (CDIAL 13973).

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for breath, then the Marwadi for to pant.

You'll want to know: चाबणो.
चाबणो — chābṇo — "to chew".
रोटी चाबो। roṭī chābo. Chew your flatbread.
Past (I …): म्हैं चणा चाब्या, mhaĩ chaṇā chābyā.

The word, taken apart.
चाबणो (chābṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit carvati, "chews"; CDIAL lists Marwari cābṇo under it (CDIAL 4711).
