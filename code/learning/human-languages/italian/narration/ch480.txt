Italian, chapter 480: Perdonare.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
offendersi — to take offence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for enthusiasm, then the Italian for to be moved.

You'll want to know: offendersi.
offendersi — "to take offence".
Si è offeso perché non l'abbiamo invitato. He took offence because we did not invite him.
The past participle is offeso. To offend someone: offendere qualcuno.

The word, taken apart.
offendersi is the reflexive of offendere, a learned borrowing from Latin offendere "to strike against, to displease" (Lewis & Short).

perdono comes from perdonare "to forgive", usually traced to Late Latin perdōnāre, built on Latin dōnāre "to give" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
sfogarsi — to let off steam, to pour one's heart out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for forgiveness, then the Italian for to regret.

You'll want to know: sfogarsi.
sfogarsi — "to let off steam, to pour one's heart out".
Quando sono arrabbiato mi sfogo con la corsa. When I am angry I let off steam by running.
Lo sfogo is the outburst; sfogarsi con qualcuno — to open up to someone.

The word, taken apart.
sfogarsi is built from s- and foga "rush, heat", usually traced to Latin fuga "flight, running away" (Lewis & Short).
