French, chapter 481: Une mauvaise entorse.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'entorse — sprain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to limp, then the French for bone.

You'll want to know: l'entorse.
l'entorse — "sprain".
Il s'est fait une entorse à la cheville au tennis. He sprained his ankle playing tennis.
Feminine. Une entorse au règlement — a breach of the rules.

The word, taken apart.
l'entorse comes from the old verb entordre "to twist", built from en- and tordre, which is inherited from Latin torquēre "to twist" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
tordre — to twist, to wring; (se tordre) to twist (a joint).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for bone, then the French for sprain.

You'll want to know: tordre.
tordre — "to twist, to wring; (se tordre) to twist (a joint)".
Je me suis tordu le pied dans l'escalier. I twisted my foot on the stairs.
For a joint it is reflexive: se tordre la cheville. Present je tords, il tord; past j'ai tordu. Se tordre de rire — to double up laughing.

The word, taken apart.
tordre is inherited from Vulgar Latin torcere, a form of Latin torquēre "to twist" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: enfler.
enfler — "to swell".
Sa cheville a enflé pendant la nuit. Her ankle swelled during the night.
Enflé — swollen. The everyday word is often gonfler.

The word, taken apart.
enfler is inherited from Latin īnflāre "to blow into, to puff up" (EtymDB), built on flāre "to blow" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
le kiné — physiotherapist (informal).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to swell, then the French for splint.

You'll want to know: le kiné.
le kiné — "physiotherapist (informal)".
Je vais chez le kiné deux fois par semaine. I go to the physio twice a week.
Short for le kinésithérapeute. A woman is la kiné. In Belgium also le kiné; in Québec le physiothérapeute.

The word, taken apart.
le kiné is short for kinésithérapeute, a learned word built from Greek kínēsis "movement" and therapeutḗs "one who treats".
