# älter als — older than

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ALT-01, GE-LEX-C89-ADJ1-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "old" and "fast". (*Alt. Schnell.*)

[PAUSE 1s] And remember **als**, from chapter two hundred and fifty-two:
"when", for one time in the past. It has a second job.

## Grammar Lens: -er, then als
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-KOMPARATIV-ALS-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C89-ADJ1-01, GE-LEX-BRUDER-01, GE-LEX-C96-STADT1-05, GE-LEX-C90-ADJ2-02] -->

To say "more", German adds **-er** to the adjective, as English does with
"faster". "Than" is **als**:

> *Der Zug ist schneller als das Auto.* — The train is faster than the car.
> *Mein Bruder ist älter als ich.* — My brother is older than me.

Many short adjectives with *a*, *o* or *u* take an umlaut as well: *alt*,
**älter**; *groß*, **größer**, bigger. Others do not: *klein*, *kleiner*;
*billig*, *billiger*.

A few are irregular, as in English: *gut*, **besser**; *viel*, **mehr**;
and *gern*, **lieber**, which you already have.

After *als*, a pronoun keeps its subject form: *älter als ich*, *als du*. In
some regions and in casual speech you may hear *älter wie ich*; in standard
German it is *als*.

## Why it's said this way: so … wie for the same
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SO-WIE-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ALT-01, GE-LEX-C89-ADJ1-03] -->

For "as … as", German says **so … wie**, with the plain adjective in the
middle:

> *Sie ist so alt wie ich.* — She is as old as me.
> *Das Auto ist nicht so schnell wie der Zug.* — The car is not as fast as
> the train.

*Wie* is for the same, and *als* is for more. *So alt wie*, *älter als*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ALS-EINMAL-01] -->

**alt** ← Old High German *alt* ← Proto-Germanic *\*aldaz* (EtymDB 2.1),
the parent of English *old*. EtymDB does not list the comparative, so this
part is the usual account: the old comparative ending had an *i* in it,
and that *i* pulled the vowel forward, *a* to *ä*. English went through the
same change and kept its trace in *elder*, beside the newer *older*.
