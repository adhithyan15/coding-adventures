Arabic, chapter 295: Telling a story.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حكاية (ḥikāya) — a tale, a story told aloud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for at first, then the Arabic for at that moment.

You'll want to know: حكاية.
حكاية — ḥikāya — "a tale, a story told aloud".
سمع الأطفال حكاية قبل النوم. samiʿa al-aṭfāl ḥikāya qabla an-nawm. The children heard a story before bed.
Plural حكايات. A حكاية (ḥikāya) is told; a قصة can be told or read.

The word, taken apart.
حكاية (ḥikāya) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-k-y (Lane), where ḥakā means both "to relate" and "to imitate": a tale repeats what someone said or did.

Lesson 2 of 5.
روى (rawā) — to narrate, to relate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for at that moment, then the Arabic for a tale.

You'll want to know: روى.
روى — rawā — "to narrate, to relate".
روى جدي قصة عن القرية. rawā jaddī qiṣṣa ʿan al-qarya. My grandfather told a story about the village.
Present: يروي, yarwī.
The narrator is الراوي; the taught رواية, a novel, is the same root.

The word, taken apart.
روى (rawā) is built on the native Arabic root r-w-y (Lane), which gives both "to give water to drink" and "to transmit a report or a poem". Riwāya, a novel, and rāwī, a narrator, share it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
قص (qaṣṣa) — to tell a story; to cut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a tale, then the Arabic for to narrate.

You'll want to know: قص.
قص — qaṣṣa — "to tell a story; to cut".
قصت المعلمة على التلاميذ قصة قصيرة. qaṣṣat al-muʿallima ʿalā at-talāmīdh qiṣṣa qaṣīra. The teacher told the pupils a short story.
Present: يقص, yaquṣṣ.
It takes على for the listener: قص (qaṣṣa) على الأطفال. With scissors it means to cut.

The word, taken apart.
قص (qaṣṣa) is built on the native Arabic root q-ṣ-ṣ (Lane), "to follow a track step by step" and "to clip". To tell a story is to follow events in order; qiṣṣa, a story, is its noun.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سرد (sarada) — to recount, to narrate in order.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to narrate, then the Arabic for to tell a story.

You'll want to know: سرد.
سرد — sarada — "to recount, to narrate in order".
سرد المسافر تفاصيل رحلته. sarada al-musāfir tafāṣīl riḥlatihi. The traveller recounted the details of his journey.
Present: يسرد, yasrud.
The noun سرد (sarada) is narration, as in a novel.

The word, taken apart.
سرد (sarada) is built on the native Arabic root s-r-d (Lane), first "to stitch" and "to link rings of mail one into the next", then "to carry on a story without a break".

Lesson 5 of 5.
ذات يوم (dhāta yawm) — one day, once upon a time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to tell a story, then the Arabic for to recount.

You'll want to know: ذات يوم.
ذات يوم — dhāta yawm — "one day, once upon a time".
ذات يوم خرج الصياد إلى البحر. dhāta yawm kharaja aṣ-ṣayyād ilā al-baḥr. One day the fisherman went out to sea.
It opens a story, like the taught ذات مرة, once.

The word, taken apart.
ذات يوم (dhāta yawm) puts yawm, "a day", which is usually traced to an inherited Semitic word, after dhāta, the feminine of dhū, "possessor of", as in the taught ذات مرة.
