Russian, chapter 502: Clay and Crafts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
глина (glína) — clay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a sewing pattern, then the Russian for to hem.

You'll want to know: глина.
глина (glína) — "clay".
Из глины делают кувшины и чашки. Iz glíny délayut kuvshíny i cháshki. Jugs and cups are made from clay.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
глина (glína) is inherited from Proto-Slavic glina, which EtymDB traces to PIE gleh₁i-n-.

Lesson 3 of 5.
гончар (gonchár) — a potter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for clay, then the Russian for modelling clay.

You'll want to know: гончар.
гончар (gonchár) — "a potter".
Гончар делает красивые тарелки. Gonchár délayet krasívyye tarélki. The potter makes beautiful plates.
Masculine; genitive гончара.

The word, taken apart.
гончар (gonchár) is inherited from Old East Slavic gornьčarь, derived from gъrnьcь 'pot' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
мастерить (masterít) — to make things by hand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for modelling clay, then the Russian for a potter.

You'll want to know: мастерить.
мастерить (masterít) — "to make things by hand".
Папа любит мастерить в гараже. Pápa lyúbit masterít v garazhé. Dad likes making things in the garage.
Imperfective; perfective смастерить.

The word, taken apart.
мастерить (masterít) is built on мастер 'master, craftsman', which EtymDB traces through Old French maistre to Latin magister 'master'; EtymDB lists the verb without a source.
