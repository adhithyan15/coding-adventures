# Four lines, and the joiner that moves nothing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-ET-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson asked for writing **finished on time**. This one asks
for writing that **holds together**.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-MAIS-03, FR-LEX-PARCEQUE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can be
connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a
sentence stops standing on its own.

[PAUSE 2s] You hold three joiners. **et** adds. **mais** sets against. **parce
que** gives the reason.

[PAUSE 2s] And here is the thing to notice, which is easiest to see by contrast.
In German, the word for *because* drags the verb to the end of its clause: the
join is visible in the word order. **French does nothing of the kind.**

[PAUSE 2s] After **parce que**, the words stand in exactly the order they would
have stood in on their own — subject, verb, the rest. Nothing moves.

[PAUSE 2s] That makes French easier to *write* and harder to *hear*. Nothing in
the shape of the second clause tells you a reason has started; only the two
little words do. Which is why **parce que** has to be written in full, and why
it is two words rather than one.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-PARCEQUE-01, FR-GRAM-SVO-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the person you picked.

At least one must use **et** or **mais**. At least one must use **parce que** —
both words, spelled out.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary; the
whole exercise is the joining.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-GRAM-SVO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Cover your first line and read the second alone. Does it still make
sense without the first? If it does, you wrote a **list** — which is where you
were one lesson ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Then find your **parce que** line and read the words after it. They
should be in ordinary order. If you moved something, you were remembering a
different language's rule.
