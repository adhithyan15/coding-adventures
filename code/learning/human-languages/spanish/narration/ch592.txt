Spanish, chapter 592: La empresa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el empresario — the businessman, the employer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for remote work, then the Spanish for the trade union.

You'll want to know: el empresario.
el empresario — "the businessman, the employer".
Su padre es empresario. His father is a businessman.
A woman is la empresaria. Los empresarios can mean employers as a group.

The word, taken apart.
empresario is built on empresa, which was borrowed from Italian impresa 'undertaking', built on Latin prehendere 'to take hold of' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
la multinacional — the multinational company.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the trade union, then the Spanish for the businessman.

You'll want to know: la multinacional.
la multinacional — "the multinational company".
Trabaja en una multinacional. She works for a multinational.
Also an adjective: una empresa multinacional.

The word, taken apart.
multinacional is built on nación, a learned borrowing from Latin natio 'birth, a people' (EtymDB), from nasci 'to be born', with Latin multi- 'many'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la sucursal — the branch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the industry, then the Spanish for the head office.

You'll want to know: la sucursal.
la sucursal — "the branch".
El banco tiene una sucursal en mi barrio. The bank has a branch in my neighbourhood.
A branch of a bank or a shop.

The word, taken apart.
sucursal was borrowed from French succursale, which goes back to Medieval Latin succursus 'help' (EtymDB links the Spanish word to succursus), from succurrere 'to run to help', built on currere 'to run'.
