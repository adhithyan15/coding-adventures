# por isso, então — the arrow that points forward

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-LEX-PORQUE-01, PT-GRAMMAR-ACHO-QUE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Give an opinion with a reason: "I think it's going to rain,
because there's no sun." (*Acho que vai chover, porque não há sol.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *Porque* runs from the claim back to its reason. This lesson runs
the other way.

## Grammar Lens: the two arrows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-DUAS-SETAS-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C84-PORQUE84-04, PT-LEX-C143-VERBOS143-02] -->

The same two facts, in opposite orders:

> Apanhei o autocarro **porque** chovia. — I took the bus **because** it was
> raining.
> Chovia, **por isso** apanhei o autocarro. — It was raining, **so** I took
> the bus.

**Porque** points **back**, from what happened to its cause. **Por isso**
points **forward**, from the cause to what followed; its *isso*, "that",
reaches back to the cause, which is why the cause has to come first. Which
one you reach for depends only on which fact you want to say first, and that
is a choice about emphasis, not about grammar.

The tenses keep their jobs. *Chovia* is the scene, the reason; *apanhei* is
the event, the consequence.

## Grammar Lens: então and portanto
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-ENTAO-PORTANTO-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C84-PORQUE84-03, PT-LEX-C152-PORQUE152-01] -->

**então** can stand in the same place: *Chovia, **então** apanhei o
autocarro.* It is lighter and more conversational, the "so" of a friend
telling you about their day. **portanto**, which you also have, is the formal
one for a letter or an argument, though in Portugal it is also a common
filler in speech.

> chatty: *então* · neutral: *por isso* · formal: *portanto*

All three point forward. *Então* also keeps its first meaning, "then, at that
time", so in a story it can do both jobs at once.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**então** is inherited from Latin *in tunc*, "in that moment" (EtymDB 2.1;
Lewis & Short, under *tunc*): a word about time that became a word about
consequence, exactly as English "then" did. **isso** is inherited from Latin
*ipsum*, "itself, that very thing" (EtymDB 2.1), so *por isso* is "for that
very thing".

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-DUAS-SETAS-01, PT-GRAMMAR-ENTAO-PORTANTO-02, PT-LEX-PORQUE-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which way does *porque* point, and which way *por isso*? (*Porque*
**back** to the cause; *por isso* **forward** to the consequence.) Which of the
three forward words is the chatty one, and which the formal one? (***Então***
chatty; ***portanto*** formal.) What did Latin *in tunc* mean? (**At that
moment.**)
