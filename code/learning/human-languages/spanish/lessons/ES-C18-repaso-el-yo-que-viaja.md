# Review — you never learned a new stem

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-QUERER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] "I want you to want." (*Quiero que quieras*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-IR-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-QUERER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-PODER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR] -->

Four verbs came at you one after another, and each looked like a fresh thing to
memorise. Put them in a column and the memorising disappears.

| you already said | so you say |
|---|---|
| *vivo* | *viva* |
| *quiero* | *quiera* |
| *puedo* | *pueda* |
| *hago* | *haga* |

[PAUSE 2s] One move, four times: **take the *yo* form, drop the *-o*, put *-a* on
instead.**

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SUBJUNCTIVE-YO-STEM-VOWEL-SWAP, ES-GRAMMAR-QUERER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-HACER-SUBJUNCTIVE-SINGULAR] -->

Look at what rode across with the stem.

*Quiero* carries *ie*, and *quiera* keeps it. *Puedo* carries *ue*, and *pueda*
keeps it. *Hago* carries a *g* that *hacer* has nowhere else, and *haga* keeps
that too.

None of those was a new irregular form. Each was an old one, already paid for,
travelling into a new place on the back of the *yo* you had been saying for
weeks. That is why the four lessons felt repetitive: they were.

The *-a* is worth one more look. *Vivir* ends in *-ir* and *querer*, *poder* and
*hacer* end in *-er*, and all four still land on *-a*, *-as*, *-a*. The two
families that stay apart in the present tense stop being two families here.
