Gujarati, chapter 122: Bear, Jackal, Wolf, Pigeon, Duck.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
રીંછ (rī̃ch) — a bear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a rabbit, then the Gujarati for a deer.

You'll want to know: રીંછ.
રીંછ (rī̃ch) — "a bear".

The word, taken apart.
રીંછ (rī̃ch) (rīṁch) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ṛkṣa, "bear" (CDIAL 2445).

Lesson 5 of 5.
બતક (batak) — a duck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a wolf, then the Gujarati for a pigeon.

You'll want to know: બતક.
બતક (batak) — "a duck".

The word, taken apart.
બતક (batak) is a loan, usually traced to Persian baṭ, "duck". Arabic has baṭṭ, a kind of water-fowl, but Lane, under the root b-ṭ-ṭ, calls it a Persian word taken into Arabic, or else an imitation of the bird's cry.
