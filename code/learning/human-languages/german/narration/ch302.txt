Lesson 1 of 5.
die Rente — the pension.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the promotion, then the German for the turning point.

You'll want to know: die Rente.
die Rente — "the pension".
Mit fünfundsechzig ging mein Vater in Rente. At sixty-five my father retired.
In Rente gehen — to retire. In Austria: die Pension.

The word, taken apart.
Rente was borrowed from French rente "income", which is usually traced to Latin reddere "to give back" (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Ruhestand — the retirement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the turning point, then the German for the pension.

You'll want to know: der Ruhestand.
der Ruhestand — "the retirement".
Im Ruhestand reiste sie viel. In retirement she travelled a lot.
Formal. In den Ruhestand gehen — to retire.

The word, taken apart.
Ruhestand joins Ruhe "rest", from Proto-Germanic rōwō "quiet, rest" (EtymDB), and Stand "standing, state": the state of rest.

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Jubiläum — the anniversary, the jubilee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the retirement, then the German for the pensioner.

You'll want to know: das Jubiläum.
das Jubiläum — "the anniversary, the jubilee".
Die Firma feierte ihr hundertjähriges Jubiläum. The company celebrated its hundredth anniversary.
Plural: die Jubiläen. For a firm, a club or years at work.

The word, taken apart.
Jubiläum is a learned borrowing from Late Latin iūbilaeus "of the jubilee", which came from Hebrew through Greek; EtymDB lists no parent, so this follows the usual account.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Abschied — the farewell, the parting.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the pensioner, then the German for the anniversary.

You'll want to know: der Abschied.
der Abschied — "the farewell, the parting".
Beim Abschied weinten alle. At the farewell everyone cried.
Plural: die Abschiede. Abschied nehmen — to say goodbye.

The word, taken apart.
Abschied is built on abscheiden "to depart", from scheiden "to separate", Proto-Germanic skaiþaną (EtymDB).
