Lesson 1 of 6.
jīnglǐ — manager.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to defeat, then the Mandarin for to cheer someone on.

You'll want to know: jīnglǐ.
jīnglǐ — "manager".
jīnglǐ jīntiān bú zài — The manager isn't in today.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jīnglǐ is built from jīng and lǐ, both inherited from Middle Chinese, keng and liX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'constant' and the second 'to put in order', and quotes an older dictionary for the second, 'to work jade'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
请假 (qǐngjià) — to ask for time off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a manager, then the Mandarin for to be in charge.

You'll want to know: 请假.
请假 — qǐngjià — "to ask for time off".
我明天要请假 wǒ míngtiān yào qǐngjià — I need to take tomorrow off.
The new character, in pieces: 假 (jià) — eleven strokes: the person piece 亻; then a narrow piece of five strokes, a short bar that turns down, a short bar, a long vertical and two short bars against it; then four strokes, a short bar that turns down, a short bar, a stroke that runs right and sweeps down to the left, and a long stroke to the right across it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
请假 (qǐngjià) is built from 请 'to ask' and 假 (jià) 'leave', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjengX and kaeH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to beg' and the second 'leave from work'.

Script — the person piece, then two narrow pieces.
假 (jià).
Three pieces, left to right.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes: a stroke falling to the left, then a vertical.
The middle piece, five strokes. A short bar that turns down at its right end. A short bar under it. A long vertical down the left side to the foot. Then two short bars against the vertical, the upper one first.
The right piece, four strokes. A short bar that turns down. A short bar under it. A stroke that runs right and sweeps down to the left. Then a long stroke falling to the right across it to the foot.
2 + 5 + 4 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 假 (jià), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 假 (jià)? (Eleven.) Which piece comes first? (The person piece, 亻.) What is the last stroke? (A long stroke falling to the right.)
