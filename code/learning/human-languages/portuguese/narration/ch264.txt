Portuguese, chapter 264: Ida e Volta --- The Ticket, and the Missed Train.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
ida e volta — the going and the coming back.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "a ticket". (Um bilhete.) Say "I would like". (Gostaria.)
[pause 1 second]
Now you are standing at a ticket window.

You'll want to know: ida e volta, só de ida.
ida e volta — "there and back": a return ticket. só de ida — "one way", the going only.
Um bilhete de ida e volta. — A return ticket. Só de ida. — One way only.
a ida is "the going", from ir; a volta is "the coming back", from your voltar. só means "only". Brazil says passagem where Portugal says bilhete, and trem for comboio; ida e volta is the same on both sides of the ocean.

Grammar Lens: queria um bilhete para…
The whole request takes one frame:
Queria um bilhete para Lisboa, de ida e volta. — I'd like a return ticket to Lisbon.
queria is the imperfeito of querer, and at a counter in Portugal it is the everyday polite request: "I was wanting" softens into "I'd like". Gostaria is more formal. Most cities take no article, para Lisboa, but a few keep one: para o Porto.
And what comes back at you:
— Ida e volta? — Sim, ida e volta. / Não, só de ida. — A que horas parte o comboio? — Às dez.
Às is a plus as, "at the", before the hour.

The phrase, taken apart.
ir is inherited from Latin īre, "to go" (EtymDB 2.1), and ida is built on its participle ido, made a feminine noun, "the going"; EtymDB 2.1 has no row for the noun itself. volta comes from voltar, inherited from spoken Latin voltāre, "to turn round" (EtymDB 2.1), from Latin volūtāre, "to roll about" (Lewis & Short, under voluto). A return is a turning round. só is inherited from Latin sōlus, "alone" (EtymDB 2.1).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ida e volta, then só de ida]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Queria um bilhete para Lisboa, de ida e volta.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the one-way version — Queria um bilhete para o Porto, só de ida.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask the time — Desculpe, a que horas parte o comboio?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is a one-way ticket? (Só de ida.) Which past tense is queria, and why use it at a counter? (The imperfeito: it softens "I want" into "I'd like".) Which city keeps its article after para? (O Porto.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
perdi o comboio — when it goes wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ask for a one-way ticket to Porto. (Queria um bilhete para o Porto, só de ida.)
[pause 1 second]
Now the ticket is in your pocket, and the train has gone without you.

You'll want to know: perdi o comboio.
perdi o comboio — "I've missed the train".
You have perder, "to lose". Portuguese uses the same verb for a train you missed: you lost it. Its event past is the -i of dormi: perdi, and for "he" or "she", perdeu.
Perdi o comboio. — I've missed the train. Perdi o autocarro. — I've missed the bus. Perdi a mala. — I've lost my suitcase.
Portuguese says a mala where English says my suitcase: whose it is goes without saying. One warning about the present: "I lose" is irregular, perco.
And listen to the English. "I've missed" sounds like tenho perdido, but chapter fifteen showed what that form means in Portuguese: something that keeps happening lately. Tenho perdido o comboio is "I keep missing the train". For one missed train, the plain perdi.

Grammar Lens: reporting a problem.
A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:
Desculpe, tenho um problema. — Excuse me, I have a problem. Perdi o comboio. — What went wrong. Há outro comboio hoje? A que horas? — What you need.
Or the fault is not yours: O comboio não chegou, "the train didn't come". Say what happened, in the perfeito, then ask the question that gets you moving again.

The phrase, taken apart.
perder is inherited from Latin perdere (EtymDB 2.1), built on per-, "through", and dare, "to give": to give something away for good, to squander, to lose (Lewis & Short, under perdo). The dare inside it is the one that gave dar, "to give".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: Perdi o comboio. — then Perdi o autocarro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three parts — Desculpe, tenho um problema. Perdi o comboio. Há outro comboio hoje?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the fault that is not yours — O comboio não chegou.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: a habit, then one time — Perco sempre o autocarro. then Ontem perdi o autocarro.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say "I've missed the train". (Perdi o comboio.) What does tenho perdido o comboio say instead? (That it keeps happening.) Name the three parts of a complaint. (A courtesy, what went wrong, what you need.) And what are the two Latin pieces of perder? (Per- and dare, "to give".)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — one morning at the station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I've missed the train". (Perdi o comboio.)

What you've built.
Two chapters of travel, and they make one morning. You find the station, you buy the ticket, something goes wrong, and you ask your way out of it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every step is a sentence you have said: Desculpe, como se vai para a estação?, Queria um bilhete para Lisboa, de ida e volta., Desculpe, tenho um problema: perdi o comboio., Há outro comboio hoje?
[pause 2 seconds]
And when you tell someone about it afterwards, the morning becomes a story: Havia muitas pessoas na estação, por isso perdi o comboio. Scene, arrow, event.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: find it — Desculpe, como se vai para a estação?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the answer, said back — Em frente, depois à esquerda?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: buy it — Queria um bilhete para Lisboa, de ida e volta.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the problem — Desculpe, tenho um problema. Perdi o comboio.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tell it afterwards — Perdi o comboio porque havia muitas pessoas na estação.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is a return ticket? (Ida e volta.) What do you say before the problem itself? (Desculpe, tenho um problema.) In perdi o comboio porque havia muitas pessoas, which verb is the scene? (Havia.)
