Italian, chapter 407: Vetri e specchietti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il parabrezza — the windscreen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to steer, then the Italian for to skid.

You'll want to know: il parabrezza.
il parabrezza — "the windscreen".
Un sasso ha rotto il parabrezza. A stone cracked the windscreen.
Built like parasole: para- means it shields you from something. Plural unchanged.

The word, taken apart.
parabrezza is built from parare 'to ward off', which is inherited from Latin parāre 'to prepare' (Lewis & Short), and brezza 'breeze': the glass that holds back the wind.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: il tergicristallo.
il tergicristallo — "the windscreen wiper".
Accendi il tergicristallo, piove forte. Turn on the wipers, it is raining hard.
Often in the plural: i tergicristalli.

The word, taken apart.
tergicristallo is built from tergere 'to wipe', a learned borrowing from Latin tergēre (Lewis & Short), and cristallo 'glass', inherited from Latin crystallum, from Greek krýstallos 'clear ice' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
lo specchietto — the wing mirror, the car mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the windscreen, then the Italian for the windscreen wiper.

You'll want to know: lo specchietto.
lo specchietto — "the wing mirror, the car mirror".
Guarda nello specchietto prima di girare. Check the mirror before you turn.
A little specchio; lo specchietto retrovisore is the rear-view mirror.

The word, taken apart.
specchietto is a little specchio 'mirror', which is inherited from Vulgar Latin speclum, from Latin speculum 'mirror' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the windscreen wiper, then the Italian for the wing mirror.

You'll want to know: il clacson.
il clacson — "the horn (of a car)".
Il taxi suona il clacson per strada. The taxi honks its horn in the street.
Suonare il clacson — to honk. Plural unchanged.

The word, taken apart.
clacson was borrowed from English klaxon (EtymDB), first the trade name of an electric car horn, spelled the Italian way.

Lesson 5 of 5.
appannarsi — to mist up, to fog up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the wing mirror, then the Italian for the horn.

You'll want to know: appannarsi.
appannarsi — "to mist up, to fog up".
Con il freddo i vetri si appannano. In the cold the windows mist up.
Appannato — misty; eyes and memories can be appannati too.

The word, taken apart.
appannarsi is built on panno 'cloth', which is inherited from Latin pannus 'cloth, rag' (EtymDB): glass that looks covered by a veil of cloth.
