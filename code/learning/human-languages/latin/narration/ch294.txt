Latin, chapter 294: Identidem.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
identidem — again and again, repeatedly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for for a little while, then the Latin for for some time.

You'll want to know: identidem.
identidem — "again and again, repeatedly".
Īnfāns identidem flēbat. The baby cried again and again.
An adverb for an action that keeps coming back, like saepe (taught) but closer together.

The word, taken apart.
identidem is built on idem, "the same" (EtymDB): the same, and the same again. Lewis & Short analyse the middle part in more than one way.

Lesson 2 of 5.
subinde — immediately after; now and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for for some time, then the Latin for again and again.

You'll want to know: subinde.
subinde — "immediately after; now and then".
Prīmō rīsit, subinde flēvit. At first she laughed, and immediately after she wept.
Horace and later prose. In Seneca and Pliny it also means 'from time to time'.

The word, taken apart.
subinde joins sub, "close after", and inde, "from there, then" (Lewis & Short): following closely on.

Lesson 3 of 5.
crēbrō — frequently, often.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for again and again, then the Latin for immediately after.

You'll want to know: crēbrō.
crēbrō — "frequently, often".
Ad vīllam avī crēbrō veniēbāmus. We often used to come to our grandfather's villa.
The adverb of crēber (taught), 'thick, close-packed, frequent'.

The word, taken apart.
crēbrō is the ablative of crēber, "close-packed, frequent", used as an adverb (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
quotiēns — as often as; how often.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for frequently, then the Latin for mostly.

You'll want to know: quotiēns.
quotiēns — "as often as; how often".
Quotiēns in urbem veniēbat, amīcōs vīsitābat. As often as he came into the city, he visited his friends.
Its partner is totiēns, 'so often'. English quotient comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
quotiēns is built on quot, "how many" (Lewis & Short): how many times. English quotient was formed from quotiēns (EtymDB).
