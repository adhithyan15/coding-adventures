Sanskrit, chapter 412: Halts and Turning Back.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
विरामः (virāmaḥ) — a halt; a stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a blockage, then the Sanskrit for keeps away from.

You'll want to know: विरामः.
विरामः — virāmaḥ — "a halt; a stop".
लोकयानस्य विरामः ग्रामे अस्ति। lokayānasya virāmaḥ grāme asti. The bus has a halt at the village.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
विरामः (virāmaḥ) is built from vi, "away", and the root √ram, "to rest, to delight", which also gives ramate, "enjoys"; MW gives virāma as cessation, end, a stop or pause; a bus halt is that sense in modern use (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: निवर्तते.
निवर्तते — nivartate — "turns round; turns back".
वर्षायां पथिकः मार्गात् निवर्तते। varṣāyāṃ pathikaḥ mārgāt nivartate. In the rain the wayfarer turns back from the road.
Past (he …): सः मार्गात् निवृत्तवान्, saḥ mārgāt nivṛttavān.

The word, taken apart.
निवर्तते (nivartate) is built from ni, "back", and the root √vṛt, "to turn", which also gives vartate, "is present"; MW gives ni-vṛt as to turn back, with the participle nivṛtta, "turned back, returned" (MW).

धूमयानं स्थानकम् उपगच्छति। dhūmayānaṃ sthānakam upagacchati. The train is coming up to the station.

Lesson 5 of 5.
क्रोशः (krośaḥ) — a kos (a measure of distance).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for approaches, then the Sanskrit for passes by.

You'll want to know: क्रोशः.
क्रोशः — krośaḥ — "a kos (a measure of distance)".
ग्रामः इतः क्रोशं दूरम् अस्ति। grāmaḥ itaḥ krośaṃ dūram asti. The village is a kos away from here.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
क्रोशः (krośaḥ) comes from the root √kruś, "to cry out": MW gives krośa as a cry and as a measure of distance, the range of the voice in calling, the kos; it lives on as Hindi kos, a distance of some two miles (MW; CDIAL 3611).
