Russian, chapter 61: Bird, Mouse, Wolf, Bear, Fox.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
птица (ptítsa) — a bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a cow, then the Russian for a pig.

You'll want to know: птица.
птица (ptítsa) — "a bird".

The word, taken apart.
птица (ptítsa) from Proto-Slavic pŭtica, from pŭta, "bird" — inherited. Vasmer lists Latvian putns, "bird", Lithuanian putýtis, "little bird", and words for "child, young one": Latin putus, "boy", and Sanskrit putrá-, "son".

Lesson 2 of 5.
мышь (mysh) — a mouse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a pig, then the Russian for a bird.

You'll want to know: мышь.
мышь (mysh) — "a mouse".

The word, taken apart.
мышь (mysh) from Proto-Slavic myšĭ — inherited from PIE muHs, "mouse". Vasmer lists Sanskrit mūṣ-, Greek mŷs, Latin mūs and Old High German mûs: English mouse is a cousin. Trubachev adds that it may first have meant "the grey one".

Lesson 4 of 5.
медведь (medvéd) — a bear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a mouse, then the Russian for a wolf.

You'll want to know: медведь.
медведь (medvéd) — "a bear". Literally "honey-knower": the one who knows where the мёд is.

The word, taken apart.
медведь (medvéd) from Proto-Slavic medvědĭ, "honey-eater", from мёд, "honey", and the root of есть, "to eat" (Vasmer). It is a taboo name: hunters avoided the old word for "bear", the one kept in Latin ursus and Greek árktos.

Lesson 5 of 5.
лиса (lisá) — a fox.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a wolf, then the Russian for a bear.

You'll want to know: лиса.
лиса (lisá) — "a fox".

The word, taken apart.
лиса (lisá) goes with лис, "a fox", Old Church Slavonic лиса (lisá). Vasmer calls the origin unclear: tracing it to the family of Latin vulpēs and Lithuanian lãpė, "fox", is guesswork, he says, and other links are doubtful.
