# Sanskrit C2 mock rubric

Score either C2 mock against
[`task-shapes/c2.json`](../../task-shapes/c2.json). Reading, listening,
writing, and speaking are independent 100-point papers. A learner passes only
by earning at least **60/100 on every paper** in one administration; a 240/400
total cannot compensate for a failed skill, and every section must be
attempted.

## Reading — 100 points

Each of the four parts is worth 25 points. Reading has 40 one-point items, so
multiply the raw total by 2.5. Award credit only for the keyed choice. At C2,
distractors may be plausible at proposition level; the key depends on the
source's specialist argument architecture, ambiguity, intertextual stance,
competing voice, implicit premise, register effect, figurative meaning, or
precise textual evidence. External-sandhi decoding is part of comprehension,
but an isolated spelling feature must not substitute for advanced reading.

## Listening — 100 points

Each of the four parts is worth 25 points. Listening has 32 one-point items, so
multiply the raw total by 3.125. Award credit only for the keyed choice. Play
every part once at a natural 165–200 words per minute with variation, after one
unscored orienting preview. Score speaker tracking, implicit alignment, irony
and pragmatic force, interactional repair, argument architecture,
qualification, layered voice, ambiguity, cultural reference, stance shift,
and media framing. No transcript, romanization, dictionary, translator,
learner-controlled replay, or audio controls are allowed.

The designated multi-speaker parts must use two distinct actual documented
regional Sanskrit pronunciation traditions. The confidential record must
retain the actual tradition names, speaker provenance, and reviewer
confirmation by a qualified Sanskrit reviewer before operational use. A label
alone is not evidence. Documented alternatives used consistently receive full
credit when they satisfy the task.

## Writing — 100 points

The 220–280 word three-source synthesis and audience reframing is worth 40
points: accurate multi-source synthesis 10, selection and reframing 8,
organisation and cohesion 6, audience and register control 6, Sanskrit
vocabulary and grammar 7, and orthographic control 3. It must distinguish
source claims from the writer's additions while preserving material
qualification and uncertainty.

The 420–520 word contrasting extended text is worth 60 points: task fulfilment
and conceptual precision 18, architecture and cohesion 10, genre and register
mastery 10, rhetorical flexibility 7, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 10, and
orthographic control 5. A response outside its band loses only credit for
evidence it fails to provide; there is no automatic length penalty.

Orthographic control includes ordered and recognizable letter bodies, vowel
signs and conjuncts, external sandhi, continuous śirorekhā where the word
requires it, word-final visarga or anusvara, and clear word boundaries.
Romanized, traced, translated, copied, non-Devanagari, spell-checked, or
answer-model-dependent work earns no independent writing credit.

## Speaking — 100 points

The 330–360 second synthesis or mediation presentation is worth 35 points:
source synthesis and mediation 10, conceptual precision 7, organisation and
cohesion 6, audience and register control 4, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar
5, and pronunciation and fluency 3. The 360–450 second multiparty negotiation
is worth 30 points: stakeholder mediation 7, collaborative interaction and
repair 6, register adaptation 5, negotiated resolution 5, Sanskrit vocabulary
and grammar 4, and pronunciation and fluency 3. The 390–480 second sustained
challenge, defence, and reformulation is worth 35 points: reasoned defence 8,
qualification and precision 6, second-audience reformulation 6, interaction
and repair 5, register mastery 4, Sanskrit vocabulary and grammar 4, and
pronunciation and fluency 2.

Permit only paper keyword notes made during fifteen minutes of preparation,
never a written answer script or electronic notes. Accept a consistent
documented pronunciation tradition. Score paired or grouped learners only on
their own contributions; another learner's performance cannot compensate.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Classical Sanskrit in Devanagari is in scope, including external sandhi,
word-final visarga or anusvara, continuous śirorekhā, and a documented spoken
pronunciation tradition. Vedic accent and morphology remain outside this
assessment. This project-defined approximation is not an external CEFR
qualification. These learner papers, confidential masters, keys, and rubric
are not qualified Sanskrit recordings, independent human review, calibrated
double-scoring, or standard setting. Book-only validation cannot establish a
readiness or validated-pass claim. Those artifacts remain separate
prerequisites. `assessment.json` is active as a source-artifact contract; its
presence does not satisfy them.
