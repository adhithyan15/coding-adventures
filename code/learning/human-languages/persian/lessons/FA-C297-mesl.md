# مثل (mesl) — like, as

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-COMPARATIVE-TAR-01, FA-GRAMMAR-SUPERLATIVE-TARIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "this room is bigger than that room". (*In otâq az ân otâq
bozorgtar ast.*) Then "the best food". (*Behtarin ghazâ.*)

[PAUSE 1s] *-tar* says how two things differ. Describing a place needs a word
for how they are the same.

## You'll want to know: مثل
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-C297-MESL-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-EZAFE-OWNER, FA-LEX-C72-TOWN72-05, FA-LEX-C296-GHAZA-01] -->

**مثل** — *mesl* — "like, as".

> **این شهر مثل تهران است.** — *in shahr mesl-e Tehrân ast* — This city is like
> Tehran.

It takes the ezafe, *mesl-e*, and then the thing it matches: *mesl-e khâne*,
like home; *mesl-e ghazâ-ye mâdar*, like mother's cooking.

One use to keep whole: **مثل همیشه**, *mesl-e hamishe*, "as usual", word for
word "like always":

> **مثل همیشه دیر رسید.** — *mesl-e hamishe dir resid* — As usual, he arrived
> late.

Say it in one beat, *mesl*, with a clear *s*. Its second letter is new: the
next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-SCRIPT-C290-ZAL-01] -->

*mesl* is borrowed from Arabic *mithl*, "like" (EtymDB 2.1), on the Arabic
root *m-th-l*, usually glossed "to be like". Arabic says its middle letter as the *th* of English
*thin*. Persian says it *s* and keeps the Arabic letter in the spelling, the
way it kept **ذ** in *ghazâ* and says it *z*.
