Lesson 1 of 5.
avis, avis — a bird.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a goat, then the Latin for a pig.

You'll want to know: avis, avis.
avis, avis — "a bird". English aviation.

The word, taken apart.
avis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h2ewis, "bird" (EtymDB 2.1). auspex, "a bird-watcher", the priest who read omens from birds, is built on it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
lupus, lupī — a wolf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a pig, then the Latin for a bird.

You'll want to know: lupus, lupī.
lupus, lupī — "a wolf".

The word, taken apart.
lupus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European wlkwos, "wolf" (EtymDB 2.1), with Greek lykos, "wolf", and English wolf as cousins (Lewis & Short). Its p, where Latin would have qu, suggests it came in from a neighbouring Italic language such as Sabine.

Lesson 5 of 5.
serpēns, serpentis — a snake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a lion, then the Latin for a bear.

You'll want to know: serpēns, serpentis.
serpēns, serpentis — "a snake". Literally "the creeper".

The word, taken apart.
serpēns is the present participle of serpō, "to creep" (Lewis & Short), used as a noun: the creeping thing. serpō is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European serp-, "to crawl, to creep" (EtymDB 2.1).
