Telugu, chapter 473: Islands and the Deep Sea.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ద్వీపం (dvīpaṁ) — an isle; an island.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to die out, then the Telugu for to migrate.

You'll want to know: ద్వీపం.
ద్వీపం — dvīpaṁ — "an isle; an island".
ఆ ద్వీపానికి పడవలో వెళ్ళాలి. ā dvīpāniki paḍavalō veḷḷāli. You have to go to that island by boat.
A formal word; దీవి is the everyday one.

The word, taken apart.
ద్వీపం (dvīpaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit dvīpa, "land, an island" (CDIAL 6691), which CDIAL takes apart as "with water on two sides".

తిమింగలం (timiṅgalaṁ) — a whale.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: తిమింగలం.
తిమింగలం — timiṅgalaṁ — "a whale".
సముద్రంలో పెద్ద తిమింగలం కనిపించింది. samudraṁlō pedda timiṅgalaṁ kanipiñcindi. A huge whale appeared in the sea.
The biggest animal in the sea.

The word, taken apart.
తిమింగలం (timiṅgalaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit timiṅgila, the name of a huge sea creature, literally "swallower of the timi fish" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
పగడం (pagaḍaṁ) — coral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for an isle, then the Telugu for a whale.

You'll want to know: పగడం.
పగడం — pagaḍaṁ — "coral".
ఆ ద్వీపం చుట్టూ పగడాల దిబ్బలు ఉన్నాయి. ā dvīpaṁ cuṭṭū pagaḍāla dibbalu unnāyi. There are coral reefs round that island.
పగడాల దిబ్బ, a coral reef. Red coral is set in jewellery too.

The word, taken apart.
పగడం (pagaḍaṁ) is listed in DEDR as Telugu pagaḍamu, "red coral" (DEDR 3998), beside Tamil pavaḷam; DEDR compares Sanskrit pravāla, and which side borrowed is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ఉప్పెన (uppena) — a tidal surge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a whale, then the Telugu for coral.

You'll want to know: ఉప్పెన.
ఉప్పెన — uppena — "a tidal surge".
తుపాను వల్ల తీరంలో ఉప్పెన వచ్చింది. tupānu valla tīraṁlō uppena vaccindi. Because of the storm a tidal surge hit the coast.
The sea rising over the land in a storm.

The word, taken apart.
ఉప్పెన (uppena) is native Dravidian: DEDR 666 lists Telugu uppena, "a swell of the sea, an inundation", beside ubbu, "to swell, to overflow". The sea swells over the land.

Lesson 5 of 5.
గర్జించు (garjiñcu) — to roar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for coral, then the Telugu for a tidal surge.

You'll want to know: గర్జించు.
గర్జించు — garjiñcu — "to roar".
రాత్రంతా సముద్రం గర్జించింది. rātrantā samudraṁ garjiñcindi. The sea roared all night.
Past (I …): గర్జించాను, garjiñcānu.
Said of a lion, of thunder and of an angry sea.

The word, taken apart.
గర్జించు (garjiñcu) is built from Sanskrit garj, "to thunder, to roar" (CDIAL 4046, garjati), with the Telugu verb ending -ఇంచు.
