Mandarin Chinese, chapter 78: Age and Growing Up.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
多大 (duō dà) — how old (asking a person's age).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for extremely, then the Mandarin for really.

You'll want to know: 多大.
多大 — duō dà — "how old (asking a person's age)".
生日快乐你今年多大 shēngrì kuàilè nǐ jīnnián duō dà — Happy birthday! How old are you now?

The word, taken apart.
多大 (duō dà) is built from 多 'much' and 大 'big': how big, said of a person's years.

Lesson 2 of 6.
长大 (zhǎngdà) — to grow up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for really, then the Mandarin for how old.

You'll want to know: 长大.
长大 — zhǎngdà — "to grow up".
他们长大了 tāmen zhǎngdà le — They've grown up.
The character you wrote for cháng, long, is read zhǎng here, to grow.

The word, taken apart.
长大 (zhǎngdà) is built from zhǎng 'to grow' and 大 'big', both inherited from Middle Chinese, trjangX and dajH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as 'big' and 'elder'; growing is not given there, so how it got that sense is not settled.

What you've built.

[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for how old, then the Mandarin for to grow up.

You'll want to know: 前.
前 — qián — "before; ago; front".
三年前我八岁 sān nián qián wǒ bā suì — Three years ago I was eight.
The new character, in pieces: 前 (qián) — two horns over a bar, the moon piece read yuè on the left, and a blade of two verticals on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
前 (qián) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'before, first'.

Lesson 4 of 6.
前 (qián) — horns, a bar, the moon and a blade.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — horns, a bar, the moon and a blade and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.

The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say qián — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
三 年.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — sān, nián]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — horns, a bar, the moon and a blade.
前 (qián).
Two horns, two strokes: a dot on the left, a short left-falling stroke on the right.
A long horizontal under them.
The moon piece, four strokes, read yuè as in the word for month: a left side that falls down, a top that turns down the right side and hooks, and two short bars inside.
The blade, two strokes: a short vertical, then a long vertical that hooks to the left.
2 + 1 + 4 + 2 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 前 (qián), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 前 (qián)? (Nine.) What comes after the horns? (The long bar.) Which stroke hooks last? (The long vertical on the right.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 前 (qián), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 5 of 6.

You'll want to know: niánjì.
niánjì — "age (of a person)".
nǐ bàba duō dà niánjì — How old is your father?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
niánjì is built from 年 'year' and jì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, nen and kiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as a span of twelve years.
