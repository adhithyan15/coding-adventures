Malayalam, chapter 204: Poor, Dirty, Smooth, Rough, Round.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പാവം (pāvaṁ) — poor, innocent, harmless (of a person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for tight, then the Malayalam for empty.

You'll want to know: പാവം.
പാവം — pāvaṁ — "poor, innocent, harmless (of a person)".

The word, taken apart.
പാവം (pāvaṁ) is usually traced to Sanskrit pāpa, "wicked; wretched" (CDIAL 8092); in Malayalam the sense softened to "poor, harmless".

അഴുക്കുള്ള (aḻukkuḷḷa) is native Dravidian, "having dirt": aḻukku, "dirt, filth" (DEDR 283, with Tamil aḻukku), with -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697).

Lesson 3 of 5.
മിനുസമുള്ള (minusamuḷḷa) — smooth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for poor, then the Malayalam for dirty.

You'll want to know: മിനുസമുള്ള.
മിനുസമുള്ള — minusamuḷḷa — "smooth".

The word, taken apart.
മിനുസമുള്ള (minusamuḷḷa) is "having gloss": minusam, "smoothness", most likely from the verb "to glitter, be polished"; DEDR 4876 lists Malayalam minukkam, "shining, polish", with Tamil miṉ, though not minusam itself. Then comes -uḷḷa, "that has" (DEDR 697).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for dirty, then the Malayalam for smooth.

You'll want to know: പരുക്കൻ.
പരുക്കൻ — parukkan — "rough, coarse".

The word, taken apart.
പരുക്കൻ (parukkan) is native Dravidian, built on parukku, "rough surface"; DEDR 3973 lists it, beside Tamil paru and Kannada parige. DEDR 3973 compares Sanskrit paruṣa, with a query.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ഉരുണ്ട (uruṇṭa) — round.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for smooth, then the Malayalam for rough.

You'll want to know: ഉരുണ്ട.
ഉരുണ്ട — uruṇṭa — "round".

The word, taken apart.
ഉരുണ്ട (uruṇṭa) is native Dravidian, a past form of the verb "to roll": "rolled round"; DEDR 664 lists the root with Tamil uruḷ and Kannada uruḷ. DEDR 664 compares Sanskrit uṇḍeraka.
