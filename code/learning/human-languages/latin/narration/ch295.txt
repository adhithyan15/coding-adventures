Latin, chapter 295: Noctū et interdiū.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
noctū — by night, at night.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for mostly, then the Latin for as often as.

You'll want to know: noctū.
noctū — "by night, at night".
Fūrēs noctū in vīllam intrāvērunt. Thieves got into the villa at night.
An old case form of nox (taught): in the night-time, a word for when thieves and lovers go out.

The word, taken apart.
noctū is an old case form of nox, "night" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Proto-Indo-European nókʷts, "night"; English night is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
interdiū — by day, in the daytime.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for as often as, then the Latin for by night.

You'll want to know: interdiū.
interdiū — "by day, in the daytime".
Interdiū in agrīs labōrābant, noctū dormiēbant. By day they worked in the fields, at night they slept.
inter + an old form of diēs: within the day. It pairs with noctū. Caesar and Livy use it; Cicero does not.

The word, taken apart.
interdiū joins inter, "between, within", and an old form of diēs, "day" (EtymDB): within the daylight hours.

Lesson 3 of 5.
vesperī — in the evening.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: vesperī.
vesperī — "in the evening".
Vesperī omnēs ad cēnam vēnērunt. In the evening everyone came to dinner.
An old locative of vesper (taught): at evening. Herī vesperī: yesterday evening.

The word, taken apart.
vesperī is an old locative of vesper, "evening" (Lewis & Short), used as an adverb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
mātūrē — early; in good time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for by day, then the Latin for in the evening.

You'll want to know: mātūrē.
mātūrē — "early; in good time".
Agricola mātūrē surrēxit et in agrum īvit. The farmer got up early and went to the field.
From mātūrus, 'ripe, timely'. Its opposite is sērō, 'late', the adverb of sērus (taught). English mature.

The word, taken apart.
mātūrē is the adverb of mātūrus, "ripe, timely" (Lewis & Short): at the right time, and so early. English mature comes from mātūrus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dūdum — a little while ago; for a long time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for in the evening, then the Latin for early.

You'll want to know: dūdum.
dūdum — "a little while ago; for a long time".
Epistulam, quam dūdum scrīpsī, nunc mittō. I am now sending the letter that I wrote a little while ago.
With iam, 'for a long time now': the track teaches iamdūdum.

The word, taken apart.
dūdum is built, says Lewis & Short, on diū, "for a long time", and dum, "while" (EtymDB lists the same parts): it is a while since.
