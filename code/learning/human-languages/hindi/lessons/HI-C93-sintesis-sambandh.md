# दुकान कहाँ है? — answering with a landmark

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C40-DEIXIS-06, HI-LEX-C56-ROAD-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Ask where the shop is. You have been able to ask this for a long
time, and until this chapter you could not answer it.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-03, HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C56-ROAD-04] -->

| speaker | Hindi |
|---|---|
| A | दुकान कहाँ है? |
| B | स्टेशन के सामने है। |
| A | स्टेशन के पास? |
| B | हाँ, पास है। |

B's first answer gives a **side**; the second gives only a **distance**. B is
not repeating — B is conceding the weaker claim, because A asked for less.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-03, HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C93-REL-05] -->

Find the **landmark** — the noun before **के** — then read the word after it,
because that word alone carries the relation.

**पास** gives distance and no side. **सामने** and **पीछे** give a side and no
distance. Do not upgrade **पास** into a side.

## Grammar Lens: the chapter in one sentence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-01, HI-LEX-C93-REL-06, HI-LEX-C93-REL-07] -->

Hindi has no prepositions. It has **possessive phrases whose head noun names a
relation**.

**मैं दोस्त के साथ काम करने के लिए जाता हूँ।** — Two relation phrases, one
sentence, three slots each.
