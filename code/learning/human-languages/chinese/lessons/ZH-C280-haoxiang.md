## You'll want to know: hǎoxiàng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C280-HAOXIANG-01]; assesses=[] -->

*hǎoxiàng* — "as if; to look like", a guess.

> *tā hǎoxiàng bìng le* — He looks as if he's ill.

> *jīntiān hǎoxiàng yào xiàyǔ* — It looks like rain today.

Its second character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*hǎoxiàng* is built from 好 and xiàng 'to be like', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *xawX* and *zjangX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'good, fine' and the second 'to be like'; 'as if' for the pair is the word's own sense.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ZH-LEX-C256-THINK256-03, ZH-LEX-C144-MATTER144-05, ZH-LEX-C50-SLEEP50-05] -->

我觉得 is your opinion. 看来 draws a conclusion from what you see. *hǎoxiàng* is softer still: a guess about how things look.

It goes before the verb, after the person: *tā hǎoxiàng bìng le*.
