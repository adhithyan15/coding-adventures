Gujarati, chapter 38: Because, and Therefore.
5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કેમકે (kemke) — because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One chapter back, the frames that report a thought. Three chapters back — and also thirty chapters back — the word for why.
[your turn — recall: vichārũ chhũ ke, then kem]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
Since the third chapter of this book you have been able to ask કેમ?. You have never once been able to answer it.

The word, taken apart.
The answer is the question with the swallowing word stuck to it:
કેમ (why) + કે (that) = કેમકે (kemke) — because.
Both halves are already yours; nothing here is new except the join. Gujarati answers why by saying, in effect, "why-that" — and then the reason follows as a whole sentence.
(what happened) કેમકે (kemke) (the reason).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kem — then kemke. The question, then the answer's opening.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: any sentence, kemke, and a reason.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: ke alone, then kemke. One reports, one explains.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What two words is કેમકે (kemke) built from, and what does each contribute? (કેમ, why, and કે, that.) Answer a why question about anything.


Lesson 2 of 5.
કેમ? … કેમકે … (kem? … kemke …) — asking why, and answering it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back, the answering word. Three chapters back, at the same position in its chapter, the sentence that admits you are lost.
[your turn — recall: kemke, and samajto nathī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange, taken apart.
Both halves are yours, and they are the same three sounds twice over:
— કેમ? — why?
— … કેમકે (kemke) … — … because …
Say them as a pair rather than as two lessons. The question word is inside the answer, which makes this one of the easiest exchanges in the language to hold: if you can ask it you can already say most of the reply.
This is also where the repair kit pays off. If the reason comes back too fast, you now have both halves of that exchange too — ask kem, and if the answer outruns you, say samajto nathī and get it again.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — run: ask kem?, then answer yourself with kemke and a reason.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — run: the same, but reply samajto nathī and ask again.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kem and kemke back to back until the pair is one motion.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Ask why, answer it, and then say you did not follow the answer. Three sentences, all of them yours in the last four chapters.


Lesson 3 of 5.
કેમકે (kemke) reaches the page — because, on the page — four signs, all of them old.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — the word is its own pattern until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
One chapter back you wrote કે. Write it once from memory before anything is uncovered.

Script — the word is its own pattern.
Uncover કેમકે (kemke). Read it in two halves rather than four signs:
કે, મ, કે.
The કે you wrote last chapter appears twice, with મ between them. Nothing here is new to the hand — the whole word is one old syllable, a known letter, and that syllable again.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Copy કેમકે (kemke) once with the model in view. Check that both ે strokes sit at the same height.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write કેમકે (kemke) from memory, then uncover and compare. Which piece of it did you already write in the previous chapter? (કે — and it is in there twice.)


Lesson 4 of 5.
માટે (māṭe) — for, in order to.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
One lesson back: the word that answers why. Two chapters back, at the same place in its chapter: the word that joins two nouns.
[your turn — recall: kemke, and ane]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
કેમકે (kemke) looks backwards — it names the cause of something that already happened. માટે (māṭe) looks forwards — it names what something is for:
(a thing) માટે (māṭe) — "for …"
It follows the thing it applies to rather than preceding it, which is the Gujarati habit you have already met with the postpositions.
The two are worth holding apart from the start. A cause and a purpose are different answers to the same why, and languages that use one word for both make their learners guess.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: something you can name, then māṭe.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: a kemke answer and a māṭe answer to the same question.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — build: say what a thing is for.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of કેમકે and માટે looks back at a cause, and which looks forward to a purpose? (kemke back, māṭe forward.) Where does માટે stand? (After the thing it applies to.)


Lesson 5 of 5.
તેથી (tethī) — therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The two reason-words of this chapter so far: one looks back at a cause, one forward to a purpose.
[your turn — recall: kemke, māṭe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The word, taken apart.
કેમકે (kemke) puts the result first and the reason second. તેથી (tethī) does the opposite — reason first, then the conclusion drawn from it:
(reason). તેથી (tethī) (result). — "…, therefore …"
The word says so itself. તે is that, and the -થી ending means from — so તેથી (tethī) is literally from that, which is what a conclusion is. You have seen that -થી before, on the end of નથી, where it was the worn-down is; here it is the separate postposition meaning from. Same two letters, two different histories, and it is worth knowing they are not the same thing.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — build: a reason, then tethī, then the result.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: the same two facts joined by kemke, then by tethī. The order flips.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nathī and tethī back to back — the same ending, different jobs.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say two facts joined by kemke, then say them again with tethī. What changed? (The order — kemke puts the result first, tethī the reason.) Next chapter: if, and when.
