# er hat das gemacht — "he did that"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PERFEKT-DU-01, GE-LEX-GEMACHT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Third person, and the first sentence in this chapter with a word
standing between *haben* and the participle.

## You'll want to know: er hat das gemacht
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-PERFEKT-ER-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **er hat das gemacht** — "he did that."

*Hat* is the second of *haben*'s two *b*-less forms, the twin of English *has*.
And *das*, the little word you met as an article, doubles as "that" — the thing
he did.

Notice where it went. *Das* did not follow *gemacht*; it slipped in **before**
it. The participle stayed at the far end of the sentence and let the rest of the
words file in ahead of it. That is not a quirk of this one sentence, and the
next lesson but one is about it.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-PERFEKT-ER-01, GE-LEX-GEMACHT-01] -->

- `ch-ach` — the throat scrape is back, in the middle of *gemacht*.
- `final-t` — three of the four words in this sentence end in a *t*. Release
  each one; German does not let a final *t* go soft.
