# مرحبا (marḥaban) — "hello," literally "welcome, there's room for you"

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] The friendly, everyday "hi" — and your first chance to see a root
do its work. This one is built on the idea of *spaciousness*.

## You'll want to know first

- [the root system](./AR-C01-root-system.md) — three consonants, one meaning.

## Sounds you'll need

- `rtl` — read **right to left**: م ر ح ب ا. → [reference](../pronunciation-reference.md)
- `ha-heavy` — the **ح** (ḥ) is a "heavy" *h*, breathed from deep in the
  throat — not the light English *h*. *mar-ḤA-ban*.

## The word, taken apart

**مرحبا** (*marḥaban*) — "hello / welcome." Root: **ر–ح–ب** (**r–ḥ–b**), the
idea of being **wide, spacious, roomy**:

- **raḥuba** — to be spacious
- **raḥb / raḥīb** — wide, roomy
- **marḥaban** — literally "[a place of] welcome," i.e. *"there is room for
  you here."*

So *marḥaban* isn't an empty sound like "hi." It says **"welcome — make room,
come in."** Arabic greetings are almost all like this: little sentences with
real meaning inside, the same way *buenos días* hid a blessing and *adiós* hid
"to God." The reply is often *مرحبتين* (*marḥabtayn*), literally "two
welcomes" — one better than yours.
