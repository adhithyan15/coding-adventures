Punjabi, chapter 240: Grinding and Pounding.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਸਿਲ (sil) — a grinding stone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to cover, then the Punjabi for ghee.

You'll want to know: ਸਿਲ.
ਸਿਲ — sil — "a grinding stone".
ਸਿਲ ਭਾਰੀ ਪੱਥਰ ਹੈ। sil bhārī patthar hai. The grinding stone is a heavy stone.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਸਿਲ (sil) is inherited from Sanskrit śilā, "rock, crag"; CDIAL lists Punjabi sil, "stone slab used for grinding spices" (CDIAL 12459).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਉੱਖਲੀ (ukkhlī) — a wooden mortar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a grinding stone, then the Punjabi for to grind.

You'll want to know: ਉੱਖਲੀ.
ਉੱਖਲੀ — ukkhlī — "a wooden mortar".
ਉੱਖਲੀ ਵਿੱਚ ਹਲਦੀ ਹੈ। ukkhlī vich haldī hai. There is turmeric in the mortar.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਉੱਖਲੀ (ukkhlī) is inherited from Sanskrit ulūkhala, "a wooden mortar"; CDIAL lists Punjabi ukkhalī, "small wooden mortar" (CDIAL 2360). CDIAL notes that the Sanskrit word itself may come from Dravidian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ਮੋਹਲਾ (mohlā) — a pestle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for to grind, then the Punjabi for a wooden mortar.

You'll want to know: ਮੋਹਲਾ.
ਮੋਹਲਾ — mohlā — "a pestle".
ਉੱਖਲੀ ਅਤੇ ਮੋਹਲਾ ਰਸੋਈ ਵਿੱਚ ਹਨ। ukkhlī ate mohlā rasoī vich han. The mortar and pestle are in the kitchen.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਮੋਹਲਾ (mohlā) is inherited from Sanskrit musala, "pestle"; CDIAL lists Punjabi mohlā, "pestle" (CDIAL 10223). CDIAL notes that the Sanskrit word is probably not Indo-Aryan in origin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ਕੁੱਟਣਾ (kuṭṭṇā) — to pound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a wooden mortar, then the Punjabi for a pestle.

You'll want to know: ਕੁੱਟਣਾ.
ਕੁੱਟਣਾ — kuṭṭṇā — "to pound".
ਮੈਂ ਮੋਹਲੇ ਨਾਲ ਲਸਣ ਕੁੱਟਦੀ ਹਾਂ। maiṁ mohle nāl lasaṇ kuṭṭdī hāṁ. I pound garlic with the pestle.
Past (I …): ਮੈਂ ਅਦਰਕ ਕੁੱਟਿਆ, maiṁ adrak kuṭṭiā.

The word, taken apart.
ਕੁੱਟਣਾ (kuṭṭṇā) is inherited from Sanskrit kuṭṭayati, "crushes, pounds"; CDIAL lists Punjabi kuṭṭṇā, "to beat, pound, ram" (CDIAL 3241).
