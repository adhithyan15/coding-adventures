# el documento — a paper that shows or proves

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C313-ASK-18, ES-LEX-C301-ASK-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *the passport* and *the paper*. (*El pasaporte, el papel*.) A
passport is one kind of **documento**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO]; assesses=[] -->

**El documento** comes from Latin **documentum**, something that gives evidence
or instruction. Its deeper verb is **docēre**, “to teach.” English **document**,
**doctor**, **doctrine** and **docent** belong to the same family: each points to
showing, teaching or establishing something.

A **papel** is the material. A **documento** is the meaningful record on it —
or the digital equivalent. A passport is both a small book and an official
**documento**.

## Grammar Lens: useful combinations
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DOCUMENTO, ES-LEX-C313-ASK-18] -->

- **el documento de identidad** — the identity document
- **el pasaporte es un documento** — the passport is a document
- **necesito el documento** — I need the document
