Italian, chapter 457: Alla cassa.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il cassiere — the cashier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the optician, then the Italian for to remove stains from.

You'll want to know: il cassiere.
il cassiere — "the cashier".
Il cassiere mi chiede la tessera. The cashier asks me for my loyalty card.
La cassa — the till; a woman is la cassiera.

The word, taken apart.
cassiere is built on cassa 'till, box', which is inherited from Latin capsa 'box' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
gli spiccioli — the small change, the coins.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to remove stains from, then the Italian for the cashier.

You'll want to know: gli spiccioli.
gli spiccioli — "the small change, the coins".
Non ho spiccioli per il caffè. I have no change for the coffee.
The singular lo spicciolo is rare.

The word, taken apart.
spiccioli is the plural of spicciolo 'small coin'. The origin is not settled; it is often linked to spicciare 'to sort out'.

You'll want to know: il camerino.
il camerino — "the fitting room; the dressing room".
Posso provarlo in camerino? Can I try it on in the fitting room?
In a theatre, il camerino is the actors' dressing room.

The word, taken apart.
camerino is a little camera 'room', which is inherited from Latin camera 'vault, arched roof' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: la svendita.
la svendita — "the clearance sale".
C'è una svendita: tutto a metà prezzo. There is a clearance sale: everything half price.
I saldi are the seasonal sales.

The word, taken apart.
svendita is built from s- and vendita 'sale', from vendere 'to sell', which is inherited from Latin vēndere (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
mercanteggiare — to haggle, to bargain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the fitting room, then the Italian for the clearance sale.

You'll want to know: mercanteggiare.
mercanteggiare — "to haggle, to bargain".
Al mercato si può mercanteggiare un po'. At the market you can haggle a bit.
More common in speech: tirare sul prezzo.

The word, taken apart.
mercanteggiare is built on mercante 'merchant', which is inherited from a form of Latin mercārī 'to trade', from merx 'goods' (Lewis & Short).
