# કામ કરવું (kām karvũ) — "to work"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C05-BOLVUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last verb of the chapter — from the single most productive root in
the whole family, one you have met before.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-CONCEPT-C05-KAAMKARVUN-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C05-BOLVUN-01] -->

**કામ** (*kām*, "work"): **કા** (*kā*) + **મ** (*ma*). **કરવું** (*karvũ*, "to
do"): **ક** (*ka*) + **ર** (*ra*) + **વું** (*vũ*).

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C05-BOLVUN-01, GU-CONCEPT-C05-KAAMKARVUN-01] -->

**કરવું** (*karvũ*, "to do, to make") is from Sanskrit **√કૃ** (*√kṛ*, "to do"),
from PIE **\*kʷer-** — one of Sanskrit's most productive roots, met in *namaskār*
("the **making** of a bow") and *karma* ("a thing **done**"). And **કામ** (*kām*,
"work") is itself from Sanskrit *karma* — so "work-do" is, at root, "*kṛ*-thing
*kṛ*-do." Like Hindi, Gujarati builds compound verbs as **noun + કરવું**:

- **કામ કરવું** (*kām karvũ*) = "work-do" = "**to work**"
- **વાત કરવી** (*vāt karvī*) = "to converse" (*vāt*, "talk")

So *hũ kām karũ chhũ* = "I work." Learn *karvũ* and a whole grammar of "do-verbs"
opens up — the same one behind Hindi's *kām karnā* and Bengali's *kāj karā*.
