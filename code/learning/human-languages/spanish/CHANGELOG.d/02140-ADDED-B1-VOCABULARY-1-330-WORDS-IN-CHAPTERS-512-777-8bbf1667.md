## Added — B1 vocabulary: 1,330 words in chapters 512–777

- **266 new chapters (512–777) teach 1,330 new headwords, one word per lesson.** Each lesson gives the word, its English, one short example sentence with its translation, a usage note and a "The word, taken apart" origin. Each new word is practised again in the next two lessons. Every chapter sits on one of the five B1 spine nodes, so the words count at B1. The chapters fall into five tranches:
  - 512–575, SPINE-NARRATE-EVENTS (320 words): news and reports, incidents and crime, accidents and disasters, time and sequence, life events, celebrations, stories, history.
  - 576–639, SPINE-GIVE-REASONS and SPINE-EXPRESS-CONDITION (320 words): opinions and argument, causes and consequences, work and study, society, rules and the law, money, conditions and plans. Includes the B1 connectors *ya que*, *puesto que*, *dado que*, *siempre que*, *a menos que*, *con tal de que*, *en caso de que*, *a no ser que* and *a condición de que*, with the subjunctive where they need it.
  - 640–703, SPINE-HANDLE-TRAVEL (320 words): transport and the road, stations and airports, customs, lodging and complaints, finding the way, the town, nature and weather.
  - 704–767, SPINE-DESCRIBE-EXPERIENCE (320 words): feelings, character, health and the body, relationships and family, hobbies, reading and the screen.
  - 768–777, two chapters on each node (50 words): cooking, household repairs, shops, the house, phones and computers.
- **A review lesson closes every eight chapters** and revisits those chapters' 40 words, so each review fits the lesson time budget. Each tranche's last chapter also gets a short review of its own five words. With the chain's two revisits, every new atom reaches its two later revisits, the last two words included.
- **Three B1 consolidation reviews revisit 22 older B1 atoms.** The level gate found these practised only once: ES-R639-repaso-por-que-y-si, ES-R703-repaso-problemas-de-viaje and ES-R767-repaso-como-fue. They practise *explicar*, the reason chain, *deber*, causal *por*, *tuviera*/*hiciera*, *depende*, *problema* and the *-ma* nouns, *¿dónde está?*, *billete*, *feliz*, *más que*, *espero*, *durante*, and the imperfect for the scene against the preterite for the events. Each uses only words the track has already taught.
- **Sources.** Every origin was checked against EtymDB 2.1 and Lewis & Short. Where neither source supports a step, the lesson hedges it ("usually traced to …") or says the origin is not settled. Learned borrowings are told apart from inherited words. An independent review of each tranche found and fixed errors in 191 of the first 1,330 words (14.4%), and a second review found errors in 31 of the 223 replacement words (13.9%). Most were origin claims the sources do not support; the rest were Spanish examples, glosses and root slugs.
- **Root slugs** reuse the corpus's existing slug for each etymon (for example `mori-latin`, `salire-latin`, `abhorrere-latin`, `latin-vinculum`, `criticus-latin`). New slugs are added only where no slug for the etymon existed.
- **No forward references.** Every headword was checked against the text of every earlier lesson, the same way the continuity measure checks it. 223 first-draft headwords were replaced because older lessons already used them, for example *el año* (the ñ writing lessons and the A2 example *Su abuelo falleció el año pasado* already use it), *pasar*, *familiar* and *el correo* (taught inside *el correo electrónico*). Example lines that used a word taught later in the batch were reworded. Spanish's forward-reference count stays at exactly 622.
- **Hands-free first lessons.** Etymology and example wording avoids the sight cues the narration checks ("look at", "see the", "the table"). No chapter opens on a lesson that needs the eyes. The *localizador* example asks the learner to say the code, not to write it down.
