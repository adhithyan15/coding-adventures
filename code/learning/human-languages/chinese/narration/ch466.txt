Mandarin Chinese, chapter 466: Keeping Calm.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
fādāi — to stare blankly; to be lost in a daze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for pessimistic, then the Mandarin for sure of oneself.

You'll want to know: fādāi.
fādāi — "to stare blankly; to be lost in a daze".
shàngkè de shíhou tā chángcháng fādāi — In class he's often lost in a daze.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fādāi is built from 发 'to set going' and dāi 'dull'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese pjot (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to start'. The dictionary has no entry under the spelling of the second; under a variant spelling it records ngoj, glossed 'foolish', which would regularly give ái, not dāi, so the history of dāi is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
冷静 (lěngjìng) — calm; cool-headed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sure of oneself, then the Mandarin for to be lost in a daze.

You'll want to know: 冷静.
冷静 — lěngjìng — "calm; cool-headed".
你太高兴了先冷静一点儿 nǐ tài gāoxìng le xiān lěngjìng yìdiǎnr — You're getting carried away. Calm down a little first.
The new character, in pieces: 静 (jìng) — fourteen strokes in two pieces: on the left the right piece of 情, a top of four strokes over 月 yuè; on the right a short stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns into a stroke falling to the left, a stroke that runs right and turns down, two bars and a vertical with a hook. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
冷静 (lěngjìng) is built from 冷 'cold' and 静 (jìng) 'still', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lengX and dzjengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'cold' and the second 'at peace'. The dictionary lists 冷 under three readings, two of them glossed 'cold'; lengX is one of those two.

Script — the right piece of 情, then a right side of six.
静 (jìng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, eight strokes: the right piece of 情. A bar, a second bar, a vertical down through them and a long bar; then 月 under them: the stroke down the left, the stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks, and the two short bars inside.
The right piece, six strokes. A short stroke falling to the left. A bar that turns into a stroke falling to the left, like a small roof. A stroke that runs right and turns down. A long bar through it, a second bar under it, then a vertical down the middle that hooks to the left at the foot.
8 + 6 = fourteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 静 (jìng), all fourteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 静 (jìng) have? (Two: eight strokes and six.) Where have you written the left piece before? (On the right of 情.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
xīntiào — heartbeat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be lost in a daze, then the Mandarin for cool-headed.

You'll want to know: xīntiào.
xīntiào — "heartbeat".
wǒ gǎnjué xīntiào hěn kuài — I can feel my heart racing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xīntiào is built from 心 'heart' and tiào 'to jump', both inherited from Middle Chinese, sim and dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to leap'. The regular change from dew gives a rising tone; how tiào came to its falling tone is not settled.
