German, chapter 457: An der Küste.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Düne — the dune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the moss, then the German for to rustle.

You'll want to know: die Düne.
die Düne — "the dune".
Hinter den Dünen liegt der Strand. The beach lies behind the dunes.
Plural: die Dünen.

The word, taken apart.
Düne came into the standard language from Low German, a cousin of Dutch duin (EtymDB lists the word); English dune came from the same Dutch source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
die Ebbe — the low tide, the ebb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to rustle, then the German for the dune.

You'll want to know: die Ebbe.
die Ebbe — "the low tide, the ebb".
Bei Ebbe kann man weit ins Watt laufen. At low tide you can walk a long way out onto the mudflats.
Plural: die Ebben, rare. Figurative: In meinem Portemonnaie ist Ebbe — my purse is empty.

The word, taken apart.
Ebbe came into the standard language from Middle Low German ebbe (EtymDB), a cousin of English ebb.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Flut — the high tide; the flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the dune, then the German for the low tide.

You'll want to know: die Flut.
die Flut — "the high tide; the flood".
Wenn die Flut kommt, ist der Strand ganz schmal. When the tide comes in, the beach is very narrow.
Plural: die Fluten. Ebbe und Flut — the tides. Figurative: eine Flut von E-Mails — a flood of emails.

The word, taken apart.
Flut is inherited from Old High German fluot and Proto-Germanic flōduz (EtymDB); English flood is a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Bucht — the bay, the cove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the low tide, then the German for the high tide.

You'll want to know: die Bucht.
die Bucht — "the bay, the cove".
In der kleinen Bucht ist das Wasser ruhig und warm. In the little bay the water is calm and warm.
Plural: die Buchten.

The word, taken apart.
Bucht came into the standard language from Low German bucht 'bend, curve', related to biegen 'to bend' (EtymDB lists Dutch bocht 'bend' as a cousin): the coast bends inward.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Muschel — the shell, the seashell; the mussel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the high tide, then the German for the bay.

You'll want to know: die Muschel.
die Muschel — "the shell, the seashell; the mussel".
Am Strand sammeln die Kinder Muscheln. The children collect shells on the beach.
Plural: die Muscheln. Also the mussel you eat.

The word, taken apart.
Muschel is an early borrowing, Old High German muscula, from Latin mūsculus 'little mouse, muscle, mussel', from mūs 'mouse' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
