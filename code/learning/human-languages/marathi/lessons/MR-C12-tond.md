# तोंड (tõḍ) — the mouth, and the formal word standing behind it

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] **डोळा, कान** — eye, ear. Recall how **कान**'s middle got simpler —

## You'll want to know: तोंड
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-LEX-TOND]; assesses=[] -->

> **तोंड** — *tõḍ* — **mouth** (neuter)

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Nothing new. **त**, **ो**, **ं**, **ड** are all familiar. *Tõ-ḍ*, the anusvāra
nasalizing the *o*.

## The word, taken apart — the everyday word, and its formal twin
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-TOND-MUKH-DOUBLET]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-TATSAMA-BORROWING, MR-GRAMMAR-TATSAMA-TADBHAVA-SPLIT] -->

**तोंड** continues Maharastri Prakrit **तुंड** *tuṃḍa*, from Sanskrit **तुण्ड**
*tuṇḍa*, "mouth, snout, beak" — a tadbhava, worn down the way **भाऊ** was.
Marathi keeps a second, formal word for the same thing: **मुख** *mukh*,
borrowed whole from Sanskrit exactly the way **मित्र** and **कुटुंब** were —
the same everyday-versus-formal split **कुटुंब**'s chapter didn't need to show
you for a body part until now. You'll hear **तोंड** in daily speech and
**मुख** in writing, formal address, and words like *mukhya*, "chief, principal"
— literally "at the mouth/face of things."
