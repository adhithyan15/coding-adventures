Lesson 1 of 5.
कोशः (kośaḥ) — a sheath, a treasury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a flag, then the Sanskrit for a drum.

You'll want to know: कोशः.
कोशः — kośaḥ — "a sheath, a treasury".

The word, taken apart.
kośa is a Rigvedic word for a bucket, then a case, a sheath and a store-room (CDIAL 3539), and so a treasury. MW suggests, with question marks, a root √kuś and a link with kukṣi, "belly"; the origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
निधिः (nidhiḥ) — a treasure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a drum, then the Sanskrit for a sheath.

You'll want to know: निधिः.
निधिः — nidhiḥ — "a treasure".

The word, taken apart.
nidhi is built on ni, "down", and the root √dhā, "to put" (MW): a hoard is what is laid down and stored. In the Rigveda it means "setting down" and "hoard" (CDIAL 7207).

Lesson 3 of 5.
रूप्यकम् (rūpyakam) — a rupee.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a sheath, then the Sanskrit for a treasure.

You'll want to know: रूप्यकम्.
रूप्यकम् — rūpyakam — "a rupee".

The word, taken apart.
rūpyaka is built on rūpya, "beautiful, bearing a stamp", and then "silver" (CDIAL 10805), from rūpa, "form" (MW). Stamped silver became the coin, and EtymDB traces Hindi rupayā, the rupee, to rūpyaka.

Lesson 4 of 5.
पणः (paṇaḥ) — a stake, a wager, a coin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a treasure, then the Sanskrit for a rupee.

You'll want to know: पणः.
पणः — paṇaḥ — "a stake, a wager, a coin".

The word, taken apart.
paṇa is built on the root √paṇ, "to barter, bet" (MW): "a wager, a stake", and so wages (CDIAL 7714). As the name of a coin, CDIAL 7715 treats it as a separate word, perhaps Austroasiatic. Hindi pan means "promise".

Lesson 5 of 5.
शुल्कम् (śulkam) — a fee, a tax.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a rupee, then the Sanskrit for a stake.

You'll want to know: शुल्कम्.
शुल्कम् — śulkam — "a fee, a tax".

The word, taken apart.
śulka is a Rigvedic word for "price, value", and later "tax, toll" (CDIAL 12543). MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain. A Middle Indo-Aryan form, suṅka, went into Tamil as cuṅkam, "customs duty".
