# ఎందుకంటే (endukaṇṭē) — because

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TE-LEX-C74-LINK-03, TE-LEX-C74-LINK-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: what did *enduku* mean?

## You'll want to know: ఎందుకంటే
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-LEX-C74-LINK-05]; assesses=[] -->

**ఎందుకంటే** (*endukaṇṭē*) — "because".

Look at it: it *is* ఎందుకు with **అంటే** glued on — "if you ask why". Telugu answers the question by repeating it and then continuing, which is the gluing habit of your very first lesson doing real work.

**నేను వెళ్తాను, ఎందుకంటే పని ఉంది** — "I'll go, because there is work."

## Grammar Lens: the reason clause comes second
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TE-GRAMMAR-C74-LINK-05]; assesses=[] -->

Telugu can put the reason first, as **పని ఉంది కాబట్టి నేను వెళ్తాను**. With ఎందుకంటే it goes second, after the thing being explained, exactly where English puts it. Use ఎందుకంటే while that ordering is still the one you think in.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

A reason, attached to the thing it explains.
