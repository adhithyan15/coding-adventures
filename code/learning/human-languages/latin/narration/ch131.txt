Latin, chapter 131: Fog, Eagle, Dove, Raven, Duck.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nebula, nebulae — fog, mist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for thunder, then the Latin for lightning.

You'll want to know: nebula, nebulae.
nebula, nebulae — "fog, mist".

The word, taken apart.
nebula is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European nebhos, "cloud" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek nephelē, "cloud", is a close cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
aquila, aquilae — an eagle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for lightning, then the Latin for fog.

You'll want to know: aquila, aquilae.
aquila, aquilae — "an eagle".

The word, taken apart.
aquila may come from aquilus, "dark brown" (Lewis & Short), after the bird's colour, but this is uncertain. English eagle comes from it through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
columba, columbae — a dove, a pigeon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fog, then the Latin for an eagle.

You'll want to know: columba, columbae.
columba, columbae — "a dove, a pigeon".

The word, taken apart.
columba is the feminine of columbus, "a male dove" (Lewis & Short). Its origin is uncertain; Greek kolymbos, "a diving bird", is often compared (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 4 of 5.
corvus, corvī — a raven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an eagle, then the Latin for a dove.

You'll want to know: corvus, corvī.
corvus, corvī — "a raven".

The word, taken apart.
corvus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European korh2wos, "raven" (EtymDB 2.1), perhaps imitating the bird's croak; Greek korax, "raven", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).
