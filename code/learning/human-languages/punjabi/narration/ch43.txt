5 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.

The word, taken apart.
ਕੌਣ — kauṇ — who.
The same ਕ as every question word. This one has an odd history in this book: it was named once, in a romanised aside about the family, in Latin letters — never written in Gurmukhi, never practised, never a word you owned.
That is a different kind of gap from the missing ones. The book told you it existed and never handed it over.
Now hold the two families side by side, because they answer each other:
ਕ- asks, ਜ- opens the clause that answers, ਉ- points at it.

The word, taken apart.
Put them side by side. This is the neatest pattern in the chapter:
ਕਦੋਂ (kadoṁ) — when?, ਜਦੋਂ — when …
One letter apart. The ਕ asks; the ਜ answers. You learned the answer two chapters ago without knowing it was half of a pair, and the question costs you almost nothing now.
That pairing runs through the whole language, and you have now seen it three times: ਜੇ … ਤਾਂ, ਜੋ … ਉਹ, and now ਕਦੋਂ (kadoṁ) against ਜਦੋਂ.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਥ — the consonant tha.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — one new form, and why it is here until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
One chapter back you wrote ਉਹ. Write it once from memory.

Script — one new form, and why it is here.
Keep ਥ (tha) visible beside ਤ, which you have had for a long time:
ਤ — ta, the plain dental.
ਥ — tha, the same place with a push of breath behind it.
The tongue is in the same spot for both; the difference is the breath. Say ta with your hand in front of your mouth, then tha, and feel it.
Worth saying plainly, because it is the point of this whole run of chapters: this is the only new letter in seven chapters. Every joining word — ate, jāṁ, par, lekin, ki, kyoṁki, je, jadoṁ, jo, laī — was already writable with signs this book taught long ago. The words were missing; the script was not. One question needed one letter, and here it is.

Writing — notice and trace.
Finger-trace ਥ once, then trace it once in the air. Say tha with the breath audible as your finger finishes.

Writing — guided copy, model in view.
Keep the model in view and copy ਥ once, then ਤ beside it. Repair only the stroke that separates them.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which of ਤ and ਥ (tha) carries the breath? (ਥ (tha).) How many new letters have these seven chapters spent? (This one.)

The word, taken apart.
ਕਿੱਥੇ — kitthe — where.
Read it in pieces: ਕ, the ਿ sihari before it, the ੱ addak that doubles the next sound, then ਥ — new one lesson ago — and the ੇ laav.
The addak is doing real work. It doubles the ਥ (tha), which is why the word is kit-the and not ki-the. Leave it off and the word loses its middle.
This is the question that asks where somebody lives, and asking that is most of what a spoken interview at this level is for. The book has taught house, city and village for many chapters and could not ask where any of them was.

Wrap-up Recall.

The word, taken apart.
ਕਿੰਨੇ — kinne — how many, how much.
The same ਕ one last time, now asking about quantity. With it the family this book named when it taught kivēṁ is finally complete on the page:
ਕੀ what, ਕਿਵੇਂ how, ਕਿਉਂ why, ਕੌਣ (kauṇ) who, ਕਦੋਂ (kadoṁ) when,
ਕਿੱਥੇ (kitthe) where, ਕਿੰਨੇ (kinne) how many.
Two of those you had before this run. Five arrived in the last four chapters, and only one of the five cost a new letter.
