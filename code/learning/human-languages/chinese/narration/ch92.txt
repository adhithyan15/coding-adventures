Lesson 1 of 6.
也 (yě) — also; too.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A new chapter, and it opens with the word you need when somebody else has ordered first.
[your turn — recall: say 我想要一点儿水 — wǒ xiǎngyào yìdiǎnr shuǐ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 一点儿 — yìdiǎnr, a little]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say 他是学生 — tā shì xuésheng, he's a student]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: 也.
也 — yě — "also; too".
我也要 wǒ yě yào — Me too; I'd like one too.
他也是学生 tā yě shì xuésheng — He's a student too.
The next lesson puts the character in your hand. You have written it before, as the right half of 他.

The word, taken apart.
也 (yě) is inherited from Middle Chinese yaeX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a particle that closes a sentence'. 'Also' is not given there, so how it came to mean 'also' is not settled.

What you've built.
也 goes in front of the verb: 我也要 (wǒ yě yào), 他也是学生. English puts too at the end; Mandarin does not.

Script — the right half of 他.
也 (yě).
One piece, and your hand already knows it: the right half of 他, stroke for stroke.
A hooked turn: a horizontal that rises a little, turns down the right side and hooks to the left.
A short vertical from above it, down through it.
The long bend: down the left from above the horizontal, along the foot to the right, and a hook up at the end.
Three strokes.

Lesson 3 of 6.
càidān — menu.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

You'll want to know: càidān.
càidān — "menu".
qǐng gěi wǒ càidān — The menu, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
càidān is built from cài 'a dish', the cài you know, and dān, both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshojH and tan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'greens fit to eat' and 'single'. A sheet that lists the dishes is the modern sense of the pair.

What you've built.
qǐng gěi wǒ càidān: 请, then 给 + the person + the thing, the pattern of the chapter on giving.

You'll want to know: fúwùyuán.
fúwùyuán — "waiter; attendant".
fúwùyuán, wǒ yào càidān — Excuse me, the menu, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fúwùyuán is built from fú 'to serve', wù 'a task' and yuán, all inherited from Middle Chinese, bjuwk, mjuH and hjwen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to serve', 'affairs' and 'a number of things'. 'A member of staff' is a later sense of yuán.

What you've built.
In a restaurant or a shop, fúwùyuán is also how you call the person serving: say it, then what you want.

You'll want to know: biéde.
biéde — "something else; other".
nǐ yào biéde ma — Would you like anything else?
wǒ xiǎngyào biéde dōngxi — I'd like something else.
Its first character comes later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
biéde is built from bié 'other; apart' and de, the 的 you write. bié is inherited from Middle Chinese bjet (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'different; to part'; where de comes from is not settled.

What you've built.
nǐ yào biéde ma is the waiter's question; bú yào, xièxie is the answer when you have all you need.

Lesson 6 of 6.
我也要 (wǒ yě yào) — ordering at a table for two, and agreeing with 也.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The chapter closes here, at a table for two.
[your turn — recall: say biéde, something else — R1, one lesson back]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say jiǎozi, dumplings — R3, durable]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say xūyào, to need — R2]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

The exchange.
A: fúwùyuán, qǐng gěi wǒmen càidān — Excuse me, could we have the menu? B: 好 你们喝什么 hǎo, nǐmen hē shénme — Of course. What will you drink? A: 我要水 wǒ yào shuǐ — Water for me. C: 我也要 wǒ yě yào — Me too. B: nǐmen yào biéde ma — Anything else? A: wǒ yào jiǎozi — Dumplings for me. C: wǒ yě yào jiǎozi — Dumplings for me too. B: 好 hǎo — Right you are.
Ordering is yào and the thing; agreeing is 也 (yě) in front of it. Nobody says I would like: 我要 does the job.

Reading — the exchange at once.
[once you have stopped driving — read: the exchange through once without stopping, then answer the question below]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What does C order?

What you've built — ordering at a table.
qǐng gěi wǒmen càidān — the menu, please.
我要水 wǒ yào shuǐ — water for me.
我也要 wǒ yě yào — me too.
nǐmen yào biéde ma — anything else?
Listen. Nǐmen hē shénme?
Speak. Order a drink, then agree with somebody else's order using 也 (yě).
