Hindi, chapter 436: Drops and Showers.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बूँद (bū̃d) — a drop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a handkerchief, then the Hindi for to huddle up.

You'll want to know: बूँद.
बूँद — bū̃d — "a drop".
छत से पानी की बूँदें टपक रही थीं। chat se pānī kī bū̃dẽ ṭapak rahī thīṁ. Drops of water were dripping from the roof.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
बूँद (bū̃d) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit bindu, "a drop, a spot" (CDIAL 9240), through a form bundu; CDIAL lists Hindi bū̃d, "drop". The dot on a forehead, बिंदी, comes from the same word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
मेघ (megh) — a cloud (literary).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to huddle up, then the Hindi for a drop.

You'll want to know: मेघ.
मेघ — megh — "a cloud (literary)".
पुराने गानों में मेघ बहुत आते हैं। purāne gānoṁ meṁ megh bahut āte haiṁ. Clouds come up a lot in old songs.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
मेघ (megh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit megha, "a cloud", literally "the sprinkler" (Monier-Williams); poems and songs of the rains are full of it, while talk says बादल.

Lesson 5 of 5.
बरसना (barasnā) — to pour (of rain); to rain down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for steam, then the Hindi for a raincoat.

You'll want to know: बरसना.
बरसना — barasnā — "to pour (of rain); to rain down".
रात भर बादल बरसे। rāt bhar bādal barse. It poured all night long.
Past (it …): वह बादल ज़ोर से बरसा, vah bādal zor se barsā.

The word, taken apart.
बरसना (barasnā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit varṣati, "it rains" (CDIAL 11394), which lists Hindi barasnā; वर्षा, rain, is the same root.
