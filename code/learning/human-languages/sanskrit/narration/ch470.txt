Sanskrit, chapter 470: Inside the Body.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
फुप्फुसः (phupphusaḥ) — the lungs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a smell, then the Sanskrit for smells.

You'll want to know: फुप्फुसः.
फुप्फुसः — phupphusaḥ — "the lungs".
धूमः फुप्फुसं पीडयति। dhūmaḥ phupphusaṃ pīḍayati. Smoke harms the lungs.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
फुप्फुसः (phupphusaḥ) MW records phupphusa (Suśruta) and phuphusa, "the lungs"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

You'll want to know: आमाशयः.
आमाशयः — āmāśayaḥ — "the stomach".
आमाशयः भोजनं पचति। āmāśayaḥ bhojanaṃ pacati. The stomach digests food.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आमाशयः (āmāśayaḥ) is built from āma, "raw, undigested", and āśaya, "a resting place"; MW gives "the receptacle of the undigested food, the stomach" (MW).

Past (he …): सः शान्तं श्वसितवान्, saḥ śāntaṃ śvasitavān.

The word, taken apart.
श्वसिति (śvasiti) comes from the root √śvas, "to breathe", the root of श्वासः (MW).
