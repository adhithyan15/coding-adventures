Japanese, chapter 162: Broken, Cracked and Repaired.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こわれる (kowareru) — to be broken; to break down.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to get dry, then the Japanese for to dry.

You'll want to know: こわれる.
こわれる — kowareru — "to be broken; to break down".
やすい かさは よく こわれます。 — yasui kasa wa yoku kowaremasu — "Cheap umbrellas often break."
Polite こわれます, kowaremasu; an ichidan verb. Its transitive partner is こわす, kowasu.

The word, taken apart.
こわれる (kowareru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and built on the same root as kowasu, "to break"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
しゅうり (shūri) — repair; mending.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to shatter, then the Japanese for to come off.

You'll want to know: しゅうり.
しゅうり — shūri — "repair; mending".
ちちは いすの しゅうりが とくいです。 — chichi wa isu no shūri ga tokui desu — "My father is good at mending chairs."

The word, taken apart.
しゅうり (shūri) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shū, "discipline, master" (Middle Chinese sjuw) and ri, "reason, arrangement" (liX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
