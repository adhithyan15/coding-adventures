# la encuesta — the survey

## You'll want to know: la encuesta
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-01] -->

**la encuesta** — the survey, the poll.

> Hicieron una **encuesta** entre los usuarios.

*Hacer una encuesta* is the verb it goes with — you **make** a survey, not
*do* one. *Según la encuesta* — according to the survey.

## Grammar Lens: asking one person and asking a population
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-02, ES-LEX-PREGUNTAR-03] -->

You already have *preguntar*. An *encuesta* is not more of it — it is a
different act.

| what you do | what you get |
|---|---|
| **preguntar** | an answer from somebody |
| **una encuesta** | a picture of what a group thinks |

*Preguntar* wants one reply and stops. An *encuesta* is built to be counted,
which is why Spanish treats it as a thing you **make** rather than something
you say. That is also why a notice will report *lo que dice la encuesta* — the
survey speaks, not the people in it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-02, ES-ETYMON-QUAERERE] -->

Latin **inquisita**, things sought out, from **inquirere**: **in-** plus
**quaerere**, to seek.

Now look at what you already hold. **Querer** is that same **quaerere**. The
verb that means *to want* in Spanish meant *to seek* in Latin, and *encuesta*
is its other child — so **wanting and asking are one word underneath**, which
is truer of people than either word admits on its own.

English took the same root repeatedly: an **inquest**, an **inquiry**, a
**question**, a **quest**, and **require** — all seekings.
