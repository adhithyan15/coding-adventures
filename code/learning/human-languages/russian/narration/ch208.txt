Russian, chapter 208: To feel, To notice, To hear, To sniff, To touch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
чувствовать (chúvstvovat) — to feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to cough, then the Russian for to sneeze.

You'll want to know: чувствовать.
чувствовать (chúvstvovat) — "to feel".

The word, taken apart.
чувствовать (chúvstvovat) comes from чувство, "a feeling", which Vasmer puts with чуять, "to sense", and чуть, "barely". He relates чуять to Sanskrit kaví-, "a seer", Greek koéō, "I perceive", and Latin caveō, "I beware".

Lesson 2 of 5.
замечать (zamechát) — to notice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to sneeze, then the Russian for to feel.

You'll want to know: замечать.
замечать (zamechát) — "to notice".

The word, taken apart.
замечать (zamechát) is за- with -мечать, from метить, "to mark": to notice is to mark a thing in the mind. Vasmer reports a comparison of метить with Latin mētior, "I measure".

Lesson 5 of 5.
трогать (trógat) — to touch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to hear, then the Russian for to sniff.

You'll want to know: трогать.
трогать (trógat) — "to touch".

The word, taken apart.
трогать (trógat) — Ukrainian трогати means "to pull". It has been compared with Latvian treksne, "a push", and Old Norse þreka, "to press"; Goryaev proposed a link with Latin trahō, "I drag", and one with Latin tergeō, "I wipe", Vasmer finds doubtful, so the origin is open.
