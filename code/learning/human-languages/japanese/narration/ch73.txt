Lesson 1 of 5.
ひのいり (hinoiri) — sunset.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the evening meal, then the Japanese for the morning sun.

You'll want to know: ひのいり.
ひのいり — hinoiri — "sunset".

The word, taken apart.
ひのいり (hinoiri) is native: hi, "sun", the particle no, "of", and iri, "going in", from iru, "to enter": "the going-in of the sun" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2). It is inherited rather than borrowed.

Lesson 2 of 5.
こんや (konya) — tonight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the morning sun, then the Japanese for sunset.

You'll want to know: こんや.
こんや — konya — "tonight".

The word, taken apart.
こんや (konya) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kon, "now, this" (Middle Chinese kim) and ya, "night" (yaeH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

The word, taken apart.
あさって (asatte) is a native word, inherited rather than borrowed. JMdict writes it with characters for "bright", "after" and "day", read as a whole for their meaning. Its older origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
まいあさ (maiasa) — every morning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the day after tomorrow, then the Japanese for the day before yesterday.

You'll want to know: まいあさ.
まいあさ — maiasa — "every morning".

The word, taken apart.
まいあさ (maiasa) mixes the two layers: mai, "every", the on'yomi of its first character, borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese mwojX), and native asa, "morning", read as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
