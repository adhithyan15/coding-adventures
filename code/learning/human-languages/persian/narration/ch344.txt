Persian, chapter 344: Vows and sweets.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نذر (nazr) — a vow (to God).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to cheer, then the Persian for a medal.

You'll want to know: نذر.
نذر (nazr) — "a vow (to God)".
او نذر کرد که اگر حالش خوب شود به فقیرها کمک کند. U nazr kard ke agar hâlash khub shavad be faqir-hâ komak konad. She vowed that if she got well she would help the poor.
Written with ذ. Nazr kardan "to make a vow"; it is kept by cooking or giving.

The word, taken apart.
nazr is borrowed from Arabic nadhr, "a vow" (EtymDB); Persian says the ذ as z.

Lesson 2 of 5.

نذری (nazri) — "votive food, food given out for a vow".
همسایه ها برای همه نذری پخش کردند. Hamsâye-hâ barâ-ye hame nazri pakhsh kardand. The neighbours handed out votive food to everyone.
nazr + -i: food cooked to keep a vow and shared with neighbours and strangers.

The word, taken apart.
nazri adds the Persian suffix -i to nazr "a vow", which is borrowed from Arabic nadhr (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
حلوا (halvâ) — halva.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for a vow, then the Persian for votive food.

You'll want to know: حلوا.
حلوا (halvâ) — "halva".
در مراسم ختم حلوا پخش کردند. Dar marâsem-e khatm halvâ pakhsh kardand. At the memorial service they handed out halva.
Persian halva is flour, butter, sugar and saffron, served at mourning ceremonies.

The word, taken apart.
halvâ is borrowed from Arabic ḥalwā (EtymDB), which EtymDB derives from ḥulw "sweet".

Lesson 4 of 5.
شله زرد (shole zard) — saffron rice pudding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for votive food, then the Persian for halva.

You'll want to know: شله زرد.
شله زرد (shole zard) — "saffron rice pudding".
مادرم برای نذری شله زرد پخت. Mâdaram barâ-ye nazri shole zard pokht. My mother cooked saffron rice pudding as votive food.
Rice, sugar, saffron and rosewater, decorated with cinnamon. shole "a soft rice dish" + zard "yellow".

The word, taken apart.
shole zard joins shole "a soft rice dish" and zard "yellow, golden", a native Persian word that EtymDB lists without a parent.
