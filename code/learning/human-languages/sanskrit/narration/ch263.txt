Sanskrit, chapter 263: I will go tomorrow.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi) — I will go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Close the last chapter with one quick choice: several people including the speaker are the object, so say अस्मान् (asmān). Now say गमिष्यति (gamiṣyati), “will go.” That known form is the bridge to one new speaker form.

You'll want to know: गमिष्यामि.
गमिष्यामि — gamiṣyāmi — means “I will go.” Hear the long -āmi at the end. For now, learn the complete form as one usable promise.

Taken Apart.
गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi) comes from the root गम् (gam), “go,” with the ordinary future formation -इष्य- (-iṣya-), and ends in first-person -आमि (-āmi). Whitney describes this Sanskrit s-future and its -iṣya- / -sya- formation in Sanskrit Grammar §§932–935. This lesson makes no claim beyond that transparent grammatical history.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: “will go” — gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “I will go” — gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: hear gamiṣyāmi and answer “I will go”]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Without looking back, say “I will go.” (गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi).)


Lesson 2 of 4.
The speaker inside the future — hear the first-person singular future ending in a taught form.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Contrast गमिष्यति, “will go,” with गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi), “I will go.”

Grammar Lens: first-person singular.
In the complete form गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi), the ending -आमि (-āmi) marks the speaker: “I.” The future portion comes before it. This is a first-person singular ramp. Learn this whole form before extending the pattern.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: another person “will go” — gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the speaker says “I will go” — gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: name the ending that carries “I” — -āmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which complete taught form means “I will go,” and which ending marks the speaker? (गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi); -आमि.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
Who will go? — choose will go or I will go from the speaker cue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Listen for the ending before you translate.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: “he will go” — saḥ gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “I will go” — ahaṃ gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: teacher points away — gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: teacher points to you as speaker — gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the pair in order: “will go; I will go.” (Gamiṣyati; gamiṣyāmi.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Tomorrow, I will go — say independently that I will go tomorrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Recall the one word for “tomorrow.” (श्वः, śvaḥ.)

Guided Practice.
Put the known time word first, then choose the speaker form.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: “Tomorrow, I will go” — śvaḥ gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “Tomorrow, he will go” — śvaḥ saḥ gamiṣyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: change “he will go” to “I will go” — gamiṣyāmi]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
You have made a decision for tomorrow. Say only: “Tomorrow, I will go.” (श्वः गमिष्यामि (gamiṣyāmi)।).
