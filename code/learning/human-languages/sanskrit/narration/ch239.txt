Sanskrit, chapter 239: Hope, Love, Affection, Compassion, Pity.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आशा (āśā) — hope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a policeman, then the Sanskrit for a driver.

You'll want to know: आशा.
आशा — āśā — "hope".

The word, taken apart.
āśā is built on ā- and the root √śaṃs, "to recite, praise; to wish" (MW; CDIAL 1456). It lives on as Hindi ās, "hope".

Lesson 2 of 5.
प्रेम (prema) — love.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a driver, then the Sanskrit for hope.

You'll want to know: प्रेम.
प्रेम — prema — "love".

The word, taken apart.
prema is the nominative of preman, "love", built on the root √prī, "to please, love" (MW; CDIAL 8999). The root is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares English friend. Priya, "dear", comes from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
स्नेहः (snehaḥ) — affection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for hope, then the Sanskrit for love.

You'll want to know: स्नेहः.
स्नेहः — snehaḥ — "affection".

The word, taken apart.
sneha is built on the root √snih, "to be sticky, oily" (MW), which you met in snihyati. It first meant "grease, oiliness" and then "love" (CDIAL 13802): affection is what sticks. Sindhi nehu, "affection", comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
दया (dayā) — compassion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for love, then the Sanskrit for affection.

You'll want to know: दया.
दया — dayā — "compassion".

The word, taken apart.
dayā is built on the root √day, which MW glosses "to divide, impart" and "to have pity on" (CDIAL 6185). It is the base of dayālu, "kind", which you have already met.

Lesson 5 of 5.
करुणा (karuṇā) — pity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for affection, then the Sanskrit for compassion.

You'll want to know: करुणा.
करुणा — karuṇā — "pity".

The word, taken apart.
karuṇā is the noun of karuṇa, "mournful, compassionate" (CDIAL 2811). Its root is uncertain: MW suggests √kṝ, and CDIAL 2809 suggests √kṛ with a question mark. It is one of the great virtues of Buddhism, compassion.
