Latin, chapter 305: Scelus et fraus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
scelus, sceleris — a crime, a wicked deed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to catch, then the Latin for to seize.

You'll want to know: scelus, sceleris.
scelus, sceleris — "a crime, a wicked deed".
Omnēs cīvēs dē hōc scelere loquēbantur. All the citizens were talking about this crime.
Neuter, 3rd declension. The strongest word for a wrong: a crime that also offends the gods.

The word, taken apart.
scelus is an inherited word whose origin is not settled; Lewis & Short compare a Sanskrit root meaning "to stumble".

Lesson 2 of 5.
facinus, facinoris — a deed; a bad deed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to seize, then the Latin for a crime.

You'll want to know: facinus, facinoris.
facinus, facinoris — "a deed; a bad deed".
Facinus audāx erat, sed nōn turpe. It was a daring deed, but not a shameful one.
Neuter, 3rd declension, from faciō. Alone it often means a crime; praised, a fine deed.

The word, taken apart.
facinus is built on faciō, "to do, to make" (EtymDB): a thing done, and very often a wicked thing done.

Lesson 3 of 5.
scelerātus, scelerāta, scelerātum — wicked, criminal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a crime, then the Latin for a deed.

You'll want to know: scelerātus, scelerāta, scelerātum.
scelerātus, scelerāta, scelerātum — "wicked, criminal".
Ille vir scelerātus patrem suum fefellit. That wicked man deceived his own father.
The past participle of scelerō, 'to stain with crime', used as an adjective.

The word, taken apart.
scelerātus is the participle of scelerō, "to pollute with crime", built on scelus, "crime" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
fraus, fraudis — fraud, deceit; harm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a deed, then the Latin for wicked.

You'll want to know: fraus, fraudis.
fraus, fraudis — "fraud, deceit; harm".
Mercātor fraude multam pecūniam accēpit. The merchant got a lot of money by fraud.
Feminine, 3rd declension. In old law also 'harm': sine fraude, without loss. English fraud.

The word, taken apart.
fraus is an inherited word whose earlier origin is not settled (Lewis & Short, EtymDB). English fraud comes from it through French.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dolus, dolī — a trick, cunning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for wicked, then the Latin for fraud.

You'll want to know: dolus, dolī.
dolus, dolī — "a trick, cunning".
Hostēs dolum parāvērunt, sed dux eum animadvertit. The enemy prepared a trick, but the leader noticed it.
Masculine, 2nd declension. The wooden horse was the most famous dolus of the old stories.

The word, taken apart.
dolus matches Greek dolos, "bait, trick"; EtymDB lists it as taken from Greek, while Lewis & Short treat the two as cousins.
