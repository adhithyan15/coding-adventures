Sanskrit, chapter 469: The Senses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
इन्द्रियम् (indriyam) — a sense; an organ of sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for reminds, then the Sanskrit for grateful.

You'll want to know: इन्द्रियम्.
इन्द्रियम् — indriyam — "a sense; an organ of sense".
नरस्य पञ्च इन्द्रियाणि सन्ति। narasya pañca indriyāṇi santi. A person has five senses.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
इन्द्रियम् (indriyam) is built on Indra, the god's name: MW's first sense is "belonging to Indra", then "bodily power" and "a sense, an organ of sense" (MW).

वृद्धस्य दृष्टिः दुर्बला अस्ति। vṛddhasya dṛṣṭiḥ durbalā asti. The old man's eyesight is weak.

दृष्टिः (dṛṣṭiḥ) comes from the root √dṛś, "to see", the root of दर्शयति and दृष्टवान्; MW gives "seeing" and "sight, the faculty of seeing" (MW).

You'll want to know: जिघ्रति.
जिघ्रति — jighrati — "smells".
बालिका पुष्पं जिघ्रति। bālikā puṣpaṃ jighrati. The girl smells the flower.
Past (she …): सा पुष्पं घ्रातवती, sā puṣpaṃ ghrātavatī.

The word, taken apart.
जिघ्रति (jighrati) comes from the root √ghrā, "to smell", whose present stem doubles the root (ji-ghr-a) (MW).
