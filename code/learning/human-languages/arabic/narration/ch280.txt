Lesson 1 of 5.
عميل (ʿamīl) — a client.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a tape, then the Arabic for glue.

You'll want to know: عميل.
عميل — ʿamīl — "a client".

The word, taken apart.
عميل (ʿamīl) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-m-l, "to work, to do" (Lane). ʿAmīl is "one who acts for another", an agent, a client.

The word, taken apart.
مهندس (muhandis) is built on handasa, "geometry". Lane's sources derive it from hindāz, arabicized from Persian andāz, "measuring".

The word, taken apart.
محامي (muḥāmī) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-m-y, "to protect, to defend" (Lane). Muḥāmī is the Form III participle, "one who defends"; the lawyer is a modern sense.

Lesson 5 of 5.
صيدلي (ṣaydalī) — a pharmacist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a lawyer, then the Arabic for a pilot.

You'll want to know: صيدلي.
صيدلي — ṣaydalī — "a pharmacist".

The word, taken apart.
صيدلي (ṣaydalī) is the druggist, from ṣaydalānī. Lane's sources derive that word from a place called Ṣaydalān, or from ṣaydal, or call it a Persian word arabicized. Lane also records the by-form ṣandalānī, which points to ṣandal, "sandalwood"; the origin is not settled.
