# يكتب (yaktubu) — the present, for everyone

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C37-PRESENT-I-01, AR-LEX-C282-AMAL282-04, AR-LEX-C282-AMAL282-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I write". (*Aktub.*) Then say the Arabic for a sentence and a
barber. (*Jumla*, *ḥallāq*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Chapter thirty-seven gave you the present for one person only:
**أ** at the front means *I*. A story needs *he*, *she* and *we* as well, and
they come from the same front door.

## Grammar Lens: one stem, four prefixes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PRESENT-PERSONS-01]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C31-KATABA-01] -->

Keep the stem *-ktub-* and change the letter in front of it:

> **أكتب** *aktubu*, I write · **تكتب** *taktubu*, you write · **يكتب**
> *yaktubu*, he writes · **تكتب** *taktubu*, she writes · **نكتب** *naktubu*,
> we write

*Ta-* does two jobs: "you" said to a man, and "she". The sentence around it
tells them apart. Said to a woman, "you write" adds *-īna* at the back,
*taktubīna*, and "they write" adds *-ūna*, *yaktubūna*.

The *-u* at the end is the present's own vowel. At the end of a sentence it
is usually dropped, which is why chapter thirty-seven wrote *aktub*; inside a
sentence you will hear it.

## Grammar Lens: the same door on other verbs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PRESENT-PERSONS-01, AR-CONCEPT-C28-DHAHABA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C31-QARAA-01, AR-LEX-C117-THINGS117-04, AR-LEX-C73-SCHOOL73-05] -->

> **هي تكتب رسالة.** — *hiya taktubu risāla* — She is writing a letter.

> **نحن نذهب إلى المدرسة.** — *naḥnu nadhhabu ilā al-madrasa* — We go to
> school.

*Dhahaba* gives *nadhhabu*; *qaraʾa* gives *yaqraʾu*, he reads. One present
covers "I write" and "I am writing"; English splits what Arabic keeps as one.
*Huwa* is "he", *hiya* "she", *naḥnu* "we", and they are often left out,
because the prefix already says who.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C283-PRESENT-PERSONS-01, AR-GRAMMAR-C37-PRESENT-I-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter at the front means *we*? (**ن**, *na-*.) And *he*?
(**ي**, *ya-*.) Why can **تكتب** mean two people? (*Ta-* is "you" and
"she": **the sentence decides**.) Where is the *-u* dropped? (**At the end of
a sentence.**)
