# ऊ — the same letter, holding the note longer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-127] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: **उ** — what sound does it carry, and when does a
word use it instead of the hook?

## Script you'll notice: ऊ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-128]; assesses=[] -->

**ऊ** — *ū*, the long one.

The sound is the same vowel held for longer, and the letter says so: it is
**उ with one loop added on the right**.

What it is made of:

- the continuous body of **उ**
- a separate right-hand loop
- the top shirorekhā

## Script: the pair, side by side
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-128, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-127] -->

| letter | sound | body |
|---|---|---|
| उ | u | bowl and loop |
| ऊ | ū | the same, plus a right loop |

Put them next to each other and the extra loop is the only difference. That is
not a coincidence of shape — **the script pairs short and long vowels by
building the long one out of the short one**, and you will see the same trick
again.

This letter is worth having before you can use it. The Hindi for *I will come*
begins with a long *ū* sitting where no consonant can carry it, so the whole
of the future tense waits on this one loop.

## Writing: ऊ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-128] -->

- **1.** curve down and left around the upper bowl, then sweep back through the waist and around the lower loop without lifting
- **2.** lift at the waist and sweep the right-hand loop up, around, and down-left
- **3.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 2.** The pen comes up 2 times and no more.

> Verified three-stroke teaching form; everyday handwriting may join or simplify
> the loops differently.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari ऊ stroke order.svg’, panels 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
