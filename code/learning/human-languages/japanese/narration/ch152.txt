Japanese, chapter 152: Hurrying to Get Out of the House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
したく (shitaku) — preparations; getting things ready.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sleeve, then the Japanese for to suit.

You'll want to know: したく.
したく — shitaku — "preparations; getting things ready".
あさの したくは かんたんです。 — asa no shitaku wa kantan desu — "Getting ready in the morning is simple."

The word, taken apart.
したく (shitaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shi, "branch, support" (Middle Chinese tsye) and taku, "degrees, consider" (dak) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
いそぐ (isogu) — to hurry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to suit, then the Japanese for preparations.

You'll want to know: いそぐ.
いそぐ — isogu — "to hurry".
あさは いつも いそぎます。 — asa wa itsumo isogimasu — "In the morning I am always in a hurry."
Polite いそぎます, isogimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
いそぐ (isogu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
でかける (dekakeru) — to go out; to set off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for preparations, then the Japanese for to hurry.

You'll want to know: でかける.
でかける — dekakeru — "to go out; to set off".
ちちは まいあさ でかけます。 — chichi wa maiasa dekakemasu — "My father goes out every morning."
Polite でかけます, dekakemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
でかける (dekakeru) is native Japanese, built from de, "going out", the stem of deru, and kakeru, "to set about", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
