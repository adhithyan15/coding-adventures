Script.
০ (digit zero).
That is the Bengali digit zero.
Put it beside the zero you already read: 0. A ring, closed, standing on the line, meaning none. Bengali writes it the same way, and means the same thing.
That is not a coincidence and it is worth knowing before the other nine arrive. Wikipedia describes the Bengali numerals as a variety of the Hindu–Arabic numeral system — the same system as the digits on your keyboard, not a rival one. They are positional, they are read left to right, and ০ (digit zero) does the same job at the end of a number as 0 does at the end of 10.
There is no Bengali WORD for zero in this book yet. Today it is a shape you can read.

Script.
২ (digit two).
That is the Bengali digit two, and you can say it: দুই.
Set it beside 2. The top curls the other way and the foot is a diagonal rather than a bar, but the gross shape is the same — a turn at the top, a stroke running down to the right. Nobody would call ২ (digit two) a two from nothing; anybody who already reads 2 can be told once and keep it.
So the count of shapes that cost you almost nothing now stands at two, and both of them mean exactly what the shape you already know means. Remember that. It stops being true in this chapter, and the place where it stops is the whole point of the chapter.

Now put it beside a letter you have had since the opening chapters:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
the digit three. shape: ৩.
the letter o. shape: ও.
They are the same rounded body. ও carries a stroke down its right-hand side; ৩ (digit three) does not, and that is the entire difference between a number and a letter here.
This is the only digit in the chapter that collides with a Bengali letter you have been taught, and the fix is one stroke wide: if it has the tail on the right, it is a letter.

And here your count runs out. This book has taught you এক to পাঁচ and no further, so you can now read a number you have no Bengali word for. Reading is still worth having on its own. A page number, a price and a date are all read far more often than they are said aloud.

It is two loops, stacked. It is the shape of an 8.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
shape: two stacked loops. in the digits you already read: eight. in Bengali: four.
shape: upright with one bowl. in the digits you already read: nothing. in Bengali: eight.
Read the top row again. Every reflex you have says eight, and it is wrong by four every single time — on a price, on a bus number, on a year.
The first three digits of this chapter arrived meaning exactly what their shape already meant to you. This one arrives meaning something else, and it is not a near miss: ৪ (digit four) and 8 are the same drawing.
The defence is the lesson before this one. If you are unsure, ask how many bowls: two bowls is four; one bowl is eight.

[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]

Script.
Ten shapes are in your hand. Here they are in order:
০ (digit zero) ১ (digit one) ২ (digit two) ৩ (digit three) ৪ (digit four) ৫ (digit five) ৬ (digit six) ৭ (digit seven) ৮ (digit eight) ৯ (digit nine) (the ten digits).
A rule with an edge at each end, and both edges matter:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
the band: where the shapes AGREE. what it means for you: ০ (digit zero) is a 0 and means zero. ২ (digit two) is close to a 2 and means two. Trust these.
the band: where they are simply NEW. what it means for you: ১ (digit one) ৩ (digit three) ৫ (digit five) ৬ (digit six) ৮ (digit eight) ৯ (digit nine) carry no Western digit's shape at all, so there is nothing to be misled by. ৩ (digit three) is the one to watch against a letter, not against a digit: ও is the same body with a stroke down its right side.
the band: where they LIE. what it means for you: ৪ (digit four) is drawn as an 8 and means four. ৭ (digit seven) is drawn as a 9 and means seven. Nowhere else.
Two of ten. Say them as a pair and they stay a pair: the eight is a four, the nine is a seven — and in both cases the Bengali digit that really means eight or nine is a shape you would never mistake for anything.
That is the whole rule. Eight shapes ask you to learn them; two ask you to un-learn something first.

What you've built this chapter.
Sixty script lessons taught you letters, and one of them, long ago, taught you to read তিন. That was the word. Until this chapter you could not read ৩ (digit three), and so you could not read a price, a date, a clock face or a page number — the things a reader meets far more often than they meet a sentence.
Ten shapes closed that. Eight of them were simply learned; ০ (digit zero) and ২ (digit two) cost almost nothing because the Bengali digits and the digits on your keyboard are one system rather than two; and two of them — ৪ (digit four) and ৭ (digit seven) — had to be taken away from a reflex before they could be given to you.
What this chapter did not do is teach you to SAY six, seven, eight, nine or zero. Those words are not in this book yet. You can read every number on the page and speak the first five of them, and the gap between those two abilities is now the most useful thing left undone in this column.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read ৪৭. (Forty-seven — not ninety-eight.) Which numbers can you now read? (All of them.) Which can you say? (One to five.)
