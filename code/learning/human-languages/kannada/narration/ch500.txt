Kannada, chapter 500: What Someone Is Like.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಸ್ವಭಾವ (svabhāva) — temperament; nature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for envy, then the Kannada for self-confidence.

You'll want to know: ಸ್ವಭಾವ.
ಸ್ವಭಾವ — svabhāva — "temperament; nature".
ನನ್ನ ತಮ್ಮನ ಸ್ವಭಾವ ತುಂಬಾ ಶಾಂತ. nanna tammana svabhāva tumbā śānta. My younger brother's nature is very calm.
A person's own way of being: ಸ್ವ, own, joined to the Sanskrit word for being.

The word, taken apart.
ಸ್ವಭಾವ (svabhāva) is borrowed from Sanskrit svabhāva, "one's own way of being, natural disposition" (Monier-Williams): sva, "own", and bhāva, "being".

What you've built.

ಶಾಲೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಅವನ ನಡತೆ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ. śāleyalli avana naḍate cennāgide. His conduct at school is good.
Built on ನಡೆ, to walk: the way you walk through life.

The word, taken apart.
ನಡತೆ (naḍate) is native Dravidian: DEDR 3582 lists Kannada naḍe, "to walk, go, happen", and naḍate, "conduct, behaviour".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ವರ್ತನೆ (vartane) — attitude; way of behaving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for personality, then the Kannada for conduct.

You'll want to know: ವರ್ತನೆ.
ವರ್ತನೆ — vartane — "attitude; way of behaving".
ಅತಿಥಿಗಳ ಮುಂದೆ ಮಗುವಿನ ವರ್ತನೆ ವಿಚಿತ್ರವಾಗಿತ್ತು. atithigaḷa munde maguvina vartane vicitravāgittu. The child's behaviour in front of the guests was strange.
The verb is ವರ್ತಿಸು, to behave.

The word, taken apart.
ವರ್ತನೆ (vartane) is borrowed from Sanskrit vartana, "turning, rolling" (CDIAL 11354), from the root vṛt, "to turn, to be"; in Kannada it is the way a person carries on.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ಅನುಕರಿಸು.
ಅನುಕರಿಸು — anukarisu — "to imitate".
ಮಕ್ಕಳು ದೊಡ್ಡವರನ್ನು ಅನುಕರಿಸುತ್ತಾರೆ. makkaḷu doḍḍavarannu anukarisuttāre. Children imitate grown-ups.
Past (I …): ಅನುಕರಿಸಿದೆ, anukariside.
The one imitated takes -ಅನ್ನು. ಅನುಕರಣೆ is imitation.

The word, taken apart.
ಅನುಕರಿಸು (anukarisu) is built on Sanskrit anukaraṇa, "the act of imitation" (Monier-Williams): anu, "after", and kṛ, "to do", with the verb ending -isu.
