German, chapter 303: Die Beerdigung.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Tod — the death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the anniversary, then the German for the farewell.

You'll want to know: der Tod.
der Tod — "the death".
Nach dem Tod seiner Frau lebte er allein. After the death of his wife he lived alone.
The plural is rare. The adjective is tot, already known.

The word, taken apart.
Tod is inherited from Old High German tōd, from Proto-Germanic dauþuz "death" (EtymDB), the same source as English death.

Lesson 2 of 5.
beerdigen — to bury.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: beerdigen.
beerdigen — "to bury".
Sie wurde in ihrem Heimatdorf beerdigt. She was buried in her home village.
Built on die Erde. Begraben means the same.

The word, taken apart.
beerdigen is built on Erde "earth", an inherited Germanic word with English earth and Gothic airþa among its cousins (EtymDB): to put into the earth.

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Friedhof — the cemetery, the graveyard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: der Friedhof.
der Friedhof — "the cemetery, the graveyard".
Der Friedhof liegt hinter der Kirche. The cemetery lies behind the church.
Plural: die Friedhöfe.

The word, taken apart.
Friedhof is inherited from Old High German frīthof "an enclosed yard" (EtymDB): the first part is usually traced to an old word for an enclosure, later heard as Frieden "peace".
