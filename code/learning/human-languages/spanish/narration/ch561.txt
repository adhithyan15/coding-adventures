Spanish, chapter 561: La partida.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
marcharse — to leave, to go away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the get-together, then the Spanish for the reunion.

You'll want to know: marcharse.
marcharse — "to leave, to go away".
Se marchó sin decir adiós. He left without saying goodbye.
Common in Spain; irse is used everywhere.

The word, taken apart.
marcharse is built on marchar, which was borrowed from French marcher 'to walk, to march' (EtymDB). The French verb is usually traced to a Frankish word for marking out, pressing with the feet.

You'll want to know: zarpar.
zarpar — "to set sail, to leave port".
El barco zarpó a las ocho. The ship set sail at eight.
Only for ships and boats.

The word, taken apart.
zarpar comes from old Italian sarpare 'to weigh anchor' (EtymDB). Where the Italian word came from is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la partida — the departure; the game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to leave, then the Spanish for to set sail.

You'll want to know: la partida.
la partida — "the departure; the game".
La partida del tren se retrasó. The train's departure was delayed.
Una partida de cartas is a game of cards. Un partido is a match.

The word, taken apart.
partida is built on the participle of partir, which is inherited from Latin partīre 'to divide' (EtymDB).

regreso is a learned borrowing from Latin regressus 'a going back' (Lewis & Short), from regredī 'to step back'.
