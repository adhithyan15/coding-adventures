# producir — to produce

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01, ES-LEX-C310-ASK-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **reducir** and **la ducha**. The verb coming next is their
close relative, and it will not make you learn anything twice.

## You'll want to know: producir
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01] -->

**producir** — to produce, to make.

> El huerto **produce** unos cien kilos al mes.

*Producir comida* — to produce food. *La producción* is the noun, and **el
producto** is the thing that comes out.

## Grammar Lens: nothing new here, which is the point
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01, ES-LEX-C462-JEFE-01] -->

*Reducir* taught you this whole verb, five chapters ago. *Producir* is the
same class and takes the same two irregularities:

| where | reducir | producir |
|---|---|---|
| the **z** before *c* | redu**zc**o | produ**zc**o |
| the **j** past | redu**j**e | produ**j**e |

So the subjunctive is *produzca* — the form on a notice, as *reduzca* was on
the road sign — and the past is *produjo*, never *producí*.

That is worth stopping on. Learning *reducir* bought you the whole *-ducir*
corner of that class — *producir*, and *traducir* and *introducir* when you
meet them — the verbs that take the **j** past as well as the **z**.

Not the whole class, though. *Reducir*'s own lesson warned you: *conocer*
shares the **-zc-** and not the past, going *conocí*. Two groups, one
spelling, and the *-ducir* verbs are the half that also changes its past.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C467-ALIMENTO-01, ES-LEX-C310-ASK-02] -->

**pro-**, forward, plus **ducere**, to lead — the *ducere* already under
*reducir* and under *la ducha*.

To produce is to **lead something forward**, out where it can be seen. A
farmer leads a crop forward out of the ground; a factory leads goods forward
out of a door. English keeps the picture in **produce** as a noun, the stuff
itself, and in a theatre **producer**, who leads the show out in front of an
audience.

Set them side by side and the prefix does all the work: *reducir* leads back,
*producir* leads forward, and *conducir* — which *reducir*'s table already
showed you — leads along.
