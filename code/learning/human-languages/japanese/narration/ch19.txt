Japanese, chapter 19: Reading — Words, Greetings, and the Cues You Have Only Heard.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) (te, mimi, kuchi, hana, kao, me) (wakarimashita, wakarimasen, mō ichido, onegaishimasu) (hai, iie, konnichiwa, arigatō, sayōnara, sumimasen) — six words, nothing said first.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You have written every sign in every one of these. Not one word is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them before you read them.

Reading.
て みみ くち はな かお め.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once. Do not sound out each sign — look at the whole word and let it arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again, and notice which came at once and which you had to build. Both are fine. The difference is what practice removes.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two of these are one sign long. て and め are whole words, and you have written both as signs on their own.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is worth noticing, because it is the thing hiragana does that an alphabet does not: a sign can be a letter and a word at the same time, and nothing on the page tells you which it is. Only knowing the word does.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six is enough for one sitting.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which of the six is one sign long and means eye?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What was new? (Nobody said them first. You read them.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) (te, mimi, kuchi, hana, kao, me) (wakarimashita, wakarimasen, mō ichido, onegaishimasu) (hai, iie, konnichiwa, arigatō, sayōnara, sumimasen) — the doorway words, on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you six words. These are the six the book opened with.
[pause 1 second]
Every one of them reached your ear before it reached a page. Today that order is reversed.

Reading.
はい いいえ こんにちは ありがとう さようなら すみません.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice that the two longest are the two you say most often. Length is not difficulty here; familiarity is.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
こんにちは ends in は, and you know that sign says ha. In this word it is read wa, because the greeting keeps a spelling from an older sentence that nobody finishes any more.
[pause 2 seconds]
You do not need the history to read it. You need to know that this one word is spelled the way it is for a reason, and that reading a language means meeting a handful of such words rather than a rule you can apply.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and one of them is a small piece of history.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
Which line thanks somebody?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which sign is read differently in one of them? (は, in こんにちは.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) (te, mimi, kuchi, hana, kao, me) (wakarimashita, wakarimasen, mō ichido, onegaishimasu) (hai, iie, konnichiwa, arigatō, sayōnara, sumimasen) — the cues you have only ever heard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six greetings. These are the longest lines the book has set on one page.
[pause 1 second]
Every one of them is a cue you already use. None is new.

Reading.
ここ すこし わかりました わかりません もういちど、おねがいします もうすこし、ゆっくりいってください.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping. The list gets longer as it goes; let it.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and notice that the last two are the only ones with a comma in them, and that the comma falls exactly where you pause when you say it.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
わかりました and わかりません differ in three signs at the end and in nothing else. Understood, and not understood.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is the pair worth being able to read rather than only hear. A cue you can only recognise when somebody says it to you is a cue you can only use when they do. Read once off a page, it becomes something you can reach for.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six lines, and the last one is fourteen signs long — the longest stretch of Japanese this book has asked you to take in at once.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Which line says you did NOT understand?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which two differ only at the end? (わかりました and わかりません.)
