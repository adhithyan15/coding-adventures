Portuguese, chapter 219: War, Peace, Army, Soldier, Tradition.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a guerra — war.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for a platform, a quay, then the Portuguese for a passport.

You'll want to know: a guerra.
a guerra — "war".

The word, taken apart.
guerra from Frankish werra, "strife, confusion" (EtymDB 2.1) — borrowed. It came into Late Latin as werra.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for peace, then the Portuguese for an army.

You'll want to know: o soldado.

The word, taken apart.
soldado is "one paid": built on an old word for pay, from Late Latin solidus, a gold coin. Italian soldato is the same word.

tradição from Latin traditio, traditionis, "a handing over" (EtymDB 2.1) — a learned borrowing from Latin. The inherited form of the same Latin word, traição, means "betrayal".
