# घ — one character, met first in घटते

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-C276-GHATATE-01, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-218, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-212] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one: say **घटते** (*ghaṭate*), "happens". Then say the sounds of **ग** and **ध**. (*ga*, *dha*.)

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time: the one that opens **घटते**.

## Script you'll notice: घ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-226]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-218, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-212] -->

**घ** — *gha*.

It is a **consonant**: *g* with a breath after it, as **ध** is *d* with a breath.

What it is made of:

- a continuous upper curl, middle hook, lower bowl, and rising right side
- a short descending lower stem
- the top shirorekhā

You have met it here:

- **घटते** *ghaṭate* — happens

## Writing: घ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-226] -->

- **1.** start below the headline at the upper-left, curve down and clockwise around the upper curl, turn back left through the middle hook, continue clockwise around the lower bowl, and rise along the right side to the headline
- **2.** without lifting, come back down the right side to the lower junction
- **3.** descend the short stem below the bowl
- **4.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 1.** The pen comes up 1 time and no more.

> The source shows three movements; native writers draw घ as two pen-down strokes (83% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 68 of 82). This ductus follows the three movements in order and direction, lifting only before the headline: after rising to the headline the pen comes back down the right side into the short lower stem.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-घ-order.gif’, strokes 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 9 May 2009.
