## HL-C356 — Russian's one number was a joining word in disguise, and a word for a hundred is what named half the language family

**HL-C350's fourth zero-numeral track, closed.** Russian's row said it held one
numeral, and the note underneath was the finding: **один** was taught as half of
the **один … другой** pattern — *one … the other* — which is a **joining word
wearing a numeral's clothes**. The track had learned to ask **сколько?** one
chapter earlier and could understand no answer to it. Chapters 23–26 add 19
lessons and close `RU-A1-Q-01`, `RU-A1-Q-02` and `RU-A1-L-09`; coverage moves
104/228 (46%) to 107/228 (47%).

**A CORPUS-SCAN LESSON THAT GENERALISES: a word can be present, correct, and not
taught.** The probe behind HL-C350 counted *odin* as taught because it is, and
the inventory's note is what disambiguated it. Marwadi had the same shape from
the other side — seventeen matches for **दो** that were all the imperative verb
"give" — and Urdu had it too, its two matches for **دو** sitting inside
**دوست**, "friend". **Three of the four zero-numeral tracks had a numeral-shaped
string in the corpus and no numeral in the curriculum.** A corpus grep answers
"is this string present"; only the inventory answers "is this taught".

**IT COST NO NEW CYRILLIC LETTER**, the fourth track in this campaign where the
census said the script was not the blocker. Marwadi needed one sign for twelve
numbers; Urdu and Russian needed none.

**THE BEST ETYMOLOGY IN THE CAMPAIGN, and it is a fact about the field rather
than about a word.** **сто** continues \*ḱm̥tóm. The branches that kept its first
sound as a *k* — Latin **centum**, Greek **hekatón**, Welsh **cant** — are the
**centum** languages; the branches that turned it into an *s* — Slavic, Iranian,
Indo-Aryan, Baltic — are the **satem** languages, after the Avestan word for the
same number. **A word for a hundred is what the two halves of the Indo-European
family are named after.** Teaching Russian's hundred teaches the name of the
division it sits on, and the *s* is the same one that survived in **шесть** and
**семь** two chapters earlier.

**TWO PAIRS SHAPED BY THE NUMBER STANDING NEXT TO THEM, IN TWO FAMILIES.**
**восемь** has neither an *s* nor an *m* in its history and took both from
**семь**; **девять** has no *d* and took it from **десять**. Persian's *hasht*
borrowed its *h*- from *haft* for exactly the same reason, in a different
branch, with no contact between them — and both tranches shipped in this
campaign, so the corpus can now show the parallel rather than assert it.
Counting aloud is what does this to words.

**WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT CLAIMED, and why saying so is the useful part.** A
Russian numeral **governs** the noun after it: два/три/четыре take the genitive
singular and пять upward the genitive plural. This track has taught the genitive
in exactly one place, after **до**. So the tranche teaches the numbers, tells the
reader in as many words that counting THINGS is a genitive chapter rather than a
numeral one, and records that telling as `RU-GRAMMAR-NUMERAL-CASE-DEBT-01` —
**which is a different atom from the rule, on purpose.** `RU-A1-Q-01`'s probe
names it nowhere; `RU-A1-C-05` stays null. A probe that had quietly claimed the
case government would have been the flattering lie the whole HL-C350 file exists
to catch.

The same discipline caught a prose error on the way: a draft said Russian
ordinals use "the machinery this book has already used for adjectives". The
track teaches NO adjective — `Prilagatelnoe` is 0/N and the suite asserts it.
The sentence now says the opposite, which is the true and more useful thing.

**`RU-A1-L-09` IS A DERIVATION THAT PAID OFF.** The point exists because somebody
refused to drop Spanish's superscript letters in abbreviations as
untransferable, and wrote the note "DERIVED, NOT DROPPED … the same demand — a
numeral carrying a written grammatical ending — and Russian answers it with a
hyphen instead of a superscript." **1-й** is *pervyy*; the letters after the
hyphen are the adjective agreement, which is the information an English reader
writing **1st** for everything loses. A point derived rather than dropped
eventually gets taught; a point dropped never does.
