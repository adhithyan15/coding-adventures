# der Mond — "the moon"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-GOTT-PLUS-TAG-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the first weekday, the thing it is named after.

## You'll want to know: der Mond
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-MOND-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-DER-DIE-DAS-02] -->

> **der Mond** — "the moon."

A *der*-word, and the same inherited word as English **moon**. German rounded
the vowel and added a *d* on the end; nothing else happened to it.

The moon was the first Germanic god-slot in the week, which is why it comes
first here.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MOND-01] -->

- `long-o` — a long, round *o*, the vowel of English *moan*, not the *oo* of
  *moon*.
- `d-t-final` — the final *d* hardens to a *t* at the end of the word, the same
  hardening you met in *und*. So it comes out *mohnt*.
