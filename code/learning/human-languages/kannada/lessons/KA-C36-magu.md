# ಮಗು (magu) — "child," before it picks a gender

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C35-SNEHITA-01, KA-ETYMON-C35-SNEHITA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Snēhita* and *geḷeya* were two words for one idea. This word is
the opposite kind of pair-maker: one word that the next two lessons will
split in two.

## You'll want to know: ಮಗು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C36-MAGU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ಮಗು** — *magu* — **baby, child** (no gender specified)

> **ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಮಗು.** — *idu nanna magu.* — "This is my child."

## The word, taken apart: the plain root, before gender
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C36-MAGU-02]; assesses=[] -->

**ಮಗು** goes back to Proto-Dravidian **\*maka**, "child" — a plain root with
no gender marked on it at all. Hold that root in mind: the next lesson adds
one ending to it and gets "son"; the one after adds a different ending and
gets "daughter." Chapter 12 solved the parallel problem — telling siblings
apart — with four completely separate words. This root solves a smaller
problem the opposite way: one shape, several endings.
