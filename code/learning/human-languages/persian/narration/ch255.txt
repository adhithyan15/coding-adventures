Persian, chapter 255: To shout, To brush one's teeth, To weigh, To bite, To chew.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
داد زدن (dâd zadan) — to shout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for to fold, then the Persian for to hang up.

You'll want to know: داد زدن.
داد زدن — dâd zadan — "to shout".

The word, taken apart.
dâd zadan pairs dâd, "cry", with zadan, "to strike". dâd is the old word for "justice" (Middle Persian dād, "law, justice", EtymDB), and a shout is usually explained as first a cry for justice. zadan, "to strike", is an inherited verb; it is usually traced to Middle Persian zadan.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: گاز گرفتن.
گاز گرفتن — gâz gereftan — "to bite".

The word, taken apart.
gâz gereftan is "to take a bite": gâz, "bite", is a native word, and gereftan, "to take", is inherited. gereftan, "to take", is an inherited verb; EtymDB takes it back to the Indo-European root gʰrebh₂-, "to seize".
