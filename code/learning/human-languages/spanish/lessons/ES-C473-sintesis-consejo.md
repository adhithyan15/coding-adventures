# (el consejo a otras bibliotecas) — read it, then answer

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01, ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **aconsejar** and **en buen estado**. The question below uses
the first and the answer uses the second.

## Reading: the last paragraph
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02, ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-03] -->

The library that lends objects, asked what others should do:

> A quien pregunta, le **recomienda** empezar con pocos objetos, aceptar solo
> lo que esté en buen **estado** y explicar muy bien las normas desde el primer
> día.

Three pieces of advice, joined by commas and a final **y**. Spanish lists like
this without repeating the verb, so *recomienda* governs all three
infinitives: *empezar*, *aceptar*, *explicar*.

## Grammar Lens: the question will not use the paragraph's verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01, ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02] -->

This is the thing to carry away. The text says *recomienda*. The question
says:

> ¿Qué **aconseja** a otras bibliotecas?

| the text | the question |
|---|---|
| **recomienda** empezar | ¿qué **aconseja**? |

Nothing has changed except the verb, and an exam does this on purpose: it
checks that you hold the **meaning** rather than the word. If you learned
*recomendar* and not *aconsejar*, the question looks like it is about something
else.

So treat the two as one idea with two surfaces, and read past the swap.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-01, ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-02, ES-LEX-C473-CONSEJO-03] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say all three pieces of advice, keeping one verb and three
infinitives the way the text does.

[PAUSE 3s] Now answer the question as asked — starting *Les aconseja…* — and
notice you are saying the same thing with the other verb.
