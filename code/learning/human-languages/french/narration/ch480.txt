French, chapter 480: Une jambe dans le plâtre.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la fracture — fracture, break (of a bone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for burn, then the French for bump.

You'll want to know: la fracture.
la fracture — "fracture, break (of a bone)".
La radio montre une fracture du poignet. The X-ray shows a broken wrist.
The everyday verb is se casser: il s'est cassé la jambe.

The word, taken apart.
la fracture is a learned borrowing from Latin frāctūra "a break", from frangere "to break" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le plâtre — plaster cast; plaster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for bump, then the French for fracture.

You'll want to know: le plâtre.
le plâtre — "plaster cast; plaster".
Mon fils a eu la jambe dans le plâtre pendant six semaines. My son had his leg in a cast for six weeks.
Être dans le plâtre — to be in a cast. Also building plaster.

The word, taken apart.
le plâtre comes from Old French plastre, which goes back to Latin emplastrum "a plaster for a wound", a borrowing from Greek émplastron.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: la béquille.
la béquille — "crutch".
Elle marche avec des béquilles depuis son accident. She has been walking on crutches since her accident.
Also the kickstand of a bike or scooter.

The word, taken apart.
la béquille is usually traced to bec "beak", for the curved handle; the origin is not fully settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
boiter — to limp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for plaster cast, then the French for crutch.

You'll want to know: boiter.
boiter — "to limp".
Depuis sa chute, mon chien boite un peu. Since his fall, my dog limps a little.
The adjective boiteux means limping, and also shaky: une excuse boiteuse.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of boiter is not settled. A link with boîte, once the socket of a joint, has been proposed.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'os — bone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for crutch, then the French for to limp.

You'll want to know: l'os.
l'os — "bone".
Le chien ronge un os dans le jardin. The dog is gnawing a bone in the garden.
Masculine. Singular un os with a sounded s; plural des os, where the s is usually silent.

The word, taken apart.
l'os is inherited from Latin os, ossis "bone" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).
