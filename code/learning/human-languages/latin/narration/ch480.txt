Latin, chapter 480: Cliff, Jagged rock, Sheer, Steep, To climb.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
rūpēs, rūpis — a cliff, a rock face.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to graft, then the Latin for the grape harvest.

You'll want to know: rūpēs, rūpis.
rūpēs, rūpis — "a cliff, a rock face".
Caprae in rūpibus altīs ambulant. Goats walk on the high cliffs.
Third declension, feminine: rūpēs, rūpis f. A broken rock face, from rumpō 'to break'.

The word, taken apart.
rūpēs is built on rumpō 'to break' (Lewis & Short): rock broken off, a cliff. EtymDB lists it without a parent.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cautēs, cautis — a jagged rock, a crag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the grape harvest, then the Latin for a cliff.

You'll want to know: cautēs, cautis.
cautēs, cautis — "a jagged rock, a crag".
Nāvis in cautēs cadit et frangitur. The ship runs onto the jagged rocks and breaks up.
Third declension, feminine: cautēs, cautis f., mostly plural. Sharp rocks on a coast or a mountain.

The word, taken apart.
cautēs is inherited; Lewis & Short and EtymDB connect it with cōs 'whetstone', from PIE ḱeh₃- 'to sharpen': a sharp rock.

Lesson 3 of 5.
praeruptus, praerupta, praeruptum — sheer, very steep, broken off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cliff, then the Latin for a jagged rock.

You'll want to know: praeruptus, praerupta, praeruptum.
praeruptus, praerupta, praeruptum — "sheer, very steep, broken off".
Via in monte praerupta est, itaque mūlī lentē eunt. The road up the mountain is very steep, so the mules go slowly.
An adjective of three endings, the participle of praerumpō 'to break off in front'. Of rock: sheer.

The word, taken apart.
praeruptus is the past participle of praerumpō 'to break off in front', from prae- and rumpō 'to break' (Lewis & Short): land broken off sharply.

Lesson 4 of 5.
arduus, ardua, arduum — steep, high; difficult.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a jagged rock, then the Latin for sheer.

You'll want to know: arduus, ardua, arduum.
arduus, ardua, arduum — "steep, high; difficult".
Sēmita ardua est, et viātōrēs lassī sunt. The path is steep, and the travellers are weary.
An adjective of three endings. Rēs ardua, a hard task. English arduous comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
arduus is inherited, but its origin is not settled: Lewis & Short link it, doubtfully, with alō 'to nourish' and altus 'high', and EtymDB gives no parent. English arduous comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
scandō, scandere — to climb, to mount.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sheer, then the Latin for steep.

You'll want to know: scandō, scandere.
scandō, scandere — "to climb, to mount".
Amīcī montem scandunt et urbem procul cōnspicantur. The friends climb the mountain and catch sight of the city far off.
scandō, scandere, scandī, scānsum. English scan (to count out the feet of a verse) comes from it, and ascend and descend from its compounds.

The word, taken apart.
scandō is inherited from PIE skend- 'to jump, to climb' (EtymDB). English scan comes from it, and ascend and descend from its compounds ascendō and dēscendō (Lewis & Short).
