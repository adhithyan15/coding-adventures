# के बिना — the opposite, for the price of one word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *with a friend* in Hindi. Now say it again and listen to how
much of it you would have to change to mean the opposite.

## You'll want to know: के बिना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-02]; assesses=[] -->

**चाय के बिना** — without tea.

One word changed. The shape did not move.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-02, HI-LEX-C93-REL-01] -->

| slot 3 | the whole phrase | English |
|---|---|---|
| साथ | दोस्त के साथ | with a friend |
| बिना | चाय के बिना | without tea |

This is what the shape buys. Once slot 3 is a **swappable position**, every new
relation costs exactly one word instead of a new piece of grammar — and this
chapter is going to swap it five more times.

English does not work this way. *With* and *without* look related because one
is built out of the other, but *in front of* and *behind* are unrelated words
that share nothing. In Hindi all of them share **के**, and that is the part
that is doing the grammatical work.

## Grammar Lens: two ways to write it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-02] -->

**बिना** is the one relation word in this chapter that is also allowed to stand
**in front of** its noun — **बिना चाय के** is said too, and means the same
thing.

Learn the slot-3 version. It is the one that matches everything else you are
about to meet, and a reader who knows the ordinary order will still understand
the other one when a paper prints it.
