French, chapter 346: Causes et conséquences.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
causer — to cause.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the opponent, then the French for to argue a case.

You'll want to know: causer.
causer — "to cause".
Le brouillard a causé un accident sur l'autoroute. The fog caused an accident on the motorway.
Another causer, informal, means to chat: causer avec un ami.

The word, taken apart.
causer is built on cause, which comes from Latin causa "cause, reason, lawsuit" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
provoquer — to bring about, to cause; to provoke.

You'll want to know: provoquer.
provoquer — "to bring about, to cause; to provoke".
Ce médicament peut provoquer de la fatigue. This medicine can cause tiredness.
To cause, often something bad; also to provoke a person.

The word, taken apart.
provoquer comes from Latin prōvocāre "to call forth, to challenge" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built on vocāre "to call".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la conséquence — the consequence.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to cause, then the French for to bring about.

You'll want to know: la conséquence.
la conséquence — "the consequence".
Cette décision aura des conséquences pour tout le monde. This decision will have consequences for everyone.
En conséquence — accordingly.

The word, taken apart.
conséquence is a learned borrowing from Latin cōnsequentia (EtymDB), from cōnsequī "to follow after", built on sequī "to follow" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
entraîner — to lead to, to bring about; to train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to bring about, then the French for the consequence.

You'll want to know: entraîner.
entraîner — "to lead to, to bring about; to train".
Le froid entraîne souvent des rhumes. The cold often leads to colds.
In sport, s'entraîner is to train; the coach is l'entraîneur.

The word, taken apart.
entraîner is built from en- and traîner "to drag", which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin tragīnāre, based on Latin trahere "to pull, to draw" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
découler — to follow (from), to result from.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the consequence, then the French for to lead to.

You'll want to know: découler.
découler — "to follow (from), to result from".
Tous nos problèmes découlent de cette erreur. All our problems follow from this mistake.
Découler de — to flow from. Formal.

The word, taken apart.
découler is built from dé- "from" and couler "to flow", which is inherited from Latin cōlāre "to filter, to strain" (Lewis & Short).
