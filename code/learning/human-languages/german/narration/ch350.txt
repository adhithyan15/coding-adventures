German, chapter 350: Arbeitgeber und Arbeitnehmer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Arbeitgeber — the employer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for qualified, then the German for to master.

You'll want to know: der Arbeitgeber.
der Arbeitgeber — "the employer".
Mein Arbeitgeber bezahlt mir das Ticket für den Bus. My employer pays for my bus ticket.
Plural: die Arbeitgeber; a woman is die Arbeitgeberin. Literally the work-giver.

The word, taken apart.
Arbeitgeber joins Arbeit "work", from Old High German arbeit and Proto-Germanic arbaidiz "labour, hardship", to Geber "giver", from geben "to give" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
einstellen — to hire, to take on; to adjust; to stop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the employer, then the German for the employee.

You'll want to know: einstellen.
einstellen — "to hire, to take on; to adjust; to stop".
Das Restaurant stellt zwei neue Köche ein. The restaurant is hiring two new cooks.
Separable: sie stellt ein, sie hat eingestellt. Also to adjust: die Uhr einstellen; and to stop: die Arbeit einstellen.

The word, taken apart.
einstellen is built from ein "in" and stellen "to put, to place", inherited from Old High German stellen and Proto-Germanic stallijaną (EtymDB): you put someone into a job.

Lesson 4 of 5.
beschäftigen — to employ; to occupy (sich beschäftigen mit).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the employee, then the German for to hire.

You'll want to know: beschäftigen.
beschäftigen — "to employ; to occupy (sich beschäftigen mit)".
Die kleine Bäckerei beschäftigt zehn Leute. The small bakery employs ten people.
Inseparable: sie beschäftigt, sie hat beschäftigt. Sich mit etwas beschäftigen — to deal with something. Beschäftigt — busy — is the same family.

The word, taken apart.
beschäftigen comes from Middle High German scheftec "busy, active" (EtymDB), of the family of schaffen "to make, to create": you give people work to do.

Lesson 5 of 5.
befristet — fixed-term, temporary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to hire, then the German for to employ.

You'll want to know: befristet.
befristet — "fixed-term, temporary".
Mein Vertrag ist auf ein Jahr befristet. My contract is fixed-term, for one year.
The participle of befristen — to set a time limit. The opposite is unbefristet — permanent.

The word, taken apart.
befristet is the participle of befristen, built on Frist "time limit", inherited from Middle High German vrist, Old High German frist and Proto-Germanic fristiz (EtymDB).
