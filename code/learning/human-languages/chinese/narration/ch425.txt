Mandarin Chinese, chapter 425: The Hotel.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhùsù — accommodation; a place to stay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for speed per hour, then the Mandarin for halfway.

You'll want to know: zhùsù.
zhùsù — "accommodation; a place to stay".
zhèr de zhùsù hěn guì — Accommodation is expensive here.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhùsù is built from zhù 'to stay' and sù 'to lodge for the night', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjuH and sjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stop' and the second 'a lodging'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
酒店 (jiǔdiàn) — big hotel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for halfway, then the Mandarin for a place to stay.

You'll want to know: 酒店.
酒店 — jiǔdiàn — "big hotel".
这家酒店很大 zhè jiā jiǔdiàn hěn dà — This hotel is very big.
The new character, in pieces: 酒 (jiǔ) — ten strokes in two pieces: on the left the water piece of 洗, three strokes; on the right a bar on top, a frame, a short stroke falling to the left and a vertical bending right inside it, a short bar, and a bar closing the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
酒店 (jiǔdiàn) is built from 酒 (jiǔ) 'wine' and 店 'a shop, an inn', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsjuwX and temH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as wine and sweet wine; how the pair came to mean a big hotel is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
酒 (jiǔ) — the water piece, then a jar.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — the water piece, then a jar and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — xǐ, diàn]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the water piece, then a jar.
酒 (jiǔ).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the water piece, three strokes, as in 洗: two dots, then a stroke that rises to the right.
The right piece, seven strokes. A bar across the top. A vertical down the left, and a bar that turns down the right side. Inside, a short stroke falling to the left and a vertical that bends to the right at the foot. A short bar across them. Then a bar that closes the foot.
3 + 7 = ten.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 酒 (jiǔ), all ten, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 酒 (jiǔ)? (Ten.) What is the left piece? (The water piece, as in 洗.) Is the frame closed at the foot? (Yes, the last bar closes it.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 酒 (jiǔ), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 4 of 6.
客房 (kèfáng) — hotel guest room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a place to stay, then the Mandarin for a big hotel.

You'll want to know: 客房.
客房 — kèfáng — "hotel guest room".
这家酒店有一百间客房 zhè jiā jiǔdiàn yǒu yìbǎi jiān kèfáng — This hotel has a hundred guest rooms.

The word, taken apart.
客房 (kèfáng) is built from 客 'a guest' and 房 'a room', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khaek and bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

fángkǎ — "room key card".
wǒ de fángkǎ bú jiàn le — My room card has gone missing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fángkǎ is built from 房 'a room' and kǎ 'a card'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese bjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); kǎ is usually traced to English card.
