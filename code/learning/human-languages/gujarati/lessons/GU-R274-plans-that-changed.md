# Review — what won't happen, and what was going to

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C273-PERFECT-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C149-ISH-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "Arun has gone home". (*Aruṇ ghare gayo che*.) Then "I will come tomorrow". (*hũ kāle āvīś*.)

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C274-NEGATIVE-FUTURE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-NEGATIVE-SHORT-VERBS-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-WAS-GOING-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-WAS-GOING-BUT-01, GU-LEX-VARSAAD-01, GU-LEX-C85-TRAVEL-02, GU-LEX-C49-CARE-02, GU-LEX-BAJAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s each]
- [YOU SAY: I won't come tomorrow. She won't go today.]
- [YOU SAY: We won't eat at home. He won't take the medicine.]
- [YOU SAY: I was going to go to the market, but it rained.]
- [YOU SAY: Mira was going to come, but the train was late.]
- [YOU SAY: I was going to write a letter, but I came home late.]

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C274-NEGATIVE-FUTURE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-NEGATIVE-SHORT-VERBS-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-WAS-GOING-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C274-WAS-GOING-BUT-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "I won't go"? (**હું નહીં જાઉં**.) Does it change for a woman? (No.) "We were going to go"? (**અમે જવાના હતા**.) What turns **જવાનો છું** into a plan that is over? (**હતો** in place of **છું**.)
