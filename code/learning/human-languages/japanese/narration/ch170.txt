Japanese, chapter 170: The New Year and the Weeks After Next.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
さらいしゅう (saraishū) — the week after next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for every month, then the Japanese for to repeat.

You'll want to know: さらいしゅう.
さらいしゅう — saraishū — "the week after next".
さらいしゅう ひっこします。 — saraishū hikkoshimasu — "I will move house the week after next."

The word, taken apart.
さらいしゅう (saraishū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sa, "again" (Middle Chinese tsojH), rai, "coming" (loj) and shū, "week", whose Middle Chinese reading the sources here do not give (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
いわう (iwau) — to celebrate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the year after next, then the Japanese for the New Year.

You'll want to know: いわう.
いわう — iwau — "to celebrate".
らいしゅう ちちの たんじょうびを いわいます。 — raishū chichi no tanjōbi o iwaimasu — "Next week we will celebrate my father's birthday."
Polite いわいます, iwaimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
いわう (iwau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
