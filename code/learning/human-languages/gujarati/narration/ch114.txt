Gujarati, chapter 114: Millet, Wheat, Maize, Aubergine, Cabbage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
બાજરી (bājrī) — millet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for chickpeas, then the Gujarati for mung beans.

You'll want to know: બાજરી.
બાજરી (bājrī) — "millet".

The word, taken apart.
બાજરી (bājrī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from bājjara, "millet", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 9201).

Lesson 3 of 5.
મકાઈ (makāī) — maize.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for millet, then the Gujarati for wheat.

You'll want to know: મકાઈ.
મકાઈ (makāī) — "maize".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists મકાઈ (makāī) under the lexical Sanskrit markaka, the name of a bird, with a cross-reference to markaṭaka (CDIAL 9879). It does not explain how the name came to the grain, so its origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
રીંગણ (rī̃gaṇ) — an aubergine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for wheat, then the Gujarati for maize.

You'll want to know: રીંગણ.
રીંગણ (rī̃gaṇ) — "an aubergine".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Gujarati rīṁgṇī, the egg-plant, and rīṁgṇũ, its fruit, under the lexical Sanskrit riṅgaṇī, the name of another plant (CDIAL 10738). રીંગણ (rī̃gaṇ) (rīṁgaṇ) is the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
કોબી (kobī) — cabbage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for maize, then the Gujarati for an aubergine.

You'll want to know: કોબી.
કોબી (kobī) — "cabbage".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of કોબી (kobī) is not fully settled. It matches Hindi kobhī and gobhī, "cabbage", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit gojihvikā, "a particular kind of plant" (CDIAL 4270), but CDIAL lists no Gujarati form there.
