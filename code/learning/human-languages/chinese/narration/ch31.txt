Lesson 2 of 7.
他 (tā) — he; him.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for not bad, then the Mandarin for she.

You'll want to know: 他.
他 — tā — "he; him".
他是我的儿子 tā shì wǒ de érzi — He's my son.
The new character, in pieces: 他 (tā) — the person piece from 你 on the left, and on the right a three-stroke shape read yě. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
他 (tā) is inherited from Middle Chinese tha (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses it 'not I', the other person, and calls this the everyday form of the character. From somebody other than me to he is the usual explanation, but the step itself is not settled.

Script — the person piece, then yě.
他 (tā).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes. The same two as in 你: a left-falling stroke, then a vertical down to the foot.
The right piece, three strokes, read yě. A horizontal that runs right, turns down and hooks. A short vertical through the middle of it. Then a long vertical that bends right along the bottom and hooks up at the end.
2 + 3 = five, and the first two your hand already knows.

Lesson 4 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for she, then the Mandarin for he.

You'll want to know: 他们.
他们 — tāmen — "they; them".
他们是中国人 tāmen shì Zhōngguó rén — They're Chinese.
The new character, in pieces: 们 (men) — the person piece on the left again, and on the right a three-stroke piece read mén, the word for door, which gives the sound. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
他们 (tāmen) is built from 他 (tā) 'he' and the plural ending men, which goes on words for people. No dictionary we use records an older form of the ending, so its history is not settled.

Script — the person piece, then a doorway.
们 (men).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes, exactly as in 他 (tā) and 你.
The right piece, a doorway, three strokes. A dot at the top left. A vertical down the left side. Then a horizontal across the top that turns down the right side and hooks in at the foot.
2 + 3 = five. The doorway is read mén, and alone it is the word for door; here it gives 们 (men) its sound and loses its tone.

You'll want to know: 我们.
我们 — wǒmen — "we; us".

The word, taken apart.
我们 (wǒmen) is built from 我 'I' and the plural ending men. 我 is inherited from Middle Chinese ngaX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary glosses as what you call yourself; no dictionary we use records an older form of the ending, so its history is not settled.
