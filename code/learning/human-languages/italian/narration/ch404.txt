7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
bloccarsi — to freeze, to get stuck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the email, then the Italian for the inbox.

You'll want to know: bloccarsi.
bloccarsi — "to freeze, to get stuck".
Se il computer si blocca, aspetta un minuto. If the computer freezes, wait a minute.
Of a door or a car too: la porta si è bloccata.

The word, taken apart.
bloccarsi is built on blocco "block", which was borrowed from French bloc "a heavy piece, a block" (EtymDB), from Middle Dutch blok "a tree trunk, a log".

Lesson 2 of 7.
riavviare — to restart, to reboot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the inbox, then the Italian for to freeze.

You'll want to know: riavviare.
riavviare — "to restart, to reboot".
Se non funziona, riavvia il telefono. If it does not work, restart the phone.
Avviare is to start; ri- is again.

The word, taken apart.
riavviare is built from ri- "again" and avviare "to start off", from a and via "way, road", inherited from Latin via (Lewis & Short): to set something on its way again.

Lesson 3 of 7.
il virus — the virus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to freeze, then the Italian for to restart.

You'll want to know: il virus.
il virus — "the virus".
Se apri quell'allegato, prendi un virus. If you open that attachment, you will get a virus.
For computers and for illness. Masculine, invariable.

The word, taken apart.
virus is taken whole from Latin vīrus "slime, poison" (Lewis & Short); the computer sense came from English.

il portatile — "the laptop".
Porto il portatile in vacanza, se devo lavorare. I take my laptop on holiday, if I have to work.
As an adjective, portable: un televisore portatile.

The word, taken apart.
portatile is built on portare "to carry", inherited from Latin portāre "to carry" (Lewis & Short): a computer you can carry.

Lesson 5 of 7.

You'll want to know: l'antivirus.
l'antivirus — "the antivirus (software)".
Se hai un buon antivirus, sei più tranquillo. If you have a good antivirus, you can feel calmer.
Masculine, invariable: l'antivirus, gli antivirus.

The word, taken apart.
antivirus is built from the Greek prefix anti- "against" and virus, from Latin vīrus "slime, poison" (Lewis & Short).
