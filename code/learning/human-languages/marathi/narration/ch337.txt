Marathi, chapter 337: Legends and Ballads.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दंतकथा (dantkathā) — a legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for an underground passage, then the Marathi for a treasure.

You'll want to know: दंतकथा.
दंतकथा — dantkathā — "a legend".
या तलावाबद्दल एक जुनी दंतकथा आहे. yā talāvābaddal ek junī dantkathā āhe. There is an old legend about this lake.
Feminine.
A tale passed on by word of mouth.

The word, taken apart.
दंतकथा (dantkathā) is built from Sanskrit danta, 'a tooth', and kathā, 'a story' (Monier-Williams); it is usually explained as a tale passed from mouth to mouth.

You'll want to know: पोवाडा.
पोवाडा — povāḍā — "a heroic ballad".
जत्रेत एक गायक शूर सरदाराचा पोवाडा गात होता. jatret ek gāyak śūr sardārācā povāḍā gāt hotā. At the fair a singer was singing a ballad of a brave chieftain.
Masculine.
A Marathi ballad of heroic deeds.

The word, taken apart.
पोवाडा (povāḍā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Marathi povāḍā, 'a historical ballad', under Sanskrit pravāda, 'a speaking forth; talk, report' (CDIAL 8795).

Lesson 3 of 5.
अमर (amar) — immortal; undying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a legend, then the Marathi for a heroic ballad.

You'll want to know: अमर.
अमर — amar — "immortal; undying".
त्या वीराचं नाव इतिहासात अमर झालं. tyā vīrācaṁ nāv itihāsāt amar jhālaṁ. That hero's name became immortal in history.
अमर (amar) keeps one form: अमर (amar) कथा, अमर (amar) नाव.

The word, taken apart.
अमर (amar) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit amara, 'undying, immortal' (Monier-Williams).
