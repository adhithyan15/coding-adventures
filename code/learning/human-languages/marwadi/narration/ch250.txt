Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 250: Lamps and Festive Dishes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मनावणो (manāvṇo) — to celebrate (a festival); to coax.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for Diwali, then the Marwadi for worship.

You'll want to know: मनावणो.
मनावणो — manāvṇo — "to celebrate (a festival); to coax".
म्हैं दिवाळी घरै मनाई। mhaĩ divāḷī gharai manāī. I celebrated Diwali at home.
Past (I …): म्हैं होळी मनाई, mhaĩ hoḷī manāī.

The word, taken apart.
मनावणो (manāvṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit manyate, "thinks, honours"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari manāvaï, "persuades", under it. "To celebrate" a feast is probably "to honour" it (CDIAL 9857).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for worship, then the Marwadi for to celebrate.

You'll want to know: पकवान.
पकवान — pakvān — "festive dishes; fried delicacies".
आज मां पकवान बणावै है। āj mā̃ pakvān baṇāvai hai. Today my mother is making festive dishes.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पकवान (pakvān) goes back to Sanskrit pakvānna, "cooked or dressed food" (Monier-Williams), built from pakva, "cooked", and anna, "food".

Lesson 4 of 5.
आरती (ārtī) — the waving of a lamp in worship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for festive dishes, then the Marwadi for festive joy.

You'll want to know: आरती.
आरती — ārtī — "the waving of a lamp in worship".
मां आथण आरती करै है। mā̃ āthaṇ ārtī karai hai. Mother waves the lamp in worship in the evening.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आरती (ārtī) is inherited from Sanskrit ārātrika, the waving of a lamp before an image at night. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry; it lists Hindi and Gujarati ārtī (CDIAL 1315).
