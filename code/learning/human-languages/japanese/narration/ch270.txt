Japanese, chapter 270: Who Did It?.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はんにん (hannin) — a culprit; the criminal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to bark, then the Japanese for to make a noise.

You'll want to know: はんにん.
はんにん — hannin — "a culprit; the criminal".
はんにんが にげた とき、月が ひかっていました。 — hannin ga nigeta toki, tsuki ga hikatte imashita — "When the culprit got away, the moon was shining."

The word, taken apart.
はんにん (hannin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are han, "crime" (Middle Chinese bjomX) and nin, "person" (nyin) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
その (sono) — that (one); the one mentioned.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an incident, then the Japanese for a report to the police.

You'll want to know: その.
その — sono — "that (one); the one mentioned".
その おとこを みましたか。 — sono otoko o mimashita ka — "Did you see the man?"

The word, taken apart.
その (sono) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a report to the police, then the Japanese for that (one).

You'll want to know: さわる.
さわる — sawaru — "to touch".
なにも さわらないで ください。 — nanimo sawaranaide kudasai — "Please do not touch anything."
Polite さわります, sawarimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おとこが かばんに さわりました。, otoko ga kaban ni sawarimashita.

The word, taken apart.
さわる (sawaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
