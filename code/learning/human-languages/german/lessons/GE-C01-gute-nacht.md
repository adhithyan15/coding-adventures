# Gute Nacht — good night, and the ending that finally changes

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The greeting that breaks the *guten* streak — and shows you exactly
what German's endings are tracking.

## You'll want to know first
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-GUT-02, GE-LEX-NACHT-02] -->

- [gut](./GE-C01-gut.md) · [Nacht](./GE-C01-nacht.md)

## The word, taken apart — by assembling it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-GUTE-NACHT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**Gute Nacht** = **gut** ("good") + **Nacht** ("night") = "good night," *GOO-te
nahkht*.

## Grammar Lens: *gute*, not *guten*
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-ADJ-ACC-FEM-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ADJ-ACC-MASC-02, GE-LEX-NACHT-02] -->

Three greetings in a row were *gut**en*** (Tag, Morgen, Abend — all
masculine). This one is *gut**e***. The reason is the one you set up: *Nacht*
is **feminine**, and a feminine noun in the "(I wish you a) good ___"
accusative takes the ending **-e**, not **-en**. So the endings line up:

- *gut**en** Tag* / *Morgen* / *Abend* — masculine → **-en**
- *gut**e** Nacht* — feminine → **-e**

That single vowel is German's adjective quietly reporting the noun's gender —
exactly the job *bueno/buena* and *bon/bonne* did in Spanish and French, just
with German's own endings.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-PRAGMATICS-GUTE-NACHT-03]; assesses=[] -->

Like Spanish *buenas noches* and French *bonne nuit*, *Gute Nacht* is the
**bedtime** farewell — "sleep well," said on parting for the night, not an
evening hello (that's *Guten Abend*). Same split French makes; German keeps
the greeting (*Abend*) and the send-off (*Nacht*) apart.
