German, chapter 485: Nacken, Ellbogen und Knöchel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Nacken — back of the neck, nape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for eyebrow, then the German for eyelash.

You'll want to know: der Nacken.
der Nacken — "back of the neck, nape".
Nach der Arbeit am Computer tut mir der Nacken weh. After working at the computer my neck hurts.
Plural: die Nacken. Der Hals is the whole neck; der Nacken is the back of it.

The word, taken apart.
Nacken is inherited from Middle High German nacke, from Proto-Germanic hnakkô "nape, neck" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: der Ellbogen.
der Ellbogen — "elbow".
Er hat sich beim Sturz den Ellbogen verletzt. He hurt his elbow in the fall.
Plural: die Ellbogen. Also spelt der Ellenbogen.

The word, taken apart.
Ellbogen is inherited from Middle High German elenboge and Old High German elinbogo, from Proto-Germanic alinabugô "elbow" (EtymDB): the bend of the forearm.

Handgelenk is built on Hand and Gelenk "joint", inherited from Middle High German gelenke and Old High German gilenki "joint, bend, waist" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Knöchel — ankle; knuckle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for elbow, then the German for wrist.

You'll want to know: der Knöchel.
der Knöchel — "ankle; knuckle".
Beim Wandern habe ich mir den Knöchel verstaucht. I sprained my ankle while hiking.
Plural: die Knöchel. Also a knuckle of the finger.

The word, taken apart.
Knöchel is usually traced to a small form of Knochen "bone", from Middle High German knöchel; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.

Lesson 5 of 5.
die Hüfte — hip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for wrist, then the German for ankle.

You'll want to know: die Hüfte.
die Hüfte — "hip".
Meine Oma bekommt eine neue Hüfte. My grandma is getting a new hip.
Plural: die Hüften.

The word, taken apart.
Hüfte is usually traced to Old High German huf, which EtymDB derives from Proto-Germanic hupiz "hip"; EtymDB sets Hüfte beside English hip, a cousin.
