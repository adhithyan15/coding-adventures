# मोठा · मोठी · मोठं — three endings, one rule

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-MOTHA, MR-LEX-MAJHE, MR-GRAMMAR-POSSESSIVE-THREE-GENDERS] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **माझा · माझी · माझं** from chapter nine. **उजवा · उजवी · उजवं**
from chapter forty-four. The same three endings, twice, and never named.

## Grammar Lens: the adjective takes the gender of its noun
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-ADJ-AGREEMENT]; assesses=[MR-LEX-MOTHA, MR-LEX-UJVA, MR-LEX-DAAVA, MR-LEX-GHAR, MR-LEX-KHOLI] -->

> **मोठा** *moṭhā* — with a masculine noun
>
> **मोठी** *moṭhī* — with a feminine noun
>
> **मोठं** *moṭhaṁ* — with a neuter noun

Marathi has three genders where most European languages have two, and an
adjective that ends in **-आ** in its dictionary form changes to match all
three. You have owned the machinery since chapter nine; what you did not have
was permission to use it on a new word.

> **मोठा मित्र** — a big friend (masculine)
>
> **मोठी खोली** — a big room (feminine)
>
> **मोठं घर** — a big house (neuter)

Say those three aloud and hear that only the last syllable moves. That is what
makes the rule cheap: learn one adjective and you have learned three forms,
and every **-आ** adjective after this costs you nothing extra.
