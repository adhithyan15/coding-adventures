# Words that carry their own reason

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C36-MILAQA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C36-TABAQ-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Name the plate and the spoon in Arabic and spell each one aloud. Then
use this chapter's *why* and *because* to explain where the spoon got its name.

## Why it's said this way: a tool named for what it does
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C36-MILAQA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C36-MILAQA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C36-TABAQ-01, AR-CONCEPT-C36-TABAQ-02] -->

| word, spelled out | root | what the root means |
|---|---|---|
| **ملعقة** *milʿaqa* — *mīm · lām · ʿayn · qāf · ة* | **ل-ع-ق** | he licked, he lapped |
| **طبق** *ṭabaq* — *ṭāʾ · bāʾ · qāf* | **ط-ب-ق** | to cover, to fit over |

So a spoon is **the licking-thing** and a plate is **the covering-thing**. Nothing
in either word had to be learned as an arbitrary noise: the root says what the
object is for, and the shape says it is an object.

**أطباق** is the plural of **طبق**, in the same broken shape as **أكواب**. One
broken plural is a surprise; two in the same shape is a **pattern**, and a pattern
is something you can use on the next word.

## Guided Practice: because, and when
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C36-MILAQA-02, AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-01, AR-CONCEPT-C24-ILA-AL-GHAD-02] -->

- [YOU SAY: ask why the spoon is called that, then answer — *limādhā? … li-anna …*]
- [YOU SAY: until tomorrow — *ilā l-ghad*]
- [YOU SAY: the other farewell built on the same word — *ilā l-liqāʾ*]

**إلى الغد** is the same *ilā*, **until**, with a new word in the slot. And
**الغد** has its reason inside it too: the root **gh-d-w** means **to set out at
dawn**, so *tomorrow* in Arabic is named after the hour you begin it.

## Why it's said this way: a night that points at the morning
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-01, AR-CONCEPT-C27-TUSBIH-ALA-KHAYR-02] -->

**تصبح على خير** is the everyday **good night**, and it says nothing about the
night. Literally: **may you wake up into goodness**.

English, French, German and Spanish all wish the sleeper a quiet *night*. Arabic
looks past the night to the far side of it — *tuṣbiḥ* carries the morning root, and
**خير** is the goodness the wellbeing answer already uses. The phrase is built
entirely from two things met long before bedtime came up.

## How to answer: the word that erases what was owed
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C23-AFWAN-02] -->

**عفوا** *ʿafwan* answers thanks as **you are welcome**, and the same word, on a
different tone, means **excuse me** and **pardon**. The reason is the root
**ʿ-f-w**, to **erase**: a debt, an intrusion or a mistake, all wiped away by one
word.
