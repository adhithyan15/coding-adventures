Lesson 1 of 5.
जरूरत (jarūrat) — a need.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for hope, then the Marwadi for a demand.

You'll want to know: जरूरत.
जरूरत — jarūrat — "a need".

The word, taken apart.
जरूरत (jarūrat) is a loan from Arabic ḍarūra, "need, necessity", which reached Marwadi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ḍ-r-r (Lane). Marwadi says the ḍ, which Persian pronounces z, as j.

Lesson 2 of 5.
लालच (lālach) — greed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a demand, then the Marwadi for a need.

You'll want to know: लालच.
लालच — lālach — "greed".

The word, taken apart.
लालच (lālach) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from lālitya, "amorousness". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 11029); the match is through Hindi lālac. It is built on lalita, "amorous".

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: सपनो.
सपनो — sapno — "a dream".

The word, taken apart.
सपनो (sapno) goes back to Sanskrit svapna, "sleep, dream" (CDIAL 13904), with the sv worn down to s. CDIAL gives no Marwari form there, and it takes forms like Nepali sapanā, "dream", as early loans from Sanskrit or possibly inherited words.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मरजी (marjī) — one's will, a choice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a dream, then the Marwadi for an expectation.

You'll want to know: मरजी.
मरजी — marjī — "one's will, a choice".

The word, taken apart.
मरजी (marjī) is a loan from Arabic marḍī, "pleasing, approved", which reached Marwadi through Persian. Its Arabic root is r-ḍ-w (Lane). Persian says it marzī; Marwadi says the z as j.
