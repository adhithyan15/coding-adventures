Latin, chapter 367: Since, Lest, But That.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cum — when, since, although (with the subjunctive).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for therefore, then the Latin for for that reason.

You'll want to know: cum.
cum — "when, since, although (with the subjunctive)".
Cum aeger essem, domī mānsī. Since I was ill, I stayed at home.
As a preposition cum takes the ablative: cum amīcō, with a friend. As a conjunction with the subjunctive it gives a reason or the scene: cum essem, since I was. With the indicative it means when.

The word, taken apart.
The conjunction cum was written quom in old Latin (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): it comes from the stem of the relative quī "who, which", and is a different word from the preposition cum "with".

Lesson 2 of 5.
nē — so that not, lest; that (after fearing).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for for that reason, then the Latin for when.

You'll want to know: nē.
nē — "so that not, lest; that (after fearing)".
Portam claudō nē fūr intret. I shut the gate so that a thief may not come in.
Nē + subjunctive gives a negative aim: ut says so that, nē says so that not. After a verb of fearing, timeō nē means I fear that. Nē … quidem means not even.

The word, taken apart.
nē is the old Latin negative; Lewis & Short compare Sanskrit na and Old English ne, and it is usually traced to a Proto-Indo-European negative ne. It also lies behind nōn and nihil and, through Old English, behind English no.

Lesson 3 of 5.
quīn — but that, that (after a denied doubt); why not.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for when, then the Latin for so that not.

You'll want to know: quīn.
quīn — "but that, that (after a denied doubt); why not".
Nōn dubitō quīn vērum dīcās. I do not doubt that you are telling the truth.
Quīn + subjunctive follows a doubt or a hindrance that is denied: nōn dubitō quīn, I do not doubt that. Alone, quīn can mean why not.

The word, taken apart.
quīn is usually taken as the old adverb quī "how" with the negative -ne (Lewis & Short): how not, why not.

Lesson 4 of 5.
quōminus — that … not (after hindering), from doing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for so that not, then the Latin for but that.

You'll want to know: quōminus.
quōminus — "that … not (after hindering), from doing".
Nōn recūsō quōminus hoc faciam. I do not refuse to do this.
Quōminus + subjunctive follows verbs of hindering and refusing: nōn recūsō quōminus, I do not refuse to.

The word, taken apart.
quōminus is built from quō "by which" and minus "less" (Lewis & Short): by which the less, so that not.

Lesson 5 of 5.
tamquam — as, in the same way as; as if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for but that, then the Latin for that … not.

You'll want to know: tamquam.
tamquam — "as, in the same way as; as if".
Loquitur tamquam rēx sit. He speaks as if he were king.
Tamquam compares: in the same way as. Tamquam or tamquam sī + subjunctive is as if: tamquam rēx sit.

The word, taken apart.
tamquam is built from tam "so" and quam "as" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): so as.
