Lesson 1 of 5.
しぼる (shiboru) — to squeeze; to wring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to swell, then the Japanese for to let cool.

You'll want to know: しぼる.
しぼる — shiboru — "to squeeze; to wring".
みかんを しぼって、のみました。 — mikan o shibotte, nomimashita — "I squeezed a mandarin and drank the juice."
Polite しぼります, shiborimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが ふくを しぼりました。, haha ga fuku o shiborimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しぼる (shiboru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for to let cool, then the Japanese for to squeeze.

You'll want to know: つぶす.
つぶす — tsubusu — "to mash; to crush".
いもを つぶして ください。 — imo o tsubushite kudasai — "Please mash the potatoes."
Polite つぶします, tsubushimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもが いもを つぶしました。, kodomo ga imo o tsubushimashita.

The word, taken apart.
つぶす (tsubusu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the transitive partner of tsubureru, "to be crushed", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to mash, then the Japanese for to add.

You'll want to know: ちぎる.
ちぎる — chigiru — "to tear into pieces".
やさいを てで ちぎりました。 — yasai o te de chigirimashita — "I tore the vegetables by hand."
Polite ちぎります, chigirimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): こどもが のりを ちぎりました。, kodomo ga nori o chigirimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ちぎる (chigiru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana; its kanji spelling writes chi with the kanji for "thousand", whose native reading is chi, and the rest with the kanji of kiru, "to cut" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
