# श — the letter you have been thanking people with

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 1s] The hook from before: where did it sit, and what did it replace?

[PAUSE 2s] Today is a whole letter, and one you have been looking at since your
first page.

## Script you'll notice: श
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-145]; assesses=[] -->

**श** — *śa*, the *sh* of English *ship*.

What it is made of:

- a joined upper loop, descending outer curve, lower loop and diagonal tail
- a top-to-bottom right stem
- the top shirorekhā

The left-hand side is more work than most letters ask for, and it is **all one
run** — the pen does not come up until the tail is finished.

## Script: the third sibilant
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-145] -->

| letter | sound |
|---|---|
| स | *sa* |
| ष | *ṣa* |
| श | *śa* |

You could already draw two of the three. **This is the last one**, and with it
the hissing letters are complete.

Words that have been yours from the start:

- **शुक्रिया** — thank you
- **शाम** — evening
- **शनिवार** — Saturday

The first of those was among the first words in the book, and the letter that
opens it is only being drawn now. The opening chapters taught whole words, and
the letters inside them went unnamed.

## Writing: श
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-145] -->

- **1.** start at the upper loop's lower inner tip, sweep left and clockwise around the upper loop, descend the outer curve, curl around the lower loop, and continue down-right through the diagonal tail without lifting
- **2.** lift and draw the right stem top-to-bottom
- **3.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 2.**

> Verified three-stroke teaching form; everyday handwriting may join or simplify
> the body differently.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein,
> ‘Deva-श-order.gif’, strokes 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
