\chapter*{Pronunciation --- a reference, not a chapter}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Pronunciation (reference)}
\markboth{Pronunciation}{Pronunciation}

Not to be read before starting. German spelling is largely phonetic --- mostly
you say what you see --- so this page is short. Each lesson names the sounds
its word needs and points here for more.

\section*{Vowels}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{a} = open \emph{ah}; \textbf{o} = \emph{oh}; \textbf{u} =
    \emph{oo} (\emph{gut} = \emph{goot}).
  \item Umlauts shift the vowel forward: \textbf{\"a} $\approx$ \emph{eh},
    \textbf{\"u} = the front-rounded \emph{ee} (= French \emph{u}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Consonants that differ}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ch} after \emph{a/o/u} = a guttural \emph{loch} sound
    (\emph{Nacht} = \emph{nahkht}); softer after \emph{e/i}.
  \item \textbf{z} = \emph{ts}; \textbf{s} before a vowel = \emph{z};
    \textbf{\ss} = a sharp \emph{ss}.
  \item \textbf{h} at word-start \emph{is} pronounced (unlike Spanish/French).
  \item \textbf{w} = English \emph{v}; \textbf{v} = English \emph{f};
    \textbf{j} = English \emph{y}.
  \item \textbf{Final devoicing:} written \emph{-d/-g/-b} are said
    \emph{-t/-k/-p} (\emph{Abend} = \emph{AH-bent}, \emph{Tag} = \emph{tahk}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Two habits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Every noun is Capitalized} --- \emph{Tag}, \emph{Nacht} ---
    not just names.
  \item \textbf{Final devoicing} (above).
\end{itemize}

\section*{The High German Consonant Shift (a decoding tool)}

Not about \emph{saying} words but \emph{recognizing} them: German pushed
certain consonants forward, so English and German cousins differ predictably.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
English & $\to$ German & example \\
\midrule
d & t & goo\textbf{d} $\to$ gu\textbf{t}, \textbf{d}ay $\to$ \textbf{T}ag \\
t & s / ss / z & wa\textbf{t}er $\to$ Wa\textbf{ss}er, \textbf{t}hat $\to$ da\textbf{s} \\
p & pf / f & a\textbf{pp}le $\to$ A\textbf{pf}el, shi\textbf{p} $\to$ Schi\textbf{ff} \\
k & ch & ma\textbf{k}e $\to$ ma\textbf{ch}en, boo\textbf{k} $\to$ Bu\textbf{ch} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

(A \emph{different} shift from Grimm's Law, the older PIE$\to$Germanic change
shared by both English and German --- see the \emph{Nacht} lesson.)
