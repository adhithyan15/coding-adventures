# ಕಣ್ಣು (kaṇṇu) — "eye," the same word across four languages

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C13-DEHADA-BHAGAGALU-01, KA-LEX-C36-MAGALU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Tale*, *kai* — Chapter 13's head and hand, and nothing more of
the body since. This chapter adds four words to that short list, starting
above the nose.

## You'll want to know: ಕಣ್ಣು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C37-KANNU-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ಕಣ್ಣು** — *kaṇṇu* — **eye**

> **ನನ್ನ ಕಣ್ಣು.** — *nanna kaṇṇu.* — "My eye."

## The word, taken apart: nearly one word, four times over
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-ETYMON-C37-KANNU-02]; assesses=[KA-ETYMON-C36-MAGALU-03] -->

**ಕಣ್ಣು** continues Proto-Dravidian **\*kaṇ**, "eye." Tamil **கண்** (*kaṇ*)
and Malayalam **കണ്ണ്** (*kaṇṇŭ*) sit close beside it — and Telugu
**కన్ను** (*kannu*) sits closest of all, nearly identical letter for
letter. Four languages that otherwise pull apart on nearly everything —
tense, the *p* → *h* law, half their vocabulary — agree almost completely
on this one, the same closeness the last chapter found between Kannada's
*magaḷu* and Tamil and Malayalam's own *makaḷ*.
