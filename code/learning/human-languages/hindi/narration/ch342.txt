Lesson 1 of 7.
क़िस्सा (qissā) — an anecdote; a tale.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a reader, then the Hindi for to be sold.

You'll want to know: क़िस्सा.
क़िस्सा — qissā — "an anecdote; a tale".
दादा जी के पास युद्ध के बहुत क़िस्से थे। dādā jī ke pās yuddh ke bahut qisse the. Grandfather had lots of stories from the war.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
क़िस्सा (qissā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic qiṣṣa, "a story". It is a tale told aloud, often a true one from someone's life.

उस नेता ने अपनी आत्मकथा लिखी। us netā ne apnī ātmakathā likhī. That leader wrote his autobiography.

The word, taken apart.
आत्मकथा (ātmakathā) joins Sanskrit ātman, "self", and कथा, a story (Monier-Williams): the story of oneself, told by oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a publisher, then the Hindi for an edition.

You'll want to know: अनुवाद.
अनुवाद — anuvād — "a translation".
मीरा ने उस कहानी का अंग्रेज़ी में अनुवाद किया। Mīrā ne us kahānī kā angrezī meṁ anuvād kiyā. Mira translated that story into English.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
अनुवाद (anuvād) is Sanskrit anuvāda, "a saying after, a repeating" (Monier-Williams): saying again in another language what was first said in one.
