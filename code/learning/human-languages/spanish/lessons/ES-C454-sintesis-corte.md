# (calle cortada) — read it, then do it yourself

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-01, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **las obras** and **la vía**. Each one closes something below.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-01, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-03] -->

Two notices on the same morning. Read both, then answer.

**1 — on a barrier**

> Calle cortada por **obras** hasta el día 12. Paso de peatones por la acera
> de enfrente.

**2 — over the loudspeaker**

> Atención. Por un **accidente** en la **vía**, el tren con destino Valencia
> saldrá con retraso. El **servicio** de autobús sustituirá al tren hasta las
> once.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: how long the street is shut, and where to walk]
- [YOU SAY: why the train is late]
- [YOU SAY: what is replacing it, and until when]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-03, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-04, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-01] -->

**Por un accidente en la vía** is doing the work of an English clause —
"because there has been an accident on the line". *Por* plus a noun is how
Spanish gives a cause on a notice, and it is shorter than any sentence you
could build with *porque*. The same shape opened the first notice: *cortada
por obras*.

Then notice **el servicio de autobús sustituirá al tren**. The announcement
names the *service*, not the buses, and the service is what acts. An English
announcement would more likely say "buses will replace the train". Spanish
prefers the institution as the subject in this register, which is the opposite
of what the delivery card did — and the difference is that a card is
apologising while an announcement is informing.

**Cortada** agrees with *la calle*: feminine, because the street is.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-01, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-02, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-03, ES-LEX-C454-CORTE-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines: say the
street is closed for works, say until when, say there has been an accident on
the track, and say a bus service is replacing the train.

Then check yourself: did you use *por* plus a noun to give the reason, and did
*cortada* agree with what it describes?
