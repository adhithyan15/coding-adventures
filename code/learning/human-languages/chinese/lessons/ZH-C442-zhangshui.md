## You'll want to know: zhǎngshuǐ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C442-RIVER442-04]; assesses=[] -->

*zhǎngshuǐ* — "to rise after rain (of a river)".

> *zhè jǐ tiān xiàyǔ hé lǐ zhǎngshuǐ le* — It has rained these last few days, and the river has risen.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: zhǎng is said with a rising tone before shuǐ, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*zhǎngshuǐ* is built from *zhǎng* 'to swell' and 水 'water', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *trjang* and *sywijX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as a great flood of water; today's falling-rising tone is irregular beside it.
