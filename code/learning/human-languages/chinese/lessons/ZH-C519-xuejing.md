## You'll want to know: xuějǐng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C519-OUTDOORS519-04]; assesses=[] -->

*xuějǐng* — "a snowy landscape".

> *wǒ méi kàn guo zhème hǎokàn de xuějǐng* — I've never seen such a lovely snowy landscape.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

Two third tones meet in this word: xuě is said with a rising tone before jǐng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*xuějǐng* is built from 雪 'snow' and *jǐng* 'a view', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *sjwet* and *kjaengX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, after an older book, as rain that has set, and the second 'bright' and 'an image'.
