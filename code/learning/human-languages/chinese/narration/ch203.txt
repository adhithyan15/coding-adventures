Mandarin Chinese, chapter 203: Renting a Flat.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zū — to rent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for customer, then the Mandarin for checkout.

You'll want to know: zū.
zū — "to rent".
wǒ xiǎng zū yí ge fángzi — I want to rent a flat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zū is inherited from Middle Chinese tsu (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to store up' and 'a tax'. Renting is not given there, so how it came is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
房东 (fángdōng) — landlord; landlady.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for checkout, then the Mandarin for to rent.

You'll want to know: 房东.
房东 — fángdōng — "landlord; landlady".
我的房东人很好 wǒ de fángdōng rén hěn hǎo — My landlord is a nice person.
The new character, in pieces: 东 (dōng) — five strokes: a bar, a stroke that falls to the left and turns right, a vertical that hooks to the left, then a short stroke falling to the left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
房东 (fángdōng) is built from 房 'house, room' and 东 (dōng) 'east', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjang and tuwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). How the pair came to mean the owner who lets a room is not settled.

Script — a bar, a turn, a hook and two short strokes.
东 (dōng).
One piece, five strokes, top to bottom. Count the strokes in their turns.
First, a bar across the upper middle.
Second, a stroke from above the bar, falling down to the left and turning to run right.
Third, a vertical down the middle, from above the turned stroke and down through it, hooking to the left at its foot.
Last, two short strokes at the foot: one falling to the left, then a dot on the right.
1 + 1 + 1 + 2 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 东 (dōng), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 东 (dōng)? (Five.) Which stroke hooks? (The vertical down the middle.) What comes last? (The dot on the right.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
yājīn — a deposit paid in advance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for landlord, then the Mandarin for rent for a home.

You'll want to know: yājīn.
yājīn — "a deposit paid in advance".
yājīn shì liǎng ge yuè de fángzū — The deposit is two months' rent.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yājīn is built from yā 'a pledge' and jīn 'gold, money', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'aep and kim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as putting one's mark on a document, so how it came to mean a pledge is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
hétong — contract; written agreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for rent for a home, then the Mandarin for a deposit paid in advance.

You'll want to know: hétong.
hétong — "contract; written agreement".
fángdōng de hétong nǐ kànle ma — Have you read the landlord's contract?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hétong is built from hé 'to join' and 同 'together', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hop and duwng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'alike' and 'together'. The dictionary already pairs the two to gloss the first, in the sense of joining; a written agreement is not given there, so how the word came to it is not settled. In the word the second loses its tone.
