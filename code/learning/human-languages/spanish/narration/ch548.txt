Spanish, chapter 548: El banquete.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el matrimonio — the marriage; the married couple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the engagement, then the Spanish for the ring.

You'll want to know: el matrimonio.
el matrimonio — "the marriage; the married couple".
Su matrimonio duró cincuenta años. Their marriage lasted fifty years.
Un matrimonio is also a married couple: En el quinto vive un matrimonio mayor.

The word, taken apart.
matrimonio is a learned borrowing from Latin mātrimōnium 'marriage, wedlock' (EtymDB), built on māter 'mother'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el banquete — the banquet, the wedding meal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the marriage, then the Spanish for the ceremony.

You'll want to know: el banquete.
el banquete — "the banquet, the wedding meal".
Después de la boda hubo un banquete. After the wedding there was a banquet.

The word, taken apart.
banquete is usually traced to French banquet or Italian banchetto, a small form of banco 'bench', a Germanic word. The feast was named after the little benches the guests sat on.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el brindis — the toast (with a drink).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the ceremony, then the Spanish for the banquet.

You'll want to know: el brindis.
el brindis — "the toast (with a drink)".
El padrino hizo un brindis por los novios. The best man made a toast to the couple.
The same form in the plural: los brindis.

The word, taken apart.
brindis comes from the German toast bring dir's 'I bring it to you' (EtymDB), said to have been picked up from German soldiers in the 1500s.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la luna de miel — the honeymoon.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: la luna de miel.
la luna de miel — "the honeymoon".
Pasaron la luna de miel en Cuba. They spent their honeymoon in Cuba.
Irse de luna de miel — to go on honeymoon.

The word, taken apart.
luna de miel was built on Latin lūna 'moon' and mel 'honey' (Lewis & Short), and is usually said to copy English honeymoon, through French lune de miel.
