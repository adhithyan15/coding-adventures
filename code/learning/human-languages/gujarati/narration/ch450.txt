Gujarati, chapter 450: Words of Love.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
વહાલ (vahāl) — fondness; tender affection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be won over, then the Gujarati for pleased.

You'll want to know: વહાલ.
વહાલ — vahāl — "fondness; tender affection".
દાદીને નાના બાળક પર ઘણું વહાલ છે. dādīne nānā bāḷak par ghaṇũ vahāl che. Grandmother is very fond of the little child.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
વહાલ (vahāl) is inherited from Sanskrit vallabha, "dear, desired"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "affection" (CDIAL 11427).

Lesson 2 of 5.
સ્નેહ (sneh) — affection; loving warmth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for pleased, then the Gujarati for fondness.

You'll want to know: સ્નેહ.
સ્નેહ — sneh — "affection; loving warmth".
બંને બહેનો વચ્ચે ઘણો સ્નેહ છે. banne baheno vacce ghaṇo sneh che. There is great affection between the two sisters.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સ્નેહ (sneh) is a tatsama, Sanskrit sneha, which Monier-Williams gives first as "oiliness" and then as tenderness and love; the older spoken form neh comes from the same word (CDIAL 13802).

Lesson 3 of 5.
મમતા (mamtā) — a mother's love.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for fondness, then the Gujarati for affection.

You'll want to know: મમતા.
મમતા — mamtā — "a mother's love".
માતાની મમતા સૌથી મોટી છે. mātānī mamtā sauthī moṭī che. A mother's love is the greatest of all.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મમતા (mamtā) is a tatsama: Monier-Williams gives Sanskrit mamatā as the state of feeling "mine", from mama, "my"; Gujarati uses it for a mother's tender love.

You'll want to know: મોહવું.
મોહવું — mohvũ — "to charm; to captivate".
નાના બાળકનું સ્મિત બધાને મોહે છે. nānā bāḷaknũ smit badhāne mohe che. A little child's smile charms everyone.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બધાનું હૃદય મોહ્યું, teṇe badhānũ hṛday mohyũ.

The word, taken apart.
મોહવું (mohvũ) is inherited from Sanskrit mōhayati, "bewilders"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to allure, charm" (CDIAL 10362).
