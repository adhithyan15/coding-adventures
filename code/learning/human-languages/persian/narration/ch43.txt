Persian, chapter 43: Lemon, Yoghurt, Onion, Breakfast, Lunch.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
لیمو (limu) — a lemon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for grapes, then the Persian for an orange.

You'll want to know: لیمو.
لیمو — limu — "a lemon".

The word, taken apart.
limu is usually traced to an Indian word, Sanskrit nimbū, "lime" (CDIAL 7247; EtymDB sets the two side by side). Arabic laymūn took the word from Persian (EtymDB), and English lemon comes from it by way of Arabic.

Lesson 4 of 5.
صبحانه (sobhâne) — breakfast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for yoghurt, then the Persian for an onion.

You'll want to know: صبحانه.
صبحانه — sobhâne — "breakfast".

The word, taken apart.
sobhâne is sobh, "morning", with the Persian ending -âne, "belonging to": the morning meal. sobh is borrowed from Arabic ṣubḥ, "morning" (root ṣ-b-ḥ, Lane).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ناهار (nâhâr) — lunch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Persian for an onion, then the Persian for breakfast.

You'll want to know: ناهار.
ناهار — nâhâr — "lunch".

The word, taken apart.
nâhâr is built from nâ-, "not", and âhâr, "food" (EtymDB): the word first meant "fasting, not having eaten", and came to name the meal that ends the fast. Both parts are native Persian.
