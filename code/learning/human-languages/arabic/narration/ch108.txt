Lesson 1 of 5.
يمكن (yumkin) — it is possible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be able to, then the Arabic for to be able to.

You'll want to know: يمكن.
يمكن — yumkin — "it is possible".

The word, taken apart.
يمكن (yumkin) is built on the native Arabic root m-k-n, "to have power, to be firm in place" (Lane). Yumkin is the Form IV verb amkana, "to be possible", in the present tense: "it is possible".

Lesson 2 of 5.
لدى (ladā) — with, in one's possession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to be able to, then the Arabic for it is possible.

You'll want to know: لدى.
لدى — ladā — "with, in one's possession".

The word, taken apart.
لدى (ladā) is an old preposition of place, "at, with". The grammarians set it beside ladun, "at, near", and it is not built on a verbal root; its deeper origin is not settled.

Which two days have names instead of numbers? (Al-jumʿa, "the gathering," and as-sabt, a Semitic sister of "Sabbath.")

You'll want to know: حق.
حق — ḥaqq — "a right".

The word, taken apart.
حق (ḥaqq) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-q-q, "to be true, to be due" (Lane). Ḥaqq is what is true and what is due: a right.
