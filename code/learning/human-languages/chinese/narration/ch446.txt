Mandarin Chinese, chapter 446: The Old Town.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
hútòng — lane; alley (in an old city).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a seashell, then the Mandarin for to ebb.

You'll want to know: hútòng.
hútòng — "lane; alley (in an old city)".
wǒ zhù zài yì tiáo xiǎo hútòng lǐ — I live in a little old lane.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
hútòng names the narrow lanes of old northern cities. No dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
城 (chéng) — town; walled city.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to ebb, then the Mandarin for a narrow old lane.

You'll want to know: 城.
城 — chéng — "town; walled city".
城里有很多老房子 chéng lǐ yǒu hěn duō lǎo fángzi — There are lots of old houses in the town.
The new character, in pieces: 城 — nine strokes in two pieces: on the left the earth piece of 地, three strokes; on the right 成 chéng, six strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
城 (chéng) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses it as the walls of a city and, after an older book, as what holds the people and their goods.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — the earth piece, then 成.
城 (chéng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the earth piece, three strokes, as on the left of 地: a short bar, a vertical, and a stroke that rises to the right.
The right piece, 成 chéng, six strokes, as you wrote it: a bar; a long stroke falling to the left; a small bar that turns down and hooks; the long curved stroke with a hook; a short stroke falling to the left across it; and a dot at the top right.
3 + 6 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 城 (chéng), all nine, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 城? (Nine.) What is the left piece? (The earth piece, as in 地.) What is the right piece? (成 chéng.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
古城 (gǔchéng) — ancient walled town; old town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a narrow old lane, then the Mandarin for a walled town.

You'll want to know: 古城.
古城 — gǔchéng — "ancient walled town; old town".
我们在古城里走了一个下午 wǒmen zài gǔchéng lǐ zǒu le yí ge xiàwǔ — We spent the afternoon walking round the old town.

The word, taken apart.
古城 (gǔchéng) is built from 古 'ancient' and 城 (chéng) 'a walled town', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kuX and dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 6 of 6.

You'll want to know: 进城.
进城 — jìnchéng — "to go to town; to move to the city".
村子里很多人进城打工 cūnzi lǐ hěn duō rén jìnchéng dǎgōng — A lot of people from the village move to the city for work.

The word, taken apart.
进城 (jìnchéng) is built from 进 'to enter' and 城 (chéng) 'a walled town', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsinH and dzyeng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go forward'.
