6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
болельщик (bolélshchik) — a fan, a supporter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a greenhouse, then the Russian for to prune.

You'll want to know: болельщик.
болельщик (bolélshchik) — "a fan, a supporter".
Болельщики пели весь матч. Bolélshchiki péli ves match. The fans sang throughout the match.
Masculine; feminine болельщица. Болеть за + accusative — to support a team.

The word, taken apart.
болельщик (bolélshchik) is built on болеть 'to be ill', and so 'to suffer for, to support', with the ending -льщик (EtymDB).

Все трибуны были полны. Vse tribúny býli polný. All the stands were full.
Feminine. Also a speaker's platform.

The word, taken apart.
трибуна (tribúna) goes back to Latin tribūnus 'a tribune' (EtymDB), usually through Italian tribuna, a raised platform.

Lesson 4 of 6.
подбадривать (podbádrivat) — to cheer on, to encourage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a stand, then the Russian for a whistle.

You'll want to know: подбадривать.
подбадривать (podbádrivat) — "to cheer on, to encourage".
Родители подбадривают детей во время соревнований. Rodíteli podbádrivayut detéy vo vrémya sorevnovániy. Parents cheer their children on during competitions.
Imperfective; perfective подбодрить. Built on бодрый — brisk, cheerful.

The word, taken apart.
подбадривать (podbádrivat) is built on под- and бодрый 'brisk, cheerful'; EtymDB has no entry for the verb, and бодрый is inherited from Proto-Slavic bъdrъ 'alert, cheerful' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ликовать (likovát) — to rejoice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a whistle, then the Russian for to cheer on.

You'll want to know: ликовать.
ликовать (likovát) — "to rejoice".
После победы весь стадион ликовал. Pósle pobédy ves stadión likovál. After the victory the whole stadium rejoiced.
Imperfective; present ликую, ликуешь. Rather bookish.

The word, taken apart.
ликовать (likovát) is usually traced to Church Slavonic ликъ 'a chorus, a round dance', so rejoicing was first dancing; EtymDB lists the verb without a source.
