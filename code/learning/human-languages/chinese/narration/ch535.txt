Mandarin Chinese, chapter 535: If You're Willing.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
愿意 (yuànyì) — to be willing (to).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give one's word, then the Mandarin for may all go as you wish.

You'll want to know: 愿意.
愿意 — yuànyì — "to be willing (to)".
要是你愿意我们明天就去 yàoshi nǐ yuànyì wǒmen míngtiān jiù qù — If you're willing, we'll go tomorrow.
The new character, in pieces: 愿 (yuàn) — fourteen strokes in four pieces: a bar and a long stroke falling to the left, like a cliff; under it 白, five strokes, and 小, three strokes; and 心 at the foot, four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
愿意 (yuànyì) is built from 愿 (yuàn) and 意 'what one means', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwonH and 'iH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to desire' and 'to think of'.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what would happen.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yuànyì, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: yàoshi nǐ yuànyì wǒmen míngtiān jiù qù — If you're willing, we'll go tomorrow.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to give one's word, then the Mandarin for may all go as you wish, then say yuànyì again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Lesson 2 of 6.
愿 (yuàn) — a cliff over 白 and 小, then 心.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — a cliff over 白 and 小, then 心 and Guided Practice.

Script — a cliff over 白 and 小, then 心.
愿 (yuàn).
Four pieces: the cliff, then what it shelters, then 心 at the foot. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The cliff, two strokes: a bar, then a long stroke falling to the left down from its left end.
Under the cliff, 白, five strokes, then 小, three strokes, as you wrote them: a short stroke falling to the left, a vertical, a bar that turns down, two short bars; then a vertical that hooks to the left, a short stroke on its left and a dot on its right.
心, four strokes, as you wrote it, across the foot: a dot, a long curve that hooks up, then two dots.
2 + 5 + 3 + 4 = fourteen.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 愿 (yuàn), all fourteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 愿 (yuàn) have? (Four: the cliff, 白, 小 and 心.) Which piece comes last? (心, at the foot.)

如愿 — rúyuàn — "to get what one wished for".
zhǐyào nǐ nǔlì jiù néng rúyuàn — As long as you work hard, you'll get what you hoped for.

The word, taken apart.
