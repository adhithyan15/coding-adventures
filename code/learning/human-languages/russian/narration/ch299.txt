Russian, chapter 299: The emergency call.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
носилки (nosílki) — a stretcher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to come out unhurt, then the Russian for to overturn.

You'll want to know: носилки.
носилки (nosílki) — "a stretcher".
Раненого несли на носилках. Ránenovo neslí na nosílkakh. The wounded man was carried on a stretcher.
Plural only: носилки (nosílki), genitive носилок.

The word, taken apart.
носилки (nosílki) is built on носить "to carry", which is inherited from Proto-Slavic nositi, from PIE h₁neḱ- "to carry" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: сирена.
сирена (siréna) — "a siren".
Вдалеке выла сирена. Vdaleké výla siréna. A siren was wailing in the distance.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
сирена (siréna) is usually traced through French sirène to Greek Seirḗn, a sea creature of legend whose song drew sailors; EtymDB lists no parent.

Lesson 3 of 5.
тревога (trevóga) — an alarm; anxiety.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a stretcher, then the Russian for a siren.

You'll want to know: тревога.
тревога (trevóga) — "an alarm; anxiety".
Ночью в школе зазвенел сигнал тревоги. Nóchyu v shkóle zazvenél signál trevógi. At night the alarm went off in the school.
Also "worry": с тревогой — anxiously. Пожарная тревога (trevóga) — a fire alarm.

The word, taken apart.
тревога (trevóga) is inherited from Proto-Slavic trĭvoga (EtymDB); its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
дежурный (dezhúrnyy) — the person on duty; on duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a siren, then the Russian for an alarm.

You'll want to know: дежурный.
дежурный (dezhúrnyy) — "the person on duty; on duty".
Дежурный врач сразу пришёл. Dezhúrnyy vrach srázu prishyól. The doctor on duty came at once.
An adjective that also serves as a noun: дежурный (dezhúrnyy) — the one on duty. Дежурить — to be on duty.

The word, taken apart.
дежурный (dezhúrnyy) is built on French de jour "for the day" (EtymDB), with the Russian ending -ный: the one whose day it is.

Lesson 5 of 5.
вызывать (vyzyvát) — to call (for), to send for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an alarm, then the Russian for the person on duty.

You'll want to know: вызывать.
вызывать (vyzyvát) — "to call (for), to send for".
Сосед вызвал скорую помощь. Soséd výzval skóruyu pómoshch. The neighbour called an ambulance.
Imperfective; perfective вызвать (výzvat), вызову. Вызывать врача, такси.

The word, taken apart.
вызывать (vyzyvát) is built on вы- "out" and звать "to call", an inherited Slavic verb: to call someone out. EtymDB lists no parent for the compound.
