Latin, chapter 348: Ōrāculum.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ōrāculum, ōrāculī — an oracle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a nymph, then the Latin for a fleece.

You'll want to know: ōrāculum, ōrāculī.
ōrāculum, ōrāculī — "an oracle".
Lēgātī ad ōrāculum missī sunt. Envoys were sent to the oracle.
Neuter, from ōrō, 'to speak, to pray' (taught). Both the place where a god answered and the answer itself.

The word, taken apart.
ōrāculum is built on ōrō, "to speak, to pray", with the ending -culum (Lewis & Short). English oracle comes from it through French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
vātēs, vātis — a seer, a prophet; a poet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fleece, then the Latin for an oracle.

You'll want to know: vātēs, vātis.
vātēs, vātis — "a seer, a prophet; a poet".
Vātēs fāta urbis cecinit. The seer sang of the fates of the city.
Masculine or feminine, 3rd declension. Poets liked to call themselves vātēs, inspired singers.

The word, taken apart.
vātēs is inherited; EtymDB traces it to a Proto-Indo-European word for "seer", and Lewis & Short compare an Old Irish word for a seer.

Lesson 3 of 5.
prōdigium, prōdigiī — a portent, a wonder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an oracle, then the Latin for a seer.

You'll want to know: prōdigium, prōdigiī.
prōdigium, prōdigiī — "a portent, a wonder".
Lapidibus pluit; hoc prōdigium omnēs terruit. It rained stones; this portent frightened everyone.
Neuter. Roman historians list the prōdigia of each year: a rain of stones, a cow that spoke. English prodigy.

The word, taken apart.
prōdigium is explained by Lewis & Short as standing for prōdicium, from prōdīcō, "to foretell": a sign that speaks of what will come. English prodigy comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ōmen, ōminis — an omen, a sign.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a seer, then the Latin for a portent.

You'll want to know: ōmen, ōminis.
ōmen, ōminis — "an omen, a sign".
Hoc ōmen bonum esse putāvērunt. They thought that this omen was good.
Neuter, 3rd declension. A chance word or sight that seemed to foretell the future.

The word, taken apart.
ōmen had an old form osmen, says Varro, quoted by Lewis & Short; its earlier origin is not settled. English omen is the same word.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vāticinor, vāticinārī — to prophesy, to foretell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a portent, then the Latin for an omen.

You'll want to know: vāticinor, vāticinārī.
vāticinor, vāticinārī — "to prophesy, to foretell".
Vātēs bellum longum vāticinātus est. The seer foretold a long war.
vāticinor, vāticinārī, vāticinātus sum, a deponent, from vātēs + canō.

The word, taken apart.
vāticinor is built on vātēs, "seer" (Lewis & Short), with a second part from canō, "to sing": to sing as a seer.
