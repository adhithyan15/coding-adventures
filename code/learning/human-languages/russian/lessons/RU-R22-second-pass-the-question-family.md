# One stem, a whole family of questions

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-KAKOY-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] The word for *which, what kind of* — the one this chapter finally
taught after naming it two chapters earlier. Say it before reading on.

## You'll want to know: they are all one stem with different endings
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-QUESTION-FAMILY-01, RU-LEX-KAKOY-01] -->

The question words are not a list to memorise separately. **Every one of them is
the same ancient question-stem wearing a different ending**: кто who, что what,
какой which, как how, and the rest of the set beside them.

Once the stem is visible, a word you have not met is half-readable on sight —
which is what a family is for, and why the chapter taught them together rather
than one per page.

## Grammar Lens: который hangs a clause, ли hangs a doubt
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-KOTORYY-CLAUSE-01, RU-LEX-KOTORYY-01, RU-GRAMMAR-LI-01] -->

Two of the family do more than ask:

- **который** — who, which, that. It takes a whole clause and hangs it on a
  noun, which is how a sentence stops being short.
- **ли** — whether. You met it six chapters earlier welded inside **или**, and
  the book left it alone until it could stand on its own.

**ли** is the one to hold. It marks a question you are not asking out loud —
a doubt reported rather than posed — and English reaches for *whether* in the
same place. Its relative **который** has the same cousin in English *whether*,
which is a link worth keeping because it makes both words feel less arbitrary.
