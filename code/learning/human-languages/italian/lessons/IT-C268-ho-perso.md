# ho perso il treno — when it goes wrong

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-ANDATA-E-RITORNO-01, IT-LEX-SCUSI-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Ask for a one-way ticket to Rome. (*Vorrei un biglietto per Roma,
solo andata.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the ticket is in your pocket, and the train has gone without
you.

## You'll want to know: ho perso il treno
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-HO-PERSO-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C234-VERBI234-05, IT-GRAMMAR-PASSATO-PROSSIMO-02, IT-LEX-C258-COSE258-04] -->

**ho perso il treno** — "I've missed the train".

You have **perdere**, "to lose". Italian uses the same verb for a train you
missed: you **lost** it. Its past does not follow the *-uto* pattern of
*credere*, *creduto*. The participle is short: **perso**.

> **Ho perso il treno.** — I've missed the train.
> **Ho perso la coincidenza.** — I've missed my connection.
> **Ho perso il biglietto.** — I've lost my ticket.

Italian says *il* biglietto where English says *my* ticket: whose it is goes
without saying.

## Grammar Lens: reporting a problem
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-HO-UN-PROBLEMA-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C115-SCUOLA115-03, IT-LEX-C258-COSE258-03, IT-GRAMMAR-CE-02] -->

A complaint to a stranger has three parts, and the first is a courtesy:

> **Scusi, ho un problema.** — Excuse me, I have a problem.
> **Ho perso il treno.** — What went wrong.
> **C'è un altro treno oggi? A che ora?** — What you need.

Or the fault is not yours: *Il treno è in ritardo*, "the train is late". Say
what happened, in the passato prossimo, then ask the question that gets you
moving again.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**perdere** is inherited from Latin *perdere* (EtymDB 2.1), built on *per-*,
"through", and *dare*, "to give": to give something away for good, to
squander, to lose (Lewis & Short, under *perdo*). Its Latin participle was
*perditus* (Lewis & Short), but Italian made new ones of its own: the short
**perso**, and the longer *perduto*, which sounds older and more literary.
Neither source says how *perso* was formed, so that step is left open here.
