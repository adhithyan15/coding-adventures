Spanish, chapter 641: En taxi.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el taxi — the taxi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the van, then the Spanish for the driver.

You'll want to know: el taxi.
el taxi — "the taxi".
Cogemos un taxi a la estación. We are taking a taxi to the station.
Coger un taxi in Spain; in much of Latin America say tomar un taxi, because coger is vulgar there.

The word, taken apart.
taxi is short for taxímetro, which came from French taximètre, a meter that measures the charge; it is built on Medieval Latin taxa "charge, rate" and Greek métron "measure" (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
el taxista — the taxi driver.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the driver, then the Spanish for the taxi.

You'll want to know: el taxista.
el taxista — "the taxi driver".
El taxista conoce todas las calles. The taxi driver knows every street.
One form for a man or a woman: el taxista, la taxista.

The word, taken apart.
taxista is built on taxi with -ista, the ending for someone who does a job, which comes from Greek -istḗs through Latin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la tarifa — the fare, the rate; the price list.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the taxi, then the Spanish for the taxi driver.

You'll want to know: la tarifa.
la tarifa — "the fare, the rate; the price list".
La tarifa de noche es más cara. The night fare is more expensive.
For a phone or for electricity too: una tarifa plana is a flat rate.

The word, taken apart.
tarifa comes from Arabic taʿrīfa "a notice, a list of rates" (EtymDB), from the verb ʿarrafa "to make known".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el trayecto — the journey, the stretch (from one place to another).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the taxi driver, then the Spanish for the fare.

You'll want to know: el trayecto.
el trayecto — "the journey, the stretch (from one place to another)".
El trayecto dura veinte minutos. The journey takes twenty minutes.
One leg of the way; el viaje is the whole trip.

The word, taken apart.
trayecto comes from Latin trāiectus "a crossing over, a passage" (Lewis & Short), from trāicere "to throw across", built from trans and iacere "to throw".

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fare, then the Spanish for the journey.

You'll want to know: parar.
parar — "to stop".
¿Puede parar aquí, por favor? Can you stop here, please?
Pararse is to come to a stop; in Latin America it also means to stand up.

The word, taken apart.
parar is inherited from Latin parāre "to make or get ready, to prepare" (Lewis & Short; EtymDB).
