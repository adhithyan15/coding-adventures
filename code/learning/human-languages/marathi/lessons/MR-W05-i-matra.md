# ि — short *i*, written before, read after

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

Say *dhanyavād* once from memory. Every mark in this chapter hangs on a consonant your hand already knows.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-DHANYAVAD-WRITE-01] -->

> ि

The short-*i* mark is the one that breaks the rule you would guess. It is
**written to the LEFT** of its consonant and **read to the RIGHT** of it:
**कि** is *ki*, never *ik*. The eye meets the mark first; the ear meets it
second.

Draw the upright on the left of the letter, then carry the curve up and over
the headline so it hooks back to the right. The hook is what tells this mark
apart from its long partner, which you will meet later.

## Writing — observe and trace
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: observe-trace -->

Keep **कि** in front of you. Trace the mark alone twice, then trace it beside
**क** twice more. Say *ki* each time your pen finishes the hook, so the hand and
the ear learn the same order the page will use.
