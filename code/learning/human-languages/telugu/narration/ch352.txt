Telugu, chapter 352: The Chase.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
వెంబడించు (vembaḍiñcu) — to pursue; to chase.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to release, then the Telugu for to free from captivity.

You'll want to know: వెంబడించు.
వెంబడించు — vembaḍiñcu — "to pursue; to chase".
పోలీసులు దొంగను కారులో వెంబడించారు. pōlīsulu doṅganu kārulō vembaḍiñcāru. The police chased the thief in a car.
Past (I …): వెంబడించాను, vembaḍiñcānu.
వెంబడి means close behind; తరుము is the rougher, more spoken word.

The word, taken apart.
వెంబడించు (vembaḍiñcu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 5488 lists Telugu vembaḍi, "along with", and vembaḍincu, under the word for "the back".

Lesson 2 of 5.
దాక్కో (dākkō) — to hide oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to free from captivity, then the Telugu for to pursue.

You'll want to know: దాక్కో.
దాక్కో — dākkō — "to hide oneself".
దొంగ చెట్టు వెనక దాక్కున్నాడు. doṅga ceṭṭu venaka dākkunnāḍu. The thief hid behind a tree.
Past (I …): దాక్కున్నాను, dākkunnānu.
దాచు is to hide a thing; దాక్కో (dākkō) is to hide yourself.

The word, taken apart.
దాక్కో (dākkō) is native Dravidian: DEDR 63 lists Telugu dāgu, "to lie hidden", beside dācu, "to hide, conceal"; దాక్కో adds the self-ending -kō.

Lesson 3 of 5.
లొంగిపోవు (loṅgipōvu) — to surrender; to give in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to pursue, then the Telugu for to hide oneself.

You'll want to know: లొంగిపోవు.
లొంగిపోవు — loṅgipōvu — "to surrender; to give in".
చివరికి దొంగ పోలీసులకు లొంగిపోయాడు. civariki doṅga pōlīsulaku loṅgipōyāḍu. In the end the thief surrendered to the police.
Past (I …): లొంగిపోయాను, loṅgipōyānu.
To whom takes -కు: పోలీసులకు లొంగిపోవు (loṅgipōvu).

The word, taken apart.
లొంగిపోవు (loṅgipōvu) is native Dravidian: DEDR 698 lists Telugu loṅgu, "to yield, submit", with a query, and pōvu, "to go" (DEDR 4572), completes the action.

Lesson 4 of 5.
పట్టించు (paṭṭiñcu) — to turn in (to the police).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to hide oneself, then the Telugu for to surrender.

You'll want to know: పట్టించు.
పట్టించు — paṭṭiñcu — "to turn in (to the police)".
గ్రామస్తులు దొంగను పోలీసులకు పట్టించారు. grāmastulu doṅganu pōlīsulaku paṭṭiñcāru. The villagers turned the thief in to the police.
Past (I …): పట్టించాను, paṭṭiñcānu.
The causative of పట్టు, to catch: to get someone caught. Not the same as పట్టించుకో, to pay attention.

The word, taken apart.
పట్టించు (paṭṭiñcu) is native Dravidian, the causative of paṭṭu, "to hold, catch" (DEDR 4034).

Lesson 5 of 5.
తప్పుకో (tappukō) — to step aside; to withdraw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for to surrender, then the Telugu for to turn in.

You'll want to know: తప్పుకో.
తప్పుకో — tappukō — "to step aside; to withdraw".
పోలీసు జీపు రాగానే జనం తప్పుకున్నారు. pōlīsu jīpu rāgānē janaṁ tappukunnāru. As soon as the police jeep came, people stepped aside.
Past (I …): తప్పుకున్నాను, tappukunnānu.
Also to pull out: పోటీ నుంచి తప్పుకో (tappukō), to withdraw from a contest.

The word, taken apart.
తప్పుకో (tappukō) is native Dravidian: tappu, "to miss, slip, escape" (DEDR 3071), with the self-ending -kō: to slip out of the way.
