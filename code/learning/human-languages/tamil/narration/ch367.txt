Tamil, chapter 367: Wisdom and Ignorance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ஞானம் (ñāṉam) — wisdom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a formula, then the Tamil for to define.

You'll want to know: ஞானம்.
ஞானம் — ñāṉam — "wisdom".
அனுபவத்தால் ஞானம் வருகிறது. aṉupavattāl ñāṉam varukiṟatu. Wisdom comes with experience.
A deeper word than அறிவு, knowledge; ஞானி, a sage.

The word, taken apart.
ஞானம் (ñāṉam) is borrowed from Sanskrit jñāna, "knowledge" (CDIAL 5281). Tamil writes the cluster jñ with one letter, ஞ, as Pali does with ñāṇa.

Lesson 2 of 5.
அறியாமை (aṟiyāmai) — ignorance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for to define, then the Tamil for wisdom.

You'll want to know: அறியாமை.
அறியாமை — aṟiyāmai — "ignorance".
அவன் அறியாமையால் அந்தத் தவறைச் செய்தான். avaṉ aṟiyāmaiyāl antat tavaṟaic ceytāṉ. He made that mistake out of ignorance.
அறி, to know + -ஆ-, not + -மை: not-knowing. பொறாமை, envy, has the same shape.

The word, taken apart.
அறியாமை (aṟiyāmai) is native: aṟi, "to know" (DEDR 314), with the negative -ஆ- and the abstract ending -மை: "the state of not knowing".

Lesson 3 of 5.
அறிஞர் (aṟiñar) — a scholar.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for wisdom, then the Tamil for ignorance.

You'll want to know: அறிஞர்.
அறிஞர் — aṟiñar — "a scholar".
அவர் ஒரு பெரிய தமிழ் அறிஞர். avar oru periya tamiḻ aṟiñar. He is a great Tamil scholar.
-ஞர், one who: கலைஞர், an artist, has the same ending.

The word, taken apart.
அறிஞர் (aṟiñar) is native: aṟi, "to know" (DEDR 314), with the ending -ஞர், "one who", as in கலைஞர்: "one who knows".

Lesson 4 of 5.
மேதை (mētai) — a genius.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for ignorance, then the Tamil for a scholar.

You'll want to know: மேதை.
மேதை — mētai — "a genius".
அந்தச் சிறுவன் கணிதத்தில் ஒரு மேதை. antac ciṟuvaṉ kaṇitattil oru mētai. That boy is a genius at mathematics.
X-இல் மேதை (mētai), a genius at X.

The word, taken apart.
மேதை (mētai) is borrowed from Sanskrit medhā, "mental vigour, intelligence, wisdom" (Monier-Williams); Tamil writes the final -ā as -ஐ and the dh as த.

Lesson 5 of 5.
போதி (pōti) — to teach formally; to preach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a scholar, then the Tamil for a genius.

You'll want to know: போதி.
போதி — pōti — "to teach formally; to preach".
அந்த ஆசிரியர் மாணவர்களுக்கு நேர்மையைப் போதித்தார். anta āciriyar māṇavarkaḷukku nērmaiyaip pōtittār. That teacher taught the students honesty.
Past (I …): போதித்தேன், pōtittēṉ.
A formal, often moral teach: the person taught takes -க்கு, the lesson -ஐ.

The word, taken apart.
போதி (pōti) is a verb built on Sanskrit bodha, "waking, understanding" (CDIAL 9314), from the root budh, "to wake, to know": to make someone understand.
