# (reading) — six words under one line

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-BHAT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Every word below you have written. Not one is new.

[PAUSE 1s] What is new is that nobody says them first.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SKILL-READ-WORDS]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01, BN-LEX-C10-JOL-01] -->

> ভাত
> চা
> জল
> দুধ
> ভাই
> বোন

[PAUSE 3s] Read down the list once, without stopping.

[PAUSE 3s] Again, and look at the top of each word rather than at the letters.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C10-DUDH-01, BN-LEX-C11-BHAI-01, BN-LEX-C11-BON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Bengali hangs its letters from a line instead of standing them on
one. That line runs unbroken across a whole word and stops at its edge.

[PAUSE 2s] So the line is a word boundary you can see before you have read
anything. A Bengali word looks like one object first and a row of signs second,
which is the opposite of how you learned to write it.

[PAUSE 2s] **ভাই** and **বোন** are the shortest pair here and the easiest to
tell apart at a glance, for exactly that reason.
