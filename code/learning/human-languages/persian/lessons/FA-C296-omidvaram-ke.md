# امیدوارم که (omidvâram ke) — I hope that, I'd like to, I dream of

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C138-AYANDE138-05, FA-GRAMMAR-MIKHAHAM-SUBJ-01, FA-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-COPULA-AM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to hope", then "I want to go". (*Omidvâr budan*, *mikhâham
beravam*.) Then "I'm well", in one word. (*Khubam.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Describing an experience runs forward as well as back: what you
hope for next, and what you would like to become.

## Grammar Lens: omidvâram ke, and the subjunctive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-OMIDVARAM-KE-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C229-ZAMAN229-04, FA-GRAMMAR-SUBJUNCTIVE-BE-01] -->

*Omidvâr* takes the *-am* of *khubam*: **امیدوارم**, *omidvâram*, "I am
hopeful", "I hope". What you hope for follows *ke* in the subjunctive, because
it has not happened:

> **امیدوارم که سال دیگر به تهران بروم.** — *omidvâram ke sâl-e digar be
> Tehrân beravam* — I hope to go to Tehran next year.

Chapter one hundred and thirty-eight showed *omidvâram hâlat khub bâshad*,
"I hope you are well". The *bâshad* there is this subjunctive, and the *ke*
has been left out, as speech often does.

## Grammar Lens: what you'd like, and what you dream of
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-DUST-DARAM-SUBJ-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-DUST-DASHTAN, FA-LEX-C90-CAN90-04, FA-LEX-C250-KAR250-02, FA-LEX-C250-KAR250-05, FA-LEX-C40-VERBS40-01] -->

*Dust dâram*, "I like", with a subjunctive after it is "I'd like to":

> **دوست دارم مهندس بشوم.** — *dust dâram mohandes beshavam* — I'd like to
> become an engineer.

*Beshavam* is *shodan* in the subjunctive, on its stem *shav-*; in speech it
is shortened to *besham*. *Ârezu*, "a wish, a dream", works the same way with
*dâram*:

> **آرزو دارم که یک روز خلبان بشوم.** — *ârezu dâram ke yek ruz khalabân
> beshavam* — My dream is to become a pilot one day.

So three strengths: *mikhâham*, I want; *dust dâram*, I'd like; *ârezu dâram*,
I dream of. All three take the subjunctive.
