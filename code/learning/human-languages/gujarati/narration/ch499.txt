Gujarati, chapter 499: Saying Goodbye.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
મરણ (maraṇ) — a death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a dowry, then the Gujarati for a divorce.

You'll want to know: મરણ.
મરણ — maraṇ — "a death".
દાદાના મરણથી આખા કુટુંબને દુઃખ થયું. dādānā maraṇthī ākhā kuṭumbne duḥkh thayũ. The whole family grieved at my grandfather's death.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
મરણ (maraṇ) is inherited from Sanskrit maraṇa, "dying"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word (CDIAL 9869).

Lesson 2 of 5.
અવસાન (avsān) — a passing away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a divorce, then the Gujarati for a death.

You'll want to know: અવસાન.
અવસાન — avsān — "a passing away".
ગયા મહિને મારા મામાનું અવસાન થયું. gayā mahine mārā māmānũ avsān thayũ. My uncle passed away last month.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
અવસાન (avsān) is a tatsama, Sanskrit avasāna, which Monier-Williams lists under ava-so as "conclusion, termination" and also as "death"; Gujarati uses it as the polite word for a death.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a death, then the Gujarati for a passing away.

You'll want to know: ગુજરવું.
ગુજરવું — gujarvũ — "to pass away".
દાદી ઊંઘમાં જ ગુજરી ગયાં. dādī ū̃ghmā̃ ja gujrī gayā̃. Grandmother passed away in her sleep.
Past (he …): તે ગુજરી ગયો, te gujrī gayo.

The word, taken apart.
ગુજરવું (gujarvũ) is usually traced to Persian guzar-, the stem of the verb "to pass, go by".

Lesson 4 of 5.
બેસણું (besṇũ) — a prayer meeting after a death.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a passing away, then the Gujarati for to pass away.

You'll want to know: બેસણું.
બેસણું — besṇũ — "a prayer meeting after a death".
સોમવારે સાંજે દાદાનું બેસણું છે. somvāre sā̃je dādānũ besṇũ che. Grandfather's prayer meeting is on Monday evening.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
બેસણું (besṇũ) is built on બેસવું (besvũ), "to sit", which goes back to Sanskrit upaviśati, "sits down" (CDIAL 2245): the family sits together to receive visitors.

The word, taken apart.
અર્પવું (arpavũ) goes back to Sanskrit arpayati, "places upon, offers", the causative of ṛ, "to go" (Monier-Williams).
