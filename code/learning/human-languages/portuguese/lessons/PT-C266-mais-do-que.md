# mais … do que — more than

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-MAIS-OU-MENOS-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Answer *tudo bem?* with a shrug. (*Mais ou menos.*) Literally,
"more or less".

[PAUSE 1s] Those two words, **mais** and **menos**, are the whole of this
lesson's new grammar.

## You'll want to know: mais … do que
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-MAIS-DO-QUE-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C66-ADJ3-01, PT-LEX-C66-ADJ3-05, PT-LEX-C74-CIDADE2-02, PT-LEX-C92-VIAGEM92-04, PT-LEX-C67-ADJ4-02] -->

To compare two things, put **mais**, "more", in front of the adjective and
**do que**, "than", in front of the second thing:

> O comboio é **mais** rápido **do que** o autocarro. — The train is faster
> than the bus.
> O avião é **mais** caro **do que** o comboio. — The plane is dearer than the
> train.
> És **mais** alto **do que** eu. — You are taller than me.

English often builds the comparison into the adjective, *faster*, *taller*;
Portuguese builds it with *mais*. After *do que* a person stays
in the plain form, *eu*, *tu*: *do que eu*. You will also hear plain *que*,
*mais rápido que o autocarro*, in Portugal and in Brazil alike.

Its opposite is **menos**, "less": *O autocarro é **menos** caro **do que** o
comboio* — the bus is less expensive than the train.

## Grammar Lens: o mais, and two that refuse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-O-MAIS-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C65-ADJ2-01, PT-LEX-C175-VERBOS175-05, PT-LEX-C205-VERBOS205-01] -->

For the most of all, keep *mais* and give the noun its article:

> *o comboio **mais** rápido* — the fastest train
> *a cidade **mais** bonita* — the most beautiful city

The adjective still comes after the noun, and the article does the work of
English *-est*. Who it is the most among comes with *de*: *a cidade mais
bonita **de** Portugal*.

A few adjectives refuse *mais*, and two of them come first. *Bom* becomes **melhor**, "better", and *mau*
becomes **pior**, "worse": *o comboio é melhor do que o autocarro*. You have
heard both already, inside **melhorar**, "to get better", and **piorar**, "to
get worse".

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**mais** is inherited from Latin *magis*, "more" (EtymDB 2.1), and so is the
**mas** of chapter two hundred and sixty: "more" became "rather", and "rather"
became "but". **menos** has no parent in EtymDB 2.1; it is usually traced to
Latin *minus*, "less" (Lewis & Short), which English kept whole as **minus**.
**melhor** is inherited from Latin *melior*, "better" (EtymDB 2.1).
