# આભાર (ābhār) — "thank you," an obligation gladly owed

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Gujarati's everyday "thank you" — and it comes from the idea of being
*indebted*.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-AABHAAR-01]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

**આ** (the independent long *ā*, for a word-initial vowel) + **ભા** (*bhā*) + **ર**
(*r*) → **આભાર** (*ābhār*). Note again: no top line.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-CONCEPT-C01-AABHAAR-01, GU-CONCEPT-C01-NAMASTE-01] -->

**આભાર** (*ābhār*, "thanks, gratitude") is from Sanskrit **આભાર** (*ābhāra*, "a
burden, an obligation"), from **આ-** (*ā-*, "toward") + the root **ભૃ** (*bhṛ*,
"to bear, carry"). To thank someone is to acknowledge you now *bear an
obligation* to them — the same idea as English **oblige** (Latin *ob-* + *ligāre*
"to bind") and Portuguese *obrigado*. That root *bhṛ* is Proto-Indo-European
**\*bʰer-**, the ancestor of English **bear** (to carry) and Latin *ferre*. (The
more Sanskritic **ધન્યવાદ** *dhanyavād* is also used, but *ābhār* is the warm,
everyday Gujarati word.)
