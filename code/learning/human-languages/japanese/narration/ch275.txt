Japanese, chapter 275: The Great Wave.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
つなみ (tsunami) — a tsunami; a tidal wave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for frightening, then the Japanese for evacuation.

You'll want to know: つなみ.
つなみ — tsunami — "a tsunami; a tidal wave".
つなみの ばあいは、やまに にげて ください。 — tsunami no baai wa, yama ni nigete kudasai — "In case of a tsunami, please run to high ground."

The word, taken apart.
つなみ (tsunami) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tsu, "harbour", and nami, "wave" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

かわの みずが はやく ながれています。 — kawa no mizu ga hayaku nagarete imasu — "The river is running fast."
Polite ながれます, nagaremasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): くるまが ながれました。, kuruma ga nagaremashita.

The word, taken apart.
ながれる (nagareru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).

ながす (nagasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the transitive partner of nagareru, "to flow".
