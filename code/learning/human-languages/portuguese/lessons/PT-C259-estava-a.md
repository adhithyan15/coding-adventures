# estava a dormir quando… — the scene, and what cut into it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01, PT-GRAMMAR-PERFEITO-I-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was sleeping", then "I slept". (*Dormia*, then *dormi*.)

[PAUSE 1s] There is a second way to say the first one, and in Portugal it is
the way you will hear most.

## Grammar Lens: estava a, and an infinitive
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-ESTAVA-A-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-SER-ESTAR-02, PT-LEX-LER-02] -->

Take **estar**, put it in the imperfeito, add **a** and an infinitive:

> **Estava a dormir.** — I was sleeping.
> **Estava a ler.** — I was reading.
> **Estávamos a falar.** — We were talking.

*Estar* follows the plain *-ar* recipe here: *estava*, *estavas*, *estava*,
*estávamos*, *estavam*. Only the *estar* changes; the infinitive after *a*
stays as it is.

*Estava a ler* and *lia* both paint the scene. *Estava a* holds it still at one
moment, a reading caught in progress; *lia* can also mean "I used to read".
The present works the same way: *estou a ler*, I am reading right now.

This *a* plus infinitive is the Portuguese norm. In Brazil, and in parts of
southern Portugal, the same idea takes a form ending in *-ndo*: *estava
dormindo*. Both are understood everywhere.

## Grammar Lens: quando, where the event comes in
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-QUANDO-CENA-02]; assesses=[PT-LEX-C53-COISAS2-01, PT-GRAMMAR-JOGAR-BRINCAR-TOCAR-04] -->

**quando** means "when", and it puts a scene and an event into one sentence:

> *Estava a dormir **quando** a minha mãe telefonou.*
> — I was sleeping when my mother phoned.
> *Estava a ler **quando** o telefone tocou.*
> — I was reading when the phone rang.

The *estava a* half is the scene, still running. The *quando* half is the
event that cut into it, in the perfeito: *telefonou*, *tocou*. You can turn the
sentence round, and the tenses keep their jobs: *Quando o telefone tocou, eu
estava a ler.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**estar** is inherited from Latin *stāre*, "to stand" (EtymDB 2.1), so *estava
a ler* is, word for word, "I was standing at reading": caught in the middle
of it. **quando** is inherited from Latin *quandō*, "when" (EtymDB 2.1; Lewis &
Short), almost unchanged.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-ESTAVA-A-01, PT-GRAMMAR-QUANDO-CENA-02, PT-GRAMMAR-CENA-EVENTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What goes between *estava* and the infinitive? (***A.***) In *estava
a ler quando o telefone tocou*, which half is the scene? (***Estava a ler.***)
Which past does the *quando* half take here? (The **perfeito** — *tocou*, the
event.) And what does *estar* mean, at its root? (**To stand.**)
