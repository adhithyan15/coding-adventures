Lesson 1 of 5.
せんたくもの (sentakumono) — the laundry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a heated table, then the Japanese for heating.

You'll want to know: せんたくもの.
せんたくもの — sentakumono — "the laundry".

The word, taken apart.
せんたくもの (sentakumono) mixes the two layers: sentaku, "washing", Sino-Japanese sen, "wash", and taku, "rinse" (Middle Chinese draewk), borrowed from Chinese, with native mono, "thing" (JMdict; Unihan, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
せんざい (senzai) — detergent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for heating, then the Japanese for the laundry.

You'll want to know: せんざい.
せんざい — senzai — "detergent".

The word, taken apart.
せんざい (senzai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sen, "wash" and zai, "medicinal preparation" (Middle Chinese dzejH) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かま (kama) — a rice pot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for detergent, then the Japanese for a teacup.

You'll want to know: かま.
かま — kama — "a rice pot".

The word, taken apart.
かま (kama) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
