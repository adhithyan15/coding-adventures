Arabic, chapter 251: Waiter, Manager, Employee, Colleague, Company.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نادل (nādil) — a waiter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for rubber, then the Arabic for a triangle.

You'll want to know: نادل.
نادل — nādil — "a waiter".

The word, taken apart.
نادل (nādil) is a modern word for a waiter. It has the shape of an active participle of n-d-l, a root Lane uses for mindīl, "a napkin"; how the waiter sense arose is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مدير (mudīr) — a manager, a director.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a triangle, then the Arabic for a waiter.

You'll want to know: مدير.
مدير — mudīr — "a manager, a director".

The word, taken apart.
مدير (mudīr) is built on the native Arabic root d-w-r, "to turn round" (Lane). Mudīr is the Form IV participle, "one who makes things turn", a manager.

موظف (muwaẓẓaf) is built on the native Arabic root w-ẓ-f, "to appoint (work); a daily allowance" (Lane). Muwaẓẓaf is the Form II passive participle, "one given a fixed post".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a manager, then the Arabic for an employee.

You'll want to know: زميل.
زميل — zamīl — "a colleague".

The word, taken apart.
زميل (zamīl) is built on the native Arabic root z-m-l (Lane). Lane's zamīl is one who rides behind another on the same beast; a colleague is a later sense.
