Russian, chapter 452: Food shopping.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
булочная (búlochnaya) — a bakery (shop).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an addressee, then the Russian for to issue.

You'll want to know: булочная.
булочная (búlochnaya) — "a bakery (shop)".
Утром я покупаю хлеб в булочной. Útrom ya pokupáyu khleb v búlochnoy. In the morning I buy bread at the bakery.
An adjective used as a noun. Traditionally чн is pronounced шн here.

The word, taken apart.
булочная (búlochnaya) is built on булочка, булка 'a roll' with an adjective ending: the shop for rolls. EtymDB lists булка without a deeper source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
гастроном (gastronóm) — a grocery store, a food shop.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to issue, then the Russian for a bakery.

You'll want to know: гастроном.
гастроном (gastronóm) — "a grocery store, a food shop".
В гастрономе на углу мы купили сыр и колбасу. V gastronóme na uglú my kupíli syr i kolbasú. We bought cheese and sausage at the grocery store on the corner.
Masculine. Also an old word for a gourmet.

The word, taken apart.
гастроном (gastronóm) is usually traced, probably through French gastronome, to Greek gastḗr 'belly' and nómos 'law': first a gourmet, then a food shop. EtymDB does not list it, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
прилавок (prilávok) — a counter (in a shop).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bakery, then the Russian for a grocery store.

You'll want to know: прилавок.
прилавок (prilávok) — "a counter (in a shop)".
Продавец стоит за прилавком. Prodavéts stoít za prilávkom. The shop assistant stands behind the counter.
Masculine; the о drops out: прилавка.

The word, taken apart.
прилавок (prilávok) is built from при- 'beside' and лавка 'a bench; a shop', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic lavŭka 'a bench, a shop' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
продавщица (prodavshchítsa) — a shop assistant (woman).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a grocery store, then the Russian for a counter.

You'll want to know: продавщица.
продавщица (prodavshchítsa) — "a shop assistant (woman)".
Продавщица улыбнулась и дала мне сдачу. Prodavshchítsa ulybnúlas i dalá mne sdáchu. The shop assistant smiled and gave me my change.
The feminine of продавец.

The word, taken apart.
продавщица (prodavshchítsa) is the feminine of продавец 'a seller', from продавать 'to sell', on давать 'to give', which is inherited from Proto-Slavic dāvati (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
взвешивать (vzvéshivat) — to weigh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a counter, then the Russian for a shop assistant.

You'll want to know: взвешивать.
взвешивать (vzvéshivat) — "to weigh".
Продавщица взвешивает яблоки. Prodavshchítsa vzvéshivayet yábloki. The shop assistant weighs the apples.
Imperfective; perfective взвесить (взвешу, взвесишь). Взвесить решение — to weigh up a decision.

The word, taken apart.
взвешивать (vzvéshivat) is built from вз- and -вешивать, from вес 'weight'. EtymDB lists the verb without a deeper source.
