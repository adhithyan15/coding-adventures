## Assessment: the pre-A1 task inventory, a speaking paper, and the sandhi decision

Sanskrit's pre-A1 four-skill task inventory is checked in, with the assessment
specification it cites. `reading-reach` now measures the track — the chapter-64
passage's 51 words against a 1-word ceiling, **3/3 parts in reach**, with a floor
committed.

**Why a classical language gets a speaking paper.** The obvious answer — test
reading and writing and stop — would quietly change what this book is, and it is
wrong about the world. **Samskrita Bharati**, the largest Sanskrit-teaching
movement there is, teaches Sanskrit *through conversation*: a graded ladder of
Pravesha, Parichaya, Shiksha and Kovida, and a four-year certificate named
**सम्भाषणतः शास्त्रपर्यन्तम्**, *from conversation to the śāstras*. The order in
that title is the argument. Speech is where the training starts, not what is
bolted on at the end. So four skills is the right shape, and the contract says
who the listener is: a trained speaker of the living spoken register.

**The sandhi decision.** Written Sanskrit joins its words, and an examination
has to say which form it requires, because external sandhi changes the surface
of nearly every word in a sentence. Pre-A1 and A1 present and accept **unjoined**
text — which is what the book itself writes, and what lets a first reader see
the joints while they are still learning where they are; a candidate who applies
sandhi correctly is not penalised. From A2, reading input is joined and written
production is scored for it, because by then the machinery has been taught and a
reader who cannot undo a join cannot read. Internal sandhi is in scope
throughout.

**What the writing paper scores** is the **word-final visarga or anusvāra**, by
name rather than folded into general orthography. Sanskrit's noun families are
told apart by how a word ends, and these two carry most of that work — the
corpus writes **1,011** visargas against **103** anusvāras. Confusing them is
not a spelling slip: it puts the word in the wrong family, and everything
agreeing with it goes wrong too. The **reading** part scores telling them apart,
because a reader has to see the difference before a writer can make it.

**The writing ramp, measured.** 50 script lessons and **not one writing-stage
directive**, while the pre-A1 paper requires delayed recall and dictation.
