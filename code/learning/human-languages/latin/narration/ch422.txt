7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
dīvitiae, dīvitiārum — riches, wealth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for harbour dues, then the Latin for to gain.

You'll want to know: dīvitiae, dīvitiārum.
dīvitiae, dīvitiārum — "riches, wealth".
Dīvitiae virum nōn semper fēlīcem faciunt. Riches do not always make a man happy.
dīvitiae, dīvitiārum f. pl., built on dīves, rich.

The word, taken apart.
dīvitiae is built on dīves, dīvitis "rich" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 7.
egeō, egēre — to be in need (of), to lack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to gain, then the Latin for riches.

You'll want to know: egeō, egēre.
egeō, egēre — "to be in need (of), to lack".
Pecūniā egeō. I am short of money.
egeō, egēre, eguī + ablative or genitive. Indigeō is a stronger form; English indigent comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of egeō is not settled; Lewis & Short give no sure account. English indigent comes from its compound indigeō.

egestās, egestātis f., built on egeō, to be in need.

The word, taken apart.
egestās is built on egeō "to be in need" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -tās that makes a state.

Lesson 4 of 7.
careō, carēre — to be without, to lack.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to be in need, then the Latin for want.

You'll want to know: careō, carēre.
careō, carēre — "to be without, to lack".
Multī cīvēs domō carent. Many citizens have no home.
careō, carēre, caruī + ablative: careō pecūniā, I have no money. Unlike egeō, it says the thing is absent without saying that you need it.

The word, taken apart.
careō is inherited from Proto-Italic kazēō (EtymDB), from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to cut": to be cut off from a thing.

Lesson 5 of 7.
abundō, abundāre — to have plenty (of), to abound; to overflow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for want, then the Latin for to be without.

You'll want to know: abundō, abundāre.
abundō, abundāre — "to have plenty (of), to abound; to overflow".
Urbs frūmentō abundat. The city has plenty of grain.
abundō, abundāre, abundāvī, abundātum + ablative, built on unda, a wave: to overflow. English abound and abundant.

The word, taken apart.
abundō is built from ab "away, over" and undō "to surge", from unda "a wave" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to flow over. English abound and abundant come from it.
