German, chapter 68: To carry, To send, To count, To paint, To clean.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tragen — to carry; to wear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to live, then the German for to call.

You'll want to know: tragen.
tragen — "to carry; to wear".

The word, taken apart.
tragen from Old High German tragan from Proto-Germanic draganą, "to draw, to drag" — inherited, a cousin of English draw and drag. (EtymDB 2.1).

schicken from Middle High German schicken, "to arrange, to put in order" (EtymDB 2.1) — inherited; "to send" grew out of arranging for something to go.

You'll want to know: zählen.
zählen — "to count".

The word, taken apart.
zählen from Old High German zellen from Proto-Germanic taljaną — inherited, the same verb as English tell in its old sense "to count", as in a bank teller. (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to count, then the German for to paint.

You'll want to know: putzen.
putzen — "to clean".

The word, taken apart.
putzen is inherited, but its origin is uncertain. The usual account ties it to Middle High German butze, "lump, piece" (EtymDB 2.1), as if cleaning were picking the lumps off.
