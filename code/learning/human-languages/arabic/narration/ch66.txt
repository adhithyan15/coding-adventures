Arabic, chapter 66: Rice, Fruit, Apple, Bananas, Potatoes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أرز (aruzz) — rice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to go down, then the Arabic for fish.

You'll want to know: أرز.
أرز — aruzz — "rice".

The word, taken apart.
أرز (aruzz) is an old loan. It is usually traced, probably by way of Aramaic, to Greek oryza, "rice" (EtymDB), a word the Greeks had from further east. Lane lists aruzz under ʾ-r-z as a well-known grain.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: فاكهة.
فاكهة — fākiha — "fruit".

The word, taken apart.
فاكهة (fākiha) is built on the native Arabic root f-k-h, "fruit; to enjoy" (Lane). Lane explains fākiha as fruit of any kind, a thing eaten for enjoyment.

Lesson 3 of 5.
تفاحة (tuffāḥa) — an apple.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for rice, then the Arabic for fruit.

You'll want to know: تفاحة.
تفاحة — tuffāḥa — "an apple".

The word, taken apart.
تفاحة (tuffāḥa) is built on the native Arabic root t-f-ḥ, "the apple" (Lane). Lane quotes a source calling it an Arabic word, not arabicized; Hebrew tappuaḥ, "apple", is its cousin. Tuffāḥa is one apple.

Lesson 4 of 5.
موز (mawz) — bananas.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for fruit, then the Arabic for an apple.

You'll want to know: موز.
موز — mawz — "bananas".

The word, taken apart.
موز (mawz) is a loan. EtymDB traces it to Middle Persian mōz, which is usually taken back to Sanskrit mocā, "banana". Lane lists it under m-w-z as a well-known tree and its fruit.

Lesson 5 of 5.
بطاطس (baṭāṭis) — potatoes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an apple, then the Arabic for bananas.

You'll want to know: بطاطس.
بطاطس — baṭāṭis — "potatoes".

The word, taken apart.
بطاطس (baṭāṭis) is a modern loan from a European name of the potato, usually traced to Spanish patata, which goes back to Taíno batata, "sweet potato". It has no Arabic root.
