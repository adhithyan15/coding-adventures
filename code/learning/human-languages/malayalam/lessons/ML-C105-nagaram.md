# നഗരം — a city

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C105-COUNTRY-01, ML-LEX-C80-ORIGIN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *a country*. Then say the word you were given for a settled place
somebody is from.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C105-CITY-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C80-ORIGIN-01] -->

**നഗരം** (*nagaraṁ*) — **a city**.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **രാജ്യം** | *rājyaṁ* | a country |
| **നഗരം** | *nagaraṁ* | a city |
| **ഊര്** | *ūrŭ* | a settled place — **yours already** |

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C105-CITY-01, ML-LEX-C80-ORIGIN-01] -->

**The line between the bottom two is not as sharp as the table makes it look**,
and it is worth saying so rather than teaching you a rule that Malayalam does not
keep.

**നഗരം** is **Sanskrit** and the more formal of the two. **ഊര്** is
**Dravidian**, old, and the everyday one — the word you were told is buried in
place-names the length of the south. A large place will usually be called
**നഗരം**; a smaller one **ഊര്** — but the same place can take either, and which
one a speaker reaches for has as much to do with **register** as with size.

**What is reliable is the register, not the size.** If you want the formal word,
you want this one.
