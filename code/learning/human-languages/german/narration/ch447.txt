German, chapter 447: Friseur und Optiker.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Friseur — the hairdresser, the barber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to patch, then the German for to dry.

You'll want to know: der Friseur.
der Friseur — "the hairdresser, the barber".
Einmal im Monat geht er zum Friseur. He goes to the hairdresser once a month.
Plural: die Friseure. A woman: die Friseurin. Also spelled Frisör.

The word, taken apart.
Friseur was borrowed from French friseur 'hairdresser', from friser 'to curl, to frizz' (EtymDB); French today mostly says coiffeur.

Lesson 2 of 5.
föhnen — to blow-dry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to dry, then the German for the hairdresser.

You'll want to know: föhnen.
föhnen — "to blow-dry".
Nach dem Waschen föhnt sie sich die Haare. After washing her hair she blow-dries it.
Der Föhn — the hair dryer, and also a warm wind from the mountains.

The word, taken apart.
föhnen is built on Föhn, a warm dry wind in the mountains, which goes back through Old High German phōnno to Latin favōnius 'mild west wind' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). The hair dryer was named after the wind.

der Optiker — "the optician".

The word, taken apart.
Optiker is built on Optik 'optics', a learned word from Greek optikós 'of sight'. EtymDB does not list the German word.

Beim Juwelier kaufen sie die Ringe für die Hochzeit. They buy the wedding rings at the jeweller's.
Plural: die Juweliere. A woman: die Juwelierin.

The word, taken apart.
Juwelier is built on Juwel 'jewel', which came from Old French joiel (EtymDB), usually traced to Vulgar Latin jocāle, built on Latin jocus 'joke, play'.
