# मैं पाँच बजे आऊँगा — the item the paper prints

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02, HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Read this out loud before reading on.

> मैं पाँच बजे आऊँगा।

## Grammar Lens: four words, four things you were taught
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02, HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01, HI-CONCEPT-C03-MAIN-01] -->

That line is reading item 8 of the first practice paper, word for word. Until
this chapter two of its four words were unreadable.

| word | what it does |
|---|---|
| मैं | who |
| पाँच बजे | when |
| आऊँगा | the verb, carrying the tense |

The answer the paper wants is *I will come at five*, and the two wrong answers
offered are past and present. **The only thing telling them apart is the ending
on the last word.**

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-04, HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02] -->

The second paper asks the same thing as a conversation:

> — आप कब आएँगे?
> — चार बजे।

**आएँगे** because the question is to somebody addressed as **आप**. The answer
needs no verb at all: a number and **बजे** is a complete reply, the way *at four*
is in English.

## How to answer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-03, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-01] -->

Say each of these out loud:

- you will come at five
- you will go by train, not by bus
- she will come in ten minutes

[PAUSE 4s]

The second and third are items 13 and 11 of the same papers. All three are the
same shape: a person, a circumstance, and a verb whose ending does the work.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C88-FUT-02, HI-LEX-C88-TIME-01, HI-LEX-C88-FUT-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which single word in the paper's line tells you it is the future?
(**आऊँगा** — its ending.) How many words does a full answer to *when* need?
(Two — the number and **बजे**.) Which ending does a question to **आप** take?
(The plural, **आएँगे**.)
