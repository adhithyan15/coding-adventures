Sanskrit, chapter 82: Lake, Well and Shore.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सरः (saraḥ) — a lake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a garden, then the Sanskrit for a forest.

You'll want to know: सरः.
सरः (saraḥ) — "a lake", still जलम् where the lotus grows.

The word, taken apart.
Saras is built on the root √sṛ, "to flow, run": MW glosses it "anything flowing", and so a lake or a pool. It is already in the Rigveda.

What you've built.
Still water.

Lesson 2 of 5.
कूपः (kūpaḥ) — a well.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a forest, then the Sanskrit for a lake.

You'll want to know: कूपः.
कूपः (kūpaḥ) — "a well", water from under the ground.

The word, taken apart.
Kūpa first meant a hole or hollow, in the Rigveda, and then a well (CDIAL 3400). MW tentatively splits it into ku + ap, "water", but that is a guess. It lives on as Hindi kūā, "well".

What you've built.
Water from below.

Lesson 3 of 5.
तडागः (taḍāgaḥ) — a pond, a tank.

Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a lake, then the Sanskrit for a well.

You'll want to know: तडागः.
तडागः (taḍāgaḥ) — "a pond", a village tank with steps.

The word, taken apart.
Taḍāga appears in the ritual handbooks and the epics (CDIAL 5635), but its origin is unclear: MW gives no root. It lives on as Hindi talāu, "pond".

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 5.
द्वीपः (dvīpaḥ) — an island.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a well, then the Sanskrit for a pond.

You'll want to know: द्वीपः.
द्वीपः (dvīpaḥ) — "an island", land with water on both sides: द्वि, "two".

The word, taken apart.
Dvīpa is built from dvi, "two", and ap, "water": land with water on both sides (MW, CDIAL 6691).

What you've built.
Land in the water.

Lesson 5 of 5.
तीरम् (tīram) — a shore, a bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pond, then the Sanskrit for an island.

You'll want to know: तीरम्.
तीरम् (tīram) — "a bank", "a shore", the edge of a नदी or the समुद्रः.

The word, taken apart.
Tīra is built on the root √tṝ, "to cross over" (MW): the bank is where a crossing ends. It lives on as Hindi tīr, "bank" (CDIAL 5842).

What you've built.
The water's edge.
