## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-GRAMMAR-C309-PAST-PERSON-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-PAST-STEMS-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-ONGOING-PAST-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-HABITUAL-PAST-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-WHILE-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C309-THERE-WAS-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C310-FIRST-THEN-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C310-BEFORE-AFTER-01, KA-GRAMMAR-C310-CONJUNCTIVE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] New forms: **ಅತಿಥಿಗಳು** (*atithigaḷu*, "guests"), **ಅತಿಥಿ** with the plural **-ಗಳು**; **ನಿಲ್ದಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ** (*nildāṇadalli*, "at the station"); **ಅವರನ್ನು** (*avarannu*, "them"); **ಅವರ** (*avara*, "her"), said with respect; **ಕರೆದುಕೊಂಡು ಬಂದರು** (*karedukoṇḍu bandaru*, "brought (them)"), literally "having called (them) along, came"; **ಹೊರಟರು** (*horaṭaru*, "set off, left"), the past of **ಹೊರಡು**.

[PAUSE 2s] The scene is **ಮಾಡುವಾಗ** and **ಅಡುಗೆ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು**; the habit is **ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು**, with **ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ**; the background is **ಇತ್ತು**, and **ಇದ್ದರು** says where the grandparents were. The **ಇದೆ** of "now" is the present. Every other verb is an event. **ಹೋಗಿ**, **ಕುಡಿದು** and **ನಡೆದು** carry no time of their own: the verb after them does.
