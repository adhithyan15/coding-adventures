German, chapter 253: Zuerst, Dann, Schließlich — A Story in Order.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
dann — and the verb comes next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "first" and "afterwards". (Zuerst. Danach.)
[pause 1 second]
A story needs one more step word between those two: dann, "then".

Grammar Lens: the step word, then the verb.
In a German statement the verb is the second piece. When a step word takes the first place, the verb comes straight after it, and the subject moves behind the verb:
Zuerst kaufte ich einen Kaffee. — First I bought a coffee. Dann las ich die Nachricht. — Then I read the message. Danach ging ich. — Afterwards I went.
English keeps "I" in front: "then I read". German says dann las ich, not dann ich las. The verb holds its place, and the subject gives way.
Und does not take a place of its own. After und dann, the verb still follows dann: … und dann kam der Zug.
Dann and danach overlap. Dann is the next step, and the shortest way on. Danach leans on the time: after that.

The word, taken apart.
dann from Old High German danne from Proto-Germanic þan, "at that time" (EtymDB 2.1), which EtymDB sets beside English then. In chapter forty-eight you met its twin, denn, "because": the two were once one word, and German gave dann the when and denn the why.

Lesson 2 of 4.
schließlich — the step that closes the story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "then I went". (Dann ging ich.) Now "first I bought a coffee". (Zuerst kaufte ich einen Kaffee.)

You'll want to know: schließlich.
schließlich — "in the end, finally": schlees-likh.
Schließlich kam der Zug. — In the end the train came.
You already have endlich, "at last". The two are close, and the difference is the waiting. Endlich sighs: at last, after too long. Schließlich counts: the last step, in order.
Schließlich has a second life outside stories, "after all": Er ist schließlich mein Bruder, he is my brother, after all.

Grammar Lens: the frame of a told story.
Four step words, and every one takes the first place, so the verb follows it at once:
Zuerst wartete ich. Dann kam der Zug. Danach fuhr ich zwei Stunden. Schließlich kam ich in Berlin an.
Around those steps sits the scene: Es war Samstag. Es regnete. A told story is the scene, the steps in order, and the end. You do not need all four step words every time: zuerst … dann … schließlich is already a story.

The word, taken apart.
schließlich is schließen, "to close", + -lich. Schließen goes back through Middle High German sliezen to Old High German sliozan, "to close, to conclude" (EtymDB 2.1). EtymDB records schließlich without taking it apart, so the split into verb and ending is given here as the plain reading of the word: the closing one.

Lesson 3 of 4.
(reading) — a journey, told in order.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Chapter fifty-two gave you forty-eight words in a row, in the present. This passage is twice as long, and it happened last Saturday.
[pause 1 second]
Every word in it has come up before this lesson.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
Es war Samstag, und es regnete. Ich wartete am Bahnhof, aber der Zug hatte Verspätung. Zuerst kaufte ich einen Kaffee. Dann las ich die Nachrichten auf meinem Handy. Als der Zug endlich kam, war ich sehr müde. Ich fuhr zwei Stunden. Danach kam ich in Berlin an. Dort wartete meine Großmutter. Sie lachte, als sie mich sah. Wir gingen zusammen in ein Restaurant. Ich hatte Hunger und nahm einen Fisch, und meine Großmutter nahm eine Suppe. Sie erzählte mir eine Geschichte, und wir lachten. Schließlich war es Abend. Ich war müde, aber sehr glücklich.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then read it a second time and listen for the step words that put the day in order.

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
Four step words carry the day: zuerst, dann, danach, schließlich. After each one the verb comes next: zuerst kaufte ich, dann las ich, danach kam ich, schließlich war es. Dort does the same job in Dort wartete meine Großmutter.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every verb is a Präteritum. Some take -te: regnete, wartete, kaufte, lachte, erzählte. Some change their vowel: las, kam, fuhr, sah, gingen, nahm. And hatte and war you have said since chapters twenty-five and twenty-eight.
[pause 2 seconds]
Two lines turn on als. Als der Zug endlich kam is one moment that ended the waiting; als sie mich sah is the moment the grandmother laughed. The first stands in front, so verb meets verb at the comma: kam, war. The second follows its main clause: Sie lachte, als sie mich sah.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 15 seconds]
How many step words put the events of the passage in order?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many words in that passage were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Why does Dann las ich put las before ich? (Dann takes the first place, and the verb comes second.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which word marks the one moment the train came? (Als.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — the journey, retold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "then I read the message". (Dann las ich die Nachricht.) And "in the end it was evening". (Schließlich war es Abend.)

What you've built.
Two chapters, and a German story is yours to tell. The scene in the Präteritum: Es war Samstag, und es regnete. The steps, each with its verb straight after it: zuerst, dann, danach, schließlich. One moment marked with als. And a last line about how it felt.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now tell the passage back, without reading it. It does not have to be word for word. It has to be in order, and every step word has to pull its verb in behind it.
