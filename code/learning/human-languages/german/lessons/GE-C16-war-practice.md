# Practice — was, were, and why

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-BIN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] No new words. Present and past, side by side, and then the account
of how one verb ended up like this.

## Grammar Lens: the past, assembled
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-WARST-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-WAR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-WAREN-01] -->

Six forms, one stem, four endings, no surprises. Said in order:

- **ich war**, **du warst**, **er war** — and the first and third are the same
  word, which the present tense would never allow;
- **wir waren**, **ihr wart**, **sie waren**.

Compare that with the present, where five different shapes came from three
different verbs. The past of *sein* is the part of the paradigm that behaved.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ICH-WAR-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-DU-WARST-01, GE-LEX-MUEDE-01, GE-LEX-GUT-02] -->

| German | English |
|---|---|
| *Wo warst du?* | Where were you? |
| *Ich war in Berlin.* | I was in Berlin. |
| *Wie war es?* | How was it? |
| *Es war gut. Aber ich bin müde.* | It was good. But I am tired. |

*Wo* ("where") and *in* are read straight off the English here; neither is asked
of you yet.
