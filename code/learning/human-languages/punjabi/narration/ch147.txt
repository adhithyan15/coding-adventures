Lesson 1 of 5.
ਅਮੀਰ (amīr) — rich.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for true, then the Punjabi for false.

You'll want to know: ਅਮੀਰ.
ਅਮੀਰ — amīr — "rich".

The word, taken apart.
ਅਮੀਰ (amīr) is a loan from Arabic amīr, "commander, prince", which reached Punjabi through Persian. Its Arabic root is ʾ-m-r (Lane). Punjabi uses it for "rich".

Lesson 3 of 5.
ਜਵਾਨ (javān) — young.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for rich, then the Punjabi for poor.

You'll want to know: ਜਵਾਨ.
ਜਵਾਨ — javān — "young".

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL lists Punjabi javāṇ and juāṇ, "a youth", under yuvāna, "young", built on Sanskrit yuvan (CDIAL 10510), with the y- to j- change of jāṇā. In the same entry it marks Sindhi juānu, with a plain n, as taken from Persian javān, the Sanskrit word's cousin, and Hindi javān is usually traced to Persian too. The word taught here also has a plain n, so how much Persian shaped it is not settled.

ਕਮਜ਼ੋਰ — kamzor — "weak".

The word, taken apart.
ਕਮਜ਼ੋਰ (kamzor) is a loan, usually traced to Persian kamzōr, "weak". It is built from kam, "little", and zōr, "strength".
