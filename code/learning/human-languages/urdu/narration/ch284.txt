Urdu, chapter 284: A Crash on the Road.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
رفتار (raftār) — speed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a punishment, then the Urdu for handcuffs.

You'll want to know: رفتار.
رفتار — raftār — "speed".
میں کم رفتار سے جاؤں گا۔ maiṅ kam raftār se jāūṅ gā. I will go at a low speed.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
رفتار (raftār) is usually traced to Persian raftār, "a going, a gait", from the Persian verb to go. It is feminine: تیز رفتار, high speed.

You'll want to know: ٹکرانا.
ٹکرانا — ṭakrānā — "to collide; to crash into".
بس ایک ٹرک سے ٹکرائی۔ bas ek ṭrak se ṭakrāī. The bus crashed into a lorry.
Past (it …): وہ گاڑی ٹکرائی, voh gāṛī ṭakrāī.

The word, taken apart.
ٹکرانا (ṭakrānā) is built on ṭakkar, "a blow", which CDIAL lists for Hindi under ṭakkarā (CDIAL 5424). What you crash into takes سے: ٹرک سے ٹکرائی.

[pause 2 seconds]

الٹنا — ulaṭnā — "to overturn".
ٹرک سڑک پر الٹ گیا۔ ṭrak saṛak par ulaṭ gayā. The lorry overturned on the road.
Past (it …): وہ ٹرک الٹ گیا, voh ṭrak ulaṭ gayā.

The word, taken apart.
الٹنا (ulaṭnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin ulaṭnā, "to be overturned", under ullaṭati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 2368). A vehicle الٹ گئی when it lands on its side or its roof.

سواری (savārī) is usually traced to Persian savārī, "riding", from savār, a rider. It is feminine whoever the passenger is: بیس سواریاں.
