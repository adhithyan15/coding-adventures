すいか (suika) — a watermelon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a peach, then the Japanese for a pear.

You'll want to know: すいか.
すいか — suika — "a watermelon".

The word, taken apart.
すいか (suika) is Sino-Japanese, borrowed from Chinese, "western melon": sui, "west", and ka, "melon" (Middle Chinese kwae). Sui is a later Chinese reading of the character for west, not the usual sei (JMdict; Unihan, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
にんじん (ninjin) — a carrot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a watermelon, then the Japanese for a white radish.

You'll want to know: にんじん.
にんじん — ninjin — "a carrot".

The word, taken apart.
にんじん (ninjin) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are nin, "person" (Middle Chinese nyin) and jin, "ginseng" (srim) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). JMdict lists ginseng as a second sense: the name first belonged to that root and later passed to the carrot.

Lesson 5 of 5.
しお (shio) — salt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a carrot, then the Japanese for tofu.

You'll want to know: しお.
しお — shio — "salt".

The word, taken apart.
しお (shio) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
