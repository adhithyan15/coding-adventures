Sanskrit, chapter 276: What Happened, and What Was Going On.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
सः गतवान् (saḥ gatavān) — the past that follows the doer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "the boy went", with the past you know. (bālaḥ agacchat.) Then say "yesterday". (hyaḥ.) This lesson gives a second past, the one the stories in this book are told in.

You'll want to know: गतवान्, गतवती.
सः गतवान्। saḥ gatavān. — "He went."
सा गतवती। sā gatavatī. — "She went."
गतवान् (gatavān), "went", is a describing word made from the verb: -vān for a man, -vatī for a woman.
सः ह्यः गृहं गतवान्। saḥ hyaḥ gṛhaṃ gatavān. — "He went home yesterday."
मीरा ह्यः आपणं गतवती। mīrā hyaḥ āpaṇaṃ gatavatī. — "Mira went to the shop yesterday."
For several people, गतवन्तः (gatavantaḥ); for two, गतवन्तौ (gatavantau):
ते ह्यः ग्रामं गतवन्तः। te hyaḥ grāmaṃ gatavantaḥ. — "They went to the village yesterday."
It needs no अस्मि: a man says ahaṃ gatavān, "I went", a woman ahaṃ gatavatī.

You'll want to know: the stem under it.
The part before -vat- is the verb's -ta form, which describes on its own: गतः (gataḥ), "gone". Each verb has its own, given with it in the dictionary:
[a table of 6 rows, read aloud]
the verb: "does". the past (a man): कृतवान् (kṛtavān).
the verb: पठति. the past (a man): पठितवान् (paṭhitavān).
the verb: खादति. the past (a man): खादितवान् (khāditavān).
the verb: लिखति. the past (a man): लिखितवान् (likhitavān).
the verb: पश्यति. the past (a man): दृष्टवान् (dṛṣṭavān).
the verb: आगच्छति. the past (a man): आगतवान् (āgatavān).
The thing done stays as it was:
सः अन्नं खादितवान्। saḥ annaṃ khāditavān. — "He ate the food."
मीरा पुस्तकं पठितवती। mīrā pustakaṃ paṭhitavatī. — "Mira read the book."
"Said" comes from the other "say" root, √vac: उक्तवान् (uktavān; Monier-Williams gives ukta there).
सा किम् उक्तवती? sā kim uktavatī? — "What did she say?"
The past you know still stands: agacchat and gatavān both say "went", and both are correct. This book tells its stories with gatavān, one shape for every verb (Pāṇini 1.1.26 names -ta and -tavat together).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "He came home" — saḥ gṛham āgatavān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "She came home" — sā gṛham āgatavatī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "They came home" — te gṛham āgatavantaḥ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say that Mira went to the shop, then that Arun read the book.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"She went"? (सा गतवती.) "I did the work", said by a man? (अहं कार्यं कृतवान्.) Who decides -vān or -vatī? (The one who did it.)


Lesson 2 of 5.
सः पठति स्म (saḥ paṭhati sma) — what was going on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "he read the book". (saḥ pustakaṃ paṭhitavān.) That tells one finished event. This lesson tells the scene around it.

You'll want to know: the present with स्म.
सः पठति स्म। saḥ paṭhati sma. — "He was reading."
स्म (sma) after a present puts it in the past: "he was reading", or "he used to read". The verb keeps its present ending. Lanman's vocabulary describes it as a present that tells the past (he cites Whitney §778).
बालाः क्रीडन्ति स्म। bālāḥ krīḍanti sma. — "The children were playing."
सा प्रतिदिनं पठति स्म। sā pratidinaṃ paṭhati sma. — "She used to read every day."

You'll want to know: यदा … तदा.
यदा (yadā) is "when", opening the scene; तदा (tadā), "then", opens what broke into it. The scene takes स्म, the event the past:
यदा अहं पठामि स्म, yadā ahaṃ paṭhāmi sma, तदा मीरा आगतवती। tadā mīrā āgatavatī. — "When I was reading, Mira came."
यदा सः खादति स्म, yadā saḥ khādati sma, तदा अहं गतवान्। tadā ahaṃ gatavān. — "While he was eating, I left."
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
the sentence: सः पठितवान्. what it tells: one event: he read.
the sentence: सः पठति स्म (saḥ paṭhati sma). what it tells: the scene: he was reading.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I was reading" — ahaṃ paṭhāmi sma]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was eating" — ahaṃ khādāmi sma]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was writing" — ahaṃ likhāmi sma]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that the children were playing, then that Mira came while you were reading.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"He was playing"? (सः क्रीडति स्म.) "When I was reading, Arun came"? (यदा अहं पठामि स्म, तदा अरुणः आगतवान्.) Which form tells the scene? (The present with स्म.)


Lesson 3 of 5.
घटते (ghaṭate) — happens.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "when I was reading, Mira came". (yadā ahaṃ paṭhāmi sma, tadā mīrā āgatavatī.)

You'll want to know: घटते.
घटते — ghaṭate — "happens". It ends in -te, as रोचते (rocate) does.
अत्र किं घटते? atra kiṃ ghaṭate? — "What is happening here?"
एतत् प्रतिदिनं घटते। etat pratidinaṃ ghaṭate. — "This happens every day."
"It happened" is its -ta form, घटितम् (ghaṭitam), the way गतः says "gone":
ह्यः किं घटितम्? hyaḥ kiṃ ghaṭitam? — "What happened yesterday?"

The word, taken apart.
घटते (ghaṭate) is built on the root √ghaṭ. Monier-Williams gives its first sense as being busy with something or striving for it, and a later one as happening, taking place; he gives ghaṭita as "happened". It lives on as Marathi ghaḍṇẽ, "to happen" (CDIAL 4407).

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ghaṭate]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What is happening here?" — atra kiṃ ghaṭate?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "What happened yesterday?" — hyaḥ kiṃ ghaṭitam?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does घटते (ghaṭate) mean? ("Happens".) "What happened?" (किं घटितम्?)


Lesson 4 of 5.
घ (gha) — one character, met first in घटते (ghaṭate).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: घ and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: say घटते (ghaṭate), "happens". Then say the sounds of ग and ध. (ga, dha.)
[pause 2 seconds]
One character this time: the one that opens घटते (ghaṭate).

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: घ.
घ — gha.
It is a consonant: g with a breath after it, as ध is d with a breath.
What it is made of:
a continuous upper curl, middle hook, lower bowl, and rising right side.
a short descending lower stem.
the top shirorekhā.
You have met it here:
घटते ghaṭate — happens.

Writing: घ.
1. start below the headline at the upper-left, curve down and clockwise around the upper curl, turn back left through the middle hook, continue clockwise around the lower bowl, and rise along the right side to the headline.
2. without lifting, come back down the right side to the lower junction.
3. descend the short stem below the bowl.
4. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 1. The pen comes up 1 time and no more.
The source shows three movements; native writers draw घ (gha) as two pen-down strokes (83% of HP Labs India's native-writer samples, 68 of 82). This ductus follows the three movements in order and direction, lifting only before the headline: after rising to the headline the pen comes back down the right side into the short lower stem.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-घ (gha)-order.gif’, strokes 1–3, Wikimedia Commons, 9 May 2009.
