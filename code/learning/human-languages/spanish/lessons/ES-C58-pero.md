# pero — the word that lets two true things stand together

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PORQUE-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] With *porque* you can give a reason: one thing **because of**
another. That word makes two facts agree.

[PAUSE 1s] Now you need the opposite tool — the one for two facts that
**do not** agree, and are both true anyway.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

- `r-tap` — one flick of the tongue, not the rolled *rr*. **pero** = *PEH-ro*.
  (The rolled version is a different word entirely, so this flick is worth
  getting right.)

## The word, taken apart: pero
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-PERO, ES-ETYMON-PERO-PER-HOC]; assesses=[] -->

**pero** was **two** Latin words: **per hoc** — *by this*, *through this*.

[PAUSE 2s] You can still feel the route it took. *By this* became *for all
that*, and *for all that* is already a contrast: I grant you the first thing,
and here is the second anyway. The phrase wore down into one short word and kept
only the contrast.

> *Estoy cansado, **pero** estudio.*

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-PERO] -->

Notice what *pero* does **not** do. It does not deny the first half. *Estoy
cansado* stays true — tiredness is not cancelled by studying.

[PAUSE 2s] That is the whole job: two things that pull in opposite directions,
both standing. *Porque* fuses two facts into one reason; *pero* keeps two facts
apart and admits both. Between them you can say almost anything.

[PAUSE 2s] And it is the first word of every argument you will ever make.
Nobody argues by listing things they agree with.
