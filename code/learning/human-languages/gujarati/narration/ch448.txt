Gujarati, chapter 448: Laughing Together.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
હાસ્ય (hāsya) — laughter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to experience, then the Gujarati for to delight in.

You'll want to know: હાસ્ય.
હાસ્ય — hāsya — "laughter".
મીરાના હાસ્યથી આખું ઘર ખુશ થઈ ગયું. Mīrānā hāsyathī ākhũ ghar khuś thaī gayũ. Mira's laughter made the whole house happy.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
હાસ્ય (hāsya) is a tatsama, Sanskrit hāsya, "laughter, mirth", as Monier-Williams records it; it shares its root with હસવું (hasvũ), "to laugh".

Lesson 2 of 5.
હસાવવું (hasāvvũ) — to make someone laugh.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to delight in, then the Gujarati for laughter.

You'll want to know: હસાવવું.
હસાવવું — hasāvvũ — "to make someone laugh".
અરુણની વાર્તાઓ બધાને હસાવે છે. Aruṇnī vārtāo badhāne hasāve che. Arun's stories make everyone laugh.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બાળકને હસાવ્યું, teṇe bāḷakne hasāvyũ.

The word, taken apart.
હસાવવું (hasāvvũ) is the causative of હસવું (hasvũ), "to laugh", which is inherited from Sanskrit hasati, "laughs" (CDIAL 14021).

Lesson 4 of 5.
મજાક (majāk) — a joke; good-natured teasing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to make someone laugh, then the Gujarati for a smile.

You'll want to know: મજાક.
મજાક — majāk — "a joke; good-natured teasing".
અરુણે મજાક કરી અને બધા હસ્યા. Aruṇe majāk karī ane badhā hasyā. Arun made a joke and everyone laughed.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મજાક (majāk) is usually traced to Arabic madhāq, "taste, relish", which reached Gujarati through Persian, where it is said mazāq; the sense "a joke" is said to have grown up on the way.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ચીડવવું (cīḍavvũ) — to tease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a smile, then the Gujarati for a joke.

You'll want to know: ચીડવવું.
ચીડવવું — cīḍavvũ — "to tease".
મોટો ભાઈ નાની બહેનને રોજ ચીડવે છે. moṭo bhāī nānī bahenne roj cīḍve che. The big brother teases his little sister every day.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે બાળકને ચીડવ્યું, teṇe bāḷakne cīḍavyũ.

The word, taken apart.
ચીડવવું (cīḍavvũ) is the causative partner of ચિડાવું (ciḍāvũ), "to get irritated": to tease is to make someone cross. Both go back to a reconstructed ciḍ-, "be angry" (CDIAL 4794).
