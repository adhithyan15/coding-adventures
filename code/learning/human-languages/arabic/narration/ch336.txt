Arabic, chapter 336: Gifts and blessings.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
أهدى (ahdā) — to gift; to dedicate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a giant, then the Arabic for a dwarf.

You'll want to know: أهدى.
أهدى — ahdā — "to gift; to dedicate".
أهدت البنت وردة إلى أمها. ahdat al-bint wardatan ilā ummihā. The girl gave her mother a rose.
Present: يهدي, yuhdī.
The taught هدية, a gift, is its noun. Of a book: أهدى (ahdā) الكتاب إلى أمه, he dedicated it to his mother.

The word, taken apart.
أهدى (ahdā) is built on the native Arabic root h-d-y (Lane), "to guide", the root of the taught hadiyya, a gift. It is Form IV, "to send a gift".

Lesson 2 of 5.
استقبل (istaqbala) — to receive guests; to welcome.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a dwarf, then the Arabic for to gift.

You'll want to know: استقبل.
استقبل — istaqbala — "to receive guests; to welcome".
استقبل الأهل الضيوف عند الباب. istaqbala al-ahl aḍ-ḍuyūf ʿinda al-bāb. The family welcomed the guests at the door.
Present: يستقبل, yastaqbil.
A hotel reception desk is الاستقبال.

The word, taken apart.
استقبل (istaqbala) is built on the native Arabic root q-b-l (Lane), "to be in front of, to face". It is Form X, "to go to meet"; mustaqbal, the future, is what comes to meet us.

Lesson 3 of 5.
بارك (bāraka) — to bless; to congratulate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to gift, then the Arabic for to receive guests.

You'll want to know: بارك.
بارك — bāraka — "to bless; to congratulate".
بارك الأصدقاء للعروسين. bāraka al-aṣdiqāʾ li-l-ʿarūsayn. The friends congratulated the bride and groom.
Present: يبارك, yubārik.
بارك (bāraka) لـ someone: to congratulate them. The wish مبروك is the same root.

The word, taken apart.
بارك (bāraka) is built on the native Arabic root b-r-k (Lane), said of a camel that kneels and stays. Baraka, a blessing, is good that stays; Form III bāraka is "to wish blessing on".

دفع العريس مهرا للعروس. dafaʿa al-ʿarīs mahran li-l-ʿarūs. The groom paid a bride gift to the bride.
The groom gives it to the bride; it is hers.

The word, taken apart.
مهر (mahr) is built on the native Arabic root m-h-r (Lane), where mahr is the gift a man gives his bride; muhr, a foal, is written the same.

Lesson 5 of 5.
عشية (ʿashiyya) — the eve; the evening before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to bless, then the Arabic for a dowry.

You'll want to know: عشية.
عشية — ʿashiyya — "the eve; the evening before".
سافرنا عشية (ʿashiyya) العيد. sāfarnā ʿashiyyat al-ʿīd. We travelled on the eve of the holiday.
عشية (ʿashiyya) الانتخابات: on the eve of the election.

The word, taken apart.
عشية (ʿashiyya) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-sh-w (Lane), where ʿashiyy is the evening; the taught ʿashāʾ, dinner, is the evening meal.
