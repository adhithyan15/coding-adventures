Mandarin Chinese, chapter 346: Thinking It Over.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
kǎolǜ — to think something over; to consider.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for online friend, then the Mandarin for postcode.

You'll want to know: kǎolǜ.
kǎolǜ — "to think something over; to consider".
wǒ zài kǎolǜ qù bu qù Zhōngguó — I'm thinking over whether to go to China.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kǎolǜ is built from kǎo and lǜ, both inherited from Middle Chinese, khawX and ljoH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to check' and 'to complete', and the second 'to think'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
感想 (gǎnxiǎng) — impressions afterwards; thoughts.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to think something over, then the Mandarin for a point of view.

You'll want to know: 感想.
感想 — gǎnxiǎng — "impressions afterwards; thoughts".
看了这本书你有什么感想 kàn le zhè běn shū nǐ yǒu shénme gǎnxiǎng — What did you make of the book once you'd read it?
Two third tones meet in this word: gǎn is said with a rising tone before xiǎng, and the spelling keeps both marks.

The word, taken apart.
感想 (gǎnxiǎng) is built from 感 'to feel' and 想 'to think', both inherited from Middle Chinese, komX and sjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to move' and the second 'to think'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
以为 (yǐwéi) — to wrongly assume; to take it that (often wrongly).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a point of view, then the Mandarin for your impressions afterwards.

You'll want to know: 以为.
以为 — yǐwéi — "to wrongly assume; to take it that (often wrongly)".
我以为你不来了 wǒ yǐwéi nǐ bù lái le — I thought you weren't coming.

The word, taken apart.
以为 (yǐwéi) is built from 以 'to take' and 为 'to make', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yiX and hjwe (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to use' and lists the second as the everyday spelling of a word glossed 'to make'. How the pair came to mean a belief that turns out wrong is not settled.
