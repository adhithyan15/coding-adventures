Italian, chapter 515: Ghiaccio e neve.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pattinare — to skate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to dive under, then the Italian for diving board.

You'll want to know: pattinare.
pattinare — "to skate".
D'inverno pattiniamo sul ghiaccio in piazza. In winter we skate on the ice in the square.
Skates are i pattini.

The word, taken apart.
pattinare is built on pattino "skate"; the origin of pattino is not settled, and it may come from an old word patta "paw".

You'll want to know: lo scarpone.
lo scarpone — "boot (for skiing or hiking)".
Gli scarponi da sci sono pesanti. Ski boots are heavy.
A big scarpa: -one makes things bigger.

The word, taken apart.
scarpone is built on scarpa "shoe" with -one "big"; scarpa is usually traced to a Germanic word, and its origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la pista — slope, track, runway.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to skate, then the Italian for boot.

You'll want to know: la pista.
la pista — "slope, track, runway".
Oggi le piste sono piene di neve fresca. Today the slopes are full of fresh snow.
La pista da sci, la pista ciclabile, la pista da ballo — the dance floor.

The word, taken apart.
pista is usually traced to pestare "to tread", from Late Latin pistāre "to pound", built on Latin pinsere "to crush" (Lewis & Short): a track is ground trodden flat.

Lesson 4 of 5.
lo sciatore — skier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for boot, then the Italian for slope.

You'll want to know: lo sciatore.
lo sciatore — "skier".
Ci sono molti sciatori sulla pista. There are lots of skiers on the slope.
A woman is la sciatrice.

The word, taken apart.
sciatore is built on sci "ski", which was borrowed from Norwegian ski, from Old Norse skíð "a stick of wood, a snowshoe" (EtymDB, under French ski).

Lesson 5 of 5.

la seggiovia — "chairlift".
Saliamo in seggiovia fino in cima. We go up to the top by chairlift.
Built from seggio "seat" and via "way".

The word, taken apart.
seggiovia is a modern word built from seggio "seat", from Latin sedēre "to sit" (Lewis & Short), and via "way".
