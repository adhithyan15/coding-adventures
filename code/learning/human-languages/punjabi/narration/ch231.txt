Punjabi, chapter 231: Combing and Plaiting.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ਤੇਲ (tel) — oil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for loose trousers, then the Punjabi for a hanging earring.

You'll want to know: ਤੇਲ.
ਤੇਲ — tel — "oil".
ਮੈਂ ਵਾਲਾਂ ਉੱਤੇ ਤੇਲ ਮਲਦੀ ਹਾਂ। maiṁ vālāṁ utte tel maldī hāṁ. I rub oil into my hair.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਤੇਲ (tel) is inherited from Sanskrit taila, "sesame oil, oil"; CDIAL lists Punjabi tel, "oil" (CDIAL 5958).

Lesson 3 of 6.
ਗੁੱਤ (gutt) — a plait of hair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for oil, then the Punjabi for to braid.

You'll want to know: ਗੁੱਤ.
ਗੁੱਤ — gutt — "a plait of hair".
ਕੁੜੀ ਦੀ ਗੁੱਤ ਲੰਮੀ ਹੈ। kuṛī dī gutt lammī hai. The girl's plait is long.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਗੁੱਤ (gutt) comes from the same root as ਗੁੰਦਣਾ, Sanskrit guphati, "strings together": CDIAL lists Punjabi gundṇā, "to braid hair", under it, but has no Punjabi row for the noun; it lists only Lahnda gutt, "plait of hair hanging down woman's back" (CDIAL 4205).

ਪਰਾਂਦਾ — parāndā — "a hair tassel; coloured yarn tied into a plait".
ਭੈਣ ਦੀ ਗੁੱਤ ਵਿੱਚ ਲਾਲ ਪਰਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ। bhaiṇ dī gutt vich lāl parāndā hai. There is a red tassel in my sister's plait.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ਪਰਾਂਦਾ (parāndā) is inherited: CDIAL sets it under Sanskrit prānta, "end, edge", and lists Punjabi parā̃dā, "particoloured yarn used by women for tying up their hair" (CDIAL 8940).
