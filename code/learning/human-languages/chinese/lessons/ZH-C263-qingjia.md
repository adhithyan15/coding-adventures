## You'll want to know: 请假
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C263-LEAVE263-03]; assesses=[] -->

**请假** — *qǐngjià* — "to ask for time off".

> **我明天要请假** *wǒ míngtiān yào qǐngjià* — I need to take tomorrow off.

The new character, in pieces: 假 — eleven strokes: the person piece 亻; then a narrow piece of five strokes, a short bar that turns down, a short bar, a long vertical and two short bars against it; then four strokes, a short bar that turns down, a short bar, a stroke that runs right and sweeps down to the left, and a long stroke to the right across it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**请假** (*qǐngjià*) is built from 请 'to ask' and 假 'leave', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tshjengX* and *kaeH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to beg' and the second 'leave from work'.
