## You'll want to know: flagrō, flagrāre
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C318-CREMO-03]; assesses=[] -->

**flagrō, flagrāre** — "to blaze, to be on fire".

> Domus flagrābat, et vīcīnī aquam portābant.
>
> *The house was blazing, and the neighbours were carrying water.*

*flagrō, flagrāre, flagrāvī*: to blaze, and of people, to burn with a feeling. English *flagrant*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**flagrō** is inherited from Proto-Italic *\*flagros*, "burning" (EtymDB), of the same family as *flamma*, "flame". English *flagrant* comes from it.
