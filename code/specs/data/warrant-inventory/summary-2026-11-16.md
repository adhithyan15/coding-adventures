Re-run (backlog VER30-INV2) of the 2026-11-14 survey with a new `recursive-types` blocker tag. Read-only survey of `code/packages/rust/*` at `origin/main` a522491e (snapshot via `git archive`; the stale main checkout was not used). Generated by `extract.py` -> `classify.py` -> `analyze.py` -> `analyze2.py` -> `make_summary.py` (scratch copies, see `method-delta-2026-11-16.md`); the per-crate data is `inventory-2026-11-16.csv` beside this file. Rule for the primary tier (so you can argue with it): take the crate's non-trivial public entries (crate-level `pub fn`, `pub mod` fns, `impl` methods of public types; constructors, `fmt`/`clone`-style trait glue, no-argument `&self` getters and text-rendering conveniences such as `*_hex` are excluded from the vote). Each entry gets the set of features its signature needs. The crate's *required set* is the union over its cheapest 60% of entries, forced to include every entry of a public object type with 2 or more `&mut self` methods and of any streaming type. Primary tier = the most expensive tier in the required set; everything else becomes secondary blockers. T4 is decided first: bin-only, cdylib/staticlib, FFI export names, re-export glue, effect-dominated signatures (`Path`/`File`/`Duration`/raw pointers), or a name pattern (`-cli`, `-repl`, `-daemon`, `-integration`, `-uart` ...) combined with a mostly-impure surface.

## 5. WX-D2 and WX-D1 are model-quality upgrades, not unlocks

The signature scan finds only 2 crates whose *signature* demands a list fold (e.g. `fn sum(&[i32]) -> i32`). The real WX-D2 demand is quality: 48 T0-T2 hash/codec/KDF/checksum-style crates (398 ports) can be adopted with known answers, laws and round trips, but only an independent loop model (`fold` over `to_list`/`slice`, as WX-D VER04k section 0 describes for gf256 `gmul`, leb128, SHA schedules) makes the evidence differential. Treat WX-D1/D2 as a per-adopter quality multiplier that WX-F and WX-G postconditions also lean on, not as something that unblocks new crates.

## 8. Caveats

- Ports are same-name directory counts (exact after normalisation). Rust crates whose siblings are named differently (`sha256` vs `sha-256`, `*-native`) are undercounted; `intel4004-gatelevel` shows 7 but its siblings may exist under other names.
- 1008 of the 1499 crates have no ports at all (Rust-only, mostly TLS/X.509/HTTP-framing, smart-home, board-vm, chief-of-staff). Their value is Rust-only evidence plus the runtime jail; the demand ordering above is by ports, and a security-weighted ordering would put WX-G (tls13-*, rsa-pss-*, http1-*, 18 crates alone) ahead of where the cumulative table puts it.
- The scan does not see macro-generated items, `pub use` of items from dependencies (counted as glue), or behaviour behind features; trait-only APIs (`closure-pass-*`) are filed as T3 generics.
- `required_capabilities.json` counts include test-only declarations (e.g. `sha512` lists fs/env capabilities used only by unit tests). The CSV keeps the raw count and notes `test-only-caps:n` and `non-test-caps:...`.
- A crate's tier says what Warrant must gain to adopt its principal API. Individual pure functions inside T3 crates (e.g. the 47 T0 helpers in `intel4004-simulator`) can often be adopted earlier as slices; `t0_entries`/`pub_entries` in the CSV shows how many.
