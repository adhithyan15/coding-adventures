# el cliente, la cliente — the person on the other side of the counter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *the shop*. (*La tienda*.) Now name the person who buys there:
**el cliente** or **la cliente**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-CLIENTE]; assesses=[] -->

**Cliente** comes from Latin **cliens**, a person connected to and protected by
a patron. The relationship changed over time. In modern Spanish, a **cliente**
is a customer of a shop or a client who uses a professional service.

The word itself can stay the same for a man or a woman; the article does the
work: **el cliente**, **la cliente**. You may also meet **clienta** for a woman.

## Grammar Lens: meaning in a scene
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CLIENTE, ES-LEX-C311-ASK-06] -->

Picture a counter. One person works for the shop; the other asks for a product
or service. That second person is **el cliente** or **la cliente**.

- **el cliente de la tienda** — the shop's customer
- **la cliente necesita ayuda** — the customer needs help
