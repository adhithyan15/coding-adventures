Persian, chapter 19: The Ten Persian Digits.
5 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
۰ ۱ (sefr yek) — two digits, and a direction that reverses.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script — the shapes, and the direction and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which way does a Persian line run? Say yek.

Script — the shapes, and the direction.
۰ — sefr, zero ۱ — yek, one.
Persian has its own ten digit signs, and every price ticket, telephone number and page number in Iran is written in them. A reader who can say yek and cannot read ۱ still cannot read a price.
۰ is a dot, not a ring. Fix that first, because a European eye reads a dot as a decimal point — and Persian's nine is the ring.
Now the part no earlier chapter has had to say: numbers run left to right, inside a line that runs right to left. Ten is ۱۰ — one, then zero, in the European order — while the words around it move the other way. The page changes direction at the number and changes back after it.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: ۰ — a dot; ۱ — a stroke]
[your turn — read: ۱۰ — dah, ten, left to right]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Unicode Arabic chart; Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
A Persian line runs right to left. Which way do the digits inside it run?


Lesson 2 of 5.
۲ ۳ (do se) — one tooth apart.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script — the shapes and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write ۰ and ۱, then read ۱۰.

Script — the shapes.
۲ — do, two ۳ — se, three.
۳ is ۲ with one more tooth. That is the whole difference, and it is small enough that the two have to be written as a pair rather than one at a time — say do and se aloud as the hand makes them and the shapes stop competing.

Lesson 3 of 5.
۴ ۵ ۶ (chahâr panj shesh) — the three Persian keeps for itself.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script — the shapes and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write ۲ and ۳, and say which has the extra tooth.

Script — the shapes.
۴ — chahâr, four ۵ — panj, five ۶ — shesh, six.
These three are the reason this chapter exists as more than a list.
Seven of the ten digits are drawn the same way in Persian and in Arabic. ۴, ۵ and ۶ are not: Arabic writes those three with quite different shapes. A reader who picked up the digits from an Arabic book will read an Iranian price wrongly at exactly these three places and nowhere else.
If you drill three of the ten harder than the rest, drill these.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: ۴, ۵, ۶, saying chahâr, panj, shesh]
[your turn — read: ۱۴, ۱۵, ۱۶, left to right]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.

Lesson 4 of 5.
۷ ۸ ۹ (haft hasht noh) — a mirror pair, and the ring.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script — the shapes and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write ۴, ۵, ۶ — the three Arabic draws differently.

Script — the shapes.
۷ — haft, seven ۸ — hasht, eight ۹ — noh, nine.
۷ and ۸ are mirror images of each other, one opening upward and one downward. Write them as a pair, every time, and the mirror stops being a problem — the same trick that worked on ۲ and ۳.
۹ is a ring with a tail, and this is where a dot-shaped zero pays off. Had ۰ been a ring too, the page would carry two rings and no way to separate them.
Ten signs, four lessons. The set is closed.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: ۷ and ۸ as a pair, then ۹]
[your turn — run: ۰ ۱ (sefr yek) ۲ ۳ (do se) ۴ ۵ ۶ (chahâr panj shesh) ۷ ۸ ۹ (haft hasht noh) (the ten Persian digits) once, slowly]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Unicode Arabic chart; Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]

Lesson 5 of 5.
Payoff — reading the figures.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count one to twenty, then say which way digits run inside a Persian line.

Guided Practice.
Read all ten signs shuffled and say each as a Persian word.
Read ۱۰, ۱۱, ۲۰ and ۱۰۰ and say each — dah, yâzdah, bist, sad.
Hear ten amounts named and write each in Persian figures.
Write the ten signs from dictation, out of order, and check ۴, ۵ and ۶ against the models rather than against memory.
Say what a reader can now do that could not be done four chapters ago: read a price, a page number and a telephone number off an Iranian page.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Unicode Arabic chart; Omniglot, Numbers in Persian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Write a hundred in Persian figures.
