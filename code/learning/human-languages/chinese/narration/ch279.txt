You'll want to know: 第二天.
第二天 — dì'èr tiān — "the next day; the following day".
第二天我们去了山上 dì'èr tiān wǒmen qù le shān shàng — The next day we went up the mountain.
In a story, 第二天 (dì'èr tiān) is the day after the one you are telling; 明天 counts from today.
山上 is 山 with 上, the short form of shàngbian: up on the mountain.
The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. It is the dì of dìyī, first.

The word, taken apart.
第二天 (dì'èr tiān) is built from 第, which puts a number in order, 二 'two' and 天 'day'. All three are inherited from Middle Chinese, dejH, nyijH and then (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the first 'order, sequence'. 'The next day' for the whole is the phrase's own sense: the second day of a story.

What you've built.
第 (dì) before a number makes it an ordinal: dìyī, first, is one you say already, and 第二 is second.
第二天 counts from the day the story is on, so it can follow a day in the past: 第二天天气很好 dì'èr tiān tiānqì hěn hǎo.

Script — the bamboo piece of 笑, then a five-stroke body.
第 (dì).
Two pieces, and you write the first already.
The bamboo piece, six strokes, as on top of 笑: on the left a left-falling stroke, a short horizontal from it and a dot; then the same three on the right.
The body, five strokes: a horizontal that turns down at its right end; a horizontal under it; a stroke that goes down, right, down again and hooks to the left, folding the way the foot of 号 does; the vertical down the middle; and last a left-falling stroke to the lower left.
6 + 5 = eleven. The left-falling stroke comes after the vertical, last of all.

…的时候 (… de shíhou) — when, and what was going on.

Grammar Lens: …的时候 and 正在, for what was going on.
…的时候 (… de shíhou) after a clause says when; 正在 in the other clause says what was already going on at that moment.
他来的时候 我正在吃饭 tā lái de shíhou, wǒ zhèngzài chī fàn — When he came, I was eating. 我回家的时候 妈妈正在做饭 wǒ huí jiā de shíhou, māma zhèngzài zuòfàn — When I got home, Mum was cooking.
The clause before 的时候 names the moment, and a bare verb is enough: 他来的时候. The other clause is the scene that was already running, with the 正在 of 我正在吃饭.
正在 has no tense of its own: the story around it puts the scene in the past.
All three characters of 的时候 are ones you write, so it prints whole for the first time.

You'll want to know: 突然.
突然 — tūrán — "suddenly; all of a sudden".
突然下雨了 tūrán xiàyǔ le — Suddenly it started to rain.
他突然来了 tā tūrán lái le — He turned up out of the blue.
The next lesson puts the first character in your hand. The second is the 然 of 然后.

The word, taken apart.
突然 (tūrán) is built from 突 and 然, both inherited from Middle Chinese. The Guangyun lists 突 under two readings: thwot, glossed 'the look of coming out', and dwot, glossed 'to butt' and 'to deceive'; tū is the regular modern sound of thwot. 然 is nyen, glossed as a particle and 'so, like this' (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). 'Suddenly' for the pair is the word's own sense, which neither gloss gives.

What you've built.
突然 goes before the verb, or at the front of the clause: 突然下雨了, 他突然来了 (tā tūrán lái le).
With …的时候 (… de shíhou) it breaks into a scene: 我看书的时候 他突然来了 wǒ kàn shū de shíhou, tā tūrán lái le.

大 with a dot, four strokes, at the foot, as on the right of 然: the bar, the left-falling stroke, the right-falling stroke, and the dot at its upper right last.

You'll want to know: 一下子.
一下子 — yíxiàzi — "all at once; in no time".
天气一下子冷了 tiānqì yíxiàzi lěng le — All at once the weather turned cold.
一 rises before the fall of xià: yíxiàzi, as in yí ge.
Every character in it is one you write.

The word, taken apart.
一下子 (yíxiàzi) is built from 一 'one', 下, here a single go, and the ending 子. All three are inherited from Middle Chinese, 'jit, haeX and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary lists 下 under two readings, haeX and haeH, and glosses the first 'below' and 'to go down'; neither 'a go' nor 'all at once' is given there, so both are the modern word's own senses.

What you've built.
突然 (tūrán) says it came without warning; 一下子 (yíxiàzi) says the change took no time at all.
Both go before the verb: 天气一下子冷了 tiānqì yíxiàzi lěng le. The 了 at the end is the 了 of a change.
