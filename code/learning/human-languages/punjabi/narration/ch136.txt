Punjabi, chapter 136: Palm, Fingernail, Brain, Heel, Ankle.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ਤਲੀ (talī) — a palm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Punjabi for a waist, then the Punjabi for an elbow.

You'll want to know: ਤਲੀ.
ਤਲੀ — talī — "a palm".

The word, taken apart.
ਤਲੀ (talī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from tala, "base; palm, sole" (CDIAL 5731).

You'll want to know: ਦਿਮਾਗ਼.
ਦਿਮਾਗ਼ — dimāg — "a brain".

The word, taken apart.
ਦਿਮਾਗ਼ (dimāg) is a loan from Arabic dimāgh, "brain", which reached Punjabi through Persian. Its Arabic root is d-m-gh (Lane).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: ਅੱਡੀ.
ਅੱਡੀ — aḍḍī — "a heel".

The word, taken apart.
ਅੱਡੀ (aḍḍī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from aḍḍi, "heel", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 191). CDIAL, following Bloch, takes it from Dravidian.
