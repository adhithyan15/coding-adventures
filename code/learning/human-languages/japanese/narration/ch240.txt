Japanese, chapter 240: Likes and Skills.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
じょうず (jōzu) — skilful; good (at).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a magic trick, then the Japanese for shogi.

You'll want to know: じょうず.
じょうず — jōzu — "skilful; good (at)".
あねは うたが じょうずでした。 — ane wa uta ga jōzu deshita — "My elder sister was good at singing."

The word, taken apart.
じょうず (jōzu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are jō, "above" (Middle Chinese dzyangH) and zu, "hand" (syuwX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: へた.
へた — heta — "unskilful; poor (at)".
おとうとは やきゅうが へたでした。 — otōto wa yakyū ga heta deshita — "My younger brother was bad at baseball."

The word, taken apart.
へた (heta) is written with two characters meaning "below" and "hand", read as a whole word rather than character by character; its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
すき (suki) — liked; fond of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for skilful, then the Japanese for unskilful.

You'll want to know: すき.
すき — suki — "liked; fond of".
きょねん あねは まんがが すきでした。 — kyonen ane wa manga ga suki deshita — "Last year my elder sister liked comics."

The word, taken apart.
すき (suki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it comes from suku, "to like", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

きらい (kirai) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it comes from kirau, "to dislike", and it is usually written in kana (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
