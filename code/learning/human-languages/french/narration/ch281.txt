French, chapter 281: Le reportage.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le reportage — the report, the feature (on TV, radio or in a paper).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the listener, then the French for the microphone.

You'll want to know: le reportage.
le reportage — "the report, the feature (on TV, radio or in a paper)".
J'ai vu un reportage sur la vie à la montagne. I watched a feature about life in the mountains.
Faire un reportage — to report on something, on location.

The word, taken apart.
reportage is built with the suffix -age on the verb meaning "to carry back, to report" (EtymDB), which comes from Latin reportāre "to carry back", built on portāre "to carry" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'envoyé spécial — the special correspondent.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the microphone, then the French for the report.

You'll want to know: l'envoyé spécial.
l'envoyé spécial — "the special correspondent".
Notre envoyé spécial est arrivé sur place ce matin. Our special correspondent arrived on the spot this morning.
Envoyé is the past participle of envoyer: someone sent.

The word, taken apart.
envoyé comes from envoyer, inherited from Late Latin inviāre "to put on the way", built on via "way" (Lewis & Short); spécial is a learned borrowing from Latin speciālis (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
le correspondant — the correspondent; a pen friend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the report, then the French for the special correspondent.

You'll want to know: le correspondant.
le correspondant — "the correspondent; a pen friend".
Le correspondant du journal vit à l'étranger depuis dix ans. The paper's correspondent has lived abroad for ten years.
At school, un correspondant is an exchange partner or a pen friend.

The word, taken apart.
correspondant is built on correspondre, a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin correspondēre "to answer together", from con- and respondēre "to answer" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
la dépêche — the dispatch, the wire story.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the special correspondent, then the French for the correspondent.

You'll want to know: la dépêche.
la dépêche — "the dispatch, the wire story".
Une dépêche est arrivée à midi. A wire story came in at noon.
A short news item sent by an agency to the papers.

The word, taken apart.
dépêche is built on dépêcher "to send off quickly", which comes from Old French despeechier "to set free", formed as the opposite of empêcher "to hinder"; the root is usually traced to Latin pedica "a fetter" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'agence de presse — the news agency.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the correspondent, then the French for the dispatch.

You'll want to know: l'agence de presse.
l'agence de presse — "the news agency".
L'agence de presse envoie des dépêches toute la journée. The news agency sends out wire stories all day long.
Other agencies: une agence de voyages, une agence immobilière.

The word, taken apart.
agence is usually traced to Italian agenzia (EtymDB lists no parent), which goes back to Latin agere "to do, to act" (Lewis & Short); presse is built on presser, from Latin premere "to press".
