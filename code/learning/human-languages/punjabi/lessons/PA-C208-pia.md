## Grammar Lens: direct owner
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-FORM-PIA-208]; assesses=[PA-LEX-MAINU, PA-LEX-JANA, PA-GRAM-NU-DATIVE, PA-GRAMMAR-NA-INFINITIVE] -->

> **ਪੈਣਾ** — *paina* — to have to
>
> **ਪਿਆ** — *piaa* — had to; masculine singular perfective form

Hear and say the two whole forms before reading them. Keep **ਪਿਆ** whole. This
chapter uses it only after one familiar infinitive in one past-necessity frame.
It does not open the wider agreement system.

## The form, taken apart — source boundary
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-PIA-208, PA-LEX-MAINU, PA-LEX-JANA, PA-GRAM-NU-DATIVE, PA-GRAMMAR-NA-INFINITIVE] -->

Punjabi University Patiala's reference grammar describes the **-ਣਾ ਪੈ-**
construction as “have to,” says that the person uses **ਨੂੰ**, and gives both
present or future and past examples. AQA's official Panjabi grammar lists the
infinitive, past and perfective forms, and auxiliary **ਪੈ**.
Sources: [Punjabi University reference grammar](https://pt.learnpunjabi.org/assets/A%20Reference%20Grammar_Final.pdf)
and [AQA Panjabi grammar](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/panjabi/a-level/panjabi-7682/specification/subject-content/grammar).

Those sources support this modern construction. Keep the exact form whole;
do not invent a separate etymology for it. This chapter teaches only the
masculine singular perfective in one “had to” frame, not a present or future
obligation, a feminine or plural form, a full agreement paradigm, or any
other sense of the auxiliary.
