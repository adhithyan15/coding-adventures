# युवाम् (yuvām) — you two, as the object

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-PRON-C260-TVAM-02, SA-PRON-C261-AVAM-01, SA-GRAMMAR-NUMERAL-DUAL] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One familiar addressee as object is **त्वाम्** (*tvām*). The
speaker plus one other is **आवाम्** (*āvām*). Turn the dual toward two
addressees.

## You'll want to know: युवाम्

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-PRON-C261-YUVAM-02]; assesses=[] -->

**युवाम्** (*yuvām*) means “you two” as the object.

> **सः युवाम् पश्यति।**
>
> *saḥ yuvām paśyati.*
>
> **He sees you two.**

Whitney §491 lists **युवाम्** beside **आवाम्**. Both are independent dual
forms; the opening tells you whether the pair includes the speaker or consists
of the two people addressed.

## Taken Apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

As a grammatical form, **युवाम्** comes from the second-person pronoun
paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's *Sanskrit
Grammar* §491 records it as the independent dual used for both subject and
object. This lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.

Source: Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* (1889), §491.
