Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 228: Summer Sun.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
लूखो (lūkho) — parched; dry and rough.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a Persian wheel, then the Marwadi for a drought.

You'll want to know: लूखो.
लूखो — lūkho — "parched; dry and rough".
आज हवा लूखी है। āj havā lūkhī hai. The air is parched today.

The word, taken apart.
लूखो (lūkho) is inherited from Sanskrit rūkṣa, "dry, rough"; CDIAL lists Hindi lūkhā, "dry, not greasy", and a Gujarati cousin. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 10799).

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a drought, then the Marwadi for parched.

You'll want to know: तपणो.
तपणो — tapṇo — "to blaze; to be hot (of the sun)".
जेठ मांय सूरज घणो तपै है। jeṭh mā̃y sūraj ghaṇo tapai hai. In early summer the sun blazes.
Past (it …): वो सूरज तप्यो, vo sūraj tapyo.

The word, taken apart.
तपणो (tapṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit tapati, "is hot, heats"; CDIAL lists Hindi and Gujarati verbs for being heated under it, and Marwadi tapṇo keeps the old p. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 5671).

Lesson 3 of 6.
पसीनो (pasīno) — sweat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for parched, then the Marwadi for to blaze.

You'll want to know: पसीनो.
पसीनो — pasīno — "sweat".
जेठ मांय पसीनो आवै है। jeṭh mā̃y pasīno āvai hai. You sweat in early summer.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
पसीनो (pasīno) is inherited from Sanskrit prasvinna, "sweated"; CDIAL lists Hindi pasīnā and a Gujarati word for sweat. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 8897).

Lesson 5 of 6.
सूकणो (sūkṇo) — to dry up; to wither.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for sweat, then the Marwadi for cool.

You'll want to know: सूकणो.
सूकणो — sūkṇo — "to dry up; to wither".
जेठ मांय खेत सूकै है। jeṭh mā̃y khet sūkai hai. The field dries up in early summer.
Past (it …): वो खेत सूक्यो, vo khet sūkyo.

The word, taken apart.
सूकणो (sūkṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed śuṣkati, "becomes dry"; CDIAL lists the Old Marwari verb sūkaï, "dries up" (CDIAL 12552). सूको, dry, is its relative.
