Mandarin Chinese, chapter 73: At the Station.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
下车 (xiàchē) — to get off (a bus or train).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for road, then the Mandarin for to arrive.

You'll want to know: 下车.
下车 — xiàchē — "to get off (a bus or train)".
我们下车吃饭 wǒmen xiàchē chī fàn — Let's get off and eat.
The new character, in pieces: 下 (xià) — a bar, a vertical hanging from it, and a dot to the right: 上 turned over. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
下车 (xiàchē) is built from 下 (xià) 'to go down' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Script — a bar, a vertical, a dot.
下 (xià).
One piece, three strokes.
A long horizontal across the top.
A vertical hanging down from its middle.
A dot to the right of the vertical, slanting down to the right.

Lesson 4 of 6.
huǒchēzhàn — railway station.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get off, then the Mandarin for to get on.

You'll want to know: huǒchēzhàn.
huǒchēzhàn — "railway station".
huǒchēzhàn hěn yuǎn — The station is a long way off.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huǒchēzhàn is built from 火车 'train' and zhàn 'a stop', inherited from Middle Chinese treamH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary glosses as the everyday word for standing alone. The sense 'station' is not given there, so how it got it is not settled.

Lesson 6 of 6.
piào — ticket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for railway station, then the Mandarin for airport.

You'll want to know: piào.
piào — "ticket".
wǒ mǎi le piào — I've bought the tickets.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
piào is written with a character the Guangyun records, under an older form, as phjiew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'flying fire'. That is probably a different word: the ticket sense is not given there, and phjiew would regularly give piāo, not piào. How the character came to write the word for ticket is not settled.
