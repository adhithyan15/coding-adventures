# કોઈ નથી — nobody, nothing, never

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NA-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NAHI-01, GU-LEX-C99-DEF99-05, GU-LEX-C100-DEF100-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *don't worry*, then *not now*. And two words from the chapters on
this and that: **કોઈ**, *someone*, and **કંઈક**, *something*.

## Grammar Lens: કોઈ with નથી
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-KOI-NATHI-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C99-DEF99-05, GU-NEG-NATHI-01] -->

> **અહીં કોઈ નથી.** — *ahī̃ koī nathī.* — There is nobody here.

Gujarati needs no separate word for *nobody*. It says *someone is-not*:
**કોઈ**, then **નથી**.

## You'll want to know: કંઈ and ક્યારેય
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C145-KAI-NATHI-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-KYAREY-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C100-DEF100-01, GU-Q-KYAARE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C145-NEGATE-01] -->

**કંઈક** is **કંઈ** with an extra **-ક**. Before a negative, Gujarati uses the
shorter **કંઈ** (*kaī̃*):

> **હું કંઈ નથી ખાતો.** — *hũ kaī̃ nathī khāto.* — I'm not eating anything.

**ક્યારેય** (*kyārey*) is **ક્યારે**, *when*, with **-ય**, *ever*, on the end.
With **નથી** it means *never*:

> **તે ક્યારેય ચા નથી પીતી.** — *te kyārey cā nathī pītī.* — She never drinks tea.

The shape is the same three times: an *any* word, then **નથી**.
