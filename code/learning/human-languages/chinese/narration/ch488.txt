Mandarin Chinese, chapter 488: What Do You Want to Be.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gōngchéngshī — engineer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be out of work, then the Mandarin for a factory.

You'll want to know: gōngchéngshī.
gōngchéngshī — "engineer".
wǒ yǐhòu xiǎng dāng gōngchéngshī — I want to be an engineer.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gōngchéngshī is built from 工 'work', chéng 'a measure' and shī 'a master', all inherited from Middle Chinese, kuwng, drjeng and srij (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a rule' and the third 'a model to follow'; the sense 'engineering' is modern and not given there.

kēxuéjiā — "scientist".

The word, taken apart.
kēxuéjiā is built from kē 'a grade', 学 'to learn' and 家 'a household', all inherited from Middle Chinese, khwa, haewk and kae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'a rule' and 'a class'. The senses 'science' and 'someone skilled in it' are modern and not given there.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fānyì — to translate; to interpret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a chef, then the Mandarin for a scientist.

You'll want to know: fānyì.
fānyì — "to translate; to interpret".
nǐ néng bāng wǒ fānyì zhège ma — Can you translate this for me?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fānyì is built from fān 'to turn over' and yì 'to translate', both inherited from Middle Chinese, phjon and yek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to overturn' and the second 'to pass words on', the name of the interpreters for the peoples of the north.
