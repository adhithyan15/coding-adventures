Spanish, chapter 437: Decidir, Coincidir, Equivocarse.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
decidir — to cut the question off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ofrecer. The shop has offered you Thursday or Saturday. Say what you now have to do.

You'll want to know: decidir.
decidir — to decide. decidirse — to make up your mind.
Usted decide — you decide. It is what a notice says when it has finished offering and the choice is yours.

The word, taken apart.
de- off, plus -cidir from Latin caedere, to cut. To cut the question off.
The same caedere is under a family that all involve cutting something away:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
word: decidir. what is cut: the question.
word: preciso. what is cut: cut exactly to size.
word: conciso. what is cut: cut short.
decidir and decidirse are not the same. Decido el menú is choosing a thing; me decido is the inner moment when you stop hesitating. Spanish marks that difference with the reflexive, where English leans on decide versus make up my mind.

Lesson 2 of 7.
coincidir — to fall on the same day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el grupo. Say decidir. The Tuesday group meets at the hour you already have something else. Name that problem.

You'll want to know: coincidir.
coincidir — to coincide, and in everyday use to clash.
Me coincide con el trabajo — it clashes with work. That is the ordinary reason somebody gives for not taking a place.

The word, taken apart.
co- together, plus the same -incidir family as decidir — things falling together.
Your English eye can read it without help, and that is the point worth taking:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
coincidir means coincide.
decidir means decide.
dividir means divide.
But the everyday meaning has drifted. English coincide is neutral or lucky; Spanish coincidir, said about a timetable, is almost always a complaint. The form is transparent and the use is not, which is exactly where a confident reader gets caught.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
To clash? (coincidir.) What does the co- add? (Together.) Is it usually good news? (No — about a timetable it is a complaint.)


Lesson 3 of 7.
equivocarse — when two things sound the same.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el pedido. The restaurant has sent two salads instead of a salad and a soup. Say what they did.

You'll want to know: equivocarse.
equivocarse — to be mistaken, to get it wrong.
Nos hemos equivocado — we have made a mistake. It is how a shop apologises without naming who is at fault.

The word, taken apart.
aequus equal, plus vox voice. Equal voice — two things that sound the same and get taken for each other.
That is a precise picture of what a mistake is, and the same two pieces give you a word you may already recognise:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: equivocarse. the equal voice in it: to confuse two things.
word: equívoco. the equal voice in it: ambiguous.
It is almost always reflexive. You say me equivoco, not equivoco, because the error happens to you rather than being done to something else. Spanish uses that reflexive for a whole family of things that befall a person — caerse, olvidarse, perderse — and it is worth noticing as a group rather than memorising one at a time.

Lesson 4 of 7.
tardar — how long a thing takes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say el retraso. That lesson mentioned a second word for lateness and did not teach it. Say it if you remember; here it is either way.

You'll want to know: tardar.
tardar — to take time, to be long about something.
Tardo una hora — it takes me an hour. ¿Cuánto tarda? — how long does it take?

Grammar Lens: the delay and the duration.
el retraso and tardar are not interchangeable, and the difference is worth having exactly:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
you mean: it is later than promised. you say: lleva retraso.
you mean: it takes this long. you say: tarda una hora.
A journey that tarda two hours is not late — two hours is what the journey costs. A train with retraso has broken a promise. English blurs both into late and takes, and a reader who maps one to one will misread a station announcement.
Both come from tardus, slow, so the shared root is real and the split is recent.

Lesson 5 of 7.
cenar — the meal as a verb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say comer. You can say you eat. Say that you eat in the evening, and notice how many words English needs.

You'll want to know: cenar.
cenar — to have dinner, to dine. la cena is the meal itself.
Cenamos a las nueve — we have dinner at nine.

The word, taken apart.
Spanish makes a verb out of each meal, where English has to say have plus a noun:
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
Spanish verb: desayunar. English needs: to have breakfast.
Spanish verb: comer. English needs: to have lunch.
Spanish verb: cenar. English needs: to have dinner.
comer is the trap in that list. You already hold it as to eat, and in Spain it also means specifically to have lunch — ¿has comido? at four in the afternoon is asking whether you have had lunch, not whether you have eaten anything at all today.
So the three verbs are a set, and comer belongs to it as well as to the general sense.

Lesson 6 of 7.
(decidir, coincidir, equivocarse, tardar, cenar) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say all five: to decide, to clash, to get it wrong, to take time, to have dinner.

Grammar Lens: the reflexive marks what happens to you.
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
plain: decido. reflexive: me decido. the difference: choosing a thing vs making up your mind.
plain: equivoco. reflexive: me equivoco. the difference: the error befalls you.
equivocarse is almost never plain, because a mistake is something that happens to a person. The same shape covers caerse, olvidarse and perderse — a family worth noticing together rather than one at a time.
coincidir takes no reflexive and still points at you: me coincide uses the indirect pronoun, not the reflexive, because the clash is reported to you rather than done by you.

What you've built.
Seven chapters ago you could name things. You can now arrange an evening and say what went wrong with it. You can be offered two dates, decide, say the other one clashes, admit a mistake, say how long something takes and name the meal at the end of it.
tardar closes a debt: the chapter on delays mentioned it in passing and did not teach it, and a word shown but never given is one a reader half-knows.
