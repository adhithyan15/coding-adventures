Lesson 1 of 6.
すいじ (suiji) — kitchen work; cooking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for animal feed, then the Japanese for to jump.

You'll want to know: すいじ.
すいじ — suiji — "kitchen work; cooking".
ちちは にちようびに すいじを します。 — chichi wa nichiyōbi ni suiji o shimasu — "My father does the cooking on Sunday."

The word, taken apart.
すいじ (suiji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sui, "cook" (Middle Chinese tsyhwe) and ji, "matter" (dzriH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

じゃぐち (jaguchi) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: Sino-Japanese ja, "snake" (Middle Chinese zyae), borrowed from Chinese and native guchi, "mouth" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ふっとうする (futtō suru) — to come to the boil.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a cooking stove, then the Japanese for tap water.

You'll want to know: ふっとうする.
ふっとうする — futtō suru — "to come to the boil".
やかんの ゆが ふっとうしたら、たまごを いれて ください。 — yakan no yu ga futtōshitara, tamago o irete kudasai — "When the water boils, put the eggs in."
Polite ふっとうします, futtōshimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): やかんの みずが ふっとうしました。, yakan no mizu ga futtōshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
ふっとうする (futtō suru) is the Sino-Japanese (kango) noun futtō with the native verb suru, "to do": two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are fu, "seethe" (Middle Chinese pj+jH) and tō, "leap" (dong) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
