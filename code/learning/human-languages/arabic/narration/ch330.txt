Arabic, chapter 330: Prison.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سجن (sajana) — to imprison, to jail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for independence, then the Arabic for a country’s people.

You'll want to know: سجن.
سجن — sajana — "to imprison, to jail".
سجن (sajana) اللص سنتين. sujina al-liṣṣ sanatayn. The thief was jailed for two years.
Present: يسجن, yasjun.
The noun سجن (sajana), sijn, a prison, is spelled the same.

The word, taken apart.
سجن (sajana) is built on the native Arabic root s-j-n (Lane), "to shut up, to confine"; sijn is the place of it.

هرب سجين من السجن ليلا. haraba sajīn min as-sijn laylan. A prisoner escaped from the prison at night.
Plural: سجناء, sujanāʾ.

The word, taken apart.
سجين (sajīn) is built on the native Arabic root s-j-n (Lane), "to confine". The faʿīl pattern gives the one confined.

كانت الزنزانة صغيرة ومظلمة. kānat az-zinzāna ṣaghīra wa-muẓlima. The cell was small and dark.
Plural: زنازين, zanāzīn.

The word, taken apart.
زنزانة (zinzāna) is not in the old dictionaries Lane drew on; it belongs to later Arabic, and the origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
قيد (qayd) — a shackle; a restriction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a prisoner, then the Arabic for a prison cell.

You'll want to know: قيد.
قيد — qayd — "a shackle; a restriction".
وضعت الشرطة القيد في يدي اللص. waḍaʿat ash-shurṭa al-qayd fī yaday al-liṣṣ. The police put the handcuffs on the thief’s hands.
Plural: قيود, quyūd.
على قيد (qayd) الحياة: alive, "in the bond of life".

The word, taken apart.
قيد (qayd) is built on the native Arabic root q-y-d (Lane), where qayd is the hobble put on a horse or camel, and qayyada, "to tie; to note down".

Lesson 5 of 5.
أفرج (afraja) — to release, to set free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a prison cell, then the Arabic for a shackle.

You'll want to know: أفرج.
أفرج — afraja — "to release, to set free".
أفرجت الشرطة عن الرجل في المساء. afrajat ash-shurṭa ʿan ar-rajul fī al-masāʾ. The police released the man in the evening.
Present: يفرج, yufrij.
It takes عن: أفرج (afraja) عن السجين.

The word, taken apart.
أفرج (afraja) is built on the native Arabic root f-r-j (Lane), "to open a gap; to relieve"; faraj is relief from trouble. It is Form IV, "to open the way out".
