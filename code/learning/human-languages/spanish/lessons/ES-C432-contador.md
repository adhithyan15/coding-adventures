# el contador — the thing that counts

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CONTAR-08, ES-LEX-C432-CASA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *contar*. Say *la lavadora*. Do the same move again: the ending
on the verb, and say what the machine must be.

## You'll want to know: el contador
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-02]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CONTAR-08] -->

**el contador** — the meter. The box that counts your water, your gas, your
electricity.

**contar** carries two senses you already hold — to count, and to tell a story.
The meter takes the counting one.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C432-CASA-02, ES-LEX-AGUA-01] -->

Twice in two lessons the same ending has handed you a word. That is worth
pausing on, because it is the difference between a vocabulary you memorise and
one you can extend.

| you know | you can reach |
|---|---|
| lavar | la lavadora |
| contar | el contador |

**el contador del agua** is the phrase you will actually meet, on notices about
somebody coming to read it. Note the gender: this one is masculine where the
washing machine was feminine, and nothing about the ending decides that — the
machine takes whatever gender its own word already had.
