# The question that is only a voice

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-KYA-POLAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **क्या आप आ सकते हैं?** — the question you got one lesson ago.
Now say it again and leave the first word out.

## Sounds you'll need: the rise carries it alone
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SOUND-QUESTION-RISE-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-SAKNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-KYA-POLAR-01] -->

> **आप आ सकते हैं?** — *āp ā sakte haiṁ?* — **Can you come?**

Word for word that is a statement. What makes it a question is the **pitch of
the last two syllables going up**, and nothing else. Speakers drop **क्या** far
more often than they use it, so this is the ordinary form and the particle is
the careful one.

Say the pair and listen, do not read:

- **आप आ सकते हैं.** — flat at the end — *you can come*
- **आप आ सकते हैं?** — rising at the end — *can you come?*

Two things follow. First, you can ask any yes-or-no question in Hindi today
without learning another word — every statement in this book is already a
question if you lift the end of it. Second, and less comfortable: a listening
paper will hand you the rise **without** the particle, so the ear has to catch
it. A candidate reading only for **क्या** will hear a statement and answer
nothing.
