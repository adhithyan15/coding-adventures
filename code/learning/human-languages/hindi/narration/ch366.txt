Hindi, chapter 366: Reasonable or Not.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
वाजिब (vājib) — reasonable, fair (of a price, a demand).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to hold up, then the Hindi for to get (someone) to accept.

You'll want to know: वाजिब.
वाजिब — vājib — "reasonable, fair (of a price, a demand)".
यह दाम वाजिब है। yah dām vājib hai. This price is reasonable.

The word, taken apart.
वाजिब (vājib) is usually traced to Arabic wājib, "due, necessary", which reached Hindi through Persian.

Lesson 3 of 6.
भ्रम (bhram) — a misconception, an illusion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for reasonable, then the Hindi for legitimate.

You'll want to know: भ्रम.
भ्रम — bhram — "a misconception, an illusion".
यह एक भ्रम है कि पैसे से सब कुछ मिलता है। yah ek bhram hai ki paise se sab kuch miltā hai. It's a misconception that money buys everything.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
भ्रम (bhram) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit bhrama, "wandering; confusion" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for legitimate, then the Hindi for a misconception.

You'll want to know: बहकाना.
बहकाना — bahkānā — "to lead astray, to mislead".
कुछ लोगों ने बच्चों को बहकाया। kuch logoṁ ne baccoṁ ko bahkāyā. Some people led the children astray.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे बहकाया, usne mujhe bahkāyā.

The word, taken apart.
बहकाना (bahkānā) is the causative of बहकना, "to be misled", which CDIAL files under Sanskrit vyathate, "trembles, goes astray" (CDIAL 12164).
