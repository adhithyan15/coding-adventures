Spanish, chapter 729: Defectos de cada día.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
terco — stubborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for skilful, then the Spanish for wit.

You'll want to know: terco.
terco — "stubborn".
Es tan terco como su padre. He is as stubborn as his father.
Cabezota is the colloquial word in Spain.

The word, taken apart.
For terco the origin is not settled. It is found in old Spanish, and no Latin or Germanic source has been proved.

You'll want to know: perezoso.
perezoso — "lazy".
Los domingos soy muy perezoso. On Sundays I am very lazy.
Un perezoso is also the animal, a sloth.

The word, taken apart.
perezoso is built on pereza "laziness" with the ending -oso; pereza is usually traced to Latin pigritia.

Lesson 3 of 5.
la pereza — laziness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for stubborn, then the Spanish for lazy.

You'll want to know: la pereza.
la pereza — "laziness".
Me da pereza salir con este frío. I cannot be bothered to go out in this cold.
Me da pereza + infinitive — I cannot be bothered to.

The word, taken apart.
pereza is usually traced to Latin pigritia "laziness", built on piger "slow, lazy"; Latin -itia became Spanish -eza, as in tristeza.

Lesson 4 of 5.
entrometido — nosy, meddling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for lazy, then the Spanish for laziness.

You'll want to know: entrometido.
entrometido — "nosy, meddling".
Mi vecina es muy entrometida. My neighbour is very nosy.
Entrometerse — to meddle. Feminine entrometida.

The word, taken apart.
entrometido is the participle of entrometer, built on entre "between" and meter "to put", which is inherited from Latin mittere "to send" (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: la curiosidad.
la curiosidad — "curiosity".
Lo pregunto por curiosidad. I am asking out of curiosity.
Por curiosidad — out of curiosity.

The word, taken apart.
curiosidad was borrowed from Latin cūriōsitās (EtymDB), built on cūriōsus, from cūra "care".
