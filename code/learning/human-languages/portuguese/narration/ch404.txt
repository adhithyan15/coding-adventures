Lesson 1 of 5.
o gasóleo — diesel (fuel).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to honk, then the Portuguese for to mist up.

You'll want to know: o gasóleo.
o gasóleo — "diesel (fuel)".
O meu carro anda a gasóleo. My car runs on diesel.
In Brazil: o diesel. Petrol is a gasolina.

The word, taken apart.
gasóleo is built from gás and óleo 'oil'; óleo goes back to Latin oleum 'olive oil' (Lewis & Short).

a mangueira — "the hose".
O empregado pôs a mangueira no carro. The attendant put the hose into the car.
Also a garden hose. A mangueira is the mango tree too, a different word.

The word, taken apart.
mangueira is built on manga 'sleeve' with -eira: a long cloth tube. Manga is inherited from Latin manica 'sleeve' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
abastecer — to fill up (with fuel); to supply.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for fuel, then the Portuguese for the hose.

You'll want to know: abastecer.
abastecer — "to fill up (with fuel); to supply".
Paramos na bomba para abastecer. We stop at the petrol station to fill up.
Also to supply a shop or a town: abastecer o mercado.

The word, taken apart.
abastecer is built from a- and Old Portuguese bastecer 'to provide', which EtymDB lists. It is usually linked with bastar 'to be enough', but the details are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
atestar — to fill to the top; to certify.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hose, then the Portuguese for to fill up.

You'll want to know: atestar.
atestar — "to fill to the top; to certify".
Antes de uma viagem longa, ele atesta o depósito. Before a long trip, he fills the tank to the top.
In formal writing, atestar also means to certify: um atestado médico, a doctor's note.

The word, taken apart.
EtymDB gives two sources: the sense 'fill to the top' is built on testo 'lid', inherited from Latin testum; the sense 'certify' is a learned borrowing from Latin attestārī 'to bear witness'.
