Japanese, chapter 384: To the Airfield.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ひこうじょう (hikōjō) — an airfield.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for swimming in the sea, then the Japanese for to be see-through.

You'll want to know: ひこうじょう.
ひこうじょう — hikōjō — "an airfield".
この しまには ちいさい ひこうじょうが あります。 — kono shima ni wa chiisai hikōjō ga arimasu — "There is a small airfield on this island."

The word, taken apart.
ひこうじょう (hikōjō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are hi, "fly" (Middle Chinese pj+j), kō, "going" (haeng) and jō, "place" (drjang) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たびだつ (tabidatsu) — to set off on a journey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for takeoff, then the Japanese for landing (of a plane).

You'll want to know: たびだつ.
たびだつ — tabidatsu — "to set off on a journey".
あには あした くうこうから たびだちます。 — ani wa ashita kūkō kara tabidachimasu — "My brother sets off from the airport tomorrow."
Polite たびだちます, tabidachimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが くうこうから たびだちました。, ani ga kūkō kara tabidachimashita.

The word, taken apart.
たびだつ (tabidatsu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from tabi, "journey" and datsu, "setting out" (from tatsu); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
