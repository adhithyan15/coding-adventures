Italian, chapter 270: More Than, For Three Years --- Comparing and How Long.
3 lessons. All 3 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 3.
più … di — more than.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "see you later". (A più tardi.) Literally, "to more late".
[pause 1 second]
That più is the whole of this lesson's new grammar.

You'll want to know: più … di.
To compare two things, put più, "more", in front of the adjective and di, "than", in front of the second thing:
Il treno è più veloce dell'autobus. — The train is faster than the bus. L'aereo è più caro del treno. — The plane is dearer than the train. Sei più alto di me. — You are taller than me.
English sometimes builds the comparison into the adjective, faster, taller; Italian builds it the same way almost every time, with più. The di welds to the article, as a does: del, della, dell'. Before a name or a stressed pronoun it stays plain: di me, di te.
Its opposite is meno, "less": L'autobus è meno caro del treno — the bus is less expensive than the train.

Grammar Lens: il più.
For the most of all, keep più and give the noun its article:
il treno più veloce — the fastest train la città più bella — the most beautiful city.
The adjective still comes after the noun, as Italian adjectives mostly do, and the article does the work of English -est. Who it is the most among comes with di: il treno più veloce del mondo, the fastest train in the world.

The word, taken apart.
più is inherited from Latin plūs, "more" (EtymDB 2.1). Latin pl became Italian pi, the same change that made plicāre into piegare. English borrowed plūs whole as plus, and built on it plural and surplus.

Lesson 2 of 3.
da tre anni — how long, and still going.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the train is faster than the bus". (Il treno è più veloce dell'autobus.) Then say "a year". (Un anno.)
[pause 1 second]
An experience has a length. Italian measures it two ways, and the choice depends on whether it is over.

You'll want to know: da tre anni.
Studio l'italiano da tre anni. — I have been studying Italian for three years. Abito qui da due mesi. — I have lived here for two months.
Listen to the verbs. English says have been studying, a form that reaches back into the past. Italian says studio, the plain present, because the studying is still going on today. da with a length of time means "from that far back, up to now".
That is the one to practise until it is automatic, because an English speaker's instinct sends them to the past, and the past here says something different.

Grammar Lens: per, for a stretch that is over.
When the stretch has ended, the verb goes into the passato prossimo and the length takes per:
Ho abitato a Roma per due anni. — I lived in Rome for two years. (and then I left).
So one English word, for, splits in two. da + present: it is still going on. per + passato prossimo: it is over. And durante, which you have, is "during": it names the stretch something happened inside, durante il viaggio, "during the journey", not its length.

The phrase, taken apart.
da is two Latin prepositions run together: dē, "from, down from", and ab, "from, away from" (EtymDB 2.1 lists it as a compound of the two). So da tre anni is, quite literally, "from three years": counting from a point three years back, up to the present moment.

[your turn — say: the stretch that is over — Ho abitato a Roma per due anni.]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which tense goes with da tre anni, and why? (The present — it is still going on.) Which word takes over when the stretch is finished? (Per, with the passato prossimo.) And what is da, taken apart? (Dē and ab, "from" twice.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
Review — describe an experience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I have been studying Italian for three years". (Studio l'italiano da tre anni.)

What you've built.
Two chapters gave you the four things that turn a fact into an esperienza: how it felt, what you hope, how it compared, and how long it went on.
Studio l'italiano da tre anni. Prima era difficile ed ero nervoso, ma adesso sono contento. Secondo me l'italiano è più facile della matematica. Spero di parlare bene.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count the moves: a length still running, da tre anni; a feeling in the past, ero nervoso, against one now, sono contento; a comparison, più facile della matematica, marked as an opinion; a hope, spero di.
