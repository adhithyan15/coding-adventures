Mandarin Chinese, chapter 107: On the Move.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tiào — to jump.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for bicycle, then the Mandarin for to ride.

You'll want to know: tiào.
tiào — "to jump".
wǒ de gǒu hěn xǐhuan tiào — My dog loves jumping.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiào is inherited from Middle Chinese dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to leap'. The regular change from dew gives tiáo, with a rising tone, not the falling tiào of today; how it came to its tone is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday actions.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tiào]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tiào, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ de gǒu hěn xǐhuan tiào — My dog loves jumping.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for bicycle, then the Mandarin for to ride, then say tiào again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does tiào mean? ("To jump".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
bān — to move house; to move (something heavy).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to jump, then the Mandarin for to climb.

You'll want to know: bān.
bān — "to move house; to move (something heavy)".
nǐ néng bāng wǒ bān zhuōzi ma — Can you help me move the table?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bān has no older form on record under this character. The dictionary lists the same sound, pan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), under an older character glossed 'to transport', which is probably the same word, though that is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
líkāi — to leave a place.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to move house, then the Mandarin for to stop.

You'll want to know: líkāi.
líkāi — "to leave a place".
wǒmen jǐ diǎn líkāi — What time are we leaving?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
líkāi is built from lí 'to part' and 开 'to open', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lje and khoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary says parting at a short distance is lje, at a long one is another word.

What you've built.
