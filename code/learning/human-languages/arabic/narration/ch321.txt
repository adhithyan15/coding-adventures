Arabic, chapter 321: Robbers and gangs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
سارق (sāriq) — a robber.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to give news, then the Arabic for to clarify.

You'll want to know: سارق.
سارق — sāriq — "a robber".
ركض السارق بين السيارات. rakaḍa as-sāriq bayna as-sayyārāt. The robber ran between the cars.
Plural سارقون. لص is the everyday word; سارق (sāriq) is the one who stole.

The word, taken apart.
سارق (sāriq) is built on the native Arabic root s-r-q (Lane), "to take a thing secretly". Sāriq is its active participle, "one who steals".

Lesson 2 of 5.
نشال (nashshāl) — a pickpocket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to clarify, then the Arabic for a robber.

You'll want to know: نشال.
نشال — nashshāl — "a pickpocket".
سرق نشال محفظتي في الحافلة. saraqa nashshāl miḥfaẓatī fī al-ḥāfila. A pickpocket stole my wallet on the bus.

The word, taken apart.
نشال (nashshāl) is usually traced to the native Arabic root n-sh-l, "to snatch out quickly". The faʿʿāl pattern names someone who does a thing as his trade.

Lesson 3 of 5.
عصابة (ʿiṣāba) — a gang, a band.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a robber, then the Arabic for a pickpocket.

You'll want to know: عصابة.
عصابة — ʿiṣāba — "a gang, a band".
اعتقلت الشرطة عصابة من خمسة رجال. iʿtaqalat ash-shurṭa ʿiṣāba min khamsat rijāl. The police arrested a gang of five men.
Also a bandage or headband: one root, "what binds".

The word, taken apart.
عصابة (ʿiṣāba) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-ṣ-b (Lane), "to bind round". Lane gives ʿiṣāba as a band tied round the head, and as a band of men bound together.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سلب (salaba) — to rob, to snatch away.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a pickpocket, then the Arabic for a gang.

You'll want to know: سلب.
سلب — salaba — "to rob, to snatch away".
سلب رجلان المسافر في الطريق. salaba rajulān al-musāfir fī aṭ-ṭarīq. Two men robbed the traveller on the road.
Present: يسلب, yaslub.

The word, taken apart.
سلب (salaba) is built on the native Arabic root s-l-b (Lane), "to strip a man of his clothes or goods"; salb is taking away, and so salbī, negative.
