# el doctor / la doctora — the teacher-word at the clinic

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MEDICO] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Recall **el médico**, the physician. Spanish has another word you
will hear when making an appointment: **el doctor** or **la doctora**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-DOCTOR]; assesses=[] -->

Medieval Latin **doctor** meant “teacher,” from **docēre**, “to teach.” English
kept the same academic word. Spanish also uses **doctor** for a physician and
as a form of address. That does not erase the older academic meaning: context
tells you whether the doctor holds a doctorate or is treating a patient.

## Grammar Lens: name the person
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-DOCTOR, ES-LEX-MEDICO] -->

The ending follows the person: **el doctor**, **la doctora**. To ask for an
appointment, use the frame **pedir cita con el doctor**. **Médico** remains the
more specific profession-word; **doctor** is the word the mock message uses.
