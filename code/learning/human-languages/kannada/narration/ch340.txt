Lesson 1 of 6.
ದಂತಕಥೆ (dantakathe) — a legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for to scatter, then the Kannada for a stampede.

You'll want to know: ದಂತಕಥೆ.
ದಂತಕಥೆ — dantakathe — "a legend".
ಈ ಕೋಟೆಯ ಬಗ್ಗೆ ಒಂದು ದಂತಕಥೆ ಇದೆ. ī kōṭeya bagge ondu dantakathe ide. There is a legend about this fort.
Plural: ದಂತಕಥೆಗಳು, dantakathegaḷu.
A story told from one generation to the next, partly history and partly not.

The word, taken apart.
ದಂತಕಥೆ (dantakathe) is a Kannada compound whose second part is ಕಥೆ, "a story", from Sanskrit kathā (CDIAL 2704); the origin of the first part is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
ಪುರಾಣ (purāṇa) — a myth; an old legend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a stampede, then the Kannada for a legend.

You'll want to know: ಪುರಾಣ.
ಪುರಾಣ — purāṇa — "a myth; an old legend".
ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಮಲಗುವ ಮುಂಚೆ ಒಂದು ಪುರಾಣ ಕಥೆ ಹೇಳಿದರು. ajji malaguva muñce ondu purāṇa kathe hēḷidaru. Before bed Grandmother told an old legend.
Plural: ಪುರಾಣಗಳು, purāṇagaḷu.
The old Sanskrit story collections are the ಪುರಾಣಗಳು. In talk, ಪುರಾಣ (purāṇa) can also be a long, boring tale.

The word, taken apart.
ಪುರಾಣ (purāṇa) is borrowed from Sanskrit purāṇa, "ancient, belonging to olden times" (CDIAL 8283), then the name of collections of old tales.

Lesson 3 of 6.
ದೈತ್ಯ (daitya) — a giant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a legend, then the Kannada for a myth.

You'll want to know: ದೈತ್ಯ.
ದೈತ್ಯ — daitya — "a giant".
ಕಥೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಒಬ್ಬ ದೈತ್ಯ ಬೆಟ್ಟದ ಮೇಲೆ ಇದ್ದನು. katheyalli obba daitya beṭṭada mēle iddanu. In the story a giant lived on the hill.
Plural: ದೈತ್ಯರು, daityaru.
As an adjective it means huge: ದೈತ್ಯ (daitya) ಮರ, a giant tree.

The word, taken apart.
ದೈತ್ಯ (daitya) is borrowed from Sanskrit daitya, the name of a race of demons in the old stories (Monier-Williams); in Kannada it also means a giant.

ಧೈರ್ಯವಂತ ಹುಡುಗ ರಾಕ್ಷಸನನ್ನು ಸೋಲಿಸಿದನು. dhairyavanta huḍuga rākṣasanannu sōlisidanu. The brave boy defeated the ogre.

The word, taken apart.
ರಾಕ್ಷಸ (rākṣasa) is borrowed from Sanskrit rākṣasa, "demoniacal", and as a noun "a demon" (CDIAL 10672).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ಶಾಪ (śāpa) — a curse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a giant, then the Kannada for a demon.

You'll want to know: ಶಾಪ.
ಶಾಪ — śāpa — "a curse".
ರಾಕ್ಷಸನ ಶಾಪದಿಂದ ರಾಣಿ ಕಲ್ಲಾದಳು. rākṣasana śāpadinda rāṇi kallādaḷu. Through the demon's curse the queen turned to stone.
A curse is "given": ಶಾಪ (śāpa) ಕೊಡು, to curse.

The word, taken apart.
ಶಾಪ (śāpa) is borrowed from Sanskrit śāpa, "a curse, abuse" (CDIAL 12393), from the root śap, "to curse".
