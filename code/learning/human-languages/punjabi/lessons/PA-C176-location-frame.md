# A thing, a place, then ਹੈ

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-DEM-EH-01, PA-PRON-OH-01, PA-LEX-C89-PLACE89-03, PA-LEX-C89-PLACE89-04, PA-POSTPOSITION-VICH-01, PA-Q-KITTHE-01, PA-LEX-HAI-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the two pointing pairs: **ਇਹ / ਉਹ** and **ਇੱਥੇ / ਉੱਥੇ**.
Ask **ਕਿੱਥੇ?**. Then say **ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ** and leave **ਹੈ** waiting; Punjabi keeps
its verb for the end.

## Grammar Lens: a thing, a place, then ਹੈ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PA-GRAMMAR-EXISTENTIAL-LOCATION-01]; assesses=[PA-POSTPOSITION-VICH-01, PA-LEX-C71-HOME71-02, PA-LEX-C67-FOOD67-03, PA-LEX-HAI-02, PA-GRAMMAR-VERB-LAST-02] -->

> **ਕਿਤਾਬ ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ।**
>
> *kitāb ghar vich hai.*
>
> The book is at home.

The frame has three pieces:

1. the thing that is present — **ਕਿਤਾਬ**;
2. its place — **ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ**; and
3. **ਹੈ** at the end.

Punjabi does not need a separate word matching English *there* in *there is*.
The placed thing plus **ਹੈ** states that the thing is present in that location.

## Grammar Lens: pointing without a postposition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-GRAMMAR-EXISTENTIAL-LOCATION-01, PA-LEX-C89-PLACE89-03, PA-LEX-C89-PLACE89-04, PA-LEX-C89-PLACE89-05, PA-LEX-HAI-02] -->

When the location itself is **here** or **there**, the familiar place word can
stand directly before **ਹੈ**:

> **ਦੁਕਾਨ ਇੱਥੇ ਹੈ।** — *dukān itthe hai.* — The shop is here.
>
> **ਦੁਕਾਨ ਉੱਥੇ ਹੈ।** — *dukān utthe hai.* — The shop is there.

The second sentence is the short practical answer this chapter is building:
**ਦੁਕਾਨ ਉੱਥੇ ਹੈ**.
