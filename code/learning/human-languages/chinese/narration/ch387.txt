Mandarin Chinese, chapter 387: Money In, Money Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
shōurù — income.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for good value for money, then the Mandarin for to turn bad (of the weather).

You'll want to know: shōurù.
shōurù — "income".
yīnwèi tā de shōurù bù gāo suǒyǐ tā měitiān zài jiā zuòfàn — His income isn't high, so he cooks at home every day.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shōurù is built from shōu 'to gather' and rù 'to go in', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syuw and nyip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to take in'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cúnkuǎn — savings (in the bank).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to turn bad (of the weather), then the Mandarin for income.

You'll want to know: cúnkuǎn.
cúnkuǎn — "savings (in the bank)".
wǒ yǒu yìdiǎnr cúnkuǎn — I've got a little money saved.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cúnkuǎn is built from cún 'to keep' and kuǎn 'a sum', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzwon and khwanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be present' and the second 'sincere'. The sense 'a sum of money' is not given there.

Lesson 3 of 5.
zhàngdān — bill (to pay).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for income, then the Mandarin for savings (in the bank).

You'll want to know: zhàngdān.
zhàngdān — "bill (to pay)".
zhège yuè de zhàngdān wǒ hái méi fù — I haven't paid this month's bills yet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhàngdān is built from zhàng 'an account' and dān 'a single sheet'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese tan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'single'; the dictionary has no entry for the first, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
wǎnggòu — to shop online.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for savings (in the bank), then the Mandarin for a bill (to pay).

You'll want to know: wǎnggòu.
wǎnggòu — "to shop online".
wǒ juéde wǎnggòu bǐ qù shāngdiàn piányi — I think shopping online is cheaper than going to the shops.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wǎnggòu is built from wǎng 'a net' and gòu 'to buy', both inherited from Middle Chinese, mjangX and kuwH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to buy back'. The word is a modern short form.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: yùsuàn.
yùsuàn — "budget".

The word, taken apart.
yùsuàn is built from yù 'beforehand' and suàn 'to count', both inherited from Middle Chinese, yoH and swanX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first, among other senses, 'before'.
