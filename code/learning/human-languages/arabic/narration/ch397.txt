6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
بكالوريوس (bakālūriyūs) — a bachelor's degree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a thesis, then the Arabic for a curriculum.

You'll want to know: بكالوريوس.
بكالوريوس — bakālūriyūs — "a bachelor's degree".
حصل أخي على البكالوريوس في التجارة. ḥaṣala akhī ʿalā al-bakālūriyūs fī at-tijāra. My brother got a bachelor's degree in commerce.

The word, taken apart.
بكالوريوس (bakālūriyūs) is a modern loan, usually traced to medieval Latin baccalaureus, the holder of a first degree. Arabic keeps the Latin ending.

Lesson 4 of 6.
مقرر (muqarrar) — a syllabus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a master's degree, then the Arabic for a doctorate.

You'll want to know: مقرر.
مقرر — muqarrar — "a syllabus".
هذا الكتاب جزء من المقرر. hādhā al-kitāb juzʾ min al-muqarrar. This book is part of the syllabus.

The word, taken apart.
مقرر (muqarrar) is built on the native Arabic root q-r-r, "to settle, to be still" (Lane). Muqarrar is the Form II passive participle, what has been fixed: a set syllabus. قرار, a decision, is from the same root.

[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: تدريب.
تدريب — tadrīb — "training".
يحتاج الموظفون الجدد إلى تدريب. yaḥtāju al-muwaẓẓafūn al-judud ilā tadrīb. New employees need training.

The word, taken apart.
تدريب (tadrīb) is built on the native Arabic root d-r-b, "to be used to, practised" (Lane). Tadrīb is the Form II verbal noun, making someone practised: training. مدرب, a coach, is from the same root.
