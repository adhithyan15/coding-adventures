Gujarati, chapter 373: A Way Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ઉપાય (upāy) — a way out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for alert, then the Gujarati for to include.

You'll want to know: ઉપાય.
ઉપાય — upāy — "a way out".
જો આ ઉપાય કામ કરશે તો તાવ જલદી ઊતરશે. jo ā upāy kām karśe to tāv jaldī ūtarśe. If this remedy works, the fever will soon come down.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ઉપાય (upāy) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit upāya, "coming near, a means, a way" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 2 of 5.
નિરાકરણ (nirākaraṇ) — a solution (to a problem); a settling.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to include, then the Gujarati for a way out.

You'll want to know: નિરાકરણ.
નિરાકરણ — nirākaraṇ — "a solution (to a problem); a settling".
જો બંને બાજુ વાત કરશે તો આ વિવાદનું નિરાકરણ થશે. jo banne bāju vāt karśe to ā vivādnũ nirākaraṇ thaśe. If both sides talk, this dispute will be settled.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
નિરાકરણ (nirākaraṇ) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit nirākaraṇa, "driving away, removing" (Monier-Williams). Gujarati uses it for clearing a problem away: its solution, a sense the source does not give.

મુશ્કેલી — muśkelī — "a difficulty".
જો કોઈ મુશ્કેલી આવશે તો મને ફોન કરો. jo koī muśkelī āvśe to mane phon karo. If any difficulty comes up, phone me.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
મુશ્કેલી (muśkelī) is usually traced to Persian mushkil, "difficult", a word of Arabic origin, with the Gujarati ending -ī.
