Mandarin Chinese, chapter 112: Way In, Way Out.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
出口 (chūkǒu) — exit; way out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for crossroads, then the Mandarin for to turn a corner.

You'll want to know: 出口.
出口 — chūkǒu — "exit; way out".
出口在前边 chūkǒu zài qiánbian — The way out is up ahead.
The new character, in pieces: 出 (chū) — two halves like 山, one on top of the other, with one long vertical down through both. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
出口 (chūkǒu) is built from 出 (chū) 'to go out' and 口 'mouth, opening', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and khuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the opening you go out by.

Lesson 2 of 6.
出 (chū) — two halves like 山, one long vertical.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — two halves like 山, one long vertical and Guided Practice.

山 口.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — shān, kǒu]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two halves like 山, one long vertical.
出 (chū).
The top half, two strokes: a short vertical that turns right along its foot, then the short vertical on the right.
The long vertical, down the middle, through the top half and on into the bottom one.
The bottom half, two strokes, wider: a vertical that turns right along the foot, then the vertical on the right.
2 + 1 + 2 = five. Unlike 山, the middle vertical comes third.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 出 (chū), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 出 (chū)? (Five.) Which stroke comes third? (The long vertical down the middle.) Which character you know does it look like, twice? (山.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 出 (chū), pinned stroke paths and medians.

You'll want to know: tuī.
tuī — "to push".
qǐng tuī mén — Push the door, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuī is inherited from Middle Chinese thwoj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the character 'to push' under a second reading, tsyhwij, and lists thwoj beside it; tuī continues thwoj.

Lesson 5 of 6.
lā — to pull.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for entrance, then the Mandarin for to push.

You'll want to know: lā.
lā — "to pull".
bú yào tuī qǐng lā — Don't push, pull.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
lā is inherited from Middle Chinese lop (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to break' and 'to crush'. The regular change from lop would give là, not lā, and pulling is not given there; how the word came to its sound and sense is not settled.
