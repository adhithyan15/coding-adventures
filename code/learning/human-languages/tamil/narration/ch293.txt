Tamil, chapter 293: It Depends and What Would Have Been.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
-ஐப் பொறுத்தது (-aip poṟuttatu) — it depends.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "If it rains, I will stay at home." (maḻai peytāl nāṉ vīṭṭil iruppēṉ.) Then the word for the weather. (vāṉilai.)

You'll want to know: … -ஐப் பொறுத்தது.
நாளை வருவீர்களா? அது வானிலையைப் பொறுத்தது. nāḷai varuvīrkaḷā? atu vāṉilaiyaip poṟuttatu. — "Will you come tomorrow? It depends on the weather."
வருவீர்களா (varuvīrkaḷā, "will you come?"). பொறுத்தது is "it rests on", from பொறு, "to bear", the verb inside பொறுத்துக்கொள். The thing it depends on takes -ஐ, and the first letter of பொறுத்தது doubles in writing: வானிலையைப் பொறுத்தது. With a person, it hands them the choice: அது உங்களைப் பொறுத்தது (atu uṅkaḷaip poṟuttatu, "it's up to you").
Before another verb it becomes பொறுத்து, "depending on":
விலையைப் பொறுத்து வாங்குவேன். vilaiyaip poṟuttu vāṅkuvēṉ. — "I'll buy it depending on the price."
A more formal partner, for writing: … -ஐச் சார்ந்திருக்கிறது (… -aic cārntirukkiṟatu, "it relies on …"), as in இது உங்கள் முடிவைச் சார்ந்திருக்கிறது (itu uṅkaḷ muṭivaic cārntirukkiṟatu, "this depends on your decision").

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Will you come tomorrow? It depends on the weather." — nāḷai varuvīrkaḷā? atu vāṉilaiyaip poṟuttatu.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "It's up to you" — atu uṅkaḷaip poṟuttatu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'll buy it depending on the price" — vilaiyaip poṟuttu vāṅkuvēṉ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one plan of yours that depends on something, and what it depends on]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"It depends on the weather"? (வானிலையைப் பொறுத்தது.) "It's up to you"? (அது உங்களைப் பொறுத்தது.) "Depending on the price"? (விலையைப் பொறுத்து.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
வந்திருந்தால் (vantiruntāl) — if it had, it would have.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "having come" (vantu) and "if (I) was" (iruntāl). Then "It depends on the weather." (atu vāṉilaiyaip poṟuttatu.)

You'll want to know: -இருந்தால் … -இருப்பேன்.
எனக்கு நேரம் இருந்திருந்தால், நான் வந்திருப்பேன். eṉakku nēram iruntiruntāl, nāṉ vantiruppēṉ. — "If I had had time, I would have come."
Two pieces you know make the if-clause: the -உ form and இருந்தால், "if (it) was". வந்து + இருந்தால் becomes வந்திருந்தால் (vantiruntāl), "if (someone) had come"; the -உ drops before the இ. With இரு itself: இருந்திருந்தால், "if (there) had been".
The main clause takes இருப்பேன், "I will be", on the same -உ form: வந்திருப்பேன், "I would have come". After போய் the இ stays whole: போயிருப்பேன், "I would have gone".
Both halves are about what did not happen: there was no time, and nobody came.

You'll want to know: would have, for everyone.
நீங்கள் வந்திருந்தால், நாம் சேர்ந்து கடலுக்குப் போயிருப்போம். nīṅkaḷ vantiruntāl, nām cērntu kaṭalukkup pōyiruppōm. — "If you had come, we would have gone to the sea together."
The ending of இருப்பேன் changes for the person, as every future does: போயிருப்போம், "we would have gone"; வந்திருப்பான், "he would have come". For "it", இருக்கும்:
அது நன்றாக இருந்திருக்கும். atu naṉṟāka iruntirukkum. — "That would have been nice."
The same form also makes a guess about the past: அவன் வந்திருப்பான் can mean "he must have come (by now)". The rest of the sentence tells you which.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If I had had time, I would have come" — eṉakku nēram iruntiruntāl, nāṉ vantiruppēṉ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you had come, we would have gone to the sea together" — nīṅkaḷ vantiruntāl, nām cērntu kaṭalukkup pōyiruppōm]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "That would have been nice" — atu naṉṟāka iruntirukkum]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If (you) had come"? (வந்திருந்தால் (vantiruntāl).) "I would have come"? (வந்திருப்பேன்.) "I would have gone"? (போயிருப்பேன்.) "It would have been nice"? (நன்றாக இருந்திருக்கும்.) Did it happen? (No.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
Six lines about a trip — six connected lines about a trip in the past, with a feeling, a reason and an if.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last connected message you wrote was about a familiar day. This one tells a story: six lines about a trip, to your home town or anywhere else.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one reader: a friend, by name.

You'll want to know.
A told story is not a list of events. It has a scene, with -கொண்டிருந்தேன் or -த போது. It has events in the plain past, kept in order with முதலில், அதன் பிறகு, the -உ / -இ form and கடைசியில். It has at least one reason, with அதனால், -ததால் or ஏனென்றால், and a feeling, with எனக்கு … வந்தது or எனக்கு … -ஆக இருந்தது.
A good one ends by looking at what might have been: … -இருந்தால், … -இருப்பேன்.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
On paper, in Tamil script, write six lines to your reader about a trip, real or invented, one line for each of these:
the scene: when it was, and what you were doing as it began, with -கொண்டிருந்தேன் or -த போது;
the first thing you did, with முதலில்: a ticket, a seat, or the way you asked;
what happened next, with அதன் பிறகு or an -உ / -இ form, and one thing that went wrong: a missed bus, a missing bag, or rain and a wet shirt;
why, or what followed, with அதனால், -ததால் or ஏனென்றால்;
how it ended and how you felt, with கடைசியில் and எனக்கு … வந்தது or -ஆக இருந்தது;
one line about what might have been, with -இருந்தால் … -இருப்பேன்.
[pause 3 seconds]
There is no model on this page, and no word bank. Use only words you already write. The whole exercise is choosing the right past for each line, and joining the lines.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now read your lines aloud. Mark every verb s for scene and e for event. Line one should be mostly s; lines two and three mostly e.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then a spelling check. If it rained in your story, did you write ஈரம் or ஈரமாக? It opens with the letter ஈ: a bar, two legs, two dots, not the curled இ. Inside a word, after a consonant, the same long ī is the sign ீ.
[pause 2 seconds]
Last, read line six alone. Does the if-clause end in -இருந்தால், and the main verb in -இருப்பேன்?
