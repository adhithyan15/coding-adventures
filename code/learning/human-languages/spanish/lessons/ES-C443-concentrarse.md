# concentrarse — gathered to a point

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-19, ES-LEX-EXAMEN, ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **el centro** and **el examen**. Say **acercarse**. The exam is

## You'll want to know: concentrarse
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-EXAMEN] -->

**concentrarse** — to concentrate.

*No me puedo concentrar* — I cannot concentrate. *Concéntrate en el examen* —
concentrate on the exam. **la concentración** is both the mental state and, in a
notice, a gathering of people in a square.

It takes **en**: *concentrarse **en** algo*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-04, ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-19] -->

**con-** together, plus **centr-** from **el centro**, plus the verb ending. To
concentrate is **to bring together to a centre**, and the reflexive says you are
doing it to yourself.

| piece | what it does |
|---|---|
| con- | together |
| centr- | **el centro** |
| -arse | to yourself |

That is the third verb in this chapter built by putting a prefix and an ending
on a word you already owned — *acercarse* on *cerca*, *concentrarse* on
*centro*. Look for the bare word inside before you look anything up.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C443-ANIMO-04] -->

**El centro** is not originally about the middle of a town.

Greek **kéntron** was **a goad** — the spike used to drive cattle — and then the
fixed point of a pair of compasses, the spike you press into the paper so the
other leg can swing round it. Latin took it as *centrum*, and the geometry sense
is what survived.

So a centre is, at bottom, **the point that does not move while everything else
turns around it**. That is a better picture than "the middle", and it explains
*concentrarse*: you are pulling a scattered mind in towards the one fixed point.

English has the same spike in **eccentric** — *ek-*, out of, plus the centre. An
eccentric wheel is one whose axle is off the fixed point, and an eccentric
person is one whose life turns around a different one.
