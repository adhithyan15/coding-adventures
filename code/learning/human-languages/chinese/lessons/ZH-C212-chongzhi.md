## You'll want to know: chōngzhí
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C212-SERVICE212-03]; assesses=[] -->

*chōngzhí* — "to top up (a card or a phone)".

> *nǐ de shǒujī chōngzhí le ma* — Have you topped up your phone?

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*chōngzhí* is built from *chōng* 'to fill' and *zhí* 'a value', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsyhuwng* and *driH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'full' and the second 'to hold' and 'to match'. The dictionary gives the second a departing tone, so the rising tone of zhí is not the regular outcome, and how it came is not settled.
