Mandarin Chinese, chapter 51: How You Feel.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
gāoxìng — happy; glad.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for health, then the Mandarin for to fall ill.

You'll want to know: gāoxìng.
gāoxìng — "happy; glad".
wǒ hěn gāoxìng — I'm so pleased.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gāoxìng is built from gāo 'high' and xìng 'mood', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaw and xingH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'high'; it gives the second reading no gloss of its own, so 'mood' is the usual explanation, not a settled one.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how you are.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: gāoxìng]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: gāoxìng, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: wǒ hěn gāoxìng — I'm so pleased.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for health, then the Mandarin for to fall ill, then say gāoxìng again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does gāoxìng mean? ("Happy; glad".) Say it once more.
