French, chapter 369: Selon les cas.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
selon — according to; depending on.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for in case, then the French for otherwise.

You'll want to know: selon.
selon — "according to; depending on".
Selon le journal, il va neiger demain. According to the newspaper, it is going to snow tomorrow.
Selon moi — in my view. C'est selon — it depends.

The word, taken apart.
selon comes from Old French selonc, selunc (EtymDB), usually traced to a Vulgar Latin sublongum "along", built on longus "long" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
éventuel — possible (if it happens).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for otherwise, then the French for according to.

You'll want to know: éventuel.
éventuel — "possible (if it happens)".
Nous avons gardé de l'argent pour les frais éventuels. We kept some money for any possible costs.
A false friend: éventuel means possible, not final. Feminine éventuelle.

The word, taken apart.
éventuel is a learned formation on Latin ēventus "an outcome, an event" (Lewis & Short), from ēvenīre "to come out, to happen", built on venīre "to come".

Lesson 3 of 5.
éventuellement — possibly, if need be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for according to, then the French for possible.

You'll want to know: éventuellement.
éventuellement — "possibly, if need be".
Je peux éventuellement venir samedi. I can possibly come on Saturday.
A false friend: not eventually, which is finalement.

The word, taken apart.
éventuellement is built from éventuel and -ment. éventuel rests on Latin ēventus "an outcome", from ēvenīre "to happen" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
faute de — for lack of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for possibly, then the French for the possibility.

You'll want to know: faute de.
faute de — "for lack of".
Faute de temps, nous n'avons pas visité le musée. For lack of time, we did not visit the museum.
Faute de mieux — for want of anything better.

The word, taken apart.
faute is inherited through Old French from a Vulgar Latin fallita "a shortcoming" (EtymDB), from Latin fallere "to deceive, to fail" (Lewis & Short).
