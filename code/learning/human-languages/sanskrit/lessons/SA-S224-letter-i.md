# इ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-123, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-122, SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-223] -->

[PAUSE 1s] Before the new one, the ones you have already met: ओ · फ · ◌ै. Say what each of them does.

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time. One only — and you have been saying it
for pages without knowing which mark on the page it was.

## Script you'll notice: इ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-224]; assesses=[] -->

**इ** — *i*.

It is an **independent vowel**: the full-size letter a vowel wears when it opens a word and has no consonant to lean on. Inside a word the same vowel is written as a small sign instead.

What it is made of:

- a descending upright joined to an upper bowl
- a lower bowl and diagonal tail in the same run
- the top shirorekhā

You already say these, and every one of them has इ somewhere inside it:

- **इदानीम्** *idānīm* — now

## Writing: इ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-SCRIPT-RECOG-224] -->

- **1.** descend the upright from the headline, turn left around the upper bowl, sweep right through the waist and around the lower bowl, then finish down-right through the tail without lifting
- **2.** lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right

**Pen lifts: 1.** The pen comes up 1 times and no more.

> verified modern printed teaching form; everyday handwriting may simplify the curves

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting
> here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari इ stroke order.svg’, panels 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
