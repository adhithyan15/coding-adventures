Marathi, chapter 428: Spending and Saving.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
उधळणे (udhaḷṇe) — to squander.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for the remainder, then the Marathi for to repay.

You'll want to know: उधळणे.
उधळणे — udhaḷṇe — "to squander".
त्याने सगळा पगार एका आठवड्यात उधळला. tyāne sagḷā pagār ekā āṭhvaḍyāt udhaḷlā. He squandered his whole salary in one week.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने उधळलं, tyāne udhaḷlaṁ.
Also to scatter: फुलं उधळणे (udhaḷṇe), to shower flowers.

The word, taken apart.
उधळणे (udhaḷṇe) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi udhaḷṇẽ, "to scatter; to spend lavishly", under Sanskrit uddhūlayati, "sprinkles with powder" (CDIAL 2027).

Lesson 2 of 5.
शिल्लक (śillak) — a balance; what is left.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to repay, then the Marathi for to squander.

You'll want to know: शिल्लक.
शिल्लक — śillak — "a balance; what is left".
महिनाअखेर माझ्याकडे थोडी शिल्लक राहते. mahināakher mājhyākaḍe thoḍī śillak rāhte. At the end of the month I have a little left over.
Feminine.
शिल्लक (śillak) राहणे, to be left over; खात्यातली शिल्लक (śillak), the account balance.

The word, taken apart.
शिल्लक (śillak) is a Marathi word for what remains of money or stock; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
गुंतवणूक (guntavṇūk) — an investment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to squander, then the Marathi for a balance.

You'll want to know: गुंतवणूक.
गुंतवणूक — guntavṇūk — "an investment".
घर ही चांगली गुंतवणूक आहे. ghar hī cāṅglī guntavṇūk āhe. A house is a good investment.
Feminine.
गुंतवणूक (guntavṇūk) करणे, to invest; a feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
गुंतवणूक (guntavṇūk) is built on गुंतवणे, "to entangle; to invest", with the noun ending -णूक; the origin of the verb is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ऐपत (aipat) — what one can afford; means.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a balance, then the Marathi for an investment.

You'll want to know: ऐपत.
ऐपत — aipat — "what one can afford; means".
घर घेण्याइतकी माझी ऐपत नाही. ghar gheṇyāitkī mājhī aipat nāhī. I cannot afford to buy a house.
Feminine.
Usually with the owner in -ची: माझी ऐपत (aipat), my means.

The word, taken apart.
ऐपत (aipat) is a Marathi word for how much money one has to spend; its origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
किफायतशीर (kiphāyatśīr) — economical; good value.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for an investment, then the Marathi for what one can afford.

You'll want to know: किफायतशीर.
किफायतशीर — kiphāyatśīr — "economical; good value".
बसचा प्रवास किफायतशीर असतो. bascā pravās kiphāyatśīr asto. Travelling by bus is economical.
It does not change: किफायतशीर (kiphāyatśīr) दर, किफायतशीर (kiphāyatśīr) गाडी.

The word, taken apart.
किफायतशीर (kiphāyatśīr) is a loan built on Arabic kifāyat, "sufficiency, thrift", with the Persian adjective ending -śīr; the exact path into Marathi is not settled.
