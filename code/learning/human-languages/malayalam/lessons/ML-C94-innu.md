# ഇന്ന് (innŭ) — today

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C23-DIVASAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *day*. You have had the word for a day since early on. You have
never had a word for **this** one.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-LEX-C94-TODAY-01]; assesses=[] -->

**ഇന്ന്** — *innŭ* — **today.**

> **ഇന്ന് ഞാൻ പോകുന്നു.** — *innŭ ñān pōkunnu* — "**Today** I go."

The word goes **in front**, before the person and the verb. That is where a
time word sits in this language, and you have been putting things there since
you learned to say *now*.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C94-TODAY-01] -->

This is smaller than it looks and it opens more than it looks.

Every sentence you have made so far has floated free of any particular day. You
could say that you go, that you went, that you will go — and nothing in the
sentence said **when**. A listener had to guess from the room.

**One word ends that.** The verb was already carrying the tense; **ഇന്ന്**
gives it a day to sit on.
