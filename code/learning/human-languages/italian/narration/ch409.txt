Italian, chapter 409: Una gomma a terra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
forare — to get a puncture; to pierce.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the tank, then the Italian for to refuel.

You'll want to know: forare.
forare — "to get a puncture; to pierce".
Abbiamo forato in autostrada. We got a puncture on the motorway.
Ho forato — I have a flat tyre; forare un foglio — to punch a hole in a sheet.

The word, taken apart.
forare is inherited from Latin forāre 'to bore, to pierce' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
lo pneumatico — the tyre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to get a puncture, then the Italian for the puncture.

You'll want to know: lo pneumatico.
lo pneumatico — "the tyre".
Gli pneumatici da neve sono obbligatori qui. Snow tyres are required here.
In speech, la gomma. The article is lo, plural gli pneumatici, though il pneumatico is heard too.

The word, taken apart.
pneumatico is a learned borrowing from Latin pneumaticus, from Greek pneumatikós 'of wind or air', built on pneûma 'air, breath' (EtymDB): a tyre full of air.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il cric — the jack (for a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the puncture, then the Italian for the tyre.

You'll want to know: il cric.
il cric — "the jack (for a car)".
Senza il cric non possiamo cambiare la ruota. Without the jack we cannot change the wheel.
It sounds like the clicking it makes. Plural unchanged: i cric.

The word, taken apart.
cric came from French cric, a word that seems to imitate the click of the mechanism; the deeper origin is not settled.
