Sanskrit, chapter 22: Verbs of Mind and Motion.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
शृणोति (śṛṇoti) — he, she or it hears.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
A short run of everyday verbs, one at a time.

You'll want to know: शृणोति.
शृणोति (śṛṇoti) — "he, she or it hears".
From √श्रु (śru), 'to hear' — the same root as Latin clueo and Greek kleos, 'fame', which is what gets heard about you. It gives श्रुति (śruti), 'that which is heard': the Vedas, as against smṛti, 'that which is remembered'.

Lesson 2 of 4.
नयति (nayati) — he, she or it leads.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did śṛṇoti mean?

You'll want to know: नयति.
नयति (nayati) — "he, she or it leads".
From √नी (nī), 'to lead'. It is inside netṛ, 'leader', and naya, 'polity' — the art of leading people, which Sanskrit treats as a subject with a name.

Lesson 3 of 4.
क्रीडति (krīḍati) — he, she or it plays.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did nayati mean?

You'll want to know: क्रीडति.
क्रीडति (krīḍati) — "he, she or it plays".
From √क्रीड् (krīḍ). The noun krīḍā covers a child's game, a sport, and divine play — līlā's plainer cousin — so the word does not separate the trivial from the cosmic.

Lesson 4 of 4.
दहति (dahati) — he, she or it burns.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did krīḍati mean?

You'll want to know: दहति.
दहति (dahati) — "he, she or it burns".
From √दह् (dah), and its distant English cousin is day — by way of a Germanic word for the burning warmth of the sky, not the daylight itself.
