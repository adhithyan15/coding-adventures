Lesson 1 of 5.
la musculation — weight training, bodybuilding.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to water, then the French for watering can.

You'll want to know: la musculation.
la musculation — "weight training, bodybuilding".
Je fais de la musculation deux fois par semaine. I do weight training twice a week.
Informally la muscu.

The word, taken apart.
la musculation is built on muscle, a learned borrowing from Latin musculus "a little mouse, a muscle", from mūs "mouse" (Lewis & Short): a moving muscle looked like a mouse under the skin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'haltère — dumbbell, weight.

You'll want to know: l'haltère.
l'haltère — "dumbbell, weight".
Il soulève des haltères devant le miroir. He lifts dumbbells in front of the mirror.
Masculine: un haltère. The h is silent.

The word, taken apart.
l'haltère comes, through Latin, from Greek haltēres, weights that athletes held while jumping, built on hállesthai "to jump".

Lesson 3 of 5.
étirer — to stretch; (s'étirer) to stretch (oneself).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for weight training, then the French for dumbbell.

You'll want to know: étirer.
étirer — "to stretch; (s'étirer) to stretch (oneself)".
Après le sport, je m'étire pendant dix minutes. After sport I stretch for ten minutes.
Mostly reflexive for the body: s'étirer. Un étirement — a stretch.

The word, taken apart.
étirer is built from é- and tirer "to pull". The origin of tirer is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la sueur — sweat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for dumbbell, then the French for to stretch.

You'll want to know: la sueur.
la sueur — "sweat".
Après une heure de course, il était tout en sueur. After an hour's running he was dripping with sweat.
En sueur — sweating.

The word, taken apart.
la sueur is inherited, through Old French suor, from Latin sūdor "sweat" (EtymDB), related to sūdāre "to sweat" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
échauffer — to warm up; (s'échauffer) to warm up (before sport).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to stretch, then the French for sweat.

You'll want to know: échauffer.
échauffer — "to warm up; (s'échauffer) to warm up (before sport)".
Les joueurs s'échauffent avant le match. The players warm up before the match.
Mostly reflexive in sport: s'échauffer. L'échauffement — the warm-up.

The word, taken apart.
échauffer is inherited from Old French eschaufer (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin excalefāre, a form of Latin calefacere "to make warm", built on calēre "to be warm" (Lewis & Short).
