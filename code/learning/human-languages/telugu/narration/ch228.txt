Telugu, chapter 228: Crab, Scorpion, Bat, Nest, Tail.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
పీత (pīta) — a crab.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for an enemy, then the Telugu for a bridegroom.

You'll want to know: పీత.
పీత — pīta — "a crab".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of పీత (pīta) is uncertain. DEDR and CDIAL list no word of this shape meaning "crab"; CDIAL's pīta, "drunk" and "yellow", are different words.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Before the new one: say the Telugu for a bridegroom, then the Telugu for a crab.

You'll want to know: తేలు.
తేలు — tēlu — "a scorpion".

The word, taken apart.
తేలు (tēlu) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil tēḷ and Malayalam tēḷ (DEDR 3470). DEDR lists Telugu tēlu, "scorpion".

Lesson 4 of 5.
గూడు (gūḍu) — a nest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a scorpion, then the Telugu for a bat.

You'll want to know: గూడు.
గూడు — gūḍu — "a nest".

The word, taken apart.
గూడు (gūḍu) is native Dravidian. DEDR 1883 lists a nasalised gūḍu, "nest, cage", with Tamil kūṭu and Kannada gūḍu, "nest". DEDR compares Sanskrit kūṭa, "trap for catching deer" (CDIAL 3397).
