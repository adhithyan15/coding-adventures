# क्षीरम् (kṣīram) — "milk," and the dessert it left behind

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-PANIYAM-WATER, SA-ETYMON-PA-DRINK-POTION] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "water," and the root it is built on. (*Pānīyam*; **पा**, "to
drink.") Not every drink-word in Sanskrit has so tidy an answer.

## You'll want to know: क्षीरम्
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-KSHIRAM-MILK]; assesses=[] -->

| Sanskrit | sound | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| क्षीरम् | *kṣīram* | **milk** |
| क्षीर | *kṣīra* | milk — the bare stem, still living inside Hindi *khīr* |

Neuter again, marked by the same **-म्** as **पानीयम्**.

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**क्ष** is a new stacked pair: **क** *k* fused under **ष**, the hissed
retroflex *s* — together they say *kṣa*, one push, not two. Then the long-*ī*
sign **ी**, **र** *ra*, and the neuter **म्** you already know: *kṣī-ra-m*.

## The word, taken apart — a disputed root, and a live dessert
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-KSHIRA-DISPUTED]; assesses=[SA-HISTORY-TATSAMA-TADBHAVA] -->

Where **पानीयम्**'s history is settled, **क्षीरम्**'s is not. One account
traces it to an old Proto-Indo-European word for milk; another argues it was
borrowed from a substrate language spoken where Indo-Iranian speakers first
arrived in the region. Neither side has won.

What is clear is the family it sits in: Avestan **xšuuidi-** and Persian
*šir* say roughly the same word, but as **relatives**, not children
— all three come down from one shared Proto-Indo-Iranian ancestor, the way
*gṛhṇāti* and English *grab* are cousins rather than the same word wearing
different clothes.

The word's liveliest descendant is not a drink. Hindi **खीर** (*khīr*) is a
sweet rice pudding cooked in milk — worn down, तद्भव — and English borrowed
that dessert whole, as **kheer**.
