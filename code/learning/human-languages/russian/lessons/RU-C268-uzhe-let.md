# уже два года; два года назад — how long, and how long ago

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C75-NUM1-01, RU-LEX-C214-MESTO214-02, RU-LEX-C68-TIME1-04, RU-GRAMMAR-COMPARATIVE-IRREGULAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "already", "back" and "a year". (*Uzhé. Nazád. God.*) Then
"more" and "less". (*Bólshe. Ménshe.*) Comparing things leads to
comparing times: how long, and how long ago.

## Grammar Lens: how long, in the present
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-UZHE-PRESENT-DURATION-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C41-VERB4-02, RU-LEX-C189-KACH189-05, RU-LEX-C137-PAS137-02, RU-LEX-ZHIT, RU-LEX-C72-WHERE1-01] -->

> **Я уже два года учу русский.** — *ya uzhé dva góda uchú rússkiy* —
> I've been learning Russian for two years.

English says "I have been learning". Russian keeps the present, because
the learning is still going on; **уже**, "already", marks how far it has
come. In the same way: **Я давно живу здесь.** — *ya davnó zhivú zdes* —
I've lived here for a long time.

## Grammar Lens: how long ago, in the past
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-NAZAD-AGO-01]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-BYL-PAST-01, RU-LEX-C69-TIME2-03] -->

**Назад**, "back", follows a stretch of time to mean "ago":

> **Два года назад я был в Москве.** — *dva góda nazád ya byl v Moskvé* —
> Two years ago I was in Moscow.
> **Час назад позвонила мама.** — *chas nazád pozvoníla máma* — Mum rang
> an hour ago.

## Grammar Lens: год, года, лет
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-GOD-GODA-LET-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-DVA-01, RU-LEX-PYAT-01, RU-LEX-DVADTSAT-01, RU-LEX-C255-VESHCH255-02, RU-LEX-SKOLKO-01] -->

After a number, the word for a year changes: **один год**; **два, три,
четыре года**; from **пять** to **двадцать**, **лет**. Ask someone's age
with it: **Сколько тебе лет?** — *skólko tebé let?* And answer: **Мне
двадцать лет.** — *mne dvádtsat let* — I'm twenty.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-C71-SEASONS-04, RU-LEX-C68-TIME1-04] -->

**лет** is the counting form of **лето**, "summer". EtymDB 2.1 traces
*лето* to Proto-Slavic *\*lěto*, which already meant "year", so a long
count of years is a count of summers. **год** goes back through Old East
Slavic *godŭ*, "time, year", to Proto-Slavic *\*godŭ*, "the suitable time"
(EtymDB).
