Lesson 1 of 5.
どこ (doko) — where.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the fifth of the month; some day, then the Japanese for the sixth of the month.

You'll want to know: どこ.
どこ — doko — "where".

The word, taken apart.
どこ (doko) is native: do-, the root of "which", with -ko, "place", as in koko, "here". It is inherited rather than borrowed, and JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.

Lesson 3 of 5.
うしろ (ushiro) — behind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for where, then the Japanese for nearby.

You'll want to know: うしろ.
うしろ — ushiro — "behind".

The word, taken apart.
うしろ (ushiro) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: まんなか.
まんなか — mannaka — "the very middle".

The word, taken apart.
まんなか (mannaka) is a native compound, inherited rather than borrowed: ma, "true, very", with a nasal beat added, and naka, "middle" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
