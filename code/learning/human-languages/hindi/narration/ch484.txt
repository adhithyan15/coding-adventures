Lesson 1 of 5.
दातुन (dātun) — a twig toothbrush.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for force, then the Hindi for to tire someone out.

You'll want to know: दातुन.
दातुन — dātun — "a twig toothbrush".
गाँव में दादा आज भी दातुन से दाँत साफ़ करते हैं। gā̃v meṁ dādā āj bhī dātun se dā̃t sāf karte haiṁ. In the village my grandfather still cleans his teeth with a twig.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
दातुन (dātun) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi dātun, "tooth stick", under Sanskrit dantapavana, "tooth-cleaning" (CDIAL 6157): a twig, often of neem, chewed at one end.

Lesson 2 of 5.
कटवाना (kaṭvānā) — to have (something) cut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to tire someone out, then the Hindi for a twig toothbrush.

You'll want to know: कटवाना.
कटवाना — kaṭvānā — "to have (something) cut".
शादी से पहले अरुण ने नाई से बाल कटवाए। śādī se pahle Aruṇ ne nāī se bāl kaṭvāe. Before the wedding Arun had his hair cut at the barber's.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बाल कटवाए, usne bāl kaṭvāe.

The word, taken apart.
कटवाना (kaṭvānā) is the causative of kāṭnā, "to cut", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit kártati, "cuts" (CDIAL 2854): with -vā-, someone else does the cutting for you. The person who does it takes se: nāī se.

Lesson 3 of 5.

नहलाना — nahlānā — "to bathe someone".
माँ रोज़ सुबह बच्चे को नहलाती है। māṁ roz subah bacce ko nahlātī hai. Mother bathes the baby every morning.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को नहलाया, usne bacce ko nahlāyā.

The word, taken apart.
नहलाना (nahlānā) is the causative partner of nahānā, "to bathe", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit snāti, "bathes" (CDIAL 13786): to give someone else a bath.

Lesson 4 of 5.
मलना (malnā) — to rub in; to massage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to have (something) cut, then the Hindi for to bathe someone.

You'll want to know: मलना.
मलना — malnā — "to rub in; to massage".
नहाने से पहले दादी ने बच्चे के पैरों पर तेल मला। nahāne se pahle dādī ne bacce ke pairoṁ par tel malā. Before the bath, my grandmother rubbed oil into the baby's legs.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने हाथों पर तेल मला, usne hāthoṁ par tel malā.

The word, taken apart.
मलना (malnā) is a tadbhava: CDIAL lists Hindi malnā, "to rub, shampoo", under marati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 9870). An oil massage for a baby is māliś, and the verb for it is malnā.

Lesson 5 of 5.
उस्तरा (ustarā) — a razor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to bathe someone, then the Hindi for to rub in.

You'll want to know: उस्तरा.
उस्तरा — ustarā — "a razor".
नाई ने उस्तरे से दाढ़ी बनाई। nāī ne ustare se dāṛhī banāī. The barber shaved the beard with a razor.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
उस्तरा (ustarā) is usually traced to Persian ustura, "razor". dāṛhī banānā, "to make the beard", is how Hindi says to shave.
