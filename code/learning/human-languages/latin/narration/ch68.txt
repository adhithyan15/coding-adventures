Latin, chapter 68: Sick, Tired, Hungry, Thirsty.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
aeger, aegra, aegrum — sick, ill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for medicine, then the Latin for an illness.

You'll want to know: aeger, aegra, aegrum.
aeger, aegra, aegrum — "sick, ill". The opposite of sānus.

The word, taken apart.
aeger, "sick", has no sure origin; Lewis & Short list only tentative comparisons, and no proposal is generally accepted.

Lesson 2 of 5.
fessus, fessa, fessum — tired.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an illness, then the Latin for sick.

You'll want to know: fessus, fessa, fessum.
fessus, fessa, fessum — "tired".

The word, taken apart.
fessus is the old participle of fatīscor, "to grow weary, to gape open". It belongs with fatīgō, "to tire out" (Lewis & Short). English fatigue comes from that verb through French.

Lesson 3 of 5.
famēs, famis — hunger.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for sick, then the Latin for tired.

You'll want to know: famēs, famis.
famēs, famis — "hunger". English famine.

The word, taken apart.
famēs has no sure origin. One proposal, which EtymDB 2.1 records, ties it to a Proto-Indo-European root dhH-, "to disappear".

Lesson 4 of 5.
sitis, sitis — thirst.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for tired, then the Latin for hunger.

You'll want to know: sitis, sitis.
sitis, sitis — "thirst". aqua answers it.

The word, taken apart.
sitis is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European dhgwhitis, "a wasting, a decline" (EtymDB 2.1), the form behind Greek phthisis, "decline, consumption"; Lewis & Short still called it doubtful.

cūra, cūrae — "care, worry". English cure and curator.

The word, taken apart.
cūra has no sure origin. EtymDB 2.1 records a proposal that traces it to Proto-Indo-European kweys-, "to heed", but it is not settled.
