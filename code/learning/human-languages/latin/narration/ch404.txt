Lesson 1 of 6.
rhētor, rhētoris — a teacher of public speaking.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for knowing, then the Latin for inexperienced.

You'll want to know: rhētor, rhētoris.
rhētor, rhētoris — "a teacher of public speaking".
Iuvenēs apud rhētorem dīcere discunt. Young men learn to speak in public with a rhetoric teacher.
rhētor, rhētoris m., borrowed from Greek rhētōr, a speaker. The last stage of a Roman education was the school of the rhētor.

The word, taken apart.
rhētor was borrowed from Greek rhētōr "a public speaker" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English rhetor and rhetoric come from the same Greek word.

Lesson 5 of 6.
elementum, elementī — a first principle, an element; (plural) the ABC.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for logic, then the Latin for geometry.

You'll want to know: elementum, elementī.
elementum, elementī — "a first principle, an element; (plural) the ABC".
Puer prīma elementa discit. The boy is learning his ABC.
elementum, elementī n. In the plural, elementa are the letters, the first steps of any subject, and the four elements: earth, water, air and fire. English element.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of elementum is not settled: Lewis & Short connect it with alō "to nourish", and EtymDB pairs it with Greek stoicheion "an element", the word it translates. English element comes from it.
