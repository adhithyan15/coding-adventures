Mandarin Chinese, chapter 178: An Ordinary Day.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
正常 (zhèngcháng) — normal; as usual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fresh, then the Mandarin for horrible to eat.

You'll want to know: 正常.
正常 — zhèngcháng — "normal; as usual".
没事我觉得很正常 méishì wǒ juéde hěn zhèngcháng — It's nothing: I think that's perfectly normal.
The new character, in pieces: 常 (cháng) — eleven strokes: three short strokes on top, the cover piece of 学, 口 under it, and at the foot a frame with a long vertical down through it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
正常 (zhèngcháng) is built from 正 'right, proper' and 常 (cháng), both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyengH and dzyang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as a length of two fathoms and as an official's title; the sense 'usual' is not given there, so how the pair came to mean normal is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
常 (cháng) — three strokes, a cover, 口 and a cloth.

Script — three strokes, a cover, 口 and a cloth.
常 (cháng).
Four pieces, top to bottom. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
Three short strokes on top: a vertical in the middle, a dot to its left, and a short stroke on its right falling to the left.
Then the cover piece of 学, two strokes: a short stroke falling down on the left, then a horizontal that turns down and hooks.
Then 口, three strokes, as you write it.
At the foot, the cloth piece, three strokes: a short vertical on the left, a horizontal that turns down and hooks, and last a long vertical down through the middle.
3 + 2 + 3 + 3 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 常 (cháng), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 常 (cháng)? (Eleven.) What sits under the cover? (口.) Which stroke comes last? (The long vertical through the cloth piece.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
日子 (rìzi) — day; days (of one's life).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for horrible to eat, then the Mandarin for normal.

You'll want to know: 日子.
日子 — rìzi — "day; days (of one's life)".
小时候的日子很快乐 xiǎoshíhou de rìzi hěn kuàilè — My childhood days were happy.

The word, taken apart.
日子 (rìzi) is built from 日 'sun, day' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, nyit and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). In the word the second loses its tone.
