# claro — light, and clear

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-SI-CLARO, ES-LEX-VERDE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **sí, claro** and **verde**. You have been saying the first
for a very long time. This lesson is about what its second word means.

## You'll want to know: claro
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-02] -->

**claro** — light, pale, bright, clear.

*Un dormitorio muy claro* — a very light bedroom, one that gets the sun.
*Verde claro* — light green. *Azul claro* — pale blue. The opposite is
**oscuro**, dark.

Beyond colour: *el agua está clara* — the water is clear. *No me queda claro*
— I'm not clear about it.

## Grammar Lens: the word you have been using without meeting
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03, ES-LEX-SI-CLARO] -->

**Sí, claro** has been in your mouth since early on, as a way of agreeing —
"yes, of course". You were told then that it is Latin *clarus*, bright — but
never that you could point it at a wall, because the phrase does not need the
colour sense to work.

It is the same word. *Claro* on its own means **it's clear**, and agreeing
with somebody by saying "clear!" is how Spanish arrives at "of course".

| what you say | what it is doing |
|---|---|
| *sí, claro* | of course — the fixed phrase |
| *un cuarto claro* | a light room — the adjective |
| *claro que sí* | of course — the phrase, emphasised |

This is the fourth time a fixed phrase has turned out to have an ordinary word
inside it. *Mira* gave you *mirar*, *el punto* gave *apuntar*, *interesante*
gave *interesar*, and now *sí, claro* gives *claro*.

**Check every new word against the phrases you already say.** By this stage
of a language, a surprising share of new vocabulary is not new.

## Grammar Lens: colour words with claro and oscuro
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03, ES-LEX-VERDE-01] -->

A colour modified by *claro* or *oscuro* **stops agreeing**:

| one thing | more than one |
|---|---|
| *una pared verde* | *unas paredes verd**es*** |
| *una pared verde claro* | *unas paredes verde claro* |

Add the shade and the pair freezes. The reason is that *verde claro* is
standing in for "of a light green colour" — a description of the shade rather
than of the walls — and Spanish leaves such compounds unchanged.
