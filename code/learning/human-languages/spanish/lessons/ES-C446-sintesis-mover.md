## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-01, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-04, ES-LEX-C446-MOVER-02] -->

**Directo** appears twice and means two different things, and nothing but the
noun tells you which.

*En directo*, about the match: nothing between the event and the screen — live.
*Un tren directo*: nothing between the two stations — no changes. Both are the
same picture, a straight line with nothing interrupting it, applied once to time
and once to distance. Spanish did not need two words because it named the line
rather than the thing.

Then look at **vemos** and **miro**. *¿Vemos el partido?* — the match arrives at
their eyes, and *ver* is right. *Miro los horarios* — she is aiming her eyes at
a timetable on purpose, and *mirar* is right. A Spanish speaker would not swap
them, and the pair in one short exchange is the clearest test of the
distinction you will get.
