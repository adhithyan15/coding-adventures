Japanese, chapter 406: At the Shrine Gate.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
とりい (torii) — a shrine gate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to become rough, then the Japanese for to cool off.

You'll want to know: とりい.
とりい — torii — "a shrine gate".
じんじゃの まえに あかい とりいが あります。 — jinja no mae ni akai torii ga arimasu — "There is a red gate in front of the shrine."

The word, taken apart.
とりい (torii) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, spelled with the kanji for "bird" and "reside"; how the gate came by that name is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
けいだい (keidai) — shrine grounds; temple grounds.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a shrine gate, then the Japanese for to pass under.

You'll want to know: けいだい.
けいだい — keidai — "shrine grounds; temple grounds".
けいだいで こどもが あそんでいます。 — keidai de kodomo ga asonde imasu — "Children are playing in the shrine grounds."

The word, taken apart.
けいだい (keidai) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kei, "boundary" (Middle Chinese kjaengX) and dai, "inside" (nwojH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
かんぬし (kannushi) — a Shinto priest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pass under, then the Japanese for shrine grounds.

You'll want to know: かんぬし.
かんぬし — kannushi — "a Shinto priest".
かんぬしが まつりの したくを しています。 — kannushi ga matsuri no shitaku o shite imasu — "The priest is getting ready for the festival."

The word, taken apart.
かんぬし (kannushi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from kan, "god" (from kami) and nushi, "master"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).
