# അതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല (atukoṇṭŭ ñān vannilla) — so I did not come

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C144-WHY144-01, ML-LEX-C73-KAARANAM-01, ML-GRAMMAR-C339-AYIRUNNU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "so, therefore". (*atukoṇṭŭ*.) Then "a reason; because". (*kāraṇaṁ*.)

## Grammar Lens: the reason first, then അതുകൊണ്ട്
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C341-SO-RESULT-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C144-WHY144-01, ML-LEX-C73-KAARANAM-01, ML-LEX-C63-FEEL-05, ML-GRAMMAR-C339-AYIRUNNU-01] -->

**അതുകൊണ്ട്** is **അത്**, "that", with **കൊണ്ട്**, "with": "with that", "so". It points forward,
from a reason to what it caused.

> **എനിക്ക് പനിയായിരുന്നു. അതുകൊണ്ട് ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല.**
>
> *enikkŭ paniyāyirunnu. atukoṇṭŭ ñān vannilla.* — "I had a fever. So I did not come."

**കാരണം** points the other way, back from the result to its reason:

> **ഞാൻ വന്നില്ല, കാരണം എനിക്ക് പനിയായിരുന്നു.**
>
> *ñān vannilla, kāraṇaṁ enikkŭ paniyāyirunnu.* — "I did not come, because I had a fever."

Same facts, opposite order. Pick the one that puts first what your listener needs first.

## Grammar Lens: അതുകൊണ്ടാണ് — that is why
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C341-ATHUKONDANU-01]; assesses=[ML-LEX-C127-CARE-02, ML-LEX-C144-WHY144-01] -->

Add **-ആണ്** and the result is singled out: "that is why". The verb after it takes **-അത്**,
"the …-ing", so the whole sentence says "it is with that that the …-ing happened":

> **മഴയായിരുന്നു. അതുകൊണ്ടാണ് ഞാൻ വൈകിയത്.**
>
> *maḻayāyirunnu. atukoṇṭāṇŭ ñān vaikiyatŭ.* — "It was raining. That is why I was late."

**വൈകി** gives **വൈകിയത്**, and **വന്നു** gives **വന്നത്**, the same way.
