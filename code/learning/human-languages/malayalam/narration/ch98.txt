Malayalam, chapter 98: Games and Films.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
കളി (kaḷi) — a game.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say this is beautiful — the word and the ending together.

You'll want to know.
കളി — kaḷi — a game, and also play itself.
ഇത് കളിയാണ്. — itŭ kaḷiyāṇŭ — "This is a game."

The word, taken apart.
You built that ending without being told. കളി (kaḷi) ends in the same vowel ഭംഗി does, so the same യ slides in before ആണ്:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
word: ഭംഗി. joined to ആണ്: ഭംഗിയാണ് (bhaṁgiyāṇŭ).
word: കളി. joined to ആണ്: കളിയാണ് (kaḷiyāṇŭ).
You have seen this done before, and not only last chapter. കുട്ടി has been taking the same യ since ഇത് എന്റെ കുട്ടിയാണ്, and for the same reason: the word ends in a vowel, ആണ് begins with one, and something has to stand between them.

What you've built.
This is the book's first word for play.
You could already say that you read, that you eat, that you work, that you go. Every one of those has a point outside itself. കളി (kaḷi) names the kind of doing that is its own point — and until this lesson the book had given you no word for it.

Lesson 2 of 4.
കളിക്കുക (kaḷikkuka) — to play.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say a game. Then say to read.

You'll want to know.
കളിക്കുക — kaḷikkuka — to play.
ഞാൻ കളിക്കുന്നു. — ñān kaḷikkunnu — "I play."

The word, taken apart.
The noun is sitting inside the verb. കളി is the game; കളിക്കുക (kaḷikkuka) is doing it.
And the shape is one you have had for a long time. Put it beside the reading verb:
[a table of 2 rows, read aloud]
വായിക്കുക becomes വായിക്കുന്നു, reads.
കളിക്കുക (kaḷikkuka) becomes കളിക്കുന്നു, plays.
Verb after verb in this book already ends in -ിക്കുക — സംസാരിക്കുക, വായിക്കുക, ചോദിക്കുക, കുടിക്കുക — so this one arrives asking nothing new of you. The only new part is the two syllables at the front.

Lesson 3 of 4.
സിനിമ (sinima) — a film.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say a game. Then say the borrowed word for a place that serves food.

You'll want to know.
സിനിമ — sinima — a film, and the cinema as well.
ഇത് നല്ല സിനിമയാണ്. — itŭ nalla sinimayāṇŭ — "This is a good film."

The word, taken apart.
A borrowed word again, like the eating-place word — and this one kept far more of what it came with than that one did.
Its last letter is മ, and a consonant in this script carries an a of its own: മ is ma. So സിനിമ (sinima) ends in the vowel അ, ആണ് opens with a vowel too, and — as with കളി (kaḷi) — something has to stand between the two.
Here that something is യ once more: സിനിമയാണ്.
Do not read that as a vowel brings യ. Which letter slides in is settled by which vowel the word ends in, and you already have one that goes the other way:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
ends in: ഇ. word: ഭംഗി. joined to ആണ്: ഭംഗിയാണ് (bhaṁgiyāṇŭ).
ends in: ഇ. word: കളി. joined to ആണ്: കളിയാണ് (kaḷiyāṇŭ).
ends in: അ. word: സിനിമ. joined to ആണ്: സിനിമയാണ് (sinimayāṇŭ).
ends in: ഉ. word: പശു. joined to ആണ്: പശുവാണ് (paśuvāṇŭ).
The cow is the row to look at. ഇ and അ both take യ; a full ഉ takes വ. The gap gets filled either way — the vowel at the end says with what.
The faint half-u is not one of these vowels. എന്ത് ends in one, and it has been welding to ആണ് since your very first question: എന്ത് + ആണ് is എന്താണ് — entāṇŭ — with nothing slid in and that breathed -ŭ quietly given up instead. നല്ലത് does the same: നല്ലതാണ്.
That -ŭ is not a full vowel, so when an ending arrives it steps out of the way rather than sending a letter in ahead of it.

Lesson 4 of 4.
(a game, playing, a film) (kaḷiyuṁ sinimayuṁ enna ōrmma) — from cold.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Close the lessons before this one. Say a game, then to play, then a film.

Grammar Lens: the vowel at the end chooses.
ആണ് opens with a vowel. A word that ends in a full vowel cannot sit straight against it, so a letter comes in between. Which letter is not free — the word's own last vowel settles it:
[a table of 4 rows, read aloud]
ends in: ഇ. word: ഭംഗി. joined to ആണ്: ഭംഗിയാണ് (bhaṁgiyāṇŭ).
ends in: ഇ. word: കളി. joined to ആണ്: കളിയാണ് (kaḷiyāṇŭ).
ends in: അ. word: സിനിമ. joined to ആണ്: സിനിമയാണ് (sinimayāṇŭ).
ends in: ഉ. word: പശു. joined to ആണ്: പശുവാണ് (paśuvāṇŭ).
Three rows out of four take യ, and that is exactly why the fourth is worth holding on to. If you carry away vowels take യ, the cow will catch you out. What you want is the last vowel decides — and ഇ and അ happen to decide the same way, while ഉ does not.
And the breathed half-u at the end of a word is not in this table at all. എന്ത് + ആണ് is എന്താണ് — entāṇŭ — with nothing slid in and that faint -ŭ given up instead, and നല്ലത് gives നല്ലതാണ് the same way. Those do not end in a full vowel, so the question this table answers never arises for them.

What you've built.
കളി is the game. കളിക്കുക (kaḷikkuka) is doing it — the noun still visible inside the verb, wearing an ending you had long before this chapter:
ഞാൻ വായിക്കുന്നു. — I read. ഞാൻ കളിക്കുന്നു. — I play.
Two lines built the same way, and only one of them was ever taught to you as a pattern. The other you assembled.
