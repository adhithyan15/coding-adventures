# آنکھ — "eye," and a fifth letter learns to breathe

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-NAAK, UR-GRAMMAR-MERA-GENDER-AGREEMENT] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **merī nāk** — my nose, feminine. The next word on the face
is feminine too, and it puts the two-eyed **ھ** on a letter it has never
touched.

## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-LEX-AANKH]; assesses=[] -->

> **آنکھ** — *āṅkh* — **eye**

> **میری آنکھ۔** — *merī āṅkh.* — "my eye."

## The letters in this word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-SCRIPT-DO-CHASHMI-HE] -->

From the right edge: **آ**, alif carrying long *ā* (the same mark you
already read in **آپ**), then **ن**, then **ک** *k*, then **ھ**.

**کھ** extends the two-eyed *he* to a fifth base letter. It has already
aspirated **ج** in *samajh-*, **ٹ** in *ṭhīk*, **ڑ** in *paṛh-*, and **چ**
in *pūchh-*; here it does the same job to **ک**, giving *kh*.

The **ن** in this word is not pronounced as its own consonant. It marks the
**آ** before it as nasal — the vowel said through the nose, with no separate
*n* sound following it. Say **āṅkh** by nasalizing the *ā*, not by adding a
clean *n*.

## The word, taken apart — a third real cousin
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-ETYMON-AANKH-EYE]; assesses=[UR-LEX-AANKH] -->

**āṅkh** continues Sanskrit *ákṣi*, from Proto-Indo-European
**\*h₃ekʷ-**, "eye, to see." English **eye** descends from that same root.
This chapter has now found two settled Indo-European cousins in a row —
**ناک**/*nose*, **آنکھ**/*eye* — after **کان**'s honest dead end.

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-LEX-AANKH, UR-ETYMON-AANKH-EYE, UR-SCRIPT-DO-CHASHMI-HE] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Which letter does **کھ** newly pair the two-eyed *he* with, and
name two letters it already paired with? (**ک**; any two of **ج، ٹ، ڑ،
چ**.) What job does **ن** do in **آنکھ**? (**Marks the آ as nasal — it is
not its own consonant.**) Is **آنکھ**'s English cousin settled? (**Yes —
*eye*.**)
