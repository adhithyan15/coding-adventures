Arabic, chapter 73: Book, Notebook, Pen, Sheet of paper, School.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
كتاب (kitāb) — a book.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a kitchen, then the Arabic for a garden.

You'll want to know: كتاب.
كتاب — kitāb — "a book".

The word, taken apart.
كتاب (kitāb) is built on the native Arabic root k-t-b, "to write" (Lane). Kitāb is "a writing", hence "a book".

Lesson 2 of 5.
دفتر (daftar) — a notebook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a garden, then the Arabic for a book.

You'll want to know: دفتر.
دفتر — daftar — "a notebook".

The word, taken apart.
دفتر (daftar) came into Arabic from Persian. Lane calls it a Persian word, arabicized, and quotes Ibn Durayd calling its derivation unknown. The Persian word is usually traced to Greek diphthera, "prepared hide, parchment".

Lesson 3 of 5.
قلم (qalam) — a pen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a book, then the Arabic for a notebook.

You'll want to know: قلم.
قلم — qalam — "a pen".

The word, taken apart.
قلم (qalam) is listed by Lane under q-l-m as a reed prepared for writing. Many trace it to Greek kalamos, "reed, reed pen" (EtymDB), perhaps through Aramaic; Lane gives no foreign source, and the account is not settled.
