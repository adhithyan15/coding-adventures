# اس نے کھایا (us ne khāyā) — who did it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-C264-PAST-01, UR-GRAMMAR-C264-WAS-01, UR-LEX-C46-FAMILY46-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "he went home". (*voh ghar gayā*.) Going has no object. Eating has one: "he ate bread". That past is built another way.

## You'll want to know: نے after the doer
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C265-ERGATIVE-01]; assesses=[UR-LEX-ROTI, UR-LEX-C113-THINGS113-03, UR-LEX-LIKHNA, UR-LEX-C61-HOME61-02, UR-LEX-PARHNA] -->

> **میں نے کھایا۔**
>
> *maiṅ ne khāyā.* — "I ate."

With a verb that has an object, the doer takes **نے** (*ne*), and the verb follows the THING, not the doer:

> **اس نے روٹی کھائی۔**
>
> *us ne roṭī khāī.* — "He ate bread."

**روٹی** (*roṭī*) is feminine, so **کھائی** (*khāī*), even for a man. **وہ** (*voh*) becomes **اس** (*us*) before **نے**:

> **سارا نے خط لکھا۔**
>
> *Sārā ne khat likhā.* — "Sara wrote a letter."

> **سارا نے کتاب پڑھی۔**
>
> *Sārā ne kitāb paṛhī.* — "Sara read a book."

**خط** (*khat*) is masculine: **لکھا**. **کتاب** (*kitāb*) is feminine: **پڑھی**.

| the doer | with نے |
|---|---|
| **میں** | **میں نے** (*maiṅ ne*) |
| **ہم**, **آپ**, **تم** | **ہم نے**, **آپ نے**, **تم نے** |
| **وہ**, one | **اس نے** (*us ne*) |
| **وہ**, several | **انہوں نے** (*unhoṅ ne*) |

## You'll want to know: کیا, دیا, لیا, کہا, and مجھے
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[UR-GRAMMAR-C265-ERGATIVE-02]; assesses=[UR-LEX-KAAM, UR-LEX-C46-FAMILY46-04, UR-LEX-C48-VERBS48-04, UR-LEX-PANI, UR-LEX-KAHNA, UR-LEX-C51-VERBS51-03] -->

Four common pasts: **کیا** (*kiyā*), "did", from **کرنا**; **دیا** (*diyā*), "gave"; **لیا** (*liyā*), "took"; **کہا** (*kahā*), "said". **کیا** *kiyā* is spelled like **کیا** *kyā*, "what": the romanization tells them apart.

> **میں نے کام کیا۔**
>
> *maiṅ ne kām kiyā.* — "I did the work."

When the thing is a person with **کو** (*ko*), the verb stays masculine singular:

> **اس نے سارا کو دیکھا۔**
>
> *us ne Sārā ko dekhā.* — "He saw Sara."

**مجھے** (*mujhe*) is **میں** with **کو** inside it, "me, to me"; so too **اسے** (*use*), **ہمیں** (*hameṅ*), **تمہیں** (*tumheṅ*), **انہیں** (*unheṅ*):

> **سارا نے مجھے دیکھا۔**
>
> *Sārā ne mujhe dekhā.* — "Sara saw me."

> **اس نے مجھے پانی دیا۔**
>
> *us ne mujhe pānī diyā.* — "He gave me water."

One verb with an object goes the other way: **لانا** (*lānā*), "to bring", takes no **نے**, like **آنا** (*ānā*):

> **وہ پانی لایا۔**
>
> *voh pānī lāyā.* — "He brought water."

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[UR-GRAMMAR-C265-ERGATIVE-01, UR-GRAMMAR-C265-ERGATIVE-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "He ate bread"? (**اس نے روٹی کھائی**.) Why **کھائی**? (**روٹی** is feminine.) "She saw me"? (**اس نے مجھے دیکھا**.) And **وہ گیا**: does it take **نے**? (No: going has no object.)
