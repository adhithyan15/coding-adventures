Latin, chapter 373: How Things Turn Out.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
effectus, effectūs — a result, an effect; performance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for effective, then the Latin for to rouse.

You'll want to know: effectus, effectūs.
effectus, effectūs — "a result, an effect; performance".
Effectus cōnsiliī bonus fuit. The result of the plan was good.
effectus, effectūs m., built on efficiō. English effect.

The word, taken apart.
effectus is the noun built on efficiō "to bring about" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English effect comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
cōnsequor, cōnsequī — to follow; to achieve, to obtain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to rouse, then the Latin for a result.

You'll want to know: cōnsequor, cōnsequī.
cōnsequor, cōnsequī — "to follow; to achieve, to obtain".
Quod voluit, cōnsecūtus est. He achieved what he wanted.
Deponent: cōnsequor, cōnsequī, cōnsecūtus sum. Cōnsequitur ut, it follows that. English consequence.

The word, taken apart.
cōnsequor is built from con- and sequor "to follow" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to follow all the way to the goal. English consequence comes from its family.

Lesson 3 of 5.

prōveniō, prōvenīre — "to come forth, to grow; to turn out".
Frūmentum hōc annō bene prōvēnit. The grain grew well this year.
prōveniō, prōvenīre, prōvēnī, prōventum. Of a harvest, to come up; of a plan, to turn out. English provenance comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
succēdō, succēdere — to turn out well, to succeed; to come next.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to follow, then the Latin for to come forth.

You'll want to know: succēdō, succēdere.
succēdō, succēdere — "to turn out well, to succeed; to come next".
Cōnsilium nōn successit. The plan did not succeed.
succēdō, succēdere, successī, successum. With the dative it is also to take someone's place. English succeed and success.

The word, taken apart.
succēdō is built from sub "up to, next" and cēdō "to go" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to come up close, to come next. English succeed and success come from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
redundō, redundāre — to overflow; to result (in), to fall back (on).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to come forth, then the Latin for to turn out well.

You'll want to know: redundō, redundāre.
redundō, redundāre — "to overflow; to result (in), to fall back (on)".
Haec culpa in tē redundat. This fault falls back on you.
redundō, redundāre, redundāvī, redundātum. Built on unda, a wave: water flowing back over, and blame or credit coming back. English redound and redundant.

The word, taken apart.
redundō is built from red- "back" and undō "to surge", from unda "a wave" (Lewis & Short). English redound and redundant come from it.
