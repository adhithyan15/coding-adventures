German, chapter 314: Verzaubert.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
zaubern — to conjure, to do magic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the witch, then the German for the wizard.

You'll want to know: zaubern.
zaubern — "to conjure, to do magic".
Der Zauberer zauberte eine Blume aus dem Hut. The magician conjured a flower out of the hat.
Regular: zauberte, hat gezaubert.

The word, taken apart.
zaubern is inherited from Middle High German zoubern (EtymDB), built on Zauber "magic", Proto-Germanic taubrą.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

verzaubern is built on zaubern, Middle High German zoubern (EtymDB), with ver-: to change by magic.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: verwandeln.
verwandeln — "to turn into, to transform".
Die Hexe verwandelte ihn in einen Frosch. The witch turned him into a frog.
In etwas verwandeln; reflexive, sich verwandeln — to change into.

The word, taken apart.
verwandeln is inherited from Middle High German verwandelen (EtymDB), built on wandeln "to change", a relative of wenden "to turn".

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Drache — the dragon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to turn into, then the German for the curse.

You'll want to know: der Drache.
der Drache — "the dragon".
Der Drache wohnte in einem Berg. The dragon lived in a mountain.
Weak noun: den Drachen. Der Drachen, with -n, is a kite.

The word, taken apart.
Drache comes from Old High German trahho (EtymDB), an early loan from Latin dracō "a serpent, a dragon" (Lewis & Short), itself from Greek drakōn.
