# Synthesis — asking, and answering

Vivo.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-IR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-GRAMMAR-PRO-DROP] -->

Something quietly enormous happened over the last four chapters, and it is
worth saying out loud before you use it.

Spanish has **three** verb families. You now hold the singular of all three.
That is not three facts — it is a machine: hand it any regular verb you meet
for the rest of your life, and you can already say what you do with it, what a
friend does, and what somebody else does.

Two question words turn that machine outward. **¿Qué?** asks *what*. **¿Dónde?**
asks *where*. With them, the forms stop being an exercise and start being a
conversation.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-QUE, ES-LEX-DONDE, ES-LEX-EN, ES-LEX-COMER, ES-LEX-BEBER, ES-LEX-CAFE, ES-LEX-VIVIR] -->

> — ¿**Qué** bebes?
> — **Bebo** café. ¿Y tú?
> — Yo también. ¿**Dónde** vives?
> — **Vivo en** Madrid.

Four lines, and every single word in them is something you built yourself. No
new vocabulary was needed to get here — only the endings, one slot at a time.

Notice what the questions cost: nothing. You did not learn a question *form*.
You put **¿qué?** or **¿dónde?** in front of a verb you already had.
