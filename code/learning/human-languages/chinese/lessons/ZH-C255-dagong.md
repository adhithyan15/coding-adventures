## You'll want to know: 打工
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C255-PARTTIME255-01]; assesses=[] -->

**打工** — *dǎgōng* — "to work part-time; to have a part-time job".

> **他晚上打工** *tā wǎnshang dǎgōng* — He works a part-time job in the evenings.

The new character, in pieces: 工 — three strokes: a short bar, a vertical down from its middle, and a long bar across the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**打工** (*dǎgōng*) is built from 打 'to strike' and 工 'work', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *taengX* and *kuwng* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to strike' and the second 'skill'. Middle Chinese *taengX* would regularly give dǎng; dǎ is irregular. How the pair came to mean casual work is not settled.
