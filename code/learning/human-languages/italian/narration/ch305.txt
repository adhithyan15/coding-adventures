Italian, chapter 305: Acqua e fango.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'alluvione — the flood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the evacuee, then the Italian for to evacuate.

You'll want to know: l'alluvione.
l'alluvione — "the flood".
Dopo l'alluvione la piazza era piena di fango. After the flood the square was full of mud.
Feminine: un'alluvione.

The word, taken apart.
alluvione is a learned borrowing from Latin alluviō "an overflowing" (Lewis & Short), from alluere "to wash against".

Lesson 3 of 5.
la frana — the landslide.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the flood, then the Italian for to flood.

You'll want to know: la frana.
la frana — "the landslide".
Una frana ha chiuso la strada. A landslide has closed the road.
Essere una frana — to be hopeless at something: sono una frana in matematica.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of frana is not settled; a link with Latin vorāgō "an abyss" has been proposed (Lewis & Short gives vorāgō).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: la valanga.
la valanga — "the avalanche".
Una valanga è caduta sulla strada. An avalanche came down on the road.
Also a flood of things: una valanga di lettere.

The word, taken apart.
valanga is usually traced to northern Italian dialects; the deeper origin is not settled (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 5 of 5.
annegare — to drown.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the landslide, then the Italian for the avalanche.

You'll want to know: annegare.
annegare — "to drown".
Un uomo è annegato nel fiume. A man drowned in the river.
With essere: è annegato.

The word, taken apart.
annegare is inherited from Vulgar Latin adnecāre (EtymDB), from Latin necāre "to kill": first, to kill by water.
