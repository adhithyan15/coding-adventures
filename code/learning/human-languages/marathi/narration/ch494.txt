Marathi, chapter 494: Family Ties.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
नाते (nāte) — a relationship; kinship.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to shiver with cold, then the Marathi for to limp.

You'll want to know: नाते.
नाते — nāte — "a relationship; kinship".
आमचं आणि त्यांचं जवळचं नातं आहे. āmcaṁ āṇi tyāñcaṁ javaḷcaṁ nātaṁ āhe. We and they are closely related.
Neuter. Plural: नाती, nātī.
Sentences write the neuter as नातं. नातं जोडणे, to form a tie.

The word, taken apart.
नाते (nāte) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi nātẽ, "kinship, kinsman", under the reconstructed jñātra, "kinship" (CDIAL 5279).

Lesson 2 of 5.
नातेवाईक (nātevāīk) — a relative.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to limp, then the Marathi for a relationship.

You'll want to know: नातेवाईक.
नातेवाईक — nātevāīk — "a relative".
सुट्टीत सगळे नातेवाईक आजीच्या घरी जमले. suṭṭīt sagḷe nātevāīk ājīcyā gharī jamle. In the holidays all the relatives gathered at Grandmother's house.
Masculine.
Plural नातेवाईक (nātevāīk). Built on नाते (nāte), kinship.

The word, taken apart.
नातेवाईक (nātevāīk) is a Marathi derivation of नाते (nāte), "kinship" (CDIAL 5279); the origin of the ending -vāīk is not settled.

परिवार — parivār — "a household; one's family".
आमचा परिवार मोठा आहे. āmcā parivār moṭhā āhe. Ours is a big family.
Masculine.
More formal than कुटुंब.

The word, taken apart.
परिवार (parivār) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit parivāra, "surroundings; followers" (CDIAL 7876).

Lesson 4 of 5.
अनाथ (anāth) — an orphan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a relative, then the Marathi for a household.

You'll want to know: अनाथ.
अनाथ — anāth — "an orphan".
त्या संस्थेत अनाथ मुलांची काळजी घेतली जाते. tyā saṁsthet anāth mulāñcī kāḷjī ghetlī jāte. Orphaned children are cared for at that institution.
Masculine.
Also an adjective: अनाथ (anāth) मुलगी. अनाथालय, an orphanage.

The word, taken apart.
अनाथ (anāth) is a tatsama, taken from Sanskrit anātha, "having no master or protector" (Monier-Williams).
