Italian, chapter 500: Ago e filo.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
cucire — to sew.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for faithfulness, then the Italian for to confide.

You'll want to know: cucire.
cucire — "to sew".
Mia nonna cuce i vestiti per tutta la famiglia. My grandmother sews clothes for the whole family.
Present: cucio, cuci, cuce. La macchina da cucire — the sewing machine.

The word, taken apart.
cucire is inherited from Latin cōnsuere "to sew together" (Lewis & Short), through a reshaped Vulgar Latin form whose details are not settled.

You'll want to know: l'ago.
l'ago — "needle".
Mi passi l'ago e il filo? Can you pass me the needle and thread?
The plural is gli aghi. Cercare un ago in un pagliaio — a needle in a haystack.

The word, taken apart.
ago is inherited from Latin acus "a needle" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ricamare — to embroider.

Before the new one: say the Italian for to sew, then the Italian for needle.

You'll want to know: ricamare.
ricamare — "to embroider".
Ha ricamato il suo nome sul fazzoletto. She embroidered her name on the handkerchief.
The noun is il ricamo.

The word, taken apart.
ricamare comes from Arabic raqama "to mark, to embroider" (EtymDB lists raqm "a mark"), probably through Medieval Latin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'uncinetto — crochet; crochet hook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for needle, then the Italian for to embroider.

You'll want to know: l'uncinetto.
l'uncinetto — "crochet; crochet hook".
D'inverno faccio sciarpe all'uncinetto. In winter I crochet scarves.
Lavorare all'uncinetto — to crochet; lavorare a maglia — to knit.

The word, taken apart.
uncinetto is a little form of uncino "hook", inherited from Latin uncīnus "a hook" (Lewis & Short), from uncus "bent".

Lesson 5 of 5.
rammendare — to darn, to mend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to embroider, then the Italian for crochet.

You'll want to know: rammendare.
rammendare — "to darn, to mend".
Ho rammendato il buco nel calzino. I darned the hole in the sock.
For mending a hole by sewing; for other repairs say riparare.

The word, taken apart.
rammendare is built from r- and ammendare "to correct", from Latin ēmendāre "to free from faults" (Lewis & Short), from menda "a fault".
