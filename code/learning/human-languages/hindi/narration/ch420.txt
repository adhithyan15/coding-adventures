Hindi, chapter 420: Slopes and Passages.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ढलान (ḍhalān) — a slope.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a building, then the Hindi for a signboard.

You'll want to know: ढलान.
ढलान — ḍhalān — "a slope".
ढलान पर गाड़ी धीरे चलाइए। ḍhalān par gāṛī dhīre calāiye. Drive slowly on the slope.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
ढलान (ḍhalān) is built on the stem of ढलना, "to slope, to go down", which goes back to ḍhalati, "bends over, falls", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 5581); CDIAL does not list the noun itself.

गलियारा — galiyārā — "a corridor; a passage".
ट्रेन के गलियारे में लोग खड़े थे। ṭren ke galiyāre meṁ log khaṛe the. People were standing in the corridor of the train.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
गलियारा (galiyārā) is built on गली, "a lane", from galī, "a defile, a lane", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 4085): a lane inside a building or a train. CDIAL does not list galiyārā itself.

The word, taken apart.

You'll want to know: लुढ़कना.
लुढ़कना — luṛhaknā — "to roll (down)".
पत्थर ढलान से नीचे लुढ़क गया। patthar ḍhalān se nīce luṛhak gayā. The stone rolled down the slope.
Past (it …): वह गेंद सड़क पर लुढ़की, vah gẽd saṛak par luṛhkī.

The word, taken apart.
लुढ़कना (luṛhaknā) is traced to Sanskrit luṭhati, "rolls, wallows" (CDIAL 11079), which lists Hindi luṛhaknā, "to roll, slip".

Lesson 5 of 5.
गड़ना (gaṛnā) — to dig into; to sink in (of something pointed).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to push in, then the Hindi for to roll.

You'll want to know: गड़ना.
गड़ना — gaṛnā — "to dig into; to sink in (of something pointed)".
जूते की कील मेरे पैर में गड़ रही थी। jūte kī kīl mere pair meṁ gaṛ rahī thī. The nail in my shoe was digging into my foot.
Past (it …): वह काँटा पैर में गड़ गया, vah kā̃ṭā pair meṁ gaṛ gayā.

The word, taken apart.
गड़ना (gaṛnā) is filed by CDIAL under Sanskrit gaḍati, "drops, runs as liquid" (CDIAL 3973), which lists Hindi gaṛnā, "to sink into"; CDIAL does not say how the one sense led to the other, so take the link as probable.
