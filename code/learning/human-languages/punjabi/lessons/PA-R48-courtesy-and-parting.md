# ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ ਕਰਕੇ — the politeness words, and what is inside them

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-MIHARBANI, PA-LEX-MAAF] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say *please*. Then say *sorry*. Both are longer than the English and
both have a verb inside — say which verb.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-LEX-MIHARBANI, PA-LEX-MAAF, PA-GRAM-NU-DATIVE] -->

Punjabi's two commonest courtesies are not words for *please* and *sorry* at all.
They are **things done**:

| what you say | what it is built from |
|---|---|
| **ਮਿਹਰਬਾਨੀ ਕਰਕੇ** | *having done a kindness* |
| **ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰੋ** | *make pardoned* |
| **ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ** | *to those who have come — life* |

All three run on the same verb of doing or a case ending, and none of them is a
fixed politeness particle you could swap for another. The **ਨੂੰ** on the third is
the same dative ending the book uses elsewhere: the welcome is grammatically
*addressed* to the arrivals rather than describing them.

## Guided Practice: leaving, and the registers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-GRAMMAR-FUTURE-AANGE-04, PA-PHRASE-RABB-RAKHA-04, PA-LEX-SHUBH-RAAT, PA-LEX-NA] -->

[PAUSE 1s]
- [YOU SAY: we will meet — and say what the **-ਾਂਗੇ** ending is doing]
- [YOU SAY: the parting that hands someone to God's keeping — *rabb rākhā*]
- [YOU SAY: the formal, Sanskritic good night — *shubh rāt*]
- [YOU SAY: the bare negative — *nā*]

Three partings, three registers. *Rabb rākhā* carries a blessing and belongs to
people who know each other; *shubh rāt* is the formal one; and the everyday
sign-off is neither.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PA-FORM-ECHO-REDUPLICATION, PA-LEX-SIR, PA-LEX-MIHARBANI] -->

[PAUSE 3s] What is literally inside the word for *please*? (**Having done a
kindness.**) What does the echo in **ਠੀਕ-ਠਾਕ** add — a new meaning, or a shade of
one? (**A shade** — the echo softens it toward *getting by*.) And which body part
is a cousin of English *horn* rather than *head*? (**ਸਿਰ**.)
