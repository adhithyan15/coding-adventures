## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Two endings, three families. (*-ando* for *-ar*; *-iendo* for
everything else.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-GERUND-AR, ES-GRAMMAR-PROGRESSIVE-ESTAR-GERUND, ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A, ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A-WHY] -->

Three chants.

[PAUSE 2s] **First.** *Hablando, comiendo, viviendo.* — one form each, no
agreement, no persons. The smallest thing this book has asked of you.

[PAUSE 2s] **Second.** *Estoy comiendo.* — *estar* carries the person, the
gerund never moves. And it means **now**: for tomorrow you still say *voy a
comer*.

[PAUSE 2s] **Third.** *Veo a María*, but *veo la casa.* — the little *a* in
front of a person, and nothing in front of a house.

## Grammar Lens: both of these are Latin's absence
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-PROGRESSIVE-NOT-FUTURE, ES-GRAMMAR-GERUND-ER-IR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The gerund is what is left of a Latin form after its case endings
fell off — which is why it has exactly one shape and agrees with nothing.

[PAUSE 2s] The personal *a* is what grew back **because** case endings fell
off. Latin marked the receiving end with an ending; Spanish marks it with a
preposition, but only where it matters, which is people.

[PAUSE 2s] One rule is a leftover and the other is a repair. Same cause.
