French, chapter 461: Bruine et averses.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la bruine — the drizzle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for sunny, then the French for the thermometer.

You'll want to know: la bruine.
la bruine — "the drizzle".
Une bruine fine tombe depuis ce matin. A fine drizzle has been falling since this morning.
Very light rain.

The word, taken apart.
bruine is usually traced to Latin pruīna 'hoarfrost' (Lewis & Short), but the path is not settled, and EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'averse — the rain shower, the downpour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the drizzle, then the French for to drizzle.

You'll want to know: l'averse.
l'averse — "the rain shower, the downpour".
Nous avons attendu la fin de l'averse sous un arbre. We waited for the shower to end under a tree.
A feminine noun. Short and heavy.

The word, taken apart.
averse comes from à verse 'pouring down', from verser 'to pour', which goes back to Latin versāre, from vertere 'to turn' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'éclaircie — the bright spell, the sunny interval.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to drizzle, then the French for the rain shower.

You'll want to know: l'éclaircie.
l'éclaircie — "the bright spell, the sunny interval".
Après la pluie, il y a eu une belle éclaircie. After the rain there was a lovely bright spell.
A feminine noun. Des averses et des éclaircies — sunshine and showers.

The word, taken apart.
éclaircie is built on éclaircir 'to brighten, to clear up', from clair 'clear', which is inherited from Latin clārus (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dégager — to clear (of the sky); to free.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the rain shower, then the French for the bright spell.

You'll want to know: dégager.
dégager — "to clear (of the sky); to free".
Vers midi, le ciel s'est dégagé. Around midday the sky cleared.
Le ciel se dégage — the sky is clearing; dégager le passage — to clear the way.

The word, taken apart.
dégager is built from dé- and gage 'pledge' (EtymDB lists the verb), which comes from a Frankish word: first to take back what was pledged, then to set free.
