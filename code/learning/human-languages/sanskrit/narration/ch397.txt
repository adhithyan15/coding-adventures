Sanskrit, chapter 397: Lamps and Hymns.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ज्वालयति (jvālayati) — lights; kindles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an inner sanctum, then the Sanskrit for a devotee.

You'll want to know: ज्वालयति.
ज्वालयति — jvālayati — "lights; kindles".
माता सायं दीपं ज्वालयति। mātā sāyaṃ dīpaṃ jvālayati. Mother lights a lamp in the evening.
Past (she …): सा दीपं ज्वालितवती, sā dīpaṃ jvālitavatī.

The word, taken apart.
ज्वालयति (jvālayati) is built on the root √jval, "to blaze", which gives jvalati, "burns"; MW gives its causative, "makes blaze", as to set on fire, light or kindle, and KRM gives the causative participle jvālita (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
दीपशिखा (dīpaśikhā) — a lamp's flame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a devotee, then the Sanskrit for kindles.

You'll want to know: दीपशिखा.
दीपशिखा — dīpaśikhā — "a lamp's flame".
वायुना दीपशिखा चलति। vāyunā dīpaśikhā calati. The lamp's flame flickers in the wind.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
दीपशिखा (dīpaśikhā) is built from dīpa, "a lamp", and śikhā, "a tuft, a point of flame"; MW gives the compound as the flame of a lamp (MW).

दीपे कर्पूरः ज्वलति। dīpe karpūraḥ jvalati. Camphor burns in the lamp.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कर्पूरः (karpūraḥ) is the word MW gives for camphor; MW records only a commentator's guess at a root √kṛp, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

स्तोत्रम् — stotram — "a hymn of praise".
बालाः सायं स्तोत्रं गायन्ति। bālāḥ sāyaṃ stotraṃ gāyanti. The children sing a hymn in the evening.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्तोत्रम् (stotram) is built on the root √stu, "to praise", with the ending -tra; MW gives stotra as praise, a hymn of praise (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
स्तौति (stauti) — sings the praises of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for camphor, then the Sanskrit for a hymn of praise.

You'll want to know: स्तौति.
स्तौति — stauti — "sings the praises of".
शिष्यः श्लोकेन गुरुं स्तौति। śiṣyaḥ ślokena guruṃ stauti. The disciple praises his teacher in a verse.
Past (he …): सः गुरुं स्तुतवान्, saḥ guruṃ stutavān.

The word, taken apart.
स्तौति (stauti) comes from the root √stu, "to praise, to hymn", which also gives stotra, "a hymn"; MW gives the participle stuta, "praised, hymned" (MW).
