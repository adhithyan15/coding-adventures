## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-02, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05] -->

A message between two people sharing a flat. Read it, then answer.

> Oye, mañana viene mi hermana.
>
> He **pintado** el **dormitorio** de azul **claro** y huele fuerte, así que
> deja la ventana abierta.
>
> ¿Puedes **meter** las cajas del pasillo en el trastero? No me ha dado
> tiempo.
>
> Y si sale el sol, **tiende** las toallas antes de irte. Gracias.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: what colour the bedroom is now, and what to do about the smell]
- [YOU SAY: the two jobs being asked for]
- [YOU SAY: what has to happen before the towels go out]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04] -->

**De azul claro** carries both of this chapter's small grammar points at once.
*De* marks the resulting colour, and *azul claro* does **not** agree with
anything — no *azules claros* even if there were several walls, because the
pair has frozen into a name for the shade.

**Meter las cajas en el trastero** is *meter* doing exactly its job: a
storeroom is an inside, and *poner* would be wrong. Then **tiende** is the
*tú* command of a stem-changing verb, so the **ie** is there — *tiende*, never
*tende*.

Notice the register throughout. *Oye*, *¿puedes…?*, *deja* — every one is
*tú*, because this is a flatmate and not a counter. Set it beside the notices
in the chapters before this one and the difference is visible in the verb
endings alone.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-01, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-02, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-03, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-04, ES-LEX-C455-CUARTO-05] -->

flatmate: say you have painted the bedroom a light colour, ask them to put the
