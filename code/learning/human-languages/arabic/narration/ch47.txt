Lesson 1 of 4.
إ (alif with hamza below) — the alif that opens إلى.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ص and د once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say إلى اللقاء (ilā l-liqāʾ), "see you". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: إ.
إ (alif with hamza below) — alif with a hamza below, read i at the start of a word.
It opens إلى (ilā), "until", in إلى اللقاء (ilā l-liqāʾ), "see you". You already write ا and the hamza ء. Put the small hamza under the alif and the word opens on i; put it over the alif (أ) and it opens on a or u.

Writing: إ.
Put your pen on إ (alif with hamza below) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this character or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 2 of 4.
ى (alif maqṣūra) — the letter that ends إلى.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ى and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write د and إ (alif with hamza below) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say إلى اللقاء (ilā l-liqāʾ), "see you". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ى.
ى — alif maqṣūra, a long ā at the very end of a word.
It ends إلى (ilā) in إلى اللقاء. It looks like ي with its two dots taken away, and that is the whole trick: no dots, no y. It only ever stands at the end of a word, and there it is read as a long ā.

Writing: ى.
1. start here: curve from above the baseline through the upper half of the S.
2. without lifting, continue through the wide flat lower curve and finish near the baseline — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Arabic Language Learning Notes, Learn How to Write Arabic Letters — Basic Naskh: Alif Maqsura ى (alif maqṣūra), isolated form and stroke order (updated 2025-01-29; accessed 2026-08-23).

Lesson 3 of 4.
ف (fāʾ) — the letter inside عفوا.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ف and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write إ (alif with hamza below) and ى (alif maqṣūra) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say عفوا (ʿafwan), "you're welcome". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ف.
ف — fāʾ, the sound f.
It is the second letter of عفوا (ʿafwan), "you're welcome". The letter is a small closed head on a broad bowl, with one dot above.

Writing: ف.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the small closed head.
2. continue left through the broad bowl without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper dot last — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet ف (fāʾ) ق (qāf), independent Faa at 00:01.7–00:03.3 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-23).

Lesson 4 of 4.
ق (qāf) — the letter inside اللقاء.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 2 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say إلى اللقاء (ilā l-liqāʾ), "see you". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ق.
ق — qāf, a k made far back in the throat.
It is inside اللقاء (al-liqāʾ), "the meeting", in إلى اللقاء. Its head is the same as ف (fāʾ), which you wrote in the last lesson, but it carries two dots and, standing alone, its bowl dips deeper, below the line.

Writing: ق.
1. start here: loop counterclockwise around the small closed head.
2. continue down and left through the deep bowl without lifting.
3. lift once, then place the upper-right dot.
4. lift again, then place the upper-left dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Introduction to Arabic: Egyptian Arabic for first-year students, Alphabet ف (fāʾ) ق (qāf), independent Qaf at 00:01.5–00:03.5 (University of Oregon Libraries Open Textbook, 2023; accessed 2026-08-23).
