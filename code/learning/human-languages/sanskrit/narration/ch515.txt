Sanskrit, chapter 515: How Does It Taste?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
स्वादः (svādaḥ) — a flavour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for leftovers, then the Sanskrit for a lid.

You'll want to know: स्वादः.
स्वादः — svādaḥ — "a flavour".
एतस्य सूपस्य स्वादः उत्तमः अस्ति। etasya sūpasya svādaḥ uttamaḥ asti. This dal has a fine flavour.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
स्वादः (svādaḥ) comes from the root √svad, "to be sweet, to relish"; MW gives svāda as taste, flavour, savour (MW).

आमः — āmaḥ — "raw; uncooked".
एतत् शाकम् आमम् अस्ति। etat śākam āmam asti. This vegetable is raw.

The word, taken apart.
आमः (āmaḥ) is the adjective MW records as raw, uncooked, the opposite of pakva, "cooked"; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
पक्वः (pakvaḥ) — ripe; cooked.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for tasty, then the Sanskrit for raw.

You'll want to know: पक्वः.
पक्वः — pakvaḥ — "ripe; cooked".
एतत् आम्रं पक्वं न अस्ति। etat āmraṃ pakvaṃ na asti. This mango is not ripe.

The word, taken apart.
पक्वः (pakvaḥ) comes from the root √pac, "to cook", and MW treats it as that root's past participle: cooked, baked, and so ripe (MW).
