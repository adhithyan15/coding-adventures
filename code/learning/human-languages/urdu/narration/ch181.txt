Urdu, chapter 181: To spend, To pay off, To measure, To weigh, To check.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خرچ کرنا (kharch karnā) — to spend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to mix; to introduce, then the Urdu for to join.

You'll want to know: خرچ کرنا.
خرچ کرنا — kharch karnā — "to spend".

The phrase, taken apart.
خرچ کرنا (kharch karnā) joins kharch, "spending", and karnā, "to do". kharch is Arabic kharj, "a going out; expenditure", which reached Urdu through Persian (Lane, root kh-r-j); karnā comes from Sanskrit karoti, "does" (CDIAL 2814).

Lesson 2 of 5.
چکانا (chukānā) — to pay off, to settle (a bill).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to join, then the Urdu for to spend.

You'll want to know: چکانا.
چکانا — chukānā — "to pay off, to settle (a bill)".

The word, taken apart.
چکانا (chukānā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from cukk, "fall short of, stop", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 4848). It is the causing form of chuknā, "to be finished". CDIAL takes cukk from Prakrit cukka, "fallen, mistaken", which replaced Sanskrit cyuta, "fallen".
