Mandarin Chinese, chapter 165: Coats and Scarves.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
大衣 (dàyī) — overcoat; coat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to put on make-up, then the Mandarin for to have a shave.

You'll want to know: 大衣.
大衣 — dàyī — "overcoat; coat".
我的大衣在哪儿 wǒ de dàyī zài nǎr — Where's my coat?
The new character, in pieces: 衣 (yī) — six strokes: a dot and a horizontal on top, then a long stroke falling to the left, a short vertical that flicks up to the right, a short stroke falling to the left, and a long stroke falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
大衣 (dàyī) is built from 大 'big' and 衣 (yī) 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, daH and 'j+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the garment of the upper body: the big garment over the rest.

What you've built.
One more word for everyday things.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Script — a dot and a bar, then the body.
衣 (yī).
One piece, six strokes.
The top, two strokes. A dot. A long horizontal under it.
The body, four strokes. A long stroke falling to the lower left. A short vertical inside it that flicks up to the right at its foot. A short stroke on the right falling to the left. Last, a long stroke falling to the lower right.
2 + 4 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 衣 (yī), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 衣 (yī)? (Six.) What comes first? (A dot, then a horizontal.) Which stroke flicks up? (The short vertical inside the body.)

Lesson 3 of 6.
máoyī — sweater; jumper.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to have a shave, then the Mandarin for an overcoat.

You'll want to know: máoyī.
máoyī — "sweater; jumper".
wǒ de máoyī hěn hǎokàn — My sweater looks nice.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
máoyī is built from máo 'hair, wool' and 衣 (yī) 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, maw and 'j+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first as the hair of brows and of beasts.

wǒ mǎi le liǎng tiáo kùzi — I bought two pairs of trousers.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tiáo is inherited from Middle Chinese dew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'a small branch': a long, thin thing. Its use for counting is not given there.

qúnzi — "skirt".
zhè tiáo qúnzi hěn hǎokàn — This skirt looks nice.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
qúnzi is built from qún 'skirt' and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjun and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which writes the first another way and glosses it as the garment of the lower body. In the word the second loses its tone.

Lesson 6 of 6.
wéijīn — scarf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for measure word for long things, then the Mandarin for a skirt.

You'll want to know: wéijīn.
wéijīn — "scarf".
wàibian hěn lěng nǐ yào dài yì tiáo wéijīn — It's cold outside, so take a scarf.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wéijīn is built from wéi 'to go round' and jīn 'a cloth'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese kin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the first is listed there as hjw+j, glossed 'to surround': a cloth that goes round the neck.
