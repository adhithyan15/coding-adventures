French, chapter 406: La voiture de près.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la carrosserie — the bodywork (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to tow, then the French for the garage owner.

You'll want to know: la carrosserie.
la carrosserie — "the bodywork (of a car)".
La carrosserie est rayée sur le côté. The bodywork is scratched on the side.
Un carrossier repairs bodywork.

The word, taken apart.
carrosserie is built on carrosse 'coach', which was borrowed from Italian carrozza, from carro and so from Latin carrus 'wagon' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le capot — the bonnet (of a car).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the garage owner, then the French for the bodywork.

You'll want to know: le capot.
le capot — "the bonnet (of a car)".
Le mécanicien ouvre le capot. The mechanic opens the bonnet.
In American English: the hood.

The word, taken apart.
capot is usually traced to cape 'cloak with a hood', from Late Latin cappa: the engine wears a hood. EtymDB has no source line for this sense.

La porte for a house, la portière for a car or a train.

Les valises sont dans le coffre. The suitcases are in the boot.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la banquette — the back seat; the bench seat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the car door, then the French for the boot.

You'll want to know: la banquette.
la banquette — "the back seat; the bench seat".
Les enfants dorment sur la banquette arrière. The children are asleep on the back seat.
La banquette arrière — the back seat.

The word, taken apart.
banquette is a small banc 'bench', probably borrowed from Occitan banqueta. Banc comes from a Germanic word for 'bench' (EtymDB).
