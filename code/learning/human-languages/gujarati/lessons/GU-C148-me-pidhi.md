# મેં ચા પીધી — the past that agrees with the thing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-GAYO-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-AAVYO-01, GU-LEX-C55-HOUSEWORK-05, GU-LEX-CHA, GU-LEX-PAANI] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I went* and *she came*. Then three old words: **પીવું**, *to
drink*; **ચા**, *tea*; **પાણી**, *water*.

## Grammar Lens: હું becomes મેં
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C148-ERGATIVE-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C55-HOUSEWORK-05, GU-LEX-CHA, GU-CONCEPT-C03-HUN-01] -->

**હું ગયો** keeps *I* in its plain form. A verb that does something *to*
something, like drinking tea or eating rice, changes that in the past: the doer
is marked, and **હું** becomes **મેં** (*mẽ*).

> **મેં ચા પીધી.** — *mẽ cā pīdhī.* — I drank tea.

**મેં** is the same for a man and a woman. The verb has stopped listening to the
drinker.

## You'll want to know: the verb follows the tea
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C148-OBJECT-AGREE-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-PAANI, GU-LEX-DUDH, GU-GRAMMAR-C146-POSS-AGREE-01] -->

**પીધી** ends in **-ી** because **ચા** is feminine. Change the drink and the verb
changes with it:

- **મેં ચા પીધી.** — *mẽ cā pīdhī* — tea, feminine;
- **મેં પાણી પીધું.** — *mẽ pāṇī pīdhũ* — water, neuter;
- **મેં દૂધ પીધું.** — *mẽ dūdh pīdhũ* — milk, neuter.

This is the agreement **મારી ચા** showed you: the ending follows the thing.
English has nothing like it, so it is worth saying slowly a few times.
