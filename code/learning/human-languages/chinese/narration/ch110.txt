Lesson 1 of 7.
外语 (wàiyǔ) — foreign language.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to charge a battery, then the Mandarin for to download.

You'll want to know: 外语.
外语 — wàiyǔ — "foreign language".
我上网学外语 wǒ shàngwǎng xué wàiyǔ — I learn foreign languages online.
The new character, in pieces: 外 (wài) — on the left the three-stroke top half of 多; on the right a vertical with a dot beside it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
外语 (wàiyǔ) is built from 外 (wài) 'outside' and 语 'speech', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwajH and ngjoX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the speech from outside.

Script — the top of 多, then a vertical and a dot.
外 (wài).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, three strokes, the top half of 多: a short left-falling stroke, a stroke across that turns and sweeps down to the left, and a dot inside.
The right piece, two strokes: a long vertical, and a dot to its right.
3 + 2 = five.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 外 (wài), all five, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 外 (wài)? (Five.) Where have you written its left piece? (At the top of 多.) What is on the right? (A vertical and a dot.)

Lesson 3 of 7.
gāngqín — piano.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to download, then the Mandarin for foreign language.

You'll want to know: gāngqín.
gāngqín — "piano".
tā jiā yǒu gāngqín — Her family has a piano.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
gāngqín is built from gāng 'steel' and qín, a stringed instrument, both inherited from Middle Chinese, kang and gim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary describes the second as an instrument of five strings, later seven. The piano is a modern sense of the pair.

Lesson 4 of 7.
tán — to play an instrument; to pluck.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: tán.
tán — "to play an instrument; to pluck".
wǒ huì tán gāngqín — I can play the piano.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tán is inherited from Middle Chinese dan (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to shoot' and named in a game of flicking pieces. Playing an instrument is not given there, so how the word came to that sense is not settled.

huàhuà — "to paint pictures; to draw".
háizi xǐhuan huàhuà — Children love painting.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huàhuà is built from huà 'to paint' and its object huà 'a picture', one word used twice, inherited from Middle Chinese hweaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary explains painting as laying the five colours on the likeness of things.
