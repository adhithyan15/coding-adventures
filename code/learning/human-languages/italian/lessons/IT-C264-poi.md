# prima, poi, alla fine — the events, in order

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-LEX-PRIMA-02, IT-LEX-DOPO-02, IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Chapter twenty-eight put a dinner in order: *prima* the first
course, *dopo* the second. Say "before" and "after". (*Prima*, *dopo*.)

[PAUSE 1s] Now the same words go to work on a story. The scene is painted; the
events need a running order.

## Grammar Lens: three places in a story
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-GRAMMAR-POI-01, IT-GRAMMAR-ALLA-FINE-02]; assesses=[IT-LEX-C63-COSE3-05, IT-LEX-C60-VERBI5-01, IT-LEX-C-ERA-02] -->

> **Prima** ho comprato il biglietto. — First I bought the ticket.
> **Poi** ho aspettato il treno. — Then I waited for the train.
> **Alla fine** sono tornato a casa. — In the end I went back home.

**Poi** is the plain "then, next". It stands between two events and points
from the first to the second, and you can use it as many times as the story
has events: *poi … poi … poi*.

**Dopo** does a little more. It can stand alone, "afterwards", but it can also
take a noun: *dopo il caffè*, "after the coffee". *Poi* cannot.

**Alla fine** is "in the end": the last event, and usually the outcome. You
have **infine**, "finally", too; *infine* closes a list, *alla fine* closes a
story. *C'era molto traffico, ma alla fine sono tornato a casa* — there was a
lot of traffic, but in the end I got home.

Notice what these words do **not** touch: the scene. *Faceva freddo* needs no
*poi*. Order belongs to the events.

## The word, taken apart: one root, twice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-ETYMON-POI-03]; assesses=[IT-ETYMON-DOPO-03] -->

**poi** comes from Latin *post*, "after, behind", through a Vulgar Latin *pos*
that had already dropped the *t* (EtymDB 2.1). And you met *post* before:
**dopo** is *dē* + *post*, two Latin words welded together. So *poi* and
*dopo* are the same root, once bare and once with *dē* in front. English
keeps *post* in **postpone** and **postscript**.

**fine**, "end", in *alla fine*, is Latin *fīnis*, "boundary, end" — inherited
(EtymDB 2.1). English **final**, **finish** and **define** share it.
