Spanish, chapter 760: Teatro y disfraces.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el disfraz — the costume, the disguise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to act, then the Spanish for the performance.

You'll want to know: el disfraz.
el disfraz — "the costume, the disguise".
Mi disfraz de carnaval es de pirata. My carnival costume is a pirate costume.
Una fiesta de disfraces — a fancy-dress party.

The word, taken apart.
disfraz is built on disfrazar (EtymDB), from Old Spanish desfrezar. For desfrezar the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 5.
el maquillaje — make-up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the costume, then the Spanish for to dress up.

You'll want to know: el maquillaje.
el maquillaje — "make-up".
Los actores necesitan mucho maquillaje. The actors need a lot of make-up.
Maquillarse — to put on make-up.

The word, taken apart.
maquillaje was borrowed from French maquillage, built on maquiller "to make up", which is usually traced to a northern French dialect verb meaning to work, to do.

Lesson 4 of 5.
el estreno — the premiere, the first showing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to dress up, then the Spanish for make-up.

You'll want to know: el estreno.
el estreno — "the premiere, the first showing".
Fuimos al estreno de la película. We went to the premiere of the film.
Estrenar — to show for the first time, to wear for the first time.

The word, taken apart.
estreno is built on estrenar, which is usually traced to Latin strēna "an omen; a New Year gift" (Lewis & Short): using something new for the first time.

Lesson 5 of 5.
interpretar — to play (a role); to interpret.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for make-up, then the Spanish for the premiere.

You'll want to know: interpretar.
interpretar — "to play (a role); to interpret".
Interpreta a un médico en la película. He plays a doctor in the film.
Also to interpret a language or a text.

The word, taken apart.
interpretar is usually described as a learned borrowing from Latin interpretārī "to explain", built on interpres "go-between, translator".
