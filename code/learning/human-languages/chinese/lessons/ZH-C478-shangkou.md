## You'll want to know: 伤口
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C478-HURT478-02]; assesses=[] -->

**伤口** — *shāngkǒu* — "wound; cut".

> **我感觉伤口很疼体温也高了** *wǒ gǎnjué shāngkǒu hěn téng tǐwēn yě gāo le* — My wound really hurts, and my temperature has gone up too.

The new character, in pieces: 伤 — six strokes in three pieces: on the left 亻, two strokes; at the top right a short stroke falling to the left and a bar; and under them 力 *lì*, two strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**伤口** (*shāngkǒu*) is built from 伤 'to hurt' and 口 'an opening', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *syang* and *khuwX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to harm' and the second 'the mouth'.
