Spanish, chapter 654: En el avión.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
despegar — to take off; to unstick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the tray, then the Spanish for the stopover.

You'll want to know: despegar.
despegar — "to take off; to unstick".
El avión despega a las seis. The plane takes off at six.
The noun is el despegue, take-off.

The word, taken apart.
despegar is built on pegar "to stick" with des- "un-"; pegar is inherited from Latin picāre "to smear with pitch" (EtymDB). A plane unsticks itself from the ground.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to take off, then the Spanish for to land.

You'll want to know: el piloto.
el piloto — "the pilot; the driver (racing)".
El piloto habla por el altavoz. The pilot is speaking over the loudspeaker.
For a woman, la piloto.

The word, taken apart.
piloto was borrowed from Italian piloto (EtymDB), first the man who steered a ship into port. Its deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la azafata — the flight attendant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the pilot, then the Spanish for the crew.

You'll want to know: la azafata.
la azafata — "the flight attendant".
La azafata nos trae agua. The flight attendant brings us water.
Latin America says la aeromoza or la sobrecargo; the neutral term is auxiliar de vuelo.

The word, taken apart.
azafata is built on azafate "tray", which comes from Arabic safaṭ "basket" (EtymDB). The first azafata was a lady who held the queen’s tray.
