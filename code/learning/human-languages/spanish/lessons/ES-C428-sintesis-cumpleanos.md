# una invitación — reading it, and answering it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-03, ES-ORTH-C428-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the two things you would say at a birthday. One congratulates
and one raises a glass.

## Reading
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-01, ES-ORTH-C428-02, ES-ORTH-C424-01] -->

Read this invitation aloud, saying every symbol by name:

> **Cumpleaños de Ana**
> Sábado, a las ocho.
> Confirma en ana_lopez arroba ejemplo punto example

The address line is written in words here because that is how it is **said**. On
a screen it would read *ana_lopez@ejemplo.example*, and saying it aloud requires
the four names: *guion bajo*, *arroba*, *punto*, and the letters themselves.

A real Spanish address would end *punto es*; this one ends in the reserved
teaching word instead, so that nobody is written to by accident.

## Grammar Lens: answering it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-03, ES-ORTH-C428-04, ES-ORTH-C428-05] -->

| moment | what you say |
|---|---|
| arriving | ¡Felicidades! |
| the first glass | ¡Salud! |
| somebody sneezes | ¡Salud! |

Two words cover the whole evening, and one of them covers two of the three
moments.

If the invitation had been to a farm rather than a flat, you would also have the
word for whatever was in the field: *un animal*.
