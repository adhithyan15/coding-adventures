Italian, chapter 527: Che cosa leggi?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la biografia — biography.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to devour, then the Italian for to reread.

You'll want to know: la biografia.
la biografia — "biography".
Sto leggendo la biografia di un famoso pittore. I am reading the biography of a famous painter.
L'autobiografia — autobiography.

The word, taken apart.
biografia is a learned word built from Greek bíos "life" and -graphía "writing" (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: il saggio.
il saggio — "essay; non-fiction book".
Ha scritto un saggio sulla storia della musica. She wrote an essay on the history of music.
As an adjective, saggio means wise; that word has a different origin.

The word, taken apart.
saggio "essay" comes from Late Latin exagium "a weighing, a test" (EtymDB): an essay tries out an idea.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il fumetto — comic (strip).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for science fiction, then the Italian for essay.

You'll want to know: il fumetto.
il fumetto — "comic (strip)".
Da piccolo leggevo fumetti tutto il giorno. As a child I read comics all day.
Literally "a little puff of smoke": the speech balloon.

The word, taken apart.
fumetto is built on fumo "smoke" (EtymDB), inherited from Latin fūmus, with -etto: the speech balloon looks like a puff of smoke.

Lesson 5 of 5.
il poeta — poet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for essay, then the Italian for comic.

You'll want to know: il poeta.
il poeta — "poet".
Il poeta legge le sue poesie in piazza. The poet reads his poems in the square.
Masculine but ending in -a: il poeta, i poeti; a woman is la poetessa.

The word, taken apart.
poeta is a learned borrowing from Latin poēta (EtymDB), from Greek poiētḗs "a maker", from poieîn "to make".
