Hindi, chapter 27: Good Night.
2 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 2.
शुभ रात्रि (śubh rātri) — "good night," the formal doublet in action.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Hindi keeps two versions of "night" side by side — one for everyday speech, one for set phrases. Here's where the formal one gets used.

You'll want to know — शुभ रात्रि — "auspicious night".
शुभ रात्रि (śubh rātri) — "good night" — literally "auspicious night." Notice: this uses रात्रि (rātri), the very Sanskrit word last lesson's raat descended from — but here, kept in its full, unworn Sanskrit form. This is a classic tatsama/tadbhava doublet: rātri (tatsama, a direct, unchanged Sanskrit borrowing) sits alongside raat (tadbhava, the same word worn down by centuries of ordinary sound change) — Hindi keeps both, one for everyday speech, one for set formal phrases like this one.

You'll want to know — शुभ — "auspicious," from a root meaning "to shine".
शुभ (śubh) — "auspicious, good, fortunate" — comes from the Sanskrit verbal root शुभ् (śubh), whose core sense is "to be beautiful, splendid." This traces to a well-sourced Proto-Indo-European root, ḱewbʰ- ("to be beautiful"). Some scholars propose a further, more speculative connection beyond that — to (s)kewh₁- ("to perceive") or ḱew- ("to shine") — but that deeper link, not the ḱewbʰ- root itself, is the genuinely uncertain part. Either way, the underlying idea is a familiar one: auspiciousness imagined as a kind of radiance or beauty — something good "shines."

Lesson 2 of 2.
ट (ṭa) — one run, then the headline.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Warm-up.
[pause 1 second]
Before the new one: फ — how many pen lifts did it take?
[pause 2 seconds]

Script you'll notice: ट.
ट — ṭa.
What it is made of:
a descending central stem joined to a counterclockwise open round body.
the top shirorekhā.
The word open is doing real work there. The round part comes back up toward the stem and stops short of meeting it, leaving a gap at the upper right. Keep that gap when you draw it.

Script: the tongue goes back.
ट (ṭa) is a retroflex: the tongue curls back and taps the roof of the mouth, further back than it goes for त. Two different letters for what an English ear hears as one t, and Hindi uses both.
Words that were yours long before this page:
रोटी — bread.
घंटा — an hour.

Writing: ट.
1. start at the central stem's headline junction, descend, turn left through the upper shoulder, and sweep counterclockwise around the open round body to its up-right tip without lifting.
2. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 1. Everything below the headline is one unbroken run, and only ढ is built the same way.
Verified two-stroke teaching form fitted to the bundled printed outline; everyday handwriting may narrow or simplify the curve differently.
This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — handwriting here is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Opiaterein, ‘Deva-ट (ṭa)-order.gif’, strokes 1–2, Wikimedia Commons, 10 May 2009.
