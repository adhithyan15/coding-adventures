# laetus eram — how it felt

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C69-AFFECTUS-04, LA-LEX-C69-AFFECTUS-05, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "happy", then "sad". (*Laetus. Trīstis.*) Then "I was", the
open past. (*Eram.*)

## Grammar Lens: the feeling agrees with who felt it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-GRAMMAR-C287-AFFECTUS-01]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C212-QUAL212-03, LA-LEX-C241-QUAL241-03, LA-LEX-C148-QUAL148-03] -->

A feeling in a story is a state, so it takes the imperfect, and the
adjective matches the person who felt it:

> **Laetus eram.** — I was happy (a man speaking).
> **Laeta eram.** — I was happy (a woman speaking).
> **Sollicitī erāmus.** — We were worried.
> **Trīstis erat.** — He was sad, or she was: *trīstis* serves both.

[PAUSE 2s] *Anxius*, *sollicitus* and *īrātus* follow *laetus*: *-us* for a
man, *-a* for a woman, *-ī* for several. *Trīstis* has one form for a man
and a woman, and *trīstēs* for several.

## Why it's said this way: the cause, with quod
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C284-QUOD-CAUSA-02, LA-LEX-C78-VERBA4-05, LA-LEX-C78-VERBA4-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The cause of a feeling takes the *quod* you met as a reader,
and here both verbs are scene:

> **Laeta eram, quod caelum serēnum erat.** — I was happy, because the sky
> was clear.
> **Trīstis eram, quod pluēbat.** — I was sad, because it was raining.

[PAUSE 2s] Latin can also put the feeling in a verb: *gaudēbam*, "I was
glad", beside *laetus eram*; *timēbam*, "I was afraid". The adjective
describes the person; the verb tells what the person was doing inside.

## The word, taken apart: anxius and sollicitus
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-LEX-C212-QUAL212-03, LA-LEX-C241-QUAL241-03] -->

Two of these feelings are bodily, and their own lessons said how. **anxius**
is built on *angō*, "to squeeze, to choke" (Lewis & Short): worry as a
tight throat. **sollicitus** is built from *sollus*, "whole", and *cieō*,
"to stir up" (Lewis & Short): stirred through and through. English took
them over as *anxious* and *solicitous*.
