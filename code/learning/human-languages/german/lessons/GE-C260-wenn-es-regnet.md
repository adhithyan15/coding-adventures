# wenn es regnet — a real condition

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WETTER-02, GE-GRAMMAR-WENN-FALLS-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "it's raining". (*Es regnet.*) Then "if you have time".
(*Wenn du Zeit hast.*)

## Grammar Lens: the present, for tomorrow too
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-WENN-PRAESENS-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-MORGEN-TOMORROW-01, GE-LEX-C111-FAHRT111-01, GE-LEX-BLEIBEN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-WEATHER-SEIN-04] -->

A real condition is one that may well come true. German puts both halves
in the present, even when they are about tomorrow:

> *Wenn es morgen regnet, fahren wir nicht.* — If it rains tomorrow, we
> won't go.
> *Wenn ich müde bin, nehme ich ein Taxi.* — If I'm tired, I take a taxi.

English does the same in the *if* half: "if it rains". German usually
goes one step further and keeps the other half in the present as well,
where English would say "we won't go". The word *morgen* already tells you it is
tomorrow.

## Why it's said this way: verb, comma, verb
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-KOMMA-VERB-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-SECOND-02, GE-GRAMMAR-ALS-EINMAL-01, GE-LEX-DANN-01, GE-GRAMMAR-DANN-VERB-ZWEI-02] -->

You heard it first with *als*. When the *wenn* clause comes first, it fills
the first place of the whole sentence, so the main verb comes next, right
after the comma:

> *Wenn es morgen* **regnet**, **fahren** *wir nicht.*

Two verbs side by side, one ending the condition, one opening the result.

Turn it round, and each clause keeps its own order:

> *Wir* **fahren** *nicht, wenn es morgen* **regnet**.

Some speakers add *dann* to the result. It takes the place after the
comma, and the verb still comes second: *Wenn es regnet, dann fahren wir
nicht.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WETTER-02] -->

**regnen** is built on **der Regen**, from Old High German *regan*, which
EtymDB 2.1 sets beside English *rain*: one inherited word. *Es regnet*
and "it rains" both need an empty *es*, *it*, because nobody does the
raining.
