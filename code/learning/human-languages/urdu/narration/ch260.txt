Lesson 1 of 5.
کوفتہ (koftā) — a meatball.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for fine white flour, then the Urdu for semolina.

You'll want to know: کوفتہ.
کوفتہ — koftā — "a meatball".

The word, taken apart.
کوفتہ (koftā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian kōfta, "pounded". It is the past participle of kōftan, "to pound".

Lesson 3 of 5.
کڑھی (kaṛhī) — kadhi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a meatball, then the Urdu for a tikka.

You'll want to know: کڑھی.
کڑھی — kaṛhī — "kadhi".

The word, taken apart.
کڑھی (kaṛhī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kvathita, "boiled" (CDIAL 3637). Sanskrit already used its feminine for a spiced decoction made with milk.

Lesson 5 of 5.
گلاب جامن (gulāb jāmun) — a gulab jamun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for kadhi, then the Urdu for a jalebi.

You'll want to know: گلاب جامن.
گلاب جامن — gulāb jāmun — "a gulab jamun".

The phrase, taken apart.
گلاب جامن (gulāb jāmun) is "rose-water jāmun". gulāb, "rose-water", is Persian, from gul, "rose", and āb, "water"; jāmun, the dark jambolan fruit the sweet resembles, is listed by CDIAL under jambūna, a form beside Sanskrit jambula (CDIAL 5136).
