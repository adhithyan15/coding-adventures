# ಓದಲು (ōdalu) — in order to read

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-LEX-C73-ABLE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: what does *tumbā* do to an adjective?

## You'll want to know: ಓದಲು
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-LEX-C73-ABLE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**ಓದಲು** (*ōdalu*) — "to read", in the sense of "in order to read".

It is ಓದು ("to read") with **-ಅಲು** (*-alu*) in place of its ending. That is the
whole change, and it turns a verb into a purpose.

**ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದಲು ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ** (*pustaka ōdalu hōguttēne*) — "I am going to read a
book", meaning I am going in order to read one.

## Grammar Lens: three endings, three ways to join two actions
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-GRAMMAR-C73-ABLE-03]; assesses=[] -->

The purpose comes before the verb it serves, like the when-clause did.
Kannada builds a sentence by stacking what leads up to the action in front of it
and putting the finished verb last, every time.

So you have now met three ways to hang one verb off another, and all three are
endings rather than words: **-ಇ** for "having done it", **-ಆಗ** for "at the time
of doing it", and **-ಅಲು** for "in order to do it". English needs *and*, *when*
and *to* for those three; Kannada needs three shapes of one verb.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

A reason to do something, said as part of the same sentence.
