Japanese, chapter 315: And Then.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
すると (suruto) — thereupon; at that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to decrease, then the Japanese for to think of.

You'll want to know: すると.
すると — suruto — "thereupon; at that".
すると、いぬが ほえました。 — suruto, inu ga hoemashita — "At that, the dog began to bark."

The word, taken apart.
すると (suruto) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

ところが (tokoroga) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: JMdict writes it with the kanji of tokoro, "place", and the particle ga, and it is usually written in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
みはる (miharu) — to keep watch; to guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for but, then the Japanese for here and there.

You'll want to know: みはる.
みはる — miharu — "to keep watch; to guard".
おまわりさんが みちを みはっています。 — omawarisan ga michi o mihatte imasu — "A police officer is keeping watch on the road."
Polite みはります, miharimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おまわりさんが えきを みはりました。, omawarisan ga eki o miharimashita.

The word, taken apart.
みはる (miharu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from mi, "seeing" (from miru), and haru, "to stretch" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
