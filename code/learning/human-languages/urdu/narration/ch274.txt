Urdu, chapter 274: If, Then, or Else.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
اگر … تو (agar … to) — if, then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "if". (agar.) Then "then I'll stay at home". (to maiṅ ghar par rahūṅ gā.) اگر opens a condition; تو opens what follows.

You'll want to know: اگر … تو.
اگر وقت ہے، agar vaqt hai, تو آئیے۔ to āiye. — "If you have time, come."
اگر (agar), "if", opens the condition; تو (to), "then", opens the result. In talk, تو often drops out.
اگر آپ چاہتے ہیں، agar āp chāhte haiṅ, تو ہم چلیں گے۔ to ham chaleṅ ge. — "If you like, we'll go."

You'll want to know: ورنہ, or else.
جلدی چلیے، jaldī chaliye, ورنہ گاڑی چھوٹ جائے گی۔ varnā gāṛī chhūṭ jāe gī. — "Hurry, or we'll miss the train."
ورنہ (varnā), "otherwise", turns the condition round: do this, or that will happen.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If you have time, come" — agar vaqt hai, to āiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you have time, sit down" — agar vaqt hai, to baiṭhiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If you have time, stay" — agar vaqt hai, to rukiye]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Invite someone politely: if you have time, come. Then warn them: hurry, or we'll miss the train.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which word opens the condition? (اگر.) Which opens the result? (تو.) "Or else"? (ورنہ.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
اگر بارش ہوئی (agar bārish huī) — if it rains.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "it will rain". (bārish ho gī.) Then "if you have time, come". (agar vaqt hai, to āiye.) Now a condition about tomorrow.

You'll want to know: the plain past after اگر.
اگر بارش ہوئی، agar bārish huī, تو ہم گھر پر رہیں گے۔ to ham ghar par raheṅ ge. — "If it rains, we'll stay at home."
ہوئی (huī) is the plain past, but the rain has not come: Urdu pictures it as already there, and the result is in the future.
اگر گاڑی چھوٹ گئی، agar gāṛī chhūṭ gaī, تو ہم بس سے جائیں گے۔ to ham bas se jāeṅ ge. — "If we miss the train, we'll go by bus."

You'll want to know: the future after اگر.
اگر وہ کل آئے گی، agar voh kal āe gī, تو ہم بازار جائیں گے۔ to ham bāzār jāeṅ ge. — "If she comes tomorrow, we'll go to the market."
The future can stand in both halves. Either way, the result is what will happen.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, we'll stay at home" — agar bārish huī, to ham ghar par raheṅ ge]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, we'll go by bus" — agar bārish huī, to ham bas se jāeṅ ge]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "If it rains, we'll take a taxi" — agar bārish huī, to ham ṭaiksī leṅ ge]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say: if it rains, we'll stay at home. Then: if she comes tomorrow, we'll go to the market.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains"? (اگر بارش ہوئی (agar bārish huī).) Has it rained? (No: Urdu pictures it.) "If she comes tomorrow"? (اگر وہ کل آئے گی.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
شرط (shart) — a condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "if she comes tomorrow, we'll go". (agar voh kal āe gī, to ham jāeṅ ge.) Her coming is the condition.

You'll want to know: شرط.
شرط — shart — "a condition". It is feminine.
ایک شرط ہے۔ ek shart hai. — "There's one condition."
میں ایک شرط پر آؤں گا۔ maiṅ ek shart par āūṅ gā. — "I'll come on one condition."
شرط یہ ہے کہ آپ بھی آئیں گے۔ shart yih hai ki āp bhī āeṅ ge. — "The condition is that you'll come too."

The word, taken apart.
شرط (shart) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic sharṭ, "a condition".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: shart, then ek shart par]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'll go on one condition" — maiṅ ek shart par jāūṅ gā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does شرط (shart) mean? ("A condition".) "On one condition"? (ایک شرط (shart) پر.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Review — if, then, or else.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "the bus is cheaper than a taxi" and "this is the best". (bas ṭaiksī se sastī hai, yih sab se achchhā hai.) Then "I've lived here for two years". (maiṅ do sāl se yahāṅ rahtā hūṅ.)

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: I hope she'll come. If she comes tomorrow, we'll go to the market.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If you have time, come.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: Hurry, or we'll miss the train.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If it rains, we'll stay at home.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: If we miss the train, we'll go by bus.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: I'll come on one condition.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"If it rains"? (اگر بارش ہوئی (agar bārish huī).) Now the other way round: what does ورنہ warn about? (What will happen if you don't.) In اگر وہ کل آئے گی, which time is the verb in? (The future.)
