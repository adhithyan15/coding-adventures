# la lectura — the reading

## You'll want to know: la lectura
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-04]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04] -->

**la lectura** — the reading.

> El servicio ya no es solo de **lectura**.

You have met this word once without being given it. [La
factura](./ES-C441-factura.md) put it in its own table — *leer, to read |
lectura, a reading* — as an example of the **-ura** ending, then went back to
invoices. Here it is as yours.

*Un club de lectura* — a book club. *La lectura del contador* — the meter
reading.

## Grammar Lens: three things one word covers
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-04, ES-LEX-LEER-05] -->

English splits these with different words. Spanish keeps one.

| what is meant | English reaches for |
|---|---|
| the activity | reading |
| the thing read | a text, a book |
| the interpretation | a reading of |

All three are *la lectura*, and you have already used the third without
noticing. When this book asked you for *the two readings of* **como**, it was
asking for two **lecturas** — two ways the sentence can be taken.

So the word is not only about books. Anything you can take a meaning from has
a *lectura*, including a meter and an argument.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C472-ENCUESTA-04, ES-LEX-C441-TRATO-04] -->

*Leer* is Latin **legere**, and *-ura* is the ending *la factura* gave you:
the thing an action produces. A *lectura* is what reading makes.

*Legere* first meant **to gather**, and the reading sense came later — you
gather letters the way you gather grain. That gathering is still visible in
English **collect**, **select** and **elect**, all of them picking things out,
and in **legible**, able to be gathered, and **lecture**, a reading aloud.

So the ending was predictable and the root was already yours. The only new
thing here is permission to use the word.
