Latin, chapter 168: To fold, To own, To make ready in advance, To lend, To propose.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
plicō, plicāre — to fold.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to calculate, then the Latin for to knock.

You'll want to know: plicō, plicāre.
plicō, plicāre — "to fold".

The word, taken apart.
plicō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European plek-, "to fold, to plait" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek plekō, "to plait", is a cousin (Lewis & Short). English ply and complicate come from its family.

Lesson 2 of 5.
possideō, possidēre — to own, to possess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to knock, then the Latin for to fold.

You'll want to know: possideō, possidēre.
possideō, possidēre — "to own, to possess".

The word, taken apart.
possideō is usually explained as potis, "master, able", with sedeō, "to sit" (EtymDB 2.1): to sit as master of something, so to own.

Lesson 4 of 5.
commodō, commodāre — to lend.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to own, then the Latin for to make ready in advance.

You'll want to know: commodō, commodāre.
commodō, commodāre — "to lend".

The word, taken apart.
commodō is built on commodus, "fitting, convenient" (Lewis & Short), itself con-, "with", and modus, "a measure": to make something available, to lend.
