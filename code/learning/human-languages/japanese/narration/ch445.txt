Japanese, chapter 445: Aches and Pains.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ずつう (zutsū) — a headache.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for one's weight, then the Japanese for snoring.

You'll want to know: ずつう.
ずつう — zutsū — "a headache".
けさから ずつうが します。 — kesa kara zutsū ga shimasu — "I have had a headache since this morning."

The word, taken apart.
ずつう (zutsū) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are zu, "head" (Middle Chinese duw) and tsū, "pain" (thuwngH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

The word, taken apart.
ずきずき (zukizuki) is a native Japanese mimetic word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 4 of 5.
はきけ (hakike) — nausea; feeling sick.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a stomach ache, then the Japanese for throbbing.

You'll want to know: はきけ.
はきけ — hakike — "nausea; feeling sick".
ふねで はきけが しました。 — fune de hakike ga shimashita — "I felt sick on the boat."

The word, taken apart.
はきけ (hakike) is a mixed word: native haki, "vomiting", from haku, joined to Sino-Japanese ke, "spirit, feeling" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), a reading borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
