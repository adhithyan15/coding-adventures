Lesson 1 of 5.
радость (rádost) — joy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for hunger, then the Russian for thirst.

You'll want to know: радость.
радость (rádost) — "joy".

The word, taken apart.
радость (rádost) is рад, "glad", with -ость, the suffix that makes nouns of quality. рад goes back to Proto-Slavic radŭ; Vasmer finds a link with Old English rōt, "cheerful", likely, but reports a rival account.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: страх.
страх (strakh) — "fear".

The word, taken apart.
страх (strakh) from Proto-Slavic straxŭ — inherited. Its deeper origin is not settled: one account Vasmer reports gives it a first sense of "numbness" and compares Lithuanian stregti, "to freeze stiff".

You'll want to know: гнев.

The word, taken apart.
гнев (gnev) from Proto-Slavic gněvŭ — inherited. Vasmer thinks it probably belongs with гнить, "to rot", so the first sense would have been "rot, poison"; others start from a sense "to burn".

Lesson 4 of 5.
грусть (grust) — sadness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for fear, then the Russian for anger.

You'll want to know: грусть.
грусть (grust) — "sadness".

The word, taken apart.
грусть (grust) goes with the verb грустить, "to be sad". Vasmer reports the usual link with груда, "a heap", and with Lithuanian graudùs, "brittle; sad, moving", from a root "to crush" (Lithuanian grūsti, "to pound").

Lesson 5 of 5.
усталость (ustálost) — tiredness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for anger, then the Russian for sadness.

You'll want to know: усталость.
усталость (ustálost) — "tiredness".

The word, taken apart.
усталость (ustálost) is устать, "to get tired", with -ость, the suffix for nouns of quality. устать is у- with стать, "to stand, to become" (Vasmer); Old Church Slavonic уставати meant "to cease": a tired person has come to a stop.
