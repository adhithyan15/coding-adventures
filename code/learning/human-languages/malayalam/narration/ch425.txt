Malayalam, chapter 425: Protest.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
പ്രതിഷേധം (pratiṣēdhaṁ) — a protest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for authority, then the Malayalam for a leader.

You'll want to know: പ്രതിഷേധം.
പ്രതിഷേധം — pratiṣēdhaṁ — "a protest".
വില കൂടിയതിനെതിരെ നഗരത്തിൽ പ്രതിഷേധം നടന്നു. vila kūṭiyatinetire nagarattil pratiṣēdhaṁ naṭannu. There was a protest in the city against the price rise.
Against something: -ന് എതിരെ പ്രതിഷേധം (pratiṣēdhaṁ).

The word, taken apart.
പ്രതിഷേധം (pratiṣēdhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit pratiṣedha, "keeping back, warding off, prohibition" (Monier-Williams). A protest pushes back against a decision.

Lesson 2 of 5.
പ്രതിഷേധിക്കുക (pratiṣēdhikkuka) — to protest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a leader, then the Malayalam for a protest.

You'll want to know: പ്രതിഷേധിക്കുക.
പ്രതിഷേധിക്കുക — pratiṣēdhikkuka — "to protest".
ഫീസ് കൂട്ടിയതിൽ വിദ്യാർത്ഥികൾ പ്രതിഷേധിച്ചു. phīsŭ kūṭṭiyatil vidyārtthikaḷ pratiṣēdhiccu. The students protested against the fee increase.
Past: പ്രതിഷേധിച്ചു, pratiṣēdhiccu.
The cause takes -ഇൽ: ഫീസ് കൂട്ടിയതിൽ, over raising the fee.

The word, taken apart.
പ്രതിഷേധിക്കുക (pratiṣēdhikkuka) is built from Sanskrit pratiṣedha (Monier-Williams), with the Malayalam verb ending -ിക്കുക. It is the verb of പ്രതിഷേധം (pratiṣēdhaṁ).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a protest, then the Malayalam for to protest.

You'll want to know: സമരം.
സമരം — samaraṁ — "a strike; a struggle".
തൊഴിലാളികൾ ഇന്ന് മുതൽ സമരം തുടങ്ങി. toḻilāḷikaḷ innŭ mutal samaraṁ tuṭaṅṅi. The workers began a strike from today.
To strike is സമരം (samaraṁ) ചെയ്യുക.

The word, taken apart.
സമരം (samaraṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit samara, "a coming together; a hostile encounter, a struggle" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ഹർത്താൽ (harttāl) — a general strike.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for to protest, then the Malayalam for a strike.

You'll want to know: ഹർത്താൽ.
ഹർത്താൽ — harttāl — "a general strike".
ഹർത്താൽ കാരണം കടകൾ എല്ലാം അടഞ്ഞുകിടന്നു. harttāl kāraṇaṁ kaṭakaḷ ellāṁ aṭaññukiṭannu. Because of the general strike, all the shops stayed shut.
On a ഹർത്താൽ (harttāl) day, shops close and buses stop.

The word, taken apart.
ഹർത്താൽ (harttāl) was borrowed from Hindustani haṛtāl, "closing shops in mourning or protest", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit haṭṭa, "a market" (CDIAL 13944).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ജാഥ (jātha) — a march; a procession.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a strike, then the Malayalam for a general strike.

You'll want to know: ജാഥ.
ജാഥ — jātha — "a march; a procession".
കുട്ടികൾ ഗ്രാമത്തിലൂടെ ഒരു ജാഥ നടത്തി. kuṭṭikaḷ grāmattilūṭe oru jātha naṭatti. The children held a march through the village.
A march is നടത്തുക, organised.

The word, taken apart.
ജാഥ (jātha) is usually traced to Hindustani jathā, "a band, a group of people"; its history before that is not settled.
