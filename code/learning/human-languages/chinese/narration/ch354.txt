Lesson 1 of 6.
tíwèn — to put a question (in class or a meeting).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to argue back, then the Mandarin for to share someone's view.

You'll want to know: tíwèn.
tíwèn — "to put a question (in class or a meeting)".
yǒu wèntí zuìhǎo xiàkè yǐhòu tíwèn — If you have a question, it's best to ask after class.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tíwèn is built from tí and 问 'to ask', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dej and mjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to carry by the hand' and the second 'to inquire'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
话题 (huàtí) — topic of conversation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to share someone's view, then the Mandarin for to put a question.

You'll want to know: 话题.
话题 — huàtí — "topic of conversation".
我觉得这个话题很有意思 wǒ juéde zhège huàtí hěn yǒu yìsi — I think this is an interesting topic.
The new character, in pieces: 题 (tí) — fifteen strokes in two pieces: on the left 是 shì, nine strokes, its last stroke sweeping out long under the right side; on the right the page piece, six strokes: a bar, a short left-falling stroke, a narrow box, then a left-falling stroke and a dot under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
话题 (huàtí) is built from 话 'talk' and 题 (tí) 'a heading', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hwaejH and dej (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'speech' and the second 'the title of a book' and, quoting an older dictionary, 'the forehead'.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — shì, then the page piece.
题 (tí).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 是, nine strokes, narrower than usual: the sun box on top, a long horizontal, then the lower part, whose last stroke sweeps out long to the right, under the right piece.
The right piece, the page piece, six strokes: a horizontal, a short stroke falling to the left, a vertical, a horizontal that turns down, a left-falling stroke inside, then a dot at the foot.
9 + 6 = fifteen, and the first nine your hand already knows.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 题 (tí), all fifteen, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces in 题 (tí)? (Two.) What is the left piece? (是.) What does its last stroke do? (It sweeps out long, under the page piece.)

You'll want to know: tímù.
tímù — "title; question on a test".
wǒ kàn bu dǒng zhège tímù — I can't make sense of this question.

The word, taken apart.
tímù is built from 题 (tí) 'a heading' and mù 'the eye', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dej and mjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the title of a book' and, quoting an older dictionary, the second 'the human eye'.
