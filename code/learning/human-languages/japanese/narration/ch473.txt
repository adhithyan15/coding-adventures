Japanese, chapter 473: Simmering and Melting.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
にこむ (nikomu) — to stew; to simmer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for wheat flour, then the Japanese for soybeans.

You'll want to know: にこむ.
にこむ — nikomu — "to stew; to simmer".
やさいを ながく にこんで ください。 — yasai o nagaku nikonde kudasai — "Please simmer the vegetables for a long time."
Polite にこみます, nikomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ははが にくを にこみました。, haha ga niku o nikomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
にこむ (nikomu) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: ni, "boiling (niru)" and komu, "going in" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for soybeans, then the Japanese for to stew.

You'll want to know: とかす.
とかす — tokasu — "to melt something; to dissolve".
なべで さとうを とかしました。 — nabe de satō o tokashimashita — "I melted the sugar in a pot."
Polite とかします, tokashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): ちちが こおりを とかしました。, chichi ga kōri o tokashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とかす (tokasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: the transitive partner of tokeru, "to melt", and its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
