## Assessment: the pre-A1 task inventory, beside the richest real Indic ladder

Telugu's pre-A1 four-skill task inventory is checked in, with the assessment
specification it cites. `reading-reach` now measures the track — the chapter-83
passage's 25 words against a 1-word ceiling, **3/3 parts in reach**, with a floor
committed. Before this the track was not in the table at all.

**Telugu has the closest thing to a four-skill ladder of any Indic track here,
and the spec says so plainly.** SiliconAndhra ManaBadi, affiliated to Potti
Sreeramulu Telugu University, runs eight named levels — BalaBadi I, BalaBadi II,
Pravesam, Prasunam, Prakasam, Pramodam, Prabhasam, Prabodham — awarding a Junior
Certificate at Prakasam and a Senior Certificate at Prabhasam, each earned by an
annual PSTU examination that tests **reading, writing, listening, speaking and
comprehension**: five components, one more than this contract has.

It is still not the target, for reasons worth stating rather than waving at: it
is a **school ladder for heritage learners** whose rungs are years of attendance;
it publishes **no per-skill pass rule** and no CEFR mapping; and PSTU's own
certificates require *prior* Telugu proficiency to enter, so they begin above
where this book begins. The one thing missing is the published per-skill rule,
and that is the one thing a contract cannot borrow.

**What the writing paper scores** is the **వత్తు**, the subscript consonant.
Telugu builds a conjunct by hanging a reduced second consonant *below* the base
letter rather than beside it, so a line occupies three tiers and a reader's eye
has to look down as well as along — an error with no Latin-script analogue, and
the corpus builds 1,683 of them. Beside it: the **తలకట్టు**, the headstroke that
makes a line of Telugu read as a line at all.

**The writing ramp, measured.** 49 script lessons and **not one writing-stage
directive** in any of them, while the pre-A1 paper requires delayed recall and
dictation. Note the letters themselves are in good order — 64 taught against 64
shown, none never-taught — so what is missing is the staging of the hand, not
the script.
