Hindi, chapter 123: How's It Going?.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
क्या हाल है? (kyā hāl hai?) — how's it going?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for permission to go, then the Hindi for I have to go.

You'll want to know: क्या हाल है?
क्या हाल है? (kyā hāl hai?) — "how's it going?", "how are things?".
हाल is "condition, state". Friendlier than आप कैसे हैं?

The word, taken apart.
क्या हाल है? (kyā hāl hai?) is "what is the state?". hāl comes from Arabic ḥāl, "state, condition", which reached Hindi through Persian; its root is ḥ-w-l (Lane).

What you've built.
An everyday opener.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kyā hāl hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kyā hāl hai?, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: ask kyā hāl hai?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for permission to go, then the Hindi for I have to go, then say kyā hāl hai? again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does क्या हाल है? (kyā hāl hai?) mean? ("How's it going?".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
सब ठीक? (sab ṭhīk?) — all well?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for I have to go, then the Hindi for how's it going.

You'll want to know: सब ठीक?
सब ठीक? (sab ṭhīk?) — "all well?", "everything fine?".
सब is "all", and ठीक is the "fine" you know. The answer: हाँ, सब ठीक.

The word, taken apart.
सब ठीक? (sab ṭhīk?) is "all fine?". sab, "all", comes from Sanskrit sarva, "all, whole" (CDIAL 13276).

What you've built.
A two-word greeting and its answer.

Lesson 3 of 5.
बहुत दिन हो गए (bahut din ho gae) — it's been a long time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for how's it going, then the Hindi for all well.

You'll want to know: बहुत दिन हो गए.
बहुत दिन हो गए (bahut din ho gae) — "it's been a long time", literally "many days have become".
बहुत and दिन are both yours already.

The word, taken apart.
बहुत दिन हो गए (bahut din ho gae) is "many days have become". bahut comes from Sanskrit bahutva, "abundance" (CDIAL 9190), and din from dina, "day" (CDIAL 6328).

What you've built.
A greeting for someone you have not seen in a while.

Lesson 4 of 5.
क्या चल रहा है? (kyā cal rahā hai?) — what's going on?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for all well, then the Hindi for it's been a long time.

You'll want to know: क्या चल रहा है?
क्या चल रहा है? (kyā cal rahā hai?) — "what's going on?", "what's up?".
It is चलना, "to go, to move", in the right-now form: what is moving in your life?

The word, taken apart.
क्या चल रहा है? (kyā cal rahā hai?) is "what is moving?". calnā, "to move, go", comes from Sanskrit calati, "goes away" (CDIAL 4715).

What you've built.

Lesson 5 of 5.
खाना खाया? (khānā khāyā?) — have you eaten?

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for it's been a long time, then the Hindi for what's going on.

You'll want to know: खाना खाया?
खाना खाया? (khānā khāyā?) — "have you eaten?".
खाना is both "food" and "to eat": you ate the eating. Around a mealtime it is a greeting and a kindness.

The word, taken apart.
खाना खाया? (khānā khāyā?) is "food, eaten?". The noun khānā, "food", comes from Sanskrit khādana, "food" (CDIAL 3867), and the verb from khādati, "chews, eats" (CDIAL 3865).

What you've built.
How's it going, all well, long time, what's up, have you eaten.
