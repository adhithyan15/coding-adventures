Japanese, chapter 376: Fast Trains and Local Lines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しんかんせん (shinkansen) — the bullet train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to ride past one's stop, then the Japanese for to get on board.

You'll want to know: しんかんせん.
しんかんせん — shinkansen — "the bullet train".
しんかんせんは ひこうきより べんりです。 — shinkansen wa hikōki yori benri desu — "The bullet train is more convenient than the plane."

The word, taken apart.
しんかんせん (shinkansen) is Sino-Japanese (kango): three Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shin, "new" (Middle Chinese sin), kan, "main part" (kanH) and sen, "line" (sjenH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
とばす (tobasu) — to skip; to let fly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a private railway, then the Japanese for a route.

You'll want to know: とばす.
とばす — tobasu — "to skip; to let fly".
とっきゅうは ちいさい えきを とばします。 — tokkyū wa chiisai eki o tobashimasu — "The limited express skips the small stations."
Polite とばします, tobashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): きゅうこうが えきを とばしました。, kyūkō ga eki o tobashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とばす (tobasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict). It is the partner of tobu, "to fly", that makes something fly or skip (JMdict).
