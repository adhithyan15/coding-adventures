Portuguese, chapter 370: Caso contrário.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a menos que — unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for in case, then the Portuguese for depending on.

You'll want to know: a menos que.
a menos que — "unless".
Vamos a pé, a menos que chova. We will walk, unless it rains.
Takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
a menos que is built from a, menos "less", inherited from Latin minus "less" (Lewis & Short), and que.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a não ser que — except if, unless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for depending on, then the Portuguese for unless.

You'll want to know: a não ser que.
a não ser que — "except if, unless".
Amanhã não trabalho, a não ser que o chefe me chame. Tomorrow I am not working, unless the boss calls me.
Takes the subjunctive; it means the same as a menos que.

The word, taken apart.
a não ser que is built from não "not", inherited from Latin nōn (Lewis & Short), ser "to be", and que: "if it is not that".

Lesson 3 of 5.
senão — otherwise, or else; but (rather).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for unless, then the Portuguese for except if.

You'll want to know: senão.
senão — "otherwise, or else; but (rather)".
Temos de sair já, senão perdemos o comboio. We have to leave now, or else we miss the train.
One word for or else; se não in two words means if not: se não chover.

The word, taken apart.
senão is built from se "if", from Latin sī, and não "not", from Latin nōn (EtymDB): "if not, then".

Lesson 4 of 5.
eventual — possible, potential (that may happen).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for except if, then the Portuguese for otherwise.

You'll want to know: eventual.
eventual — "possible, potential (that may happen)".
Levo um guarda-chuva para uma eventual chuva. I am taking an umbrella in case of rain.
A false friend: eventual means possible, not final.

The word, taken apart.
eventual is a learned formation on Latin ēventus "an occurrence, an event" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from ēvenīre "to come out, to happen", on venīre "to come".

Lesson 5 of 5.
eventualmente — possibly, if need be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for otherwise, then the Portuguese for possible.

You'll want to know: eventualmente.
eventualmente — "possibly, if need be".
Eventualmente, podemos jantar mais tarde. If need be, we can have dinner later.
A false friend: eventually is finalmente or por fim.

The word, taken apart.
eventualmente is built on eventual "possible" with the adverb ending -mente; eventual rests on Latin ēventus "an occurrence" (Lewis & Short).
