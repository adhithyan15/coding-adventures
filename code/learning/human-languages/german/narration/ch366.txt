German, chapter 366: Theorie und Praxis.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Hypothese — the hypothesis.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for apart from that, then the German for if need be.

You'll want to know: die Hypothese.
die Hypothese — "the hypothesis".
Die Forscherin will ihre Hypothese mit einem Experiment prüfen. The researcher wants to test her hypothesis with an experiment.
Plural: die Hypothesen. The stress is on the third syllable: hy-po-THE-se.

The word, taken apart.
Hypothese is a learned borrowing from Late Latin hypothesis, from Greek hypóthesis "a base, the basis of an argument" (EtymDB), built from hypó "under" and thésis "a setting down": what you put underneath.

Theoretisch kann man die Strecke in zwei Stunden fahren. In theory you can drive the route in two hours.
Theoretisch …, praktisch … — in theory …, in practice …

Lesson 3 of 5.
unter Umständen — under certain circumstances, possibly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the hypothesis, then the German for in theory.

You'll want to know: unter Umständen.
unter Umständen — "under certain circumstances, possibly".
Unter Umständen dauert die Reparatur eine Woche. Under certain circumstances the repair may take a week.
Written short as u. U. Der Umstand — the circumstance; Umstände machen — to cause trouble.

The word, taken apart.
Umstand joins um "around" to Stand "standing, position" (EtymDB). It is usually explained as a rendering of Latin circumstantia "a standing around", from circumstāre "to stand around" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
gegebenenfalls — if applicable, should the case arise.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for in theory, then the German for under certain circumstances.

You'll want to know: gegebenenfalls.
gegebenenfalls — "if applicable, should the case arise".
Gegebenenfalls rufe ich Sie morgen noch einmal an. If necessary, I will call you again tomorrow.
Written short as ggf., very common on forms. Formal.

The word, taken apart.
gegebenenfalls grew out of gegebenen Falls "in the given case", from gegeben, the participle of geben "to give", and Fall "case", Old High German val (EtymDB), with the adverb ending -s.

Lesson 5 of 5.
wenigstens — at least.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for under certain circumstances, then the German for if applicable.

You'll want to know: wenigstens.
wenigstens — "at least".
Es hat geregnet, aber wenigstens war es warm. It rained, but at least it was warm.
Close to mindestens, which mostly goes with numbers: mindestens drei Tage.

The word, taken apart.
wenigstens is built on the superlative of wenig "little", from Middle High German weinec (EtymDB), with the adverb ending -ens: "at the very least".
