German, chapter 474: Staunen und Neugier.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
staunen — to be amazed, to marvel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for silly, then the German for to tickle.

You'll want to know: staunen.
staunen — "to be amazed, to marvel".
Die Touristen staunten über die hohen Berge. The tourists marvelled at the high mountains.
Staunen über + accusative — to be amazed at.

The word, taken apart.
staunen came into standard German from Swiss German stūnen "to stiffen, to freeze up" (EtymDB): someone who is amazed stands still and stares.

What you've built.

Lesson 4 of 5.
die Neugier — curiosity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for astonished, then the German for confused.

You'll want to know: die Neugier.
die Neugier — "curiosity".
Aus Neugier öffnete er das Paket. Out of curiosity, he opened the parcel.
No plural. Also die Neugierde. The adjective is neugierig — curious.

The word, taken apart.
Neugier is built on neu "new", inherited from Old High German niuwi, from Proto-Germanic niwjaz (EtymDB), and Gier "greed, craving": a craving for new things.

sprachlos is built on Sprache "speech", inherited from Old High German sprāhha, from Proto-Germanic sprēkijō (EtymDB), with -los "without".
