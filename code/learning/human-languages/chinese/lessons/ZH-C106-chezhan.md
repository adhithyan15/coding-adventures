## You'll want to know: 车站
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C106-STOP106-01]; assesses=[] -->

**车站** — *chēzhàn* — "station; stop".

> **车站在哪儿** *chēzhàn zài nǎr* — Where's the station?

The new character, in pieces: 站 — on the left a five-stroke piece read lì, 'to stand'; on the right a vertical with a short bar to its right, over 口. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**车站** (*chēzhàn*) is built from 车 'vehicle' and 站 'to stand', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *tsyhae* and *treamH* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second as an everyday word for standing alone. A place where vehicles stand is the usual explanation, but the step is not given there.
