Latin, chapter 66: Chest, Leg, Cheek, Nail, Healthy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
pectus, pectoris — the chest, the breast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for the forehead, then the Latin for a beard.

You'll want to know: pectus, pectoris.
pectus, pectoris — "the chest, the breast".

The word, taken apart.
pectus is probably inherited: EtymDB 2.1 traces it through Proto-Italic pektos, "chest", to a Proto-Indo-European peg-, "breast", a root with few other witnesses. English pectoral comes from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
unguis, unguis — a fingernail, a claw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a leg, then the Latin for a cheek.

You'll want to know: unguis, unguis.
unguis, unguis — "a fingernail, a claw".

The word, taken apart.
unguis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h3nogwh-, "nail, claw" (EtymDB 2.1 writes h3negh-, without the w that the Latin gu needs). Greek onyx, "claw, nail" (Lewis & Short), and English nail are cousins.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sānus, sāna, sānum — healthy, sound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cheek, then the Latin for a fingernail.

You'll want to know: sānus, sāna, sānum.
sānus, sāna, sānum — "healthy, sound". English sane: sound in mind.

The word, taken apart.
sānus has no secure origin. Lewis & Short compare Greek sōs, "safe and sound", and EtymDB 2.1 records a proposed Proto-Indo-European swan-, "healthy", but neither link is settled.
