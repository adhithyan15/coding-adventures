# கடை — a sign that wraps the letter

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C68-SHOP-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the page: you can already say *kaḍai* and *vilai*. Now read the first one.

## Script you'll notice: கடை — a sign that wraps the letter
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-SCRIPT-READ-KADAI-01]; assesses=[] -->

**கடை** is as short as a Tamil word gets: two letters, one sign, nothing new.

| piece | | |
|---|---|---|
| **க** | *ka* | the letter with its own built-in *a* |
| **டை** | *ṭai* | ட with the **ை** sign, the same one you read at the end of சட்டை |

> **க · டை → கடை**

Look at where the **ை** sits. It is drawn to the **left** of its letter but said **after** it,
which is the habit you first met on the ே of மேலே. Two signs now, both hanging in front of the
letter they belong to.

And notice what the same two letters do without the sign: **கடு**, **கடி** and **கடை** are
three different words with the same skeleton. In Tamil the sign is not decoration.
