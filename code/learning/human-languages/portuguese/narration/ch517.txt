Portuguese, chapter 517: Flautas e trompetes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a flauta — flute; recorder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for cello, then the Portuguese for harp.

You'll want to know: a flauta.
a flauta — "flute; recorder".
Na escola, todos aprendem a tocar flauta. At school, everyone learns to play the recorder.
Flauta de bisel — recorder; flauta transversal — flute.

The word, taken apart.
flauta is usually traced to Old Occitan flaut, a word usually taken to imitate the sound of blowing; the origin is not settled.

O vizinho toca trompete à noite. The neighbour plays the trumpet at night.
A trombeta is an older word for a trumpet.

The word, taken apart.
trompete is usually traced to French trompette, a diminutive of trompe "a horn", which goes back to a Germanic word.

o saxofone — "saxophone".
Ouvimos um solo de saxofone no bar. We heard a saxophone solo in the bar.
Often shortened to o sax.

The word, taken apart.
saxofone comes from French saxophone (EtymDB), built on the surname of its Belgian inventor and Greek phōnḗ "sound".

Lesson 4 of 5.
o acordeão — accordion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for trumpet, then the Portuguese for saxophone.

You'll want to know: o acordeão.
o acordeão — "accordion".
Nas festas da aldeia, o avô tocava acordeão. At the village fêtes, Grandpa played the accordion.
In Brazil: o acordeom or a sanfona.

The word, taken apart.
acordeão was borrowed from French accordéon (EtymDB), from German Akkordion, built on Akkord "a chord".

Lesson 5 of 5.
afinar — to tune.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for saxophone, then the Portuguese for accordion.

You'll want to know: afinar.
afinar — "to tune".
Antes do concerto, os músicos afinam os instrumentos. Before the concert, the musicians tune their instruments.
Desafinar — to sing or play out of tune.

The word, taken apart.
afinar is built from a- and fino "fine, thin": to make a sound fine and exact. Fino is usually traced to Latin fīnis "end" (EtymDB), but the path of meaning is not settled.
