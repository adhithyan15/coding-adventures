# ¡Felicidades! — the word you say, not the word you describe with

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-FELIZ, ES-ORTH-C428-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el guion bajo*. The symbols are finished; what follows is what
you say to people. Say *feliz*, then try to congratulate somebody with it — and
notice the adjective will not do it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C428-03]; assesses=[] -->

**¡Felicidades!** — "congratulations."

It is *felicidad*, "happiness," in the **plural** — literally *happinesses*.
Spanish wishes several at once, which is what a congratulation is: not a
description of a state but a handful of good wishes thrown at somebody.

The relationship to *feliz* is visible: *feliz* → *felicidad* → *felicidades*.
An adjective, the noun made from it, and that noun pluralised into an act.

## Grammar Lens: which one on which occasion
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C428-03, ES-LEX-FELIZ] -->

Spanish has two congratulation words and they are not interchangeable:

| word | for |
|---|---|
| **¡Felicidades!** | a birthday, a saint's day, a wedding — an **occasion** |
| ¡Felicitaciones! | an exam passed, a job won — an **achievement** |

*Felicidades* is the one an A1 paper wants, because A1 life is birthdays rather
than promotions. In much of Latin America *felicitaciones* is the commoner of
the two; in Spain *felicidades* covers nearly everything.

And note the marks: an exclamation in Spanish opens with **¡** and closes with
**!**, the same doubling you learned for questions.
