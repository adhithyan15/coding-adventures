# читал or прочитал — the reading, and the book read through

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-L-GENDER-01, RU-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-PARTNER, RU-LEX-PISAT] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name the partners you met with the first verbs: *читать,
прочитать*; *писать, написать*. One is the doing, the other the deed done.
In the present only the doing is used. In the past both are, and the
choice decides the story.

## Grammar Lens: the doing, or the deed done
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-IMPERFECTIVE-PROCESS-01, RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-PERFECTIVE-RESULT-01]; assesses=[RU-LEX-CHITAT, RU-LEX-C52-SCHOOL-01, RU-LEX-C69-TIME2-02] -->

> **Вчера я читал книгу.** — *vcherá ya chitál knígu* — Yesterday I was
> reading a book.
> **Вчера я прочитал книгу.** — *vcherá ya prochitál knígu* — Yesterday I
> read the book, to the end.

The **imperfective** shows the reading going on, and says nothing about
the last page. The **perfective** sees the deed whole: finished, with a
result. The book is read.

## Grammar Lens: a habit, and a bare fact
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-IMPERFECTIVE-PROCESS-01, RU-LEX-C85-TIME85-05, RU-LEX-C82-DEF82-02, RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-LI-PLURAL-01, RU-LEX-C192-VESHCH192-01] -->

The imperfective has two more jobs. A **habit**: **Я часто читал
газету.** — *ya chásto chitál gazétu* — I often read the paper. And the
**bare fact** that something happened at all:

> **— Ты читал эту книгу? — Да, читал.** — *ty chitál étu knígu? da,
> chitál* — Have you read this book? Yes, I have.

## Why it's said this way: more pairs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-PAST-PERFECTIVE-RESULT-01, RU-LEX-PISAT, RU-LEX-C135-ACT135-03, RU-LEX-C113-THINGS113-02, RU-LEX-C255-VESHCH255-04] -->

> **Мы посмотрели фильм.** — *my posmotréli film* — We watched the film,
> all of it.
> **Я писал сообщение, но не написал.** — *ya pisál soobshchéniye, no ne
> napisál* — I was writing a message, but did not get it written.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-VIDET] -->

Russian grammar calls aspect **вид**, "a look, a view", which EtymDB 2.1
takes through Proto-Slavic *\*vidŭ* to PIE *\*weyd-*, "to see, to know":
the root of **видеть** and of English *wit*. Two aspects, two
views of one deed: from inside, while it goes on, or from outside, whole.
The prefix *про-* means "through": **прочитать**, to read through.
