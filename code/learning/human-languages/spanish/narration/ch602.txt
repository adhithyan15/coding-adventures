Spanish, chapter 602: Las elecciones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el voto — the vote.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the minister, then the Spanish for the town councillor.

You'll want to know: el voto.
el voto — "the vote".
Cada voto es importante. Every vote matters.
Votar — to vote.

The word, taken apart.
voto is a learned borrowing from Latin votum 'a vow, a wish', from vovere 'to vow' (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
las elecciones — the elections.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the town councillor, then the Spanish for the vote.

You'll want to know: las elecciones.
las elecciones — "the elections".
Las elecciones son en mayo. The elections are in May.
Plural for a political vote; in the singular la elección is a choice.

The word, taken apart.
elecciones is the plural of elección, a learned borrowing from Latin electio 'choice', built on eligere 'to pick out', from legere 'to gather' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
el candidato — the candidate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the vote, then the Spanish for the elections.

You'll want to know: el candidato.
el candidato — "the candidate".
Hay tres candidatos para alcalde. There are three candidates for mayor.
A woman is la candidata. Also used for a job.

The word, taken apart.
candidato is a learned borrowing from Latin candidatus 'clothed in white', built on candidus 'white': Roman candidates wore white togas (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Spanish for the elections, then the Spanish for the candidate.

You'll want to know: el votante.
el votante — "the voter".
Muchos votantes no fueron a votar. Many voters did not go to vote.
The same form for a woman: la votante.

The word, taken apart.
votante is built on votar, which comes from voto, a learned borrowing from Latin votum 'a vow' (EtymDB).
