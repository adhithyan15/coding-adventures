# la lune — the Moon

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The seven-day week is one of the oldest things any language carries,
and the French day-names are a **map of the sky**. Before we read the map, one
of the objects on it.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-LUNE-01]; assesses=[] -->

- `front-rounded-u` — the French **u** has no English equivalent. Say "ee," and
  then, without moving your tongue, round your lips as though for "oo." That is
  **u**. So **lune** is *lün*, never *loon*.
- `silent-final-e` — the closing **-e** is a whisper, not a syllable of its own.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**la lune** is feminine, so it takes **la**, the article you already know.

It comes from Latin **lūna**, which English kept in a small family:

- **lunar** — of the Moon
- **lunation** — one full cycle of it
- **lunatic** — someone the Moon was believed to have unsettled, back when
  moonlight was thought to disturb the mind
