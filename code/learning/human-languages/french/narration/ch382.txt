French, chapter 382: Enfreindre les règles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
enfreindre — to break (a rule, the law).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the tolerance, then the French for to demand.

You'll want to know: enfreindre.
enfreindre — "to break (a rule, the law)".
Il a enfreint le code de la route. He broke the highway code.
Conjugated like peindre: il enfreint, ils enfreignent.

The word, taken apart.
enfreindre is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin infrangere "to break into", reshaped from Latin infringere "to break" (Lewis & Short), built on frangere "to break".

transgresser — "to transgress, to break (a rule)".
Les adolescents aiment transgresser les règles. Teenagers like to break the rules.

transgresser is built on Latin trānsgressus, from trānsgredī "to step across" (Lewis & Short), built on gradī "to step".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la sanction — the penalty, the sanction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the offence, then the French for to transgress.

You'll want to know: la sanction.
la sanction — "the penalty, the sanction".
Les sanctions sont lourdes pour ce genre d'infraction. The penalties are heavy for this kind of offence.
It can also mean approval, but mostly it is a penalty.

The word, taken apart.
sanction is a learned borrowing from Latin sānctiō "a decree, a penalty clause" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from sancīre "to make sacred, to decree".
