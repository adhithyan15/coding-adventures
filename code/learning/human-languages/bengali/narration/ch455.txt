Bengali, chapter 455: Patience and Eagerness.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ধৈর্য (dhoirjô) — patience.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for huddled up, then the Bengali for to hesitate.

You'll want to know: ধৈর্য.
ধৈর্য — dhoirjô — "patience".
শিশুদের পড়াতে ধৈর্য লাগে। shishuder pôṛāte dhoirjô lāge. Teaching children takes patience.
ধৈর্য (dhoirjô) ধরা, to be patient; ধৈর্য (dhoirjô) হারানো, to lose patience.

The word, taken apart.
ধৈর্য (dhoirjô) is a tatsama, Sanskrit dhairya, "firmness, constancy, calmness, patience" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
তর সওয়া (tôr sôoyā) — to be able to wait (mostly with not: can't wait).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for keen interest, then the Bengali for eager.

You'll want to know: তর সওয়া.
তর সওয়া — tôr sôoyā — "to be able to wait (mostly with not: can't wait)".
ছুটি আসছে, মীরার আর তর সইছে না। chhuṭi āschhe, mīrār ār tôr sôichhe nā. The holidays are coming and Mira can't wait any longer.
Past (he/she …): সেই ছেলের তর সইল না, sei chheler tôr sôilô nā.
The one who waits is in the genitive: আমার তর সইছে না, I can't wait.

The word, taken apart.
তর সওয়া (tôr sôoyā) is tôr, "waiting", whose origin is not settled, with sôoyā, "to bear, to put up with" (CDIAL 13304).
