# ಟ — one character, met inside words you already say

## Warm-up

about where a word came from?

[PAUSE 2s] One character this time — the last new one in this book so far, and
one you have read inside a dozen everyday words.

## Script you'll notice: ಟ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-151]; assesses=[] -->

**ಟ** — *ṭa*.

It is a **consonant**, and in this script a consonant is never bare: it comes with an *a* already in it. So it is not *t*, it is **ṭa** — the one made with the tongue curled back, which is a different letter from the ತ you already trace and a different sound to a Kannada ear.

You already say these, and every one of them has ಟ somewhere inside it:

- **ಬಟ್ಟೆ** *baṭṭe* — cloth
- **ಬೆಟ್ಟ** *beṭṭa* — a hill
- **ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ** *hoṭṭe* — the stomach
- **ಊಟ** *ūṭa* — a meal

## Writing: ಟ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[KA-SCRIPT-RECOG-151] -->

- **1.** start at the tip of the top curl and curve round to the left and down into the waist
- **2.** without lifting, go round the small loop counterclockwise and close it at the waist
- **3.** without lifting, go down the left side and round the left lobe, rising into the middle point
- **4.** without lifting, drop round the right lobe and climb the right side
- **5.** without lifting, curl in to the short tick in the middle
- **6.** without lifting, come back and round the upper bowl to its tip

**Pen lifts: 0.** The whole letter is one run, from the curl at the top
through the small loop and both lobes to the tip of the upper bowl. Like ಲ,
ಟ has no curled bar on top.

> This is one attested teaching order and not a national standard — Kannada
> handwriting is taught with school-to-school variation. Source: Gopala Krishna
