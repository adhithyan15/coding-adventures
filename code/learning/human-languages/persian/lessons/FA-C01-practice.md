# Chapter 1 — one tiny exchange, one tiny mark

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-SALAM-01] -->

Say **سلام** once to greet and once to answer.

## The exchange
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-SALAM-01, FA-SCRIPT-RTL-01] -->

[YOU READ: each Persian line from right to left]

- A: **سلام** — *salâm*
- B: **سلام** — *salâm*
- A: **ممنون** — *mamnun*
- B: **بله** or **نه** — *bale* or *na*

The later three words stay as whole-word supports: you read them as shapes you
know, not letter by letter. The next section checks that you can say them
without the page.

## Guided Practice — say it back
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-MAMNUN-01, FA-ETYMON-MAMNUN-MNN-02, FA-LEX-BALE-01, FA-LEX-NA-01, FA-ETYMON-NA-INDO-IRANIAN-02, FA-SCRIPT-SHORT-VOWELS-01] -->

- Someone holds a door for you. What do you say? (***Mamnun.***)
- What does *mamnun* literally describe? (**Someone grateful, or obliged** — from
  the Arabic consonant family **m-n-n**, favour and kindness.)
- "Are you ready?" Answer yes. (***Bale.***) Now answer no. (***Na.***)
- *na* did not come from Arabic. Where is it from? (**An old Indo-Iranian
  negative.**)
- Which vowels does Persian writing leave for you to supply? (**The short
  ones.**)

- [YOU SAY: the four-line exchange, choosing **bale** or **na**]

- [YOU SAY: the old idea behind **salâm** — "peace"]
