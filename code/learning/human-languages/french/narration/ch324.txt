Lesson 1 of 5.
envahir — to invade; to overrun.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the crown, then the French for to crown.

You'll want to know: envahir.
envahir — "to invade; to overrun".
L'armée a envahi le pays voisin. The army invaded the neighbouring country.
Figurative: les touristes envahissent la plage.

The word, taken apart.
envahir is inherited from Vulgar Latin invādīre, from Latin invādere "to go into, to attack" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: conquérir.
conquérir — "to conquer; to win over".
Ils ont conquis toute la région en un an. They conquered the whole region in a year.
Past participle conquis. Also conquérir le public — to win the audience over.

The word, taken apart.
conquérir comes from Old French conquerre, from Vulgar Latin conquaerere "to seek to gain" (EtymDB), built on quaerere "to seek".

Lesson 4 of 5.
la conquête — the conquest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the invasion, then the French for to conquer.

You'll want to know: la conquête.
la conquête — "the conquest".
La conquête de l'espace a commencé en 1957. The conquest of space began in 1957.
Informal: sa dernière conquête — his latest girlfriend or boyfriend.

The word, taken apart.
conquête comes from Old French conqueste, usually traced to Vulgar Latin conquaesita "things sought and won", built on quaerere "to seek" (Lewis & Short).
