# Review — the scene, and what cut into it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01, IT-LEX-ERA-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Build "I was reading" from *leggere*. (*Leggevo.*) Then say "I was
tired". (*Ero stanco*, or *ero stanca*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01, IT-LEX-ERA-01, IT-LEX-C-ERA-02, IT-LEX-FACEVA-01, IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01] -->

Four lessons, and every piece of a scene is in your hands. The recipe,
**-vo**, on any regular verb: *parlavo*, *leggevo*, *dormivo*. The one verb
that keeps its Latin shape: *ero*, *era*, *c'era*. The weather, made rather
than being: *faceva freddo*. And the division of labour: the imperfetto paints,
the passato prossimo drops the events in.

[PAUSE 2s] The joint between the two is **mentre**. Whatever follows it is the
scene, still running; the rest of the sentence is the event.

> *Mentre leggevo, mia madre ha telefonato.*
> *Mentre aspettavo l'autobus, ha cominciato a piovere.*

The second one is worth hearing twice. The rain is an event here, because it
**began**: *ha cominciato*. Once it is falling, it is scene again: *pioveva*.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-SCENA-EVENTO-01, IT-ETYMON-IMPERFECTUS-02, IT-ETYMON-IMPERFETTO-BA-02, IT-LEX-FACEVA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] After *mentre*, which past? (The **imperfetto** — the scene.) Why is
*ha cominciato a piovere* in the passato prossimo? (The rain **began**: that is
an event.) What does *imperfetto* mean, taken apart? (**Not carried through.**)
And where did its *-v-* come from? (The ***b*** of Latin *amābam*.)
