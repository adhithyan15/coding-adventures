## You'll want to know: 风衣
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C218-WIND218-01]; assesses=[] -->

**风衣** — *fēngyī* — "a windbreaker; a light coat against the wind".

> *chūntiān fēng hěn dà wǒ chángcháng chuān fēngyī* — It's windy in spring, so I often wear a windbreaker.

The new character, in pieces: 风 — four strokes: a long stroke falling to the left, a bar that turns and hooks down to the right, and inside them a short stroke to the left and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**风衣** (*fēngyī*) is built from 风 'wind' and 衣 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *pjuwng* and *'j+j* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the first, after an older book, as the messenger of heaven and earth.
