Mandarin Chinese, chapter 268: Exams.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bàomíng — to sign up; to enter (one's name).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a joke, then the Mandarin for funny.

You'll want to know: bàomíng.
bàomíng — "to sign up; to enter (one's name)".
nǐ bàomíng le ma — Have you signed up?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bàomíng is built from bào 'to report' and 名 'a name', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pawH and mjieng (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to report'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
口试 (kǒushì) — oral exam.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for funny, then the Mandarin for to sign up.

You'll want to know: 口试.
口试 — kǒushì — "oral exam".
明天我们学校有口试 míngtiān wǒmen xuéxiào yǒu kǒushì — Our school has oral exams tomorrow.

The word, taken apart.
口试 (kǒushì) is built from 口 'the mouth' and 试 'a test', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khuwX and syiH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to use'. The sense 'a test' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for saying what I do.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dá'àn — answer to a question.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an oral exam, then the Mandarin for to pass an exam.

You'll want to know: dá'àn.
dá'àn — "answer to a question".
nǐ de dá'àn duì le — Your answer is right.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dá'àn is built from dá 'to answer' and àn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, top and 'anH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to match' and the second 'a low table'. How the pair came to mean an answer is not settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
jiěshì — to explain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pass an exam, then the Mandarin for the answer to a question.

You'll want to know: jiěshì.
jiěshì — "to explain".
zhège dá'àn nǐ néng jiěshì ma — Can you explain this answer?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiěshì is built from jiě and shì, both inherited from Middle Chinese, keaX and syek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to explain' and 'to untie', and the second 'to let go' and 'to untie'.
