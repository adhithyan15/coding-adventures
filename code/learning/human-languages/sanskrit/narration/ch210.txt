Lesson 1 of 5.
आचार्यः (ācāryaḥ) — a preceptor, a teacher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a painter, then the Sanskrit for a craftsman.

You'll want to know: आचार्यः.
आचार्यः — ācāryaḥ — "a preceptor, a teacher".

The word, taken apart.
ācārya is built on ācāra, "conduct, the rules of behaviour", from ā- with the root √car, "to move, behave" (MW; CDIAL 1072). MW explains it as "one who knows or teaches the rules". Tamil āciriyar, "teacher", was borrowed from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
प्रधानाचार्यः (pradhānācāryaḥ) — a head teacher.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a craftsman, then the Sanskrit for a preceptor.

You'll want to know: प्रधानाचार्यः.
प्रधानाचार्यः — pradhānācāryaḥ — "a head teacher".

The word, taken apart.
pradhānācārya is a compound of pradhāna, "chief, principal", built on pra- and the root √dhā, "to put" (MW), and ācārya, "teacher". MW does not list the compound, so its use for a school's head teacher is a modern pairing.

Lesson 3 of 5.
विद्यार्थी (vidyārthī) — a pupil, a learner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a preceptor, then the Sanskrit for a head teacher.

You'll want to know: विद्यार्थी.
विद्यार्थी — vidyārthī — "a pupil, a learner".

The word, taken apart.
vidyārthī is a compound of vidyā, "knowledge", built on the root √vid, "to know", and arthin, "one who seeks" (MW): a pupil is one who seeks knowledge. MW already glosses it "a student", and Gujarati and Kannada use the same word.

The word, taken apart.
sahapāṭhī is built on saha, "with", and pāṭhin, "one who has studied, a student", from the root √paṭh, "to read, recite" (CDIAL 8039). MW does not list the compound, so the pairing for a classmate is modern.

Lesson 5 of 5.
चिकित्सकः (cikitsakaḥ) — a doctor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a pupil, then the Sanskrit for a classmate.

You'll want to know: चिकित्सकः.
चिकित्सकः — cikitsakaḥ — "a doctor".

The word, taken apart.
cikitsaka is built on cikitsā, "medical treatment", from cikitsati, the desiderative of √cit, "to perceive, attend to" (MW). The desiderative means "wishes to attend to", and so "cares for, treats medically".
