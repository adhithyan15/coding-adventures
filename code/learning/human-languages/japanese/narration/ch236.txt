Japanese, chapter 236: Films and Music.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
えいが (eiga) — a film; a movie.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for judo, then the Japanese for a championship win.

You'll want to know: えいが.
えいが — eiga — "a film; a movie".
せんしゅう かぞくと えいがに いきました。 — senshū kazoku to eiga ni ikimashita — "Last week I went to a film with my family."

The word, taken apart.
えいが (eiga) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ei, "reflect, project" (Middle Chinese 'jaengH) and ga, "picture" (hweaH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
みる (miru) — to watch; to see.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a championship win, then the Japanese for a film.

You'll want to know: みる.
みる — miru — "to watch; to see".
きのう うちで えいがを みました。 — kinō uchi de eiga o mimashita — "Yesterday I watched a film at home."
Polite みます, mimasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
みる (miru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
