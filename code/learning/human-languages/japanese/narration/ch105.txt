Lesson 1 of 5.
はかま (hakama) — a hakama (pleated trousers).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a ball, then the Japanese for a bell.

You'll want to know: はかま.
はかま — hakama — "a hakama (pleated trousers)".

The word, taken apart.
はかま (hakama) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ゆかた (yukata) — a cotton kimono.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a bell, then the Japanese for a hakama.

You'll want to know: ゆかた.
ゆかた — yukata — "a cotton kimono".

The word, taken apart.
ゆかた (yukata) is a native word. JMdict writes it with characters for "bathe" and "clothes", read as a whole; it is usually explained as yu, "bath", with katabira, a light robe, shortened. That account is not settled.
