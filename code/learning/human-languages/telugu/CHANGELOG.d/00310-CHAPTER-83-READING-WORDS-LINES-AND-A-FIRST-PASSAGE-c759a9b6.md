## Chapter 83 — reading: words, lines, and a first passage

Telugu's reading rung. No task shapes are declared, so it moves no number.

Three lessons, 6 -> 10 -> 25 tokens, no new word and no new LETTER in any: the
script ladder has taught 49 signs, and everything here is built from those.

- **Words**: the lesson reads DOWNWARDS. Telugu stacks a consonant under its
  neighbour instead of beside it, so **పుస్తకం** is two syllables wide and three
  levels deep while **నది** has nothing below the line. The tall words are not
  longer, they are deeper.
- **Lines**: there is no verb in the first four. **ఇది పుస్తకం** is *this book*
  with nothing between the words, and it is a complete sentence. Telugu does not
  say *is*, and a reader has to stop waiting for the word that joins them.
- **Passage**: a room described from one spot, and that shape was forced rather
  than chosen. A language with no word for *is* can still say **ఉంది** and
  **లేదు** — it is here, it is not — so the first passage a Telugu reader can
  hold is an inventory of one place. **ఉన్నారా?** makes that word a question and
  makes it respectful at once, which is why the teacher gets it and the farmer
  does not: respect lives in the verb.

A first draft passed both checks and was still thrown away: it read *river root*
and *wind very*. Mechanical validity is not the bar for a reading passage, and
the version here means something.

The banned-words gate caught "just" in my own prose. Reworded.
