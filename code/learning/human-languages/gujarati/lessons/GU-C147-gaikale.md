# ગઈકાલે, સવારે, ઘરે — the -e of *when* and *where*

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-HATO-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-HATU-HATA-01, GU-LEX-C79-WEEK2-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *I was there*, then *they were here*. And the word for
*yesterday*: **ગઈકાલ**.

## Grammar Lens: -e on a time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-E-TIME-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-C79-WEEK2-04, GU-LEX-C79-WEEK2-03, GU-LEX-SAVAAR-01, GU-LEX-BAPOR-01, GU-LEX-SAANJ-01, GU-LEX-RAAT-01, GU-LEX-AAJ-01, GU-LEX-C79-WEEK2-02] -->

**ગઈકાલ** names the day. To put something *on* that day, Gujarati adds **-ે**:

> **ગઈકાલે હું ત્યાં હતો.** — *gaīkāle hũ tyā̃ hato.* — Yesterday I was there.

The same **-ે** turns every part of the day, and every day, into a *when*:

- **આજ** → **આજે** — *āje* — today;
- **આવતીકાલ** → **આવતીકાલે** — *āvtīkāle* — tomorrow;
- **સવાર** → **સવારે** — *savāre* — in the morning;
- **બપોર** → **બપોરે** — *bapore* — at midday;
- **સાંજ** → **સાંજે** — *sānje* — in the evening;
- **રવિવાર** → **રવિવારે** — *ravivāre* — on Sunday.

**રાત** is the one that changes shape: **રાત્રે** (*rātre*), *at night*, keeps an
older form with **ત્ર**.

## You'll want to know: -e on a place, and the two-way કાલે
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-GRAMMAR-C147-GHARE-01, GU-GRAMMAR-C147-KAALE-TENSE-01]; assesses=[GU-LEX-GHAR-01, GU-CONCEPT-C04-KAALE-01, GU-CONCEPT-C04-MALISHUN-01] -->

**ઘર** takes it too: **ઘરે** (*ghare*), *at home*.

**કાલે** has been in the book since **કાલે મળીશું**, *see you tomorrow*. It
also means *yesterday*, and the verb decides which:

- **કાલે મળીશું.** — a future verb, so *tomorrow*;
- **કાલે હું ઘરે હતી.** — a past verb, so *yesterday*.

- [YOU SAY: the answer with **નહીં**: **આવતીકાલે નહીં, ગઈકાલે** — *āvtīkāle nahī̃, gaīkāle*]
