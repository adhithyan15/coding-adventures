# इ — short *i* with nothing to lean on

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01, MR-SCRIPT-A-INDEPENDENT-01, MR-SCRIPT-U-INDEPENDENT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You hang a short *i* in front of a consonant with **ि**. You have the
independent **अ** and the independent **उ**. The independent short *i* is still
missing, and a word that starts with it cannot be written without it.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-SCRIPT-I-INDEPENDENT-01]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01, MR-LEX-TITHE, MR-SCRIPT-THA-01] -->

> इ

**इ** is the letter form of the same short *i*. Devanagari's rule has not changed
since chapter five: a **mark** when a consonant carries the vowel, a **letter**
when nothing does. **अ**, **आ**, **उ**, **ऊ**, **ए** are already yours; this is
the sixth.

You need it for exactly one reason right now, and it is a good one. The word you
learned last chapter points AWAY from you. Its near partner begins with this
vowel, and until now the book could say **तिथे** and not its opposite.

## Writing — guided copy
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-SCRIPT-I-INDEPENDENT-01, MR-SCRIPT-I-MATRA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: guided-copy -->

Copy **इ** twice with the model visible. Then write the short-*i* mark beside it
and say which of the two can start a word.
