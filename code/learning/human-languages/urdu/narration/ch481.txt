Urdu, chapter 481: Getting Better.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
افاقہ (ifāqā) — an easing (of an illness); relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to lay (someone) down, then the Urdu for to cover someone up.

You'll want to know: افاقہ.
افاقہ — ifāqā — "an easing (of an illness); relief".
دوا سے مریض کو افاقہ ہوا۔ davā se marīz ko ifāqā huā. The medicine brought the patient some relief.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
افاقہ (ifāqā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ifāqa, "recovering, coming to oneself".

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: بحال.
بحال — bahāl — "restored; back to normal".
ایک ہفتے بعد اس کی صحت بحال ہو گئی۔ ek hafte bād us kī sehat bahāl ho gaī. A week later her health was back to normal.

The word, taken apart.
بحال (bahāl) is usually traced to Persian bahāl, "restored", from ba, "to, in", and Arabic ḥāl, "state": back in its proper state.

مالش کرنا — mālish karnā — "to massage".
امی نے دادی کی کمر کی مالش کی۔ ammī ne dādī kī kamar kī mālish kī. Mum massaged my grandmother's back.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے سر کی مالش کی, us ne sar kī mālish kī.

The word, taken apart.
مالش کرنا (mālish karnā) is built from مالش, usually traced to Persian mālish, "rubbing", and کرنا, "to do".

دبانا — dabānā — "to press gently".
بیٹی نے ابو کا سر دبایا۔ beṭī ne abbū kā sar dabāyā. The daughter gently pressed her father's head.

The word, taken apart.
دبانا (dabānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin dābnā, "to press", under dabb, "press", a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 6173).
