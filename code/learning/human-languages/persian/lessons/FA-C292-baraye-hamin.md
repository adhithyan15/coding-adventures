# برای همین (barâ-ye hamin) — so: the arrow that points forward

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C92-WHY92-03, FA-LEX-C92-WHY92-02, FA-LEX-BE-NAZAR-E-MAN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "because", two ways. (*Chon*, *zirâ*.) Then "in my opinion".
(*Be nazar-e man.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An opinion needs its reasons, and a reason needs a result. Persian
can join the two from either end.

## Grammar Lens: two arrows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-BARAYE-HAMIN-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C129-QUAL129-01, FA-LEX-C36-VERBS36-01, FA-LEX-C66-TIME66-03, FA-LEX-C83-DEF83-02] -->

One fact, three ways to join it:

> **خسته بودم، برای همین زود خوابیدم.** — *khaste budam, barâ-ye hamin zud
> khâbidam* — I was tired, so I went to sleep early.

> **چون خسته بودم، زود خوابیدم.** — *chon khaste budam, zud khâbidam* —
> Because I was tired, I went to sleep early.

> **زود خوابیدم، زیرا خسته بودم.** — *zud khâbidam, zirâ khaste budam* — I went
> to sleep early, because I was tired.

**برای همین**, *barâ-ye hamin*, comes after the cause and points forward to the
result. *Barâ-ye* is "for", and *hamin* is "this very": "for this very
reason". *Chon* and *zirâ* sit on the cause itself and point back to it.
*Chon* can open the sentence; *zirâ* mostly follows the result, and sounds
written.

## Grammar Lens: pas, the short one
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-PAS-SO-01]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C93-WHY93-01, FA-LEX-C152-ZAMAN152-05, FA-GRAMMAR-FEKR-MIKONAM-KE-01] -->

**پس**, *pas*, is "so" when you draw a conclusion, often at the front of what
you conclude:

> **پس قطار دیر میرسد.** — *pas qatâr dir miresad* — So the train will be
> late.

*Banâbarin*, "therefore", does the same job in writing. *Pas* is also the
*pas* of *pas az*, "after": a result comes after its cause.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*hamin* is *ham*, "same", with *in*, "this". EtymDB 2.1 takes *ham* through
Middle and Old Persian back to an Indo-European word for "same", the one
English kept as **same**. *Pas* goes back, in EtymDB, through Middle Persian
*ps*, read *pas*, to an Indo-European word meaning "behind, afterwards".
