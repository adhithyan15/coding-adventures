Italian, chapter 514: In acqua.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il tuffo — dive, plunge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for athlete, then the Italian for to pant.

You'll want to know: il tuffo.
il tuffo — "dive, plunge".
Ho fatto un tuffo dal molo. I dived in from the pier.
Fare un tuffo — to go for a dip.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of tuffo is not settled; it is often taken to imitate the sound of a body hitting water, or traced to a Germanic word for dipping.

Lesson 2 of 5.
la cuffia — swimming cap; headphones.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to pant, then the Italian for dive.

You'll want to know: la cuffia.
la cuffia — "swimming cap; headphones".
In piscina la cuffia è obbligatoria. In the pool a swimming cap is compulsory.
Also headphones: le cuffie.

The word, taken apart.
cuffia comes from Late Latin cofia "a cap, a bonnet" (EtymDB), probably of Germanic origin.

D'estate faccio un corso di vela. In summer I take a sailing course.
Andare a vela — to sail; la barca a vela — sailing boat.

The word, taken apart.
vela is inherited from Latin vēla, the plural of vēlum "a sail, a covering" (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
immergersi — to dive under, to immerse oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for swimming cap, then the Italian for sail.

You'll want to know: immergersi.
immergersi — "to dive under, to immerse oneself".
Ci siamo immersi per vedere i pesci. We dived under to watch the fish.
The past participle is immerso. Immergersi nella lettura — to lose oneself in reading.

The word, taken apart.
immergersi comes from Latin immergere "to dip into" (Lewis & Short), from mergere "to dip, to sink".

Lesson 5 of 5.
il trampolino — diving board; springboard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for sail, then the Italian for to dive under.

You'll want to know: il trampolino.
il trampolino — "diving board; springboard".
Ha paura di saltare dal trampolino alto. He is afraid to jump from the high board.
Also a ski jump.

The word, taken apart.
trampolino is built on trampolo "stilt" (EtymDB), which comes from a German word like trampeln "to trample".
