# क्या …? — the yes-or-no question

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01, HI-CONCEPT-C07-HAAN-01, HI-CONCEPT-C07-NAHIN-01] -->

**जब … तब**. Then say **हाँ** and **नहीं** — two answers with no question.

## Grammar Lens: क्या at the FRONT means nothing
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-KYA-POLAR-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-SAKNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-VERB-FINAL-01, HI-CONCEPT-C02-KYA-01] -->

> **क्या आप कल आ सकते हैं?** — *kyā āp kal ā sakte haiṁ?* —
> **Can you come tomorrow?**

Put **क्या** at the very front and it stops meaning *what*: it marks the sentence
as a yes-or-no question and nothing else moves, because Hindi cannot invert the
way English does with *can you*.

Position is the whole difference:

- **आप क्या कर सकते हैं?** — *what can you do?* — inside
- **क्या आप आ सकते हैं?** — *can you come?* — in front

Answer either with **हाँ** or **नहीं**. A language that cannot invert reaches for
a particle; Persian and Russian do the same.
