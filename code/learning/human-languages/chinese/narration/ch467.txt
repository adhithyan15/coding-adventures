Mandarin Chinese, chapter 467: What Kind of Person.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
píqi — temper; temperament.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give a gentle smile, then the Mandarin for peaceful inside.

You'll want to know: píqi.
píqi — "temper; temperament".
wǒ bàba de píqi bǐ wǒ māma hǎo — My dad is better-tempered than my mum.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
píqi is built from pí 'the spleen' and qì 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bjie and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which explains the first, after an older book, as the organ of earth that helps the stomach. Temper as the breath of the spleen is the usual explanation, not one the dictionary gives.

You'll want to know: 个性.
个性 — gèxìng — "individuality; a strong personality".
tā hěn yǒu gèxìng — She has a lot of personality.
The new character, in pieces: 性 (xìng) — eight strokes in two pieces: on the left the heart piece, a dot, a second dot and a long vertical; on the right 生 shēng, five strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
个性 (gèxìng) is built from 个 'single' and 性 (xìng) 'nature', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaH and sjengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'one-sided' and the second 'nature and conduct'.

Script — the heart piece, then 生.
性 (xìng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, three strokes: the heart piece. A short dot on the left, a short dot on the right, then a long vertical down between them.
The right piece, 生, five strokes. A short stroke falling to the left at the top, a bar, a second bar, a vertical down through them, then a long bar across the foot.
3 + 5 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 性 (xìng) have? (Two: three strokes and five.) Which written character is on the right? (生.)

You'll want to know: 天性.
天性 — tiānxìng — "one's nature; an inborn trait".
安静是他的天性 ānjìng shì tā de tiānxìng — Being quiet is in his nature.

The word, taken apart.
天性 (tiānxìng) is built from 天 'heaven' and 性 (xìng) 'nature', both inherited from Middle Chinese, then and sjengH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the dark heaven above' and the second 'nature and conduct': the nature heaven gives.

Lesson 6 of 6.
huópō — full of life; vivacious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: huópō.
huópō — "full of life; vivacious".
wǒ nǚ'ér hěn huópō yě hěn ài xiào — My daughter is very lively and laughs a lot.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
huópō is built from 活 'alive' and pō 'to splash'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese hwat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'not dead'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the second, so its history is not settled.
