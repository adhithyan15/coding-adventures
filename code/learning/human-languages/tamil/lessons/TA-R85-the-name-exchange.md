# (உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன?) — four words, and the one that is missing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C02-EN-01, TA-LEX-C02-ENNA-01] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say the word for *my*. Then the word for *what*.

## Grammar Lens: the sentence with no verb in it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C02-EN-PEYAR-01, TA-LEX-C02-UNGAL-PEYAR-ENNA-01, TA-LEX-C02-EN-01] -->

**என் பெயர் …** is *my name …*, and there the sentence stops. No word stands
where English puts **is**.

That is not an abbreviation or a casual drop. Tamil **does not need a verb to
link two things**: put the two nouns side by side and the link is understood.
The same silence sits inside the question — **உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன?** is *your name
what?*, three words doing what English needs four to do.

Once you have seen it here, you have seen it everywhere it happens.

## The exchange — both sides, end to end
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C02-UNGAL-PEYAR-ENNA-01, TA-LEX-C02-EN-PEYAR-01, TA-LEX-C02-MAGIZHCCI-01, TA-LEX-C02-PRACTICE-01] -->

| move | what you say |
|---|---|
| ask | **உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன?** |
| answer | **என் பெயர் …** |
| close it warmly | **மகிழ்ச்சி** |

**மகிழ்ச்சி** is worth its own moment: the word is *joy*, and nothing more. It
is not a built phrase meaning *pleased to meet you* — it is one noun, offered,
and the occasion supplies the rest.
