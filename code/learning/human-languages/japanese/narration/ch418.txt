Japanese, chapter 418: Filling In a Form.
5 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しょめい (shomei) — a signature.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to carry in one's arms, then the Japanese for to wheel along.

You'll want to know: しょめい.
しょめい — shomei — "a signature".
ここに しょめいを おねがいします。 — koko ni shomei o onegai shimasu — "Your signature here, please."

The word, taken apart.
しょめい (shomei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are sho, "signature" (Middle Chinese dzyoH) and mei, "name" (mjieng) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ひにち (hinichi) — a date (for something planned).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for filling in a form, then the Japanese for a box on a form.

You'll want to know: ひにち.
ひにち — hinichi — "a date (for something planned)".
よやくの ひにちを きめました。 — yoyaku no hinichi o kimemashita — "We decided on the date of the booking."

The word, taken apart.
ひにち (hinichi) is built from both layers of the vocabulary: the native Japanese (wago) hi, "day", inherited rather than borrowed, and the Sino-Japanese nichi, "day" (Middle Chinese nyit), an on'yomi borrowed from Chinese (JMdict; KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
