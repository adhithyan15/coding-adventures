# कर रही हूँ — which of the three words listens

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **रहा** ends in **-आ**. Before reading on, guess what it does when a
woman is speaking, and guess which of the other two words joins in.

## You'll want to know: कर रही हूँ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02]; assesses=[] -->

**कर रही हूँ** (*kar rahī hū̃*) — "I am doing," said by a woman.

One letter has changed, and it is in the middle word.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| speaker | the three words |
|---|---|
| a man | कर रहा हूँ |
| a woman | कर रही हूँ |

The stem stays bare either way. The copula **हूँ** is about *who is speaking*
rather than about gender, so it does not move. Only **रहा** bends, and it bends
the way every **-आ** word you have met bends.

That is the tidiest arrangement you have seen yet: **one word per job, and only
the one that describes the action listens to gender.**

## Grammar Lens: the same ending, a fourth time
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C89-CONT-02, HI-LEX-C89-CONT-01] -->

| masculine | feminine |
|---|---|
| काला | काली |
| बोलता | बोलती |
| था | थी |
| रहा | रही |

Four places, one alternation. Every time a Hindi word ends in **-आ** and
describes somebody, **-ई** is what it becomes for a woman — and by now you should
expect it before you are told.
