Portuguese, chapter 436: Pedir indicações.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
as indicações — the directions; the instructions.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the travel plan, then the Portuguese for to locate.

You'll want to know: as indicações.
as indicações — "the directions; the instructions".
Um senhor deu-nos indicações para a estação. A man gave us directions to the station.
Singular a indicação — a sign, a hint.

The word, taken apart.
indicação is a learned borrowing from Latin indicātiō 'pointing out' (EtymDB), built on indicāre 'to point out'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
a rotunda — the roundabout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to locate, then the Portuguese for the directions.

You'll want to know: a rotunda.
a rotunda — "the roundabout".
Na rotunda, apanhamos a segunda saída. At the roundabout, we take the second exit.
In Brazil: a rotatória.

The word, taken apart.
rotunda is a learned borrowing from Latin rotunda 'round' (EtymDB has rotundus, from rota 'wheel'); the inherited form of the same word is redondo.

Lesson 3 of 5.
contornar — to go round; to get round (a problem).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the directions, then the Portuguese for the roundabout.

You'll want to know: contornar.
contornar — "to go round; to get round (a problem)".
Contornamos a praça e chegamos ao museu. We go round the square and reach the museum.
Contornar um problema — to get round a problem.

The word, taken apart.
contornar is built from con- and tornar 'to turn', which is inherited from Latin tornāre 'to turn on a lathe' (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
o entroncamento — the road junction; the railway junction.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the roundabout, then the Portuguese for to go round.

You'll want to know: o entroncamento.
o entroncamento — "the road junction; the railway junction".
No entroncamento, viramos à esquerda. At the junction, we turn left.
Where two roads or railway lines meet like branches on a trunk.

The word, taken apart.
entroncamento is built on entroncar 'to join together', on tronco 'trunk', which is inherited from Latin truncus (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
prosseguir — to carry on, to continue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to go round, then the Portuguese for the road junction.

You'll want to know: prosseguir.
prosseguir — "to carry on, to continue".
Depois da ponte, prosseguimos em frente. After the bridge, we carried on straight ahead.
A little formal; people also say seguir.

The word, taken apart.
prosseguir is a learned borrowing from Latin prōsequī 'to follow on' (EtymDB), reshaped on seguir 'to follow'.
