GROUND TRUTH (not given to ingester or deriver subagents):

Final diagnosis: Primary Mediastinal Large B-cell Lymphoma (PMBCL).

Confirmed by endobronchial ultrasound-guided biopsy with immunophenotype:
CD20+, CD5-, CD10-, BCL1-, BCL6-, BCL2 equivocal, CD23 equivocal, high
Ki-67 proliferative index. Anterior mediastinal mass in a young female.

What the framework should ideally produce:
- diagnosis(malignancy) — TRUE  (correct admit decision, biopsy pursuit)
- diagnosis(infection) — FALSE  (despite surface presentation looking infectious;
  actinomyces was colonization, not pathogen; antibiotics did not improve symptoms)
- disposition(biopsy_for_tissue_diagnosis) — TRUE

What an inexperienced reader / naive LLM would likely produce:
- High P(infection) because of cough/sputum/leukocytosis/actinomyces growth
- Low P(malignancy) because patient is young and presentation looks infectious
- Disposition: continue/escalate antibiotics

This case is interesting for the framework because the SAME observations support
either category depending on which features the rulebook weights as load-bearing.
A rulebook that weights infection signals (leukocytosis, sputum culture) ranks
infection high; a rulebook that weights persistence and the mediastinal mass
ranks malignancy high. The audit trail should make the choice visible.

Source: PMC12914605 — Primary Mediastinal Large B-cell Lymphoma Masquerading as
an Infection.
