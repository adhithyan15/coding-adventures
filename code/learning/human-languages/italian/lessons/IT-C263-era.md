# era, c'era — the two words a scene is made of

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-IMPERFETTO-01, IT-GRAMMAR-CE-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Build "I was speaking" from *parlare*. (*Parlavo.*) Now say "there
is". (*C'è.*)

[PAUSE 1s] Hold on to both. This lesson joins them.

## You'll want to know: era
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-ERA-01]; assesses=[IT-LEX-ESSERE-02] -->

One verb refuses last lesson's recipe, and it is the one you use most:
**essere**. Its imperfetto is short, and it is old:

> **ero** — I was · **eri** — you were · **era** — he, she or it was
> **eravamo** — we were · **eravate** — you all were · **erano** — they were

**Era** is the form you will hear most, because a scene is mostly about things
and places rather than about you:

> *La stazione era piccola.* — The station was small.
> *Ero stanco, ma ero contento.* — I was tired, but I was happy.

## You'll want to know: c'era
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[IT-LEX-C-ERA-02]; assesses=[IT-GRAMMAR-CE-02] -->

You have **c'è** and **ci sono**, "there is" and "there are". Move them into
the imperfetto and you have the way a scene opens:

> **c'era** — there was · **c'erano** — there were
> *C'era il sole.* — The sun was out. (Literally, "there was the sun".)
> *C'erano molte persone.* — There were a lot of people.

Same **ci**, same apostrophe where it meets a vowel. Only the verb has moved
into the past.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**era** ← Latin *erat*, "he was, she was" — inherited, with only the final *t*
worn away. Latin's past of "to be" was irregular already, *eram, erās, erat*,
and it never had the *b* of *amābam*, so it never took the *-v-* either. That is
why this one form looks nothing like *parlava*. (Lewis & Short, under *sum*.)

The *ero* for "I" got its *-o* late, on the same usual account as *parlavo*:
old Italian said *io era*. EtymDB's only row for an Italian *era* is the noun "an era, an
age", a different word altogether, from Latin *aera*.
