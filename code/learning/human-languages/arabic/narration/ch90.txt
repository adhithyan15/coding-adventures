Arabic, chapter 90: Here, There, Right, Shop, Street.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
هنا (hunā) — here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for in front of, then the Arabic for behind.

You'll want to know: هنا.
هنا — hunā — "here".

The word, taken apart.
هنا (hunā) is an old Arabic pointing word of place. It is not built on a three-letter root; Hebrew hēnnā, "hither", is often compared.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: هناك.
هناك — hunāka — "there".

The word, taken apart.
هناك (hunāka) is built from hunā, "here", with the distance ending -ka, the same ending heard in dhālika, "that".

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: يمين.
يمين — yamīn — "right (side)".

The word, taken apart.
يمين (yamīn) is built on the native Arabic root y-m-n, "the right hand; good fortune" (Lane). Hebrew yamin, "right hand", heard in the name Benjamin, is its cousin.

متجر (matjar) is built on the native Arabic root t-j-r, "to trade" (Lane). Matjar is the ma- noun of trading, "a place of trade".

Lesson 5 of 5.
شارع (shāriʿ) — a street.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for right, then the Arabic for a shop.

You'll want to know: شارع.
شارع — shāriʿ — "a street".

The word, taken apart.
شارع (shāriʿ) is built on the native Arabic root sh-r-ʿ, "to enter upon, to open a way" (Lane). Lane's shāriʿ is an open road; the same root gives sharīʿa, "a path, a law".
