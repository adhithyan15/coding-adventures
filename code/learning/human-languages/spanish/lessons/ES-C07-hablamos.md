# hablamos — "we speak"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-SINGULAR] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I speak", then "you speak" to a friend. (*Hablo. Hablas.*)

## Grammar Lens: the first form that is not about one person
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-1PL]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-LEX-HABLAR] -->

Every verb form you have met so far — in every tense — has been about **one**
person. That changes here.

Drop the **-ar**, add **-amos**:

> **hablamos** — *we speak.*

One word again, no pronoun, and now it covers you and somebody else.

This is the form you will reach for constantly and have not had: *we speak*, *we
work*, *we study*. Everything you could say about yourself, you can now say
about a group you belong to.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-1PL] -->

The **-mos** is remarkable for how little happened to it.

Latin marked *we* with **-mus**: *amāmus*, "we love". Spanish says *-mos*. One
vowel changed in two thousand years, and nothing else — no consonant lost, no
syllable worn away, no analogy pulling it out of shape.

Compare what became of the third person, which lost its **-t** entirely
(*hablat* → *habla*). Endings do not decay at the same rate, and this one barely
decayed at all.

**And Portuguese and Italian kept it too** — *falamos*, *parliamo*. If you ever
meet another Romance language, *-mos* or *-mo* will be waiting for you, doing
the same job.
