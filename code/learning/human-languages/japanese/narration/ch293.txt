Japanese, chapter 293: Fireworks over the River.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
はなび (hanabi) — fireworks.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to die, then the Japanese for to support.

You'll want to know: はなび.
はなび — hanabi — "fireworks".
なつは かわで はなびが あります。 — natsu wa kawa de hanabi ga arimasu — "In summer there are fireworks by the river."

The word, taken apart.
はなび (hanabi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from hana, "flower", and bi, "fire" (from hi) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

うちあげる — uchiageru — "to set off (fireworks); to launch".
かわの うえで はなびを うちあげます。 — kawa no ue de hanabi o uchiagemasu — "They set off fireworks over the river."
Polite うちあげます, uchiagemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): まちが はなびを うちあげました。, machi ga hanabi o uchiagemashita.

Lesson 3 of 5.
やたい (yatai) — a food stall; a street stall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for fireworks, then the Japanese for to set off (fireworks).

You'll want to know: やたい.
やたい — yatai — "a food stall; a street stall".
えきの まえに やたいが ならんでいます。 — eki no mae ni yatai ga narande imasu — "Food stalls stand in a row in front of the station."

The word, taken apart.
やたい (yatai) is a mixed word, native and Sino-Japanese: ya, "house", is native Japanese, and tai, "a stand", is a Chinese character read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese doj) (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

にぎわう (nigiwau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
