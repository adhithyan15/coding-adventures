Japanese, chapter 349: Expecting the Unexpected.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
よそう (yosō) — an expectation; a forecast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the hour, then the Japanese for consecutive holidays.

You'll want to know: よそう.
よそう — yosō — "an expectation; a forecast".
けっかが よそうと ちがったら、もう いちど しらべます。 — kekka ga yosō to chigattara, mō ichido shirabemasu — "If the result is not what we expected, we will investigate again."

The word, taken apart.
よそう (yosō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are yo, "beforehand" (Middle Chinese yoH) and sō, "idea" (sjangX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
のがす (nogasu) — to miss a chance; to let slip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for the situation, then the Japanese for an opportunity.

You'll want to know: のがす.
のがす — nogasu — "to miss a chance; to let slip".
この きかいを のがしたら、つぎは らいねんです。 — kono kikai o nogashitara, tsugi wa rainen desu — "If we miss this chance, the next one is next year."
Polite のがします, nogashimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あには きかいを のがしました。, ani wa kikai o nogashimashita.

The word, taken apart.
のがす (nogasu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
せっかく (sekkaku) — at great pains; specially.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an opportunity, then the Japanese for to miss a chance.

You'll want to know: せっかく.
せっかく — sekkaku — "at great pains; specially".
もし あめが ふったら、せっかくの りょこうが ちゅうしに なります。 — moshi ame ga futtara, sekkaku no ryokō ga chūshi ni narimasu — "If it rains, the trip we planned so carefully will be called off."

The word, taken apart.
せっかく (sekkaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are setsu, "fold" (Middle Chinese tsyet) and kaku, "angle" (kaewk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription). The two join as sek-kaku; JMdict notes that it is usually written in kana.
