Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 30: Bring It Over: The Food Counter's Own Word.
7 lessons. You can do the first 1 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
Hear lāvo — bring it.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the closing payoff and the three-word transport payoff, then write bus and make it.

You'll want to know.
lāvo — bring it.
Two beats, and the fourth asking word in this track. Say them in the order you met them: dikhāvo, karo, lo, do, lāvo. Every one ends the same way.
lāvo is the one a food counter needs. You do not ask for tea to be shown to you; you ask for it to be brought. The source prints it with a food word, which is exactly where the next lesson puts it.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which asking word would you use for tea, dikhāvo or lāvo?


Lesson 2 of 7.
लावो (lāvo) — bring it, on the page.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Say bring it, then write wind and show it.

You'll want to know.
ला + वो becomes लावो — lāvo — bring it.
Three signs, none new. Its ending वो is the same one that closes दिखावो. Write लो and लावो (lāvo) one under the other: same first letter, and everything after it is what separates taking from bringing.

Guided Practice.
Look at the pair, cover it, wait five seconds, and write both from their meanings: first take it, then bring it.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
From lāvo, write the word that asks for something to be brought over.


Lesson 3 of 7.
Hear chāy lāvo — bring tea.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Recall the seven-word food payoff, say the request from Chapter 26, then write the sweet crumbled wheat dessert, tea, and bring it.

You'll want to know.
chāy lāvo — bring tea.
Two words. The food comes first and the asking word last, the same order you already use in ye kapṛā dikhāvo, with the pointing word left out because you are not choosing between cups in front of you.
Any of the seven food words fits the first slot. Say the line three times, with chāy, then roṭī, then dāl, and notice that nothing else in it moves.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
What does chāy lāvo ask for?


Lesson 4 of 7.
चाय लावो। (chāy lāvo.) — the order on the page.

Warm-up.
[pause 24 seconds]
Write ट, then write flatbread and hard wheat rolls.

You'll want to know.
चाय | लावो (lāvo)।.
Two written words and a stop. Now write the same line twice more, replacing चाय with रोटी and then with बाटी. Only the first word changes, and you have written all three of those words before.

Guided Practice.
Read the line once. Cover it for ten seconds and write it. Then write the two variants from memory and check that लावो (lāvo) is identical in all three.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
Write the line that orders tea.


Lesson 5 of 7.
Retrieve bring it against the other four — the bringing word and the order it makes.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Write very and a little, then say which one makes a description stronger.

Guided Practice.
Five asking words are now in play. Hear them shuffled and give each meaning. Read five cards: दिखावो, करो, लो, दो, लावो (lāvo). Write all five from sound, then write the tea order and say which of the five it uses.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6; Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Write bring it, then the whole tea order.


Lesson 6 of 7.
Payoff — five asking words, five jobs.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Recall the three-counter request payoff and write the request itself.

Guided Practice.
All five end in the same sound and none of them is interchangeable.
Hear all five shuffled and name each one's job.
Produce all five from their jobs alone, without the sounds.
Read five cards and match each to one line you already know.
Write all five from dictation, then write the tea order, the counter-offer, and the closing question that asks for a final price.
Pass each skill separately. Five ways to ask is not five ways to be understood: refusing, thanking somebody for doing it, and asking again more softly are all still untaught.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6; Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 9.
[question — say your answer, then pause 45 seconds]
Complete all four scored checks, then write the asking word a food counter needs.


Lesson 7 of 7.
Close the writing loop for ल, व and च — delayed closure for three consonants the asking words lean on.

Warm-up.
[pause 22 seconds]
Recall the five-word asking payoff, then write child and wind.

Guided Practice.
Write five single signs from sound cues: ल, व, च, छ, ख. Two of those five are breathed partners of the two before them; say all five and mark which two carry the breath.
Then write लावो, लो, दिखावो, चाय लावो। (chāy lāvo.), and पाछे मिलसू from dictation. Repair only the missed sign and rewrite its word once.

Wrap-up Recall.
Sources: Marwari Pathshala, Lesson 6; Unicode Devanagari chart.
[question — say your answer, then pause 16 seconds]
Write the plain ch, then its breathed partner.
