Marathi, chapter 279: Telling It in Order.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
त्यानंतर (tyānantar) — first, then, after that, in the end.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "then" and "after". (mag, nantar.) Then "I used to go". (mī jāyco.)

You'll want to know: first, then, after that.
आधी मी चहा घेतला. मग मी पुस्तक वाचलं. ādhī mī cahā ghetlā. mag mī pustak vāclaṁ. — "First I had tea. Then I read a book."
A story keeps its events in order with four words:
आधी — first.
मग — then.
त्यानंतर (tyānantar) — after that: त्या, "that", and नंतर, "after".
शेवटी — in the end.
Each one opens its sentence.

Grammar Lens: -नंतर after a noun.
शाळेनंतर मी घरी आलो. śāḷenantar mī gharī ālo. — "After school I came home."
नंतर also stands after a noun, in its bent form: शाळा becomes शाळेनंतर (śāḷenantar), "after school". त्यानंतर (tyānantar) is the same thing done to तो.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "First I had tea. Then I read a book." — ādhī mī cahā ghetlā. mag mī pustak vāclaṁ.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After that, suddenly, it rained" — tyānantar acānak pāūs paḍlā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After school I came home" — śāḷenantar mī gharī ālo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 40 seconds]
Say three things you did yesterday, in order, with ādhī, mag and śevṭī.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"First"? (आधी.) "After that"? (त्यानंतर (tyānantar).) "In the end"? (शेवटी.) "After school"? (शाळेनंतर.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
करून (karūn) — having done it, and then.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "pleased to meet you". (bheṭūn ānand jhālā.) भेटून is "having met": the form this lesson teaches has been in your mouth since the first meeting.

You'll want to know: -ऊन, 'having done'.
मी घरी जाऊन झोपलो. mī gharī jāūn jhoplo. — "I went home and slept."
Take -णे off and put -ऊन on: करणे becomes करून (karūn), बसणे becomes बसून (basūn), पाहणे becomes पाहून (pāhūn). A stem ending in a vowel keeps it: जाणे becomes जाऊन (jāūn), येणे becomes येऊन (yeūn), घेणे becomes घेऊन (gheūn).
The tense comes once, at the end: जाऊन झोपलो is "went and slept". तो चहा घेऊन आला (to cahā gheūn ālā), "having taken tea he came", is how Marathi says "he brought tea".

Grammar Lens: -ऊन or -ल्यावर.
घरी पोहोचल्यावर मी झोपलो. gharī pohocalyāvar mī jhoplo. — "After I reached home, I slept."
-ऊन joins two actions of one doer, one after the other. -ल्यावर says "after", and the doer can change: मी घरी पोहोचल्यावर आई आली (mī gharī pohocalyāvar āī ālī), "after I reached home, Mother came".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I went home and slept" — mī gharī jāūn jhoplo]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "He brought tea" — to cahā gheūn ālā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "After I reached home, Mother came" — mī gharī pohocalyāvar āī ālī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 24 seconds]
Say that you went home and slept, in one sentence.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Having done"? (करून (karūn).) "Having gone"? (जाऊन.) "He brought tea"? (तो चहा घेऊन आला.) Which joins two doers? (-ल्यावर.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
ऋतू (ṛtū) — a season.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the summer, the monsoon and the winter. (unhāḷā, pāvsāḷā, hivāḷā.) Each of them is one of these.

You'll want to know: ऋतू.
ऋतू — ṛtū — "a season".
मला पावसाळा हा ऋतू खूप आवडतो. malā pāvsāḷā hā ṛtū khūp āvaḍto. — "I like the monsoon season very much."
Its first letter is the vowel you know as the sign ृ in नृत्य, standing on its own. Marathi says it ru: ऋतू (ṛtū) is said rutū.

The word, taken apart.
ऋतू (ṛtū) is a tatsama: Sanskrit ṛtu, "a fixed time, a season" (Monier-Williams). CDIAL has no entry for it. Everyday Marathi names three seasons; the Sanskrit calendar counts six, and वसंत, "springtime", is one of them.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ṛtū, said rutū]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I like the monsoon season very much" — malā pāvsāḷā hā ṛtū khūp āvaḍto]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three seasons, one after another]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ऋतू (ṛtū) mean? ("A season".) How is it said? (rutū.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) — the vowel letter at the start of ऋतू (ṛtū).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say ऋतू (ṛtū), "a season". Then find the sign ृ in नृत्य, "dance", and say its sound: ru.
One new character comes next: the first letter of ऋतू (ṛtū).

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script.
ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)).
ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) is the standing form of the sign ृ. The sign hangs on a consonant (नृत्य, हृदय); the letter starts a word (ऋतू (ṛtū)). Its careful transliteration is ṛ; Marathi says it ru. Keep it apart from र, the consonant ra: ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) is a vowel letter, and it stands alone.

Writing: ऋ — guided copy.
Keep ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) in front of you for this first pass.
1. start at the open upper-left tip, sweep right through the short shoulder, then turn down-left through the diagonal hook.
2. lift at the middle body's left edge, loop anticlockwise round its little ball, then run down to the right and swing the lower bowl left, round the bottom, and out to the right without lifting.
3. lift and draw the right stem top-to-bottom.
4. lift and draw the top shirorekhā left-to-right.
Pen lifts: 3. This is one sourced learner order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Saurmandal, ‘Devanagari ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) stroke order.svg’, panels 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
[pause 1 second]
Copy ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)) twice beside the model, saying ru as you finish each one. Then write ऋतू (ṛtū) once.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Which letter starts ऋतू (ṛtū)? (ऋ (ṛ (Marathi ru)).) How does Marathi say it? (ru.) Where does its sign ृ stand? (On a consonant: नृत्य.)
[once you have stopped driving — question: With no model, write the vowel letter at the start of ṛtū, then the word.]


Lesson 5 of 6.
(reading) (gelyā varṣī pāvsāḷyāt mī gāvālā gelo.) — a monsoon day in the village.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, to read the printed text yourself. The questions are about what you read, so save the whole lesson for when you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Eighty-six words, every one met before or glossed after it. The writer is Arun. He says मला पावसाळा हा ऋतू (ṛtū) खूप आवडतो, and then he tells you why.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Reading.
गेल्या वर्षी पावसाळ्यात मी गावाला गेलो. मला पावसाळा हा ऋतू (ṛtū) खूप आवडतो. आधी मी बसने स्टेशनला गेलो. मग गाडीने गावाला निघालो. गाडीत मी पुस्तक वाचत होतो. अचानक खूप पाऊस आला. डोंगर हिरवे होते. संध्याकाळी गावाला पोहोचल्यावर मी नदीवर गेलो. नदी भरलेली होती. लहान असताना मी रोज इथे यायचो. मग मी घरी आलो. आई चहा करत होती. आम्ही चहा घेऊन अंगणात बसलो. पाऊस पडत होता. आईने मला एक गोष्ट सांगितली. त्यानंतर (tyānantar) आम्ही भाकरी खाल्ली. रात्रभर पाऊस पडत होता. शेवटी मी लवकर झोपलो. तो दिवस खूप चांगला होता.
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage once without stopping; do not translate]
[pause 3 seconds]
[once you have stopped driving — read: the passage again, and sort every verb: event, scene or habit]

Why it's said this way.
[pause 2 seconds]
New forms: गेल्या वर्षी (gelyā varṣī, "last year"), the "gone" year; घरी (gharī, "home"); बसने (basne, "by bus"), with the -ने of a tool; and पाऊस आला, "rain came", which is how Marathi says a downpour began.
[pause 2 seconds]
The scene is वाचत होतो, करत होती and पडत होता; the habit is यायचो. आईने … सांगितली and आम्ही भाकरी खाल्ली follow the thing: a story, a bhakri. Every other verb is an event.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: what Arun was doing on the train when the rain came]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: what Mother was doing when he came home]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which season Arun likes, with ऋतू (ṛtū)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 20 seconds]
What did Arun use to do every day when he was small?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Why is पडत होता in the scene form? (The rain was going on while they sat.) Which word starts the last event? (शेवटी.)
