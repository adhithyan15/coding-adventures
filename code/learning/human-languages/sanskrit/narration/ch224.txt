Lesson 1 of 5.
केन्द्रम् (kendram) — a centre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a hermitage, then the Sanskrit for a monastery.

You'll want to know: केन्द्रम्.
केन्द्रम् — kendram — "a centre".

The word, taken apart.
kendra was borrowed from Greek kentron, "point, centre of a circle" (MW), with Greek astronomy. English centre comes from the same Greek word through Latin centrum.

Lesson 2 of 5.
अट्टालिका (aṭṭālikā) — a tall building.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: अट्टालिका.
अट्टालिका — aṭṭālikā — "a tall building".

The word, taken apart.
aṭṭālikā is built on aṭṭāla, "watch-tower" (CDIAL 185), from aṭṭa, "high; tower". CDIAL 180 reports Mayrhofer's view that aṭṭa is non-Aryan, so it was probably borrowed. MW glosses aṭṭālikā "a palace".

Lesson 3 of 5.
अन्धकारः (andhakāraḥ) — darkness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a centre, then the Sanskrit for a tall building.

You'll want to know: अन्धकारः.
अन्धकारः — andhakāraḥ — "darkness".

The word, taken apart.
andhakāra is built on andha, "blind, dark", and kāra, "making", from the root √kṛ (CDIAL 386): what makes blind. It lives on as Gujarati ãdhārũ and Marathi ãdhār, "darkness".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ज्योतिः (jyotiḥ) — radiance, a flame.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a tall building, then the Sanskrit for darkness.

You'll want to know: ज्योतिः.
ज्योतिः — jyotiḥ — "radiance, a flame".

The word, taken apart.
jyotiḥ is the nominative of jyotis, "light", built on the root √jyut, "to shine", which MW calls a form of √dyut. It is a Rigvedic word (CDIAL 5300), and jyotsnā, "moonlight", comes from the same root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
भूमिः (bhūmiḥ) — the ground, land.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for darkness, then the Sanskrit for radiance.

You'll want to know: भूमिः.
भूमिः — bhūmiḥ — "the ground, land".

The word, taken apart.
bhūmi is built on the root √bhū, "to be, become" (MW), the root of bhavati: the earth is where things come to be. It is a Rigvedic word (CDIAL 9557). MW compares √bhū with English be.
