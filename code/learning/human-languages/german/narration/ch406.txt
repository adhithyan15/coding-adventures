German, chapter 406: Einen Platz finden.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
einparken — to park (in a space).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the dent, then the German for to dent.

You'll want to know: einparken.
einparken — "to park (in a space)".
Sie parkt das große Auto ohne Probleme ein. She parks the big car without any problems.
Separable: ich parke ein, ich habe eingeparkt. Parken alone is more general.

The word, taken apart.
einparken is built from ein- 'in' and parken 'to park', which is usually traced to English to park. EtymDB does not list the German verb, so the line is hedged.

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Parklücke — the parking space (between cars).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to park, then the German for to pull out.

You'll want to know: die Parklücke.
die Parklücke — "the parking space (between cars)".
Wir suchen zwanzig Minuten eine Parklücke. We look for a parking space for twenty minutes.
Plural: die Parklücken. Die Lücke — the gap.

The word, taken apart.
Parklücke is built from parken 'to park' and Lücke 'gap', inherited from Old High German luccha (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
das Parkhaus — the multi-storey car park (US: parking garage).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to pull out, then the German for the parking space.

You'll want to know: das Parkhaus.
das Parkhaus — "the multi-storey car park (US: parking garage)".
Das Parkhaus am Bahnhof ist am Wochenende voll. The car park at the station is full at the weekend.
Plural: die Parkhäuser. An underground one is die Tiefgarage.

The word, taken apart.
Parkhaus is built from parken 'to park' and Haus 'house', inherited from Proto-Germanic hūsą (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
der Parkschein — the parking ticket (from a machine).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the parking space, then the German for the multi-storey car park.

You'll want to know: der Parkschein.
der Parkschein — "the parking ticket (from a machine)".
Den Parkschein legt er hinter die Windschutzscheibe. He puts the parking ticket behind the windscreen.
Plural: die Parkscheine. It comes from a machine, der Parkautomat.

The word, taken apart.
Parkschein is built from parken and Schein 'certificate, slip', which first meant 'shine, appearance' and goes back to Proto-Germanic skīnaną 'to shine' (EtymDB).
