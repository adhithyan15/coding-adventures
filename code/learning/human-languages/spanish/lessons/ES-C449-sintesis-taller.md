# (dejar la bici en el taller) — read it, then do it yourself

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say **arreglar** and **el freno**. Both are on the slip below.

## Guided Practice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-02, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03] -->

The slip handed back across the counter. Read it, then answer.

> **Taller de bicicletas — resguardo**

> Bicicleta azul, sin cesta. Entra el martes.

> Los **frenos** de atrás no van. Se cambian.

> La **rueda** delantera está bien; solo hay que apretarla.

> Presupuesto: 24 €. Se **arregla** para el jueves.

[PAUSE 3s]
- [YOU SAY: which part is being replaced and which is only being tightened]
- [YOU SAY: what it will cost and when it will be ready]
- [YOU SAY: what day the bike went in]

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05] -->

Look at **se cambian** and **se arregla**. Neither says who does the work,
and on a repair slip that is the point: the shop is not the subject of a
sentence, the job is. Spanish gets there with *se* plus the verb, and the verb
agrees with the thing being done — *frenos* is plural so *cambian* is plural,
*la bicicleta* is singular so *arregla* is singular.

English reaches for the passive here: *the brakes are being changed*. Spanish
reaches for *se*. Once you notice it you will see it on every notice and every
menu: *se alquila*, *se vende*, *se ruega*.

And **solo hay que apretarla** does the same job a third way — no subject at
all, only a thing that must be done.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-01, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-02, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-04, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-05] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Now your turn, out loud and then in writing. Four lines handing a
bike over: say what is wrong with the brakes, say the front wheel is fine, ask
whether they can fix it by Friday, and ask them to pass you the drill so you
can do one part yourself.

Then check yourself: did you use *se* for the work being done, and did the
verb agree with the part rather than with the shop?
