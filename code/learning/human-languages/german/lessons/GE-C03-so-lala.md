# so lala — "so-so," and the tune carries it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-GUT-02, GE-LEX-NICHT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can now say *gut* and *nicht so gut*. Between them sits the
honest middle, and German fills it with a word that means nothing whatsoever.

## You'll want to know: so lala
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-SO-LALA-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **so lala** — "so-so, middling, could be worse."

Said *zo LAH-lah*, with the voice rising and falling across the *lala* — a
shrug set to music. Say it flat and it lands wrong; the tune is most of the
meaning.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SO-LALA-01] -->

There is nothing to take apart, and that is the point. **so** is English **so**.
**lala** is not a word at all — it is the same *lalala* you hum when you do not
know the lyrics, pressed into service as a meaning.

This is not a German quirk. Reach for "so-so" in almost any language and you
find a small repeated noise doing the work, because a shrug is easier to sing
than to say.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SO-LALA-01] -->

The shrug collection, four deep across the books in this series:

| Language | The shrug | Literally |
|---|---|---|
| German | *so lala* | "so la-la" |
| French | *comme ci, comme ça* | "like this, like that" |
| Spanish | *más o menos* | "more or less" |
| Italian | *così così* | "so so" |

Three of the four work by **repetition** — say a small thing twice and it comes
out meaning *neither one nor the other*.
