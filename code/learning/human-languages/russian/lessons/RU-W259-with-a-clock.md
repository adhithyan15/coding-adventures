# The same writing, with a clock

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 1s] A task you can only do untimed is not yet a task you can do.

## Writing — timed production
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-YA, RU-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-FIRST-PERSON-U, RU-SCRIPT-L-01, RU-SCRIPT-M-01, RU-SCRIPT-T-01, RU-PUNCT-TOCHKA-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: timed-assessment-production -->

**What the target publishes.** Russian's A1 exam is the state test TORFL at its
Elementary Level, **ТЭУ**. The Pushkin Institute describes it as **five
separate subtests** — Lexis and Grammar, Reading, Writing, Listening and
Speaking — taking about three and a half hours in all.

What this book does **not** quote is the writing paper's own minutes or length.
It has not yet transcribed the current paper into `task-shapes/a1.json`, so
check the provider's current materials before the exam you book.

[PAUSE 2s] So the clock here is this book's, not the exam's, and it is short.
Set a **three-minute timer**. Write by hand to one reader you name: who you
are, what you do, what you like. Lines you own, a dot at the end of each.

[PAUSE 2s] When the three-minute timer rings, **stop**, even mid-word.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-SCRIPT-L-01, RU-SCRIPT-M-01, RU-SCRIPT-T-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Spend at most one minute checking, and do not rewrite the timed
lines. Hurry shows in the hand first: check **л** against **м**, and the bar
over **т**, before you check anything else.

[PAUSE 2s] Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson, because it changes
how you study rather than how you write today.

[PAUSE 2s] TORFL scores each subtest **on its own**, and this book's target is
**66 percent in every one of the five**. Some TORFL materials describe a
narrower allowance for one subtest; this book does not plan around it.

[PAUSE 2s] So the paper that decides your result is **the one you are worst
at**, and there are five of them, not four. Strong reading cannot carry weak
writing.
