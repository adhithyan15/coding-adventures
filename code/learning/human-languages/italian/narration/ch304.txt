Lesson 1 of 6.
il terremoto — the earthquake.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bang, then the Italian for the collapse.

You'll want to know: il terremoto.
il terremoto — "the earthquake".
Il terremoto è durato venti secondi. The earthquake lasted twenty seconds.
A lively child is also un terremoto.

The word, taken apart.
terremoto is inherited from Latin terrae mōtus "a moving of the earth" (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: le macerie.
le macerie — "the rubble, the debris".
Sotto le macerie c'è ancora qualcuno. There is still someone under the rubble.
Mostly plural.

The word, taken apart.
macerie is a learned borrowing from Latin māceria "a garden wall" (Lewis & Short): first a dry-stone wall, later its fallen stones.

Lesson 4 of 6.
lo sfollato — the evacuee, the displaced person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the tremor, then the Italian for the rubble.

You'll want to know: lo sfollato.
lo sfollato — "the evacuee, the displaced person".
Gli sfollati dormono nella palestra della scuola. The evacuees are sleeping in the school gym.
From sfollare, to empty of people.

The word, taken apart.
sfollato is the past participle of sfollare "to empty of people", built on folla "crowd", which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin fullāre "to tread cloth", built on fullo "a fuller" (Lewis & Short).
