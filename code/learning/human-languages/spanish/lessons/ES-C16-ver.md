# ver — learn the verb before its past

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-VER]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] **Ver** means “to see.” Its written **v** uses the Spanish sound you
already know: it is not contrasted with a separate English-style **v** sound.
Say **ver**.

## Grammar Lens: three present forms
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-VER-PRESENT-SINGULAR]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VER, ES-GRAMMAR-ER-PRESENT-SINGULAR, ES-LEX-CAFE] -->

| person | present |
|---|---|
| yo | **veo** |
| tú | **ves** |
| él / ella / usted | **ve** |

The **tú** and third-person forms resemble the familiar **-er** row. The **yo**
form keeps an extra vowel: **veo**. Reuse known **café**: **Veo café** — “I see
coffee.”

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ETYMON-VER-VIDERE]; assesses=[ES-LEX-VER] -->

Spanish **ver** continues Latin **vidēre**, “to see,” through Old Spanish
**veer**. Loss of the consonant between vowels helps explain the shorter shape;
the two adjacent vowels later contracted in the infinitive. English **video**
belongs to the same Latin family, a memory bridge rather than a new Spanish
word for this lesson.
