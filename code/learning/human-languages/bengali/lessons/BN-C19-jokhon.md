## You'll want to know: যখন
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-LEX-C19-JOKHON-01]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C07-ASHA-01, BN-LEX-C10-CHA-01] -->

> **যখন আমি আসি, তখন চা খাই** — *jôkhon āmi āshi, tôkhon chā khāi* — **when I come, then I drink tea.**

Bengali does not leave the pair half-finished the way English does. **যখন** opens
the clause and **তখন** answers it, and a fluent speaker says both. English would
say *when I come, I drink tea* and let the *then* be understood; Bengali puts it
in.

## Grammar Lens: one frame, three prefixes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-LEX-C19-JOKHON-01, BN-GRAMMAR-C19-JE-01] -->

| word | prefix | job |
|---|---|---|
| **যখন** | **য-**, the relative | when … (opens a clause) |
| **তখন** | **ত-**, the demonstrative | then (answers it) |
| **কখন** | **ক-**, the interrogative | when? (asks) |

One ending, three heads. The same three letters begin **যে** (relative),
**তুমি** (demonstrative in origin) and **কি** and **কেমন** (interrogative) —
which is to say the reader has been meeting this pattern since chapter three
without being shown it.
