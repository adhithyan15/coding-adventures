Mandarin Chinese, chapter 66: The Weather.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
天气 (tiānqì) — weather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to open, then the Mandarin for to shut.

You'll want to know: 天气.
天气 — tiānqì — "weather".
今天天气很好 jīntiān tiānqì hěn hǎo — The weather is lovely today.
The new character, in pieces: 气 (qì) — a short fall, two short bars, and a long stroke that turns, slants out and hooks. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
天气 (tiānqì) is built from 天 'sky' and 气 (qì) 'breath', both inherited from Middle Chinese, then and khj+jH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the breath of the sky.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how you are.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for to open, then the Mandarin for to shut, then say tiānqì again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 天气 (tiānqì) mean? ("Weather".) Say it once more.

Script — a fall, two bars, a hook.
气 (qì).
One piece, four strokes.

Two short horizontals under it, the second a little lower and shorter.
A long horizontal that turns down, slants out to the right and hooks up at the end.

Lesson 3 of 7.
雨 (yǔ) — rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to shut, then the Mandarin for weather.

You'll want to know: 雨.
雨 — yǔ — "rain".
请问明天有雨吗 qǐngwèn míngtiān yǒu yǔ ma — Excuse me, will it rain tomorrow?
The new character, in pieces: 雨 (yǔ) — a lid, a frame with a line down its middle, and four drops inside. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
雨 (yǔ) is inherited from Middle Chinese hjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which the dictionary explains, through an older book, as water coming down from the clouds.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — tiān, qì]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — a lid, a frame and four drops.
雨 (yǔ).
One piece, built from the top down.
A horizontal across the top, the lid.
A frame, two strokes: a short vertical down the left, then a horizontal that turns down the right side and hooks to the left.
A vertical down the middle from the lid.
Four dots, two on the left of the middle line, then two on the right.
1 + 2 + 1 + 4 = eight.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 雨 (yǔ), all eight, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 雨 (yǔ)? (Eight.) What comes last? (The four dots, the left pair first.) Which stroke hooks? (The right side of the frame.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 雨 (yǔ), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 5 of 7.
xiàyǔ — to rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for weather, then the Mandarin for rain.

You'll want to know: xiàyǔ.
xiàyǔ — "to rain".
xiàyǔ wǒmen bù dǎqiú — It's raining, so we're not playing ball.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
xiàyǔ is built from xià 'to come down' and 雨 (yǔ) 'rain', both inherited from Middle Chinese, haeX and hjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): rain comes down.
