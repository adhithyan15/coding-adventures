Portuguese, chapter 458: Na serra.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a serra — the mountain range, the hills; the saw.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for cork, then the Portuguese for to prune.

You'll want to know: a serra.
a serra — "the mountain range, the hills; the saw".
Passámos o fim de semana na serra. We spent the weekend in the mountains.
A ridge of peaks looks like the teeth of a saw. Serrar — to saw.

The word, taken apart.
serra is inherited from Latin serra 'saw' (EtymDB): a mountain ridge has the jagged outline of a saw's teeth.

Lesson 4 of 5.
o desfiladeiro — the gorge, the narrow pass.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the hillside, then the Portuguese for the summit.

You'll want to know: o desfiladeiro.
o desfiladeiro — "the gorge, the narrow pass".
O rio corre no fundo do desfiladeiro. The river runs at the bottom of the gorge.
A pass so narrow that people walk in single file.

The word, taken apart.
desfiladeiro is built on desfilar 'to march in file', which was borrowed from French défiler, on fil 'thread', Latin fīlum (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
escalar — to climb (a rock or a mountain).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the summit, then the Portuguese for the gorge.

You'll want to know: escalar.
escalar — "to climb (a rock or a mountain)".
Eles escalaram a rocha sem medo. They climbed the rock without fear.
Escalar uma montanha — to climb a mountain. In Brazil, escalar also means to pick players for a team.

The word, taken apart.
escalar is built from escala 'ladder' and -ar (EtymDB); escala is a learned borrowing from Latin scāla 'flight of steps'.
