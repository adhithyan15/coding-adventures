Lesson 1 of 5.
vītis, vītis — a vine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for hide, then the Latin for linen.

You'll want to know: vītis, vītis.
vītis, vītis — "a vine".

The word, taken apart.
vītis is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European weh1itis, "a twining plant", from weh1y-, "to twist, to weave" (EtymDB 2.1); vieō, "to plait", is from the same root (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
quercus, quercūs — an oak.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for linen, then the Latin for a vine.

You'll want to know: quercus, quercūs.
quercus, quercūs — "an oak".

The word, taken apart.
quercus is inherited, probably from Proto-Italic kwerkus, "oak" (EtymDB 2.1). It is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European perkwus, "oak", with the first sound changed to match the second, as in coquō, "to cook". English fir is a distant cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
laurus, laurī — a laurel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an oak, then the Latin for a pine.

You'll want to know: laurus, laurī.
laurus, laurī — "a laurel".

The word, taken apart.
laurus has no sure origin; it is usually thought to be a loan from a lost Mediterranean language. German Lorbeer, "laurel", was borrowed from it.
