Lesson 1 of 5.
स्वामी (svāmī) — a master, an owner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a maternal grandfather, then the Sanskrit for a father's brother.

You'll want to know: स्वामी.
स्वामी — svāmī — "a master, an owner".

The word, taken apart.
svāmī is the nominative of svāmin, built from sva "own" with the suffix -min (MW; CDIAL 13930): "one who has property", an owner, a master. MW sets sva beside Latin suus "his own".

सेवकः — sevakaḥ — "a servant, an attendant".

The word, taken apart.
sevaka is built from the root √sev "attend on, serve" with the agent suffix -aka (CDIAL 13592): "one who attends", a servant. MW keeps it apart from a rare sevaka "one who sews", from √siv.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a servant, then the Sanskrit for a queen.

You'll want to know: मन्त्री.
मन्त्री — mantrī — "a minister".

The word, taken apart.
mantrī is the nominative of mantrin "wise; a counsellor", built on mantra "counsel, sacred text" (CDIAL 9838). mantra comes from the root √man "think", which MW sets beside Latin monere "warn" and English mean.

The word, taken apart.
senāpati is built from senā "army" and pati "lord, master" (CDIAL 13589), already in the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa. MW sets pati beside Greek posis "husband" and Latin potis "able".
