German, chapter 504: Säen und umgraben.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
umgraben — to dig over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for watering can, then the German for compost.

You'll want to know: umgraben.
umgraben — "to dig over".
Im Frühling graben wir das Beet um. In spring we dig over the flower bed.
Separable: er gräbt um, hat umgegraben.

The word, taken apart.
umgraben is built on um "around, over" and graben "to dig", inherited from Old High German graban, from Proto-Germanic grabaną (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: säen.

Im April säen wir Karotten und Salat. In April we sow carrots and lettuce.

The word, taken apart.
säen is inherited from Old High German sāen, from Proto-Germanic sēaną "to sow" (EtymDB); English sow is a cousin.

You'll want to know: die Ernte.
die Ernte — "harvest".
Die Ernte war dieses Jahr sehr gut. The harvest was very good this year.
Plural: die Ernten. The verb: ernten — to harvest.

The word, taken apart.
Ernte is usually traced to Old High German arnōt "harvest", built on an old Germanic word for the harvest season; EtymDB lists the German word without a source.
