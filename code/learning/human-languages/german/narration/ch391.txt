German, chapter 391: Steuern und Zinsen.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
die Steuer — the tax.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the fortune, then the German for the payment.

You'll want to know: die Steuer.
die Steuer — "the tax".
Auf Zigaretten zahlt man hohe Steuern. You pay high taxes on cigarettes.
Plural: die Steuern. Careful: das Steuer is the steering wheel.

The word, taken apart.
Steuer "tax" is usually traced to Old High German stiura "a prop, a support", and so a contribution; EtymDB lists English steer as a cousin of the word.

Lesson 2 of 5.
der Zins — the interest (on money).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the payment, then the German for the tax.

You'll want to know: der Zins.
der Zins — "the interest (on money)".
Die Bank zahlt nur ein Prozent Zinsen. The bank pays only one per cent interest.
Mostly plural: die Zinsen. In southern Germany and Austria der Zins can also mean rent.

The word, taken apart.
Zins is inherited from Middle High German and Old High German zins "tax, tribute, interest", an early borrowing from Latin cēnsus "assessment, tax" (EtymDB), from cēnsēre "to assess".

Lesson 3 of 5.
investieren — to invest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the tax, then the German for the interest.

You'll want to know: investieren.
investieren — "to invest".
Sie hat ihr Geld in eine kleine Firma investiert. She invested her money in a small firm.
Perfekt: hat investiert. Zeit investieren — to invest time.

The word, taken apart.
investieren goes back to Latin investīre "to clothe, to cover" (Lewis & Short), built on vestis "garment"; the money sense is usually said to have come later, through Italian investire.

Lesson 4 of 5.
anschaffen — to acquire, to buy (something lasting).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the interest, then the German for to invest.

You'll want to know: anschaffen.
anschaffen — "to acquire, to buy (something lasting)".
Wir wollen uns eine neue Waschmaschine anschaffen. We want to buy a new washing machine.
Separable: wir schaffen an, wir haben angeschafft. Often with sich. Die Anschaffung — the purchase.

The word, taken apart.
anschaffen is built from an and schaffen "to make, to get done", inherited from Old High German scaffan and Proto-Germanic skapjaną "to create" (EtymDB), a cousin of English shape.

Lesson 5 of 5.
versichern — to insure; to assure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to invest, then the German for to acquire.

You'll want to know: versichern.
versichern — "to insure; to assure".
Wir haben das Haus gegen Feuer versichert. We have insured the house against fire.
Inseparable: er versichert, er hat versichert. Also to assure: Ich versichere dir, dass … Die Versicherung — the insurance.

The word, taken apart.
versichern is built with ver- on sicher "safe", inherited from Old High German sihhur, which goes back to Latin sēcūrus "free from care" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): you make a thing safe.
