## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C309-KIN-18]; assesses=[] -->

**el pariente** --- "the relative."

Latin **parere** meant to bring forth, to give birth. The one giving birth was a
**parens**, and that is English *parent*. It is also *postpartum*, "after the
bringing forth," and it is hiding inside *viper* --- *vivipara*, the creature that
bears its young alive.

Now the warning, and it matters more than the etymology. *Pariente* does **not**
mean parent. It means any relative at all --- the cousin, the aunt, the
grandmother. This is your second false friend, and a sharper one than *chico*,
because here the words really are related and the meanings still drifted apart.

One more caution, because the resemblance is very convincing. English *apparent*
looks like it belongs here and does not: that is Latin *apparēre*, "to appear,"
a different verb that happens to look almost identical. Two Latin verbs, nearly
the same shape, unrelated in sense.

Related words can mislead you exactly because they are related.
