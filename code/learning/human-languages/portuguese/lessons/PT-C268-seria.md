# seria — it would be

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-CONDICIONAL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Build "I would speak". (*Falaria.*) Ask politely for help with
*poder*. (*Poderia ajudar-me?*)

## You'll want to know: seria
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-SERIA-01]; assesses=[PT-LEX-SER-02, PT-ETYMON-SER-ROOTS-02, PT-LEX-C66-ADJ3-04] -->

**seria** — "it would be", "he or she would be".

**ser** refused the imperfeito's recipe and gave you *era*. Its condicional
does not refuse: the whole infinitive plus **-ia**, **seria**, **serias**,
**seria**, **seríamos**, **seriam**. *Seria* is the one you will use most,
because it judges a situation rather than a person:

> *Seria difícil.* — It would be difficult.
> *Seria mais rápido de comboio.* — It would be quicker by train.

*De comboio*, "by train", and *de autocarro*, "by bus", are how Portugal says
how you travel.

## Grammar Lens: seria bom
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-SERIA-BOM-02]; assesses=[PT-GRAMMAR-BOM-04, PT-LEX-C214-PORQUE214-02, PT-LEX-DEPENDE-01, PT-LEX-C67-ADJ4-01] -->

**Seria bom** + infinitive — "it would be good to, it would be nice to":

> *Seria bom viajar juntos.* — It would be nice to travel together.

That is a wish with the person left out, softer than *gostaria* and less
private than *espero*. And it sits naturally after a *depende*, as the verdict
on each branch:

> *Depende. De comboio seria mais rápido, mas de autocarro seria mais barato.*

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ser** is inherited from Latin *sedēre*, "to sit" (EtymDB 2.1), as chapter
sixteen showed, and **-ia** is the worn Latin *habēbat*, "he had", of the last
lesson. So *seria* began as something like "had to be". Neither source records
forms this late, so that step is the usual explanation rather than a cited
one.
