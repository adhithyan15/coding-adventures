German, chapter 455: Wasser in der Natur.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Wasserfall — the waterfall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to climb down, then the German for to rest.

You'll want to know: der Wasserfall.
der Wasserfall — "the waterfall".
Der Wasserfall ist nach dem Regen sehr laut. The waterfall is very loud after the rain.
Plural: die Wasserfälle.

The word, taken apart.
Wasserfall is built from Wasser 'water' and Fall 'fall', both inherited: Fall goes back to Old High German val (EtymDB).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 3 of 5.
der Tümpel — the small pond, the pool.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the waterfall, then the German for the marsh.

You'll want to know: der Tümpel.
der Tümpel — "the small pond, the pool".
Im Tümpel hinter dem Haus leben Frösche. Frogs live in the little pond behind the house.
Same form in the plural. Smaller than a Teich.

The word, taken apart.
Tümpel is inherited through Middle High German tümpfel from Old High German tumphilo 'pool' (EtymDB). EtymDB lists English dimple as a cousin.

Lesson 4 of 5.
plätschern — to splash, to babble (of water).

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: plätschern.
plätschern — "to splash, to babble (of water)".
Im Garten plätschert ein kleiner Brunnen. A little fountain splashes in the garden.
A gentle, pleasant sound of water: das Plätschern des Wassers.

The word, taken apart.
plätschern is a sound word that imitates water splashing; EtymDB does not list it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fließen — to flow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the small pond, then the German for to splash.

You'll want to know: fließen.
fließen — "to flow".
Der Fluss fließt langsam durch das Tal. The river flows slowly through the valley.
Strong; Perfekt with sein: fließt, floss, ist geflossen.

The word, taken apart.
fließen is inherited from Old High German fliozan and Proto-Germanic fleutaną (EtymDB). English fleet in its old sense 'to float' is a cousin.
