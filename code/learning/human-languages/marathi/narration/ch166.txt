Lesson 1 of 5.
वाया घालवणे (vāyā ghālavṇe) — to waste.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to observe, then the Marathi for to take part.

You'll want to know: वाया घालवणे.
वाया घालवणे — vāyā ghālavṇe — "to waste".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of वाया, "in vain", is not settled: CDIAL has no entry for it. घालवणे is the "make" form of घालणे, which CDIAL lists, as "to pour", under a reconstructed ghalyati, "pours" (CDIAL 4457).

Lesson 4 of 5.
वाहून नेणे (vāhūn neṇe) — to carry, to transport.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to keep quiet, then the Marathi for to be afraid.

You'll want to know: वाहून नेणे.
वाहून नेणे — vāhūn neṇe — "to carry, to transport".

The word, taken apart.
वाहून is "having carried", from Marathi vāhṇẽ, "to carry", which CDIAL lists under Sanskrit vāhayati, "drives (a chariot)" (CDIAL 11612). नेणे comes from Sanskrit nayati, "leads, carries off" (CDIAL 6966).

Lesson 5 of 5.
मारणे (mārṇe) — to hit; to kill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to be afraid, then the Marathi for to carry.

You'll want to know: मारणे.
मारणे — mārṇe — "to hit; to kill".

The word, taken apart.
मारणे (mārṇe) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mārayati, "kills" (CDIAL 10066). It is the "make" form of mṛ, "to die": to kill is to make die. CDIAL glosses the Marathi verb "to strike, kill".
