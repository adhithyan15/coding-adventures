# இருக்கை (irukkai) — a seat

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C69-GOING-02, TA-LEX-C69-GOING-03, TA-LEX-C174-IDAM174-05, TA-LEX-IRU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say the words for a bus, a train and a passenger. (*pēruntu*, *rayil*, *payaṇi*.) Then "to be, to stay". (*iru*.)

## You'll want to know: இருக்கை
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C289-IRUKKAI-01]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C02-NII-NIINGAL-01, TA-GRAMMAR-C75-POLAR-01, TA-LEX-C69-GOING-02, TA-LEX-C69-GOING-03] -->

**இருக்கை** — *irukkai* — "a seat".

> **இது உங்கள் இருக்கையா?**
>
> *itu uṅkaḷ irukkaiyā?* — "Is this your seat?"

> **பேருந்தில் இருக்கை இல்லை.**
>
> *pēruntil irukkai illai.* — "There is no seat on the bus."

You can hear **இரு** at its front: a seat is a place to be. On a bus, in a train, in a cinema or a hall, **இருக்கை** is the word for the place you sit.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**இருக்கை** (*irukkai*) is native Dravidian. DEDR 480 lists Tamil *irukkai*, "sitting, seat, residence", under *iru*, which it glosses "to exist, remain, sit down". So the verb you know as "to be, to stay" also meant "to sit", and the seat is named from it.
