# je devrais — what you ought to do about it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FR-LEX-DEVOIR-03, FR-GRAMMAR-MODAL-CHAIN-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I must leave". (*Je dois partir.*) Then ask a stranger for a
coffee. (*Je voudrais un café.*)

[PAUSE 1s] An explanation often ends on a third move: what somebody ought to
do. That move has its own form, and you already own its ending.

## You'll want to know: je devrais
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-JE-DEVRAIS-01]; assesses=[FR-LEX-TRAVAILLER-06, FR-LEX-DORMIR-04, FR-LEX-C235-VERBES235-03] -->

**je devrais** — "I should".

It is *devoir* with the soft ending of *je voudrais*: **je devrais**, **tu
devrais**, **il devrait**, **vous devriez**. A bare infinitive follows, with
nothing in between, exactly as after *je dois*:

> **Je devrais** travailler. — I should work.
> **Tu devrais** dormir. — You should sleep. *(to a friend)*
> **Vous devriez** vous reposer. — You should rest. *(to anyone you call
> vous)*

*Je dois travailler* has no way out. *Je devrais travailler* says I know I
ought to, and have not promised. That softness is the register advice
usually wants. The ending is the one in *voudrais*, and chapter two hundred
and sixty-seven gives the whole form its name and its recipe.

## Grammar Lens: advice, with its reason
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-GRAM-TU-DEVRAIS-02]; assesses=[FR-LEX-ON-01, FR-GRAM-DEUX-FLECHES-01, FR-GRAM-JE-PENSE-QUE-01] -->

Advice sits naturally at the end of an arrow:

> *Tu es fatigué, **donc tu devrais** dormir.* — You're tired, so you should
> sleep.
> ***Je pense que tu devrais** te reposer.* — I think you should rest.

With **on**, it becomes "we ought to": ***On devrait** partir.* — We ought
to leave. Said late in an evening, it is a polite way to end it.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**devoir** is inherited from Latin *dēbēre*, "to owe" (EtymDB 2.1), and
*dēbēre* is a compound: *dē*, "from", plus *habēre*, "to have" (Lewis &
Short, under *debeo*). To owe is to **have something from someone**: you
are holding what is theirs. So *je devrais* is, at root, "I would owe it":
the debt is there, and nobody is collecting yet.
