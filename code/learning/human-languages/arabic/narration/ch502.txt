Arabic, chapter 502: Talkers and the curious.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ثرثار (tharthār) — talkative; a chatterbox.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for sensible, then the Arabic for confidence.

You'll want to know: ثرثار.
ثرثار — tharthār — "talkative; a chatterbox".
جارنا ثرثار ويتكلم ساعات. jārunā tharthār wa-yatakallamu sāʿāt. Our neighbour is talkative and talks for hours.

The word, taken apart.
ثرثار (tharthār) goes with the verb tharthara, "to chatter"; its doubled shape, thar-thar, is usually said to echo endless talk, but the origin is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
متحفظ (mutaḥaffiẓ) — reserved.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for confidence, then the Arabic for talkative.

You'll want to know: متحفظ.
متحفظ — mutaḥaffiẓ — "reserved".
زوج خالتي رجل متحفظ ولا يتكلم كثيرا. zawj khālatī rajul mutaḥaffiẓ wa-lā yatakallamu kathīran. My aunt's husband is a reserved man and does not talk much.

The word, taken apart.
متحفظ (mutaḥaffiẓ) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-f-ẓ, "to keep, to guard" (Lane). Ḥafiẓa, to keep, which you know, is from the same root; mutaḥaffiẓ is one who keeps himself to himself.

Lesson 3 of 5.
فضولي (fuḍūlī) — nosy, curious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for talkative, then the Arabic for reserved.

You'll want to know: فضولي.
فضولي — fuḍūlī — "nosy, curious".
سأل الجار الفضولي عن راتبي. saʾala al-jār al-fuḍūlī ʿan rātibī. The nosy neighbour asked about my salary.
It can be mild (curious) or rude (nosy); the noun فضول is curiosity.

The word, taken apart.
فضولي (fuḍūlī) is built on the native Arabic root f-ḍ-l, "to be over, to be in excess" (Lane). Fuḍūl first meant what is more than enough; fuḍūlī is one who busies himself with what is not his.

Lesson 4 of 5.
حنون (ḥanūn) — affectionate, tender.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for reserved, then the Arabic for nosy.

You'll want to know: حنون.
حنون — ḥanūn — "affectionate, tender".
الأم الحنون تعانق طفلها. al-umm al-ḥanūn tuʿāniqu ṭiflahā. The tender mother hugs her child.
It often keeps one form for a man and a woman: الأم الحنون.

The word, taken apart.
حنون (ḥanūn) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-n-n, "to yearn, to feel tenderness" (Lane). The noun ḥanān is tenderness.
