German, chapter 469: Enttäuscht und erleichtert.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
enttäuschen — to disappoint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to get worked up, then the German for to rage.

You'll want to know: enttäuschen.
enttäuschen — "to disappoint".
Der Film hat mich enttäuscht. The film disappointed me.
Enttäuscht sein von — to be disappointed by or in. Inseparable: hat enttäuscht.

The word, taken apart.
enttäuschen is built on ent- "away" and täuschen "to deceive", from Middle High German tiuschen (EtymDB); it is usually explained as first meaning "to free someone from a delusion".

Lesson 3 of 5.
die Erleichterung — relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to disappoint, then the German for disappointment.

You'll want to know: die Erleichterung.
die Erleichterung — "relief".
Zu unserer Erleichterung war niemand verletzt. To our relief, nobody was hurt.
No plural in this sense. Erleichtert — relieved.

The word, taken apart.
Erleichterung is built on erleichtern "to make lighter", from leicht "light", which is inherited from Old High German līht, from Proto-Germanic linhtaz (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
aufatmen — to breathe a sigh of relief.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: aufatmen.
aufatmen — "to breathe a sigh of relief".
Nach der Prüfung atmeten alle auf. After the exam, everyone breathed a sigh of relief.
Separable: ich atme auf, hat aufgeatmet.

The word, taken apart.
aufatmen is built on auf "up" and atmen "to breathe", inherited from Old High German ātamōn, from Proto-Germanic ēþmaz "breath" (EtymDB).

Regular: hat geseufzt. The noun: der Seufzer — a sigh.

The word, taken apart.
seufzen comes from Middle High German siufzen (EtymDB); its older history is not settled.
