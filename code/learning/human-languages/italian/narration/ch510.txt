Italian, chapter 510: In campo.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
l'attaccante — forward, striker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for penalty, then the Italian for to book.

You'll want to know: l'attaccante.
l'attaccante — "forward, striker".
Il nostro attaccante è il più veloce del campionato. Our striker is the fastest in the league.
From attaccare "to attack".

The word, taken apart.
attaccante is built on attaccare "to attack, to attach"; the origin of attaccare is not settled, though a Germanic word for a stake is often suggested.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: il difensore.
il difensore — "defender".
Il difensore ha fermato l'attaccante. The defender stopped the striker.
In court it is also the lawyer for the defence.

The word, taken apart.
difensore is a learned borrowing from Latin dēfēnsor "a defender" (EtymDB), from dēfendere "to ward off".

You'll want to know: il centrocampista.
il centrocampista — "midfielder".
Gioco come centrocampista nella squadra della scuola. I play midfield in the school team.
Built on centrocampo "midfield".

The word, taken apart.
centrocampista is built on centro "centre", from Latin centrum, and campo "field", from Latin campus (Lewis & Short), with -ista.

Lesson 4 of 6.

You'll want to know: il titolare.
il titolare — "first-team player; holder, owner".
Oggi gioco titolare, non in panchina. Today I am in the starting line-up, not on the bench.
Also the owner of a shop: il titolare del bar.

The word, taken apart.
titolare is a learned borrowing from Late Latin titulāris "of a title" (EtymDB), from titulus "an inscription, a title".

Lesson 5 of 6.
parare — to save (a shot); to ward off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for midfielder, then the Italian for first-team player.

You'll want to know: parare.
parare — "to save (a shot); to ward off".
Il portiere ha parato un tiro difficile. The goalkeeper saved a difficult shot.
La parata — a save. Also parare un colpo — to ward off a blow.

The word, taken apart.
parare is inherited from Latin parāre "to prepare, to make ready" (EtymDB); the sense "to ward off" grew from getting ready to meet a blow.
