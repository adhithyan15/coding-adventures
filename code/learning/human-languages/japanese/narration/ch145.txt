Japanese, chapter 145: Writing: Four Lines, and the Joint You Do Not Write.
1 lesson. It needs your eyes or your hands, so save it for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 1.
Four lines, and the joint you do not write — four connected lines to one reader, held together by time words and by the subject Japanese leaves out.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach it, but leave this section until you have stopped: Wrap-up recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson asked for writing finished on time. This one asks for writing that holds together.
[pause 1 second]
Pick one ともだち who will read it.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Connected writing is not longer writing. Four short lines can be connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a line stops standing on its own.
[pause 2 seconds]
In many languages the joints are little words between the sentences. This book has not taught you those for Japanese, and you do not need them yet. Your four lines are held together by two things you already own.
[pause 2 seconds]
The first is a time word at the front of each line. あさ, then ゆうがた: the reader follows your day because each line says where in it they are.
[pause 2 seconds]
The second is the joint you do not write. Chapter 138 taught you that Japanese leaves out who does it when that is clear. Say who once, or not at all, and every line after it leans on the one before. Name yourself in every line and you have written a list, not a message.

Writing — connected composition.
[pause 2 seconds]
Write four lines to the ともだち you picked. The first greets them with こんにちは. The other three walk through your day in order, each opening on a time word and closing on a polite verb: おきます, はたらきます, かえります or ねます.
[pause 3 seconds]
Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary; the whole exercise is the joining.
[pause 2 seconds]
End every line with 。, a small open circle, not a dot.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read your third line alone. It names no one: who comes home, or goes to bed? Only the lines before it say. It leans on its neighbours, and that is what connected means.
[pause 2 seconds]
Then count how many lines name who does it. If every line does, you wrote a list, which is where you were one lesson ago.
[once you have stopped driving — question: Write the polite verb that can close the last line of your day: going to bed.]
