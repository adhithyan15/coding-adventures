Japanese, chapter 216: Feeling Unwell.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
びょうき (byōki) — an illness; a disease.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a neck, then the Japanese for a cough.

You'll want to know: びょうき.
びょうき — byōki — "an illness; a disease".
こどもは びょうきですか。 — kodomo wa byōki desu ka — "Is the child ill?"

The word, taken apart.
びょうき (byōki) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are byō, "illness" (Middle Chinese bjaengH) and ki, "air, spirit" (khj+jH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
ぐあい (guai) — condition; how one feels.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: ぐあい.
ぐあい — guai — "condition; how one feels".
ぐあいは いいですか。 — guai wa ii desu ka — "Are you feeling well?"

The word, taken apart.
ぐあい (guai) is built from Sino-Japanese gu, "tool, means" (Middle Chinese gjuH), borrowed from Chinese, and native ai, "fitting", from au, "to meet, fit" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
つかれる (tsukareru) — to get tired.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for an illness, then the Japanese for condition.

You'll want to know: つかれる.
つかれる — tsukareru — "to get tired".
まいにち つかれますか。 — mainichi tsukaremasu ka — "Do you get tired every day?"
Polite つかれます, tsukaremasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
つかれる (tsukareru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
なおる (naoru) — to get better; to heal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for condition, then the Japanese for to get tired.

You'll want to know: なおる.
なおる — naoru — "to get better; to heal".
びょうきは もう なおりましたか。 — byōki wa mō naorimashita ka — "Has your illness got better yet?"
Polite なおります, naorimasu; a godan verb. Its partner なおす, naosu, means "to fix".

The word, taken apart.
なおる (naoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it pairs with naosu, "to fix" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
