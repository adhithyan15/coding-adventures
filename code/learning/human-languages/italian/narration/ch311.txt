Italian, chapter 311: Il giorno del sì.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sposarsi — to get married.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to settle in, then the Italian for to return to one's country.

You'll want to know: sposarsi.
sposarsi — "to get married".
Ci siamo sposati in un piccolo paese. We got married in a small village.
Sposare qualcuno — to marry someone.

The word, taken apart.
sposarsi is built on sposo "bridegroom", inherited from Latin spōnsus "one promised", from spondēre "to promise solemnly" (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: la fede.
la fede — "the wedding ring; faith".
Porta la fede alla mano sinistra. She wears her wedding ring on her left hand.
The ring is a sign of fede, faith and trust.

The word, taken apart.
fede is inherited from Latin fidēs "trust, faith" (EtymDB): the ring is a pledge of faith.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il bouquet — the bouquet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the wedding ring, then the Italian for the veil.

You'll want to know: il bouquet.
il bouquet — "the bouquet".
Alla fine la sposa lancia il bouquet. At the end the bride throws the bouquet.
From French; said bu-KEH.

The word, taken apart.
bouquet was borrowed from French bouquet "a little wood, a bunch"; EtymDB traces the French word to Old French bochet, a little wood, and has no Italian entry.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la bomboniera — the wedding favour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the veil, then the Italian for the bouquet.

You'll want to know: la bomboniera.
la bomboniera — "the wedding favour".
Abbiamo scelto bomboniere di vetro. We chose glass wedding favours.
A small gift with sugared almonds, given at weddings and christenings.

The word, taken apart.
bomboniera is usually traced to French bonbonnière "a sweet box", built on bonbon "sweet", from bon "good", Latin bonus (Lewis & Short).
