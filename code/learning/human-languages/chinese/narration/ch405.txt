Lesson 1 of 6.
zhídá — to go direct; to go without changing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a sleeping berth, then the Mandarin for a hard seat.

You'll want to know: zhídá.
zhídá — "to go direct; to go without changing".
zuò gāotiě kěyǐ zhídá Běijīng — The high-speed train takes you straight to Beijing.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhídá is built from zhí 'straight' and dá 'to get through', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drik and dat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'upright' and the second 'to reach through'.

进入 — jìnrù — "to go into".
wǎng qián zǒu jiù kěyǐ jìnrù chēzhàn — Go straight on and you can get into the station.
The new character, in pieces: 入 (rù) — two strokes: a short stroke falling to the left, then a long stroke falling to the right that starts higher, above the top of the first. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
进入 (jìnrù) is built from 进 'to go forward' and 入 (rù) 'to go in', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsinH and nyip (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'forward' and the second 'within' and 'to take in'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
入 (rù) — two strokes, the right one on top.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. Script — two strokes, the right one on top.
入 (rù).
One piece, two strokes.
First, a short stroke falling to the left, from the middle down to the lower left.
Then a long stroke falling to the right. It starts higher, above the top of the first, and sweeps down to the lower right.
In 人 the left stroke is the long one and the right stroke leans on it; in 入 (rù) it is the other way round.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 入 (rù), all two, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 入 (rù)? (Two.) Which one starts higher? (The second, falling to the right.) How is it unlike 人? (In 入 (rù) the right stroke is the long one.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
mòbānchē — last bus or train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go into a place, then the Mandarin for to go in and out.

You'll want to know: mòbānchē.
mòbānchē — "last bus or train".
wǒmen zuò mòbānchē qù jīchǎng — We're taking the last train to the airport.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
mòbānchē is built from mò 'last', bān 'a shift' and 车 'vehicle', all inherited from Middle Chinese, mat, paen and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first as the tip of a tree and 'the end', and the second as dividing jade tokens, so the sense 'a shift, a run' is not given there.
