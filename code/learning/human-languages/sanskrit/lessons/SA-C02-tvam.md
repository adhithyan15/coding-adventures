# त्वम् (tvam) — the familiar "you"

## Warm-up

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[SA-LEX-BHAVAN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The other "you" — the one you use with friends, and the ancestor of
English *thou*.

## Sounds you'll need

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-LEX-TVAM]; assesses=[] -->

*tvam* — one beat. The *tv-* is a single push, *t* and *v* said together, and
then the lips close on *m*.

## The word, taken apart

<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[SA-ETYMON-TVAM-THOU]; assesses=[] -->

*Tvam* (familiar "you") is from Proto-Indo-European \**tū* — the direct ancestor
of English archaic **thou**, Latin *tū*, French *tu*, and Hindi *tū*. Unlike
*bhavān*, it takes an ordinary second-person verb. Its possessive is *tava*
("your"), which you will need in the very next question.
