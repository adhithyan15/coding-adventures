# любить — "to love, and to like"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last verb of the chapter is English **love**, and it lets you say
every verb you own in a real sentence.

## You'll want to know first — любить
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-LEX-LYUBIT]; assesses=[RU-LEX-YA, RU-GRAMMAR-TWO-VERB-FAMILIES] -->

> **любить** — *lyubít'* — **to love**, and **to like**

One verb for both: with a person, *love*; with an activity, closer to *like*.

> **Я люблю.** — *ya lyublyú* — **I love.**

And **ты любишь** — an **-ишь**, so *любить* joins *говорить* and *видеть*, not
*знать*. The stress moves too: *lyublyú* on the ending, *lyúbish* on the stem.

## Grammar Lens: the letter that appears out of nowhere
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-GRAMMAR-L-EPENTHESIS]; assesses=[RU-GRAMMAR-CONSONANT-SWAP-I-FORM, RU-LEX-VIDET] -->

*Любить* has no **л** after the **б**. *Люблю* does.

You have met this shape. **Видеть** swapped **д** for **ж** in *вижу* and
nowhere else. Here it happens by insertion: stems ending in **б, п, в, м, ф**
push an **л** in — but **only in the *I* form**.

So **люблю**, then **любишь, любят**, with no **л** at all. Learn the *I* form
and the rest behaves.

## Grammar Lens: now say everything at once
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[RU-LEX-CHITAT, RU-LEX-PISAT, RU-LEX-DUMAT, RU-LEX-ZHIT, RU-LEX-POMOGAT, RU-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-FIRST-PERSON-U] -->

**Любить** takes a bare infinitive, as English *like* does — the dictionary
form goes straight in:

> **Я люблю читать.** — I like reading.
> **Я люблю писать.** — I like writing.
> **Я люблю думать.** — I like thinking.
> **Я люблю помогать.** — I like helping.
> **Я люблю жить.** — I love living.

## The word, taken apart — love, belief, and by your leave
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[RU-ETYMON-LYUBIT-LOVE]; assesses=[RU-ETYMON-BRAT-BEAR, RU-ETYMON-SPRASHIVAT-PRAY, RU-ETYMON-POMOGAT-MIGHT, RU-ETYMON-PISAT-PAINT, RU-GRAMMAR-ASPECT-PARTNER, RU-GRAMMAR-SUPPLETIVE-ASPECT-PAIR, RU-LEX-BRAT, RU-LEX-SPRASHIVAT] -->

**любить** is Indo-European \**leubʰ-*, and English **love** is the same word —
no borrowing, no detour. The family is wider than it looks: **lief**;
**believe** (*be-* plus the root — to hold a thing dear enough to trust);
**leave** in *by your leave*; and Latin *libet*, "it pleases", behind
**libido**.

Its partner **полюбить** deserves an honest line: not *to finish loving* but
**to come to love**, the moment the feeling starts. A pair does not always mean
*doing* against *done* — which is why they get a later chapter of their own.

And the root thread: **бр-** carries (*bear*), **-прос-** asks (*pray*),
**мог-** is able (*might*), **люб-** holds dear (*love*) — with **пис-** from last
chapter still scratching its mark (*picture*).
