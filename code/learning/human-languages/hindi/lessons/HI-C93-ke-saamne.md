# के सामने — in front of

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-03, HI-LEX-C69-GOING-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *near the house*. Now say the word for a railway station. You
are one word away from the sentence an A1 listening paper is most likely to
set you.

## You'll want to know: के सामने
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-04]; assesses=[] -->

**दुकान स्टेशन के सामने है।** — The shop is in front of the station.

## The two words
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C93-REL-03] -->

| Hindi | English |
|---|---|
| स्टेशन के पास | near the station |
| स्टेशन के सामने | in front of the station |

English changed from one word to three. Hindi changed from one word to one.

Notice what **सामने** does not do. It ends in **-ए**, which on an adjective
would be an ending that agrees with something — but this word never changes,
whoever or whatever is in front of whatever. **A slot-three word is fixed.**
The bending in this shape all happens in slot 2, and slot 2 is already done
before slot 3 arrives.

## Grammar Lens: front of what
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C93-REL-04, HI-LEX-C93-REL-01] -->

English *in front of* is ambiguous in a way Hindi's is not. *In front of the
station* can mean the near side of the building, or it can mean a position
further along the road. **के सामने** means the first: **facing it, across from
it, on the side you would stand to look at it.**

That is the reading a direction-giver intends, and the reading an exam item
expects.
