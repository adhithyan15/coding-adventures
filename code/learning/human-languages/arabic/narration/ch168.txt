Arabic, chapter 168: Arm, Body, Ankle, Throat, Headache.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ذراع (dhirāʿ) — an arm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to escape, to run away, then the Arabic for to disappear.

You'll want to know: ذراع.
ذراع — dhirāʿ — "an arm".

The word, taken apart.
ذراع (dhirāʿ) is built on the native Arabic root dh-r-ʿ, "the forearm; a cubit" (Lane). Hebrew zeroaʿ, "arm", is its cousin.

Lesson 3 of 5.
كاحل (kāḥil) — an ankle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an arm, then the Arabic for the body.

You'll want to know: كاحل.
كاحل — kāḥil — "an ankle".

The word, taken apart.
كاحل (kāḥil) has no entry in Lane. It shares its letters with k-ḥ-l, "to blacken the eyelids with kohl" (Lane), the root of kuḥl, "kohl", and of English alcohol, from al-kuḥl; how the ankle came by the word is not settled.

صداع (ṣudāʿ) is built on the native Arabic root ṣ-d-ʿ, "to split" (Lane). Ṣudāʿ has the fuʿāl shape of illness words, like suʿāl, "a cough".
