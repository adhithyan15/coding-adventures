## You'll want to know: lièchēyuán
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C403-TRAVELLER403-04]; assesses=[] -->

*lièchēyuán* — "train attendant".

> *zhǎo bu dào zuòwèi dehuà kěyǐ wèn lièchēyuán* — If you can't find your seat, ask the train attendant.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*lièchēyuán* is built from *liè* 'a row', 车 'vehicle' and *yuán* 'a member of staff', all inherited from Middle Chinese, *ljet*, *tsyhae* and *hjwen* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the first 'a row, an order' and the third as the number of things; a train is a row of carriages, and the sense 'staff' for the third is not given there.
