\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This book teaches Spanish one word at a time --- and it teaches each word by
taking it apart. A huge fraction of Spanish vocabulary is not arbitrary: it
is Latin, lightly worn by a thousand years of use, and English borrowed from
the same Latin so often that the two languages are full of hidden cousins.
So no word here is a fresh thing to memorize. \emph{gracias} arrives next to
\emph{grace}, \emph{gratitude}, \emph{gratuity}, \emph{ingrate}, and
\emph{congratulate} --- a family you have owned your whole life. The bet is
simple and, as far as we can tell, how the brain actually works: you learn
the new by welding it to the known.

Three things follow from that, and shape every lesson:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One word per lesson, gone deep.} Not ``the ten greetings''
    --- \emph{hola} is a lesson, \emph{buenos d\'{i}as} is a lesson. A few
    minutes each, the size of a single errand or commute.
  \item \textbf{The widest honest web of cousins.} Every word reaches for
    all the English relatives it can \emph{truthfully} claim --- because the
    more live connections a word has, the more places it sticks. False
    cousins are flagged, not smuggled in.
  \item \textbf{The reason, not the rule.} Why is it \emph{buenos d\'{i}as},
    plural? Because it is the fossil of a blessing, \emph{``may God give you
    good days.''} We learn the culture and the idiom hiding inside the
    grammar --- the things Spanish 101 skips.
\end{itemize}

Pronunciation is a short reference at the front, not a gate: each lesson
tells you the sounds it needs, when it needs them. A companion set of
practice lessons --- the same content sliced for spaced repetition in the
car --- lives beside this book in the repository. This book is the
continuous read; those are the practice.

A living document, grown one word at a time, released under CC~BY-SA~4.0 so
that if it helps anyone else learn Spanish this way, they are free to use,
adapt, and pass it on.
