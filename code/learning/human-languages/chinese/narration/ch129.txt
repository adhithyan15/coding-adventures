Mandarin Chinese, chapter 129: Quiet and Noisy.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ānjìng — quiet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for concert, then the Mandarin for guitar.

You'll want to know: ānjìng.
ānjìng — "quiet".
wǒ de fángjiān hěn ānjìng — My room is quiet.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ānjìng is built from ān 'peaceful' and jìng 'calm', both inherited from Middle Chinese, 'an and dzjengX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary glosses the second with the first.

Lesson 2 of 6.
吵 (chǎo) — noisy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for guitar, then the Mandarin for quiet.

You'll want to know: 吵.
吵 — chǎo — "noisy".
他的音乐太吵了 tā de yīnyuè tài chǎo le — His music is far too loud.
The new character, in pieces: 吵 (chǎo) — 口 on the left and 少 on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
吵 (chǎo) is inherited from Middle Chinese tsrhaewX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'sound', a different word from the chǎo of stir-frying, which has the same old form. The dictionary notes that the character first had another reading, mjiewX, glossed 'the call of a pheasant'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
吵 (chǎo) — 口, then 少.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script — 口, then 少 and Guided Practice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The word first, then the character inside it.
[your turn — recall: say chǎo — R1, one lesson back, before it decays]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
口 少.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — kǒu, shǎo]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — 口, then 少.
吵 (chǎo).
Two pieces, left before right, and both are old friends.
口, three strokes, small and narrow on the left: the left side, the top that turns down the right side, and the bar that closes the bottom.
少, four strokes, on the right: the vertical down the middle, the short stroke on its left, the dot on its right, then the long left-falling stroke that sweeps under them.
3 + 4 = seven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 吵 (chǎo), all seven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the two pieces of 吵 (chǎo)? (口 and 少.) Which side is narrow? (The left, 口.) What does the last stroke do? (It sweeps down to the left under 少.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 吵 (chǎo), pinned stroke paths and medians.
