# حكى (ḥakā) — to tell a story

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-GRAMMAR-C284-STORY-STEPS-01, AR-LEX-C99-WORLD99-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "first", "then", "after that". (*Awwalan*, *thumma*, *baʿda
dhālika*.) And the Arabic for a story. (*Qiṣṣa*.)

[PAUSE 1s] You can put a story in order now. This is the verb for handing it
on.

## You'll want to know: حكى
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[AR-LEX-C284-QISSA284-01]; assesses=[AR-LEX-C55-FAMILY55-01, AR-LEX-C86-ADJ86-01] -->

**حكى** — *ḥakā* — "he told (a story)", "to tell, to relate".

> **حكت جدتي قصة جميلة.** — *ḥakat jaddatī qiṣṣatan jamīla* — My
> grandmother told a lovely story.

It ends like *qaḍā*, and it bends the same way: *ḥakaytu*, I told, as
*qaḍaytu*, I spent. The present is *yaḥkī*, he tells, and with *kāna* in
front, *kānat taḥkī*, she used to tell.

*Qāla* reports what somebody said; *ḥakā* passes on a whole story, a piece of
news or something that happened.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**حكى** (*ḥakā*) is built on the native Arabic root *ḥ-k-y*, "to relate,
to narrate; to imitate" (Lane). Lane gives both senses side by side: to tell
a story is to say again what was said or done, and to imitate is to do again
what someone did.
