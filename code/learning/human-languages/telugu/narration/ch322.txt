Telugu, chapter 322: Feelings and Hopes.
5 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 5.
దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) — grief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "sorrow, distress" (bādha), "comfort, ease" (sukhaṁ) and "a story" (katha). Then "my bag is lost". (nā sañci pōyindi.)

You'll want to know: దుఃఖం.
దుఃఖం — duḥkhaṁ — "grief".
ఆ కథ చదివి నాకు దుఃఖం వచ్చింది. ā katha cadivi nāku duḥkhaṁ vaccindi. — "Reading that story, I was moved to grief."
బాధ is the everyday hurt and sadness of a bad day. దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) is heavier: grief, the sorrow that brings tears. Like a fever, it "comes" to you: నాకు దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) వచ్చింది.
The mark after దు is a breath, ḥ. Many speakers let it run into the next sound, close to dukkhaṁ. The next lesson is about that mark.

The word, taken apart.
దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) is borrowed from Sanskrit duḥkha, "difficulty, pain" (CDIAL 6375), with Telugu's noun ending -ం. CDIAL notes it was formed after sukha, the word you know as సుఖం: the two are a pair, ease and its opposite.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ః.
ః — the visarga, ḥ: a short breath after a vowel. It is two small rings, one above the other, written after its syllable: దు + ః becomes దుః, duḥ.
Do not confuse it with ం, one ring, which is ṁ. The visarga lives in words taken from Sanskrit, and most of them are learned words; దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) is the one you will meet most.

Writing: ః — copy the printed shape.
Write ః (ḥ) yourself, slowly, and larger than it is printed: two small rings the same size, one above the other, both on the right of the syllable they follow.
This book does not yet say where the pen starts on this shape, or which way it travels. That is taught with real variation from school to school, and it waits here until it can be written down from a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it puts the shape in your hand in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 5.
నాకు భయంగా ఉంది (nāku bhayaṅgā undi) — how I feel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "fear" (bhayaṁ), "anger" (kōpaṁ) and "happily" (santōṣaṅgā). Then say "grief". (duḥkhaṁ.) Which mark sits after its first syllable? (ః, the visarga, ḥ.)

You'll want to know: నాకు … -గా ఉంది.
నాకు చాలా సంతోషంగా ఉంది. nāku cālā santōṣaṅgā undi. — "I am very happy."
నాకు భయంగా ఉంది. nāku bhayaṅgā undi. — "I am afraid."
The one who feels is in the dative, నాకు, as in నాకు తెలుగు వచ్చు. The feeling takes -గా, and ఉంది, "it is", closes the sentence: "to me, fearfully, it is". So too నాకు బాధగా ఉంది, "I feel sad"; నాకు చలిగా ఉంది, "I feel cold"; నాకు ఆకలిగా ఉంది, "I am hungry".
Some feelings "come" instead, as దుఃఖం (duḥkhaṁ) does:
నాకు కోపం వచ్చింది. nāku kōpaṁ vaccindi. — "I got angry."

You'll want to know: … అనిపించింది, I felt.
రాత్రి ఒంటరిగా నడుస్తున్నప్పుడు నాకు భయంగా అనిపించింది. rātri oṇṭarigā naḍustunnappuḍu nāku bhayaṅgā anipiñcindi. — "Walking alone at night, I felt afraid."
For how a moment felt, put the -గా feeling before అనిపించింది, "it seemed": "to me, fearfully, it seemed". ఒంటరిగా (oṇṭarigā, "alone"). The scene is the -తున్నప్పుడు of a story; the feeling is the event in it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
వెళ్ళాలని ఉంది (veḷḷālani undi) — what I would like.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I must go" (nēnu veḷḷāli) and "I am happy" (nāku santōṣaṅgā undi). Then "a dream", "hope" and "a wish". (kala, āśa, kōrika.)

You'll want to know: నాకు … -ఆలని ఉంది.
నాకు తెలుగు బాగా నేర్చుకోవాలని ఉంది. nāku telugu bāgā nērcukōvālani undi. — "I would like to learn Telugu well."
నాకు మా ఊరికి వెళ్ళాలని ఉంది. nāku mā ūriki veḷḷālani undi. — "I would like to go to my home town."
Take the -ఆలి of "must", add అని, "saying": వెళ్ళాలి + అని becomes వెళ్ళాలని. Then నాకు … ఉంది: "to me there is (the thought) that I must go". The wish sits in the dative, like a feeling.

You'll want to know: my wish, my dream, my hope.
తెలుగులో ఒక పుస్తకం రాయాలని నా కోరిక. telugulō oka pustakaṁ rāyālani nā kōrika. — "My wish is to write a book in Telugu."
వైద్యుడు కావాలని నా కల. vaidyuḍu kāvālani nā kala. — "My dream is to become a doctor."
ఆ ఉద్యోగం వస్తుందని నా ఆశ. ā udyōgaṁ vastundani nā āśa. — "My hope is that I will get that job."
The wish comes first, closed by -ఆలని or -అని; నా కోరిక, నా కల or నా ఆశ comes last. In కావాలని, కా- has its plain sense, "to become". A job "comes" to you: ఉద్యోగం వస్తుంది.
