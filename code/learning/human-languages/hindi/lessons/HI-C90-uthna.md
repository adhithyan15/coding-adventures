# उठना — where a day starts

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C28-SUBAH-01, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can say **सुबह** and you can build a habit with **-ता**. Try to
say what you do first every morning, and find the verb missing.

## You'll want to know: उठना
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03]; assesses=[] -->

**उठना** (*uṭhnā*) — to get up, to rise.

**मैं सुबह उठता हूँ।** — I get up in the morning.
**मैं सुबह सात बजे उठती हूँ।** — I get up at seven in the morning.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-127] -->

The stem is **उठ-**, and it opens with a vowel where no consonant can carry the
mark — so the word begins with the **full letter उ**, which is why this is the
first word in the track to start that way.

The verb is broader than English *get up*. It covers a person leaving a bed, a
person standing from a chair, smoke rising, and a price going up. What all of
those share is **movement away from the ground**, and Hindi treats that as one
action.

Its opposite, **बैठना**, is *to sit* — the same idea in the other direction.

## Grammar Lens: it takes the endings you already have
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-03, HI-CONCEPT-C05-BOLTAHUN-01] -->

| what you mean | the form |
|---|---|
| I get up (habit) | उठता हूँ |
| I am getting up | उठ रहा हूँ |
| I used to get up | उठता था |
| I will get up | उठूँगा |

Four tenses on one new stem, and you had all four endings before you had the
verb. That is what a stem costs you now: **one word, and the whole grid comes
with it.**
