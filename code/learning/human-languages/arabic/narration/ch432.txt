Arabic, chapter 432: Fuel and engines.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
وقود (waqūd) — fuel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for congestion, then the Arabic for to turn off.

You'll want to know: وقود.
وقود — waqūd — "fuel".
اشترينا الوقود من محطة صغيرة. ishtaraynā al-waqūd min maḥaṭṭa ṣaghīra. We bought fuel at a small station.

The word, taken apart.
وقود (waqūd) is built on the native Arabic root w-q-d, "to burn, to blaze" (Lane). A waqūd is what is burned.

محرك (muḥarrik) — an engine; a motor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for fuel, then the Arabic for petrol.

You'll want to know: محرك.
محرك — muḥarrik — "an engine; a motor".
كان محرك السيارة صاخبا. kāna muḥarrik as-sayyāra ṣākhiban. The car's engine was noisy.

The word, taken apart.
محرك (muḥarrik) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-r-k, "to move" (Lane). It is the Form II participle, "the thing that sets something moving".

Lesson 5 of 5.
زود (zawwada) — to supply; to fill up (with).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an engine, then the Arabic for brakes.

You'll want to know: زود.
زود — zawwada — "to supply; to fill up (with)".
زود العامل السيارة بالبنزين. zawwada al-ʿāmil as-sayyāra bi-l-banzīn. The worker filled the car up with petrol.
Present: يزود, yuzawwid.
It takes bi- before the thing supplied.

The word, taken apart.
زود (zawwada) is built on the native Arabic root z-w-d, "provisions for a journey" (Lane). It is Form II, "to give someone provisions": the root of zād, food for the road.
