# mātrā kī tālikā — degree, on one page

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-04] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Name the word that hedges an answer and the word that asks a
quantity, and say what the two have in common.

## Grammar Lens: the five
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-01, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-02, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-04, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-05] -->

| what you want to do | what you say |
|---|---|
| hedge an answer | थोड़ा / थोड़ी |
| describe how an action went | अच्छा / बुरा |
| measure one thing against another | X से बड़ा |
| ask a quantity | कितना / कितनी |
| admire something | कितना अच्छा! |

**Every one of the five ends in -आ and bends.** That is the thread through the
chapter, and it means none of them is a new kind of word — they are adjectives
doing five different jobs.

## Grammar Lens: what Hindi does without
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-02, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C92-DEG-04] -->

| English has | Hindi has |
|---|---|
| good **and** well | one word |
| how much **and** how many | one word |
| bigger, more interesting | the adjective, unchanged |

Three places where English carries extra machinery and Hindi carries none. A
learner coming the other way has to build those distinctions; you get to drop
them.

## Grammar Lens: से, doing a third job
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C92-DEG-03, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-01] -->

| use | example |
|---|---|
| from | घर से |
| by way of | बस से |
| measured against | आदमी से बड़ा |

One postposition, three jobs, and the same idea under all three: the thing
**proceeds from** the noun. A comparison starts at the thing you measure
against.
