Mandarin Chinese, chapter 343: Far from Home.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
yuèguāng — moonlight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for sweet osmanthus flowers, then the Mandarin for one's native place.

You'll want to know: yuèguāng.
yuèguāng — "moonlight".
jīntiān wǎnshang de yuèguāng hěn hǎo — The moonlight is lovely tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
yuèguāng is built from 月 'the moon' and guāng 'light', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngjwot and kwang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'bright'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for one's native place, then the Mandarin for bright moonlight.

You'll want to know: 多年.
多年 — duōnián — "for many years".
我们多年没有一起过中秋了 wǒmen duōnián méiyǒu yìqǐ guò Zhōngqiū le — We haven't spent the moon festival together for years.

The word, taken apart.
多年 (duōnián) is built from 多 'many' and 年 'a year', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ta and nen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'many'.

You'll want to know: zhùfú.
zhùfú — "blessing; good wishes".
Zhōngqiū nà tiān wǒ shōudào le péngyou de zhùfú — On the day of the moon festival I got good wishes from my friends.

zhùfú is built from 祝 'to bless' and fú 'good fortune', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyuwH and pjuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'virtue' and 'divine help'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
fēnxiǎng — to share.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for deep in one's heart, then the Mandarin for good wishes sent to someone.

You'll want to know: fēnxiǎng.
fēnxiǎng — "to share".
wǒ hé tóngshì fēnxiǎng le wǒ de yuèbǐng — I shared my mooncakes with my colleagues.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fēnxiǎng is built from fēn 'to divide' and xiǎng 'to offer', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pjun and xjangX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hand out' and lists the second under an older spelling, 'to offer up'.
