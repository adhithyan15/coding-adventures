# शर्मीला — the ending that turns a feeling into a character

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **मेहनती**. You saw an ending build a person out of a noun. The
same thing happens here with a different ending, and this one bends.

## You'll want to know: शर्मीला
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04]; assesses=[] -->

**शर्मीला** (*śarmīlā*) — "shy, bashful."

Built from **शरम** / **शर्म** (*śaram*), "shame, modesty," plus the ending
**-ईला**.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

The ending **-ईला** is not the same as the **-ई** of *mehnatī*, and the
difference is exactly the difference this chapter is about.

**-ई** says *has this thing*. **-ईला** says *is made of this thing* — it builds
a settled quality, a disposition rather than an episode:

| noun | with **-ईला** |
|---|---|
| शरम — shame | शर्मीला — shy by nature |
| रंग — colour | रंगीला — colourful, flamboyant |
| नशा — intoxication | नशीला — intoxicating |

That is why **शर्मीला** is the right word for an exam question about character.
Someone can *feel* shame at a moment; a *śarmīlā* person is one for whom it is a
standing trait. This is settled character, not mood — the distinction the whole
chapter turns on.

**शर्म** itself is Persian, and it is the same word as English **shame**: both
descend from the same ancient root for covering oneself.

## Grammar Lens: back to the bending shape
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-04, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-03, HI-LEX-C83-CHAR-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C41-ADJ-SYSTEM] -->

| adjective | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| बुरा | बुरा | बुरी |
| मेहनती | मेहनती | मेहनती |
| शर्मीला | शर्मीला | शर्मीली |

**-ईला** ends in **-आ**, so the rule applies as usual and the word bends. The
long ending does not protect it: look at the **last letter**, always.
