Lesson 1 of 5.
गुल्फः (gulphaḥ) — an ankle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an eyebrow, then the Sanskrit for the skin.

You'll want to know: गुल्फः.
गुल्फः — gulphaḥ — "an ankle".

The word, taken apart.
gulpha "the ankle", also kulpha in the Rigveda, is perhaps borrowed from Dravidian: CDIAL 4216, after Burrow, takes the wavering k- and g- as a sign of that and compares a Dravidian word for "hoof". It lives on as Marathi gophā "ankle" (CDIAL 4216).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कक्षः (kakṣaḥ) — a room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an ankle, then the Sanskrit for a finger.

You'll want to know: कक्षः.
कक्षः — kakṣaḥ — "a room".

The word, taken apart.
kakṣa first meant "a hiding place" and "the armpit", which MW calls the most concealed part of the body; its feminine kakṣā also names a walled enclosure or a private room. MW compares Latin coxa "hip", and EtymDB counts it as inherited from Indo-European.

Lesson 4 of 5.
आसन्दी (āsandī) — a chair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a finger, then the Sanskrit for a room.

You'll want to know: आसन्दी.
आसन्दी — āsandī — "a chair".

The word, taken apart.
āsandī "a chair, a stool" is old, in the Atharvaveda, and comes from ā "on" + the root √sad "sit" (CDIAL 1485). MW sets √sad beside Latin sedere and English sit: an inherited Indo-European root.

Lesson 5 of 5.
फलकम् (phalakam) — a board, a plank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a room, then the Sanskrit for a chair.

You'll want to know: फलकम्.
फलकम् — phalakam — "a board, a plank".

The word, taken apart.
phalaka "a board, a plank, a bench" is found from the Brāhmaṇas on (MW). CDIAL 9053 refers it to the root √phal "burst, split", the root MW files it under, and MW compares √phal with English split. Nepali kept it as phalek "board, plank" (CDIAL 9053).
