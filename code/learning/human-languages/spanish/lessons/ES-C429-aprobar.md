# aprobar — to pass, and who is really doing the approving

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-02, ES-LEX-EXAMEN] -->

[PAUSE 2s] You can name the exam and the mark. Say what you did with the exam
when the mark was a five or better.

## You'll want to know: aprobar
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-04]; assesses=[] -->

**aprobar** — to pass, as in to pass an exam.

*He aprobado el examen.* — "I have passed the exam."

The mark that does it is a **five out of ten** and upward.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-04] -->

Latin **approbare** — *ad-* ("towards") plus **probare**, "to test something and
find it good."

Look at the direction that gives you. English says *I passed the exam*, with the
student doing the acting and the exam standing still. The Latin behind
*aprobar* says something closer to **the examiner tested the work and found it
good** — the judgement travels the other way.

*Probare* is a generous root, and you can read its family off the page:

| word | what it tests |
|---|---|
| **prob**ar | to try, to taste, to test |
| **prueb**a | a test, a piece of evidence |
| a**prob**ar | to test and find good |

English *probe*, *proof*, *prove* and *approve* all come from the same verb.

## Grammar Lens: the vowel that breaks in the middle
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C429-EDU-04, ES-LEX-C429-EDU-03] -->

*Aprobar* is one of the verbs whose **o** splits into **ue** when the stress
lands on it:

| form | stem |
|---|---|
| yo apr**ue**bo | stressed |
| nosotros apr**o**bamos | unstressed |

You have seen this before in *poder* and *contar*. The rule has not changed: the
stem breaks where the stress falls and stays whole where it does not.
