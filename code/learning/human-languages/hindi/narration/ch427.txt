Hindi, chapter 427: Credit and Instalments.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
उधार (udhār) — credit; a loan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a balance, then the Hindi for a cash machine.

You'll want to know: उधार.
उधार — udhār — "credit; a loan".
दुकानदार ने मुझे उधार पर चीनी दी। dukāndār ne mujhe udhār par cīnī dī. The shopkeeper gave me sugar on credit.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
उधार (udhār) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit uddhāra, "raising" and also "a loan" (CDIAL 2018), which lists Hindi udhār, "a debt".

Lesson 2 of 5.
क़िस्त (qist) — an instalment.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a cash machine, then the Hindi for credit.

You'll want to know: क़िस्त.
क़िस्त — qist — "an instalment".
हम हर हफ़्ते एक क़िस्त देते हैं। ham har hafte ek qist dete haiṁ. We pay one instalment every week.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
क़िस्त (qist) is usually traced to Arabic qisṭ, "a portion, a share", through Persian: a debt paid in portions.

You'll want to know: हिसाब.
हिसाब — hisāb — "an account; a reckoning".
दुकानदार सब ग्राहकों का हिसाब रखता है। dukāndār sab grāhakoṁ kā hisāb rakhtā hai. The shopkeeper keeps an account for every customer.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
हिसाब (hisāb) is usually traced to Arabic ḥisāb, "counting, reckoning", through Persian. हिसाब करना is to settle up.

Lesson 5 of 5.
चुकाना (cukānā) — to pay off; to settle (a debt).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an account, then the Hindi for a broker.

You'll want to know: चुकाना.
चुकाना — cukānā — "to pay off; to settle (a debt)".
मैंने पूरा क़र्ज़ चुका दिया। maiṁne pūrā qarz cukā diyā. I paid off the whole loan.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दुकान का बिल चुकाया, usne dukān kā bil cukāyā.

The word, taken apart.
चुकाना (cukānā) goes back to cukk, "fall short of, stop", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 4848), which lists Hindi cukānā, "to settle": to bring a debt to its end.
