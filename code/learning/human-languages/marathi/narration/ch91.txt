Marathi, chapter 91: Stairs, Mirror, Lamp, Fan, Key.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जिना (jinā) — stairs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a cupboard, then the Marathi for the ground.

You'll want to know: जिना.
जिना — jinā — "stairs".

The word, taken apart.
जिना (jinā) is a loan, usually traced to Persian zīna, "ladder, stairs". Marathi writes the z as ज.

Lesson 2 of 5.
आरसा (ārsā) — a mirror.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for the ground, then the Marathi for stairs.

You'll want to know: आरसा.
आरसा — ārsā — "a mirror".

The word, taken apart.
आरसा (ārsā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ādarśa, "mirror" (CDIAL 1143). It is built on dṛś, "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
पंखा (paṅkhā) — a fan.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a mirror, then the Marathi for a lamp.

You'll want to know: पंखा.
पंखा — paṅkhā — "a fan".

The word, taken apart.
पंखा (paṅkhā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit pakṣa, "wing, feather", whose form pakṣaka also meant "fan" (CDIAL 7627). CDIAL lists Marathi pākh, "wing", and suggests, with a query, that the nasal comes from a crossing with Sanskrit puṅkha, "the feathered end of an arrow".

Lesson 5 of 5.
किल्ली (killī) — a key.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a lamp, then the Marathi for a fan.

You'll want to know: किल्ली.
किल्ली — killī — "a key".

The word, taken apart.
किल्ली (killī) comes from kīlla, a form CDIAL reconstructs beside Sanskrit kīla, "stake, peg" (CDIAL 3202). CDIAL notes an Indo-European origin proposed for kīla, but thinks its variant shapes support a non-Aryan one.
