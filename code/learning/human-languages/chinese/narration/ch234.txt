Mandarin Chinese, chapter 234: Rain or Shine.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
tuīchí — to put off; to postpone.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for slender, then the Mandarin for skin.

You'll want to know: tuīchí.
tuīchí — "to put off; to postpone".
míngtiān xiàyǔ wǒmen de yěcān yào tuīchí — It's going to rain tomorrow, so our picnic will have to wait.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tuīchí is built from tuī 'to push' and chí 'slow', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thwoj and drij (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'slow' and 'leisurely'.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: qǔxiāo.
qǔxiāo — "to cancel; to call off".
xiàyǔ le bǐsài qǔxiāo le — It's raining, so the match is off.

The word, taken apart.
qǔxiāo is built from qǔ 'to take' and xiāo 'to melt away', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tshjuX and sjew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to take in' and the second 'to wipe out' and 'to use up'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
雨衣 (yǔyī) — raincoat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to call off, then the Mandarin for some other day.

You'll want to know: 雨衣.
雨衣 — yǔyī — "raincoat".
下雨了风也很大我有雨衣 xiàyǔ le fēng yě hěn dà wǒ yǒu yǔyī — It's raining and it's windy too; good thing I've got a raincoat.

The word, taken apart.
雨衣 (yǔyī) is built from 雨 'rain' and 衣 'garment', both inherited from Middle Chinese, hjuX and 'j+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
duǒyǔ — to shelter from the rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for some other day, then the Mandarin for a raincoat.

You'll want to know: duǒyǔ.
duǒyǔ — "to shelter from the rain".
xiàyǔ le wǒmen qù fàndiàn duǒyǔ — It started raining, so we sheltered in a restaurant.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.
Tone: duǒ and yǔ are both third tones, so the first rises and it sounds like duóyǔ.

The word, taken apart.
duǒyǔ is built from duǒ 'to hide' and 雨 'rain'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese hjuX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); no dictionary we use records an older form of the first, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.
