Lesson 1 of 5.
کیل (kīl) — a nail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a bucket, then the Urdu for a rope.

You'll want to know: کیل.
کیل — kīl — "a nail".

The word, taken apart.
کیل (kīl) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kīla, "stake, peg" (CDIAL 3202). CDIAL notes that both an Indo-European and a non-Aryan source have been proposed.

ہتھوڑا (hathauṛā) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from hastakūṭa, "hand hammer", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 14028). It is built from hasta, "hand", and kūṭa, "hammer".

Lesson 5 of 5.
بجلی (bijlī) — electricity; lightning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a lock, then the Urdu for a fan.

You'll want to know: بجلی.
بجلی — bijlī — "electricity; lightning".

The word, taken apart.
بجلی (bijlī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vidyullatā, "forked lightning" (CDIAL 11745). It is built from vidyut, "lightning", and latā, "creeper": lightning as a forking vine. The sense "electricity" came later.
