Hindi, chapter 360: Objecting and Persuading.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आपत्ति (āpatti) — an objection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to get tangled, then the Hindi for to raise (a topic).

You'll want to know: आपत्ति.
आपत्ति — āpatti — "an objection".
मुझे तुम्हारे विचार पर कोई आपत्ति नहीं है। mujhe tumhāre vicār par koī āpatti nahīṃ hai. I have no objection to your idea.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
आपत्ति (āpatti) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit āpatti, "an occurrence; a mishap" (Monier-Williams). Monier-Williams does not give the sense of an objection, which is a later Hindi one.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
दृष्टिकोण (dṛṣṭikoṇ) — a point of view.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an objection, then the Hindi for condemnation.

You'll want to know: दृष्टिकोण.
दृष्टिकोण — dṛṣṭikoṇ — "a point of view".
हर आदमी का अपना दृष्टिकोण होता है। har ādmī kā apnā dṛṣṭikoṇ hotā hai. Everyone has their own point of view.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
दृष्टिकोण (dṛṣṭikoṇ) joins two Sanskrit words, dṛṣṭi, "sight", and koṇa, "an angle, a corner" (Monier-Williams): the angle one looks from. The compound is a modern one.

You'll want to know: धमकाना.
धमकाना — dhamkānā — "to threaten".
दुकानदार ने चोर को धमकाया। dukāndār ne cor ko dhamkāyā. The shopkeeper threatened the thief.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने मुझे धमकाया, usne mujhe dhamkāyā.

The word, taken apart.
धमकाना (dhamkānā) is inherited from dhammakka, "a threat", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 6736).

Lesson 5 of 5.
फुसलाना (phuslānā) — to coax, to wheedle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a point of view, then the Hindi for to threaten.

You'll want to know: फुसलाना.
फुसलाना — phuslānā — "to coax, to wheedle".
बच्चा दवा नहीं पी रहा था, इसलिए माँ ने उसे फुसलाया। baccā davā nahīṃ pī rahā thā, isliye māṁ ne use phuslāyā. The child wouldn't take the medicine, so his mother coaxed him.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने बच्चे को फुसलाया, usne bacce ko phuslāyā.

The word, taken apart.
फुसलाना (phuslānā) is a tadbhava, inherited from Sanskrit spṛśati, "touches" (CDIAL 13815). CDIAL lists the Hindi verb, "to cajole", under this entry; the step from touching to coaxing is not spelled out there.
