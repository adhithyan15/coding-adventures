Telugu, chapter 83: Reading — Words, Lines, and a First Passage.
3 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 3.
(reading) — six words, read down as well as along.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Every word below you have written. Not one is new.
[pause 1 second]
What is new is that nobody says them first.

Reading.
పుస్తకం తలుపు కుర్చీ నది గాలి దీపం.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down the list once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and look at పుస్తకం and కుర్చీ. Something in each of them sits below the line rather than on it.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
Telugu stacks a consonant underneath its neighbour instead of putting it alongside. So a word can be two syllables wide and three levels deep.
[pause 2 seconds]
A reader has to look down a word as well as along it, and that is the habit this list is for. నది and గాలి have nothing below; పుస్తకం has two things.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, and the tall ones are not longer — they are deeper.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six words down the list, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Which word is the lamp?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Which way does a Telugu word grow when it gets complicated? (Downwards.)


Lesson 2 of 3.
(reading) — six lines, and no word for is.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last lesson gave you words. These put them into sentences.
[pause 1 second]
Nothing here is new.

Reading.
ఇది పుస్తకం. ఇది తలుపు. కుర్చీ ఇక్కడ. పుస్తకం సులభం. నిజం. ఇవాళ.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read down once, without stopping.
[pause 3 seconds]
Again — and look for the verb in the first four lines.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
There is none. ఇది పుస్తకం is this book with nothing between them, and it is a complete sentence: this is a book.
[pause 2 seconds]
Telugu does not say is. Two words placed together are already a statement, and a reader has to stop waiting for the word that joins them.
[pause 2 seconds]
The last two lines are one word each and still complete. That is the same rule taken as far as it goes.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the six lines, once, in order, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 13 seconds]
What English word has no Telugu equivalent in these lines?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
How many of the six were new? (None.)
[pause 2 seconds]
What joins ఇది and పుస్తకం? (Nothing — and that is the sentence.)


Lesson 3 of 3.
(reading) — the first passage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Six words, then six lines. Now nine of them together, all about one room.
[pause 1 second]
Twenty-five words, and not one of them is new.

Reading.
ఇది పుస్తకం. పుస్తకం సులభం. ఇది తలుపు. కుర్చీ ఇక్కడ ఉంది. దీపం ఇక్కడ ఉంది. గురువు ఇక్కడ ఉన్నారా? రైతు ఇక్కడ లేదు. రెండవ పుస్తకం ఇక్కడ ఉంది. ఇవాళ చాలా సులభం.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read it once without stopping. Do not translate as you go — let the sentences arrive.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now read it again, and notice that every line is about the same place.

You'll want to know.
[pause 2 seconds]
That is not a stylistic choice. A language that has not yet taught you a word for is can still say ఉంది — it exists here — and లేదు — it does not. So the first passage a Telugu reader can hold is a room described from one spot: everything is here, or it is not.
[pause 2 seconds]
ఉన్నారా? is the same word made into a question and made respectful at once, which is why the teacher gets it and the farmer does not. Telugu marks respect in the verb, and you have now read it doing so.
[pause 2 seconds]
This is the first whole passage the book has asked you to hold at once, and it works because everything in it was paid for.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: the passage aloud, once, without stopping]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[repeat that twice]
[question — say your answer, then pause 14 seconds]
Who is not in the room?

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
What two words carry the whole passage? (ఉంది and లేదు — here, and not here.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Why does the teacher get ఉన్నారా? (Respect lives in the verb.)
