Lesson 2 of 5.
नींबू (nīmbū) — a lemon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for oil, then the Marwadi for grapes.

You'll want to know: नींबू.
नींबू — nīmbū — "a lemon".

The word, taken apart.
नींबू (nīmbū) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from nimbū, "the lime". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 7247); the match is through Hindi nīmbū. CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word itself from an Austroasiatic language.

Lesson 3 of 5.
आटो (āṭo) — flour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for grapes, then the Marwadi for a lemon.

You'll want to know: आटो.
आटो — āṭo — "flour".

The word, taken apart.
आटो (āṭo) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from ārta, "flour", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants. CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 1338); the match is through Hindi āṭā and Gujarati āṭo. CDIAL compares Persian ārd, "flour".

Lesson 4 of 5.
कांदो (kā̃do) — an onion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a lemon, then the Marwadi for flour.

You'll want to know: कांदो.
कांदो — kā̃do — "an onion".

The word, taken apart.
कांदो (kā̃do) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from kanda, "bulbous root". CDIAL gives no Marwari form in this entry (CDIAL 2723); the match is through Hindi kā̃dā and Gujarati kā̃do. CDIAL thinks the Sanskrit word itself probably came from Dravidian.

Lesson 5 of 5.
मिठाई (miṭhāī) — sweets.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for flour, then the Marwadi for an onion.

You'll want to know: मिठाई.
मिठाई — miṭhāī — "sweets".

The word, taken apart.
मिठाई (miṭhāī) is a noun built with -āī on the word for "sweet". CDIAL lists that word, Hindi miṭh and Gujarati mīṭhũ, "sweet", under Sanskrit miṣṭa, "dainty, sweet" (CDIAL 10299), where Old Marwari has sumīṭha, "very sweet".
