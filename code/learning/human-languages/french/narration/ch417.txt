French, chapter 417: À la douane.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le douanier — the customs officer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the hold, then the French for the boarding.

You'll want to know: le douanier.
le douanier — "the customs officer".
Le douanier a demandé ce qu'il y avait dans la boîte. The customs officer asked what was in the box.
Built on la douane 'customs'.

The word, taken apart.
douanier is built on douane 'customs', which is usually traced through Old Italian doana to Arabic dīwān 'register, office'. EtymDB does not list the word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
fouiller — to search (a bag, a person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to declare, then the French for the scanner gate.

You'll want to know: fouiller.
fouiller — "to search (a bag, a person)".
Un agent a fouillé mon sac à dos. An officer searched my rucksack.
La fouille — the search.

The word, taken apart.
fouiller goes back to Vulgar Latin fodiculāre, from Latin fodere 'to dig' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB links it with fouir 'to dig'.

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: transiter.
transiter — "to change planes, to pass through".
Ce vol est moins cher, mais il transite par deux aéroports. This flight is cheaper, but it goes through two airports.
Le transit — transit.

The word, taken apart.
transiter is built on transit, a learned borrowing from Latin trānsitus 'a passing across', from trānsīre, built on īre 'to go' (Lewis & Short).
