## Chapter 33 — reading: six words, six lines, and a first paragraph

Urdu's reading rung, and the first connected Urdu the track has asked anyone to
read rather than to write or to say.

Three lessons, ramping 6 -> 14 -> 37 tokens. No new word in any of them, and --
the part that reshaped the chapter -- **no new LETTER either**.

- A first draft was written to the usual rule (every word occurs in an earlier
  lesson) and passed that check on the first attempt: 51 tokens, nothing unseen.
  It then failed `script-closure` with **fourteen untaught glyphs in one lesson**.
- The reason is worth writing down. A word can be taught whole while its letters
  have never been taught: چائے, جوتا, قمیض, دوست, ٹھیک and خدا حافظ are all
  known words, and between them they need چ ج ٹ ق ض د ھ خ ح ظ ف, none of which
  the script ladder has reached. Urdu has taught **fifteen letters** and ten
  digits. For a WORD lesson that gap is a gloss; for a READING lesson it is the
  whole exercise, because reading is the one skill that cannot route around a
  letter you cannot decode.
- So the passage was rebuilt from the taught set alone: ا آ ل م ن ر س ش ک ہ ں ی
  ے پ و. That is why it says nothing about tea, shoes or goodbyes, and why it
  leans on naming sentences -- یہ ... ہے -- which is the shape the book has
  drilled hardest and which needs no letter outside the fifteen.
- `glyph-coverage` also rejected **U+1E41 ṁ** in a romanization field. Urdu
  romanizes its nasal as **ṅ** 38 times and ṁ three; the three are in fields that
  never reach a typeset page, and mine did. Corrected to the track's own
  convention.
- pre-A1 reading moves 0/3 -> 3/3, floor raised. Urdu declares no A1 shape yet.

Noted while here and deliberately not fixed: `session-map.md` stops at session
134 while the track has 162 lessons. It is not gated by any test, and bringing
25 lessons of earlier work into it is a separate piece of work from this rung.
