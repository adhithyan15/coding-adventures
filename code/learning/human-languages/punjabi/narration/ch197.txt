Lesson 2 of 5.
One noun for a daily routine — daily routine or the usual ordered activities of a day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve the familiar first and last words of the identity frame.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ (dinchariyaa) — dinchariā — daily routine; the usual ordered activities of a day.
Hear and say the whole word before reading it. Keep it as one whole noun rather than inventing smaller Punjabi pieces. This lesson owns the word alone; the complete identity frame comes only after a separate retrieval.

The word, taken apart — where the trail runs out.
An Australian Government Punjabi health page uses this exact word in an everyday routine context. It supports only the present-day daily-routine sense taught here; this lesson makes no claim about other specialised uses. Source: Australian Government Medicare Mental Health Check In.
The Punjabi origin of ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ (dinchariyaa) remains unknown in the sources used here. For a memory comparison only, Apte records the matching Sanskrit form dinacaryā with the sense “daily occupation, daily routine of business.” This comparison does not establish a Sanskrit-to-Punjabi transmission and does not turn its parts into a productive Punjabi rule: keep the present-day Punjabi noun whole. Source: V. S. Apte, The Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary, p. 0813-1, Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries.

Lesson 4 of 5.
Put the new noun into one identity frame — this is a daily routine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve ਇਹ, then the new noun, then ਹੈ.

Grammar Lens.
ਇਹ ਦਿਨਚਰਿਆ ਹੈ (eh dinchariyaa hai) — eh dinchariā hai — this is a daily routine.
Keep the new whole noun between familiar ਇਹ and ਹੈ. Familiar ਹੈ stays last in the already-owned identity frame. This chapter teaches only this one label; it does not add an inflection rule or a full routine narrative.
