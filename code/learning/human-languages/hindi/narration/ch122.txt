Hindi, chapter 122: It's Getting Late.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
छुट्टी (chuṭṭī) — a holiday, a day off, leave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for come again, then the Hindi for let's go.

You'll want to know: छुट्टी.
छुट्टी (chuṭṭī) — "a holiday", "a day off", "leave from work".
आज छुट्टी (chuṭṭī) है — "today is a holiday", with the आज you know.

What you've built.
A day off.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: chuṭṭī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: chuṭṭī, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say āj chuṭṭī hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Hindi for come again, then the Hindi for let's go, then say chuṭṭī again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does छुट्टी (chuṭṭī) mean? ("A holiday, a day off, leave".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
देर हो गई (der ho gaī) — it has got late.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for let's go, then the Hindi for a holiday.

You'll want to know: देर हो गई.
देर हो गई (der ho gaī) — "it has got late", "I am late".
देर is the lateness you know; हो गई is "has become". The first reason to leave.

What you've built.
A reason to go.

Lesson 3 of 5.
आराम से (ārām se) — take it easy; comfortably.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a holiday, then the Hindi for it has got late.

You'll want to know: आराम से.
आराम से (ārām se) — "comfortably", and as a parting, "take it easy".
आराम is the rest you know, with से, "with". आराम से (ārām se) बैठिए — "sit comfortably"; at the door, आराम से (ārām se) alone is "take it easy".

What you've built.
An unhurried goodbye.

Lesson 4 of 5.
इजाज़त (ijāzat) — permission, leave to go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for it has got late, then the Hindi for take it easy.

You'll want to know: इजाज़त.
इजाज़त (ijāzat) — "permission".
अब इजाज़त (ijāzat) दीजिए — "now please give me leave to go", the most courteous way to excuse yourself from a host.

What you've built.
A formal way out of a room.

Lesson 5 of 5.
जाना है (jānā hai) — (I) have to go.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for take it easy, then the Hindi for permission to go.

You'll want to know: जाना है.
जाना है (jānā hai) — "have to go".
मुझे जाना है (jānā hai) — "I have to go", with the person in मुझे. जाना is "to go".

What you've built.
A holiday, it's late, take it easy, leave to go, I have to go.
