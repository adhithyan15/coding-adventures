Latin, chapter 505: Mercy and kindness.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
clēmēns, clēmentis — merciful, mild.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to pretend, then the Latin for to hide.

You'll want to know: clēmēns, clēmentis.
clēmēns, clēmentis — "merciful, mild".
Rēx clēmēns captīvīs ignōscit. The merciful king pardons the prisoners.
One ending; ablative usually clēmentī. Of a ruler or a judge: gentle in punishing. English clement.

The word, taken apart.
clēmēns is a native Latin adjective; Lewis & Short calls its origin doubtful, so it is not settled. English clement and inclement come from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
clēmentia, clēmentiae — mercy, clemency.

You'll want to know: clēmentia, clēmentiae.
clēmentia, clēmentiae — "mercy, clemency".
Victor clēmentiam hostibus ostendit. The victor shows mercy to the enemy.
Feminine, first declension. A virtue of rulers: mercy toward those in your power.

The word, taken apart.
clēmentia is built on clēmēns "merciful" (Lewis & Short). English clemency comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
benignitās, benignitātis — kindness, generosity.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for merciful, then the Latin for mercy.

You'll want to know: benignitās, benignitātis.
benignitās, benignitātis — "kindness, generosity".
Benignitās hospitis nōs dēlectat. Our host's kindness delights us.
Feminine, third declension, from benignus — kind. English benignity.

The word, taken apart.
benignitās is built on benignus "kind", which Lewis & Short and EtymDB explain as bonus or bene "good, well" with genus "kind": of a good kind. English benign comes from benignus.

benevolus, benevola, benevolum — "kindly, well-wishing".
Vīcīnus benevolus nōbīs semper adest. Our kindly neighbour is always there for us.
bene 'well' + volō 'to wish'. Often of a friend's goodwill.

The word, taken apart.
benevolus is built on bene "well" and volō "to wish" (Lewis & Short).
