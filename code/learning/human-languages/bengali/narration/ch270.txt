Bengali, chapter 270: Which Way, and How Far.
4 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
দূর (dūr) — far; a distance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "so we stayed at home". (tāi āmrā bāṛite thāklām.) Then কাছে, "near".

You'll want to know: দূর.
দূর — dūr — "far; a distance".
বাজার আমাদের বাড়ি থেকে দূরে। bājār āmāder bāṛi theke dūre. — "The market is far from our house."
দূরে, with -এ, is "far away, at a distance", the opposite of কাছে. থেকে gives the point it is measured from. দূর থেকে is "from far away": নদীটা দূর থেকে দেখলাম (nôdīṭā dūr theke dekhlām), "I saw the river from far away".

The word, taken apart.
দূর (dūr) is a tatsama: Sanskrit dūra, "distant", and as a noun "distance" (CDIAL 6495). CDIAL lists the Bengali word under it as dur: Bengali does not lengthen the vowel, and the long ূ (-ū) is the Sanskrit spelling kept.

[your turn — say: "The market is far from our house" — bājār āmāder bāṛi theke dūre]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "The station is near" — sṭeshôn kāchhe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does দূর (dūr) mean? ("Far; a distance".) "Far away"? (দূরে.) Its opposite? (কাছে.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
ূ (-ū) — the sign under the দ of দূর (dūr).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: ূ, Guided Practice and Wrap-up Recall.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say দূর (dūr), "far". Then write তু and উ: the u sign and the u letter you know.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: ূ.
ূ — -ū, the long u, written under its consonant: দূর (dūr). Bengali says it the same as ু; the long sign is a spelling, mostly in words from Sanskrit, as ী is beside ি.
Set the print of দূ beside দু. Under দু hangs a small closed loop with a short slanting tail at its lower right. Under দূ hangs a rounded hook that is open on the right, like a backward C, with a short slanting tail at its lower end.

Writing: ূ — copy the printed shape.
Put your pen beside দূ and copy the shape you can see, slowly and large: the দ, then the open hook under it, then its tail. Check the copy against the print: open, not closed like ু.
This book does not yet tell you where to start ূ (-ū) or which way the pen goes. That is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying the printed shape needs no such source, and it is how the shape becomes yours in the meantime.

Lesson 3 of 4.
… কীভাবে যাব? কত দূর? (… kībhābe jābô? kôtô dūr?) (kībhābe jābô? kôtô dūr?) — asking the way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "The market is far from our house". (bājār āmāder bāṛi theke dūre.) Then "I will go". (jābô.)

You'll want to know: asking.
স্টেশনে কীভাবে যাব? sṭeshône kībhābe jābô? — "How do I get to the station?"
কীভাবে (kībhābe), "how", is কী, "what", with ভাবে, "in the manner": "how shall I go?". কত (kôtô), "how much", with দূর (dūr) asks the distance:
স্টেশন কত দূর? sṭeshôn kôtô dūr? — "How far is the station?"
To ask a stranger, start with মাফ করবেন, "excuse me".

You'll want to know: giving the way.
The answer often uses the polite future, "you will go", with সোজা, "straight on", বাঁদিকে (bā̃dike), "to the left", and ডানদিকে (ḍāndike), "to the right": বাঁ, "left", ডান, "right", with দিক, "a side", and -এ.
সোজা গিয়ে মোড় থেকে ডানদিকে যাবেন। স্টেশন বাজারের পাশে। sojā giye moṛ theke ḍāndike jāben. sṭeshôn bājārer pāshe. — "Go straight, then turn right at the corner. The station is next to the market."
And the distance: দশ মিনিট হাঁটতে হবে (dôsh miniṭ hā̃ṭte hôbe), "it's a ten-minute walk".
