## You'll want to know: scōpae, scōpārum
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C552-DOMI-05]; assesses=[] -->

**scōpae, scōpārum** — "a broom".

> Servus scōpīs culīnam pūrgat.
>
> *The slave cleans the kitchen with a broom.*

Feminine, first declension, plural: a bundle of twigs. Italian *scopa* — broom — comes from it.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**scōpae** first meant "twigs" (Lewis & Short), and a bundle of twigs made a broom. Lewis & Short connects it with a root meaning "to prop", but the origin is not settled.
