French, chapter 298: Le choc.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
renverser — to knock over, to run over; to spill.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the accident report, then the French for the skid.

You'll want to know: renverser.
renverser — "to knock over, to run over; to spill".
Une voiture a renversé un enfant à vélo. A car knocked over a child on a bike.
Also to spill: renverser du café.

The word, taken apart.
renverser is built on Old French enverser "to turn upside down", from envers "reverse", which is inherited from Latin inversus, from vertere "to turn" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
le blessé — the injured person, the casualty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the skid, then the French for to knock over.

You'll want to know: le blessé.
le blessé — "the injured person, the casualty".
Les blessés ont été emmenés à l'hôpital. The injured were taken to hospital.
Un blessé grave, un blessé léger — a seriously, a slightly injured person.

The word, taken apart.
blessé is the past participle of the verb meaning "to wound", which is usually traced to a Frankish word for "to bruise"; the origin is not fully settled (EtymDB lists no parent).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: l'airbag.
l'airbag — "the airbag".
L'airbag s'est ouvert pendant le choc. The airbag opened in the crash.
Official French says le coussin gonflable, the usual word in Québec.

The word, taken apart.
airbag was borrowed from English airbag (EtymDB), a bag filled with air.
