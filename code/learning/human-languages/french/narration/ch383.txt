French, chapter 383: Qui peut participer.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
l'exigence — the requirement, the demand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the penalty, then the French for to penalise.

You'll want to know: l'exigence.
l'exigence — "the requirement, the demand".
Ce poste a beaucoup d'exigences. This job has a lot of requirements.
Feminine. Être exigeant — to be demanding.

The word, taken apart.
exigence is usually traced to Late Latin exigentia "urgency, need", from exigere "to drive out, to demand" (Lewis & Short), built on agere "to drive".

You'll want to know: le critère.
le critère — "the criterion".
Le prix est mon premier critère. Price is my first criterion.
The plural is les critères.

The word, taken apart.
critère is usually traced, as a learned word, to Late Latin critērium, from Greek kritḗrion "a means of judging", built on krínein "to judge".

Lesson 3 of 5.
éligible — eligible.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the requirement, then the French for the criterion.

You'll want to know: éligible.
éligible — "eligible".
À dix-huit ans, on est éligible au conseil municipal. At eighteen, you are eligible for the town council.
One form for both genders. Éligible à une aide — eligible for aid.

The word, taken apart.
éligible is usually traced to Late Latin ēligibilis "that may be chosen", from ēligere "to pick out", built on legere "to choose, to gather" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
l'admission — the admission, the entry.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: l'admission.
l'admission — "the admission, the entry".
Les résultats d'admission arrivent en juin. The admission results come out in June.
Feminine. From admettre.

The word, taken apart.
admission is a learned borrowing from Latin admissiō "a letting in" (EtymDB), from admittere "to let in", built on mittere "to send" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
exclure — to exclude, to rule out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for eligible, then the French for the admission.

You'll want to know: exclure.
exclure — "to exclude, to rule out".
On ne peut pas exclure un retard. A delay cannot be ruled out.
Like conclure: past participle exclu.

The word, taken apart.
exclure is a learned borrowing from Latin exclūdere "to shut out" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), built from ex "out" and claudere "to shut".
