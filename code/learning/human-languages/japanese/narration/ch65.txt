Lesson 1 of 5.
こい (koi) — a carp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sardine, then the Japanese for a flavour.

You'll want to know: こい.
こい — koi — "a carp".

The word, taken apart.
こい (koi) is a native Japanese word, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, and its older origin is not settled. Its kanji was attached to it later as a kun'yomi, a native reading given to a Chinese character (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

The word, taken apart.
せみ (semi) is the kun'yomi of the character for the cicada and is counted a native word; a link with the Chinese word for the cicada has been suggested, and its origin is not settled (KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
ごぼう (gobou) — burdock root.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a dragonfly, then the Japanese for a potato.

You'll want to know: ごぼう.
ごぼう — gobou — "burdock root".

The word, taken apart.
ごぼう (gobou) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are go, "ox, cow" (Middle Chinese ngjuw) and bō, "burdock" (baeng) (Unihan, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese after Baxter–Sagart). JMdict marks it as usually written in kana.
