German, chapter 483: Gips und Krücken.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
der Gips — plaster cast; plaster.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to disinfect, then the German for scar.

You'll want to know: der Gips.
der Gips — "plaster cast; plaster".
Mein Arm war sechs Wochen im Gips. My arm was in plaster for six weeks.
Einen Gips bekommen — to get a cast. Plural, rare: die Gipse.

The word, taken apart.
Gips comes from Middle High German gips, from Latin gypsum, which was borrowed from Greek gýpsos "gypsum" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: humpeln.
humpeln — "to limp, to hobble".
Nach dem Spiel humpelte der Spieler vom Platz. After the game, the player limped off the pitch.
Perfekt with sein when you go somewhere: Er ist nach Hause gehumpelt.

The word, taken apart.
humpeln is usually traced to Low German, and EtymDB sets it beside Dutch hompelen "to hobble".

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: der Rollstuhl.
der Rollstuhl — "wheelchair".
Seit dem Unfall sitzt sie im Rollstuhl. Since the accident she has been in a wheelchair.
Plural: die Rollstühle.

The word, taken apart.
Rollstuhl is built on rollen "to roll" and Stuhl "chair" (EtymDB), which is inherited from Old High German stuol, from Proto-Germanic stōlaz.

Lesson 5 of 5.
heilen — to heal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to limp, then the German for wheelchair.

You'll want to know: heilen.
heilen — "to heal".
Die Wunde heilt langsam. The wound is healing slowly.
Perfekt with sein when a wound heals: Die Wunde ist gut geheilt. With haben when a doctor heals someone.

The word, taken apart.
heilen is built on heil "whole, unhurt", which is inherited from Old High German heil, from Proto-Germanic hailaz (EtymDB): to make whole.
