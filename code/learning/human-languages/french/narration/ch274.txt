French, chapter 274: Ce qui est arrivé.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
survenir — to occur, to arise unexpectedly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for lately, then the French for soon.

You'll want to know: survenir.
survenir — "to occur, to arise unexpectedly".
Un problème est survenu pendant le voyage. A problem arose during the trip.
Conjugated like venir, with être: il est survenu.

The word, taken apart.
survenir comes from Latin supervenīre "to come upon, to arrive unexpectedly", from super "over" and venīre "to come" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
dérouler — to unfold, to take place (as se dérouler); to unroll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for soon, then the French for to occur.

You'll want to know: dérouler.
dérouler — "to unfold, to take place (as se dérouler); to unroll".
Le concert s'est déroulé dans le parc. The concert took place in the park.
Mostly reflexive in this sense: se dérouler. Without se, it means to unroll a carpet or a map.

The word, taken apart.
dérouler is built on rouler "to roll", which comes from Old French roeler, built on Latin rota "wheel" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
avoir lieu — to take place, to be held.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to occur, then the French for to unfold.

You'll want to know: avoir lieu.
avoir lieu — "to take place, to be held".
La réunion a eu lieu hier. The meeting took place yesterday.
For planned events: la fête aura lieu samedi.

The word, taken apart.
lieu is inherited from Latin locus "place", and avoir from Latin habēre "to have, to hold" (Lewis & Short): to have a place.

Lesson 4 of 5.
précéder — to precede, to come before.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to unfold, then the French for to take place.

You'll want to know: précéder.
précéder — "to precede, to come before".
Un grand silence a précédé la réponse. A long silence came before the answer.
Le jour précédent — the day before, in a story.

The word, taken apart.
précéder is a learned borrowing from Latin praecēdere "to go before", from prae "before" and cēdere "to go" (Lewis & Short).
