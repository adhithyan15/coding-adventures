Marathi, chapter 522: Rooms of the House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दिवाणखाना (divāṇkhānā) — a living room.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a saw, then the Marathi for to plaster.

You'll want to know: दिवाणखाना.
दिवाणखाना — divāṇkhānā — "a living room".
पाहुणे दिवाणखान्यात बसले होते. pāhuṇe divāṇkhānyāt basle hote. The guests were sitting in the living room.
Masculine.
The oblique is दिवाणखान्या-: दिवाणखान्यात.

The word, taken apart.
दिवाणखाना (divāṇkhānā) is a loan from Persian dīvān-khāna, "the hall where guests are received", from dīvān, "court", and khāna, "house".

Lesson 2 of 5.
शयनगृह (śayangṛh) — a bedroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for to plaster, then the Marathi for a living room.

You'll want to know: शयनगृह.
शयनगृह — śayangṛh — "a bedroom".
आमच्या घरात दोन शयनगृहं आहेत. āmcyā gharāt don śayangṛhaṁ āhet. There are two bedrooms in our house.
Neuter. Plural: शयनगृहं, śayangṛhaṁ.

The word, taken apart.
शयनगृह (śayangṛh) is a tatsama compound of Sanskrit śayana, "lying down, sleeping", and gṛha, "house" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 5 of 5.
टांगणे (ṭāṅgṇe) — to hang up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a terrace, then the Marathi for a floor tile.

You'll want to know: टांगणे.
टांगणे — ṭāṅgṇe — "to hang up".
मी दिवाणखान्याच्या भिंतीवर एक चित्र टांगलं. mī divāṇkhānyācyā bhintīvar ek citra ṭāṅglaṁ. I hung a picture on the living room wall.
Past, with -ने (he …): त्याने चित्र टांगलं, tyāne citra ṭāṅglaṁ.
The place takes -वर: भिंतीवर टांगणे (ṭāṅgṇe).

The word, taken apart.
टांगणे (ṭāṅgṇe) is inherited: CDIAL lists Marathi ṭā̃gṇẽ, "to hang up", under ṭaṅg, "hang up" (CDIAL 5436).
