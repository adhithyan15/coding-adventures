# Chapter 1 — Practice and Recall

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] No new words. Just the greetings — and the English and Romance words
they secretly contain.

## Rapid recall

[PAUSE 1s each]
- [YOU SAY: hello, one person — *salvē*]
- [YOU SAY: hail, formally — *avē*]
- [YOU SAY: goodbye, one person — *valē*]
- [YOU SAY: thank you — *grātiās tibi agō*]
- [YOU SAY: "thus" (a way to say yes) and "not" — *ita* / *nōn*]

## The taproot in action

[PAUSE 2s] Each greeting is an etymological hub. Trace them:
- [YOU SAY: which greeting root gives *save, safe, salute*? (*salvus*, from *salvē*)]
- [YOU SAY: which gives *value, valid, prevail*? (*valēre*, from *valē*)]
- [YOU SAY: which gives *grace, gratitude, grazie, gracias*? (*grātia*, from *grātiās*)]

## Roots you now carry

[PAUSE 2s]
- *salvus* "safe" → **save, safe, salvage, salvation, salute**
- *valēre* "be strong" → **value, valid, valiant, prevail, convalesce**
- *grātia* "grace" → **grace, gratitude, gratis, congratulate**; Romance *grazie/gracias*
- *nōn* ← *ne-* → English **no, not, none**; cousin of Sanskrit *na*

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Catullus wrote *avē atque valē* at his brother's grave. Translate it,
and say what each verb literally wishes. ("Hail and farewell"; *avē* "fare well"
on meeting, *valē* "be strong" on parting.)
