Japanese, chapter 147: Washing Up with Soap and Hot Water.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
あらう (arau) — to wash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to wake someone, then the Japanese for sleep.

You'll want to know: あらう.
あらう — arau — "to wash".
まいあさ かおを あらいます。 — maiasa kao o araimasu — "I wash my face every morning."
Polite あらいます, araimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
あらう (arau) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 5 of 5.
せっけん (sekken) — soap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pour over oneself, then the Japanese for hot water.

You'll want to know: せっけん.
せっけん — sekken — "soap".
せっけんで てを あらいます。 — sekken de te o araimasu — "I wash my hands with soap."

The word, taken apart.
せっけん (sekken) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are seki, "stone" (Middle Chinese dzyek) and ken, "alkaline salt" (keamX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
