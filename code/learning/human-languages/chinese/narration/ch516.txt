Lesson 1 of 6.
luòyè — fallen leaves.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to escape the summer heat, then the Mandarin for to bring in the harvest.

You'll want to know: luòyè.
luòyè — "fallen leaves".
qiūtiān gōngyuán lǐ dōu shì luòyè — In autumn the park is full of fallen leaves.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
luòyè is built from luò 'to fall' and yè 'a leaf', both inherited from Middle Chinese, lak and yep (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary says the first is the word for what trees do when their leaves come down, and glosses the second 'branches and leaves'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
树 (shù) — tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to bring in the harvest, then the Mandarin for fallen leaves.

You'll want to know: 树.
树 — shù — "tree".
秋天的树最好看 qiūtiān de shù zuì hǎokàn — Trees look their best in autumn.
The new character, in pieces: 树 (shù) — nine strokes in three pieces: on the left the tree piece, four strokes, as in 机; in the middle 又, two strokes, the second shrunk to a short stroke; and on the right a bar, a vertical that hooks to the left, and a dot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
树 (shù) is inherited from Middle Chinese dzyuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the general name for trees and 'to set up'; the dictionary also lists dzyuX, glossed 'to prop up'. The falling tone of shù comes from dzyuH.

Script — the tree piece, 又, and three strokes on the right.
树 (shù).
Three pieces, left to right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the tree piece, four strokes, narrow, as in 机: a short bar, a vertical down through it, a stroke falling to the left from the crossing, then a short dot to the right.
The middle piece, 又, two strokes: a short bar that turns into a stroke falling to the left, then a short stroke falling to the right across it.
The right piece, three strokes: a bar, a long vertical down the right side that hooks to the left at the foot, then a dot on the left of the vertical.
4 + 2 + 3 = nine.

[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 4 of 6.
果树 (guǒshù) — fruit tree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for fallen leaves, then the Mandarin for a tree.

You'll want to know: 果树.
果树 — guǒshù — "fruit tree".
我外公家有一些果树 wǒ wàigōng jiā yǒu yìxiē guǒshù — There are some fruit trees at my grandad's.

The word, taken apart.
果树 (guǒshù) is built from 果 'fruit' and 树 (shù) 'a tree', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaX and dzyuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'bold' and 'firm', and also 'the fruit of a tree'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
shùlín — woods; a grove.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a tree, then the Mandarin for a fruit tree.

You'll want to know: shùlín.
shùlín — "woods; a grove".
wǒ hé sūnnǚ zài shùlín lǐ zǒu le liǎng ge xiǎoshí — My granddaughter and I walked in the woods for two hours.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shùlín is built from 树 (shù) 'a tree' and lín 'a wood', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyuH and lim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which says, after an older dictionary, that land out in the open is called lim, and after another, that flat land with stands of trees is.

The word, taken apart.
chéngshú is built from 成 'to complete' and shú, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and dzyuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'finished' and the second with the first: done, as fruit is done when it is ripe.
