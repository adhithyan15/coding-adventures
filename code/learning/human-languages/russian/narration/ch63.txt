Russian, chapter 63: White, Black, Red, Blue, Green.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
белый (bélyy) — white.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a duck, then the Russian for a goat.

You'll want to know: белый.
белый (bélyy) — "white".

The word, taken apart.
белый (bélyy) from Proto-Slavic bělŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian bãlas, "white", Latvian bãls, "pale", Greek phalós, "white", and Sanskrit bhāti, "shines", from PIE bʰel-, "to shine".

Lesson 3 of 5.
красный (krásnyy) — red.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for white, then the Russian for black.

You'll want to know: красный.
красный (krásnyy) — "red".

The word, taken apart.
красный (krásnyy) is built on краса, "beauty", and first meant "beautiful", as in прекрасный, "lovely"; the name of Moscow's Red Square first meant "the beautiful square". Vasmer thinks краса is probably related to Old Norse hrósa, "to boast".

Lesson 5 of 5.
зелёный (zelyónyy) — green.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for red, then the Russian for blue.

You'll want to know: зелёный.
зелёный (zelyónyy) — "green".

The word, taken apart.
зелёный (zelyónyy) from Proto-Slavic zelenŭ — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian žãlias, "green", and žélti, "to grow over", and further to золото, "gold", and Sanskrit hári-, "yellow". German gelb, "yellow", comes from the same PIE root, ǵʰelh₃-, "to be green, yellow".
