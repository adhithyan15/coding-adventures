Lesson 1 of 5.
велосипед (velosipéd) — a bicycle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a ship, then the Russian for a boat.

You'll want to know: велосипед.
велосипед (velosipéd) — "a bicycle".

The word, taken apart.
велосипед (velosipéd) comes from French vélocipède, from Latin vēlōx, "swift", and pēs, pedis, "foot" (Vasmer).

Lesson 2 of 5.
чемодан (chemodán) — a suitcase.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a boat, then the Russian for a bicycle.

You'll want to know: чемодан.
чемодан (chemodán) — "a suitcase".

The word, taken apart.
чемодан (chemodán) came through Tatar čamadan from Persian jāma-dān, "a place for clothes": jāma, "garment", with dān, "holder" (Vasmer). An English traveller noted it in 1618.

Lesson 3 of 5.
путешествие (puteshéstviye) — a journey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bicycle, then the Russian for a suitcase.

You'll want to know: путешествие.
путешествие (puteshéstviye) — "a journey".

The word, taken apart.
путешествие (puteshéstviye) is путь, "way, road", with шествие, "a procession", from шествовать, "to march". путь from Proto-Slavic pǫtĭ, a cousin of Sanskrit pánthās, "path", and Latin pōns, "bridge" (Vasmer).

Lesson 4 of 5.
отпуск (ótpusk) — holidays, leave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a suitcase, then the Russian for a journey.

You'll want to know: отпуск.
отпуск (ótpusk) — "holidays, leave".

The word, taken apart.
отпуск (ótpusk) is от, "away", with пуск, from пустить, "to let go", Proto-Slavic pustiti (Vasmer): leave is being let go for a while.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ковёр (kovyór) — a rug, a carpet.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a journey, then the Russian for holidays.

You'll want to know: ковёр.
ковёр (kovyór) — "a rug, a carpet".

The word, taken apart.
ковёр (kovyór) from Old Russian kovĭrŭ; its sounds are unusual, so it is probably a loan. Vasmer suggests, as possible, Danube or Volga Bulgar kavər, compare Turkic kiviz, "felt blanket", and Mongolian kebis, "carpet".
