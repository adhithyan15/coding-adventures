Sanskrit, chapter 346: Saying No to a Claim.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
आक्षेपः (ākṣepaḥ) — an objection.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for proves, then the Sanskrit for certainty.

You'll want to know: आक्षेपः.
आक्षेपः — ākṣepaḥ — "an objection".
कस्य आक्षेपः अस्ति? kasya ākṣepaḥ asti? Who has an objection?
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
आक्षेपः (ākṣepaḥ) is built on ā and the root √kṣip, "to throw"; MW gives ā-kṣepa "drawing together" and, among its later senses, "objection" (MW).

What you've built.
One more word for giving reasons and opinions.

Lesson 3 of 5.
निरस्यति (nirasyati) — rejects (a claim).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an objection, then the Sanskrit for refutes.

You'll want to know: निरस्यति.
निरस्यति — nirasyati — "rejects (a claim)".
अध्यापकः मम उत्तरं निरस्यति। adhyāpakaḥ mama uttaraṃ nirasyati. The teacher rejects my answer.
Past (he …): सः मम उत्तरं निरस्तवान्, saḥ mama uttaraṃ nirastavān.

The word, taken apart.
निरस्यति (nirasyati) is built on nir, "out", and the root √as, "to throw"; MW gives nir-√as "to cast out" and "to reject, to refuse", and nir-asta "rejected, refuted" (MW).

अत्र संशयः न अस्ति। atra saṃśayaḥ na asti. There is no doubt about it.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
