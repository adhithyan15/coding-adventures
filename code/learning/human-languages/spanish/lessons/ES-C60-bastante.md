# bastante — an ending you can already read

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-FRIEND-ANTE-ANT, ES-LEX-MUY] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The ending ***-ante***, from chapter fifty-two. *Estudiante* — the
one who studies. It turns a verb into *the one doing it*.

[PAUSE 1s] Keep that in mind; it is about to hand you a word for free.

## The word, taken apart: bastante
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-BASTANTE, ES-ETYMON-BASTANTE-BASTAR]; assesses=[ES-FRIEND-ANTE-ANT] -->

There is a verb ***bastar***, *to be enough*. Put your ending on it:

> **bast-** + **-ante** → ***bastante***, "sufficing".

[PAUSE 2s] So *bastante* does not mean *a lot*. It means **enough** — and from
there, *quite*, *fairly*. It is the amount that suffices, not the amount that
impresses.

> *Estoy* ***bastante*** *cansado.* — I'm quite tired.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEGREE-SCALE]; assesses=[ES-LEX-MUY, ES-LEX-POCO] -->

You now hold a scale, and it is worth saying out loud in order:

| | |
|---|---|
| ***poco*** | little |
| ***bastante*** | enough, quite |
| ***muy*** | very |

[PAUSE 2s] Notice where *bastante* sits. English speakers reach for it thinking
*quite a lot*, and land higher than they meant. It is the middle of the scale,
not the top — the top is *muy*.

[PAUSE 2s] And unlike *muy*, it is not fussy about what follows: *bastante
café*, *bastante grande*. Only *muy* insists on leaning on an adjective or an
adverb.
