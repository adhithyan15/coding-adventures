Hindi, chapter 408: Miles and Distances.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मील (mīl) — a mile.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a convoy, then the Hindi for to wash away.

You'll want to know: मील.
मील — mīl — "a mile".
स्टेशन यहाँ से दो मील है। sṭeśan yahā̃ se do mīl hai. The station is two miles from here.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
मील (mīl) is borrowed from English mile; road signs now give kilometres, but people still count in miles.

Lesson 2 of 5.
कोस (kos) — a kos (about two miles).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to wash away, then the Hindi for a mile.

You'll want to know: कोस.
कोस — kos — "a kos (about two miles)".
दादा कहते थे कि नदी तीन कोस दूर है। dādā kahte the ki nadī tīn kos dūr hai. Grandfather used to say that the river is three kos away.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
कोस (kos) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit krośa, "a shout", and then "the distance a shout carries" (CDIAL 3611), which lists Hindi kos, about two miles.

Lesson 3 of 5.
फ़ासला (fāslā) — a distance; a gap.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a mile, then the Hindi for a kos.

You'll want to know: फ़ासला.
फ़ासला — fāslā — "a distance; a gap".
दोनों गाँवों के बीच दस मील का फ़ासला है। donõ gā̃voṁ ke bīc das mīl kā fāslā hai. The two villages are ten miles apart.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
फ़ासला (fāslā) is usually traced to Arabic fāṣila, "a separating space, an interval", which came to Hindi through Persian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
राह (rāh) — a path; a way (literary).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a kos, then the Hindi for a distance.

You'll want to know: राह.
राह — rāh — "a path; a way (literary)".
यह राह जंगल से होकर जाती है। yah rāh jaṅgal se hokar jātī hai. This path goes through the forest.
Feminine.
Songs and sayings prefer राह (rāh); in plain talk people say रास्ता.

The word, taken apart.
राह (rāh) is usually traced to Persian rāh, "a road, a way", the word inside चौराहा, where four ways meet.

Lesson 5 of 5.
भगाना (bhagānā) — to chase off; to drive (a vehicle) fast.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a distance, then the Hindi for a path.

You'll want to know: भगाना.
भगाना — bhagānā — "to chase off; to drive (a vehicle) fast".
ड्राइवर ने गाड़ी बहुत तेज़ भगाई। ḍrāivar ne gāṛī bahut tez bhagāī. The driver drove the car very fast.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने कुत्ते को भगाया, usne kutte ko bhagāyā.

The word, taken apart.
भगाना (bhagānā) is the causative partner of भागना, "to run away" (CDIAL 9361): to make a dog run off, or to make a car run fast.
