Malayalam, chapter 129: Things You Carry.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
കിടക്ക (kiṭakka) — a bed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the bathroom, then the Malayalam for the front yard.

You'll want to know: കിടക്ക.
കിടക്ക (kiṭakka) — "a bed", the first thing in a മുറി.

The word, taken apart.
കിടക്ക (kiṭakka) is native Dravidian, from the verb "to lie down, rest"; DEDR 1524 lists the root with Tamil kiṭa and Kannada keḍe.

What you've built.
Where the sleeping happens.

Lesson 2 of 5.
താക്കോൽ (tākkōl) — a key.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for the front yard, then the Malayalam for a bed.

You'll want to know: താക്കോൽ.
താക്കോൽ (tākkōl) — "a key". താക്കോൽ എവിടെ? — "where is the key?", the question every house hears.

The word, taken apart.
താക്കോൽ (tākkōl) is native Dravidian, built on tāḻ, "bolt, bar, lock"; DEDR 3179 lists the root with Tamil tāḻ and Kannada tāḻ. DEDR 3179 compares Sanskrit tāḍa(ka).

What you've built.
The thing that is always lost.

Lesson 3 of 5.
കുട (kuṭa) — an umbrella.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a bed, then the Malayalam for a key.

You'll want to know: കുട.
കുട (kuṭa) — "an umbrella", which in മഴക്കാലം nobody leaves home without.

The word, taken apart.
കുട (kuṭa) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil kuṭai, Kannada koḍe and Telugu goḍugu (DEDR 1663). DEDR 1663 compares Sanskrit utkūṭa.

What you've built.
Kerala's most needed object.

Lesson 4 of 5.
കണ്ണട (kaṇṇaṭa) — glasses, spectacles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for a key, then the Malayalam for an umbrella.

You'll want to know: കണ്ണട.
കണ്ണട (kaṇṇaṭa) — "spectacles". It begins with കണ്ണ്, "eye".

The word, taken apart.
കണ്ണട (kaṇṇaṭa) is native Dravidian, built on kaṇṇŭ, "eye"; DEDR 1182 lists Malayalam kaṇṇaṭa, "spectacles", itself, beside Tamil kaṇṇāṭi, "mirror".

What you've built.
An eye word you can spot.
