Gujarati, chapter 306: The Relief Camp.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
સ્વયંસેવક (svayansevak) — a volunteer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to be buried, then the Gujarati for rubble.

You'll want to know: સ્વયંસેવક.
સ્વયંસેવક — svayansevak — "a volunteer".
એક સ્વયંસેવકે બાળકોને દૂધ આપ્યું. ek svayansevke bāḷkone dūdh āpyũ. A volunteer gave the children milk.
A masculine or feminine noun, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
સ્વયંસેવક (svayansevak) is built from Sanskrit svayam, "oneself, of one's own accord", and sevaka, "one who serves" (Monier-Williams): someone who serves of their own will.

Lesson 2 of 5.
રાહત (rāhat) — relief.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for rubble, then the Gujarati for a volunteer.

You'll want to know: રાહત.
રાહત — rāhat — "relief".
વરસાદ અટક્યો ત્યારે લોકોને રાહત થઈ. varsād aṭakyo tyāre lokone rāhat thaī. When the rain stopped, people felt relieved.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
રાહત (rāhat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic rāḥa, "rest, ease". Gujarati uses it for the ease that comes after trouble, રાહત થઈ, and for the help sent to people in need.

Lesson 3 of 5.
છાવણી (chāvṇī) — a camp.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a volunteer, then the Gujarati for relief.

You'll want to know: છાવણી.
છાવણી — chāvṇī — "a camp".
ગામના લોકો હવે એક છાવણીમાં રહે છે. gāmnā loko have ek chāvṇīmā̃ rahe che. The villagers are living in a camp now.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
છાવણી (chāvṇī) goes back to Sanskrit chādana, "covering", and CDIAL lists the Gujarati noun, "huts, a cantonment" (CDIAL 5017): a place of shelters put up for a time.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ઊમટવું (ūmaṭvũ) — to pour in; to throng (of people).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a camp, then the Gujarati for grain.

You'll want to know: ઊમટવું.
ઊમટવું — ūmaṭvũ — "to pour in; to throng (of people)".
આખા શહેરમાંથી લોકો મદદ લઈને ઊમટ્યા. ākhā śahermā̃thī loko madad laīne ūmaṭyā. People poured in from all over the city, bringing help.
Past (it …): તે ટોળું ઊમટ્યું, te ṭoḷũ ūmaṭyũ.

The word, taken apart.
ઊમટવું (ūmaṭvũ) probably goes back to Sanskrit únmṛṣṭa, "rubbed off", and so "made clear": CDIAL lists Gujarati umaṭvũ, "to appear, come to light", there (CDIAL 2133). The sense of crowds that ઊમટે છે, pour out all at once, is the modern language's.
