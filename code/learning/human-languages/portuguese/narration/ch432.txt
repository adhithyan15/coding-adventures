Lesson 1 of 6.
o fusível — the fuse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the thermostat, then the Portuguese for to air.

You'll want to know: o fusível.
o fusível — "the fuse".
Saltou um fusível e a cozinha ficou sem luz. A fuse blew and the kitchen lost its lights.
Saltar — to blow, of a fuse or a breaker.

The word, taken apart.
fusível came from French fusible 'that can melt', built on Latin fūsus, from fundere 'to melt, to pour' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
fundir — to blow out (of a bulb or fuse); to melt; to merge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to air, then the Portuguese for the fuse.

You'll want to know: fundir.
fundir — "to blow out (of a bulb or fuse); to melt; to merge".
A lâmpada da casa de banho fundiu-se. The bathroom bulb has blown.
For a bulb, fundir-se is the usual form. It also means to merge: as duas empresas fundiram-se.

The word, taken apart.
fundir comes from Latin fundere 'to pour, to melt' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 6.
o disjuntor — the circuit breaker.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fuse, then the Portuguese for to blow out.

You'll want to know: o disjuntor.
o disjuntor — "the circuit breaker".
O disjuntor desligou quando liguei o forno. The circuit breaker tripped when I turned on the oven.
People also say o quadro for the box of breakers.

The word, taken apart.
disjuntor came from French disjoncteur, built on Latin disiungere 'to unyoke, to separate', on iungere 'to join' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 6.
recarregar — to recharge; to reload.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to blow out, then the Portuguese for the circuit breaker.

You'll want to know: recarregar.
recarregar — "to recharge; to reload".
Recarrego o telemóvel durante a noite. I recharge my phone overnight.
Also to top up a travel card: recarregar o passe.

The word, taken apart.
recarregar is built from re- and carregar 'to load' (EtymDB), which goes back to Latin carrus 'cart'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
o apagão — the power cut, the blackout.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the circuit breaker, then the Portuguese for to recharge.

You'll want to know: o apagão.
o apagão — "the power cut, the blackout".
Houve um apagão em todo o bairro. There was a power cut in the whole neighbourhood.
Built on apagar, to switch off.

The word, taken apart.
apagão is built on apagar 'to put out, to switch off' with -ão; apagar goes back to Latin pācāre 'to calm, to pacify' (Lewis & Short).
