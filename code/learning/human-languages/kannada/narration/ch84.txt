Kannada, chapter 84: The Body Asks.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ (bāyārike) — thirst.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for anger, then the Kannada for laughter.

You'll want to know: ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ.
ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ (bāyārike) — "thirst". It begins with ಬಾಯಿ (bāyi), "mouth", which you already know: thirst is a dry mouth.
ನನಗೆ ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ (nanage bāyārike) — "I am thirsty".

What you've built.
Hunger's partner: the body asking for water.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: bāyārike]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: bāyārike, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say nanage bāyārike]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Kannada for anger, then the Kannada for laughter, then say bāyārike again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ (bāyārike) mean? ("Thirst".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
ಸುಸ್ತು (sustu) — weariness, feeling worn out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for laughter, then the Kannada for thirst.

You'll want to know: ಸುಸ್ತು.
ಸುಸ್ತು (sustu) — "weariness", the everyday word for feeling worn out.
ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆ (sustāgide) — "I'm worn out". ಆಯಾಸ is a little more formal; ಸುಸ್ತು (sustu) is what you hear at home.

What you've built.
The everyday cousin of āyāsa.

You'll want to know: ಬೆವರು.
ಬೆವರು (bevaru) — "sweat", and also "to sweat".
On a hot day in the ಬಿಸಿಲು (bisilu), "sunshine", this is the word you need.

What you've built.
