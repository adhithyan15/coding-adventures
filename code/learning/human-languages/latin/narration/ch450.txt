Latin, chapter 450: Complaint, To complain, To cheat, Grasping, Dirt.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
querēla, querēlae — a complaint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rotten, then the Latin for a tavern hostess.

You'll want to know: querēla, querēlae.
querēla, querēlae — "a complaint".
Hospitēs multās querēlās dē caupōne habent. The guests have many complaints about the innkeeper.
First declension, feminine: querēla, querēlae f., also spelled querella. English quarrel comes from it through French.

The word, taken apart.
querēla is built on queror 'to complain' with the suffix -ēla (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English quarrel comes from it through Old French.

Lesson 2 of 5.
conqueror, conquerī — to complain (loudly), to complain of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a tavern hostess, then the Latin for a complaint.

You'll want to know: conqueror, conquerī.
conqueror, conquerī — "to complain (loudly), to complain of".
Viātor dē cīmicibus et dē pretiō conqueritur. The traveller complains about the bedbugs and about the price.
Deponent: conqueror, conquerī, conquestus sum. English conquer does not come from it; that word goes back to conquīrere 'to seek out'.

The word, taken apart.
conqueror is built from con-, which strengthens, and queror 'to complain' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English conquer looks like it but comes from a different verb, conquīrere 'to seek out'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
fraudō, fraudāre — to cheat, to defraud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a complaint, then the Latin for to complain.

You'll want to know: fraudō, fraudāre.
fraudō, fraudāre — "to cheat, to defraud".
Caupō malus hospitēs fraudat. The bad innkeeper cheats his guests.
fraudō, fraudāre, fraudāvī, fraudātum, with the person in the accusative and the thing in the ablative. English defraud comes from dēfraudāre.

The word, taken apart.
fraudō is built on fraus, fraudis 'harm, deceit' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English fraud comes from the noun and defraud from the compound dēfraudāre.

Lesson 4 of 5.
rapāx, rapācis — grasping, greedy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to complain, then the Latin for to cheat.

You'll want to know: rapāx, rapācis.
rapāx, rapācis — "grasping, greedy".
Caupō rapāx magnum pretium prō cēnā parvā rogat. The grasping innkeeper asks a big price for a small dinner.
An adjective of one ending: rapāx, genitive rapācis. Also of wild beasts: ravenous. English rapacious comes from it.

The word, taken apart.
rapāx is built on rapiō 'to seize, to snatch' with the suffix -āx 'given to' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English rapacious comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
sordēs, sordis — dirt, filth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to cheat, then the Latin for grasping.

You'll want to know: sordēs, sordis.
sordēs, sordis — "dirt, filth".
In cubiculō sordēs sunt, et hospes īrātus est. There is dirt in the bedroom, and the guest is angry.
Third declension, feminine: sordēs, sordis f., mostly plural sordēs, sordium. English sordid comes from the adjective sordidus.

The word, taken apart.
sordēs is built on sordeō 'to be dirty' (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English sordid comes from the related adjective sordidus.
