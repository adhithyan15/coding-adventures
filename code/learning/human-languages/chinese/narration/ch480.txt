Mandarin Chinese, chapter 480: Symptoms.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tóuyūn — dizzy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to give blood, then the Mandarin for blood pressure.

You'll want to know: tóuyūn.
tóuyūn — "dizzy".
wǒ gǎnjué yǒudiǎnr tóuyūn — I feel a bit dizzy.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tóuyūn is built from 头 'head' and yūn, both inherited from Middle Chinese, duw and hjunH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a halo round the sun or moon. The level tone of yūn is irregular, and the sense 'dizzy' is not given there.

What you've built.
One more word for saying how it was.

Lesson 3 of 6.
liú bítì — to have a runny nose.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for dizzy, then the Mandarin for to itch.

You'll want to know: liú bítì.
liú bítì — "to have a runny nose".
wǒ gǎnmào le liǎng tiān xiànzài hái zài liú bítì — I've had a cold for two days, and my nose is still running.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
liú bítì is built from liú 'to flow', bí 'nose' and tì, all inherited from Middle Chinese, ljuw, bjijH and thejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary explains the first, after an older book, as water moving, and glosses the third as tears; the sense 'mucus of the nose' is not given there.

The word, taken apart.
guòmǐn is built from 过 'beyond' and mǐn 'quick', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaH and minX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go beyond' and the second 'quick' and 'clever': too quick to react. The medical sense is modern.
