Gujarati, chapter 461: Saving and Spending.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કૃપણ (kṛpaṇ) — miserly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for deceit, then the Gujarati for to cajole.

You'll want to know: કૃપણ.
કૃપણ — kṛpaṇ — "miserly".
મારા કાકા ઘણા કૃપણ છે. mārā kākā ghaṇā kṛpaṇ che. My uncle is very miserly.

The word, taken apart.
કૃપણ (kṛpaṇ) is a tatsama, Sanskrit kṛpaṇa, as Monier-Williams records it; its older senses are "pitiable, miserable", and the sense "miserly" grew from them.

Lesson 2 of 5.
ઉડાઉ (uḍāu) — wasteful with money.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for to cajole, then the Gujarati for miserly.

You'll want to know: ઉડાઉ.
ઉડાઉ — uḍāu — "wasteful with money".
ઉડાઉ દીકરાએ બધા પૈસા વેડફ્યા. uḍāu dīkrāe badhā paisā veḍaphyā. The spendthrift son wasted all the money.

The word, taken apart.
ઉડાઉ (uḍāu) is probably built on the stem of ઉડાડવું (uḍāḍvũ), "to make fly", which CDIAL also glosses "to squander" (CDIAL 1697), with the adjective ending -u: someone who squanders. That verb goes back to Sanskrit uḍḍīyatē, "flies up"; CDIAL does not list uḍāu itself, so the link is probable, not recorded.

You'll want to know: કંગાળ.
કંગાળ — kangāḷ — "penniless; destitute".
ધંધામાં ખોટ ગઈ અને તે કંગાળ થઈ ગયો. dhandhāmā̃ khoṭ gaī ane te kangāḷ thaī gayo. His business made a loss and he became penniless.

The word, taken apart.
કંગાળ (kangāḷ) goes back to a reconstructed kaṅkāla-, "poor, miserable"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "poor" (CDIAL 2604).

Lesson 4 of 5.
સખાવત (sakhāvat) — charity; open-handed giving.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for wasteful with money, then the Gujarati for penniless.

You'll want to know: સખાવત.
સખાવત — sakhāvat — "charity; open-handed giving".
તે વેપારીએ ગામ માટે ઘણી સખાવત કરી. te vepārīe gām māṭe ghaṇī sakhāvat karī. That merchant gave generously to the village.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
સખાવત (sakhāvat) is usually traced to Arabic saxāwa, "generosity", which reached Gujarati through Persian.
