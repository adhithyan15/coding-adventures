Japanese, chapter 179: Tea, Sake and Spills.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
こうちゃ (kōcha) — black tea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for milk, then the Japanese for to chill.

You'll want to know: こうちゃ.
こうちゃ — kōcha — "black tea".
あさは こうちゃを のみます。 — asa wa kōcha o nomimasu — "In the morning I drink black tea."

The word, taken apart.
こうちゃ (kōcha) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "crimson" (Middle Chinese huwng) and cha, "tea" (drae) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おさけ (osake) — rice wine; sake, alcohol.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to chill, then the Japanese for black tea.

You'll want to know: おさけ.
おさけ — osake — "rice wine; sake, alcohol".
ちちは おさけを すこし のみます。 — chichi wa osake o sukoshi nomimasu — "My father drinks a little sake."

The word, taken apart.
おさけ (osake) is built from polite o- and native sake, "rice wine, alcohol", inherited rather than borrowed; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to pour, then the Japanese for to spill.

You'll want to know: あたためる.
あたためる — atatameru — "to warm up; to heat".
ぎゅうにゅうを あたためます。 — gyūnyū o atatamemasu — "I warm up the milk."
Polite あたためます, atatamemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
あたためる (atatameru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and it is built on the same stem as atatakai, "warm" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
