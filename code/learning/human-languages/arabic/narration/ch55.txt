Arabic, chapter 55: Grandmother, Uncle, Uncle, Husband, Wife.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جدة (jadda) — a grandmother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a doctor, then the Arabic for a grandfather.

You'll want to know: جدة.
جدة — jadda — "a grandmother".

The word, taken apart.
جدة (jadda) is the feminine of jadd, "grandfather", an old native noun, filed under the root j-d-d (Lane), whose verb jadda is "to cut".

Lesson 4 of 5.
زوج (zawj) — a husband.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an uncle, then the Arabic for an uncle.

You'll want to know: زوج.
زوج — zawj — "a husband".

The word, taken apart.
زوج (zawj) is listed by Lane under z-w-j as one of a pair, a mate. It is usually traced, through Aramaic zūgā, to Greek zeugos, "yoke, pair" (EtymDB); Lane gives no foreign source.
