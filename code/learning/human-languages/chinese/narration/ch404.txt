Mandarin Chinese, chapter 404: Tickets, Please.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
shòupiàochù — ticket office.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a train attendant, then the Mandarin for the head of a station.

You'll want to know: shòupiàochù.
shòupiàochù — "ticket office".
qù shòupiàochù zěnme zǒu — How do I get to the ticket office?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
shòupiàochù is built from shòu 'to sell', 票 'a ticket' and chù 'a place'. The first and the last are inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyuwH and tsyhoH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to sell goods' and 'a place'; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 6.
检票 (jiǎnpiào) — to check tickets.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the head of a station, then the Mandarin for a ticket office.

You'll want to know: 检票.
检票 — jiǎnpiào — "to check tickets".
现在检票了 xiànzài jiǎnpiào le — They're checking tickets now.
The new character, in pieces: 检 (jiǎn) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the tree piece, four strokes, as on the left of 机; on the right a long stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right, like a roof, then a short bar, two dots and a short stroke falling to the left, and a long bar across the foot. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
检票 (jiǎnpiào) is built from 检 (jiǎn) 'to check' and 票 'a ticket'. The first is inherited from Middle Chinese kjemX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed as the seal on a document and 'to check'; no dictionary we use records an older form of 票, so its history is not settled.

Script — the tree piece, then a roof and what is under it.
检 (jiǎn).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, the tree piece, four strokes. A short bar. A vertical down through it. A stroke falling to the left from the crossing. Then a short dot to the right.
The right piece, seven strokes. A long stroke falling to the left and a long stroke falling to the right: a roof. Under it a short bar. Then two dots side by side and a short stroke falling to the left beside them. Last, a long bar across the foot.
4 + 7 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 检 (jiǎn), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 检 (jiǎn) have? (Two.) Which comes first? (The tree piece, as in 机.) What closes the foot? (A long bar.)

Lesson 5 of 6.
wòpù — sleeping berth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to check tickets, then the Mandarin for to buy a ticket on board.

You'll want to know: wòpù.
wòpù — "sleeping berth".
wǒ xiǎng mǎi liǎng zhāng wòpù — I'd like two sleeper berths.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
wòpù is built from wò 'to lie down' and pù 'a bed', both inherited from Middle Chinese, ngwaH and phuH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to sleep' and the second 'to set out, to lay'; the bed sense of the second is not given there.
