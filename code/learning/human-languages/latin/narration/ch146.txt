Latin, chapter 146: Annoying, Useful, Clean, Dirty, Straight.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
molestus, molesta, molestum — annoying.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for fierce, then the Latin for pleasant.

You'll want to know: molestus, molesta, molestum.
molestus, molesta, molestum — "annoying".

The word, taken apart.
molestus is built on mōlēs, "a heavy mass, a burden" (Lewis & Short): burdensome. English molest comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
rēctus, rēcta, rēctum — straight, right.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for clean, then the Latin for dirty.

You'll want to know: rēctus, rēcta, rēctum.
rēctus, rēcta, rēctum — "straight, right".

The word, taken apart.
rēctus is the past participle of regō, "to keep straight, to direct" (Lewis & Short), usually traced to Proto-Indo-European h3regtos, "straightened" (EtymDB 2.1). English right is a cousin.
