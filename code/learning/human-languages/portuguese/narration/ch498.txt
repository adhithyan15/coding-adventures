Portuguese, chapter 498: Amigos e vizinhos.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
conviver — to socialise; to live together.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for betrayal, then the Portuguese for to reconcile.

You'll want to know: conviver.
conviver — "to socialise; to live together".
Gosto de conviver com os colegas depois do trabalho. I like socialising with my colleagues after work.
Conviver com — to get on with, to live alongside.

The word, taken apart.
conviver is a learned borrowing from Latin convīvere "to live together, to dine together" (Lewis & Short), built on vīvere "to live".

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

o convívio — "get-together; social life".
No sábado há um convívio na associação do bairro. On Saturday there is a get-together at the residents' association.
In Portugal um convívio is often a party or a shared meal.

The word, taken apart.
convívio is a learned borrowing from Latin convīvium "a feast, a dinner party" (Lewis & Short), built on vīvere "to live".

Lesson 4 of 6.

You'll want to know: o confidente.
o confidente — "confidant".
O meu melhor amigo é o meu confidente. My best friend is my confidant.
A woman is a confidente.

The word, taken apart.
confidente is a learned borrowing, perhaps through Italian, from Latin cōnfīdēns "trusting" (Lewis & Short), from cōnfīdere "to trust fully", built on fīdere "to trust".

Lesson 5 of 6.
a lealdade — loyalty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for neighbourhood, then the Portuguese for confidant.

You'll want to know: a lealdade.
a lealdade — "loyalty".
Admiro a lealdade do meu cão. I admire my dog's loyalty.
The adjective is leal.

The word, taken apart.
lealdade is inherited, through Old Portuguese, from Medieval Latin lēgālitās (EtymDB), built on lēgālis "of the law", from lēx "law".
