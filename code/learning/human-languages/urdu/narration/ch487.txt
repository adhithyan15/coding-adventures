Urdu, chapter 487: A Wedding.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
منگنی (mangnī) — an engagement to marry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to pass (of time), then the Urdu for the prime of life.

You'll want to know: منگنی.
منگنی — mangnī — "an engagement to marry".
میرے بھائی کی منگنی ہو گئی۔ mere bhāī kī mangnī ho gaī. My brother has got engaged.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
منگنی (mangnī) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin maṅgnī, "betrothal", under Sanskrit mārgaṇa, "asking" (CDIAL 10073): the asking for a bride.

Lesson 5 of 5.
رخصت کرنا (rukhsat karnā) — to see off; to send off (a bride).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the wedding procession, then the Urdu for a bridegroom's veil of flowers.

You'll want to know: رخصت کرنا.
رخصت کرنا — rukhsat karnā — "to see off; to send off (a bride)".
رات کو سب نے دلہن کو رخصت کیا۔ rāt ko sab ne dulhan ko rukhsat kiyā. At night everyone saw the bride off.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے مہمانوں کو رخصت کیا, us ne mehmānoṅ ko rukhsat kiyā.

The word, taken apart.
رخصت کرنا (rukhsat karnā) is built from رخصت, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic rukhṣa, "leave, permission", and کرنا, "to do": to give someone leave to go.
