Latin, chapter 82: Table, Chair, Bed, Window.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
mēnsa, mēnsae — a table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fig, then the Latin for a nut.

You'll want to know: mēnsa, mēnsae.
mēnsa, mēnsae — "a table".

The word, taken apart.
mēnsa, "table", has no sure origin. It is often linked to mēnsus, "measured", the participle of mētior, "to measure", but the link is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
sella, sellae — a chair, a seat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a nut, then the Latin for a table.

You'll want to know: sella, sellae.
sella, sellae — "a chair, a seat".

The word, taken apart.
sella comes from an older sedla (Lewis & Short), built on Proto-Indo-European sed-, "to sit" (EtymDB 2.1), the root of sedeō, "to sit". English saddle is a cousin from the same root.

Lesson 3 of 5.
lectus, lectī — a bed, a couch.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a table, then the Latin for a chair.

You'll want to know: lectus, lectī.
lectus, lectī — "a bed, a couch".

The word, taken apart.
lectus is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European legh-, "to lie" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek lekhos, "bed", is a cousin (Lewis & Short), and so is English lie. It is a different word from lēctus, "chosen".

Lesson 4 of 5.
fenestra, fenestrae — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a chair, then the Latin for a bed.

You'll want to know: fenestra, fenestrae.
fenestra, fenestrae — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
fenestra has no sure origin; it is often thought to be a loan from Etruscan, and the old link with Greek phainō, "to show", that Lewis & Short give is not generally accepted. English defenestrate is built on it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
cubiculum, cubiculī — a bedroom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a bed, then the Latin for a window.

You'll want to know: cubiculum, cubiculī.
cubiculum, cubiculī — "a bedroom". English cubicle.

The word, taken apart.
cubiculum is built on cubō, "to lie down" (Lewis & Short), with -culum, an ending for a place or tool: a room to lie down in.
