# décembre — December

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The twelfth month, and it says **ten**. This is the last and largest
of the four miscounts, and after it the year closes.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FR-LEX-DECEMBRE-07]; assesses=[] -->

- **décembre** = *day-SAHM-bruh*. The **é** is the bright *ay* of **février**,
  the **c** before *e* is an *s*, and the **em** is nasal.
- The familiar *-bruh* closes it for the fourth and final time.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**décembre** is Latin **december**, "the **tenth**," from **decem**.

That root is one of the most productive you have met. It gave you the number
**dix**; it gives English **decimal**, **decade** and **decimate**; and it
reached an American pocket as the **dime**, through Old French *disme* from
Latin *decima*, "a tenth part."

So every December, in French and English alike, you say the Roman word for ten
and mean the twelfth month. Spanish carries the identical fault: *diciembre* is
*decem* too.
