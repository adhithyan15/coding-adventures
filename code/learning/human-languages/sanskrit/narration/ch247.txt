Lesson 1 of 5.
बाणः (bāṇaḥ) — an arrow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for wild honey, then the Sanskrit for a sweet dish.

You'll want to know: बाणः.
बाणः — bāṇaḥ — "an arrow".

The word, taken apart.
bāṇa is a reed-shaft and so an arrow, already in the Rigveda (CDIAL 9203). Its origin is uncertain: CDIAL reports proposed Austroasiatic sources without accepting one. It lives on as Hindi bān, "arrow".

You'll want to know: खड्गः.
खड्गः — khaḍgaḥ — "a sword".

The word, taken apart.
khaḍga is a sword, and also the rhinoceros (MW). MW suggests, with a question mark, a root √khaḍ for √khaṇḍ, "to break", so its origin is uncertain; CDIAL 3786 takes the rhinoceros word as non-Aryan. Bengali khāg means "sword" (CDIAL 3787).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कवचम् (kavacam) — armour.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an arrow, then the Sanskrit for a sword.

You'll want to know: कवचम्.
कवचम् — kavacam — "armour".

The word, taken apart.
kavaca is a coat of mail or jacket, in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa. CDIAL 2957 follows Mayrhofer in taking it from Dravidian, while MW suggests a root √ku. MW also gives it for a mystical syllable used as an amulet.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ध्वजः (dhvajaḥ) — a flag.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a sword, then the Sanskrit for armour.

You'll want to know: ध्वजः.
ध्वजः — dhvajaḥ — "a flag".

The word, taken apart.
dhvaja is a banner or flag, and also a flag-staff, already in the Rigveda (CDIAL 6897). MW takes it from a short form dhvaj, "banner", which it derives, with a question mark, from √dhū, "to shake", and √aj, "to drive", so its origin is uncertain.

Lesson 5 of 5.
पटहः (paṭahaḥ) — a drum.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for armour, then the Sanskrit for a flag.

You'll want to know: पटहः.
पटहः — paṭahaḥ — "a drum".

The word, taken apart.
paṭaha is a kettledrum or war-drum (MW). Its origin is disputed: CDIAL 7696 lists a Munda source and a Dravidian one, both with a question mark, and calls a third derivation, from prahṛta, "struck", very doubtful. Bengali paṛā, "drum", comes from it.
