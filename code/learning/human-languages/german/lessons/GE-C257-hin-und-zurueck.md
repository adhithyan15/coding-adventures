# hin und zurück — there and back

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C111-FAHRT111-02, GE-LEX-C93-ADJ5-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "the ticket". (*Die Fahrkarte.*) And "simple, easy".
(*Einfach.*) At a ticket counter, *einfach* has a second job.

## You'll want to know: hin und zurück
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-HIN-UND-ZURUECK-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C148-VERB148-01, GE-LEX-C193-THING193-02] -->

**hin und zurück** — "there and back", a return: *hin oont tsoo-RÜCK*.

The counter question is short, and so is the answer:

> *Einfach oder hin und zurück?* — One way or return?
> *Hin und zurück, bitte.* — A return, please.
> *Was kostet das?* — How much is that?

**Einfach** at the counter means a single, one way. **Hin** is "there,
away from here", and **zurück** is "back". The return half of the journey
is the word you already have: *die Rückfahrt*.

## Grammar Lens: nach for a city, zum for a place in town
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-FAHRKARTE-NACH-02]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-ZUM-ZUR-02, GE-LEX-C174-VERB174-02, GE-LEX-C192-THING192-05, GE-LEX-WELCHER-01] -->

A ticket goes **nach** a city, or a country that takes no article:

> *Eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin, bitte.* — A ticket to Berlin, please.
> *Einmal nach Hamburg, hin und zurück.* — One return to Hamburg.

Inside the town, you went *zum Bahnhof* and *zur Post*. Between towns, you
travel *nach Berlin*.

Then the two questions for the platform:

> *Wann fährt der Zug ab?* — When does the train leave?
> *Von welchem Gleis?* — From which platform?

In Switzerland the ticket is often *das Billett*, and a return is
*retour*: *einmal Bern retour*. In Austria, too, a return is often *hin und
retour*. *Fahrkarte* is understood everywhere.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C193-THING193-02] -->

**zurück** is usually explained as *zu*, "to", + *Rück-*, "back": in
Middle High German, *ze rucke*, "to the back". The *Rück-* is the same as
in *der Rücken* and *die Rückfahrt*; EtymDB 2.1 lists *zurück* beside
Dutch *terug*, built the same way, but does not take it apart.

**hin** is listed by EtymDB beside English *hence*, "from here"; both point
away from where the speaker stands.
