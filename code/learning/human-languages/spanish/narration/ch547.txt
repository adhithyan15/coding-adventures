Spanish, chapter 547: Los novios.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el novio — the boyfriend; the groom.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the grandson, then the Spanish for the nappy.

You'll want to know: el novio.
el novio — "the boyfriend; the groom".
Su novio es de Bogotá. Her boyfriend is from Bogotá.
On the wedding day el novio is the groom, and los novios are the couple.

The word, taken apart.
novio is inherited from spoken Latin novius 'newly married' (EtymDB), built on novus 'new'.

Lesson 3 of 5.
comprometerse — to get engaged; to commit oneself.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the boyfriend, then the Spanish for the girlfriend.

You'll want to know: comprometerse.
comprometerse — "to get engaged; to commit oneself".
Se comprometieron en Navidad. They got engaged at Christmas.
Comprometerse a + infinitive is to promise to: Me comprometo a ayudar.

The word, taken apart.
comprometerse comes from Latin comprōmittere 'to promise each other' (Lewis & Short), built on com- and prōmittere 'to promise', from mittere 'to send'.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the girlfriend, then the Spanish for to get engaged.

You'll want to know: el compromiso.
el compromiso — "the engagement; the commitment".
Anunciaron su compromiso en la cena. They announced their engagement at dinner.
A false friend: compromise is el acuerdo or la concesión.

The word, taken apart.
compromiso comes from Latin comprōmissum 'a mutual promise' (EtymDB), built on prōmittere 'to promise', from mittere 'to send'.
