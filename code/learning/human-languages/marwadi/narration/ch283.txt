Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 283: Pearls and Embroidery.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
मोती (motī) — a pearl.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a garland, then the Marwadi for to string beads.

You'll want to know: मोती.
मोती — motī — "a pearl".
यो मोती घणो महंगो है। yo motī ghaṇo mahaṅgo hai. This pearl is very expensive.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
मोती (motī) is inherited from Sanskrit mauktika, "a pearl"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari motī, "pearl" (CDIAL 10365).

Lesson 3 of 5.
जड़णो (jaṛṇo) — to set (a stone); to inlay.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for a pearl, then the Marwadi for to pierce.

You'll want to know: जड़णो.
जड़णो — jaṛṇo — "to set (a stone); to inlay".
काको अंगूठी माथै मोती जड़ै है। kāko aṅgūṭhī māthai motī jaṛai hai. My uncle sets a pearl on a ring.
Past (I …): म्हैं मोती जड़्यो, mhaĩ motī jaṛyo.

The word, taken apart.
जड़णो (jaṛṇo) is inherited from a reconstructed jaḍati, "joins, sets", which CDIAL takes from Dravidian; it lists Old Marwari jaṛāū, "inlaid" (CDIAL 5091).

Lesson 4 of 5.
कसीदो (kasīdo) — embroidery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to pierce, then the Marwadi for to inlay.

You'll want to know: कसीदो.
कसीदो — kasīdo — "embroidery".
यो कसीदो सुंदर है। yo kasīdo sundar hai. This embroidery is beautiful.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
कसीदो (kasīdo) is usually traced to Persian kashīda, "drawn out", used of embroidered work; Marwadi gives it the -ो ending of a masculine noun.

Lesson 5 of 5.
सजावणो (sajāvṇo) — to decorate.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marwadi for to inlay, then the Marwadi for embroidery.

You'll want to know: सजावणो.
सजावणो — sajāvṇo — "to decorate".
छोरी घर सजावै है। chhorī ghar sajāvai hai. The girl decorates the house.
Past (I …): म्हैं घर सजायो, mhaĩ ghar sajāyo.

The word, taken apart.
सजावणो (sajāvṇo) is inherited from Sanskrit sajjayati, "equips, prepares"; CDIAL lists Old Marwari sājaï, "adorns", and Hindi sajānā, "to put in order, decorate" (CDIAL 13091).
