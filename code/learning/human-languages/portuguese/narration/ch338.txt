Portuguese, chapter 338: Discordo de ti.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
o desacordo — the disagreement.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to persuade, then the Portuguese for the firm belief.

You'll want to know: o desacordo.
o desacordo — "the disagreement".
Estamos em desacordo sobre quase tudo. We disagree about almost everything.
Estar em desacordo — to disagree.

The word, taken apart.
desacordo is built from des- and acordo "agreement", from the verb acordar in its old sense "to agree", usually traced to Vulgar Latin accordāre, a formation on Latin cor, cordis "heart" (Lewis & Short).

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

Lesson 2 of 5.
discordar — to disagree.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the firm belief, then the Portuguese for the disagreement.

You'll want to know: discordar.
discordar — "to disagree".
Discordo de ti: o filme não é nada aborrecido. I disagree with you: the film is not boring at all.
Discordar de alguém — to disagree with someone. Politely: permite-me discordar.

The word, taken apart.
discordar is a learned borrowing from Latin discordāre "to be at variance, to differ" (Lewis & Short), from discors, built from dis- "apart" and cor, cordis "heart".

Lesson 3 of 5.
objetar — to object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the disagreement, then the Portuguese for to disagree.

You'll want to know: objetar.
objetar — "to object".
Ninguém objetou quando ela propôs uma pausa. Nobody objected when she suggested a break.
Objetar que… — to object that… Formal; AO90 spelling, older objectar.

The word, taken apart.
objetar is a learned borrowing from Latin obiectāre "to throw before or against, to set against" (Lewis & Short), formed on obicere, built from ob "against" and iacere "to throw".

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: a objeção.
a objeção — "the objection".
Alguma objeção? Então começamos. Any objections? Then we begin.
Fazer uma objeção — to raise an objection. Older spelling objecção.

The word, taken apart.
objeção is a learned borrowing from Latin obiectiō "a throwing or putting before" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), from obicere "to throw against", built on iacere "to throw".

Lesson 5 of 5.
contradizer — to contradict.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to object, then the Portuguese for the objection.

You'll want to know: contradizer.
contradizer — "to contradict".
O meu irmão contradiz tudo o que eu digo. My brother contradicts everything I say.
Conjugated like dizer: contradigo, contradizes, contradiz.

The word, taken apart.
contradizer is a learned borrowing from Latin contrādīcere "to speak against" (EtymDB), built from contrā "against" and dīcere "to say"; Portuguese reshaped it on dizer.
