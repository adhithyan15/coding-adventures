Hindi, chapter 323: Victory and Defeat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
हराना (harānā) — to defeat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for an arrow, then the Hindi for for the most part.

You'll want to know: हराना.
हराना — harānā — "to defeat".
छोटी सेना ने बड़ी सेना को हराया। choṭī senā ne baṛī senā ko harāyā. The small army defeated the big one.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दुश्मन को हराया, usne duśman ko harāyā.

The word, taken apart.
हराना (harānā) is the causative partner of हारना, to lose, and CDIAL 14061 lists both under Sanskrit hārayati: to make the other side lose.

Lesson 2 of 5.
पछाड़ना (pachāṛnā) — to throw down; to overpower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for for the most part, then the Hindi for to defeat.

You'll want to know: पछाड़ना.
पछाड़ना — pachāṛnā — "to throw down; to overpower".
एक सिपाही ने दो दुश्मनों को पछाड़ा। ek sipāhī ne do duśmanoṁ ko pachāṛā. One soldier overpowered two enemies.
Past, with ने (he or she …): उसने दुश्मन को पछाड़ा, usne duśman ko pachāṛā.

The word, taken apart.
पछाड़ना (pachāṛnā) is inherited from pracchaṭ, a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 8493), with the sense of knocking down. It is the wrestler's word: to throw the other to the ground, and so to beat them.

Lesson 3 of 5.
विजय (vijay) — a victory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to defeat, then the Hindi for to throw down.

You'll want to know: विजय.
विजय — vijay — "a victory".
विजय के बाद शहर में जुलूस निकला। vijay ke bād śahar meṁ julūs niklā. After the victory a procession went through the city.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
विजय (vijay) is Sanskrit vijaya, "victory" (Monier-Williams), the formal word beside जीत. Hindi makes it feminine.

Lesson 4 of 5.
शहीद (śahīd) — a martyr.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to throw down, then the Hindi for a victory.

You'll want to know: शहीद.
शहीद — śahīd — "a martyr".
युद्ध में सौ सिपाही शहीद हुए। yuddh meṁ sau sipāhī śahīd hue. A hundred soldiers fell in the war.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
शहीद (śahīd) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic shahīd, "a witness" and then one who dies for a cause. शहीद होना is to fall for one's country.

Lesson 5 of 5.
संधि (sandhi) — a treaty; a peace pact.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a victory, then the Hindi for a martyr.

You'll want to know: संधि.
संधि — sandhi — "a treaty; a peace pact".
आख़िर में दोनों राजाओं ने संधि की। ākhir meṁ donõ rājāoṁ ne sandhi kī. In the end the two kings made a treaty.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
संधि (sandhi) is Sanskrit saṁdhi, "a joining, an agreement" (Monier-Williams). Grammar uses the same word for the joining of sounds; history uses it for the joining of two sides.
