Malayalam, chapter 41: Pointing, and Asking.
7 lessons. All 7 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 7.
ഇത് (itŭ) — this one — the thing near me.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you POINT at them.

You'll want to know: ഇത്.
ഇത് — itŭ — this one — the thing near me.
Say it, and picture the thing it lands on as you say it. That is the whole word: it does not mean anything on its own, it means whatever the speaker is pointing at.
Its partner, the far one, comes in the next lesson. The two of them differ by one sound at the front — i- for near — and that is not a coincidence. It is the whole system, and the last lesson of this chapter says so.

The word, taken apart.
ഇത് (itŭ) is native Dravidian: the old near-pointing base with an ending meaning "thing". DEDR 410 lists Malayalam itu with Tamil itu.

Lesson 5 of 7.
ആര് (ārŭ) — who? — asking about a person.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can already name things. This is how you ASK about them.

You'll want to know: ആര്.
ആര് — ārŭ — who? — asking about a person.
Say it with an asking voice. It points at no one: it asks your listener to name the person.
ārŭ opens with a long ā-. Do not hear it as the short a- of far that അത് atŭ and അവിടെ aviṭe share: it has nothing to do with distance. Tamil, next door, says yār; Malayalam's word is the same one with its first y- worn away.

The word, taken apart.
ആര് (ārŭ) is native Dravidian, the question stem yā- worn down to ā-. DEDR 5151 lists it with Tamil yār, ār and Kannada yār, ār.

[your turn — say: it once with a word you already know after it]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say neñcŭ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What is ārŭ? (who? — asking about a person.) Does its long ā- mean far, like the short a- of atŭ? (No — it is the same word as Tamil's yār, its y- worn away; it asks about a person.)

Lesson 7 of 7.
i- / a- / e- — six words, one machine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the six words from this chapter out loud, in order. Listen to how they begin.

Grammar lens: one slot changes.
Five of these six words are three beginnings, reused; only the person question stands apart.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
meaning: near. word: ഇവിടെ iviṭe.
meaning: far. word: അവിടെ aviṭe.
meaning: question. word: എവിടെ eviṭe.
Malayalam spells the pattern out longest — ഇവിടെ (iviṭe), അവിടെ (aviṭe), എവിടെ (eviṭe) — which makes it the easiest to hear.
Change the front, and the meaning walks from near to far to a question. Nothing else in the word moves.
The same three beginnings run the thing-words too: ഇത് itŭ and അത് atŭ start exactly like the first two rows. This chapter did not teach you the matching question word for things — that one arrives later, and when it does you will already know what it has to start with.
This is worth more than the six words are. When you meet a new word in this family later, you will not have to be taught all three of it — you will be taught one and work out the others.

[your turn — say: the near word, then the far word, then the question word]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which beginning means near? (i-) Far? (a-) And which one turns the word into a question? (e-).
