Portuguese, chapter 308: Que susto!.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
o susto — the fright, the scare.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fracture, then the Portuguese for the plaster.

You'll want to know: o susto.
o susto — "the fright, the scare".
Que susto! Pensei que estava sozinho em casa. What a fright! I thought I was alone in the house.
Pregar um susto a alguém — to give someone a fright.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of susto is not settled; EtymDB lists it only beside Spanish susto.

What you've built.

assustar is built on susto "a fright"; EtymDB lists it beside Spanish asustar, and the origin is not settled.

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fright, then the Portuguese for to frighten.

You'll want to know: o pânico.
o pânico — "panic".
Houve pânico no centro comercial. There was panic in the shopping centre.
Entrar em pânico — to panic.

The word, taken apart.
pânico comes from Greek panikós "of Pan" (EtymDB), the god of the wild whose sudden cry was said to spread terror.

Lesson 4 of 6.
desmaiar — to faint.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to frighten, then the Portuguese for panic.

You'll want to know: desmaiar.
desmaiar — "to faint".
Com o calor, uma senhora desmaiou no autocarro. In the heat, a lady fainted on the bus.
O desmaio — the faint.

The word, taken apart.
desmaiar came from Old French desmaier (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin exmagāre "to rob of strength".

Lesson 5 of 6.
torcer — to twist, to sprain; to support (a team).

torcer — "to twist, to sprain; to support (a team)".
Torci o pé nas escadas. I twisted my ankle on the stairs.
Torcer por uma equipa — to support a team.

The word, taken apart.
torcer is inherited from Vulgar Latin torcēre, from Latin torquēre "to twist" (EtymDB).
