Mandarin Chinese, chapter 180: Coming Over.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
做客 (zuòkè) — to visit as a guest; to be a guest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to iron, then the Mandarin for a wardrobe.

You'll want to know: 做客.
做客 — zuòkè — "to visit as a guest; to be a guest".
我去他家做客喝了两杯茶 wǒ qù tā jiā zuòkè hē le liǎng bēi chá — I went round to his place and had two cups of tea.

The word, taken apart.
做客 (zuòkè) is built from 做 'to do, to be' and 客 'guest'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese khaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'guest'; 做 has no form in that dictionary, so the history of the first part is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about plans.

Lesson 2 of 5.
zhǔrén — host; owner.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a wardrobe, then the Mandarin for to visit as a guest.

You'll want to know: zhǔrén.
zhǔrén — "host; owner".
zhǔrén hěn rèqíng — The host was very welcoming.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhǔrén is built from zhǔ 'to be in charge' and 人 'person', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyuX and nyin (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): the person in charge of the house.

Lesson 3 of 5.
kànwàng — to call on; to visit (a person).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to visit as a guest, then the Mandarin for the host.

You'll want to know: kànwàng.
kànwàng — "to call on; to visit (a person)".
zhōumò wǒ qù kànwàng wǒ de lǎoshī — At the weekend I'm going to call on my teacher.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
kànwàng is built from 看 'to look' and wàng 'to gaze', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khanH and mjangH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second with this very word.

Lesson 4 of 5.
过来 (guòlai) — to come over.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for the host, then the Mandarin for to call on someone.

You'll want to know: 过来.
过来 — guòlai — "to come over".
你什么时候过来 nǐ shénme shíhou guòlai — When are you coming over?

The word, taken apart.
过来 (guòlai) is built from 过 'to pass' and 来 'to come', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kwaH and loj (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): to pass over to here. In the word the second loses its tone.

The word, taken apart.
yuē is inherited from Middle Chinese 'jak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to bind' and 'sparing': to bind someone to a time.
