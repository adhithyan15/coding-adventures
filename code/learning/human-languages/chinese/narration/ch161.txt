Mandarin Chinese, chapter 161: Rice and Meat.
6 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
大米 (dàmǐ) — uncooked rice; rice grain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to get the jobs done, then the Mandarin for to look after.

You'll want to know: 大米.
大米 — dàmǐ — "uncooked rice; rice grain".
我去买大米 wǒ qù mǎi dàmǐ — I'm going to buy rice.
The new character, in pieces: 米 (mǐ) — six strokes: two short strokes slanting in toward the middle, a horizontal, a vertical through it, then from the crossing a stroke falling to the left and one falling to the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
大米 (dàmǐ) is built from 大 'big' and 米 (mǐ) 'grain', both inherited from Middle Chinese, daH and mejX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as the seed of a cereal.

Script — a cross with two strokes above and two below.
米 (mǐ).
One piece, six strokes, built round a cross.
Two short strokes first, slanting in toward the middle: a dot from the upper left, then a short stroke from the upper right falling to the left.
The cross. A long horizontal, then a vertical down through its middle.
The legs, from the crossing. A stroke falling to the lower left, then one falling to the lower right.
2 + 2 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 米 (mǐ), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 米 (mǐ)? (Six.) Which two come first? (The two short strokes above the cross.) Which come last? (The two legs, left, then right.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 米 (mǐ), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 6.
fèn — measure word for a serving; portion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to look after, then the Mandarin for uncooked rice.

You'll want to know: fèn.
fèn — "measure word for a serving; portion".
wǒ yào liǎng fèn mǐfàn — Two portions of rice, please.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
fèn has no older form of this word in any dictionary we use: the Guangyun lists the character only as pin, glossed 'refined', which is neither this sound nor this sense. Its history is not settled.
