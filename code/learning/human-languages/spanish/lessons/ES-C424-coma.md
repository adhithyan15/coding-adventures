# la coma — where Spanish and English disagree about lists

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el punto*. This mark is its smaller relative: it divides
without finishing.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-ORTH-C424-02]; assesses=[] -->

**la coma** — "the comma." From Greek *kómma*, "a piece cut off," which is
what a comma does to a sentence.

In a list, the items are divided by commas — and **in front of the final *y*,
Spanish writes no comma**:

> Tengo un hermano, una hermana **y** un perro.

English allows *a brother, a sister, and a dog*. Spanish does not put a comma
there. This is one of the few orthographic points where a habit carried from
English is visible to a marker.

## Grammar Lens: when y becomes e, and o becomes u
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-02] -->

The joining word itself changes shape to avoid two identical vowel sounds
colliding:

| written | becomes | before a word starting with |
|---|---|---|
| y | **e** | *i-* or *hi-* |
| o | **u** | *o-* or *ho-* |

- padres **e** hijos — parents and children
- siete **u** ocho — seven or eight

You met this already without being told its name: *simpática **e**
inteligente* in the character chapter changed for exactly this reason.
