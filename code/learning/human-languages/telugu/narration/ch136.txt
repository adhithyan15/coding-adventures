Telugu, chapter 136: Guava, Sugar, Tall, Short, Heavy.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
జామపండు (jāmapaṇḍu) — a guava.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a lemon, then the Telugu for grapes.

You'll want to know: జామపండు.
జామపండు — jāmapaṇḍu — "a guava".

The word, taken apart.
జామపండు (jāmapaṇḍu) is jāma, usually traced to Sanskrit jambū, "the rose-apple tree" (CDIAL 5131), a name passed on to the guava, and native paṇḍu, "fruit" (DEDR 4004), which DEDR compares with Sanskrit phala.

Lesson 3 of 5.
పొడవు (poḍavu) — tall, long.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for a guava, then the Telugu for sugar.
[pause 2 seconds]
An adjective goes before the noun and does not change: పాత పుస్తకం, కొత్త పుస్తకం.

You'll want to know: పొడవు.
పొడవు — poḍavu — "tall, long".

The word, taken apart.
పొడవు (poḍavu) is native Dravidian, one word with Kannada poḍe (DEDR 4484). DEDR lists Telugu poḍavu, "height, length; tall, long".

Lesson 4 of 5.
పొట్టి (poṭṭi) — short.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Telugu for sugar, then the Telugu for tall.

You'll want to know: పొట్టి.
పొట్టి — poṭṭi — "short".

The word, taken apart.
పొట్టి (poṭṭi) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil poṭi, "that which is small", and Kannada puṭṭa, "smallness" (DEDR 4259). DEDR compares Sanskrit puṭṭ-, "to be small" (CDIAL 8256), and Prakrit phiḍḍa, "dwarf"; CDIAL, after Burrow, takes the Sanskrit from Dravidian.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: బరువు.
బరువు — baruvu — "heavy".

The word, taken apart.
The origin of బరువు (baruvu) is uncertain. DEDR does not list it. It is often linked to Sanskrit bhāra, "burden, weight" (CDIAL 9459), but the link is not settled.
