Hindi, chapter 387: Very, Extremely.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
बेहद (behad) — extremely, beyond measure.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for stubbornness, then the Hindi for eligible.

You'll want to know: बेहद.
बेहद — behad — "extremely, beyond measure".
अगर आज भी बेहद गर्मी रही तो स्कूल बंद रहेंगे। agar āj bhī behad garmī rahī to skūl band raheṅge. If it is extremely hot again today, the schools will stay shut.

The word, taken apart.
बेहद (behad) is usually traced to Persian behadd, "without limit". It joins Persian be-, "without", to Arabic ḥadd, "a limit".

Before the new one: say the Hindi for eligible, then the Hindi for extremely.

अत्यंत — atyant — "extremely (formal)".
अगर रोगी की हालत अत्यंत गंभीर होगी तो उसे अस्पताल ले जाना पड़ेगा। agar rogī kī hālat atyant gambhīr hogī to use aspatāl le jānā paṛegā. If the patient's condition is extremely serious, he will have to be taken to hospital.

The word, taken apart.
अत्यंत (atyant) is a tatsama, borrowed from Sanskrit atyanta, "excessive, very great" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ज़रा (zarā) — a little, a bit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for extremely, then the Hindi for extremely (formal).

You'll want to know: ज़रा.
ज़रा — zarā — "a little, a bit".
ज़रा धीरे बोलो, वरना बच्चा जाग जाएगा। zarā dhīre bolo, varnā baccā jāg jāegā. Speak a little more quietly, or the baby will wake up.

The word, taken apart.
ज़रा (zarā) is usually traced to Arabic dharra, "a speck, an atom", which reached Hindi through Persian. Persian says zarra; the Hindi sense is a little.
