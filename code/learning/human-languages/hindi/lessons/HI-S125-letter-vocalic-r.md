# ऋ — the independent vowel waiting inside ऋतु

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-117] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Find **च** in **चार**. Say *ca*. Then put the word away.

One new character comes next. It gets its own short lesson before the seasons
lesson asks you to read or write it.

## Script you'll notice: ऋ and its Hindi value
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-RECOG-125]; assesses=[] -->

**ऋ** is an **independent vowel**. The careful transliteration is **ṛ**. In
modern Hindi words such as **ऋतु**, you will normally hear it as **ri**, so
**ऋतु** is said *ritu*. Keep both labels honest: **ṛ** tells you which inherited
Devanagari vowel this is; **ri** tells you what ordinary Hindi speech does with
it.

Look for the right stem and headline, then the hooked and rounded body hanging
to their left. Do not confuse **ऋ** with **र**: **र** is the consonant *ra*;
**ऋ** stands on its own as a vowel letter.

## Writing: ऋ — supported formation
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-SCRIPT-WRITE-125]; assesses=[] -->

Keep **ऋ** visible for this first pass.

- **1.** sweep the short upper-left shoulder right, then turn down-left through
  its hook
- **2.** lift and curve around the rounded middle body, continuing into the
  lower rightward tail
- **3.** lift and draw the right stem from top to bottom
- **4.** lift and draw the headline from left to right

**Pen lifts: 3.** This is one sourced learner order, not a claim that every
school joins the strokes identically. Source: Saurmandal, “Devanagari ऋ stroke
order.svg,” panels 1–4, Wikimedia Commons, 5 August 2023.
