## You'll want to know first — one word
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-BARAN]; assesses=[] -->

> **باران** — *bârân* — **rain**

Five letters, all already yours: **ب ا ر ا ن** — *b*, long *â*, *r*, long
*â*, *n*.

## The word, taken apart — a cousin only inside the family
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-ETYMON-BARAN]; assesses=[] -->

**باران** continues Middle Persian **wārān**, from Indo-Iranian
\**wáHr*, "water, rain," itself from Indo-European \**weh₁r-*, "water."
Its cousins stayed close to home: Avestan **vāra**, "rain," and Sanskrit
**vār**, "water," share the identical root. English **rain** is not part of
this family at all — its own origin is disputed and confined to the
Germanic languages, with no secure Indo-European cousin beyond them — so, as
with **رفتن** and **گفتن** far earlier, the honest answer here is that no
English cousin is claimed.

## Grammar Lens: what small talk about the sky follows
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-HAL-E-SHOMA, FA-PRAGMATICS-WELLBEING-FORMAL, FA-LEX-CHETOR, FA-LEX-KHUBAM-REPLY, FA-SCRIPT-KHUBAM] -->

**خوبم، ممنون** — a shape a sky word can now attach to.
