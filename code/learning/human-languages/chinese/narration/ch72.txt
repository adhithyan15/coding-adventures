Mandarin Chinese, chapter 72: Getting About.
7 lessons. You can do the first one in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
火车 (huǒchē) — train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for salty, then the Mandarin for to order food.

You'll want to know: 火车.
火车 — huǒchē — "train".
这儿的火车很长 zhèr de huǒchē hěn cháng — The trains here are long.
The new character, in pieces: 火 (huǒ) — a dot on each side, and 人 between them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
火车 (huǒchē) is built from 火 (huǒ) 'fire' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xwaX and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription): a fire carriage, from the days of steam.

What you've built.
One more word for times and partings.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: huǒchē]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: huǒchē, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: zhèr de huǒchē hěn cháng — The trains here are long.]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Mandarin for salty, then the Mandarin for to order food, then say huǒchē again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does 火车 (huǒchē) mean? ("Train".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 7.
火 (huǒ) — two sparks around 人.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 8 seconds for the answer]
人 大.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — rén, dà]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two sparks around 人.
火 (huǒ).
One piece, four strokes.
A short dot on the left, slanting down to the right.
A short left-falling stroke on the right, falling toward the middle.
人, two strokes: a long left-falling stroke from the top down to the lower left, then a long stroke falling to the right from its middle.
2 + 2 = four.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 火 (huǒ), all four, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 火 (huǒ)? (Four.) Which character you know is its last two strokes? (人.) What comes first? (The dot on the left.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 火 (huǒ), pinned stroke paths and medians.


Lesson 3 of 7.
开车 (kāichē) — to drive.

Before we start: this one needs your eyes, so it is not fully a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, because the lesson points at something written down. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to order food, then the Mandarin for train.

You'll want to know: 开车.
开车 — kāichē — "to drive".
明天我们开车去看山 míngtiān wǒmen kāichē qù kàn shān — Tomorrow we're driving out to see the mountains.
The new character, in pieces: 开 (kāi) — two bars, with two legs down through them. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
开车 (kāichē) is built from 开 (kāi) 'to open' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, khoj and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). Driving is not among the senses of the first there; opening up, setting going, is the likely step, but it is not settled.

What you've built.
One more word for times and partings.

二 车.
[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — èr, chē]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — two bars, two legs.
开 (kāi).
One piece, four strokes, the bars first.
A short horizontal on top, then a long horizontal below it.
A left-falling stroke from the upper bar, down through the lower one to the bottom left.
A vertical on the right, straight down through both bars.

Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 开 (kāi), all four, with the model in front of you]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 开 (kāi)? (Four.) Which come first, the bars or the legs? (The bars.) Which leg is straight? (The right one.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 开 (kāi), pinned stroke paths and medians.
