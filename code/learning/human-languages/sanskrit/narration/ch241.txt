Sanskrit, chapter 241: For the sake of, By which, By that, That, However.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
कृते (kṛte) — for the sake of.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for daily, then the Sanskrit for constantly, always.

You'll want to know: कृते.
कृते — kṛte — "for the sake of".

The word, taken apart.
kṛte comes from kṛta, "done", the past participle of the root √kṛ, "to do" (MW); it is its locative. "In the doing of", and so "for the sake of"; mama kṛte is "for me".

Lesson 2 of 5.
येन (yena) — by which, so that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for constantly, always, then the Sanskrit for for the sake of.

You'll want to know: येन.
येन — yena — "by which, so that".

The word, taken apart.
yena comes from the relative pronoun stem ya-, "which", as its instrumental (MW): "by which", and so "so that". It pairs with tena, "by that", from the next lesson.

Lesson 3 of 5.
तेन (tena) — by that, therefore.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for for the sake of, then the Sanskrit for by which.

You'll want to know: तेन.
तेन — tena — "by that, therefore".

The word, taken apart.
tena is the instrumental of the pronoun stem ta-, "that", whose neuter is tad (MW). The stem is inherited from Indo-European: MW compares Greek to, "the", and Gothic thata, the cousin of English that.

Lesson 4 of 5.
यत् (yat) — that (introducing a clause).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for by which, then the Sanskrit for by that.

You'll want to know: यत्.
यत् — yat — "that (introducing a clause)".

The word, taken apart.
yat is the neuter form of the relative pronoun stem ya-, "which, what" (MW). Used at the head of a clause it means "that", the way English that introduces "he said that...". Hindi jo, "who, which", comes from the same stem (CDIAL 10391).

Lesson 5 of 5.
परन्तु (parantu) — however, but.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for by that, then the Sanskrit for that.

You'll want to know: परन्तु.
परन्तु — parantu — "however, but".

The word, taken apart.
parantu is built on param, "beyond, further, but", from para, "far, other", and tu, "but, on the other hand" (MW). Two words for "but" joined into one.
