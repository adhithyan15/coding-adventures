French, chapter 506: Le résultat.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
le match nul — draw, tie (in sport).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for pitch, then the French for to score.

You'll want to know: le match nul.
le match nul — "draw, tie (in sport)".
Les deux équipes ont fait match nul. The two teams drew.
Faire match nul — to draw.

The word, taken apart.
le match nul joins match, borrowed from English, and nul "null, none", from Latin nūllus "not any" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
qualifier — (se qualifier) to qualify; to describe as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for ranking, then the French for to knock out.

You'll want to know: qualifier.
qualifier — "(se qualifier) to qualify; to describe as".
L'équipe s'est qualifiée pour la finale. The team qualified for the final.
Reflexive in sport: se qualifier pour. Also to describe: qualifier quelqu'un de menteur.

The word, taken apart.
qualifier is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin quālificāre, built from quālis "of what kind" and facere "to make" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
le vainqueur — winner, victor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to knock out, then the French for (se qualifier) to qualify.

You'll want to know: le vainqueur.
le vainqueur — "winner, victor".
Le vainqueur du match jouera la finale. The winner of the match will play in the final.
Traditionally the same form for a woman: elle est sortie vainqueur; la vainqueure is also found today, especially in Québec.

The word, taken apart.
le vainqueur is built on vaincre "to defeat", which is inherited from Latin vincere "to conquer" (Lewis & Short).
