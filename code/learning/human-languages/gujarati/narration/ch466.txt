Lesson 1 of 5.
કાયર (kāyar) — cowardly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for egotism, then the Gujarati for to get used to.

You'll want to know: કાયર.
કાયર — kāyar — "cowardly".
કાયર માણસ જોખમ જોઈને ભાગી જાય છે. kāyar māṇas jokham joīne bhāgī jāy che. A coward runs away at the sight of danger.

The word, taken apart.
કાયર (kāyar) is inherited from Sanskrit kātara, "confused, discouraged, cowardly"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "timid" (CDIAL 3027).

આગ વખતે દાદાએ ઘણું શૌર્ય બતાવ્યું. āg vakhte dādāe ghaṇũ śaurya batāvyũ. During the fire my grandfather showed great valour.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ખમીર (khamīr) — grit; mettle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for fearless, then the Gujarati for valour.

You'll want to know: ખમીર.
ખમીર — khamīr — "grit; mettle".
મારા દાદામાં ઘણું ખમીર હતું. mārā dādāmā̃ ghaṇũ khamīr hatũ. My grandfather had a lot of grit.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ખમીર (khamīr) is usually traced to Arabic xamīr, "leaven", through Persian. Gujarati also uses it for the spirit that makes a person rise to a challenge, probably a picture of dough rising.

તોફાનમાં પણ તેની હિંમત ડગમગી નહીં. tophānmā̃ paṇ tenī himmat ḍagmagī nahī̃. Even in the storm his courage did not waver.
Past (it …): તે હોડી ડગમગી, te hoḍī ḍagmagī.

ડગમગવું (ḍagmagvũ) goes back to a reconstructed ḍag-, "tremble"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to tremble" (CDIAL 5522).
