Latin, chapter 334: Sacrificium.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sacerdōs, sacerdōtis — a priest, a priestess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a feast, then the Latin for to feast.

You'll want to know: sacerdōs, sacerdōtis.
sacerdōs, sacerdōtis — "a priest, a priestess".
Sacerdōs ante āram stābat. The priest was standing before the altar.
Masculine or feminine, 3rd declension, from sacer, 'holy'.

The word, taken apart.
sacerdōs is built on sacer, "holy" (EtymDB), with a second part whose origin is not settled: one who performs holy things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
victima, victimae — a sacrificial animal, a victim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to feast, then the Latin for a priest.

You'll want to know: victima, victimae.
victima, victimae — "a sacrificial animal, a victim".
Victima alba ad āram ducta est. A white victim was led to the altar.
Feminine. A larger animal for sacrifice, such as an ox. English victim.

The word, taken apart.
victima is a Latin word whose origin is not settled; Lewis & Short suggest, with doubt, a root seen in vigeō, "to be strong". English victim comes from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
immolō, immolāre — to sacrifice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a priest, then the Latin for a sacrificial animal.

You'll want to know: immolō, immolāre.
immolō, immolāre — "to sacrifice".
Sacerdōs taurum deae immolāvit. The priest sacrificed a bull to the goddess.
immolō, immolāre, immolāvī, immolātum: first, to sprinkle the victim with salted grain.

The word, taken apart.
immolō is built on mola, "meal, salted grain", with in- (Lewis & Short): to sprinkle the victim with sacred meal before the sacrifice.

Lesson 4 of 5.
voveō, vovēre — to vow, to promise solemnly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a sacrificial animal, then the Latin for to sacrifice.

You'll want to know: voveō, vovēre.
voveō, vovēre — "to vow, to promise solemnly".
Dux deae templum novum vōvit. The general vowed a new temple to the goddess.
voveō, vovēre, perfect vōvī: short o in the present, long in the perfect and in its noun.

The word, taken apart.
voveō is inherited; EtymDB traces it to a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "to vow", though Lewis & Short call the origin doubtful. English vow and vote come from its noun vōtum.

Lesson 5 of 5.
vōtum, vōtī — a vow; a wish.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sacrifice, then the Latin for to vow.

You'll want to know: vōtum, vōtī.
vōtum, vōtī — "a vow; a wish".
Post naufragium nautae vōtum solvērunt. After the shipwreck the sailors fulfilled their vow.
Neuter, from voveō. Vōtum solvere: to keep a vow. English vote and vow.

The word, taken apart.
vōtum is built on voveō, "to vow" (EtymDB): a thing vowed. English vote comes from it, and vow through French.
