Kannada, chapter 118: Glasses, Clock, Bag, Pen, Paper.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ಕನ್ನಡಕ (kannaḍaka) — glasses, spectacles.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for soap, then the Kannada for a towel.

You'll want to know: ಕನ್ನಡಕ.
ಕನ್ನಡಕ — kannaḍaka — "glasses, spectacles".

The word, taken apart.
ಕನ್ನಡಕ (kannaḍaka) is native Dravidian, one word with Tamil kaṇṇāṭi (DEDR 1182). DEDR lists kanaḍaka, "pair of spectacles", beside kannaḍi, "mirror, lens". The entry explains the Tamil word as kaṇ, "eye", with āṭi, "mirror".

[once you have stopped driving — read: the digits aloud — ೨, ೪, ೫, ೬, ೭, ೮, ೯, ೦]

You'll want to know: ಗಡಿಯಾರ.
ಗಡಿಯಾರ — gaḍiyāra — "a clock, a watch".

The word, taken apart.
ಗಡಿಯಾರ (gaḍiyāra) came from Hindustani ghaṛiyāl, "a gong, a clock". That goes back to Sanskrit ghaṭikā (CDIAL 4406), the small pot that marked time as a water-clock.

Lesson 4 of 5.
ಲೇಖನಿ (lēkhani) — a pen.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Kannada for a clock, then the Kannada for a bag.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say what the colon: and the brackets ( ) tell a reader.
[once you have stopped driving — find: ಠ, then say ṭha]

You'll want to know: ಲೇಖನಿ.
ಲೇಖನಿ — lēkhani — "a pen".

The word, taken apart.
ಲೇಖನಿ (lēkhani) is Sanskrit lekhanī, "instrument for scraping", taken over whole as a learned loan, a tatsama, with the final -ī shortened (CDIAL 11103). The tool that scratched letters became the pen.
