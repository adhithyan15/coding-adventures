Urdu, chapter 483: A New Baby.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حاملہ (hāmilā) — pregnant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to have (something) cut, then the Urdu for to trim.

You'll want to know: حاملہ.
حاملہ — hāmilā — "pregnant".
میری بہن حاملہ ہے۔ merī bahan hāmilā hai. My sister is pregnant.

The word, taken apart.
حاملہ (hāmilā) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ḥāmila, "carrying", and so "pregnant".

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: جننا.
جننا — jannā — "to give birth to".
بلی نے چار بچے جنے۔ billī ne chār bachche jane. The cat had four kittens.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے دو بچے جنے, us ne do bachche jane.

The word, taken apart.
جننا (jannā) is inherited from Sanskrit janáyati, "causes to be born, begets" (CDIAL 5102).

Lesson 3 of 5.
پیدا ہونا (paidā honā) — to be born.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for pregnant, then the Urdu for to give birth to.

You'll want to know: پیدا ہونا.
پیدا ہونا — paidā honā — "to be born".
میرا بھائی جنوری میں پیدا ہوا۔ merā bhāī janvarī meṅ paidā huā. My brother was born in January.
Past (she …): وہ لاہور میں پیدا ہوئی, voh lāhaur meṅ paidā huī.

The word, taken apart.
پیدا ہونا (paidā honā) is built from پیدا, usually traced to Persian paidā, "appearing, born", and ہونا, "to be".

Lesson 5 of 5.
نومولود (naumaulūd) — a newborn.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to be born, then the Urdu for birth.

You'll want to know: نومولود.
نومولود — naumaulūd — "a newborn".
نومولود بچہ دن بھر سوتا ہے۔ naumaulūd bachchā din bhar sotā hai. A newborn baby sleeps all day long.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
نومولود (naumaulūd) is built from Persian nau, "new", and Arabic maulūd, "born", the way it is usually traced.
